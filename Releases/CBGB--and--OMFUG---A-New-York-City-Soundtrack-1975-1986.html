A 101-track, 4-CD box set documenting the bands that performed at CBGB & OMFUG — the Bowery venue that served as the crucible of New York’s punk, new wave, no wave, and hardcore scenes from 1975 to 1986. Compiled by Rob Tannenbaum, with liner notes by Tannenbaum and project management by John Reed and Russell Beecher.

Scene Overview

The four discs follow a rough chronological arc through CBGB’s eleven-year lifespan:

  • CD 1 (1975–76): The earliest strata — proto-punk and glam-adjacent acts (The Dictators, Magic Tramps, Cherry Vanilla, Tuff Darts) alongside first-wave breakthroughs (Patti Smith, Ramones, Television, The Heartbreakers, Dead Boys, Talking Heads). The disc establishes CBGB’s unusual eclecticism from the outset.
  • CD 2 (1977–79): The scene’s commercial horizon expands. Blondie, Mink DeVille, and Tom Verlaine/Richard Lloyd solo tracks sit alongside no wave pioneers (Suicide, Mars, Theoretical Girls), R&B cross-pollination (Genya Ravan, Material), and Richard Hell & The Voidoids. James Chance & The Contortions close with a live “Jailhouse Rock.”
  • CD 3 (1979–82): Post-punk and no wave consolidation. The Cramps, DNA (4), The Lounge Lizards, The dB’s, Love of Life Orchestra, Polyrock, and Dark Day represent the more experimental wing. The disc ends with Minor Threat’s “Straight Edge” (0:47) — the shortest track and a genre-founding document.
  • CD 4 (1982–86): Hardcore’s ascendancy and the scene’s late phase. Bad Brains, Beastie Boys (early hardcore incarnation), Sonic Youth’s early noise, Bush Tetras, Rhys Chatham, and James Blood Ulmer. The set closes on The Ordinaires’ “Grace.”
