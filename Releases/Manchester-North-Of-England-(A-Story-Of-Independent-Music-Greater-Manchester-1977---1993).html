Overview

Manchester North Of England (A Story Of Independent Music Greater Manchester 1977–1993) is a deep-archive Cherry Red box set documenting the independent music output of Greater Manchester across sixteen years and seven discs. Each disc carries a subtitle taken from a track on that disc, and the sequence traces a rough chronological narrative from punk and post-punk (1977) through Madchester and the early rave/dance crossover (1991–1993), ending with a demo-era Oasis track.

The packaging is defined by images from two photographers who shot the Manchester scene throughout this period: Kevin Cummins, whose images of Joy Division in the snow became canonical documents of the post-punk era, and Paul Slattery, who shot the early Madchester period. The cover model credit — “Steven Morrissey” for Morrissey — situates the set within the broader Manchester mythology.

Disc 1 (subtitled “Cranked Up Really High”) is the densest archival layer: Buzzcocks, Slaughter And The Dogs, John Cooper Clarke, Joy Division, and a run of Factory/Rabid-era Manchester acts sit alongside now-obscure punk and early post-punk groups (The Nosebleeds, The Drones, Jilted John, X-O-Dus, Harlem Spirit). By Disc 7 (“Sons Of The Stage”), the tracklist opens into early-1990s dance and baggy territory: 808 State, The Chemical Brothers, Oasis’s “Columbia (Demo)”, and Sub Sub (later Doves).

The range of artists — from bona fide legends to one-release wonders — makes this an unusually comprehensive regional document. The 1977–1993 window deliberately begins before the Factory Records story starts and ends before Britpop consolidates the Manchester myth into a commercially legible brand.

A related earlier compilation, Manchester North Of England (Bop Cassettes, 1988), compiled by Sarah Champion, documents a smaller slice of the same scene from the same city but is a separate release.

Notable Tracks and Narrative Threads

Factory Records / Joy Division lineage

Disc 1’s “She’s Lost Control” by Joy Division (ID: 3898) is one of the seminal Factory 45s. From it, the Factory thread runs forward through New Order’s “Temptation” (Disc 3), Section 25’s “Looking From A Hilltop” (Disc 3), A Certain Ratio’s “The Fox” (Disc 2), Quando Quango’s “Love Tempo” (Disc 3), and The Durutti Column’s “Lips That Would Kiss” (Disc 2) — all Factory artists. The Haçienda, opened 1982, is name-checked directly in Disc 3’s subtitle “Love At The Hacienda” and its closing track by Graham Fellows.

Post-punk to funk crossover

Biting Tongues, A Certain Ratio, Quando Quango, and The Durutti Column all represent the early-1980s Manchester post-punk/funk turn. A Certain Ratio in particular were Factory’s attempt to synthesize funk with Wire-influenced post-punk.

Madchester and baggy

Disc 5–6 trace the Madchester moment: Happy Mondays’s “24 Hour Party People” (Disc 5), The Stone Roses’s “What The World Is Waiting For” (Disc 6), Electronic’s “Getting Away With It” (Disc 6), and the rave side via 808 State and A Guy Called Gerald’s “Voodoo Ray.”

Dance/rave thread

From Gerald Simpson (A Guy Called Gerald, “Voodoo Ray”, Disc 5) through MC Tunes / 808 State “The Only Rhyme That Bites” (Disc 6), the Ruthless Rap Assassins (Disc 6), and Sub Sub’s “Space Face” (Disc 7, Sub Sub later became Doves) to Lionrock (Disc 7) — the compilation charts how Manchester’s dance scene developed in parallel with the guitar bands.

End point: Oasis demo

The closing track — Oasis (2)‘s “Columbia (Demo)” — is a pointed editorial statement: by 1993 the box set’s story is over, and the Britpop era it closes out begins with a band whose origins the set claims for this 16-year independent tradition.

Morrissey connection

Morrissey appears both as cover model and as track contributor (Disc 5, “The Last Of The Famous International Playboys”). The Smiths predate the set’s endpoint but are absent — the set tracks independent-label activity specifically. The cover model credit to “Steven Morrissey” reads as a deliberate nod to the Smiths-era persona.

Context and Related Releases

This set is one of several Cherry Red regional archive compilations from the same period:

  • Keeping Control - Independent Music From Manchester 1977-1981 — earlier, narrower Manchester window
  • Dreams To Fill The Vacuum - The Sound Of Sheffield 1977-1988 — Cherry Red Sheffield equivalent
  • Revolutionary Spirit - The Sound Of Liverpool 1976-1988 — Cherry Red Liverpool equivalent
  • Moving Away From The Pulsebeat (Post-Punk Britain 1977-1981) — UK-wide Cherry Red post-punk survey
  • Manchester North Of England (Bop Cassettes, 1988) — contemporaneous Manchester compilation, compiled by Sarah Champion; different and smaller selection

Open Questions

  • No compilation credit (compiler/liner notes author) is listed in the Discogs entry — Resolved (web-sourced): Mick Middles wrote the liner notes; Mark Radcliffe wrote the foreword. Neither credit appears in the Discogs entry. See Mick Middles.
  • Disc 1’s heading track “Cranked Up Really High” appears as a disc title credit with no position number — may be an intro/spoken-word element rather than a track.
  • Several tracks are noted as demos or edits (e.g. “Breakdown (Demo)”, “7 Reasons (Demo)”, “Columbia (Demo)”); whether these are first-issue appearances of these versions is not confirmed from Discogs alone.
  • “Revenge” (Disc 6, ID: 44608) — confirmed as Revenge (2) (Peter Hook’s post-New Order project) via vault cross-check.
  • Sub Sub (Disc 7) later became Doves — now documented: see Sub Sub and Doves. Same three members (Jimi Goodwin, Jez Williams, Andy Williams (4)), reformed after studio fire c.1997.
  • The set’s 7-disc arc deliberately ends at 1993; the editorial rationale (and whether a companion volume covering 1993–2000 exists) is unknown from Discogs.
  • Discogs release credits confirmed (2026-06-19): Photography by Kevin Cummins and Paul Slattery; Model credit to Morrissey (as “Steven Morrissey”). No compiler, producer, or editorial credits in the Discogs entry. The Discogs record matches what was already documented.
