Overview

Twenty-two-track survey of Los Angeles punk from 1977 to 1981, covering both the first-wave Hollywood art-punk scene and the later beach-cities hardcore that replaced it. The compilation is framed as two overlapping narratives — the Hollywood strand (X, Germs, Weirdos, Bags, Dils, Flesh Eaters, Zeros, Black Randy) and the harder Orange County / beach-city strand (Middle Class, Agent Orange, Adolescents, T.S.O.L., Circle Jerks). Part of Stuart Baker’s Punk 45 series on Soul Jazz Records, which approached American punk geographically: Akron (2015), Cleveland (2015), and Los Angeles (2016).

Photographers Ed Colver and Jenny Lens — two of the core documentarians of the LA scene — both have photography credits on the sleeve.

Artist Notes

Hollywood First Wave (1977–1979)

X (5) — Formed 1977. John Doe, Exene Cervenka, Billy Zoom, D.J. Bonebrake. One of the most musically sophisticated bands of the first wave; released Los Angeles (1980) on Slash Records, produced by Ray Manzarek. “We’re Desperate” is from an early 45.

Flesh Eaters — Led by Chris D. (Chris Desjardins). Deathrock / post-punk edge within the punk scene. Discogs ID 257142. “Disintegration Nation” dates from their early period.

The Weirdos — Art-punk band led by John Denney and Cliff Roman. Discogs ID 261756. Among the first LA punk acts, known for theatrical performances and the track “We Got The Neutron Bomb.”

Urinals — Later renamed 100 Flowers. Discogs ID 177624. Members: John Talley-Jones, Kevin Barrett, Kjehl Johansen. Lo-fi, confrontational minimalism. Both “I’m White And Middle Class” (T.5) and “Ack Ack Ack Ack” (T.10) are included.

The Stooges — Iggy Pop and the Stooges, credited here as “Iggy And The Stooges.” Discogs ID 39770. “I Got A Right” (1977) is a raw single recorded after the Stooges’ formal split — its inclusion frames the compilation’s lineage back to Michigan proto-punk’s arrival in California.

Germs — Led by Darby Crash (Jan Paul Beahm). Discogs ID 257157. “Forming” (1977, their debut single) is the earliest Germs recording. Active 1977–1980.

Black Randy & The Metrosquad — Anarchic Hollywood outfit with a rotating cast. Discogs ID 779553. Members included Joe Nanini, K.K. Barrett, Pat Garrett — the same network that fed into the Randoms and Eyes. Black Randy (John Morris) ran DANGERHOUSE RECORDS alongside David Brown.

The Zeros — San Diego / Los Angeles Chicano punk band. Discogs ID 261758. Members: Javier Escovedo, Hector Penalosa, Robert Alan Lopez, Victor Penalosa. Predecessors in many ways to the broader SoCal punk sound; their 1977 single predates most LA punk records.

Randoms — Loose collective from the Dangerhouse/Metrosquad network: John Duchac, K.K. Barrett, Pat Garrett. Discogs ID 327345. The same members (Barrett, Garrett, Duchac) also appear in Black Randy & The Metrosquad.

The Bags — Direct feeder into The Gun Club. Members Rob Ritter, Terry Graham, and Patricia Morrison later joined the Gun Club or related acts.

The Dils — Brothers Chip Kinman and Tony Kinman. Discogs ID 263827. “Class War” (1977) is their most durable track, later covered by Mission Of Burma.

The Deadbeats — Los Angeles punk band. Discogs ID 314392. Appear twice on the compilation (“Brainless,” “Final Ride”), suggesting a particularly strong archive of material. Later issued an alias as The Suction Cups (2).

The Hollywood Squares — Obscure Hollywood-era band. Discogs ID 1984683. Members: Bob Schell (2), Eddie Vincent (2). One of the rarer inclusions on the compilation.

Orange County / Beach Hardcore (1979–1981)

The Middle Class — Santa Ana hardcore band. Discogs ID 219590. Members: Jeff Atta, Mike Atta, Bruce Atta, Matt Simon, Mike Patton (2). Released the Out of Vogue EP (1978) on Joke Records — often cited as one of the first hardcore punk records and an antecedent of the faster, more aggressive style that defined 1980–81 HC. “Out Of Vogue” opens the compilation.

Agent Orange (7) — Huntington Beach hardcore / surf-punk band. Discogs ID 255055. Members: Mike Palm (vocals/guitar), Brent Liles (bass), Derek O’Brien (drums). Also featured Steve Soto, Dusty Watson across later lineups. “Bloodstains” (from the Bloodstains EP, 1979) is their signature early track.

Circle Jerks — Formed 1979 by Keith Morris (ex-Black Flag) and Greg Hetson. Discogs ID 253257. “What’s Your Problem” is from Group Sex (1980). See full article Circle Jerks.

Adolescents — Orange County / Fullerton band. Discogs ID 277723. “Amoeba” is from their 1981 self-titled debut on Frontier Records. Members included Rikk Agnew, Steve Soto (also in Agent Orange), Frank Agnew.

T.S.O.L. — True Sounds of Liberty, Long Beach. Discogs ID 255060. Members across various eras include Jack Grisham (vocals), Ron Emory (guitar), Mike Roche (bass), Todd Barnes / Mitch Dean / Jay O’Brien (drums). “World War III” from their 1981 debut EP on Posh Boy Records.

Simpletones — Southern California HC band. Discogs ID 255062. Members included Josh Freese (later drummer for Vandals, Devo, Nine Inch Nails, etc.), Jay Lansford, Danny Ruiz.

Eyes — Band formed from the broader Dangerhouse / Metrosquad network. Discogs ID 310822. Notably featured Charlotte Caffey (later of The Go-Go’s), D.J. Bonebrake (later of X), Joe Nanini and Joe Ramirez (both Metrosquad/Randoms). “TAQN” is one of their few recorded tracks.

Scene Context

The compilation maps two overlapping LA punk generations:

  1. 1977–1979 Hollywood first wave: Bands like the Weirdos, Zeros, Germs, Bags, and X emerged from a relatively small Hollywood club circuit (the Masque, Whisky a Go Go, etc.), influenced by British punk and the New York scene. This wave had a broader range of sounds — art-punk, power pop, deathrock — and was documented by labels Dangerhouse and Slash Records, and by photographers Ed Colver and Jenny Lens.

  2. 1979–1981 South Bay / OC hardcore: Middle Class, Agent Orange, Circle Jerks, Adolescents, and T.S.O.L. represented a harder, faster style that codified what became known internationally as “hardcore punk.” Posh Boy and Frontier Records were the primary labels.

The Stooges’ “I Got A Right” (1977) functions as a root document — Iggy Pop had relocated to Los Angeles by this period, and the track was recorded with the reunited Stooges lineup, bridging Detroit proto-punk into the LA scene.
