Discogs ID: 17685382
Compiled by: Bill Brewster & John Reed
Genres: Post-Punk, Funk, Dance-Punk

Overview

A 49-track, three-disc box set anthology surveying the British post-punk dancefloor between 1978 and 1984 — the period when punk’s angular energy collided with funk, disco, dub, and jazz. Assembled by DJ historians Bill Brewster (of Last Night a DJ Saved My Life fame) and John Reed, with liner notes by Brewster. Released on Cherry Red.

The compilation’s thesis is spelled out in its subtitle: “Militant Funk & The Post-Punk Dancefloor.” It documents the moment when British post-punk bands stopped rejecting rhythm and started weaponising it — absorbing African and Caribbean polyrhythm, American funk bass, New York no-wave dissonance, and European synth-pop into an urgently political dance music. Acts range from Factory Records alumni to small-press cassette obscurities, from chart-adjacent names (The Stranglers, Simple Minds) to genuine deep cuts (The 012, Machine Gun Hogg & Co., Normil Hawaiians).

The title track is “Shake (The Foundations)” by Glaxo Babies, which closes disc three.

Scene Context

The compilation spans six years (1978–1984) and sits at the intersection of several overlapping British post-punk currents:

The Manchester–Factory axis: A Certain Ratio (disc 1 opener) and Quando Quango represent Factory Records’ funk turn — ACR absorbing Latin percussion and jazz bass, Quando Quango bridging post-punk and proto-house. Both were produced in close orbit of Factory Records.

Bristol and the South West: The Pop Group’s “Rob A Bank” and Glaxo Babies’ title track anchor Bristol’s confrontational funk-punk scene. Maximum Joy (featuring ex-Pop Group members) also represents this lineage.

The Stiff/pub-rock diaspora into funk: Ian Dury’s “Trust Is A Must” and John Cooper Clarke’s “Post-War Glamour Girl” represent the spoken-word and pub-rock wings finding rhythm. Ian Dury (Discogs artist ID: 126430) was accompanied by The Blockheads.

The Bauhaus/Beggars orbit: Tones On Tail (Daniel Ash’s side project from Bauhaus) shows the goth-funk crossover. Visage (Steve Strange, Midge Ure, Rusty Egan) represents Blitz-kid synth-funk.

The electronic/synth wing: Thomas Leer and Vicious Pink point toward Sheffield-inflected electronic pop.

Ska and jazz crossovers: Blue Rondo À La Turk (jazz-influenced Latin funk), Modern Romance (Latin pop), and Fun Boy Three (post-Specials political pop) represent the ska/jazz wing of early-80s UK dance music.

The margins: Several tracks represent genuinely obscure post-punk funk: The 012, Machine Gun Hogg & Co., Group Therapy (7), Vee VV, Surface Mutants — bands that barely registered commercially but whose music fits the compilation’s thesis precisely.

About the Compilers

Bill Brewster (Discogs ID: 78111) — DJ, music historian, co-author of Last Night a DJ Saved My Life (1999, with Frank Broughton), widely regarded as the definitive history of the DJ. His compilation work on Cherry Red has focused on the intersection of post-punk and dance music.

John Reed (Discogs ID: 405780) — Author and music journalist, has written extensively on post-punk British music. Co-produced this compilation with Brewster.

Label

Cherry Red (label ID: 5174) — UK archival label, parent: Cherry Red Records Ltd. (ID: 269699). Long-running independent specialising in post-punk, new wave, and alternative reissues and compilations. Catalog number CRCDBOX107 places this in their box-set series.
