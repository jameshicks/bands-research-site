Mouth by Mouth

Artist: His Name Is Alive Released: April 13, 1993 Label: 4AD (UK/Europe); 4AD / Warner Bros. Records (US) Catalog: CAD 3006 CD (UK); 9 45214-2 (US) Master ID: 5882 (Discogs) Recorded: 1992, Warren’s House Recording, Livonia, Michigan Produced by: His Name Is Alive (self-produced) Mastered by: Nimbus (UK pressing)

Third studio album by His Name Is Alive, and their first fully self-produced and self-mixed record. Released on 4AD on April 13, 1993. UK CD runs 17 tracks; US pressing omits “The Homesick Waltz” (16 tracks) and had samples excised due to clearance issues.

Background

Mouth by Mouth was assembled from two distinct bodies of recordings made in 1992. One set came from His Name Is Alive proper; the other from The Dirt Eaters, a parallel indie rock band nurtured by Defever but led by guitarist Melissa Elliott. The Dirt Eaters were a more conventional song-oriented outfit, and their material — including Elliott’s four songwriting credits — gave the album a harder-edged, more outward-facing character than HNIA’s previous records.

The previous two LPs (Livonia, Home Is In Your Head) were mixed and shaped by 4AD founder Ivo Watts-Russell and house producer John Fryer. Mouth by Mouth was the first time Defever had full control over the mixing and assembly. Watts-Russell remained loosely involved but was considerably less hands-on.

The album was recorded entirely at Defever’s home in Livonia — the same domestic recording practice that defined the band’s pre-4AD cassette era.

Music

Mouth by Mouth is a noisier, more rock-forward record than its predecessors. Where Livonia and Home Is In Your Head trafficked in gauzy, layered dream-pop, this LP brings guitars and drums more to the fore. The Trouser Press called it “very close to being a ‘normal’ rock record” while maintaining creative unpredictability. AllMusic classed it under Dream Pop, Alternative/Indie Rock, and Experimental Rock.

Multiple vocalists take lead on different tracks — the album does not have a single front voice. Karin Oliver, previously the dominant presence, now shares the mic with Karen Neal, Chelle Marie, and Denise James, each handling specific tracks. Defever’s sampler is less foregrounded than on earlier records, replaced by guitar-driven textures. Elden M. (Discogs ID 248120) contributes noise and vocals throughout.

Defever’s eclecticism is apparent across the runtime: traces of obscure ’60s pop, reggae, Japanese noise, free jazz, and straightforward indie rock coexist without genre program. The 1992 Dirt Eaters sessions add a garage-pop energy to several tracks.

The UK and European pressings contain cinematic dialogue samples scattered through the record. The most prominent is a Jack Nicholson monologue from Ironweed (1987, dir. Héctor Babenco) on track 16 “The Dirt Eaters”: “I’d eat all the dirt in this yard for you. And all the weeds. And all the dog bones too, if you asked me.” The same sample appeared in full on the earlier The Dirt Eaters EP (4AD, 1992); the Mouth by Mouth UK pressing uses a shortened version with the “And all the weeds” line removed. All samples were excised from the US pressing for clearance reasons. The Nicholson line later inspired the title of a 4AD compilation, …and dog bones too. (Wikipedia — The Dirt Eaters (EP), as of 2025-06-26)

Notes:

  • Track 12 “Blue Moon” is credited to “LX Chilton” (i.e. Alex Chilton). It covers Big Star’s version of the Rodgers/Hart standard — Chilton’s arrangement, not the original songwriting credit.
  • Track 1 “Baby Fish Mouth” takes its name from the When Harry Met Sally (1989) Pictionary scene.
  • Track 5 title is the opening line of the Prayer of St. Francis.
  • Track 17 “The Homesick Waltz” is a hidden bonus track on the UK CD — not listed on the back cover or front tracklist, but printed with lyrics on page 8 of the CD booklet. Because it was unlisted/hidden on the source master, it was simply omitted from the US pressing. Absent from UK vinyl, cassette, and US pressings.
  • Tracks 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 9, 11, 16 are credited on the US pressing “Courtesy Of The Dirt Eaters” — confirming the dual-session origin of the album.

The Christgau comparison to Big Star’s Third/Sister Lovers is apt: both records are dense, emotionally disorienting, made from a position of creative freedom rather than commercial calculation, and distribute vocals across multiple voices. Mouth by Mouth was widely treated as a career high at the time of release and remains one of the most critically regarded HNIA records.

Context in HNIA Discography

This album closes the initial 4AD run before HNIA’s mid-decade silence. It was followed by King Of Sweet (1993, Perdition Plastics), a limited-edition US release, and then Stars On E.S.P. in 1996 — a pivot toward gospel and smooth soul with a larger collective of collaborators.

In 2024, 4AD released How Ghosts Affect Relationships 1990–1993, a retrospective compilation covering the era that ends with this album.
