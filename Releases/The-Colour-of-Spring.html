The Colour of Spring

Third studio album by Talk Talk. Released February 1986 on EMI (UK catalog number EMC 3506). Produced by Tim Friese-Greene. Discogs master ID: 26239.

The album marks the point where Talk Talk decisively shed their synth-pop origins in favour of a more organic, expansive sound — a template that led directly to Spirit of Eden (1988) and Laughing Stock (1991). The session roster expanded dramatically: Steve Winwood plays organ on three tracks, alongside jazz and session players rarely heard on pop records of the era.

Recording

Recorded in 1985 at Battery Studios and Videosonics Studios, both in London. The sessions took place across 1985 with the album released 17 February 1986 (UK). The recording period predates the album release by several months, overlapping with the lead single release.

Retrospective

The album is now viewed as the transitional work between Talk Talk’s synth-pop phase and the experimental direction of Spirit of Eden (1988). Its retrospective reputation has risen considerably alongside interest in the later albums.

Production Notes

The Colour of Spring was produced entirely by Tim Friese-Greene, who had co-produced It’s My Life (1984) with Colin Thurston. Here Friese-Greene took sole control and began developing the studio-as-instrument approach that would fully flower on Spirit of Eden. He plays on nearly every track in multiple roles — piano, organ, mellotron, and even the Variophon (an electronic wind instrument) alongside Hollis on “April 5th” and “Chameleon Day.”

The use of Steve Winwood on organ across three tracks (“Happiness Is Easy,” “I Don’t Believe In You,” “Living In Another World”) is notable — Winwood brought a gospel and blues feel that reinforced the album’s move away from synthesisers toward live, acoustic textures.

Robbie McIntosh appears on five tracks in varied roles (electric guitar, dobro, acoustic guitar). Danny Thompson — veteran of Pentangle and countless jazz sessions — plays acoustic bass on “Happiness Is Easy.” The children’s choir on “Happiness Is Easy” is credited to “Children From The School Of Miss Speake”; The Ambrosian Singers appear (as “Ambrosia Choir”) on the closing “Time It’s Time.”

Front illustration by James Marsh, who became the defining visual identity for Talk Talk through this album and its successors.

“Life’s What You Make It” was released 18 November 1985 in Germany (and other continental markets), then 6 January 1986 in the UK — making it a late-1985 release in Europe but an early-1986 UK release, several weeks before the album (17 February 1986). The “Give It Up” single used Parlophone (rather than EMI) in several territories including the UK.
