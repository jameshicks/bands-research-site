Overview

A five-disc retrospective covering the Liverpool independent music scene from 1976 to 1988, compiled for Cherry Red by Richard Anderson. The box spans from the proto-punk/pub rock of Deaf School through the Zoo Records generation (Big In Japan, Echo & The Bunnymen, The Teardrop Explodes), the synth-pop and new wave diversions of the early 1980s, and out through the indie guitar pop and psych of the mid-to-late 1980s (The La’s, The Icicle Works, The Pale Fountains).

The title is borrowed from The Wild Swans’ 1982 Zoo Records single — itself one of the most celebrated records ever to come out of Liverpool — which opens disc three.

The set is organised chronologically across five broadly defined periods: 1976–1980, 1980–1981, 1982–1983, 1983–1985, and 1986–1988. It stands alongside other Cherry Red archival compilations of the era (see Moving Away From The Pulsebeat (Post-Punk Britain 1977-1981), Action Time Vision (A Story Of Independent UK Punk 1976-1979)) as part of a sustained effort to document regional UK post-punk.

Key Labels Represented

This box draws primarily on material originally issued on Liverpool’s independent scene labels of the era:

  • Zoo Records — the central label of the Liverpool post-punk explosion; home to Big In Japan, The Teardrop Explodes, Echo & The Bunnymen, Those Naughty Lumps, The Wild Swans
  • Cherry Red — archival reissue label that packaged and issued this box
  • Inevitable Records, WEA, Korova — major and indie distributed labels appearing within individual tracks
  • Dindisc — OMD’s label at time of “Bunker Soldiers”
  • ZTT Records — Frankie Goes To Hollywood’s label at time of “Two Tribes”

Scene Context

The compilation documents one of the densest regional post-punk scenes in the UK. Several threads run through it:

  • The Zoo Records nucleus (1978–1982). Bill Drummond and Dave Balfe’s label was ground zero for Liverpool post-punk. Big In Japan functioned as a training ground: Holly Johnson (later Frankie Goes To Hollywood), Ian Broudie (Lightning Seeds), Budgie (Siouxsie & The Banshees), and Bill Drummond himself all passed through. The Teardrop Explodes and Echo & The Bunnymen followed directly from that milieu.

  • Pre-punk/proto-punk (1976–1978). Deaf School, operating in the art-school cabaret tradition, are the earliest act represented. The Spitfire Boys and Yachts represent the Liverpudlian punk/power-pop fringe of 1977.

  • Synth pop and new wave crossover (1980–1983). OMD, A Flock Of Seagulls, China Crisis, and Dead Or Alive all emerged from the same post-punk substrate but moved into electronic/synth textures. OMD and A Flock Of Seagulls in particular achieved major international commercial success from this base.

  • Will Sergeant’s solo track (disc 3). “Themes For ‘Grind’ Scene V” appears as a solo credit for the Echo & The Bunnymen guitarist — one of several moments on the box where the scene’s cross-pollination surfaces in solo or side-project credits.

  • The La’s (disc 5). Two early tracks from The La’s — “Get Down Over” and “Way Out” — represent the final chapter of the box and the continuity between the post-punk generation and the Britpop-adjacent indie that would follow.

  • The Revolutionary Army Of The Infant Jesus. The closing track “Come Holy Spirit” (9:31) by this long-running Liverpool art-collective is both the longest track on the set and the most structurally unusual — ambient, devotional, and far outside the pop/punk frame of most of the compilation.
