Bouquet Of Steel

Sheffield scene compilation, 1980. Released on Aardvark (catalog STEAL 2). Fifteen tracks across two sides documenting a cross-section of the Sheffield post-punk underground at the turn of the decade. Compiled by Marcus Featherby. Discogs master ID: 50779 (primary release ID: 871094).

Overview

Bouquet Of Steel is the most comprehensive single document of the Sheffield indie underground circa 1980. Where Fast Product had focused on a tighter circle of post-punk acts and Cabaret Voltaire were already building their own label infrastructure at Western Works, Aardvark cast a wider net — capturing fifteen bands ranging from the already-prominent (Artery (2), The Comsat Angels, I’m So Hollow) to the near-unknown (Disease (2), Shy Tots, De Tian). The title is an obvious play on “bouquet of flowers” and Sheffield’s steel industry identity.

The production credits map the small web of local producers who were facilitating Sheffield’s independent recording activity at the time: Marcus Featherby (who also compiled the set), Roy Neave, Kevin Bacon (The Comsat Angels member), and Peter Wilson. Notably, Cabaret Voltaire appear in a producer role for Vendino Pact’s “Secret Thinking” — not as performers — underlining their role as scene facilitators as well as recording artists.

The most consequential personnel credit is Ian Burden as co-writer on the Musical Janeens track A7. The Musical Janeens was one of Burden’s pre-Human League vehicles (alongside The Musical Janeens’s later alias Graph (2)), and he joined The Human League shortly after this compilation’s release.

Context: Sheffield 1980

By 1980 Sheffield had developed a genuinely distinct post-punk identity, driven by the steel city’s industrial geography and a handful of overlapping networks:

  • Aardvark (107 Whitham Road, Sheffield S10 2SL) — Marcus Featherby’s operation, releasing local acts across the spectrum from post-punk to electronic.
  • Fast Product — Bob Last’s label, which had launched The Human League and Gang of Four; more nationally-connected but Sheffield-rooted.
  • Western Works — Cabaret Voltaire’s own studio, which they used to produce outside acts as well (evidenced here by Vendino Pact).
  • Neutron Records — The Comsat Angels’ label home at roughly this period.

Bouquet Of Steel sits at the intersection of these networks. The Comsat Angels were already signed elsewhere by this point; their appearance here (self-produced, with Peter Wilson as co-producer) suggests the track predates or runs parallel to their main label work. Artery, the most prominent Sheffield post-punk band on the set, had Simon Hinkler among its members — he later joined The Mission.

Open Questions

  • No notes/liner text available in Discogs for either pressing — any contextual sleeve text is unknown.
  • Steve Hopkins (known Aardvark producer from other releases) does not appear in credits here; unclear if he was involved in any capacity.
  • “Chorale” producer discrepancy (Paul Shaft vs. Ken Patten) between the two pressings suggests a data entry issue on Discogs rather than two different mixes.
  • Retro Records (5) issued a reissue (release ID: 9817996) with no year recorded — unknown when and in what format.
  • Kevin Bacon’s production credits here (three tracks) suggest a broader local production role beyond his Comsat Angels membership; vault page now exists at People/Kevin Bacon.md.
  • Roy Neave (three production credits) has no vault page; unclear if he had wider Sheffield ties.
  • The label name “Aardvark” and catalog prefix “STEAL” are presumably a pun (steel/steal, Sheffield steel).
