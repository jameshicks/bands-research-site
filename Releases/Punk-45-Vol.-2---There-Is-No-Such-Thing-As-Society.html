Overview

The second volume in Soul Jazz Records’ Punk 45 series dedicated to UK punk and post-punk, covering 1977–81. Where Vol. 1 focused on a broader sweep of early UK punk, this instalment tilts toward the post-punk and DIY end of the spectrum — more angular, more regional, more obscure. The title is drawn from Margaret Thatcher’s 1987 Woman’s Own interview (“There is no such thing as society”), applied retroactively to frame the anarchic social context of the original recordings. The subtitle itemises the lifestyle satire that runs through several of the tracks.

The compiler is not listed in the Discogs release-level credits (the primary credit is “Various” only), but the series is consistently associated with Stuart Baker at Soul Jazz Records, whose Punk 45 sublabel (Discogs ID: 660794) runs parallel to the main Soul Jazz imprint. The release bears the SJRCD/SJRLP prefix rather than a dedicated sublabel catno, placing it on the main Soul Jazz imprint directly.

The 24 tracks span bands from London, Birmingham, Sheffield, Leeds, Edinburgh, and Inverness, with several acts making their only appearance on a major compilation here.

Notable Artists and Connections

Artists with existing vault pages

  • Swell Maps (ID: 78465) — Birmingham DIY pioneers; “Real Shocks” here. Nikki Sudden, Epic Soundtracks, Jowe Head all have People pages.
  • Television Personalities (ID: 83074) — “Part Time Punks,” their debut TVP single (1978); Daniel Treacy, Edward Ball.
  • ‘O’ Level (ID: 635043) — “East Sheen”; shares TVP lineup (same nucleus as TVPs). Edward Ball on vocals.
  • The Mekons (ID: 263823) — “32 Weeks”; Leeds, early post-punk, formed at Leeds University.
  • Josef K (ID: 155979) — “Radio Drill Time”; Edinburgh, Postcard Records. Paul Haig and Malcolm Ross.
  • The Cravats (ID: 261869) — “You’re Driving Me”; Redditch, saxophone-driven post-punk.
  • The Killjoys (ID: 329724) — “Johnny Won’t Get To Heaven”; Birmingham punk with Kevin Rowland (pre-Dexys).
  • The Prefects (ID: 264303) — “Going Through The Motions”; Birmingham punk, Robert Lloyd later founded The Nightingales.
  • The Art Attacks (ID: 642594) — “Neutron Bomb”; Savage Pencil (Edwin Pouncey) on vocals.
  • The Lines (ID: 829027) — “White Night”; self-produced.
  • Eric Random (ID: 194976) — “23 Skidoo” (solo track, not a 23 Skidoo band recording); Manchester, New Hormones.

Notable connections within the compilation

  • Television Personalities / ‘O’ Level overlap: Tracks 10 and 18 feature the same musicians — Gerard Bennett (bass), John Bennett (2) (drums), Edward Ball (guitar/vocals) — functioning as two faces of the same project. ‘O’ Level was effectively a TVPs parallel alias.
  • The Freeze (2) → Cindytalk: Gordon Sharp (vocals, track 22) is the same Gordon Sharp who later recorded as Cindytalk and appeared on This Mortal Coil’s It’ll End In Tears (4AD, 1984).
  • The Killjoys → Dexys Midnight Runners: Kevin Rowland (vocals, track 8) formed Dexys Midnight Runners after The Killjoys dissolved.
  • The Prefects → The Nightingales: Robert Lloyd (vocals, track 24) went on to found The Nightingales.
  • The Shapes (2): Produced by John A. Rivers, who recurs across multiple UK post-punk productions (also has a People vault page).
  • The Rings (2): Twink (ID: 429354) on vocals — Twink was John Charles Waters, previously of Tomorrow and Pink Fairies; the Rings were one of his later ventures; Martin Gordon (producer) was previously of Sparks.
  • Barney Bubbles (track 3): Barney Bubbles (Colin Fulcher, ID: 1651454) credited for sleeve design on Johnny Moped’s “Incendiary Device.” The prominent graphic designer behind many Stiff/Radar/F-Beat sleeves of the era.
  • Mike Kemp (track 1): Mike Kemp produced The Users’ track; also recurs in Swell Maps credits.
  • Savage Pencil / Edwin Pouncey (track 2): The Art Attacks vocalist, later known as cartoonist Savage Pencil, published in NME and Wire.

Series Context

This is the second of two Soul Jazz Punk 45 UK compilations. The first was Punk 45 Vol. 1 (cat# SJRCD271 / SJR LP271, 2012) — also subtitled Underground Punk And Post-Punk In The UK 1977–81. The series sits alongside Soul Jazz’s other period-document compilation series (New York Noise, Deutsche Elektronische Musik, etc.) under the curatorial hand of Stuart Baker.

The Punk 45 sublabel (Discogs ID: 660794) also issued US city-specific punk volumes (Akron, Cleveland, Los Angeles). The UK volumes appear on the main Soul Jazz imprint.
