Label: Cherry Red (Cherry Red Records Ltd., label ID: 5174) Catalogue number: CRCDBOX74 Format: 4xCD Box Set (Compilation) Year: 2019 Country: UK Discogs release ID: 13926266

Overview

A 105-track, 4-CD box set collecting British DIY punk and post-punk recordings from 1977 to 1981. Compiled by John Reed (also credited as producer, alongside Richard Anderson) with sleeve notes by Andy Peart and Mark Paytress. The title is taken from a track by Patrik Fitzgerald.

The set cuts across the full spectrum of the UK’s post-77 underground: anarcho and street punk, no wave-influenced art-punk, bedroom DIY, post-punk funk, feminist groups, Scottish post-punk, electronic/synth outfits, and the earliest recordings of bands that went on to major careers. It emphasises small-label and self-released material — flexi-discs, cassette-only releases, regional singles — and draws from across the UK: London, Sheffield, Birmingham, Leeds, Edinburgh, Bristol, and elsewhere.

The user initially associated it with Soul Jazz Records. This is Cherry Red — Soul Jazz and Cherry Red are separate UK archival reissue operations with overlapping territory (both have done extensive punk/post-punk comp series).

Scene Context

The 1977–1981 window this compilation covers is the moment between first-wave punk’s commercial breakthrough and the consolidation of post-punk into more polished major-label forms. What it documents is the UK underground’s response: bands working outside standard industry channels, often releasing on tiny regional imprints or their own labels, recording cheaply and rapidly.

Key threads running through the selection:

DIY label culture. Many tracks here originate on now-archetypal small labels: Rough Trade, Fast Product, Factory Records, Postcard Records, and dozens of one-off regional singles imprints. The set functions partly as a survey of that ecosystem.

Feminist post-punk. The Raincoats, Au Pairs, Mo-Dettes, Essential Logic (featuring Lora Logic), Marine Girls (featuring Tracey Thorn), Vivien Goldman — all represented here. This was a distinctive strand of the UK scene that ran parallel to the more male-dominated street punk and art-rock.

Northern post-punk. The Fall (Manchester), Josef K (Edinburgh), Fire Engines (Edinburgh), Girls At Our Best (Leeds), The Sisters Of Mercy (Leeds), The Passage (Manchester), Artery (2) (Sheffield), Blurt (Stroud/London).

Electronic minimalism. Thomas Leer / Robert Rental’s “International” is one of the most significant electronic tracks on any UK punk/post-punk compilation; both were among the earliest UK synth artists self-releasing. pragVEC, Second Layer, The Associates also represent the synth-electronic tendency.

Before they were famous. The compilation catches several bands at early or obscure moments: Blancmange before their synth-pop hits; Thompson Twins before their chart era; The Sisters Of Mercy on one of their earliest recordings; The Legendary Pink Dots at the very start; The Membranes with an early track; Tracey Thorn via Marine Girls before Everything But The Girl.

  • Rough Trade — original home of several acts here including The Raincoats, Scritti Politti, Essential Logic, Vivien Goldman

  • Postcard Records — Josef K, Orange Juice scene (Edinburgh/Glasgow)
  • Factory Records — Manchester scene context; The Fall briefly connected
  • John Cooper Clarke — Mancunian punk poet, here with “Suspended Sentence”
  • Alternative TV — Mark Perry’s band; directly referenced in vault
  • Subway Sect — Vic Godard’s band; Vic Godard in vault

  • Robert Rental — co-writer on Thomas Leer’s “International”
