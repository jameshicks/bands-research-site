Context

This compilation maps the Leeds independent scene from the earliest post-punk stirrings through the city’s goth/darkwave peak and into the late-80s indie wave, drawing on roughly a decade of activity across a wide range of local labels and self-releases.

CD 1 is dominated by the Leeds DIY class of 1978–81. The opening track gives the comp its title: The Mekons’ debut single from 1978, recorded in the same Fast Product / Rough Trade orbit as Gang of Four (also here with “Damaged Goods”) and Delta 5. Scritti Politti appear with “Messthetics”, their anti-music-industry broadside also released on Rough Trade. Girls At Our Best and Expelaires flesh out the city’s post-punk cluster. The disc ends with a run of punk-adjacent acts — Abrasive Wheels, Icon A.D., The Expelled — reflecting the Leeds hardcore punk scene that existed alongside the artier post-punk contingent.

CD 2 covers Leeds’ role as a founding city of UK goth. Sisters of Mercy are present with “Temple Of Love” (Merciful Release, 1983), one of the most significant early goth singles. The March Violets, Red Lorry Yellow Lorry, and Ghost Dance all orbited the Sisters’ Merciful Release / Red Rhino milieu. The Three Johns brought a jagged political edge; Vicious Pink blended synth-pop with the darkness of the Leeds goth scene. The Wedding Present close the disc with their earliest indie-pop, bridging the goth era toward the C86 / jangly-indie generation.

CD 3 documents the late-80s flowering — rougher, more eclectic. Pale Saints (shoegaze), Cud (indie pop/funk), Bridewell Taxis (psychedelia), and the more obscure end of the local tape-and-7” scene. Age Of Chance (CD 2) and The Mission (also CD 2) bridge the goth era to the more commercially ambitious late-80s acts.
