Dreams To Fill The Vacuum - The Sound Of Sheffield 1977-1988

4-CD box set compilation documenting the Sheffield post-punk, new wave, and indie underground across eleven years. Issued 2019 on Cherry Red (cat# CRCDBOX83). Discogs release ID: 14564344 (UK pressing); 19436515 (Japan pressing, Solid Records / Cherry Red, 2020).

Overview

A comprehensive archival survey of Sheffield’s independent music scene from 1977 to 1988. Compiled by Oli Hemingway and Richard Anderson, the set draws on recordings by 83 acts across four discs organised chronologically. It ranges from the earliest post-punk groups active in the late-1977/1978 period through to mid-1980s indie and industrial acts. The liner-note book included contributions from Graham Wrench, Kevin Donoghue, Marcus Featherby, Martin X Russian, Nick Canute, and Roger Quail. Mastered by Oli Hemingway.

The title track is taken from I’m So Hollow’s 1980 single on Hollogram Records — the first standalone release of that name (Discogs release 866986).

Cherry Red’s catalog number sits within their archival reissue arm. The label is a sublabel of Cherry Red Records Ltd. See Cherry Red.

Scene Context

Sheffield in this period produced a disproportionate concentration of post-punk and new wave activity. The compilation draws heavily on the ecosystem around labels Fast Product, Western Works, FON Studios, and Fon Records, as well as one-off singles on local imprints. The overlap between acts is significant: The Human League spun off Heaven 17 and British Electric Foundation when Martyn Ware and Ian Craig Marsh departed in 1980. Clock DVA, Chakk, and The Anti Group (TAGC) orbit the industrial/electronic end; Artery (2), The Danse Society, and Hula (2) represent the darker post-punk strain; Pulp, ABC, UV Pop, and In The Nursery the indie and synth-pop side.

Nick Robinson appears as a session guitarist on three separate tracks (3-15, 3-20, 4-9), suggesting a recurring role in late-era Sheffield recordings.
