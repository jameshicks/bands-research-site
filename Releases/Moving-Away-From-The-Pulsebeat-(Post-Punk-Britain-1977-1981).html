Moving Away From The Pulsebeat (Post-Punk Britain 1977-1981)

Label: Cherry Red
Catalog Number: CRCD5BOX169
Year: 2024
Country: Europe
Format: 5xCD Compilation
Discogs ID: 30377411

A five-disc, 105-track archival survey of British post-punk covering 1977–1981. Compiled by John Reed with sleeve notes by Jon Harrington and Mark Paytress. The title is borrowed from the Buzzcocks track on disc one, itself a meditation on rhythm and dislocation. Mastered by Simon Murphy at Another Planet Music.

This is the third or fourth entry in Cherry Red’s sprawling John Reed–curated post-punk box set series, sitting alongside Optimism - Reject (UK D-I-Y Punk and Post-Punk 1977-1981) and Shake The Foundations (Militant Funk & The Post-Punk Dancefloor 1978-1984). Where those compilations carved out specific subgenres (DIY punk; funk-inflected post-punk), this box casts a wider net across the full post-punk spectrum: pub-era art-punk, angular post-punk, goth precursors, early synth-pop, feminist post-punk, dub-inflected experimentalism, and outsider pop.

Scene Context

The compilation spans a dense four-year window during which British popular music fractured after punk into dozens of divergent trajectories. Several distinct scenes are represented:

Art/DIY post-punk: The Pop Group, Gang of Four, Scritti Politti, Young Marble Giants, Swell Maps, The Raincoats, Au Pairs, Essential Logic — the post-punk political avant-garde. Many of these acts had ties to the independent network of labels like Rough Trade, Fast Product, and Y Records.

Goth/dark post-punk precursors: Siouxsie & The Banshees, The Cure, Joy Division, Killing Joke, Theatre Of Hate, The Sisters Of Mercy, In Camera, Sad Lovers And Giants — the nascent goth scene.

Industrial/experimental: Throbbing Gristle, The Cravats, The Flying Lizards, The Diagram Brothers, Occult Chemistry.

Synth-pop emergence: Tubeway Army, The Human League, Second Layer, pragVEC, Boots For Dancing.

Post-punk with funk/dub: The Slits, Rip Rig & Panic, Jah Wobble, 23 Skidoo, The Pop Group, Section 25.

Scene cross-connections: Disco Zombies and Club Tango share membership (Dave Henderson, Andy Ross). New Order appear with “In A Lonely Place” — the original B-side to “Ceremony,” their debut single issued just months after Joy Division dissolved. The Teardrop Explodes appear with the Zoo Records version, placing them in the Liverpool/Zoo axis alongside Echo & The Bunnymen.

Punk survivors moving toward post-punk: Buzzcocks, The Clash, Stiff Little Fingers, The Undertones, The Jam, The Soft Boys.

Notable inclusions: The Teardrop Explodes “Camera Camera” is the Zoo Records version (Julian Cope’s Liverpool label); Gang Of Four’s “Armalite Rifle” is the Fast Product version predating their EMI debut; New Order’s “In A Lonely Place” is the original version, released as the B-side to their debut single following Joy Division’s dissolution.
