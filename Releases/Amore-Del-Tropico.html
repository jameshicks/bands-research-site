Amore Del Tropico

Artist: The Black Heart Procession
Year: 2002
Label: Touch And Go (tg232cd)
Format: CD / LP
Recorded: SDRL (San Diego Recording Lab), November 2001 – May 2002
Mastered: John Golden

Fourth studio album by The Black Heart Procession. A concept album: a noir tropical murder mystery told across all 15 tracks — a man driven to violence by a doomed love, set in an exotic, heat-soaked locale. The album insert includes a poster of the protagonist standing over his dead lover, magnifying glass raised. Title is Italian (“Love of the Tropics”).

A significant tonal departure from the band’s earlier spare gothic work. The tropical setting introduces bossa nova and Latin-tinged textures — the title track “Tropics Of Love” has a jaunty, almost Pink Panther bossa-nova quality. The closest precedent the band cited (via critics) was Tom Waits’ Frank’s Wild Years — a concept record where narrative and atmosphere do equal work. Jenkins recorded the album at SDRL (San Diego Recording Lab), his home studio, which he had built partly for this project.

The ensemble is large: strings (cello, violin, double bass), trumpet, Optigan, backing vocal choir, lap steel. The album was always conceived with a visual dimension — a companion film followed in 2004.

Notes

The Optigan — Tobias Nathaniel is credited with Optigan, a toy optical organ from the 1970s that plays pre-recorded loop discs. Its presence gives several tracks a warped, ghostly texture. BHP had used one on earlier records.

The Waiter — “The Waiter #4” continues a recurring character thread across the BHP catalog; earlier “Waiter” tracks appeared on 3 (1998) and Amore Del Tropico continues the sequence.

String writing — No arranger is listed; the cello and violin parts were likely developed in session or by the players themselves. Charles Curtis is a notable presence: a classically trained cellist and drone/minimalist composer associated with the San Diego / UCSD scene, whose work spans La Monte Young collaborations and free improvisation.

The backing vocal ensemble — four women credited across the bulk of the album (tracks 2, 5, 8, 11, 14), creating a choir-like quality on the more elaborate arrangements. Arabella Makalani, Liz Janes, Susanna Waiche appear together on four tracks; Traci Woolley and Liz Schier appear on selected tracks only.

Production — Self-produced by The Black Heart Procession, with Jenkins also credited as engineer under his Paulo Zappoli alias. Zack Reinig handled mixing. Recorded over a long session window (six months, Nov 2001–May 2002).

The Companion Film: The Tropics of Love (2004)

Released two years after the album on Touch And Go (tg239dvd). Directed, written, and edited jointly by Pall Jenkins and Matthew Hoyt. Runtime: approximately 70 minutes. Described as a “silent” film — no dialogue, with the album’s audio serving as the complete soundtrack. The band starred in it. Each of the 15 tracks creates a new chapter, so the film maps directly onto the album’s sequence.

The Discogs release (ID 2715369) credits Hoyt and Jenkins as “Film Director, Screenwriter, Film Editor” alongside the full album personnel list — confirming it’s the same recording with the film laid over it.
