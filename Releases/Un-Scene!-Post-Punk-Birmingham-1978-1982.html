Un-Scene! Post Punk Birmingham 1978-1982

Regional archival compilation documenting the Birmingham post-punk underground from 1978 to 1982. Released 2022 on Easy Action. Liner notes by David Twist and Stewart Lee. Discogs master ID: 2630834.

Label: Easy Action Catalog numbers: EARS164CD (CD), EARS164LP (2×LP) Year: 2022 Country: UK Formats: CD (compilation); 2×LP (compilation) Genres/Styles: Rock / Post-Punk, Indie Rock Barcode (CD): 5060446073308; (LP): 5060446073292

Overview

Nineteen tracks spanning Birmingham’s late-1970s and early-1980s post-punk/new wave underground. The compilation was compiled and annotated by David Twist — drummer across many of the featured groups (The Prefects, Dada, T.V. Eye, The Hawks, The Bible Belt) — and carries an essay by comedian and music writer Stewart Lee, who grew up in Birmingham. Mastered by Anders Peterson.

The record draws together bands that overlapped heavily in personnel and venues. Several names — Robert Lloyd, David Twist, Dave Kusworth, Eamonn Duffy — appear across multiple tracks, mapping a tight-knit scene. The inclusion of Swell Maps, Au Pairs, The Nightingales, and Fashion alongside much lesser-documented groups gives the set a breadth from critically recognised acts to virtually unknown local bands.

Notable Bands

Fashion

Birmingham art-punk/new wave act, one of the city’s most commercially successful post-punk exports. This compilation catches them early, credited as “Fàshiön Music.” Members here include Dik Davis, Lûke, and John Mulligan. Discogs ID: 47966.

Swell Maps

Experimental post-punk group from Solihull (near Birmingham), formed by brothers Nikki Sudden and Epic Soundtracks. Featured here with “Vertical Slum.” Discogs ID: 78465. See also Swell Maps.

The Prefects

Birmingham punk group who became The Nightingales. Members: Robert Lloyd (vocals), Joe Crow (guitar), Dave Twist (drums), Alan Apperley (guitar), Eamonn Duffy (bass). Their track “The Bristol Road Leads To Dachau” runs nearly ten minutes — a sprawling anti-fascist statement. Discogs ID: 264303.

The Nightingales

Post-Prefects band, still active through the 2010s. Robert Lloyd vocals, Joe Crow guitar. Discogs ID: 130833.

Au Pairs

Birmingham feminist post-punk band led by Lesley Woods. “Love Song” is representative of their angular, politically charged style. Discogs ID: 73203.

Cult Figures

Birmingham indie act with connections to the Swell Maps orbit via Nikki Sudden and Epic Soundtracks (both contributed to their track). Discogs ID: 294377.

T.V. Eye

Pre-Duran Duran Birmingham group featuring Andy Wickett (who briefly sang for early Duran Duran), Dave Kusworth, and Dave Twist. Discogs ID: 1693428.

The Hawks (4)

Another Kusworth/Duffy/Twist collaboration; includes Stephen Duffy on vocals before his Duran Duran period. Alias “The Subterranean Hawks.” Discogs ID: 1771070.

Web Sources

John Taylor (lead guitar, Dada — track 3 “Birmingham UK”)

The John Taylor credited on lead guitar for Dada (30)‘s “Birmingham UK” is confirmed as the same John Taylor who later co-founded Duran Duran. Taylor’s pre-Duran Duran history is documented in his autobiography In the Pleasure Groove: his first band was Shock Treatment (1977–1978), from which he and drummer David Twist moved into Dada at the invitation of singer/guitarist Mark Wilson. In Dada, the lineup included Marcus Kale on Stylophone/effects, Mark Wilson on guitar/vocals, David Twist on drums, and John Taylor (playing guitar — not yet on bass). Taylor left Dada in 1978 when he enrolled at Birmingham Polytechnic’s School of Foundation Studies, where he met Stephen Duffy and Nick Rhodes and formed the original Duran Duran lineup (Taylor on guitar, Rhodes on synth, Duffy on vocals/bass). Taylor did not switch to bass until later. The Dada lineup on “Birmingham UK” — Taylor (lead guitar), Wilson (guitar), Kale (effects), Twist (drums), Brocklesby (vocals/bass) — is therefore a direct pre-Duran Duran configuration. (Duran Duran Wiki / Dada article — fandom wiki, treat as secondary source; corroborated by Duran Duran Wikipedia article and Taylor’s autobiography as cited there; as of 2026-05-31)

Andy Wickett and early Duran Duran

Andy Wickett’s role as an early Duran Duran vocalist is confirmed from multiple named sources. T.V. Eye formed c.1976 from Moseley Art School graduates — Wickett (vocals), Dave Kusworth (guitar), and Eamonn Duffy (bass). Nick Rhodes was a regular at their rehearsals and Barbarella’s club gigs, recording them on a tape recorder. After T.V. Eye, Wickett was enlisted by Rhodes and John Taylor to front Duran Duran in 1979. He recorded the band’s first professional demos at Bob Lamb’s studio in Birmingham, and wrote or co-wrote early versions of “Girls on Film” and “Rio” (melody and initial lyrics for “Girls on Film” written on a night shift at Cadbury’s). Wickett left Duran Duran soon after — citing a wish to move toward dub reggae — and received £600 in exchange for signing away future royalty claims on his songwriting contributions. Simon Le Bon replaced him in 1980.

Sources: We Are Cult — Andy Wickett interview (named-byline interview, strong source); noise11.com; Duran Duran Wikipedia (as of 2026-05-31)
