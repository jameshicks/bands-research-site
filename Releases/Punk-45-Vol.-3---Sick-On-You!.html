Punk 45 Vol. 3 — Sick On You! One Way Spit! After The Love & Before The Revolution

Label: Soul Jazz Records (label ID: 27182) Catalogue numbers: SJR LP279 (2xLP) / SJRCD279 (CD) Format: 2xLP Compilation / CD Compilation Year: 2014 Country: UK Discogs release IDs: 5667941 (LP), 5701301 (CD) Master ID: 689378

Overview

The third volume in Soul Jazz Records’ Punk 45 series, subtitled Proto-Punk 1969–1977 (LP sleeve) / Proto-Punk 1970–77 (CD). Compiled by Stuart Baker (Soul Jazz founder/compiler, Discogs artist ID: not listed separately on this release — no release-level compiler credit in Discogs data). The release carries no named compiler credit on Discogs; Stuart Baker is credited across the broader Punk 45 series.

The compilation spans US, UK, Canadian, Australian (implied), and French proto-punk tracks, covering the period before punk codified into a genre: raw, self-released, or obscure singles from bands working in isolation, often predating the Ramones/Sex Pistols moment by several years. The title tracks — “Sick On You” (Hollywood Brats) and “One Way Spit” (Debris’) — frame the arc from proto-punk experimentation to the cusp of 1977.

Notable highlights include an early track by Cabaret Voltaire recorded well before their post-punk breakthrough, the pre-Joe Strummer/The Clash band The 101’ers, Death (8) (the Detroit proto-punk Hackney brothers, rediscovered decades later), Electric Eels and Mirrors (the Cleveland proto-punk axis), and the Killer Kane Band featuring New York Dolls bassist Arthur Kane.

Production Credits

No release-level production/compiler credits are listed in Discogs for either pressing. The Punk 45 series is overseen by Soul Jazz founder Stuart Baker.

Connections

  • Soul Jazz Records — issuing label; part of their Punk 45 series
  • Electric Eels — Cleveland proto-punk; “Cyclotron” features Nick Knox (later The Cramps)
  • Mirrors — Cleveland; overlapping personnel with Electric Eels (Jamie Klimek, Paul Marotta, Jim Jones (3))
  • Cabaret Voltaire — Sheffield; “Makes Your Mouth Go Funny” is a very early recording
  • The 101’ers — Joe Strummer’s pre-Clash band; “Keys To Your Heart” produced by Roger Armstrong
  • Crime (2) — San Francisco proto-punk; “Hot Wire My Heart”
  • Hollywood Brats — London; lost proto-punk recordings finally issued; title track “Sick On You”
  • Death (8) — Detroit; Hackney brothers proto-punk, rediscovered by A Rough Guide to Detroit
  • Killer Kane Band — post-New York Dolls project of Arthur Kane
  • Simply Saucer — Hamilton, Ontario proto-punk; longest track on the comp (6:45)
  • Roger Armstrong — produced both The 101’ers and The Count Bishops tracks; key figure in UK pub rock/punk scene
