Punk 45: Burn Rubber City Burn! Akron, Ohio: Punk And The Decline Of The Mid West 1975–80

Label: Soul Jazz Records (Punk 45 sublabel) Cat#: SJR LP299 (LP) / SJRCD299 (CD) Year: 2015 Country: UK Discogs LP ID: 6584811 | Discogs CD ID: 6727997 Master ID: 807516 Formats: 2xLP Compilation / CD Limited Edition Compilation Genres: Rock — Post-Punk, Punk

Overview

One of a series of regional punk/post-punk documents compiled by Stuart Baker under the Soul Jazz Records “Punk 45” sublabel banner. This volume is a dedicated survey of Akron, Ohio — a city that produced an unusually concentrated cluster of art-punk and new-wave acts in the late 1970s, centred around the Clone Records scene and the shared personnel web connecting Devo, Tin Huey, The Waitresses, and others.

The title “Burn Rubber City Burn!” plays on Akron’s rubber industry heritage — the city was the world’s rubber capital for most of the 20th century, home to Goodyear, Firestone, and Goodrich. By the mid-1970s that industry was in steep decline, and the compilation frames punk’s emergence as a direct response to that economic collapse. The 18-track selection runs from 1975 to 1980.

Compiled and annotated by Stuart Baker. Mastered by Duncan Cowell and Pete Reilly. Design by Adrian Self and Edwin Starfooker.

Featured Artists

Bizarros (Discogs ID: 147913)

Three tracks — the largest representation on the comp. Akron art-punk act featuring Nick Nicholis (vocals), Terry Walker, Gerald Parkins, Donald Parkins, Rick Garberson. Released “Lady Doubonette” b/w “I Bizarro” in 1976 on Gorilla Records (2). Full LP From Akron (1977, Clone Records), then signed to Mercury for self-titled LP (1979). See Bizarros.

Devo (Discogs ID: 12834)

Two tracks — pre-fame recordings. “Mechanical Man” (written by Mark Mothersbaugh) and “Auto Modown” (written by Gerald V. Casale, credited as J. Casale on this comp). Both are early recordings predating their international breakthrough. Members include Mark Mothersbaugh, Gerald V. Casale, Bob Mothersbaugh, Bob Casale, Alan Myers. See Devo.

Tin Huey (Discogs ID: 312146)

Two tracks — “Squirm You Worm” and “Puppet Wipes.” Akron art-punk/new-wave group sharing members with The Waitresses: Chris Butler, Ralph Carney, Stuart Austin. Major label release on Warner Bros.: Contents Dislodged During Shipment (1979). See Tin Huey.

The Waitresses (Discogs ID: 67819)

One track — “The Comb,” written by Chris Butler. The Waitresses bridged the Akron art-punk scene with commercial new wave. Patty Donahue (vocals), Chris Butler (guitar), Ralph Carney (saxophone), Billy Ficca (drums), Mars Williams (sax), Tracy Wormworth (bass). See The Waitresses.

Chi-Pig (Discogs ID: 1936297)

Two tracks — “Ring Around The Collar” and “Apu Api.” Duo: Deborah Smith and Susan Schmidt. Released one LP: Bountiful Living (1978, Chi-Pig Records, self-produced). See Chi-Pig.

Rubber City Rebels (Discogs ID: 916473)

Two tracks — “Kidnapped” and “Such A Fool.” Akron punk band signed to Capitol Records. Members: Buzz Clic, Bob Clic, Rod Firestone (also credited as Rod Bent on this comp), Stix Pelten, Brandon Matheson. Appeared on From Akron (1977) sampler alongside Bizarros and Devo. See Rubber City Rebels.

Jane Aire And The Belvederes (Discogs ID: 588123)

One track — “When I Was Young” (written by Liam Sternberg). A UK-based outfit despite the Ohio connection — members included Jon Moss (later Culture Club), Lu Edmonds, Gavin Povey, Glyn Havard. Jane Aire herself was an Ohio émigré. See Jane Aire And The Belvederes.

15-60-75 / Numbers Band (Discogs ID: 321554)

One track — “Narrow Road” (6:08), the longest cut on the comp. Name variations include “15 60 75 Numbers Band,” “The Numbers Band.” Kent, Ohio blues-punk group led by Robert Kidney, with member Terry Hynde (sister of Chrissie Hynde of The Pretenders). Active from early 1970s; debut LP Jimmy Bell’s Still In Town (1976, Water Bros. Records). See 15-60-75.

Hammer Damage (2) (Discogs ID: 327861)

One track — “Laugh.” Members: Dave Zagar, Mike Hammer (2), George Cabaniss, Scott Winkler. Note: Dave Zagar also appears as a member of Rubber City Rebels. See Hammer Damage (2).

Denis DeFrange (Discogs ID: 929144)

Two tracks — “The Manikin Shuffle” (with Mark Frazier) and “Sector Wars” (solo). Name variant “Defrance” used on sleeve notes. No label or additional context in Discogs beyond these appearances.

Ralph Carney (Discogs ID: 216657)

One solo track — “Closet Bears.” Carney was also a core member of Tin Huey and The Waitresses, and is one of the most connected figures in the Akron scene. Groups also include Kamikaze Ground Crew, Oranj Symphonette, Labatomy, and others. See Ralph Carney.

Scene Notes

The compilation documents a tight web of interconnected personnel:

  • Chris Butler and Ralph Carney both appear in Tin Huey and The Waitresses
  • Dave Zagar appears in both Hammer Damage (2) and Rubber City Rebels
  • Clone Records (Akron) was the primary first home for multiple artists here — Bizarros (From Akron, 1977) and Tin Huey (Puppet Wipes, 1977) both appeared on the label’s early sampler/single output
  • Rubber City Rebels appeared on the From Akron sampler alongside Bizarros
  • Terry Hynde (15-60-75) is the sister of Chrissie Hynde (The Pretenders), who herself grew up in Akron
