Overview

Keeping Control: Independent Music From Manchester 1977-1981 is an 82-track, three-disc archival survey of the Manchester independent music scene across the late punk and post-punk era. Released by Cherry Red in 2023, it covers a tighter geographic and temporal frame than Cherry Red’s broader UK-wide compilations (cf. Moving Away From The Pulsebeat (Post-Punk Britain 1977-1981), Action Time Vision (A Story Of Independent UK Punk 1976-1979)): every act here is Manchester or Greater Manchester-rooted, and the window stops at 1981 — before most of these bands dissolved, evolved, or crossed over.

The set spans the full spectrum of the era: first-wave punk (Buzzcocks, Slaughter And The Dogs), post-punk experimentalism (Joy Division, Magazine, The Fall, A Certain Ratio), quirky indie (Jilted John, The Freshies, Alberto Y Lost Trios Paranoias), art-funk (Dislocation Dance, Manicured Noise), and dozens of near-forgotten single-release acts that have rarely appeared on any reissue. The inclusion of Martin Hannett as a credited artist (his own track “The Music Room” on disc 3) is unusual — Hannett is far better known as the producer who defined the Factory Records sound, and his appearance here as a solo performer underscores the compilation’s depth of field.

Compared to the 1988 Manchester North Of England (Bop Cassettes/City Life), which documented the city’s indie scene at the Madchester threshold, Keeping Control reaches further back and further underground — many of its acts never made it onto national labels and are represented here by tracks from cassettes, flexi-discs, or very small pressings.

Keeping Control fits squarely in Cherry Red’s regional archival series: each box focuses on one UK city or region for a defined punk/post-punk era.

Context & Related Compilations

This release is distinct from the 1988 Manchester North Of England (Bop Cassettes / City Life): that earlier comp captured the scene on the brink of Madchester (1988), whereas Keeping Control looks back from 2023 to cover the earlier 1977-1981 formative period.

The broader Cherry Red regional post-punk archive (see label list above) makes Keeping Control part of a UK-wide documentation project. Among its direct Manchester peers in the vault, the Moving Away From The Pulsebeat (Post-Punk Britain 1977-1981) comp covers the same years but is UK-wide and Factory-heavy; Keeping Control goes wider and weirder into the Manchester underground beyond Factory.

Tracks pointing outward to other vault entries:

  • Joy Division’s “Digital” links to Singles Going Steady context (Buzzcocks also here) and Factory Records’ earliest output.
  • Magazine’s “A Song From Under The Floorboards” links to Magazine’s third album and Howard Devoto’s post-Buzzcocks arc.
  • New Order’s “Everything’s Gone Green” is the band’s debut as New Order, the first release after Joy Division dissolved following Ian Curtis’s death (May 1980).
  • Ludus and The Diagram Brothers both on New Hormones — Richard Boon’s label.
  • The Frantic Elevators: future Simply Red frontman Mick Hucknall’s pre-fame band.
  • Martin Hannett appearing as solo performer is the deepest-cut inclusion: he is otherwise documented in the vault purely as producer (Martin Hannett).

Open Questions

  • Whether liner notes beyond Alderman’s foreword were written by a named author — not confirmed from web sources.
  • Track sources (original labels, catalog numbers for each track’s original release) are not in Discogs for this compilation — would require liner booklet or web research.

  • Several very obscure acts (Genocide (20), Mellotron (2), Sister Ray (4), Eddie Mooney & The Grave, Speed (13)) have minimal Discogs presence; their histories are likely recoverable only from web sources or the booklet.
