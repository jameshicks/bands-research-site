Manchester North Of England

Manchester / North of England indie and post-punk regional compilation, 1988. Released on Bop Cassettes in association with City Life (2) (the Manchester listings magazine). Two formats issued simultaneously: vinyl LP (BC V 001) and cassette (BC 001). Compiled by Sarah Champion. Discogs master ID: 371064. Primary release IDs: 1036662 (vinyl LP), 3123328 (cassette).

Overview

Manchester North Of England is a 1988 snapshot of the Manchester and wider North of England indie underground at the precise moment it was tipping toward mainstream attention. The compilation was put together by Sarah Champion, a Manchester-based music writer and broadcaster who would go on to write And God Created Manchester (1990), one of the key texts documenting the Madchester era. The label pairing — Bop Cassettes with City Life (2) (Manchester’s alternative listings magazine) — situates this as a local press / local scene document rather than a London-gatekept compilation.

The fourteen tracks divide roughly into three tiers by subsequent trajectory:

  1. Acts that broke nationally — Inspiral Carpets (then on their own Cow label, here contributing the pre-LP track “Joe”), James (already on Blanco Y Negro / Sire but about to sign to Fontana), The Railway Children (Factory / Virgin), and Bradford (band) (soon to sign to Rough Trade).
  2. Acts with cult or critical reputations — The Man From Delmonte (key Bop Cassettes band, guitar-pop), Dub Sex (Salford post-punk/funk, later Cut Deep), Milltown Brothers (Burnley / Manchester; later Heavenly / London Records).
  3. Acts that remained regional or dissolved — New Morning, The Waltones, Jean Go Solo, Penny Priest, Johnny Dangerously (3), Raintree County, Peppelkade 14.

The compilation doubles as an early document of what would be called Madchester — the Inspiral Carpets track predates their breakthrough single “Joe” (proper release 1989), and the James track “Sky Is Falling” appears to be a pre-Strip-mine / pre-Gold Mother recording. However, the mood is not yet baggy: most tracks here are indie guitar pop or post-punk with funk inflections rather than the acid-house crossover that would define the city’s next wave.

One notable hidden story: “Johnny Dangerously (3)” is listed in Discogs under the real name John Bramwell — who later founded and fronted I Am Kloot, the Manchester art-rock band active through the 2000s–2010s. His contribution here, “Subway Life,” predates I Am Kloot by more than a decade.

Artist Profiles

The Man From Delmonte

Manchester guitar-pop. Discogs ID: 300953. Members: Danny Ward, Howard Goody, Martin Vincent (2), Mike West (2), Roxy Walsh, Sheila Seal. Closely tied to Bop Cassettes — their subsequent releases (1989–1990: “Big Noise”, “My Love Is Like A Gift You Can’t Return”, “The Man From Delmonte E.P.”, “Catholic Boys On Mobilettes”) all appeared on Bop Cassettes. Still active as of 2025 (self-released “Better Things”). Track: “Australia Fair” (A1).

Inspiral Carpets

Manchester indie / psychedelic. Discogs ID: 19564. Members: Clint Boon (organ), Graham Lambert (guitar), Craig Gill (drums), Martyn Walsh (bass), plus varying lead vocalists (Stephen Holt → Tom Hingley). Started on the Debris label (1987 debut single), then issued via own Cow imprint from 1989. Signed to Mute imprint Cow and crossed over with Life (1990) and The Beast Inside (1991). “Joe” here is likely a demo / pre-Cow recording predating the 1989 single of the same name. Also contributed to Name Variations: “Inspiral Carpet”, “The Inspiral Carpets”. Track: “Joe” (A2).

James

Manchester post-punk / indie rock. Discogs ID: 12094. Members include Tim Booth (vocals), Larry Gott (guitar), Jim Glennie (bass), Gavan Whelan (then-drummer), Andy Diagram (trumpet), Mark Hunter (keyboards), Saul Davies. Formed 1982; debuted on Factory Records (Jimone EP, 1983). By 1988 on Blanco Y Negro / Sire (Strip-mine LP). “Sky Is Falling” does not appear in the primary Discogs discography as a standalone release, suggesting it’s either an outtake or pre-Strip-mine recording. Broke through commercially with “Sit Down” (Fontana, 1991). Track: “Sky Is Falling” (B1).

Bradford (band)

Bradford / Manchester post-punk. Discogs ID: 278910. Members: Ewan Butler, Ian Michael Hodgson, John Baulcombe, Jos Murphy, Mark McVittie; produced by Stephen Street (The Smiths producer). Issued singles on Debris (1986) then Rough Trade. Discogs name variation: no variations listed. “Lust Roulette” likely predates Rough Trade period. Track: “Lust Roulette” (B2).

The Railway Children

Wigan / Manchester indie. Discogs ID: 12218. Members: Gary Newby (vocals/guitar), Brian Bateman (guitar), Stephen Hull (bass), Guy Keegan (drums). Associated with Factory Records and later Virgin. Active 1985–1992 approx. Known for melodic guitar-pop with Factory-adjacent aesthetics. Track: “Sunflower Room” (B3).

Milltown Brothers

Burnley / Manchester indie pop. Discogs ID: 267266. Members: Matt Nelson (3) and Simon Nelson (brothers), Barney Williams, Nian Brindle, James Fraser (3). Later signed to Heavenly / London Records; issued Slinky (1991) and Valve (1993). Track: “Janice Is Gone” (B5).

Dub Sex

Salford post-punk / funk. Discogs ID: 267314. Members: Mark Hoyle, Roger Cadman, Tim Costigan, Dave Rumney, Guy Ainsworth, Chris Bridgett, Cathy Brooks. Name variation: “Marine Escape”. Debut releases on Skysaw Records and Ugly Man Records (1986–1987), then Cut Deep (1988–1989). Later issued a reunion record on Optic Nerve Recordings (2019). Track: “Instead Of Flowers (Dubmix)” (B7).

Labels

Bop Cassettes

Manchester independent. Discogs label ID: 83763. No parent label. The Man From Delmonte’s main label home (multiple 1989–1990 releases). This compilation (BC V 001 / BC 001) is their only documented compilation release.

City Life (2)

Manchester alternative listings magazine. Discogs label ID: 884840. Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Life_%28magazine%29. Internet Archive snapshot: https://web.archive.org/web/20051204015746/http://www.citylife.co.uk/. Co-billed as publisher/sponsor of this compilation alongside Bop Cassettes.

Context

Manchester North Of England occupies a specific moment in the pre-Madchester timeline. By 1988 the Haçienda was two years into booking acid house nights, but the guitar bands documented here were still largely operating in the post-punk / C86 indie mode. The compilation is roughly contemporary with:

  • The Inspiral Carpets’ first real releases (debut single on Debris, 1987)
  • James finishing Strip-mine (Blanco Y Negro / Sire, 1988)
  • The Railway Children moving from Factory to Virgin
  • The nationwide emergence of Manchester as a music-media story (NME’s “Madchester” coverage begins 1988–1989)

Compiled by Sarah Champion, whose writing would directly document this transition. The Bop Cassettes / City Life pairing reflects a broader ecosystem of Manchester-rooted independent infrastructure — record shops, fanzines, listings magazines, and micro-labels — that supported the scene before national label money arrived.

Open Questions

  • No liner notes available in Discogs for either pressing; sleeve text (if any) by Sarah Champion is unknown.
  • “Sky Is Falling” by James does not appear as a standalone Discogs entry — may be an exclusive compilation recording or an unreleased outtake.
  • Peppelkade 14 is credited with a misspelling on the sleeve (“Pepplekade 14”); their later releases corrected the name. Minimal Discogs presence.
  • Jean Go Solo, New Morning, Penny Priest — no further Discogs releases found; these may be one-time appearances with no further recording activity.
  • The compiler credit reads “Tape Compiled By” on both Discogs entries — suggests the vinyl LP may actually have been compiled from a pre-existing cassette sequence rather than the other way around.
  • Raintree County members (Craig Ferguson (3), Ian Scanlan, Michael Peek, Vincent Carroll) have no wider Discogs presence.
  • Whether Sarah Champion had any other compilation credits on Discogs beyond this release and her 1988–1997 active period remains unclear from Discogs data alone.
