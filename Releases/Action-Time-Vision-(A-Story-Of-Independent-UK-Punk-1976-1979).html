Action Time Vision (A Story Of Independent UK Punk 1976-1979)

Label: Cherry Red (label ID: 5174) Catalogue number: CRCDBOX27 Format: 4xCD Box Set (Compilation; Remastered) Year: 2016 Country: UK Discogs release ID: 9580683 (also 9647671 for alternate pressing) Master ID: 1136055

Overview

A 111-track, 4-CD box set documenting independent UK punk from 1976 to 1979 — the first wave and its immediate aftermath, before post-punk fully diverged into separate currents. Compiled and licensed by John Reed and Eddy Ball, with liner notes by Kris Needs (introduction) and Andy Davis (band biographies). Remastered by Simon Murphy.

The title is taken from the opening track of disc three: “Action Time Vision” by Alternative TV, the band of Mark Perry (of Sniffin’ Glue fanzine fame). The selection spans the original punk class of 1976–77 (The Damned, Eater, The Rezillos) through the broader independent punk ecosystem of 1977–79, including Belfast and Glasgow acts, anarcho-punk precursors, early Oi!, and several bands that were proto-post-punk but still operating squarely in the punk idiom.

This is a companion volume to Optimism - Reject (UK D-I-Y Punk and Post-Punk 1977-1981) (Cherry Red CRCDBOX74, 2019), which concentrates on the post-punk end of the same period. The two boxes overlap in a handful of artists but are otherwise distinct. Both are part of Cherry Red’s ongoing archival series of large-format punk/DIY box sets.

The user initially associated this compilation with Soul Jazz Records. It is Cherry Red — both labels run deep archival reissue series in related territory (Soul Jazz’s Punk 45 series, Cherry Red’s “Story Of…” series), so the confusion is understandable.

Scene Context

This box set covers the original punk moment (1976–77) and its consolidation into a broader independent underground (1978–79), capturing the movement before it diverged sharply into post-punk experimentalism on one hand and Oi!/street punk on the other.

First-wave class of 1976–77

The set opens with several foundational tracks: The Damned’s “New Rose” (arguably the first UK punk single, October 1976), Eater (2)‘s “Outside View” (one of the earliest records by any punk band), and The Rezillos’ “I Can’t Stand My Baby” (Scotland’s first punk single). The Cortinas and The Drones were active by early 1977.

Northern Irish punk

Two tracks spotlight Belfast and Northern Ireland’s distinct punk scene: Stiff Little Fingers’ “Suspect Device” (their debut single, 1978, written by Jake Burns and journalist Gordon Ogilvie, addressing the political situation directly) and Rudi (“Big Time”) and The Outcasts (“Just Another Teenage Rebel”) — both Belfast bands associated with the Good Vibrations label run by Terri Hooley.

Scottish punk

The Rezillos (Edinburgh), Johnny And The Self Abusers (“Saints And Sinners”) — the latter being the pre-split record of the band that became Simple Minds. The Skids appear with “Reasons,” Stuart Adamson’s early band before Big Country. Protex (Belfast/Glasgow).

Early Oi! and street punk

Sham 69 (“I Don’t Wanna”), Angelic Upstarts (“The Murder Of Liddle Towers” — a political song about a death in police custody), Cockney Rejects (“Flares ‘N’ Slippers”), and Menace (7) (“G.L.C.”) represent the nascent street punk/Oi! tendency that would crystallize in 1980–82.

Anarcho-punk precursors

Poison Girls and Fatal Microbes appear at the far end of the compilation — both were associated with the anarcho-punk scene that would crystallize around Crass and the Southern Studios / Crass Records infrastructure. Poison Girls (led by Vi Subversa) were contemporaries and labelmates of Crass.

Pre-fame appearances

  • Joy Division — “Failures” appears here (early track, preceding their Factory Records debut)
  • Tubeway Army / Gary Numan — “That’s Too Bad,” the band’s debut single before Numan went solo and hit with “Are ‘Friends’ Electric?” and “Cars”
  • The Fall — “Psycho Mafia” from their 1977 debut EP
  • Adam And The Ants — “Zerox” from the Dirtbox period, before the Ants’ massive chart success
  • Nipple Erectors — featuring Shane MacGowan before he formed The Pogues. “King Of The Bop” also credits Shanne Bradley
  • Riff Raff (6) — Billy Bragg’s pre-solo punk band; “Cosmonaut” is one of their Riff Raff recordings
  • The Killjoys — features Kevin Rowland before he formed Dexys Midnight Runners
  • The Panik — already has a vault page; features members who went on to other post-punk work
  • Lockjaw — “Radio Call Sign” credits Simon Gallup, who would later become The Cure’s long-serving bassist

The Vortex live recordings

Two tracks are credited as recorded live at The Vortex: “Nothing To Declare” by Suspects (6) and “Bunch Of Stiffs” by Mean Street. The Vortex was a key punk club in Wardour Street, London, active 1977–78.

The Hollywood Brats note

Hollywood Brats’ “Sick On You” closes CD 3. The Hollywood Brats are often cited as proto-punk, recording in the early 1970s but going unreleased until 1980; their inclusion at the end of a disc signals the compilation’s awareness of pre-history.
