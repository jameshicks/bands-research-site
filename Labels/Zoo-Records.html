Zoo Records

Oct 12, 20267 min read

Zoo Records

Discogs label ID: 6052
Location: Liverpool (later also London N1)
Active: 1978–c.1982
Founders: Bill Drummond and David Balfe
Also styled: ZOO!, ZOO! Records

Independent Liverpool label, the first dedicated outlet for the post-punk scene centred on Eric’s club. Zoo launched or documented the careers of Echo & The Bunnymen, The Teardrop Explodes, Big In Japan, Lori & The Chameleons, Those Naughty Lumps, The Wild Swans, and Expelaires.

History

Origins (1978–1979)

Zoo Records was set up by Bill Drummond and David Balfe in Liverpool in 1978–79. The address on early releases was The ZOO, 1 Chicago Buildings, Whitechapel, Liverpool 1 — effectively a management operation as much as a label. Drummond and Balfe had both been members of Big In Japan, the short-lived Liverpool group whose alumni dispersed across the entire Merseyside scene; the label was a direct extension of that network.

The catalog number series was CAGE (later with a TEAR prefix for some Teardrop Explodes releases, and a separate SIR prefix for the Lori & The Chameleons single, issued on Sire in the UK as a Zoo-distributed or co-branded release).

The first release was Big In Japan’s EP From Y To Z And Never Again (CAGE 001, 1978), on which Drummond and Balfe both played — Drummond on guitar, Balfe on bass — alongside Holly Johnson, Ian Broudie, Jayne Casey, and Budgie (2). The EP was produced variously by Noddy Knowler, Rob Dickins (tracks A1/A2), and Big In Japan themselves.

CAGE 002 was Iggy Pop’s Jacket by Those Naughty Lumps (1979), for which Balfe contributed sleeve design; CAGE 003 was The Teardrop Explodes’ debut single Sleeping Gas (1979), engineered by Balfe and produced by The Chameleons (2) — Drummond and Balfe’s production alias. CAGE 004 was The Pictures On My Wall by Echo & The Bunnymen (1979), the debut release by the band, produced by The Chameleons (2). CAGE 005 was the Teardrop Explodes’ Bouncing Babies (1979), produced by Mike Burson.

The Chameleons (2): Drummond and Balfe as Producers

Throughout Zoo’s active years, Drummond and Balfe operated under the joint alias The Chameleons (2) for production work on the label and elsewhere. They produced The Teardrop Explodes’ Sleeping Gas, Echo & The Bunnymen’s The Pictures On My Wall, and wrote and produced the Lori & The Chameleons single Touch (SIR 4025 / Zoo, 1979), which was Drummond and Balfe’s own project — a Balearic proto-electronic pop confection credited to a fictional act.

Balfe also produced the Expelaires single To See You / Frequency (CAGE 007, 1979) under his own name.

Growth and Transition (1980–1982)

As the label’s artists were picked up by major-distributed labels, Zoo continued to issue catalogue:

  • The Teardrop Explodes moved to Mercury (via Korova) but some Zoo-catalog pressings remained in circulation; the Treason single (CAGE 008, 1980) and When I Dream (TEAR 1, 1980) came out on Zoo.
  • Echo & The Bunnymen moved to Korova/WEA; Drummond continued as their manager and producer (crediting himself as producer on Crocodiles, 1980, and subsequent albums through Songs To Learn & Sing, 1985).
  • The Wild Swans’ Revolutionary Spirit (12 CAGE 009, 1982) was Zoo’s last significant original release — a 12” produced by Louis Vincent, written by Paul Simpson (a Teardrop Explodes alumnus), Gerrard Quinn, and Jeremy Kelly.

By 1982 the label’s address had shifted to 132 Liverpool Road, London N1, signalling that Drummond and Balfe had both relocated south.

Later Issues

Zoo-catalog material continued to appear on retrospective releases. A double LP ‘The Zoo’ Uncaged 1978–1982 (Document, 1990) compiled the label’s key singles. Zoology (Head Heritage / Zoo Records, 2004) reissued Teardrop Explodes Zoo-era material. The Teardrop Explodes compilation Culture Bunker 1978–82 (Universal, 2023) appeared with a Zoo Records credit.

Key People

Bill Drummond (Discogs ID: 149534)

Born William Ernest Drummond, 1953, South Africa; raised Scotland. Drummond arrived in Liverpool via art school and became involved in the Eric’s scene as manager and musician. He was a guitarist on the Big In Japan EP (CAGE 001) and designed sleeves for Zoo releases. As The Chameleons (2) with Balfe, he co-produced the first Echo & The Bunnymen and Teardrop Explodes Zoo singles. After Zoo wound down, he became manager of Echo & The Bunnymen through to 1985 and produced their albums on Korova — Crocodiles (1980), Heaven Up Here (1981), A Promise (1981), Porcupine (1983), Ocean Rain (1984), and the compilation Songs To Learn & Sing (1985).

In parallel with his Echo work, Drummond co-wrote songs under his own name (notably “The Lonely Spy” / “Touch” for Lori & The Chameleons, and material later credited on various reissues). His solo work ran through Creation Records: The Man (1986) and The King of Joy (1987).

From 1987, Drummond formed The KLF with Jimmy Cauty, operating through aliases including The Justified Ancients Of Mu Mu, The Timelords, and K Foundation. KLF became one of the most commercially successful UK acts of the early 1990s, scoring UK no. 1 hits with “Doctorin’ The Tardis” (1988, as The Timelords), “3 A.M. Eternal,” “Last Train To Trancentral,” and “Justified & Ancient” (1991, feat. Tammy Wynette). The duo withdrew their catalog from sale in 1992 and notoriously burned £1 million in cash as a K Foundation art project in 1994. Drummond has continued as an artist, writer, and provocateur.

Aliases: King Boy D, Aurora Borealis (2), Time Boy, The Daytonas, Tenzing Scott Brown

David Balfe (Discogs ID: 101351)

Born 1958, Liverpool. Balfe’s career ran from bass player to label boss. He co-founded Dalek I (with Alan Gill) in 1977, then joined Big In Japan in 1978. In 1979 he replaced Paul Simpson as keyboardist in The Teardrop Explodes, playing on Kilimanjaro (Mercury, 1980), Treason (Zoo, 1980), and Wilder (Mercury, 1981), and contributing arrangements to posthumous Teardrop releases. As The Chameleons (2) with Drummond, he produced early Zoo releases. As engineer, he also contributed to Sleeping Gas (1979).

After the Teardrop Explodes disbanded (1983), Balfe set up a second label, Food (Food Records, Discogs label ID: 6124), which signed Blur (whose debut Leisure was co-produced by Balfe), Jesus Jones, and others. EMI took full control of Food in 1994. Balfe subsequently moved into freelance writing and directing.

Aliases: A Chameleon, Milk (10), Dave Balfe

Production Alias: The Chameleons (2) (Discogs artist ID: 269794)

Drummond and Balfe’s joint production alias. Credits include:

Connections

  • Korova — Warners-distributed label that took Echo & The Bunnymen and (via Mercury) Teardrop Explodes after Zoo; Drummond produced both acts for Korova
  • Food — David Balfe’s post-Zoo label; signed Blur; later EMI-owned
  • The KLF — Bill Drummond’s subsequent main project
  • Dalek I Love You — David Balfe was a founding member
  • Big In Japan — the immediate predecessor band and personnel source for Zoo
