Overview

Irma Unlimited is the deep house and electronic arm of the Irma Records Srl group, which is headquartered in Bologna, Italy. The parent company describes itself as an Italian record company handling management, music promotion, and physical and digital distribution. Within the large Irma family — which includes over 40 sublabels spanning jazz, Afro, lounge, and dance — Unlimited was the dedicated house imprint.

The catalog runs from 1999 to at least 2006 and is primarily US-pressed 12” singles, alongside Italian-pressed compilations. The label’s contact address in New York (Suite 1002, 29th Street) suggests a US licensing / distribution operation running in parallel with the Bologna base. The Irma Unlimited catalog number series uses “IU” prefixes for singles (IU001 through IU037-plus) and “IRMA” or “UNLI” prefixes for LPs and compilations.

The label’s genre identity is deep house with Afro, Latin, jazz, and spiritual inflections — squarely in the late-1990s/early-2000s New York and Italian deep house tradition. Key recurring producers include Don Carlos, Maurizio Belladonna, and Pastaboys. The most internationally noted release is the 2003 single Chimes Of Freedom by Atelewo, produced by Osunlade and Pastaboys.

History

Founding period (1999–2000)

The first Discogs-dated release is House Of Irma (A Travelling Guide Into Trippy House) (1999, cat no. IRMA 494750-2), a compilation that established the trippy/spiritual deep house aesthetic. The label launched its 12” singles series in 2000 with Dream On Baby (Ron Trent Remixes) by Sarah Jane Morris (IU001), remixed and produced by Ron Trent with mixing by Ben Grassini. That first single signaled the label’s orientation: established US deep house names appearing on an Italian imprint.

The 2000 compilation Irma Unlimited (Spring / Summer House Collection) (IRMA 498338-2) arrived alongside Irma Unlimited 05 (Fall / Winter House Collection) (IRMA 501099-1), establishing a seasonal release pattern. Maurizio Belladonna’s Midnight House LP (UNLI 498308-2) and Jaymz Nylon’s Afrotech EP (IRMAUNLIMITED02) followed in 2000, as did Black Mighty Orchestra’s To The Sky (IRMA 498118-1).

Peak output (2001–2003)

The label’s most prolific period. Key 2001 releases: Don Carlos’s debut album The Music In My Mind (IRMA 504378-2) — a full LP with keyboard contributions from Luca Martegani, Ricky D.T., and Stefano Lucato, and vocal appearances by Kym Mazelle, Michelle Weeks, and Taka Boom. Jestofunk’s Stellar Funk / Be A Warrior (IU011), written by Claudio “Moz-Art” Rispoli, Farias, and Alessandro Staderini, brought the Italian dance-funk act to the label.

The 2001 compilation The Afro House Of Irma - Afrodesia Vol. 2 (IRMA 501638-2), compiled by Fabrizio Carrer, continued the Afro-house thread alongside releases from Duran Y Garcia, Dom Um Romao (remixed by Jerome Sydenham, DJ Uovo, and LTJ X-Perience), and Holland Tunnel Project.

2002 introduced Gazzara (The Spirit Of Summer, IU016) — the Francesco Gazzara / Massimo Sanna duo — and DJ Rodriguez’s The Summer Is Coming / My Magic Carpet (IU015, UK pressing). The House Of Irma Vol. 3 (Another Journey Into Trippy House) (IRMA 507676-2) further extended the trippy-house compilation series.

In 2003 Pastaboys brought Osunlade into the label’s orbit for Chimes Of Freedom (IU025), the collaboration released under the name “Atelewo” (credited as “Osunlade and Pasta Boys Presents Atelewo”). This was — per available Discogs data — the only Atelewo release outside Yoruba Records, and the only time Osunlade appeared on the Irma Unlimited roster. Osunlade and Pastaboys are jointly credited as producers. The same year saw Don Carlos and Michelle Weeks unite on Take Me Higher (IU030), and Maurizio Belladonna’s Inspirational Grooves LP (UNLI 511384-1).

Late period (2004–2006)

Pastaboys’s LP Daylight In The Invisible World (UNLI 515298-2, 2004) was the label’s most fully realized album statement from that act. Pastaboys also released Free Your Mind (Remixes Part 1) (IU033, 2004) featuring Wunmi. Don Carlos’s retrospective Mediterraneo - Club Favourites Collection ‘90/98 (UNL 804 LP, 2004) collected earlier material.

The final credited release is The Jazz House Independent 5th Issue (IRM 828 CD, 2006), a compilation series that had been running through the label since its early days (at least 3rd, 4th, and 5th issues documented). After 2006 no further Discogs entries appear under this label ID.

Recurring Production / Creative Credits

Beyond the primary artists, several names recur throughout the catalog as producers, engineers, and designers:

Producers: Don Carlos (7 release credits as producer/composer), Marco Duranti (3), Maurizio Belladonna (3), Osunlade (2, via Atelewo), Kaskade (2), Finn Bjarnson (2), Arnaldo DeSouteiro (2).

Engineers: Pipo (4) — mastering, 6 releases; Ben Grassini — engineer/mixing, 4 releases (incl. IU001 and various later singles); Don Grossinger — DMM mastering, 4 releases; Phil Austin — mastering, 3 releases; Ohm Guru — engineer, 3 releases.

Design: Gianluca Polinori — artwork/cover concept, 6 releases; Umbi Damiani — design concept, 6 releases; Elena Fiumi — photography/computer artwork, 5 releases. These three form the label’s core visual identity team.

Compilations curator: Fabrizio Carrer — compiled/selected, 5 releases (responsible for the Afro House and Jazz House series).

  • Ben Grassini — recurring mixing engineer (4 releases); connects to Yoruba Records where he also worked with Osunlade

Open Questions

  • The roster tool returns 135 release rows but the unique-masters catalog shows ~53 entries; the gap is pressings/variants. The “135 releases” figure in the original stub likely counts all Discogs release entries (including re-issues and territory variants).
  • Don Carlos (ID: 1188) is credited with 12 releases, extending to 2013 — later than any other artist on the label. The 2013 date on his entry may reflect a reissue or licensing rather than new activity; the DB shows his last Irma Unlimited single as IU030 (2003). Needs clarification.
  • The New York contact address (214 West 29th Street) suggests a US operational presence, but whether Irma Unlimited had a dedicated US office or this was the distribution partner’s address is unclear from Discogs.
  • No information in Discogs on how the label ended — whether it was folded, rebranded, or simply went dormant after 2006.
  • Kaskade (ID: 19116) is credited as producer on 2 Irma Unlimited releases — this would be the now-famous EDM DJ/producer in his early deep-house phase. Which releases these are is not confirmed from the credits summary alone.
