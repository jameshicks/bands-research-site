Not Two Records

Polish independent record label based in Kraków, founded in 1998 by Marek Winiarski (previously co-founder of GOWI Records). Specialises exclusively in free jazz, freely improvised music, and the broader European and American avant-garde. Discogs label ID: 94657. Website: nottwo.com.

As of the Discogs dump the label has issued approximately 356 releases under the catalog prefix MW (for Marek Winiarski), running from MW 701-2 (1998) to MW 1050+ in the early 2020s.

Overview

Not Two Records was launched in Kraków in 1998, growing out of Marek Winiarski’s earlier involvement with GOWI Records, another Polish jazz imprint. From its first releases the label’s focus was sharp: document free improvisation and free jazz, both by Polish musicians and by the international figures who passed through Poland on tour.

Early years (1998–2004): Polish base, international guests

The first catalog batch (MW 701–707) appeared in 1998 and mixed local Polish artists with the first imported names. Early signings included the duo of Adam Pierończyk and Leszek Możdżer (Live In Sofia, MW 701-2), the trio NRD (Sport I Religia, MW 702-2), Hasidic New Wave (Live In Cracow, MW 706-2), and a debut by the Arek Skolik trio (Checkmate, MW 707-2). The catalog quickly broadened to include ensembles such as the Simple Acoustic Trio (MW 712-2, 727-2) and the accordion trio Motion Trio (Play-Station, 2001). The first Satoko Fujii appearance came in 2003 with Zephyros by the Satoko Fujii Quartet (MW 752-2), an early sign of the label’s growing international reach.

Mid period (2005–2010): Chicago axis, American free jazz heavyweights

From 2005 the catalog pivoted toward the American free jazz and Chicago improvised-music scenes. Vandermark 5’s Alchemia (MW 750-2, 2005) signalled the arrival of Ken Vandermark as one of the label’s most prolific and closely associated artists. Anthony Braxton appeared in a duo with Chris Dahlgren (ABCD, MW 768-2, 2006). The late 2000s brought a flood of collaborations anchored by Joe McPhee, who became the single most-released artist on the label across his career there (16 releases as primary artist). The landmark The Damage Is Done (MW 823-2, 2009) united McPhee with Peter Brötzmann, Kent Kessler, and Michael Zerang, a release that drew sustained international attention to the label.

Drummer Klaus Kugel (Discogs 353136) and bassist Rafal Mazur (Discogs 1988671) emerged as key recurring Polish musicians during this period, frequently appearing on releases alongside the visiting Americans. Trombonist Steve Swell began a long and prolific association with Not Two, eventually accumulating 11 releases as primary artist. The Hungarian composer-violist Szilárd Mezei also became a recurring presence from 2008.

2010–2020: European expansion, ongoing pillars

The catalog continued to diversify geographically: Spanish pianist Agustí Fernandez (9 releases), French double bassist Joëlle Léandre (6 releases), Norwegian drummer Paal Nilssen-Love (4 releases), Swedish tuba player Per Åke Holmlander, Slovenian percussionist Zlatko Kaučič (7 releases), and Portuguese saxophonist Rodrigo Amado all became recurring contributors. Swedish collective Swedish Azz appeared 7 times between 2010 and 2013. Mats Gustafsson produced several releases and appeared as a performer (notably Hidros6 Knockin’ with Nu-Ensemblen, MW 924-2, 2014). Barry Guy and his various formations (Barry Guy New Orchestra, London Jazz Composers Orchestra) joined from 2013.

Peter Brötzmann remained a regular presence well into this period, including the confrontational Ears Are Filled With Wonder duo with Heather Leigh Murray (MW 940-2, 2016) and the live Konstrukt/Brötzmann collaboration Eklisia Sunday (MW 891-2, 2013) with the Turkish free jazz group Konstrukt.

The label also hosted Evan Parker (2 releases, 2011–2012), Matthew Shipp (4 releases, 2009–2020), Joe Morris (8 releases, 2008–2023), and Joe Fonda (6 releases, 2016–2021). The DKV Trio (Dave Rempis, Kent Kessler, Michael Zerang) appeared 6 times between 2012 and 2019, culminating in the expanded The Fire Each Time with Joe McPhee (MW 982-2, 2019).

Production consistency

The label’s house aesthetic runs consistent across the catalog. Designer Małgorzata Lipińska handled artwork and layout on 93 releases — a near-defining visual voice. Photographer Marek Wajda contributed to 39 releases and photographer Krzysztof Penarski to 35. Sound engineer Rafal Drewniany handled recording, mixing, and mastering on 54 releases. Marek Winiarski is credited as producer or executive producer on 76 releases across the full catalog.
