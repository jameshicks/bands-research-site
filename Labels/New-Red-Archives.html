From Discogs

Independent punk / hardcore label, established and based in San Francisco, CA since 1998. The label was founded in Brooklyn, New York in 1987 by Nicky Garratt. Bands include UK Subs, No Use For a Name, Hogan’s Heroes, Social Unrest, Anti-Flag, Samiam, Reagan Youth, and others. The New Red Archives catalog was acquired by Cleopatra Records in 2012. In 2013 New Red Archives became a division of Cleopatra Records.

Many releases have been repeatedly repressed / reissued. The label design can sometimes be used to roughly date the pressings. The first 8-9 years of production, most LPs have one black label stating the label name and a design of a female figure with 7 dancing devil-type characters above and behind her. In 1995 this was changed to a white label stating the label name with 4-headed, 4-winged bird-type beast. Even later issues — likely 2000s — completely do away with the New Red Archives specific label on one side.

— Discogs label profile, ID 38655

Overview

New Red Archives was founded in 1987 by UK Subs guitarist Nicky Garratt in Brooklyn, New York, as an independent punk and hardcore label; by 1998 it had relocated to San Francisco, where it remained based. The label’s output peaked in the 1990s — nearly half its full catalogue — issuing records by UK Subs, No Use For a Name, Anti-Flag, Samiam, Reagan Youth, Social Unrest, and Hogan’s Heroes, among others, with many titles repeatedly repressed and reissued across different label designs. In 2012 the New Red Archives catalogue was acquired by Cleopatra Records, and the following year the label became an active division of Cleopatra, continuing to release punk material through the 2010s and 2020s.

Notes (web)

Label relocations — Brooklyn → Hollywood → San Francisco

Brooklyn → Hollywood (summer 1990). In a 2020 interview republished via PM Press (original in Peek-a-Boo Magazine), Garratt described the move as a deliberate restart: “I moved to California, to Los Angeles and restarted it.” The label’s Brooklyn address was 43 Broadway 4F1, Brooklyn, NY 11211; the Hollywood address was 6520 Selma Ave., Suite #1305, Hollywood, CA 90028. The interview gives no further detail on why Los Angeles specifically, but Garratt’s framing (“restarted”) suggests it was treated as a relaunch rather than a purely incidental change of address. He had been working at Caroline Records in New York before founding NRA; the move ended that period. [https://blog.pmpress.org/2020/02/18/nicky-garratt-uk-subs-hedersleben-in-peek-aboo-magazine/]

Hollywood → San Francisco (by 1998). The same interview gives a direct reason for this second move: “I moved up to San Francisco because I was playing in a band called ‘Ten Bright Spikes’ with singer from Social Unrest.” Garratt bought a house in San Francisco and carried the label north with him — a personal/musical relocation rather than a deliberate label relaunch. Ten Bright Spikes released two records on NRA in 1991 (Vertical Brando, NRA24; The La Mancha Candidate E.P., NRA25), and Social Unrest became one of the label’s core roster bands through the 1990s and 2000s. The Alex Ogg UK Subs history (uksubstimeandmatter.net) independently confirms the sequence of Garratt residences as “New York City, Los Angeles, San Francisco.” [https://blog.pmpress.org/2020/02/18/nicky-garratt-uk-subs-hedersleben-in-peek-aboo-magazine/] [https://uksubstimeandmatter.net/index.php?Itemid=146&id=16903&option=com_content&view=article]

What was not found. No source gives a specific motivation for choosing Los Angeles (over other US cities) for the 1990 restart. The interview does not name who the Ten Bright Spikes vocalist from Social Unrest was, nor exactly when Garratt relocated to San Francisco before the 1998 label address change.
