Antilles Records

Aug 19, 20266 min read

Antilles Records

Discogs label ID: 998 Parent label: Island Records (parent ID: 8377) Label Code: LC 6176 / LC 06176 Active: 1975–1999 (label launched August 1975; dissolved January 1999) Location: New York (US imprint; also issued in Canada from early 1976)

Overview

Antilles was the primary experimental and jazz subsidiary of Island Records for the US market, and one of the more eclectic imprints to emerge from Island’s 1970s expansion. It was established through a holding company called Antilles Communications Ltd., with the label itself launched in August 1975. Its initial catalog was wide-ranging — UK folk, African music, blues reissues, ambient electronics, progressive rock, and free jazz all appeared in the first two years alongside Fripp & Eno records and Nick Drake reissues.

The label’s identity shifted considerably across its 24-year lifespan, passing through at least three distinct phases driven by corporate changes at Island and, later, at PolyGram.

Phase 1: 1975–1981 — Eclectic Island Imprint

The early Antilles catalog (cat numbers AN-7001 through roughly AN-820) was essentially Island’s US umbrella for anything that didn’t fit its pop operation. Brian Eno’s first three solo-era records on Island all appeared on Antilles in the US: Discreet Music (AN-7030, 1975), Evening Star (AN-7018, 1976, with Robert Fripp as Fripp & Eno), and Music For Films (AN-7070, 1978). Nick Drake’s Five Leaves Left and Bryter Layter were reissued here. British folk artists (Shirley Collins, The Albion Country Band, Fairport Convention offshoot projects, June Tabor) appeared alongside African music compilations and Chicago blues reissues.

The no-wave scene entered the catalog via two landmarks. No New York (AN-7067, 1978), produced by Brian Eno, documented four bands — The Contortions, Teenage Jesus And The Jerks, Mars, and DNA — in what became the defining document of the genre. Eno also designed the cover. Also in 1978: Ultravox’s Systems Of Romance (AN-7069). Suicide’s second, self-titled album (AN-7080, 1980), produced by Ric Ocasek, appeared on Antilles in the US; it’s a foundational electronic rock record.

The early 1980s saw Kid Creole And The Coconuts release several singles and LPs on Antilles (AN-802 through AN-807 roughly), connecting the label directly to the ZE Records orbit. ZE Records and Island shared US distribution infrastructure, and several ZE artists — including Cristina (AN-803, 1980) and Was (Not Was) (AN-805, 1980) — had Antilles releases during this period. The Waitresses’ “I Know What Boys Like” single (ANS-105, 1980) also came out on Antilles.

Phase 2: 1982–1988 — New Jazz Focus, Renamed New/Disk

Around 1980, Antilles Communications Ltd. renamed itself Island Entertainment Group (not publicized until 1987). By the mid-1980s the label had narrowed to concentrate on experimental and avant-garde jazz, and was briefly operating as New/Disk (Discogs label ID 132266) before reverting to Antilles proper in 1989 when PolyGram completed its purchase of Island’s remaining stake.

Key artists in this period:

  • Ornette Coleman — Of Human Feelings (AN-2001, 1982), the first Harmolodic release to appear on a major-distributed imprint, with Prime Time including Bern Nix, Denardo Coleman, and Jamaaladeen Tacuma.
  • Ronald Shannon Jackson And The Decoding Society — Mandance (AN-1008, 1982) and Barbeque Dog (AN-1015, 1983).
  • Anthony Braxton — Six Compositions: Quartet (AN-1005, 1982).
  • Philip Glass — Koyaanisqatsi soundtrack (ASTA-1, 1983), one of the label’s biggest-selling records.
  • The Lounge Lizards — Live In Tokyo - Big Heart (1986) and No Pain For Cakes (208 279, 1987). No Pain For Cakes featured the core downtown lineup: John Lurie (alto sax, producer, cover painting), Evan Lurie (piano), Marc Ribot (guitar, banjo, cornet), Roy Nathanson (reeds), Curtis Fowlkes (trombone), Erik Sanko (bass), Dougie Bowne (drums), E.J. Rodriguez (percussion), Jill Jaffe (violin). Engineered and mixed by Seigen Ono.
  • Defunkt — In America (ANC-8730, 1988).
  • Power Tools — Strange Meeting (1988), the trio of Bill Frisell, Melvin Gibbs, and Ronald Shannon Jackson.
  • Allen Ginsberg — The Lion For Real (AN-8750, 1989).

Phase 3: 1989–1999 — PolyGram Jazz Division

After PolyGram’s full acquisition of Island in 1989, Antilles reverted to its proper name and refocused strictly on jazz, eventually moving to PolyGram’s Verve division in 1992. This phase produced a large catalog of mainstream and post-bop jazz, with Courtney Pine becoming the label’s most prolific artist (59 releases in the DB). Key vault-adjacent artists in this period:

Marc Ribot — Rootless Cosmopolitans (ANC-8749, 1990), his debut solo record. Features Don Byron (clarinet), Roy Nathanson (sax), Anthony Coleman (keyboards), Brad Jones (bass). Co-produced by Ribot and Hugo Dwyer.

Evan Lurie — Selling Water By The Side Of The River (AN-8754, 1990). Intimate piano-led record featuring Marc Ribot (guitar), Alfredo Pedernera (bandoneon), Jill Jaffe (violin, viola), John Beal (bass).

Steve Turre — three albums: Right There (1991), Sanctified Shells (314-514-186-2, 1993), and Rhythm Within (314-527-159-2, 1995). Sanctified Shells featured Dizzy Gillespie as special guest, plus Robin Eubanks and Charlie Sepulveda. Steve Turre’s conch shell work is central to those sessions.

John Lurie — The Lounge Lizards solo credits (artist ID 143566) appear on Antilles in 1995.

Gil Evans — appeared on Antilles in the early 1980s.

The label was dissolved in January 1999 following the PolyGram/MCA merger that created Universal Music Group.

Label Connections

  • Island Records — parent; Island’s US distribution infrastructure underpinned Antilles throughout
  • ZE Records — shared Island/Antilles US distribution; overlapping rosters (Kid Creole, Cristina, Was (Not Was)) in 1980–81
  • Verve Records — Antilles was folded into Verve’s PolyGram division from 1992
