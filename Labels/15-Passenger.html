Overview

15 Passenger is an independent label founded in 2017, operating as the primary home for Cursive and its core members — vocalist Tim Kasher, bassist Matt Maginn, guitarist Ted Stevens, and producer Mike Mogis. The catalogue sits squarely in the Omaha post-hardcore and indie rock scene, running to twelve releases across the 2010s and 2020s. No parent label or corporate structure is on record, suggesting it functions as a band-controlled imprint.

Notes (web)

Why Cursive left Saddle Creek for 15 Passenger

Multiple 2017–2019 interviews with Tim Kasher consistently describe the departure as amicable and driven by a desire for catalog control and DIY independence — not by specific tensions with Saddle Creek Records.

Catalog control as the seed. The clearest origin story comes from a 2017 The Reader interview: “That’s definitely where it started” — referring to bassist Matt Maginn’s desire to control Cursive’s back catalog — “but we also want to release stuff from artists we like.” (thereader.com, 2017-12-06)

Returning to DIY ethos. Kasher consistently framed the move as reclaiming the spirit that existed before Saddle Creek grew into a national entity. In a 2017 Charleston City Paper interview he said: “That spirit of releasing records ourselves is still there, but Saddle Creek hasn’t been our label for a long time.” He added they were older and preferred “control and do it ourselves.” (charlestoncitypaper.com, 2017-10-11)

No hostility toward Saddle Creek. In the same period Kasher praised the label: “We started putting out stuff with Saddle Creek early on in their existence and got to watch them figure it out as they went along which was a good education on how to do things.” (thereader.com, 2017-12-06) A Little Village interview echoed this — Kasher said “nothing against Saddle Creek, but we’re pretty DIY.” (littlevillagemag.com, interview circa 2017)

Matt Maginn’s role. Maginn (bassist) is the operational driver: he had previously worked at Saddle Creek and helped Conor Oberst launch Team Love. His experience made him the natural person to run 15 Passenger’s business side. (search result snippet, allmusic.com)

The label name. Named after a 15-passenger van the band has used over the years; Maginn reportedly still drives one. (thereader.com, 2017-12-06)

Timeline note. The formal split from Saddle Creek had already occurred before 15 Passenger launched — Cursive’s last Saddle Creek release was I Am Gemini (2012). The 2017 label launch came after several years without a label home, not as an immediate pivot away from Saddle Creek.

What was checked but not found: No source was found stating any specific disagreement, contract dispute, or stated tension between Cursive and Saddle Creek. No Saddle Creek statement or response to the departure was located. The absence of conflict reporting across multiple independent interviews over 2017–2019 is consistent with the amicable characterization.
