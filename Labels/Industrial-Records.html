Industrial Records

Discogs ID: 9095 (sublabel of Industrial Records Ltd., Discogs ID: 278963) Location: London, UK Active: 1977–1981 (company incorporated 6 January 1977; Certificate of Incorporation no. 1396998 received 11 June 1979; company closed January 1982 following TG split; reactivated 4 November 2008 via Mute/EMI sub-licensing; returned to independence 23 September 2010) Founders: Throbbing Gristle (Genesis P-Orridge, Peter Christopherson, Cosey Fanni Tutti, Chris Carter (2)) Motto: “Industrial Music For Industrial People”

Overview

Industrial Records was founded in September 1977 by the performance art/music group Throbbing Gristle primarily as a self-release vehicle, but it rapidly became a conduit for a loose international network of artists working at the extreme margins of recorded sound. The label’s name — and its motto — provided industrial music with its genre designation, a term that stuck despite the catalogue being too varied to fit any single sonic description.

TG’s founding intent was anti-commercial: no major label deal, no conventional distribution, direct mail-order sales from their base at the COUM Transmissions collective space (and later Beck Road, Hackney). Releases were deliberately difficult — limited editions, challenging packaging, transgressive content — and the catalogue was numbered with the IR prefix from the outset. The cassette series (IRC prefix) ran in parallel, documenting TG’s own live performances as well as releases by affiliated artists including Cabaret Voltaire, Clock DVA, and Richard H. Kirk solo.

Beyond TG’s own records, Industrial signed or distributed work by San Francisco provocateur Monte Cazazza (often described as TG’s “sixth member”), Swedish post-punk group The Leather Nun, synth pioneers Robert Rental and Thomas Leer, Australian industrial group SPK, and — most incongruously — a vintage jazz single by 1930s cabaret star Elisabeth Welch. The final release before closure was William S. Burroughs’s Nothing Here Now But The Recordings (IR0016, 1981), a spoken-word/tape document co-produced by Peter Christopherson and Genesis P-Orridge, with a direct line to the cut-up literary tradition that influenced TG from the start.

The label closed when Throbbing Gristle disbanded at their final concert at the Lyceum, London, on 29 May 1981.

Complete Catalog

The catalog divides into three series: IR (vinyl/physical releases, IR0001–IR0016), IRC (cassette series, IRC 0–IRC 35), and IRVC (video cassette, IRVC 1–2). The following covers all original-era releases (1977–1981).

Note on IR0001: IR0001 was always a cassette — Best Of… Volume II, originally hand-distributed in an edition of ~50 copies in 1976 (predating the label’s formal registration). It was the literal first Industrial Records catalog number. A 1978 variant (30 copies, White Christmas B-side) circulated under “IR0001”; it was also reissued as IRC 1 in 1979 and again in 1983 on CHMS. The vinyl catalog starts cleanly at IR0002 (The Second Annual Report, 1977). (Resolved via Discogs, 2026-05-07.)

Key People

TG Founders

  • Genesis P-Orridge (Discogs ID: 22533; real name Neil Megson) — bass, violin, vocals, conceptual driver. Coordinated the Burroughs LP release; sleeve notes credited as “Tony Graves.” Former COUM Transmissions. Later: Psychic TV, Thee Temple ov Psychick Youth.
  • Peter Christopherson (Discogs ID: 34069; alias “Sleazy”) — tape, machines, electronics, sleeve photography and design. Co-produced the Burroughs LP. Former Hipgnosis (2) (graphic design). Later: Coil, Psychic TV.
  • Cosey Fanni Tutti (Discogs ID: 93112; real name Christine Newby) — lead guitar, cornet, effects. Contributed piano and vocals to Monte Cazazza’s Something For Nobody. Later: Chris & Cosey, Carter Tutti, Carter Tutti Void.
  • Chris Carter (2) (Discogs ID: 83832) — synthesizers, electronics, rhythms. Engineered Monte Cazazza’s Something For Nobody. Solo release: The Space Between (IRC 32). Later: Chris & Cosey, Carter Tutti, Carter Tutti Void.

Other Key Figures

  • Monte Cazazza (Discogs ID: 57533) — San Francisco-based performance artist and provocateur. Three IR/IRC releases (IR0005, IR0010, IRC 28). Often described as Industrial Records’ “sixth member.” Collaborated closely with TG, Genesis P-Orridge in particular.
  • William S. Burroughs (Discogs ID: 50077) — Beat author; Nothing Here Now But The Recordings (IR0016) drawn from his tape archive at the Beat Hotel. Co-ordinated by Christopherson and James Grauerholz.
  • Steve Angel (Discogs ID: 368590) — recurring lacquer-cut engineer across nearly all IR vinyl releases.
  • Sinclair/Brooks (Discogs ID: 778177) — producer credit on multiple TG releases (IR0008, IR0009, IR0010, IR0013, IR0015). An alias of Throbbing Gristle themselves.

Distribution and Mail-Order Model

Industrial Records operated almost entirely outside conventional retail distribution. Sales were handled by direct mail order, with customers communicating via post. The label maintained a deliberate mystique: minimal information on releases, limited editions often with unusual packaging (camouflage bags, hand-stamped sleeves), and catalogue numbers hand-etched into vinyl runout grooves by lacquer-cutter Steve Angel. This model made each purchase a deliberate act and kept the label’s commercial exposure to a minimum — consistent with TG’s anti-spectacle aesthetic derived from their COUM Transmissions performance art background.

The IRC cassette series — 35+ live and studio tapes, primarily documenting TG concerts in 1979–1980 — was sold through the same mail-order operation, offering an archive of the live work that TG always considered central to their practice alongside the studio records.

During the 1980s after the label closed, many of the cassette releases were reproduced by labels including Flowmotion, alongside numerous unofficial bootlegs.

Closure and Legacy

Throbbing Gristle played their final concert at the Lyceum, London, on 29 May 1981, and disbanded immediately. Industrial Records Ltd. closed in January 1982. The label’s dissolution fed directly into several successor projects:

  • Psychic TV — formed by Genesis P-Orridge with Peter Christopherson and others; initially on Temple Records.
  • Coil — formed by Peter Christopherson and John Balance; released on various labels.
  • Chris & Cosey — Cosey Fanni Tutti and Chris Carter; signed to Rough Trade Records and later Conspiracy International.

The “industrial” label, already attached to a genre by 1981, spread to cover a wide range of post-TG artists working in noise, power electronics, EBM, and experimental electronics. Labels such as Fetish Records, Some Bizzare, and Wax Trax! carried the industrial aesthetic forward through the 1980s.

Industrial Records Ltd. was reactivated in November 2008 to handle sub-licensed reissues through Mute/EMI, then returned to independence in September 2010, releasing material by X-TG (the remaining TG members following P-Orridge’s departure) and Carter Tutti Void.

Open Questions

  • The Discogs profile gives founding as 1976 (when TG formed) but official registration was 6 January 1977 and first release (IR0002) dated 1977. The “1976” date in the DB profile may refer to TG’s formation rather than label registration.
  • Exact pressing quantities for original IR vinyl runs are not documented in Discogs.
  • IRC 0 (Best Of Volume I) predates IRC 1 (Best Of Volume II) — the exact dating and relationship of these two earliest cassettes is slightly unclear from Discogs data alone.
  • Dorothy Max Prior’s affiliation with the label beyond IR0014 — she later joined Rema-Rema and Psychic TV; the Discogs profile lists her as a Psychic TV member but the IR single appears to be her only release on the label.
  • “Tony Graves” credited on IR0014 sleeve notes is an alias of Genesis P-Orridge (confirmed by Discogs name variations on artist 22533).
