Chapter 22

UK independent record label founded by Craig Jennings (Discogs artist ID: 1731717), based in Warwick (6 New Street, Warwick, CV34 4RX). Active from 1984 through the early 1990s, with a minor revival of activity from 2021 onwards. Discogs label ID: 9010.

Overview

Chapter 22 was a Midlands-rooted British independent label that served as the home of some of the most distinctive UK indie and alternative acts of the late 1980s. Founded by Craig Jennings — who doubled as manager for the label’s biggest act, Pop Will Eat Itself — the label launched in 1984 with Balaam And The Angel’s debut single “World Of Light” and quickly grew into a significant player in the UK indie scene.

The label’s catalog spans a wide arc of post-punk and alternative rock: goth-adjacent acts (Balaam And The Angel, The Wild Flowers, The Mission’s early singles), C86-era indie pop (Mighty Mighty, The Pastels), grebo/sample-driven rock (Pop Will Eat Itself), noise-drone (Loop), Brummie indie-jangle (Ned’s Atomic Dustbin), and the post-punk veterans Suicide and Alternative TV. This eclecticism was one of the label’s defining characteristics — it was not a genre label so much as a quality-focused indie operation with a particular ear for guitar-driven but sonically adventurous music.

The label’s most internationally visible release was Suicide’s A Way Of Life (1988, CHAP LP 35), produced by Ric Ocasek. Pop Will Eat Itself’s Chapter 22 releases (Box Frenzy, 1987) were the commercial peak; PWEI’s signing to RCA followed their Chapter 22 success. Loop’s Fade Out (1988) and Ned’s Atomic Dustbin’s early singles (1990) were also key catalog entries.

Craig Jennings ran the associated Raw Power Management operation; he continued managing Pop Will Eat Itself after the label transitioned artists to major deals. The label appears in Discogs under the alternate credit entity ID 1027047 and a related company L506625.

Connections

  • Suicide — released A Way Of Life (1988), their third studio LP, produced by Ric Ocasek. Both Alan Vega and Martin Rev credited as writers and performers. Engineer: Joe Barbaria.
  • Pop Will Eat Itself — label’s cornerstone act; Craig Jennings managed them through RCA years and beyond. Box Frenzy (1987) the pivotal release. The Designers Republic’s visual language developed here.
  • The Mission — two early Chapter 22 singles (“I”, “II”, 1986) before signing to Mercury. The Mission self-produced these; engineer Colin Richardson. Lineup: Wayne Hussey, Craig Adams, Mick Brown, Simon Hinkler.
  • The Pastels — Glasgow indie pop; two releases in 1989. Producer Richard Mazda. Stephen Pastel and Aggi contributed sleeve design.
  • Alternative TV — Mark Perry’s long-running post-punk project; Dragon Love LP (1990) and “The Sol E.P.” on Chapter 22.
  • Ned’s Atomic Dustbin — Stourbridge band; early singles and EPs (1990) produced by Jessica Corcoran and the band themselves. Precursor to their Furtive/Columbia deal.
  • Loop (3) — Robert Hampson’s noise-drone project; Fade Out LP (1988) and associated singles. Self-produced with Paul Kendall.
  • Mighty Mighty — Birmingham indie pop; C86 generation; four singles and Sharks LP (1988). Several UK indie chart Top 10 entries.
  • Balaam And The Angel — Cumbrian goth-rock band; label’s first act (1984); produced by John A. Rivers.
  • The Designers Republic — Sheffield design studio behind Pop Will Eat Itself artwork; appeared on 14 Chapter 22 releases. Foundational to PWEI corporate aesthetic.

Research Queue

  • The Mission’s two Chapter 22 singles (“I” and “II”, 1986) predate their Mercury deal — confirm whether these were released with or without a subsequent licensing/distribution arrangement, and whether Wayne Hussey has discussed the Chapter 22 period in any interview.
  • Confirm whether Loop’s Fade Out (Chapter 22, 1988) had a simultaneous or subsequent US release, and if so on which label.
  • The Pastels’ Sittin’ Pretty (1989) lists producer Richard Mazda — confirm whether this was the same Richard Mazda who worked with Siouxsie And The Banshees and other UK post-punk acts, or a different person.
