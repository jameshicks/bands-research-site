Overview

Founded in Hong Kong in 1987 by Klaus Heymann as a budget-priced classical offshoot of HNH International Ltd. — then Asia’s largest record distributor — Naxos was built to satisfy Far Eastern demand for affordable classical CDs. Its early catalogue was recorded primarily in Slovakia and Hungary, drawing on ensembles such as the Capella Istropolitana and the Slovak Radio Symphony Orchestra; from 1994 the label broadened to take in artists from Western Europe and North America, adding contemporary composers including Henryk Górecki, Philip Glass, Michael Nyman, and John Tavener alongside established instrumentalists such as Jenö Jandó and Maria Kliegel. With over 12,000 catalogue credits spanning Baroque, Classical, Romantic, and Modern repertoire, and distributed worldwide through NGL Naxos Global Logistics GmbH, Naxos grew into one of the largest classical music labels in the world.
