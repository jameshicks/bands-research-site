Capitol Records

Discogs ID: 654 Parent: Capitol Music Group (Discogs ID: 126439) Founded: April 8, 1942, Hollywood, California (as “Liberty Records”; renamed June 4, 1942) Address: 1750 North Vine Street, Hollywood, CA 90028 Label Code: LC 0148 / LC 00148

One of the original “Big Six” US major labels. Founded by lyricist Johnny Mercer, producer Buddy G. De Sylva, and record store owner Glenn Wallichs. By 1946 had sold 42 million records. Acquired by British company EMI (Electrical and Musical Industries) in January 1955 for $8.5 million — the first major US label to come under foreign ownership. EMI began releasing Capitol product in the UK and Europe in January 1956. Made part of the EMI Worldwide Division in 1979. In 2001, merged with Priority Records under the Capitol Music Group umbrella. Purchased by Vivendi S.A. (Universal Music Group parent) in September 2012; as of 2024, part of Universal’s Interscope Capitol Labels Group.

Capitol is far too large to fully enumerate from Discogs — the label has tens of thousands of releases spanning eight decades. What follows focuses on connections to the vault’s primary scenes: post-punk, no wave, art rock, punk, indie, hip-hop avant-garde.

The Band (1968–1977)

Capitol’s deepest connection to the vault’s scene is as the primary label for The Band across their entire original run. All studio albums and key live records issued on Capitol:

Members — Rick Danko, Levon Helm, Garth Hudson, Richard Manuel, Robbie Robertson — recorded all original studio output on Capitol. Numerous anthology and best-of compilations followed on the label into the 2000s and beyond (1,442 release entries on Discogs as of the current DB).

Television (1992)

Television — the CBGB-era post-punk art-rock band (Tom Verlaine, Richard Lloyd, Billy Ficca, Fred Smith) — signed to Capitol for their 1992 reunion/self-titled album, their first new record since Adventure (1978). The album was released worldwide on Capitol across multiple pressings (US, Canada, UK, Europe, Japan, Chile). Their original 1970s albums (Marquee Moon, Adventure) had been on Elektra; Capitol was the reunion home. See Tom Verlaine, Richard Lloyd, Billy Ficca, Fred Smith (7).

Butthole Surfers (1992–1993)

Butthole Surfers moved to Capitol for two albums after years on Touch and Go / Latino Buggerveil:

ReleaseYear
Piouhgd1992
Independent Worm Saloon1993

Both were major-label attempts to break the band wider. Paul Leary’s guitar and Gibby Haynes’s provocateur approach remained intact. See also Trance Syndicate Records (their later indie home) and Paul Leary.

Beastie Boys and Grand Royal (1989–2004)

Beastie Boys joined Capitol in 1989 after Licensed to Ill on Def Jam, beginning one of the label’s most creatively important relationships with the indie/alt-hip-hop world. Key Capitol releases:

Mario Caldato Jr. was principal engineer/producer from Check Your Head onward.

Capitol distributed and co-released product on the Beastie Boys’ own Grand Royal imprint (Discogs label IDs: 2236 and 584293), which operated as a Capitol sublabel from 1992 until its closure c. 2001. See Grand Royal for the full Grand Royal roster (At The Drive-In, Luscious Jackson, Liquid Liquid reissues, Atari Teenage Riot, Buffalo Daughter, Bis, Money Mark, Sean Lennon, Propellerheads, etc.).

Grand Royal Sublabel — Scene Highlights

Beyond the Beastie Boys catalog, Grand Royal (as a Capitol sublabel) released or distributed several scene-adjacent records of note:

  • At The Drive-In — Relationship of Command (2000, Grand Royal/Capitol), produced by Ross Robinson, mixed by Andy Wallace. Their breakout major-label record.
  • Liquid Liquid — Compilation LP (1997, Grand Royal GR 049), produced by Ed Bahlman; a reissue of their 99 Records no-wave dance catalog. See Ed Bahlman and 99 Records.
  • De Facto (2) — 456132015 (2001, Grand Royal GR 096); Omar Rodríguez-López and Cedric Bixler-Zavala’s side project.
  • Atari Teenage Riot — Not Your Business E.P. (1996) and Burn, Berlin, Burn! (1997) on Grand Royal.
  • Luscious Jackson — Multiple albums including Natural Ingredients (1994), Fever In Fever Out (1996), Electric Honey (1999).
  • Money Mark — Mark’s Keyboard Repair (1996), Push The Button (1998).
  • Bis — Several releases 1996–1999 including The New Transistor Heroes (1997).
  • Buffalo Daughter — Multiple releases 1996–1999.
  • Sean Lennon — Into The Sun (1998), Home - 5/8 (1998).
  • Propellerheads — Decksandrumsandrockandroll (1998) and History Repeating (1997, with Shirley Bassey).

Radiohead (1992–2008)

Radiohead were signed to Capitol in North America throughout their career under EMI. Their entire major discography from Pablo Honey (1993) through In Rainbows (2008 US release) appeared on Capitol in the US market (the underlying releases being on Parlophone in the UK). Hundreds of pressings across formats. Key figures include Thom Yorke, Jonny Greenwood, Colin Greenwood, Ed O’Brien, Phil Selway, and longtime collaborators Nigel Godrich (producer) and Stanley Donwood (artwork).

Mantronix / Wondress Hutchinson (1989–1990)

Mantronix featuring Wondress Hutchinson issued two UK Top 10 singles on Capitol:

  • Got To Have Your Love (1989)
  • Take Your Time (Remix) (1990)

Other Vault-Adjacent Artists

  • Blondie — 222 Capitol release entries, primarily reissues of the Chrysalis catalog from 1997 onward (Parallel Lines, Eat To The Beat, Autoamerican, etc.). Original studio albums were on Chrysalis, not Capitol.
  • R.E.M. — IRS-era catalog (Document, Eponymous) reissued on Capitol in the US from the late 1990s; their active major-label output was on Warner Bros.
  • Megadeth — Long-running Capitol roster artist 1986 onward (1,400+ releases). Heavy metal, not vault-scene, but notable.
  • Pink Floyd — 559 Capitol entries; early US pressings of Pink Floyd material came via Capitol (as EMI’s US arm), before the band’s EMI deal applied.

Label History Notes

  • Founded as “Liberty Records” April 8, 1942; renamed Capitol June 4, 1942 to avoid conflict with Liberty Music Shops (NYC)
  • EMI acquisition January 1955 for $8.5 million — first major US label under foreign ownership
  • Stereo discs introduced August 18, 1958
  • Made part of EMI Worldwide Division, 1979
  • Capitol Records Nashville (ID: 115663) operates as a separate country music arm
  • Sold to Universal/Vivendi September 2012; now part of Interscope Capitol Labels Group
