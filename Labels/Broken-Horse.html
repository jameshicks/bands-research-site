Overview

Broken Horse is a Manchester-based indie and lo-fi label that began releasing records in 2002. Its catalogue spans indie rock, acoustic, pop rock, and post-rock styles, with nineteen of its roughly twenty-seven releases appearing in the 2000s and activity tapering off to seven in the 2010s. The roster skews toward singer-songwriter and chamber-pop-adjacent artists, notably including Chicago-based Kevin Tihista and Liam Hayes alongside UK acts Ellis Clark, Patrick Cleandenim, and Charles Douglas. No parent label or corporate structure is on record.

Notes (web) — transatlantic connections

The Parasol Records link. The clearest structural explanation for Broken Horse’s US-heavy roster is a distribution partnership with Parasol Records, an American independent based in Urbana, Illinois. The Doleful Lions’ 2002 debut Out Like A Lamb (BKH-CD 001, the label’s first release) was credited “Parasol Records / Broken Horse UK” — meaning Broken Horse handled UK/European distribution for Parasol titles. Wikipedia – Doleful Lions (as of 2026-11-12).

Kevin Tihista’s Home Demons Vol. 1 (2005) was co-released on both Parasol and Broken Horse, reinforcing that he was delivered to Broken Horse through the same Parasol channel. Subsequent Tihista releases (On This Dark Street, 2012; Modern Standard, 2013) appeared on Broken Horse alone — suggesting the relationship had deepened into direct signing beyond the initial distribution arrangement. By 2009 Broken Horse was described as Tihista’s “UK label” and had reportedly purchased him home-recording equipment to facilitate new recordings, per a note Tihista posted on the Drowned in Sound message boards that year. Wikipedia – Kevin Tihista (as of 2026-11-12).

Liam Hayes / Plush. Plush’s Fed had been released exclusively in Japan on the After Hours label in 2002; it remained obscure for six years until Broken Horse issued the European release in August 2008. Bright Penny followed in 2009, again on Broken Horse. No Parasol co-release is documented for Hayes — the acquisition of Fed appears to have been Broken Horse seeking out a critically under-noticed album rather than a pipeline deal, though the label’s taste (formed through the Parasol relationship) evidently ran toward melodic US indie. Wikipedia – Fed (album) (as of 2026-11-12).

Broader pattern. The same UK-distribution-for-US-indie model is visible elsewhere in the catalogue: The Autumn Defense’s self-titled album was released in the US on Broadmoor and by Broken Horse in the UK/Europe; The Autumn Defense are a Wilco side project, also from the Chicago/Illinois indie sphere. This suggests Broken Horse’s transatlantic connections were primarily through the Urbana/Illinois indie network (Parasol and its orbit) rather than through any founder with personal US ties — though no founder name has been identified from public sources.
