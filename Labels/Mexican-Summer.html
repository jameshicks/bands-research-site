Mexican Summer

Discogs ID: 131596
Address: 87 Guernsey St., Brooklyn, NY 11222
Parent: Kemado Records (founded 2002 by Andrés Santo Domingo + Thomas Clapp)
Founded: 2008 (as Kemado imprint for small-edition vinyl)
Active: 2008–present
Total releases: ~903 (Discogs)
Sublabels: Anthology Recordings, Software (2), Marfa Myths, Looking Glass (4)
Web: mexicansummer.com | bandcamp.com/mexicansummer

Independent Brooklyn label. Established in 2008 by Kemado Records, Inc. as an imprint focused on limited vinyl editions; it rapidly grew into the primary identity of the Kemado operation. In 2014, Kemado became the Kemado Group and shifted toward media rights, management, and distribution, with Mexican Summer continuing as the label arm and Anthology Recordings (relaunched 2015) as an archival/reissue imprint alongside Anthology Editions (book publishing, 2016).

The label operates in tandem with Anthology, an archival imprint and book publisher focused on licensed reissues and definitive physical presentation.

History

2008: Founding Class

Mexican Summer launched with a founding cohort drawn from the psych and metal edges of the Brooklyn/NY underground, anchored by a pre-existing relationship to Dungen (Sweden) through Kemado Records.

  • MEX 001 (2008): Dungen — Sätt Att Se 12” — the first release; produced, recorded, and written by Gustav Ejstes; Reine Fiske (guitar, bass), Johan Holmegard (drums); mastered by Carl-Michael Herlöfsson; executive producer Stefan Kéry
  • MEX 002 (2008): Nachtmystium — Worldfall (3 pressings) — black metal
  • MEX 003 (2008): Headdress — Turquoise
  • MEX 006 (2008): The Tallest Man On Earth — Shallow Grave (3 pressings) — Kristian Matsson
  • MEX 007 (2008): Ariel Pink’s Haunted Graffiti — Can’t Hear My Eyes 7” — band: Ariel Pink (vocals, guitar/bass), Tim Koh (bass, drums, backing vocals), Cole M.G.N. (drums, keyboards, backing vocals), Jimi Hey (backing vocals, guitar), Kenny Keys (2) (bass, keyboards), Giles Miller (saxophone)

The genre breadth of the founding class — Swedish psych-rock, USBM, lo-fi pop — defined Mexican Summer’s “no genre restriction” operating principle.

2009: Lo-Fi Moment

The 2009 wave aligned the label with chillwave, lo-fi indie, and the bedroom recording explosion then centered on blogs and Bandcamp:

  • MEX 012 (2009): Kurt Vile — God Is Saying This To You… (12 pressings) — Vile’s breakthrough 7”; also released contemporaneously on Woodsist
  • MEX 014 (2009): Wooden Shjips — Contact — numbered limited-edition 12” at 45 RPM; two tracks: “Contact” (Serge Gainsbourg cover, 8:19) / “I Hear The Vibratios (E-Z Version)” (Ripley Johnson + Wooden Shjips, 7:17); recorded and mixed by Don Rifle and Tedrick G. 2010: Best Coast and Expansion

  • MEX 042 (2010): Linda Perhacs — Parallelograms reissue — 1970 psych-folk LP licensed for US repress
  • MEX 053 (2010): Best Coast — Crazy For You (14 pressings) — Bethany Cosentino + Bobb Bruno; recorded, mixed, and engineered by Lewis Pesacov; design by David Rager. One of the label’s first major commercial/critical moments.

  • MEX 060 (2010): Dungen — Skit I Allt (5 pressings)

2011: Software, OPN, and the Scene Peak

  • SFT 010 (2011): Oneohtrix Point Never — Replica (6 pressings) — produced by Daniel Lopatin, Joel Ford, Al Carlson; mixed by Al Carlson; mastered by Joe Lambert

Software (2) Sublabel

Software (2) (Discogs ID 245098) is a Mexican Summer sublabel run in collaboration with Daniel Lopatin (Oneohtrix Point Never). It housed the electronics / experimental wing of the label:

  • SFT 001 (2011): Ford & Lopatin — Channel Pressure — Daniel Lopatin + Joel Ford
  • SFT 010 (2011): Oneohtrix Point Never — Replica
  • SFT 013 (2013): Autre Ne Veut — Anxiety
  • SFT 027–032: OPN reissues and archival releases

Scene Context and Connections

Mexican Summer emerged from the same 2009 lo-fi / chillwave wave that produced Woodsist and Acéphale. The Woodsist connection is structural: Kurt Vile appeared on both labels in 2009 (MEX 012 and Woodsist’s God Is Saying This To You overlap); Real Estate (2) had early releases on both. Jarvis Taveniere (15 producer credits at Mexican Summer) is a member of Woods (2) — Jeremy Earl’s Woodsist backbone band.

The Dungen thread runs deep: Gustav Ejstes’s band was on Kemado Records before Mexican Summer existed; their presence on MEX 001 is a legacy connection. The Dungen / Woods (2) Myths 003 collaboration (MEX 244, 2018) crystallizes the overlap.

Weyes Blood (Natalie Mering) is the label’s most significant long-term act — debut in 2014, most-pressed LP in 2016 (Front Row Seat To Earth, 16 pressings), and still active in 2025. She also designs her own releases (9 design credits) and appeared on the Myths 002 compilation alongside Ariel Rosenberg.

Ariel Pink is a recurring presence across 27 releases (as Ariel Pink’s Haunted Graffiti and as Ariel Pink solo), spanning 2008–2021. His production credits (11) and writing credits (7) reflect his dual role as artist-in-residence.

Cate Le Bon (29 releases, 2019–2026) became the label’s dominant 2020s act; her self-production practice and role as producer for others (Samur Khouja as engineering partner) parallel Mering’s earlier trajectory.

The Marfa Myths festival — an annual event in Marfa, TX linking Mexican Summer artists with the West Texas art world — generated its own sublabel (Marfa Myths, ID 1443742) and a compilation (MEX 211, 2015) and became one of the label’s primary live/cultural touchpoints.

Al Carlson at Gary’s Electric (Brooklyn) is to Mexican Summer what Anders Peterson was to Acéphale — the dominant studio presence across the catalog with 69 engineer + 26 producer credits, also playing instruments (sax, piano, organ, baritone sax, flute) on many sessions.

Marfa Myths Festival

Annual multidisciplinary music festival held in Marfa, Texas, co-presented by Mexican Summer and Ballroom Marfa (a nonprofit contemporary arts foundation based in Marfa). Mexican Summer curates the event; Ballroom Marfa provides the institutional and local infrastructure.

First edition: March 8, 2014 — a single-day event at El Cosmico campground. Called “Mexican Summer Festival” in its first year (the “Marfa Myths” name came with the 2015 expansion). Ballroom Marfa invited five Mexican Summer roster acts to perform outdoors; lineup included Connan Mockasin, Weyes Blood, Quilt, ARP, and No Joy. Free admission. Poster design by Rob Carmichael (2). Promoted via Marfa Public Radio (KRTS 93.5 FM). [source: https://www.ballroommarfa.org/program/mexican-summer-festival/]

2015 expansion: March 13–15, 2015. Expanded to three days and rebranded as Marfa Myths. Added multidisciplinary programming: sound installation, film screening, and recording residency. Lineup included Iceage, Grouper, Tamaryn, Steve Gunn, Weyes Blood, Bitchin Bajas, and others. Recording residency yielded Myths 001 (Dev Hynes + Connan Mockasin EP, written and recorded in Marfa). Visual art: Liz Harris (Grouper) created a semi-permanent outdoor mural; Gregg Kowalsky presented “Tape Chants” live installation; sound bath curated by Jefre Cantu-Ledesma. Film in partnership with CineMarfa and Anthology Recordings. Sam Falls concurrent exhibition. [source: https://www.ballroommarfa.org/program/marfa-myths-2015/]

Curatorial structure: Festival curated by Mexican Summer, co-presented with Ballroom Marfa. Described as featuring “emerging and established artists and musicians to work creatively and collaboratively across music, film, and visual arts contexts” — including artists from within and outside the Mexican Summer / Software Recording Co. rosters. Capped at approximately 800 attendees; deliberately small-scale and non-commercial in contrast to mainstream festival formats. No identified individual named curators in public programming — curatorial credit is institutional (Mexican Summer as label). [source: https://www.ballroommarfa.org/program/marfa-myths-2019/]
