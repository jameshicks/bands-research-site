Stones Throw Records

Founded: 1996
Location: Los Angeles, California (originally San Mateo, CA)
Founder: Peanut Butter Wolf (Chris Manak)
Genre focus: Hip-hop, beat music, funk/soul, electronic; expanded over time into neo-soul, indie pop, experimental
Catalog prefix: STH (main series), STH7 (7” series), STH4 (12” promo series), STWL (web/promo)

Overview

Stones Throw is one of the most influential independent labels in post-1990 American music. Founded by DJ/producer Peanut Butter Wolf as an outlet for underground West Coast hip-hop, it evolved across three decades into a broad-spectrum independent with a reputation for curating idiosyncratic artists across hip-hop, funk revival, soul, electronic, and contemporary pop. The label’s identity has always been inseparable from its founder’s collector and DJ sensibility, reflected in extensive archival and reissue work alongside original releases.

Jeff Jank (Discogs ID: 346738) served as in-house art director and designer across virtually the entire catalog — 309 releases on Discogs — giving the label’s output a consistent visual identity. Label manager / A&R coordinator Eothen Alapatt (aka Egon, Discogs ID: 104275) served as general manager 2000–2011 and handled project coordination across 135+ releases. Mastering engineer Dave Cooley (Discogs ID: 219192) mastered 239 releases; Kelly Hibbert (Discogs ID: 496007) handled another 149.

Label History by Era

1996–1999 — West Coast Underground Hip-Hop

The label launched out of San Mateo with a tight circle of Bay Area and L.A. underground acts. The first release (STH 2001) was Charizma & Peanut Butter Wolf’s My World Premiere, honoring the deceased rapper Charizma, PBW’s early collaborator who was killed in 1993. Early roster: Rasco, Lootpack (featuring Madlib), Homeliss Derilex, Fanatik, Rob Swift, A-Trak.

  • STH 2001 (1996): My World Premiere — Charizma & Peanut Butter Wolf
  • STH 2006 (1997): The Unassisted — Rasco
  • STH 2017 (1998): My Vinyl Weighs A Ton Instrumentals — Peanut Butter Wolf
  • STH 2019 (1999): Soundpieces: Da Antidote! — Lootpack (Madlib’s main-act debut on the label)

2000–2003 — The Quasimoto / Madlib Expansion & Madvillain

Madlib’s alias Madlib / Quasimoto became the center of gravity. The Unseen (STH 2025, 2000) was the first Quasimoto album. PBW’s debut LP My Vinyl Weighs A Ton (STH 2017) appeared in 2001 as a full album. Yesterdays New Quintet (another Madlib alias) debuted in 2001. The Madlib/J Dilla collaboration Jaylib released Champion Sound (STH 2062, 2003).

The label also began a sustained archival/reissue thread: rare soul and funk compilations (e.g., The Funky 16 Corners, STH 2038, 2001).

2004 — Madvillainy & the Commercial-Critical Peak

Madvillainy (STH 2065, 2004) — Madvillain (Madlib + MF DOOM) — became the label’s signature release and one of the most acclaimed hip-hop albums of the 2000s. Produced entirely by Madlib, MCed by MF DOOM. Executive-produced by PBW; designed by Jeff Jank; mixed and mastered by Dave Cooley.

The same year: J Dilla’s Donuts was in production. Oh No debuted with The Disrupt (STH 2097). Aloe Blacc made his debut.

2005–2006 — J Dilla’s Donuts & the Decade Survey

Donuts (STH 2126, 2006) was recorded by J Dilla largely in hospital as he was dying of a rare blood disease; released on his 32nd birthday, February 7, 2006 — he died three days later. Entirely self-produced instrumental hip-hop, 31 tracks. It became a foundational text in beat music.

Chrome Children (STH 2150, 2006) was PBW’s ten-year anniversary compilation. Stones Throw Ten Years (STH 2142, 2006) documented the label’s first decade. The Third Unheard: Connecticut Hip Hop 1979–1983 (STH 2083, 2004) exemplified the label’s archival work.

2007–2010 — Funk Revival, Guilty Simpson, Mayer Hawthorne

Guilty Simpson emerged as a key MC presence. Dam-Funk’s debut 5-LP Toeachizown (STH 2232, 2009) signaled the label’s embrace of cosmic funk/boogie. Mayer Hawthorne debuted with A Strange Arrangement (STH 2219, 2009), introducing the label to neo-soul/blue-eyed soul and mainstream crossover potential — Hawthorne wrote, produced, and played virtually all instruments himself.

Aloe Blacc’s Good Things (STH 2245, 2010) and “I Need A Dollar” broke the label into mainstream pop/soul.

2011–2014 — Diversification

The label broadened significantly: post-punk (Anika, STH 2261), funk-soul (The Stepkids, Steve Arrington), Jonwayne’s rap, Homeboy Sandman, Strong Arm Steady. The Minimal Wave Tapes series (2010–12) showed a new synth-pop/minimal-wave curatorial interest. The documentary Our Vinyl Weighs A Ton (2014) served as a label retrospective.

Tuxedo (Mayer Hawthorne + Jake One) debuted on the label in 2015.

2015–2020 — NxWorries, Sudan Archives, Broadening Palette

NxWorries (Anderson .Paak + Knxwledge), Yes Lawd! (STH 2370, 2016) — a major moment in neo-soul/hip-hop crossover. Sudan Archives (Brittney Parks) debuted with an EP (STH 2390, 2017) and grew into a major artist through Athena (STH 2412, 2019) and Natural Brown Prom Queen (STH 2449, 2022). Washed Out signed for Mister Mellow (STH 2387, 2017), an unusual move into chillwave territory.

2020–present — Global Indie Expansion

Continued to build the catalog with John Carroll Kirby, Benny Sings, Kiefer, Koreatown Oddity, Pearl and the Oysters, Haruomi Hosono reissue (STH 2515, 2025), and Eddie Chacon. NxWorries returned with Why Lawd? (STH 2500, 2024).

Connections

  • Peanut Butter Wolf — founder and creative director; his DJ/collector identity shaped the label’s curatorial voice
  • Jeff Jank — art director across virtually the entire catalog; Jank’s cartoonish illustration style became the visual signature of the label
  • Madlib — most prolific artist on the label; also operated as Quasimoto, Yesterdays New Quintet, Beat Konducta, Sound Directions, and many other aliases
  • MF DOOM — via Madvillain; Madvillainy is the label’s signature release
  • J Dilla — Donuts (2006) was recorded on his deathbed; became one of the most influential instrumental hip-hop albums
  • NxWorries — Anderson .Paak’s breakthrough vehicle on the label
  • Sudan Archives — grew from debut EP (2017) to one of the label’s most prominent current acts
  • Mayer Hawthorne — brought crossover soul credibility; wrote/produced/played nearly everything himself
  • Dam-Funk — cosmic funk revivalist; Toeachizown (2009) a landmark of the style
  • Washed Out — unusual signing for Mister Mellow (2017); reflects PBW’s willingness to sign outside the label’s typical wheelhouse
  • Aloe Blacc — “I Need A Dollar” (2010) was the label’s largest mainstream crossover hit
