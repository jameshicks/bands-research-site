Manhattan Records

EMI/Capitol Records imprint, active 1984–1987 (then merged into EMI Manhattan Records). Founded in September 1984 by Bruce Lundvall as a division of Capitol Records, Inc. and Capitol Industries–EMI Inc. Used as the US manufacturing and distribution vehicle for the reactivated Blue Note Records catalog from 1985 through approximately 1987, before Blue Note continued under its own catalog series under EMI’s Capitol umbrella. Discogs label entry: Manhattan Records.

Corporate Structure

Manhattan Records was established as a US pop/adult contemporary imprint for EMI, operating as a division of Capitol Records, Inc. and Capitol Industries–EMI Inc. Lundvall had previously served as president of Columbia Records and Elektra Records; EMI approached him via Bhaskar Menon at Capitol to create Manhattan (a pop label, East Coast-based) and simultaneously to revive the dormant Blue Note jazz label. He partnered with producer Michael Cuscuna on the Blue Note side. Wikipedia, Manhattan Records (as of 2026-06-29); Ted Panken / Lundvall interview, 2009

Under EMI’s US structure, Lundvall headed three labels simultaneously: Blue Note (jazz), Angel Records (classical), and Manhattan Records (adult pop/rock). JazzTimes obituary

In June 1987, Manhattan Records merged with EMI America Records to form EMI Manhattan Records. That entity rebranded as EMI USA in 1989, then EMI Records USA in 1991, and was closed in 1997. EMI America Records, Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-29)

Distribution and Pressing

Manhattan Records releases were distributed via CEMA (Capitol-EMI Music Distribution), EMI’s North American distribution arm serving Capitol, EMI America, Manhattan, and Angel Records. Physical vinyl manufacturing for US Manhattan-era Blue Note releases used Direct Metal Mastering (DMM) technology throughout.

Label text on US Manhattan-era Blue Note releases reads: ”© [year] Manhattan Records, a division of Capitol Records, Inc.” French pressings (by EMI France / Pathé Marconi) carry: “MFD FOR MANHATTAN RECORDS, A DIVISION OF CAPITOL RECORDS, INC. AND CAPITOL INDUSTRIES – EMI INC. BY EMI FRANCE.” The French pressings also used DMM but are generally regarded as inferior-sounding to the US pressings. Collecting Blue Note blog, label guide

Conflict / open question: The existing entry states Blue Note releases in the 1985–1987 window were pressed at Wakefield Manufacturing in Phoenix, AZ. The web sources consulted do not confirm or deny this — they identify label text and DMM technique but not a specific pressing plant for the US Manhattan-era releases. The Wakefield attribution may come from physical pressing ring analysis (see existing entry note re: ring size and 5-digit processing number) and should be treated as unverified from web sources.

Blue Note 85100 Series (Manhattan Period, 1985–1987)

EMI phased out Blue Note in 1979 when it acquired United Artists Records (which had absorbed Liberty Records in 1968, which had absorbed Blue Note in 1966). The label lay dormant until 1985. The high-profile relaunch event was a Town Hall concert in New York in February 1985, reuniting classic Blue Note artists (Art Blakey, McCoy Tyner, etc.) with newly signed performers.

During the Manhattan period, Blue Note releases carried Blue Note Records label branding on the physical disc — not “Manhattan Records” branding. The series is cataloged as the “Manhattan 85100 series” to distinguish its origin within the corporate structure. Catalog numbers use BT-85xxx (12-inch LP) and BABB/BTDK prefixes for box sets.

The 85100 series ran from 1985 to approximately 1987 and comprised both new studio recordings and a smaller number of reissues/archival releases:

When Blue Note Separated from Manhattan Records

Manhattan Records merged with EMI America in June 1987 to form EMI Manhattan Records. Blue Note was not part of that merger entity — it continued as its own label under the Capitol/EMI umbrella. By 1988–1989, Blue Note releases were issued under Blue Note Records with a new catalog numbering system (B1-XXXXX for vinyl, CDP 7 XXXXX 2 for CD), clearly distinct from the Manhattan-era BT-85xxx series. Jazz Discography Project — Blue Note 1989–1990 discography

The practical reconstitution of Blue Note as its own imprint thus occurred around 1987–1988, coinciding with the Manhattan/EMI America merger that removed the Manhattan vehicle from the equation. Blue Note carried on under Capitol’s umbrella without the Manhattan intermediary. The trigger was the corporate consolidation of Manhattan with EMI America rather than any deliberate creative decision to re-separate Blue Note.

In 2001, the Manhattan label was relaunched without the EMI prefix as a division of EMI Classics by producer Arif Mardin, and in 2006 was placed under the Blue Note Label Group’s management during EMI’s reorganization. Wikipedia, Manhattan Records (as of 2026-06-29)
