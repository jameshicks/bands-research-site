Overview

Trade 2 operated as a sublabel of Island Records during the 1990s, co-run by Geoff Travis, the founder of Rough Trade, lending it an independent sensibility within a major-label structure. Its catalogue of around thirty releases spans an eclectic mix of indie rock, Britpop, drum n bass, and experimental music, all contained within that single decade. The label maintained a companion imprint, Trade 2 Singles Club, and its roster centred on acts including Spring Heel Jack and Tiger (6), with John Coxon and Yuki Miyake among the credited artists.

Notes (web)

Origins

After founding Rough Trade in 1976, Geoff Travis sold the label to One Little Indian Records around 1993–94. The relationship did not last: “Their culture and the way they work just didn’t fit in with our ideals and the way we work” (Travis, interview, November 1996). By the summer of 1996 he had left One Little Indian entirely.

His response was to set up Trade 2 — a label owned by Island Records — as a continuation of the model he had used before with Blanco y Negro Records (co-run with WEA, 1983) and Blue Guitar (co-run with Chrysalis). In a November 1996 interview he described the arrangement plainly: “Our response to that is to start a new label called Trade 2 with Island Records so again, we’re part of a major corporation.” furious.com interview, November 1996

The aim was financial stability combined with creative autonomy: “We’re pretty happy at the moment. We have a pretty stable financial structure. We have freedom to do what we want to do.” The label was explicitly conceived as a vehicle for innovative and commercial-in-their-own-time acts, not purely experimental releases — Travis acknowledged there remained a gap he wanted to close: “We’re not really in a position able to do one-off’s and experimental things that we’d like to do.” furious.com

The model — an independent-minded figure given a funded imprint within a major — was consistent with what Travis had done throughout the 1980s. Trade 2 was not a formal joint venture between Rough Trade and Island; “the two companies have no relationship” (furious.com, 1996). It was Travis personally operating under Island’s umbrella.

What ended the arrangement

No contemporaneous source states explicitly why Trade 2 stopped releasing records. The circumstantial picture is coherent, however:

  • Chris Blackwell, who had championed indie-flavored Island imprints, was forced out by PolyGram on 31 October 1997. worldmusicviews.com
  • After Blackwell’s departure, several Island indie imprints were wound down — 4th & B’way, for example, was shut in 1998. Wikipedia — 4th & B’way Records
  • Trade 2’s last Discogs-catalogued release dates from 1998 (Super 400, cat. TRDCD1005).
  • Geoff Travis founded Tugboat Records — an independent label affiliated with Rough Trade, based in London — in May 1998, suggesting he had already moved on from the Island structure by that point. Wikipedia — Tugboat Records
  • PolyGram itself was acquired by Seagram and folded into Universal Music Group in December 1998. CNN Money, 10 Dec 1998

The most probable sequence: Blackwell’s forced exit in late 1997 removed the Island executive most sympathetic to indie-led imprints; this destabilized Trade 2’s internal sponsorship; Travis wound down releases through early–mid 1998 and re-established himself independently with Tugboat in May 1998, ahead of PolyGram’s dissolution into Universal. No source provides a termination agreement, a specific closing date, or a quote from Travis or Island confirming the reason.
