Overview

Atavistic Records is a Chicago-based independent label founded in 1985 by Kurt Kellison in Columbus, Ohio, initially as a video imprint producing live VHS recordings by bands such as Live Skull and The Flaming Lips. Kellison relocated the label to Chicago in 1988, and through the 1990s–2000s Atavistic became one of the most consequential US archival and experimental labels, operating in several distinct lanes simultaneously:

  1. No Wave and noise rock reissues — landmark archival compilations and reissues of Teenage Jesus And The Jerks, Mars, Swans, The Static (Theoretical Girls side project), 8 Eyed Spy, and Live Skull.
  2. Glenn Branca catalog — a sustained relationship with Glenn Branca producing and reissuing the majority of his symphonic guitar-orchestra catalog (Symphonies Nos. 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 10, 13, and more) beginning in 1992.
  3. Contemporary Chicago experimental/post-rock — Vandermark 5 (23 releases, 1997–2008), Boxhead Ensemble (9 releases, 1997–2006), Chris Brokaw, Jarboe, God Is My Co-Pilot, and Bobby Conn.
  4. Free jazz reissues via the Unheard Music Series — extensive reissue of European and American free jazz (FMP catalog via Archive FMP Edition sublabel, Peter Brötzmann, The Sun Ra Arkestra, Joe McPhee, Han Bennink, and many others).

According to Wikipedia (citing an AllAboutJazz interview), John Corbett served as the primary producer across much of the catalog — Discogs credits him on 68 Atavistic releases in producer and reissue-producer roles, by far the most of any individual on the label.

House designer Paula M. Froehle handled art direction and package design for approximately 65 releases, and ATAWorld handled layout on a further 39. The recurring mastering engineers were John McCortney (40 releases), Mike Hagler (19), and Robert Weston (15). Wharton Tiers produced or mixed material for 12 releases.

Connections

  • Glenn Branca — anchor artist; Atavistic issued the bulk of his recorded catalog
  • Swans / Michael Gira — major back-catalog reissue relationship (15 releases); Gira also performed on Branca Symphony No. 3
  • Lydia Lunch — 18 releases spanning reissues and new recordings; strong No Wave lineage
  • Teenage Jesus And The Jerks — key No Wave reissue (Lunch-related)
  • Mars — 78+ (1996) reissue; members include Mark Cunningham and Nancy Arlen
  • Vandermark 5 — label’s primary contemporary jazz act (23 releases; Ken Vandermark, Jeb Bishop, Dave Rempis, Kent Kessler)
  • Boxhead Ensemble — Chicago post-rock chamber ensemble linked to Michael Krassner; featured David Grubbs, Ken Vandermark, Rick Rizzo of Eleventh Dream Day
  • Jarboe — 7 solo releases post-Swans; collaborated with Lary Seven
  • Peter Brötzmann — European free jazz archival via UMS sublabel; also handled own artwork and design
  • Han Bennink — UMS releases (multiple configurations)
  • The Sun Ra Arkestra — 8 archival Sun Ra releases 2001–2008
  • Lee Ranaldo / Thurston Moore — Sonic Youth members appearing on Branca recordings
  • 8 Eyed Spy — 1997 CD reissue
  • God Is My Co-Pilot — two 1990s releases
  • Chris Brokaw — Red Cities (2001), I Was Born, But… (2004)
  • Wharton Tiers — house producer and mixer (12 releases); Swans-associated engineer
  • John Corbett — primary producer across catalog; 68 credited appearances
  • Young God Records — Michael Gira’s own label; Atavistic and Young God share Swans catalog overlap
  • David Grubbs — Boxhead Ensemble featured artist; ex-Squirrel Biscuit, Gastr del Sol
  • The Scissor Girls — Chicago noise act, 3 releases
  • Chrome Cranks — 3 releases including archival compilation
  • Wrekmeister Harmonies — early release (2009) before Thrill Jockey
