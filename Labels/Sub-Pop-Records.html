Sub Pop Records

Independent record label. Seattle, Washington. Founded 1986/1988. Discogs IDs: Parent company 5131 (Sub Pop Records); operating label 77343 (Sub Pop). 8,600+ releases.

The definitive label of the Seattle grunge scene — and, after surviving near-bankruptcy in 1991, one of the most consistently significant American independent labels of the 1990s–2020s. Co-founded by Bruce Pavitt and Jonathan Poneman in 1988 as a formal label (Pavitt’s Sub Pop fanzine and cassette zine preceded it from 1980). The label grew out of deliberate mythomaking around Seattle: colored vinyl limited editions, hyperbolic press releases, and aggressive UK press cultivation turned local punk and metal-adjacent noise into a global phenomenon.

Founding and Origins

Bruce Pavitt launched Sub Pop as a print fanzine in Olympia, Washington in 1980 — “Sub Pop” being short for Subterranean Pop. It evolved into a cassette compilation series, then a music column in the Seattle Rocket newspaper. The first proper Sub Pop record — Sub Pop 100 (SP10, 1986) — was a compilation LP featuring Wipers, U-Men, Sonic Youth, and others, assembled while Pavitt was based in Olympia.

Jonathan Poneman joined as co-founder and the label relocated to Seattle. The label’s defining phase began in 1987 with the first two proper catalog entries: Green River Dry As A Bone (SP11) and Soundgarden Screaming Life EP (SP12) — both produced by Jack Endino at Reciprocal Recording.

The Sub Pop 200 three-LP compilation (SP25, 1988, limited 5,000 copies) was the scene-defining document: Mudhoney, Soundgarden, Tad, Nirvana, Swallow, Green River, Blood Circus, Skin Yard, and more, with liner notes by Everett True. It planted the seed that exploded into grunge.

Era 1 — Grunge Explodes (1987–1991)

The first four catalog years established Sub Pop as the center of a scene. Jack Endino at Reciprocal Recording (Seattle) produced nearly everything. All key releases were issued as limited colored vinyl 7”s with sleeve photography by Charles Peterson.

Landmark singles:

  • SP12: Soundgarden Screaming Life EP (1987) — Endino-produced; Cornell, Thayil, Yamamoto, Cameron; cover concept by Bruce Pavitt
  • SP11: Green River Dry As A Bone EP (1987) — Mark Arm and Steve Turner before Mudhoney; Jeff Ament and Stone Gossard before Pearl Jam
  • SP17: Soundgarden Fopp EP (1988)
  • SP18: Mudhoney “Touch Me I’m Sick / Sweet Young Thing Ain’t Sweet No More” (1988) — the breakout single; Endino recorded; Mark Arm, Steve Turner, Matt Lukin, Dan Peters; B-side split with Sonic Youth
  • SP19: Tad “Daisy / Ritual Device” (1988)
  • SP23: Nirvana “Love Buzz / Big Cheese” (1988) — first Nirvana single
  • SP25: Sub Pop 200 compilation (1988) — scene document; 5,000 copies

First LPs:

  • SP27/GRCD 51: Tad God’s Balls (1989) — Endino
  • SP34: Nirvana Bleach (1989/90) — Endino; Cobain, Novoselic; Chad Channing drums (most tracks), Dale Crover (tr.2, 6, 13); Jason Everman guitar; cover photo Tracy Marander; inner photos Charles Peterson; recorded for $606.17
  • SP44: Mudhoney Mudhoney (1989) — self-titled debut LP
  • SP60: The Afghan Whigs Up In It (1990) — Cincinnati band; their first Sub Pop album
  • SP61: Mark Lanegan The Winding Sheet (1990) — Screaming Trees vocalist solo debut; Kurt Cobain appeared on two tracks

Also active in this period: The Fluid, Blood Circus, Screaming Trees (singles), Love Battery, Swallow (3), Skin Yard, Girl Trouble, Das Damen, Les Thugs, Rapeman (Steve Albini).

The UK connection: Everett True of Melody Maker championed Sub Pop heavily from 1989 onward, writing the first major UK coverage of Mudhoney and Nirvana. The label sent Everett True and Andrew Lamber to Seattle in 1989 for what became the first UK press trip to the scene. This created the feedback loop that made “grunge” a global genre.

Era 2 — Near Bankruptcy and Survival (1991–1995)

Nirvana’s Nevermind (September 1991, DGC) was the industry earthquake — but Sub Pop had already sold off a percentage of the band’s contract to DGC to raise operating funds. The label received a buyout but missed the full upside. By 1991–1992 the label was in serious financial trouble, unable to pay pressing plants or staff.

The Warner Bros. deal: in 1995 Warner Bros. purchased a 49% stake in Sub Pop, providing the capital to stabilize operations. Bruce Pavitt departed around this time; Poneman continued as sole leader.

Despite the chaos, the early 1990s catalog was strong:

  • SP62/98: Beat Happening Jamboree (1991) and Dreamy (1991) — Calvin Johnson’s K Records band on Sub Pop
  • SP79: L7 Smell The Magic (1991) — all-female LA punk; Butch Vig produced
  • SP89: Tad 8-Way Santa (1991)
  • SP105: Mudhoney Every Good Boy Deserves Fudge (1991)
  • SP107: Codeine Frigid Stars LP (1991) — slowcore New York trio; Sub Pop’s outlier at the time
  • SPCD 121/302: Sunny Day Real Estate Diary (1994) — Seattle emo; Jeremy Enigk, Nate Mendel, William Goldsmith, Daniel Hoerner
  • SP246: Diary LP pressing (1994)
  • SP 302: Sunny Day Real Estate self-titled (LP2) (1994)

Sunny Day Real Estate Diary is one of the foundational emo records, recorded without an official producer credit — the “crew” credit for Matt Conlon (“everything else”) reflects DIY circumstances. Nate Mendel and William Goldsmith subsequently joined Foo Fighters.

Also: The Afghan Whigs released Congregation (1992) and Gentlemen (1993, Elektra) on Sub Pop before moving to a major; Black Love (1996) and subsequent records returned to Sub Pop.

Era 3 — Rebuilding and Diversification (1996–2001)

Post-Warner deal, Sub Pop widened its scope beyond Seattle rock while keeping a Pacific Northwest base. Key releases:

  • SP338: Modest Mouse Broke EP (1996) — first Sub Pop release for Modest Mouse (they’d been on Up Records)
  • SP361: Earth (2) Pentastar: In The Style Of Demons (1996) — doom/drone; Dylan Carlson
  • SP370: Sebadoh Harmacy (1996) — Lou Barlow’s band, previously on Homestead/Domino
  • SP365: Elevator To Hell Part Three (1996) — Eric’s Trip spinoff
  • SP323: Jeremy Enigk Return Of The Frog Queen (1996) — SDRE vocalist’s first solo record
  • SP565: Red House Painters Old Ramon (2001) — Mark Kozelek; Sub Pop debut after 4AD run
  • SP531: Love As Laughter Sea To Shining Sea (2001) — Sam Zurick

Also during this period: Six Finger Satellite, Damien Jurado (early singles), Eric’s Trip, Velocity Girl, Jale, Seaweed, Fastbacks, Reverend Horton Heat, Screaming Trees (late singles), Dwarves, the Supersuckers.

Modest Mouse note: The Brock/Sub Pop relationship spans from 1996’s Broke EP through the band’s full-length run on the label (This Is A Long Drive was on Up Records; The Lonesome Crowded West arrived on Up in 1997 before Sub Pop reissued it; The Moon & Antarctica on Epic). Sub Pop also signed Holopaw partly via Isaac Brock’s recommendation.

Era 4 — The 2000s Expansion (2002–2010)

The label’s second golden era, built on folk, indie pop, and lo-fi singer-songwriter records that proved Sub Pop’s range extended far beyond Seattle noise.

Pivotal 2000s signings and releases:

  • SPCD 550: The Shins Oh, Inverted World (2001) — Albuquerque indie pop; James Mercer; Phil Ek produced; became famous via Garden State soundtrack (2004)
  • SPCD 600: Iron And Wine The Creek Drank The Cradle (2002) — Sam Beam home recording; lo-fi folk; Sub Pop’s first major folk signing
  • SPCD 630: Iron And Wine Our Endless Numbered Days (2004)
  • SPCD 655: Wolf Parade Apologies To The Queen Mary (2005) — produced by Isaac Brock (tracks 1–3, 6, 8–12) and self-produced (4, 5, 7); Spencer Krug + Dan Boeckner; mixed by Jace Lasek
  • SPCD 720: Wolf Parade At Mount Zoomer (2008)
  • SP 777: Fleet Foxes Fleet Foxes (2008) — Seattle folk-rock; Robin Pecknold; Phil Ek produced; cover painting by Pieter Bruegel the Elder; Ed Brooks mastered
  • SPCD 781: Fleet Foxes Sun Giant EP (2008)
  • SP 845: Beach House Teen Dream (2010) — Baltimore dream pop; Victoria Legrand + Alex Scally; their Sub Pop debut after Carpark Records
  • SP 888: Fleet Foxes Helplessness Blues (2011)

Also: Hot Hot Heat Make Up The Breakdown (2002), Beachwood Sparks Once We Were Trees (2001), Damien Jurado I Break Chairs (2001/02), Mark Lanegan Field Songs (2001), No Age, Dum Dum Girls.

Connections

  • Bruce Pavitt — co-founder; Sub Pop fanzine origin; departed ~1995
  • Jonathan Poneman — co-founder; continues
  • Jack Endino — house producer 1987–1993; Reciprocal Recording; produced nearly all key grunge-era records
  • Phil Ek — key 2000s producer; produced The Shins, Fleet Foxes, Built To Spill
  • Isaac Brock — produced Wolf Parade Apologies; label connection through Modest Mouse and Holopaw
  • Reciprocal Recording — Jack Endino’s Seattle studio; functional “house studio” 1987–1991
  • Green River — proto-grunge; first Sub Pop band of note; spawned Pearl Jam and Mudhoney
  • Mudhoney — the signature Sub Pop band; still on roster
  • Nirvana — biggest commercial success; left for DGC but Sub Pop retained back catalog
  • Soundgarden — first Sub Pop band (SP12); left for A&M
  • Holopaw — signed via Isaac Brock connection (per Sub Pop’s own description)
  • Hardly Art — sublabel
