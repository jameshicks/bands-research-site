Third Man Records

Independent label. Nashville, Tennessee (HQ); Detroit, Michigan; London, UK. Discogs ID: 89238 Parent: Third Man Records, LLC (Discogs ID: 264288) Founded: 2001 (Detroit); physical Nashville store opened March 2010

History

Third Man Records was founded in 2001 in Detroit, Michigan by Jack White, Ben Swank, and Ben Blackwell. The label’s early years were largely conceptual — it functioned as a home imprint for White’s projects before it had a physical space, first appearing as a co-credit alongside XL Recordings on The White Stripes’ Elephant (2003, XLCD 162).

In March 2010, Third Man opened its flagship building at 623 7th Avenue South, Nashville, Tennessee — a multi-purpose facility housing label offices, a vinyl record store, a photo studio (the Blue Room), a darkroom, and a small performance stage (the Blue Room stage). This Nashville complex became the label’s operational headquarters and the physical home of its live recording series and Blue Series drop-in sessions.

A Detroit retail and manufacturing location, Third Man Records Cass Corridor, opened in November 2015 at 441 West Canfield Street, Detroit. In February 2017, Third Man opened an in-house vinyl pressing plant, Third Man Pressing (also at 441 West Canfield), which now manufactures the majority of the label’s releases. A separate mastering studio, Third Man Mastering, launched in January 2019, also based at the Detroit Cass Corridor address, with in-house engineers Warren Defever and a second staff engineer.

A London retail location was also established; details in the Discogs DB are sparse but it is listed in the label profile as a third location.

The White Stripes (active through 2011 disbandment), The Raconteurs, and The Dead Weather are the three bands most associated with the label’s founding identity. Since The White Stripes’ breakup, Third Man has been the sole label for all their posthumous and archival releases. Jack White’s solo catalog — Blunderbuss (2012), Lazaretto (2014), Boarding House Reach (2018), Fear Of The Dawn (2022), Entering Heaven Alive (2022) — is also entirely Third Man.

Blue Series notable entries

The Blue Series (Discogs ID 356625) is a continuous run of drop-in session 7”s produced by Jack White. Known examples include:

  • Gap Dream — 58th Street Fingers (TMR-253, 2013)
  • Courtney Barnett — Boxing Day Blues (Revisited) (TMR-321, 2015)
  • Mdou Moctar (TMR release, 2019)
  • Many early entries feature artists passing through Nashville who recorded 1–2 songs directly to tape at Third Man Studio

The Vault Subscription Series

Third Man Records Vault (Discogs ID: 307889) is a quarterly mail-subscription service launched by Third Man. Subscribers at the Platinum tier receive a package each quarter containing:

  • One Vault LP (exclusive pressing or special edition)
  • One Vault 7” single
  • A novelty item (which has ranged from T-shirts and posters to concert DVDs and additional 7”s)

Each quarterly bundle is cataloged as “Package 1,” “Package 2,” etc. Some packages include split-color or colored vinyl pressings available only to attendees of live Blue Room recordings, with standard black vinyl available separately.

Scene Context

Third Man sits at the intersection of several overlapping scenes:

Detroit garage rock lineage. The label’s founders and earliest roster — The White Stripes, Whirlwind Heat, The Gories, The Greenhornes, Dex Romweber Duo — draw directly from the Detroit/Midwest garage rock scene of the late 1990s–2000s. Warren Defever (His Name Is Alive) and Bill Skibbe provide the Detroit studio link.

Nashville roots and country crossover. The Nashville headquarters brought in Wanda Jackson, Loretta Lynn, Pokey LaFarge, Margo Price, Lillie Mae Rische — establishing a second identity around honky-tonk, country, and Americana rooted recording.

Vinyl fetishism and direct-to-disc recording. Third Man has been one of the most vocal advocates for vinyl-only releases, direct-to-acetate processes, and strictly analog recording chains. The Third Man Live series (all-analog, direct-to-disc after 2012) and the Voice-o-Graph recording booth (used on Neil Young’s A Letter Home, 2013) are high-profile demonstrations of this philosophy.

Heavy music and experimental signing strategy. From 2017 onward, TMR expanded its roster to include Boris (3), Sleep, Melvins, Wolf Eyes, Laughing Hyenas, Earth (2), and Dinosaur Jr., adding a significant heavy/drone/psych wing to the catalog. This reflects the label’s curatorial range beyond the garage-rock founding identity.

Early 2020s expansion. Recent signings — Hotline TNT, Snooper (3), Sheer Mag, Gina Birch, Natalie Bergman — suggest continued willingness to work with younger indie/punk acts and elder-punk figures.
