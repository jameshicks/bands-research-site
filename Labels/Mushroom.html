Label formed by Michael Gudinski and Ray Evans (2) in Melbourne, Australia in 1972. Label Code: LC 4917 Note: For the publisher, see Mushroom (8), for (unofficial) releases, use Mushroom (6).

Its earliest signings included Skyhooks and Split Enz and by the mid Seventies it was Australia’s leading independent label, in partnership with Festival Records Pty. Ltd. The company entry associated most to this label is Mushroom Records Pty. Ltd.

Mushroom went from strength to strength in the 1980s as Split Enz had substantial international success and they signed Kylie Minogue. Subsidiary labels like Liberation Records & White Label Records were also set up, as well as branches in London and Auckland.

In 1993 half the company was sold to Rupert Murdoch’s Newscorp and the remainder in 1998. Murdoch merged it with his Festival Records Pty. Ltd. and Mushroom Distribution Services (MDS) to form the company Festival Mushroom Records Pty. Ltd. in 1999.

When FMR was sold to Warner Music in 2005, they revived the Mushroom Records name as an imprint.

Overview

Founded in Melbourne in 1972 by Michael Gudinski and Ray Evans, Mushroom built itself into Australia’s leading independent label through the mid-1970s on the strength of early signings Skyhooks and Split Enz, distributed nationally via Festival Records. The 1980s brought international expansion — branches in London and Auckland, subsidiary imprints including Liberation Records and White Label Records — as well as the signing of Kylie Minogue, whose success gave the label genuine global reach. Rupert Murdoch’s Newscorp acquired a 50% stake in 1993 and the remainder in 1998, after which Mushroom was merged with Festival Records and Mushroom Distribution Services to form Festival Mushroom Records Pty. Ltd. in 1999. When Warner Music purchased FMR in 2005, the Mushroom name was revived as an imprint, carrying a catalogue that spans Pop Rock, Alternative Rock, Synth-pop, Hard Rock and House.
