100% Silk

Discogs ID: 232335
Parent: Not Not Fun Records
Founded: 2011
Origin: Los Angeles, California
Run by: Amanda Brown (2)
Sublabel: House of Silk
Total releases: ~279 (Discogs)

Dance and electronic sublabel of Not Not Fun Records, owned and run by Amanda Brown (2) — NNF co-founder and recording artist. Where NNF covers the full spectrum of American underground experimental music, 100% Silk is specifically oriented toward house, italo-influenced electronics, lo-fi club music, and related dance forms. Launched 2011; catalog runs to 160+ entries by 2025. Has been a significant space for queer and trans artists in underground dance music throughout its run.

Founding and the 2011 Class

The label’s first release was SILK001: Ital’s Theme by Ital (3) (Daniel Martin-McCormick, 2011) — a digital/cassette single that established the label’s minimal, lo-fi house aesthetic before any physical infrastructure existed.

The 2011 founding class defined the label’s character immediately:

  • Ital (3) (Daniel Martin-McCormick) — SILK001, Only For Tonight (SILK010); aliases include Relaxer (2) and Sex Worker
  • Octo Octa (Maya Bouldry-Morrison) — Let Me See You (SILK011), Rough, Rugged, And Raw (SILK025); the label’s most prolific founding artist, 14 releases 2011–2014
  • Magic Touch (6) (Damon Palermo) — I Can Feel The Heat (SILK009); 9 releases 2011–2014
  • Maria Minerva (Maria Juur) — Sacred & Profane Love (SILK013); Estonian artist drawn into the NNF/Silk orbit
  • The Deeep — Muddy Tracks (SILK002, 70 pressings catalogued); mixed/mastered by Brandon Hocura
  • Gillette (2) — SILK005
  • Sir Stephen — SILK (2011–2012, 3 releases)
  • Innergaze — 2011
  • Malvoeaux — 2011

Amanda Brown’s own project LA Vampires — formed with M. Geddes Gengras and Nicholas Malkin — appeared on 100% Silk from 2012 onward (Freedom 2K with Octo Octa, SILK031; 10 Outta 10 with Cologne (4), 2019), having previously released on the parent label NNF.

Peak Years: 2012–2014

The 2012–2014 period saw the label expand from its minimal-house foundations into a broader electronic palette:

  • Strategy (3) — 6 releases 2012–2014; Boxy Music (SILK021), Boxology (SILK066)
  • Fort Romeau — Kingdoms (SILK022, 2012)
  • Polysick — Flow FM (SILK026, 2012), Under Construction (SILK049, 2013); 5 releases
  • Coyote Clean Up — Frozen Solid (SILK027, 2012), 2 Hot 2 Wait (SILK044, 2013)
  • Jonas Reinhardt — Foam Fangs (SILK029, 2012)
  • Jupiter Jax (Rudi Agius) — City Life ‘88 (SILK048, 2013); 11 releases 2013–2024, the most of any label artist over the long term
  • Pharaohs (2) — Replicant Moods (SILK045, 2013)
  • Golden Donna — II (SILK064, 2014)
  • Luca Lozano — Isolation Distorts (SILK070, 2014)

SILK031 (Freedom 2K, LA Vampires / Octo Octa, 2012) is a landmark release: produced by Octo Octa, recorded by Brian Foote — the collaboration between the label’s owner and its foundational artist captures the 100% Silk sensibility at its clearest.

A 2013 mixtape — 100% Silk Presents Donors Club Elite Mixtape — served as a label sampler and document of the scene.

Cherushii and the Mid-Period (2013–2016)

Cherushii (Chelsea Faith Dolan, 1983–2016) became one of the label’s most distinctive voices from 2013 onward: Far Away So Close (SILK081, 2015), Memory Of Water (SILK085, 2015), and a split with Maria Minerva (SILK102, recorded pre-2016, released 2019). Her work combined classic house structure with an emotional directness that set it apart from the label’s more minimal output.

Chelsea Faith Dolan died in the Ghost Ship warehouse fire in Oakland on December 2, 2016 — one of 36 people killed. Her posthumous releases, including Cherushii & Maria Minerva (SILK102, 2019; design by Britt Brown, additional production by Brian Foote and David Last), have been issued carefully. The 2019 release was assembled from material recorded before her death. See the dedicated section below for full assembly details.

SILK102: Cherushii & Maria Minerva — Posthumous Assembly

Release date: February 15, 2019 (100% Silk, SILK102). 12-inch vinyl, six tracks plus one closing edit.

Origin of the collaboration: Minerva and Dolan bonded during an eight-show US DIY tour in 2013, covering over 3,000 miles together. In the period leading up to the Ghost Ship fire they began work on a proper collaborative album, and by December 2016 had written, recorded, and mixed “the better part of six songs” together. [source: Factmag, 2019-01-15; Resident Advisor, 2019; Cherushii Bandcamp]

Who assembled it: No single initiator is named in published sources. Maria Minerva is described as having spent “two years of working to finish the project amid a difficult grieving process” after Dolan’s death. [source: KQED, 2019] Three collaborators assisted with post-production on the incomplete tracks:

  • David Last — Cherushii’s romantic and creative partner; they had built Last Faith Studio together just two weeks before the Ghost Ship fire. Last is credited with additional production and mixing. [source: RA, 2018; KQED, 2019]
  • Brian Foote — recurring 100% Silk engineer (also recorded Freedom 2K, 2012); credited with additional production and mixing.
  • Adam Gunther — credited with vocal recording.

Full production credits per the Bandcamp release page [source: Bandcamp]:

  • Additional production: David Last, Brian Foote
  • Vocal recording: Adam Gunther
  • Mixing: David Last, Brian Foote
  • Mastering: Eric Hanson
  • Design: Britt Brown
  • Special thanks: Colleen Dolan, Sabrina Fox

Closing track: “A Day Without You [Leech edit]” — a stripped ambient rework by Los Angeles producer Leech, featuring Maria Minerva’s vocals.

Label statement (100% Silk): “the sound of true union, Faith’s divine dance floor DNA threaded through Minerva’s chic pop hooks, rhythmic and revelatory, the best of both beautiful worlds.” [source: RA]

Britt Brown (100% Silk co-owner) on Cherushii: “She wasn’t one of those people who’d write a song because she’s sad and it’s a sad song. … It was this revved up, joyful party song.” [source: KQED, 2019]

Maria Minerva on Cherushii before her death: “Right before her passing, things were going so great for her. She was doing bigger and better gigs every week, producing a lot—she never stopped.” [source: KQED, 2019]

The 2015 compilation Sunset Silk (SILK080) documented the label’s mid-period breadth.

Catalog Milestone and Late Period (2017–present)

SILK100 — Sensate Silk (2017), a compilation — marked the label’s 100th catalog entry and served as a state-of-the-union document.

Akasha System has become the dominant presence in the later catalog: 13 releases from 2016 to 2025, more than any other artist. Echo Earth (SILK117, 2019), Ancient Path Complete (SILK151, 2023), Phytopia (SILK150, 2023), Heliocene (2025), Sun Archive (SILK161, 2025).

Other ongoing contributors: Jupiter Jax (Rudi Agius) — 11 releases through 2024; Pleasure Model — 8 releases 2016–2023; Body-san — 8 releases 2015–2024; Foans — 7 releases 2016–2023.

Silk To Dry The Tears (SILK101, 2018) and 2nd Life Silk (SILK133, 2021) are label compilations from this era.

House of Silk is a sublabel (Discogs ID: 570522) with 5 releases (HOS001–HOS005, 2013–2022) — see House Of Silk for the full catalog. The imprint appears to have targeted more vocal and R&B-inflected deep house. Artists include EZLV / Romy Hoffman, Sir Stephen, Gemini Lion, Newbody, and Torrello. Britt Brown and Matthew Tammariello appear on its later release, tying it into the main 100% Silk production infrastructure.

LGBTQ+ and Trans Context

100% Silk has been a notably queer and trans-inclusive space within underground dance music. Several of its most significant founding artists are trans women: Octo Octa (Maya Bouldry-Morrison) and Magic Touch (Damon Eliza Palermo both came out publicly during and after their Silk period. Octo Octa went on to co-found T4T LUV NRG (trans for trans love energy) with Eris Drew, consolidating this identity work into an explicitly trans label framework. The queer character of 100% Silk was present from the beginning but not programmatically stated — it emerged from Amanda Brown’s personal networks and the communities the label naturally drew from.

Scene Context

100% Silk launched at a moment when lo-fi house and bedroom dance music were gaining critical mass outside the traditional club circuit — the same underground networks that NNF occupied on the psych/noise side were producing dance music with similar DIY aesthetics. The label’s relationship to its parent (NNF) is both organizational and cultural: Britt Brown’s design work is continuous across both, mastering engineers like Jared Carrigan and Alex Nagle appear on both catalogs, and artists like LA Vampires and Maria Minerva released on both labels.
