Fire Records

UK independent label, London. Active 1984–present. Discogs ID: 2051. Parent company: Fire Records Of London Ltd. (Discogs ID: 672671). Companies House number: 03000256.

Founded mid-1980s by Clive Solomon (Discogs ID: 1909264) and Johnny Waller (Discogs ID: 555838). James Nicholls (Discogs ID: 600128) assumed operational/creative control c.2001 and was formally appointed a company director on 24 May 2017; Clive Solomon retains 75%+ share ownership and remains a director. One of the longest-running UK independent labels; catalog spans from early post-punk and indie-pop through jangle, lo-fi, psychedelia, and contemporary experimental and folk.

Singles catalogued under the BLAZE prefix; albums under FIRE LP. Reissue series (early 2000s) used SFIRE prefix.

History

Era 1: Founding Years (1984–1993)

Fire launched in 1984 with the debut single by 1000 Mexicans — Diving For Pearls (FIRE 1). Solomon and Waller rapidly built a roster of UK post-punk and indie-pop bands, many from Bristol and Scotland.

First wave of key signings (1985–86): Pulp (first single Little Girl (With Blue Eyes), FIRE 5, 1985), The Blue Aeroplanes (debut single and LP Tolerance, 1985–86), Colenso Parade, The Rose Of Avalanche, Close Lobsters, The Farm, The Times, Lives Of Angels, and Sign Language.

By 1987–88 the label had added Spacemen 3 (Playing With Fire, FIRE LP16, 1989), Television Personalities, The Parachute Men, The Perfect Disaster, and Dave Graney (Australian artist, London-based).

The label reached into the US through licensing and direct signings: Gigolo Aunts, Neutral Milk Hotel (Everything Is, BLAZE 79, 1995), Everclear (World of Noise, FIRE CD46, 1995), Leatherface, The Halo Benders, 1-2 Japanese, Fitz of Depression, and The Karl Hendricks Trio.

Pulp’s early catalog was extensively reissued on Fire — Masters of the Universe: Pulp on Fire 1985–86 (FIRE LP36, 1994) gathered their label output before they signed to Island.

Teenage Fanclub appeared on the sublabel Flip Records (2) — Deep Fried Fanclub (FLIPCD002, 1995) collected early singles. Spacemen 3’s Translucent Flashbacks (FLIP CD003, 1995) was also on Flip.

Era 1b: Parallel venture — Paperhouse Records (1990–1993)

While still running Fire, Solomon co-founded Paperhouse Records (1990–1993) as a joint venture with David E. Barker, owner of Glass Records. Paperhouse issued Teenage Fanclub’s debut A Catholic Education (1990), records by The Pastels, The Servants, Daniel Johnston, and Dim Stars (Richard Hell). The label folded in 1993. (Wikipedia — Paperhouse Records)

Era 1c: The Pulp dispute and late-1990s decline

A prolonged and fractious relationship with Pulp ended the decade in acrimony. Pulp spent roughly 1984–1993 on Fire; the band later described the period as marked by poor promotion, underfunding, and delayed releases — Separations (completed 1989) was held until 1992.

Exit deal (c.1992–93). After a period in which Island Records informally subsidised singles through the Sheffield imprint Gift Records but withheld further funding once it learned of Pulp’s existing Fire obligations, Geoff Travis and Jeanette Lee of Rough Trade’s management arm negotiated Pulp’s release from Fire. The settlement required Fire to receive “a portion of the band’s future major-label earnings.” (Wikipedia — Pulp (band), as of 2026-10-01) Pulp guitarist Mark Webber later confirmed that “Fire did very well out of it, they continued to earn royalties from future releases for many years,” without specifying a figure or percentage. (The Strange Brew — Mark Webber on the rise of Pulp) In a 1998 interview Jarvis Cocker corroborated ongoing payments, stating that Solomon “still gets money off our records now, because of [the fact that] he went to law school, so he drafted a contract that was morally wrong.” (stubermania.substack.com — Jarvis Cocker 1998 interview)

What is confirmed: Rough Trade’s management arm (Travis/Lee) brokered the exit; Fire received an ongoing share of future major-label (Island) earnings. What is not confirmed in any public source: the specific percentage or monetary terms of Fire’s share, whether the agreement was time-limited or perpetual, and which named individual at Rough Trade Management led the negotiation. No primary source (contract, contemporaneous press report, label or management statement) has surfaced to document specific figures.

The most public flashpoint came in 1994 with the Masters of the Universe: Pulp on Fire 1985–86 compilation (FIRE LP36). Cocker wrote liner notes for the release; Solomon rejected them as containing thinly veiled criticism of the label and replaced them with notes he wrote himself. Cocker later said: “The thing that gets my goat is that I wrote a sleeve note, which Clive Solomon refused to print. I don’t know why because it didn’t say anything completely derogatory about Fire. So when it came to Fire’s reissue of ‘It’, I refused to help them.” (PulpWiki — Masters of the Universe; quote attributed to Jarvis Cocker, original source not independently verified)

By the turn of the millennium, Fire had effectively stalled. A Record Collector profile (2014, ISSN 02651548 — paywalled via Gale Academic OneFile, ID A389507759, article title “Roaring fire: Fire Records managing director James Nicholls on the indie’s rejuvenation and 30-year lifespan”) states the label slowed to a standstill “after increasing costs and an all too public spat with Pulp” and an A&R direction that had drifted. Solomon’s departure was not abrupt: Nicholls “persuaded Solomon that Fire was worth reigniting, and they started it up again slowly in around 2001.” (Gale abstract)

James Nicholls describes the label at the point of his joining (c.2001) as having “imploded towards the end of the ’90s and was just holding on by the skin of its teeth.” He was at that time working as a designer by day and musician by night. Separately he recalled running the label “on my own in a dilapidated house-share in the worst area of Nottingham” approximately ten years before a 2014 interview, i.e. c.2004. (Bandcamp label profile, 2024; Drowned in Sound archived interview, 2014)

Conflict — “taken over in 2002” vs. Companies House records. The vault previously stated “Taken over in 2002 by James Nicholls.” Companies House filings for Fire Records Of London Limited (company number 03000256) show Nicholls was appointed as director on 24 May 2017 — not 2002. Clive Solomon has been a director since 3 April 1995 and remains an active director with 75% or more share ownership and voting rights (PSC status, notified 6 April 2016, identity verified 5 September 2025). Gerald Solomon (presumably a relative of Clive) served as company secretary from 3 April 1995 and resigned 9 December 2020. (Companies House — officers; Companies House — PSC)

The most likely reconciliation: Nicholls assumed operational/creative control of the label around 2001–2002 (consistent with multiple journalistic accounts) but was not formally appointed to the company directorship until 2017. The label may have operated informally under his management for 15+ years before he became a statutory director. The “taken over” framing in earlier sources (and the previous vault entry) likely reflects functional/operational control rather than legal ownership.

Era 2: Under James Nicholls (2001–present)

Solomon wound down A&R involvement by c.2001 (see conflict note in Era 1c above regarding Nicholls’s formal directorship date). James Nicholls — described as “a former designer and musician” and also identified in a 2014 Drowned in Sound interview as having previously run the label from “a dilapidated house-share in the worst area of Nottingham” — took over as creative/managing director and relaunched with a strong reissue programme (SFIRE series) alongside new signings. Nicholls was formally appointed a company director on 24 May 2017 per Companies House; Clive Solomon retains 75%+ share ownership and remains a director. (Companies House)

Major early Nicholls-era signing: Bark Psychosis, whose comeback album Codename: Dustsucker (FIRECD90, 2004) was a landmark post-rock release.

The label broadened considerably through the 2000s–2020s: Giant Sand / Howe Gelb, Guided By Voices / Robert Pollard, Pere Ubu, Jane Weaver, Josephine Foster, Death And Vanilla, Bardo Pond, The Chills, ESG, Vanishing Twin, Throwing Muses / Kristin Hersh, Mission Of Burma, The Dream Syndicate, Rocket From The Tombs, The Black Lips, Virginia Wing, Modern Studies, The Pictish Trail, Islet, Faten Kanaan, Brigid Mae Power, Marina Allen, Wreckless Eric, and many others.

Pigbag retrospective compilations Volume One and Volume Two (both 2010) were also released in this era.

BBC Radiophonic Workshop archival releases appeared on Fire 2021–22.

Current address: 4 Tyssen Street, London E8 2FJ. Director: James Nicholls.

Johnny Waller — Background (pre-Fire)

Johnny Waller (died October 1996, aged 40, in a bicycle accident in London) was a music journalist and scene organiser based first in Dunfermline/Edinburgh, then London.

Kingdom Come fanzine (September 1977 – late 1979, Dunfermline). Waller founded and edited the Scottish punk fanzine Kingdom Come, first issue published September 29, 1977 in Dunfermline — described by kinemagigz.com as “Fife’s first music fanzine.” He published a new issue every few weeks; at least 17 issues appeared before he wound it down in late 1979 when he relocated to London. (Highest confirmed issue number: 14, dated January 1979 per a British Library-held copy and a Popsike auction listing; kinemagigz.com states the run reached “at least seventeen issues.“) Starting price: £0.15, rising to £0.20 by the final issues. Contributors included Sandy Fenwick and Stuart Adamson (of The Skids). The fanzine covered the early Scottish punk and post-punk scenes and included early features on The Scars, Simple Minds, The Skids, and The Rezillos, as well as the Edinburgh independent label Fast Product. Waller’s work was influential in placing these acts in a wider post-punk critical context. (kinemagigz.com, as of 2026-07-18; punk77.co.uk/band/scars/, as of 2026-07-18; popsike.com — issue 14 auction; stillunusual.tumblr.com — issue 11)

Deep Cuts Records (Edinburgh, 1979). Waller founded the short-lived independent label Deep Cuts Records in Edinburgh. Two 7” singles were released on the imprint, both in 1979: Visitors — Electric Heat EP, and The Fakes — Production (also cited as Sylvia Clarke in one source — possible A-side/title discrepancy). He also managed the Edinburgh band Visitors during this period. (kinemagigz.com; coffeetablenotes.blogspot.com — Big Gold Dreams)

Sounds journalist (c.1980–1984). After relocating to London, Waller wrote for the UK weekly music paper Sounds. Confirmed bylined pieces include a Phil Collins interview (Sounds, 25 December 1982) and a review of The Sound’s All Fall Down LP. He also chaired a punk debate transcribed in Sounds (25 December 1982), hosted at Spotlight Publications. The kinemagigz.com profile states he was a “frequent contributor” to Sounds, as well as writing for NME, Record Mirror, and Zig Zag. (kinemagigz.com; thegenesisarchive.co.uk; punkrocker.org.uk/punkscene/punkdebate)

Photo agency. The kinemagigz.com source also notes Waller co-ran a photo agency in London alongside his work at Fire Records, though no further detail is given. (kinemagigz.com)

Eurythmics biography (1985). Waller authored a biography of Eurythmics, published in 1985. (kinemagigz.com; thegenesisarchive.co.uk — The Phil Collins Story)

Source note. The kinemagigz.com page (https://www.kinemagigz.com/kingdom_come.htm) is the single most detailed source on Waller’s pre-Fire biography; it appears to be a fan-maintained site covering the Scottish post-punk scene with primary-level specificity (release titles, dates, personnel). Cross-checked against Big Gold Dreams blog (2019) and punk77.co.uk, both of which corroborate the Deep Cuts/Sounds details independently. Treat as reliable secondary source with strong curation.
