Al Maslakh

Beirut-based label releasing Lebanese experimental/improvised music and international musicians recorded in Lebanon. Discogs label ID: 73145. Founded c. 2005; run by Mazen Kerbaj out of Sin El Fil, Beirut (later Berlin after Kerbaj relocated in 2015). The name means “The Slaughterhouse” in Arabic. Sister label Johnny Kafta’s Kids Menu (ID 242840) covers experimental rock. Catalog runs MSLKH 01–028, 2005–2024.

URLs: almaslakh.org · bandcamp: almaslakh.bandcamp.com · soundcloud: soundcloud.com/al-maslakh

Overview / History

Al Maslakh emerged from the Beirut free-improvisation scene that coalesced in the early 2000s around a small cluster of musicians — principally Mazen Kerbaj (trumpet), Sharif Sehnaoui (guitar), Raed Yassin (bass), and Christine Sehnaoui (alto saxophone). The label’s mandate, as stated on Discogs, is to release music “involving Lebanese musicians or music by international musicians recorded in Lebanon.” This framing covers two overlapping streams: documents of the local scene (solo records, duo and trio sessions) and records by visiting European and American improvisers who came to perform and record in Beirut.

Mazen Kerbaj handled artwork and design for virtually every release in the catalog — consistent visual identity throughout. Fadi Tabbal (Tunefork Studios, Beirut) became the label’s primary recording and mixing engineer from around 2010, appearing on at least a dozen releases.

The label launched in 2005 with Kerbaj’s own solo trumpet record Brt Vrt Zrt Krt (MSLKH 01), the quartet Rouba3i5 (MSLKH 02, featuring Kerbaj, both Sehnaouisen, and Norwegian percussionist Ingar Zach), and the first Peter Brötzmann / Michael Zerang Beirut recording (MSLKH 03). That three-record opening set the template: solo statement, Lebanese collective document, international visiting artist.

By 2018 the label had documented the core trio — “A” Trio (Kerbaj, Sehnaoui, Yassin) — across three records including the landmark collaboration with AMM (AAMM, MSLKH 21, 2018), pairing the Beirut trio with Eddie Prévost and John Tilbury. Recent releases (2020–2024) show the label continuing under Kerbaj from Berlin, with newer projects including Sawt Out, Tony Elieh’s Hic Up, and Thuluth.

Connections to the Lebanese Experimental Scene

Al Maslakh sits at the center of a tightly networked Beirut scene that built up through the 2000s. The core figures — Kerbaj, Sharif Sehnaoui, Christine Sehnaoui, Raed Yassin, Tony Elieh, Charbel Haber — all cross-pollinate in multiple group configurations:

  • “A” Trio (Kerbaj / S. Sehnaoui / R. Yassin) — the label’s house trio; three records on Al Maslakh.
  • Johnny Kafta Anti-Vegetarian Orchestra — larger collective (Kerbaj, Sehnaoui, Yassin, Elieh, Haber, Malek Rizkallah); primarily on sister label Johnny Kafta’s Kids Menu.
  • Karkhana — pan-Arab/European improv supergroup including Kerbaj, Sehnaoui, Zerang, Elieh, Sam Shalabi, Maurice Louca, Umut Çağlar, Özün Usta.
  • Fadi Tabbal (Tunefork Studios) — the scene’s principal recording engineer from ~2010; appeared on at least 10 Al Maslakh releases, giving the mid-period catalog a consistent sonic signature.
  • Recurring European collaborators — the label’s international reach reflects both Beirut’s position as a hub for visiting European improvisers and Kerbaj’s own network in the Berlin/Vienna free-improv world (especially after his 2015 relocation). Visits from AMM, Eddie Prévost, Michel Waisvisz (who died 2008, making Shortwave a late document), Peter Brötzmann, and Axel Dörner map the transatlantic free-music circuit onto a Lebanese address.
  • Discrepant (London) — listed among Kerbaj’s top labels outside Al Maslakh; indicates broader international distribution.

Open Questions

  • Mazen Kerbaj’s role post-Berlin-relocation (2015+) — label continues but output thins; whether Al Maslakh remains Beirut-anchored or effectively Berlin-based now.
