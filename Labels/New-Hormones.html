Oct 27, 20269 min read

New Hormones

Discogs ID: 8784
Active: 1977; 1980–1982 (then dormant; occasional reissue activity 2025)
Location: 50 Newton Street, Manchester M1 2EA, UK
Catalog prefix: ORG (primary); CAT (secondary series)
Run by: Richard Boon (Buzzcocks manager)
Founded by: Buzzcocks (Pete Shelley / Howard Devoto)

Pioneering Manchester independent label. Released the Buzzcocks’ debut Spiral Scratch EP in January 1977 — one of the foundational documents of British punk’s DIY self-release model — then lay dormant until 1980. From 1980 to late 1982 it operated as a proper label under Richard Boon’s management, releasing twenty-plus records across the ORG and CAT catalog series. The roster spans post-punk, industrial, new wave, and jazz-inflected pop, anchored by Dislocation Dance, Ludus, Eric Random, and The Diagram Brothers. Stuart James served as the informal house producer across most of the catalog. Lacquer cutting in the early phase by George Peckham / Porky (5) (Porky Prime Cut); from 1981 onward almost exclusively by BilBo (3) (Denis Blackham).

The most unlikely single release in the catalog is ORG 18 (Earthbound Ghost Need, 1982, Eric Random And The Bedlamites), which draws in Stephen Mallinder and Richard H. Kirk of Cabaret Voltaire alongside Ian Runacres (Dislocation Dance), Andy Diagram, Fraser and Lawrence from The Diagram Brothers, and Dids (Ludus) — effectively the entire New Hormones extended family plus the Sheffield industrial underground, on one 12”.

Connections

  • Buzzcocks as founders: Pete Shelley not only founded the label but played guitar on the Tiller Boys release (ORG 3), blurring the line between label owner and roster act.
  • Richard Boon → Rough Trade: After New Hormones folded, Boon went to Rough Trade, where he worked on their trade magazine (“The Catalogue” / label ID 51167 in Discogs). He took the New Hormones aesthetic into the broader indie infrastructure.
  • Stuart James as house producer: James produced or co-produced a majority of the ORG series, from Ludus’s debut through the Dislocation Dance catalog. After New Hormones folded he continued working with Dislocation Dance on their German Ariola single.
  • Cabaret Voltaire thread: Stephen Mallinder produced Eric Random’s ORG 6 (1980); both Mallinder and Richard H. Kirk appeared on ORG 18 (1982). Eric Random became a full Cabaret Voltaire collaborator/session player through the mid-1980s (appeared on 2X45, Micro-Phonies, Sensoria, Behind The Iron Curtain).
  • Martin Hannett as “Martin Zero”: Hannett produced Spiral Scratch under a pseudonym — his only known use of it. By 1977 he was not yet producing for Factory; this was an early credit.
  • Martin Rushent on ORG 5: Rushent produced The Decorators’ Twilight View (1980); he would go on to produce the Human League’s Dare (1981) — one of the defining synth-pop records.
  • Graham Revell on ORG 20: Revell’s saxophone on Ludus’s Danger Came Smiling (1982) — he was a member of Australian industrial group SPK. His presence on a Manchester indie label is an unusual international connection.
  • Dale Hibbert on ORG 25: Hibbert produced Gods Gift (2)‘s Discipline (1982). He is better known as the original bass player in The Smiths (played on their first demos, left before recording began).
  • Malcolm Garrett on ORG 3: Garrett designed the Tiller Boys sleeve — he was the primary graphic designer for the Buzzcocks and later Duran Duran and Magazine.
  • Andy Diagram as connective tissue: Appears on ORG 9 and 17 (Diagram Brothers), on Dislocation Dance releases, and on ORG 18 (Bedlamites) — he is present on more New Hormones releases than almost any individual.
  • Bryn Jones on ORG 17: Did the logo design for Diagram Brothers’ Some Marvels of Modern Science. Possibly the same Bryn Jones who later recorded as Muslimgauze — unconfirmed.
  • Bop Cassettes: Eric Random appeared on a release from Bop Cassettes in 1988 (Ian Runacres’ label, see Dislocation Dance). The New Hormones scene maintained links into the late 1980s through informal networks.

Unreleased / Planned Recordings

New Hormones funded but could not afford to release several significant records:

  • The Fall — paid for the recording session that became Bingo-Master’s Break-Out! EP; couldn’t release due to cost; The Fall eventually released it on Step-Forward Records (1978)
  • Cabaret Voltaire — sent tapes; not released
  • Gang of Four — sent tapes; not released

Also noted: Boon mentioned wanting to release something with Basil from Yargo but couldn’t.

Northern Lights Cassette Magazine

New Hormones funded and distributed Northern Lights, a quarterly cassette magazine. Appeared four times between April 1981 and February 1982. Each edition issued in a limited run of 500, with booklet, badge, stickers, and a sweatshirt offer included. Separate from the CAT cassette series.

Open Questions

  • Bryn Jones (5) on ORG 17 logo — probably not Muslimgauze: Muslimgauze’s Bryn Jones (b. 17 June 1961, Salford) began releasing as E.g Oblique Graph in 1982, Muslimgauze in 1983 — geographic proximity to Manchester is possible but no direct link found
  • ORG 2 (The Secret Public) — fanzine credited to Linder Sterling and Jon Savage together; what was Jon Savage’s role specifically?
