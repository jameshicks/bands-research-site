LTM Recordings

Full name: Les Temps Modernes (original name) Founded: Edinburgh, 1983 Current base: Norfolk, England Founder: James Nice Catalog prefix: CSBT (1983–84) → LTM V. (1985–87) → LTMCD (1990–) Based: Edinburgh (1983); Brussels (1987–91); UK (1991–present) Focus: Post-punk / new wave originals (1983–87); Factory, Factory Benelux, and Crépuscule reissues (1997–); Auteur Labels compilation series (2008–)

Origin

Founded by James Nice in Edinburgh in 1983 as Les Temps Modernes — the name taken from Jean-Luc Godard’s production company (itself a reference to Chaplin’s Modern Times). It began as a fanzine in 1982, then became a cassette label in 1983 while Nice was still in sixth form. Early releases championed the Scottish and Northern English post-punk underground: Section 25, The Happy Family, Crispy Ambulance.

Initial pressing/distribution handled by Fast Forward Records. During Nice’s Glasgow University years (1984–87), Rough Trade handled pressing and distribution.

The original catalog numbering used Roman numerals with a CSBT prefix (e.g. CSBT V.I, CSBT V.II), then switched to LTM V.X–onwards as the label evolved.

Brussels Period (1987–1991)

Nice relocated to Brussels in 1987, where he worked directly at Les Disques du Crépuscule — and subsequently at PIAS (Play It Again Sam). He left Crépuscule specifically over a 23 Skidoo contract dispute. This period embedded him in the Crépuscule network and gave him firsthand knowledge of the catalog, the personnel, and the label’s relationship with Factory Benelux. (The 23 Skidoo conflict was eventually resolved — 23 Skidoo released new material under the revived Crépuscule in 2012–13.)

During the Brussels years, LTM released albums by Tuxedomoon, Peter Principle, and Steven Brown, plus Section 25 and Josef K reissue CDs. This is the first LTM reactivation (~1989–91).

Solicitor period and second reactivation

After Brussels, Nice trained and practiced as a solicitor — representing Factory artists in disputes against London Records in the mid-1990s. He met Tony Wilson at the Factory Charles Street office in 1992 to discuss a full Durutti Column reissue campaign; Factory’s collapse that year prevented it.

LTM was reactivated again from 1997 — this time primarily as a systematic reissue vehicle for material orphaned by Factory’s 1992 collapse. The LTMCD series numbers run sequentially from ~2302 onward. Core focus: Section 25, Crispy Ambulance, The Wake, The Names, Paul Haig, Anna Domino, Blaine L. Reininger, Minny Pops, 23 Skidoo, Vini Reilly / The Durutti Column.

Factory Records Ltd. (2012)

Following Factory’s 1992 receivership, the catalog was in legal limbo for years. Nice — together with Peter Saville (Factory’s designer), Alan Erasmus (Factory co-founder), and Oliver Wilson (Tony Wilson’s son) — established Factory Records Ltd. to preserve and administer the Factory brand. The entity issued only one release: a vinyl reissue of Section 25’s From the Hip. Nice also collaborated with Saville on the Use Hearing Protection 40th-anniversary box set for Warner.

Factory Benelux Revival (2012)

In 2012, Nice revived the Factory Benelux imprint — with the explicit blessing of Michel Duval and Annik Honoré. New releases under the FBN name: Section 25, The Wake, The Names. Factory Benelux had ceased new releases in 1988; Crépuscule had used the name for CD reissues 1990–91; this 2012 revival was the first genuinely new activity under the imprint in over two decades.

Crépuscule Revival (2012–)

Simultaneously, Nice revived Les Disques du Crépuscule itself — again with Duval and Honoré’s blessing. New Crépuscule releases from 2012: Helen Marnie, Marsheaux, 23 Skidoo, Penelope Queen (daughter of Isabelle Antena of Antena). The label continues to issue reissues and occasional new material. Stephen Mallinder’s Clinker (TWI 1256, 2021) is its most recent notable release.

James Nice

Born 6 January 1966 in Essex. Also writes non-music books under the pen name James Hayward (military history, WWII double agents — Double Agent Snow / Hitler’s Spy, Simon & Schuster, 2014 among others).

LTM began as a fanzine in 1982 while Nice was in sixth form in Edinburgh, converting to a cassette label in 1983. Dave McCullough gave the early Crispy Ambulance cassettes five stars in Sounds. Nice has described the label’s early aesthetic as explicitly influenced by Crépuscule and El Records.

He first visited Brussels in 1986 — meeting Tuxedomoon and Anna Domino, describing the Crépuscule atmosphere as “Paris in the 1920s.” He joined the label’s staff in 1987, starting in the mailroom and rising to A&R and reissue work. He ran a Crépuscule sublabel called Interior. While at Crépuscule he also worked on Futurism & Dada Reviewed (a highbrow art compilation that eventually appeared on Sub Rosa after he left). He departed specifically over a 23 Skidoo contract dispute — not a general falling-out. After Brussels he trained and practiced as a solicitor, representing Factory artists in legal disputes against London Records in the mid-1990s.

Shadowplayers: The Rise and Fall of Factory Records — the Factory history:

  • Shadowplayers documentary (LTMDVD 2391, May 2006): 2h15, 19 chapters, ~40 interviews including Tony Wilson, Peter Saville, Peter Hook, Howard Devoto, Vini Reilly; described by Nice as “more like an audiobook that you can also watch”

Nice self-describes: “I’m probably a better researcher than a writer… World wars, punk wars — it’s all history, all about finding the narrative.”

Also credited as compiler/producer on 250+ LTM release entries in Discogs.

Other Label Relationships

Beyond Factory/FBN/Crépuscule, LTM has reissued catalog from:

  • Sarah Records — Field Mice full back catalog (LTMCD 2419/2421/2423, 2005); The Wake (also on Sarah); The Orchids. Sarah Records (Bristol, 1987–95) is aesthetically adjacent to the post-punk underground — independent, anti-commercial, meticulous.
  • Postcard Records — Josef K reissues: Sorry For Laughing (LTMLP 2549) and The Only Fun In Town (2LP expanded, limited to 500); both February 2019. Josef K appeared on early Crépuscule (TWI 023).
  • New Hormones — Auteur Labels volume (LTMCD 2492); Manchester-based, associated with Buzzcocks, Howard Devoto, Ludus, Diagram Brothers, Biting Tongues, Dislocation Dance, Eric Random
  • Compact Organisation (Tot Taylor / Paul Kinder, est. 1981) — A Young Person’s Guide to Compact reissue
  • Object Music, Robs Records, Independent Project Records — Auteur Labels series

Key Relationships

  • Les Disques du Crépuscule — Nice worked there 1987–91; LTM revived the label 2012; Crépuscule is the organizational parent of what became LTM’s reissue focus
  • Factory Records — primary reissue target from 1997; Nice co-founded Factory Records Ltd. (2010) with Erasmus, Saville, and Oliver Wilson
  • Factory Benelux — Nice revived the imprint 2012; FBN had operated under Crépuscule 1980–88
  • 23 Skidoo — significant LTM reissue history (29 release credits)
  • Cabaret Voltaire — Factory-adjacent; Crépuscule connection via TWI 018
  • Blurt — LTM reissued the Blurt catalog extensively 2009–2010: Blurt + Singles (2009), Cut It! (2010), The Factory Recordings (2008 compilation). Blurt performed at the Shadowplayers book launch (Rough Trade East, London, 2010).

LTM vs. Mute Grey Area — Factory Reissues

Finding (web research, 2026-08-25): The premise that Mute’s Grey Area sublabel and LTM Recordings divided Factory Records reissue rights between them is a misconception. The two labels operated in entirely separate catalog territories and there was no division to speak of.

What Grey Area actually reissued

The Grey Area of Mute Records was founded in 1990 to reissue material that influenced Daniel Miller — its core catalog is Throbbing Gristle / Industrial Records, Can (Spoon Records), Cabaret Voltaire’s Rough Trade and early recordings, Wire, DAF, Einstürzende Neubauten, SPK, and other Industrial/experimental acts. Factory Records artists (Section 25, Crispy Ambulance, The Wake, Durutti Column, A Certain Ratio, Happy Mondays, Quando Quango, Minny Pops, The Names, etc.) do not appear in the Grey Area discography. The sole overlap between the two orbits is Cabaret Voltaire, who had one Factory release (FAC 094, the “Yashar” 12”, 1983) — but Grey Area reissued their Rough Trade era material, not that Factory single. Sources: Grey Area discography — Wikipedia; RetroFuturista — Grey Area label.

How LTM licensed Factory material

LTM’s access to the Factory catalog rested on two structural features of Factory’s contracts:

  1. Artists owned their own masters. Factory’s founding contracts gave artists ownership of their recordings — the label retained nothing. When Factory went into receivership in November 1992, there was therefore very little for the receiver to sell. This is why Nice could license Section 25 material directly from Factory (who had no interest in back catalog beyond Joy Division and New Order) and, after 1992, could approach artists directly since their rights had reverted to them. Nice has stated explicitly: “most of the bands owned their own music so I’ve always been able to deal with them direct.” Source: Louder Than War interview — James Nice.

  2. Joy Division / New Order were the exception. New Order and Joy Division retained their masters under the same artist-ownership principle, but given their commercial scale they were managed through major-label channels (London Records post-1992, eventually Warner). Nice has said it is “far better for core Joy Division and New Order catalogue to be managed and distributed by a major label” — confirming he does not hold those rights and does not seek them. LTM’s Factory reissues are therefore the non-major Factory acts: Section 25, Crispy Ambulance, The Wake, Quando Quango, Minny Pops, The Names, Durutti Column, etc. Source: The Original Sin fanzine interview — James Nice (2010).

  3. “Marginal catalogue” pragmatics. Nice has also noted that licensing from major labels for physical reissues of lesser-known catalog has become easier, not harder, over time: “it is easier and cheaper to licence catalogue from major labels these days, as they are not interested in physical reissues of marginal catalogue any more.” This applies to the Factory-adjacent acts where label ownership (rather than artist ownership) eventually settled in major-label hands post-receivership. Source: The Original Sin fanzine interview.

Summary

LTM and Mute Grey Area are not competitors or co-rights-holders in the Factory catalog. They cover orthogonal territories: Grey Area = Industrial/experimental/Mute-adjacent; LTM = Factory/FBN/Crépuscule post-punk underground. LTM’s Factory reissues are licensed artist-direct (the normal case under Factory’s contracts) or, where a major holds catalog rights, licensed from that major. There is no territorial split, no artist-specific allocation, and no documented communication or agreement between the two labels regarding Factory material.
