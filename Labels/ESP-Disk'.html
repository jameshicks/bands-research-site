ESP-Disk’

Discogs ID: 33952 (ESP-Disk’; parent: ESP-Disk’ Ltd., ID 278965) Founded: 1963, New York City Founder: Bernard Stollman Active: 1964–1976 (original run); revived 2005–present Genre: Free jazz, avant-garde, experimental, psychedelic folk/rock Catalog prefix: ESP-XXXX (main series); ESP-2000s (rock/underground); ESP-3000s (historical reissues)

One of the defining independent labels of the American 1960s avant-garde. Founded and run almost entirely as a one-man operation by lawyer-turned-impresario Bernard Stollman, ESP-Disk’ issued the foundational recordings of the free jazz movement alongside countercultural folk-rock, spoken word, and experimental work. Operating from a succession of New York addresses between 1964 and 1976, the label documented artists largely rejected by major labels, pressing runs were tiny, and the label was chronically under-funded — yet the catalog it produced became the core of the free jazz canon.

History

Origins (1963–1964)

Bernard Stollman, a New York attorney, founded the label intending to record Esperanto music — the name was originally “Esperanto-Disk,” shortened to ESP-Disk’. The first release (ESP-1001, 1964) was Ni Kantu En Esperanto by Duncan Charters, Frans Jahger, and Julius Balbin, a set of songs in the constructed language. Within a year Stollman had pivoted decisively toward the free jazz underground.

The Free Jazz Core (1965–1967)

The label’s signature period. In 1965 alone ESP issued the debuts or key early recordings of Albert Ayler, Pharoah Sanders, Sun Ra, Giuseppi Logan, the New York Art Quartet, the Byron Allen Trio, Paul Bley, Ran Blake, and Lowell Davidson. Milford Graves and Sonny Morgan’s Percussion Ensemble (ESP-1015) was among the most radical of the era.

Engineer Richard Alderson recorded or engineered an enormous share of the catalog — 111 releases in Discogs credits — and was responsible for capturing the label’s sonic character: live, unadorned, often a single session recorded quickly and cheaply. Producer Harry Smith (18 releases) oversaw a significant portion of the non-jazz work. Elmer Jared Gordon served as production aide across 15 releases.

By 1966–1967 the label had expanded into rock and counterculture with The Fugs (ESP-1018, 1028, 1038), The Godz, Pearls Before Swine, The Holy Modal Rounders, and spoken word releases (William S. Burroughs, Dr. Timothy Leary, Tuli Kupferberg solo). The label documented the full spectrum of 1960s dissent in a single catalog.

Later Years and Dormancy (1968–1976)

Output slowed after 1968 as finances deteriorated. The label continued issuing new work alongside archival jazz releases (Bud Powell broadcast recordings, Billie Holiday broadcasts, Charlie Parker) and more underground folk and rock. The Fugs remained a flagship act. Charles Manson’s LIE (ESP-2003, 1974) arrived as a controversy. The label ceased operations around 1976.

Reissue History and Revival (1992–present)

In 1992 Stollman licensed the back catalog (all 115 original titles) to German label ZYX Music, which reissued everything on CD. That deal lasted six years. Subsequent licensing agreements followed with Dutch label Calibre and Italian label Abraxas (which claimed an exclusive worldwide deal for manufacturing and sub-licensing). Abraxas issued material under the Get Back in-house label or ESP Disk (Italy).

In 2005 the New York operation resumed manufacturing independently, issuing reissues of the original catalog alongside previously unreleased material and new recordings. The label has remained active since, releasing contemporary artists including Flow Trio, Matthew Shipp, William Parker, and many others.

Key Artists

Albert Ayler (Discogs ID: 202309)

Albert Ayler is the label’s most-documented artist by release count (102 pressings across all formats; 47 primary-credit releases in the original period). His 1965 trio date Spiritual Unity (ESP-1002) — with Gary Peacock (bass) and Sunny Murray (drums) — is the label’s defining record. Ayler plays on Bells (ESP-1010, 1965), Spirits Rejoice (ESP-1020, 1965), New York Eye And Ear Control (ESP-1016, 1966), and posthumously on Prophecy (ESP-3030, 1975).

Sun Ra (Discogs ID: 35328)

Sun Ra recorded three canonical albums with his Arkestra: The Heliocentric Worlds Of Sun Ra, Vol. I (ESP-1014, 1965), Vol. II (ESP-1017, 1966), and Nothing Is… (ESP-1045, 1966). The Arkestra lineup on these sessions included Marshall Allen (piccolo flute, alto sax), John Gilmore (tenor sax), Pat Patrick (baritone sax), Ronnie Boykins (bass), Robert Cummings (bass clarinet), and Sun Ra himself on piano, marimba, celesta, and timpani.

The Fugs (Discogs ID: 262227)

The Fugs — Ed Sanders, Tuli Kupferberg, Ken Weaver — released three studio albums on ESP: The Fugs First Album (ESP-1018, 1966), The Fugs (ESP-1028, 1966, with Allen Ginsberg liner notes), and Virgin Fugs (ESP-1038, 1967). The band bridged the label’s free jazz and counterculture constituencies.

Pearls Before Swine (Discogs ID: 270577)

Pearls Before Swine — principally Tom Rapp (vocals, guitar) — released One Nation Underground (ESP-1054, 1967) and Balaklava (ESP-1075, 1968). Both were produced and engineered by Richard Alderson. The band used highly eclectic instrumentation (autoharp, banjo, sarangi, clavioline, English horn).

Pharoah Sanders (Discogs ID: 21147)

Pharoah Sanders debuted on record with Pharaoh (ESP-1003, 1965), with Jane Getz (piano), William Bennett (bass), Marvin Pattillo (percussion), and Stan Foster (trumpet). ESP-1003 is among the earliest documents of his work.

Patty Waters (Discogs ID: 145245)

Patty Waters released Sings (ESP-1025, 1966) — a duo album of standards and originals with Burton Greene (piano, harp) on the B-side and Waters herself on piano for the A-side — and the live College Tour (ESP-1055, 1966). Sings is notable for its Side B performance of “Black Is The Color Of My True Love’s Hair,” an extended free improvisation that became one of the label’s most celebrated tracks.

Marion Brown (Discogs ID: 216235)

Marion Brown recorded the Marion Brown Quartet (ESP-1022, 1966) — Brown (alto sax), Alan Shorter (trumpet), Reggie Johnson (bass), Rashied Ali (drums) — and Why Not (ESP-1040, 1968).

Burton Greene (Discogs ID: 343984)

Burton Greene released Burton Greene Quartet (ESP-1024, 1966) and appeared as sideman on Patty Waters Sings. His trio released On Tour (ESP-1074, 1968).

Milford Graves (Discogs ID: 341514)

Milford Graves co-led Percussion Ensemble (ESP-1015, 1965) with Sunny Morgan — a stark two-drum session considered a landmark of free percussion. Graves appears as New York Art Quartet drummer on ESP-1004.

Gato Barbieri (Discogs ID: 177049)

Gato Barbieri debuted on ESP with In Search Of The Mystery (ESP-1049, 1967) under the Gato Barbieri Quartet name, his first full album as a leader.

Don Cherry (Discogs ID: 42757)

Don Cherry appears on New York Eye And Ear Control (ESP-1016, 1966) alongside Ayler, Peacock, Tchicai, Rudd, and Murray. 30 releases credited on ESP over the years.

The Label’s Aesthetic

ESP-Disk’ operated without conventional A&R: Stollman largely recorded whoever came to him, with minimal rehearsal and low budgets. This resulted in an unusually consistent rawness — the recordings sound live and immediate, often captured in a single session by Richard Alderson (who also served as “Recording Director” on some credits). The label’s covers, handled variously by Howard Bernstein, Jordan Matthews, David Michalowski, and Sandra Stollman, have a characteristic hand-made graphic quality.

The label published no liner notes on many releases, or only brief, non-analytical text. The music was allowed to stand without explanation — an approach that marked it as oppositional to the mainstream jazz industry’s promotional apparatus.
