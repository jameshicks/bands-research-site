Anticon

Independent record label and music collective. Founded 1998, Oakland, California, USA. Discogs label ID: 1105.

Anticon began as a loose collective of underground rappers who defined a strand of left-field, literary hip-hop often called “abstract rap” or “anti-rap.” It grew into a proper label whose roster expanded to include experimental electronic, indie rock, and pop artists through the 2010s. The label’s self-description: “a label for a movement… bound by 8 brands of genre defiance.”

History

Founding & Cassette Era (1997–1999)

Anticon grew out of a network of MC/producer collectives active in the late-1990s US underground. Core founding figures included Sole (Tim Holland, real name James Timothy Holland Jr.), Dose One (Adam Drucker), Odd Nosdam (David Madson), Alias (3) (Brendon Whitney), Jel (Jeffrey Logan), DJ Mayonnaise, Moodswing9, and The Pedestrian. Slug (of Atmosphere) and Buck 65 (Richard Terfry) were early associates.

Oakland vs. Cincinnati — founding geography: The label was founded in Oakland, California. The Cincinnati association comes from two overlapping facts: (1) the 1997 Scribble Jam festival, held in Cincinnati, was where several core members first connected as a collective; (2) Odd Nosdam and Yoni Wolf (Why?) both grew up in Cincinnati, and Dose One also came from the Cincinnati area (relocating via New Jersey). Sole and Alias (3) were from Maine; Jel from Chicago; The Pedestrian from Los Angeles. After Scribble Jam, Sole was persuaded by DJ Stef to move to the Bay Area — he recalled: “She showed me an amazing time, and the reception I got through her world was, like, night and day compared to trying to break into the East Coast scene.” Sole convinced the rest to follow. The collective initially crammed into a two-and-a-half-bedroom apartment on Lester Avenue in Lake Merritt, Oakland, then moved to a warehouse in West Oakland around 1999. Wikipedia describes the label as “based in Los Angeles, California,” which likely reflects a later operational address rather than the founding location. [Source: KQED Arts, https://www.kqed.org/arts/13979349/anticon-history-doseone-jel-sole-alias-clouddead-odd-nosdam-oakland; TapeOp interview, https://tapeop.com/interviews/43/anticon]

The label’s first release was the cassette Anticon Presents: Hip-Hop Music For The Advanced Listener (1998), a compilation showcasing Sole, Dose One, Alias, Slug, Eyedea, the Sebutones, and others. A follow-up, Anticon Presents: Music For The Advancement Of Hip Hop (ABR002, 1999), was released on cassette the next year.

The breakthrough early release was the Deep Puddle Dynamics double-LP The Taste Of Rain… Why Kneel (ABR0009, 1999) — a supergroup featuring Dose One, Sole, Alias, Slug, with production from Jel, Alias, DJ Mayonnaise, Moodswing9, DJ Abilities, and A.N.T. The artwork was by Odd Nosdam. This record established the Anticon sound: dense wordplay, abstract imagery, beatmaking rooted in the SP-1200/MPC tradition but tilting toward collage.

Early Catalog & Core Roster (2000–2004)

Themselves (Dose One + Jel) debuted with Them (ABR0010, 2000) — an abstract hip-hop LP with guest appearances from Sole, The Pedestrian, Moodswing9, and Mr. Dibbs. Alias released his debut Final Act (ABR0012-1, 2001), followed by The Other Side Of The Looking Glass (abr0022, 2002) and Muted (abr0036, 2003), cementing him as the label’s leading beatmaker.

Sage Francis released Personal Journals (abr0021, 2002) — a double-LP produced by Sixtoo, Jel, Alias, Odd Nosdam, Controller 7, DJ Mayonnaise, and others — which brought the label broader indie attention and is considered one of its defining statements. Francis also put out The Makeshift Patriot EP (abr0027v, 2002). He subsequently moved to his own Strange Famous Records and Epitaph, releasing only briefly on Anticon.

Odd Nosdam released No More Wig For Ohio (abr0028, 2003), establishing his reputation as a composer of moody, cinematic instrumental hip-hop. Sole released Selling Live Water (abr0026, 2003) and Plutonium (abr0030, 2003).

Buck 65 (Richard Terfry, from Nova Scotia) appeared on the label with Man Overboard (2000) and was an early associate, though his primary catalog went through other imprints (Warner, V2).

Minneapolis multi-instrumentalist Dosh (Martin Chavez Dosh) joined with his self-titled debut (abr0032, 2003), introducing a live-instrument jazz/electronic approach that widened the label’s sound.

Cross-Genre Expansion & Peak Period (2005–2009)

The 2005–2009 period saw Anticon’s most celebrated releases.

13 & God — a transatlantic collaboration between Themselves (Dose One + Jel) and The Notwist’s Markus Acher, Micha Acher, and Martin Gretschmann (aka Console) — released their self-titled debut (abr0050, 2005, co-released on Alien Transistor). Members included Adam Drucker (Dose One), Jeffrey Logan (Jel), Dax Pierson, Jordan Dalrymple, Markus Acher, Martin Gretschmann, and Micha Acher. A second LP, Own Your Ghost (abr0104, 2011), followed.

Why? — centered on songwriter/rapper Yoni Wolf (Jonathan Avram Wolf; also credited as “Why? (9)” and alias “The Pony (2)”) alongside his brother Josiah Wolf and collaborators including Odd Nosdam and members of Fog — released Elephant Eyelash (ABR0055, 2005), a genre-dissolving blend of hip-hop cadence, indie rock, and baroque pop. Their Alopecia (ABR0080, 2008, co-released with Tomlab) is widely considered the label’s artistic high-water mark; additional performers included Dose One, Jel, Andrew Broder, and Ned Torrisi. Why? continued with Eskimo Snow (ABR0098, 2009) and Mumps, Etc. (2012) before moving to Joyful Noise Recordings.

Subtle — Dose One’s main “band” project, with members Adam Drucker, Dax Pierson, Jeffrey Logan (Jel), Jordan Dalrymple, Alexander Kort, and Marty Dowers — released via Lex Records rather than Anticon directly, but was integral to the collective’s extended family. Years active: 2002–2008.

Zach Hill (of Hella) released his avant-rock solo LP Astrological Straits (ABR0086, 2008) — a 2xLP with guests including Chino Moreno (Deftones), Les Claypool, Dean Spunt (No Age), Randy Randall (No Age), and Tyler Pope — the label’s most pronounced detour into noise-rock and math-rock territory.

Themselves released CrownsDown (ABR0096, 2009), mixed by Odd Nosdam and Yoni Wolf.

New Voices & Broadening Roster (2010–2014)

Baths (Will Wiesenfeld) debuted with Cerulean (ABR0105, 2010), an album of broken-beat electronic pop that brought the label a new following in the emerging post-dubstep/IDM-adjacent scene. Baths continued with Obsidian (ABR0138, 2013) and Romaplasm (ABR0168, 2017).

Serengeti (Chicago rapper/storyteller) released a string of records including Family&Friends (abr0111, 2011), C.A.R. (ABR0118, 2012), and the Kenny Dennis series. Son Lux (Ryan Lott) released At War With Walls And Mazes (ABR0082, 2008) and We Are Rising (abr0114, 2011).

Young Fathers (Edinburgh trio) had early releases on Anticon — Tape One (ABR0132, 2012), Tape Two (ABR0134, 2013), and Dead (abr0142, 2014, co-released with Big Dada) — before moving to Big Dada/Ninja Tune for their broader career. Their Dead won the Mercury Prize in 2014.

Tobacco (3) (Thomas Fec, of Black Moth Super Rainbow) released Fucked Up Friends (abr0089LP, 2008), Maniac Meat (ABR0103, 2010), and additional EPs on the label.

Dose One continued releasing solo work into the 2010s, including G Is For Deep (ABR0121, 2012) and a collaborative Less Is Orchestra LP with Alias (ABR0169, 2018).

Later Catalog (2015–2018)

Later releases include Deradoorian’s The Expanding Flower Planet (ABR0152, 2015), Pictureplane’s Technomancer (ABR0153, 2015), Slugabed’s Inherit The Earth (ABR0165, 2017), and the Alias / Dose One collaborative LP Less Is Orchestra (ABR0169, 2018). The label went quiet after 2018 with no major releases documented in Discogs beyond that point.
