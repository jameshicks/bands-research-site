Overview

B-Unique Records is a London-based independent label founded in 2001 by Mark Lewis and Martin Toher, operating under the parent company B-Unique Records Ltd. Its catalogue is concentrated in 2000s UK indie and alternative rock — 135 releases in that decade, drawing on a recurring pool that includes the Kaiser Chiefs (both Ricky Wilson and Nick Hodgson appear in credits alongside the band) plus producers Stephen Street and Cenzo Townshend. Output dropped sharply in the 2010s to 33 releases and had all but ceased by the 2020s, suggesting the label’s commercial moment was tightly bound to the mid-2000s indie boom.

Notes (web)

Founding

B-Unique was set up around 2000–2001 by Mark Lewis (61) and Martin Toher, both veterans of major-label A&R — Lewis from London Records (later absorbed into Warner Music Group), Toher from A&M (later merged into Mercury). A third co-founder, mixer Mike ‘Spike’ Stent, contributed equally but departed early. The label’s origin is recalled by Lewis as a pub conversation: “It started, as so many of these stories do, on pint three, in the pub.” Each of the three founders put in £10,000 of redundancy money, and the company launched from a house in Acton with just Lewis, Toher, and a third staffer, Paul Harris. (Music Business Worldwide)

Toher on the gap between major-label experience and independent reality: “After you’ve been in A&R at a major label for 10 years, you think you know a lot, but you’ve actually been incredibly cushioned from the harsh realities.” (ibid.)

The Ordinary Boys — the bridge to Kaiser Chiefs

B-Unique’s first meaningful commercial foothold came with The Ordinary Boys in 2003, signed against competition from RCA and Polydor. Lewis recalled the approach: “We were the indie punching above our weight; RCA were super keen to sign them, Polydor too. But we camped out in Brighton 24/7.” (ibid.) This signing proved pivotal because it connected the label to the band that would tip them off to Kaiser Chiefs.

How Kaiser Chiefs were signed

Kaiser Chiefs had released a self-titled album as Parva on the Mantra imprint of Beggars Banquet (the album 22, 2003, with singles “Heavy”, “Good Bad Right Wrong” and “Hessles”), and were dropped when Mantra closed. They regrouped, changed their name to Kaiser Chiefs — inspired by manager James Sandom’s connection to Leeds United and Lucas Radebe’s former South African club Kaizer Chiefs — and sought a longer-term deal with fresh material. (Wikipedia, Kaiser Chiefs article)

The connection to B-Unique came through Sam Preston of the Ordinary Boys. During a support slot where Kaiser Chiefs opened for the Ordinary Boys, Lewis did not watch the support act. Preston confronted him afterwards: “You didn’t watch the support band?! You should — they’re amazing.” Lewis attended the next night and recalled: “I’ve just watched four hits in a row.” (Music Business Worldwide)

Kaiser Chiefs chose B-Unique over an offer from Atlantic Records. Lewis noted of that decision point: “That was the first time we came up against the major labels for a signing.” His account of the label’s signing instinct: “For us, instinct is everything. We never talk ourselves into signing something — it’s usually physical.” (ibid.)

Their first release on B-Unique was “I Predict a Riot” (November 2004, BUN088-7). The debut album Employment followed in 2005 (BUN093CDX). (Wikipedia, Kaiser Chiefs article)

Was B-Unique built around the Kaiser Chiefs?

Not built around them — but the label credits the Ordinary Boys relationship and Sam Preston’s tip as the decisive intervention. Lewis has stated directly: “I don’t think B-Unique would be around today if it wasn’t for Preston.” The scale of Employment’s success exceeded internal expectations: when pre-orders came in, Toher’s response was “Oh my God. There’s only three of us; this is getting out of control.” Their original ambition for the album had been a Silver certification (60,000 UK sales). (Music Business Worldwide) Employment went on to reach No. 1 in the UK, and B-Unique released the band’s first four top-10 albums (2005–2008) in a distribution arrangement with Polydor/Universal.

The 2008 Atlantic/Warner deal

In late 2007 or early 2008 B-Unique switched its distribution and marketing arrangement from Universal/Polydor to Warner Music UK, integrating into Atlantic Records UK with effect from February 2008. The deal covered all new B-Unique signings; existing artists (Kaiser Chiefs, The Twang, The Automatic) remained contractually signed to B-Unique but continued to be licensed to Polydor/Universal under those prior agreements. As part of the arrangement B-Unique also established a management division, with Lewis and Toher working alongside Atlantic UK president Max Lousada. (Billboard snippet, search-result snippet only — article is paywalled; content corroborated by MBW.)

Post-2010 contraction — what the evidence shows

The Kaiser Chiefs’ label trajectory, 2011–2016. B-Unique remained the primary credited label on Kaiser Chiefs releases through the band’s fourth album Off With Their Heads (2008). The 2011 album The Future Is Medieval was released “on B-Unique Records, under exclusive licence to Polydor Records Ltd” for the physical UK edition — B-Unique still the rights-holder. The 2012 compilation Souvenir: The Singles 2004–2012 appeared on catalogue number LIB137CD (the Liberator imprint, a Universal catalogue vehicle), suggesting the contractual relationship was winding down. The 2014 album Education, Education, Education & War still credited B-Unique alongside Fiction Records, Caroline Records and ATO Records — B-Unique was therefore a named rights-holder on that release. By the 2016 album Stay Together (Fiction/Caroline International only) B-Unique’s name had disappeared entirely from the album credits. Kaiser Chiefs signed with Polydor directly for the 2019 album Duck. (Wikipedia, Kaiser Chiefs discography, as of 2026-11-20; Wikipedia, Education, Education, Education & War, as of 2026-11-20; Wikipedia, Stay Together (album), as of 2026-11-20)

Did losing Kaiser Chiefs cause the wind-down? The evidence suggests the relationship ended gradually and was not the primary cause of the label’s reduced activity. The MBW interview (January 2021) records Lewis and Toher attributing the post-2010 slowdown to market conditions rather than a single departure: “As that decade wore on, everyone in that [guitar] space ran out of headroom. I think we exhausted the public’s love with indie guitar acts.” Around 2010 they had noticed “the skinny-jeans-and-winklepickers second coming of UK indie guitar music was flagging.” (MBW, Jan 2021) Kaiser Chiefs’ own commercial profile was also declining by this point — The Future Is Medieval (2011) and Education, Education, Education & War (2014) were considerably less successful than Employment or Yours Truly, Angry Mob.

The publishing pivot. Rather than wind down, B-Unique diversified into music publishing, a move that began around 2010. This became the label’s primary growth area alongside reduced but continued A&R activity. (ibid.)

Kodaline and the Sony partnership. After Kaiser Chiefs’ active release period ended, B-Unique signed Irish band Kodaline, releasing their debut EP (2012), debut album In a Perfect World (2013, BUN + Sony Music Entertainment credit), and subsequent releases including Coming Up for Air (2015, B-Unique/Sony). The label’s post-Kaiser Chiefs years were therefore not a wind-down but a pivot — fewer indie guitar releases, continued operation with new acts under Sony partnership. (Wikipedia, Kodaline, as of 2026-11-20; MBW ibid.)

Summary. B-Unique’s reduced release volume after 2010 reflects the collapse of the mid-2000s UK indie guitar market rather than a single triggering event such as Kaiser Chiefs leaving. Kaiser Chiefs remained associated with B-Unique (as named rights-holder) through at least 2014; the label retained creative control across the band’s first five albums and compilation. The label was still operating as of 2020–2025 (Kodaline One Day At A Time on BUN673; Kingfishr Halcyon 2025). The 2008 switch from Universal/Polydor to Atlantic/Warner was the more structurally significant inflection point; the eventual departure of Kaiser Chiefs to Fiction/Caroline by 2016 removed the label’s highest-profile act but did not close it.
