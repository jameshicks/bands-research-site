Creeping Bent

Glasgow, Scotland independent record label. Discogs label ID: 17333. Founded by Douglas MacIntyre (Discogs: 893550). Active from 1994 (first performance event) with vinyl/CD releases from 1995. Sublabel: Singles Club (ID 448107). Website: http://www.creepingbent.net/discography.htm

Founder note: Discogs credits Douglas MacIntyre as founder. The research brief attributed founding to “Raymond McGinlay” — this name does not appear on Discogs for this label. Possible confusion with another figure or an alternate name. Needs web verification.

Overview

Creeping Bent is a Glasgow-based independent label whose catalog runs from 1995 through the present (releases as late as 2025 on Discogs). It operates as a curator of Scottish post-punk, experimental, and leftfield pop, with a strong emphasis on the lineage connecting early-80s Glasgow art-punk (Jazzateers, Article 58, Josef K) through 90s noise and indie to 2000s-era experimental folk and electronics. The label was intimately bound up with the Glasgow music scene centered on venues like the 13th Note.

Beyond releasing records, Creeping Bent explicitly used catalog numbers for non-record events: exhibitions, performances, fanzines, clothing, and DJ sets — treating the label as an organizational umbrella for a cultural scene rather than purely a discographic enterprise. Notable non-release slots include Bent 070 (2004 Fire Engines / Magic Band show) and Bent 085 (2006 Triptych event featuring Sun Ra’s Arkestra, Fire Engines, Future Pilot AKA).

Douglas MacIntyre is not only the label’s founder but also a musician — member of Article 58, Jazzateers, The Sexual Objects, Port Sulphur, and other groups. Stephen Lironi (Revolutionary Corps Of Teenage Jesus; ex-Jazzateers, Altered Images) was a key in-house producer. Mastering across the catalog was dominated by Paul McGeechan (18 releases).

Label History by Era

1994–1995 — Launch and First Releases

The label’s first catalog entry (Bent 001) was a performance/exhibition at Tramway, Glasgow, December 1994. The first vinyl releases arrived in 1995: Bent 002 (Spacehopper), Bent 004 (The Secret Goldfish’s Seasick), and the flagship Bent 005 — the Frankie Teardrop E.P. by The Revolutionary Corps Of Teenage Jesus vs. Suicide, produced by Stephen Lironi, featuring Alan Vega vocals on “Frankie Teardrop” remakes and new Alan Vega / RCTJ material.

1996–1999 — The Core Catalog

The mid-period is the heart of the Creeping Bent identity. Key activity:

  • Adventures In Stereo (11 releases, 1996–1998) — the label’s most prolific 90s act, a group built around Jim Beattie (ex-Primal Scream) and associates.
  • The Secret Goldfish became the longest-running roster artist (21 releases, 1995–2017), anchoring the label throughout its life.
  • Future Pilot A.K.A. (Sushil K. Dade) released through 1997–2007, incorporating South Asian vocals, Scottish folk, and electronics — a hallmark of the label’s eclecticism.
  • The Revolutionary Corps Of Teenage Jesus collaborated repeatedly with Alan Vega, releasing Righteous Lite™ (1999, Bent 045CD) — industrial/experimental with Vega co-writing and voicing.
  • A Singles Club sub-series (from 1997) served as sampler compilations featuring roster artists.
  • Bent 014 was a fanzine, The Erotic Urges Of Creeping Bent. Bent 017 was clothing. The label’s identity as a broader cultural project was explicit.

2000–2007 — Expansion and Experimentation

  • The Nectarine No. 9 (12 releases, 1998–2006) — featuring Gareth Sager (ex-Pop Group, Rip Rig & Panic) and David Henderson (ex-Fire Engines). Received Transgressed & Transmitted (2001, BBQCD 221) was co-released with Beggars Banquet, the label’s most prominent mainstream co-release.
  • Bill Wells and Isobel Campbell released Ghost Of Yesterday (2002, Bent 067CD), a Billie Holiday tribute — one of the label’s most critically noted releases.
  • Jazzateers archival material: Jazzateers: Rough 46 (2013, Bent 073) and Don’t Let Your Son Grow Up To Be A Cowboy (Unreleased Recordings 1981–82) (2016, Bent 061) — produced partly by Edwyn Collins, excavating the early-80s Glasgow art-pop scene that MacIntyre was part of.
  • The Sexual Objects debut Full Penetration 7” (2007, Bent 093) — MacIntyre’s own band, with David Henderson, Ian Holford, Simon Smeeton.
  • Future Pilot A.K.A.’s Secrets From The Clockhouse (2006, Bent 091CD) featured an extraordinary guest list: Stuart Murdoch, Kim Gordon, Thurston Moore, Grant McLennan, Robert Forster, Damo Suzuki, Mike Watt, Karine Polwart, Alasdair Gray.

2008–Present — Ongoing

  • Gareth Sager solo and with The Hungry Ghosts: Slack Slack Music (2009, Bent 088), Juicy Rivers (2018, Bent 090CD).
  • Port Sulphur: Compendium (2020, Bent 082).
  • The Sexual Objects continued with Cucumber (2010), Orangutang (2025, Bent 093 — catalog number reused).
  • Josef K archival release (2022) — another thread tying the label back to early-80s Glasgow post-punk.
  • Quad 90 (2025, LNFG187) — the label’s most recent Discogs entry.

Connections

  • Glasgow post-punk lineage: Creeping Bent is explicitly rooted in the early-80s Glasgow scene — Jazzateers (MacIntyre, Lironi, Skinner, Burgoyne, Band), Article 58, Josef K, Fire Engines (David Henderson). The label repeatedly issued archival material from these acts.
  • Douglas MacIntyre: Founder, producer, and bassist/vocalist in multiple label acts (Jazzateers, The Sexual Objects, Port Sulphur). The label is essentially his creative imprint.
  • Stephen Lironi: Key sonic architect; his production defines the RCTJ / Alan Vega releases. Connections to Altered Images, Article 58, Jazzateers.
  • Gareth Sager: The Pop Group / Rip Rig & Panic veteran; central to The Nectarine No. 9 and his own solo output on the label.
  • David Henderson: Ex-Fire Engines vocalist; core member of The Nectarine No. 9 and The Sexual Objects.
  • Suicide / Alan Vega: The very first release (Bent 005) paired RCTJ with Suicide. Vega returned as collaborator on several subsequent RCTJ releases. This positions Creeping Bent within a lineage from New York proto-punk as much as Scottish post-punk.
  • Edwyn Collins / Orange Juice: Collins produced Jazzateers sessions (1981–82 sides released 2016). Orange Juice and Jazzateers share overlapping personnel and scene.
  • Beggars Banquet: Co-released The Nectarine No. 9’s Received Transgressed & Transmitted (BBQCD 221, 2001) — the label’s most mainstream co-release.
  • No confirmed connection to Fire Records found in Discogs data.

Singles Club Sublabel

See full article: Singles Club (Discogs ID: 448107).

Singles Club was a Creeping Bent series of split 7” singles (with CD editions for some entries) pairing roster artists. Twelve numbered entries were confirmed (1997–2000), with #3, #4, and #7 unaccounted for in the Discogs database. The series ran alongside the main catalog using the standard Bent numbering:

Open Questions

  • Founder identity: Brief credits “Raymond McGinlay” as founder; Discogs says Douglas MacIntyre. Are these the same person? An alias? A different role (e.g., McGinlay = financial backer, MacIntyre = creative director)? Web research needed.
  • Urusei Yatsura, Lungleg, The Yummy Fur: Listed in the research brief as Creeping Bent artists. None appear in the Discogs catalog for this label (Urusei Yatsura is on ché; The Yummy Fur on Guided Missile; Lungleg returns no Discogs results). Possible association through shared scene or compilations, but not confirmed as label releases.
  • Bent 046 (“060402 / ?”) and Bent 074 (Article 58 “Echoes, Thrash & Harmony” — unreleased?): Profile notes flag these as unknown or possibly unreleased.
  • Paul McGeechan (Paw Paw mastering): Dominant mastering engineer across 18 releases — worth a People stub if researched further.
  • Sambassadeur (Swedish): Why is a Swedish indie pop act on this Glasgow label? No context in DB.
