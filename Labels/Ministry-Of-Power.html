Overview

Ministry Of Power was Test Dept.’s own imprint, launched in 1983 to give the group control over their output. The first three releases (MOP 1–3) were issued through Some Bizzare Records; the fourth, ‘Gododdin’, was put out independently before the label settled into an affiliation with Jungle Records. The catalogue divides roughly evenly across the 1980s and 1990s — seven and six releases respectively — spanning industrial, experimental, and modern classical terrain. Beyond Test Dept. themselves, the label’s credited roster includes producer Ken Thomas, composer John Eacott, and engineers Brett Turnbull and Alistair Adams.

Notes (web)

Some Bizzare departure and the Gododdin gap (1987–1990)

Test Dept.’s archive confirms A Good Night Out (1987) was “TD’s final LP for Some Bizzare.” archive.testdept.org.uk The discography notes the whole Some Bizzare relationship ran 1983–1987, with releases distributed through Phonogram / Some Bizzare.

Paul Jamrozy has spoken about the split in interviews. A search-engine snippet from his interviews (source not pinned to a single URL after checking C86 Show, KALX, ReGen Pt1 & Pt2, youngcoconutmusic.com, retrofuturista.com — none contained the passage in their fetched text) records him saying:

“To give them due credit, Some Bizzare were responsible for bringing many of the best experimental and alternative acts under one roof and were almost untouchable for a long period. […] that relationship soured, as it has with many of the other artists that worked with Some Bizarre.”

He also described the Some Bizzare signing as “not sure if it was the best move we ever made, but we got some interesting products out through them.” — snippet cited at search result for query “Test Dept Paul Jamrozy Some Bizarre A Good Night Out interview label departure reason” (2026-11-20); exact interview URL not confirmed — likely narcmagazine.com NARC DS30 feature or elgarajedefrank.es (the El Garaje interview text was truncated in the fetch). This quote needs source verification — see Research Queue.

After the Some Bizzare split, there was a one-off release on Belgium’s Sub Rosa (Terra Firma, 1988), and then Gododdin (1989) came out purely on the Ministry of Power imprint — no distributor partner. The archive describes the Gododdin project as “a collaboration with Welsh avant-garde theatre company Brith Gof” and the album was billed to both groups. The fact that Gododdin was an unusual interdisciplinary theatre/music project (staged in an abandoned Cardiff car factory, critiquing Thatcherism through a medieval Welsh poem) may account for why it was kept on the wholly independent Ministry of Power rather than placed with a new commercial partner — no source states this explicitly, however. archive.testdept.org.uk

Jungle Records affiliation (from 1990)

Jungle Records became Test Dept.’s primary distributor from 1990, handling Ministry of Power releases including Pax Britannica, Pax Americana, New World Order, Proven In Action, and Bang On It!. Jungle also compiled Legacy (1994), described as a “Jungle Records singles compilation 1990–1993.” archive.testdept.org.uk No source found that explains why Jungle was chosen or how the affiliation was arranged — no band interview or label history covers this specifically.
