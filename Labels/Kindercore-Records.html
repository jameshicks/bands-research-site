Ryan Lewis and Daniel Geller founded Kindercore Records in 1996, in the indie-scene-haven of Athens, GA. After years of ups and downs the label went dormant from 2003-2007, returned with a new roster of bands as well as various non music projects and went dormant again a few years later.

Overview

Kindercore Records was an indie label co-founded by Ryan Lewis and Daniel Geller in Athens, GA in 1996, emerging from one of America’s most fertile indie-rock scenes. The catalogue divides into two phases: a late-1990s run of 22 releases and a 2000s run of 30, with a dormancy period from 2003 to 2007 in between. Its roster centred on Athens-adjacent acts including Of Montreal and Kevin Barnes, working primarily in indie pop, indie rock, and psychedelic rock, with a lo-fi undercurrent throughout. After reviving post-2007 — with new bands and reportedly non-music projects alongside — the label went quiet again, leaving a small 2010s footprint of two releases. The sublabel Electronic Watusi Boogaloo operated under the same umbrella.

Notes (web)

Closure 2002–2003: the I.D.E.A. / Telegraph Company buyout

In 2001 founders Daniel Geller and Ryan Lewis partnered with I.D.E.A. (also known as The Telegraph Company) with the stated aim of strengthening the label’s administration. The partnership deteriorated over roughly 18 months. Per an October 2003 report at excellentonline.com, the founders lost creative control: Telegraph/I.D.E.A. signed bands they had rejected and acts they had never heard of. Both founders’ own groups — I Am The World Trade Center (Geller’s band, described as the second-biggest-selling act on Kindercore at the time) and The Agenda (Lewis’s band) — were dropped from the roster. On 12 November 2002 the founders were informed the label would close; two days later they were told it would not close but would instead be run by CEO Stanley Hartman, with Geller and Lewis fired. Geller and Lewis subsequently filed suit against I.D.E.A., The Telegraph Company, and key executives for breach of contract, slander, and libel. A legal-defence fund was launched to help offset costs; Quinn Heraty of the firm Heraty Hall represented the plaintiffs. By January 2004 the suit was described as having grown “ugly and expensive” (spinme.com snippet). Lewis and Geller eventually regained the legal rights to the Kindercore name and catalogue; the 2007 Tiny Mix Tapes revival announcement states they had “only recently” recovered those rights at that point.

The closure was therefore the result of a hostile outside-investor takeover, not distributor failure or a deliberate pause by the founders themselves. Sources checked find no evidence that a specific distribution deal (e.g. the earlier Emperor Norton arrangement) was the precipitating cause.

Sources: excellentonline.com — “You thought you’d heard the end of Kindercore?” (Oct 2003) · spinme.com — “Kindercore Lawsuit Gets Ugly, Expensive” (Jan 2004) · tinymixtapes.com — “Kindercore Records Is Back” (2007)

2007 revival and non-music projects

When the revival was announced (tinymixtapes.com, 2007), the label signed three Athens-area acts: King of Prussia, Ruby Isle, and USSR. Alongside the new music roster, the announcement listed the following non-music projects planned for the label’s website and broader output:

  • A comics anthology titled The Trouble Revolution (described as already announced at the time of the revival news)
  • Feature videos
  • Contributions from writers
  • T-shirt designs
  • Other unspecified “surprises”

No subsequent sources found that confirm which of these non-music projects were actually completed or released. The catalogue shows no releases between 2003 and 2011 (next entries: Form A Sign by Grape Soda and Maybe These Are The Breaks by Masters Of The Hemisphere, both 2011), suggesting the second dormancy set in before much of the announced output materialised.

Source: tinymixtapes.com — “Kindercore Records Is Back; Loves You More Than Your Mom & Dad” (2007)
