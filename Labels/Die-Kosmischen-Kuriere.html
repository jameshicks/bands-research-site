Overview

Die Kosmischen Kuriere was a West German krautrock label founded by Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser as the direct successor to Ohr, issuing only three original LPs (catalogue numbers KK 58001–KK 58003) before Kaiser renamed it Kosmische Musik, marketed in France as Cosmic Music. Throughout its brief original run the label was distributed by Metronome. Its roster drew on the same scene as Ohr — Dieter Dierks, Harald Grosskopf, Jürgen Dollase, and Gille Lettmann among them — covering krautrock, space rock, and psychedelic territory. The 1990s surge in Discogs-linked credits reflects the wave of Pilz/Ohr/Kosmische Musik reissues carrying the original label design, overseen by Bernhard Mikulski.

Notes (web) — Rename to Kosmische Musik

Short answer: No source gives an explicit stated reason. The closest the available record comes is a structural/ownership change: on 18 May 1973 Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser and Gille Lettmann bought out co-founder Peter Meisel’s shares in OHR Musik Produktion GmbH. The Eurock profile of the label notes that this buyout “prepared the start of a new sound,” and the rename to Kosmische Musik follows immediately after, suggesting it was at minimum a fresh-start rebranding tied to full ownership passing to Kaiser and Lettmann. https://www.eurock.com/Display.aspx?Content=CosmicCouriers.aspx

Edgar Froese / naming dispute — nature and timing (web research 2026-11-07):

The German-language Wikipedia article on Kosmische Musik states: “Edgar Froese verweigerte Kaiser die Verwendung des Namens Kosmische Musik, an dem er die Rechte beanspruchte.” (“Edgar Froese refused Kaiser permission to use the name Kosmische Musik, claiming rights to it.”) https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosmische_Musik (as of 2026-11-07; articles drift). The studioinnocenti blog (a secondary narrative source) repeats the same claim with the framing that Froese “denied him the use of the name ‘Cosmic Music’ where he claims the paternity rights.” https://studioinnocenti.blogspot.com/2009/01/rolf-ulrich-kaiser-prophet-of-kosmische.html

Formal or informal? No source consulted — German Wikipedia, English Wikipedia (Kosmische, Edgar Froese, Cosmic Couriers, Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser, Tangerine Dream), studioinnocenti, janreetze, eurock — describes this as a formal legal challenge (Klage, Abmahnung, lawsuit, cease-and-desist, or trademark filing). The language used is consistently “verweigerte” (refused/withheld) and “beanspruchte die Rechte” / “claimed paternity rights” — the vocabulary of an informal assertion of originator status rather than a court action. Froese’s basis for the claim was that he coined the term “Kosmische Musik” in 1971, which English Wikipedia corroborates: “The term ‘kosmische Musik’ was coined in 1971 by Edgar Froese and later used by record producer Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser as a marketing term.” https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosmische_Musik The Jan Reetze blog post, which covers the broader Kaiser/Lettmann story in detail, does not mention the naming dispute at all. https://janreetze.blogspot.com/2011/05/rolf-ulrich-kaiser-gille-lettmann_1460.html

Timing relative to the 1973 rename: No source gives a specific date for Froese’s refusal. German Wikipedia places it in the context of the broader mid-1970s disputes (multiple artists suing Ohr/Kosmische over unpaid royalties and the Cosmic Jokers scandal, 1973–75). Kaiser adopted the name “Kosmische Musik” for his label after the three Kosmischen Kuriere releases in 1973; Froese’s objection is described as a consequence of that adoption, not as a prior constraint that prevented it. The label operated under the Kosmische Musik name until approximately 1975, so the refusal evidently did not result in an immediate name change — Kaiser continued using the name. This is consistent with the dispute being an informal claimed-rights objection rather than a legally enforceable challenge.

Conclusion: The available evidence supports characterising the Froese objection as an informal assertion of originator/paternity rights, not a formal legal challenge. It occurred after Kaiser had already renamed the label to Kosmische Musik (post-three-release rename in 1973), and it did not compel a further name change. No primary source (court record, contemporary press article, interview with Froese or Kaiser directly addressing this) has been located. The German Wikipedia claim remains the sole named source; the studioinnocenti blog appears to derive from it. The eclipsed.de forum thread on this topic (https://forum.eclipsed.de/threads/rolf-ulrich-kaiser-gille-lettmann-die-story-der-kosmischen-kuriere.596/) could not be retrieved (access error) and may contain further detail.

What the sources rule out: None of the sources consulted — Wikipedia (en/de), Eurock, Coulthart/feuilleton, cvinyl.com, the Rough Trade snippet — describe the rename as driven by a legal dispute over “Die Kosmischen Kuriere” itself, nor as a deliberate artistic-manifesto shift. The artistic coherence argument (the label’s later identity as Kosmische Musik being the more programmatic, genre-defining name) is a plausible inference but nothing in print attributes it to Kaiser or Lettmann as intention.

Legal troubles that ended the label are a separate matter: Kaiser’s release of recordings as The Cosmic Jokers without participating musicians’ consent led to West German courts voiding Kaiser’s contracts, ending the label around 1975. Klaus Schulze was a primary complainant. This is documented at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_Couriers and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cosmic_Jokers_(album).

Sources checked and returning nothing on the rename reason: English Wikipedia (Cosmic Couriers, Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser, Ohr record label, The Cosmic Jokers album); cvinyl.com label guide; johncoulthart.com/feuilleton 2024-07-08; Eurock Kaiser profile; eclipsed.de forum thread (503 error, could not retrieve). The WDR-Kaiser PDF (dneitzer.home.ktk.de) was unreadable binary in this context.

Conclusion: The most defensible single cause is the May 1973 buyout of Peter Meisel’s shares, which gave Kaiser and Lettmann full control and appears to have prompted a clean-break rebrand. No primary source (interview, press release, contemporary article) has been located that states Kaiser’s own reason. The Froese trademark objection — if the German Wikipedia claim is accurate — would be a consequence of the rename, not its cause.
