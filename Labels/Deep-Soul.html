Deep Soul

New York-registered sublabel of Nervous Records, operated 1998–2000. Discogs label ID: 2400.

Run jointly by Byron Stingily, Mike Dunn, and Mike Weiss (Nervous Records founder) as an executive-producer triumvirate. All releases manufactured and distributed by Nervous, Inc. and carry the “NE-DS” catalog prefix. The label served as a vehicle for Chicago house projects — many tied to Stingily and Dunn’s orbit — released through the New York infrastructure of Nervous.

Background

Deep Soul operated as one of roughly twenty sublabels Nervous Records maintained across its history (alongside Wreck Records, Sorted Records, Strapped Records, and others). The label was not purely a Chicago operation: Nervous Records was a New York house and garage institution, and its parent company Nervous, Inc. handled all manufacturing and distribution. What Deep Soul brought was the specific sensibility of Stingily and Dunn — deep, soulful Chicago house rooted in gospel-inflected vocal traditions, raw drum machine aesthetics, and live instrumentation.

Byron Stingily was at this point a Nervous mainstay: his debut solo single “Don’t Fall In Love” appeared on Nervous in 1995, and his album The Purist (Manifesto/Nervous, 1997) was the centerpiece of his solo career to that point. Mike Dunn — who records under numerous aliases including MD III, MDZ, The MD X-Spress, and The Disco Kidz — had been a Chicago house producer since the 1980s. The two had collaborated previously (Dunn produced “I’m Wit’cha Baby,” the B-side of Stingily’s 1995 debut). Mike Weiss provided the label infrastructure and executive oversight.

The label was active for approximately three years, with its catalog running from 1998 through 2000. No further releases appear after catalog number NE-DS 20445.

Key Releases

”Return Of Tha Lost Soul (In Memory Of A Friend)” — Mike Dunn (NE-DS 20331, 1998)

The label’s most personally weighted release. Mike Dunn’s memorial dedication to a friend, built around his signature deep groove. The track features live flute from Paul Mertens and trumpet (credited as Max Crawford) from Dave Max Crawford on side B — unusual for a pure house production. Additional keyboards from Rob “Diggy” Morrison. All three executive producers (Stingily, Dunn, Weiss) are credited.

”Reaching For The Sky” — Deus (2) (NE-DS 20347, 1999)

The label’s most elaborately produced track. Produced by Maurice Joshua (Chicago house veteran, one of the authors of “This Is Acid”), with string arrangements co-credited to Joshua and Ron Carroll. Backing vocals by Carroll and Kim English. Personnel includes additional keyboards from Rob Morrison and Tim Gant, and assistant engineering from Stevie J. The cover of Peabo Bryson’s composition gives the label its most gospel-adjacent moment.

”Let It Go” — Soul Brothers (6) featuring Joi Cardwell (NE-DS 20445, 2000)

The label’s final (or near-final) original release and the most widely distributed: a 2009 UK repress confirms ongoing catalogue licensing. Joi Cardwell — a New York vocalist with an extensive Nervous Records history — provides the vocal feature. Written by Dunn, Stingily, Cardwell, and Bill Dickens, with all instruments except guitar played by Dickens and guitar by Keith Henderson. Produced by the Stingily/Dunn team. Mastered by Duncan Stanbury. Soul Brothers (6) is a group entity that includes both Stingily and Dunn.

Scene Context

Deep Soul sat at the crossroads of Chicago house and New York underground garage/house. Its operator team was itself cross-city: Dunn and Stingily were Chicago figures; Weiss ran a New York institution. The label’s catalog reflects this: “Reaching For The Sky” brings in Chicago veterans Maurice Joshua and Ron Carroll; “Let It Go” draws on NYC vocalist Joi Cardwell; “Return Of Tha Lost Soul” is pure Chicago Dunn aesthetic.

The Nervous Records ecosystem in the late 1990s included a cluster of sublabels aimed at distinct micro-audiences: Wreck Records for hip-hop, Sorted Records for harder club sounds, and Deep Soul for soulful house. The “NE-DS” catalog prefix tied releases to the Nervous catalogue while signaling their distinct identity. The label’s short lifespan (three years, roughly eight original masters) is consistent with Nervous’s approach of launching targeted sublabel vehicles and winding them down as market conditions changed.

Maurice Joshua’s production of NE-DS 20347 is the label’s most explicit tie to the original Chicago acid house lineage — Joshua co-wrote “This Is Acid” (1987) and was among the first wave of Chicago house producers. His appearance on a 1999 Nervous sublabel underscores the ongoing New York/Chicago personnel traffic through the late-1990s soulful house world.

Mike Dunn’s alias The Disco Kidz (NE-DS 20349) and the Soul Brothers (6) group vehicle (NE-DS 20445, shared with Stingily) show the label also functioning as an alias-project outlet for its own operators.
