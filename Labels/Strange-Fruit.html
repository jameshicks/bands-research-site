Strange Fruit

Discogs Label ID: 1567
Parent label: Strange Fruit Records Ltd. (ID: 347359)
Sublabels: Nighttracks (ID: 7335), Raw Fruit Records (ID: 72668), Strange Roots (ID: 100082), The Peel Sessions (ID: 313331)
Active: 1986–2004
Release count: ~1,070 releases in Discogs DB

Overview

Strange Fruit was an independent UK label established in 1986 by Clive Selwood and John Peel, operating as the primary commercial outlet for authorized BBC session recordings — principally John Peel’s Radio 1 Peel Sessions, which had been broadcast since 1967 but never systematically released. The label was a subsidiary imprint of Strange Fruit Records Ltd. (ID: 347359), the company entity. The name was drawn from the song written by Abel Meeropol and made famous by Billie Holiday.

The label’s first release was New Order’s 1982 Peel Session (SFPS001, July 1986), followed by sessions from the core punk, post-punk, and new wave acts who had been BBC regulars in the late 1970s and early 1980s. By design, revenue from major-name releases (Joy Division, The Smiths, The Cure, Siouxsie & The Banshees) cross-subsidized releases by less commercially viable acts — a curatorial philosophy that made Strange Fruit an unusually broad archive. The label released recordings dating from the 1960s (Jimi Hendrix, Bonzo Dog Doo-Dah Band, T. Rex) through to the early 2000s. It was part of the Zomba Group of companies and closed in 2004 when Zomba merged with BMG. The final release was a compilation of New Order’s complete Peel Sessions — bookending the label’s run symmetrically. John Peel died in October 2004, six months later. Clive Selwood died in June 2020.

Per Wikipedia, Strange Fruit had sufficient commercial success to spawn four subsidiary labels covering Evening Show sessions (Nighttracks), BBC concert recordings (Raw Fruit Records / Friday Rock Show), world and roots music sessions (Strange Roots), and a later catalogue series (The Peel Sessions).

BBC Personnel & Producers

The credit summary across the label’s ~1,010 tracked releases reveals a consistent core of BBC Radio production staff:

Producers (most frequently credited):

  • Dale Griffin — 244 releases; top BBC producer across the label’s catalog; was the Mott the Hoople drummer before becoming John Peel’s in-house producer
  • Tony Wilson (BBC) — 178 releases (distinct from Factory Records’ Anthony H. Wilson; this is BBC producer Tony Wilson)
  • Bob Sargeant — 103 releases; produced sessions across punk, post-punk, and indie acts
  • John Walters — 75 releases; Peel’s long-serving producer and show co-creator
  • Malcolm Brown — 65 releases
  • Jeff Griffin — 36 releases
  • Mark Radcliffe — 32 releases (later BBC Radio 2 presenter)
  • Pete Ritzema — 32 releases

Session engineers (most frequently credited):

  • Mike Robinson — 104 releases (S.M. / session manager credits)
  • Dave Dade — 94 releases
  • Don Walker — 74 releases (mastering/remastering)
  • Mike Engles — 72 releases
  • Nick Gomm — 51 releases

Design: Wyke Studios handled sleeve design on approximately 160 releases, making them the dominant visual identity of the label’s physical catalog. Peter Saville / Peter Saville Associates contributed designs to ~12 releases — notably for Joy Division and New Order sessions.

Nighttracks detail

Nighttracks (SFNT prefix) covered sessions recorded for the Radio 1 Evening Show hosted principally by Janice Long. Notable releases: Thompson Twins The First Session (SFNT005, 1987), The Mighty Lemon Drops The Janice Long Session (SFNT004, 1987), Danielle Dax The Janice Long Session (SFNT006, 1988), Close Lobsters The Janice Long Session (SFNT008, 1988), XTC Drums and Wireless (CDNT008, 1994), The Mission Salad Daze (CDNT005, 1994), Julian Cope Floored Genius 2 (CDNT003, 1993), The Chameleons The Radio 1 Evening Show Sessions (CDNT001, 1993).

Raw Fruit Records detail

Raw Fruit (FRS prefix) specialized in BBC Friday Rock Show and Reading Festival concert recordings. Key releases: Jethro Tull Live At Hammersmith ‘84 (FRSCD004, 1990), Hawkwind The Friday Rock Show Sessions (FRSCD005, 1992), Diamond Head The Friday Rock Show Sessions (FRSCD006, 1992).

Roster Highlights

Strange Fruit’s catalog spans genre boundaries unusually broadly for a single label, owing to Peel’s own eclectic taste. The top artists by Discogs release count (all formats/pressings):

Post-punk / New Wave core: New Order (58), Joy Division (41), Siouxsie & The Banshees (23), The Damned (23), The Cure (22), The Wedding Present (21), Gang of Four (16), Stiff Little Fingers (16), Buzzcocks (14), Wire (12), The Smiths (12), The Fall (9)

Connections

  • John Peel — co-founder; the sessions are recordings from his BBC Radio 1 show
  • Clive Selwood — co-founder; ran the label until its 2004 closure
  • Dale Griffin — lead BBC session producer across the catalog; formerly of Mott the Hoople
  • John Walters — Peel’s long-term BBC producer; credited on 75+ Strange Fruit releases
  • Bob Sargeant — BBC producer; 103 releases
  • Andy Kershaw — BBC Radio 1 presenter; launched Strange Roots sublabel 1994
  • Factory Records — several Factory artists (Joy Division, New Order, A Certain Ratio) had significant Strange Fruit releases; Tony Wilson (BBC) credits not to be confused with Factory’s Anthony H. Wilson
  • Dutch East India Trading — US distributor for many Strange Fruit releases in the early 1990s (DEI catalog numbers on US pressings)
  • Peter Saville — designed several Joy Division and New Order sessions releases
  • John Peel — the broader topic page covering Peel’s career and influence

  • Strange Roots sublabel (ID: 100082) shows only 3 releases in Discogs despite Wikipedia describing a larger catalog of Andy Kershaw world music sessions — confirm whether additional releases exist under different catalog schemes or label attributions.
