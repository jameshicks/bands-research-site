Overview

Adventures In Reality Recordings was a UK Industrial and Experimental imprint based in Coventry, founded by Alan Rider in 1981 as an offshoot of the A.I.R. fanzine he ran. The label’s catalogue was concentrated in the early-to-mid 1980s — nine releases that decade — before going dormant around 1985 following the split of Rider’s duo Stress (2), after which Rider moved on to the project Dance Naked. The label was revived briefly in the early 1990s and a small number of further releases appeared in the 2020s. Its roster drew from the Coventry post-industrial underground and included Attrition, Audio Leter, and Bubnjar Bob Oliver alongside Rider’s own projects.

Attrition connection — how the label relationship came about

The Attrition–Adventures In Reality Recordings relationship was rooted in a pre-existing personal network, not a cold fanzine pitch. Alan Rider had been a writer for Attrition frontman Martin Bowes’s own Coventry fanzine Alternative Sounds before launching Adventures In Reality. Bowes later described Rider’s zine approvingly — “Alan started as a writer for Alternative Sounds and then started his own zine… ‘Adventures in Reality’ was a great zine too!” — and noted how he loved seeing writers from his own publication launch their own projects. blankexpressionzine.wordpress.com, 2017-07-03

Beyond the fanzine overlap, Rider was directly embedded in Attrition’s live operations in the early 1980s: he served as the band’s lighting and films director, accompanying their performances with an array of slide and cine projectors, and occasionally performing on stage with them. Cassette Culture Podcast, “Post-Punk Publishing, with Attrition and Adventures In Reality”; post-punk.com Dance Naked interview with Alan Rider

Rider himself called Attrition “the Adventures In Reality house band,” and described the record/tape label as having grown partly out of his own direct involvement with bands like Attrition. The label released several of the band’s earliest recordings: Fear / Devoid (ARR 002, 1982), a split with Audio Leter (ARR0012, 1983), and Death House (ARR 11, 1983). Torn Light Records, “Essential But Disposable”: Alan Rider’s Adventures in Reality & Zines in Coventry, UK

The joint academic interview “Totally inspired by punk” (Martin Bowes and Alan Rider, Punk & Post-Punk journal, December 2022) addresses their shared Coventry history and the mutual role of DIY zine and tape culture in binding the two together, but is behind a paywall and could not be retrieved. intellectdiscover.com

Summary: The mechanism was a direct personal connection pre-dating the label. Rider was a scene insider who had written for Bowes’s fanzine, worked Attrition’s live shows, and was deeply embedded in the band’s early operations. The label releases followed naturally from that proximity rather than from any formal or arms-length A&R process.
