Acéphale

Discogs ID: 133287
Origin: New York, New York
Active: 2008–2015 (approx.)
Total releases: ~75 (Discogs)

New York independent label. Founded 2008; active through approximately 2015. No parent or sublabels. The operator is not named in any Discogs entry. The label’s catalog spans hypnagogic pop, chillwave, dream-pop, witch house, and lo-fi electronic music — a remarkably coherent snapshot of the American and international underground from 2008–2014. Named for the French word “acéphale” (headless), likely referencing Georges Bataille’s secret society of the same name. Operated by Patrik (surname possibly North) — identified by name in a 2013 Pure X interview (Nate Grace: “A big reason why we decided to work with Patrik [from Acephale] is because he takes that stuff just as serious as we do”); the Acéphale SoundCloud account is registered under “Patrick North” (New York). A Patrick North later appears as US Label Head at XL Recordings — the connection has not been formally confirmed in press.

History

Founding and the 2008–2009 Class

The founding release — ACE001 — was SALEM (6)‘s Yes I Smoke Crack (2008). SALEM (6) (Heather Marlatt, Jack Donoghue, John Holland (5) from Michigan) is retroactively identified as one of the originators of witch house; Acéphale’s decision to lead with this release placed the label at the edge of that nascent scene before the genre had a name.

The 2009 wave established the label’s broader shape:

  • ACE002: CFCF (Michael Silver, Montreal) — You Hear Colors / Invitation To Love — 12 pressings, the label’s highest-pressure single; design by Rory Them Finest
  • ACE003: Air France (Joel Karlsson + Henrik Markstedt, Sweden) — No Way Down / On Trade Winds
  • ACE004: Memory Cassette (Dayve Hawk) — Call & Response — co-released with Swedish label Sincerely Yours (YOURS0111); design by Rory Them Finest, artwork by Scott Eastwood
  • ACE005: Memory Tapes (Dayve Hawk) — Seek Magic — US pressing (UK on Something In Construction); art direction and design by Them Finest, cover art by Tomory Dodge
  • ACE006: Midnight Juggernauts (Australia) — This New Technology

The presence of Memory Cassette and Memory Tapes as two separate Acéphale entries reflects Dayve Hawk’s prolific output during 2009; both aliases are the same person (real name Dayve Hawk; also records as Weird Tapes).

Final Releases (2014–2015)

ACE029 / ACE031: Doss (2) (Aimee Bowen) — Doss EP and The Way I Feel (2014). Doss was Acéphale’s last significant act; her 5 releases mark the label’s final creative phase before apparent dormancy by 2015. Alias: Talk To Animals.

Null (11) — 1 release, 2015. Final Discogs entry.

Design Identity

Them Finest (Rory; Discogs ID 1845222, URL themfinest.com, Twitter @RoryThemFinest) — 19 design/art direction credits across the catalog; the label’s primary visual identity. Also credited on specific releases as “Rory Them Finest” (Discogs ID 2228564). Korallreven (Daniel Tjäder) also carried 6 design credits and was closely integrated into the label’s production aesthetic.

Swedish Axis

A disproportionate share of the label’s infrastructure is Swedish: Korallreven (Daniel Tjäder) is Swedish; Air France are Swedish (Joel Karlsson + Henrik Markstedt); ACE004 was co-released with Sincerely Yours (Swedish label); mastering engineer Anders Peterson (20 credits) and mixer Mathias Oldén (4 credits) are Swedish; mastering engineer Andreas Tilliander (2 credits) is Swedish. Whether this reflects the label operator’s personal connections or Korallreven’s role as label anchor is unclear.

Scene Context

Acéphale’s catalog is a compressed document of the “hypnagogic pop” / chillwave moment: the convergence of lo-fi bedroom recording, internet distribution, and a renewed interest in 1980s synth-pop textures that crested around 2009–2011. The founding SALEM release anticipates witch house; the Memory Cassette/Tapes cluster is central chillwave; Korallreven and Air France represent the Scandinavian dream-pop adjacent to that world; Pure X and Porcelain Raft pull toward guitar-based shoegaze and slowcore; How To Dress Well introduced R&B-influenced vocals and academic affect into the mix.

Forest Swords (Matthew Barnes) appears as a writer on Total Loss (How To Dress Well, 2012) — he co-wrote track 2 — placing him in the Acéphale orbit unexpectedly. Ezekiel Honig co-wrote track 1. Both are producers in their own right on other labels.

Timothy Stollenwerk’s mastering credits here connect Acéphale to the Woodsist and Grouper worlds — a recurring node across seemingly unrelated scenes.
