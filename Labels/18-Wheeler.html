Overview

18 Wheeler was an independent label based in Dunellen, NJ, operated by Tom Scharpling in the early 1990s. Its small catalogue of two releases from 1992 spans lo-fi, indie rock, and alternative rock. The roster is notable for drawing in key figures of the East Coast indie underground: Dump (James McNew of Yo La Tengo), Mac McCaughan, and Portastatic (McCaughan’s solo project), suggesting Scharpling had meaningful connections into the Chapel Hill and NYC indie scenes despite operating out of a small New Jersey town.

Notes (web)

Scharpling’s route into the Chapel Hill / NYC indie network

18 Wheeler began as a fanzine Scharpling published out of Dunellen, NJ before he spun it into a record label. Multiple sources confirm the fanzine-to-label trajectory: he described it as writing “about music and do stuff in a funny way,” produced at an offset printer, initially mostly himself writing under different names. (stereogum.com, search snippet, 2021)

Mac McCaughan / Portastatic. Scharpling interviewed McCaughan for the 18 Wheeler fanzine — his own words in a 2014 Line of Best Fit feature: “I interviewed Mac for my fanzine 18 Wheeler.” (thelineofbestfit.com, accessed 2026-11-21) The fanzine relationship preceded the label deal. The Indy Week feature on Portastatic adds that Scharpling was by then a WFMU DJ who suggested McCaughan release the home recordings he had been accumulating outside of Superchunk, and was “willing to release the material” on 18 Wheeler. McCaughan supplied three songs; the label put them out as the “Starter” 7” in 1993, the first Portastatic release. (indyweek.com, accessed 2026-11-21) A second Portastatic single (Scrapbook X’s) followed on 18 Wheeler in 1995 per the Wikipedia Portastatic article. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portastatic, as of 2026-11-21)

Jon Wurster link. Scharpling met Superchunk drummer Jon Wurster at a My Bloody Valentine show at the Ritz in New York City in 1992, at which point Scharpling had his 18 Wheeler fanzine. (Stereogum search snippet, 2021) Scharpling described McCaughan and Superchunk as “true peers” he could befriend, confirming the friendship ran alongside and beyond the label relationship.

James McNew / Dump. A TapeOp interview with McNew (issue 8) provides a direct account of the 18 Wheeler connection. McNew describes Scharpling as a pre-existing friend: “I put out a single before the first CD that came out on the 18 Wheeler label in 1992. I’d sorta been using the 4 track for a long time and my friend Tom, who runs the label, suggested putting out a single.” (tapeop.com/interviews/8/dump, accessed 2026-11-22) This establishes: (1) Scharpling and McNew were already friends before the release; (2) it was Scharpling who suggested the single — McNew did not approach the label cold. The interview does not explain how the two originally met. The first Dump release (self-titled 7”, 1992) appeared as catalogue EWR 001, predating the first Portastatic release by a year; a second Dump EP followed in 1994. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dump_(band), as of 2026-11-21) Scharpling later directed music videos for Yo La Tengo, indicating the relationship was durable. (Stereogum search snippet, 2021) How the friendship originally formed — whether through WFMU, the NJ/Hoboken scene, the fanzine world, or another route — is not stated in any source found.
