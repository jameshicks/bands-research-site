Label founded by Gary Levermore of Third Mind Records in 1983 but taken over by Grim Humour fanzine’s Richard Johnson (4) (aka Richo) after the first release.

Dedicated to reflecting many areas of underground music it has, over the years, released everything from noise-rock, post-punk, Japanese noise, improv, abstract electronics, so-called ‘outsider’ music and beyond. With its DIY sensibility it is still going strong and operates alongside Richo’s other labels, Lumberton Trading Company and Winter Hill Recordings, his occasional Adverse Effect journal and his groups, Splintered and Theme. Fourth Dimension has also started publishing books.

— Discogs label profile, ID 21689

Overview

Fourth Dimension Records was founded in 1983 by Gary Levermore of Third Mind Records, but passed to Richard Johnson — known as Richo, editor of the Grim Humour fanzine — after a single release, and has been his imprint ever since. The label charts a consistent course through the underground: noise-rock, post-punk, Japanese noise, free improv, abstract electronics, and outsider music, with a DIY ethos that has kept it active across four decades. The catalogue was sparse in the 1980s and 1990s (around 14 releases) and nearly silent in the 2000s (just 2), but surged sharply from the 2010s onward — 16 releases that decade and 21 in the 2020s alone. It sits at the centre of a small network of Richo’s ventures: the sublabels Lumberton Trading Company and Winter Hill Recordings, the Adverse Effect journal, and his groups Splintered and Theme; Fourth Dimension has also branched into book publishing.

Notes (web)

Levermore–Johnson handover

The handover was an organic exit rather than a planned succession or a falling-out. According to Richard Johnson in a QRD magazine interview (November 2010), the second Fourth Dimension release — the first with catalogue number FDS01, a flexi disc — was a direct collaboration: Levermore helped Johnson produce a flexi disc for the Grim Humour fanzine. When that release was complete, Levermore told Johnson he would not be continuing with Fourth Dimension after it. Johnson’s own account: “When Gary told me he wouldn’t be operating FD after this second release, it just made sense for me to continue with the name.” (QRD #45, silbermedia.com/qrd/archives/45fourth.html, November 2010 — URL times out but quote reproduced in multiple search-result snippets citing this source directly.)

The Fourth Dimension blog (fourthdimensionrecords.blogspot.com/2017/06/fourth-dimension-records.html, as of 2026-11-21) confirms the same framework: “Gary was responsible for the first two releases only, however, before Richard Johnson (who had started to publish Grim Humour fanzine) took over.” No reason for Levermore’s departure is given on the blog.

What this establishes: Levermore stepped back voluntarily and unprompted after the second release; Johnson was already embedded in the label’s work via the Grim Humour flexi collaboration and continued under the same name as a natural extension of that involvement. No source names a reason for Levermore’s exit (commercial decision, loss of interest, focus on Third Mind, or other).

Note on release count: The QRD snippet says “two releases before Richard Johnson took over,” but the Discogs profile says “after the first release.” The blog confirms two releases. The first Discogs catalogue entry in the database is FDS02 (Persian Flowers, 1984), which suggests FDS01 — the Grim Humour flexi — may be unindexed or mislabelled on Discogs. The two-release account from Johnson’s own interview is more authoritative here.

Late-1990s dormancy and 2000s return

No source names an explicit reason for the label going dormant. What the available evidence establishes is a cluster of circumstances that coincide with the gap.

Dormancy context — Splintered’s end: The label’s near-silence from 1998 to 2006 correlates directly with the collapse of Johnson’s main band. Splintered ground to a halt shortly after a European tour in late 1997; the last Fourth Dimension release before the gap was Splintered’s Noumena (FDCD60, 1998). With the band that had anchored much of the label’s output gone, Johnson’s energy appears to have shifted. No source states this was the reason, but the timing is exact. (industrialart.eu/en/artists/splintered-uk-2/, fetched 2026-11-21; peel.fandom.com/wiki/Splintered — snippet only, weak source)

Formation of Theme: Following Splintered’s dissolution, Johnson formed Theme with Stuart Carter and Hassni Malik in 2000. Theme is described as Johnson’s principal post-Splintered creative outlet. The label’s Wikipedia article specifically names Theme as one of the acts that marked the relaunch — suggesting Theme’s activity and Fourth Dimension’s return were intertwined rather than the label reviving to release Theme. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Dimension_Records, as of 2026-11-21)

Return context — relocation and new connections: Johnson’s personal blog (richo-richardjohnson.blogspot.com, fetched 2026-11-21) notes he moved from Canterbury, UK to Kraków, Poland in late 2005 “indirectly, all because of a girl.” The first post-gap release, Circle’s Arkades (FDLP67), appeared in 2006 — Circle are a Finnish band, and the European connection is suggestive, though no source links the relocation to the relaunch directly. The blog’s January 2010 posts describe Johnson actively relaunching the label’s online presence, consistent with the surge in releases from 2010 onward.

What no source confirms: Why precisely the label went silent (personal circumstances, lack of suitable material, financial, other), and what specifically triggered the return in 2006. The QRD #45 and #72 interviews have now been retrieved in full from Wayback Machine snapshots (see below) and neither addresses this question.

QRD #45 and #72 — full interview content confirmed via Wayback Machine (2026-11-22):

Both QRD interviews were retrieved via archived snapshots and read in full:

  • QRD #45 (November 2010), archived at web.archive.org/web/20111114165438/http://www.silbermedia.com/qrd/archives/45fourth.html: The interview covers label origins, funding (Johnson worked at the University of Kent in Canterbury from 1983 to 2005 and used surplus wages), motivations, artist relations, demos policy, and distribution. It does not mention the late-1990s dormancy, Splintered’s dissolution, or what prompted Fourth Dimension’s return. The only time-gap reference is the mention that Johnson “moved from England to Krakow, Poland” at some point, described as having no effect on operations.

  • QRD #72 (April 2015), archived at web.archive.org/web/20230522061553/http://www.silbermedia.com/qrd/archives/72fourthd.html: A follow-up interview focused entirely on the contemporary landscape — digital distribution, Spotify, record stores closing, crowdfunding (Johnson notes he launched an Indiegogo campaign with limited success), social media. No mention of the dormancy period, the gap years, or the relaunch.

Conclusion: Neither interview contains Johnson’s account of the dormancy or return. The gap period is not addressed in either QRD piece. The question cannot be answered from these sources.
