Overview

Setanta is a London-based independent label, parent entity Setanta Records Ltd., that built its identity around Edwyn Collins and a cluster of Irish-expat acts — The Divine Comedy, The Frank and Walters, A House, and The Harvest Ministers. Its catalogue of 153 releases peaks sharply in the 1990s (86 releases) in styles running from indie rock and Britpop to indie pop and alternative rock, with a smaller tail into the 2000s and 2010s. The label also ran a short-lived US office from 1995 to 1998, based first at Bar/None Records in Hoboken, NJ, then in Manhattan’s East Village. It is connected to the related copyright and publishing entities Setanta Records, Setanta Songs, and Setanta Songs Ltd.

Notes (web)

Keith Cullen’s motivation for signing Irish-expat acts

Cullen gave the clearest statement of his reasoning in an interview with Melody Maker (date not recorded in the source):

“I started because there were loads of talented bands in Ireland who had nowhere to go. The Irish labels wouldn’t know a talented band if it kicked them in the teeth, and the major labels over here tend to pick up on anything that gets a buzz.”

Source: Keith Cullen (Setanta Records) on The Frank and Walters (paulmcdermott.ie, retrieved 2026-10-23), quoting a Melody Maker interview.

The drive was therefore diagnostic: he perceived a specific market failure — Irish independent labels blind to their own talent, London majors only chasing pre-buzzed acts — and filled the gap himself. Running a record label had been his “only ambition” since his mid-teens (Keith Cullen (author), Wikipedia). He had moved to London in 1985, squatted in Camberwell, and worked as a bicycle courier before the label’s first release in 1989; the proximity to newly arriving Irish acts in south London was circumstantial but structurally important.

The focus on Irish acts was not framed as ethnic solidarity or diaspora identity but as a talent-supply argument: quality bands existed in Ireland, the domestic infrastructure was inadequate, and Cullen positioned Setanta as the outlet they lacked. He later broadened the roster to British and American acts, and by the time of the label’s closure he reportedly stopped signing Irish artists altogether, expressing frustration at what he described (in a late Irish Times interview) as their “apathy.” Source: One last spin for Setanta Records (Irish Times, retrieved 2026-10-23).

The paulmcdermott.ie interview also notes that Cullen did not sign The Frank and Walters because they were Irish specifically — he attributed their appeal to individual qualities (distinctive style, humour, personality) rather than nationality.

Sources checked, nothing further found on this question: M. G. Leonard retrospective (mgleonard.com), The Blackpool Sentinel piece on Brian and Setanta (theblackpoolsentinel.com), Hot Press “Home truths from abroad” (hotpress.com) — none contained Cullen quotes on this topic.
