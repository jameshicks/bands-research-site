From Discogs

British recording studio and cutting facility, opened in October 1976, located in London, UK. Owned by Phil Wainman, with original manager John Mackswith (formerly of Lansdowne Studios).

The studio originally consisted of a main recording studio and a cutting room (cutting room completed in January 1977), with a mix room and second studio being added later. The rooms were all designed by Eastlake’s Tom Hidley, except for Studio 2 which was too small for a full Eastlake treatment. Before Utopia Studios, the first studio to set-up in Spencer Court was Sound Developments Studio, established in 1971 by Roger Sinclair.

Utopia was sold in 1991 to John Hudson and Kate Hudson, owners of Mayfair Studios, and was rebranded as “Mayfair Village”, ultimately closing in June 1992.

Overview

Utopia Studios was a London recording and disc-cutting facility founded in October 1976 by Phil Wainman at Spencer Court — a site previously occupied by Roger Sinclair’s Sound Developments Studio — with rooms designed by Eastlake acoustician Tom Hidley. It opened its cutting room in early 1977 and later expanded to include a mix room and a second studio, operating through the late 1970s and 1980s with engineers including James Guthrie, Andy Jackson, and Tim Palmer among its regulars. Vinyl cut at Utopia is identifiable by a distinctive lyre symbol etched in the runout groove, often rendered as “ƱTOPIA.” The studio was sold in 1991 to John and Kate Hudson, owners of Mayfair Studios, rebranded as Mayfair Village, and closed in June 1992.
