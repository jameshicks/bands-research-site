Overview

Sub Aqua was a UK indie label founded by Jeff Barrett, active from 1988. Its entire catalogue of six releases falls within the 1980s and sits within Indie Rock and Indie Pop. The roster — Laugh, East Village (2), The Wishing Stones, Bill Prince, and Andy Kerr — maps closely to the late-80s UK independent scene. No parent label or sublabels are on record, and the label’s corporate structure remains undocumented.

Notes (web)

Sub Aqua in context: Head → Sub Aqua → Heavenly

Sub Aqua was Barrett’s second label imprint, following directly from his first, Head (also known as Head Records). Head was funded and distributed by Mike Chadwick’s Revolver Distribution — a relationship rooted in Barrett having worked at Chadwick’s Revolver record shop in Bristol from 1983. Head folded in 1987 (the debut East Village single was recorded just as it collapsed), and Barrett immediately launched Sub Aqua as a continuation; East Village released two EPs on Sub Aqua in 1987 and 1988. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavenly_Recordings, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kelly_(musician)

No source states a single specific reason for Sub Aqua’s wind-down. The available evidence points to a cluster of factors:

  1. Financial fragility common to both imprints. Andy Hackett (a contemporary) quipped that “as each one folded, I think he realized you could run away from debt if you called it something else” — implying under-capitalisation and debt were recurring issues at both Head and Sub Aqua. heavenlyjukebox.wordpress.com (unattributed blog; treat as weak/anecdotal source)

  2. The catalogue ran its course. Sub Aqua’s Discogs credits are all dated 1988, and East Village — the label’s main act alongside Laugh and The Wishing Stones — had effectively split up (reportedly live on stage) by the time their debut album appeared in 1993. Bob Stanley later lent them money to record Drop Out after “the collapse of Sub Aqua.” saintetiennedisco.com/full/zplayers/jeffbarrett.html

  3. A deliberate aesthetic step forward. Martin Kelly (musician) described the transition explicitly: “Sub Aqua was kind of halfway to Heavenly. The idea with the new label was to get further away from just being an ‘indie’ label, to push things on commercially.” The rise of acid house and the crossover success of the Madchester scene (Stone Roses, Happy Mondays) gave Barrett the cultural rationale to attempt something more commercially ambitious. heavenlyjukebox.wordpress.com

  4. Acid house as a catalyst. Barrett himself noted (per the same source) that acid house was “a palate-cleansing thing, culturally” that “recharged my batteries.” This renewed energy fed directly into conceiving Heavenly.

Direct line to Heavenly Recordings

The connection is structural as well as biographical. Heavenly was co-founded in 1990 by Barrett with Mike Chadwick — the same Chadwick who had funded and distributed Head via Revolver Distribution. The Capersville press agency (which Barrett ran from 1988, representing Creation acts, Factory Records artists including Happy Mondays and New Order, The KLF, and Inspiral Carpets) kept Barrett connected to the independent music ecosystem between Sub Aqua’s collapse and Heavenly’s launch. Heavenly’s debut release was The World According To Sly & Lovechild (HVN1, 1990), facilitated by Barrett’s connection with Andrew Weatherall. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavenly_Recordings

In summary: Sub Aqua wound down circa 1988–1989 through a combination of financial unsustainability and a conscious decision to regroup under a more commercially oriented model. The Mike Chadwick relationship was the organisational thread connecting all three ventures (Head → Sub Aqua → Heavenly), and Capersville served as the bridging infrastructure while Barrett assembled the resources and partners for Heavenly.
