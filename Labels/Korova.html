Korova

UK record label. Discogs label ID: 13057. Founded 1979 by Rob Dickins; active through the early 1980s, folded c. 1984. Reactivated 2003–2007. Distributed by the Warner/Elektra/Asylum (WEA) group.

Overview

The name “Korova” is a reference to the Korova Milk Bar in Stanley Kubrick’s film adaptation of Anthony Burgess’s A Clockwork Orange (“Korova” is Russian for “cow”). The label was founded in 1979 by Rob Dickins as an imprint for Echo & The Bunnymen, who became its flagship act throughout the label’s original run. Alongside the Bunnymen, Korova was a home for UK post-punk and new wave artists including The Sound (2), Strawberry Switchblade, Dalek I, and Bette Bright. It operated under the WEA umbrella (label code LC 7259) and released records under two catalog-number series: KOW (singles and EPs) and KODE (albums).

The label went through a first phase (1979–1985) anchored by its post-punk roster, then a gap of roughly 18 years. WEA reactivated the Korova name in 2003 to issue 25th-anniversary remastered editions of Echo & The Bunnymen’s first six LPs, and went on to sign new acts (Cosmic Rough Riders, The Storys, The Sounds, Keisha White, Skye). The second phase collapsed in 2007.

Founders and Management

  • Rob Dickins — founded the label in 1979; later became chairman of WEA UK.
  • Zoo Management — Bill Drummond and David Balfe’s management operation handled Echo & The Bunnymen during the early Korova years and were also involved as producers on the Bunnymen’s debut.

Catalog — Reactivation (2003–2007, selected)

The reactivation used a new catalog scheme (KOW1001+, KODE1001+). Key releases:

  • Echo & The Bunnymen — 25th-anniversary remastered LPs (2003–2004)
  • Cosmic Rough Riders — The Stars Look Different From Down Here (KODE1003, 2006)
  • The Sounds — Dying To Say This To You (KODE1012P, 2006)
  • Keisha White — Out Of My Hands (KODE 1006, 2006)
  • Skye (6) — Mind How You Go (KODE1005, 2006)
  • Jeff Finlin — Angels In Disguise (KODE 1007, 2006)
  • Airhead (3) — Boing!! (9031-74679-2, 1991) — [Note: Airhead’s releases appear to date to 1991–92, suggesting KOW numbering overlap with the post-1990 period before the label formally folded]
  • Electrafixion — Burned (KODE1017, 2007)

Key Artists

Echo & The Bunnymen (Discogs ID: 45115)

The label’s anchor act from inception through the reactivation era. All five of their 1980s studio LPs appeared on Korova: Crocodiles (KODE 1, 1980), Heaven Up Here (1981), Porcupine (KODE 6, 1983), Ocean Rain (KODE 8, 1984), and the 1987 self-titled. Crocodiles was produced by Bill Drummond, David Balfe, and Ian Broudie (credited as “The Chameleons (2)”). Porcupine was produced by Ian Broudie.

The Sound (2) (Discogs ID: 171148)

Liverpool post-punk band led by Adrian Borland. Released three LPs on Korova: Jeopardy (KODE 2, 1980), From The Lions Mouth (KODE 5, 1981), and All Fall Down (1982, catalog not confirmed on this label — may have shifted to Statik). The band co-produced Jeopardy with Nick Robbins.

Strawberry Switchblade (Discogs ID: 101349)

Glasgow duo (Rose McDowall and Jill Bryson). Their debut LP Strawberry Switchblade (KODE 11, 1985) and several singles including “Since Yesterday” (KOW 38T, 1984) and “Jolene” (KOW 42T, 1985) appeared on Korova. The Discogs roster shows 73 releases attributed to them on the label, reflecting extensive UK/Japan single variants.

Dalek I (Discogs ID: 83379)

Liverpool electronic-pop act. Released the album Dalek I Love You (KODE 7, 1983) and several 12” singles including “Holiday In Disneyland” (KOW 25, 1982).

Bette Bright (Discogs ID: 955059)

Solo releases 1980–1981, including the single “Hello, I Am Your Heart” (KOW 3, 1980). Also released as Bette Bright And The Illuminations (KOW 14P, KOW 18).

Lori & The Chameleons (Discogs ID: 248501)

Early Korova act (1979–1980), pre-dating the main post-punk roster. Singles: “Touch” (SIR 4025, 1979) and “The Lonely Spy” (KOW 5, 1980).

The Residents (Discogs ID: 6708)

US avant-garde collective. Their UK releases George & James (KODE 9, 1984) and Ralph Before ‘84: Volume I (KODE 10, 1984) appeared on Korova, part of an ad-hoc licensing relationship.

Ian McCulloch (Discogs ID: 37859)

Echo & The Bunnymen frontman. His solo single “September Song” (KOW 40, 1984) was released on Korova alongside the band’s catalog.

Open Questions

  • The exact end date of the original Korova run is unclear — releases appear through 1985 (Strawberry Switchblade LP, Ellery Bop single), so “folded c. 1984” in the stub was slightly early. The label may have continued in a reduced capacity to 1986.
  • All Fall Down by The Sound (2) (1982) is not confirmed as a Korova release in this dataset — it may have appeared on another label (possibly Statik Records).
  • The Teardrop Explodes, listed in the original stub as a Korova act, do not appear anywhere in the Discogs catalog for this label. They were primarily on Zoo/Mercury; the stub was in error.
  • Rob Dickins’s precise role after founding (A&R, chairman, etc.) and the exact founding year of the imprint within 1979 are not in the Discogs data.
  • Airhead (3) releases show up in the Korova roster (1991–1992); it’s unclear whether this represents a final late phase of the original label or an early precursor to the 2003 reactivation.
