From Discogs

Factory Records Australasia was officially launched in July 1985 by Andrew Penhallow in partnership with Factory. Penhallow had previously been a partner with Paul Gardiner in GAP Records, a label distributed by EMI (Australia) Limited (and later by CBS Records Australia Limited) which licensed Factory releases and other foreign labels for Australia.

Factory Australasia was Factory’s most successful foreign offshoot with a number of singles and albums reaching into the Australian Top 20, Top 10 and even Top 5 charts.

All Factory Australasia records and three CDs were manufactured and distributed by CBS Records Australia. All other CDs were imported from the UK. Most Factory Australasia releases featured the same tracks and catalog number as the UK versions, although there were several small differences, and some Australia-only releases.

1990 saw the demise of Factory Australasia - mainly because Factory headquarters in Manchester wanted to secure a better commercial backing through a major label. Factory boss Tony Wilson travelled to Sydney and, together with Penhallow, held negotiations with several Australian based labels. Eventually, Festival Records made the highest offer and in June 1990 the entire catalogue moved to the Sydney based company. Although Penhallow kept a caretaker role for Factory releases on Festival, he concentrated on his own label, Volition, which focused on Australian acts.

Overview

Factory Records Australasia was the Australian subsidiary of Factory, launched in July 1985 by Andrew Penhallow, who had previously co-run GAP Records — a label that licensed Factory releases and other foreign catalogue for Australia through EMI and CBS distribution. It became Factory’s most commercially successful foreign offshoot, placing singles and albums in the Australian Top 10 and even Top 5; all physical releases were manufactured and distributed by CBS Records Australia, with UK compact discs imported separately. The catalogue of 32 releases is confined entirely to the 1980s, spanning Synth-pop, New Wave, Electro, and Indie Rock. In mid-1990 Factory’s Manchester management — led by Tony Wilson — travelled to Sydney and negotiated a move to Festival Records, closing the Australasia imprint; Penhallow subsequently launched Volition to focus on Australian acts.

Notes (web) — 1990 Festival Records transition

What the sources establish

The Discogs label profile (the most detailed primary source found) states that in 1990 Factory’s Manchester management — specifically Tony Wilson — travelled to Sydney and, together with Penhallow, held negotiations with “several Australian based labels.” Festival Records “made the highest offer” and in June 1990 the entire catalogue transferred to the Sydney-based company. The stated rationale was that Factory headquarters “wanted to secure a better commercial backing through a major label.” (Discogs label 37178)

Factory’s UK finances were already under strain by 1990. The Haçienda nightclub had been subsidised almost entirely by New Order’s record sales, losing up to £50,000 per month in earlier years and continuing to haemorrhage money through 1990–92. (side-line.com; live4ever.uk.com) Factory moved into the expensive FAC 251 offices on Charles Street, Manchester in September 1990, the same quarter the Australasian deal closed. (Wikipedia — Factory Records, as of 2026-09-29) The label would not collapse until November 1992, when debts exceeded £2 million, driven by the Happy Mondays’ £380,000 Barbados recording and New Order’s £430,000 Republic sessions. The financial pressure in mid-1990 was therefore real but not yet terminal — the switch to Festival appears consistent with a deliberate drive to extract better commercial terms from a major-affiliated partner at a moment when the Australasian deal was up for renegotiation, rather than a distress sale.

Andrew Penhallow’s transition to Volition to focus on Australian acts also points to the change being partly strategic on his end — he retained a caretaker role for the Factory/Festival releases but used the handover to redirect his own energies. (Wikipedia — Volition Records, as of 2026-09-29; Wikipedia — Andrew Penhallow, as of 2026-09-29)

Festival Records was by 1990 an established major-scale Australian distributor with a long track record of handling UK independent labels (including Island, A&M, and Chrysalis in earlier periods) and was already distributing several Australian independent labels including Au Go Go and Waterfront. (Wikipedia — Festival Records, as of 2026-09-29) Its competitive strength as a bidder is consistent with its position as Australia’s dominant independent-turned-major distributor of the era.

What remains unknown

No source found gives the names of the other Australian labels that bid alongside Festival, the financial terms of the June 1990 deal (advance, royalty rate, contract length), or the duration of the Festival arrangement (the Discogs profile says “1990 saw the demise of Factory Australasia” but Festival carried the catalogue through at least part of the early 1990s). The Scream City fanzine #5 interview with Penhallow (conducted by Matthew Robertson) is referenced in multiple sources as substantive on the early Factory Australasia period but is not available as a retrievable web page; it may contain deal context.
