Alligator Records

Discogs ID: 26968 Parent company: Alligator Records & Artist Management, Inc. (Discogs ID: 353856) Sub-labels: Alligator Blues Masterpiece (ID: 1381093), Deluxe Edition (2) (ID: 660815) Founded: 1971, Chicago, IL Founded by: Bruce Iglauer Website: alligator.com

Chicago-based independent blues record label, widely regarded as the most prominent contemporary blues label in the United States. Founded by Bruce Iglauer (born 1947) after he used his personal savings to record his favourite Chicago band, Hound Dog Taylor & The House Rockers, when his employer Delmark Records declined to do so. The first release (cat. 4701) is Hound Dog Taylor’s self-titled debut, recorded in 1971. Iglauer has served as producer on over 942 credited releases in the Discogs catalog — making him the single most prolific production credit across the label’s entire output.

Label History by Era

1971–1977: The Founding Years

Iglauer quit his day job at Delmark Records and began promoting Hound Dog Taylor & The House Rockers and his new company full time. The label’s catalog numbers ran sequentially from 4701. Early releases established the “Genuine Houserockin’ Music” aesthetic: raw, electric Chicago and Southern blues with minimal overdubs.

Key early releases:

  • AL 4701 — Hound Dog Taylor And The House Rockers (1971): debut; produced by Bruce Iglauer and Hound Dog Taylor; engineered by Stu Black; band: Hound Dog Taylor (guitar/vocals), Brewer Phillips (guitar), Ted Harvey (drums).
  • AL 4702 — Big Walter Horton With Carey Bell (1972)
  • AL 4703 — The Son Seals Blues Band (1973): engineered by Stu Black; Son Seals (guitar/vocals); Johnny “Big Moose” Walker (organ); designed by Peter Amft.
  • AL 4705 — Somebody Loan Me A Dime (1974): Fenton Robinson
  • AL 4706 — I Got What It Takes (1975): Koko Taylor’s debut; produced by Iglauer, Koko Taylor, and Mighty Joe Young; engineered by Stu Black; session band includes Sammy Lawhorn and Bill Heid (keys).
  • AL 4710 — I Hear Some Blues Downstairs (1977): Fenton Robinson

1978–1983: Expanding the Roster

The label grew its roster substantially in the late 1970s, bringing in new national talent and launching the Living Chicago Blues compilation series (AL 7701–7706, 1978–1980), a six-volume document of the Chicago blues scene featuring Eddie Shaw, Magic Slim, Carey Bell, Pinetop Perkins, Lonnie Brooks, A.C. Reed, and others.

  • AL 4713 — Ice Pickin’ (1978): Albert Collins’s Alligator debut; produced by Iglauer, Dick Shurman, and Richard McLeese; band includes A.C. Reed (tenor sax), Allen Batts (keys), Casey Jones (2) (drums), Aron Burton (bass), Larry Burton (guitar).
  • AL 4719 — Frostbite (1980): Albert Collins
  • AL 4720 — Chicago Fire (1980): Son Seals
  • AL 4722/4726 — Tony Mathews, The New Johnny Otis Show (1981)
  • AL 4723 — Stone Crazy! (1981): Buddy Guy; band includes Phil Guy (guitar), Ray Allison (drums), J.W. Williams (bass).
  • AL 4725 — Frozen Alive! (1981): Albert Collins
  • Reggae and Jamaican music releases appeared 1980–1984 under the “AL 8300” catalog block: Black Slate, The Mighty Diamonds, Mutabaruka, The Abyssinians, Edi Fitzroy, Augustus Pablo — a short-lived but notable diversification.

1984–1990: The Mainstream Blues Boom

A turning point came with the Grammy-winning Showdown! (AL 4743, 1985), a three-way collaboration between Albert Collins, Robert Cray, and Johnny Copeland — produced by Iglauer and Dick Shurman, engineered and mixed by Justin Niebank, mastered by Tom Coyne. The album reached a broad mainstream audience and helped trigger a national blues revival. Robert Cray would go on to major commercial success; the album stands as Alligator’s most crossover release of the decade.

Other key 1984–1990 entries:

  • AL 4735 — Guitar Slinger (1984): Johnny Winter’s Alligator debut
  • AL 4739 — Strike Like Lightning (1985): Lonnie Mack
  • AL 4740 — Queen Of The Blues (1985): Koko Taylor
  • AL 4744 — Bar Room Preacher (1985): Jimmy Johnson (8)
  • AL 4746 — Live From Chicago - Mr. Superharp Himself! (1986): James Cotton
  • AL 4750/4752 — Lonnie Mack, Albert Collins (1986)
  • AL 4758 — Edge Of The City (1987): The Kinsey Report
  • ALCD 4781 — Ace Of Harps (1990): Charlie Musselwhite
  • ALCD 4784 — Jump For Joy (1990): Koko Taylor
  • ALCD 4790 — Harp Attack! (1990): Billy Branch, Carey Bell, James Cotton, Junior Wells
  • AL 4780 — The Uppity Blues Women (1990): Saffire -The Uppity Blues Women’s debut

The Genuine Houserockin’ Music compilation series (AL 101–104+, 1986–1990) served as a label sampler/introduction.

1991–2000: Anniversaries and Consolidation

The label marked its 20th anniversary with The Alligator Records 20th Anniversary Collection (ALCD 105/6, 1991), compiled by Iglauer, Bob DePugh, and David Forte. The 25th (ALCD 110/111, 1996) and 30th (ALCD 112/13, 2001) anniversary sets followed.

Luther Allison signed to Alligator and released Soul Fixin’ Man (ALCD 4820, 1994), produced by Jim Gaines, featuring The Memphis Horns (Andrew Love, Wayne Jackson). His follow-up Blue Streak (ALCD 4834, 1995) and Reckless (ALCD 4849, 1997) were recorded largely in Europe and mixed by Jim Gaines and Rusty McFarland; Reckless features Bernard Allison (guitar/vocals) and The Memphis Horns on select tracks.

Shemekia Copeland debuted with Turn The Heat Up (ALCD 4857, 1998).

2001–2010: Broadening the Roots Sound

The label signed acts beyond strict Chicago blues: JJ Grey & Mofro (country-soul-rock, from 2007), Mavis Staples (Have A Little Faith, ALCD 4899, 2004), Anders Osborne, Marcia Ball, Janiva Magness, and Tommy Castro.

2010–Present: Legacy and New Guard

Christone -Kingfish- Ingram’s debut Kingfish (ALCD 4990, 2019) — produced by Tom Hambridge, engineered by Zach Allen, mixed/mastered by Michael Saint-Leon — became one of Alligator’s highest-profile new-artist launches in decades. The label celebrated its 50th anniversary with a comprehensive collection (AL 5000, 2021).

Active newer signings include Selwyn Birchwood, GA-20, Toronzo Cannon, Carolyn Wonderland, and Curtis Salgado.

Corporate Structure

  • Alligator Records & Artist Management, Inc. (ID: 353856) is the Illinois corporation, incorporated 19 August 1980; IL company number CORP_52139821

Open Questions

  • Discogs does not reveal when Exceleration Music Partners acquired the parent holding entity, or what prompted the corporate restructuring
  • Bruce Iglauer’s year of birth (1947) is asserted from context — not confirmed in Discogs
  • The reggae/Jamaican music period (1980–1984, AL 8300 catalog block) is lightly documented; deeper research would clarify what drove that diversification and when/why it ended
  • Rusty McFarland’s specific mixing credits on Reckless are confirmed (Discogs); his other work is not in the DB
  • The label’s catalog number scheme jumped from 4701-series to 5000-series around 2020 — no Discogs explanation for the gap spans
