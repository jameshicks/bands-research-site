About / History

ché Records was an independent label from East London, co-founded in 1991 by Vinita Joshi and Nick Allport. It was a direct continuation of their earlier venture Cheree Records, which had been forced into liquidation after falling prey to dishonest financial backers — a relationship Joshi later described as “misguided involvement of their financial backers.” When Cheree folded, Allport and Joshi immediately regrouped under the ché name, operating through the legal entity Ché Trading Limited (PO Box 653, London E18 2NX).

The label’s first four releases (CHÉ 1S through CHÉ 4 CD, 1991–1992) were all by Disco Inferno, who had been present on Cheree’s final releases and transferred seamlessly to the new imprint. This gave ché an immediate association with abrasive, experimental post-punk — a tone the label would both maintain and expand.

Through the mid-1990s ché grew into one of the UK’s most eclectic indie imprints, spanning post-rock (Disco Inferno, Magic Hour), shoegaze (Slipstream (2)), art-rock (18th Dye, Lilys), noise-pop (Urusei Yatsura), and international left-field acts (Bardo Pond, Seam). The label operated without a house sound; its consistency was curatorial rather than stylistic.

Funding came partly through a relationship with Sire Records/Seymour Stein. When Sire pushed for a partnership with a mainstream UK distributor (Coalition), the label’s ethos was compromised. Joshi later said she “could no longer put my hand on my heart and tell bands we were the best label to work with.” She described feeling “a bit pushed out at the end of the Che days” — and when the label wound down around 1997–2000, she founded Rocket Girl independently. Allport’s subsequent activities are not documented on Discogs.

The catalog ran to at least CHÉ 90 (Helen Love, 2000). Two sublabels operated under the ché umbrella: Ché Trading Limited Presents…, used for split releases and compilations, and i, an ambient/electronic-leaning offshoot whose catalog included Piano Magic, Füxa, and Transient Waves.

Key Production Credits

Charlie McIntosh was the primary in-house producer/engineer for the Disco Inferno era (CHÉ 1S – CHÉ 4 CD), co-producing all four releases and engineering the debut. He co-produced with Richard Charlton on CHÉ 3 T and CHÉ 4 CD. Cactus Design (credited as “Cactus”) handled sleeve/visual design across multiple early releases including CHÉ 1S through CHÉ 5 and the Ché Trading Limited Presents… split. External production credits include Steve Albini (18th Dye tracks on the Does The Word Duh comp) and Zion Train (Slipstream LP).
