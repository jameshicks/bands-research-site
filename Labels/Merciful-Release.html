Overview

Merciful Release was founded by Andrew Eldritch, frontman of Leeds gothic rock band The Sisters of Mercy, with the explicit aim of getting his music onto the radio — a DIY impulse that produced the Sisters’ debut 7” “Damage Done” in 1980 as a run of 1,000 copies. By 1984 the label had negotiated a distribution deal with Warner Bros. (Elektra in the US) while Eldritch retained ownership and creative control, a structure that allowed Merciful Release to function as an artist-run imprint within a major’s distribution network. Beyond the Sisters, the roster drew heavily from the Leeds post-punk and goth scene: The March Violets, Salvation, James Ray (also releasing under aliases The MK Ultra and La Costa Rasa), and The Sisterhood. The catalogue is concentrated in the 1980s — 22 of 38 selected releases fall in that decade — before the label went into effective hiatus in the late 1990s, since which it has only issued Sisters of Mercy reissues.

Notes (web) — The March Violets departure from Merciful Release

The March Violets released two singles on Merciful Release in 1982: Religious As Hell (EP, August 1982, MR013) and Grooving In Green (November 1982) — note Discogs credits only the first; Wikipedia lists both (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_March_Violets, as of 2026-11-18). After those two releases the band started their own imprint Rebirth (3) in 1983, through which they had considerable indie chart success (Snake Dance #2, Walk Into The Sun #1 UK indie).

What the sources say about the reason: Every interview and secondary source that touches on the departure describes it as a “bust-up” with Andrew Eldritch but provides no specifics. The fullest on-record statement comes from band members themselves, in which a Violet declined to elaborate: “Well, there was a bit of a bust-up and that’s kind of all I wanna say about it now.” (sisterswiki.org/The_March_Violets_-_Biography — returns 403, cited from search-result snippet only.) The same source notes the rift was later healed: Denbigh joined a Sisters of Mercy live lineup as “Nurse to the Doktor,” and guitarist Tom Ashton’s RadATL interview (2021) describes Eldritch as a close collaborator who invited him to join the Sisters full-time — Ashton declined out of loyalty to his bandmates. (radatl.com/2021/11/21/tom-ashton-subvon/)

The blog Plunder the Tombs (2010) speculates that it cannot have been comfortable given that Denbigh was sharing a house with Sisters bassist Craig Adams when the split happened, and places the March Violets in “a very long line of people in the UK Goth scene to have a falling out with Mr Andrew Eldritch.” (plunderthetombs.blogspot.com) This is a fan blog, not a named-byline press source; treat as background colour only.

Conclusion: No published source discloses the specific cause — creative, financial, or personal. The band members have consistently declined to elaborate beyond “bust-up.” The fact that the Violets moved immediately to their own label (rather than signing elsewhere) is consistent with either a financial/contractual dispute or a desire for independence, but no source makes this explicit. The subsequent reconciliation (Denbigh touring with Sisters) suggests the split was not catastrophic.
