415 Records

San Francisco independent record label, active 1978–1986 (and in name through the early 1990s on reissues). Discogs label ID: 16439. The label’s name refers both to the San Francisco telephone area code and, per label lore, to the police code 415 for “disturbing the peace.”

History

Founding (1978–1979)

415 Records was founded in San Francisco in 1978 by three figures from the city’s music retail and promotion world: writer and promoter Howie Klein, Aquarius Records (San Francisco) store owner Chris Knab, and music retailer and collector Butch Bridges. Their first release was a single by The Nuns — “Decadent Jew / Savage” (cat. 001, 1978), produced by Joey D’Kaye, with personnel including Alejandro Escovedo (guitar) and Jennifer Miro (piano, vocals). The Nuns had been one of San Francisco’s earliest punk groups and had appeared at the Sex Pistols’ final concert at Winterland.

In 1979, Butch Bridges sold his share in the label to Queenie Taylor, a longtime employee of Bill Graham Presents, the management and booking agency. Bill Graham Presents helped 415 acts secure opening slots for nationally touring bands including Duran Duran, establishing early visibility for the label’s roster.

Distribution Deal with Columbia (c. 1979–1980)

415 Records signed a distribution deal with Columbia Records, which gave the label national reach through Columbia’s infrastructure while retaining independent A&R. This arrangement is typical of the era’s “distributed indie” model. Releases from this period carry both 415 and Columbia catalog numbers; Columbia’s co-branding placed 415 releases in chain stores across the US.

The Core Years (1979–1983)

The label’s key signing period ran from 1979 through 1983. David Kahne — based at Hyde Street Recording in San Francisco — became the de facto house producer, responsible for the majority of the label’s significant releases:

Early signings included Pearl Harbor And The Explosions (single, 1979), SVT (1979, featuring Jack Casady of Jefferson Airplane on bass), the Mutants (1980), Units (synth-pop duo, 1980), The Offs (1980), and Red Rockers (1981, a New Orleans-based band relocated to SF). The bill for major label 415 acts was rounded out by Romeo Void (1981–1984), Translator (1982–1983), Wire Train (1983–1987), Pop-O-Pies (1981), and Roky Erickson And The Aliens (1981).

Howie Klein left 415 at some point during this period. Daniel Levitin (later a music cognition researcher and author; Discogs ID 779334) helped run the label for approximately three months after Klein’s departure.

Sandy Pearlman Acquisition and Renaming (mid-1980s)

After Levitin’s brief tenure, Sandy Pearlman — the veteran producer and manager best known for his work with Blue Öyster Cult and Black Sabbath — purchased the label. Pearlman brought in Tom Schedler as head of A&R. He subsequently renamed the label Popular Metaphysics (Discogs label ID: 768543) and released records through its co-branding agreement with Columbia for a small roster including acts identified in the Discogs profile by IDs 129005, 832557, and 1705207.

Despite the renaming, “415 Records” continued to appear on reissue and compilation releases into the 1990s — the 1996 Translator retrospective Everywhere That We Were carries a 415 / Columbia / Legacy credit.

House Aesthetic and Key Personnel

David Kahne — House Producer

David Kahne is credited on 83 releases in the Discogs 415 catalog, making him by far the dominant creative figure on the production side. His San Francisco base at Hyde Street Recording enabled close relationships with the label’s roster throughout the early 1980s. His 415 credits include the full Romeo Void catalog through Instincts (1984), both Translator LPs (Heartbeats And Triggers, 1982; No Time Like Now, 1983), the early Red Rockers releases (Condition Red EP, 1981; Good As Gold, 1983; China single, 1983), and multiple Wire Train records (Chamber Of Hellos EP, 1983; … In A Chamber LP, 1984). After 415, Kahne went on to produce The Bangles, Fishbone, Paul McCartney, The Strokes, and many others. See David Kahne for his full career.

Design and Artwork

A tight design circle recurs across the catalog. Frank Zincavage (Romeo Void bassist) contributed cover design, photography, and illustration across 28 releases. Trudy Fisher’s photography appears on 31 releases and is the signature visual identity of the label — her shots define the look of both Translator albums, Wire Train, and numerous singles. Peter Soe, Jr. handled cover design on 11 releases. Ken Korematsu provided design on 11. Diane Zincavage (Frank’s sister) handled art mechanicals on 16 releases.

Outside Producers

Beyond Kahne, the label brought in significant outside producers for specific artists:

  • Ric Ocasek (The Cars) — produced Romeo Void’s Never Say Never (1981) and Benefactor (1982), bringing a major-label sheen to the SF new wave sound
  • Rick Chertoff and William Wittman — produced Red Rockers’ Schizophrenic Circus (1984) and associated singles
  • Peter Maunu — produced Wire Train’s Between Two Words (1985)
  • Tim Palmer — produced Wire Train’s Ten Women (1987) and the “She Comes On” single

Roster

Romeo Void

Romeo Void (Discogs ID: 163487) were the label’s most commercially successful act and the group with the most 415-era releases (67 on the label in total). Formed in San Francisco around 1979, they comprised Debora Iyall (vocals), Peter Woods (guitar), Benjamin Bossi (saxophone), Frank Zincavage (bass), and a rotating drum chair (John Stench on early singles; Larry Carter on later work; Aaron Smith on Instincts).

Translator (3)

Translator (3) (Discogs ID: 313686) formed in Los Angeles and relocated to San Francisco, signing to 415 in 1982. Members: Steve Barton and Robert Darlington (guitar, vocals), Larry Dekker (bass), Dave Scheff (drums). Both their 415 LPs were produced and engineered by David Kahne and mastered by Paul Stubblebine.

Red Rockers

Red Rockers (Discogs ID: 10382) were a New Orleans-based post-punk band who signed to 415. Core members: John Thomas Griffith (vocals, guitar, keyboards), Darren Hill (bass, vocals), James Singletary (lead guitar), Jim Reilly (drums), Shawn Paddock (guitar). Their biggest hit was “China” (1983, produced by David Kahne), which received significant MTV airplay.

Wire Train

Wire Train (Discogs ID: 156354) were a San Francisco post-punk/new wave band who joined 415 in 1983. Members: Kevin Hunter (vocals, guitar), Kurt Herr (guitar, vocals), Anders Rundblad (bass, vocals), Federico Gil Solá / Brian MacLeod (drums). Wire Train had the longest continuous 415 presence, releasing through 1987, well after most of the other SF-based acts had moved on.

Pearl Harbor And The Explosions

Pearl Harbor And The Explosions (Discogs ID: 316440) appear on one 415 single — “Drivin’” (1979), produced by David Kahne. Members included Pearl E. Gates (vocals), John Hanes, Hilary Hanes, and Peter Dunne. The band released their debut LP on Warner Bros. in 1980 and are notable as one of Kahne’s earliest production credits; John Hanes also later appeared as a member of Romeo Void.

Rank & File

Rank & File (Discogs ID: 10386) — the cowpunk band led by brothers Chip Kinman and Tony Kinman with Alejandro Escovedo — are associated with 415 Records in the label’s history and in Kahne’s career overview, but Discogs catalogs their releases primarily on Slash rather than on 415. The Kahne connection (he produced their debut Sundown, 1982) is the link; the Slash/415 co-release described in some sources is not confirmed in Discogs catalog data.

The Nuns

The Nuns (Discogs ID: 244089) — one of San Francisco’s first punk bands — released 415’s first single in 1978. Personnel on the debut single included Alejandro Escovedo (guitar) and Jennifer Miro (piano, vocals), with photography by Mick Rock.

Roky Erickson And The Aliens

Roky Erickson And The Aliens (Discogs ID: 1148977) appear on the roster for 1981 — ex-13th Floor Elevators frontman Roky Erickson recording with his band The Aliens. Members: Roky Erickson, Duane Aslaksen, Jeff Sutton, Bill Miller, Steven Morgan Burgess, John “Fuzzy Furioso” Oxendine.

SVT

SVT (Discogs ID: 413525) were a San Francisco band notable for featuring Jack Casady (Jefferson Airplane, Hot Tuna) on bass alongside Brian Marnell (vocals, guitar), Nick Buck (keyboards, vocals), and Bill Gibson (drums, vocals). Their 415 single Heart Of Stone (1979) is among the label’s earliest releases.

Units

Units (Discogs ID: 172298) were a San Francisco synth-pop duo — Scott Ryser and Rachel Webber — who released Digital Stimulation (1980, cat. 415A-0003) on 415. One of the label’s few purely electronic acts, mastered by Paul Stubblebine.

Pop-O-Pies

Pop-O-Pies (Discogs ID: 1069398) released The White EP (1981, cat. 415A-0009), produced by David Kahne.

Until December

Until December (Discogs ID: 107843) were the label’s late-era major act, releasing through 1986. Members: Adam Sherburne, Brian Weisberg, Chuck Frazier, Greg Senzer, Tim Huthert. Their producer was Ken Kessie rather than Kahne, marking a later production generation. They released a self-titled LP (1986, BFC 40438) plus multiple singles on Columbia through 415.

Scene Context

415 Records occupied the center of San Francisco’s post-punk/new wave moment, 1979–1984. The label’s timing and geography placed it at the confluence of two currents: the city’s pre-existing punk scene (The Nuns, The Offs, the Mutants) and the incoming new wave synthesizer and art-rock sensibility arriving from New York and the UK.

The Columbia distribution deal distinguished 415 from most comparable regionally-focused independents of the era — it gave the label’s records national retail presence at a time when indie distribution was piecemeal. This hybrid structure (independent creative decisions, major distribution) was later widely adopted but was comparatively unusual in 1979–1980.

The label’s collapse (or transformation into Popular Metaphysics under Sandy Pearlman) around 1985–1986 coincided with the general commercial contraction of the SF new wave scene. The artists who had been 415’s core — Romeo Void, Red Rockers, Translator — had all reached or passed their commercial peaks by 1984–1985. Wire Train carried on through 1987 but without significant US chart impact. Until December was the label’s late attempt to find a hit act; their self-titled LP and singles on Columbia had minor MTV presence but didn’t break nationally.

David Kahne’s departure from the SF scene to pursue The Bangles (Columbia, 1984) effectively marked the end of the label’s creative core. The specific combination of Kahne’s production style — tightly engineered, rhythm-forward, sympathetic to SF new wave’s tension between punk energy and pop structure — and the label’s Columbia infrastructure defined the 415 sound more than any single artist.
