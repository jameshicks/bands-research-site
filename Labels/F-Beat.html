F-Beat

UK new wave label founded in late 1979 / early 1980 by Jake Riviera, Andrew Lauder, and Elvis Costello as the direct commercial successor to Radar Records. Discogs label IDs: 33663 (trading label “F-Beat”) and 279188 (holding company “F-Beat Records Ltd.”). Label code: LC 6792.

Distributed by RCA (not WEA as carried over from Radar — the F-Beat era saw a shift to the RCA network for its main catalog series), though some early releases and continental pressings appeared on WEA-affiliated catalog numbers. The holding company F-Beat Records Ltd. was incorporated on 5 November 1979 (initially under a different name) and renamed 20 February 1980. Its first release was “I Can’t Stand Up For Falling Down” (XX1) on 8 February 1980.

F-Beat Records Ltd. is also the immediate parent of Demon Records (sublabel ID: 48002), confirming that the F-Beat corporate entity gave birth to the broader Demon/Edsel reissue infrastructure that Andrew Lauder would build through the 1980s and 90s. F-Beat Records Ltd. was later acquired by Crimson Productions Ltd. (6 April 1998); Crimson’s ultimate parent is listed in the Discogs DB.

Founders and Corporate Structure

Jake Riviera (Discogs ID: 755945) — Manager of both Elvis Costello and Nick Lowe. Had co-founded Stiff Records with Dave Robinson (3) in 1976, then partnered at Radar. When Radar’s staff were dismissed at end of 1979, Riviera led the formation of F-Beat. The Discogs F-Beat profile specifically credits him as the label’s founder. He later established Demon Records with Andrew Lauder and Elvis Costello.

Andrew Lauder (Discogs ID: 1477914) — A&R architect at Radar and previously at Liberty / United Artists UK. Co-founded F-Beat with Riviera and Costello; the same trio is credited in the F-Beat Records Ltd. corporate profile. Went on to found Edsel Records and Silvertone Records separately from the Demon structure.

Elvis Costello (Discogs IDs: 55029 as solo; 254761 as Elvis Costello & The Attractions) — As primary recording artist and co-founder, Costello’s catalog was F-Beat’s commercial spine. On select releases he is producer-credited under his real name, Declan Patrick Aloysius MacManus (Discogs ID: 409185).

Nick Lowe (Discogs ID: 150897) — Backbone of the founding generation and the label’s dominant producer, even though he is not named in the Discogs corporate text. His production credits across the F-Beat catalog are more numerous than any other single producer (175 releases credited).

Chronological Narrative

1980: Launch

The label launched in February 1980 with “I Can’t Stand Up For Falling Down” (XX1) — a Sam & Dave cover by Elvis Costello & The Attractions, produced by Nick Lowe. It reached UK #4. The debut was followed by a cascade of product in the first year: the Get Happy! album (XXLP, 1980, produced by Nick Lowe, engineered by Roger Bechirian), Rockpile’s sole album Seconds Of Pleasure (XXLP 7, 1980), Carlene Carter’s Musical Shapes (XXLP 3, 1980), and Clive Langer & The Boxes (XX2, XX4).

The early singles used the clean XX numbering: XX1 through XX9 in 1980 alone. Key 1980 singles:

1981: Costello’s Country Turn and Trust

1981 saw two contrasting Elvis Costello & The Attractions albums. Trust (XXLP 11, January 1981) was the more conventional new-wave follow-up to Get Happy!, produced by Nick Lowe with Roger Bechirian as associate producer. Almost Blue (XXLP 13, October 1981) was a curveball: an album of country covers recorded in Nashville with veteran country producer Billy Sherrill, featuring John McFee on pedal steel and The Nashville Edition on backing vocals. The Attractions played on both.

Carlene Carter — daughter of June Carter Cash and step-daughter of Johnny Cash — released three albums on F-Beat in 1980–81. Her presence on the label was partly a connection via Nick Lowe, whom she would marry in 1979.

1982: Imperial Bedroom and Nick The Knife

The 1982 peak brought two landmark records. Imperial Bedroom (XXLP, 1982) was produced by Geoff Emerick — the Beatles’ Abbey Road engineer — with Steve Nieve’s orchestrations and photography by David Bailey. It is widely considered Costello’s artistic high-water mark of the F-Beat era. Nick The Knife (XXLP 14, 1982) marked Nick Lowe’s first full F-Beat album proper (his earlier F-Beat singles had preceded it), self-produced with the Cowboy Outfit lineup (Martin Belmont, Billy Bremner, Terry Williams, Paul Carrack, James Eller, Bobby Irwin) plus Carlene Carter on piano.

Also in 1982: The Blasters (US roots-rock) appeared on F-Beat for their UK releases (XXLP 15); Paul Carrack’s Suburban Voodoo (XXLP 18); and the mysterious Blanket Of Secrecy (XXLP 16) appeared with an art-rock LP.

1983: Punch The Clock and The Abominable Showman

Punch The Clock (XXLP 19 / ZL 25464, 1983) marked a commercial shift — Costello brought in Clive Langer & Alan Winstanley (fresh from producing Madness and Dexys Midnight Runners) as producers. The album featured TKO Horns (Jim Paterson on trombone, Dave Plews on trumpet, Paul Speare on tenor sax/flute, Gary Barnacle on saxophone), Caron Wheeler and Claudia Fontaine on backing vocals, and string arrangements by David Bedford. It was a consciously polished, radio-friendly record.

Nick Lowe’s The Abominable Showman (XXLP 18, 1983) was his second F-Beat album, co-produced by Roger Bechirian. The Cowboy Outfit lineup (Martin Belmont, Paul Carrack, Bobby Irwin, James Eller) backed Lowe, with Robert Kirby providing string arrangements.

1984–1985: Goodbye Cruel World and Cowboy Outfit

Goodbye Cruel World (ZL 70317, 1984), again produced by Clive Langer & Alan Winstanley, was widely regarded as a commercial misfire and was later disowned by Costello. Nick Lowe’s Nick Lowe And His Cowboy Outfit (ZL 70338, 1984), co-produced by Colin Fairley and Paul Bass, consolidated the country-rock direction he would pursue through the decade. The Coward Brothers (a Costello/T-Bone Burnett side project, RPS-183) appeared in 1985.

1986: King Of America and Blood & Chocolate — Final Costello Albums

The last two Costello albums on F-Beat were the most artistically adventurous. King Of America (1986), released as “The Costello Show Featuring The Attractions And The Confederates,” was co-produced by Declan Patrick Aloysius MacManus (Costello under his real name) and T-Bone Burnett, with Larry Hirsch as third producer. Musicians included James Burton (guitar), Mitchell Froom (organ), Jim Keltner (drums), Ron Tutt (drums), and Jerry Scheff (bass) — essentially an American session band.

Blood & Chocolate (X FIEND 80, 1986) was produced by Nick Lowe and Colin Fairley, and featured Costello’s “Napoleon Dynamite” alias. Cait O’Riordan (ex-Pogues) sang on two tracks (B4, B5) and co-wrote A3. It marked the end of the Costello/F-Beat relationship.

1991–1994: Late Catalog

After the Costello/Lowe era ended, F-Beat continued on a smaller scale with a cluster of early-1990s acts:

  • Blab Happy — UK band; LP Boat (BH1 / XXLP20, 1991) and singles Down (XX41T, 1991), Blinding EP (XX43CD, 1993)
  • The Gutter Brothers — Gone To The Dogs (XXCD21, 1991)
  • Nicola Hitchcock — A Bowl Of Chalk (XXCD 23, 1993), Pick Up Your Coat (XX44CD, 1993)
  • Phil Burdett & The New World Troubadours — two releases (1993)

Open Questions

  • The stub notes WEA distribution; Discogs data is ambiguous — some pressings use WEA-affiliated numbering (FB, K), others use RCA (ZL, ZB, ZT). The distribution arrangement may have shifted mid-label-life or varied by territory.
  • Dave Robinson (3) co-founded Stiff with Riviera but is not named in F-Beat’s corporate history; the brief’s mention of Robinson as a co-founder of F-Beat may be a conflation with the Stiff origin story.
  • Specific departure date of Costello from F-Beat and terms of any subsequent deal not available in Discogs.
  • The Coward Brothers (“The People’s Limousine,” RPS-183, 1985) appear on a Japanese pressing only — full international availability unclear from the DB.
  • Blanket Of Secrecy (XXLP 16, 1982) — identity and context not established from Discogs alone.
