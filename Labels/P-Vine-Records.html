P-Vine Records

Japanese independent record label. Founded 1975, based in Shibuya, Tokyo. Discogs label ID: 10119. Parent company: P-Vine, Inc. (Discogs label ID: 332865).

Overview

P-Vine Records was established in 1975 by Yasufumi Higurashi and Akira Kochi as a specialist blues imprint in Tokyo. Over five decades the catalog expanded far beyond its origins to cover funk/soul, jazz, Latin, hip hop, Krautrock reissues, Japanese indie, and licensed Western alternative and indie rock. As of the Discogs dump, the main label lists 7,654 releases, making it one of Japan’s most prolific independent labels.

The label is structured under P-Vine, Inc. and operates an extensive network of sublabels — over 90 on Discogs — each scoped to a micro-genre or series (e.g. blues retrospectives, jazz collections, soul reissues, Japanese techno). Most sublabels function as series imprints rather than fully independent operations.

Blues and Soul Origins (1975–1990s)

P-Vine began as a specialist reissue label for pre-war and postwar American blues. The early catalog (1980–1990) concentrated on Chicago blues (Magic Sam, Buddy Guy / Junior Wells, Eddie Taylor, Magic Slim, John Littlejohn, Robert Lockwood Jr.), Texas blues (Lightnin’ Hopkins, Johnny Winter), and soul (O.V. Wright, Irma Thomas, Shirley Brown, Lyn Collins). The James Brown universe — The J.B.’s, Fred Wesley, Bobby Byrd — was also licensed heavily from the start, as were The Sugarhill Gang and Grandmaster Flash & The Furious Five, making P-Vine an early conduit for hip hop in Japan (from 1989).

The label’s deep blues and soul expertise is encoded in its sublabel network: Blues ‘N’ Soul Masters, Modern / Kent Blues Treasures, Anthology Of The Blues Archive Series, Great Unknown Blues Singers, Jewel / Paula Jazz Master Collection, Stax On P-Vine, and many others function as curated reissue series with Japanese liner notes and mastering.

Key internal figures on the catalog side include liner note writer and supervisor Toshikazu Kanazawa (144 releases) and compiler Hiroshi Suzuki (128 releases), who shaped the editorial framing of most P-Vine blues and jazz reissues.

The Indie / Alternative Licensing Arm (Late 1990s–2010s)

From the late 1990s onward, P-Vine became a significant Japan-market licensor for Western independent and avant-garde music. The catalog during this period spans multiple strands:

Krautrock retrospectives: P-Vine issued the full Can back-catalog (2004–2006, 2006 second series) and the complete NEU! catalog (2001–2002, 2007, 2012). Holger Czukay’s solo work also appeared on the label. These reissues were significant for bringing canonical Krautrock to Japanese audiences in a well-annotated format.

Post-rock / experimental: Godspeed You Black Emperor! Japan releases (F# A# ∞, Slow Riot, Lift Yr. Skinny Fists) appeared on P-Vine from 2000. Set Fire To Flames also appeared via P-Vine. Fennesz had a sustained presence (2002–2025), as did Jim O’Rourke (1998–2015) — both of whom are also connected through the Fenn O’Berg trio (The Return Of Fenn O’Berg, 2002, PCD-23325).

US indie rock: Superchunk had their entire back-catalog issued on P-Vine in 2002, and continued releasing through the 2000s. Deerhoof had a sustained relationship (2003–2011), with Japanese editions of Apple O’, Milk Man, The Runners Four, Friend Opportunity, Reveille, and Offend Maggie. Mice Parade had 10 releases across 1998–2022.

No wave / post-punk: Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s full catalog was reissued on P-Vine in 2009–2010: Press Color, Mambo Nassau, Zulu Rock, Suspense, One For The Soul, Best Off. Einstürzende Neubauten had their early catalog reissued in a single 2008 batch: Kollaps, Zeichnungen Des Patienten O.T., Halber Mensch, Haus Der Luege, Fuenf Auf Der Nach Oben Offenen Richterskala, plus Alles Wieder Offen (their then-current LP).

Folk / singer-songwriter: Bonnie “Prince” Billy (9 releases, 1999–2010), Cat Power (7 releases, 1996–2006), Sufjan Stevens (8 releases, 2005–2010), David Grubbs (10 releases, 2000–2013), The Innocence Mission (11 releases, 2003–2015).

Art-pop / avant: David Sylvian had 18 releases (2007–2022), placing Japan-market versions of his solo catalog alongside collaborative works. Antony And The Johnsons appeared from 2005. Arthur Russell reissues arrived in 2005–2006 (World Of Echo, Springfield).

Red Krayola: 14 releases spanning 1998–2016 — one of the larger relationships in the experimental rock space.

Sister Vanilla / JAMC Connection

P-Vine released the debut (and only) album by Sister Vanilla — the recording alias of Linda Reid, younger sister of Jim Reid and William Reid of The Jesus And Mary Chain.

Little Pop Rock (2005, PCD-23573) was a Japan-exclusive release. It was produced by all three Reids together with Ben Lurie (JAMC’s guitarist), who also co-wrote all songs. The album preceded the UK release on Chemikal Underground by two years, with P-Vine functioning as the first label to put the record out. See Linda Reid for the full account.

Open Questions
