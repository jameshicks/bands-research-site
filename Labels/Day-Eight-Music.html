Day Eight Music

Record label. Discogs label ID: 27224. Label code: LC 7898. Distributed via Atlantic/WEA in Europe. Sublabel: Day Eight US (Discogs ID: 417273).

Overview

Jonas Hellborg’s own imprint, founded in Sweden in the early 1980s and used as his primary release vehicle through the mid-1990s. Catalog numbers prefixed DEM (vinyl) / DEMCD (CD). The label is described on Discogs simply as “Swedish label. Label Code: LC 7898.” Hellborg’s father Bo Hellborg served as production coordinator and business manager across virtually the entire catalog; Kenneth Rasmusson handled production coordination and logistics on most releases from 1989 onward.

Day Eight Music released not only Hellborg’s own albums but also records by guest artists who worked with him — Michael Shrieve, Trilok Gurtu, Ginger Baker, Jens Johansson, Anders Johansson, and Deadline. Hellborg produced almost everything on the label. The earliest catalog entry is a 7” single (Let Me Try with Robert Blennerhed, DEM S001). The first full album (All Our Steps…, 1983) was co-credited to Hellborg, Shrieve, and Michael Smith. After 1996’s Temporal Analogues Of Paradise, Hellborg moved to Bardo Records for subsequent releases.

The most consistent recurring engineers were Lave Lindholm (early-period Sweden sessions), Oz Fritz (New York sessions, 1989–1993), and Scud Noonan (mixing, 1992–1996). Mastering through the vinyl era was handled entirely by Peter Dahl at Polar Studios, Stockholm. Howie Weinberg took over mastering for the CD era (1991–1992 releases). Albert Wiking provided cover photography on 19 releases; Mats Bredberg did art direction / cover design for 12.

Hellborg: Career Context

Jonas Hellborg (born June 7, 1958, Gothenburg) debuted internationally with a solo bass performance at Montreux Jazz Festival in 1981 (his first release, The Bassic Thing, appeared that year on Amigo). He became best known from his years with Mahavishnu Orchestra and his collaboration with John McLaughlin. Other documented associations include Bill Laswell’s Material, the Niels & The New York Street Percussionists, RAF (6), Shining Path, Art Metal, and the Jonas Hellborg Group. After the Day Eight era he continued on Bardo Records into the 2000s, working extensively with Indian musicians.

Notable Releases in Detail

Axis (DEM LP 006, 1986) — Hellborg’s most NYC-connected release of the period. Tracked at Electric Lady and RPM Studios, New York, with additional work in Sweden. Bernie Worrell on keyboards, Anton Fier on drums, Bernard Fowler and Cindy Shrieve on vocals, Nicky Skopelitis programming Fairlight. Robert Musso engineered in New York; Lave Lindholm in Sweden. This is the record that placed Hellborg firmly in the downtown-jazz / Laswell orbit.

Bass (DEM CD 009, 1988) — A straightforward band record anchored by the Ginger Baker–Hellborg partnership that would continue into the early 1990s. Bernie Worrell again on keys; Bernard Fowler on vocals. Hellborg self-produced.

Dissident (DEMCD 027, 1991) — Deadline project, the most explicitly Bill Laswell-inflected thing on the label. Laswell co-produced alongside Hellborg; Bootsy Collins adds a second bass voice alongside Laswell and Hellborg. Howie Weinberg mastered at Masterdisk. Oz Fritz recorded.

The Silent Life (DEM CD 026, 1991) — Solo acoustic bass, Hellborg alone. Oz Fritz recorded; Vivi Kuhnle designed.

Ode To A Tractor (DEMCD 030, 1992) — The most ad-hoc collective on the label: Bill Laswell, Peter Brötzmann, RAF (6), Jens and Anders Johansson, and Jamal Evans. Hellborg produced and held the session together.

Abstract Logic (DEM 035, 1995) / Temporal Analogues Of Paradise (DEM036, 1996) — The Hellborg–Shawn Lane collaborations; represent the final phase of the Day Eight era. Kofi Baker (Ginger Baker’s son) on the first; Jeff Sipe (as “Apt. Q-258”) on the second.

  • Distribution arrangement with Atlantic/WEA: terms and start date not in Discogs
