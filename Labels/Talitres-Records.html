Overview

Talitres Records is a French independent label based in Bordeaux, founded in 2001 by Sean Bouchard and Xavier Simon. Simon departed early to launch his own imprint, Drunk Dog, leaving Bouchard to develop the label’s identity in indie pop, folk, and post-punk. In 2012 the label rebranded as simply Talitres with a new logo design. Its catalogue of over 120 releases spans three roughly equal decades of activity, with a roster that includes Emily Jane White, Motorama, and Flotation Toy Warning; beyond releasing records, the operation also encompasses booking and publishing arms.

Notes (web)

Xavier Simon’s departure and the Drunk Dog split

The split appears to have been amicable. In an interview with indiepoprock.fr (undated, but referencing the label’s early period), Sean Bouchard says: “J’ai donc fondé Talitres en août 2000 avec mon ami Xavier Simon, dorénavant propriétaire du label Drunk Dog.” The phrase “mon ami” (my friend) is the only tone indicator in any public source; Bouchard treats it as a neutral fact rather than something fraught. (indiepoprock.fr — Interview de Sean Bouchard)

A separate 2025 interview on jbcqvf.fr has Bouchard describe “mounting a record label with a friend who left the boat fairly quickly” — again no name given there, no negative language. (jbcqvf.fr, Feb 2025)

No source addresses whether Simon’s departure shaped Talitres’ direction. None of the interviews with Bouchard discuss what changed artistically or operationally after Simon left, nor what Simon contributed during the brief co-founding period. The label went on under Bouchard with a small core team; the indiemusic.fr interview mentions “Édouard Massonnat et Myriam Philip” as team members by 2016 with no mention of Simon. (indiemusic.fr, 2016)

The Drunk Dog label has a Bandcamp presence (drunkdog.bandcamp.com) but carries no public information identifying its founder or history. Discogs lists it as label ID 51573.

What was not found. No source — French or English, press or interview — gives the reason for Simon’s departure, its timing relative to the first release, or any account of what his role was in the label’s early A&R or operations. The question of strategic impact on Talitres’ direction cannot be answered from available sources; Bouchard has not discussed it in any indexed interview.

Vladislav Parshin, Maxim Polivanov, and the Russian artist cluster

Parshin and Polivanov are Motorama members, not separate acts. Vladislav Parshin is Motorama’s founder, vocalist, and primary songwriter; Maxim Polivanov is Motorama’s guitarist. Their presence as individual roster entries on Discogs reflects their member credits on Motorama releases, not separate solo signings. (kierontyler.blogspot.com, July 2013)

How Motorama came to Talitres. Sean Bouchard attended Tallinn Music Week in March 2012, where he encountered Motorama playing in a Design and Architecture gallery. He signed them shortly after; their second album Calendar was released on Talitres in November 2012. The signing helped the label subsequently secure a US distribution deal with MVD. (Wikipedia — Talitres, as of 2026-10-26)

Parshin’s side project Utro (Утро) followed via the same Motorama relationship. Parshin formed Утро (2) (Utro, “The Morning”) in 2010 as a parallel Russian-language post-punk project. Talitres released Utro’s mini-album Солнце (The Sun) in October/November 2015. The Talitres welcome announcement describes Utro as “Formed by Vladislav Parshin (Motorama) in 2010,” making explicit that the Utro signing was an extension of the existing Motorama–Talitres relationship rather than an independent discovery. (talitres.com/en/news/utro-utro.html)

What was not found. No interview with Sean Bouchard or Motorama members gives a more granular account of the Tallinn Music Week encounter — whether Bouchard approached them, how quickly the deal was concluded, or whether an intermediary was involved. The Kieron Tyler 2013 interview with Parshin only notes in passing that “French label Talitres picked Motorama up” without elaborating on the mechanism. No source names a specific intermediary, booking agent, or journalist who facilitated the introduction.
