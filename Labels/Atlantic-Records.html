Atlantic Records

Discogs label ID: 681 (as “Atlantic”; parent: Atlantic Recording Corporation, ID 23937) Founded: 1947, New York City Founders: Ahmet Ertegun, Herb Abramson (with financial backing from Dr. Vahdi Sabi) Key subsequent partners: Nesuhi Ertegun (jazz A&R), Jerry Wexler (joined 1953) Acquired by: Warner Bros.-Seven Arts, 1967 Current ownership: Warner Music Group

One of the foundational American independent labels of the postwar era. Founded as a small operation in a New York hotel room, Atlantic became synonymous with R&B, soul, and jazz excellence through the 1950s and 60s before pivoting decisively into rock in the late 1960s — signing Led Zeppelin, Yes, Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young, and others. The label’s consistent thread across three decades was a tightly integrated production team: engineer/producer Tom Dowd, arranger/producer Arif Mardin, and partner/producer Jerry Wexler.

History

Founding and the R&B Years (1947–1955)

Ahmet Ertegun — son of the Turkish ambassador to the United States — and Herb Abramson launched Atlantic Records in 1947 operating from a single room at the Jefferson Hotel in Washington D.C. before relocating to New York. The early catalog was built on blues, boogie-woogie, and jump R&B. Key early signings included Big Joe Turner, Ruth Brown (who anchored the label financially in its first years), and LaVern Baker. Doo-wop group The Clovers and vocal group The Cardinals were also early roster mainstays.

Jerry Wexler joined in 1953 as a partner (having coined the term “rhythm and blues” as a Billboard journalist), bringing editorial instincts and production energy that sharpened the label’s identity. Tom Dowd came aboard around the same period as chief recording engineer — his earliest Atlantic-credited work dates to 1953.

Herb Abramson was drafted in 1953 and the label’s day-to-day fell to Ertegun and Wexler. ATCO Records (originally “Atlantic’s Company”) was launched in 1955 as a pop-oriented sublabel; Bobby Darin would become its breakout star. Miriam Abramson ran operations during Herb’s absence.

The Ray Charles Period and Jazz Division (1954–1960)

Ray Charles joined Atlantic in 1952 (his catalog from those sessions appeared under the label through the mid-1950s onward) and his recordings there — including The Genius of Ray Charles (1959) — defined Atlantic’s blend of gospel fervor and R&B. Tom Dowd engineered many of these sessions starting in the mid-1950s. Charles departed for ABC-Paramount in 1959.

Ahmet’s brother Nesuhi Ertegun joined around 1955 and took charge of Atlantic’s jazz division, transforming it into a serious catalog. Nesuhi’s signings included:

  • The Modern Jazz Quartet — recorded for Atlantic 1956 onward
  • Charles Mingus — albums including Pithecanthropus Erectus (1956), The Clown (1957), Blues & Roots (1960), Mingus Ah Um (recorded 1959; released on Columbia, but Atlantic held earlier material)
  • John Coltrane — recorded at Atlantic 1959–1961, producing landmark albums Giant Steps (1960), My Favorite Things (1961), Olé Coltrane (1961)
  • Ornette Coleman — the free jazz breakthrough sequence: Something Else!!!! (1958), Tomorrow Is the Question! (1959), The Shape of Jazz to Come (1959), Change of the Century (1960), Free Jazz (1961)
  • Jimmy Giuffre, Lennie Tristano, Chris Connor, Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers

Tom Dowd engineered most of these jazz sessions. Nesuhi’s jazz roster gave Atlantic a reputation for artistic seriousness that sat alongside Wexler’s commercial R&B instincts.

Soul and R&B Dominance (1960–1967)

Through the early-to-mid 1960s Atlantic consolidated its position as the premier soul and R&B label in America. Key artists of this era:

  • Solomon Burke — signed 1961, credited as an architect of soul
  • Ben E. King — departed The Drifters for a solo career at Atlantic
  • The Drifters — Atlantic from 1953; charted throughout the early 1960s
  • The Coasters — signed to ATCO sublabel
  • Wilson Pickett — signed 1964; recorded at Stax in Memphis (Tom Dowd engineering) and later at FAME Studios in Muscle Shoals
  • Otis Redding — distributed through Atlantic via Stax
  • Sam & Dave — similarly distributed via Stax
  • Joe Tex, Don Covay, Clarence Carter

Bert Berns was active as a songwriter and producer at Atlantic during this period (1963–65) before leaving to co-found Bang Records in 1965.

Arif Mardin joined as a staff arranger around 1962–63, initially working in a supervisory capacity on jazz releases before moving into full production. His early credits include supervising sessions for Max Roach, Dorothy Ashby, and the Young Rascals.

Aretha Franklin and the Muscle Shoals Revolution (1967)

The single most transformative event in Atlantic’s mid-period history was signing Aretha Franklin from Columbia Records in 1966. Her first Atlantic session, produced by Jerry Wexler at FAME Studios in Muscle Shoals, Alabama, yielded I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You (1967) and Respect (1967). The sessions drew on the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section — and established a template Wexler would return to repeatedly.

Tom Dowd’s Engineering Role

Tom Dowd (Discogs ID: 180298) was Atlantic’s staff engineer from approximately 1953 and became one of the most important figures in American recorded sound. His Atlantic-credited work spans:

  • 1953–1958: All of Atlantic’s core R&B and jazz sessions — Ray Charles, LaVern Baker, the MJQ, Ruth Brown, Chris Connor
  • 1959–1965: The jazz modernist sessions — Coltrane, Coleman, Mingus; also rock and roll transitional material
  • 1966–1975: Moved into producing as well as engineering — Cream (Disraeli Gears, Wheels of Fire, 1967–68), The Allman Brothers, Aretha Franklin

Dowd was an early adopter of multi-track recording, working with 8-track machines in the late 1950s when most studios used two. Atlantic’s profile notes that he developed special stereo techniques that he described as “sound in the round” — broad center imaging rather than extreme left-right placement.

Arif Mardin’s Production Career

Arif Mardin (Discogs ID: 15446) arrived at Atlantic after winning the Quincy Jones scholarship to Berklee. His Atlantic production credits (from the Discogs data):

  • 1968: Son-of-a-Preacher Man (Dusty Springfield), Eddie Harris, Brook Benton, King Curtis
  • 1970: Dusty in Memphis (co-producer), Roberta Flack, Brook Benton’s “Rainy Night in Georgia” sessions
  • 1974–75: Average White Band (AWB, Cut the Cake), Bee Gees
  • 1975–81: Chaka Khan, Bette Midler, Spinners

Mardin’s full career at Atlantic is covered in detail at Arif Mardin.

The Rock Pivot (1967–1972)

The 1967 sale to Warner Bros.-Seven Arts gave Atlantic financial stability and wider distribution without immediate interference in creative decisions. Ahmet Ertegun moved aggressively into rock, signing British and American acts:

  • Cream — signed 1966 (UK deal); Fresh Cream, Disraeli Gears (1967), Wheels of Fire (1968)
  • Led Zeppelin — signed by Ahmet in 1968; Led Zeppelin (1969), Led Zeppelin II (1969), through In Through the Out Door (1979). Led Zeppelin’s own imprint Swan Song was an Atlantic sublabel (ID: 34571)
  • Yes — signed 1969; released through Atlantic from Yes (1969) through the Tormato era
  • Crosby, Stills & Nash — debut 1969; Déjà Vu (1970, as CSNY) was one of the label’s biggest sellers
  • King Crimson — In the Court of the Crimson King (1969) on Atlantic
  • Emerson, Lake & Palmer — signed 1970
  • The Allman Brothers Band — signed 1969; Tom Dowd produced and engineered their key records
  • Iron Butterfly — signed late 1960s
  • MC5 — signed 1969
  • Mott the Hoople — Atlantic 1969
  • Vanilla Fudge — ATCO, 1967 onward
  • Buffalo Springfield — Ahmet produced some sessions

By the early 1970s, Atlantic’s rock roster had become as important as its soul catalog.

WEA and the 1970s Expansion

When Kinney National Company purchased Warner Bros.-Seven Arts in 1969, Atlantic became part of a larger conglomerate. The Warner-Elektra-Atlantic (WEA) distribution network was created after Elektra Records was purchased from Jac Holzman in 1970. Ahmet Ertegun served on the committee overseeing all three labels.

The mid-1970s Atlantic roster expanded further:

  • Average White Band — Scottish funk act signed 1974, produced by Arif Mardin
  • Spinners — r&b group signed 1972, produced by Thom Bell and Arif Mardin
  • ABBA — international distribution deal; Atlantic handled US releases from 1974
  • Bette Midler — signed 1972; produced by Barry Manilow, later Mardin
  • Roberta Flack and Donny Hathaway — soulful duo; Hathaway’s Everything Is Everything and subsequent albums

The Saul Bass-designed “W” lozenge logo appeared on sleeves starting January 1975.

Connections

  • ATCO Records — pop/rock sublabel, co-run from the beginning
  • Swan Song — Led Zeppelin’s imprint, administered by Atlantic
  • Cotillion — soul/gospel sublabel
  • Bang Records — founded by Bert Berns after leaving Atlantic
  • Stax Records — Atlantic had a distribution deal 1960–1968 (Otis Redding, Sam & Dave, Booker T. & the M.G.’s)
  • FAME Studios — Muscle Shoals recording base used by Wexler for Aretha, Pickett, and others
  • Warner Bros. Records — acquired Atlantic in 1967; later became co-member of WEA group
  • Elektra Records — WEA partner from 1970
