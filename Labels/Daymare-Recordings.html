Daymare Recordings

Discogs ID: 29944 Active: ~2002/2003–present Origin: Japan (Tokyo)

Japanese indie label founded by Tadashi Hamada, operating as an imprint of DIW Products Group (Discogs parent ID 82511). Known for experimental, metal, and avant-garde releases, with a strong interest in Japanese and international heavy/noise music. Co-released Merzbow + Boris (3)‘s Rock Dream (2007). Also released Japanese pressings of Red Sparowes albums (see Red Sparowes). Boris releases include Smile -Live At Wolf Creek- (2008), Chapter Ahead Being Fake (2009, split with Torche), Klatter (2011), and Präparat (2013). Tadashi Hamada (ID 1925842) credited as A&R on several Boris/Daymare releases.

Label History and Operational Relationship with DIW Products Group

DIW Products Group structure

DIW Products Group is the label/production arm of Disk Union, the major Japanese independent music retailer. The acronym DIW derives from “Discs in the World,” a music magazine originally sold at Disk Union stores. DIW Products Group functions as an umbrella for multiple imprints, each with distinct curatorial identities; the group’s online storefront at diwproducts.net lists Daymare Recordings alongside Diwphalanx Records, Niw! Records, SOMETHIN’COOL, THINK!, and others. [source: DIW Records - Wikipedia, Diwphalanx Records - Wikipedia, diwproducts.net]

DIW Records (the original jazz-focused label, home to Art Ensemble of Chicago, David Murray, John Zorn, Peter Brötzmann, etc.) is a separate imprint within the same group — a subsidiary of Disk Union, not a parent of Daymare. The two labels share the DIW Products Group distribution umbrella but operate with entirely separate curatorial remits. [source: DIW Records - Wikipedia]

Shared distribution infrastructure

Daymare’s catalog is sold and fulfilled through Disk Union’s retail and online infrastructure. The label’s international mail order contact is daymarerecordings@diskunion.co.jp, and its full catalog of approximately 380 products appears on diskunion.net/diw/. Release credits on Daymare titles (e.g. Boris/Merzbow’s Klatter, 2011; Gensho expanded edition, 2016) are credited jointly as “Daymare Recordings / DIW / Disk Union,” indicating that manufacturing, distribution, and retail logistics flow through the shared DIW/Disk Union apparatus. [source: diskunion.net Daymare page, Discogs releases 2707906 and 8270696]

Independent curatorial identity

Despite the shared distribution back-end, Daymare functions with a distinctly autonomous curatorial identity that is Hamada’s own, not derived from DIW Records’ jazz/avant-garde roster. The label’s focus — doom, sludge, drone, post-metal, noise, grindcore, black metal, and experimental music — places it in a different scene entirely from DIW Records’ output. Hamada defines the label’s ethos around a broad conception of “hardcore” as self-awareness and carving out one’s own space; he programs the label’s flagship annual live event, Leave Them All Behind (first held 2009, at Daikanyama Unit, Tokyo), which has featured Sunn O))), Isis, Godflesh, Neurosis, Converge, Earth, Sleep, Envy, and Boris alongside Japanese acts including Endon, Coffins, and Cohol. [source: Japan Times, 2014-07-08, daymarerecordings.com]

Hamada also handles A&R decisions independently, as evidenced by his individual credit on Discogs release pages for Boris/Daymare titles (Discogs person ID 1925842).

Japan licensing and distribution for international labels

In addition to releasing original Japanese material, Daymare serves as Japan licensee and/or distributor for several international heavy music labels, including Hydra Head Records, Southern Lord Records, Neurot Recordings, Ipecac Recordings, Third Man Records, and Sargent House. This function is handled through the Disk Union/DIW infrastructure but is driven by Hamada’s A&R relationships. [source: web search results citing Riffipedia/Metal Archives]

Summary

Daymare Recordings is best understood as an autonomous imprint with its own curatorial identity, using DIW Products Group as its distribution, retail, and manufacturing platform. It is not operationally integrated with DIW Records (the jazz label) beyond both being Disk Union imprints under the DIW Products Group umbrella. Hamada controls A&R and programming; Disk Union handles logistics.
