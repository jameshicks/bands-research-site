Overview

Bubble Core Records is the US independent label founded and operated by Adam Pierce, best known as the primary release vehicle for his Mice Parade project. The label is centered on Pierce’s own output but has consistently extended to collaborators, friends, and outlier acts that share his aesthetic orbit — ranging from lo-fi shoegaze and post-rock in the early 1990s through experimental electronics, free jazz, Icelandic folk, and ambient guitar music.

Discogs credits 73 releases to the label. Bubble Core operates in tandem with FatCat Records for many Mice Parade records: Pierce typically issues US pressings on Bubble Core while FatCat handles UK/European editions. Several Mice Parade releases have also appeared on Japanese labels (P-Vine Records, Afterhours Inc.).

The label’s origins predate its first Discogs entry: the tagline claims 1989 for a cassette tape, with the first formal catalog number (BC-004) appearing in 1995 and the earliest Discogs-credited pressing dated 1992.

Founder

Adam Pierce (Discogs ID: 14704) — drummer, multi-instrumentalist, composer, engineer, and producer. Pierce plays nearly all instruments on Mice Parade recordings and produces/engineers much of the label’s output. He is also a member of The Dylan Group (with Dylan Cristy), HiM (with Doug Scharin), Swirlies, Igloo (2), and The Philistines Jr.

Label Context and Aesthetics

Bubble Core spans a wide stylistic range across its catalog: early releases sit in lo-fi shoegaze and experimental cassette culture (1992–1996); the mid-period is dominated by the guitar-and-percussion post-rock of The Dylan Group and the polyrhythmic instrumental music of Mice Parade and HiM (1997–2003); later releases expand to Japanese ambient/electronic (Nobukazu Takemura), Icelandic folk-ensemble music (Stórsveit Nix Noltes), and Philadelphia post-rock (Relay). The label’s curatorial logic is personal rather than genre-defined — most artists have a direct connection to Pierce himself.

The key co-release relationship is with FatCat Records (Discogs ID: 264), the Brighton-based UK label that co-released several Mice Parade records (Obrigado Saudade, Bem-Vinda Vontade) and distributed the project internationally. FatCat’s own roster (múm, Sigur Rós, Amiina) overlaps with the Icelandic aesthetic present in the Stórsveit Nix Noltes release.

The Corners Of The Mouth (1997) compilation is notable as a document of the label’s reach into the mid-1990s UK/European electronic underground — Mouse On Mars, Oval, Seefeel, and Scanner alongside Pierce’s own projects, packaged as a benefit for a music education organization called “The School Of Sound.”
