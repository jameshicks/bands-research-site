Bella Union

Discogs ID: 2201. UK independent record label. Founded 1997 by Simon Raymonde (Cocteau Twins) following the Cocteau Twins’ disbandment. Based in Brighton (6–7 Gloucester St, Brighton BN1 4EW). ~2,699 releases in Discogs. Sublabels: Series 7 (instrumental concept series, 2001), Bella Union Records, Popstock (2), Popstock Records (Spanish imprint).

Note: The Discogs label profile describes BU as “UK label run by former Cocteau Twins’ multi-instrumentalist Simon Raymonde since 1997” — Robin Guthrie (the other CT co-founder) is not listed as an ongoing operator, consistent with Guthrie having stepped back to pursue solo work while Raymonde remained the day-to-day label head.

One of the most consistently artistically credible UK indie labels of the 2000s–2020s. Known for a warmly eclectic aesthetic spanning post-rock, chamber pop, indie folk, singer-songwriter, and experimental — built around Raymonde’s personal taste rather than any genre mandate. Discovered Fleet Foxes, Beach House, Father John Misty; worked with Dirty Three, Lift To Experience, The Czars (and John Grant’s subsequent solo career), Midlake, Explosions In The Sky, Spiritualized, Patti Smith, Mercury Rev, The Flaming Lips.

Founding

Raymonde founded Bella Union in 1997 immediately after Cocteau Twins dissolved. The label’s first release (BELLA CD 1) was his own solo single “Blame Someone Else” (1997). The early Bella Union shared the Cocteau Twins’ aesthetic orbit — dream pop, post-rock, European song — but expanded rapidly.

Also on the early roster: Robin Guthrie (the other Cocteau Twins co-founder, who released solo records and the Violet Indiana project on Bella Union). The label effectively served as a home for the post-Cocteau Twins extended network before finding its own identity.

Dirty Three

Dirty Three (Australian post-rock trio: Warren Ellis, Mick Turner, Jim White) were among Bella Union’s most sustained early presences, with five studio albums released on the label: Ocean Songs (1998), Whatever You Love, You Are (2000), She Has No Strings Apollo (2003), Cinder (2005), and Toward The Low Sun (2012). Their long relationship anchored the label’s credibility in the post-rock world.

Lift To Experience

Lift to Experience (The Texas–Jerusalem Crossroads, 2001, BELLACD 23) — a double album of apocalyptic Texan rock — remains one of the label’s most celebrated early releases.

The Czars and John Grant

The Czars were Bella Union artists from 2000 (Before… But Longer) through Goodbye (2004). Their dissolution brought frontman John Grant to a solo career, and Queen Of Denmark (2010, BELLACD235X) became one of the label’s most critically acclaimed records — Grant became a cornerstone Bella Union artist, releasing further albums Pale Green Ghosts and Grey Tickles, Black Pressure.

Midlake

Midlake (Denton, Texas) joined with Bamnan And Slivercork (2004). The Trials Of Van Occupanther (2006, BEU 412) brought them to widespread attention; The Courage Of Others (2010, BELLACD224) was widely acclaimed. One of the label’s most important US signings of the mid-2000s.

Breakthrough: Fleet Foxes (2008)

Fleet Foxes (Seattle) signed to Bella Union for UK/Europe. Their self-titled debut (2008, BELLACD167) became a commercial and critical breakthrough — one of the most acclaimed albums of 2008 globally. Helplessness Blues (2011) followed. The Fleet Foxes success transformed the label’s profile and resources.

Beach House

Beach House (Baltimore) joined in 2007 with their self-titled debut (Beach House, BELLACD144P) and Devotion (2007, BELLACD160P). Teen Dream (2010, BELLACD225) was the breakthrough; Bloom (2012, BELLACD334) consolidated their status. One of the label’s most consistent long-term acts.

Father John Misty

Father John Misty (Josh Tillman, formerly J. Tillman on the label) joined with Fear Fun (2012, BELLACD332P). One of the label’s most prominent artists of the 2010s, releasing I Love You, Honeybear and Pure Comedy through Bella Union.

Catalog Structure

Bella Union has used several catalog number prefixes over its lifetime:

BELLA CD / BELLACDCD albumEarly series, 1997 onward
BELLA V / BELLAV7” vinyl singleLong-running singles series, from BELLAV 8 (1998) to BELLAV 800+ (2018+); ~235 releases
BEUVinyl album / CDMid-period album series, 2004–2012 approx.
BELLA [number] CDCD albumLater period catalog numbering
BELLA [number] VLP vinylLater period vinyl

The BELLAV series is the primary 7” singles spine: it runs from BELLAV 8 (Sing-Sing’s “Feels Like Summer”, 1998) through at least BELLAV 800+ and encompasses ~235 pressings. It is not a self-contained “7-inch subscription series” but rather the standard singles prefix for vinyl format releases.

Series 7 Sublabel

Series 7 (Discogs ID: 338968) is a distinct sublabel of Bella Union — not the same as the BELLAV singles catalog. It was a bespoke concept series conceived and curated by Simon Raymonde, launched 2001:

  • Concept: Seven instrumental albums, by seven different artists, each exactly seven tracks, recorded in seven days (from conception to completion), capped by a limited-edition compilation of all seven artists with a bonus track each
  • Completed: Five of the planned seven releases; sixth and seventh were never completed
  • Active years: 2001–2002

Open Questions

Notes (web) — Series 7 gap research (2026-06-29)

Web trawl conducted to answer: were Series 7 Parts 6 and 7 ever commissioned, and if so, by whom?

Finding: no evidence found. Extensive searches across music press (Drowned in Sound, The Line of Best Fit, Loud and Quiet, AllMusic, Brainwashed, Norman Records), Wikipedia (Bella Union article and Variaciones Espectrales article), Simon Raymonde interviews (Line of Best Fit 20-year retrospective, Loud and Quiet, audiofemme.com, culturecollide.com), and fan sources produced no named artists for Parts 6 or 7. The most informative fan source (verian.wordpress.com, Aug 2016) explicitly notes “Information is sketchy” and identifies only “5, maybe, of the 7” — acknowledging uncertainty even about which five were released. No commissioning announcements, no cancellation statements, no label communications on the topic have been located.

Conclusion: Parts 6 and 7 appear to have been planned (the concept was explicitly “seven artists”) but the artists were never publicly identified and the releases were never made. The most likely cause is that the label moved on after 2002 without completing the series — consistent with Bella Union’s rapid expansion in that period — but no primary source confirms this. The question remains open at the primary-source level.

Notes (web) — Françoiz Breut signing (2026-09-28)

Web trawl conducted to answer: how did Françoiz Breut come to be signed to Bella Union?

Structural finding: the debut was a UK license, not an original signing. Breut’s self-titled debut album was released in France in 1997 on Lithium (Vincent Chauvier’s label) and in the UK in 1998 on Bella Union (BELLACD9). This is a territorial licensing arrangement rather than an original signing. Her second album Vingt à trente mille jours (2001, BELLACD25) was released on Lithium/Virgin France and Bella Union — again a France/UK split. (Wikipedia, Françoiz Breut album, as of 2026-09-28; Wikipedia, Vingt à trente mille jours, as of 2026-09-28)

Dominique A connection. The debut was written and arranged by her then-boyfriend Dominique Ané (Dominique A), a leading figure in 1990s French indie-chanson who recorded for Lithium. Lithium was the common thread: both artists were on Chauvier’s label in France. Raymonde’s decision to license Breut’s debut for the UK likely came via the Lithium relationship. (Wikipedia, Françoiz Breut, as of 2026-09-28; Benzine Magazine interview, April 2021)

No primary source on record. Every interview with Simon Raymonde checked (Line of Best Fit 20-year retrospective, Loud and Quiet, Long Live Vinyl 2017, Drowned in Sound label focus) mentions Breut only in passing as an early signee — none describes how the connection was made. No interview with Breut herself discusses the Bella Union relationship. The specific mechanism (who approached whom, how Raymonde encountered the record) has not been documented in any publicly available source found.

Conclusion: The Bella Union relationship was built on territorial licensing of Lithium product for the UK/Europe — consistent with the label’s early model of picking up European artists Raymonde admired. The Dominique A authorship of the debut and both artists’ presence on Lithium in France is the most plausible pathway. The precise origin of the Raymonde–Chauvier/Breut connection remains unconfirmed at the primary-source level.

Research Queue

  • Did Bella Union hold a direct licensing deal with Lithium (Vincent Chauvier’s label) covering multiple French artists, or was the Breut license a one-off? — answerable from a contemporary interview with Chauvier or Raymonde, or from the label credits across other Lithium artists’ UK releases.
