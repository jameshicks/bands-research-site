About / History

King Crab Records is a small Australian independent label founded in 2001 by Melbourne musician Mick Turner (of Dirty Three, Tren Brothers, Boxhead Ensemble). The label functions primarily as a vehicle for Turner’s own solo work and for releases by his close collaborators and associates within the Melbourne underground and post-rock scene.

The catalog is deliberately compact — roughly a dozen releases across two decades — with an emphasis on idiosyncratic solo records and limited editions. Release frequency is irregular; years pass between entries. The label’s most notable international connection is the 2007 Australian edition of Blue Trees (KC 007), co-credited to Mick Turner / Tren Brothers, which shares its master with the US Drag City edition (DC344CD). This release connects the label directly to the Drag City orbit.

King Crab’s earliest releases (2001–2002) show a curatorial impulse beyond purely Turner’s own output: the label issued a Warren Ellis solo violin piece, a Joel Silbersher full-length, an EP by Burd Early (James Angelos), and a release by C.B.M. — a project with lyrics by actor/musician Noah Taylor. By the mid-2000s the catalog contracts around Turner himself. The 2013 reissue of Don’t Tell The Driver (originally a Japan-only CDr from 2005) brought that record to a domestic Australian audience.

The label’s final documented release is Swimmers (2018) by Caroline No (2), a duo of Caroline Kennedy and Helen Johnstone, with Turner contributing guitar and keyboards — suggesting King Crab remains a platform for Turner’s broader circle even in its later, quieter years.
