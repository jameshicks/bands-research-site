BYG Records

Origin: Paris, France Founded: 1967 Founders: Jean Karakos (Jean Georgakarakos), Jean-Luc Young, Fernand Boruso Discogs label ID: 13232 Parent (current): Charly Acquisitions Ltd (Discogs: 554208)

French free jazz and avant-garde label, co-founded by Jean Karakos alongside Jean-Luc Young and Fernand Boruso. Its most concentrated activity was 1969–1971, when it documented American free jazz musicians recording in Paris — largely connected to the Actuel Festival / Pan-African Festival in Algiers. The core output appeared under the Actuel sublabel imprint.

Pan-African Festival / Algiers Connection

The 1969 Pan-African Cultural Festival (Algiers, July 1969)

The First Pan-African Cultural Festival was held in Algiers, 21 July – 1 August 1969, organized by the Algerian government under Houari Boumédiène. It drew African heads of state, intellectuals, and artists, and was attended by Black American exile figures including Eldridge Cleaver (Black Panther Party), who was living in Algiers at the time. The festival had a strongly post-colonial, Third Worldist political character. [source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/]

American jazz musicians who performed at Algiers included: Archie Shepp, Grachan Moncur III, Sunny Murray, Dave Burrell, Clifford Thornton, Alan Silva, Burton Greene, Lester Bowie, Malachi Flavors, Jeanne Lee, Randy Weston, Philly Joe Jones, Cal Massey, and Hank Mobley, among others. [source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/] Shepp performed wearing a djellaba and declared “We’re black Americans, we’re Africans from the United States, we’re Africans first and foremost” — embedding the performance in an explicitly Pan-Africanist politics. [source: https://isaath.com/archie-shepp-algeria/]

The Recruitment and Paris Sessions (August–Autumn 1969)

Jacques Bisceglia, a jazz photographer working with BYG who was present at the Algiers festival, used the occasion to recruit musicians for Paris recording sessions. Specifically, it was Claude Delcloo — editor of Actuel magazine and A&R producer for BYG — who sent Bisceglia to the festival with the explicit brief to recruit musicians and bring them back to Paris. [source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/]

After the festival ended, a group of the participating avant-garde musicians flew to Paris — arriving at Orly airport, where limousines waited to take them to the Prince of Wales Hotel — and recording sessions began in August 1969. Sessions took place primarily at Studio Saravah (Passage des Abbesses) and Studio Davout (Porte de Bagnolet). One session followed another in quick succession. [source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/]

Which artists were at Algiers vs. Paris-only

The confirmed Algiers performers (July 1969) — who then traveled to Paris for BYG sessions — include: Archie Shepp, Grachan Moncur III, Sunny Murray, Dave Burrell, Clifford Thornton, Alan Silva, Burton Greene, Lester Bowie, Malachi Flavors, Jeanne Lee, Randy Weston, Philly Joe Jones, Cal Massey, and Hank Mobley. [source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/]

The following key BYG artists were not at the Algiers festival; they were already in Paris or arrived independently:

  • Art Ensemble of Chicago (Roscoe Mitchell, Joseph Jarman, Lester Bowie, Malachi Favors): arrived in Paris fall 1968, well before the festival. They had been invited to Europe by Claude Delcloo in spring 1969; Mitchell, Jarman, Bowie, and Favors left Chicago on 28 May 1969. After Bisceglia returned from Algiers, contact was made through Steve McCall, who was already in Paris and helped bring the Art Ensemble into the BYG orbit. [sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Ensemble_of_Chicago | https://patricksisson.com/freedom-summer-the-bygactuel-label-documented-a-free-jazz-exodus-to-france/]
  • Anthony Braxton, Leo Smith, and Leroy Jenkins: Braxton moved to Paris in June 1969, following the Art Ensemble. He had been invited to Europe by Delcloo but was “beaten to the draw” by the Art Ensemble group. His BYG debut was recorded 10 September 1969 at Studio Saravah, with Jenkins, Smith, and Steve McCall. [source: https://www.jazzdiscography.com/Artists/Braxton/brax-1969-1970.php]
  • Don Cherry: recorded Mu (Parts 1 & 2) with Ed Blackwell on 22 August 1969 in Paris. No source confirms Cherry attended the Algiers festival; his presence in Paris at the time appears independent of the Algiers recruitment. [source: https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2015/10/the-greatest-week-in-the-history-of-avant-garde-jazz/] — unconfirmed whether Algiers or Paris-only.

The Paris sessions captured not only the Algiers alumni but this additional cohort of AACM musicians who arrived through Delcloo’s earlier European invitation.

The resulting output was approximately 52 LPs released between 1969 and 1972, all on the Actuel sublabel. [source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/]

The “Actuel” Name: Magazine and Sublabel

Actuel was an avant-garde French cultural magazine. Claude Delcloo was its founder — he created the first version as a kind of fanzine before Jean-François Bizot later expanded it into the more prominent publication. Delcloo was a French drummer with deep connections to the Black American avant-garde scene; at BYG he served as A&R producer and executive producer for the Actuel series, and appeared as a drummer on multiple sessions (including the Africanasia album with Arthur Jones). He was the person who (a) invited AACM musicians to Europe in spring 1969 and (b) dispatched Bisceglia to Algiers to recruit musicians. Jacques Bisceglia served as photographer for the magazine and was Delcloo’s contact for the Algiers recruitment. The BYG record label and the Actuel sublabel name both derive from this magazine, not from any festival. [sources: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/ | https://www.crj-online.org/v11/CRJ-Actuel.php | https://www.bygrecords.com/story]

Note: the “B” in BYG is Boruso (Fernand Boruso), not Bisceglia — a common confusion, flagged by Wikipedia. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYG_Records, as of 2025-06-30]

The Festival Actuel (Amougies, Belgium, October 1969)

The Festival Actuel (also called Festival d’Amougies) was a separate event from the Algiers Pan-African Festival. It was organized by BYG/Actuel in late October 1969 (24–28 October), held in the small Belgian village of Amougies after being banned by French authorities following post-May ‘68 political tensions. Approximately 15,000–20,000 attended. Performers included Pink Floyd, Yes, Soft Machine, Ten Years After, Captain Beefheart, Gong, Art Ensemble of Chicago, Archie Shepp, and Philly Joe Jones. Frank Zappa and Pierre Lattès served as masters of ceremony. [sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYG_Records | https://wiki.killuglyradio.com/wiki/Actuel_Festival | https://patricksisson.com/freedom-summer-the-bygactuel-label-documented-a-free-jazz-exodus-to-france/]

The Festival Actuel was a significant financial expenditure for BYG and is cited as contributing to the label’s eventual disorganization and 1973 bankruptcy. [source: https://patricksisson.com/freedom-summer-the-bygactuel-label-documented-a-free-jazz-exodus-to-france/]

Summary of the Mechanism

The chain was: (1) Claude Delcloo (Actuel magazine founder, BYG A&R) invites AACM musicians to Europe in spring 1969 — Art Ensemble departs Chicago 28 May 1969, Braxton/Smith/Jenkins follow in June; (2) Delcloo sends Bisceglia to the Algiers Pan-African Festival (July 1969) as photographer with explicit brief to recruit musicians; (3) Bisceglia recruits the assembled free jazz musicians on-site (Shepp, Moncur, Murray, Burrell, Thornton, Silva, Greene, Bowie, Flavors, Lee, Weston, Jones, Massey, Mobley, and others); (4) musicians travel from Algiers to Paris (August 1969) — limousines at Orly, Prince of Wales Hotel — for intensive studio sessions at Saravah and Davout; (5) Art Ensemble, Braxton, and other AACM members already in Paris are also brought into the sessions via Steve McCall connection; (6) 52 LPs issued 1969–1972 on the Actuel sublabel; (7) BYG separately organizes the Festival Actuel at Amougies, Belgium (October 1969), a large outdoor festival mixing free jazz with progressive rock. The Actuel sublabel name and the Festival Actuel both take their name from the Actuel magazine, not from the Algiers festival.

Fondation Maeght and the Paris Free Jazz Milieu (1969–1970)

The Fondation Maeght (Saint-Paul-de-Vence, French Riviera) and BYG/Actuel operated in overlapping but institutionally distinct orbits during 1969–1970. They were not the same project, but shared artists, a city (Paris), and a moment.

The Fondation Maeght Concerts

The Fondation Maeght is a private art foundation established by Aimé and Marguerite Maeght in 1964 in Saint-Paul-de-Vence. Beginning in 1969, it hosted a series of free jazz and contemporary music concerts — the “Nuits de la Fondation Maeght” — organized by journalist and critic Daniel Caux (who later co-founded Shandar with Chantal Darcy). The concerts were financed by the Maeght family and recorded for release on Shandar. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandar, as of 2026-07-13; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Caux, as of 2026-07-13]

The Institutional Separation: Shandar vs. BYG

All documented Fondation Maeght recordings were released on Shandar, not BYG. Shandar was a distinct label co-founded by Daniel Caux and Chantal Darcy, financed by the Maeght family. BYG and Shandar were contemporaries in the same Parisian avant-garde milieu but had no documented business relationship. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandar, as of 2026-07-13]

Shared Artists as the Bridge

The connection between BYG/Actuel and the Fondation Maeght milieu is not institutional but personnel-based. Several artists recorded for both:

  • Alan Silva: recorded Luna Surface for BYG Actuel (1969), and appeared as bassist in the Sun Ra Arkestra at the Fondation Maeght (August 1970, released on Shandar). Silva was a key figure in the broader Paris free jazz scene of 1969–1971, working across both contexts. [sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Silva, as of 2026-07-13; https://daily.bandcamp.com/label-profile/byg-records-jazz-label-profile]
  • Cecil Taylor: performed at Fondation Maeght (29 July 1969) — one of the earliest documented free jazz concerts there — and was in Paris for the same 1969 summer that launched the BYG Actuel sessions. No BYG release by Taylor is known; his Paris recordings went to Shandar.
  • Sun Ra: released The Solar-Myth Approach Vols 1 & 2 on BYG Actuel, and separately recorded the 1970 Maeght concerts for Shandar. Both BYG and Shandar released Sun Ra.
  • Albert Ayler: documented at Fondation Maeght (1970, Shandar). Not confirmed as a BYG artist. His presence in Paris in 1970 overlapped with the BYG scene but he did not record for the label.

Chronological Context

The Cecil Taylor Maeght concert (29 July 1969) occurred almost simultaneously with the Algiers Pan-African Cultural Festival (21 July – 1 August 1969), while BYG’s Jacques Bisceglia was recruiting musicians in Algiers. The BYG Paris sessions began in August 1969, just after both events. This places the Maeght concert and the BYG recruitment in the same compressed timeframe — July–August 1969 — as parallel expressions of the same moment: a wave of American free jazz musicians in Europe who were largely invisible at home. [source: https://pan-african-music.com/en/crazy-jazzmen-of-africa-byg-and-the-1969-pan-african-music-festival-in-algeria/]

Daniel Caux and the Maeght Context

Daniel Caux was a Paris-based journalist (writing for Combat, Jazz Hot, and L’Art vivant) who became the leading French advocate for American free jazz and new music in the late 1960s. He organized the 1970 Maeght concerts, which he framed as an introduction to “the great outsiders of Black American free jazz” — bringing Sun Ra outside the United States for the first time. Caux had no documented role at BYG. His institutional home was Shandar, financed by the Maeghts. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Caux, as of 2026-07-13]

Summary

BYG/Actuel and the Fondation Maeght/Shandar concerts represent two parallel but institutionally separate channels through which American free jazz reached France in 1969–1970. BYG operated through the Algiers festival recruitment (Claude Delcloo/Bisceglia) and the Actuel magazine network, producing studio albums at Saravah and Davout. The Maeght concerts were a live concert series organized by Daniel Caux, financed by the Maeght family, recorded for Shandar. The overlap is in personnel — notably Alan Silva and Sun Ra — and in the shared cultural moment, not in business structure or institutional affiliation.
