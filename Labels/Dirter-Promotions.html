Overview

Dirter Promotions is a UK experimental label run by Steve Pittis, in operation since the 1980s and catalogued on Discogs from 1991 onward. Its catalogue spans noise, industrial, and ambient music across four decades, with releases peaking in the 2010s (25 releases) after a measured output in the 1990s and 2000s. Two sublabels—United Dirter and Wildwood—extend the operation, and the roster has drawn international figures such as Merzbow (Masami Akita) alongside UK-based artists including Andrew Liles and Denis Blackham.

Notes (web)

Dirter Promotions and Merzbow

Dirter’s relationship with Merzbow (Masami Akita) is demonstrably ongoing rather than a one-off licensing deal, spanning at minimum 1995–2017 across seven distinct releases:

ReleaseYearNotes
Pinkream1995/9610” vinyl (1995) then CD (1996), cat DPROMCD37; recorded ZSF Produkt Studio June–Aug 1995
Arctic Twilight / Anthem Of The Sun1996Split with Slugbait, DPROMCD43
24 Hours – A Day Of Seals20024CD box set, DPROMCD50; phonographic copyright held by Dirter Promotions, suggesting original production rather than licensing (Discogs snippet)
Keio Line2008Collaboration with Richard Pinhas; triple LP; Steve Pittis credited with sleeve design and layout (Wikipedia: Keio Line)
Another Merzbow Records20103CD compilation collecting Merzbow tracks from out-of-print compilations on other labels (Blast First, Susan Lawly, 1991–2001); limited to 1,000 copies (Beatport listing)
Wildwood2015Benefit CD for Wildwood Trust (UK wildlife charity supporting two rescued European brown bears); Steve Pittis credited as project coordinator; “all proceeds from sales” to the charity (dirterpromotions.com)
Wildwood II2017LP; dedicated to Wildwood Trust; Steve Pittis again credited as project coordinator (Soundohm listing)

The structure varies across releases. The Wildwood and Wildwood II releases were charity-driven projects initiated by Dirter, with Akita contributing new recordings — these are clearly originals produced for the label, not licensed back-catalogue. The Another Merzbow Records compilation necessarily involved licensing tracks from third-party labels (Blast First, Susan Lawly) or clearances direct from Akita. The 1990s releases (Pinkream, Arctic Twilight) appear to be original Dirter productions given the Dirter catalogue numbers and the Pinkream CD’s 1,000-copy press with Dirter holding copyright.

Steve Pittis is credited personally on multiple releases (sleeve design on Keio Line; project coordinator on Wildwood and Wildwood II), and receives special thanks on Houjoue — consistent with an ongoing direct working relationship between Pittis and Akita rather than arms-length licensing through an intermediary.

Dirter and the broader Japanese experimental scene. Merzbow is not an isolated case. The catalogue shows Dirter released Tokyo 1988 (2007) by Marble Sheep and Good-Bye John Peel: Live In London 2004 (2005) by Acid Mothers Temple & The Melting Paraiso UFO. These are drawn from different corners of the Japanese underground (psychedelic rock, noise), suggesting Pittis had either recurring contacts in Japan or plugged into a small network of UK intermediaries who facilitated these releases. No interview or primary source found that explains the origin of Pittis’s Japan connections.

What was not found. No interview with Steve Pittis or Masami Akita was found that describes how the Dirter–Merzbow relationship started, who approached whom, or the formal terms (licensing fees, revenue splits, exclusivity). No UK distributor documentation was found. The question of how it began — and whether it started as a licensing deal that evolved into a production relationship, or was always direct — remains open.
