Matador Records

Independent record label. New York City. Founded 1989. Discogs ID: 194 (operating label “Matador”); parent company Beggars Group (ID 613604) — acquired 50% in 2002.

One of the two or three most significant American independent labels of the 1990s–2000s, alongside Sub Pop and Drag City. Founded in Chris Lombardi’s New York apartment; shaped by the arrival of co-owner Gerard Cosloy (ex-Homestead Records) in 1990. The early Matador sound is downtown New York indie-rock with lo-fi sensibility: Superchunk, Pavement, Liz Phair, Yo La Tengo, Jon Spencer Blues Explosion. In the 2000s the label expanded globally — Interpol, Dizzee Rascal, Sonic Youth — and in the 2010s became home to a new generation of singer-songwriters and indie acts (Kurt Vile, Perfume Genius, Car Seat Headrest, boygenius, Queens of the Stone Age).

Catalog numbering uses the OLE prefix throughout. 5,595 releases in Discogs.

Founding and the OLE Catalog

The label began in 1989 when Chris Lombardi brought Austrian duo H.P. Zinker to Wharton Tiers’s Fun City studio to record OLE 001 — …And There Was Light (1990). The first proper releases were out of Lombardi’s NYC apartment. Early roster additions: Dustdevils (OLE 014), Railroad Jerk (OLE 005), Superchunk (OLE 004).

Gerard Cosloy — who had run Homestead Records, one of the essential American underground labels of the 1980s — joined as co-owner in 1990. The same year Matador released the North American version of Teenage Fanclub’s debut LP A Catholic Education (OLE 012-2) — the first record to generate significant press attention. The label moved from Lombardi’s apartment to a series of increasingly large offices in Manhattan, finally settling at 134 Grand Street, New York.

The OLE numbering ran continuously and all releases — North American, European, and licensed international — share the same sequence.

Era 1 — NYC Underground (1990–1992)

The early roster was rooted in underground American and international indie rock. Key releases:

  • OLE 004: Superchunk self-titled (1990) — Chapel Hill, NC; Mac McCaughan; one of Matador’s longest-serving acts
  • OLE 009: Unsane self-titled (1991) — NYC noise rock
  • OLE 011: Mecca Normal Water Cuts My Hands (1991) — Canadian feminist punk
  • OLE 035: Superchunk No Pocky For Kitty (1991)
  • OLE 038: Pavement Slanted And Enchanted (1992) — Stockton, CA; Stephen Malkmus and Spiral Stairs; the record that defined early-1990s American lo-fi indie rock
  • OLE 044: Pavement Watery, Domestic EP (1992)
  • OLE 045: Come Eleven:Eleven (1992)
  • OLE 046: Bettie Serveert Palomine (1992) — Dutch group; Carol van Dyk; early international signing

The label also issued OLE 006, a reissue of Albert Ayler’s New York Eye And Ear Control (1991) — signaling that Matador’s scope was never strictly confined to rock.

Era 2 — The Breakthrough Years (1993–1995)

Matador’s commercial and critical peak as a major independent. Three of the most significant American rock records of the decade arrived in rapid succession:

  • OLE 051: Liz Phair Exile In Guyville (1993, OLE 051-2) — Chicago; a track-by-track response to the Rolling Stones’ Exile On Main St.; produced by Brad Wood; Nash Kato (Urge Overkill) shot the cover photo; instantly canonical
  • OLE 052: The Jon Spencer Blues Explosion Extra Width (1993)
  • OLE 055: The Fall The Infotainment Scan (1993, licensed) — first of two Fall LPs on Matador
  • OLE 069: Yo La Tengo Painful (1993, OLE 069-2) — Hoboken, NJ trio; Georgia Hubley, Ira Kaplan, James McNew; continuing relationship that produced seven+ Matador LPs
  • OLE 079: Pavement Crooked Rain, Crooked Rain (1994) — their commercial high-water mark; “Cut Your Hair” (OLE 082)
  • OLE 083/084: Guided By Voices Vampire On Titus / Bee Thousand (1994) — Dayton, OH; Robert Pollard; lo-fi prolific; Matador’s most important second-wave signing
  • OLE 096: Pizzicato Five Five By Five (1994) — Tokyo art-pop duo; Matador as North American licensee for Japanese catalog
  • OLE 105: Jon Spencer Blues Explosion Orange (1994)
  • OLE 107: Liz Phair Whip-Smart (1994) — sophomore, more polished; Atlantic/Matador distribution deal in effect

The Atlantic deal: During the early 1990s Matador entered into a distribution partnership with Atlantic Records (then later Capitol Records held a 49% stake). This brought distribution muscle but complicated the label’s independence. In 1999 Lombardi and Cosloy bought back Capitol’s 49% share.

Additional 1994–1995: Helium (OLE 078, OLE 124), Chavez (OLE 104), Cat Power debut (OLE 116-7), Smog (Julius Caesar, OLE 097), 18th Dye, Silkworm, Portastatic, Come.

Era 3 — Mid-1990s Expansion (1996–1999)

Post-buyback stabilization; the roster widened significantly. Spoon, Cat Power, and Sleater-Kinney all arrived in this period.

  • OLE 194: Yo La Tengo Genius + Love = Yo La Tengo (1996) — retrospective double-LP; consolidates their catalog
  • OLE 197: Pavement Brighten The Corners (1997) — final full-tilt Matador LP before Pavement’s 1999 disbandment
  • OLE 201: Spoon Telephono (1996) — Austin, TX; Britt Daniel and Jim Eno; debut; Matador dropped them after a second single — they returned later on Merge/Elektra and came back to Matador by the 2000s
  • OLE 202: Cat Power What Would The Community Think (1996) — Chan Marshall; Atlanta, GA; early lo-fi
  • OLE 214: R.L. Burnside A Ass Pocket Of Whiskey (1996) — blues elder from Mississippi; Jon Spencer collaboration
  • OLE 222: Yo La Tengo I Can Hear The Heart Beating As One (1997, OLE 222-2) — their defining LP; produced by Roger Moutenot
  • OLE 241: Guided By Voices Mag Earwhig! (1997)
  • OLE 269: Sleater-Kinney Dig Me Out (1997, licensed from Kill Rock Stars) — and Call The Doctor (OLE 268-2, 1998)
  • OLE 300: Cornelius Fantasma (1998) — Keigo Oyamada; Tokyo; licensed from Trattoria
  • OLE 319: Cornelius Chapter 8 Seashore And Horizon (1998)
  • OLE 322: Jon Spencer Blues Explosion Acme (1998)
  • OLE 335: Unwound Challenge For A Civilized Society (1998)

Also in this period: Belle & Sebastian (North American licensee from 1998, 198 releases on Matador total), Mogwai (North American from 1999), Boards of Canada (OLE ~350 range), Arab Strap, Pole, Matmos.

Era 4 — Interpol and the 2000s (2000–2008)

The label’s second peak, anchored by Interpol and a series of international signings that expanded Matador’s commercial reach.

  • OLE 545: Interpol Turn On The Bright Lights (2002) — Manhattan post-punk revival; Paul Banks, Daniel Kessler, Carlos D., Samuel Fogarino; produced and engineered by Peter Katis; one of the decade’s most-discussed rock debuts
  • OLE 570: Interpol “Obstacle 1” single (2002)
  • OLE 616: Interpol Antics (2004)
  • OLE 584: The New Pornographers Electric Version (2003) — Vancouver indie pop supergroup; A.C. Newman, Dan Bejar, Neko Case
  • OLE 626: Cat Power The Greatest (2006) — Memphis soul-influenced; Chan Marshall with Hi Rhythm Section
  • OLE 643: Cat Power Speaking For Trees (2005)
  • OLE 660: Spoon Gimme Fiction (2005) — Spoon returned to Matador after the Merge detour; Kill The Moonlight and Gimme Fiction established them as the label’s most consistent rock act
  • OLE 754: Cat Power Jukebox (2008) — covers album
  • OLE 829: Sonic Youth The Eternal (2009) — Sonic Youth’s final studio album; arrived on Matador after DGC/Geffen; Kim Gordon, Thurston Moore, Lee Ranaldo, Steve Shelley

Dizzee Rascal (Boy In Da Corner, 2003) was licensed North America from XL Recordings — an early signal of Matador’s genre range. Stephen Malkmus & The Jicks (from 2003, OLE series); Mission of Burma reunion (from 2004).

Era 5 — 2010s: New Generation (2009–2020)

The label maintained its identity as a discovery vehicle while expanding its roster further.

  • OLE 887: Kurt Vile Childish Prodigy (2009) — Philadelphia; lo-fi guitar-rock; debut on Matador
  • OLE 938: Kurt Vile Smoke Ring For My Halo (2011) — breakthrough
  • OLE 942: Perfume Genius Learning (2010) — Mike Hadreas; Seattle; debut; sparse piano pop
  • OLE 1088: Car Seat Headrest Teens Of Style (2015) — Will Toledo; Bandcamp-to-label pipeline
  • OLE 1091: Car Seat Headrest Teens Of Denial (2016) — full-band rock; their commercial breakthrough
  • Queens Of The Stone Age …Like Clockwork (2013) — Josh Homme; their Matador debut (127 releases total)
  • boygenius EP (2018); The Record (2023) — Julien Baker, Phoebe Bridgers, Lucy Dacus supergroup
  • Julien Baker (from 2017, 70 releases)
  • Lucy Dacus (from 2016, 60 releases)

Also: Jay Reatard, Times New Viking, Cold Cave, Kim Gordon solo (No Home Record, 2019), Mdou Moctar, Horsegirl, Water From Your Eyes.

The Beggars Group Acquisition

In 2002, Beggars Group (parent of 4AD, XL Recordings, Rough Trade, Too Pure) purchased 50% of the legal entities behind Matador. This gave Matador worldwide marketing support through Beggars’s network while Lombardi and Cosloy retained creative control. The arrangement differs from Sub Pop/Warner (49% stake, 1995) in that Beggars is itself an independent entity — no major-label money involved.
