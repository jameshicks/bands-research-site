Overview

Ready Records was a Toronto-based independent label founded on 29 March 1979 by Andrew Crosbie, Angus MacKay, and others, oriented toward rock and dance music. Its catalogue is overwhelmingly concentrated in the 1980s — home to New Wave and synth-pop acts including the Spoons and its associated members — before officially closing in 1985. Two imprints that began as Ready spinoffs, Fringe Product and Kids’ Records, outlasted the parent label and continued as independent operations. Compilation releases from Ready’s back catalogue have since been issued on CD through MapleMusic.

Notes (web) — Fringe Product and Kids’ Records: roster continuity and split rationale

Kids’ Records

Kids’ Records was founded in November 1981 — four years before Ready’s closure — by children’s music producer Bill Usher, with Ready Records’ principals Angus MacKay and Andy Crosbie involved at the label’s inception. The impetus was purely genre-driven: Ready was a rock and new wave operation with no infrastructure for children’s content, so a separate imprint was created to house that material. No Ready Records adult-roster artists carried over; the catalogues are genre-distinct. Kids’ Records was distributed by A&M Records of Canada from 1981 to 1986, after which Usher’s office handled distribution directly (per a June 1986 Billboard report cited in the Wikipedia article on Bill Usher). At its peak it was described as the largest children’s record company in North America. Bill Usher ran it until approximately 1990. The shared postal address — P.O. Box 670 Station A, Toronto ON M5W 1G2 — with Fringe Product suggests the operations shared administrative infrastructure.

Sources: Wikipedia — Bill Usher (as of 2026-11-21); Wikipedia — Fringe Product (as of 2026-11-21); search-result snippets from Kids’ Records Discogs

Fringe Product

Fringe Product was founded in 1981 by Ben Hoffman, proprietor of The Record Peddler (an import retail store) and Record Peddler Distribution (UK and US import distribution), active from the late 1970s to 2000. It is described as having originated as a Ready Records spinoff (Wikipedia — Fringe Product, as of 2026-11-21), but the connection appears to be structural/distribution rather than a roster transfer: Hoffman ran an independent import and distribution operation, and Fringe Product grew out of that infrastructure. Ready’s rock roster did not carry over in bulk — Fringe Product’s catalogue skewed toward punk, hardcore, metal, and industrial (Dayglo Abortions, Sacrifice, Razor, Slaughter), plus Canadian distribution licenses for US acts (Bad Brains, Dead Kennedys, Black Flag, Butthole Surfers). In 1988 Fringe Product Inc. was incorporated as a formal entity separate from Record Peddler.

Documented artist crossover — Teenage Head. Teenage Head released Endless Party (1984) on Ready Records (cat. LR 046, in the catalogue table above) and later released Electric Guitar (1988) on Fringe Product. This is the clearest confirmed case of an artist appearing on both labels, though the Fringe Product release came three years after Ready’s closure, not as a concurrent transition.

No other specific Ready roster artists have been confirmed by web sources as moving to Fringe Product. The Wikipedia article on Fringe Product makes no mention of Ready Records or any roster carryover, and the Ready Records official site (readyrecords.ca) likewise does not discuss Fringe Product.

Sources: Wikipedia — Fringe Product (as of 2026-11-21); Wikipedia — Teenage Head (as of 2026-11-21, via search snippet); Fringe Product — Metal Archives

Summary assessment

Both imprints were created while Ready was still active (both 1981, four years before the 1985 closure), but neither was a roster-continuation vehicle. Kids’ Records was a deliberate genre spinoff for children’s content, co-founded by Ready’s principals; Fringe Product grew from Ben Hoffman’s separate import/distribution business and operated on an almost entirely different genre roster. The clearest confirmed artist crossover between the Ready universe and Fringe Product is Teenage Head, though that transfer post-dates Ready’s closure. No web source located describes a planned migration of Ready’s acts to either imprint.

What was not found: No interview with Crosbie, MacKay, or Hoffman that discusses the deliberate structuring rationale for splitting the imprints; no contemporaneous press coverage (e.g. RPM Weekly) confirming the founding circumstances beyond what is summarised above.
