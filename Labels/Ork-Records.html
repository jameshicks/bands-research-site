Ork Records

Discogs ID: 38538
Founded: 1975, New York City
Address: P.O.B 159, Cooper Station, N.Y., N.Y. 10003
Co-founders: Terry Ork and Charles Ball
Active: August 1975–1979 (16 singles total)
Reissued by: Numero Group (2015, Ork Records: New York, New York)

American DIY independent punk label. Widely considered one of the first (if not the first) US DIY punk record labels, alongside Whiplash Records (Rick Rivets / George Csanka / A.C. Doback). Founded by Terry Ork — born William Terry Collins, manager of Television, former Cinemabilia film bookstore manager and Warhol Factory associate — and Charles Ball, who ran the business operations. Address was a Cooper Station P.O. box in lower Manhattan.

Numero Group on the label’s legacy: “Ork Records lit the fuse in 1975 and then, like the fabled Roman candle in Kerouac’s On the Road, burned brightly for a while and then burned out by 1979” — credited with birthing “punk, no-wave, power pop, and the next four decades of indie rock.”

Launched with Television’s debut single “Little Johnny Jewel” (1975) and released a run of crucial 7”s through the late 1970s: Richard Hell’s first solo recordings, Alex Chilton in his post-Big Star phase, Chris Stamey’s debut, and late-period singles by Cheetah Chrome and the Student Teachers. Charles Ball later founded Lust-Unlust Music for the No Wave scene.

Founding

Terry Ork came to the label from Cinemabilia, a Manhattan film bookstore he managed, where he also worked alongside Richard Hell. It was at Cinemabilia that he encountered the Neon Boys (Verlaine, Hell, Ficca), introduced Richard Lloyd to complete the Television lineup, and began managing the band. He had previously been part of Andy Warhol’s Factory set and worked briefly at Interview magazine. His approach was aesthetic rather than commercial — Richard Hell described him as “a joyous gay hedonist who loved French thought and art” and noted he called everyone “Dog.” The label was founded in August 1975 primarily to release Television’s debut when no established label would.

Charles Ball handled business operations; his role was described as “keeping the machinery of the business running” while Ork sought out new bands. Ball: graduate of Mercersburg Academy and Sarah Lawrence College; died August 14, 2012, age 61, heart attack, St. Petersburg, Florida.

The first pressing of “Little Johnny Jewel” was approximately 1,000 copies, recorded at CBS Studios.

Key Artists

Television

New York City rock band. Formed 1973. Members: Tom Verlaine (guitar, vocals), Richard Lloyd (guitar), Fred Smith (7) (bass), Billy Ficca (drums). Richard Hell (born Richard Lester Meyers) was an early member before departing to form the Heartbreakers. Active 1973–1978, reunions 1991–1993 and 2001–2023. Tom Verlaine died January 28, 2023, ending the band permanently.

Little Johnny Jewel (81975, 1975) was both Television’s debut and the label’s entire reason for existing.

Richard Hell

Born Richard Lester Meyers, October 2, 1949, Lexington, Kentucky. Originally co-founded Television with Tom Verlaine under his birth name before adopting the Hell persona. His Ork EP Another World (81976, 1976) — featuring Robert Quine, Ivan Julian, and Marc Bell, produced by Craig Leon — contains the first recording of “(I Belong To The) Blank Generation,” his signature song. The lineup would become the Voidoids.

Alex Chilton

Born William Alexander Chilton, December 28, 1950, Memphis, Tennessee. Died March 17, 2010, New Orleans. Singer of the Box Tops (“The Letter,” 1967) and co-founder of Big Star. His Ork EP Singer Not The Song (81978, 1977) — a 5-track 33⅓ EP — is a post-Big Star document, produced with Memphis collaborators Jon Tiven, Tommy Hoehn, Ken Woodley, and Richard Rosebrough, photographed by William Eggleston. Also produced and played drums on Chris Stamey’s debut single.

Chris Stamey

Born Christopher Charles Stamey, December 6, 1954, Chapel Hill, North Carolina. The Summer Sun (81982, 1977) was his debut single, produced and backed by Alex Chilton. Later co-founded the dB’s.

Mick Farren

Born Michael Anthony Farren, September 3, 1943, Cheltenham, England. Died July 27, 2013, London. Journalist, writer, and musician; leader of The Deviants and a figure in London counterculture. His Ork single Play With Fire (cat. 81980, 1977) has “Lost Johnny” — co-written with Lemmy Kilmister — as the B-side. Personnel were uncredited: Marc Bell (drums), Jon Tiven (guitar), Doug Snyder (2) (bass, direction). The A-side is a Rolling Stones cover (Nanker Phelge). His presence on Ork is an anomaly — an English proto-punk connecting into the NYC orbit via Tiven and Bell.

Cheetah Chrome

Born Eugene O’Connor. Guitarist of the Dead Boys. His 1979 Ork single Still Wanna Die was one of the label’s last original releases.

Numero Group Reissue (2015)

Numero Group reissued the complete Ork catalog in 2015 as Ork Records: New York, New York (master ID 904723) — a 4LP box set. This was the primary mechanism by which the label’s catalog became widely available again. Discogs links Ork Records’ URLs directly to the Numero reissue pages.

Open Questions

  • Link Cromwell: confirmed as a 1960s stage name for Ritchie Adams. The 1977 Ork single (cat. ORK 81981) features Lenny Kaye on uncredited bass/vocals; produced by Ritchie Adams and Larry Kusik. Status: essentially an oddity / novelty revival release on the label.
  • Prix (Discogs ID 1163385) — who are they? Jon Tiven produced; Tommy Hoehn co-wrote. Memphis-adjacent?
  • Marbles (5) (Discogs ID 1131025) — lineup still unknown beyond Amos Poe film documentation. Marbles (5) stub exists.
  • The Erasers — Resolved (web research, 2026-06-27). New York art-punk duo formed c. 1974 by Susan Springfield (guitar, vocals) and Jane Fire (drums); also Richie Lure (guitar, Walter Lure’s brother), Jody Beach (bass), David Ebony. Recorded two tracks for an Ork single produced by Richard Lloyd: “I Won’t Give Up” and “(It Was So) Funny (The Song That They Sung).” The single was never issued — one track surfaced on a 1982 ROIR cassette compilation; both tracks were first properly released on the Numero Group 2015 box set. No Discogs release entry exists for an original Ork pressing. Sources: Wikipedia — Erasers (band); Flavorwire profile.
  • Lester Bangs — Resolved (web research, 2026-06-27). Bangs recorded “Let It Blurt” / “Live” at Big Apple Studios, NYC, 1977 — planned as an Ork EP. Ork ran out of funds before release. The recording was acquired by Spy Records and released in 1979 as Spy 003. Personnel: Lester Bangs (vocals), Robert Quine + Jody Harris (guitars), Jay Dee Daugherty (drums, producer), mixed by John Cale. The track appears on the 2015 Numero box set. There was never an Ork-catalog release. Sources: 78sand45s.wordpress.com; Discogs Spy 003.
  • The Feelies on Ork: confirmed they released only in the Numero Group era (cat. 81984, 2014), not in the original 1975–1979 run.
  • Mick Farren Ork release: fully documented above. B-side “Lost Johnny” (Farren/Kilmister) was the more significant track.
  • Exact closing date of the label — Discogs last original release is 1979 (Cheetah Chrome / Student Teachers / UK pressings). Precise month unknown.
