Table Of The Elements

Atlanta-based American experimental music label founded in 1993 by Jeff Hunt. Discogs label ID: 12701. Each catalogue number is composed using a chemical symbol from the periodic table of elements paired with its atomic number (e.g., “Li 3” for Lithium, “As 33” for Arsenic), giving the label its name and its filing logic. The label focused on minimalist composition, drone, free improvisation, avant-garde, American Primitive guitar, and archival reissues of canonical 1960s experimentalism. Active from 1993 through at least 2010 across nine sublabels; the Discogs catalog records approximately 224 releases across the full family.

Founding and Identity

Founded by Jeff Hunt, a music producer and graphic designer based in Atlanta. Hunt served as executive producer and art director across the vast majority of the catalog, with Susan Archie and Jon Malic recurring as primary designers across dozens of releases. The label’s periodic-table catalog numbering system is both a filing system and an aesthetic statement — the atomic number determines the catalog number, so releases were not issued in strict sequence but placed within the table’s logic.

Kristina Johnson (credited as Kris Johnson) co-produced numerous releases as executive producer, and Zach Cincotta served in the same capacity on several titles, suggesting a small inner circle managing production. Jim O’Rourke appeared repeatedly as engineer and producer. Chris Griffin (2) was the primary mastering engineer across at least 18 releases; James Plotkin mastered 14. David Daniell served both as a label artist and as engineer/mixer on multiple releases.

The in-house visual style was consistent and austere. Savage Pencil (Edwin Pouncey) contributed etched artwork to numerous releases including David Daniell’s I-IV-V-I. Susan Archie handled design, layout, and art direction across roughly 39 releases, making her the dominant visual presence in the catalog.

Core Roster

Tony Conrad

The label’s most prominent artist by release count: 28 primary releases. Tony Conrad’s relationship with Table Of The Elements was foundational — the label’s inaugural run included Outside The Dream Syndicate (1993, cat. Li 3), a collaboration with Faust, reissuing a 1972 recording that had been suppressed for decades. Conrad’s landmark archival release on the label was Early Minimalism Volume One (1997, cat. As 33), a four-disc box set documenting his 1960s drone compositions for overdubbed violin (1964) alongside new performances of April–June 1965 works with a German ensemble. That release also featured Jim O’Rourke on electric violin for the May 1965 disc, recorded by John McEntire.

Other Conrad releases on the label include The Pyre Of Angus Was In Kathmandu (1993), The Japanese Room At La Pagode / May (1995, with Gastr Del Sol), Fantastic Glissando (2003), and the Inside The Dream Syndicate archival series.

Faust

24 releases, second only to Conrad. The label reissued major Faust archival material throughout the 1990s and 2000s, including Rien (1994), The Faust Concerts Vol. 1 (cat. Fe 26) and Vol. II (cat. Co 27), both 1994, and Outside The Dream Syndicate Alive (2005). The original Outside The Dream Syndicate was produced by Uwe Nettelbeck and features Jean-Hervé Peron, Werner Diermaier, Rudolf Sosna alongside Conrad.

John Cale

15 releases, spanning 2000–2006. Table Of The Elements published the Inside The Dream Syndicate archival series, which documented the Theatre Of Eternal Music / Dream Syndicate sessions. Volume I: Day Of Niagara (1965) (2000, cat. W 74) credited Cale, Conrad, Angus MacLise, La Monte Young, and Marian Zazeela — the full original ensemble — as performers and composers. Volume II: Dream Interpretation (2001, Audio ArtKive 04) and New York In The 1960s (2004, cat. Fr 87) continued the series. The release of these recordings was significant; La Monte Young had withheld them for decades and their publication was contentious.

John Fahey

8 releases, 1997–2006. The label issued Womblife (1997, cat. Rb 37) and Georgia Stomps, Atlanta Struts, And Other Contemporary Dance Favorites (1998, cat. Sr 38). The capstone was Sea Changes & Coelacanths: A Young Person’s Guide To John Fahey (2006, cat. At 85), a triple-LP box set compiling archival and live recordings spanning the 1950s through the 1990s. The release was produced by Jim O’Rourke (sides A–B), Jon Philpot (sides C–F), and Jeff Hunt / Kristina Johnson (sides G), with David Daniell engineering sides C–F and Andrew Burnes editing the closing material. Liner notes by Byron Coley.

Rhys Chatham

7 releases, 2002–2006. Primarily concentrated on the Radium (3) sublabel. Die Donnergötter (2006) and An Angel Moves Too Fast To See (For 100 Electric Guitars, Electric Bass, And Drums) (2006) were the major titles; the latter was also released on vinyl via Radium. Guitar Trio Is My Life! (2008, Radium) was credited to Rhys Chatham & His Guitar Trio All-Stars. The Eliane Radigue release Adnos I-III (2002, cat. Cs 55) carries liner notes by Chatham, reflecting his role as an advocate within the label’s network.

Other Major Artists

  • Angus MacLise / La Monte Young / Marian Zazeela: 6 releases each, all tied to the Inside The Dream Syndicate series (2000)
  • Captain Beefheart / The Magic Band: 5 releases — the Grow Fins archival series on the Xeric sublabel (1999–2001), raw demo and live recordings from 1965–68 predating Safe As Milk
  • Zeena Parkins: 5 releases, including Nightmare Alley (1993, cat. H 1), the label’s first release, featuring electric and acoustic harp produced by David Shea and engineered by Bryce Goggin and Doug Henderson
  • Derek Bailey: 4 releases, 1993–1997, including New Year Messages 1-4 (1993, cat. Na 11) and Music And Dance (1997, cat. Zr 40, with Min Tanaka)
  • Gastr Del Sol: 4 releases, 1995–2006
  • Keiji Haino: 4 releases, including I Said, This Is The Son Of Nihilism (1995, cat. Ar 18)
  • Loren MazzaCane Connors: 4 primary releases on the label; also on Xeric’s Mmmr (1997) with Jean-Marc Montera, Thurston Moore, and Lee Ranaldo
  • Richard Youngs: 4 releases, including Advent (1997, cat. Nb 41)
  • Eliane Radigue: Adnos I-III (2002, cat. Cs 55), a three-disc box set co-released with The Sandwalking Company; one of the first official releases of this major work
  • Bernhard Günter: 2 releases — Un Peu De Neige Salie (1996, cat. Ga 31) and Details Agrandis (1998)
  • Arnold Dreyblatt: 3 releases, including Live At Federal Hall National Memorial, 1981 (2006)
  • Pauline Oliveros: Primordial Lift (1999, cat. I 53)
  • Laurie Spiegel: Harmonices Mundi (2003, cat. Pm 61)
  • Lee Ranaldo: 3 primary releases; also appeared on Xeric’s Mmmr; Countless Centuries Fled Into The Distance Like So Many Storms (2008, cat. Bk 97)
  • David Daniell: I-IV-V-I (2008, cat. Es 99 / TOE-LP-99), a single-sided 12” etched vinyl of solo guitar work; limited edition. Daniell also appears on Xeric’s Coastal (2006)
  • Fennesz: June (2008, cat. Fm 100)
  • Oren Ambarchi: A Final Kiss On Poisoned Cheeks (2008, cat. Am 95)
  • Stephen O’Malley: Keep An Eye Out (2009, cat. Md 101)

Catalog Numbering System

The periodic table structure is both navigational and conceptual. Elements with lower atomic numbers were released earlier: Hydrogen (H 1) was Zeena Parkins’ Nightmare Alley, Lithium (Li 3) was Outside The Dream Syndicate, Sodium (Na 11) was Derek Bailey’s New Year Messages. The system does not require sequential release order — gaps exist where elements were reserved or unissued. By the late catalog the system had been supplemented with alpha-numeric prefixes (TOE-LP-99, TOE-CD-802) for Radium and other sublabels, which used their own independent numbering.
