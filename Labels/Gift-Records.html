Overview

Gift Records was a short-lived Sheffield imprint founded in the early 1990s by Steve Beckett, co-founder of Warp Records, with Warp as its parent label. Its primary significance is as the pre-Island home of Pulp, whose early releases it issued before the band made their major-label move; the roster also included Pulp-adjacent figures Candida Doyle and Jarvis Cocker, plus Sheffield design collective The Designers Republic. All 11 of the label’s known releases fall within the 1990s, spanning indie rock, Britpop, and synth-pop styles that map closely to Pulp’s sound in that era.

Why Gift closed — the Pulp/Island transition

Gift was set up specifically to give Pulp a release outlet during a contractual impasse, and it closed once that function was served. The two sides of the decision are documented in separate sources:

Beckett’s account (label side): Steve Beckett explained that Gift was a vehicle of convenience, not an ongoing imprint. Because Warp’s identity in the early 1990s was rooted in dance and electronic music, the label could not put a guitar-based act like Pulp through it in the normal way. Beckett set Gift up as “a platform for them” specifically because Pulp “were in a deal that wasn’t working for them” (the Fire Records entanglement). Once Pulp moved to Island, Beckett said “there really wasn’t any reason to keep [Gift] going.” — Steve Beckett, self-titled magazine guide to Warp’s first 20 years, source

Cocker’s account (band side): Jarvis Cocker described Gift as a lifeline rather than a long-term home. He confirmed there was “a kind of legal problem” trapping the band, and that “Steve and Rob [Mitchell] helped us out by offering to let us release stuff on that.” He framed Warp/Gift as rescuers, not as a label they actively chose over Island. The three Gift singles then became Intro: The Gift Recordings, released on Island in October 1993 as the band’s formal Island debut compilation. Cocker: “if we’d have been stuck and not been able to release anything for a year and a half we would have been screwed.” — Jarvis Cocker speaking at the AIM Awards, Music Week, 2017

The underlying legal problem — Fire Records: Pulp were contractually bound to Fire Records under a deal signed in the late 1980s. When Island Records began informally funding the Gift singles, Island discovered the undisclosed Fire obligation and withdrew further support due to legal risk. Cocker’s manager Suzanne Catty had to secure a £5,000 family loan to keep “Babies” (October 1992) on schedule. The band then changed management to Rough Trade, who negotiated Pulp’s release from Fire Records in exchange for Fire receiving a percentage of future major-label earnings. This buyout cleared the path for Island to sign Pulp formally in July 1993. — Wikipedia, Pulp (band) (as of 2026-09-27)

Synthesis: Gift’s closure was not a case of Beckett dropping Pulp, nor of Pulp choosing to leave a label they wanted to stay on. Gift was purpose-built as a temporary platform while Pulp’s Fire contract was resolved, with Island informally involved from the outset. When the legal path to Island cleared, there was nothing left for Gift to do. Both accounts — Beckett’s and Cocker’s — describe a coordinated transition, not a split.
