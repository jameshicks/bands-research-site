Overview

Little Brother Records was a small independent label based in Portland, Oregon, founded in 1993. Its catalogue of seven known releases spans experimental, noise, indie rock, punk, and acoustic music, concentrated almost entirely in the 1990s. The roster draws together notably varied figures — avant-garde guitarist Nels Cline, acoustic guitar legend John Fahey, and experimental artists Wayne Peet and Carole Kim — suggesting ties to both the West Coast experimental scene and wider underground networks. The label appears to have wound down after a single release in the 2000s.

Web sources

Label operator

Little Brother Records was run by Mike Hogan from Portland, Oregon (mailing address PO Box 33677, Portland OR 97292). Hogan operated the label from its first release in 1993 through at least 2003, overseeing approximately 16 releases. The label had a joint venture with Broker’s Tip Records (run by Bob Nastanovich of Pavement) to release the 1994 Minutemen tribute compilation Our Band Could Be Your Life — A Tribute to D Boon and the Minutemen. (Source: search-result snippet from littlebroker.bandcamp.com label description; confirmed by Bandcamp page littlebroker.bandcamp.com)

Connection to Nels Cline

Mike Hogan was directly involved in the production of the Nels Cline Trio’s Chest as part of what the credits call a “Production Junta” (alongside Jeff Gauthier, Alex Cline, and Nels Cline himself). Chest was the label’s first release (June 1996), recorded at Sage & Sound in Los Angeles in June 1993 (with additional tracks recorded July 1995 at New Zone Studio, engineered by Wayne Peet). The three-year gap between recording and release reflects the material’s difficult placement: Nels Cline considered it too extreme for his usual label Enja Records. Hogan’s producer credit indicates the connection was artistic as well as commercial — he was embedded in the sessions, not simply a licensee. Little Brother went on to release two more Cline projects: Pillow Wand (1997, with Thurston Moore) and Sad (1998). (Sources: Chest (album) — Wikipedia; nelsclinetrio.bandcamp.com; search-result aggregation)

No source found explaining how Hogan and Nels Cline first connected (i.e., who introduced them or whether Hogan approached the trio or vice versa). Given Hogan’s production role, the relationship predates the label itself.

Connection to John Fahey

John Fahey was living in Salem, Oregon in the mid-1990s (in a boardinghouse), recording in his room. Earlier sessions (1990) were done at Spectrum Sound in Portland. The Mill Pond (1997) was produced by Scott Colburn, a Seattle-based engineer/producer active in the Northwest experimental scene (credits include Sun City Girls, Amy Denio, Climax Golden Twins). Colburn confirmed working with Fahey in a Tape Op interview, though without detail about the Mill Pond sessions specifically. (Tape Op interview with Scott Colburn, issue 11)

No source explicitly states how Mike Hogan/Little Brother came to release the Fahey material. The most plausible inference is that Scott Colburn, already embedded in the same Northwest experimental network as Hogan, served as the bridge — but this is not confirmed in any source checked. Fahey’s Oregon residency in this period (Salem boardinghouse, earlier Portland recording) placed him in geographic proximity to the label’s world.
