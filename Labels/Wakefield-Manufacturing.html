Wakefield Manufacturing

Custom record pressing plant and mastering facility based in Phoenix, Arizona. In continuous operation from approximately 1957 (initially as “Sidney J. Wakefield”) until 1989. Discogs label ID: 367883. Parent entity: Wakefield Manufacturing, Inc. (Discogs ID: 966891).

Subsidiary/variant entities:

  • Sidney J. Wakefield & Company (Discogs ID: 1160605) — the earliest operating name, used circa 1957–1963
  • Wakefield Custom (Discogs ID: 919577) — the custom-order division

Overview

Wakefield Manufacturing was one of the primary independent record pressing operations west of the Mississippi for three decades. Founded by Sidney J. Wakefield in Phoenix, it was known for high-quality vinyl pressings weighted toward jazz and classical recordings. The plant’s annual production reportedly reached 4.5 million records at its peak. It served both regional Arizona clients and major national labels, including ECM Records, Angel Records, Warner Bros. Records, Reprise Records, Geffen Records, and, from 1985 to 1987, the reactivated Blue Note catalog pressed under Manhattan Records.

Over its thirty-year operational history Wakefield credited more than 7,000 “Pressed By” roles in Discogs alone (total credits across all entity types approach 8,000). The plant closed in 1989 at 1745 West Linden Street, Phoenix — its final address of record.

Sidney J. Wakefield founded and owned the operation. In 1975 he retired and handed control to his son Richard Wakefield (known as Dick), who became president and general manager. In 1976 Wakefield introduced its “TQ” premium disc line. In 1983 the company added cassette duplication at a satellite plant in Pasadena, California, before closing the whole operation in 1989.

The Discogs entry for the parent holding company, Wakefield Manufacturing, Inc. (ID: 966891), is used for printed corporate credits on releases; the main label entry (ID: 367883) covers the actual pressing plant credits.

John Bittner is the engineer with the deepest documented history at Wakefield, cutting lacquers for ECM’s entire US pressing run through the 1970s (Jarrett, Metheny, Garbarek, Braxton), Rounder Records’ folk catalog, Delmark blues releases, and the avant-garde jazz of Improvising Artists Inc. (Paul Bley, Sam Rivers, Pat Metheny, Anthony Braxton).

Roger Seibel arrived around 1982, overlapping with the Blue Note/Manhattan contract years, and departed in 1986 to found SAE Mastering independently in Phoenix. His Wakefield period is credited in runouts by the initials “RS” or the signature “Roger.” He is the most extensively documented Wakefield engineer in the vault; see Roger Seibel for his full career.

Identification Guide (Runout Markings)

Wakefield pressings are identified by several logo stamps and processing number conventions, which changed over time:

EraRunout MarkerNotes
Up to 1969SJW-#### matrix prefixSidney J. Wakefield era
c. 1969–1979Stamped (W) “Tulip” / “(ᵛᵛ)” logo + 5-digit numberThe Tulip logo is the classic identifier
c. 1979(W) logo + WA suffix to catalog numberTransitional
c. 1980Stamped W over M logo + 5-digit numberPost-Sidney era
c. 1986WA3 stamp + WAKEFIELD CO. stamp + W over M logoLate period
c. 1988A WAKEFIELD PRESSING stamp + 5-digit numberFinal years

When the WM logo is absent (Neil Young, Blue Note/Manhattan pressings), collectors rely on pressing ring diameter and the 5-digit processing number pattern for attribution.

Pressing ring diameters (inner label area boundary):

  • 1963–64: 73mm
  • 1964–1981: 33mm (single ring)
  • 1980–1989: two-step 33mm/70mm (“muffin top”), with 43mm or 46mm shadow ring; also 34/63mm and 33/72mm variants

Printed code: “95” appears on US K-Tel pressings from Wakefield.

Open Questions

  • The exact founding year of the plant is uncertain. Discogs says “circa 1957” and the first documented pressing in the DB is 1958, but trade press documentation (referenced in the Discogs profile via Google Books Billboard links) may have earlier records.
  • Annual production figures (the “4.5 million records” peak) are not dated in available sources — unclear which year or era this figure refers to.
  • The nature of the Pasadena, California cassette duplication plant (opened 1983) is undocumented beyond the one-line mention — whether it was a Wakefield-owned facility or a contracted operation, and when it closed, is unknown.
  • The two unidentified mastering engineers (DN / DⓃ and RN / RⓃ) remain uncredited by the Discogs community.
  • Allen Landau’s active dates at Wakefield (circa 1978–1981 per Discogs profile) need independent verification — he may have overlapped with or preceded Bittner.
  • How Wakefield’s closure in 1989 related to broader industry pressures (the CD transition, consolidation of pressing plant market) would require trade press sources to document properly.
  • Whether any Wakefield equipment or personnel transitioned to another Phoenix-area facility after 1989 is unknown.
