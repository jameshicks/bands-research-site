Overview

Creativeman Disc is the US branch of the Japanese label Creativeman Disc. (also referenced as Creative Man USA), active through the 1990s with thirteen catalogue entries. Its roster is rooted firmly in the British punk tradition — Sex Pistols, their original engineer and producer Dave Goodman, Andy Blade of Eater, and associates Andy Peak and Brian Chevette — giving a Japanese-American imprint an unusually concentrated connection to first-wave UK punk. Beyond the punk core, the label’s output stretches into alternative rock, indie rock, and avant-garde territory.

Notes (web)

Creativeman Productions: promoter background

Creativeman’s parent, Creativeman Productions Co., Ltd., was established in 1990 as a Tokyo-based concert promoter. As of 2026 it is led by CEO Naoki Shimizu and bills itself as the largest independent music promoter in Asia, with Summer Sonic (launched 2000) as its flagship festival. source: musicbusinessworldwide.com, 2026-08-12

How the label arm appears to have worked

No primary source (press interview, company statement, or music-press profile) was found that directly explains the founding rationale or licensing mechanism of Creativeman Disc. The pattern visible in the catalogue and in release credits points to two overlapping channels:

  1. Tour-to-record pipeline. Creativeman Productions booked UK punk acts (Sham 69, GBH, Chaos UK, Angelic Upstarts, etc.) for Japan tours in the early 1990s. Live recordings from those tours were released on Creativeman Disc — e.g., Sham 69’s Live in Japan (CMD-001, 1993) and GBH’s Live in Japan (CMD-005, 1994). Wikipedia’s article on the Sham 69 release notes Creativeman Disc as the US distributor alongside Dojo’s UK release, suggesting the label acted as the North American outlet for Japan-recorded live product it already owned or co-owned. source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_in_Japan_(Sham_69_album), as of 2026-11-19

  2. Dave Goodman tape licensing. The Sex Pistols releases (CMD-056, CMD-058, CMD-059, CMD-060) are all drawn from recordings controlled by Dave Goodman, the band’s original live engineer and demo producer, who independently licensed his archive tapes to multiple labels throughout the 1980s–90s. Goodman initiated many of these archival releases himself. No source names the specific deal between Goodman and Creativeman Disc, but the relationship is consistent with Goodman’s well-documented practice of placing his tapes with small independent labels. source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Goodman_(record_producer), as of 2026-11-19

US address and structure

Creativeman Disc (US) was registered at 1875 Century Park East, Suite 1165, Los Angeles, CA 90067 — a commercial office address in Century City. This is consistent with a small licensing/distribution operation rather than a full label infrastructure. Distribution for some releases is credited to Cargo Records (Canada) and its US affiliate Cargo Music. [source: Discogs label entry for Creativeman Disc, discogs.com/label/28007]

What this does not explain

No source was found that explains: who at Creativeman Productions decided to establish the US label arm; whether the label was a deliberate diversification from promotion or an opportunistic vehicle; or how the relationship with Dave Goodman was first established.
