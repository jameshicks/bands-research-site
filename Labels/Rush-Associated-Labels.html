Overview

Russell Simmons and Lyor Cohen launched Rush Associated Labels in 1990 as a Def Jam sub-group modeled on the CBS Associated Labels concept — an umbrella framework designed to extend creative autonomy to fellow artists and producers while remaining under CBS distribution. Its four sub-imprints (Dew Doo Man Records, JMJ, No Face, and P.R.O. Division) drew a roster that included Erick Sermon, Warren G, and Montell Jordan, with output concentrated in Gangsta, G-Funk, and Hardcore Hip-Hop across roughly 160 releases in the 1990s. The venture proved short-lived: all the sub-labels failed commercially and the company was absorbed into Def Jam Music Group around 1998.
