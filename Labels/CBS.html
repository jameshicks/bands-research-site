CBS Records

Major American record company, 1938–1991. Founded as a subsidiary of the Columbia Broadcasting System (CBS), it grew into one of the “Big Six” major labels of the late twentieth century, controlling Columbia Records and Epic among its key imprints. In 1988 Sony Corporation acquired CBS Records for approximately $2 billion, and by 1991 the CBS brand was retired in favour of Sony Music Entertainment. Discogs label record ID: 19936 (CBS Records, parent company); 3072 (CBS, the operating label).

History

CBS Records traces its origins to the Columbia Phonograph Company, which CBS acquired in 1938. By the postwar era CBS Records had consolidated Columbia as its flagship US imprint and launched Epic Records in 1953 as a second pop/rock outlet. Through the 1960s and 1970s the group controlled one of the most diverse catalogs in popular music — signing artists across rock, jazz, classical (under CBS Masterworks), soul, and country.

In the UK the company operated as CBS United Kingdom Ltd. (Discogs ID: 272164), trading under the plain CBS brand (ID: 3072) rather than the Columbia name (which EMI controlled in Britain). This meant that artists distributed through CBS in the US — including acts on Columbia — were issued in the UK under the CBS imprint with its own catalog numbering. The Psychedelic Furs’ debut, for example, was a Columbia release in North America but a CBS release in Europe.

Through the 1980s the label signed major post-punk and new wave acts in the UK via its CBS Records Ltd. operations. Adam and the Ants, The Psychedelic Furs, and Nina Hagen all appeared on CBS internationally during this period.

The label also distributed Philadelphia International Records through the 1970s and into the 1980s — Gamble & Huff’s Philadelphia soul empire reached its audience largely through CBS’s distribution network.

In 1987 Sony began negotiations and completed the purchase of CBS Records in January 1988 for $2 billion USD. The transition to Sony Music Entertainment Inc. was formalised in 1991, at which point the CBS label name was retired globally.

Adam and the Ants / Adam Ant

Adam And The Ants — CBS UK issued Kings of the Wild Frontier (1980, CBS 84549) and Prince Charming (1981, CBS 85268), alongside their hit singles “Antmusic,” “Stand and Deliver,” and “Dog Eat Dog.”

Nina Hagen

Nina Hagen — Released on CBS internationally from 1979 through the mid-1980s, including Nunsexmonkrock (1982) and Fearless (1983).

Soft Machine

Soft Machine — CBS UK issued their progressive-rock run including Third (1970, CBS 66246), Fourth (1971, CBS 64280), Fifth (1972, CBS 64806), and Six (1973).

Matching Mole

Matching Mole — Both studio albums on CBS UK: Matching Mole (1972, CBS S 64850) and Matching Mole’s Little Red Record (1972, CBS S 65260). Also the single “O Caroline” (CBS).

Robert Wyatt

Robert Wyatt — The End of an Ear (1970, CBS 64189), his first solo album after leaving Soft Machine, appeared on CBS.

Kaoma

Kaoma — The lambada act’s global hits Worldbeat and Dançando Lambada (1989) were released on CBS internationally.

Philadelphia International (distributed)

Philadelphia International Records — Distributed through CBS in the 1970s–80s. Gamble & Huff’s soul catalog (The O’Jays, Harold Melvin & the Blue Notes, Teddy Pendergrass) reached consumers via CBS’s distribution infrastructure.
