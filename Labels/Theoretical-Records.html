Theoretical Records

Self-release imprint used by Theoretical Girls, The Static, and Glorious Strangers. New York City, 1978–1980. Discogs label ID: 92964.

About / History

Theoretical Records was a minimal self-financed imprint operating at the edge of New York’s No Wave scene in the late 1970s. The label has no documented contact info, profile text, or parent — it was purely a distribution vehicle, likely funded out-of-pocket to press and sell 7” singles at gigs and through downtown record shops.

The label is primarily identified with Glenn Branca, who was the central figure in both of the bands that anchored its first two releases. Theoretical Girls — the band Branca co-led with Jeffrey Lohn — issued the label’s first single in 1978, with no catalog number assigned. The lineup printed on the sleeve was Branca, Lohn, Margaret DeWys, and Wharton Tiers. The A-side “U.S. Millie” was written by Lohn; the B-side “You Got Me” was written by Branca. Both sides were in the angular, guitar-driven style typical of the No Wave downtown circuit.

The label’s second release (TR 02, 1979) was by The Static, another Branca-linked group, this time with Barbara Ess and Christine Hahn. The single was produced by Dan Graham (the conceptual artist who was deeply embedded in the downtown scene) and Mark Bingham, and all music was written by Branca. The two sides are “My Relationship” and “Don’t Let Me Stop You.”

The third and final release (TR-03, 1980) steps slightly outside the immediate Branca orbit. Glorious Strangers — led by Wharton Tiers and his partner Carol Tiers, with additional members Jan Ewing and Jean Seton Shaw — released the single “Why Don’t You Join The Army?” / “Media, Media.” Wharton Tiers wrote both A-side and the music on the B-side; Carol Tiers co-wrote the lyrics on the B-side. The record was engineered by Mark Hood and mastered at Masterdisk by Bob Ludwig, making it a notably more professionally finished production than the first two releases.

After TR-03, no further releases have been documented under the Theoretical Records imprint. Branca moved on to his own solo work and eventually to larger label relationships. The three singles stand as a small but historically significant document of the self-organized No Wave 7” micro-economy.

Notes (web)

Label ownership and administration

The Glenn Branca archive at Northwestern University (Deering Library, Evanston IL) contains an item catalogued as “Theoretical Records: Blank stationery” — physical letterhead or administrative paper stock held in Branca’s personal collection. This is the strongest available evidence that Theoretical Records was Branca’s own administrative entity, not a shared imprint. No equivalent stationery or correspondence from Lohn or Tiers has been documented in publicly accessible sources. The archive does not contain financial records or pressing contracts for the label that are described in the online finding aid. Source: Northwestern University finding aid, Glenn Branca collection (accessed 2026-06-08).

After Theoretical Records ceased activity, Branca launched Neutral Records in 1982 in partnership with White Columns gallerist Josh Baer — a separate and much better-documented label. The Wire’s Alan Licht obituary notes that 99 Records (Ed Bahlman) was a parallel downtown imprint that Branca also had ties to. Neither Lohn nor Tiers appears in any account of Neutral Records’ founding. Source: The Wire, “Glenn Branca 1948–2018” by Alan Licht (accessed 2026-06-08).

Pressing quantities

No pressing quantities for any of the three singles have been located in any publicly available source — Discogs, Wikipedia, AllMusic, Rate Your Music, the Acute Records reissue blog, or music press. This gap remains open. The Acute Records blog post (acuterecords.com/blog/?page_id=230), which accompanied the 2002 CD reissue of the Theoretical Girls material, could not be fetched during this research session (connection error). It is a potential source for pressing context if accessible.

Theoretical Girls single catalog number

The matrix/runout on the Theoretical Girls single has been cited in passing as “41071/2-78” — a matrix stamp, not a label-assigned catalog number. This is consistent with the Discogs listing of “none”: the sleeve and label carried no printed catalog number. The matrix code is a pressing plant reference, not an official imprint designation. Source: search results aggregating Discogs matrix data (punkscrawledartist.com, rarevinyl.com; direct page access blocked). This should not be entered as a catalog number but could be noted as matrix data if added to the Releases file.

Conflict: track sequence on Theoretical Girls single

The vault’s Releases table lists A-side as “U.S. Millie” (Lohn) and B-side as “You Got Me” (Branca). Wikipedia’s Theoretical Girls article reverses this, listing the single as “You Got Me” (Branca) / “U.S. Millie” (Lohn) — i.e. “You Got Me” on the A-side. Discogs is the authoritative source here; this conflict should be resolved against the Discogs release page (r1040428) rather than Wikipedia. Source: Wikipedia, Theoretical Girls (as of 2026-06-08).

Conflict: The Static vs. Theoretical Girls — chronological order

Multiple sources describe Branca forming a band he originally called The Static in the mid-1970s before he met Lohn; when Lohn joined, the project became Theoretical Girls. This is a different band from The Static (Ess/Hahn/Branca) that released TR 02 in 1979. The vault’s current framing (Theoretical Girls as the first band, The Static on Theoretical Records as a later offshoot) is correct as a discographic description — the Theoretical Records releases are what matters here — but the naming history is more complicated than the article implies. Wikipedia’s Glenn Branca article conflates the two phases of The Static. The two-band reading (Theoretical Girls 1977–81, The Static 1979 separate trio) is supported by Discogs entity separation (artist IDs 125108 and 297535 respectively) and by the Far Out Magazine and The Wire accounts. Source: Wikipedia, Glenn Branca (as of 2026-06-08); The Wire, Alan Licht (accessed 2026-06-08).

Dan Graham’s role

The Wire confirms that Dan Graham — credited as co-producer on The Static single (TR 02) — was also the person who booked Theoretical Girls’ first performance (at Franklin Furnace) and was deeply involved in the group’s formation via his friendship with Jeffrey Lohn. His presence on TR 02 is thus not incidental: he was the scene’s key institutional connector across multiple Theoretical Records releases. Source: The Wire, Alan Licht (accessed 2026-06-08).
