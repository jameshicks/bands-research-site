Jade Tree

Discogs ID: 18608 Active: 1990–2011 Origin: Wilmington, Delaware Founded by: Darren Walters and Tim Owen Catalog prefix: JT#### Total releases: ~716 Parent (catalog): Epitaph Records (acquired catalog rights 2017)

Independent punk and post-hardcore label out of Wilmington, Delaware. Operated for two decades as one of the defining labels of 1990s–2000s emo, post-hardcore, and indie rock. Home to Cap’n Jazz, The Promise Ring, Jets To Brazil, Lifetime (2), Kid Dynamite (3), Joan Of Arc, and Strike Anywhere. Also released These Arms Are Snakes’ first two LPs. In 2017, Epitaph purchased the rights to Jade Tree’s catalog.

History

Foundation and early years (1990–1995)

Jade Tree was founded in Wilmington, Delaware by Darren Walters and Tim Owen. The label emerged from the East Coast hardcore/punk underground and initially operated in small-run vinyl and tape formats before building out a more structured catalog. Early releases drew from the mid-Atlantic punk scene.

One of the label’s earliest high-water marks was Lifetime (2)‘s Hello Bastards (1995), produced and engineered by Steve Evetts — one of Jade Tree’s most-used producers. Lifetime, from New Jersey, bridged melodic hardcore and post-hardcore in a way that presaged much of what Jade Tree would become known for. Hello Bastards featured Ari Katz, Dan Yemin, Dave Palaitis, Pete Martin, and Scott Golley.

Peak years: Chicago emo and the JT sound (1996–2001)

The second half of the decade marked Jade Tree’s commercial and critical peak. The label signed a cluster of bands from the Chicago and Midwest post-hardcore/emo scene that would define the genre’s second wave.

Cap’n Jazz (Chicago) released their catalog through Jade Tree — the posthumous Analphabetapolothology double-LP compilation (JT1036, 1998) collected their recordings and became a foundational emo text, mastered by Alan Douches and compiled/recorded by Casey Rice. Core members included Tim Kinsella and Mike Kinsella, Davey Von Bohlen, Victor Villarreal (2), and Sam Zurick.

The Promise Ring became Jade Tree’s highest-volume artist (71 catalog entries). Their 30° Everywhere (JT1026, 1996) and Nothing Feels Good (JT1035, 1997) defined midwestern emo for a generation. Nothing Feels Good — produced by J. Robbins with engineering from Doug Easley, Davis McCain, and Stuart Sikes, mastered by Douches — is the label’s most-pressed release at 22 pressings. The Promise Ring lineup on that record: Davey Von Bohlen, Jason Gnewikow, Scott Beschta, Daniel Didier, and Tim Burton.

Jets To Brazil arrived at the end of the decade: Orange Rhyming Dictionary (JT1038, 1998), produced by J. Robbins, engineered by Stuart Sikes, mastered by Douches, featured Blake Schwarzenbach (ex-Jawbreaker) alongside Jeremy Chatelain and Chris Daly. 19 pressings — the label’s second-most-pressed title.

Owls (2001) — the Tim and Mike Kinsella post-Cap’n Jazz project, with Victor Villarreal and Sam Zurick — was recorded by Steve Albini and mastered by John Golden. Albini’s presence on a Jade Tree release was notable.

J. Robbins (ex-Jawbox / Burning Airlines) was the dominant producer of this era, with 60 production credits across the label. Jason Gnewikow handled much of the label’s visual identity (97 design credits) in addition to playing guitar in The Promise Ring.

Dan Yemin (Lifetime) reappeared as a founder of Kid Dynamite (3), whose 30 catalog entries made them one of Jade Tree’s most-catalogued artists. The Yemin thread — Lifetime → Kid Dynamite → Paint It Black — runs through multiple label eras.

Later period and wind-down (2002–2011)

Strike Anywhere’s Change Is A Sound (JT1060, 2001) at 14 pressings was among the label’s later-period standouts. Pedro The Lion (40 catalog entries) and Joan Of Arc (35) sustained output through the mid-2000s.

These Arms Are Snakes released This Is Meant To Hurt You (2003) and Oxeneers Or The Lion Sleeps When Its Antelope Go Home (2004) on Jade Tree — their most-pressed TAAS release at 13 pressings — before the band moved to Hydra Head Records.

Brian McTernan contributed 39 production credits across the catalog, joining Steve Evetts (45) as one of the label’s regular producers outside of Robbins.

The label wound down activity around 2010–2011. In 2017, Epitaph Records purchased the Jade Tree catalog.

Connections

  • Hydra Head Records — overlapping post-hardcore underground; Jade Tree alumni (TAAS, others) moved to HH; both labels were defining forces of the 2000s underground
  • Dischord Records — J. Robbins (Jawbox, Burning Airlines) bridges the DC post-hardcore scene and Jade Tree production
  • Jawbreaker — Blake Schwarzenbach founded Jets To Brazil after Jawbreaker’s dissolution; Jade Tree provided a home for the next chapter
  • Cap’n Jazz → The Promise Ring / Joan Of Arc / Owls — Kinsella family tree; Davey Von Bohlen in both Cap’n Jazz and Promise Ring; the Chicago emo diaspora was largely housed at Jade Tree
  • Lifetime (2) → Kid Dynamite (3) — Dan Yemin thread; two bands, one label, across two eras
  • Epitaph Records — acquired Jade Tree catalog 2017
