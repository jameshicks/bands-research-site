Captured Tracks

Discogs ID: 134201 Origin: Brooklyn, New York Active: 2008–present Founded by: Mike Sniper Parent: Omnian Music Group (749582) Sublabels: Excavations (1904182) Label Code: LC 30356 Catalog prefix: CT-### (standard); FNCT### (Flying Nun NZ reissues); CTBOX-### (box sets) Catalog size: ~1,382 releases (Discogs)

Independent label founded in 2008 by Mike Sniper — a Brooklyn figure who had simultaneously designed sleeves for early Sacred Bones releases (Dead Luke SBR-006, Nice Face SBR-007, 2008). Captured Tracks and Sacred Bones ran parallel out of the same Brooklyn DIY orbit in the late 2000s; Blank Dogs appeared on both labels in their founding years.

Founding Story — Mike Sniper

Sniper grew up on the Jersey Shore and played bass in DC Snipers before moving to New York. After college he worked at Midnight Records (Chelsea), a niche store that also operated as a label, and later at Academy Records in Williamsburg — where he worked alongside Sacred Bones founder Caleb Braaten. (SLUG Magazine, as of 2026-10-02). That retail immersion — “you listen to music 8 hours a day and talk about music 8 hours” — formed his taste across psychedelia, garage rock, punk, and power pop. (STAF Magazine interview, as of 2026-10-02)

Alongside the record-store jobs Sniper took on freelance design and illustration work for indie labels and bands including Tyvek, Black Lips, Jay Reatard, and Dead Moon, as well as sleeve work for early Sacred Bones releases. (The Vinyl Factory, 10-year interview, as of 2026-10-02) He also co-ran Radio Heartbeat, a power-pop and punk reissue imprint, but grew frustrated by the genre’s insularity: “It’s a genre with a lot of people who only listen to that type of music, which always frustrated me.” (Diffuser, 5th anniversary interview, 2013, as of 2026-10-02)

Around age 27–28 Sniper started the solo project Blank Dogs “out of frustration of not finding the music I wanted to play.” Friends Mike Simonetti (Troubleman Records) and Larry Hardy (In The Red Recordings) released early Blank Dogs records, giving Sniper a template for independent releasing without touring obligations. The label name came from a Blank Dogs tape titled Captured Tracks Volume 1 — the title was suggested by Vivian Girls’ Cassie, and Sniper chose it because, as he put it, “it did not evoke anything” and left the imprint open-ended. (The Vinyl Factory, as of 2026-10-02)

In 2008 Sniper liquidated his personal collection of rare power-pop 45s on eBay, raising approximately $12,000–$15,000 (figure varies by interview; $12–13k per Diffuser; ~$15k per SLUG Magazine). He used the proceeds to press the label’s first releases — a Blank Dogs EP and the debut by Dum Dum Girls — operating from a basement space beneath Academy Records in Williamsburg. The label formally launched in 2008; the first numbered release (CT-001) shipped in 2009. (Diffuser; Merry-Go-Round Magazine; SLUG Magazine, all as of 2026-10-02)

The label signed its first numbered release (CT-001) to Dum Dum Girls in 2009 and rapidly became central to the wave of lo-fi/jangle/dream-pop that defined Brooklyn indie circa 2010–2014. Beach Fossils, Wild Nothing, and DIIV established the label’s aesthetic early; Mac DeMarco became its dominant commercial force after signing in 2012, eventually accounting for 160 catalog entries — the most of any CT artist. The label also distributed a substantial tranche of Flying Nun back-catalog (New Zealand acts The Clean, Chills, Bats, etc.) via the FNCT sub-series.

Parent company Omnian Music Group (749582) sits above CT and presumably handles distribution. The Excavations sublabel (1904182) appears to operate as a companion imprint.

Era Overview

2008–2010 — Founding / Lo-Fi Wave

First release was a 2008 compilation (Captured Tracks Volume One, Blank Dogs-led). First numbered release CT-001 was the Dum Dum Girls debut (2009, written by Dee Dee (18)). Beach Fossils (CT-67, 2010) and Wild Nothing Gemini (CT-68, 2010) arrived near-simultaneously, cementing the label’s lo-fi/bedroom-pop identity. The Soft Moon (CT-85, 2010) brought a darker, motorik edge. All three were produced/self-recorded by their respective songwriters — Dustin Payseur, Jack Tatum, and Luis Vasquez (2) — with minimal outside involvement. Josh Bonati (ID 631289, Sacred Bones’ house mastering engineer) appeared uncredited on Gemini, indicating early cross-label overlap in technical services.

2011–2013 — Expansion

DIIV Oshin (CT-158, 2012, 19 pressings) was the label’s first breakout of the era — recorded and mixed by Daniel Schlett at Strange Weather Studio; lineup: Zachary Cole Smith (songs, production), Andrew Bailey (3), Colby Hewitt, Devin Ruben Perez, Ben “Wolf” Newman (drums on selected tracks). Mac DeMarco arrived with Rock And Roll Night Club (CT-140, 2012, 17 pressings) and 2 (CT-164, 2012, 26 pressings) — both self-recorded by DeMarco, mastered by Josh Bonati — establishing the jangly, tape-warped sound he would define. Saâda Bonaire, a 1980s West German act, was reissued (CT-181, 2013, 10 pressings).

2014–2015 — Peak Indie Moment

Mac DeMarco Salad Days (CT-193, 2014, 31 pressings — most-pressed CT release) and Another One (CT-228, 2015, 15 pressings) extended DeMarco’s run. Salad Days credits: DeMarco self-written/performed/recorded; Josh Bonati mastered; Ryan McCardle layout; Stefan Marx typography; Coley Brown cover photo. Perfect Pussy Say Yes To Love (CT-192, 2014, 6 pressings) was one of the label’s most critically noted punk-adjacent releases — recorded by Shaun Sutkus, mastered by Bonati, design by Ryan McCardle.

2016–2019 — Consolidation / DIIV Maturation

DIIV Is The Is Are (CT-231, 2016, 15 pressings) and Deceiver (CT-300, 2019, 12 pressings) continued the label’s flagship act. Mac DeMarco This Old Dog (CT-260, 2017, 30 pressings) was his second-most-pressed release. Widowspeak (2011–2024, 53 releases) and Molly Burch (2016–2023, 45 releases) became steady mid-tier presences. Drahla signed from Leeds (2018, 22 releases).

2020s — New Signings / Ongoing

The Lemon Twigs Everything Harmony (CT-358, 2023, 9 pressings) and A Dream Is All We Know (CT-375, 2024, 11 pressings) marked the label’s most prominent newer signing — Brian and Michael D’Addario self-produced with elaborate orchestral arrangements (string quartet Friction Quartet; horn sections; self-engineered by Michael D’Addario; mastered by Paul Gold and Paul D. Millar). Mac DeMarco remained on roster through 2026.

FNCT Sub-Series (Flying Nun Reissues)

Captured Tracks distributed a substantial run of Flying Nun Records back-catalog under the FNCT prefix — New Zealand acts including The Clean, The Chills, The Bats, The Verlaines, Skeptics, Snapper, Toy Love, Tall Dwarfs, Able Tasmans, Look Blue Go Purple, Bird Nest Roys, and Chris Knox. These were reissues of 1980s–1990s New Zealand post-punk/indie releases, making CT a significant conduit for Flying Nun catalog in the North American market.

Connections

  • Sacred Bones — Mike Sniper designed SBR sleeves (Dead Luke SBR-006, Nice Face SBR-007, 2008) in parallel with founding CT; Blank Dogs appeared on both labels
  • Josh Bonati — Sacred Bones’ house mastering engineer also credited on multiple CT releases (Gemini, Salad Days, Say Yes To Love)
  • Flying Nun — FNCT series; Captured Tracks acted as US reissue partner for NZ catalog

Notes (web)

The Soft Moon / Luis Vasquez — Label Departure and Death

The Soft Moon’s move away from Captured Tracks happened between 2015 and 2018, not after 2021. The final CT studio album was Deeper (2015); the final CT release was Deeper Remixed (2016, co-released with aufnahme + wiedergabe). By 2018 Vasquez had signed to Sacred Bones for Criminal, recorded in a basement studio in Berlin (his new home city at the time). He remained on Sacred Bones for the final album Exister (2022). The 2021 end-date in the Discogs roster reflects the tail of CT catalog activity (reissues, compilations) rather than an active signing end.

No public statement from Vasquez or Captured Tracks explaining the label change has been found. No interview surfaces a specific reason; the move coincided with Vasquez relocating from the US to Berlin. (post-punk.com announcement, 2018; Wikipedia — The Soft Moon, as of 2026-10-19)

Separately, Vasquez (Jose Luis Vasquez, born May 30, 1979) died on January 18, 2024, aged 44. His body was found at a Los Angeles residence belonging to techno DJ Silent Servant (John Juan Mendez) after Vasquez’s wife requested a welfare check. The coroner determined the cause of death was the combined effects of fentanyl, cocaine, ketamine, and ethanol. Mendez and his partner Simone Ling also died. (The Quietus obituary; Consequence)
