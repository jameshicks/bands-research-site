BPitch Control

Berlin-based independent electronic label founded by Ellen Allien in 1999. One of the defining German imprints for techno, electro, and leftfield electronic music through the 2000s and into the 2020s. Discogs label ID: 1466. Label Code: LC 11753. Parent company: BPitch Control GmbH (Discogs ID: 305080).

History

BPitch Control launched in Berlin in 1999 with a run of vinyl-only singles — the earliest carrying the catalog prefix BP-CTRL or BPITCH CONTROL before standardizing on the BPC prefix. The first few releases were by Various Artists compilations capturing the emerging Berlin techno scene. Smash TV (Holger Zilske and Michael Schmidt) arrived in 2001 and became one of BPitch’s most prolific acts. Ellen Allien was simultaneously artist and label owner: her own debut singles appeared on BPC within the first year.

1999–2002 — Founding and Early Catalog

The label’s early catalog centered on a tight Berlin clique. Paul Kalkbrenner released his debut dB+ EP (BPC 012, 2000) and the Superimpose album (BPC016LP, 2001), establishing an early signature sound of melodic techno with driving structure. Ellen Allien’s own Stadtkind LP (BPC021, 2001) gave the label its first full artist statement: a document of Berlin club life and electro-influenced hardware techno.

Sascha Funke was present from the start (BPC 011, 2000), as were Toktok vs. Soffy O. (Missy Queen’s Gonna Die, BPC014, 2000) and Kiki, who would become one of the most prolific roster members. Smash TV arrived in 2001 with Rock On Boy (BPC 020) and went on to release several albums on the label through 2008. Compilations — Kollektiv (BPC 019, 2000), Berlin 2001 (BPC 031, 2001) — served as periodic label showcases.

2002–2006 — Core Era and International Reach

The period from 2002 to 2006 represents BPitch’s most concentrated critical mass. Key developments:

  • Modeselektor debuted with Death Medley (BPC 054, 2002) and built a reputation for a rawer, hip-hop-inflected take on Berlin techno, releasing several albums on BPC including Turn Deaf! (BPC 099, 2004) and Hello Mom! (BPC 115, 2005).
  • Apparat (real name Sascha Ring) released several EPs on BPC from 2003–2006, including Koax (BPC 070, 2003) and Can’t Computerize It (BPC 097, 2004). His later collaboration with Ellen Allien produced the landmark joint LP Orchestra Of Bubbles (BPC 125, 2006), a sleek album of electro and IDM that brought the label significant international press attention.
  • Moderat — the trio of Modeselektor’s Gernot Bronsert and Sebastian Szary with Apparat’s Sascha Ring — first released as a unit on BPC with Auf Kosten Der Gesundheit (BPC 057, 2003), a teaser that pointed toward their later full debut.
  • Paul Kalkbrenner released Self (BPC 083, 2004), a major album in his catalog, and the Reworks series (BPC 136–142, 2006), remixes of his back catalog by various artists.
  • Ellen Allien released Berlinette (BPC 065, 2003) and Thrills (BPC 106, 2005), her first internationally distributed LPs, as well as the joint project Orchestra Of Bubbles with Apparat.
  • Tomas Andersson, Ben Klock, and Housemeister added to the label’s breadth.

The Boogybytes mix CD series — curated by Kiki, Sascha Funke, Modeselektor, and later Ellen Allien and Seth Troxler — ran from BPC 120 (2006) to BPC 210 (2010) and captured the label’s live DJ culture.

Design across this era was dominated by Pfadfinderei (Discogs ID: 100515), a Berlin design collective credited across 46 releases in the BPitch catalog — their vector-based, circuit-board-inflected sleeve aesthetic became as recognizable as any sonic signature.

2007–2010 — Peak Profile and Berlin Calling

Paul Kalkbrenner’s 2008 film Berlin Calling — in which he starred as a DJ falling apart during a festival run — produced the soundtrack album (BPC 185, 2008), one of the label’s best-selling releases. Tracks from the film (“Aaron,” “Azure,” “Sky And Sand” — the latter featuring vocals by his brother Fritz Kalkbrenner) became widely played, and “Sky And Sand” in particular became a pop-crossover hit in Germany.

Moderat released their self-titled debut LP (BPC 200, 2009) — recorded by Modeselektor (Gernot Bronsert, Sebastian Szary) and Apparat (Sascha Ring). It was a major critical success, blending dubstep textures, electro rhythms, and Sascha Ring’s vocals into a widely accessible electronic sound. Tracks included “A New Error,” “Rusty Nails,” “Seamonkey,” and “Slow Match” (featuring Paul St. Hilaire). The album reached well beyond the club audience.

Other notable 2009 arrivals: Delay (Symptoms, BPC 193), Telefon Tel Aviv (Immolate Yourself, BPC 188), and the Moderat singles run.

2011–2019 — Transition, Dillon, and Later Chapters

By the early 2010s several founding-era artists had moved on (Modeselektor, Apparat, Moderat all shifted to Monkeytown Records or other labels for later releases). BPitch began transitioning toward new signings:

  • Dillon (Dillon Figueiredo Ribeiro), a Brazilian-German vocalist, released This Silence Kills (BPC 244CD, 2011), her debut album — a spare, piano-and-electronics song record with genuine pop crossover reach. She remained on the label through 2022’s 6abotage (BPX 026CD).
  • Aerea Negrot, Chaim, Skinnerbox (2), Joy Wellboy, and System Of Survival filled out the 2011–2015 period.
  • Telefon Tel Aviv’s The Birds (BPC 247, 2012) was the last TTA release on BPC.
  • The catalog prefix shifted from BPC to BPX around 2019, signaling a new chapter: Ellen Allien’s Alientronic LP (BPX 001, 2019) opened the series, followed by a Alientronic remix run.

2020–present — We Are Not Alone and Current Roster

The We Are Not Alone series (WANA- prefix) — a club compilation series curated by Ellen Allien — launched in 2020 as a vehicle for new and emerging Berlin-affiliated artists, running through multiple volumes by 2022. The sublabel We Are Not Alone (2) (Discogs ID: 2448583) was formalized for this project.

Current roster activity (2022–2025) has included Ash Code / Ellen Allien collaboration (Dance And Kill, BPX 027, 2023), Crystal Geometry, Rosa Anschütz, Boston 168, Lebanon Hanover, and Dillon.

Catalog

Catalog prefix history: BP-CTRL / BPITCH CONTROL (1999–2000) → BPC (2001–2018) → BPX (2019–present). Catalog ran from BPC 001 (1999) to at least BPC 342 (2019) before BPX numbering began.

Connections

  • Ellen Allien — founder and central creative force across the label’s entire history
  • Paul Kalkbrenner — career-defining artist; Berlin Calling (2008) was a cultural landmark for the label
  • Moderat — trio album (2009) representing the convergence of the BPitch and Modeselektor/Apparat networks
  • Apparat — Orchestra Of Bubbles with Ellen Allien (2006) was a high-water mark
  • Modeselektor — Hello Mom! (2005) and Happy Birthday! (2007) brought the label wider audience
  • Telefon Tel Aviv — late addition; Immolate Yourself (2009) was released shortly before Charles Cooper’s death
  • Dillon — leading artist of the label’s 2010s chapter
  • Pfadfinderei — visual identity designers across the peak BPC era
  • Monkeytown Records — label later used by Modeselektor and Moderat after BPitch
  • We Are Not Alone (2) — sublabel for the WANA compilation series (2020–)

Open Questions

  • Exact founding date (year 1999 is clear; month not in Discogs)

  • The Memo and Spielwiese sublabels have almost no release data in Discogs — their specific function (singles series? specific genre?) is not clear from DB alone
  • Paul Kalkbrenner’s departure from BPitch Control — his last release on BPC is from 2022 (BPC cat no) but his main output moved elsewhere after Berlin Calling; the exact terms of any ongoing relationship are not in Discogs
