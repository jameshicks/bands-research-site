Y Records

Early 1980s UK independent label. Based at 42 Leinster Square, London W2 (contact defunct by 1981 per Discogs). Label Code: LC 8074. Distributed by Rough Trade. Discogs label ID: 5338.

Owned and run by Disc O’Dell (real name Richard O’Dell; also credited as Dick O’Dell and Dirk Mephisto), who managed The Pop Group (from 1978), The Slits (from 1979), and later William Orbit, Ed Harcourt, and Bat for Lashes. Before managing bands, O’Dell did lighting for Curved Air and Deep Purple (c. 1970), moved into tour managing with the Sensational Alex Harvey Band (mid-1970s) and The Stranglers (1977). He founded Y Records in 1980 after both The Pop Group and The Slits lost their label deals. O’Dell later co-founded Guerilla Records with William Orbit (1990). (source: bristolarchiverecords.com) The label takes its name from the “Y” in the Bristol post-punk scene’s self-referential framing: the idea of a collective, questioning sensibility.

Sublabel: Y America (Discogs ID: 57171) — US-market pressings, primarily used for Shriekback releases.

About / History

Y Records launched in 1980 as an outlet for the Bristol post-punk axis centred on The Pop Group and their associates. Its founding releases — the Pop Group/Slits split single (Y1), The Pop Group’s second album For How Much Longer Do We Tolerate Mass Murder? (Y 2), and the We Are Time compilation (Y 5) — established the label’s character: confrontational, politically charged, and sonically restless, drawing on dub, funk, free jazz, and punk noise in equal measure.

The label was set up near Portobello Road, West London (the Leinster Square, W2 address is nearby Notting Hill), reflecting that while The Pop Group were Bristol-born, Y Records operated from London under O’Dell’s management infrastructure. Rough Trade’s distribution gave the label national reach through the independent network — and O’Dell has described the relationship as more than logistical: “Rough Trade was absolutely pivotal to making things happen through their distribution. But it wasn’t just the distribution. It was a hub of like minded people.” (source: thevinylfactory.com)

Founding context. Both The Pop Group and The Slits had lost their label deals by 1979 — The Pop Group cut ties with Radar Records (who released their debut single in March 1979 and debut LP Y), and The Slits were dropped by Island Records. O’Dell, already managing both bands, responded by founding Y Records: “The Pop Group & The Slits were both dropped by their labels. It was the beginning of the indie explosion & I just met Geoff Travis & the Rough Trade people.” (source: bristolarchiverecords.com) The label’s first release was the double-sided single pairing the two acts (Y1, 1980).

From 1981 the roster diversified rapidly beyond the founding Pop Group/Slits axis. Pigbag — a brass-heavy post-punk dance ensemble spun off from The Pop Group — became the label’s commercial backbone, and their 12-inch “Papa’s Got A Brand New Pigbag” (Y10, 1981) was Y Records’ biggest hit, reaching the UK singles chart. Pigbag remained the label’s most prolific act through 1983, releasing two studio albums (Dr Heckle And Mr Jive, Lend An Ear) and numerous singles.

Shriekback arrived in 1982 and quickly became the label’s most productive act in terms of release volume. Formed by Barry Andrews (ex-XTC, ex-Robert Fripp’s League of Gentlemen), Dave Allen (5) (ex-Gang of Four), and Carl Marsh, Shriekback cut an angular funk-inflected art-rock sound entirely distinct from the Bristol contingent. Their debut EP My Spine Is The Bassline (Y 27) and the mini-albums Tench and Sexthinkone were all Y Records releases, as were the later LPs Care (YLP 502) and Jam Science (YLP 504). US pressings appeared on the Y America sublabel.

Other notable 1982 signings included Maximum Joy — another Pop Group offshoot, led by Simon Underwood and featuring the multi-instrumentalist Janine Rainforth, with production by Adrian Sherwood on their Station M.X.J.Y. album — and the New York percussion ensemble Pulsallama, whose two Y singles extended the label’s transatlantic reach.

Y Records also released avant-garde and free-improvisation material that sat outside the post-punk mainstream: Diamanda Galás’s debut recording The Litanies of Satan (Y 18, 1982), Sun Ra’s Strange Celestial Road (Y 19, 1982) and Nuclear War (RA 1, 1982), and the double-LP Double Indemnity by free improviser Steve Beresford and cellist Tristan Honsinger (Y 9, 1981). This eclecticism was underlined by the label’s in-house compilation Birth Of The Y (Y 33 1/3, 1982), which drew on the full width of the roster — Pigbag, Maximum Joy, Diamanda Galás, Pulsallama, Sun Ra, Shriekback, Disconnection, and others.

The label was also home to New York hip-hop group The Fearless Four (YT 105, 1982) and funk/disco act R.A.P.P., reflecting O’Dell’s broad taste and transatlantic connections.

Closure. The Vinyl Factory places the label’s fold in “late 1983.” (source: thevinylfactory.com) The Wikipedia article states Y Records “were eventually incorporated into Kaz Records, part of Castle Communications”; Kaz Records Ltd was incorporated on 20 September 1983, consistent with a late-1983 wind-down. (source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Records, Kaz Records) After closure O’Dell co-founded the progressive house label Guerrilla with William Orbit in 1990. The Discogs catalog note of “defunct as of 1981” refers to the Leinster Square address only, not the label’s activity — releases continued at least to late 1983 and Shriekback’s Jam Science (YLP 504) carries a 1984 date, possibly as a licensing or distribution arrangement extending past Y Records’ active life.

Key Artists

The Pop Group

Bristol post-punk band, the label’s founding act. Members: Mark Stewart (vocals), Simon Underwood (bass), Gareth Sager (guitar/saxophone), John Waddington (guitar), Bruce Smith (drums), Dan Catsis (bass on later recordings). Discogs artist ID: 33932. For How Much Longer Do We Tolerate Mass Murder? (1980) is the key Y Records release — self-recorded and self-mixed by the band and Dave Anderson (3).

The Slits

Post-punk all-female band, fellow Bristol/London axis artists. Three releases on Y in 1980: the Man Next Door single, the split single with The Pop Group (Y1), and the bootleg retrospective LP. Discogs artist ID: 33930.

Pigbag

The label’s most commercially successful act and highest-volume releaser (95 releases in Discogs catalog across pressings). Bristol brass-funk ensemble emerging from The Pop Group’s orbit — Simon Underwood produced much of their early work. Discogs artist ID: 55839.

Shriekback

The label’s second-most-prolific act (66 releases). Formed 1981 from ex-members of XTC (Barry Andrews), Gang of Four (Dave Allen (5)), and Associates/Magazine (Carl Marsh). Self-produced throughout their Y tenure. Key engineers: John Fryer (Blackwing Studio, 24 releases), Ian Caple (21 releases). Discogs artist ID: 25725.

Maximum Joy

Post-punk dance ensemble, another Pop Group offshoot. Simon Underwood (bass/producer) is the throughline. Adrian Sherwood produced Station M.X.J.Y. (Y 28, 1982). Personnel included Janine Rainforth (vocals/violin/clarinet), Charlie Llewellin (drums), John Waddington (guitar/vocals), Tony Wrafter (flute/sax/trumpet), Kev Evans (bass/piano/vocals). Discogs artist ID: 47968.

Pulsallama

New York-based all-female percussion ensemble. Two Y singles (1982–1983). Part of the NYC no-wave/punk scene. Discogs artist ID: 336483.

Diamanda Galás

Greek-American avant-garde vocalist. The Litanies of Satan (Y 18, 1982) — her debut recording — was executive produced by Disc O’Dell and produced (music production) by Dave Hunt. A key document of the label’s reach into extreme experimental music. Discogs artist ID: 25413.

Sun Ra / The Sun Ra Arkestra

Two releases: Strange Celestial Road (Y 19) and Nuclear War (RA 1), both 1982. Discogs IDs: 35328 (Sun Ra), 2219395 (The Sun Ra Arkestra).

The Fearless Four

New York hip-hop group. Rockin’ It (YT 105, 1982). One of the earliest hip-hop releases on a UK independent label. Discogs artist ID: 13868.

Glaxo Babies

Bristol post-punk band. Limited Entertainment EP (Y6, 1980). One of the earliest Y releases, contemporaneous with The Pop Group and Slits. Discogs artist ID: 95809.

Mouth

Post-punk / funk act. Who’s Hot? (Y 20, 1982) and “Voyage To The Bottom Of The Sea” on Birth Of The Y. Members included Nellee Hooper (percussionist; simultaneously in Maximum Joy and The Wild Bunch), Andy Guy, Jamie Hill (2), and Rob Merrill. The most notable Y Records act in retrospect given Hooper’s subsequent career. Discogs artist ID: 433227.

Disconnection (2)

Avant-garde / disco / dub act. Bali Ha’i (Y 32, 1982) — a dub-disco reimagining of the Rodgers & Hammerstein song — opens the Birth Of The Y compilation. Core member: Angela Jaeger (also of The Drowning Craze and later Pigbag). The 1984 IRS Records follow-up We Love You featured strings by Vicky Aspinall (ex-The Raincoats) and vocals by Eddi Reader. Discogs artist ID: 425404.

Connections

  • Disc O’Dell — founder/owner/producer; later co-founded Guerilla Records
  • Rough Trade — distributor
  • 99 Records — licensed Maximum Joy’s “Stretch” for US release (1981)
  • The Pop Group — founding act; alumni went on to Maximum Joy, Pigbag, Mark Stewart And The Maffia, Rip Rig & Panic
  • Rip Rig & Panic — formed from Pop Group and Slits members (Gareth Sager, Bruce Smith, Neneh Cherry, Sean Oliver, Andrea Oliver); released on Virgin, not Y Records, but organically connected to the scene
  • On-U Sound — Adrian Sherwood’s label; intersects via Maximum Joy production
  • Gang of Four — Dave Allen (Shriekback) was their bassist before joining Y Records orbit
  • Dennis Bovell — produced R.A.P.P. track on Birth Of The Y; dub reggae connection

Lesser-Known Artists (Web Research Notes)

Tymon Dogg (Y 29, Battle Of Wills, 1982)

Born Stephen John Murray (1950, Formby, Lancashire). Multi-instrumentalist (violin, guitar, vocals). Early career: signed to Apple Records as a teenager (absconded with the masters), then a brief release on The Moody Blues’ Threshold label. Was a housemate of Joe Strummer in a squatted Westbourne Grove property in the early 1970s, and the two performed together with The 101ers. Dogg contributed “Lose This Skin” to The Clash’s Sandinista! (1980) — composing music and lyrics, singing and playing violin — and composed and played piano on “Death Is A Star” for Combat Rock (1982). He later joined Strummer’s Mescaleros (2000–2002). His Y Records LP Battle Of Wills was self-recorded, playing all instruments, funded in part by a £500 police assault settlement. The reissue promotional copy describes it as “the last release from any artist on the pioneering independent label” — though Discogs dates it 1982 and the label notionally continued through 1983. (source: tymondogg.bandcamp.com, normanrecords.com, Wikipedia/Tymon_Dogg)

Mouth (Y 20, Who’s Hot?, 1982)

Mouth debuted with a single on Revolver Records in 1981 before signing to Y. Their Y Records release featured “tribal drumming and improvised arrangements” blending post-punk with dub. Listed among O’Dell’s Bristol-scene management clients alongside Maximum Joy and Pigbag. (source: thevinylfactory.com, bristolarchiverecords.com)

Disconnection (2) (Y 32, Bali Ha’i, 1982)

A mutant disco cover of the Rodgers & Hammerstein standard from South Pacific. Recorded at Berry Street Studio, mastered at The Town House. Vocals by Angela Jaeger. Produced and arranged by Disconnection (2). The Vinyl Factory describes the track as a collaboration involving O’Dell, Simon Underwood, and “Alan Wilder (Depeche Mode)” — but the synthesizer player is credited as “Andy Wilder” in other sources (including a prior search result citing Discogs credits). This is unresolved: Alan Wilder joined Depeche Mode in 1982 and a side session for Y Records is not implausible, but the name discrepancy needs confirmation against the actual sleeve credits. (source: thevinylfactory.com) The single was reissued in 2016 by Optimo Music and has since become a collector’s item. (source: Optimo Bandcamp)

Open Questions

  • Rip Rig & Panic are not in the Y Records Discogs catalog; they released on Virgin. Their connection is biographical (shared personnel with Pop Group/Slits) rather than contractual.
  • Christopher Reeves / Peter Jennings (5 releases each, 1982) — unclear who these artists are; sparse Discogs entries and no web hits found.
  • Disconnection (2) synthesizer player: “Andy Wilder” (Discogs/search result) vs. “Alan Wilder (Depeche Mode)” (Vinyl Factory) — needs sleeve credit verification. See Disconnection (2) for full credits; Discogs has “Andy Wilder.”
  • Tymon Dogg “last release” claim: reissue copy calls Battle Of Wills (Y 29, 1982) the final Y Records release, but Discogs has Shriekback Jam Science (YLP 504) dated 1984. Possible explanation: Shriekback’s later releases were licensing/distribution arrangements rather than new Y Records signings.
  • O’Dell’s background pre-Pop Group (1978): Bristol Archive Records confirms he managed The Pop Group from 1978 and The Slits from 1979, and had prior career doing lighting for Curved Air and Deep Purple (c.1970) and tour managing Sensational Alex Harvey Band (mid-1970s) and The Stranglers (1977) — but these details came from a search result summary, not a directly fetched source.
