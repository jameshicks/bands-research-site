Direct Hit started as a retail record store in Dallas, Texas by Kelly (Keys) Handran. The store was located in the historic Exposition Park arts district.

Through the record store, an independent record label was established to publish local musicians. Most bands are now defunct, as is the Record Store and Label, but the history captured is a time capsule of the Era.

Overview

Direct Hit Records was founded in 1989 by Kelly “Keys” Handran as a retail record store in Dallas, Texas, operating out of the historic Exposition Park arts district. The store spawned an independent label to document local musicians, releasing punk, garage rock, and hardcore acts through the 1990s — its entire catalogue is concentrated in that decade. The label subsequently relocated from Dallas to Garland, Texas, and then to San Francisco, California before both the store and label ceased operations. Its small catalogue survives as a time capsule of the Dallas DIY underground of that era.

Notes (web)

Ownership

Search-result snippets from Dallas Observer articles (behind 403 paywall; snippets cited below) identify the label as co-owned by Sean Handran and his wife Kelly “Keys” Handran. The PAO Productions blog (2025) refers to “Kelly Keys (now Handran)”, indicating Kelly took the Handran surname on marriage; Sean appears to be her husband and co-operator. Discogs credits Kelly (Keys) Handran as founder.

Sources: Dallas Observer search-result snippets via Google; PAO Productions — Ghosts of DFW music history: Direct Hit Records (2025-04-13)

Dallas store closure (1995)

The Exposition Park store closed July 31, 1995. The PAO Productions blog (2025) identifies two contributing forces: (1) mainstream chain stores such as Blockbuster Music began stocking more independent releases, eroding the niche that indie shops depended on; (2) Kelly Handran’s personal exhaustion with the grind and the pressures of raising a new family. The label continued releasing records for several years after the store closed.

Source: PAO Productions — Ghosts of DFW music history: Direct Hit Records (2025-04-13)

Garland and San Francisco / Daly City relocations — reason not found

No source located states why the label moved from Dallas to Garland, or from Garland to San Francisco / Daly City.

What the sources do establish:

  • The grunnenrocks label directory (which appears to have had live contact-address data for the label) records a Garland, TX address and states: “The label used to be from Garland, TX, USA, they relocated in 1999 or 2000.” No reason given. Source: grunnenrocks.nl — Direct Hit (snippet; page accessed 2026-11-13).
  • The same grunnenrocks page gives the label’s final mailing address as 89A Bellevue Ave., Daly City, CA — Daly City is a city immediately south of San Francisco proper. The label closed after the move, with approximately 35 total releases by 2000. Source: grunnenrocks.nl — Direct Hit (snippet; page accessed 2026-11-13).
  • The PAO Productions blog (2025) explains the eventual label closure in personal terms (Kelly Handran’s fatigue, raising a family) but does not name the Garland or SF moves explicitly, and does not link either relocation to a specific act or scene shift.

The most likely explanation consistent with available facts: the moves followed Sean and/or Kelly Handran’s personal relocations for work or family reasons (the PAO blog notes family pressure as the ultimate closure factor), but no contemporary source confirms this.
