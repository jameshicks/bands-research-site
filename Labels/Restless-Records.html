Restless Records

US independent record label based in Los Angeles, California. Discogs label ID: 653. Parent label: Rykodisc (Discogs ID: 818). Label code: LC 01715.

Active from 1985/1986 through the early 2000s. Approximately 1,963 unique release masters in Discogs (across ~2,063 pressings/editions). Catalog numbers follow a 7 72xxx series (LPs/CDs) and 7 71xxx series (certain specialty releases); suffix -1 = vinyl, -2 = CD, -4 = cassette.

Founding and History

Restless was founded in early 1986 by Joe Regis and William Hein as a sublabel of Enigma Records (3), a California independent that distributed punk, metal, and alternative product. The label was conceived specifically for the alternative market, operating out of El Segundo, California (later moving to 1901 S. Bundy Drive, Los Angeles 90025).

Restless merged back into Enigma Records in 1991 as the parent company went through financial difficulties. In 1992, Restless re-emerged and acquired the rights to the catalog of Twin-Tone Records, the Minneapolis independent home to early The Replacements, Soul Asylum, The Jayhawks, and Ween. This gave Restless a substantial back-catalog alongside its active signings.

In 2001, Restless was purchased by Rykodisc as part of that label’s revitalization efforts following a short-lived Rykodisc/Palm Pictures merger. Restless continued to operate as a division of Rykodisc thereafter.

Notable Releases

The Flaming Lips — Hear It Is (1986)

Discogs ID: 724026. Cat no: 7 72173-2. The Flaming Lips’ debut album, recorded in Oklahoma City. Lineup: Wayne Coyne (guitar, vocals), Michael Ivins (bass, vocals), Richard English (drums, vocals). Produced by Randy Burns and the band. Engineered by Randy Burns. Photography by Michele Vlasimsky. The band’s raw early sound before later psychedelic expansions; released when the label was only months old.

The Dead Milkmen — Big Lizard In My Backyard (1985)

Discogs ID: 384714. Cat no: 72054-4. The Philadelphia punk band’s debut. Lineup: Rodney Anonymous (vocals), Joe Jack Talcum (guitar), Dave Blood (bass), Dean Clean (drums). Produced by the band and John Wicks. One of the label’s earliest releases, establishing its comedic-punk strand.

They Might Be Giants — They Might Be Giants (1987)

Discogs ID: 435516. Cat no: 7 72603-2. Self-titled debut by the Brooklyn duo John Flansburgh and John Linnell. Produced by Bill Krauss; executive-produced by Mark Bowyer and Matthew Hill. Engineered by Al Houghton, Alex Noyes, and Bill Krauss. Cover art by Rodney Alan Greenblat. The album that launched the band’s long relationship with the label.

Diamanda Galás — Saint Of The Pit (1986)

Discogs ID: 1855320. Cat no: 7 71418-1. Multi-octave soprano avant-garde performance: Galás performed all vocals, organ (Hammond), and synthesizer. Produced by Galás and Gareth Jones. Unusual presence on an otherwise punk-and-rock-dominated label; part of a series of Galás releases including You Must Be Certain Of The Devil (1988) and The Litanies Of Satan (1989).

Colin Newman — It Seems (1988)

Discogs ID: 506730. Cat no: 72260-2. The Wire frontman’s solo album recorded in Belgium with a jazz-inflected ensemble including Marc Hollander (clarinet), Robert Gotobed (drums), and Malka Spigel (vocals). Produced by Newman and Gilles Martin.

The Golden Palominos — This Is How It Feels (1993)

Discogs ID: 138977. Cat no: REST-73621 (approximate). The first of three Restless-era Golden Palominos albums. Produced by Anton Fier. Key personnel: Bill Laswell (bass), Bernie Worrell (Hammond organ), Bootsy Collins (guitar), Nicky Skopelitis (guitar), Amanda Kramer (keyboards), Lori Carson (voice/co-writer), Lydia Kavanagh (co-writer). Engineered by Oz Fritz and Bruce Calder. Mastered by Howie Weinberg.

The Golden Palominos — Pure (1994)

Discogs ID: 51611. Produced by Anton Fier. Returned the core Fier/Laswell/Carson axis with additional guitar from Knox Chandler and Nicky Skopelitis, keyboards by Amanda Kramer, and a second vocalist in Lydia Kavanagh. Engineers: Oz Fritz, Bruce Calder, Matt Stein, Rich Costey. Mastered by Howie Weinberg.

The Golden Palominos — Dead Inside (1996)

Discogs ID: 71984. The final Palominos studio album. Stripped-down compared to predecessors: bass by Bill Laswell (on selected tracks only), guitars by Knox Chandler, production/rhythms/concept by Anton Fier. Spoken word by Nicole Blackman. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Mixed and recorded by Dan Gellert.

Material — Memory Serves / One Down (1992)

Reissues of Bill Laswell’s avant-funk project Material on Restless. Cat nos 7 72653-2 and 7 72654-2. Also reissued Temporary Music (1979–1981).

Band Of Susans — Now (1992) / Veil (1993)

The Robert Poss-led guitar-orchestra’s US label home for the Restless era. Now (Discogs ID: 401441, cat: 7 72xxx) and Veil (Discogs ID: 621785) were both engineered by Bryce Goggin. The final studio LP Here Comes Success (1994) was on Blast First / World Service in Europe. The band’s two US studio LPs — Now and Veil — make up the core Restless output; the UK releases were on Blast First. For full detail see Band Of Susans.

Wipers — The Circle (1988)

Discogs ID: 1331702. The sixth Wipers studio album and their final record before the band’s 1989 breakup. Personnel: Greg Sage (guitar, vocals, harmonica, producer, recorded by), Brad Davidson (bass), Steve Plouf (drums). All songs written by Sage. Cover illustration by John Noyola; interior illustration by Thea D.; design by Rudy Tuesday. Followed by the 1990 best-of compilation (The Best of Wipers and Greg Sage, Discogs 1760086) and the solo Sacrifice (For Love) (1991, Discogs 769247), which were the last Restless-era Wipers/Sage releases.

Spain — The Blue Moods Of Spain (1995)

Discogs ID: 900091. Cat no: 7 72910-2. Josh Haden’s slowcore/dream-pop debut; produced by Josh Haden. One of Restless’s most critically-regarded signings of the mid-1990s; the band has continued releasing on the label as recently as 2025.

Relationship with Enigma Records (3) and Rykodisc

Restless was founded under Enigma Records (3) (Discogs label 3, distinct from other Enigma entities), the El Segundo-based independent that distributed wide-spectrum alternative and metal in the 1980s. Enigma operated labels for different market segments; Restless targeted the “alternative” strand. After Enigma’s bankruptcy/restructuring in 1991, Restless merged back into it before reconstituting independently in 1992.

The 1992 acquisition of Twin-Tone Records (Minneapolis) brought in a crucial back-catalog including The Replacements (early albums), Soul Asylum, Jayhawks, and Ween — giving Restless catalog depth alongside its active signings.

In 2001 Rykodisc (Cambridge, MA) acquired Restless as part of post-Palm-Pictures expansion. The Rykodisc label code LC 02619 can appear on some Restless pressings in place of Restless’s own LC 01715.

Catalog Number Scheme

The primary catalog pattern is 7 72xxx-[suffix]:

  • -1 = vinyl LP
  • -2 = CD
  • -3 = 12” single
  • -4 = cassette

A secondary series 7 71xxx was used for some specialty/art releases (notably several Diamanda Galás titles). Later catalog numbers use REST 73xxx (e.g., Little Box Of Horrors, 2006: REST 73800).

Earliest confirmed release in DB: Fates Warning Night On Bröcken (1984, cat 71103-1), suggesting the label had a brief pre-1986 predecessor or precursor imprint also logged under ID 653. Main active catalog begins 1985.
