Overview

Ziriguiboom (full name “Ziriguiboom Discos”) is a Belgian record label founded in 1999 as a Brazilian-music imprint of Crammed Discs, Brussels. It was launched collaboratively by Crammed’s founder Marc Hollander and Brazilian A&R producer Béco Dranoff, whose prior work included the Red Hot + Rio project (1996). The label’s stated mission was to bring original and underexposed aspects of Brazilian music to international audiences.

The label’s commercial peak was Bebel Gilberto’s Tanto Tempo (2000), one of the best-selling Brazilian crossover albums of the early 2000s and a touchstone of the lounge/bossa nova revival. Beyond Bebel Gilberto, Ziriguiboom built a coherent roster of Brazilian and Brazil-adjacent artists — Zuco 103, Suba, Celso Fonseca, Trio Mocotó, Bossacucanova, and Cibelle — along with a steady stream of Brazilian compilation series (The Now Sound Of Brazil, Samba Soul 70!, Bossa Cuca Nova). Discogs records 239 releases across all pressings; 47 unique master records as of the trawl.

The label’s production core was tight: Béco Dranoff and Marc Hollander shared A&R across virtually the entire catalog, while Suba (Mitar Subotić), Antoine Midani, and engineer/mixer Vincent De Bast handled most of the studio work. Vincent Kenis of Crammed Discs mastered the bulk of the catalog.

Key Remixers (Tanto Tempo Remixes, 2001)

The Tanto Tempo Remixes LP (ZIR10) brought in a roster of electronic producers: Kruder & Dorfmeister, Amon Tobin (co-produced original track, provided remix), Mario Caldato Jr. (original producer of one track), João Donato, Nina Miranda (of Smoke City), and Chris Franck.

Key Releases — Credits Detail

São Paulo Confessions — Suba (1999, ZIR 03)

Producer, performer, recorded by, mixed by: Suba (sole production credit). Mastered by Vincent Kenis. Written-by credits include Tom Jobim (track 6), Arnaldo Antunes (track 10), and Vinicius De Moraes (track 6). Cibelle co-wrote track 8 with Béco Dranoff. The album established the label’s template: Brazilian songcraft processed through electronic production.

Tanto Tempo — Bebel Gilberto (2000, cat# 657036 1026-2)

Primary producer: Suba. Co-producers: Béco Dranoff, Antoine Midani, Bebel Gilberto. Mixed by: Suba (principal), Antoine Midani, Vincent De Bast. Mix consultant: Vincent Kenis. Mastered by: Alan Ward. Recorded by: Antoine Midani (tracks 1, 3–11), Walter Costa (assistant). Design: Subtle Rukus (2). Photography: Dean Northcott. 30+ pressings; co-released with Six Degrees Records for the US market.

Tanto Tempo Remixes — Bebel Gilberto (2001, ZIR10)

Original tracks co-produced by Suba, Midani, Dranoff, and Bebel Gilberto. One track (track 1) originally produced by João Donato; tracks 3, 10, 13 by Chris Franck / Nina Miranda / Bebel; track 6 by Mario Caldato Jr.; track 12 originally by Amon Tobin. Edited and mastered by Vincent Kenis. Remixers include Kruder & Dorfmeister, Amon Tobin, Arling & Cameron.

Cibelle — Cibelle (2003, ZIR15)

Produced by Apollo 9 (tracks 1, 2, 4–11); Cibelle co-producer. Post-production and additional mixing: Marc Hollander and Vincent Kenis. Mastered by Alan Ward and Vincent Kenis. A&R: Dranoff and Hollander.

Bebel Gilberto — Bebel Gilberto (2004, ZIR20)

Self-titled LP. Primary producer: Marius de Vries. 17 pressings worldwide.

Momento — Bebel Gilberto (2007, Zir 30)

Executive producers: Béco Dranoff and Tommy Manzi. A&R: Dranoff and Hollander. Mastered by Mark Wilder (ID 161744). Art direction and photography: Philippe Kliot. Bebel Gilberto credited for additional illustrations and layout.

Connections

  • Crammed Discs — parent label; founded and owned by Marc Hollander; shared address and personnel
  • Marc Hollander — A&R on virtually the entire Ziriguiboom catalog; co-founded the imprint with Béco Dranoff
  • Béco Dranoff — A&R director and executive producer; brought Red Hot + Rio experience and Brazilian music contacts; central figure 1998–2007
  • Bebel Gilberto — flagship artist; Tanto Tempo (2000), Bebel Gilberto (2004), Momento (2007)
  • Suba — key producer; sole producer of São Paulo Confessions (1999) and main producer of Tanto Tempo (2000); died November 1999 in a São Paulo studio fire
  • Antoine Midani — co-producer and engineer across the Tanto Tempo sessions and subsequent Bebel releases
  • Vincent Kenis — Crammed Discs resident engineer; mastered the bulk of the Ziriguiboom catalog
  • Amon Tobin — co-produced “Samba Da Benção” on Tanto Tempo; remixed for Tanto Tempo Remixes
  • Thievery Corporation — produced “Lonely” on Tanto Tempo
  • Six Degrees Records — US co-release partner for Tanto Tempo and several Zuco 103 releases
  • Zuco 103 — second-most-present roster artist (49 releases); Dutch group with Brazilian vocalist Lilian Vieira
  • Cibelle — third-album act; debut produced by Apollo 9 in 2003; later moved to other labels
