From Discogs

After the collapse of Fresh Records in 1982, the band UK Decay formed a new label, “UK Decay Records”, that went on to release all of the band’s back catalogue, including the album “For Madmen Only” (DK LP 1) as well as the live album “A Night For Celebration”(DK LP 2). Several singles were also re-released, including “For My Country / Unwind”, “Unexpected Guest / Dresden”, “Sexual / Twist In The Tail”.

UK Decay Records were a partnership between the band itself and Southern Studios, today known as SRD. The label is rumoured to see again the light of day, with the re-issue of UK Decay’s back catologue and probably new products.

Overview

UK Decay Records was established in 1982 when the band UK Decay took direct control of their catalogue following the collapse of Fresh Records. Run as a partnership between the band and Southern Studios — the facility later known as SRD — the label served as the home for the full UK Decay back catalogue, anchored by the studio album For Madmen Only (DK LP 1), the live album A Night for Celebration (DK LP 2), and several reissued singles spanning post-punk and early goth material. The catalogue divides into two distinct periods: four releases concentrated in the 1980s and four more in the 2010s, the latter era most likely representing the reissue programme the label had long been rumoured to undertake.

Notes (web)

Fresh Records collapse and the founding of UK Decay Records

Fresh Records background. Fresh Records was founded by Alex Howe, who had been running a Wretched Records stall in London’s Soho Market since 1978. Alan Hauser joined in 1979, bringing his Parole Records roster (which included Family Fodder, Cuddly Toys, The Wall, and Bernie Torme) to establish Fresh Records as a label. UK Decay signed to Fresh after Alex Howe offered to license their first two independent singles; their first official Fresh release was “For My Country” (September 1980). Wikipedia: Fresh Records (UK) (as of 2026-11-26); Kill Your Pet Puppy

Why Fresh Records folded. The Wikipedia article on Fresh Records (UK) states only that the label “ran out of cash and went bankrupt in 1982.” No source found gives any more specific financial detail — no named creditor dispute, distribution failure, or management breakdown is documented in publicly available sources checked (Wikipedia, Jungle Records Wikipedia, official UK Decay site, Kill Your Pet Puppy). The collapse came shortly after the release of UK Decay’s debut album For Madmen Only (December 1981). Wikipedia: Fresh Records (UK) (as of 2026-11-26)

What happened to Fresh’s staff and catalogue. Staff from Fresh Records formed Jungle Records in 1982 — founded by Alan Hauser, Graham Combi, and other former Fresh employees. Jungle bought the rights to Fresh’s recordings. UK Decay were described as “caught up in the ensuing management buy-out by what would become Jungle Records,” implying Jungle’s acquisition covered the UK Decay masters and the band had to actively negotiate out of that situation. Wikipedia: Fresh Records (UK) (as of 2026-11-26); Wikipedia: UK Decay (as of 2026-11-26); UK Decay official history

How UK Decay responded. With the help of John Loder and Southern Studios, the band bought back the rights to their back catalogue and established UK Decay Records rather than signing to another label. The stated motivation across all sources is catalogue control — no source suggests they were rejected by other labels or that any specific alternative label was considered and declined. The official band history and Wikipedia are consistent: the collapse created a window to self-own their recordings, and John Loder/Southern Studios provided the infrastructure to make that viable.
