Grönland Records

Discogs Label ID: 19672
Label Code: LC 1387 / LC 01387
Parent company: Grönland Deutschland GmbH (Discogs ID: 4155085)
Sub-label: Holunder Records (Discogs ID: 215136)
Website: groenland.com
Also appears as: Grönland, Groenland Records

Independent British-German record label founded in London in 1999 by German actor and singer Herbert Grönemeyer. The company relocated to Berlin in 2009 and has been based there since, operating from Rheinsberger Str. 48, 10435 Berlin. The name “Grönland” (German for Greenland) refers simultaneously to the country, to Grönemeyer himself, and to a track on his 1993 album Chaos (Wikipedia). The label was founded in connection with an eight-CD box set called Pop 2000, a project designed to document 20th-century German music culture, featuring artists such as Neu!, Faust, Kraftwerk, The Notwist, DAF, and Mouse on Mars.

Grönland signs a maximum of six to eight bands and projects per year, giving it a deliberately narrow and curated roster. The label functions as Grönemeyer’s primary vehicle for his own recordings but has consistently championed Krautrock/kosmische reissues and archival projects alongside contemporary artists. Its most significant archival work includes the first official CD and vinyl reissues of Neu!‘s catalog (2001 onward), posthumous Klaus Dinger material through the Japandorf project, extensive Harmonia and Harmonia 76 reissues, the complete Holger Czukay posthumous catalog, a Who’s That Man tribute compilation to Conny Plank (2013), and the ongoing Michael Rother solo catalog. The Electri_city (2014) and Electri_city 2 (2016) compilation series gathered the Düsseldorf electronic lineage (Neu!, DAF, Harmonia, La Düsseldorf, etc.) in one place.

Notable Artists / Roster

Cornerstone — Herbert Grönemeyer

Herbert Grönemeyer (Discogs ID: 163850) is the label’s founder and dominant release count (158 releases, 1998–2025). His catalog forms the commercial backbone of the label. Key albums: Mensch (2002), 12 (2007), Schiffsverkehr (2011), Dauernd Jetzt (2014), Alles (2016), Tumult (2018).

Krautrock / Kosmische Archival Catalog

The label’s most historically significant work is its stewardship of core Düsseldorf- and Kosmische-era catalogs:

  • Neu! (Discogs ID: 12636) — 71 releases. First official CD and LP reissues of the Neu! catalog (2001), Neu! ‘86 (2010), box sets, 50! (2022 anniversary set). The Neu! reissue program was a founding purpose of the label.
  • Holger Czukay (Discogs ID: 3480) — 58 releases. Extensive posthumous catalog stewardship, including Cinema (2018), reissues of Canaxis 5 (Technical Space Composer’s Crew), Der Osten Ist Rot, Full Circle (with Jah Wobble & Jaki Liebezeit), Plight & Premonition / Flux & Mutability (with David Sylvian), and collaborative archival releases.
  • Harmonia (Discogs ID: 190641) — 25 releases. Live 1974 (LPGRON78), Musik Von Harmonia, Deluxe, Documents 1975, Complete Works (2015).
  • Harmonia 76 (Discogs ID: 48044) — Tracks and Traces (CDGRON102, 2009) — the Harmonia/Brian Eno sessions reissue.
  • Klaus Dinger (Discogs ID: 388633) — 5 releases. Posthumous Japandorf material: Klaus Dinger + Japandorf / Japandorf (CDGRON124, 2013, credited to La Düsseldorf on that pressing) and 2000! (LPGRON170, 2017, credited Klaus Dinger + Japandorf / Prejapandorf). Co-producer on 2000!: Miki Yui and Kazuyuki Onouchi.
  • La Düsseldorf (Discogs ID: 152540) — appears on the Japandorf release.
  • Michael Rother (Discogs ID: 37955) — 18 releases on Grönland, 2019–2022. Complete solo catalog reissues: Flammende Herzen, Sterntaler, Fernwärme, Katzenmusik (all 2019), Dreaming (2020), plus collaborative As Long As The Light with Vittoria Maccabruni (2022).
  • Hans-Joachim Roedelius (Discogs ID: 105272) — 14 releases, 2006–2025. Works (1968–2005) (CDGRON039, 2006), Imagori and Imagori II (with Christoph H. Müller), 90 (LPGRON299, 2024).

German Pop / Singer-Songwriter

  • Philipp Poisel (Discogs ID: 1172875) — 50 releases, 2008–2022. One of Grönland’s most-released contemporary artists. Sub-label: Holunder Records (Discogs ID: 215136) was created specifically for him.
  • Philipp Dittberner (Discogs ID: 4252420) — 28 releases, 2015–2024.
  • Niels Frevert (Discogs ID: 348961) — 13 releases, 2014–2023.

Electronic / Post-Punk

  • Deutsch Amerikanische Freundschaft (Discogs ID: 12830) — 28 releases, 2017–2021. Major retrospective compilations (Das Ist DAF, Alles Ist Gut, Die Kleinen Und Die Bösen, Gold Und Liebe), remix album Reworx, and Robert Görl’s final DAF album Nur Noch Einer (2021).
  • Robert Görl (Discogs ID: 15130) — 8 releases, including solo The Paris Tapes (2018) and the Vince Clarke remix single.
  • Gang of Four (Discogs ID: 33929) — Content (CDGRON114, 2010), Who Am I? single (2011).
  • Nina Hagen (Discogs ID: 53800) — 24 releases, 2020–2026.

Contemporary Indie / Pop

  • BOY (Discogs ID: 2462608) — 29 releases, 2011–2021. Swiss indie duo (Valeska Steiner + Sonja Glass). Mutual Friends (LPGRON118, 2011), We Were Here (LPGRON148, 2015). (Wikipedia: “Boy”)
  • William Fitzsimmons (Discogs ID: 575953) — 37 releases, 2009–2025. Long-running relationship: The Sparrow And The Crow (2009), Gold In The Shadow (2011), Pittsburgh (2015), Mission Bell (2018), Ready The Astronaut (2021), Covers, Vol. 1 (2022).
  • Susanne Sundfør (Discogs ID: 747088) — 9 releases, 2010–2012. The Brothel (LPGRON117, 2010), The Silicone Veil (CDGRON120, 2012).
  • Gang of Four — see above.
  • Metric (Discogs ID: 251798) — Grow Up And Blow Away (CDGRON71, 2007).
  • Emily Haines & The Soft Skeleton (Discogs ID: 624547) — Knives Don’t Have Your Back (CDGRON65, 2007).
  • Sondre Lerche (Discogs ID: 51136) — Phantom Punch (CDGRON63, 2007).
  • Fujiya & Miyagi (Discogs ID: 50661) — 9 releases, 2007–2008.
  • David Sylvian (Discogs ID: 10069) — 13 releases, 2018–2025. Reissues: Plight & Premonition / Flux & Mutability (with Czukay), Sleepwalkers (LPGRON256, 2022), Wandermüde (with Stephan Mathieu, 2023).
  • Kinderzimmer Productions (Discogs ID: 52707) — 8 releases, 2020–2022. German hip-hop group; back-catalog reissues (LPGRON219–222).
  • We Are Scientists (Discogs ID: 360656) — 12 releases, 2018–2025.
  • Kat Frankie (Discogs ID: 1003031) — 16 releases, 2017–2022.
  • Emiliana Torrini (Discogs ID: 23910) — Miss Flower (LPGRON291, 2024).
  • Oehl (Discogs ID: 7619573) — 11 releases, 2018–2025.

Notable Compilations / Archival Projects

  • Pop 2000 - 50 Jahre Popmusik Und Jugendkultur In Deutschland (1999, Various) — the founding project.
  • Who’s That Man - A Tribute To Conny Plank (CDGRON123, 2013) — tracks by Brian Eno/Roedelius/Moebius, Phew, Eurythmics, Michael Rother, DAF, NEU!, and others.
  • Electri_city (LPGRON141, 2014) and Electri_city 2 (LPGRON161, 2016) — Düsseldorf electronic music compilations.
  • The Conny Plank Session (CDGRON140, 2015) — Duke Ellington And His Orchestra session produced by Plank.
  • Les Vampyrettes (LPGRON133, 2013) — archival Czukay/Conny Plank project.

Conny Plank credits on Grönland are almost entirely archival — his production and engineering work appears across the Harmonia, Neu!, DAF, Czukay, and La Düsseldorf reissues; the Who’s That Man tribute and The Conny Plank Session were dedicated to his legacy.

Alex Silva (Discogs ID: 212651) is the primary contemporary producer for Grönemeyer’s own albums.

Walter Schönauer (Discogs ID: 1850980) is the house designer, with 91 releases credited for art direction and design — the visual identity of the Grönland catalog.

Kai Blankenberg (Discogs ID: 279938) at Skyline Tonfabrik is the primary mastering engineer for contemporary releases.

Research Queue

  • What was Grönland’s distribution arrangement with EMI / Virgin for the early Neu! reissues (2001) and Grönemeyer’s own albums — were those co-releases or licensed distribution deals? A contemporary press release or label copy on the 2001 Neu! CDs would resolve this.
  • The Who’s That Man tribute (CDGRON123, 2013) is credited on Discogs as “Conny Plank / Various” with no additional credits on the release level — who compiled and produced the compilation? A liner-note scan or press biography would confirm the curator.
  • Confirm whether the Japandorf release (CDGRON124, 2013) was credited to La Düsseldorf on the physical sleeve or whether that is a Discogs cataloging choice — the primary credit on Discogs reads “La Düsseldorf” while the brief and subsequent 2017 release credit “Klaus Dinger + Japandorf.”
  • The Conny Plank Session (CDGRON140, 2015) credits Duke Ellington And His Orchestra — what is the provenance of this session? When was it recorded, and how did Grönland acquire the rights? A single contemporaneous press piece would answer this.
