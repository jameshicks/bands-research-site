Stealworks

Graphic design studio and imprint run by John Yates, based in San Francisco Bay Area. Discogs label ID: 284562. Web: stealworks.com / designedbyyates.com.

Stealworks functions as both the operating name for Yates’s freelance design practice and as a credit label on Discogs — releases carrying the “Stealworks” imprint are not commercially released records but rather Yates’s design studio output credited as a label entity. The name appears explicitly on Discogs release credits from roughly the 2000s onward (e.g. “Built At Stealworks,” “Yates At Stealworks”), though the studio itself was active earlier.

Overview

Stealworks is the personal design studio of John Yates (Discogs artist ID: 293237), a San Francisco Bay Area graphic designer originally from Leeds, England. The studio name consolidates design credit for Yates’s work across hundreds of punk, hardcore, and political-music releases spanning 1987 to the present.

Prior to operating under the Stealworks name, Yates worked for roughly ten years as an in-house designer at Alternative Tentacles and ran Allied Recordings (3) (Discogs label ID: 44003) from 1990 to 1999. Stealworks emerged as his primary freelance credit once the Allied chapter closed, and has been his working studio name ever since.

The studio is associated with a confrontational, collage-based aesthetic rooted in political graphic art and DIY punk — cut-and-paste construction, screen-print sensibility, dense typography, and hand-assembled packaging. Yates’s credit lines often reflect this ethos literally: “built by,” “designed and constructed,” “sleeve built by,” “assembled and constructed,” “art mechanics.”

The Virus 100 Compilation (1992)

The most significant release where Yates stepped beyond design into production: Virus 100 (Alternative Tentacles, 1992) is a Dead Kennedys tribute compilation celebrating AT’s 100th release. Yates is credited as Executive-Producer, Liner Notes author, and Sleeve / Illustration artist — making it a rare case where his role exceeded pure design. Co-executive-produced with Greg Werckman and Jason Traeger. The release features contributions from dozens of artists covering Dead Kennedys songs.

Stealworks as a Credit Imprint

On Discogs, Stealworks (label ID: 284562) appears as a design company credit rather than a releasing imprint — the label entity has no releases in the standard sense. The explicit “Stealworks” name appears in credit strings such as:

  • “Built At Stealworks” (The Sheryl Cro(w) Mags #1 Hit, 2000)
  • “Yates At Stealworks” (various credits)
  • “Designed and Built By — Stealworks” (various)

Earlier credits (pre-2000) typically used Yates’s name alone or the “Popular Mechanics” alias. The shift to foregrounding the studio name as a distinct brand is most visible from the late 1990s–2000s onward.

Aesthetic and Working Method

Yates’s design language is rooted in pre-digital cut-and-paste production — scissors, Xerox, typesetting, paste-up — and the political graphic tradition of punk and anarchist publishing. This is reflected in the credit language itself: he rarely says “Design” alone; instead, the credits name the physical act (“built,” “constructed,” “assembled,” “mechanics”). Key characteristics:

  • Dense collage imagery, often appropriating political/media images
  • Hand-lettering alongside typeset text
  • Confrontational covers suited to anarcho-punk and political hardcore
  • Willingness to use the full album package (insert booklets, inner sleeves, disc labels) as design real estate
  • Economical black-and-white or limited-color production suited to small-run pressing budgets

Open Questions

  • Discogs lists no releases directly on the Stealworks label — the entity exists only as a credit name. Stealworks.com / designedbyyates.com would carry the full client portfolio, but web research has not been done.
  • The “Popular Mechanics” alias is used on some Shredder Records credits — extent of use on other labels is not fully mapped.
  • The two earliest John Yates Discogs credits (1969 Australian Hair cast recording; 1974 UK Just Us) are likely misattributions to a different person; neither connects to the SF punk scene or Bay Area context.
  • Yates’s membership in Ella Guru is noted on Discogs but the nature of his involvement is unclear — his Discogs output is entirely design, not performance.
  • The full Punchline zine run (published by Allied Recordings) is not documented in the vault; web research could fill this gap.
