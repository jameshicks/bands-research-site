Prestige Records

American jazz record label. Founded 1949 by Bob Weinstock in New York. One of the central institutions of hard bop and post-bop in the 1950s–60s. Acquired by Fantasy Records in June 1971; the Fantasy group merged with Concord Music Group in 2004. Discogs label ID: 19591. Label code: LC 0313.

Originally founded as New Jazz Record Co. (Discogs ID: 319843), then renamed Prestige. The imprint was home to more than 17,000 release entries in the Discogs database and 37 sub-labels.

History

Founding and the Blowing Session Era (1949–1957)

Bob Weinstock launched the label in 1949 out of New York City (446 W. 50th St.). He began as a traditional jazz fan but immediately moved toward modern jazz, signing Sonny Stitt, Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, Stan Getz, Wardell Gray, James Moody, and the Modern Jazz Quartet in the early years. Weinstock’s production approach was informal — “blowing sessions” with loose structures, little rehearsal, minimal overdubbing — recorded quickly and cheaply, mostly by Rudy Van Gelder.

Van Gelder began recording for Prestige at his Hackensack, NJ home studio in the early 1950s and later moved to his Englewood Cliffs, NJ studio (Van Gelder Studio). His engineering credit appears on approximately 3,400 Prestige releases in the Discogs database — making him by far the most important technical figure in the label’s sound. The “RVG” runout etch became a mark of authenticity on first pressings.

Key figures from this early period:

  • Miles Davis — recorded prolifically for Prestige 1951–57 before moving to Columbia; classic sessions include the famous Cookin’, Relaxin’, Workin’, and Steamin’ albums recorded in two marathon Van Gelder sessions in 1956
  • Thelonious Monk — Prestige period 1952–54, before moving to Riverside
  • Sonny Rollins — extensive early catalog on Prestige
  • John Coltrane — recorded for Prestige 1956–58 concurrently with his Blue Note and Atlantic sessions; debut as a leader on Prestige in 1957

Liner notes across this era were frequently written by jazz critic Ira Gitler, who became the label’s dominant prose voice (credited on over 3,100 releases in the catalog).

The Hard Bop and Producer Era (1958–1965)

Weinstock began bringing in outside producers from 1958. Key producers of the period:

  • Ozzie Cadena — produced approximately 259 releases; central to the hard bop output of the late 1950s–early 1960s; produced Ted Curson’s Plays Fire Down Below (1963)
  • Cal Lampley — produced approximately 348 releases
  • Esmond Edwards — produced approximately 209 releases; also handled cover design and photography
  • Ira Gitler — took on some production alongside his liner-note work
  • Bob Weinstock himself — credited as producer or supervision on approximately 605 releases

Artists active in this period: Eric Dolphy (1961–64), Coleman Hawkins, Yusef Lateef, Eddie “Lockjaw” Davis, Jackie McLean, Gene Ammons, Donald Byrd, Kenny Burrell, Hank Mobley, Mal Waldron, Red Garland, Art Taylor, Paul Chambers (3), Dexter Gordon (joined 1964).

Organ Jazz and the Acid Jazz Years (1960s)

As the 1960s progressed Prestige became a leading label for Hammond B-3 organ jazz — what is now often called acid jazz or soul jazz:

  • Brother Jack McDuff — 323 releases in the Discogs catalog
  • Shirley Scott — 249 releases
  • Johnny Hammond — 116 releases
  • Richard “Groove” Holmes — 90 releases
  • Charles Earland — 129 releases (later, starting 1969)
  • Willis Jackson — 164 releases

Post-Acquisition and Fantasy Years (1971 onward)

Prestige, its sub-labels, and its publishing company Prestige Music, Inc. became part of the Fantasy group in June 1971. Bob Weinstock remained as President. After the acquisition the label shifted to a Berkeley, California address (“Prestige Records, Berkeley, California” then “Distributed By Fantasy Records, Berkeley, California”).

Key producers in the Fantasy era:

  • Don Schlitten — the most prolific overall producer in the label’s history (approximately 932 production credits); also handled cover design and photography extensively
  • Bob Porter — approximately 435 production credits; major reissue and compilation work
  • Nick Phillips (2) — reissue production

The label continued releasing new material through the 1970s (Funk Inc., Gary Bartz NTU Troop, Patrice Rushen from 1974) while simultaneously reissuing the 1950s–60s catalog in enormous volume.

Van Gelder’s Role

Rudy Van Gelder is the dominant technical presence in the Prestige catalog. His Discogs credits span:

  • ~3,399 as engineer (original recording sessions)
  • ~5,026 in broader roles including “Recorded By,” “Mastered By,” and “Remastered By” credits on reissues

The combination means Van Gelder appears in some capacity on more Prestige releases than any other individual, including the label’s owner. His studio — first Hackensack, then Englewood Cliffs — was effectively the house studio for Prestige from the early 1950s through the mid-1960s.

Liner Notes

Ira Gitler wrote liner notes on approximately 3,111 Prestige releases — the definitive prose voice of the catalog. Bob Weinstock and Rudy Van Gelder both contributed liner notes on selected releases.

Label Variations (7000 Series)

The 7000 catalog number series went through several label designs (all USA first pressings):

  • Up to 7141 — “Fireworks” label, yellow/black; address: 446 W. 50th St., NYC. Mono only.
  • 7142–c.7325 — Fireworks label (yellow/black mono, black/silver stereo); address: 203 South Washington Ave., Bergenfield, NJ.
  • c.1964 transitional — Gold/black “trident” label (12 o’clock position); no address.
  • c.1964–1968 — Blue label, trident at 3 o’clock; Bergenfield NJ address.
  • 1968–1970 — Blue label, circled trident at 12 o’clock; Bergenfield NJ.
  • 1970–1971 — Purple label, circled trident; Bergenfield NJ.
  • 1971 — Green label; Berkeley, California address.
  • 1971 onward — Green label; “Distributed By Fantasy Records, Berkeley, California.”
