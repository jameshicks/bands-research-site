From Discogs

The Business is an independent record store, music distribution company and concert venue located on Commercial Avenue in Anacortes, Washington, United States. From 1978 to 2008, The Business was located at 1717 Commercial Avenue; then from 2008 to 2016 at 402 Commercial Avenue; then from 2016 to 2024 at 216 Commercial Avenue; presently at 810 Commercial Avenue.

The store currently stocks new and used music releases on vinyl records, compact discs and audio cassettes, as well as music-oriented posters and original artworks. It also sells The Criterion Collection releases. The Business frequently holds free, all-ages shows during store hours with locally and nationally known artists from a wide variety of genres. The Business’ distribution division (sometimes referred to as The Business Distribution or The Business Distro-) handles a vast roster of independent record labels’ global direct retail and wholesale merchandise.

The Business was founded in 1978 by Glen Des Jardins. From 1990 to 2005, Bret Lunsford owned it and had a recording studio of the same name in the back room. Lunsford also started the record label Knw-Yr-Own there. From 2005 to 2010 it was owned by Liz Lovelett and Jensen Lovelett. Since 2010 it has been owned by Nick Rennis; his wife Evie Opp came in a year later. Rennis and Opp started a distribution arm in November 2011

Overview

The Business is an independent record store, music distributor, and all-ages concert venue that has operated in Anacortes, Washington since 1978. Founded by Glen Des Jardins, it changed hands several times before Bret Lunsford ran it from 1990 to 2005 — during which he established the Knw-Yr-Own label out of a back-room recording studio on the premises. Current owner Nick Rennis has held the store since 2010 and launched its distribution arm in November 2011 alongside Evie Opp, extending reach for independent labels across indie rock and experimental music. Its Discogs catalogue is modest — two releases spread across the 2010s and 2020s — but The Business Select operates as a sublabel under its umbrella.

Lunsford tenure: curatorial identity (1990–2005)

The most direct account comes from a Beat Happening feature in Exclaim! in which Lunsford articulated his philosophy explicitly: “In Olympia, I got tainted by this vision that people could make their own cultural products, and that some people might be interested in consuming these things. I just sort of brought that gospel to Anacortes.” (exclaim.ca) This frames the entire Lunsford era at The Business as a deliberate transplantation of the K Records DIY ethos — the idea that a small city could sustain its own ecosystem of self-made music and culture — from Olympia to Anacortes.

Key findings from web sources:

  • Origin of ownership. Lunsford returned to Anacortes after Beat Happening wound down following You Turn Me On (1992). He had been working informally at The Business, a used-book/photography/miscellany store run by Glen Des Jardins, since around 1988; he took formal ownership by around 1990–1995 (sources vary on the exact year of the ownership transfer, with Discogs saying 1990 and Exclaim! implying closer to 1995 for the full partnership). (exclaim.ca) — see Conflict note below.
  • Stock as curation. Lunsford stocked K Records releases and cassettes alongside his own Knw-Yr-Own output, turning the retail floor into a physical extension of the Pacific Northwest underground. The store became what Nate Ashley described as “the real musical hub” of Anacortes, above other venues of the period. (anacortesmusic.substack.com)
  • Back-room studio. Lunsford built a recording studio in the back room of The Business and ran it concurrently with the store. This was where he incubated Knw-Yr-Own as a working label. The studio became a gathering point: Phil Elverum (later Mount Eerie) worked at the store as a teenager and used the back room to make his first recordings after hours, resulting in two cassettes — Microphone and Wires and Cords — which Lunsford released on Knw-Yr-Own. Elverum also played drums in D+ before relocating to Olympia in 1997. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Microphones, as of 2026-10-03; olympiamusichistory.org)
  • Cross-pollination conduit. Lunsford described The Business as continuing “that cross pollination between Anacortes and Olympia,” extending infrastructure he had earlier built at GESCCO (a short-lived music/art venue he co-ran with Denise Crowe in downtown Olympia in the 1980s). (olympiamusichistory.org)
  • All-ages programming. Lunsford introduced live shows, coffee-shop programming, and art exhibitions to the space — an intergenerational model that mirrored K Records’ commitment to all-ages access. Subsequent owners preserved this approach; the current owners hold free, all-ages shows monthly, and the Anacortes Music Project (AMP), founded after The Business changed hands, was described as consciously replicating its spirit of “focus on the all-ages scene and creating a place for younger musicians to explore and grow.” (anacortesmusic.substack.com)
  • Knw-Yr-Own co-productions with K Records. D+‘s self-titled 1997 debut was a joint release between Knw-Yr-Own and K Records — a formal institutional link between The Business’s in-house label and Calvin Johnson’s label, rooted directly in Lunsford’s Beat Happening membership. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%2B_(band))
  • Artist network formed at The Business. Karl Blau, Phil Elverum, and Dave Matthies all resided in Anacortes simultaneously and rehearsed at The Business ca. 1999–2000, using it as a base for multi-artist touring. This network of artists — the core of the Anacortes underground — crystallised around the store during Lunsford’s tenure. (anacortesmusic.substack.com)

Conflict note

Discogs and Exclaim! differ on when Lunsford took formal ownership. Discogs states 1990; Exclaim! implies the transition to a full partnership with Des Jardins happened closer to 1995, with Lunsford working informally at the store from 1988. The Olympia Music History biography says he “owned The Business record store in Anacortes for nine years,” which is consistent with a ca. 1996–2005 window — not the 1990–2005 Discogs gives. The question of the exact start date is unresolved by current sources. (discogs.com label profile ID 1462654; exclaim.ca; olympiamusichistory.org)
