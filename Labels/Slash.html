Slash

Discogs ID: 15584 Parent Label: Slash Records (Discogs ID: 2831) Founded: 1978, Los Angeles, California Founder: Bob Biggs (Discogs artist ID: 2235159) Active: 1978–2000 (first run); relaunched 2003 Genre Focus: Punk, cowpunk, American roots, alternative rock, hardcore

History

Slash was founded in 1978 in Los Angeles by Bob Biggs. The label grew out of the LA punk scene and initially documented it, releasing records by The Plugz, the Germs, and X before the scene had wide national attention. The companion magazine Slash (also tied to Biggs) was part of the same LA punk ecosystem.

The label’s early catalog (SR-xxx series) established it as the definitive document of late-1970s LA punk. Distribution through JEM Records (an independent distributor) gave the early releases national reach in the US. By 1981 the label had grown enough to sign a distribution deal with Warner Bros. Records, which brought a much wider reach and allowed the signing of larger acts.

Through the early-to-mid 1980s, Slash became central to what critics called “cowpunk” — a blending of punk energy with American country, rockabilly, and roots music. X, produced by ex-Doors keyboardist Ray Manzarek, were the flagship act. The Blasters (blues-informed rockabilly), Los Lobos (East LA Chicano roots music), and Rank & File (country-punk) gave the label a distinctly American identity at a time when many independents were dominated by British post-punk influence.

The label also released the soundtrack to the 1980 documentary The Decline of Western Civilization (Penelope Spheeris), an essential document of the LA hardcore scene.

In the mid-1980s the roster broadened: Violent Femmes (Wisconsin folk-punk), Fear (LA hardcore), The Dream Syndicate (Paisley Underground), The Gun Club, the Knitters, and Green On Red. Faith No More arrived in 1987 with Introduce Yourself, and went on to become the label’s biggest commercial act globally through the early 1990s. L7 and Mr. Bungle (both connected to the Faith No More circle through Mike Patton) were also added.

Slash was also briefly home to UK acts distributed through London Records in the late 1980s and early 1990s: the Chills (New Zealand), Violent Femmes, Grant Lee Buffalo, BoDeans, and Soul Coughing round out a notably eclectic late-period catalog.

In 1996 the label was acquired by London Records. Warner retained most of the artist roster and catalog, and the office relocated to New York. When Universal bought PolyGram (London’s owner) in 1998, the eventual result was Slash being closed entirely in 2000. London president Roger Ames moved to Warner and retained rights to both the London and Slash names. In 2003, Ames re-licensed the Slash name back to Biggs, who relaunched the label with Will Fulton.

The parent entity “Slash Records” (ID: 2831) was used primarily for copyright credits; the operational label for releases was “Slash” (ID: 15584). Both share the same profile and history. The address on file for “Slash Records” was P.O. Box 48888, Los Angeles, CA 90048.

Catalog Number Series

Slash used several distinct catalog series reflecting different eras and territories:

SeriesTerritory/EraExample
SCAM xxxUS, 1978 (very early, possibly pre-SR numbering)SCAM 102
SR-xxxUS, 1979–1982 (core early LP sequence)SR-103 through SR-114
SRS-xxxUS, 1981–1982 (singles variant)SRS-110, SRS-113
SREP-xxxUS, 1981 (extended play variant)SREP-108
SLAP xxxUK, 1983–1985 (LP/12” prefix after Warner deal)SLAP 1, SLAP 21
LASH xxxUK, 1983 onwards (singles)LASH 1, LASH 7, LASH 21, LASH 34
LASHX xxxUK, 12” singles variantLASHX 1, LASHX 25, LASHX 37
Warner Bros. 9 2xxxx-x / 1-2xxxxUS, 1983+ (after WB distribution deal)9 25177-1, 9 25605-1
828 xxx-xEurope (London/PolyGram era)828 321-2, 828 555-2
LASCD xx / LASCDJ xxUK, 1990s (CD singles)LASCD 46, LASCDJ 45

The SR-xxx sequence is the most historically significant, numbering the early punk/cowpunk catalog from 103 (the Germs’ (GI)) through 114 (Rank & File’s Sundown). The label’s earliest single release appears to be SCAM 102 (The Plugz, 1978). SR-108 was assigned to the Germs What We Do Is Secret EP (SREP-108). Note the catalog skips from 114 to 208+ once Warner Bros. distribution began; from 1983 the US pressings used WB numbering directly.

Connections

  • Slash Records — parent label entity (Discogs ID: 2831); used for copyright credits on the same releases
  • Ruby Records — the other sublabel under Slash Records
  • Warner Bros. Records — US distribution partner from 1981 onwards
  • London Records — acquired Slash in 1996; parent under PolyGram
  • JEM Records — early US distributor (pre-1981)
  • Bob Biggs — founder and owner
  • Matt Wallace — house producer/engineer; produced Faith No More extensively
  • T-Bone Burnett — producer for multiple Slash artists (Los Lobos, etc.)
  • Mitchell Froom — keyboard player and producer (Los Lobos, several others); released his own solo LP on label (1984)
  • Steve Berlin — Los Lobos saxophonist; also acted as producer across the label’s catalog
  • Dave Alvin — The Blasters songwriter; primary writer credit across Blasters releases
  • Geza X — produced the Germs’ (GI); also associated with early Slash punk releases
  • Ray Manzarek — produced X’s early LPs (not on Discogs Slash credits, but central to X’s sound)
  • Jeff Price (2) — art direction/design across label (118 releases)
  • Louie Perez — Los Lobos drummer; art direction on many Los Lobos releases
  • The Knitters — supergroup formed from X and Blasters members; released Poor Little Critter on the Road (1985)

Open Questions

  • The exact founding date of the Slash magazine and its relationship to the label’s founding — Discogs gives 1978 for the label but the magazine slightly preceded it.
  • Bob Biggs’ background before founding the label — no realname or biography in Discogs.
  • What SCAM 102 (The Plugz, 1978) indicates about the catalog system — “SCAM” may predate the SR series, possibly from a slightly different label configuration.
  • Whether SR-106 was ever assigned (the sequence jumps from SR-105 to SRS-106 for the X single “White Girl”).
  • The full story of the 2003 relaunch with Will Fulton — Discogs data thins out significantly after 2000.
  • Distribution arrangements in territories other than US and UK (Germany, Australia, Canada) — pressings exist in all three but the sub-distribution chain isn’t fully documented in Discogs.
