Quarterstick Records

Chicago, Illinois, USA. Discogs ID: 2507. An imprint of Touch And Go, sharing its parent’s Chicago address (P.O. Box 25342, Chicago IL 60625) and phone infrastructure. Founded to release music that sat outside Touch and Go’s core noise-rock identity — quieter, more compositionally varied, rooted in folk, post-rock, post-hardcore, and chamber music. Has its own Bandcamp (quarterstick.bandcamp.com) and social accounts.

Sublabels: Quarterstick (ID 68385), Quarterstick Records, Inc. (ID 270912 — legal entity, no releases).

Overview

Quarterstick launched in 1990 with Pegboy’s debut Three-Chord Monte (QS00001) and Rollins Band’s Turned On (QS0002CD). The earliest catalog leaned on Midwest post-hardcore (Pegboy, Naked Raygun) and Touch and Go adjacents (Rollins Band, Volcano Suns, Therapy?). By 1992–1994, the label’s identity sharpened around two distinct poles:

  1. Austin-rooted Americana/string-band: Bad Livers — a bluegrass-punk hybrid produced by Paul Leary (Butthole Surfers) — were the label’s most distinctive early signing, releasing Delusions Of Banjer (1992), The Golden Years (1992), and Horses In The Mines (1994).
  2. Louisville post-rock: Rodan’s Rusty (1994) connected the label to the Louisville post-hardcore scene, a lineage that ran directly into Rachel’s, Shipping News, and eventually The For Carnation (on Touch and Go proper).

From 1995 onward, Quarterstick became one of the more consistent platforms for US post-rock and chamber-influenced indie, with June Of 44, Rachel’s, and The Sonora Pine — all orbiting the same Louisville/Chicago axis. The label’s longest commercial run was Calexico (1997–2012), the Tucson desert-pop duo whose catalog on Quarterstick eventually comprised over 60 releases.

Corey Rusk operated the label through Touch and Go’s infrastructure. Robert Weston (Bob Weston) served as the dominant house engineer across the catalog; Jason B. Noble and Rachel Grimes (Rachel’s) doubled as house designers, and the Rachel’s visual aesthetic — influenced by fine-art letterpress and gravestone rubbing — defined Quarterstick’s visual identity through the mid-1990s.

Aesthetic and Identity

Quarterstick occupied a distinct niche from its parent. Where Touch and Go was identified with volume, aggression, and noise rock, Quarterstick tended toward:

  • String-band and Americana (Bad Livers, The Mekons, Shipping News partial)
  • Louisville post-rock: the network centered on Jason Noble (Rachel’s, Rodan, Shipping News, Sonora Pine), Jeff Mueller (Rodan, June Of 44, Shipping News, Sonora Pine), and Tara Jane O’Neil (Rodan, Rachel’s, Sonora Pine, solo)
  • Chamber and composed music: Rachel’s in particular pushed toward scored, notated music — Music For Egon Schiele (1996) was written to accompany an art exhibition
  • Desert/border-crossings Americana: Calexico brought Tucson/Sonoran Desert sounds into the catalog from 1997; their long run with the label (1997–2012, 60+ releases) eventually made them Quarterstick’s highest-volume artist
  • Packaging quality: The label became known for fine-art packaging — hand-set letterpress type (Rachel’s Handwriting LP), gravestone-rubbing imagery, letterpress booklets printed by Greg King

The Louisville/Chicago overlap was central to the mid-catalog: Noble and Mueller had both been in Rodan, which recorded for Quarterstick; Noble and Rachel Grimes formed Rachel’s; Mueller and Noble formed Shipping News; the Sonora Pine connected O’Neil, Mueller, Meadows, Noble, and Frederickson.

Connections

  • Touch And Go — parent label; shared Chicago address, phone, and infrastructure; same Corey Rusk operation
  • Bad Livers — Austin roots/string-band; first three LPs (1992–1994)
  • Mark Rubin — Bad Livers bassist and tuba; core early Quarterstick presence
  • Danny Barnes — Bad Livers primary songwriter; produced later Bad Livers records
  • Paul Leary — produced Delusions Of Banjer; Butthole Surfers / Touch and Go cross-pollination
  • The Mekons — UK country-punk collective; Quarterstick was their long-term US home
  • June Of 44 — Louisville post-rock; Mueller + Scharin + Erskine + Meadows
  • Doug Scharin — June Of 44 drummer; also HiM, Codeine
  • Robert Weston — house engineer across 78 releases; also played in Volcano Suns
  • Jason B. Noble — Rachel’s, Rodan, Shipping News, Sonora Pine; performed, engineered, designed
  • Calexico — dominant long-term act; 1997–2012 run defined the label’s mature commercial identity
  • The For Carnation — Brian McMahan post-Slint; on Touch and Go proper, not Quarterstick, but connected via the Louisville network (McMahan engineered Rodan’s Rusty)
  • Slint — Louisville; Brian McMahan credited as engineer on Rodan’s Rusty (a notable connection between Louisville scenes)
  • Corey Rusk — Touch and Go / Quarterstick operator
