Sub Rosa

Belgian avant-garde and experimental label based in Brussels. Discogs label ID: 169. ~970 releases in the Discogs database; 21 registered sublabels/series. Address: 149–151 Avenue Ducpétiaux, 1060 Bruxelles. Website: subrosa.net.

About / History

Sub Rosa was founded in the early 1980s by Guy Marc Hinant (Discogs artist ID: 511877) and Frédéric Walheer (Discogs artist ID: 511875), both Brussels-based. Hinant went on to serve as the label’s chief compiler, curator, and executive producer across hundreds of releases; Walheer handled design, concept work, and production. The in-house design identity — especially from the 1990s onward — was largely executed by the studio names Mountain (2) (responsible for artwork and layout on over 220 releases) and Inspiral Icon (~93 releases), with Peter Maybury contributing prominent sleeve work through the 2000s–2010s. The label’s long-serving mastering engineer is Gabriel Séverin (Discogs ID: 489169), credited on nearly 100 releases in roles ranging from mastering and restoration to mixing and recording.

Sub Rosa launched in the post-industrial avant-garde current of early-1980s Europe. Its first releases — the Myths compilation series and LPs by SPK, Hula (2), General Strike, and Genesis P-Orridge — placed it firmly in the industrial/experimental underground alongside labels like Industrial Records, Doublevision, and Les Disques du Crépuscule. The catalog quickly expanded to spoken word and archival documents: William S. Burroughs’ Break Through In Grey Room (1986) and later the Antonin Artaud radio piece Pour En Finir Avec Le Jugement De Dieu (1996) established Sub Rosa as a serious home for literary/sonic archives.

Through the 1990s the label moved with equal ease across noise-jazz (Controlled Bleeding), industrial dance (Laibach), electronic ambience (Bill Laswell), downtown New York improv (Fred Frith, Robin Rimbaud/Scanner), spoken-word poetry (Ira Cohen), and Belgian post-rock/prog (X-Legged Sally). It also made an early bet on ambient/drone: Stars Of The Lid’s Avec Laudenum (SR155, 1999) was released on Sub Rosa before the Austin duo moved to Kranky. The Quantum sublabel (1996–1998) handled more club-oriented or remix material.

The defining project of Sub Rosa’s 2000s identity is the An Anthology of Noise & Electronic Music series, a multi-volume a-chronological survey (volumes I through VIII and beyond) curated by Hinant. Volume I (SR190, 2002) covers 1921–2001; subsequent volumes extend the timeline in both directions and across national traditions, encompassing European musique concrète, Japanese noise, American minimalism, and more. The series became a reference document for experimental music historians and archivists.

The 2010s saw the label consolidate around a core group of returning artists: the post-rock/electronica collective Oiseaux-Tempête released five albums (2013–2022); the Belgian chamber-music institution Jean-Luc Fafchamps and Musiques Nouvelles appeared repeatedly; Charlemagne Palestine did three collaborative releases including work with Rhys Chatham and Z’EV. The archival series continued with deep dives into Turkish, Greek, Indonesian, Ethiopian, and Spanish experimental traditions, as well as reissues of early electronic works by Pauline Oliveros, John Cage, Henri Pousseur, Luc Ferrari, Bülent Arel, and many others.

The label issued Discogs release ID 16161911 in 2020 as The Acid Lands (SRV500), a posthumous Burroughs archival project featuring the Iggy Pop-narrated version alongside Bill Laswell production and the Opening Performance Orchestra — the 500th numbered catalog entry.

The An Anthology Of Noise & Electronic Music Series

Sub Rosa’s most ambitious project: an ongoing a-chronological survey of recorded noise and electronic music across decades and territories. Curated by Guy Marc Hinant. Published volumes cover 1921–2001 (Vol. I, 2002), 1936–2003 (Vol. II, 2003), 1952–2004 (Vol. III, 2004), and continuing into the 2010s. Each volume pairs canonical names (Varèse, Cage, Oliveros, Ferrari) with archival rarities and national scenes. The series established Sub Rosa as the European counterpart to Something Else Press-style historical documentation.
