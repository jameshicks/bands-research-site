Overview

Flying Nun UK was the United Kingdom imprint of Flying Nun Records, the celebrated New Zealand indie label. It operated primarily in the second half of the 1980s, issuing around nine releases in that decade — predominantly indie rock and indie pop from NZ acts including The Chills and Martin Phillipps — before tapering to a single release in the 1990s. The catalogue’s breadth extends beyond its New Zealand core: Stereolab appear on the roster, adding a Space Rock and Krautrock dimension that reflects the label’s reach into the UK underground of the era. John A. Rivers, a producer closely associated with the NZ scene’s UK recordings, also carries credits under the imprint.

Notes (web)

Stereolab and Flying Nun UK

The sole Stereolab release on Flying Nun UK is Refried Ectoplasm (Switched On Volume 2) (September 1995, cat. FN331 / FNCD331). It was a co-release across three labels for three territories: Duophonic Records for the UK, Drag City for the US, and Flying Nun for New Zealand — but physical UK pressings carry the FN331 catalog number, indicating that Flying Nun UK handled or co-issued the UK pressing alongside Duophonic. (Sources: Discogs release entries for Refried Ectoplasm; tape-mag.com snippet for FN331 / FNCD331 catalog numbers.)

Territorial conflict to note: Wikipedia’s Refried Ectoplasm article (as of 2026-11-26) assigns the UK release to Duophonic and the New Zealand release to Flying Nun, with no separate Flying Nun UK territory. However, physical pressings are catalogued under FN331 — a Flying Nun UK catalog number — alongside the Duophonic D-UHF-LP09 number. The most likely reading is that Duophonic (Stereolab’s own imprint) was the originating UK label and Flying Nun UK was a co-releasing or co-distributing partner for the UK pressing, not a separate territorial licensor. This is consistent with Flying Nun UK operating from Mushroom Records’ London office at that time and making a practice of co-releasing non-NZ records. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refried_Ectoplasm

Flying Nun UK context: Roger Shepherd moved to London in mid-1994 to establish Flying Nun UK, which operated out of Michael Gudinski’s Mushroom Records’ London headquarters. The label’s UK operation ran from approximately 1994 until Mushroom closed the London office in 1997. During this period Flying Nun UK made a practice of co-releasing records by internationally recognised non-NZ indie acts — with Stereolab, Pavement, and Sonic Youth all cited as recipients. The relationship with Stereolab/Duophonic was therefore consistent with this broader 1990s strategy of partnering with credible UK underground acts. No primary source names the individual who brokered the specific Stereolab deal. (Sources: audioculture.co.nz/labels/flying-nun [https://audioculture.co.nz/labels/flying-nun]; audioculture.co.nz Roger Shepherd interview 1997 [https://audioculture.co.nz/articles/roger-shepherd-interview-1997]; beat.com.au Flying Nun history [https://beat.com.au/the-history-of-flying-nun-records-told-through-10-iconic-songs/].)

Extent of the relationship: Based on available sources, Refried Ectoplasm appears to be the only Stereolab release with a Flying Nun UK catalog number. The subsequent Switched On compilations and studio albums were released in New Zealand by Flying Nun Records proper (not the UK imprint), and by Warp Records / Duophonic in the UK. The Flying Nun website currently stocks a large Stereolab back catalogue (Switched On volumes, studio albums), but these appear to be NZ-territory stock from Flying Nun Records rather than Flying Nun UK releases. Flying Nun UK ceased operations circa 1997 when Mushroom closed its London office. (Source: [https://www.flyingnun.co.nz/collections/stereolab-1] — snippet only, page blocked 429.)

What could not be confirmed: No source names the person who personally arranged the Stereolab/Duophonic co-release deal with Flying Nun UK. No source clarifies whether this was an ongoing relationship discussed between Shepherd and Stereolab/Duophonic directly, or brokered via Mushroom’s contacts.
