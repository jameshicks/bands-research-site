Kranky

Chicago, Illinois independent record label. Founded spring 1993 by Joel Leoschke and Bruce Adams. Known for releasing ambient, drone, post-rock, slowcore, and experimental music.

Founded by: Joel Leoschke and Bruce Adams, both then working at Cargo Music Distribution in Chicago. Adams had previously worked at Kaleidoscope (a Des Plaines music distributor) and Touch & Go Records after moving to Chicago from Ann Arbor in 1987. The label was created specifically to release Labradford’s debut Prazision LP, which became Kranky’s first release. The name “kranky” was chosen partly for practical reasons — it fit better on record spines than their original idea, “Curmudgeon Records.” Both founders worked other jobs to fund the label in its early years. Adams departed mid-2000s (selling his share, later founding Flingco Sound); Brian Foote joined as co-operator around 2005. Leoschke relocated to Portland, where he continues to run the label. Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-29; Bandcamp Daily; Smile Politely

Released several Low albums including Secret Name (1999, KRANK 035) and Trust (2002). Also home to Stars of the Lid, Labradford, Godspeed You! Black Emperor (in the US), and others. Labradford’s Kranky catalog: Prazision LP (1993), A Stable Reference (1995), Mi Media Naranja (1997), E Luxo So (1999). Mark Nelson’s Pan-American project has been a long-running Kranky artist from 1997 through the 2020s. Primary label for Tim Hecker from Harmony In Ultraviolet (2006, krank102) through Shards (2025), including Ravedeath, 1972 (krank154), Virgins (KRANK 183), Konoyo (KRANK 219), and No Highs.

Home to Steve Hauschildt’s solo career post-Emeralds (2): Tragedy & Geometry (2011), Sequitur (2012), Where All Is Fled (2015), Strands (2016).

Discogs ID: 606

Also on the Kranky roster: Disappears (Lux 2010, Guider 2011, Pre Language 2012, Era 2013, Irreal 2015 — the band’s full studio run), Charalambides (Joy Shapes, krank068, 2004; Likeness, krank113, 2007; Exile, KRANK 158, 2011) and Christina Carter (Electrice, 2006; Original Darkness, 2008).
