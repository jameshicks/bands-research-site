Enjoy Records

Harlem-based independent record label founded in 1962 by Bobby Robinson (real name Morgan Clyde Robinson). Address: P.O. Box 1192, NYC 10027. Discogs label ID: 10149. Sublabel: Classic Series (4) (Discogs ID: 848278).

Active from 1962 to 1993. One of the earliest labels to release hip-hop records, beginning in 1979. Bobby Robinson produced virtually every release on the label across its entire three-decade run.

Overview

Enjoy Records was one of a cluster of independent Harlem labels Bobby Robinson built from the late 1950s onward, alongside Fire Records (2), Fury Records (2), Red Robin, Everlast, and others. While those earlier labels captured R&B, blues, and early soul, Enjoy was Robinson’s vehicle from 1962 and ultimately the one that survived longest — outlasting them all by pivoting, seventeen years in, to a brand-new genre.

When hip-hop emerged in the South Bronx and Harlem block-party circuits in the late 1970s, Robinson was already in his early sixties but moved quickly. In 1979 he put out Funky 4 + 1’s “Rappin’ and Rockin’ in the House” (cat. 6000) — the label’s first hip-hop single and one of the first hip-hop records commercially released anywhere. A cascade of releases followed across 1979–1985, covering nearly the full old-school Harlem/Bronx roster. The label went out of business in 1993.

History

Era 1: Early 45s and the King Curtis Years (1962–1965)

Enjoy opened in 1962. Its first release (cat. 1000) was “Soul Twist” by King Curtis And The Noble Knights, produced by Bobby Robinson and his brother Danny Robinson — a number-one R&B hit. The label launched with a mix of R&B, twist-era dance records, doo-wop, and blues:

  • King Curtis And The Noble Knights (11 releases, 1962–onward): the label’s flagship early act. “Soul Twist” / “Twistin’ With The King” established the format. Robinson also produced the debut Enjoy LP: Soul Twist With King Curtis (cat. ENLP 2001, 1962).
  • Titus Turner (7 releases, 1962–1964): R&B vocalist, co-wrote several tracks with Robinson.
  • Elmore James (6 releases, 1965): Blues guitar legend. Enjoy released “It Hurts Me Too,” “Look On Yonder Wall / Shake Your Money Maker,” and related sides.
  • Gladys Knight And The Pips (1 release, 1964): an early appearance before their Motown years.
  • Other early roster: The Channels, The Charts, Janet Calloway, June Bateman / Noble Watts, Rinkydinks, Jay Dee Bryant, Joe Haywood, Bobby Porter, Charlie Lucas Combo, Jim And Lee, The Hootenaires, Sam Taylor (4), Les Cooper And His Soul Rockers.

The label used sequential catalog numbers (1000–1016, then 2001–2028) for the 7” singles of this era.

Era 2: Gap Years (1966–1978)

Discogs documents only a single Enjoy release from 1969 (Joe Haywood, cat. 6000) and a handful from 1972 (Ricky Lewis, Ricky Lewis And The Afro Band). Bobby Robinson remained active as a producer throughout this period but worked primarily through other labels — Josie, Capitol, Mercury, Philips, and his own short-lived Bobby Robinson Records imprint. The Enjoy name lay dormant or nearly so for most of this decade.

Era 3: Hip-Hop Pioneer (1979–1985)

In 1979, Robinson relaunched Enjoy with a new catalog series (6000 onward, later ER-6000s) and immediately entered the nascent hip-hop market. The label’s hip-hop catalog was concentrated in six years and comprises some of the most important early recordings in the genre:

1979

  • Funky 4 + 1, “Rappin’ and Rockin’ the House” (cat. 6000) — the label’s hip-hop debut. 12”, 33⅓ RPM. A-side 16:00 / B-side 14:00. Prod. Bobby Robinson; music by Pumpkin & Friends; written by Robinson, Jeff Miree, Keith Caeser, Kevin Smith, Rodney Stone, Sharon Green.
  • Grandmaster Flash & The Furious Five, “Superappin’” (cat. 6001) — Grandmaster Flash’s commercial debut. 12”. Produced and co-written by Bobby Robinson; written by members Edward Morris, Guy Todd Williams, Keith Wiggins, Melvin Glover, Nathaniel Glover. This preceded their move to Sugar Hill Records.

1980

  • Spoonie Gee / Treacherous Three, “The New Rap Language / Love Rap” (cat. ER-6002) — a split single pairing Spoonie Gee (Robinson’s nephew) and the Treacherous Three. Music by Pumpkin & Friends; congas by Pooche Costello.
  • Grandmaster Flash & The Furious Five, “Super Rappin’ No. 2” (cat. ER-6011)
  • Treacherous Three, “The Body Rock” (cat. ER-6007) — Treacherous Three’s debut. Music by Pumpkin, Damond (2), and Jeff (8); mastered by Herb Powers Jr.
  • Treacherous Three, “At The Party” (cat. ER-6008)
  • Disco Four, “Move To The Groove” (cat. ER-6010)
  • Kool Kyle The Starchild, “Do You Like That Funky Beat” (cat. E-6006)

1981

  • Treacherous Three, “Feel The Heartbeat” (cat. ER-6013) — among the most-pressed Enjoy releases (7 pressings)
  • Treacherous Three, “Put The Boogie In Your Body” (cat. ER-6018)
  • Midnight Blue, “Enjoy With Me” (cat. ER-6021) — house act; Robinson-produced
  • Spanish Fly & The Terrible Two, “Spanglish” (cat. ER-6022) — Latin-inflected hip-hop
  • Higher Ground (2), “Shake ‘Em Up” (cat. ER-6023)
  • Doctor Ice (2), “Calling Doctor Ice” (cat. ER-6012)

1982

  • The Fearless Four, “It’s Magic” (cat. ER-6024) — debut single; musical accompaniment by Pumpkin & Friends
  • The Fearless Four, “Rockin’ It” (cat. ER-6028) — one of the earliest electro-hip-hop records; built on Kraftwerk’s “Numbers”; produced by Bobby Robinson; 12 pressings documented
  • The Masterdon Committee, “Gonna’ Get You Hot” (cat. ER-6029) — introduced Masterdon (Discogs ID: 622547) as the label’s primary drum programmer, credited on 15 releases
  • The Masterdon Committee, “Funkbox Party (Live)” (cat. 6032) — 9 pressings; widely reissued
  • The Packman, “I’m The Packman (Eat Everything I Can)” (cat. 6031)
  • Disco Four, “Country Rock And Rap” (cat. ER-6026)

1983–1985

  • Doug E. Fresh / Chill Will / DJ Barry B, “Just Having Fun” (cat. EN-6035, 1984) — Doug E. Fresh’s earliest recordings
  • The Source (2), “The Ghetto” (cat. 6034, 1984)
  • The Masterdon Committee, “Musicgram” (cat. 6033, 1984)
  • Spoonie Gee, “New Love Rap” (cat. EN-6036, 1985)
  • Crazy Eddie (3) / Jazaq, “Come On Party People (Lets Ge Down)” (cat. EN-6037, 1985)

Era 4: Late Period and Closure (1986–1993)

After the hip-hop golden period ended, the label released only sporadically. Notable late releases:

  • Project Demolition, “Kik It In Your Crib” (cat. EN-6042, 1988) — executive produced by Bobby Robinson
  • Various, “Rapper’s Enjoyment — Greatest Rap Hits From 125th, Harlem” (1984, Japan / P-Vine Special, cat. PLP-6003) — Japanese compilation; early international documentation of the label’s catalog
  • Various, The Best Of Enjoy! Records (1989, cat. HTCD 5) — career retrospective CD, co-released with Hot Productions

The label went out of business in 1993. Collectables Records and P-Vine Records (Japan) issued numerous retrospective compilations of the pre-hip-hop and hip-hop eras across the 1980s–2000s.

Production

Bobby Robinson produced virtually the entire Enjoy catalog — Discogs credits him on 155 releases as producer or executive producer, plus 71 as writer. He wrote-by or co-wrote across all eras, from King Curtis sides through the hip-hop catalog.

Key recurring production/session credits:

  • Pumpkin & Friends (Discogs ID: 175032): musical accompaniment on 30 releases — the primary house band for the hip-hop period
  • Pumpkin (Discogs ID: 35352): solo instrumental credits on 9 releases
  • Masterdon (Discogs ID: 622547): drum programming / rhythm box on 15 releases — the Masterdon Committee’s DJ/producer becomes essential to the label’s mid-period hip-hop sound
  • Damond (2) (Discogs ID: 1257266): instrumental accompaniment, 8 releases
  • Jeff (8) (Discogs ID: 451897): instrumental accompaniment, 7 releases
  • Bobby & Danny Robinson (Discogs ID: 1108944): co-production credit on 13 releases — Bobby working with his brother Danny Robinson (3) (Discogs ID: 1919482)
  • Frank Heller (Discogs ID: 69827): assistant producer, 5 releases
  • Henry Stone, Joe Stone, Paul Klein (3 releases each): executive producers on specific releases

Mastering handled primarily by:

  • Dick Charles (Discogs ID: 217621): mastered 9 Enjoy releases
  • Herb Powers Jr. (Discogs ID: 59453): mastered 8 releases

Connections

  • Bobby Robinson — founder, owner, sole producer across the label’s lifetime; Discogs ID: 119103
  • Fire Records (2) — Robinson’s concurrent/earlier label; many of the same artists and producers
  • Fury Records (2) — another Robinson label, active in parallel (Gladys Knight, Lee Dorsey, etc.)
  • King Curtis — flagship early act; “Soul Twist” (cat. 1000) was the label’s debut single; Discogs ID: 36862
  • Grandmaster Flash & The Furious Five — first hip-hop recording (1979); Discogs ID: 20590
  • Funky 4 + 1 — label’s first hip-hop release; Discogs ID: 96320
  • Treacherous Three — most prolific hip-hop act on label (1980–81); Discogs ID: 41239
  • The Fearless Four — electro-hip-hop landmark “Rockin’ It” (1982); Discogs ID: 13868
  • Spoonie Gee — hip-hop artist; Bobby Robinson’s nephew; Discogs ID: 4839
  • Doug E. Fresh — early recordings (1984); Discogs ID: 53485
  • P-Vine Records — Japanese label that released Enjoy compilations throughout the 1980s–2000s; key vehicle for the label’s international legacy
  • Y Records — London label that licensed/pressed “Rockin’ It” by The Fearless Four (YT105, 1982); distributed by Rough Trade

Open Questions

  • The 1966–1978 gap: Discogs shows virtually no Enjoy releases in this period. Whether Robinson was inactive on the label, releasing under other imprints, or whether records simply aren’t documented in the DB is unclear — web research on the Bobby Robinson discography for this period would help.
  • Spoonie Gee’s precise family relationship to Bobby Robinson (described as “nephew” in Robinson’s Discogs profile) — confirmed in DB but no further biographical context.
  • Mechanism for “Rappin’ and Rockin’ the House” being cataloged as 6000 while the 1969 Joe Haywood single also has cat. 6000 — the same catalog number was apparently reused when the hip-hop era began.
  • Exact closure date and circumstances of the 1993 shutdown are not documented in Discogs.
  • Danny Robinson (3)‘s full role in the label’s operation — credited as co-producer on 13 releases but his relationship to day-to-day label operations is not detailed in the DB.
