Doublevision

Discogs ID: 4814
From: Nottingham, England (30 Chatsworth Avenue, New Basford, Nottingham NG7 7EW)
Active: 1982–c.1987
Co-founded by: Cabaret Voltaire and Paul Smith (Blast First)

Label started by Cabaret Voltaire and Paul Smith in 1982, initially to release affordable video works (CV’s own live/visual material, and others) at around £15 when the industry norm was £40–70. Expanded into records from 1983.

The DV series was the original video imprint — VHS/Betamax releases issued from 1982 onward, separate from the DVR record series. DV7 is not cataloged on Discogs (gap confirmed).

Why Nottingham?

The Nottingham base has nothing to do with Cabaret Voltaire’s Sheffield roots and everything to do with co-founder Paul Smith (Blast First). In 1982 Smith was living in Nottingham and working for Nottinghamshire County Council. He and his friend John Moon ran a series of music-video nights at The Midland Group Arts Centre in Nottingham, screening work on cheap secondhand TVs — everything from ABC to Z’ev. Among the videos he showed was work by Cabaret Voltaire; the screening apparently went badly enough that he cut their video short. Cabaret Voltaire (Richard Kirk and Stephen Mallinder) got in touch, and from that encounter Smith and CV co-founded Doublevision as a vehicle for releasing affordable music video.

Because Smith was based in Nottingham — and the label’s practical day-to-day operations fell to him (he reportedly copied VHS tapes at home in the evenings after his council job) — Nottingham became the label’s registered address. Cabaret Voltaire were the creative/artistic force; Smith was the operational base, and his base was Nottingham, not Sheffield.

Smith later went on to found Blast First, which launched in 1985 from the same Nottingham roots, releasing Sonic Youth, Big Black and others. He became manager of Throbbing Gristle, Wire, Cabaret Voltaire and Lee Scratch Perry.

Kirk and Mallinder served as producers/engineers for several Doublevision acts in 1984: both produced Eric Random’s Mad As Mankind (DVR7); Kirk produced The Box’s Muscle In (DVR10). Chakk’s “Out Of The Flesh” (DVR6) sits directly between these in the catalog, recorded at Western Works with their involvement.
