LHI Records

Discogs ID: 90825 Parent entity: Lee Hazlewood Industries, Inc. (Discogs ID: 689041) Address: 9000 Sunset Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA, USA Active: 1967–1971 (original run); revived as reissue imprint 1990s/2000s Distributors: Decca Records (label ID 628858), 1967–68 · ABC Records (label ID 265836), 1968–71

Overview

LHI Records — Lee Hazlewood Industries — was an American independent label founded by Lee Hazlewood in 1967. It grew out of an earlier joint venture with Decca called Eden Records, renamed LHI following a Billboard announcement in the November 12, 1966 edition. Hazlewood had already built a career as a songwriter-producer behind Duane Eddy’s twangy guitar hits at Jamie Records and then as writer-producer for Nancy Sinatra’s Reprise recordings, including “These Boots Are Made For Walkin’” (1966). LHI was his attempt to run a fully independent operation from the Sunset Strip, with control over A&R, production, and artist development.

The label’s character was eclectic but distinctly Hazlewood-inflected: country noir, sunshine pop, proto-country-rock, psychedelia, and the occasional soft-rock or soul act. Hazlewood produced, wrote, and often supervised the bulk of the catalog. The label achieved its most historically notable moment when it released International Submarine Band’s Safe At Home in 1968, widely cited as a landmark early country rock album with Gram Parsons as its creative center.

LHI closed in 1971 as Hazlewood decamped for Sweden and Europe. Distribution contracts with Decca and then ABC Records — both of which would later be absorbed by MCA — ended without a successor deal. The label was revived in the 1990s and 2000s as a reissue imprint, and from the 2010s onward Light In The Attic (LITA) became the primary archival custodian of the LHI catalog.

Catalog Structure and Numbering

LHI used at least two numbering series during the original run:

  • 17000-series: Early 7” singles, 1966–1967 (pre-distributed through Decca infrastructure; these may predate the official Eden→LHI rename)
  • LHI-12000-series / S-12000-series: LPs, beginning with Safe At Home (LHI-S-12001, 1968)
  • LHI-1 through LHI-30: 7” singles from the ABC-distributed period, 1968–1971
  • LHI-3100 / LHI-3101: Late LP series, 1970 (Eve (7), Lee Hazlewood)

Swedish singles were issued under separate catalog numbers (LHI 1001, 1002) for the Scandinavian market.

Key Releases — Extended Notes

Safe At Home — The International Submarine Band (LHI-S-12001, 1968)

Produced by Suzi Jane Hokom. The defining early document of country rock, recorded before Gram Parsons joined The Byrds. Personnel:

RolePersonnel
Guitar (Lead)John Nuese
Guitar (Rhythm), Lead Vocals, Written-ByGram Parsons

PianoEarl Ball

EngineerEddie Brackett, Michael Lietz
Mastered ByKen Perry

Tracklist includes covers of Merle Haggard, Johnny Cash, Arthur “Big Boy” Crudup, Jack Clement, and Barry Goldberg, alongside Parsons originals.

The Cowboy & The Lady — Ann-Margret & Lee Hazlewood (LHI S-12007, 1969)

Lee Hazlewood produced and arranged, with Charlie McCoy as additional arranger. Associate producers Donnie Owens and Joe Nixon. Photography by Tommy Tucker (Tom Tucker (2)).

Forty — Lee Hazlewood (LHI 12009, 1969)

Recorded in London. Produced by Shel Talmy (with Hazlewood co-producing). String and orchestral arrangements by David Whitaker (most tracks), Johnny Arthey, and Big Jim Sullivan.

Dreams And Images — Arthur Lee Harper (S-12,000, 1968)

Folk-psych solo LP; all songs written by Arthur Lee Harper. Produced by Hazlewood; co-produced by Donnie Owens and Tom Thacker; arranged/conducted by Don Randi; engineered by Eddie Brackett and Michael Lietz.

Cowboy In Sweden — Lee Hazlewood (LHI 3101, 1970)

Hazlewood’s late LHI-period LP, partially recorded in Sweden and the UK. Arranger-producers include Craig Doerge, David Whitaker, Johnny Arthey; recording engineers Eddie Brackett and Mickey Crofford. Vocal contributions from Nina Lizell (tracks A2, A6, B5) and Suzi Jane Hokom (B2). Associate producers: Donnie Owens, Jack Robinson, Larry Marks (3), Shel Talmy; David Anderle produced “For A Day Like Today.”

Key People

Lee Hazlewood (Discogs ID: 225403)

Founder, primary producer, A&R. Appeared on the majority of LHI releases as producer, writer, and often performer. See Lee Hazlewood for full biography.

Tom Thacker (Discogs ID: 1229590)

West Coast promotion director at LHI from its founding; co-producer on a significant portion of the roster (13 releases as producer per credits summary). Real name: William Thomas Thacker; formerly program director of KEZY radio, Anaheim, CA.

Suzi Jane Hokom (Discogs ID: 357921)

Singer and producer. Produced Safe At Home for The International Submarine Band — an unusual role for a woman in late-1960s American rock production. Also appeared as vocalist on Hazlewood LPs and co-billed on several LHI singles. Alias: Hillary Hokom. See Suzi Jane Hokom.

Donnie Owens (Discogs ID: 312120)

Session guitarist and LHI associate producer (54 releases as producer). Member of The Wrecking Crew. Real name: Donald Lee Owens. See Donnie Owens.

Eddie Brackett (Discogs ID: 368712)

House engineer — 34 release credits on LHI, the most of any engineer on the label. Also worked the Hollywood sessions for Nancy Sinatra. See Eddie Brackett.

Don Randi (Discogs ID: 164277)

Pianist, arranger, conductor. 24 LHI releases as arranger/conductor/performer. One of the label’s core session musicians, bridging Hazlewood’s Wrecking Crew connections.

Frank Morton (3) (Discogs ID: 7423125)

Primary visual designer for LHI sleeve art — 9 releases covering illustration and painting. Created the cover and liner art for Safe At Home and Honey Ltd., among others.

Shel Talmy (Discogs ID: 195086)

UK-based producer who contributed to 15 LHI releases as producer or associate producer, most notably Forty (1969) and Cowboy In Sweden (1970). See Shel Talmy.

Larry Marks (3) (Discogs ID: 8313549)

Co-producer on 13 LHI releases. Credited as “for Lee Hazlewood Productions” on several.

Reissue Era

1990s/2000s revival

LHI was briefly revived as a reissue imprint in the 1990s and 2000s for Hazlewood back-catalog titles.

Open Questions

  • Exact founding date of Eden Records (the predecessor joint venture with Decca) — Billboard item is Nov 1966 but when was Eden formally operational?
  • Whether any LHI masters were licensed or leased rather than wholly owned by Hazlewood — the Decca joint-venture structure suggests shared ownership
  • Full A&R history: who brought in acts like Honey Ltd., The Aggregation, Hamilton Streetcar — Thacker, Hazlewood, or others?
  • The Merph Records, Inc. sublabel (Discogs ID: 1621088) — also under Lee Hazlewood Industries, Inc.; relationship to LHI unclear
  • Sanford Clark’s three LHI LPs — his earlier Sun Records career predates LHI; why LHI?
  • Barbara Randolph and Ray Sharpe were more established soul/R&B acts; how they came to LHI is undocumented in Discogs
