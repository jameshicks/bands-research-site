Overview

Manifold Records was an American experimental music label and mailorder operation founded in 1994 and run by Vince M. Harrigan, going dormant around 2007. Its catalogue concentrated in the 1990s — twelve releases across ambient, dark ambient, noise, and abstract experimental territory — tapering to six in the 2000s. The roster was eclectic even within the experimental sphere: DJ Spooky sat alongside Japanese noise figures K.K. Null and Akifumi Nakajima, a pairing unusual for a small noise imprint. Harrigan also operated five sublabels under the Manifold umbrella, including Economy Records and Endless.

Harrigan interviews and curatorial intent — search results, 2026-11-21

Short answer: No Harrigan interview or direct quote addressing the DJ Spooky / K.K. Null / Japanese noise pairing has been found in any indexed source. The following were checked and came up empty on this specific question:

  • Wikipedia article on Manifold Records (last modified 2026-02-01): confirms both artists on the roster and describes the compilations as “novel in that they featured traditionally Ambient and New Age artists alongside Noisier, edgier acts,” but contains no Harrigan quotes and no explanation of curatorial rationale. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_Records
  • V.M. Harrigan’s current Substack (manifoldhouse.substack.com): identifies him as “Formerly Manifold Records” but published posts on that platform do not discuss the label’s history or roster choices. No archived posts mentioning DJ Spooky or K.K. Null. https://manifoldhouse.substack.com/welcome
  • Manifold House website About page (manifoldhouse.org/about/): one sentence only — “Its roots come from the Experimental record label Manifold Records, founded in the early 90s, defunct since the late 2010s.” No curatorial discussion. https://manifoldhouse.org/about/
  • Sonic Boom reviews site (sonic-boom.com): two Manifold Records review pages checked; one review of a Final release, one of the Manifold zine issue 7. Contact address (Memphis P.O. Box) listed for Harrigan; no editorial quotes about the label’s philosophy. https://sonic-boom.com/review/final-1.html, https://sonic-boom.com/zines/manifold-7.html
  • Perfect Sound Forever DJ Spooky interview (furious.com/perfect/djspooky.html): focuses on DJ Spooky’s philosophy and influences; Manifold Records, Harrigan, K.K. Null not mentioned. https://www.furious.com/perfect/djspooky.html
  • Multiple web searches combining “Vince Harrigan,” “Manifold Records,” “interview,” “noise,” “DJ Spooky,” “KK Null,” “philosophy,” “curatorial”: returned only Discogs, Wikipedia, and the same handful of review-site pages above. No zine interview, music press profile, or archived label statement surfaced.

Contextual note (not a Harrigan quote): The Wikipedia/Wikiwand article text — which appears to draw on a contemporaneous source or early web copy — describes the label as having “a seeming inability to stay within any genre from release to release,” framing the eclecticism as a characteristic rather than a stated program. This language suggests reviewers of the period read the DJ Spooky / noise crossover as opportunistic or instinctive rather than the product of a declared philosophy. The same text notes the mid-1990s compilations — which mixed ambient/new age and noise acts — were the label’s highest-profile moment, associated with the short-lived “Isolationism” genre tag.

DJ Spooky connection: The Elementz DJ Toolz Series, the Manifold sublabel under which the DJ Spooky collaborations (with Merzbow, Alan Licht, Totemplow, Arto Lindsay, Quoit) appeared, is credited on Discogs to both Manifold and “Music+Art, NYC,” suggesting DJ Spooky or his camp had an active hand in shaping that strand of the catalogue. This may point to the DJ Spooky releases being artist-initiated rather than a top-down Harrigan curatorial decision — but no source confirms this. https://www.discogs.com/release/28804-DJ-Spooky-That-Subliminal-Kid-Merzbow-Subsidia-Pataphysica (snippet only; Discogs not fetched)

Dead end confirmed: The question of whether Harrigan articulated a deliberate curatorial bridge between American avant-hip-hop and Japanese noise cannot currently be answered from public web sources. No interview with Harrigan appears to have been published in any indexed music press outlet. The Manifold Records website (ceased 2007) is not archived in a fetchable form via standard search.
