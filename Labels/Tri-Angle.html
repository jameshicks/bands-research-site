Tri Angle

Discogs label ID: 205362
Also known as: Tri Angle Records
Location: Brooklyn, NY, USA / London, UK
Active: 2010 – early 2020
Founded by: Robin Carolan
Parent label: None (independent)
Sublabels: None

Electronic music imprint founded and run by Robin Carolan, operating across Brooklyn and London. Active from 2010 to early 2020, Tri Angle was one of the defining labels of early-2010s dark/atmospheric electronic music, releasing work that encompassed witch house, post-dubstep, hauntological ambient, experimental R&B, and deconstructed club music. The label’s catalog traces a clear arc from its first wave of lo-fi, fog-drenched witch house (oOoOO, Balam Acab, Holy Other) through more confrontational industrial and rhythmic work (Vessel, The Haxan Cloak, Lotic) into gospel-inflected experimental R&B (Serpentwithfeet) before closing around 2020.

Carolan also records as Lie (2) (alias listed in Discogs). Post-label, he became a composer, most notably scoring Robert Eggers’ The Northman (2022) and Nosferatu (2024) alongside collaborator Sebastian Gainsborough (Vessel).

History and Aesthetic Identity

2010 — Launch / First Wave

The label launched in 2010 with two debut releases establishing its identity immediately: Balam Acab’s See Birds EP (TRI ANGLE 01) and oOoOO’s self-titled EP (TRI ANGLE 02). Both were saturated in heavy reverb, slowed-down beats, and pitched-down or chopped vocal samples — early examples of what blogs were calling “witch house.” The accompanying compilation Let Me Shine For You (TRIANGLE00) served as a manifesto for the early roster.

Clams Casino’s Rainforest EP (2011, TRIANGLE 06) was an early outlier — Clams Casino (Al Ferrante) was already an in-demand beatmaker for A$AP Rocky and Lil B, and his Tri Angle output straddled cloud rap and atmospheric electronic music.

2011 — Holy Other, How To Dress Well

2011 brought two pivotal signings. Holy Other (David Ainley) debuted with With U (TRI ANGLE 04) — four tracks of ghostly, reverb-drenched vocals over sub-bass and drum-machine patterns that became a reference point for hauntological R&B. His follow-up album Held (2012) remains a landmark of the label’s first phase.

How To Dress Well (Tom Krell) released Love Remains (TRI ANGLE 03) on the label in 2011 — the only Tri Angle release from a project that subsequently moved to Weird World (2) / XL. Love Remains predates (or coincides with) the lo-fi R&B tag that would follow Krell; on Tri Angle it sat alongside the label’s spectral electronic aesthetic naturally.

Balam Acab’s album Wander / Wonder (TRIANGLE 07) also appeared in 2011, mixed by Sam Haar (of Blondes) and produced by Alec Koone — the fullest realization of the early witch-house sound on the label.

2012–2013 — Expansion and Critical Peak

The label expanded its aesthetic envelope significantly. Evian Christ (Joshua Leary) debuted with Kings And Them (TRI ANGLE 12), bringing a hip-hop production sensibility that attracted attention from Kanye West’s camp. AlunaGeorge (Aluna Francis + George Reid) released You Know You Like It (TRI ANGLE 14) — a pop-facing R&B release somewhat atypical for the label; the duo subsequently became a mainstream act.

The critical peak came in 2013 with three definitive releases:

  • Forest Swords — Engravings (TRIANGLE 20): Matthew Barnes’ debut album; produced and written by Barnes, mastered by Carl Saff. Dense, haunted, post-dubstep with folk-tinged melodic instincts. Widely considered one of the label’s essential records. 9 pressings.
  • The Haxan Cloak — Excavation (TRIANGLE 18): Bobby Krlic’s second album; a slow descent through bass-heavy, sub-sonic doom and dark ambient. Design by Dr. Me, photography by Cody Cobb, mastered by Matt Colton. 6 pressings.
  • Vessel — Order Of Noise (TRIANGLE 17): Sebastian Gainsborough’s debut album on the label; industrial, rhythmically disorienting.

2014 — Mid-Period Releases

Vessel’s Punish, Honey (TRIANGLE 27, 2014) was the follow-up to Order Of Noise — produced and written by Gainsborough (Sebastian Gainsborough = Vessel), with artwork by Harry Wright (3) and mastered by Matt Colton. Considered by many the label’s most sonically confrontational release.

Evian Christ’s Waterfall (TRIANGLE 22) and Duga-3 (TRIANGLE 19) continued his run. Sd Laika (Peter Runge) released That’s Harakiri (TRIANGLE 23D).

Wife (4) (James Kelly) released What’s Between (TRI026LP). Note: Earlier versions of this article incorrectly attributed Wife (4) to J’Kerian Morgan / Lotic. Wife (4) = James Kelly is a distinct artist; Lotic / J’Kerian Morgan did not release under the Wife alias.

2015 — Club Music Turn

Rabit (Eric C. Burton) arrived with Baptizm (TRIANGLE 29) and the extended Communion - Extended Edition (TRIANGLE 32DB), bringing a more explicitly deconstructed club and ballroom-adjacent sound to the label. Lotic (J’Kerian Morgan) released Heterocetera (TRIANGLE 30D).

HANZ debuted with Reducer (TRIANGLE 31).

2016 — Final Expansion Phase

Roly Porter released Third Law (TRIANGLE 33D) — ambient/classical-influenced, one of the label’s more compositionally formal offerings. Adult Jazz released Earrings Off! (Triangle 36). Katie Gately’s Color (TRIANGLE 38) and Serpentwithfeet’s blisters EP (TRI037dig3) signaled a move toward more voice-centered, R&B-adjacent experimental work.

2017–2020 — Closing Phase

Activity slowed after 2016. HANZ returned with Plasty I EP (TRI 041, 2017). The 2018 cluster included Lotic’s Power (TRIANGLE 44), Vessel’s Queen Of Golden Dogs (TRI046LP), and Serpentwithfeet’s debut album Soil (SC366) — the latter produced across tracks by Clams Casino, Katie Gately, mmph, and Paul Epworth, with Robin Carolan as executive producer. Soil was the label’s most explicitly R&B album and one of its final major releases. The label closed in early 2020.

Recurring Production / Technical Credits

The label’s visual identity was largely shaped by design collective Dr. Me (20 releases) and Collin Fletcher (11 releases). The dominant mastering engineers were Matt Colton (25 releases — Metropolis Studio, London) and Heba Kadry (16 releases). Carl Saff handled 9 releases, primarily the Forest Swords catalog.
