Waterfront Records

Australian independent record label based in Sydney, New South Wales. Founded 1982 by Steven Stavrakis. Discogs label ID: 45470. Operated until the late 1990s; physical shop closed March 2000.

Overview

Waterfront Records was started in 1982 by Steven Stavrakis while he was working at Phantom Records (3) (the Australian Phantom Records, Discogs ID: 48703). The label’s first release was the J.F.K. and The Cuban Crisis EP Careless Talk Costs Lives… (DAMP 1, January 1983).

In 1985 Stavrakis and Phantom co-worker Frank Cotterell opened the Waterfront record shop on York Street, Sydney. Later joined by Chris Dunn (another ex-Phantom colleague), the store moved to Sydney’s Haymarket and eventually back to York Street, where it closed in March 2000.

After closure, the Waterfront name was appropriated by another entity (Discogs: Waterfront Records (9)) who released records under the imprint without the knowledge of Stavrakis or Dunn. The authoritative company/copyright credit entity is the sublabel Waterfront (Discogs ID: 94942).

The label’s catalog prefix is DAMP, running from DAMP 1 (1983) through at least DAMP 183 (1994). The catalog spans Australian indie, post-punk, new wave, hardcore, punk rock, and alternative, with a strong Sydney-centred identity but international licensing relationships (Sub Pop, Alternative Tentacles, etc.).

Scene Context

Waterfront Records was central to the Sydney independent scene of the 1980s and early 1990s. Its founding at Phantom Records, Sydney’s key independent distributor, gave it strong connections to the Australian independent distribution network.

The label operated across several distinct scenes simultaneously:

  • Sydney post-punk / power pop: The Eastern Dark, Ups And Downs, Happy Hate Me Nots (late 1980s indie pop crossover)
  • Sydney punk/hardcore: Hard-Ons (the label’s signature act across the entire catalog), Massappeal
  • Sydney garage/psychedelia: Hellmenn, Tumbleweed (who moved from Wollongong to broader national recognition)
  • Alternative/jangle: Ratcat (who eventually crossed over to mainstream success)
  • Country-noir / post-gothic: Blackeyed Susans (from Perth, with strong Melbourne ties)

The label also developed significant international relationships with the US independent scene, pressing and distributing Sub Pop releases (Nirvana, Tad, Mudhoney) and Alternative Tentacles material (Jello Biafra projects, D.O.A.) in Australia. Jack Endino’s recurring presence as engineer (10 releases) reflects the Seattle/Sub Pop connection — he engineered the Australian Nirvana pressings as well as some local Sydney acts. Rob Younger of Radio Birdman was the most prolific producer on the label, appearing on 26 releases.

Captain Sensible (of The Damned) produced Hard-Ons pre-production in London — an unusual UK connection for a Sydney label.

The Waterfront shop on York Street, later Haymarket, was a physical hub for the Sydney indie community until its closure in March 2000.
