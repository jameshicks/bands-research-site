ROIR

Discogs label ID: 18316
Full name: Reachout International Records (company: Reachout International Records, Inc., ID 396693)
Founded: 1979 (first releases 1981), New York City
Founders: Neil Cooper (3) (1930–2001) and John Hanti
Address (original): 611 Broadway, Suite 725, NYC, NY 10012; later PO Box 540-160, Van Brunt Station, Brooklyn, NY 11215
Website: roir-usa.com / roir.bandcamp.com
Sublabels: ROIR Europe (36592), ROIR-UK (572407), ROIR USA (33076), The ROIR Sessions (622268)

Independent American label co-founded by Neil Cooper (3) and John Hanti in 1979, with the first releases appearing in 1981. Launched as a cassette-only operation — described by Hanti as “America’s first all-cassette record label” and “only the second in the world.” Cooper’s initial investment was approximately $60,000, recouped within six years. The label issued 106 cassette titles in total. Releases span punk, No Wave, hardcore, reggae, dub, industrial, goth, and post-punk — artists who were either unsigned, out-of-print, or whose live recordings had circulated as bootlegs. Many early cassettes were later reissued on vinyl and CD. After Cooper’s death on 13 August 2001, the label was continued by his sons Lucas and Nick Cooper.

Founding History and the 1979–1981 Gap

Neil Cooper’s background and the ’80s club

Before ROIR, Cooper worked as a booking agent for MCA Inc. and Famous Artists in the 1950s, representing artists including Shirley Bassey and Charles Mingus. He later managed a rock club in Hollywood Beach, Florida (Tight Squeeze), then took over The ’80s, a live venue on Manhattan’s Upper East Side. The club was his entry into record production: “These guys who ran small record labels would come to The ’80s with beautiful women on their arms and order champagne, and I’d be running around trying to fix an overflowing toilet.” (Wikipedia: Neil Cooper, as of 2026-03-10)

Why the two-year gap (1979–1981)

The 1979 date is the founding/incorporation of Reachout International Records, Inc. — Cooper invested approximately $60,000 and began approaching bands at The ’80s about recording contracts. The delay before the first release was a startup gestation period driven by two obstacles:

  1. Bands wouldn’t sign vinyl deals with him. Cooper was candid about this: “I wanted to put out [vinyl] LPs, but nobody wanted to make a record with me because I had no history in the [record] business.” (Wikipedia: Neil Cooper, as of 2026-03-10; same quote sourced in en-academic.com)

  2. He pivoted to cassette after observing UK precedents. Cooper noticed British artists using cassettes: “English artists like David Bowie and Bow Wow Wow and Elvis Costello were coming out with cassettes — and getting big play in [British music paper] NME.” This gave him a path: bands unwilling to sign vinyl deals would give him “works in progress, or stuff that they didn’t think was good enough for an LP and a major label commitment. I gave them advances, and they gave me the rights to put out music on cassettes.” (Wikipedia: Neil Cooper, as of 2026-03-10)

  3. Recordings were made at The ’80s and at the Peppermint Lounge. Cooper’s liner notes for the first cassette (A100, James Chance & The Contortions) state: “We recorded it on a four-track Dorkorder at my nightclub in NYC ‘The 80’s’ and at Jim Fouratt’s ‘Peppermint Lounge’.” This suggests 1979–1980 was also spent recording source material at the club. (Cooper liner note quoted in search results citing the A100 release; ROIR Wikipedia, as of 2026-05-13)

  4. John Hanti joined to launch the label. Per the EverybodyWiki Hanti article (blocked on fetch, but cited in search results): “In 1981, Hanti joined forces with Neil Cooper to launch ROIR Records.” This wording suggests Hanti came on board as a partner specifically at the point of launch rather than at incorporation — consistent with Cooper doing the groundwork from 1979 and Hanti joining to operationalize the releases. (Source: search result summary citing EverybodyWiki: John Hanti — page returned 403 on direct fetch; treat as weak/unverified until confirmed)

Summary of the gap

The 1979–1981 period was Cooper’s self-funded startup phase: he incorporated the label, secured $60,000 in capital, negotiated cassette rights from bands who rejected vinyl deals, recorded live material at The ’80s and the Peppermint Lounge, and built the initial catalog. Hanti appears to have come aboard around 1981 to help launch. The first release (A100) came in September 1981. No source identifies a specific external cause for the delay (legal disputes, distribution hold-ups, etc.) — the gap is consistent with a first-time label founder building from scratch without industry leverage.

Conflict note: Wikipedia’s ROIR article (as of 2026-05-13) states ROIR “was founded the same year that the Sony Walkman launched” — the Walkman launched in Japan in July 1979, so this framing is consistent with the 1979 date. However, Cooper’s quote implies the Walkman was a subsequent windfall (“Sony saved me”) rather than a planned part of the model at founding. The cassette format choice was inspired by UK music-press cassette releases, not by the Walkman per se.

Catalog Structure

Early releases use an “A” prefix with sequential numbering (A100 onwards). The first release was A100 (1981). Later CD and LP reissues shifted to a “RUS” prefix (RUSCD, RUSLP), which also became the catalog format for new releases from the mid-1990s onward.

The label encompasses a remarkably eclectic range: the same catalog that houses the landmark Bad Brains cassette also includes reggae, dub, industrial, gothic rock, and eventually 2000s-era post-hardcore and dubstep (Kode9).

A100: James Chance & The Contortions — Live In New York (r575493)

The first ROIR release. Personnel includes James Chance (vocals, alto sax, mixing), Bern Nix (guitar), Tomás Doncker (guitar), Colin Wade (bass), Richie Harrison (drums), Joseph Bowie (trombone), with the Discolitas on backing vocals. Concept by Neil Cooper; executive producer John Hanti; mixing engineer Stanley Moskowitz. Liner notes by Glenn O’Brien. Photography: Roberta Bayley.

A103: Suicide — 1/2 Alive (r652460)

Live cassette. Alan Vega (vocals) and Martin Rev (instruments, reprocessing). Concept by Neil Cooper; executive producer John Hanti; design by Jim Sclavunos. Liner notes by Lester Bangs. One of the last things Bangs wrote before his death in 1982.

A106: Bad Brains (r956777)

The most celebrated ROIR release. Recorded live in 1979 at the subject of considerable bootlegging; this cassette became one of the most influential hardcore documents of the era. Personnel: H.R. (vocals), Dr. Know (guitar), Darryl Jenifer (bass), Earl Hudson (drums). Concept by Neil Cooper; liner notes by Ira Kaplan (later of Yo La Tengo). The orange-cassette edition became an artifact.

A113: New York Thrash (r7624705)

Compilation cassette. Tracks from: Bad Brains, Beastie Boys (early), The Mad, Kraut, Heart Attack (2), The Undead, Adrenalin O.D., Even Worse, The Fiends, Nihilistics, False Prophets. Liner notes by Tim Sommer. Documents the early NYC hardcore scene in one snapshot.
