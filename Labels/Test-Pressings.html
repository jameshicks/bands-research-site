Test Pressings

Small UK independent record label, London (W2). Discogs label ID: 75569. Active 1980–1983. Contact address on Discogs: 32 Alexander Street, London W2.

Overview

Test Pressings operated as a micro-imprint out of West London in the early 1980s, releasing seven singles across three years. The label’s profile was run by Terry Razor (Discogs artist ID: 321104; name variations: Razor, T. Razor) — no further biographical detail is recorded in Discogs. The catalog number sequence ran TP1B/TPB1 through TP6, with one gap and some variant numbering, suggesting a small but consistently administered operation rather than a one-off vanity release.

Despite its tiny catalog, the label touched several significant corners of the early UK post-punk underground. Its first release was the debut single by Blurt, one of the period’s most uncompromising avant-jazz-punk acts. Subsequent releases brought in punk and mod-punk bands from London and the provinces, and the label’s final release — in 1983, when most of the early post-punk labels had folded — featured Pearl Harbour backed by a lineup that included Paul Simonon and Wilko Johnson.

The existing stub mentioned a possible connection to the “Rather” distribution network. Discogs does not record a “Rather” label link or distributor credit on any Test Pressings release. The label’s production fingerprints (MPO pressing plant markings appear in multiple runout etchings; lacquer cutting shared across releases by Tim Young) indicate a small but consistently commissioned manufacturing chain. No parent label or sublabels are listed in Discogs.

Releases in Detail

TPB1 — Blurt: My Mother Was A Friend Of An Enemy Of The People / Get (1980)

Blurt’s debut single, one of the earliest documents of UK avant-jazz post-punk. Credited writers: Ted Milton, Jake Milton, and Peter Creese (all credited as “Milton” or “Creese” in abbreviated form). No producer credit. Genres logged as Jazz, Rock; styles: Avantgarde, No Wave, Post-Punk.

RoleName
Written-ByTed Milton, Jake Milton, Pete Creese

The single was later reissued in 2024 on All City Records (3).

T.P. 2 — Spider King: Animals / Do You Want To Die For England (1981)

Post-punk 7” by Spider King — a solo vehicle for Mike King (Discogs: Mike King (12)), credited on the sleeve as “Spider Mike King.” King played guitar and sang and wrote all material, though all credits are listed as “uncredited” on the release (Discogs has since filled them in). Photography by Justin Thomas (6). Lacquer cut by Ray Staff (credited as “Rays”).

tp3 — Charge: Kings Cross / Brave New World / Gods Kids (1981)

A-side double header (two tracks on the A) plus a B-side, pressed at 45 RPM. Punk single. Charge self-produced alongside engineer Peter J. Reynolds (uncredited). Cover design by C-More-Tone Studios. Lacquer cut by Tim Young (“Timtom” in runout etching; “timtom-cbs MPO” in the matrix). Written by Stu Lunn.

TP 4 — Erazerhead: Apeman (1981)

The imprint’s most straightforward rock release. Erazerhead was a four-piece: Lee Drury (vocals), Jim Slim (guitar), Gary Walton (bass), Billy Wizz (drums). All songs written by Erazerhead. Lacquer cutting by Tim Young (“timtom” / “TY” in matrix, scoped by side).

T.P.5. — The Nips: Happy Song / Nobody To Love (1981)

The most high-profile release in commercial terms. The Nips — Shane MacGowan’s band before the Pogues, also known as the Nipple Erectors — released this single with Paul Weller producing (credited as “P. Weller”). Engineered by Peter Wilson (later the producer of The Gift and Café Bleu). Lacquer cut by Tim Young at MPO.

TP6 — Pearl Harbour: Voodoo Voodoo / You Don’t Fool Me (1983)

The label’s final release and its most star-studded session. Pearl Harbour (Patricia Gilbert, Discogs artist ID: 533858; also known as Pearl E. Gates) had been active in San Francisco punk scenes before relocating to the UK. For this single she assembled a band that included Paul Simonon (Clash bassist) and Nick Simonon on drums, Wilko Johnson on guitar (also producing), Nigel Dixon on guitar, Norman Watt-Roy on bass, and Blair Cunningham on drums. Track 1 (“Voodoo Voodoo”) written by Andrew Coleman (4) and Chess Avril; track 2 (“You Don’t Fool Me”) written by Pearl Harbour.

Open Questions

  • “Rather” distribution: mentioned in prior research as possibly connected to this label (“Test Pressing/Rather”). Discogs carries no distributor credit on any release. Web research would be needed to confirm or deny.
  • Terry Razor’s identity: Discogs lists only this name (ID 321104; variations: Razor, T. Razor) as the label runner. No further detail in DB. Could be a pseudonym.
  • No TP release appears with catalog number “TP 3” — the Charge single uses lowercase “tp3”. The Erazerhead single jumps to TP 4. The gap between T.P. 2 and tp3 is real; no missing release apparent.
  • Blair Cunningham on TP6: a drummer of this name played with The Pretenders in later years — Discogs lists this as the same person (ID 266077). Needs verification.
  • Nick Simonon: listed as a drummer on TP6 (Discogs ID 3678682). Unclear if related to Paul Simonon. Not documented in the vault.
