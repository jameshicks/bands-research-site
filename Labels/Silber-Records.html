Silber Records was started in 1996 by Brian John Mitchell to offer his support to artists he loved & respected. The roster has always included a global-community scope involving as many artists from Europe as from North America. The goal of Silber is not to be million-dollar-making corporation, but to build community through music.

— Discogs label profile, ID 28332

Overview

Silber Records is a small independent label founded in 1996 by Brian John Mitchell, built around community rather than commerce — the label’s stated aim was supporting artists Mitchell loved rather than chasing scale. Its catalogue spans ambient, drone, experimental, post rock, and noise, with a deliberately international roster drawing from Europe and North America in roughly equal measure. Activity picked up sharply in the 2000s (15 releases) and carried through the 2010s (13 releases), with roster artists including Yellow6, Jon Attwood, Jon DeRosa, and Joshua Heinrich. The label accumulated 274 Discogs credits across 30+ releases without ever expanding into a sublabel structure.

Notes (web) — Yellow6/Silber connection research

Question: How did Brian John Mitchell connect with Yellow6 (Jon Attwood, UK) to bring them onto Silber — was there an intermediary, festival, or online scene?

Research conducted 2026-11-16. Sources checked:

  • somewherecold.net 2016 interview with Jon Attwood (source) — confirms the Silber relationship but gives no origin story; says only that Attwood “joined Silber” and that his releases were issued on the label.
  • opus.ing Yellow6 interview (source) — covers the Make Mine Music label co-founded by Attwood; Silber not mentioned.
  • somewherecold.net 2003 interview (source) — predates Silber relationship; Attwood describes himself as unsigned and operating via Make Mine Music.
  • babysue.com interview with Brian John Mitchell (source) — no mention of Yellow6 or UK signings; general note that bands who send personal emails explaining their connection to Silber are considered (vs. ~90% of unsolicited submissions that go unchecked).
  • Indy Week profile of Silber Records (source) — describes Mitchell building his network via QRD zine (started 1993), touring as a roadie, and “the early Internet’s primitive newsgroup system”; no UK-specific detail.
  • silbermedia.com Yellow6 page and QRD archive page (http://www.silbermedia.com/yellow6/ and http://www.silbermedia.com/qrd/archives/43jonattwood.html) — both returned socket hang-up / timeout errors; content not accessible.
  • Yellow6 Bandcamp liner notes for No Memories, Only Photographs (2015, Silber 201) (source) — credits “Thanks to Brian John Mitchell @ Silber for his continued support”; no origin detail.
  • Yellow6 blog (yellow6music.blogspot.com) — one mention of Silber for 2016 Merry6mas release; no history of the relationship.
  • houseofprog.com review of Five (2013 “5 in 5” EP) — page returned CSS only; no usable text.

Established facts from this research:

  • Yellow6 was unsigned and operating via Make Mine Music as of the 2003 interview.
  • The first documented Silber release is Five (2013, Silber “5 in 5” series, 25th instalment) — a five-songs-in-five-minutes EP format used for many invited artists. (houseofprog snippet)
  • The first full-length Silber release is No Memories, Only Photographs (2015, Silber 201).
  • Mitchell’s known networking channels — QRD zine, early internet newsgroups, personal email submissions — are plausible routes, but no source names which applied to Yellow6.
  • The Indy Week profile specifically names QRD and early internet newsgroups as how Mitchell found artists like Lycia; the same channels likely applied broadly but this is not confirmed for Yellow6 specifically.

Conclusion: No published source (interview, label feature, liner note, blog post) states explicitly how Mitchell and Attwood connected. The specific origin story — intermediary, online community, demo submission, or other route — is not publicly documented in any indexed source checked. The silbermedia.com QRD interview with Attwood (issue 43) was not accessible (timeout); that page may contain relevant background and is the strongest remaining candidate.
