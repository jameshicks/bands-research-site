Ritornell

Frankfurt-based sublabel of Mille Plateaux, operated under the Achim Szepanski-run Force Inc constellation. Discogs label ID: 761. Active primarily 1999–2003, with a single late release in 2008. Dedicated to abstract experimental electronic music and sound art, distinguished from the parent label by an even stronger focus on microsound, lowercase aesthetics, and composer-led work rather than club-facing electronica.

URLs on record: mille-plateaux.com, ritornell.com.

Context

Ritornell launched in 1999 alongside the peak years of the Force Inc. / Mille Plateaux empire, which also encompassed Mille Plateaux (harder digital minimalism, Oval, Autechre reissues), and the broader Frankfurt experimental scene radiating from Achim Szepanski’s operation. Where Mille Plateaux served as the main outlet for digital minimalism and clicks-and-cuts, Ritornell skewed toward sound art, drone, and more compositionally oriented work — releasing Asmus Tietchens, Achim Wollscheid, and Christophe Charles alongside the glitch/laptop milieu.

The label’s consistent visual identity was handled almost entirely by designer Alorenz (Discogs artist ID: 2049410), who received cover or artwork credits on at least fourteen Ritornell releases. This tight design throughline gives the catalog a recognizable visual coherence.

The catalog runs to approximately 33 substantive releases (excluding a promotional CD and one release credited variously). Most output is on CD with one LP variant (Dr. Atmo’s Man Made Motion). Catalog numbers follow the RIT prefix scheme (RIT 001 through RIT 030), with a few irregularities.

Key Releases

Maschinelle Strategeme (RIT 10, 2000) — Various Artists

The label’s first compilation and effectively a showcase of the Ritornell roster at its 2000 peak. Fifteen tracks with liner notes by Kim Cascone. Contributors include Taylor Deupree, Stilluppsteypa, Akira Rabelais, Christophe Charles, Dean Roberts, Achim Wollscheid, and Terre Thaemlitz, plus an untitled track by the full Afternoon Tea quintet (Ambarchi / Fennesz / Pimmon / Rehberg / Rowe). Genres: Ambient, Experimental, Minimal.

Afternoon Tea (RIT 14, 2000)

Five-way improvised session credited jointly to Oren Ambarchi (guitar, editing, mixing), Fennesz (Christian Fennesz, computer), Pimmon, Peter Rehberg (computer), and Keith Rowe (guitar). Engineered by Chris Townend. Cover by Alorenz. The album documents a single collaborative performance and stands as one of the more celebrated documents of the Australian-European improv/laptop intersection of the period. Pimmon and Peter Rehberg are among the few credited with only “Computer” without additional detail; Ambarchi handled editing and final mixing. Discogs release ID: 74238.

α-Menge / β-Menge / γ-Menge / δ-Menge (RIT 12 / rit 22 / rit 27 / rit 29, 2000–2003) — Asmus Tietchens

Four-volume series by Hamburg veteran Asmus Tietchens constituting the label’s most sustained single-artist project. All four produced by Okko Bekker (“Produktion” credit). The Greek-letter series was Tietchens’s primary output in this period. Discogs IDs: 73882, 73961, 73931, 196684.

Wurmloch Variationen (RIT 16, 2000) — Stephan Mathieu

Debut album by Saarbrücken-based Stephan Mathieu, here credited as performer (“Played And Treated”). Liner notes by Felix Klopotek (translation by Bruce Carnevale). Cover by Alorenz. Mathieu would become one of the label’s most represented artists with three primary-credited releases.

FrequencyLib (RIT23, 2001) — Stephan Mathieu / compiled by Ekkehard Ehlers

Credited to Stephan Mathieu as primary artist and produced by Mathieu, but compiled by Ekkehard Ehlers — an unusual double-credit structure suggesting the release is a curated selection rather than a straight studio album. Artwork also by Mathieu and Alorenz. Discogs IDs: 73942, 313047 (second pressing).

Selected Random Works (RIT 15, 2000) — Random Industries (Sebastian Meissner)

Sebastian Meissner under his Random Industries alias. Liner notes by Kim Cascone; Meissner credited as “Producer [Extracted By].” Cover by Alorenz. Meissner would return as Random_Inc for Jerusalem: Tales Outside The Framework Of Orthodoxy (rit 020, 2001).

Klangmaschine_Soundmachine (RIT 26, 2002) — Various Artists

Second Ritornell compilation, marking the label’s shift toward glitch and minimal aesthetics in the later phase. Contributors include Taylor Deupree, Alva Noto (Carsten Nicolai), Tim Hecker, Terre Thaemlitz, Frank Bretschneider, SND (Mark Fell / Mat Steel), and Twerk (Shawn Hatfield). Liner notes by Marcus S. Kleiner and Marvin Chlada. Genres: Experimental, Glitch, Minimal. This roster overlaps significantly with Raster-Noton’s emerging stable.

Cathodeflower (RIT 06, 1999) and Residualism (RIT 19, 2001) — Kim Cascone

Kim Cascone placed two solo albums on Ritornell across the label’s active span, and also contributed liner notes to Maschinelle Strategeme and Selected Random Works. His Cathodeflower (1999) was a key early release; he programmed, mixed, and recorded it himself.

Microbes (rit 25, 2001) — Freiband

Dutch artist Frans de Waard under his Freiband alias. Credits include Freek Kinkelaar on guitar, bass, organ and drums, and John Conway (3) credited for “Artwork [Game Of Life]” — a conceptual nod to Conway’s cellular automaton. Mastered by Pieter Stellingwerff.

Elongated Pentagonal Pyramid (RIT 05, 1999) and Eisoptrophobia (RIT 21, 2001) — Akira Rabelais

Akira Rabelais placed two releases on Ritornell, one in the inaugural year and one in 2001. Eisoptrophobia is detailed in its credits: Rabelais played piano and handled computer work via his own Argeiphontes Lyre software alongside Adobe After Effects, SoundHack, Peak, and Pro Tools — an unusually transparent methodology for a release of this type.

Undirected / Dok (RIT 13, 2000) — Christophe Charles

Christophe Charles credited as composer (“All (de)compositions / Reconstructions By”), incorporating sampled source material from Oval and Henning Christiansen. HTML design by Harada Yasushi, suggesting a multimedia component. Cover by Alorenz.

Production Infrastructure

Design: Alorenz (Berlin) held near-total cover/artwork responsibility across the catalog — credited on at least 14 releases. This consistency gives Ritornell a unified visual identity unusual for a label of this size.

Production: The most recurring producer credit outside of self-produced work is Okko Bekker, who handled “Produktion” for all four Asmus Tietchens Menge-series albums. Dr. Atmo’s releases were co-produced with Andreas Linse. Stephan Mathieu produced his own Ritornell material.

Mastering: No single mastering engineer dominates; individual releases used Coolgate, Dean Roberts, Heimir Björgúlfsson (self-mastered), Pieter Stellingwerff, Peter Fleur, and others.

Liner Notes: Kim Cascone contributed liner notes to Maschinelle Strategeme (2000) and Selected Random Works (2000), giving him an editorial presence on the label beyond his two solo releases.

Connections

  • Mille Plateaux — parent label (ID: 270)
  • Achim Szepanski — operator of the Force Inc. constellation
  • Force Inc — sibling/parent operation
  • Raster-Noton — overlapping artist pool (Klangmaschine_Soundmachine features Alva Noto, Frank Bretschneider)
  • 12k — Taylor Deupree’s home label; Deupree appears on both Ritornell compilations and .N
  • Mego — Peter Rehberg’s label; Rehberg on Afternoon Tea
  • Asmus Tietchens — Hamburg, four-release series; key figure in German sound art lineage
  • Stephan Mathieu — three releases; went on to work with Staubgold and others
  • Kim Cascone — two releases plus liner notes; key figure in early 2000s glitch/microsound discourse
  • Sebastian Meissner — two releases under different monikers
  • Ekkehard Ehlers — compiled FrequencyLib; his own releases were on Staubgold

Open Questions / Research Queue

  • Catalog gap: no release identified for RIT 001, though RITORNELL CD1 (Cellosphere) may function as the inaugural release under a different numbering scheme
  • The Lights (RIT 003) and Autopoieses (RIT 08) are poorly documented; exact personnel unclear
  • Tripower by Crane A.K. (2008) is anomalous — five years after the label’s last prior release; no credits on Discogs. What prompted this late entry?
  • Relationship between Ritornell and the Staubgold label (both adjacent to the Frankfurt/Hamburg experimental axis) not fully characterized
