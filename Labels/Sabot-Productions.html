Overview

Sabot Productions is a Gainesville, Florida independent punk and hardcore label founded by Jordan Kleeman in 2000. It is best known as the early home of Against Me!, whose frontman Tom Gabel appears among its core roster alongside Kleeman himself, Rob McGregor, and Dustin Fridkin. The catalogue concentrates in the 2000s with 19 releases before extending into the 2010s with 12 more, and its stylistic range stretches from raw punk into acoustic and country-rock territory — a spread that broadly tracks Against Me!‘s own sonic arc across the decade.

Notes (web)

Against Me! departure from Sabot and catalogue rights

Departure circumstances. The move away from Sabot was not adversarial; it was a capacity issue. Sabot was a very small DIY operation and was not in a position to release a full-length album. When Against Me! recorded what became Reinventing Axl Rose, Kleeman himself brought the record to No Idea Records and pitched them to release it — the band members were reportedly too nervous to approach the label themselves. No Idea released the debut in March 2002. Against Me! then signed directly to Fat Wreck Chords for the second album (As the Eternal Cowboy, 2003). Throughout this transition Sabot continued operating and remained associated with the band’s early-catalogue material.

Catalogue rights — Sabot retains. Sabot Productions has conducted multiple reissues of the early Against Me! releases, which is consistent with Kleeman retaining the master rights. Documented reissues all released on Sabot:

  • Crime as Forgiven By CD repressed on Sabot, October 2005 (Punknews snippet: punknews.org/article/21819)
  • Crime as Forgiven By 7” repress on pink vinyl, Sabot, 2007 (Punknews snippet: punknews.org/article/33070)
  • The Sabot Productions website (sabotproductions.squarespace.com) continues to list Against Me!‘s early releases (Crime, Acoustic, High Pressure Low, Russian Spies / Occult Enemies) as sold-out catalogue items as of 2026-11-20

No press source or interview has been found that explicitly states the terms of catalogue ownership, and no rights dispute or transfer agreement has been publicly documented. The pattern of Sabot-issued reissues is the strongest available evidence that Kleeman retained rights; it is not a formal confirmation.

Name note — Jordan vs. Jason Kleeman. The brief identifies the founder as “Jason Kleeman”; however, every web source (Wikipedia, Punknews snippets) and the Discogs label profile consistently use “Jordan Kleeman.” This may be an error in the brief or in an upstream source. The Discogs field on this page already reads “Jordan Kleeman.” No source was found that uses “Jason.”
