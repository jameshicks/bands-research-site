Overview

2.13.61 is an American record label founded by Henry Rollins and named after his date of birth — February 13, 1961. Launched in 1990, it served primarily as a home for Rollins’ own spoken word recordings and Rollins Band material, while also releasing work by close collaborators including guitarist Chris Haskett, Suicide’s Alan Vega, and Endless Graphics. Output was heaviest in the 1990s with 11 releases, and the label has continued at a reduced pace through the 2000s, 2010s, and 2020s. It operates under the parent company 2.13.61, Inc. and has a budget-line sublabel, The 2.13.61 Cheap Seat Series.

Rollins and Alan Vega — Relationship and Label History

How they met

Rollins first encountered Suicide as a teenager around 1977 — aged approximately 16, he bought their debut LP for $2.99 alongside Ian MacKaye. Hearing “Frankie Teardrop” was a formative shock; Rollins has since called it “the single most intense song” he has ever heard. [source: Far Out Magazine; search summary citing Rollins’s own account]

The first personal meeting came on July 15, 1991 in New York City. Rollins tracked down Vega’s phone number through his agent, cold-called him, and asked if he could come over. Vega said yes; half an hour later Rollins was in his apartment — packed with paintings, sculptures, sketch pads, and notebooks of writing with barely room for Vega and his wife Liz Lamere. They spent approximately four hours together that day. Rollins later wrote that “within a few minutes, I knew I was going to be friends with Alan.” [source: LA Weekly — Rollins tribute piece, July 2016; Billboard]

Cripple Nation (1994, 2.13.61 — book)

Two summers after the 1991 meeting (c.1993), Rollins was living in NYC and would walk over to Vega and Lamere’s apartment regularly. He helped Vega assemble a manuscript of Vega’s writing and sketches — published in 1994 by 2.13.61 as Cripple Nation (ISBN 9781880985168). The 128-page paperback collects lyrics, prose, charcoal, and pen-and-ink illustrations from Vega’s Suicide-era notebooks. This was the direct product of their friendship and established 2.13.61 as a home for Vega’s non-Suicide output. [source: Goodreads; LA Weekly]

Dujang Prang (1995/1996)

Originally released in July 1995 on Musidisc (France). 2.13.61 licensed the album from Saturn Strip, Ltd. and issued their own edition in 1996 — the US release that appears in the label’s Discogs catalog. The Vega–Rollins friendship, and the existing 2.13.61 publishing relationship via Cripple Nation, made the label a natural US home for the album. [source: Discogs release pages r197724 (Musidisc 1995) and r955593 (2.13.61 1996); search summary]

Conflict note: Alan Vega.md (trawler entry) lists Dujang Prang as 1995 on Musidisc — this is the original French pressing. The 2.13.61 edition is a separate 1996 US release. Both are correct for their respective editions.

Murder One (archival 7”, co-released with In The Red)

A co-release between In The Red Recordings and 2.13.61, issued as a limited archival 7” (original pressing: 200 copies, sold out rapidly). The track — nine minutes and forty seconds, spread across two sides — was recorded by Vega and Liz Lamere in NYC. In The Red and 2.13.61 later reissued it in a new edition with two distinct picture sleeves and colored vinyl variants. This release is part of a broader partnership between the two labels producing limited archival 7” records. [source: In The Red Records]

Rollins on Vega’s death (2016)

When Vega died on July 16, 2016, Rollins published the family’s official statement on his website and dedicated a special broadcast of his KCRW radio show to Vega. The LA Weekly tribute piece he wrote was titled “Alan Vega Was One of the Few True Artists I’ve Ever Met.” He confirmed to Pitchfork that Vega had completed an unreleased album called It before his death: “There’s a finished new album called It. I heard part of it.” [source: LA Weekly; Exclaim!; KCRW]
