One Little Indian

UK independent record label, founded 1985. Discogs label ID: 311. Address (current): 34 Trinity Crescent, London SW17 7AE. Early address (1990): 250 York Road, London SW11.

On 10 June 2020 the label renamed itself One Little Independent Records (Discogs ID: 1850506) in response to complaints that the original name was racially insensitive. Both entities share the same catalog and artists; Discogs splits them at the name change date.

LC 3275.

Founders

Founded by Derek Birkett (Discogs ID: 100186) and guitarist Tim Kelly (ex-Flux Of Pink Indians). Birkett had been the bassist in Flux Of Pink Indians and also played in The Epileptics and The Licks. He went on to produce extensively for the label under his own name and as Colonel Blimp (3). The label grew directly out of the UK anarcho-punk scene centred around Flux Of Pink Indians and Southern Studios.

Label History

1985–1988: Anarcho-punk roots

The label launched with releases on the anarcho-punk continuum: Flux Of Pink Indians LPs Uncarved Block (1986) and The Fucking Cunts Treat Us Like Pricks (1986), early singles from A.R. Kane (“When You’re Sad,” 1986), and solo work from anarcho vocalist Annie Anxiety Bandez. Also on the early roster: The Cravats, The Very Things, and Loudspeaker. Derek Birkett produced the bulk of these early releases.

1988–1992: The Sugarcubes era and indie expansion

The Sugarcubes signed in 1987 and immediately elevated the label’s profile internationally. Their debut single “Birthday” (1987) and debut LP Life’s Too Good (1988) made the label a transatlantic name. Three Sugarcubes albums — Life’s Too Good (1988), Here Today, Tomorrow Next Week! (1989), Stick Around For Joy (1992) — and dozens of associated singles and international pressings dominated the label’s catalog through 1992.

Alongside The Sugarcubes the label built a strong British indie roster: Kitchens Of Distinction (five albums 1988–1994), The Heart Throbs, The Popinjays, Soul Family Sensation, Sleeping Dogs Wake, Finitribe, No-Man (progressive art-rock duo featuring Tim Bowness and Steven Wilson), Daisy Chainsaw (early home of Katie Jane Garside), Airstream, and Eskimos & Egypt.

The US connection: They Might Be Giants placed three releases on the label for UK/European distribution in 1989 (Don’t Let’s Start, Ana Ng, Lincoln).

1992–1995: The Shamen’s crossover and Björk’s solo debut

The Shamen had been on the label since 1989 and achieved a UK #1 in 1992 with “Ebeneezer Goode,” the label’s biggest commercial moment before Björk. Albums En-Tact (1990), Boss Drum (1992), Axis Mutatis (1995), and Hempton Manor (1996) gave the label sustained mainstream visibility. Derek Birkett produced The Shamen extensively.

Björk launched her solo career on One Little Indian with Debut (1993), produced by Nellee Hooper. The album sold over 4 million copies worldwide. A sequence of landmark albums followed: Post (1995), Homogenic (1997), Selmasongs (2000), Vespertine (2001), Medúlla (2004), Drawing Restraint 9 / Volta (2007), Biophilia (2011), Vulnicura (2015), Utopia (2017), Fossora (2022). Björk remained the label’s anchor artist and most commercially and critically significant act for thirty years.

Key Björk collaborators whose credits recur extensively across the label’s catalog: producer Nellee Hooper (Discogs: 11831), programmer/producer Marius de Vries (Discogs: 38208), producer/synth Mark Bell (Discogs: 167, of LFO), keyboardist/arranger Guy Sigsworth (Discogs: 13392), lyricist Sjón (Discogs: 149879), engineer/mixer Mandy Parnell (Discogs: 384377).

Visual identity for Björk’s One Little Indian output was anchored by two studios: Me Company (Paul White, Discogs: 547352) — dominant in the early solo era; and M/M (Paris) (Discogs: 768003) — dominant from Homogenic onward. Photography: Inez Van Lamsweerde & Vinoodh Matadin (Discogs: 1602997) shot extensively for Björk across this period. Fashion photographer Nick Knight (Discogs: 347680) and Jean-Baptiste Mondino (Discogs: 347682) also contributed significant sleeve photography.

The early-1990s roster also included Chumbawamba (punk-pop, five albums 1993–1996), Credit To The Nation (hip-hop, collaborated with Chumbawamba), Compulsion, and the release of Gling-Gló (1993) — a Björk jazz vocal album recorded under the name Björk Guðmundsdóttir with tríó Guðmundar Ingólfssonar.

Skunk Anansie joined in 1994, achieving significant UK chart success with Paranoid & Sunburnt (1995), Stoosh (1996), and Post Orgasmic Chill (1999). Skin (Deborah Dyer) as vocalist and Len Arran as principal songwriter.

1997–2004: Post-Sugarcubes era, Icelandic continuity, and Nude acquisition

Following The Sugarcubes’ dissolution One Little Indian maintained strong ties to the Icelandic music scene: Gling-Gló and a succession of Icelandic-language releases, including Magga Stína (1998) and Daníel Ágúst. Kukl’s archival releases (The Eye, Holidays In Europe) were issued in 2002, filling in the prehistory before The Sugarcubes.

After Nude Records went into voluntary liquidation in the early 2000s, One Little Indian acquired part of the Nude catalog. Several Nude artists continued on the label: Emiliana Torrini (whose Love In The Time Of Science 1999 had been on Nude; she continued with OLI through Me And Armini 2008), Sneaker Pimps (catalog), Cody ChesnuTT (The Headphone Masterpiece, 2002).

US-oriented signings expanded the roster in this period: Greg Dulli’s The Twilight Singers (Blackberry Belle 2003, Powder Burns 2006, Dynamite Steps 2010), Jesse Malin (The Fine Art Of Self Destruction 2002), Alabama 3 (from 1998 onward), Pernice Brothers, Black Box Recorder, Hot Snakes, Rocket From The Crypt.

Disco Inferno released Technicolour (compilation, 2004) on the label; their retrospective The 5 EPs followed in 2011 (TPLP1082CD).

2005–2015: Expanding international roster

Icelandic signing Ólöf Arnalds joined in the late 2000s. Samaris debuted in 2013. Ásgeir (In The Silence, 2013) became a major Icelandic crossover artist. Amiina released Kurr (2009). Sigur Rós catalog releases appeared on the label around 2005–2009.

UK indie-punk: The Computers, God Damn, Kill It Kid, Bad Breeding.

Singer-songwriters and folk artists: Polly Paulusma, Kathryn Williams, Marry Waterson, Astrid Williamson, Marc Carroll, Songdog, Dan Sartain.

The Paul McCartney side project The Fireman (McCartney + Youth) released Electric Arguments (2008) on the label.

2015–2022: Björk’s late catalog and rebranding

Vulnicura (2015) and Vulnicura Strings (2015), Utopia (2017), and Fossora (2022) continued Björk’s output on the label. The 2020 name change to One Little Independent was accompanied by continued catalog activity: Test Dept. reissues, Penny Rimbaud / Eve Libertine (ex-Crass), Crass archival releases, Poppy Ackroyd, Foxtrott, Fufanu, Manu Delago.
