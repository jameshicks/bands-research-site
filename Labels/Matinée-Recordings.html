Overview

Matinée Recordings is an independent pop label founded by James Tassos in Washington, DC in 1997, later relocating to Santa Barbara, California in 2002. Its catalogue of roughly 63 releases skews heavily toward the 2000s, when the label was most prolific, with a sustained run through the 2010s and continued releases into the 2020s. The roster draws primarily from indie pop — with acts like The Lucksmiths, Math And Physics Club, and Mark Monnone — spanning acoustic pop, post-punk, and synth-pop.

Notes (web)

Founding story

Matinée began in 1997 when Tassos, then working in Washington DC on affordable housing advocacy, heard Sweet William’s “Lovely Norman” and cold-emailed the band to propose releasing a 7”. He had no prior experience: “I had no idea what releasing a record meant, so I spoke to some people to learn how things are done.” The first release appeared in 1997; by 1998 the label had grown to three releases, with over twenty planned for 2001. During the label’s DC period, Tassos took a full year away from his day job to focus on it. (Pennyblack Music interview, via matineerecordings.com/pennyblack)

Relocation to Santa Barbara (2002)

The move was personal, not strategic. Tassos relocated because of “a great job offer for my wife Mary” and described it as “a bit of adventure moving across the country.” He was able to take the label with him because he works “on a consulting basis so can work from nearly anywhere.” They had “lived in DC for almost ten years” before the move. (In Love With These Times interview)

The Santa Barbara Independent (2003) corroborates: “Matinée settled into Santa Barbara last September when founder and entrepreneurial force Jimmy Tassos moved here from Washington, D.C.” The label had by then worked with 28 bands from seven countries, releasing 70 records. (Santa Barbara Independent)

Signing The Lucksmiths and Australian acts

Tassos’s own explanation: “I started working with the Lucksmiths, Sportique and Airport Girl because I loved their music and knew people from other labels who had released their records (Candle, Drive In, WIAIWYA, and Fortuna Pop!).” The connection was through the pre-existing indie pop label network — personal contacts at labels that had already worked with those acts — not through demos or direct outreach from the bands. Tassos singled out The Lucksmiths’ Staring At The Sky mini-album as possibly “Matinée’s finest release.” (Pennyblack Music interview, via matineerecordings.com/pennyblack)

Signing philosophy and aesthetic

The label was shaped by UK indie labels of the 1980s — Tassos cites Creation Records as formative: “Creation was the first indie that made me realize there was something important about label aesthetic.” The label specialises in the genre influenced by Sarah Records, Creation, K Records, and Slumberland. Tassos operates solo, has released as many as 20 records in a single year, and maintains tight quality control, having “turned down songs by bands on the label maybe five or six times.” He opposes “long wanky guitar solos, bleepy electronic garbage, unnecessarily long or repetitive songs, and uncredited samples.” (In Love With These Times interview; Santa Barbara Independent)

Some acts (e.g. Lovejoy, Slipslide, Harper Lee) contacted the label proactively by letter or demo. Others were discovered live — local DC band Bella Vista was found at a club show covering an obscure English band. (Pennyblack Music interview, via matineerecordings.com/pennyblack)
