Tiger Style

Discogs ID: 4543
Location: 149 Wooster St., 4th Floor, New York, NY 10012 (historical)
Active: 1998–2004; revived 2024–present (under Numero Group)
Parent: Numero Group (ID 475394)
Total releases: 117

New York indie rock label active 1998–2004, releasing approximately 40 albums and a dozen EPs across its original run. Founded in lower Manhattan (SoHo) by Ari Sass, Matty Wishnow, and Mike Treff — three principals who came out of the music industry (Sass and Wishnow had previously worked together at Elektra Records). Sass and Wishnow simultaneously co-founded the online music retailer Insound.com (launched March 1, 1999, with a third co-founder, Christian Anthony); Tiger Style served as Insound’s in-house imprint. Mike Treff had previously run Linkwork Records. The label became a focal point for early-2000s post-rock, indie-electronic, and slowcore crossover acts. Went on hiatus 2004; ownership passed to/remained with Insound (acquired by Alternative Distribution Alliance, a Warner Music Group subsidiary, in January 2008; Insound ceased operations in 2015). Revived in 2024 through a partnership with Numero Group — explaining why some roster artists (Her Space Holiday, Ida (3)) show Tiger Style releases through 2024–2025.

Era by Era

1998–2000 — Launch and early catalog

First release: The Letter E EP (TS-001, 1998). The label quickly assembled a core of post-rock and indie-electronic acts: Tristeza (Dream Signals In Full Circles, TS-010, 2000 — recorded by Dave Trumfio at King Size Sound Labs, Chicago), The Mercury Program (From The Vapor Of Gasoline, TS-003, 2000), and Her Space Holiday (Home Is Where You Hang Yourself, TS-007, 2000). Ida (3) also debuted on the label in 2000 (Will You Find Me, TS005). The Lilys / Aspera Ad Astra split (TS-002, 2000) was an early notable pairing. A label sampler CD circulated in Winter 2000.

2001–2002 — Peak period

The label’s most concentrated creative period. The American Analog Set delivered Know By Heart (TS-018, 2001) — the label’s defining release. The Mercury Program expanded to quartet and released All The Suits Began To Fall Off (TS-013, 2001) and A Data Learn The Language (TS-029, 2002). The American Analog Set’s Updates remix EP (TS-026, 2002) extended the Know By Heart period. Broken Spindles (Joel Peterson of The Faint) placed their self-titled IDM/electronic album (TS-030, 2002) — engineered with AJ and Mike Mogis of the Omaha scene. Rye Coalition (Jersey City NJ, post-hardcore) placed their first Tiger Style releases. 764-Hero (Seattle, TS-020, 2002) and Tara Jane O’Neil / Daniel Littleton split (Music For A Meteor Shower, TS-028, 2002) added further range.

2003–2004 — Final stretch and closure

The American Analog Set closed out with Promise Of Love (TS-047, 2003). Lucero (Memphis, country-punk) placed That Much Further West (TS-048, 2003), a broader stylistic move. The Appleseed Cast placed Two Conversations (TS045, 2003). Rye Coalition closed with Jersey Girls (TS-043, 2003). Les Savy Fav placed a split (TS-039, 2003). The label ceased operations in 2004.

2024–present — Numero Group revival

Announced April 2, 2024: Numero Group partnership absorbing Tiger Style’s near-complete catalog (40+ albums, ~12 EPs) into Numero’s digital distribution ecosystem. Physical LP reissues and box set campaigns launched starting 2025. Impetus: Ida (3) reunited at Numero’s “Numero Twenty” anniversary event in 2023 (first performance since 2010), and Numero had already had success with The American Analog Set. Ari Sass stated Numero “has an impressive track record of breathing fresh life into important bodies of music”; Ken Shipley (Numero co-founder) cited “successes with Duster, Unwound, and The American Analog Set” as positioning for the Tiger Style reintroduction. [source: shorefire.com/releases/entry/numero-group-announces-expansive-new-partnership-with-tiger-style-records]

The American Analog Set — label cornerstone

The American Analog Set (Austin TX, formed 1995 Fort Worth) placed the label’s most significant releases. Know By Heart (2001) was self-recorded and mixed by the band; mastered by Jerry Tubb. Guest appearances by Ben Gibbard (Death Cab for Cutie), Munaf Rayani (Explosions in the Sky), and Laura Krause. Final Tiger Style LP Promise Of Love (2003) also self-recorded. Primary designer: Craig McCaffrey (band member).

Members: Andrew Kenny (guitar/vocals), Craig McCaffrey (bass, also design), Shawn O’Keefe, Tom Hoff, Sean Ripple, Mark Smith (18), Jesse Lee (13).

The Mercury Program — most-represented artist

The Mercury Program (formed Gainesville FL, 1997 as a trio; became quartet when Whit Travisano joined). Signature sound: guitar + vibraphone + electric piano + drumset, no bass guitar initially. Notable for crediting themselves as engineers with intentionally deadpan technical language (“Level Adjustments And All Other Preparations For Production Performed By”), and their drum credit as the “Triangular Department.” Produced by Hank McAfee (“Binary Conversion And Anger Management By”). Primary recording engineer: Andy Baker (4) (12 releases).

Members: Tom Reno (guitar, vocals, bells, also design), Dave Lebleu (drums, vibraphone), Sander Travisano (bass), Whit Travisano (electric piano, vibraphone — joined post-debut).

  • Three distinct Tiger Style releases (the 4th Discogs entry is a duplicate pressing with no year):
    1. Christmas With Satan (TS-038, 2002) — 3-inch CD single, released November 19, 2002. 20th-anniversary reissue of the original 1982 recording. Two tracks: “Christmas With Satan” (10 min, full restored version — original commercial release had truncated Chance’s lengthy intro; this restores it) and “The Devil Made Me Do It” (from his 1983 LP Flaming Demonics). “Christmas With Satan” features moody piano intro (~3 min) then funk rhythms layered with dissonant fragments of traditional holiday songs; narrative: a suicide victim attends Lucifer’s holiday party. [sources: discogs.com/release/107016; rateyourmusic.com/release/single/james-chance/christmas-with-satan/; seattleweekly.com/music/christmas-with-satan/]
    2. Irresistible Impulse (TIG 60037, 2003) — 4-disc box set; material spans late 1970s–1980s (Contortions/James White lineups); musicians include George Scott (2), Don Christensen, Jody Harris, Adele Bertei, Pat Place — all No Wave scene veterans. Clear reissue compilation.
    3. Sax Education (TS-053, 2004) — compiled and liner notes by James Chance himself; compilation format confirmed.
  • All three are reissue/archival releases of No Wave and post-No Wave material.
  • One confirmed 2025 Tiger Style release: Home Is Where You Hang Yourself (NUM990, 2025, Numero Group / Tiger Style). This is a reissue of the 2000 debut album (original TS-007). Produced for reissue by Ken Shipley and Rob Sevier (both Numero Group principals). Mastered by Jessica Thompson. Design by Egelnick+Webb. Photography by Keely Chanteloup (same Keely credited on original Tiger Style releases). No new Her Space Holiday material on Tiger Style 2024–2025.
  • Karla Schickele (Discogs ID 418639) is a confirmed member of Ida (3) (Discogs ID 536920) — she appears in the Ida (3) member list alongside Daniel Littleton, Elizabeth Mitchell, Jean Cook, Michael Littleton, Rick Lassiter, Ruth Keating, and Storey Littleton. Also a member of Beekeeper and The Ida Retsin Family. Her Tiger Style solo releases are primarily 2001–2002: New Problems (TS-016, 2001), k. / Low split (TS-015, 2001), Those Girls (TS-015, 2001), Goldfish (TS-34, 2002), Bridges, Squares / I Wouldn’t Mind (TS-025, 2002), plus the Ida (3) Will You Find Me (TS005, 2000) and The Braille Night (2001, Tiger Style). She appears on Ida (3) releases as bass/vocals/piano player.
