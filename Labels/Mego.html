Mego

Discogs ID: 2440
Based: Vienna, Austria (Meidling district); Berlin satellite studio
Active: late 1994 – end of 2005
Relaunched as: Editions Mego (from January 2006)
Sublabels: Angelika Köhlermann (ID: 3421), Falsch (ID: 7511)

Overview

Mego was one of the defining experimental electronic labels of the post-digital era, based in the Meidling workers’ district of Vienna, Austria. Active from late 1994 to the end of 2005, it released 116 catalog items (pressings and editions included) across its original run. The label’s sound — confrontational glitch, noise, digital-process music, and laptop experimentalism — placed it at the center of the late-1990s/early-2000s Viennese scene and made it internationally influential in the canon of what would be called “post-digital” or “lowercase” electronics.

Mego was founded by Ramon Bauer, Peter Meininger, and Andreas Pieper (Pieper was Berlin-based, operating a Mego satellite studio there). Bauer and Meininger had previously co-run Mainframe (2), one of Austria’s earliest techno labels, with releases by Ilsa Gold, Kirlian, and Elin. By 1995, Pita (Peter Rehberg) joined the label crew and Peter Meininger departed. From that point Bauer and Rehberg were the two Vienna-based operators, with Pieper’s Berlin studio in support.

The label’s physical address was Ruckergasse 10/21+22, A-1120 Wien — the postal code for Meidling, a traditionally working-class district. The label closed formally at the end of 2005 and was relaunched in January 2006 as Editions Mego, initially under Rehberg’s direction.

History

Founding and Early Years (1994–1996)

The first release, MEGO 001 (Fridge Trax, 1995), was a shared debut between General Magic and Pita — the two-act split neatly encapsulates the label’s dual personality in its earliest phase: General Magic’s kinetic, club-adjacent electronics alongside Pita’s more abrasive material. MEGO 002 (Die Mondlandung, 1995) paired Elin with General Magic.

MEGO 003 (UFO Beobachtungen ‘93–‘95, 1995) came from Stützpunkt Wien 12 — a release closely tied to the label’s Viennese neighborhood identity (Wien 12 is Meidling). Fennesz appeared with MEGO 004 (Instrument, 1995), his first label appearance and an early marker of the guitar-into-laptop approach he would develop through the decade.

MEGO 005 (I L.O.V.E. You, 1995) by DJ DSL became one of the label’s biggest-selling releases (34 pressings noted on Discogs) — an anomaly in the catalog, but evidence that Mego was not programmatically anti-pop in its earliest period. MEGO 008 and MEGO 017 (both Farmers Manual, 1996) introduced one of the label’s most relentlessly conceptual acts: FM’s data-process approach to sound made them the label’s most radical early proposition.

Glitch Canon and International Recognition (1997–2001)

MEGO 016 (Hotel Paral.lel, 1997) — Fennesz — became the album through which much of the outside world first encountered the label; it pushed guitar processing into long-arc digital impressionism. MEGO 020 (Plays, 1998) extended Fennesz’s presence and was later marketed with a now-famous T-shirt edition (mego 020t).

MEGO 022 (Ich Schnitt Mich In Den Finger, 1997) — credited to Masami Akita, Reiko Azuma, Russell Haswell, and Tetsuo Sakaibara — signaled Mego’s ambition to connect the Viennese scene with Tokyo noise and international underground electronics.

MEGO 031 (The Magic Sound Of Fenn O’Berg, 1999) launched the Fenn O’Berg trio: Fennesz, Jim O’Rourke, and Peter Rehberg, credited as Fennesz / O’Rourke / Rehberg. It is one of the most celebrated releases in the live laptop-improv idiom.

MEGO 035 (Endless Summer, 2001) — Fennesz — is the label’s most canonized record. A CD of eight tracks blending guitar noise with melodic ambient construction, it became the flagship statement of Mego’s aesthetic internationally. Cover by Tina Frank; mastered by Street Romance; photography by Hertha Hurnaus.

MEGO 037 (Asuma, 2001) — Ilpo Väisänen — appeared the same year and is among the most intense pieces in the catalog: dense, grinding, low-frequency industrial electronics. Cover by Tina Frank; photos credited to Ilpo himself.

MEGO 050 (I’m Happy, And I’m Singing, And A 1, 2, 3, 4, 2001) — Jim O’Rourke — continued Mego’s relationship with the Chicago improviser/producer. MEGO 054 (The Return Of Fenn O’Berg, 2002) was the Fenn O’Berg second release, again credited to Fennesz / O’Rourke / Rehberg; artwork by Chicks On Speed.

MEGO 053 (Sheer Hellish Miasma, 2002) — Kevin Drumm — is one of the label’s most extreme noise documents, guitar/tape/synthesizer collage of near-unlistenable density.

MEGO 044 (Sun Pandämonium, 2003) — Hecker — is the key Hecker album in the Mego catalog; a dense sinusoid construction, cover co-designed by Florian Hecker and Tina Frank; mastered by Denis Blackham.

Final Phase and Closure (2004–2005)

The last numbered releases cluster in the MEGO 066–079 range. MEGO 075 (Resurrection River, 2005) — Vainio Väisänen Vega (credited as VVV: Mika Vainio, Ilpo Väisänen, and Alan Vega) — was one of the label’s final statements before closure. Written-by credits are shared equally by the three members; Tina Frank contributed sleeve design. Mastered by Denis Blackham.

MEGO 076 (0397 Post-Pop, 2005) — CoH — was among the last releases. The final numbered entry in the original Mego sequence is Mego 079 (Untitled, 2005).

Ramon Bauer’s role and Discogs credits (ID: 294843) show him as performer and technician across several releases. The label’s formal address on releases identifies him as operational through the end. Andreas Pieper’s Berlin studio role is documented in the label profile but his Discogs artist page (ID: 805676) is sparse. Peter Meininger (ID: 125842) departed by 1995 and is not credited on later releases.

Visual Identity

The label’s visual program was overwhelmingly the work of Tina Frank (Discogs ID: 316348), credited on 14 releases for artwork/design and on a further 25 for covers/graphics — making her the most consistent creative presence on Mego after the musicians themselves. Her approach — typographic clarity, fragmented digital imaging, synthetic color fields — became inseparable from the label’s identity. Secondary photography credits go to Hertha Hurnaus and Heinrich Peiszer. Frank Scheikl and the graphic design unit Inwirements also appear across several releases.

Chicks On Speed contributed distinctive photographic and collage work to the Fenn O’Berg releases (MEGO 031 and MEGO 054).

Engineering and Mastering

The label relied on two mastering engineers above all others:

  • Christoph Amann (ID: 367202) — mastered 12 releases, the single most-credited engineer in the catalog
  • Denis Blackham (ID: 294328) — mastered 10 releases; responsible for several of the late-period key albums including Sun Pandämonium and Resurrection River

Martin Siewert (ID: 121023) mixed and mastered several releases. Rashad Becker appeared on 4 releases for mastering and lacquer cutting (post-mastering; not creative).

Sub-labels

Angelika Köhlermann (ID: 3421)

A Mego sub-label founded by Gerhard Potuznik and Tex Rubinowitz of Mäuse, described as coming from “both the Cheap and Mego dynasties.” The brief was to release music “unrepresented in the current electronic music scene.” Releases include Hikashu (4 Trck Bootlg, ak002, 1998), Queen of Japan (Nightlife In Tokyo, ak009, 2001), and G.D. Luxxe (Make, ak024, 2005). The sub-label continued activity past Mego’s closure.

Falsch (ID: 7511)

Operated 2000–2005 by Florian Hecker (aka Hecker) and Oswald Berthold (aka Pxp, ID: 98139). Billed as “hypermusic on purpose.” Releases include Mäuse (Mäuse, fb02, 1999), Kim Cascone (Pulsar Studies 1–20, fb12, 1999), CoH (Hide & Seek, fb42, 2001), and Satanstornade (Live At The Neils Yard Rough Trade Shop London, fb20, 2000).
