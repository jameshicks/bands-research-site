Overview

Founded by Henry Rollins and named for his birth date of February 13, 1961, 2.13.61 Records operates as the primary imprint of 2.13.61, Inc., with a sublabel 213CD. The catalogue of roughly 50 releases is concentrated in the 1990s and 2000s, spanning Alternative Rock, Hard Rock, Punk, Spoken Word, and Audiobook — a range that mirrors Rollins’ own output as a musician and spoken-word artist. Beyond Rollins and his band, the roster extends to Nick Cave and Rowland S. Howard, pointing to ties across the broader post-punk world.

Notes (web) — Cave, Howard, and the Birthday Party connection

Personal friendship: Rollins meets Cave and Howard, 1983

Henry Rollins first encountered The Birthday Party in person on 30 March 1983 at the Roxy in Los Angeles, during Black Flag’s US touring period. He later recalled deliberately standing in front of Rowland S. Howard during the set: “I stood in front of Rowland to get all that crazy guitar. I have a tape of the show, it was really strong, amazing really.” (The Quietus, The Death of the Birthday Party; The Quietus, The Strange World of Rowland S. Howard)

That same year, Cave and Rollins met properly at a Minutemen gig at Club Lingerie, Los Angeles, in March 1983, bonding over shared battle scars from punk shows. Rollins became what Cave later called a “partner in crime” during Cave’s chaotic Los Angeles years in the mid-1980s. (Far Out Magazine) Cave described Rollins to The Independent in 1997 as someone who “used to come around the house and do push-ups on our living-room floor.” (The Quietus, Birthday Party interview)

The friendship was mutual and deep — when Cave and Rollins crashed an Australian Consulate reception in Hollywood in July 1984, Rollins documented the evening in his tour diary (later published in Get In The Van). (Far Out Magazine)

Howard on Rollins’ own releases

Howard contributed guitar to the track “Alienation” on Rollins’ spoken-word album A Short Walk on a Long Pier (originally a 1,000-copy cassette, 1985/1988; reissued vinyl Lone Wolf Records 1989; reissued on 2.13.61 in 2006 paired with Big Ugly Mouth). The collaborator on the track was Chris Haskett of the Rollins Band alongside Howard. (Wikipedia: Short Walk on a Long Pier; rowland-s-howard.com discography)

Howard also contributed to Haskett’s solo record Language (1995, cat. 213CD02), co-writing and playing guitar on four tracks: “Spider Mother”, “Mr. Twisty Wants His Car Wreck”, “Days In A Lifeboat”, and “Invisible Western”. This was a direct 2.13.61 Records release, not a reissue. (rowland-s-howard.com discography)

How the Birthday Party catalog came to 2.13.61

Rollins and Rick Rubin co-founded Infinite Zero Archive (c. 1994), a reissue sublabel under Rubin’s American Recordings / Warner Bros, dedicated to releasing “underground classic” albums on CD for the first time. When Warner dropped the venture in the late 1990s, Rollins retained ownership or licensing rights of records acquired through Infinite Zero. The four Birthday Party reissues issued on 2.13.61 in 1997 (Prayers on Fire, Hee-Haw, Junkyard, Mutiny / The Bad Seed EP) appear to have come through this pipeline. In a 2000 interview, Rollins stated: “I’ll be putting out the Birthday Party stuff, which was already put out once by 2.13.61, in April” — indicating a second round of reissues was already planned, and confirming the original 1997 run was his own prior release. (Ink 19 interview, March 2000; Wikipedia: 2.13.61)

Rollins specifically praised the final Birthday Party recordings: “I think Bad Seed and Mutiny! are ultimate records. They are just incredible. I don’t see how you top them.” (The Quietus, Birthday Party interview) The reissue program was thus a direct expression of long-standing personal admiration rooted in the early 1980s post-punk touring circuit encounter.
