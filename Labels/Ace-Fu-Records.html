Overview

Ace Fu Records is an independent label based in New York City, founded in 1997. Though it began with a handful of releases in the late 1990s, the label’s catalogue flourished in the 2000s, accumulating roughly 31 releases spanning indie rock, art rock, experimental music, and traces of hardcore. Ace Fu is best known for releasing records by Pinback and Rob Crow, both central figures in San Diego’s indie scene, alongside acts such as Annuals and Tom Zinser. Its output tapered off after the 2000s, leaving its corporate structure and any distribution arrangements undocumented.

Founding and Origin Story

Predecessor label: Air King Alliance (AKA), 1997

Eric Speck — who had worked at WFMU radio during high school, served as promotion and program director at WMUC-FM in college, and later booked shows at the New York rock venue Brownies — started a label called Air King Alliance (AKA) at the close of 1997. Its inaugural release was a split 7” pairing Chisel (featuring Ted Leo) with The Van Pelt (featuring Chris Leo), catalog number AKA 001. Speck self-financed those early releases — roughly five 7-inches and one full-length CD — through day jobs, credit cards, and later contributions from a co-runner referred to in a 2002 KindaMuzik profile only as “Ben” (surname not disclosed), who brought in programming income from his own day job. [source: lambgoat.com/labels/474/ace-fu-records/; kindamuzik.net/label/ace-fu/ace-fu/1417/]

Rebrand as Ace Fu Records, 1998

After a period in Portland, Oregon, Speck returned to the New York/New Jersey area and rebranded the operation as Ace Fu Records — the name came from a t-shirt Speck owned bearing a kung fu graphic that read “Ace Fu.” [source: kindamuzik.net/label/ace-fu/ace-fu/1417/] The label established its home office in South Williamsburg, Brooklyn. By the time of the rebrand, Speck was also working as a talent buyer at Brownies (2000–2002, when the venue closed) while running the label, and held a concurrent A&R role at Island Records. He also later did stints at Knitting Factory Records as a label manager. [source: acefu.net/staff_popup/popup.php?ename=eric+speck; radiofreebrooklyn.org/rfbhost-profile-eric-speck/]

How a New York label ended up with Pinback

Pinback were a San Diego duo — Armistead Burwell Smith IV (of Three Mile Pilot) and Rob Crow (of Thingy and Heavy Vegetable) — who began recording in 1998. The 14-song debut was initially lined up for release on San Diego’s Vinyl Communications; interest in the band surged after a showcase at the North by Northwest music festival tied to a Tim-Kerr Records presentation; the resulting label bidding war created contractual complications that held the debut album in limbo for nearly a year. When those obligations were resolved, Pinback signed with Ace Fu and the eponymous debut was released in October 1999. [source: wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinback_(album); music.fandom.com/wiki/Pinback (snippet)]

Notes on the Tim/Kerr dispute (web research, 2026-10-07)

Every available secondary source (Wikipedia article on the album, the fandom wiki biography, AllMusic snippet) uses the same phrasing: Tim/Kerr ran the North by Northwest showcase at which Pinback performed; the bidding war was triggered by the reaction to that showcase. The consistent formulation is “a Tim/Kerr label showcase” — i.e., Tim/Kerr curated or presented the showcase slot, not that Tim/Kerr was itself one of the bidding labels.

Supporting circumstantial evidence: Tim/Kerr Records shut down altogether in spring 1999 (per its Wikipedia article and the Willamette Week profile of Thor Lindsay, wweek.com, 2017-07-25). A label weeks or months from closure would be a weak bidder; the showcase-organiser reading is therefore more plausible than the bidder reading. No source found names Tim/Kerr as having made an offer.

The specific identity of the other labels in the bidding war is not stated in any source located. The pre-showcase arrangement was with Vinyl Communications (San Diego). “Former obligations” resolved before signing with Ace Fu likely refers to that Vinyl Communications commitment; no other labels named. [sources: music.fandom.com/wiki/Pinback (snippet, fandom wiki — treat as low-confidence); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim/Kerr (as of 2026-10-07); wweek.com/music/2017/07/25/thor-lindsay-heard-the-future-of-portland-and-he-put-it-on-record/]

A second factor that cemented the Ace Fu/Pinback relationship was exposure through Napster: Pinback were featured in Napster’s “discover music” promotion, which drove significant underground attention to the band around the time of Blue Screen Life (2001, ACE 015). The KindaMuzik profile credits Eric Speck and “Ben” with bringing both Pinback and Ex Models to the label. [source: kindamuzik.net/label/ace-fu/ace-fu/1417/]

The San Diego connection was therefore opportunistic rather than structural — Speck was a New York indie-scene operator (Brownies, Knitting Factory, WFMU) who happened to sign the most visible act emerging from that city’s post-emo indie scene at the moment they became available after a label dispute. Ace Fu went on to release three Pinback albums (the self-titled debut, Loro EP, and Blue Screen Life) as well as releases by other Rob Crow projects, drawing further San Diego-adjacent acts into its orbit.

Hiatus

The label went on indefinite hiatus in 2007. Speck subsequently worked in advertising technology and co-managed Pet Shop Bar, a Jersey City venue, while hosting a music show on Radio Free Brooklyn. [source: radiofreebrooklyn.org/rfbhost-profile-eric-speck/]
