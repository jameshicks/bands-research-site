Oct 30, 20264 min read

Factory Benelux

Founded: Brussels, 1980 Active: 1980–1988 (original run); 1990–91 (CD reissues under Crépuscule); 2012–present (revival) Catalog prefix: FBN (original); revived 2012 by LTM Recordings / James Nice Operated under: Les Disques du Crépuscule Parent relationship: Sister imprint of Factory Records

Origin

Factory Benelux was created as a direct consequence of Tony Wilson’s visit to Brussels on 26 April 1980, when A Certain Ratio, Section 25, and Eric Random performed at Plan K (the former sugar refinery venue that Michel Duval and Annik Honoré were running on Rue de Manchester, Brussels). Wilson struck an agreement with Duval and Honoré: Factory acts would release Benelux-territory material through a joint imprint operating from Brussels.

The label operated under the aegis of Les Disques du Crépuscule — sharing premises, staff, and Benoît Hennebert’s design infrastructure. Factory Benelux and Crépuscule were legally separate imprints but practically a single operation.

The first Crépuscule precursor publication was the fanzine Plein Soleil (June 1980). The first Factory Benelux release proper was A Certain Ratio’s “Shack Up” 7” (August 1980, FBN 1 / TWI 004) — carrying a joint Factory Benelux / Les Disques du Crépuscule identity. The early FBN catalog doubled as TWI numbers:

Roster

Factory Benelux functioned in two modes: (1) releasing “spare” or live recordings by Factory artists for the Benelux market, and (2) signing exclusive acts that Wilson’s Factory had no interest in releasing in the UK but which fitted the aesthetic.

Key artists:

  • A Certain Ratio — first FBN release; live and alternative recordings
  • Swamp Children — Taste What’s Rhythm 12” (FBN 16, 1982) and So Hot LP (FBN 21, 1982); their only LP
  • The Durutti Column (Vini Reilly) — LC (FBN 2 / TWI 005)
  • Section 25 — Always Now (FBN 3 / TWI 006); one of the most consistent FBN artists
  • New Order — Benelux single releases
  • Crispy Ambulance — Manchester; Factory-adjacent; substantial FBN catalog
  • The Wake — Scottish post-punk; FBN and later Factory proper
  • The Names — Brussels; appeared on FBWL and early Crépuscule; exclusive to Benelux network
  • Minny Pops — Dutch electronic post-punk; FBN-exclusive
  • Stockholm Monsters — Manchester; Factory Benelux and Factory
  • Cabaret Voltaire — Benelux releases
  • Crawling Chaos — Newcastle post-punk/experimental; The Gas Chair LP (FAC BN.6 / FBN 6, 1982) — their sole FBN release, illustrated by Denyse Willem

Structure and Relationship with Factory

The relationship was not a standard licensing deal. Factory donated “spare” recordings — live tapes, alternative mixes, sessions — to the Benelux operation rather than paying advances or royalties in the conventional sense. Wilson’s philosophy of artist ownership applied: Factory Benelux, like Factory itself, operated without conventional contracts.

The Crépuscule side brought in acts from outside the Factory circle entirely (Tuxedomoon, Gavin Bryars, Michael Nyman, Malaria!, etc.) — Factory Benelux remained strictly Factory-connected.

Dormancy and CD Era (1988–1991)

New releases ceased in 1988. Les Disques du Crépuscule retained the FBN name for CD reissues of key catalog titles in 1990–91: The Names, New Order Benelux material, Crispy Ambulance.

2012 Revival

In 2012, James Nice of LTM Recordings revived Factory Benelux — with the explicit blessing of Michel Duval and Annik Honoré. Nice had worked at Les Disques du Crépuscule in Brussels in the late 1980s and was uniquely positioned to steward both imprints. New releases from 2012 onward:

  • Section 25 — new material
  • The Wake — new material (including a remastered vinyl reissue of Here Comes Everybody)
  • The Names — reissues and new material
  • Mark Reeder — Berlin-based musician and producer; Factory-connected (he was Factory’s man in Berlin in the early 1980s)
  • Blurt — Sheffield-based post-punk jazz group led by Ted Milton; performed at the Shadowplayers book launch
  • Quando Quango — Manchester dance/post-punk group (Mike Pickering’s early project, pre-Haçienda DJing career)

Factory Benelux and Crépuscule were revived simultaneously and remain organizationally intertwined under Nice’s curation.

Connections

  • Les Disques du Crépuscule — parent label and shared operation; FBN ran under Crépuscule’s aegis 1980–88
  • Factory Records — founding relationship; Wilson/Duval/Honoré agreement; Factory donated recordings
  • LTM Recordings — James Nice revived FBN in 2012
  • A Certain Ratio — first FBN release; also appeared at Plan K
  • Cabaret Voltaire — Benelux releases
  • Plan K (Brussels) — the venue where Wilson met Duval and Honoré
