Merge Records

Independent record label. Chapel Hill, North Carolina. Founded 1989.

Co-founded by Mac McCaughan and Laura Ballance, both of Superchunk. One of the most significant American independent labels; built its reputation on Chapel Hill indie rock and expanded nationally. Co-released Mission Of Burma’s final album Unsound (2012) with Fire Records (UK). Other key roster: Neutral Milk Hotel, Arcade Fire, Spoon, Caribou, Lambchop, Destroyer, Fucked Up.

Arcade Fire

Discogs ID: 7496. Home label for Arcade Fire from Funeral (2004) through Reflektor (2013 co-release with Sonovox). The label’s biggest commercial breakthrough — The Suburbs (2010) won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year, the first Merge release to do so. After Reflektor, Arcade Fire moved to Columbia / Sonovox for their major-label period.

Deal scope and timeline (web research)

Signing (late 2003 / early 2004). Merge signed Arcade Fire after receiving a demo from Win Butler. The process was slow enough that the band nearly signed to a small Montreal label first; Mac McCaughan later said “it may sound like we decided quickly but it actually took us a while.” The band visited Merge’s Chapel Hill office (March or April 2004 per the Consequence piece) and committed to signing the next morning. (consequence.net, 2014)

Territorial scope. Merge handled North America only from the start. Funeral was released on Merge in Canada and the US (September 2004) and on Rough Trade Records in the UK (February 2005). This split-territory arrangement continued through Neon Bible (2007) and The Suburbs (2010), with UMG/Mercury handling international releases on those albums. Reflektor (2013) was released on Merge in the US and on Sonovox internationally. (musicbusinessworldwide.com, 2017; Wikipedia – Reflektor)

UMG layer. According to Music Business Worldwide, Jason Iley signed Arcade Fire to Universal Music Group’s Mercury imprint in 2005 (following Funeral’s success), which accounts for the international UMG releases on Neon Bible, The Suburbs, and Reflektor. Merge remained the US label throughout. No public source specifies whether Merge’s North America deal was formally exclusive or structured as a first-look/option deal. (musicbusinessworldwide.com, 2017)

Deal length / expiry. No specific contract length has been publicly disclosed. By 2015, band manager Scott Rodger stated: “We’re out of our deal, they have no deal, plus most of their catalogue has reverted back to them.” This suggests the Merge (and UMG) deal(s) had a finite term with reversion clauses, and that by mid-2015 the band had recaptured rights. (musicbusinessworldwide.com, 2017)

Sony / Columbia move (2017). Arcade Fire signed a worldwide deal with Sony Music in 2017 covering all four prior studio albums plus the forthcoming Everything Now (released on Columbia Records, July 2017). This was the first time their entire catalogue sat under a single global label. (variety.com, 2017)

What remains unknown. No public source confirms: (a) whether the Merge deal was explicitly exclusive for North America or merely a territory licence; (b) how many album options it contained; (c) whether there was any formal renegotiation between Funeral and Reflektor; (d) the exact date the Merge term ended.
