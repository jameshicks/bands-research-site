Overview

Discos Esplendor Geometrico was a Spanish industrial label founded by Gabriel Riaza and Andrés Noarbe that issued 22 albums between 1985 and 1990 before being rebranded as Geometrik Records. Its output concentrated in experimental, industrial, and ambient territory, with Esplendor Geométrico (the band sharing the founders’ project name) at its centre, joined by solo work from Arturo Lanz, Anton Ignorant, and the Hamburg-based Asmus Tietchens. The cassette arm EGK ran parallel to the main catalogue. A single release dating to the 2020s suggests at least one archival reissue under the original imprint.

Notes (web) — Tietchens–Discos EG connection

Research question: How did Asmus Tietchens (Hamburg) come to release on Discos Esplendor Geometrico — direct relationship with the founders, or via a European network intermediary?

What was searched and checked (2026-11-10):

  • Tietchens interview, EST zine #2 (1991), media.hyperreal.org/zines/est/intervs/tietchen.html — URL returned empty response (dead); could not retrieve.
  • Tietchens interview, OndaRock (“The aesthetics of abstraction”), ondarock.com — fetched. Discos EG appears only in the discography list; no discussion of how the connection formed. ondarock.com
  • Tietchens interview, Chain D.L.K., chaindlk.com — fetched. Only passing mention: “my first post-‘Sky’ records have been released on industrial labels like United Diaries and Esplendor Geométrico.” No mechanism given. chaindlk.com
  • Tietchens interview, Psychedelic Baby Magazine (2021), psychedelicbabymag.com — fetched. No mention of Discos EG, Spanish labels, or Madrid scene at all. psychedelicbabymag.com
  • Tietchens bio, kunstradio.at — fetched. Groups Discos EG with Hamster Records, Multimood, A-Mission as labels of the “international Industrial scene” active through 1989. No mechanism named. kunstradio.at
  • Esplendor Geometrico interview, Industrial Complexx — fetched. No mention of Tietchens. Did note that Esplendor Geometrico’s early 7” “Moscú Está Helado” was released on the German label Ata Tak (run by Der Plan). This is the clearest documented German connection for the band pre-label, but no link to Tietchens established. industrialcomplexx.com
  • Esplendor Geometrico interview, Peek-A-Boo Magazine (2012) — fetched. No mention of Tietchens or German label network. peek-a-boo-magazine.be
  • Esplendor Geometrico interview, Secret Thirteen — fetched. No mention of Tietchens. secretthirteen.org
  • 35 Years of Esplendor Geométrico, The Attic Mag — fetched. Labels Tietchens and Conrad Schnitzler together as “forward-thinking” releases alongside De Fabriek, Macromassa, etc. Notes that in Europe the label worked with Daft Records (Belgium, run by Dirk Ivens of The Klinik / Vomito Negro) and “some other labels” for distribution, but no Tietchens origin story. theatticmag.com
  • Wikipedia articles for both Tietchens and Esplendor Geometrico — neither mentions the connection mechanism.
  • Arturo Lanz interview, El Blister (2014) — socket hang-up; could not retrieve.

What the sources establish (without naming the mechanism):

  1. Tietchens released three LPs on Discos EG: Geboren, Um Zu Dienen (1986, EG 003), Notturno (1986, EG-011), and Stupor Mundi (1990, EGD 018).
  2. Tietchens himself groups United Dairies (UK, Nurse With Wound’s label, 1984) and Esplendor Geometrico as his first post-”Sky Records” releases on “industrial labels.” This suggests he was moving through the European industrial network, not specifically seeking out Spain. chaindlk.com
  3. Esplendor Geometrico had an early German connection via Ata Tak (Der Plan’s label), which released “Moscú Está Helado” before Discos EG was founded. industrialcomplexx.com
  4. Conrad Schnitzler (also German) released two LPs on Discos EG in 1986–87, suggesting the label was actively sourcing German industrial artists around the same time as the Tietchens releases.
  5. The label’s European distribution partner was Daft Records (Belgium). theatticmag.com

Conclusion: No source found that names the specific person or event that brokered the Tietchens–Discos EG relationship. The circumstantial picture is consistent with a broader European industrial tape/label network operating in 1984–86 (Tietchens had just released on United Dairies; Esplendor Geometrico had a pre-existing German connection via Ata Tak; Conrad Schnitzler was signed at the same time), but the direct mechanism — personal acquaintance, cassette network, mutual contact, festival, or third-party label — is not documented in any source checked.

Notes (web) — Ata Tak / Der Plan / Tietchens bridge (2026-11-10)

Research question: Did EG’s pre-label Ata Tak connection (the “Moscú Está Helado” release) involve personal contact with Der Plan members that could have bridged Tietchens to Riaza/Noarbe? Any documented Tietchens–Der Plan connection 1984–86?

What was established:

  1. “Moscú Está Helado” was EG’s contribution to the compilation Fix Planet! (Ata Tak, WR 8 / WR 8½, October 1981), not a standalone single. Der Plan appeared on the bonus 7” (tracks “Einfachheit, Brot, Klarheit, Liebe, Tod” / untitled). Tietchens does not appear anywhere on this compilation. Sources: Discogs release 86099; soundscentral.org/ata-tak/

  2. Asmus Tietchens does not appear on any Ata Tak release. The full Ata Tak discography (Der Plan, Wirtschaftswunder, Die Doraus und Die Marinas, Monitor, Raha, Minus Delta T, Holger Hiller, Lost Gringos, Picky Picnic, etc.) contains no Tietchens credit in any role. Confirmed via soundscentral.org/ata-tak/ and Discogs (checked in prior trawler pass).

  3. Der Plan member Moritz R (Moritz Reichelt) left Ata Tak and moved to Hamburg around 1983 following internal differences. Tietchens was also Hamburg-based throughout this period. This places both in the same city from 1983 onward. Source: Wikipedia, “Der Plan” article (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Plan, as of 2026-11-10).

  4. No source documents any direct contact between Moritz R and Tietchens in Hamburg. Moritz R is described as pivoting to painting after Der Plan; no interview, discography, or press piece links him to Tietchens. Searched: “Moritz R Hamburg Tietchens”, “Tietchens Der Plan connection 1984–86”, “Tietchens Pyrolator collaboration” — all returned zero direct evidence.

  5. Pyrolator (Kurt Dahlke) of Der Plan/Ata Tak: Tietchens compiled a Moebius “Kollektion” release (Bureau B) which includes a Pyrolator track, suggesting later familiarity, but the Kollektion series is a 2010s Bureau B project — well after the Discos EG period. No 1984–86 Tietchens–Pyrolator contact documented. Source: moebius-official.bandcamp.com.

Conclusion: The Ata Tak connection is real but dates to 1981 and operates at the compilation level — EG contributed one track to Fix Planet!; Der Plan were on the bonus single; Tietchens was not involved. No evidence that this relationship produced personal contact between EG and Der Plan members that persisted into 1984–86. Moritz R’s move to Hamburg (1983) is circumstantially interesting given Tietchens’s Hamburg base, but no source makes any connection between them. The Ata Tak pathway to the Tietchens–Discos EG relationship remains unconfirmed.
