Overview

Labrador is a Swedish independent record label founded in 1998 and run by Johan Angergård. Operating under the parent imprint Labrador Records and alongside a sublabel, Labrador Songs, it built its catalogue around indie pop, indie rock, shoegaze and synth-pop — releasing over 70 records in the 2000s alone before tapering in the following decade. The label’s roster has revolved around a tight circle of Swedish artists, most prominently The Radio Dept., Club 8, Karolina Komstedt, and Johan Duncanson. By 2020 the release pace had slowed dramatically, with just two releases in that decade so far.

Notes (web) — release output decline after the 2000s peak

The sharp fall in Labrador’s release count after the early 2010s appears to be the product of several converging factors rather than a single deliberate policy decision.

The Radio Dept. legal dispute (2011–2016). The label’s most critically acclaimed and internationally visible act spent years in litigation with Labrador. The band had signed contracts they later described as deeply unfavorable: master recordings and publishing rights were assigned to Labrador “for the duration of their lives plus 70 years,” and singles did not count toward contractual album quotas. Frontman Johan Duncanson told Clrvynt in 2016 that “working on music just kind of lost its fun” under these terms. The band filed suit in 2011; a Swedish lower court initially ruled against them, but they reached a settlement in early 2015. As part of the settlement they retained catalogue rights and delivered one final contractual album, Running Out of Love (October 2016), after which they left and established their own imprint, Just So! Records, for future releases. (Clrvynt interview, 2016; Wikipedia: The Radio Dept., as of 2026-10-21)

Key acts reaching natural ends or moving on. Several mid-2000s roster staples released their final Labrador records in the 2009–2013 window without obvious successors: Sambassadeur last appeared on the label in 2010 (no documented post-Labrador full releases found); The Sound Of Arrows signed to Geffen Records in 2010–2011 for their major-label push and subsequently released albums on their own Skies Above imprint; Amanda Mair, who had two releases on Labrador in 2011–2012, later moved to Despotz Records. The Mary Onettes’ final Labrador release was Portico (2014), and their 2025 material appeared on Welfare Sounds. (Wikipedia: The Sound of Arrows discography, as of 2026-10-21; Wikipedia: Amanda Mair, as of 2026-10-21)

Angergård’s own fading enthusiasm and 2018 departure. A search-result snippet from a 2017 TIDAL label profile quotes Angergård saying “twenty years without ever producing a single popular band must be his biggest achievement as a label” — suggesting a deliberately ironic but low-growth orientation. A separate search result notes that “when Angergård turned 40, his label Labrador Records didn’t hold the same shine,” though no primary source for this phrasing was located. In 2018 he formally stepped back “to tend to other business” and the label reverted to founder Bengt Rahm’s sole stewardship. (Wikipedia: Labrador Records, as of 2026-10-21; TIDAL label profile snippet, https://tidal.com/magazine/article/label-focus-labrador-records/1-38043 — page blocked, snippet only)

Summary. The decline is best understood as a combination: the Radio Dept. dispute froze the label’s flagship act for half a decade and likely chilled external relationships; several mid-tier acts simply wound down or found other homes as their momentum passed; and Angergård’s own diminishing drive culminated in a handover to Rahm in 2018, after which output dropped to one or two releases per year at most. No source found states a deliberate policy to scale back — the pattern looks organic rather than strategic.
