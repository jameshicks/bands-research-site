Rong Music

Rong Music

Oct 12, 20269 min read

Rong Music

Discogs ID: 18924
Contact: info@rongmusic.net
Founded: 2003
Based: San Francisco / New York City

Independent label founded in San Francisco in 2003 and subsequently operating across San Francisco and New York City, rooted in balearic, disco, and deep house with consistent crossovers into No Wave, jazz, indie rock, and leftfield electronics. Co-founded and co-run by DJ Spun (Aaron Axelsen) and Ben Cook, who met in San Francisco when both were into disco at a time when “there was nobody into disco” — Ben Cook had a prior connection to the Idjut Boys through their Deep Fuzz album. [source: lebronjames.co interview with DJ Spun] Best understood as a taste-based imprint rather than a genre label — its catalog links the downtown NYC dance underground (Rub N Tug, DJ Spun, How & Why?) to Californian psych-rock (Tussle), UK balearic (Mudd, Woolfy), jazz (Chico Hamilton), No Wave (James Chance & The Contortions), and DFA-orbit post-punk (Free Blood). The label also ran an ongoing promo re-edits series (RPR) with descending catalog numbers — RPR99, RPR88, RPR77, etc.

The compilation This Is Rong Music (RONG CD02, 2007, UK) was compiled by Ben Cook and Jason Drummond with a live mix by DJ Spun, and functions as a label statement-of-intent mid-run. Design across multiple releases is credited to “Mrt611@hotmail.com,” an anonymous signature that runs through early and mid-period catalog.

Catalog Eras

Founding and Early Catalog — 2003–2004 (RONG 001–007, RPR99–RPR77)

The label opened with Triangle Orchestra’s @ 137 (RONG 001, 2003) — minimal electronics, NYC context. The following year established the label’s dual personality: UK-inflected balearic from Mudd (Paul N. Murphy) on Adventures In Bricket Wood (RONG 003) with keyboards by Tom Bailey (3), and California-rooted rock-dance crossover from Tussle on Disco D’Oro (RONG 004), which received remixes from Ben Cook/Andrew Fried and DJ Spun.

The RPR promo re-edits series launched in 2004 with RPR99 — Rub N Tug under their alias Thomas And Eric (Disco Theatre Of Manhattan Presents Rub N Tug), the first Rong release credited to Eric Duncan and Thomas Bullock. How & Why?’s RPR00 re-edits had uncredited production by DJ Spun and Eric Duncan.

Balearic Peak — 2005–2007 (RONG 008–021, RPR66–RPR44, RONG LP001-002)

Mudd remained the label’s anchor artist (15 releases total). The crowning achievement of this period was the Kerry’s Caravan collaboration (RONG 011, 2005) — a session between Mudd and veteran jazz drummer Chico Hamilton, blending balearic production with live drumming, soprano saxophone, and multiple remix treatments including Ray Mang and a “Benny West Orchestral Reprise.”

Woolfy (Simon James) became the second dominant artist (11 releases 2005–2009), including two Japan-only LPs on the Rong LP sublabel alongside Mudd’s Claremont 56 (RONG LP001, 2006).

Lee Douglas emerged mid-period with Same Changes (RONG 012, 2005) and multiple follow-ups. Kaos (6)‘s Cerebral Tremolo (RONG 014, 2006) pulled in Salvatore Principato (of Liquid Liquid) for percussion and featured Can Oral on vocals.

The label-defining compilation This Is Rong Music (RONG CD02, 2007, UK pressing) collected the label’s aesthetic in a single DJ Spun mix.

DFA Crossover — 2008–2009 (RONGDFA series)

In 2008, Rong Music and DFA Records launched a joint co-release imprint under the RONGDFA catalog prefix. No press source found has explained the precise origin story (how or when the two labels first approached each other), but available evidence points to two parallel threads converging: (1) Free Blood (John Pugh XI and Madeline Davy), whose “brainy hedonism and compelling rhythms” made them, per the AllMusic biography, “a natural fit” with DFA; and (2) Rong’s curatorial interest in No Wave-adjacent sounds, which aligned with DFA’s post-punk/dance-rock identity for the Notwave compilation concept.

The RONGDFA series was co-released on both labels simultaneously; releases carry both the “Rong Music” and “DFA Records” credit with the RONGDFA catalog number. By the time The Singles (RONGDFA07) came out in late 2008, Ben Cook was described as “a recent transplant to Seattle” — suggesting the Rong label operated nomadically across its founders’ locations. [source: thestranger.com search snippet, 2008]

The Notwave compilation (RONGDFA04) was Rong Music’s concept — described as “revisiting the NY No-Wave scene of the late seventies” but with contemporary artists — and is the clearest editorial statement of the series beyond the Free Blood singles. Artists on Notwave included Striplight, Tussle, Welcome Stranger, and others. DFA’s involvement gave the series wider distribution reach.

Later Catalog — 2009–2025

Coati Mundi (Andy Hernandez, of Kid Creole & The Coconuts) landed five releases in 2009–2010, including the album Dancing For The Cabana Code In The Land Of Boo-Hoo (RONG CD07). Coati Mundi’s No More Blues (Rong 029) was co-written with Lee Douglas and Elan Polushko.

The No Wave vault yielded Incorrigible (Rong 030, 2010) by James Chance & The Contortions — produced by Matt Verta-Ray and James Chance, with a “Version Mix” featuring Brennan Green, Dennis Young, and Salvatore Principato (three members of Liquid Liquid). The “Rong Disco Edit” was credited to Rong Music itself.

Mutant Beat Dance (Beau Wanzer + Melvin Oliphant) released two 12”s in 2011. DJ Spun became the label’s primary late-period artist (7 releases, 2012–2025), culminating in The Beat By DJ Spun (West Coast Breakbeat Rave Electrofunk 1988–1994) Vol. One (BEATSPUN001, 2023).

Notes (web)

Added 2026-10 from web research.

Founders: Rong Music was co-founded and co-run by DJ Spun (Aaron Axelsen) and Ben Cook. Both were San Francisco-based when the label started; they connected through shared interest in disco and Ben Cook’s prior relationship with the Idjut Boys (Deep Fuzz album). [https://www.lebronjames.co/interviews/dj-spun] The RA label profile credits DJ Spun as the label runner; by 2008 Ben Cook had relocated to Seattle. [thestranger.com search snippet]

DFA partnership mechanics: The RONGDFA series (2008–2009) was a co-release imprint — not a distribution deal — with releases carrying both Rong Music and DFA Records credits simultaneously. No press source located explains who initiated the partnership or when it was agreed. The series appears to have been anchored by two creative rationales: (a) Free Blood’s dance-punk fit the DFA aesthetic naturally; (b) the Notwave compilation (RONGDFA04) was Rong’s concept — a contemporary take on NYC No Wave, labelled “Notwave” — which complemented DFA’s post-punk/downtown identity. [ra.co/reviews/5453, blackplastic.co.uk/2008/10/13]

Free Blood connection: Free Blood (John Pugh XI, ex-!!!; and Madeline Davy) were described as “a natural fit” with DFA. The Singles (RONGDFA07) collected tracks from RONGDFA01, RONGDFA03, and RONGDFA06, recorded at The Brothers Studio, Greenpoint, Brooklyn, 2005–2008. [allmusic.com biography, tinymixtapes.com review]

Series extent: Confirmed RONGDFA01–08 and RONGDFA12; RONGDFA09–11 unconfirmed. The series extended into 2009 with non-Free Blood releases (Scotty Coats & Wes “The” Mes, DJ Kaos), suggesting the imprint had broader scope than just the Free Blood run. [discogs.com snippet, ebay listing for RONGDFA12]
