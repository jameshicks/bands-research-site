Overview

Malicious Damage is a UK record label founded and run by Mike Coles, active from 1979 to 1982 and best known in its original run for releasing early Killing Joke material during the post-punk and new wave moment. After two decades dormant, Coles reactivated the label in 2003 in a substantially different guise, anchored around The Orb and its associated artists — Alex Paterson, Dom Beken, and others — with the catalogue shifting into dub, downtempo, and ambient territory. The revival era accounts for the bulk of the label’s total output, dwarfing the original run; a sublabel, Orbsessions, operates under its umbrella.

Notes (web)

2003 reactivation and The Orb connection

No interview or press source explicitly states a stated reason why Coles reactivated the label in 2003, but the available evidence points to a decades-long personal friendship and creative partnership between Coles and Alex Paterson as the operative logic rather than any deliberate shift away from post-punk.

Alex Paterson’s original connection to the label. Paterson was part of the Killing Joke circle from the very start — Youth’s close friend, embedded in the scene at 11 Portland Road. In a Test Pressing interview he states “Malicious Damage in 78/79 with Killing Joke. I gave the label its name.” (testpressing.org). A separate Kill Your Pet Puppy piece says the name actually came from Jolie Hughes, who spotted a bus-stop notice reading “Malicious Damage, £50 fine” — contradicting Paterson’s account (killyourpetpuppy.co.uk). Either way, Paterson was present at the label’s founding moment and has described Coles as “a friend for ages and ages.” Coles designs The Orb’s artwork, a collaboration that predates the 2003 revival.

What the 2003 relaunch actually looked like. According to a 2006 Readers Voice interview, Coles’ first moves on reactivating the label were selling Killing Joke merchandise and producing “Chaos for Breakfast,” a boxed set of replica early singles — firmly rooted in the original catalogue. He then moved toward his stated goal of releasing “new music by new bands.” The same interview notes he was in discussions with Paterson about releasing the first Transit Kings album and a new Orb album. (readersvoice.com). No source describes any active search for post-punk acts or explains why the label tilted ambient/dub rather than back to its original sound.

Synthesis. The pattern that emerges across sources: Malicious Damage relaunched through pre-existing relationships rather than a genre strategy. Paterson was inside the original label’s social world from its founding; Coles was already doing The Orb’s artwork; the reactivation appears to have grown from those ties. No press release, interview, or press report uncovered in this search states an explicit reason for making The Orb the anchor artist over a post-punk direction.

Conflict — label name origin. Paterson (Test Pressing interview) says he gave the label its name. Kill Your Pet Puppy attributes the name to Jolie Hughes reading a bus-stop notice. The discrepancy is unresolved in available sources.
