Rvng Intl.

Discogs ID: 52921 Origin: Brooklyn / New York City, USA Founded: 2003 Active: 2003–present Genres: Experimental, Electronic, Ambient, Avant-Pop, New Age, Free Jazz Sublabels: FRKWYS, Freedom To Spend, Rvng Of The Nrds, Catalog Compendium, Reflections (25) Mailing address: PO Box 20752, Tompkins Square, New York, NY 10009 Website: igetrvng.com

Overview

RVNG Intl. is an independent record label launched in 2003 by Matt Werth in Brooklyn, New York. Werth (real name Matthew Werth) is also a musician — he has been a member of Aspera, Aspera Ad Astra, Favourite Sons, William Martyr 17, and The Divine Hook-Up, among other projects, and uses the alias “Rvng Intl.” as a Discogs artist credit.

The label began under the Rvng Of The Nrds imprint (cat# NRDS series), which ran 2006–2010 and focused on DJ mix/12” club-oriented releases featuring artists like Betty Botox, Lovefingers, Jacques Renault, Greg Wilson, JD Twitch, and In Flagranti. Around 2009–2010 the label pivoted decisively toward experimental and avant-garde work, launching the RVNGNL catalogue series with Pink Skull’s Endless Bummer (RVNGNL01, 2009) and the ReRVNG archival reissue line.

The FRKWYS sublabel — a series of collaborative records pairing artists across generations and genres — became one of the label’s signature projects. Starting with early volumes featuring Excepter with Carter Tutti and JG Thirlwell (2009), it continued through pairings like Blues Control & Laraaji (FRKWYS08, 2011), Kaitlyn Aurelia Smith & Suzanne Ciani (Sunergy, FRKWYS13, 2016), Tashi Wada With Yoshi Wada And Friends (FRKWYS14, 2018), and Daniel Lentz / Ian William Craig (FRKWYS16, 2020).

The ReRVNG reissue line rehabilitated overlooked experimental and electronic work: Harald Grosskopf (Synthesist, ReRVNG01, 2011), Sensations’ Fix (Music Is Painting In The Air, ReRVNG02, 2012), K. Leimer (A Period Of Review, ReRVNG03, 2014), Craig Leon (Nommos / Visiting, ReRVNG04, 2014), Pauline Anna Strom (Trans-Millenia Music, ReRVNG10, 2017), and several more Strom retrospectives through 2023.

Resident Advisor named Rvng Intl. label of the month in January 2014; Tiny Mix Tapes included it among its favorite labels of 2015. The label has maintained approximately 100 RVNGNL catalogue numbers by the early 2020s (RVNG NL100, Satomimagae’s Awa, appeared in 2023), with ongoing activity into 2025–2026.

The label’s house visual identity is handled primarily by Will Work For Good (Discogs ID: 2930058), credited on over 200 releases across design, artwork, and layout. Kevin O’Neill (4) (ID: 2121993) handled art direction on approximately 48 releases, particularly in the earlier period.

Early History (2003–2006)

Matt Werth’s background

Matt Werth was born in Madison, Wisconsin and grew up in Little Rock, Arkansas. At age 15 he launched the punk zine Potluck and took over File Thirteen, a record label originally established in 1989 as a “self-reflective journal fanzine,” which he ran for approximately eight years. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RVNG_Intl. as of 2026-07-12]

In 1996, aged 18, Werth moved to Philadelphia to play bass for Aspera Ad Astra. While attending Temple University he met Dave Pianka (DJ/promoter known as Dave P), and together they developed the idea of RVNG Intl. — initially conceived as an event production company rather than a record label. The name was suggested by a mutual friend, Michael Zwizanski, as an anti-voweled version of “revenge,” styled in the manner of Primal Scream’s XTRMNTR. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RVNG_Intl. as of 2026-07-12]

Werth moved to Brooklyn in 2002 to work at Flyer magazine. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RVNG_Intl. as of 2026-07-12]

Making Time parties and the MX series (2003–2006)

RVNG Intl. launched in 2003 first as a party/events operation. Werth helped Dave Pianka throw a series of parties in Philadelphia called Making Time, a roving dance night that Pianka had founded in 2000. [source: https://www.passionweiss.com/2025/09/18/we-outside-making-time-25-years-dave-p-interview/] The MX mix series was conceived as a calling card for these parties — physical proof to event clients that RVNG could manage quality DJs. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RVNG_Intl. as of 2026-07-12]

The label’s first release was RVNG PRSNTS MX1, described by Werth as “basically a spray-painted CD in a spray-painted case,” compiled by Julian Grefe (later of Pink Skull). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RVNG_Intl. as of 2026-07-12] Wikipedia lists the MX series releases as follows (note: Discogs documents MX1B from 2003 as its earliest release; Wikipedia numbering may conflate MX1 and MX1B):

The Bandcamp Daily 15th-anniversary retrospective (2018) describes the first five years of the label as revolving around “club culture” and characterized the MX CDs as “an infrequent series of mix CDs from friends such as Pink Skull’s Julian Grefe, Tim Sweeney and Justine D” that “laid the template from which RVNG Intl. has grown.” [source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/2018/11/26/rvng-label-guide-bandcamp]

Werth later reflected that the mix-CD work redirected his focus away from late-night event logistics: “this is actually what I really like to do, more than those late nights.” The parties and mix series together seeded the label’s future identity before RVNG formally stepped into 12” vinyl releases. [source: https://objectsandsounds.com/bringing-worlds-together-with-matt-werth/]

Transition to the NRDS series (~2006)

The Rvng Of The Nrds imprint and its vinyl edit series (NRDS cat# series) began in 2006, building on the DJ/club network established through the Making Time parties and the MX series. The NRDS releases featured DJs and producers connected to Optimo Music, Let’s Play House, and ESP Institute, “aimed at adventurous DJs.” [source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/2018/11/26/rvng-label-guide-bandcamp] This series ran 2006–2010 before the label pivoted to experimental and avant-garde releases.

Pivot to experimental (2009–2010)

No single interview fully explains the shift, but multiple sources sketch a consistent picture of an organic, taste-driven evolution rather than a strategic decision.

Werth’s own account, as given to Objects & Sounds, frames the change in terms of gradually expanding personal taste: “RVNG was very dance and electronic-oriented in the early days…eventually we kind of evolved.” He characterised his broader philosophy as: “There’s really no pattern to what we support. I love moving from one genre or one type of music to the next, from release to release.” [source: https://objectsandsounds.com/bringing-worlds-together-with-matt-werth/]

The biographical context matters. The Fact Magazine FRKWYS label profile (May 2010) traces Werth’s listening journey from punk through mid-90s Philly noise and psychedelic music to electronic music, naming Aphex Twin’s Richard D. James Album as a “eureka moment” that opened his ears to electronic music’s potential. The label’s experimental turn, in this reading, was the natural endpoint of a listening arc that had always been moving away from straightforward club fare. [source: https://www.factmag.com/2010/05/10/label-profile-frkwys/]

The Bandcamp Daily 15th-anniversary retrospective (2018) describes the NRDS conclusion in terms of momentum rather than rupture: “The NRDS series came to a natural conclusion in 2010, and the basic principles were carried into the imprint’s next endeavour, FRKWYS.” The same piece notes that the design partnership with Will Work For Good also “happened organically. Nothing was super intentional. It was more like a mutual commitment — like a family,” suggesting the whole label culture resisted deliberate repositioning. [source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/2018/11/26/rvng-label-guide-bandcamp]

The RVNG Intl. 20th-anniversary post (igetrvng.com, 2023) reinforces the label’s resistance to self-narration: “we never quite felt comfortable with what attention came our way,” with credit consistently deflected toward the artists. [source: https://igetrvng.com/blogs/news/theres-no-time-like-no-time]

Structurally, the overlap period (2009–2010) is telling: Pink Skull’s Endless Bummer (RVNGNL01, 2009) and the first FRKWYS volumes launched while NRDS releases were still being issued, suggesting the experimental strand grew alongside — not after — the club-edit strand, and simply outlasted it. Pink Skull’s Julian Grefe had been the label’s first collaborator (MX1B, 2003), so the pivot to Endless Bummer as RVNGNL01 was as much a deepening of an existing relationship as a change of direction.

Note on inaccessible primary sources: The Juno Daily interview (May 2012, “Human Resources, Zombie Invasions & Mean Librarians: A Potted History of RVNG Intl.” by Nik Mercer) returned 403 on all fetch attempts and remains unread. This piece is the most likely source for direct Werth quotes on the pivot rationale. The Arkansas Times (May 2014) also remained inaccessible (429). If either becomes available, the above should be treated as provisional.

Visual identity origins

The design partnership with Kevin O’Neill and Karisa Senavitis of Will Work For Good began in the label’s early years — reportedly through an initial meeting at “PJ’s Pancake House in Princeton, New Jersey.” [source: https://daily.bandcamp.com/2018/11/26/rvng-label-guide-bandcamp]

Catalogue Statistics

  • RVNGNL series: RVNGNL01 (2009) through RVNGNL130+ (2025); milestone release RVNG NL100 (Awa, Satomimagae, 2023)
  • ReRVNG series: ReRVNG01 (2011) through ReRVNG20 (2023)
  • FRKWYS series: FRKWYS01/02 (2009) through FRKWYS17 (2021), plus FRKWYS4.5 (2022)
  • Total releases (Discogs, all pressings): ~542 release entries
  • Unique master works: ~110 titles under the main label

The label was most active in its experimental turn from 2011 onward. The 2017–2022 period was particularly dense, with Visible Cloaks, Helado Negro, Pauline Anna Strom, Kate NV, Lucrecia Dalt, Horse Lords, and Satomimagae all becoming major recurring artists.

Scene Connections

  • NYC experimental scene: The label is embedded in the Tompkins Square / Lower East Side experimental community. Matt Werth’s field recordings appear on Helado Negro’s This Is How You Smile alongside Jason Ajemian, Matana Roberts, and others.
  • Holly Herndon / Mathew Dryhurst network: Herndon’s Platform (co-released with 4AD) connected Rvng to the Bay Area / Stanford CCRMA academic experimental world and the Amnesia Scanner / Colin Self sphere.
  • Sun Araw / M. Geddes Gengras / The Congos (Icon Give Thank, FRKWYS09): connected the label to LA psych-dub and Jamaican roots music.
  • FRKWYS pairings regularly connect younger Rvng artists (Kaitlyn Aurelia Smith, Julianna Barwick) with pioneering figures (Suzanne Ciani, Ikue Mori, David Borden).
  • ReRVNG reissues connect the label to the history of private-press electronic and new age music: Pauline Anna Strom, K. Leimer, Syrinx (2), Ariel Kalma, Anna Homler, Tiziano Popoli.
  • Rashad Becker (mastering, 47 releases) links Rvng to Raster-Noton and the Berlin experimental mastering world.
  • Jed Bindeman and Freedom To Spend handle archival reissue research and curation alongside Werth.
  • The Haxan Cloak (Bobby Krlic) produced Helado Negro and others, connecting Rvng to the wider experimental production network.
  • Ela Minus appears as vocalist on Helado Negro’s This Is How You Smile (2019), an early Rvng-adjacent credit.
