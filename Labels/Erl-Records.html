Overview

Erl Records is an independent label whose output is almost entirely concentrated in the 1990s — twelve releases that decade plus a single 1988 entry — working in punk, noise, lo-fi, and experimental indie rock. Its roster includes Jad Fair (of Half Japanese fame) and Tim Foljahn, both veterans of the East Coast underground, alongside noisier acts such as Blue (30), Mosquito (6), and 5chin400. The label has no recorded parent or sublabel structure, and its thirty-three total Discogs credits suggest a small boutique operation rather than a broad commercial imprint.

Label background

Erl Records was founded in September 1988 by David Belemjian (also credited as Dave Belem) and Jack Wingate, based at 418 Madison Ave, Albany, NY 12210. The label grew out of a record store the pair ran under the same name at the same address. The store stocked small independent labels (Forced Exposure, Ecstatic Yod, Corewood, etc.), and the label arm released its own catalogue of 7” singles and LPs before closing in August 1994. Sources: grunnenrocks.nl label page; Burnt Hills / Jackson Wingate interview, Psychedelic Baby Mag, October 2026; Flickr Albany photo caption.

David P. Belemjian died 3 June 2025 in Loudonville, NY, aged 60. Source: search-result snippet from Legacy.com obituary listing (page itself returned 403).

The Jad Fair / Tim Foljahn connection — Mosquito as the link

The direct reason both Jad Fair and Tim Foljahn appear on Erl Records is Mosquito (6), the band they formed together with Steve Shelley (Sonic Youth) and James McNew (Yo La Tengo / Christmas), based in Hoboken, NJ. Erl released the Mosquito self-titled 7” (ERL-009, 1992) and co-released the full-length Time Was (ERL-015, June 1993) with Smells Like Records. The Half Japanese 7” Postcard (ERL-010, 1992) follows immediately in the catalogue sequence, suggesting Belemjian and Wingate signed Fair as a solo artist on the strength of the Mosquito relationship rather than independently.

Mosquito’s Time Was contains a track titled “Erl Duke” (track 2, 1:13), widely read as a tribute to or in-joke about the label and its owners — indicating a personal rather than purely transactional relationship between band and label. Sources: Mosquito Bandcamp — Time Was; Mosquito Bandcamp — “Erl Duke” track page.

No source consulted explicitly states how Belemjian and Wingate first made contact with the Mosquito members. The mechanism — whether through the Albany store’s network, through Steve Shelley’s NYC connections, or brokered by Smells Like Records — is not documented in any publicly available interview or label history found to date.

Scene context

Erl’s broader roster (William Hooker Group, Blue (30), 5chin400) skews toward noise and experimental improvisation, a sensibility consistent with the New York / East Coast underground that Mosquito represented. The co-release of Time Was with Smells Like Records (a Northwest indie distributed through a different network) suggests Erl had connections extending beyond Albany’s local scene by 1993.
