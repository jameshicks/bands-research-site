Mo Wax

Discogs ID: 111 Label Code: LC 4983 / LC 04983 Active: 1992–2002 Origin: London, UK (167 Caledonian Road, Barnsbury, N1 0SL) Parent: Beggars Group Media Ltd. (Discogs 888586)

British independent label founded and run by James Lavelle. Launched 1992, defunct 2002. Central to the mid-1990s trip-hop and DJ culture moment in the UK; also instrumental in connecting UK underground music to US hip-hop, Japanese DJ culture, and NYC post-punk reissues. 1,234 releases in Discogs.

Founder

James Lavelle (Discogs 21209; real name James Leo Lavelle; alias DJ Kutmaster Lavski) — British DJ and musician. Founded Mo Wax at around age 17. Only constant member of UNKLE, his main production project. Credited on 307 Mo Wax releases as A&R, producer, and performer.

Closure (2002)

Mo Wax shut in 2002 after a decade of operation. The closure resulted from compounding pressures across the label’s final years rather than any single event.

A&M Records deal (1995/1996–1999)

Mo Wax entered a distribution and partial-ownership arrangement with A&M Records, with worldwide distribution (outside the US) running from approximately 1995 and partial ownership signed over in 1996. Lavelle later described this deal as “the worst decision I ever made.” (Quietus, 2014; onamrecords.com) When PolyGram — A&M’s parent — was absorbed into Universal Music Group from November 1998, Mo Wax artists were swept into the Universal system. DJ Shadow, for instance, ended up at Island Records (a Universal imprint) by late 1998 due to “background issues between Mo’ Wax and their parent label A&M,” though Lavelle remained credited as A&R. The A&M relationship formally ended February 1999. (Medium / Mo Wax Where Are They Now — DJ Shadow entry)

Move to Beggars Group (c. 1999–2002)

After the A&M arrangement collapsed, Lavelle moved Mo Wax to Beggars Group, which at the time also housed 4AD, XL Recordings, Mantra, Too Pure, Wiiija, and Beggars Banquet. Beggars Group retains ownership of a portion of the Mo Wax catalogue. The exact year Beggars Group took on Mo Wax is not definitively confirmed in sources found — it would have been sometime after February 1999. (Wikipedia — Mo’ Wax; Beggars Group Wikipedia)

Why the label folded

Multiple factors contributed:

  • Competitive talent market. As Mo Wax’s profile rose, larger labels began matching or exceeding its offers. Lavelle noted: “the whole world jacked up (their prices) and people wanted £150–200,000 deals, any success as a label meant every other company tried to copy that.” (Bona Fide Magazine interview) The Bona Fide interview also records: “the end of Mo Wax was sad because everyone I tried to sign other labels just offered them more money and that kind of killed the label.”
  • Personal exhaustion. Lavelle has described the period as one of severe burnout: “Mo Wax felt like a band in which I was the lead singer. But there’s a point where you have to go ‘OK, we’re done’. The whole thing had lost its momentum and I was pretty burnt out.” (The Independent, 2014, quoted in Jaeger Oslo) He also cited wanting time after the birth of his child, noting he had been working continuously since age 14 in record retail and then the label. (Bona Fide Magazine)
  • Loss of corporate leverage. The dissolution of A&M left the label without the infrastructure that had enabled it to operate at its mid-1990s scale. The final years saw fewer high-profile signings, and the Beggars arrangement — while keeping the label indie-aligned — did not restore that capacity.

Lavelle’s summary of the period, quoted widely: “I was so fucked off with it all by then. I was so in the midst of clubs, touring and DJing…I was not very happy.” (Bona Fide Magazine)

Catalogue after closure

The catalogue is split. Releases made during the A&M period (c. 1995–1999) carry phonographic copyrights held by A&M Records Ltd., administered post-merger by Universal Island Records Ltd. (confirmed by The Private Press copyright credit). Beggars Group retains ownership of the catalogue from the post-A&M period. (Medium / Mo Wax Where Are They Now — DJ Shadow entry; Wikipedia) DJ Shadow’s Mo Wax contract concluded with a best-of compilation circa 2012, after which he released independently via his own Liquid Amber label. (Medium ibid.)

After Mo Wax: Surrender All

In 2005 Lavelle launched Surrender All (also connected to Surrender Arts / Mo Wax Arts) as an UNKLE-focused vehicle, initially in connection with Island Records following Never, Never, Land. He has stated he is unlikely to start another label like Mo Wax, noting: “I don’t want to be responsible for the careers of other artists.” (Jaeger Oslo; Search result / various sources)
