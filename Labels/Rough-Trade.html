Rough Trade

UK independent record label. Discogs label ID: 51167. Label code: LC 5661 / LC 05661. Parent: Beggars Group (Discogs ID: 114971). Founded London 1978 by Geoff Travis.

One of the defining institutions of UK independent music. Active in three distinct incarnations — original (1978–c.1991), second (c.1991–c.1999), third/relaunched (c.1999–present). Across all eras the label has released post-punk, indie rock, experimental, electronic, reggae/dub, no wave, and folk, with a consistent editorial instinct towards the unconventional. Current address: 66 Golborne Rd, London W10 5PS.

Founding and First Era (1978–1990)

Geoff Travis (born 1952) opened the Rough Trade shop on Kensington Park Road, Notting Hill in 1976, stocking import records in a co-operative, DIY spirit. The shop became a scene hub before Travis launched the label proper in 1978. The founding philosophy was explicitly co-operative and anti-corporate: artists retained rights; profits were shared. Travis later described the model as attempting to enact the politics of the music rather than simply selling it.

The label’s first release was Spizzoil, “6000 Crazy” (RTSO-1, 1978). Early singles moved fast across punk and post-punk: Stiff Little Fingers (“Suspect Device” / “Alternative Ulster”, 1978), Cabaret Voltaire (Extended Play EP, RT 003, 1978), and — notably for the vault — the Electric Eels (“Agitated / Cyclotron”, RT 008, 1978), their only UK release, credited as “Die Electric Eels.”

The distribution arm grew alongside the label. Rough Trade Distribution became a critical infrastructure for the UK independent sector through the 1980s, handling releases for 4AD, Factory Records, One Little Indian, Mute, and many others. This twin role — label and distributor — was central to Rough Trade’s power and ultimately to its collapse.

Key Artists, First Era

The Raincoats debuted on Rough Trade with their self-titled LP (ROUGH 3, 1979), followed by Odyshape (ROUGH 13, 1981). Geoff Travis co-mixed the debut alongside Adam Kidron, Mayo Thompson, and the band.

Swell Maps placed nearly their entire catalog on Rough Trade: A Trip To Marineville (ROUGH 2, 1979), In “Jane From Occupied Europe” (ROUGH 15, 1980), and multiple singles. Nikki Sudden and Epic Soundtracks’ band.

Young Marble Giants — Colossal Youth (ROUGH 8, 1980) is among the most celebrated of all Rough Trade first-era releases: skeletal post-punk minimalism from Cardiff. Members Stuart Moxham, Philip Moxham, Alison Statton.

Cabaret Voltaire were a constant presence from the first year: Mix-Up (ROUGH 4, 1979), The Voice Of America (ROUGH 11, 1980), Red Mecca (ROUGH 27, 1981), 2X45 (ROUGH 42, 1982). Later moved to their own Doublevision imprint but continued issuing on Rough Trade through the mid-1980s.

The Pop Group — anarcho-funk agitators: For How Much Longer Do We Tolerate Mass Murder? (ROUGH 9, 1980), We Are Time (ROUGH 12, 1980).

The Fall — a central pillar. Grotesque (After The Gramme) (ROUGH 18, 1980), Slates EP (RTO 71, 1981), Perverted By Language (ROUGH 62, 1983). Mark E. Smith’s group released intermittently on Rough Trade across 1980–1996.

Scritti Politti — first appearances as punk/DIY agitators: “4 ‘A’ Sides” (RT 027, 1979), Songs To Remember (ROUGH 20, 1982), transitioning into pop with Green Gartside at the helm.

This Heat — Deceit (ROUGH 26, 1981), their second album, is a key experimental/post-punk document.

Pere Ubu — US avant-rock band with a sustained Rough Trade UK presence: The Modern Dance (ROUGH 22, 1981 UK pressing), The Art Of Walking, 390 Degrees Of Simulated Stereo, Song Of The Bailing Man.

Robert Wyatt — long-running relationship: “Arauco / Caimanera” (RT 037, 1980), “Shipbuilding” (RT 115, 1982, one of the label’s most commercially successful singles), Nothing Can Stop Us (1982), Work In Progress (1984). Robert Wyatt was one of the label’s most artistically significant signings.

DNA (4) — “A Taste Of DNA” (RT 086, 1981); the No Wave band’s only UK release.

Epic Soundtracks — Popular Classical (RT 084, 1981), Rain, Rain, Rain EP (RT 104, 1982).

Wire — UK releases of Document And Eyewitness (ROUGH 29, 1981) and “Crazy About Love” (1983).

Essential Logic — Beat Rhythm News (ROUGH 5, 1979); Lora Logic’s post-X-Ray Spex outfit.

Aztec Camera — High Land, Hard Rain (ROUGH 47, 1983), debut album by Aztec Camera and Roddy Frame, one of the label’s strongest early-80s pop-adjacent signings.

The Go-Betweens — Australian post-punk: Send Me A Lullaby (ROUGH 45, 1982), Before Hollywood (ROUGH 54, 1983).

Violent Femmes — UK/European releases via Rough Trade: debut album (RTD 17, 1983 Germany), “Gone Daddy Gone.”

Einstürzende Neubauten — German releases: Zeichnungen Des Patienten O.T. (RTD 18, 1983).

The Smiths — the label’s biggest commercial act, defining its mid-1980s peak. “Hand In Glove” (RT 131, 1983), “This Charming Man” (RT 136, 1983), debut LP The Smiths (ROUGH 61, 1984), Hatful Of Hollow (ROUGH 76, 1984), Meat Is Murder (ROUGH 81, 1985), The Queen Is Dead (1986), Strangeways Here We Come (1987). Morrissey, Johnny Marr, Andy Rourke, Mike Joyce.

Pixies — UK releases of Come On Pilgrim (1988), Surfer Rosa (1988), Doolittle (1989), Bossanova (1990). A cornerstone of the label’s late-80s international roster.

The Sugarcubes — Life’s Too Good (1988) and follow-up albums; Björk’s first internationally distributed group.

Galaxie 500 — On Fire (1989), This Is Our Music (1990), Blue Thunder single.

Dead Can Dance — Within The Realm Of A Dying Sun (1987), The Serpent’s Egg (1988), Aion (1990).

Sonic Youth — UK/European release of Bad Moon Rising (1985 via Rough Trade Germany).

The label also maintained a significant reggae/dub sub-roster from the early years: Horace Andy, Gregory Isaacs, Thomas Mapfumo, Bunny Wailer, Jah Shaka, Tapper Zukie, and African releases via the ET catalog sub-series.

Catalog Numbering, First Era

Singles/EPs: RT### (7”), RTT### (later 12”), RTT###CD (CD singles), RTM### (12” EPs/mini-albums). Five early 7”s numbered RTSO1–RTSO5 (all Spizz Oil-related). Albums: ROUGH### (sequentially 1–159); RUFCD#### for four 1989 CD-only releases.

Crisis and Second Era (c.1991–c.1999)

By 1990 Rough Trade had overstretched itself, particularly in the US market. The distribution arm collapsed under debt. The US branch was shut down; the German branch (Rough Trade Deutschland) ended up in the Zomba Music Group and the name was gradually abandoned there.

In the UK, One Little Indian provided emergency financing and effectively took over the label. Geoff Travis remained nominally in charge but soon departed to start the Trade 2 label in partnership with Island Records. A side-effect of the One Little Indian arrangement: that label retained ownership of some key catalogue, including early A.R. Kane albums (who released 69 and i on Rough Trade, 1988–1994).

Dave Boyd was label manager through this period. The second era produced a smaller catalog of releases — indie and alternative acts in a more modest register. Disco Inferno (1993–1995), Levitation (2) (1991–1992), The Boo Radleys (early catalog, 1990–1993), Nightmares On Wax (1990–1999), and Mouse On Mars (1994–1999) all passed through this incarnation.

Catalog Numbering, Second Era

R###x format (starting around 250), where x indicates format: 0 = 12”, 1 = LP, 2 = CD, 3 = CD Single, 4 = Cassette, 5 = mini-album, 6 = CD mini-album, 7 = 7” single, 8 = double LP. Singles club: 45REV##.

Relaunch and Third Era (c.1999–present)

Geoff Travis regained control of the Rough Trade name around 1999–2000 and re-launched the label alongside the Tugboat Records imprint (Discogs ID: 2716). The third era catalog starts with RTRADE### prefixes (or sometimes RTRAD###).

Early relaunched signings included Spring Heel Jack (Disappeared, RTRADECD 007, 2000), Hope Sandoval & The Warm Inventions (Bavarian Fruit Bread, 2001), and in 2001 the label’s biggest commercial moment in years: The Strokes (“Hard to Explain”, RTRADESCD 023, 2001) and Is This It. The Strokes’ debut was a genuine cultural event.

In July 2001, Sanctuary Records took a 49% stake in Rough Trade to support international distribution infrastructure.

In July 2007, a financially struggling Sanctuary sold its 49% stake to the Beggars Group for £800,000 as part of Sanctuary’s preamble to being acquired by Universal. Beggars has held that stake since, with Rough Trade continuing to operate independently from Golborne Road while Beggars handles central office functions.

Key Third-Era Artists and Releases

Belle & Sebastian — UK distribution of releases from 2003 onward.

The Strokes — Is This It (2001), early career catalog.

Arcade Fire — Funeral (2004, Canadian; UK/EU via Rough Trade), Neon Bible (2007). Career-defining albums for the label’s third era.

Antony and the Johnsons / ANOHNI — I Am A Bird Now (2005 UK/EU, Mercury Prize winner), Hope There’s Someone (single 2005), You Are My Sister (single 2005), The Crying Light (2009), Swanlights (2011), Hopelessness (2016 as ANOHNI), My Back Was A Bridge For You To Cross (2023 as Anohni And The Johnsons). Long-running primary relationship; Geoff Travis and Jeannette Lee served as executive producers on the 2023 album.

Jarvis Cocker — Jarvis (2006), Further Complications (2009).

Parquet Courts — sustained relationship from 2014: Sunbathing Animal, Human Performance (2016), Wide Awake! (2018).

Warpaint — self-titled album (2014), Heads Up (2016).

Alabama Shakes — Boys & Girls (2012), Sound & Color (2015).

Sleaford Mods — English Tapas (2017), Eton Alive (2019), and beyond.

Black Midi — Schlagenheim (2019), Cavalcade (2021), Hellfire (2022). Three albums designed by David Rudnick. Geordie Greep has since released a solo album on the label.

Goat Girl — debut LP Goat Girl (2018), On All Fours (2021).

Girl Band — Holding Hands With Jamie EP (2015), The Talkies (2019).

Lankum — Between The Earth And Sky (2017), The Livelong Day (2019), subsequent albums.

Amyl and The Sniffers — debut LP (2019).

Jockstrap (4) — I Love You Jennifer B (2022).

Geordie Greep — solo releases from 2024.

Caroline (48) — releases from 2020.
