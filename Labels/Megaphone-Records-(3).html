Megaphone Records (3)

US independent record label, founded and run by Jason Willett, based initially in Frederick, Maryland and later Baltimore. Discogs label ID: 130234.

Overview

Megaphone Records grew out of a record store Jason Willett opened in Frederick, Maryland in the fall of 1990 called Megaphone Music. The store carried an expansive stock of experimental, radical, and obscure music across all genres, and regularly hosted live performances by acts including Dog Faced Hermans, God Is My Co-Pilot, Boredoms, No Safety, and Hasil Adkins. Some regular customers — including people who would later become collaborators and bandmates of Willett’s — were introduced through the store. After three years, Willett closed Megaphone Music and moved to Baltimore, where he now operates The True Vine record shop.

The label ran primarily CD-only from its launch in the early 1990s through 2012 — with the exception of two 7” singles by Jac Berrocal in 1996. Starting in 2012, Megaphone began co-releasing most titles with Knock’em Dead Records on both CD and LP, often including a limited edition on colored or transparent vinyl.

Early Phase: 1991–1994

The inaugural release (MEGAPHONE 001) was a CD reissue of The Work’s Slow Crimes, which had originally appeared on ReR Megacorp. The new pressing included bonus tracks from The Work’s 7” single See, and constituted the first widely available CD edition of the album. The Work’s core members — Mick Hobbs and Rick Wilson — appeared throughout the label’s early catalog, with Tim Hodgkinson, Bill Gilonis, Catherine Jauniaux, and Chris Cutler all featuring across related releases.

The label’s second release (MEGAPHONE 002) was Live Improvisations (1991), a free improvisation CD by Fred Frith and Tim Hodgkinson, co-released with Woof Records (WOOF 013). The sessions were recorded by Michael Gerzon. Frith performed guitar, violin, and vocals; Hodgkinson played keyboards, alto saxophone, and clarinet. The six untitled tracks total approximately 53 minutes.

A close working relationship with Tim Hodgkinson and Woof Records characterized the early catalog. Several releases were co-issued between the two labels:

  • MEGAPHONE 004 / WOOF 014 — The Goose (1992) by Ken Hyder, Tim Hodgkinson, and Valentina Ponomareva. A four-track set of free improvisation incorporating Siberian folk influences, with Ponomareva credited on all compositions alongside Hyder and Hodgkinson.
  • MEGAPHONE 014 / WOOF 016 — Each In Our Own Thoughts (1994) by Tim Hodgkinson, his first solo album. Featured Dagmar Krause (voice), Chris Cutler (drums, words), Lindsay Cooper (bassoon), Bill Gilonis (guitar), Guy Segers (bass), and a string quartet.

The first ten releases focused heavily on archival and rare material, including:

  • MEGAPHONE 003 — Magnetizer (1992) by Fat (2)
  • MEGAPHONE 005 — See (1992) by The Work (a second Work reissue)
  • MEGAPHONE 006 — All My Screws Fell Out (1993) by The Honkies
  • MEGA 007 — Down Under The Black Light (1993) by The Molecules
  • MEGAPHONE 008 — Fatal Encounters (1993) by Jac Berrocal. A CD compilation of unreleased Berrocal tracks assembling collaborations with Nurse With Wound, Catalogue (Gilbert Artman / Jean-François Pauvros), Jean-Marc Foussat, Pascal Comelade, Pierre Bastien, Jonathan Kane, and others. Jason Willett produced three tracks (with Berrocal, Pauvros, and Kane). This was the beginning of a decades-long relationship between Willett and Berrocal.
  • MEGAPHONE 009 — The Virtual Violin (1993) by Jon Rose
  • MEGAPHONE 015 — Who Eats? (1993) by The Honkies
  • MEGAPHONE 016 — Violin Music For Supermarkets (1994) by Jon Rose
  • MEGAPHONE 020 — Voodoo Boogie / Nu Är Det Allvar!! (1993) by Kräldjursanstalten

Other early releases included Jason Willett, Jad Fair, Gilles Rieder (MEGAPHONE 012, 1993, Canada), a three-way collaboration; and Happy New Year (MEGAPHONE 011, 1994) and Live From The Back Part Of The Flag (MEGAPHONE 018, 1995) by Happy New Year (2).

Mid-Phase: 1996–2000

Activity slowed in the late 1990s but continued with notable archival and new work:

  • MEGA2982 / Megaphone Record 2983 — Two Jac Berrocal 7” singles: Rock’n Roll Station (with Vince Taylor) and Noconnexion (1996). These were the only non-CD releases in this period.
  • MEGAPHONE 019 — Recordings (1996) by The Recordings
  • MEGAPHONE 021 — Exiles 1. (1996) by Exiles (3)
  • MEG022 — Anything Is Possible (2000) by Ron Anderson

Sublabels

In 1994, Willett launched a subsidiary imprint called Megaphone Limited (Discogs ID: 42755), which released CDs with handmade artwork in editions of 500 copies. It focused on Willett’s own bands and side projects, beginning with a debut release by a newly-formed duo with Martha Colburn. Willett concurrently moved into Martha’s warehouse in downtown Baltimore, which became the home base for Megaphone Studio (Discogs ID: 313684).

In 2008, the label launched Megaphone Unlimited (Discogs ID: 106925), a subsidiary focused on library music. Its only release to date was a collection drawn from the ReR Megacorp archives. (The same year, a Willett compilation appeared on another label, confusingly presenting material exclusively from the Megaphone Limited roster.)

There was also a short-lived Megaphone Cassette Special (Discogs ID: 2914889) series.

Knock’em Dead Era: 2012–present

From 2012, Megaphone began co-producing and co-releasing all new titles with Knock’em Dead Records (Discogs ID: 440602) on both CD and LP.

The first joint release was R. Stevie Moore’s 2xLP compilation Hearing Aid (MEGAPHONE 024, 2012). Moore had initially compiled the tracks for a 1996 Megaphone CD release that was shelved for decades. When Moore approached Willett again around 2011, they brought it to fruition as a double vinyl album.

Subsequent releases in the Knock’em Dead partnership included:

  • MEGA 025 — Ossification (2014) by Officer! (CD and LP editions)
  • 027 — Miniatures (2014, 2xLP reissue of the Morgan-Fisher-edited compilation of 51 composers, originally 1980), co-released with Knock’em Dead
  • 026; 029 — Slow Crimes (2014, LP repress of the label’s inaugural release)
  • MEGA 031 — Batu Malablab (2015) by Martin Schmidt (credited as M.C. Schmidt — one half of Matmos). Recorded by Schmidt with electronics contributions from Jon “Wobbly” Leidecker and mastered by Thomas Dimuzio.
  • none — In Lo Fidelity (2012) by Matmos
  • none — A Fistful Of Knuckles (2015) by People Like Us
  • none — Nothing Special (2015) by Kenny G / People Like Us
  • none — The Attitude Robots (2016)
  • MEGA034 — Sometimes Puns Are a Sign of a Damaged Brain (2017) by IG Witzelsucht
  • MEG035 — A L’Abri Des Micro-Climats (2018) by Guigou Chenevier / Sophie Jausserand (France)
  • MEG036 — Lightworks (2018) by Stop Motion Orchestra (two editions)
  • Megaphone 037 — Xmas In March (2020) by Jac Berrocal / Jason Willett / David Fenech. Mastered by Kevin Spencer. The most recent release documented at time of research.
  • MEG038 — Everyday Timebomb (2026) by Dog Faced Hermans — announced/released 2026; co-released with Knock’em Dead.

Konk Pack’s Doing The Splash (MEGAPHONE 026, 2013) was another co-release with Knock’em Dead, featuring Tim Hodgkinson on lap steel guitar alongside Roger Turner (drums) and Thomas Lehn (analogue synthesizer).
