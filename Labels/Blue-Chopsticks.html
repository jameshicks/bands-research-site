Overview

Blue Chopsticks is a small Drag City–affiliated label founded and run by guitarist and composer David Grubbs. Its nineteen-release catalogue runs from 1999 through the 2020s—heaviest in the 2000s—and orbits experimental, free improvisation, and avant-garde territory. The roster is tight: Luc Ferrari, Noël Akchoté, Andrea Belfi, and Stefano Pilia appear alongside Grubbs himself, connecting American post-rock circles to European improvisation and electroacoustics.

Notes (web) — Label founding, scope, and name

Why a separate imprint rather than Drag City proper

In an interview with Beats Per Minute (date unspecified on the page), Grubbs gave this direct account of how both Dexter’s Cigar and Blue Chopsticks came to exist:

“I think that Jim and I were constantly pestering Drag City to reissue certain records that we loved—first the Red Krayola, but then quickly Voice Crack, Merzbow, Derek Bailey, etc.—and Dan Koretzky and Dano Osborn from Drag City had the excellent idea (and the generosity) to give us a crack at directing our own label, and one that they would manufacture and distribute. That’s still my arrangement with Drag City for Blue Chopsticks—and they’re still such extraordinarily generous, game folks.”

https://beatsperminute.com/interview-david-grubbs/

The passage establishes that the separate imprint was not Grubbs’ initiative alone — Drag City proposed it as a solution to persistent reissue lobbying by Grubbs and Jim O’Rourke. Drag City retained manufacture and distribution. Blue Chopsticks is effectively the successor to Dexter’s Cigar (the Gastr del Sol–era sub-imprint co-directed with O’Rourke); when O’Rourke left the picture, the arrangement continued under Grubbs alone with a new name.

A 2000 Providence Phoenix ten-year retrospective on Drag City confirms the structural continuity, listing “Dexter’s Cigar (Gastr Del Sol-organized reissue projects)” and “Blue Chopsticks (David Grubbs’s corner)” as imprints under the Drag City umbrella, and noting that Grubbs and O’Rourke “were involved with seemingly half the records DC released in those days, as well as overseeing Dexter’s Cigar.” https://providencephoenix.com/archive/music/00/07/13/DRAG.html

One earlier Dexter’s Cigar release that the Providence Phoenix names is Folke Rabe’s What?? (1997) — another experimental/electroacoustic reissue, suggesting the curatorial thread from Dexter’s Cigar to Blue Chopsticks was consistent.

Label scope: no defined genre box

In an early interview with KindaMuzik (circa 1999–2001, exact date unclear on the page), Grubbs was asked whether a coherent aesthetic would emerge from the label. He said the answer was shaping up to be “no”:

“As the records continue to roll off the assembly lines, I think that answer that emerges will be ‘no.‘”

He noted that BC3 (Van Oehlen) had “next to nothing to do with BC1 and 2,” indicating he deliberately kept the label’s scope eclectic rather than genre-defined. https://www.kindamuzik.net/interview/david-grubbs/david-grubbs/369/

Label name origin: Herbie Nichols

Grubbs explained the label’s name in an interview published in the Chelsea News / Westside Spirit (New York local press; exact date unclear). He named it after the composition “Blue Chopsticks” by jazz pianist Herbie Nichols, which he encountered by “picking up a used copy of Herbie Nichols’ The Third World and shortly thereafter Duck Baker’s wonderful recordings on nylon-string guitar of Nichols’ compositions.” He also acknowledged he had “forgotten—or never knew— that Nichols was a poet and critic for the Black weekly newspaper, New York Age.” https://www.westsidespirit.com/news/david-grubbs-this-indeed-is-music-FC3589227

A Herbie Nichols composition “Blue Chopsticks” is confirmed by its existence on the Buell Neidlinger album Blue Chopsticks: A Portrait of Herbie Nichols (Discogs release 9082870).

Notes (web) — Grubbs / Ferrari connection

Ferrari as Blue Chopsticks’ founding release

The very first Blue Chopsticks release (BC1) was Luc Ferrari’s Interrupteur / Tautologos 3 (1999), a reissue of two earlier works. The label’s second most prominent Ferrari title is Far West News Episodes 2 & 3 (2006, BC16), a document of Luc and Brunhild Meyer-Ferrari’s travels in the American Southwest in 1998. That Ferrari occupied slot BC1 — before Grubbs’ own duo record with Mats Gustafsson at BC2 — indicates the relationship predated the label’s formal launch.

How Grubbs and the Ferraris came to be connected

No published source directly narrates a first meeting between Grubbs and Luc Ferrari. What the available record establishes:

  • By 2019 Grubbs described himself as “a long-time friend of Brunhild’s” (Brunhild Meyer-Ferrari, Luc’s wife and longtime collaborator). The phrasing at the Giorno Poetry Systems event page — “David, a long-time friend of Brunhild’s” — and the 2019 Cafe OTO event (Brunhild Ferrari in conversation with David Grubbs, 13 March 2019) confirm an established personal relationship of some depth. https://giornopoetrysystems.org/222-bowery/events/brunhild-ferrari
  • Grubbs contributed an afterword to the book Luc Ferrari: Complete Works (Ecstatic Peace Library, 2019), indicating sustained scholarly engagement with Ferrari’s output beyond the label releases. https://giornopoetrysystems.org/222-bowery/events/brunhild-ferrari
  • One source (the search aggregate citing the GPS event page) states Grubbs met Brunhild “about 20 years ago” from the vantage of circa 2019 — placing the first contact around 1998–1999, coinciding precisely with the label’s founding. It further notes that at that meeting Grubbs “had just made a radio piece about Alvin Lucier,” which suggests their paths crossed in an electroacoustic / radio-art context rather than a rock or Drag City context. This detail is cited as appearing on the GPS event page but could not be independently verified in the full page text; treat with mild caution as a search-snippet claim. https://giornopoetrysystems.org/222-bowery/events/brunhild-ferrari
  • Grubbs has explicitly listed Ferrari among his musical influences alongside Derek Bailey, Van Dyke Parks, and others, and his interest in electroacoustics (through Gastr del Sol’s musique concrète-adjacent work with Jim O’Rourke) would have made Ferrari a natural point of contact in the late 1990s European experimental circuit.

Why Ferrari at Blue Chopsticks specifically

No interview or liner note states an explicit reason. The circumstantial picture is: Grubbs had a personal friendship with Brunhild Ferrari (and presumably Luc) that developed around 1998–99 in a radio/electroacoustic context; when Grubbs launched Blue Chopsticks — a Drag City imprint for work outside the post-rock orbit — Ferrari reissues were the first thing he put out, suggesting the Ferrari relationship was both the motivation and the proof-of-concept for the label’s scope. The label’s Drag City product page for Interrupteur / Tautologos 3 simply calls it “The first release on David Grubbs’ new label!” without further explanation. https://www.dragcity.com/products/interrupteur-tautologos-3
