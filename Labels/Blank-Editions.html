Overview

Blank Editions was a Hackney, London-based record label founded in 2012 by David S. Blanco and Will Shutes, running for a decade before closing in 2022. Focused on releasing local music, its catalogue spans post-punk, experimental, ambient, and indie rock, with 17 releases concentrated in the 2010s and two more into the early 2020s. The roster included London post-punk band Housewives alongside international figures such as Thurston Moore, hinting at connections reaching beyond the label’s local brief. A sublabel, The Blank Tapes Series, operated alongside the main imprint.

Notes (web)

Thurston Moore connection

The connection was a neighbourhood coincidence, not a business pitch. According to an It’s Nice That profile of David S. Blanco (itsnicethat.com/articles/blank-editions, fetched 2026-11-11), co-founder Will Shutes kept encountering Moore around Stoke Newington (N16, the area that borders Hackney). The definitive meeting happened at the Stoke Newington Whole Foods, where Blanco’s daughter happened to be wearing a Sub Pop onesie. Rather than a formal approach, Blanco simply asked Moore if he fancied coming to a gig by one of the label’s artists — Douglas Hart — which Moore turned out to already be planning to attend. The relationship developed into a genuine friendship: the same profile notes that Moore “babysit[s] David’s kids on occasion.”

This personal connection led directly to Moore’s releases on the label:

  • Detonation b/w Germs Burn (7”, February 2014) — part of the Solo Series, limited to 500 hand-numbered copies; described by Frost Magazine as “two original compositions paying homage to N16 London and international communication actions” (frostmagazine.com, fetched 2026-11-11)
  • Sun Gift Earth (cassette, August 2014) — part of The Blank Tapes Series
  • Chelsea’s Kiss (cassette, September 2016) — limited edition cassette
  • Mx Liberty (7”, c. February 2018) — noted as sold out quickly, consistent with the label’s pattern (louderthanwar.com, fetched 2026-11-11)

The It’s Nice That article also names a tape titled Rosetta as a Blank Editions release, though this does not appear in the catalogue table above — may be a Blank Tapes Series item not captured in the top-19 Discogs list.
