Bearsville Records

Discogs IDs: 11929 (operating label “Bearsville”); 264638 (corporate entity “Bearsville Records, Inc.“) Label Code: LC 2217 / LC 02217 Active: 1970–1984 (corporate entity dissolved 22 February 1991) Location: Woodstock, NY; later offices at 8467 Beverly Blvd., Los Angeles CA 90048, and 75 East 55th St., New York NY 10022

Overview

Bearsville Records was founded in 1970 by Albert Grossman, the veteran music manager behind Bob Dylan, Janis Joplin, Peter, Paul & Mary, and The Band. It was one of the first American independent labels built around a management operation’s existing artist relationships, drawing directly from the Woodstock, NY scene that had coalesced around Grossman’s farm and the nearby recording complex he also operated, Bearsville Studios.

The label launched under the name Bearsville and was incorporated as Bearsville Records, Inc. on 26 July 1971 (initially as “New Bearsville Record Productions, Inc.,” renamed 30 August 1971). Initial distribution was through Ampex Records; after Ampex exited the records business in 1972, Bearsville moved to Warner Bros. Records, which distributed it through closure in 1984. The corporate entity was formally dissolved 22 February 1991.

The early catalog was anchored by Todd Rundgren, whose run of solo albums (Runt, Something/Anything?, A Wizard, A True Star, Todd, Initiation, Faithful, Hermit Of Mink Hollow) made him the label’s most prolific and commercially consistent artist. His band Utopia (5) also recorded for Bearsville throughout the 1970s. Foghat, a British hard rock group, rivaled Rundgren in sales with a string of platinum and gold albums including Fool For The City (1975) and the blockbuster live album Foghat Live (1977).

The Woodstock scene gave Bearsville its distinctive early character. Jesse Winchester recorded his first four albums for the label (1971–1977). Bobby Charles (New Orleans singer-songwriter, real name Robert Guidry) cut his celebrated self-titled 1972 album here. Paul Butterfield’s Better Days recorded two albums in 1973. Paul Butterfield released solo work on the label through 1980. Early presences included Hungry Chuck (1972) and the pre-fame Sparks (under the name Halfnelson, 1971, and as Sparks through A Woofer In Tweeter’s Clothing, 1972). Felix Cavaliere (of The Rascals) recorded there in the mid-1970s.

In the late 1970s and early 1980s the roster broadened: Randy Vanwarmer had a substantial pop hit with “Just When I Needed You Most” (1979); Norma Jean Wright put out disco releases produced by Bernard Edwards and Nile Rodgers (Chic’s production team); NRBQ appeared in 1983; and The dB’s closed out the catalog in 1984 with Like This, the label’s final major release.

House design on many early releases was by Milton Glaser, who had been Grossman’s designer at the predecessor Poppy Records imprint. Alen MacWeeney was the most-credited photographer across the catalog (121 release credits). Peter Corriston handled design on later releases including Foghat’s Live and several Utopia sleeves. Mastering across the label’s run was dominated by Bob Ludwig (254 release credits), with Greg Calbi and Lee Hulko also recurring. House engineering from the label’s early years through the mid-1970s was the work of Nick Jameson, who also briefly joined Foghat as bassist.

Albert Grossman died on 25 January 1986. The Bearsville Studios complex he founded passed to his widow Sally Grossman and remains operational.
