Overview

Nuf Sed was a San Francisco label run by Brandan Kearney, operating from the mid-1980s through the 1990s before closing. Its catalogue spans 12 releases — two from the 1980s and ten from the 1990s — tilted toward experimental, avant-garde, and indie rock, with a roster that included the famously reclusive avant-folk act Caroliner and the Cambridge, Massachusetts post-rock group Cul de Sac. The label is notable for its rotating series of absurdist subtitles (Nuf Sed Lubrication, Nuf Sed Anodyne Liniment, Nuf Sed Natural Tobacco Co., and others) that changed from release to release through the mid-1990s. Its parent entity was formally incorporated as the Nuf Sed Record Company, Inc.

Nuf Sed / Cul de Sac connection — research pass (2026-11-19)

Question: How did Cambridge, MA post-rock band Cul de Sac come to release on Nuf Sed, and was there a specific personal connection between Brandan Kearney and the band?

What is established:

  • Nuf Sed released two Cul de Sac records: the 7” Doldrums / ..His Teeth Got Lost In The Mattress.. (1992, cat. NSFPOS7) and the full-length I Don’t Want To Go To Bed (1995). These are geographically anomalous — Nuf Sed’s roster was otherwise entirely San Francisco-based (Caroliner, World Of Pooh, Thinking Fellers Union Local 282, Sun City Girls, Virginia Dare, Tarnation).
  • Brandan Kearney was deeply embedded in the SF experimental underground: he is described as an “unofficial nineteenth Feller” in the extended family around Thinking Fellers Union Local 282, and ran Nuf Sed as a vehicle for that scene alongside a small fanzine called Nothing Doing. (Trouserpress Thinking Fellers review, snippet)
  • The Cul de Sac 1992 single’s catalogue number (NSFPOS7) places it among a cluster of Nuf Sed releases that year including the Sun City Girls / Thinking Fellers split (NSFPOS5) — suggesting Kearney was already in contact with geographically distant bands by 1992.
  • One early search result snippet (from the Perfect Sound Forever interview context) notes that founding Cul de Sac member Robin Amos had planned “a one-off, farewell show prior to his leaving the Boston area for the west coast.” If Amos relocated to the Bay Area around the time the band formed (c. 1990–91), this could plausibly be how he or Glenn Jones met Kearney — but this connection is not confirmed by any source retrieved. (PSF interview — the page itself was fetched and did not contain the Nuf Sed detail; the information came from search snippet only)
