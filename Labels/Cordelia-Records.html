Self-owned label of Alan Jenkins, guitarist and principal songwriter of The Deep Freeze Mice. Founded in 1984 when Jenkins moved the Mice off his earlier Mole Embalming Records imprint, Cordelia became the home for the Mice’s mature catalog as well as a small but distinctive roster of US cassette-underground and UK indie acts. In the mid-1990s Jenkins opened the Cordelia Records studio in Leicester, recording hundreds of local bands across two-plus decades. The label — still run by Jenkins — has issued well over a hundred releases by Discogs count, with Jenkins stating in interview that the full studio total reaches 106 albums by matrix number.

Overview

Cordelia Records occupies a dual identity: as a releasing imprint it is primarily a vehicle for Jenkins and his extended circle, and as a studio operation it served as a document of the wider Leicester independent scene from the mid-1990s onward. The catalog falls into three loose phases:

  1. 1984–1989 — The vinyl era. The Mole catalog numbers (MOLE 1–3) were retrospective pressings of earlier material; the core catalog runs under the “ERICAT” prefix (Ericat 001–027). This phase covers the Deep Freeze Mice’s Cordelia period plus a cluster of releases by US and UK acts Jenkins found sympathetic: R. Stevie Moore, Rimarimba, Jung Analysts, Zoogz Rift, Chris Sievey, John Trubee, Dolly Mixture, and others.

  2. 1990–2003 — Transition. The Mice dissolved in 1989; Jenkins moved into Jody And The Creams and then The Creams, releasing via Cordelia. The 1994 double LP ie (credited to Alan Jenkins and The Creams) is the era’s centrepiece. The studio opens.

  3. 2004–present — The studio / CD era. Catalog numbers shift to “CD0xx” prefix. R. Stevie Moore returns for a series of Cordelia reissues and compilations (2004–2017). Jenkins’ instrumental surf project The Thurston Lava Tube releases its main catalog here. Zappa-related projects (Zappa Early Renaissance Orchestra, 8 releases), The Kettering Vampires, Culpho Dog Gymkhana, and others fill out the roster. Jenkins continues solo and ensemble releases.

The Chris Sievey Big Record (1986) is an outlier: Sievey, later known as his alter-ego Frank Sidebottom, recorded the LP with a guest appearance by Lisa Stansfield — before her commercial breakthrough — alongside drummer Mike Doherty. Jenkins’ Cordelia was one of the few labels willing to issue his experimental solo work.

The Creams Era (1988–1998)

After the Mice dissolved, Jenkins continued releasing through Cordelia under successive band names:

  • Jody And The Creams (Discogs ID: 365162) — Early incarnation; 4 releases, 1988–1991, including A Big Dog.n (Ericat 028, 1990) and Lords Of The Gromet Canning Factory (1991).
  • The Creams (Discogs ID: 799773) — The band’s main identity. Members: Jenkins, Sherree Lawrence (vocals), Alison Mackinder (vocals, violin), Ariadne Metal-Cream Pie (vocals), Blodwyn P. Teabag (keyboards), Robyn Gibson (drums), and others.
  • Alan Jenkins and The Creams — The 1994 double LP ie (cat. 20106; co-released by Raffmond, Germany) is the most ambitious release of this period: a 31-track, four-sided song-cycle with Jenkins writing all music and words and voicing the character “E & some other animals”. Personnel: Lawrence (vocals), Mackinder (vocals/violin), Blodwyn P. Teabag (keyboards), Robyn Gibson (drums), Andy Nicholls (tenor saxophone), Yukio Yung (backing vocals on one track).

Studio Era and Credits Summary

Jenkins opened the Cordelia Records studio in Leicester in the mid-1990s. Discogs credits document him engineering and mixing for acts on One Little Indian, Pickled Egg Records, Where It’s At Is Where You Are, and Fortuna Pop! across 1995–2001, as well as mixing as late as 2018.

Sublabel: Obscure Independent Classics

The name matches the compilation series Obscure Independent Classics that Jenkins ran as a compiler and coordinator — the credits summary shows Andrew Greenaway (ID: 1883661) as frequent coordinator/compiler, and Jenkins himself as compiler on multiple releases.

Connections

  • Alan Jenkins — founder and owner; runs all operations
  • The Deep Freeze Mice — flagship act; all post-1984 DFM releases
  • Mole Embalming Records — predecessor label; Jenkins’ first imprint (1979–1983)
  • Egg Plant — Jenkins’ third label, co-run with Terry Burrows
  • R. Stevie Moore — key international connection; long-running US underground / cassette artist
  • Rimarimba — Robert Cox’s minimal-electronic project; early Cordelia non-DFM LP
  • Chris Sievey — pre-Frank Sidebottom solo LP; notable Lisa Stansfield guest appearance
  • Rick Willson — primary outside engineer/producer on first two DFM Cordelia LPs
  • Neil Stout — recurring engineer; also session musician on DFM records
