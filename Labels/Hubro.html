Hubro

Norwegian jazz and experimental music label. Discogs label ID: 227734. Label code: LC 49093. Sublabel of Grappa Musikkforlag AS (Discogs ID: 181647). All releases designed by Yokoland.

Bandcamp: https://hubromusic.bandcamp.com

Overview

Hubro is the contemporary jazz and experimental music imprint of Grappa Musikkforlag AS, Norway’s largest independent music company. It is dedicated exclusively to releases from the Norwegian jazz and improvised music scene. The label is distinguished by an exceptionally consistent visual identity — every release is designed by the Oslo studio Yokoland — and by a tight production infrastructure centered on label manager Andreas Meland and engineer/producer Jørgen Træen.

The catalog spans from 2009 to the present, with 363 releases in Discogs (across all pressings) collapsing to approximately 140+ unique master titles. The output is stylistically varied but coherent: folk-jazz influenced by Hardanger fiddle traditions, free improvisation, minimalist composition, post-rock-inflected jazz, contemporary classical crossover, and experimental electronic work. Despite this range, nearly all releases share an acoustic warmth and a commitment to extended instrumental technique.

Hubro is Grappa’s flagship for adventurous contemporary music, occupying a position in the Norwegian scene roughly analogous to what ECM Records holds internationally — with overlapping personnel (notably Jan Erik Kongshaug, who engineered for ECM at Rainbow Studio and appears on several Hubro releases).

Founding and Early Catalog

The label’s first release is Collab (HUBRO CD2502, 2009) by Labfield — a duo of David Stackenäs and Ingar Zach, mixed and mastered by Italian engineer Giuseppe Ielasi. The catalog number sequence beginning “HUBRO CD25xx” indicates a continuous CD series, later joined by an LP series (“HUBRO LP35xx”). The inaugural run through 2012 established the core aesthetic: Norwegian improvisers, often combining folk material with free jazz or minimalist electronics.

The 2011 releases established the label’s identity firmly: 1982 (2)‘s Pintura introduced the Hardanger fiddle–harmonium–drums trio of Nils Økland, Sigbjørn Apeland, and Øyvind Skarbø; Huntsville’s For Flowers, Cars And Merry Wars brought drone-heavy electric guitar improvisation; Splashgirl’s Pressure added slow-building atmospheric piano trio work; Mats Eilertsen’s SkyDive connected the label to the mainstream jazz end of the Norwegian scene.

Aesthetic Identity

Hubro’s catalog resists easy genre classification but clusters around several intersecting traditions:

  • Norwegian folk fusion: the Hardanger fiddle (hardingfele) appears across the catalog — in 1982 (2), Erlend Apneseth’s work, and Benedicte Maurseth’s recent solo releases. Traditional Norwegian instruments including the langeleik and nyckelharpa recur.
  • Free improvisation and chamber jazz: Ballrogg, Ivar Grydeland, Håkon Stene, and collaborators from the European free improv scene.
  • Post-minimalist composition: Splashgirl, Christian Wallumrød, Building Instrument, and Moskus (2) work in the space between composed and improvised music with heavy use of space and texture.
  • Experimental electronics: Jørgen Træen’s own releases, Phonophani, Stein Urheim’s electronic-folk hybrids, and the electronics-inflected work of Cakewalk (4) and Stephan Meidell.
  • Cross-genre collaboration: the catalog regularly pairs Norwegian jazz musicians with folk, contemporary classical, rock, and noise figures — e.g., Jenny Hval with Kim Myhr and Trondheim Jazz Orchestra (In The End His Voice Will Be The Sound Of Paper, 2016); BJ Cole with 1982 (2) (2012); Randall Dunn (production) on several releases.

All releases are recorded in Norway (primarily Oslo and Bergen) and mastered locally. The label does not pursue international co-productions in the way ECM does, though it has released work by foreign musicians active in the Norwegian scene.

Label Manager and Production Team

Andreas Meland — Label Manager / Executive Producer

Andreas Meland (Discogs ID: 97328; real name Andreas Risanger Meland) is the label’s operational head and creative executive. He is credited as producer or executive producer on 63 releases across the catalog — effectively present on nearly every significant Hubro release from the label’s founding through at least 2021. His producing role ranges from hands-off executive oversight to active co-production with the artists.

Meland came to Hubro from the Norwegian experimental/noise scene. He previously ran Safe As Milk and performed in Düplo, Røyskatt, Norwegian Noise Orchestra, Bokfink, and Sort Mel. This background in left-field music informs the label’s openness to noise, electronics, and experimental composition alongside jazz.

His first executive-producer credit on Hubro is 1982 (2)‘s Pintura (2011). He also holds producer or A&R credits on releases from sister labels Odin and Heilo, making him Grappa’s key creative intermediary across its jazz-adjacent catalog.

Jørgen Træen — Engineer / Mixer / Producer / Performer

Jørgen Træen (Discogs ID: 97329; alias: Sir Dupermann) is the label’s most recurring engineer and producer, credited on 71 releases in an engineering role and 31 as producer or co-producer. He operates out of his Bergen-based Luftgrotten studio (credits variously cite “Duper Luftgrotten”, “Luftgrotten”). His role extends beyond the technical: on many Hubro recordings he contributes modular synthesizer overdubs, electronics, and lap steel guitar as a performer.

Træen is also a founding member of Møster! and has group affiliations with Cosmolodic Orchestra, Kaptein Kaliber, House Of Hiss, and Vestnorsk Jazzensemble. His aesthetic sensibility — rooted in Bergen’s experimental scene — shapes much of the label’s sonic texture, particularly the treatment of reverb, room sound, and synthesis on albums by Stein Urheim, Erlend Apneseth Trio, and Skarbø Skulekorps.

Helge Sten — Mastering Engineer

Helge Sten (Discogs ID: 214775; also known as Deathprod) is the label’s primary mastering engineer, credited on 74 Hubro releases. His mastering is a significant factor in the label’s sonic consistency. Sten is also a notable Norwegian experimental musician in his own right.

Visual Identity — Yokoland

Yokoland (Discogs ID: 1842763) — the Oslo design studio run primarily by Aslak Gurholt Rønsen (ID: 2398155) and Thomas Nordby (ID: 2259695) — is credited with artwork and design on 278 Hubro releases, making them the label’s exclusive visual identity provider. The Discogs profile for Hubro states explicitly: “All releases are designed by Yokoland.” Their collage-based, photography-driven aesthetic is as defining a signature as any musical element. Rønsen also takes photography credits on many sleeves; Nordby handles design and photography. The consistency of their output across 15+ years is unusual in contemporary independent labels.

Notable Cross-Label Connections

  • ECM orbit: Jan Erik Kongshaug (Rainbow Studio engineer) worked on Hubro releases including 1982 (2)‘s A/B. Nils Økland releases on both ECM and Hubro. The aesthetic overlap between Hubro and ECM (Norwegian players, acoustic clarity, extended silence) is frequently noted.
  • Rune Grammofon: Several Hubro artists also appear on this Bergen-based experimental label — Nils Økland (Bris, 2004), Jørgen Træen through Møster!. The two labels represent adjacent poles of Norwegian experimental music.
  • International production: Randall Dunn (Seattle-based producer/engineer known for Earth, Sunn O))), Boris) mixed several Hubro releases, bringing an American heavy-drone sensibility to the catalog.
