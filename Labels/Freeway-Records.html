Overview

Freeway Records was a small Los Angeles label active from 1979 into the early 1980s, founded and owned by music writer Harvey Robert Kubernik. Its catalogue of four releases spans spoken word, poetry, punk and hardcore, reflecting Kubernik’s position at the intersection of the city’s literary underground and its emerging punk scene. The roster is notably eclectic — Greg Ginn and Bill Stevenson appear alongside poet Michael C. Ford and producer Ethan James, suggesting the label served as a vehicle for Kubernik’s creative and journalistic connections rather than a conventional commercial imprint.

Kubernik’s background and the Freeway/Black Flag connection

Kubernik served as West Coast Director of A&R at MCA Records 1978–79 (his most cited act: pairing Jimmy Iovine with Tom Petty for Damn the Torpedoes). He founded Freeway Records in the late 1970s alongside this A&R work, with the label oriented toward spoken word and experimental music. His 1982 double album Voices of the Angels (Spoken Words) collected 81 short pieces from LA poets, musicians and performance artists, with liner notes by Ray Manzarek — indicating the label’s primary axis was the city’s literary/underground scene, not hardcore. grokipedia.com/page/harvey_kubernik (quality: derived/aggregated — treat as secondary)

His journalism work (Melody Maker 1975–80, Crawdaddy!, Musician, Los Angeles Times, and others) gave him extensive LA scene access and industry credibility; Wikipedia notes this writing career ran alongside and enabled his transition into production. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Kubernik (as of 2026-11-04)

The Freeway/SST co-releases. Two Black Flag releases are credited as joint Freeway/SST productions:

  1. Family Man (SST 026, 1984) — the full album. Kubernik co-produced the first side (tracks A1–A6, the spoken-word tracks featuring Henry Rollins) alongside Spot and Henry Rollins. Greg Ginn and Bill Stevenson produced the instrumental second side (recorded at Radio Tokyo). Kubernik also wrote the album’s liner notes (dated April 1984, Redondo Beach, CA). The back sleeve states “A Freeway/SST Production.” A note in the credits reads “track ‘Family Man’ appears courtesy of KCRW 89.9 FM’s Deirdre O’Donoghue ‘SNAP’ program,” indicating a radio broadcast connection. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Man_(Black_Flag_album) (as of 2026-11-04)

  2. Keep It In The Family (Discogs ID 1791772) — a Freeway Records 12” compiling tracks from Slip It In and Family Man. Back sleeve states “A Freeway/SST Production.” discogs.com/release/1791772

Was it tied to journalism covering Black Flag? No source directly states that Kubernik’s journalism work covering Black Flag led to the Freeway/SST arrangement. His documented Black Flag coverage has not surfaced in Melody Maker, LA Weekly, Creem or other outlets searched. The likeliest route — given his profile — is that his existing LA scene connections (formed through journalism and the MCA A&R period) and Freeway’s spoken-word focus converged with Black Flag’s own interest in spoken-word/experimental material for Family Man’s first side. The co-production credit and Freeway/SST co-branding suggests a commercial arrangement between the two labels for that release, brokered by Kubernik as producer on that side. No interview or contemporary press source confirming the specific origin of the deal was found after checking: Wikipedia (Kubernik, Family Man, Deirdre O’Donoghue, SST Records), blues.gr Kubernik interview, stephenkpeeples.com Q&A, Kubernik’s Corner (otherworldcottageindustries.com), punkygibbon.co.uk, grokipedia, KCRW profile (returned 429).
