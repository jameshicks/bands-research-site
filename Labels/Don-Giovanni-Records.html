Overview

Don Giovanni Records is a punk label founded in New Brunswick, New Jersey by Zach Gajewski and Joe Steinhardt in 2003. It grew steadily through the 2000s before expanding sharply in the 2010s, which account for more than half of its 200-plus releases and reflect a broadening into indie rock, folk rock, and hardcore alongside its punk core. Screaming Females and their guitarist Marissa Paternoster are among the label’s signature acts, with Laura Stevenson also a prominent name on the roster. Carl Saff and Kyle Gilbride appear as recurring production figures across the catalogue.

Notes (web)

Expansion strategy — from interviews with Joe Steinhardt

Three interviews shed light on how Don Giovanni moved beyond its New Brunswick punk origins. No comparable quotes from Zach Gajewski were found; he left the label around 2012–2013, and all post-departure interviews are Steinhardt-only.

The local-only rule and when it broke. For roughly the first nine years the label operated under an informal regional restriction. Steinhardt told Scene Point Blank (undated, accessed 2026-11-11): “A couple of times Zach and I talked about branching out into bands that weren’t from our area, and every time we talked, 3 bands came up.” The first non-regional signing was Waxahatchee in 2012 (DG-51). Steinhardt explained: “When we heard Katie and Allison moved to NYC and started Bad Banana we wrote them about doing a record but Katie told me they were done and she was working on something new. The songs she sent me were incredible.” (scenepointblank.com)

Tenement as the policy-change landmark. In a New Noise Magazine interview marking the label’s 20th anniversary, Steinhardt framed the 2015 Tenement signing (DG-83) as the moment the regional rule was formally abandoned: “The label only worked with regional bands for many, many years, but there was something so vital about Tenement that we changed our entire policy just to work with them.” (newnoisemagazine.com, accessed 2026-11-11)

Internet and community, not deliberate market expansion. In a 2021 Brooklyn Rail interview Steinhardt described the geographic broadening as an organic result of the internet dissolving regional scenes rather than a planned strategy: “The internet changed things a lot, especially with social media emerging a year or so after Don Giovanni started. It could be for worse, but what [it] did was turn regional scenes into scenes based more around common philosophies and identities.” He added: “I kept kind of documenting that same type of thing which went from being a regional focus to more national because of the community I was in spreading around the country.” (brooklynrail.org, accessed 2026-11-11)

Operational model unchanged through growth. Despite the roster expansion, Steinhardt consistently framed the label as structurally unchanged: “It’s very close to still being the DIY, one-man show that it was when I started it.” He acknowledged the trade-off: “Don Giovanni would do more business if I changed some of the core concepts of how I run the label.” (New Noise Magazine, as above)

Note on co-founder name. The brief named “Cynthia Gajewski” as co-founder; all sources — Discogs, Wikipedia, and every interview found — identify the co-founder as Zach Gajewski. No person named Cynthia Gajewski appears in any source consulted. This may be a brief error; the vault and Discogs entry both use Zach Gajewski.
