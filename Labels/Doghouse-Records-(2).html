Overview

Doghouse Records is a New York City label founded in late 1987 by Dirk Hemsath, originally as a vehicle for releasing material by his own bands, beginning with a 7” from Majority Of One. The catalogue is heavily concentrated in the 1990s and 2000s — 63 and 68 releases respectively — as the label evolved from a self-release outlet into a home for emo, punk, and indie rock acts, most notably The Get Up Kids and The All-American Rejects. The label operated two subsidiary imprints, Doghouse America, Inc. and Doghouse Records Fan Series, and maintained a smaller output into the 2010s.

Notes (web)

Label evolution and transition narrative

Doghouse was founded in late 1987 by Dirk Hemsath while he lived in Toledo, Ohio, expressly to release a 7” from his hardcore band Majority Of One (catalogue DOG 001R, 1988). For its first several years the roster was entirely Hemsath’s own projects and local Toledo-area hardcore acts. The genre shift toward midwest emo and melodic hardcore happened gradually across the early-to-mid 1990s as Hemsath signed bands such as Split Lip, Endpoint, Threadbare, and Chamberlain — acts already operating in the post-hardcore/emo orbit — before landing national-profile artists. Wikipedia, Doghouse Records (as of 2026-10-21); doghouserecords.com/about

No source names a co-founder or a dedicated A&R person beyond Hemsath himself. All evidence points to Hemsath as the sole decision-maker on signings throughout the label’s independent era.

The Get Up Kids signing (~1997)

The Get Up Kids had already built underground Midwestern buzz before Doghouse became involved. After the band self-released the Woodson EP, Doghouse approached the band and offered a two-album contract along with $4,000 to record their debut full-length. The band accepted. Their debut Four Minute Mile (September 1997, recorded in Chicago with producer Bob Weston of Shellac) sold over 40,000 copies in two years — substantial for a small indie — generating major-label interest and distribution problems. Major labels including Sub Pop, Geffen, and Mojo Records circled the band, but Doghouse held them to the contract. The buyout clause stood at $50,000. The band ultimately exited via a deal with Vagrant Records (founded by Rich Egan, who had become the band’s manager), which offered $50,000 and their own Heroes & Villains imprint. Wikipedia, The Get Up Kids (as of 2026-10-21); AV Club oral history, Something to Write Home About

The All-American Rejects signing (~2001)

Tyson Ritter and Nick Wheeler (then a duo) recorded the Same Girl, New Songs EP and mailed it unsolicited to Doghouse Records. According to Wikipedia’s account, an intern at the label rescued the disc from the trash and brought it to the label owner (Hemsath), who then signed the band. The self-titled debut was recorded in 2001 with producer/engineer Tim O’Heir at Mission Sound and Headgear Studio in Brooklyn. Doghouse released it on 15 October 2002. The album attracted DreamWorks Records, who signed the band and re-released it in early 2003; it went platinum in the US and gold in Canada. Wikipedia, The All-American Rejects (as of 2026-10-21); Wikipedia, The All-American Rejects (album) (as of 2026-10-21)

Geographic and business timeline

  • 1987–c.2003: Based in Toledo, Ohio. Hemsath also owned Lumberjack Distribution, which handled Doghouse’s distribution.
  • c.2003–2006: Relocated to Los Angeles, CA. Hemsath acquired Mordam Records, forming Lumberjack Mordam Music Group. In November 2004 Lumberjack Mordam signed a distribution deal with WEA (effective 1 January 2005), making Doghouse an affiliate of Warner Music Group with an upstream clause allowing select Doghouse artists to be promoted to WMG.
  • 2006–present: Relocated to New York City. (Discogs profile lists NYC as the base, consistent with this date.)
  • 2009: Lumberjack Mordam Music Group collapsed. Hypebot

Source for relocation stages: search-result snippet from Doghouse Records – Warner Bros. Fanon Wiki (fan wiki — treat as weak; relocation dates should be verified against a primary source).

Overall transition driver

The transition from Hemsath’s own hardcore bands to a nationally known emo label was organic rather than strategically driven: Hemsath signed bands in adjacent scenes (post-hardcore, midwest emo) throughout the early 1990s, built a reputation, and used his own distribution infrastructure (Lumberjack) to compete nationally. The Get Up Kids signing came via Doghouse approaching a buzzing regional band; the All-American Rejects signing was an unsolicited demo that an intern salvaged. No evidence of a dedicated A&R team — Hemsath appears to have made both signings personally. The WEA/WMG upstream deal (2005) represents the moment the label deliberately sought major-label reach, coinciding with the All-American Rejects’ breakout.
