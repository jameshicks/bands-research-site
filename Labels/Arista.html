Arista Records

Overview

Arista Records was launched in November 1974 when Columbia Pictures Industries, Inc. purchased and renamed Bell Records, installing Clive Davis — who had recently been fired from CBS Records — as president. Named for the New York high school honor society, the label became one of the most commercially dominant American majors of the late 1970s and 1980s, while also maintaining a distinctive taste for adventurous signings across jazz, post-punk, avant-garde, and new wave.

Davis ran Arista as a prestige imprint, hands-on in A&R and frequently credited as executive producer or co-producer on key releases. His instinct for signing artists with critical credibility rather than chart certainty distinguished Arista from most major-label competitors: the same roster that housed Barry Manilow and Whitney Houston also carried Patti Smith, Anthony Braxton, Gil Scott-Heron, Lou Reed, and Iggy Pop.

History

1974–1978: Founding Era

Arista launched with Bell’s existing catalog and artist contracts (including Barry Manilow, Bay City Rollers, and Melissa Manchester) and quickly added artists defining the emerging New York underground. The first wave of significant signings included:

  • Patti Smith — her entire catalog from Horses (1975) through Dream Of Life (1988) appeared on Arista, making it the definitive home of the Patti Smith Group. Robert Mapplethorpe shot the photography on multiple releases, and key production credits went to John Cale (Horses) and Jack Douglas (Easter).
  • Gil Scott-Heron / Gil Scott-Heron & Brian Jackson — signed from Flying Dutchman/Strata-East; their Arista run from 1975 (The First Minute Of A New Day, From South Africa To South Carolina) through 1982 (Moving Target) represents their most critically discussed period.
  • Anthony Braxton — extraordinary for a major label: Arista released Braxton’s most ambitious early work including New York, Fall 1974 (1975), Five Pieces 1975 (1975), Creative Orchestra Music 1976 (1976), For Four Orchestras (1978), and Alto Saxophone Improvisations 1979. These were facilitated through Arista’s jazz division overseen by Steve Backer.
  • Lou Reed — Rock And Roll Heart (1976), Street Hassle (1978), The Bells (1979), Growing Up In Public (1980); a commercially uneven but artistically varied run.
  • The Grateful Dead — Terrapin Station (1977), Shakedown Street (1978), Go To Heaven (1980), Reckoning (1981), In The Dark (1987).
  • Jennifer Warnes — debut releases on Arista through the late 1970s.
  • Heart — Dreamboat Annie (1976).

1979–1983: Corporate Changes and Post-Punk/New Wave Signings

In 1979, Bertelsmann AG (parent of German label Ariola) acquired Arista Records, Inc. From 1983 co-owned with RCA. The label continued its aggressive UK-facing A&R, signing a number of post-punk and early new-wave acts:

  • Simple Minds — early career albums Life In A Day (1979), Real To Real Cacophony (1979), Empires And Dance (1980), Sons And Fascination / Sister Feelings Call (1981) on Arista before moving to Virgin.
  • Stray Cats — US releases including Built For Speed (1982), after their UK breakthrough on Arista/Sire.
  • The Beat (2) (English Beat in the US) — via Go-Feet Records licensing: I Just Can’t Stop It (1980), Wha’ppen? (1981), Special Beat Service (1982).
  • Pete Shelley (Buzzcocks) — solo debut Homosapien (1981) and XL-1 (1983) on Arista.
  • Ministry — debut album With Sympathy (1983), produced by Arif Mardin, represents Ministry’s short-lived synth-pop period before departing for Sire/Warner.
  • A Flock Of Seagulls — their US releases (1982–84) appeared on Arista.
  • Iggy Pop — New Values (1979), Soldier (1980), Party (1981).
  • Graham Parker And The Rumour — several albums including The Up Escalator (1980).
  • Fashion — Fabrique (1982) and surrounding singles.
  • Thompson Twins — major global signing from 1982 through the decade’s end; Into The Gap (1984) was their commercial peak.
  • The Kinks — Sleepwalker (1977) through State Of Confusion (1983).

1984–1995: Height of Commercial Dominance and Leftfield Continuation

The mid-1980s through early 1990s saw Arista become one of the most profitable US labels, with Whitney Houston’s debut (1985), Aretha Franklin’s comeback (Who’s Zoomin’ Who?, 1985), and Dionne Warwick’s late-career revival. But unconventional signings continued:

  • Shriekback — Jam Science (1984), Oil And Gold (1985); post-punk art-dance act formed by ex-members of Gang of Four and XTC.
  • The Danse Society — goth/post-punk: Heaven Is Waiting (1984) on Arista.
  • The Lotus Eaters — No Sense Of Sin (1984).
  • The Church — Australian post-punk/psych; US releases on Arista beginning with Heyday (1985) and Starfish (1988).
  • Spiritualized — Pure Phase (1995) and surrounding singles.
  • The KLF — US releases of The White Room (1991), Justified & Ancient (1991), America: What Time Is Love? (1991).
  • OutKast — signed via LaFace Records (Arista sublabel); debut Southernplayalisticadillacmuzik (1994).
  • Toni Braxton — also via LaFace; Toni Braxton (1993).

1996–2011: Late Period and Dissolution

Arista continued as a prominent major through the late 1990s and 2000s with Sarah McLachlan, Dido, Avril Lavigne, and Santana (Supernatural, 1999 — via BMG). On September 26, 2011, the RCA Music Group was disbanded by Sony. On October 7, 2011, Arista was absorbed into RCA Records alongside LaFace Records, Jive, and J Records. The Arista imprint was revived on July 17, 2018 under Sony Music Entertainment.

Key Personnel

  • Clive Davis — President and founder; credited as executive producer on hundreds of releases across the label’s lifespan; the dominant A&R voice. Discogs producer credit: 1,810 releases.
  • Arif Mardin — Staff producer; produced Ministry’s With Sympathy, Average White Band, and others. Long-tenured Atlantic veteran who worked regularly for Arista.
  • Narada Michael Walden — Prolific in-house producer, particularly for Whitney Houston and Aretha Franklin releases in the mid-1980s through 1990s.
  • Steve Backer — Ran Arista’s jazz division (later Arista Novus); formerly at Impulse! Records; departed in the early 1980s to Windham Hill, then RCA.
  • Robert Mapplethorpe — Not a label employee, but closely associated with Patti Smith’s Arista photography through Horses, Easter, and Wave.
