Blank Tape Studios

Discogs Label IDs: 276626 (Blank Tape Studios) · 2007042 (Blank Tape Inc.) · 592178 (Blank Productions Stamford, Ct. — Connecticut follow-on facility)
Also known as: Blank Tapes; Blank Tape Inc.; B.T.S.; Blank Tapes NYC; Bob Blank Studios; Blank Studios; Blank Tapes Sound Studio; Blank Tapes Recording Studio; Blank Tapes Studio New York; Blank Tapes, N.Y.C.

Overview

Blank Tape Studios was a New York City recording and mixing facility opened in 1976 by engineer, producer, and guitarist Bob Blank. Located on the Lower East Side of Manhattan, the studio operated under a cluster of near-identical names — most commonly “Blank Tapes” — throughout its active decade.

The studio became the functional production home of the ZE Records downtown NYC circle from 1978 through roughly 1983, a period during which Bob Blank engineered or produced the majority of ZE’s catalog alongside his role as an in-house creative collaborator. Key ZE sessions include Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s Press Color (1979), James White & The Blacks’ Off White (1979), Lydia Lunch’s Queen Of Siam (1980), Kid Creole And The Coconuts’ Off The Coast Of Me (1980) and Tropical Gangsters (1982), and James White’s Flaming Demonics (1983). Blank also ran the board for Walter Steding’s self-titled LP (1980, produced by Chris Stein) and the 8 Eyed Spy studio sides (1981).

In parallel with the downtown-rock work, Blank Tape Studios was a major facility for Salsoul Records mixing sessions throughout 1978–1983. Bob Blank mixed or recorded dozens of Salsoul releases — Loleatta Holloway’s Love Sensation (1980), Inner Life, Skyy, Instant Funk, and more — making the studio a crossroads of No Wave, mutant disco, and club-oriented dance music.

An earlier and broader practice also documented at the studio: Bob Blank had been engineering Latin and salsa sessions (Charanga 76, Orquesta Cimarron, multiple Coco Records dates) since at least 1975–1976, before the downtown rock circle arrived. The studio’s first credited Discogs appearance as a physical location is 1975 (Boy Meets Boy).

Bob Blank also recorded Sun Ra’s Lanquidity (1978, Philly Jazz) at the studio.

The studio later operated under the name Blank Productions from Stamford, Connecticut (Discogs label ID 592178, address: 1597 Hope St., Stamford CT). The Connecticut facility followed the NYC studio in Blank’s career but the precise transition date is not established in Discogs data.

The 2010 double-album compilation The Blank Generation (Blank Tapes NYC 1975–1987) (Discogs master 221794; primary releases 2118358 / 2118650 on DJhistory.com / Strut Records) is the primary archival document of the studio’s output. Credited to Bob Blank (as primary artist / compiler) with compilation producers Bill Brewster, Frank Broughton, and Quinton Scott. Mastered by Simon Francis.
