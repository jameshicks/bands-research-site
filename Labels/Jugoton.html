From Discogs

Record label, company, pressing plant, and record store chain founded on 10 July 1947 in Zagreb, Croatia (then Yugoslavia) when the record label Elektroton was nationalized.

Jugoton started pressing microgroove records in 1957 and the first stereo records in 1961. On the 11 September 1963, a larger factory was opened to meet the demands of the growing market, and production numbers increased to 30,000 records a day. In 1971 cassette production started, and by 1982, 20,000 cassettes were manufactured per day. Although it frequently produced licensed releases, Jugoton lowered the number of foreign artists’ releases in favor of Yugoslav artists. The most well known programme departments were Fonoars, Discothalia and Fonolingua. At its peak, Jugoton was the largest record label, pressing plant and chain record store in Yugoslavia. After the Republic of Croatia declared independence from Yugoslavia in 1991, Jugoton formally changed its name to Croatia Records on 1 October 1991.

Overview

Jugoton emerged from the nationalization of the Zagreb record label Elektroton on 10 July 1947 and grew over four decades into Yugoslavia’s dominant music industry institution — simultaneously the country’s largest record label, pressing plant, and retail chain. The operation scaled rapidly: microgroove pressing began in 1957, stereo records followed in 1961, and a larger factory opened in 1963 capable of producing 30,000 records a day, while cassette manufacturing added in 1971 eventually reached 20,000 units daily by 1982. Though Jugoton issued licensed releases from foreign artists, it deliberately weighted its catalogue toward Yugoslav domestic acts, with key programme divisions including Fonoars, Discothalia, and Fonolingua. From the early 1970s its pressing facilities also served smaller domestic labels — including Suzy, Beograd Disk, and RTV Ljubljana — as well as independent outfits, and when Croatia declared independence the company formally renamed itself Croatia Records on 1 October 1991.
