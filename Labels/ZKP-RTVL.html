ZKP RTVL

Oct 22, 20266 min read

ZKP RTVL

Discogs ID: 223501
Yugoslav / Slovenian state record label, sublabel of RTV Ljubljana (RTV Ljubljana = Radio-Television Ljubljana). Full name: Založba kaset in plošč RTV Ljubljana (Publishing House of Cassettes and Records, RTV Ljubljana). Active as a releasing entity with 2,254 releases documented in Discogs. Released Begnagrad’s debut LP Begnagrad (1982, cat no. LD 0811).

Later became ZKP RTVS (ZKP RTV Slovenija) after Slovenian independence (1991).

History and cultural role

Founding and early years (1970–1974)

The label originated on 28 November 1970 as Kasetna produkcija RTV Ljubljana, a cassette-only imprint attached to Radio Television Ljubljana. The first releases — recordings by Plesni Orkester RTV Ljubljana and Slovenski Oktet — appeared in December 1970. The operation was established and managed by Jure Robežnik and Dušan Velkaverh of Radio Ljubljana; editorial producers were Tomaž Tozon (folk and choral), Teodor Korban (classical), and Ivo Umek (pop and rock). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZKP_RTLJ, as of 2026-10-22]

Following the introduction of vinyl production the imprint was renamed Produkcija kaset in plošč RTV Ljubljana in 1974, then consolidated and rebranded as ZKP RTLJ (Založba kaset in plošč RTV Ljubljana) the same year. This is the form that appears on most Yugoslav-era releases, though the variant “ZKP RTVL” also circulated. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZKP_RTLJ, as of 2026-10-22; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZKP_RTVS, as of 2026-10-22]

Yugoslav-era significance (1974–1990)

By the end of the 1970s ZKP RTLJ had grown into the third-largest record label in Yugoslavia, behind PGP-RTB (Belgrade) and Jugoton (Zagreb), competing also with Jugodisk (Belgrade), Suzy (Zagreb), and Diskoton (Sarajevo). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZKP_RTLJ, as of 2026-10-22]

The culture.si / culture.gov.si encyclopedia entry (RTV Slovenia Discography Unit) describes the organisation as “one of the most powerful publishing groups in the 1970s and 1980s” within Yugoslavia, covering “all music genres: rock, pop, folk music, jazz, classical music, opera, ballet, and chanson.” [source: https://culture.gov.si/en/Discography_Unit,_RTV_Slovenia, as of 2026-10-22]

By 1991 (just after the RTVL→RTVS transition) the label was producing approximately two million audio cassettes annually, 70,000 videocassettes, 50,000 vinyl records, and 50,000 laser discs, with 63.7% comprising domestic programming. Its first CD of Slovenian popular music was issued in 1989. The final cassette in the catalog appeared in December 2006 (equipment for duplication sold 2008); across its full history 2,378 cassette editions were released. [source: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalo%C5%BEba_kaset_in_plo%C5%A1%C4%8D_RTV_Slovenija, as of 2026-10-22]

As an authorized publisher of foreign titles for the Yugoslav market, ZKP RTLJ also licensed and released international artists including Prince, Bruce Springsteen, Depeche Mode, and Motörhead. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZKP_RTLJ, as of 2026-10-22]

Punk and new wave output

ZKP RTLJ was the primary outlet for the Ljubljana punk and new wave scene in the late 1970s and early 1980s. Key releases and artists:

  • Pankrti — the first Slovenian punk band, formed 1977, released most of their studio catalog on ZKP RTLJ: Dolgcajt (1980), Namesto tebe (1981), Državni ljubimci (1982, cat. LD 0748), Svoboda (1982), Rdeči (1984), Pesmi sprave (1985), Sexpok (1987). Their debut Lublana je bulana (1978) was on ŠKUC. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankrti, as of 2026-10-22]
  • Videosex — synth-pop act; ZKP RTLJ released their debut Videosex ‘84 (1984), which established the band’s “nationwide popularity” in Yugoslavia. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videosex, as of 2026-10-22]
  • Buldozer — Slovenian prog-rock/satirical band (formed 1975); ZKP RTLJ was one of their labels. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buldožer, as of 2026-10-22]

The landmark compilation Novi Punk Val (New Punk Wave, 1981, ZKP RTLJ) documented the Yugoslav punk and new wave scene with material recorded 1978–1980. Its 15 tracks featured Pankrti, Prljavo Kazaliste, Paraf, Termiti, Problemi, Berlinski zid, Grupa 92, and Buldogi. Alongside Paket aranžman and Artistička radna akcija (both Belgrade-centred), Novi Punk Val is considered a defining document of the Yugoslav punk and new wave era. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Punk_Val, as of 2026-10-22]

Avant-garde and experimental output

ZKP RTLJ released several key works outside mainstream pop:

  • Begnagrad — avant-prog/jazz-folk ensemble formed in Ljubljana 1976; their self-titled debut LP Begnagrad (1982, cat. LD 0811) was released on ZKP RTLJ. The band fused rock, jazz, free improvisation and traditional folk material; they were a prominent act of the 1970s–early 1980s Yugoslav rock scene and performed at Belgrade’s Students’ Cultural Center alongside Buldožer and Pankrti. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begnagrad, as of 2026-10-22]
  • Marko Brecelj — experimental/deconstructivist composer; his debut solo album Cocktail (1974, ZKP RTLJ) featured “an unusual combination of avant-garde and schlager music” arranged by Bojan Adamic and performed by the RTV Ljubljana Dance Orchestra. Brecelj had also led Buldozer. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marko_Brecelj, as of 2026-10-22]
  • Vinko Globokar — composer associated with the label; named in the Slovenian-language Wikipedia article on ZKP RTVS. [source: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalo%C5%BEba_kaset_in_plo%C5%A1%C4%8D_RTV_Slovenija, as of 2026-10-22]

The label also released recordings by the RTV Slovenia Symphony Orchestra and RTV Slovenia Big Band, covering the full classical and jazz spectrum of the broadcaster’s output. [source: https://culture.gov.si/en/Discography_Unit,_RTV_Slovenia, as of 2026-10-22]

Transition to ZKP RTVS (1990–1991)

When RTV Ljubljana was officially reconstituted as DO RTV Slovenija on 24 April 1990, the label began rebranding to ZKP RTVS (Založba kaset in plošč RTV Slovenija). The new logo appeared on cassette releases through the second half of 1990, often alongside the older RTVL branding. Vinyl records pressed by Jugoton continued to carry the RTVL logo into early 1991. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZKP_RTVS, as of 2026-10-22]

Post-independence the label continued as a leading Slovenian publisher. Around 2010 it adopted the expanded designation “Založba RTV Slovenija – Založba kakovostnih programov – ZKP RTV SLO,” and later informally shortened to “Založba kakovostnih programov” (Publishing House of Quality Programs). [source: https://sl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalo%C5%BEba_kaset_in_plo%C5%A1%C4%8D_RTV_Slovenija, as of 2026-10-22]
