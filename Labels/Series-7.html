Series 7

Discogs ID: 338968. Sublabel of Bella Union. Parent label ID: 2201.

An instrumental album series conceived and curated by Simon Raymonde for Bella Union, launched 2001. The concept: seven instrumental CDs, by seven different artists, each exactly seven tracks long, each recorded in seven days (from conception to completion), culminating in a limited-edition compilation of all seven artists with a bonus track each. The series ended after five releases rather than the planned seven; the sixth and seventh commissions were apparently never completed. No public statement from Raymonde or Bella Union explains why the series stopped at five. [Sources: verian.wordpress.com; discogs.com release 59448]

Concept

  • Dedicated format mandate: instrumental music only
  • Seven tracks per album
  • Each album recorded in seven days, from conception to completion
  • Seven artists planned in total
  • Final planned release: a compilation of all seven artists with one bonus track each
  • Completed: five of the planned seven releases (Parts 1–5)
  • The sixth and seventh artists were never identified publicly; their releases were never made

Notes on individual releases

Part 1 — Gwei-Lo: Band formed from the renamed Smegma (changed name late 1997); signed by Simon Raymonde. Album recorded at September Sound (tracks 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 in January 1999) and Jigsaw Studios (tracks 4, 5). [Source: thejohnsonsound.com/gwei-lo/]

Part 2 — Rothko: Track 7 features a Harold Budd sample (“Boy About Ten” from By The Dawn’s Early Light). [Source: discogs.com/release/198631]

Part 3 — Bonnevill: Instrumental duo Mick Turner (Dirty Three) and Jessica Billey (violin, piano). Seven tracks. Guest: Joel Silbersher (guitar on “Camoe”), Brian Freeman (cello, “Ides”), Jesse Poe (percussion, “Ides”). [Source: discogs.com/release/170188; iceburn666.blogspot.com]

Part 4 — Departure Lounge: Tim Keegan, Jake Kyle, Chris Anderson, Lindsay Jamieson. Recorded spring 2001 at Hum Depot (an old converted wooden house in Nashville on the site of a Civil War burial ground) and at Mar and Jill’s apartment in Nashville. [Source: verian.wordpress.com]

Part 5 — Jack Dangers: Meat Beat Manifesto’s Jack Dangers. Seven instrumental tracks: “Nano”, “Every Technique”, “No Secrets No Surprises”, “Zxero”, “Short Heavies”, “3:00 min”, “Echo in Space”. Lynn Farmer plays drums on tracks 2, 3, 4. [Source: brainwashed.com/…bella705.php; discogs.com/release/59448]

Why it stopped at 5

No public explanation has been found. The series stopped without announcement. A 2016 fan write-up notes the situation as “sketchy” — the sixth and seventh artists were never publicly identified. [Source: verian.wordpress.com]

Web research notes (2026-10)

A targeted web trawl found no interview, press release, or fan discussion where Raymonde or Bella Union explains why the series stopped. Searches covered: Stereogum, Line of Best Fit, Long Live Vinyl, Loud and Quiet, Drowned in Sound, New Noise Magazine, and general searches for the series. None mention Series 7 by name.

A second trawl (2026-10) additionally checked: Line of Best Fit “nine pivotal moments” retrospective (thelineofbestfit.com), New Noise Magazine in-depth interview (newnoisemagazine.com), Raymonde memoir Q&A (musicbookclub.substack.com), and sixeyes.blogspot.com Bella Union retrospective. None mention Series 7 or explain the series ending. The Line of Best Fit piece includes general quotes about early financial difficulties (“We weren’t making any strides forward and there wasn’t money to invest in the bands”) but these are not specific to Series 7.

A third trawl (2026-10) checked two 2025 interviews not covered previously: SF Sonic book-tour interview (sfsonic.com/interviews/simon-raymonde-interview/) and Logan Sounds Off interview March 2025 (logansoundsoff.com). Neither mentions Series 7 or any discontinued early-period label project. A Clash Music “Rock & Rules” piece (January 2025) was also attempted but the page returned only JavaScript shell content and could not be read. The Spotify/Price of Music podcast episode (January 2025) was not fetched (Spotify podcast pages are inaccessible via WebFetch). No new sources naming the reason for the series ending were found.

Indirect context: In a 2017 Long Live Vinyl retrospective, Raymonde said of Bella Union’s early years: “The early years were a bit odd, and we didn’t really know what we were doing. Some of the releases were awful, but things slowly started to make sense.” He also noted that for the label’s first four years “he didn’t have a clue about much and made a ton of mistakes.” (longlivevinyl.net) This provides general context for an ambitious early-period concept going unfinished, but is not a specific explanation for Series 7. The label also faced serious financial difficulties around 2007–2008 (distributor Pinnacle bankruptcy, V2 collapse), but those post-date the series by several years.

The most plausible inferences — financial pressure on an early-stage label, artist non-delivery, or a decision to pivot away from the concept — remain unconfirmed. No source names the reason. This question is unlikely to be resolvable without a direct interview with Raymonde or a Bella Union insider from that period.
