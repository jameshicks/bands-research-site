Audio Arts

Full name: Audio Arts Cassettes (later Audio Arts Magazine) Based: London, UK Active: 1973–2006 Discogs label ID: 56336 Sublabels: Audio Arts Supplement (ID: 898052) Founded by: William Furlong and Barry Barker Legal entity: Eo Ipso Ltd.

Overview

Audio Arts was a British sound magazine published primarily on audio cassettes, documenting contemporary artistic activity through artist or curator interviews, sound performances, and sound art. Founded in 1973 by William Furlong (born 1944, Woking) and Barry Barker, the label published 25 volumes of 4 issues each between 1973 and 2006 — later also releasing LPs and CDs. It operated from several addresses in London (Carnwath Road SW6, then Gauden Road SW4, then Briarwood Road SW4) under Furlong’s editorship.

Furlong conducted all interviews from the outset until 1996, when Professor Jean Wainwright took on the interviewer role. Each issue began with “I am here with…” followed by the artist’s name and recording location. The label’s interviewees form a who’s who of international conceptual and performance art: Joseph Beuys, Andy Warhol, Yoko Ono, R. Buckminster Fuller, Gilbert & George, Richard Hamilton, Gerhard Richter, Nam June Paik, Hermann Nitsch, Mario Merz, John Cage, Laurie Anderson, Damien Hirst, Anish Kapoor, and many others — as well as a recording of W. B. Yeats’ daughter and readings by Yeats himself (Vol. 1 No. 4, 1974).

Furlong was part of a generation of British artists of the 1960s–70s moving from traditional art forms toward conceptual art, performance, new media, and process-oriented practice — a circle that included Gilbert & George, Richard Hamilton, Bruce McLean, and Paul Richards (9) (whose Nice Style performance group was the first “pose band”). Audio Arts was his primary vehicle for documenting that international scene.

In 2004, Tate acquired the Audio Arts archive — over 200 boxes of master tapes — and a selection was exhibited at Tate Britain from March to August 2007. The archive has since been fully catalogued, digitized, and preserved there. A retrospective exhibition curated by Lucia Farinati took place in Rome’s Sound Art Museum in October–December 2006, with a new Furlong sound art piece, Conversation Pieces, made by remixing and cutting-up prior interviews. (Source: Wikipedia)

The sublabel Audio Arts Supplement (Discogs ID: 898052) ran as a series of additional releases within the main Audio Arts framework; it shows no separate releases in Discogs.

Notable Featured Artists

Beyond the interview/editorial format, the following artists appear as performers or subjects across multiple Audio Arts releases:

  • Joseph Beuys — 3 releases (Vol. 2 No. 1, 1975; V&A interviews, 1983)
  • Bruce McLean — 3 releases (performance works, 1977–1982)
  • Ian Breakwell — 4 releases (1978–1995)
  • Stephen Willats — 3 releases (1976–1983)
  • Gerhard Richter — 2 releases (1989, 1996)
  • David Toop — 2 releases (1980, 1995)
  • Hugh Davies — 2 releases (1980, 1995)
  • Dan Graham — featured on At Riverside Studios London (1979) and Live To Air (1982)
  • Dieter Roth — 2 releases (1978, 1981)
  • Michael Nyman — performer and editorial assistant on 2–3 releases

The Live To Air box set (1982) is the most expansive document of the label’s international reach, featuring Glenn Branca, The Static, Dan Graham, Lawrence Weiner / Peter Gordon, Vito Acconci, Marina Abramović (as Uwe Laysiepen / Marina Abramovic), Sonic Youth (credited under the Dan Graham track), Julia Heyward, Pat Irwin, David Cunningham, Jack Goldstein, and many others.
