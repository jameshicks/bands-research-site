Elektra Records

American record label. Discogs label ID: 651. Founded December 1950 in New York City by Jac Holzman (born Jac Easton Holzman). Initially incorporated as the Elektra-Stratford Record Corp. Sold to Warner Communications Inc. (WCI) in 1970 for $10 million, with Holzman agreeing to remain for three years. In 1974 merged with Asylum Records (Discogs ID: 33574) under WCI, forming the Elektra/Asylum imprint. Later folded into the Atlantic Records Group under Warner Music Group (Discogs parent ID: 2345).

Overview

Elektra began as a small New York folk and ethnic music label in the early 1950s, recording artists like Josh White, Theodore Bikel, Oscar Brand, and Tom Rush. By the mid-1960s Holzman pivoted toward rock and blues, signing The Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1965), Love (the band) (1966), The Doors (1967), and Tim Buckley (1966). A West Coast A&R operation centered on producer Paul A. Rothchild and engineer Bruce Botnick handled most of the major rock recordings. Holzman also started Nonesuch (Discogs ID: 652) in 1964 as a budget classical sublabel.

In 1969, Elektra made two confrontational signings outside its psychedelic-folk niche: MC5 (Kick Out the Jams, 1969, produced live at the Grande Ballroom) and The Stooges (The Stooges, 1969; Fun House, 1970). Both were dropped within two years. Nico’s The Marble Index (1968) was also an Elektra release, produced by John Cale — a one-off connection to the Velvet Underground world.

After the WCI sale the label broadened dramatically: singer-songwriters (Carly Simon, Harry Chapin, Jackson Browne via Asylum), art-rock (Queen, Yes), and new wave. Television released both their albums on Elektra — Marquee Moon (1977) and Adventure (1978) — a rare CBGB-scene signing on a major. Iggy Pop affiliate Public Image Limited had a brief US Elektra deal (1984–86: This Is What You Want…, Album). The Cure (from 1984), Pixies (1989–1993), and 10,000 Maniacs (from 1985) were 1980s/90s alt-rock signings of lasting note. The label also signed The Sugarcubes (1988), making it Björk’s first major-label home.
