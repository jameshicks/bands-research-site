Les Disques du Crépuscule

Founded: Brussels, Belgium, 1980 Active: 1980–2004 (dormant); revived 2012–present Catalog prefix: TWI (from Twilight — French: crépuscule) Associated imprint: Factory Benelux

Founders

  • Michel Duval — journalist; later worked in music publishing at Virgin and Because Music
  • Annik Honoré (12 October 1957, Mons – 3 July 2014) — journalist; coined the name Crépuscule; partner of Ian Curtis (Joy Division) in 1979–80; relationship dramatized in Anton Corbijn’s Control (2007, played by Alexandra Maria Lara); left the music industry 1985; worked subsequently as civil servant for the European Commission’s Research and Innovation department; died of cancer
  • Benoît Hennebert — co-founder and in-house design director; designed approximately three-quarters of all Crépuscule products; as Factory had Peter Saville, Crépuscule had Hennebert — but where Saville worked in cool Constructivist modernism, Hennebert used Ligne Claire (the clean-outline style of Hergé’s Tintin, itself a Belgian form) combined with 1950s jazz cover aesthetics and James Bond pastiche; recurring motifs: red wine glasses, ashtrays, houseplants, Hollywood poster illustration

Origin Story

Duval and Honoré had been promoting and organizing concerts in Brussels at Plan K — a former sugar refinery on Rue de Manchester, approximately 4,300 square metres, converted into a cultural venue. It opened 16 October 1979 with Joy Division and Cabaret Voltaire performing — the first Joy Division concert ever outside the UK, and the live debut of “Love Will Tear Us Apart.” William S. Burroughs also appeared that night. This is where Honoré met Ian Curtis.

Within six months of opening, Plan K had hosted Echo and the Bunnymen, Orange Juice, Josef K, Teardrop Explodes, The Slits, The Pop Group, James Chance, The Human League, and Winston Tong — establishing Brussels as a node on the European post-punk circuit rather than a peripheral stop.

Tony Wilson visited Plan K on 26 April 1980 (A Certain Ratio, Section 25, Eric Random performed). That visit led directly to Factory Benelux.

Through this relationship with Tony Wilson and Factory Records, Duval and Honoré set up Factory Benelux as a sister label — releasing Factory acts in the Benelux territories. Their first precursor publication was the fanzine Plein Soleil (June 1980). The first Factory Benelux release was A Certain Ratio’s “Shack Up” 7” (August 1980), carrying a joint Factory Benelux / Les Disques du Crépuscule identity.

Les Disques du Crépuscule grew out of this as a fully independent label with its own broader roster — bringing in non-Factory artists from across Europe and beyond. Factory Benelux and Crépuscule shared premises and staff but operated as separate imprints.

Philosophy and Character

Crépuscule occupied a distinctive position: not post-punk exactly, not new wave, not classical — but an aesthetically unified space across all of these. The roster combined:

  • Post-punk and industrial (Cabaret Voltaire, Josef K, The Names, Thick Pigeon)
  • Minimalist and neo-classical composition (Michael Nyman, Gavin Bryars, Wim Mertens)
  • Ambient and art-pop (Bill Nelson, Tuxedomoon, Blaine L. Reininger, Anna Domino)
  • Continental new wave (Malaria!, Antena)
  • Jazz-influenced (Mikado)
  • Spoken word and interview material (Brian Eno, Jeanne Moreau)

The unifying thread was Hennebert’s visual identity — sleeve designs that gave the catalog a coherent, elegant look regardless of genre. The label became particularly popular in Japan, where the aesthetics translated powerfully.

From Brussels With Love (TWI 007, November 1980)

The founding document — a cassette journal in a PVC wallet, 78 minutes, 21 tracks. Curated by Duval, Honoré, and Wim Mertens. Not a random sampler but a shaped statement of intent: post-punk, minimalism, ambient, and speech as equals.

Sub-labels

  • Factory Benelux — shared imprint for Factory Records Benelux releases; ceased new releases 1988; revived 2013
  • Operation Twilight (1982–83) — UK sublabel run by Patrick Moore, who later adopted the name Philip Hoare (the British cultural writer and author of Leviathan or, The Whale (2008) and Oscar Wilde’s Last Stand (1997)). Moore/Hoare was working at Rough Trade at the time; Geoff Travis and Michel Duval devised the arrangement in January 1982. He chose “Philip Hoare” — his confirmation name plus his mother’s maiden name — to avoid confusion with the astronomer Patrick Moore. Artists launched through Operation Twilight included the Pale Fountains, Paul Haig, Antena, and Tuxedomoon. Wikipedia: Philip Hoare
  • Operation Afterglow (1985–86) — UK editions of Belgian releases
  • Crépuscule Au Japon (1983–2002) — Japanese editions; the label had a devoted Japanese following
  • Crépuscule Section Française, Crépuscule America, Another Side (1984–87), Les Images du Crépuscule (video)
  • L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords (1984–89) — Crépuscule-affiliated imprint for industrial and post-industrial material, run independently by Marc Monin; manufacturing and distribution via Crépuscule, funding later passed to distributor Himalaya. Roster: Coil, Current 93, Nurse With Wound, Laibach, The Hafler Trio, Organum, 23 Skidoo, Sol Invictus, Robert Haigh, Lustmord. Approximately 25 vinyl releases (LAY 01–30 with gaps) plus 5 CD reissues. The name is a Thelemic reference. See L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords for full catalog. Note: Earlier versions of this entry listed Death in June and Psychic TV as LAYLAH artists — both are unconfirmed; neither appears in the Wikipedia discography of the label. Death in June released on their own NER imprint; Psychic TV on Some Bizzare/Temple Records.

Interferences

Crépuscule’s physical cultural outpost in Brussels. A café/bar that functioned as a label annex — live performances, screenings, art events. Opened 22 December 1983 at 1 Rue de la Tête d’Or, Brussels. Interior designed by Benoît Hennebert — an extension of his visual world into three dimensions. Analogous function to the Haçienda in Manchester: a label creating its own venue rather than relying on existing infrastructure. Ran through the mid-1980s.

Later History

1990s: Roster expanded to include Ultramarine, Cathy Claret, The Jazz Passengers, John Cale. Label relocated to Paris, concentrating on soundtracks and the French domestic market.

2004: Became dormant. Duval moved into music publishing.

2012–13: Revived by James Nice of LTM Recordings, with Duval and Honoré’s blessing. Nice had worked at Crépuscule in Brussels from 1987 — leaving specifically over a 23 Skidoo contract dispute — and returned as curator three decades later. New releases: Helen Marnie, Marsheaux, 23 Skidoo (reconciliation), Penelope Queen (daughter of Isabelle Antena), Fovea Hex (Irish ambient/experimental group). Factory Benelux revived simultaneously with Section 25, The Wake, The Names, Mark Reeder, Blurt.

Ongoing: Continues to issue reissues and occasional new material. Stephen Mallinder’s Clinker (with Benge and LoneLady, TWI 1256) released 2021.
