Creation Records

Creation Records

Discogs ID: 1658
Parent entity: Creation Records Ltd. (Discogs ID: 105755)
Address: 10 Westgate Street, London E8 3RN, England (early); later 109X Regents Park Road NW1 (from 1995)
Active: 1983–1999 (revived briefly 2011 for a compilation)
Catalog prefixes: CRE (singles), CRELP (LPs), CRECD (CDs)
Label code: LC 6306
Founded by: Alan McGee, Dick Green (Richard Green), Joe Foster

London independent record label, one of the defining institutions of British indie music from the mid-1980s through the Britpop era. Founded by Alan McGee, Dick Green, and Joe Foster — all originally from the Glasgow post-punk milieu. The label’s career arc runs from scrappy C86-adjacent jangle pop through shoegaze and dance-rock crossover to the commercial peak of Oasis’s Be Here Now (1997), followed by rapid decline and closure in 1999. At its peak in the mid-1990s, Creation was among the most commercially successful independent labels in British history; its catalog spans over 4,600 Discogs entries.

History

Origins and the Creation Artifact imprint (1983–1984)

Creation grew out of Creation Artifact, the imprint McGee and associates ran before the main label was formally established. The first Creation release was CRE 001 (“‘73 in ‘83” by The Legend!, 1983). Early signings were drawn from a loose London/Glasgow network of jangly post-punk bands: Biff Bang Pow! (McGee’s own group), The Jasmine Minks, The Loft, The Pastels, and Revolving Paint Dream. Production duties in this period fell heavily to Joe Foster.

The company was incorporated as Creation Records Ltd. on 4 December 1986, at 21 Wigmore Street, London W1.

The Jesus and Mary Chain and the noise-pop breakthrough (1984–1986)

The single that put Creation on the map was “Upside Down” by The Jesus And Mary Chain (CRE 012, 1984 / 1985), produced by Joe Foster. The JAMC’s debut Psychocandy (1985) appeared on Blanco Y Negro rather than Creation directly, but their early singles were Creation’s most commercially significant early releases. The association with the Reid brothers established Creation’s identity as a home for abrasive, feedback-drenched guitar music dressed in pop songwriting.

Other mid-1980s roster highlights include Felt (Lawrence’s bedroom-pop songwriting project, six albums on Creation 1986–1990), The Weather Prophets, The Bodines, and early records from Momus and Nikki Sudden. Bill Drummond released The Man (CRELP 014, 1990) on Creation during the KLF period.

Joe Foster left Creation in 1985 but returned periodically as a producer. His Slaughter Joe solo project also appeared on the label (CRE 019, 1985; CRELP 084, 1990).

My Bloody Valentine and the shoegaze era (1988–1992)

The signing of My Bloody Valentine in 1988 was the most consequential of McGee’s career. Their Creation debut Isn’t Anything (CRELP 040, 1988) — produced by the band themselves — defined the template for wall-of-guitar shoegaze. Loveless (CRELP 060, 1991) followed three years and enormous studio costs later, going massively over budget and nearly bankrupting the label. Its production style (layered reverse-tremolo guitars, pitch-shifted samples) was engineered primarily by Alan Moulder, who would become one of the label’s key recurring engineers.

The shoegaze generation at Creation also included:

  • Ride — formed Oxford 1988; debut Nowhere (CRELP 074, 1990), Going Blank Again (CRELP 124, 1992); disbanded 1996, reformed 2014
  • Slowdive — Reading, formed 1989; Just For A Day (CRELP 094, 1991), Souvlaki (CRELP 139, 1993), Pygmalion (CRELP 168, 1995); dropped by Creation after Pygmalion
  • Swervedriver — Oxford; Raise (CRELP 093, 1991), Mezcal Head (CRELP 143, 1993), Ejector Seat Reservation (CRELP 157, 1995)
  • The Telescopes — Birmingham; two albums on Creation 1990–1992

Key engineers across this period: Alan Moulder (117 Creation releases), Andy Wilkinson (202 releases), Anjali Dutt (90 releases), Steve Nunn (87 releases).

Screamadelica and the dance-rock crossover (1991)

The other defining moment of the early 1990s for Creation was Primal Scream’s Screamadelica (CRELP 076, 1991), produced by Andrew Weatherall and recorded over 1990–91. Primal Scream had been on Creation from their first single (CRE 017, 1985 — “All Fall Down”) and the label’s most consistent artist across all time periods; the Discogs roster counts 426 releases by Primal Scream on Creation, the highest of any act. Screamadelica fused post-acid house groove with gospel, dub, and psychedelia, winning the inaugural Mercury Prize in 1992 and transforming the label’s commercial profile.

Andrew Innes and Robert Young (2) (Throb) are co-credited as writers on nearly every Primal Scream release on Creation — each with 176 writing credits across the catalog. Martin Duffy (keyboards) appears in 110 releases in various capacities.

Sony partnership and the Britpop years (1992–1996)

In 1992 Sony Music acquired a 49% stake in Creation Records Ltd., giving Creation distribution and cash in exchange for equity. The arrangement funded the label’s expansion into the Britpop mid-90s without making it a fully major-label operation. Sony-licensed releases carried 7-digit catalog numbers prefixed SCR.

Key signings and releases in this period:

  • Teenage Fanclub — Glasgow; Bandwagonesque (CRELP 106, 1991), Thirteen (CRELP 144, 1993), Grand Prix (CRELP 173, 1995), Songs From Northern Britain (CRELP 196, 1997)
  • The Boo Radleys — Liverpool; Everything’s Alright Forever (CRELP 120, 1992), Giant Steps (1993), Wake Up! (CRELP 179, 1995), C’Mon Kids (CRELP 194, 1996)
  • Super Furry Animals — Cardiff; Fuzzy Logic (CRELP 190, 1996), Radiator (CRELP 214, 1997), Guerrilla (CRELP 242, 1999)
  • Sugar (Bob Mould) — Copper Blue (CRELP 129, 1992), Beaster (CRELP 153, 1993), File Under: Easy Listening (CRELP 172, 1994)
  • Saint Etienne — Good Humor (CRELP 225, 1998)
  • The House Of Love — The House Of Love (CRELP 034, 1988); moved to Fontana for their major breakthrough but remained Creation-associated
  • Adorable — Fake (CRELP 165, 1994)
  • Bernard Butler — People Move On (CRELP 221, 1998); Friends And Lovers (CRELP 248, 1999)

Oasis (Discogs: Oasis (2)) signed to Creation in 1993 and released their debut Definitely Maybe (CRE LP 169, 1994) — at the time the fastest-selling debut album in UK chart history. (What’s The Story) Morning Glory? (CRELP 189, 1995) became one of the best-selling albums in UK history and fundamentally altered Creation’s financial scale. Be Here Now (CRELP 219, 1997) sold extremely well on pre-orders but received a critical backlash and marked the beginning of Oasis’s decline. Production on the Oasis catalog was dominated by Owen Morris (153 Creation releases as producer/mixer) and Noel Gallagher himself (134 producer credits).

The in-house sleeve design across the Britpop era was dominated by Brian Cannon (133 Creation releases) and his Microdot (4) studio (66 releases), and photographer Michael Spencer Jones (113 photography credits), who handled most Oasis and Primal Scream sleeves.

Late period and closure (1997–1999)

Following the commercial disappointment of Be Here Now and amid Oasis’s internal tensions, the label entered decline. Alan McGee publicly discussed his drug problems and withdrew from day-to-day management. The Jesus and Mary Chain released Munki (CRELP 232, 1998) — their final album and last Creation release — and dissolved in 1999. Primal Scream’s Vanishing Point (CRELP 178, 1997) and Echo Dek (CRELP 224P, 1997) marked a return to dub-influenced experimentation; Exterminator / XTRMNTR (CRELP 239, 2000) appeared after the official label closure.

McGee announced Creation’s closure in July 1999. In 2000, he resigned as director of Creation Records Ltd. and sold the remaining assets to Sony Music.

Post-Creation (2000 onward)

McGee founded Poptones in 1999, running it through the mid-2000s with a roster including Swervedriver, The Hives, and The Coral. Dick Green co-founded Wichita Recordings in 2000 with Mark Bowen (Creation’s former A&R man). Joe Foster co-founded Poptones with McGee and later ran Rev-Ola (a reissue imprint).

A 2011 compilation Upside Down (Discogs release ID 4008657) was issued under the Creation name, drawing from the label’s catalog.

Dick Green — role and post-Creation activity

Role at Creation Records (1983–1999)

Dick Green (Richard Green) was co-founder and the label’s primary business and administrative manager throughout Creation’s existence. He had no formal title listed in press sources for most of the label’s early years; his function was the operational counterpart to McGee’s A&R instincts. In a 2000s interview on creation-records.com, Green described his early-period duty as handling relationships with manufacturers the label couldn’t pay — alongside Tim Abbott (who occupied the formal title of managing director for part of the label’s life) and McGee. creation-records.com/interviews/dick-green/

As Creation scaled through the late 1980s and into the Sony era (post-1992), Green’s role encompassed the day-to-day business infrastructure of an increasingly large operation — managing office staff, dealing with suppliers, and overseeing administrative operations. No specific secondary title (e.g. “Finance Director”, “Operations Director”) is documented in available sources beyond co-founder/business manager.

During McGee’s absence (c. 1994–1996): When McGee stepped back due to his widely reported drug-related health problems, Green ran the label. In his own words: “I managed to pull a team together and keep it together but was in constant touch with Alan, and only really doing what i had been doing anyway.” He later noted the period “nearly put me off for life more like.” creation-records.com/interviews/dick-green/

Note on “managing director” title: Tim Abbott is identified in multiple sources as Creation Records’ managing director. Green is not recorded as holding that specific title; he is described as co-founder and business manager. Facebook/Ex-Creation Records managing director Tim Abbott — this distinction should be flagged if any source attributes the MD title to Green.

Post-Creation: Wichita Recordings (2000–present)

After Creation’s closure in July 1999, Green co-founded Wichita Recordings in 2000 with Mark Bowen — the Creation A&R man who had been the custodian of Super Furry Animals’ career at Creation. Green has described the founding intent as a deliberate reaction to Creation’s late-period scale: he wanted to start “the opposite of what Creation had become, which was a huge behemoth of far too many people.” Clash Magazine, label profile

The label started with just two people working from home. Its first release was a UK edition of Bright Eyes’ Fevers and Mirrors (2000) via Saddle Creek. Green took the label’s name from the Glen Campbell song “Wichita Lineman.” Key signings included Yeah Yeah Yeahs, My Morning Jacket, Bloc Party (whose commercial success elevated the label), The Cribs, Clap Your Hands Say Yeah, Best Coast, Los Campesinos!, Peter Bjorn and John, and Simian Mobile Disco (UK deal). Wikipedia: Wichita Recordings, as of 2025-07-12

Green holds the title Director at Wichita Recordings (confirmed via LinkedIn: uk.linkedin.com/in/dick-green-6a6b681a). The label, now encompassing a management division and publishing arm, remains active as of 2025–2026, based in London, Devon and Los Angeles. Wichita Management represents artists including Gold Panda, Cloud Nothings, Open Mike Eagle, and others.

Green rarely gives interviews and maintains a low public profile outside the label’s operations. creation-records.com/interviews/dick-green/
