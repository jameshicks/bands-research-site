TVT Records

US independent record label founded August 1984 by Steve Gottlieb, a Yale/Harvard Law graduate who launched the business from his New York City apartment. Discogs label ID: 758. Parent (post-bankruptcy): The Orchard (ID: 49620). Address: 23 East 4th Street, 3rd Floor, New York, NY 10003.

Originally called Tee-Vee Toons (TeeVee Toons), TVT grew from the surprise success of the Television’s Greatest Hits TV-theme compilation series, releasing eight volumes across the label’s life plus 30+ soundtrack albums. In 1986 the name was shortened to TVT Records. TVT expanded in the late 1980s into rock and industrial music (signing Nine Inch Nails in 1988), acquired Wax Trax!‘s catalog in 1992 and thereby became a central node in the industrial/EBM scene, then pivoted hard into hip-hop and crunk through the late 1990s and 2000s (Lil Jon, Ying Yang Twins, Pitbull, Tha Eastsidaz). The label accumulated 25 Gold, Platinum, and Multi-Platinum albums before costly litigation — a $132 million fraud verdict against Universal/Island Def Jam (later reduced to $126,000 on appeal) and a $9 million loss to Slip-N-Slide Records over early Pitbull recordings — drained the company. TVT filed for bankruptcy in February 2008; The Orchard acquired artist contracts, catalog, and distribution for $6.05 million in June 2008. Music publishing assets went to TVT Music Enterprises, later sold to Reservoir Media. In 2010 Bicycle Music Company acquired 700 master recordings (including NIN, The Connells, and Television’s Greatest Hits volumes) that Prudential Financial had acquired in 2002 after TVT defaulted on a loan.

TVT was a founding member of the Association of Independent Music (A2IM) and was notable for being the first label to put its entire catalog online for free streaming and downloading (2000).

Key Personnel & Business

  • Steve Gottlieb — founder, CEO. Yale/Harvard Law. Named one of Crain’s NY “40 Under 40” in 1996.
  • Trent Reznor — signed as NIN 1988; acrimonious exit circa 1992; TVT retained royalties from future NIN releases per settlement agreement.
  • John Fryer — producer/engineer on Pretty Hate Machine (multiple tracks).
  • Flood — producer/engineer on Pretty Hate Machine.
  • Adrian Sherwood — producer on Pretty Hate Machine (“Down In It”).
  • Keith LeBlanc — producer/engineer on Pretty Hate Machine.
  • Chris Vrenna — digital editing, Pretty Hate Machine.
  • Fred Giannelli — appeared on TVT-era industrial releases.

Distribution: TVT was self-distributed; in 2008 The Orchard acquired the distribution infrastructure.

Key legal events:

  • 2002: Prudential Financial acquired early NIN, Connells, Mortal Kombat soundtracks, and first seven Television’s Greatest Hits volumes after TVT defaulted on a loan; Prudential auctioned the catalog in 2005; Bicycle Music Company acquired in 2010.
  • 2002: TVT sued Universal/Island Def Jam (Lyor Cohen) over Ja Rule; jury awarded $132 million, reduced to $126,000 on appeal.
  • 2007: Lost $9 million to Slip-N-Slide Records over pre-existing Pitbull recordings; unable to post appeal bond → bankruptcy filing February 2008.
