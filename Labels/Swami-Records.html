Overview

San Diego independent label founded in 1999 by John Reis, specializing in punk, garage rock, and indie rock. The roster is tightly drawn from Reis’s own scene: Hot Snakes and Rick Froberg—his long-running collaborators through Drive Like Jehu and Hot Snakes—are among its core acts. The catalogue peaked sharply in the 2000s with over thirty releases, fell quiet across the 2010s, and has seen a measured renewal since 2020. With 151 total credits, the label functions as both an outlet for Reis’s own projects and a home for kindred San Diego-rooted artists.

Notes (web)

Label activity and the Hot Snakes timeline

The relationship between Swami’s output and Hot Snakes’ dormancy is more complex than a simple on/off correlation.

Hot Snakes timeline: formed 1999, broke up May 2005 after an Australian tour; reunited July 2010 for an encore performance, then fully from 2011 with touring and festival shows; released Jericho Sirens in March 2018 (see below on label). Rick Froberg died June 2023, ending the band’s active period. Wikipedia, Hot Snakes article, as of 2026-11-19

During Hot Snakes dormancy (2005–2010), Swami was relatively active, not quiet. Wikipedia’s Hot Snakes article states that after the 2005 breakup, “Reis focused his energy on running the Swami Records label.” The catalogue bears this out: The Night Marchers debuted on Swami in 2008, Dan Sartain releases continued through 2007, and CPC Gangbangs appeared in 2007. Wikipedia, Hot Snakes, as of 2026-11-19

The 2010s slowdown was multi-causal. The sharp drop in releases from ~2010 onward reflects several converging factors rather than Hot Snakes dormancy alone:

  • Fatherhood. Reis’s son was born around 2006. An Ox Fanzine interview notes a gap in Reis’s own recording activity around 2005–2006 tied to the birth of his son, with Reis saying he “wasn’t playing music” while waiting for the child to be born. ox-fanzine.de/interview/swami-john-reis-11392 The Sultans went on hiatus around 2007 when both Reis and drummer Mario Rubalcaba became fathers. [Wikipedia, Sultans, snippet]
  • Night Marchers winding down. The Night Marchers, Swami’s main active act in the late 2000s, released their last Swami LP in 2013 (Allez! Allez!) after a gap since 2008.
  • Rocket from the Crypt. Rocket from the Crypt had effectively ceased regular activity by this point, removing another pipeline of Swami-adjacent releases.
  • Expense and logistics. In an Analogue Music interview (undated, context circa 2017–2018), Reis described the label as “laying low right now,” saying his focus was on performing — “I’m in full rock and roll mode, playing shows and doing the Hot Snakes and still doing Rocket from the Crypt, as well. That’s where my interests lie right now.” He also acknowledged that running a label “is also expensive to put it bluntly.” analogue.io/interviews/hot-snakes

The 2018 Hot Snakes reunion did not directly revive Swami’s catalogue. Jericho Sirens (2018) was released on Sub Pop, which also reissued all three original Hot Snakes LPs that had come out on Swami. No public explanation from Reis for the Sub Pop deal was found, but the reissue arrangement suggests Sub Pop took on the full Hot Snakes catalogue as a package. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho_Sirens; subpop.com/artists/hot_snakes

The 2020s renewal is driven by new Reis projects, not Hot Snakes. The uptick from 2020 onward came from Reis forming new bands — Plosivs (with Rob Crow, Jordan Clark, Atom Willard; self-titled LP on Swami 2022, Yell at Cloud 2025) and his solo project Swami John Reis / Swami and the Bed of Nails (Ride the Wild Night 2020/2022, How Are You Peeling? 2024). Reis also established City of Refuge studio in San Diego as production infrastructure. As of late 2024–2025, he had announced plans for six Swami releases. brooklynvegan.com/plosives-rob-crow-swami-john-reis-prep-2nd-album-share-metacine/; timesofsandiego.com/arts/2025/09/25/john-reis-rocket-from-the-crypt-san-diego-music-hall-of-fame/
