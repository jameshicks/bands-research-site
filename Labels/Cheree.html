Cheree was born out of Sowing Seeds Fanzine which was created by Paul May (of SkullF**K fame) and Nick Allport based in “the groovy top flat” South Woodford London. Sowing Seeds had evolved into one of London’s best known and loved Fanzines. It was based around the “indie” scene in the U.K. of the late 80’s and had built up a substantial circulation. Cheree Records will always be best known for The Telescopes who had their first 3 vinyl releases while they were with Cheree. The first Cheree release came in the Summer of 1988 a 7” FLEXI of Loop live from ULU London and “Forever Close Your Eyes” by The Telescopes. The first major release was to be Cheree 2, The Telescopes- Kick the Wall 7” which launched both The Telescopes and Cheree well and truly off the ground.

Overview

Cheree was a small UK indie label that grew directly out of Sowing Seeds Fanzine, a well-circulated London indie zine co-created by Paul May (also associated with SkullF**K) and Nick Allport, based in South Woodford. The label launched in Summer 1988 with a split 7” flexi pairing a live Loop recording with The Telescopes’ “Forever Close Your Eyes,” and went on to release the first three Telescopes vinyl records, cementing its legacy as that band’s creative launchpad. The catalogue of roughly 25 releases runs from 1988 through the 1990s — six in the 1980s, nineteen in the 1990s — covering shoegaze, psychedelic rock, and indie rock, with the roster also including The Pooh Sticks, The Bardots, and Richard Formby.

Notes (web)

Origin: fanzine to label

The transition from Sowing Seeds Fanzine to Cheree Records was accidental rather than planned. At some point in 1988 Allport and May failed to complete an issue of the zine, but were left with copies of a flexidisc they had produced to be cover-mounted with that issue — a split featuring a live Loop recording and The Telescopes’ “Forever Close Your Eyes”. They sold the surplus copies at a Loop show on New Year’s Eve 1988, and the success of that sale convinced them to pursue a label rather than return to the fanzine. By 1989 Sowing Seeds had formally become Cheree Records. Vinita Joshi, who was Nick Allport’s girlfriend at the time, also joined the venture and became central to its operations.
