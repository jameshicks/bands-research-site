Schematic

Discogs ID: 108 (label) / 318585 (Schematic Music Company, parent entity) Location: Miami, Florida, USA Genres: IDM, Glitch, Micro-House, Electronica, Techno Active: 1996 – present (continuous releases through 2023+) Website: schematic.net / schematicmusiccompany.bandcamp.com

US independent electronic label founded in Miami by Joshua Kay and Romulo Del Castillo, operating as a sublabel of the Schematic Music Company (Discogs ID: 318585). One of the key American IDM/glitch imprints of the late 1990s and early 2000s, placing itself alongside Tigerbeat6 and Sublight Records in the laptop-music underground of that era.

History

Origins / Soul Oddity (1995–1997)

Joshua Kay and Romulo Del Castillo had been recording together before Schematic’s founding. In 1995 they released records for the major label Astralwerks as Soul Oddity (Discogs ID: 1915). Following a dispute with Astralwerks, and after a well-received release as Phoenecia on Warp Records, the duo founded Schematic Music Company in 1997 as an independent vehicle for Miami-based electronic music.

Founding Era (1996–2000)

The first Schematic release (SCH 001, 1996) was Matrix Blaster by Metic — one of Romulo Del Castillo’s solo aliases. The second (SCH 002, 1996) was Takeshi Muto’s Flipped And Squashed — another Del Castillo alias. The label thus launched with releases by its own founders under pseudonyms, a self-sufficiency model that continued throughout its history.

Richard Devine appeared on SCH 005 in 1997 (Richard Coleman Devine EP), his earliest significant label credit, establishing Schematic as an early home for the Atlanta-based modular and DSP experimentalist. Push Button Objects (Edgar Farinas, Discogs ID: 277) released Cash (SCH 003, 1997) in the same year.

The late-1990s catalog expanded with Phoenecia’s first full-length Odd Job / Odd Jobs (SCH808 / SCH.8x8, 1999), and saw the arrival of Scott Herren via his Delarosa & Asora project (Discogs ID: 1918) — Backsum EP (SCH-013, 1999) and Agony Part 1 (SCH014CD, 2000). Herren, who would go on to international visibility as Prefuse 73 and Savath & Savalas, was an early Schematic fixture who helped define the label’s aesthetic.

Phoenecia’s Brownout (Premaster) (SCH 018CD, 2000) and Richard Devine’s Lipswitch (SCH015, 2000) — mastered by Rashad Becker, design by The Designers Republic — cemented the label’s sound and visual identity heading into the 2000s.

Peak IDM Years (2001–2005)

The early 2000s saw the label’s most internationally recognized period. Key releases:

  • Otto Von Schirach debuted with 8000 B.C. (SCH017, 2001), the first of an extensive run of releases that made his deconstructed Miami-inflected noise-club sound central to the label’s identity.
  • Richard Devine released Aleamapper (SCH-019, 2001) and Asect:Dsect (SCH23, 2003; design by MK12 Studios), two of his most acclaimed albums.
  • Dino Felipe (Dino Felipe De La Vega, Discogs ID: 17299) began an extensive label run with Flim Toby (SCH 024 CD, 2002), establishing a more playful, lo-fi electronica strand within the catalog.
  • Glen Velez — the frame-drum percussionist, a notable outlier in an otherwise electronics-dominated roster — contributed Internal Combustion (SCH.033, 2003) and External Combustion (SCH 032, 2005).
  • The compilations Ischemic Strokes 1 (SCH 007, 1998), Ischemic Folks (SCH011CD, 1999), and Well-Suited For General-Purpose Audio Work (SCH 025 LP, 2002) documented the broader ecosystem.

The label maintained close scene relationships with Autechre, Matmos, Matthew Herbert, and Jamie Lidell, all of whom are name-checked in the label’s own Discogs profile as collaborators.

Later Activity (2005–present)

After a quiet mid-2000s period, Schematic resumed prolific digital-era release activity from roughly 2009 onward, with Phoenecia continuing to deliver material (Echelon Mall, SCH-050, 2009; Demissions, SCH079, 2011; Good Man Bones, SCH 075, 2011). The catalog expanded significantly in the 2010s and 2020s through Bandcamp-era releases, with Joe Lentini (Discogs ID: 1214723) emerging as the most active artist and also the label’s primary mastering engineer, credited on ~59 releases. The label also revisited Miami-area sounds with Crash Course In Science (SCH 084/085, 2011), Danny Daze (SCH-145/152, 2020), and a broader roster of local artists. Catalog numbering became increasingly idiosyncratic (SCH-5000, SCH-666, SCH-???), reflecting a looser DIY approach.

Scene Connections

  • Tigerbeat6 — San Francisco IDM/glitch label; Schematic’s closest US peer in the early 2000s laptop underground. Both operated in the post-Warp American electronica space.
  • Sublight Records — Canadian IDM label active in the same milieu.
  • Warp Records — Phoenecia (the founders’ group) released on Warp before founding Schematic. The Designers Republic, Warp’s longtime visual partner, crossed over to design Schematic sleeves.
  • Autechre — cited in label profile as collaborators.
  • Matmos — cited as collaborators; Matmos were also active in SF/laptop underground of this period.
  • Matthew Herbert — cited as collaborators.
  • Scott Herren — Delarosa & Asora / Prefuse 73 / Savath & Savalas; early Schematic fixture who later became internationally known on Warp Records.
  • Beta Bodega — Miami label with overlapping roster; Beta Bodega Coalition 2K12 compilation released on Schematic (SCH-090, 2012).
  • Rashad Becker — Berlin mastering engineer who worked on early Schematic releases (e.g. Lipswitch).
  • The Designers Republic — Sheffield design studio; visual identity of early catalog.

Open Questions

  • Exact founding year of Schematic Music Company: profile says 1997 (post-Warp dispute); first vinyl was 1996 (SCH 001). The label may have informally existed a year before the company was incorporated.
  • Nature of the Autechre, Matmos, Matthew Herbert, Jamie Lidell, and Glen Velez “collaborations” — the Discogs profile calls these out but it’s unclear whether these were guest appearances, remix work, or live collaborations; would need release-level verification or web sources to confirm.
  • No catalog numbers in the SCH 008–012 range are fully accounted for (SCH> 008 is Takeshi Muto’s Swollen Glance One; SCH011CD and SCH009/009CD are accounted for; SCH 010 appears as a 2021 Sky Tucker release with cat# reuse).
  • Label’s relationship to the Miami bass/club scene: Otto Von Schirach’s work overlaps with Miami bass aesthetics; unclear how intentional this was as a label direction.
