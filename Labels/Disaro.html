Disaro

Independent music label from Houston, Texas. Discogs label ID: 179188. Founded 2007 by DJ Robert Disaro and James Weigel. One of the earliest physical infrastructure nodes of the witch-house scene.

Overview

Disaro was started in 2007 by DJ Robert Disaro and James Weigel in Houston, Texas. The label is widely cited as having helped launch the witch-house movement — releasing early material by SALEM (6), oOoOO (3), White Ring, and Ritualz, among others.

The first releases in the catalog pre-date the CDr series: cat numbers 002, 006, and 009 date to 2007, with early releases by G.A.S.H., White Mice, and SALEM (6). The CDr-prefixed catalog (CDR018 onward) represents a second phase beginning around 2009–2010, when witch-house was crystallizing as a recognizable internet scene. The CDR prefix seems to denote CDr (recordable CD) format specifically — the earlier numerical catalog used a different numbering scheme with no prefix.

In May 2011, after what the label’s Discogs profile describes as “a series of mishaps,” Robert Disaro handed day-to-day operations to Karkis Distribution (Discogs ID: 371700). Karkis ran the label until January 2013, at which point Robert Disaro resumed sole control. By 2012 the release pace had slowed; no releases in the database appear after 2012.

Catalog Series

The label used at least five distinct catalog number prefixes:

  • No prefix / bare number (002, 006, 009, 015) — 2007–2008 early releases
  • CDR (CDR018–CDR036) — CDr format, 2009–2010 main witch-house run
  • DV (DV001) — possibly vinyl; White Ring’s Black Earth That Made Me is catalogued here
  • DRSV (DRSV001–DRSV006) — 2011 series (Slow Head, ∆AIMON, GuMMyBeAR, Origami, etc.)
  • DM (DM001–DM003) — 2011–2012 (∆AIMON No Wrong Come Along, Weyrd Son, Textbeak)
  • DRV (DRV001) — 2012 vinyl; Slow Head’s Fire Is The Mouth Of The Snake

Key Artists

SALEM (6) (Discogs artist ID: 1300441) — Salem (Jack Donoghue, Heather Marlatt, John Holland) had the largest footprint on the label: five releases spanning 2007–2008. They were among the earliest and most prominent witch-house acts. Members: Jack Donoghue, Heather Marlatt, John Holland.

oOoOO (3) (Discogs artist ID: 1765951) — Christopher Dexter Greenspan. His 2010 CDr (CDR021) is probably the most-cited release in the witch-house canon and the one most discussed in connection with Disaro. Also a member of Drab City.

White Ring (Discogs artist ID: 1753777) — Kendra Malia, Bryan Kurkimilis, Adina Viarengo. Their Black Earth That Made Me (DV001, 2010) was the label’s most prominent vinyl release, and one of the definitive early witch-house records.

Mater Suspiria Vision (Discogs artist ID: 1820825) — Italian project with members Aura (11), Cosmotropia de Xam, Rachel Audrey, Violette De Lestrange. Second Coming (CDR022, 2010).

King Dude (Discogs artist ID: 1759378) — Thomas Jefferson Cowgill. Tonight’s Special Death (CDR033, 2010). Self-recorded and mixed; lyrics partly from Robert Frost (track 6).

Ritualz (Discogs artist ID: 2148399) — Juan Carlos Lobo Garcia. Also known as †‡†. Untitled (CDR034, 2010). Multiple aliases including Aranoë, Caótico Espectral, DJ Maquina De Muerte, Ente (4), Jason Spit.

∆AIMON (Discogs artist ID: 2138296) — Brant Showers and Nancy Showers. No Wrong Come Along (DM001, 2011).

Slow Head (Discogs artist ID: 2543640) — The label’s most prolific post-2010 artist, with four releases including the vinyl Fire Is The Mouth Of The Snake (DRV001, 2012, produced by Ian David (5)).

Scene Context

Disaro is one of the handful of small US imprints (alongside Tri Angle and Nihjgt Feelings) that gave physical form to the witch-house moment of 2009–2011. The label’s profile explicitly identifies it as having “helped launch the Witch House movement.” The majority of releases are CDrs — cheap, limited-run home-burned discs — reflecting the scene’s DIY, semi-anonymous internet distribution model. The DV- and DRV- vinyl series represent a slight escalation in production that coincides with broader media attention to the genre around 2010–2012.

The 2007–2008 early catalog predates the witch-house label by several years, suggesting Disaro began as a broader Houston underground imprint before the scene coalesced. Acts like White Mice (Houston noise) and G.A.S.H. point to a local Houston underground lineage.
