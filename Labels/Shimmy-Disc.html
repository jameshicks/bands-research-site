Shimmy Disc

Discogs ID: 23561
Origin: New York City, USA (JAF Box 1187, NY 10116)
Active: 1987–1999 (ceased operations); sold to Knitting Factory/KnitMedia 1997–1998; revived April 2020 as co-imprint with Joyful Noise Recordings
Founder: Kramer (2) (Mark Kramer)
Sublabels: Kokopop (ID: 65475), Rings of Saturn (ID: 857070), Shimmy Disc Europe (ID: 36744)

US independent label founded 1987 by Kramer (2). A neo-psychedelic and experimental label, core to the late-1980s New York avant-garde/noise/art-punk scene. Released music across an eclectic range — art rock, noise, comedy, klezmer, free improv — produced largely by Kramer at Noise New York and Noise New Jersey studios.

Structure

  • Shimmy Disc Europe (ID: 36744) — European sublabel; released Double Bummer + (1989) and The Big Sell-Out (1992) by Bongwater, among others
  • Kokopop — sublabel for more indie/pop-oriented releases (King Missile, Saints & Razors, etc.)
  • Second Shimmy — later iteration/revival imprint (ID: 123969)
  • Shimmy 500 — another later imprint (ID: 1247578); Kramer’s The History Of Crying (2017)

Closure and Revival

Closure (1997–1999)

The label’s end was driven by two overlapping legal crises. Ann Magnuson filed suit in 1994 seeking $4.5 million, alleging fraud, breach of contract, copyright infringement, and related wrongdoings arising from the dissolution of Bongwater and their personal relationship. The case settled out of court in 1997 under a confidentiality agreement; “neither party speak publicly about the details.” By that point the lawsuits had already financially crippled the label. (Magnet Magazine, 2007-06-23; Wikipedia: Kramer (musician), as of 2026-07-02)

In 1997 Kramer sold both Shimmy Disc and his recording facility (Noise New Jersey) to KnitMedia — the umbrella management entity of the Knitting Factory — with a contract requiring his continued involvement as producer and Director of A&R. The arrangement broke down quickly: Kramer sued the Knitting Factory for breach of contract and “as a result of his split with KnitMedia, he lost both Noise New Jersey and Shimmy-Disc.” Kramer has stated he returned from a 1999 European tour “determined to leave the music business for good.” (Magnet Magazine, 2007-06-23)

Timeline summary:

  • 1994 — Magnuson lawsuit filed
  • 1997 — Lawsuit settled; label sold to KnitMedia/Knitting Factory
  • 1998 — Kramer sues Knitting Factory for breach; loses the label
  • 1999 — 105th and final original-era release; Kramer exits music

Wikipedia dates the shutdown to 1999 and states 105 albums were released before it shut down. (Wikipedia: Shimmy-Disc, as of 2026-07-02)

Conflict note: The vault previously noted “circa 2000” as the closure date. Wikipedia and the Magnet Magazine profile consistently give 1999 as the last year of activity (with the Knitting Factory transaction placing the label’s loss in 1998). The “2000” figure appears to be a rounded approximation; 1999 is the better-sourced date.

Revival (2020)

On April 29, 2020, Joyful Noise Recordings announced Kramer as its 2020 Artist-in-Residence and simultaneously announced the relaunch of Shimmy Disc. (The Wire; Joyful Noise Recordings)

Nature of the partnership: The revival operates as a co-release arrangement between Joyful Noise and Shimmy Disc — not a licensing deal or acquisition. Joyful Noise founder Karl Hofstetter described Shimmy Disc as “one of the most important and fiercely adventurous labels of all time.” The first releases out of the revival are co-credited to both labels, with Shimmy Disc functioning as a relaunched imprint under Kramer’s control distributed through Joyful Noise’s infrastructure. (Joyful Noise Recordings blog)

2020 A.I.R. box set: The centrepiece of the revival was a five-LP limited-edition box set (released early 2021) collecting five Kramer projects produced throughout 2020, mixed on the Universal Audio 610 console formerly used by Brian Wilson at United Western Recorders. The five LPs became “the first 5 albums released by Kramer’s reborn label.” Individual titles were also sold as standalone digital downloads and LPs. (Joyful Noise A.I.R. product page)

First release: Songs We Sang in Our Dreams by Let It Come Down (Kramer + vocalist Xan Tyler), June 12, 2020.

Post-revival releases have included Paul Leary, J.D. Pinkus, and remastered/archival material from Daniel Johnston, Bruce Haack, and Allen Ginsberg. (Wikipedia: Shimmy-Disc, as of 2026-07-02)

Note on “Second Shimmy”: The Discogs imprint “Second Shimmy” (ID: 123969) does not appear to be separately branded in Joyful Noise’s own materials; press materials simply refer to the revived label as “Shimmy-Disc” or “Shimmy Disc.” The Second Shimmy Discogs entry may reflect internal catalog distinctions rather than a public-facing sub-brand.
