Blast First

Discogs ID: 2500 Parent label: Mute (Discogs: 636) Sublabels: Furthur (73943), HUB 100 (6656), Not (54097) Address (historical): 429 Harrow Road, London W10 4RE, UK Total releases in DB: 642 unique (738 including all pressings)

UK independent/experimental label specializing in licensing American noise rock and avant-garde acts for the British market, plus signing Free Jazz and avant-garde performers internationally. Originally set up with support from Rough Trade, it later operated as a sub-label of Mute. Run by Paul Smith (52) (Discogs: 2366783) with administrative assistance from sisters Pat Naylor and Liz Naylor. Smith also operates the fully independent imprint Blast First Petite for titles that Mute’s corporate distributors decline to release.

Founding and Context

Blast First launched in 1985 with Sonic Youth’s Bad Moon Rising (BFFP 1), co-released with Homestead Records. The label’s mandate was clear from the outset: bring American noise rock and experimental music to UK audiences who had limited access to independent US pressings. The early catalog read like a map of the downtown New York and Chicago underground — Sonic Youth, Big Black, Butthole Surfers, UT — all licensed or co-released for the UK market.

Paul Smith came to the label through connections in the UK post-punk scene: he had previously managed Throbbing Gristle, Wire, Cabaret Voltaire, and Lee Scratch Perry, and had run events for writers including Ken Kesey’s Merry Pranksters, Alan Moore, Iain Sinclair, and Michael Moorcock, as well as the multi-disciplinary arts club “Disobey.” He also co-ran Doublevision with R.H. Kirk (Cabaret Voltaire) and Stephen Mallinder. This background — arts-events, post-industrial, experimental — shaped Blast First’s aesthetic sensibility.

The early Rough Trade connection gave the label distribution infrastructure. By the late 1980s it was operating as a Mute subsidiary, a relationship that continued through the label’s peak years and into the 2000s. The “Blast First Records” credit that sometimes appears on non-Blast First releases is a known Discogs mis-credit; the correct imprint name is “Blast First.”

Era 1: 1985–1988 — American Noise Rock in the UK

The founding years were dominated by licensing and co-releasing the defining records of American noise rock:

Sonic Youth (artist ID: 17199) anchored the catalog. The first five BFFP catalog numbers were Sonic Youth releases: Bad Moon Rising (BFFP 1, 1985), the Death Valley ‘69 single with Lydia Lunch (BFFP 2, 1985), Flower 7” (BFFP 3, 1985), EVOL (BFFP 4, 1986), Starpower 12” (BFFP 7T, 1986). Ciccone Youth — the Sonic Youth side project — contributed the “Into The Groove(y) / Burnin’ Up” 12” (BFFP 8, 1986). The relationship deepened through Sister (BFFP 20, 1987) and reached its artistic peak with Daydream Nation (BFFP 34, 1988), a double LP produced by Sonic Youth with Nick Sansano, featuring cover paintings by Gerhard Richter. Sonic Youth remained on Blast First through their UK catalog until moving to DGC for Goo (1990), though reissues and compilations continued appearing on the label into the 1990s.

Big Black (artist ID: 55961) placed Atomizer (BFFP 11, 1986), the Headache 12” (BFFP 14, 1987), Songs About Fucking (BFFP 19, 1987), He’s A Whore / The Model 12” (BFFP 24, 1987), and The Rich Man’s Eight Track Tape CD compilation on the label. Steve Albini’s abrasive guitar work and drum machine production defined this period’s sound. The existing vault page Big Black covers the band.

Butthole Surfers (artist ID: 18838) released Locust Abortion Technician (BFFP 15, 1987), Hairway To Steven (BFFP 29, 1988), and Widowermaker! (BFFP 41, 1989).

Head Of David (artist ID: 55960) — a UK band rather than a US import — became an early original signing. Their debut DogBreath (BFFP 5, 1986) appeared at the start of the catalog, followed by LP (BFFP 10, 1986), Dustbowl (BFFP 18, 1988), The Saveana Mixes (BFFP 37, 1989), and Seed State (BFFP 66, 1991). Blast First produced the Saveana Mixes with Paul Smith himself credited as producer — one of his rare direct production credits. See Head Of David.

UT (artist ID: 55964) — the New York all-women noise trio — released Early Live Life (BFFP 12, 1987), Conviction (BFFP 16, 1987), In Gut’s House (BFFP 17, 1987), and Griller (BFFP 36, 1989) on the label. See Ut.

Rapeman (artist ID: 55962) — Steve Albini’s post-Big Black group — placed Budd (BFFP 27, 1988) and Two Nuns And A Pack Mule (BFFP 33, 1988) on the label.

Big Stick (artist ID: 55963) contributed two releases: Drag Racing (BFFP 6, 1986) and Crack ‘N’ Drag (BFFP 25, 1988).

Ciccone Youth (artist ID: 55966) — the Madonna-covers side project — released “Into The Groove(y) / Burnin’ Up” (BFFP 8, 1986) and The Whitey Album (BFFP 28, 1989). See Ciccone Youth.

Dinosaur Jr. (artist ID: 55965) placed Bug (BFFP 31, 1988), Freak Scene 12” (BFFP 30, 1988), and Just Like Heaven (BFFP 47, 1989). See J Mascis.

Lee Ranaldo (solo) appeared with From Here To Infinity (BFFP 9, 1987) and East Jesus (Some Recordings 1981–1991) (BFFP 103, 1994). Ranaldo also contributed photography to 17 releases across the label’s catalog — a recurring visual credit.

Savage Pencil (artist ID: 196519) — the cartoonist/artist Edwin Pouncey — released Angel Dust (Music For Movie Bikers) (FU 3 LP, 1988) on the Furthur sublabel, and designed the cover of the Nothing Short Of Total War compilation.

Not sublabel: Live Bootlegs (1986–1989)

The Not imprint (Discogs: 54097) was used for a series of live “official bootlegs”:

  • Walls Have Ears — Sonic Youth (NOT 1 (BUT 2), 1986)
  • You Got It All, Dad! We’re Gonna Hit! (Sound Of Impact.) — Big Black (NOT 2, 1986)
  • The Shit Hits The Fans — Head Of David (NOT 3, 1989)
  • Double Live — Butthole Surfers (NOT 4 (BUT 5), 1989)

Furthur sublabel (1987–1989)

The Furthur imprint (Discogs: 73943) handled a handful of releases before being absorbed back into the main catalog:

  • Songs Of Praise — A.C. Temple (FU 1, 1987)
  • Blessing And Curse — Band Of Susans 12” (FU 2T, 1987)
  • Angel Dust (Music For Movie Bikers) — Savage Pencil (FU 3 LP, 1988)
  • Hi, How Are You: The Unfinished Album — Daniel Johnston (FU 4 LP, 1988) — an anomalous early Daniel Johnston appearance
  • Hope Against Hope — Band Of Susans (FU 5 LP, 1988)
  • Blowtorch — A.C. Temple (Netherlands pressing, 1988)
  • One Hell Soundwich — Gary Panter / Jay Cotton (FU 7, 1989)

Era 2: 1989–1993 — Broadening Scope

By 1989 the label expanded beyond pure noise rock. The Nothing Short Of Total War (Part One) compilation (BFFP 13, 1989) served as a label sampler, collecting tracks from Sonic Youth, Head Of David, UT, Big Black, Rapeman, Dinosaur Jr., Lee Ranaldo, Ciccone Youth, Arsenal (2), and Big Stick — a useful cross-section of the early roster.

Band Of Susans (artist ID: 55969) — the Robert Poss-led New York guitar orchestra — became a label mainstay through this era. After their Furthur debut they moved to the main catalog: Love Agenda (BFFP 43, 1989), Hope Against Hope (FU 5 LP, 1988), Here Comes Success (BFFP 114, 1994), Wired For Sound (BFFP 111, 1995). Producer Robert Poss (artist ID: 154042) is credited on 18 releases across the label. See Band Of Susans, Robert Poss, Susan Stenger, Page Hamilton.

Glenn Branca (artist ID: 55972) released Symphony No. 6 (Devil Choirs At The Gates Of Heaven) (BFFP 39, 1989) and Symphony Nos. 8 & 10 (The Mysteries) (BFFP 106, 1994), both produced by Wharton Tiers. The personnel on Symphony No. 6 included Page Hamilton, Phil Kline, and Algis Kizys. See Glenn Branca.

The Sun Ra Arkestra (artist ID: 2219395) appeared with Out There A Minute (BFFP 42, 1989) and Live In London 1990 (BFFP 60, 1990) — an unusual excursion into free jazz that reflects Paul Smith’s eclectic curatorial range.

The Mekons (artist ID: 263823) placed a substantial catalog on Blast First from 1989–1993: The Mekons Rock n’ Roll (BFFP 40, 1989), The Dream And Lie Of… (BFFP 53, 1989), F.U.N. ‘90 (BFFP 62, 1990), The Curse Of The Mekons (BFFP 80, 1991), I ♥ Mekons (BFFP 79, 1993). See The Mekons.

Mudhoney appeared with the split single Touch Me I’m Sick / Halloween (BFFP 46, 1989) shared with Sonic Youth — a pairing that signaled the Sub Pop / Seattle connection. See Mark Arm.

A.C. Temple (artist ID: 186034) contributed Sourpuss (BFFP 45, 1989), Blowtorch (on Furthur), Belinda Backwards (BFFP 63, 1991).

Lunachicks (artist ID: 55973) released Sugar Luv (BFFP 44, 1989) and Babysitters On Acid (BFFP 52, 1990).

Beme Seed (artist ID: 55968) released a self-titled LP (BFFP 50, 1989).

The Raincoats came back with an extended play (BFFP 99, 1994). See Ana da Silva.

The Afghan Whigs (artist ID: 89572) — an anomaly on the roster, being a more mainstream alt-rock act — had a substantial presence from 1993 onward: Debonair 12” (BFFP 95T, 1993), Gentlemen LP (BFFP 90, 1993), What Jail Is Like EP (BFFP 96, 1994). Greg Dulli (artist ID: 24071) is credited as producer across 14 label releases, and as writer on 7. Their presence signals the label’s commercial flexibility under Mute.

Era 3: 1994–2000 — Electronics, Jazz, Nordic Minimalism

The mid-1990s onward saw the label shift decisively toward electronic and minimalist work, anchored by two acts from Finland.

Pan Sonic (Mika Vainio + Ilpo Väisänen; artist ID: 3822) became the defining act of this period. Originally credited as “Panasonic” until a trademark dispute with the electronics company, they released their debut Vakio on Blast First in 1995 as a 4×10” box set (BFFP 118). Subsequent releases — Osasto EP (BFFP 128, 1996), Kulma = Angle (BFFP 132, 1996), A (BFFP 9078, 1999), B (BFFP 161, 1999), Aaltopiiri (BFFP 166, 2000), and the massive box set Kesto (234.48:4) (BFFP 180BX, 2004) — established Blast First as the primary western outlet for Finnish minimal electronics. Pan Sonic also handled their own sleeve design across the catalog. See Pan Sonic, Mika Vainio, Ilpo Väisänen.

Vainio / Väisänen / Vega (artist ID: 30938) — the Pan Sonic duo with Alan Vega — released Endless (BFFP 147, 1998) on the label. See Vainio Väisänen Vega.

Suicide (artist ID: 31055) became a significant presence from 1998 onward. The label released the Suicide self-titled debut reissue (BFFP 133, 1998), The Second Album + The First Rehearsal Tapes (BFFP 162, 1999), and American Supreme (BFFP 168, 2002). The Cheree (Remix) 12” (BFFP 115, 1998) appeared as well. See Suicide, Alan Vega, Martin Rev.

Alan Vega (artist ID: 54918) solo: Station (2007). Vega also contributed cover art and illustration to 10 releases across the catalog.

Labradford (artist ID: 3547) — the Virginia drone/ambient group — released Labradford (BFFP 136, 1996), Mi Media Naranja (BFFP 144, 1997), E Luxo So (BFFP 157, 1999), and Fixed::Context (BFFP 167, 2000) on the label — four LPs that documented their arc from drone rock to near-silence. See Carter Brown (Labradford).

Merzbow (artist ID: 12551) placed Scumtron (BFFP 138, 1997) and appeared on the split compilation Ejaculation Generater 2 / ? / Emission (BFFP 143, 1997) with Masonna and MC Hellshit & DJ Carhouse. See Merzbow.

Caspar Brötzmann (artist ID: 123581) appeared with two solo releases: Merry Christmas (BFFP 97, 1994, split with F.M. Einheit) and Zulutime (BFFP 129, 1996, split with Page Hamilton). The Caspar Brötzmann Massaker also appears (Home, BFFP 110, 1994). See Caspar Brötzmann, F.M. Einheit, Page Hamilton.

The Charles Gayle Trio (artist ID: 125977) released Live At Disobey (BFFP 100, 1994) — recorded at Paul Smith’s Disobey arts events. See Charles Gayle.

Hovercraft (artist ID: 104843) released Akathisia (1996), Vagus Nerve / De-Orbit Burn split with Scanner (BFFP 134, 1997), and Experiment Below (BFFP 160, 1998).

2K / Jeremy Deller (artist IDs: 34570, 293991): **k The Millennium (BFFP 146, 1997) — a KLF-adjacent project by Bill Drummond, Jimmy Cauty (the Justified Ancients of Mu Mu), and artist Jeremy Deller. Bill Drummond (artist ID: 149534) and Jimmy Cauty (artist ID: 47965) are credited as writers on 12 releases each across the label.

The Williams Fairey Brass Band (artist ID: 29759) released Acid Brass (BFFP 150, 1997), performing acid house anthems arranged for brass band — produced by Jeremy Deller. This release became one of the label’s most celebrated oddities.

Robin Guthrie (of Cocteau Twins) released Drifting A.F.U. (BFFP 159, 1999).

Phill Niblock (artist ID: 153404): A Young Person’s Guide To Phill Niblock (BFFP 102, 1995).

Earth (2) (artist ID: 87270): Sunn Amps And Smashed Guitars Live! (BFFP 123, 1995).

Fushitsusha (artist ID: 65647): Fushitsusha (1995), Keiji Haino’s group.

Keiji Haino (artist ID: 120331): solo (1996).

Masonna (artist ID: 69023): appears 1997–1999.

Pan•American (artist ID: 12542) — Mark Nelson’s (Labradford) solo project — released 360 Business / 360 Bypass (BFFP 163, 2000).

HUB 100 sublabel (2001)

The HUB 100 imprint (Discogs: 6656) was used for compilation/compilation-adjacent releases:

  • Sonic Mook Experiment (Rare Mixes, Electronic Action And Future Rock & Roll) (hub 001, 2001)
  • I Hate Sonic Mook Experiment EP2 (Hub 001 EP2, 2001)

Era 4: 2001–2007 — Later Catalog

Liars (artist ID: 48350) — the New York/Los Angeles art-punk group — became a major signing for this final period: They Threw Us All In A Trench And Stuck A Monument On Top (BFFP 172, 2002), Fins To Make Us More Fish-like (BFFP 174, 2002), and later releases through 2022 (some possibly via Blast First Petite). See Liars.

Erase Errata (artist ID: 80777) released Other Animals (2001) and At Crystal Palace (BFFP 187, 2003).

Kaito (2) (artist ID: 184699) released Band Red and Kaito EP (BFFP 189, 2003).

Sonic Mook Experiment series: label compilations spanning 2001–2003 (BFFP 176, BFFP 177).

Alan Vega: Station (2007) was a late solo outing on Blast First proper, before Paul Smith’s activities shifted predominantly to Blast First Petite.

Sublabels

Not (Discogs: 54097)

Imprint for live “official bootlegs,” 1986–1989. Catalog numbers use the format “NOT [n] (BUT [n]).” Four releases total — Sonic Youth, Big Black, Head Of David, Butthole Surfers.

Furthur (Discogs: 73943)

Short-lived imprint for a handful of releases, 1987–1989. Seven releases including Band Of Susans’ debut, A.C. Temple, Daniel Johnston, and Savage Pencil. The imprint appears to have been folded back into the main Blast First catalog by 1989.

HUB 100 (Discogs: 6656)

Used for the Sonic Mook Experiment compilation series (2001). Two releases only.

Relationship to Mute Records

Blast First was originally set up with Rough Trade support, then became a Mute subsidiary. Mute (parent ID: 636) provided the corporate distribution infrastructure that allowed Smith to license and release American underground music at UK scale. The relationship was productive but had limits: Smith has stated that he set up Blast First Petite specifically for titles that “Mute’s corporate distributors choose not to release,” indicating that as Mute itself was absorbed into the major label system (eventually into EMI, then BMG), the margin for experimental releases narrowed. Blast First Petite, launched around 2004, became the outlet for new work (particularly later-period Pan Sonic, HTRK, Factory Floor) while Blast First remained as the legacy imprint.

Paul Smith (52)

Discogs ID: 2366783

Founder and operator of the label. Came from post-industrial management: Throbbing Gristle, Wire, Cabaret Voltaire, Lee Scratch Perry, Cath Carroll. Co-ran the Disobey arts/music events series (multi-disciplinary; brought in Ken Kesey’s Merry Pranksters, Alan Moore, Iain Sinclair, Michael Moorcock). Also ran the Doublevision label with Cabaret Voltaire’s R.H. Kirk and Stephen Mallinder. His direct production credit on record is rare — the Head Of David Saveana Mixes (BFFP 37, 1989) being the clearest case. Smith’s Wikipedia entry is at en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Smith_(music_industry_executive). He now curates the Moog Recordings Library (Discogs: 1474607). Pat Naylor and Liz Naylor assisted in label operations.
