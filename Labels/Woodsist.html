Woodsist

Aug 07, 20265 min read

Woodsist Records

Discogs ID: 85458
Founded: 2006
Location: Warwick, New York
Founder: Jeremy Earl
Sublabels: Fuck It Tapes, Hello Sunshine, Mare (2)

Independent label founded by Jeremy Earl of Woods (2) in Warwick, NY (upstate, not NYC). The label started as a vehicle for Earl’s own band but rapidly became a defining imprint of the late-2000s American lo-fi/psych underground, issuing a remarkable cluster of debut and early records between 2008 and 2012 that now read as a snapshot of a particular moment in underground rock.

Roster & Key Artists

Woods (2)

Earl’s primary band, by far the most prolific act on the label: 60 releases from 2007 through 2023. The band — also featuring Jarvis Taveniere — is in practice inseparable from the label’s identity. Woods records are the backbone of the catalog; early lo-fi cassette releases gave way to increasingly polished LPs while retaining the pastoral, acid-folk character of the earliest work.

Kevin Morby

15 releases, 2013–2022. Morby emerged from the Woods (2) lineup as a solo artist and built his solo catalog substantially on Woodsist before graduating to larger distribution. One of the clearest examples of the label functioning as a pipeline from band-member to solo career. His last credited appearance on a Woods (2) release is Sun And Shade (2011, as bassist); he does not appear on Bend Beyond (2012), placing his departure between those two records. His debut solo LP Harlem River followed in 2013.

Real Estate (2)

6 releases, 2009–2022. The New Jersey jangle-pop band issued their earliest material on Woodsist before signing to Domino/Matador. Their self-titled debut (2009) and Days (2011) are among the label’s most commercially successful records.

White Fence / Tim Presley

8 releases, 2010–2012. Tim Presley’s White Fence project issued a run of hazy, Phil Spector-via-4-track records in this period. Presley also contributed artwork to 12 other Woodsist releases, functioning as the label’s informal in-house designer for a stretch.

The Class of 2008–2009

The label’s most historically resonant moment is the cluster of debuts and early releases between 2008 and 2010. In a span of roughly 24 months Woodsist issued early records by:

  • Wavves (Nathan Williams, San Diego)
  • Vivian Girls (Brooklyn)
  • Crystal Stilts (Brooklyn)
  • Blank Dogs (NYC)
  • Sun Araw (Cameron Stallones — WOODSIST018, Boat Trip, 2008)
  • Magic Lantern (Long Beach CA — WOODSIST019, self-titled, 2008; also on Not Not Fun Records)
  • Kurt Vile (pre-Matador)
  • Thee Oh Sees
  • Ducktails (Matt Mondanile)
  • Moon Duo

The presence of both Sun Araw and Magic Lantern places Woodsist in direct overlap with the Not Not Fun Records ecosystem; Earl clearly had connections into the LA/Long Beach psych underground as well as the NYC and San Diego noise-pop scenes.

Key Personnel

Jeremy Earl

Founder, producer (19 releases as producer), and occasional artwork credit (5 releases). Primary member of Woods (2). Warwick, NY base gives the label its upstate character, distinct from the NYC scene it frequently intersected with.

Jarvis Taveniere

Co-member of Woods (2); credited as producer on 22 Woodsist releases and engineer on 21 — the label’s most consistently present behind-the-scenes figure after Earl himself.

Timothy Stollenwerk

Mastering engineer on 29 Woodsist releases — the same credit count he holds on Grouper releases, making him a direct node connecting Woodsist’s upstate psych world with the Pacific Northwest experimental scene centered on Not Not Fun Records and Type Records. Also mastered Sun Araw’s Ancient Romans.

Scene Context

Woodsist’s geographic position — upstate New York, not the city — gave it slight remove from the Brooklyn blog-indie world even as it issued records by Brooklyn-based acts. The lo-fi aesthetic that unified the 2008–2010 output was partly circumstantial (home recording, cassette-first culture) and partly programmatic: Earl consistently signed acts whose work had a handmade, tape-saturated quality regardless of origin.

The Sun Araw and Magic Lantern releases are the clearest evidence of Woodsist’s reach into the LA/Long Beach psych underground. Both Cameron Stallones and the Magic Lantern collective were also on Not Not Fun Records; Woodsist served as a companion imprint for the same material reaching a slightly different East Coast and European audience.

The Real Estate and Kurt Vile trajectories — both eventually signed to Matador — are the most-cited examples of Woodsist as a credibility feeder to the indie mainstream, a role it never explicitly sought but which defined its critical legacy by 2012.
