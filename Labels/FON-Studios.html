FON Studios

From: Sheffield, England
Active: 1985–1996
Designed by: Alan Cross, Mark Brydon, Marcus O’Hagan
Connected label: Fon Records

Sheffield’s first large commercial recording studio. Funded by the MCA Records advance paid to Chakk in 1985. Designed by Alan Cross, Mark Brydon, and Marcus O’Hagan — all of whom had architecture backgrounds. Completed summer 1985.

Became a training ground for Sheffield engineering talent: Robert Gordon (Dr Bob 4), Alan Fisch, Steve Cobby (Fila Brazillia), and Phil Jones (5) all worked there. High-profile outside bookings included David Bowie and Yazz. Alan Cross used the facility to produce and remix as DJ Chakk, Alana Gold, and other aliases.

The FON → Warp pipeline: Robert Gordon worked at FON alongside Alan Cross, then partnered with Mark Brydon as Fon Force, producing Age Of Chance, Pop Will Eat Itself, Krush, and others. In 1989 Gordon co-founded Warp Records with Steve Beckett and Rob Mitchell. The building Chakk’s £100,000 advance funded was the direct incubator for Warp Records.

Operated until 1996. The studio’s existence is the most significant material legacy of Chakk — more lasting than the records themselves.

Locations

Original site (1985–c.1990): First floor of a building at the end of Willey Street, a cul-de-sac off the Wicker, above a metal treatment works. By late 1990 the building and the street were demolished to make way for the Sheffield Inner Ring Road. (Sheffield Music Map, DHI)

City centre site (c.1990–1996): FON relocated to the Autoways building within what became Sheffield’s Cultural Industries Quarter, upstairs alongside the Audio Visual Enterprise Centre (a commercial media provision that opened in 1988). Remained a functioning studio until 1996. (Metropolitics / Red Tape Studios piece; Sheffield Music Map)

After 1996

No web-accessible source documents what specifically happened to FON Studios after it closed in 1996 — whether the physical space was sold on, the equipment dispersed, or a successor business took over. Robert Gordon, the studio’s key engineer, subsequently ran his own mastering/production/mixing facility and label “Trash & Ready”; he did not continue under the FON name. (Test Pressing interview)

The closure appears to have been a straightforward wind-down rather than a rebrand or sale: multiple contemporary and retrospective accounts treat 1996 as a clean endpoint with no successor operation mentioned.

Notes (web)

  • FON name derived from graffiti the band saw: “fuck off Nazis” (some sources: “fear of Nazis”). (Sheffield Music Map)
  • Artists recorded at the original Willey Street site included: Age of Chance, Yazz, Erasure, Hula, In the Nursery, Cabaret Voltaire, Pop Will Eat Itself, James, Psychic TV, The Funky Worm, Krush. (Sheffield Music Map)
  • Studio co-founders/managers also included Dave Taylor and Amrik Rai (former NME journalist, who also opened the FON record shop).
  • The documentary “The Beat Is the Law” (Sky Arts, 2010) reportedly features interviews with FON personnel and may contain further detail about the studio’s later history. Not accessible via web search.
