MG Records was a sublabel of GM Records (both used the same logo during the early 90s) which from 1990-1994 released mostly unofficial tapes sourced from CDs. From May 1994 (when Polish Parliament passed a new copyright law) they focused on cassette manufacturing, duplication and distribution. Later under the name GM Records they started activity as major CD and DVD plant (since 2016 the company also presses vinyl) on Polish market and are still active today. The label logo looks very close to the labels IMD (2) (Saudi Arabia), QP (2) (Saudi Arabia) and Stars (10) (Syria).

Overview

MG Records (2) was a Polish sublabel of GM Records that operated primarily in the early 1990s, releasing unofficial cassette copies of CDs by major Western acts — its roster spans Metallica, Pink Floyd, Depeche Mode, Queen, and Joy Division, with nearly all of its roughly 36 catalogue entries concentrated in that decade. The label’s bootleg tape business ended in May 1994 when Poland enacted new copyright legislation, after which the operation pivoted to legitimate cassette manufacturing, duplication, and distribution under the GM Records name. That parent company has since grown into one of Poland’s major CD, DVD, and (from 2016) vinyl pressing plants and remains active today. Notably, MG Records’ logo is near-identical to those of three unrelated Middle Eastern and Syrian labels — IMD (2), QP (2), and Stars (10) — though no documented connection between them has been established.
