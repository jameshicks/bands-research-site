Named of course, after the mighty Ashra track from “New Age of Earth”, it’s early releases were inspired by the killer run of colour themed private press series of LPs by Conrad Schnitzler; colour coded with embossed head logos, wraparound sleeves in numbered editions with several inserts. At first it was thought that the label would act as a vehicle for 10 of these LPs only, then fold; and was partly the reason why the brazen theft of the Delta-Acustic ‘head’ logo was used.

An on-off label run by Dom Martin (Earworm, Great Pop Supplement, Polytechnic Youth and Feral Child) the label was/is an outlet for some of the more Berlin school inspired kraut | motorik grooves- arguably with a leaning towards what you might call the ‘second generation’ of krautrockers; stuff like the Sky catalogue of Wolfgang Riechmann / Streetmark, Gunter Schickert, Deutsche Wertarbeit, Tyndall et al…

Never quite retired, but happy to exist at it’s own pace…

Overview

Deep Distance is a UK label run by Dom Martin—also behind Earworm, Great Pop Supplement, Polytechnic Youth, and Feral Child—dedicated to Berlin-school-inflected krautrock and motorik music, with a particular affinity for the second generation of kosmische artists: Riechmann/Streetmark, Gunter Schickert, Deutsche Wertarbeit, and Tyndall. Its name nods to the Ashra track on New Age of Earth, and its earliest releases were modelled on Conrad Schnitzler’s colour-coded private-press LP series—numbered, wraparound-sleeved editions with embossed logos and multiple inserts. Originally conceived as a vehicle for just ten records before folding, the label instead settled into an intermittent pace; the bulk of its roughly 27 releases appeared across the 2010s, with roster artists including Colin Potter, Sula Bassana, Cosmic Ground, and Justin Wright.
