Silent (aka Silent Records) is an America’s premier ambient label from San Francisco, California, USA. Originally started in 1985 (officially in 1986), rebooted in 2016. Started officially with the second release () in 1986. The direction of the label changed in 1988 when label head and founder Kim Cascone helped work on the score of the movie Twin Peaks[/I] in the UK with the sound editor Jay Boekelheide.

In 1991, Cascone started the distribution company Pulse Soniq after releasing the first CD on Silent. Then, in 1993, three imprints were created for different genres. Flask (beat-oriented ambient), Furnace (hard industrial, industrial dance), and Sulphur (alternative rock, noise, fusion, experimental electronics, found sound).

In 1996, Silent and its distribution company were left to an employee so that Cascone could work as a sound designer for Headspace, a company founded by Thomas Dolby. Silent lived on for another two years as a completely different label, focusing on house music and other beat-oriented dance music, including co-releasing some vinyl with other companies, but the label was shuttered in 1998.

Overview

Silent Records is a San Francisco ambient label founded by Kim Cascone, operating officially from 1986 and concentrated overwhelmingly in the 1990s (46 of its 58 selected releases). Its direction shifted in 1988 after Cascone collaborated on a Twin Peaks-related score project in the UK with sound editor Jay Boekelheide. By 1993 the imprint had spun off three genre-specific subsidiaries — Flask for beat-oriented ambient, Furnace for hard industrial, and Sulphur for experimental/alternative electronics — alongside the distribution arm Pulse Soniq. Cascone handed the label to an employee in 1996 to work as sound designer for Thomas Dolby’s Headspace company; Silent pivoted to house and dance music before closing in 1998, then was revived by Cascone as a Bandcamp operation in 2016 with catalogue reissues and two new volumes of the From Here To Tranquility series.

Notes (web)

Discogs profile error: “Twin Peaks score in the UK in 1988”

The Discogs label profile states Cascone “helped work on the score of the movie Twin Peaks in the UK with the sound editor Jay Boekelheide” and places this in 1988. Multiple sources identify at least three errors in this claim:

What actually happened: Cascone’s work with Jay Boekelheide and David Lynch began in 1989, not 1988. He first took a post-production internship at the Saul Zaentz Film Center in Berkeley, California, where he worked on the pilot for the Twin Peaks TV series. He then worked as an assistant on the foley stage at Skywalker Ranch in Marin County, and was subsequently hired on Lynch’s film Wild at Heart (1990). His title was Assistant Music Editor. Sources: Wikipedia — Kim Cascone (as of 2026-11-16); search result snippets from silentrecords.us bio; sound-scotland.co.uk bio.

Errors in the Discogs claim:

  • Year: 1989, not 1988. In 1988 Cascone was already running Silent Records (founded officially 1986) and releasing PGR material, but no source places him on a film score that year.
  • “The movie Twin Peaks”: Twin Peaks is a TV series (ABC pilot shot 1989, premiered April 1990). Cascone worked on the pilot episode, not a film. The film Twin Peaks: Fire Walk with Me came in 1992, after Cascone had already left the film industry (he departed in 1991).
  • “In the UK”: All identified work was in the San Francisco Bay Area — specifically the Saul Zaentz Film Center (Berkeley) and Skywalker Ranch (Marin County). No source places Cascone in the UK at any point in his career. Jay Boekelheide is an American sound editor (Academy Award for The Right Stuff, 1983); no source links him to a 1988 UK project. Sources: Wikipedia — Jay Boekelheide; IMDb search result snippet.

How the Lynch film work shaped Silent Records’ ambient direction: No source explicitly draws a causal line between the 1989 Lynch post-production work and Silent Records’ ambient pivot. What the sources do confirm is a sequence: Cascone worked in Lynch film post-production 1989–1991, then left the film industry in 1991 to focus on Silent Records, which he proceeded to develop into “the U.S.’s premier ambient electronic music label.” The label’s ambient character appears to have developed in parallel with — rather than directly caused by — the Lynch work. The eContact interview notes the label’s electronic/ambient direction grew organically as techno became popular around 1991. No interview or profile found states that the Lynch collaboration was the specific catalyst for Silent Records’ ambient output.

What Cascone was doing in 1988: The label was already running (founded 1986). The first CD on Silent was released in 1991; prior to that, releases were vinyl. Cascone was active as PGR. No source names a specific film, TV, or UK project for 1988. The Lynch work is consistently placed at 1989, not 1988.

Sources checked for this question:

  • Wikipedia — Kim Cascone — year confirmed as 1989, Bay Area locations confirmed, no UK reference
  • Wikipedia — Jay Boekelheide — filmography snippet shows no 1988 UK project
  • CEC eContact interview with Cascone — no 1988 or UK detail; label direction described as organic
  • sound-scotland.co.uk artist bio — Lynch work placed in “1980s” broadly, no UK reference
  • silentrecords.us bio — connection failed
  • subsol.c3.hu bio — connection failed
  • hermetic.com profile — 403
  • Reality Sandwich / Medium interview — 403

Conclusion: The Discogs profile text is garbled. The correct claim is that in 1989 Cascone worked as Assistant Music Editor on the Twin Peaks TV pilot and then on Wild at Heart, both in the San Francisco Bay Area, with sound editor Jay Boekelheide. The year “1988” and location “UK” appear to be errors in the Discogs profile text. No web source confirms any project — film, TV, or otherwise — linking Cascone to the UK in 1988 or at any other time.
