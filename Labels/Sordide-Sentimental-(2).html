Overview

Sordide Sentimental (2) is a Discogs administrative grouping rather than an operating label — it collects counterfeit and bootleg pressings that bear the Sordide Sentimental name, with Joy Division releases being the primary target of counterfeiters. The genuine Sordide Sentimental was a small French imprint run by Jean-Pierre Turmel and Jean-François Jamoul whose 1980 Joy Division single became a prized collector’s item; the fakes documented here draw on the same credited figures — producer Martin Hannett and photographer Anton Corbijn — whose names appear on the original pressings. The bootleg catalogue is confined to the 1980s, with at least one release documented from 1986.

Notes (web) — Scarcity and Counterfeiting of the Joy Division pressing

Why the original became scarce

The Sordide Sentimental “Licht und Blindheit” (SS 33002, March 1980) was pressed in only 1,578 numbered copies — an intentionally small run in keeping with Sordide Sentimental’s boutique, art-object philosophy. The project was pitched to Joy Division manager Rob Gretton after the model of a Throbbing Gristle single the label had already released; the intent was to give the tracks a sense of material specialness through lavish packaging (A4 trifold colour sleeve, two inserts, individual numbering) rather than wide circulation. Far Out Magazine

Distribution amplified the scarcity: the pressing was not sent to UK record shops at all. UK fans could only obtain copies by mail order or by hearing the tracks when John Peel aired them on BBC Radio 1. Far Out Magazine

Demand surged after Ian Curtis died in May 1980, just two months after the single’s release. Factory Records’ autumn 1980 re-release of “Atmosphere” introduced the song to a far wider audience, retroactively elevating the original French pressing into one of Joy Division’s most sought-after artefacts. Far Out Magazine

Peter Hook noted the tension in the strategy: “The tracks that Throbbing Gristle put out on Sordide Sentimental were never going” to achieve wider release, whereas Joy Division “put two of our best songs on it” — implying the scarcity was partly an unintended consequence of applying an experimental-music label’s model to a band that quickly became enormously popular. Far Out Magazine

Packaging itself contributed to attrition. Jean-Pierre Turmel noted the foldout cover was “very fragile packaging,” and the first ~1,000 copies shipped without any protective bag; only the final copies came with a plain plastic bag. (joydiv.org/lub.htm) This physical fragility reduced the supply of mint-condition originals still further.

As of the mid-2000s, the collector community at new-order.net had positively identified only ~275 confirmed copies in circulation out of the 1,578 pressed — a useful indicator of how many have been lost, damaged, or are simply off the market. (new-order.net)

Collector market prices

The market trajectory illustrates why counterfeiting became economically rational:

  • Early 2000s: £150–£200 typical
  • Mid-2010s: £800–£1,200 common
  • 2022: John Peel’s personal copy (no. 001108/1578, with his hand-written band-name label) realised £2,550 including buyer’s premium at Bonhams’ John Peel Archive auction, London, 14 June 2022. (Bonhams)
  • Recent (2020–2025): £1,400–£3,500+ documented; one 2025 listing reached €3,500 (~$4,200 USD). (new-order.net)

How to authenticate an original

Per joydiv.org/S5.htm:

  • Vinyl colour: all genuine originals are coloured vinyl (red, pink, clear red with paper fragments, green, grey, purple, magenta, gold-toned, smoke). Black vinyl = fake.
  • Label: black with silver writing; genuine originals have a serrated ridge around the label perimeter. Its absence is a strong fake indicator.
  • Matrix numbers: hand-written “MPO SS33002A t” / “MPO SS33002B t”. Stamped matrices indicate a counterfeit.
  • Inserts: two inserts — a blue paper with “Avertissement” text in French and English, and a “Gesamtkunstwerk” sheet. Missing inserts suggest a fake or an incomplete original.
  • Background imagery: the foldout interior shows two faces on originals; fakes frequently substitute a women-and-infant image.
  • Glue oxidation: the adhesive bonding the plastic wallet to the foldout has turned brown with 40+ years of ageing — still-clear glue indicates recent manufacture.
  • Numbering: each genuine copy bears an individual six-digit number on the cover. Un-numbered copies are fakes.

Collector authentication discussions note that even claims of “Sordide Sentimental printed bags” accompanying early copies are disputed — one experienced collector stated “I’ve never heard of any originals coming with that bag,” per the new-order.net community. (new-order.net)
