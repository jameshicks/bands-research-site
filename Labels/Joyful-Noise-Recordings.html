Joyful Noise Recordings

Discogs ID: 119541
Founded: c. 2003
Location: Indianapolis, Indiana
Total releases: 2,282 (Discogs); 41 sublabels
Genre range: Indie rock, noise, experimental, folk, post-rock, soul, electronic

Independent label based in Indianapolis, active since the early 2000s. Karl Hofstetter’s bands (Melk The G6-49, Racebannon, Abner Trio) were among the earliest signings, and Hofstetter carries recurring curatorial and liner-notes credits across the catalog, indicating close involvement with the label’s operations. First catalog release: jnr-1 (2003, Melk The G6-49 s/t). Grown into one of the more ambitious small-to-mid-size US indie labels, combining a broad experimental roster with a strong subscription/format-series culture.

Era by Era

2003–2008 — Indianapolis underground

First releases tied to the Indianapolis indie/noise scene: Melk The G6-49 (jnr-1, 2003), El Ten Eleven self-titled (JNR312, 2004), Grampall Jookabox, Child Bite, Marmoset, Racebannon. Catalog numbers suggest low output — roughly one or two releases per year through 2007, accelerating to ~6 by 2008. El Ten Eleven (bass guitar + drums duo, Kristian Dunn / Tim Fogarty) became a long-running cornerstone with 47 releases through 2026.

2009–2011 — Broadening scope

Deerhoof joined in 2009 (Live Session EP) and became the second-most-represented artist with 106 releases. Joan Of Arc (Tim Kinsella) arrived 2010 and would rack up 55 releases. Of Montreal and Dinosaur Jr. each released on JNR in 2010–11. Danielson (2) signed 2011 (Best Of Gloucester County, JNR72), eventually warranting his own “Danielson Artist Enabler Club” sublabel. Annual output climbed steeply.

2012–2016 — Format innovation and breakthrough roster

Kishi Bashi debuted with 151a (JNR92, 2012) — produced by K Ishibashi himself, mastered by Joe Lambert, design by David Woodruff / Jerrod Landon Porter — and became the label’s most-represented artist at 123 releases. The annual Flexi-Disc Series launched (2012–2016), distributing one-sided flexis by label artists as subscriber incentives. Why? signed 2013 (Golden Tickets, JNR117; Bangs, JNR125.6). Sebadoh released Defend Yourself (JNR120, 2013). Son Lux arrived with Lanterns (JNR126, 2013). Artist In Residence series launched 2014, pairing label artists for collaborative releases. Swamp Dogg, Lee Ranaldo, Thurston Moore, Sufjan Stevens, and Daniel Johnston all placed releases in this period.

2017–present — Subscription era and international expansion

VIP 7” Singles Series (2017–2019) and White Label Series (2017–2022) formalized the subscription model. Artist Enabler Clubs launched for Danielson (2), Lou Barlow, and Why? — deeper subscriber engagement tiers. Tropical Fuck Storm (Gareth Liddiard’s post-Drones Australian group) signed 2018 (A Laughing Death In Meatspace, JNR277) and became the seventh-highest release-count artist at 52 releases. Swamp Dogg’s Love, Loss, And Auto-Tune (JNR269, 2018) features Justin Vernon (Bon Iver) on “I’ll Pretend.” King Gizzard And The Lizard Wizard / Tropical Fuck Storm split Satanic Slumber Party (JNR412, 2022). Gray Area Cassette Series (2021–22) and Stone Tapes (2) sublabel continued format experimentation. CocoRosie joined 2024.

Design

David Woodruff (ID 3030830) is the dominant house designer with 158 release credits — including the alias “David J. Woodruff & Sons” (ID 3421307, 16 more credits). Ryan Hover (ID 2083721, 66 credits) is second. Adam McMillan (41 credits) handles much of the photography. Jad Fair contributes artwork across 26 releases (also a performer). Satomi Matsuzaki of Deerhoof contributes artwork for 21 releases.
