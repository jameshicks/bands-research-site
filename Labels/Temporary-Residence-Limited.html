Temporary Residence Limited

Independent record label. Brooklyn, New York (originally Baltimore, Maryland 1996–97). Discogs label ID: 9952. Also appears as “Temporary Residence Ltd.” Founded 1996 by Jeremy DeVine.

Genres: post-rock, experimental, drone, noise-rock, math rock, ambient, post-hardcore, orchestral rock. One of the longest-running and most respected US independent labels for instrumental and experimental guitar music.

Label History

Origins (1996–1999)

Founded in Baltimore in 1996 by Jeremy DeVine. Early catalog number scheme: TR01, TRR 02, TRR 06 onward. The first release (TR01) was a split 7” by Nero (7) / The (Concord Anthology) Process. DeVine early on operated both as label head and active musician — he was a member of Sonna, one of the label’s flagship early acts, and handled recording and layout himself on early releases.

The label’s earliest aesthetic was eclectic US post-hardcore and indie: Wino (3), 90 Day Men, Retsin, Tarentel, Cerberus Shoal, Halifax Pier. The ongoing Travels In Constants series (sublabel ID: 348413) began as a vehicle for limited-release EPs — volumes ran from Vol. 1 (By Water, 1999) through Vol. 25 (Prefuse 73, 2015). All 25 volumes documented — see Travels In Constants for full volume table. The Sounds Of The Geographically Challenged sublabel (ID: 648273) hosted early compilation releases including the Winter Solstice 1997 compilation (TRR 06).

By 1999–2000 the label had relocated to New York City (later Brooklyn). Catalog began to concentrate on more ambitious experimental and post-rock work. Songs: Ohia appeared on the Travels In Constants series (Vol. 14, “Fourteen,” 2001, TRR 13). Jason Molina’s work with the label gave early credibility in the indie/Americana crossover space. Low contributed The Exit Papers (A Soundtrack By Low) in 2000.

Breakthrough Era (2001–2007)

The label’s international profile solidified around two signing coups: Explosions In The Sky and MONO.

Explosions In The Sky arrived with Those Who Tell The Truth Shall Die, Those Who Tell The Truth Shall Live Forever (2001, TRR 34) — tracked by DeVine and Trevor Kampmann. Their 2003 album The Earth Is Not A Cold Dead Place (TRR 61) became one of TRL’s defining releases, engineered by John Congleton and featuring artwork by Esteban Rey. The band’s collaboration with Congleton continued on All Of A Sudden I Miss Everyone (2007, TRR 99) and Take Care, Take Care, Take Care (2011, TRR 199). By the mid-2000s Explosions were TRL’s highest-profile act internationally.

MONO (Tokyo) signed to TRL in the US from 2004. Their records One Step More And You Die (TRR 73), Walking Cloud And Deep Red Sky (TRR 68), You Are There (TRR 98), and Hymn To The Immortal Wind (TRR 148, 2009) anchored the label’s post-rock/orchestral identity. Nearly every MONO album was recorded by Steve Albini at Electrical Audio and mastered by John Golden. MONO is TRL’s most-released artist by Discogs count (153 releases on the label).

Other key 2001–2007 signings included:

  • Eluvium (Matthew Cooper) — ambient/drone, debuted with Lambent Material (TRR 57, 2003); became one of the label’s most consistent sellers.
  • Fridge (UK) — Happiness (TRR 43, 2001), EPH Reissue (TRR 48, 2002), The Sun (TRR 117, 2007).
  • Tarentel — experimental post-rock; 6 releases including From Bone To Satellite (TRR 23, 1999) and We Move Through Weather (TRR 67, 2004).
  • Cerberus Shoal — art-rock; Homb (TRR 24, 1999) through …And Farewell To Hightide (TRR 41, 2002).
  • Grails — debut Burning Off Impurities (TRR 118, 2007); continued as a major TRL act through the 2020s.
  • Maserati — instrumental rock; debut Inventions For The New Season (TRR 120, 2007); 52 releases on the label.
  • Mogwai — Scottish post-rock giants; selected US releases including Government Commissions and split releases on TRL.
  • Pelican (2) — post-metal; Small Stones (TRR 87, 2005), Mono / Pelican split (TRR 89, 2005).
  • Envy (2) — Japanese post-hardcore; Insomniac Doze (TRR 112, 2006) was their TRL debut; became one of the label’s most prolific international acts (63 releases).
  • World’s End Girlfriend — on the MONO split LP Palmless Prayer / Mass Murder Refrain (TRR-108, 2005); later joined the roster properly.
  • The Books — The Way Out (TRR 183, 2010); label distributed their back catalog (Thought For Food, The Lemon Of Pink, Lost And Safe) in limited vinyl editions.
  • By The End Of Tonight — math-rock; multiple EPs 2004–2006.
  • Sleeping People — math/prog; self-titled (TRR 84, 2005) and Growing (TRR 123, 2006).

The 100th catalog release was marked with the TRR100 Thankful compilation (TRR 100, 2006). Split 7”s through the Young Widows Split Series sublabel (ID: 867276) became a recurring format for pairing TRL artists with guests, including releases pairing Young Widows with Bonnie “Prince” Billy, Melt-Banana, Pelican (2), and My Disco.

Consolidation and Expansion (2008–2014)

The label deepened its Japanese connections and expanded into ambient and loop-based music. Key arrivals:

  • Young Widows — Louisville noise-rock; Old Wounds (TRR 138, 2008) through Easy Pain (TRR 221, 2014); 55 releases total.
  • William Basinski — New York ambient composer; TRL issued the landmark box set The Disintegration Loops (TRR 194, 2012), with individual loop LPs (TRR 223–226). DeVine handled the full art direction and design. Basinski remained one of TRL’s most important acts through the 2020s (55 releases).
  • Coliseum (2) — Louisville post-hardcore; House With A Curse (TRR 175, 2010) through Sister Faith (TRR 219, 2013).
  • Zammuto — Nick Zammuto’s project after The Books; Zammuto (TRR 209, 2012) and Anchor (TRR 227, 2014).
  • Ultraísta — supergroup (Nigel Godrich, Joey Waronker, Laura Bettinson); Ultraísta (TRR 215, 2012).
  • Tera Melos — Sacramento math-rock; Tera Melos (TRR 141, 2008) and a split with By The End Of Tonight (TRR 128, 2007).
  • Moss Icon — DC emo/post-hardcore; Complete Discography reissue (TRR 201, 2012).
  • Majeure (A.E. Paterra of Zombi (2)) — synthesizer-driven; Timespan (TRR 145, 2009) through Apex (TRR 287, 2017).
  • Inventions (Matthew Cooper / Mark T Smith) — debut Inventions (TRR 231, 2014).
  • Hauschka — German prepared-piano artist; Abandoned City (TRR 232, 2014).

The MONO Hymn To The Immortal Wind album (2009), co-produced with Steve Albini and featuring a 28-piece string/wind orchestra, stands as one of the label’s most ambitious productions.

Mid-2010s to Present (2015–2026)

TRL continued releasing landmark records across a widening range of experimental music:

  • Mogwai — Atomic (TRR 272, 2016) and Every Country’s Sun (TRR 291, 2017) — two of the label’s highest-profile releases of the decade; As The Love Continues (TRR 355, 2021); The Bad Fire (TRR 442, 2025).
  • Explosions In The Sky — The Wilderness (TRR 270, 2016); End (TRR 414, 2023).
  • William Basinski — A Shadow In Time (TRR 278, 2017); On Time Out Of Time (TRR 311, 2019); Lamentations (2020); September 23rd (TRR 435, 2024).
  • Grails — Chalice Hymnal (TRR 279/280, 2017); Anches En Maat (TRR 353, 2023); Miracle Music (TRR 434, 2025).
  • Beak> (Geoff Barrow, Will Young, Billy Fuller) — UK krautrock/rock trio; Beak>> (TRR 307, 2018) and >>> (INV201, 2018); >>>> (INV319, 2024). Catalog numbers in the INV series reflect a separate but affiliated imprint.
  • Lawrence English — Australian sound artist; Selva Oscura collaboration with Basinski (TRR 312, 2018); solo releases through the early 2020s.
  • Field Works (Stuart Hyatt) — phonographic / nature-sound art; multiple releases 2018–2022.
  • Party Dozen — Australian noise-rock/saxophone duo; The Real Work (TRR 403, 2022) and Crime In Australia (TRR 432, 2024).
  • Nina Nastasia — Riderless Horse (TRR 398, 2022) and Too Soon (2022); co-produced with Steve Albini.
  • Envy (2) — continued heavy presence; Atheist’s Cornea (TRR 259, 2015), The Fallen Crimson (TRR 341, 2020), Eunoia (TRR 436, 2024).
  • Sparkle Division (William Basinski + Preston Wendel) — electronic/new age side project; To Feel Embraced (TRR 345, 2020) and Foxy (TRR 412, 2023).
  • Kieran Hebden (Four Tet) — 41 Longfield Street Late ’80s (TRR 447, 2025), split with William Tyler.

Connections

  • Jeremy DeVine — founder and sole operator; handled art direction on 303 releases and mastering/mixing on dozens more; also a performer in Sonna (early TRL band).
  • Steve Albini — house engineer for the label’s two most prominent artists (MONO and Explosions In The Sky); his Electrical Audio studio in Chicago served as TRL’s de facto recording home from 2004 onward.
  • John Congleton — primary engineer for Explosions In The Sky from The Earth Is Not A Cold Dead Place (2003) onward.
  • Hydra Head Records — licensing partner for the MONO/Pelican split (2005); overlapping metal/post-metal roster.
  • Pelagic Records — European partner label; shares MONO, Explosions In The Sky, and other TRL artists.
  • Chemikal Underground — home label for Mogwai; TRL issued selected Mogwai US releases.
  • Southern Lord — overlapping post-metal/drone roster; shared acts include Pelican.
  • Kranky — Chicago; parallel experimental/ambient US independent; comparable roster aesthetic.
  • Explosions In The Sky — Austin post-rock; breakthrough signing 2001; remained on label through End (2023).
  • MONO — Tokyo post-rock/orchestral; most prolific TRL artist; 153 releases spanning 2004–2026.
  • William Basinski (treated as People entry) — released Disintegration Loops box (2012); one of TRL’s landmark prestige signings.
