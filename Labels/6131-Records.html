Overview

6131 Records is a US independent label co-founded in 2006 by Joey Cahill and Sean Patrick Rhorer, rooted in hardcore and punk with a roster that spans emo, indie rock, and alternative rock. The catalogue built steadily through the late 2000s and then expanded sharply in the 2010s — that decade alone accounts for nearly four times the releases of the label’s first years. Signings have ranged from heavier acts like Rotting Out to singer-songwriter Julien Baker, reflecting an unusually wide stylistic reach for a punk-rooted indie. The label has logged over 700 Discogs credits across more than 115 releases.

Notes (web) — Julien Baker signing

How Baker came to 6131: Baker self-released Sprained Ankle on Bandcamp in winter 2014, with no expectation of wide distribution. The route to 6131 ran through Ryan Azada, a Memphis musician who had spent years helping Baker’s band Forrister book shows in Memphis. Azada was also touring with Dads (2), which was already on 6131’s roster. Baker described it directly in a May 2016 interview: “6131 contacted me after hearing the record through a close friend of mine, Ryan Azada, who toured with the band Dads, a band also on 6131.” After hearing the record, 6131 approached Baker and offered to release it properly; she did not solicit the label. (Scene Point Blank, 20 May 2016)

The album was mastered for the formal release and issued on 6131 Records on 23 October 2015. (Wikipedia — Sprained Ankle, as of 2026-11-09)

Rhorer on Baker’s appeal: Sean Rhorer commented at the time of the release: “I’ve never really encountered somebody who has the ability to resonate so broadly with their songs”, noting that both his mother and punk musicians on the label’s roster were enthusiastic about her work — framing the signing less as genre expansion and more as response to exceptional cross-audience impact. No statement from Rhorer or Cahill explicitly invoking deliberate genre diversification has been located. (Memphis Flyer, 2015)

Label ethos as Baker described it: Baker recalled 6131 telling her: “You’re going to sign a three-record deal, but hopefully, you don’t stay” — indicating the label positioned itself as a launchpad rather than a long-term home for the type of artist Baker was becoming. She credited the label with knowing “how to elevate the artist and do what’s best for the artist and not just what’s best for their brand.” (Memphis Flyer, 2015)

Route to Matador: Baker released her second album Turn Out the Lights (2017) on 6131 in the US, then signed to Matador Records for wider distribution. Music Connection reported that Cahill and Rhorer “leveraged their Matador Records connections to facilitate her signing” — indicating the label actively assisted her transition outward. (Music Connection, 5 April 2017)

Conflict / minor discrepancy: Music Connection (April 2017) describes Rhorer as helping Baker “compile her previously unreleased songs into Sprained Ankle” before the Bandcamp release. Scene Point Blank and Wikipedia describe the Bandcamp release as Baker’s own self-release prior to 6131’s involvement. The Music Connection framing may compress the timeline or overstate Rhorer’s pre-signing role; both accounts agree 6131 formally released the album in October 2015.

Sources checked, no result on deliberate diversification angle: Searched for explicit statements from Cahill or Rhorer about intentional genre broadening (2015–2016 interviews, label profiles, Paste, PopMatters, Stereogum, Nuvo, Substream). None found. The available record suggests the Baker signing was opportunistic — a personal network connection rather than a strategic pivot — though the label’s pre-existing breadth (Joyce Manor, Dads, Culture Abuse, Suburban Living) shows genre-adjacent acts were already on the roster before Baker.
