Domino Records

Discogs Label ID: 26465 (Domino); parent company: Domino Recording Co. Ltd. (ID 69345) Origin: Founded in Putney, South West London, 1993 Founder: Laurence Bell (born Laurence Bonamy Simon Bell; also credited as “Blind Lorenzo Hoss” / “Laurence Repo”) Label Code: LC 10192 Catalog number schemes: WIG (albums), RUG (singles/EPs) for UK/European pressings; DOMUS then DNO for early US releases; US pressings used Jay Street, Brooklyn address from late 2014 onward (previously Washington Street, Brooklyn)

One of the most significant British independent labels of the post-Britpop era. Founded as a vehicle for American lo-fi and alt-country (Sebadoh, Royal Trux, Palace/Bonnie “Prince” Billy, Smog/Bill Callahan), Domino pivoted toward UK guitar rock in the early 2000s (Clinic, Franz Ferdinand) and broke globally with Arctic Monkeys in 2005. Since 2010 the roster has broadened to electronic, neo-soul, ambient, and experimental work while maintaining a core guitar-band presence.

Founding and Early Era (1993–1999): American Lo-Fi

Domino launched in 1993 focused almost entirely on licensing American indie/lo-fi acts for UK/European release. Early priorities:

  • Sebadoh — first major UK licensee; Bubble & Scrape (1993, WIGMC 4), Bakesale (1994), Harmacy (1996), The Sebadoh (1999)
  • Royal Trux — Cats And Dogs (1993), Twin Infinitives (1994), Accelerator (1998)
  • Palace / Bonnie “Prince” Billy — There Is No-One What Will Take Care Of You (1993), Palace Brothers (1994), Viva Last Blues (1995), I See A Darkness (1999, WIGCD 72)
  • Smog / Bill Callahan — The Doctor Came At Dawn (1996), Red Apple Falls (1997), Knock Knock (1999)
  • Silver Jews — Starlite Walker (1994), The Natural Bridge (1996), American Water (1998)
  • Flying Saucer Attack — Distance (1994), Further (1995), Chorus (1995)
  • The Pastels — Mobile Safari (1994), Illumination (1997)
  • Elliott Smith — reissued Roman Candle and self-titled (1998 reissues); Either/Or UK pressing (1997)
  • Pavement — Brighten The Corners (1997), Terror Twilight (1999)
  • Mouse On Mars — Niun Niggung (1999), Distroia (1999)
  • Gastr Del Sol — Camoufleur (1998)
  • Jim O’Rourke — Bad Timing (1997), Eureka (1999)
  • Clinic (UK) — first releases in 1999: debut EP Clinic (WIGCD 64), The Second Line (RUG91)
  • Pram — Birmingham experimental group; multiple albums 1997–2001

Domino also operated the Series 500 sublabel (ID 3169) for more experimental/electronic work, issuing early Mouse On Mars and Midnight Funk Association releases.

Building a UK Roster (2000–2004): Clinic, The Kills, James Yorkston

By the turn of the decade Domino began signing original UK acts:

  • Clinic — Internal Wrangler (2000, WIGLP 78; produced by Gareth Jones and Clinic); Walking With Thee (2001); Winchester Cathedral (2004) — the band self-produced throughout, maintaining medical-uniform stage anonymity
  • The Kills — Keep On Your Mean Side (2003, WIGCD 124); No Wow (2004) — American-British duo Alison Mosshart / Jamie Hince
  • James Yorkston and the Athletes — Moving Up Country (2002, WIGCD 107); mainstay of Domino’s Scottish folk strand
  • Four Tet (Kieran Hebden) — Pause (2001, DOMUS02CD); Rounds (2003, WIGCD 126 / DNO 0014) — career-defining electronic album; Hebden became one of the label’s most prolific producers across the roster
  • The Notwist — Neon Golden (2002, DNO 010) — German post-rock/electronic crossover
  • Stephen Malkmus — solo debut (2001, WIGCD 90); Stephen Malkmus & The Jicks Pig Lib (2003), Face The Truth (2005)
  • Bonnie “Prince” Billy continued as a core act — Ease Down The Road (2001), Master And Everyone (2003)
  • To Rococo Rot — Hotel Morgen (2004, DNO 31CD); Speculation (WIGCD234P, 2009)
  • Sons And Daughters — Love The Cup (2004); The Repulsion Box (2005)
  • Juana Molina — Segundo (2003), Tres Cosas (2004) — Argentine singer-songwriter
  • Junior Boys — Last Exit (2004, DNO 38) — Canadian synth-pop

A key 1994 compilation, Hey Drag City (WIGCD 16), documented Domino’s ties to the Chicago indie scene.

The Breakthrough (2005–2007): Arctic Monkeys and Franz Ferdinand

Two signings in 2003–2004 transformed Domino’s commercial scale:

Franz Ferdinand — signed 2003; debut single “Darts Of Pleasure” (DNO22CD, 2003), debut album Franz Ferdinand (WIGCD 136, 2004; 40+ pressings in DB); produced by Tore Johansson. You Could Have It So Much Better (WIGLP 161, 2005). The band wrote and handled production on later records.

Arctic Monkeys — debut single “I Bet You Look Good On The Dancefloor” (RUG 212, 2005); Whatever People Say I Am, That’s What I’m Not (2005) — one of the fastest-selling UK debut albums ever; 50+ pressings in DB. Subsequent albums all on Domino:

  • Favourite Worst Nightmare (2007)
  • Humbug (2009)
  • Suck It And See (2011)
  • AM (2013, WIGCD 317) — produced by James Ford and Ross Orton; Josh Homme guested on backing vocals; John Cooper Clarke co-wrote one track
  • Tranquility Base Hotel & Casino (2018)
  • The Car (2022)

James Ford (of Simian Mobile Disco) became the label’s most-used producer across this era, credited on 280 releases. Rich Costey mixed many key releases (76 credits). Dan Carey also an important house producer (107 credits).

Expansion (2007–2013): Animal Collective, Hot Chip, Blood Orange

Domino’s US operation (Brooklyn) and UK operation diverged somewhat in catalog number scheme, with the US continuing DNO identifiers into 2013. Key arrivals:

  • Animal Collective — Strawberry Jam (2007); Merriweather Post Pavilion (2009, DNO 219) — mixed by Ben H. Allen and the band; widely cited as experimental pop landmark; Fall Be Kind EP (2009)
  • Hot Chip — joined Domino for In Our Heads (2012, WIGCD 293) and subsequent records
  • Blood Orange (2) (Dev Hynes) — Coastal Grooves (2011, CD-DNO-301); Cupid Deluxe (2013)
  • Jon Hopkins — Music From The Film ‘Monsters’ (2010); Diamond Mine (with King Creosote, 2011, DS038CD); Immunity (2013) — released on sublabel Domino Documents (ID 1198242)
  • Anna Calvi — self-titled debut (2010/2011, WIGCD 260); One Breath (2013)
  • The Last Shadow Puppets (Alex Turner + Miles Kane) — The Age Of The Understatement (2008)
  • Wild Beasts — on Domino 2009–2017
  • Dirty Projectors (David Longstreth) — joined around 2009; Bitte Orca, subsequent records; Longstreth became a house producer figure (53 production credits)
  • Panda Bear — solo work on Domino from 2010
  • Robert Wyatt — reissue series from 1999 onward (EPs, archival)

Design through this era heavily dominated by Matthew Cooper (2) (1,144 release credits, the single most-credited person on the label), Rob Carmichael (2) (273 credits), and photographer Jason Evans (2) (189 credits).

Later Period (2014–present): Diversification and New Voices

  • Shirley Collins — Lodestar (2016), comeback after 38-year absence from recording
  • Tirzah — Devotion (2018) — electronic/R&B
  • Beth Gibbons (of Portishead) — Lives Of The Saints (2024)
  • Bill Ryder-Jones — former The Coral guitarist; solo albums A Bad Wind Blows In My Heart (2013), West Kirby County Primary (2015), Yawn (2018), Iechyd Da (2024)
  • Daniel Avery — electronic; joined 2018
  • Wet Leg — debut 2022; one of label’s fastest-growing newer acts (58 release entries)
  • Fat Dog (5) — debut 2023; 23 entries by 2025
  • My Bloody Valentine — m b v (2013) and reissue campaign from 2021
  • Ela Minus — electronic; acts of rebellion (2020)
  • Protomartyr (2) — Detroit post-punk; on Domino from 2017
  • Sorry (12) — UK indie; from 2017
  • Alex G (2) — from 2015
  • John Cale — joined 2011; MERCY (2023)
  • Björk — some releases from 2011
  • The Go-Betweens — anthology / reissue series (sublabel The Go-Betweens Anthology, ID 1818559)

Mastering over the label’s entire history dominated by Matt Colton (180 credits), Guy Davie (157 credits), Greg Calbi (108 credits), Heba Kadry (106 credits), and Howie Weinberg (108 credits).
