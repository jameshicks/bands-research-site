Dossier Records

German independent record label, founded 1985 in Berlin (West). Discogs label IDs: 118521 (Dossier Records, company/copyright entity) and 2189 (Dossier, the operational imprint). Parent company: Konnex Records (ID: 52008), formed in 1984 by Manfred Schiek. Label Code: LC 8603.

Overview

Dossier was set up in 1985 as the experimental arm of Konnex Records, a Berlin-based company run by Manfred Schiek. The label’s catalog bridges two worlds that rarely appeared on the same imprint: the NYC downtown avant-garde scene (Elliott Sharp, Wayne Horvitz, Rhys Chatham, Bobby Previte, Zeena Parkins, Butch Morris, Samm Bennett) and the European post-industrial underground (Chrome/Damon Edge, Z’EV, Bourbonese Qualk, Test Dept., Front Line Assembly, Throbbing Gristle, Psychic TV). The connection between those axes was Schiek himself, who operated as executive producer and designer across the catalog, and Martin Bisi, whose BC Studio in Brooklyn engineered many of the NYC-sourced sessions.

The label is now defunct. Its Berlin addresses trace a move from Prinzenallee 47b (1985) to Koloniestr. 25A (1990 and 1993) to Mauschbacher Steig 35A (1999). All lacquer cuts were handled by SST (8) in Germany.

Parent Company: Konnex Records

Konnex Records (ID: 52008) was formed in 1984 by Manfred Schiek (Discogs ID: 241303). Contact email on Discogs: konnex-dossier@gmx.de. In addition to Dossier, Konnex ran the following sublabels:

  • Atonal Records (ID: 11769) — documenting the Berlin Atonal festival
  • Dossier Records (ID: 118521) — the Dossier parent/copyright entity
  • Ear-Ruption Records (ID: 278706)
  • MVM (3) (ID: 389733)
  • Tycoon (ID: 25048)

Catalog Number Scheme

The primary LP series used the prefix ST followed by four-digit numbers beginning at 7502 (the first release, Sex With God by Minimal Man (2), 1985). The numbering ran roughly sequentially through the late 1980s, reaching ST 7560 by 1988. From around 1989 onward the label introduced:

  • DLP prefix for double LPs (e.g. DLP 7546, DLP 7552, DLP 7554, DLP 7563, DLP 7567)
  • DCD prefix for CDs (beginning around 1988–1989 with DCD 9006, continuing through DCD 9103 in 2005)
  • A separate unnumbered vinyl series using designators like ST -1-, ST -2- etc. (used for the Chrome/Damon Edge archival releases)

Total Discogs catalog: approximately 270 release entries (including pressings and editions), representing around 115 unique master releases across the full run.

Active Years

1985–2005 (final catalog entry: Column One, Dream Time, DCD 9103, 2005).

The NYC Downtown Axis (1985–1988)

Dossier’s most concentrated phase of NYC avant-garde activity ran from the label’s first year through 1988. Schiek appears to have made contact with the NYC improvised/experimental community and released a significant body of work by artists otherwise found on Nonesuch, Elektra Musician, New World, and the smaller downtown labels.

Rhys Chatham

Die Donnergötter (1987, ST 7538, release ID: 814040) is a three-piece LP documenting works from the mid-1980s guitar-ensemble phase:

Side A — “Die Donnergötter” (21:36): Seven-guitar ensemble. Chatham (conductor, mixed by, producer); J.P. (7) (drums, mixed by); Conrad Kinard (electric bass); Ben Neill, Bill Brovold, Karen Haglof, Mitch Salmen, Robert Poss, Tim Schellenbaum (electric guitars). Mixed jointly by Chatham, J.P., and Martin Bisi.

Side B1 — “Waterloo No. 2” (7:50): Chatham (trumpet, mixed by, producer); Wharton Tiers (mixed by); Gwendolyn Toth (keyboards); Don Yallech (percussion); Pat McCarty, Rob Bethea (trombone); Ben Neill, James O’Connor (2) (trumpet).

Side B2 — “Guitar Trio” (8:05): Chatham, Joe Dizney, Nina Canal (electric guitars); Michael Boone (electric bass); David Linton (drums). Co-produced and mixed with Peter Gordon.

Cover concept by Robert Longo; layout by Elisa Marshall. Mastered by SST (8). Rights societies: BMI and GEMA.

The personnel on Die Donnergötter map onto the wider downtown milieu: Ben Neill and Bill Brovold were core figures in Chatham’s guitar ensembles; Robert Poss and Karen Haglof would go on to found and join Band Of Susans; Nina Canal had played in Ut; David Linton was a fixture of the NYC noise-improv scene.

Bobby Previte

Dull Bang, Gushing Sound, Human Shriek (1987, ST 7532, release ID: 1413248) — solo LP, all instruments, composed and produced by Previte. An experimental/ambient/soundtrack record, its cover photographs are frame enlargements from Michael Di Paolo’s film “Bought & Sold” (1987). The styles — Abstract, Ambient, Experimental, Soundtrack — mark Previte in a different mode from his jazz drum work.

Samm Bennett / Takashi Kazamaki / Zeena Parkins

143 Ludlow St. NYC (1988, ST 7555, release ID: 1287000) — trio LP. Bennett (percussion, composed B side jointly with Kazamaki) / Kazamaki (percussion, compositions) / Zeena Parkins (electric harp, acoustic harp, accordion, composed B side jointly with Kazamaki). Engineered and mixed by Tom Cora. Cover and photography by Katie O’Looney. The title references 143 Ludlow Street, Lower East Side — a venue in the late-1980s NYC improvised music circuit. The two sides split authorship: Bennett/Kazamaki on the A side (12 short pieces), Parkins/Kazamaki on the B side (5 longer pieces).

Z’EV

Schönste Muziek (1986, ST 7510, release ID: 264136) — solo percussion LP. Z’EV (all sounds, all compositions). Recorded by Dick Lucas; executive producer Manfred Schiek. Five extended works for percussion and metal: “Titan’s Might,” “Gongs Be A Gong,” “Meg O’ Lith,” “Anubis Ah’ Soul,” “Sweet Dreams.” BMI and GEMA.

The European/Industrial Axis

Chrome and Damon Edge

The Dead Tech Compilations: Japan Connection

One of Dossier’s distinctive moves was documenting the Japanese noise/no-wave scene — an unusual bridge for a Berlin-based label in 1986:

Dead Tech Sampler — No Wave From Japan (ST 7512, 1986, release ID: 597203): Co-produced by Kazuyuki Kishino and Manfred Schiek. Featured: K.K. Null, Boredoms (early), Ruins (Tatsuya Yoshida + Hideki Kawamoto), Hijokaidan (Jojo Hiroshige, Junko, Toshiji Mikawa), YBO², High Rise (2), Zeitlich Vergelter (Chu Ishikawa), Absolut Null Punkt.

Dead Tech Vol. II (ST 7541, 1988, release ID: 717637): Co-produced by K.K. Null and Manfred Schiek. Featured: Zeni Geva (K.K. Null), Leningrad Blues Machine (Mitsuru Tabata), Junk Schizo, Marble Sheep, Zatopek Soccer, Off Mask 00, The Gerogerigegege (Juntaro Yamanouchi).

These two compilations gave European audiences early access to artists who became foundational to the Japanese noise canon, and positioned Dossier as an early European platform for Japanoise.

Scene Connections

  • NYC downtown, 1985–1988: Direct overlap with the circle around Martin Bisi’s BC Studio (Elliott Sharp, Wayne Horvitz, Rhys Chatham, Bobby Previte, Zeena Parkins, Samm Bennett). The Dossier catalog functions as a secondary document of the mid-1980s NYC avant-garde alongside the better-known NYC labels (Homestead Records, Nonesuch, Elektra Musician).
  • West Berlin, 1985–1990s: Alongside sibling label Atonal Records (documenting the Berlin Atonal festival), Dossier connected the Berlin post-industrial underground to both its European and North American counterparts.
  • Japan noise, 1986–1988: The two Dead Tech compilations gave K.K. Null, Boredoms, Ruins, Hijokaidan, and Gerogerigegege their earliest European releases.
  • Industrial/EBM continuum: Chrome/Damon Edge reissues, Front Line Assembly, Throbbing Gristle, Psychic TV, Test Dept., Noise Unit — Dossier’s 1990s catalog positioned it squarely within the industrial/EBM market.

Open Questions

  • Whether Dossier’s NYC releases were licensed from the artists or recorded specifically for the label is unclear from Discogs alone.
  • Column One (Dream Time, 2005) as the final entry is notable — a Berlin-based group suggests Dossier maintained some activity into the 2000s despite becoming primarily inactive after the late 1990s.
  • The precise mechanism by which Schiek initially made contact with the NYC scene (i.e. which artist or intermediary was the first contact) is not documented in any available web source. Martin Bisi as bridge is the strongest hypothesis but remains unconfirmed.

Notes (web research)

Manfred Schiek — Biography

Schiek was born 1943 and died 2016 in Berlin. Find a Grave memorial 196374423 (accessible only as search result metadata; page itself is paywalled). He had previously operated a label called Vinyl Records, then View Records (renamed due to a copyright conflict on the name “Vinyl”), before stepping away from that project in 1984 and founding three new labels simultaneously: Konnex, Atonal, and Dossier. [source: AllAboutJazz Konnex profile — Cloudflare-blocked during research; content reconstructed from Google snippet as of 2026-06-10]

The Konnex umbrella included at minimum the following imprints (per Discogs and search snippets): Konnex Records (jazz), Atonal Records (documenting Berlin Atonal festival), Dossier (experimental/industrial), Ear-Ruption Records, MVM (3), and Tycoon. Contact: konnex-dossier@gmx.de.

Manfred Schiek — Berlin–NYC Connection

The primary documented mechanism of the Berlin–NYC connection is Schiek’s own travel to New York. An AllAboutJazz profile of Konnex Records (by Celeste Sunderland — paywalled/Cloudflare-blocked but content partially surfaced via search snippets) states that in the early years of his labels Schiek was traveling to New York ten times a year to record artists, including Cecil Taylor, Sonny Fortune, Borah Bergman, Joe Fonda, and Perry Robinson for the Konnex jazz imprint. [source: AllAboutJazz Konnex Records profile — https://www.allaboutjazz.com/konnex-records-by-celeste-sunderland, content from Google snippet, as of 2026-06-10]

This level of transatlantic activity makes the Dossier downtown-NYC connections structurally unsurprising: Schiek was already operating as a Berlin-based label owner who regularly visited NYC and signed artists directly. The hypothesis that Martin Bisi served as a specific bridge is supported by Bisi’s engineering credits across at least eight of the Dossier NYC sessions — but no web source names Bisi as the initial point of contact. The more likely sequence is that Schiek arrived in NYC, encountered the downtown scene through his jazz contacts, and began commissioning or licensing sessions from artists in the Elliott Sharp / Wayne Horvitz / Rhys Chatham circle. BC Studio’s low rates and its position as the practical recording infrastructure for downtown experimental music made it the natural choice for these sessions. [source: Bearded Gentlemen Music Martin Bisi interview, https://beardedgentlemenmusic.com/2018/05/11/bc-35-album-and-interview-with-martin-bisi/ — Dossier not explicitly named in that interview]

My Bloody Valentine — This Is Your Bloody Valentine (1990, DCD 9019)

This is not an anomalous entry. Tycoon Records — the label that originally released the MBV mini-album in January 1985 — is listed by Discogs as a Konnex sublabel (ID: 25048), meaning it was part of Schiek’s Berlin operation. MBV recorded the album in December 1984 at SCS 8TK Studios, West Berlin, Germany, and released it on Tycoon in early 1985. [source: Wikipedia, This Is Your Bloody Valentine — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Your_Bloody_Valentine, as of 2026-06-10]

The 1990 Dossier release (DCD 9019; LP also issued as DLP 7501) is therefore a legitimate in-house reissue by the same company that had owned the rights since the original release, not a separate licensing deal or unauthorized pressing. Whether a specific person named “Demitri” operated Tycoon on Schiek’s behalf (suggested by one search snippet of uncertain provenance) could not be confirmed from available sources.

Summary: This Is Your Bloody Valentine is not an anomaly. MBV recorded in Berlin, signed to a Schiek label (Tycoon), and the 1990 Dossier reissue was a straightforward catalog reissue within the same corporate umbrella. The band’s early gothic/post-punk sound on this release has nothing to do with Dossier’s avant-garde catalog; Tycoon was a separate genre pocket within the Konnex group.

Chrome / Damon Edge — Reissue Arrangement

Damon Edge relocated to Paris in 1983 (not mid-80s as previously noted as approximate). He had founded Siren Records as Chrome’s proprietary label. [source: Wikipedia, Damon Edge — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Edge, as of 2026-06-10; Wikipedia, Chrome (band) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrome_(band), as of 2026-06-10]

The Dossier reissue arrangement was a direct deal between Edge/Siren and Schiek/Dossier: Siren Records retained production/publishing rights while Dossier held the phonographic copyright (℗) on the pressed releases. This is consistent with a licensing arrangement between Edge (by then European-based, in Paris) and the Berlin label. Edge continued recording as “Chrome featuring Damon Edge” with European musicians during this period; Dossier served as the primary European label for both new Chrome material and reissues of the original Siren catalog. The sequence: Edge moves to Paris 1983 → continues making records → Dossier picks up both the ongoing recording relationship and the back catalog reissue rights → over ~20 release entries spanning 1985–1990+. [source: Wikipedia Chrome (band) and Damon Edge; Discogs release pages for The Chronicles I, Another World, Into the Eyes of the Zombie King]

Edge returned to the US (Hollywood Hills) in 1988 with his wife Fabienne Shine; they divorced in the early 1990s. The Dossier reissue campaign continued through the early 1990s regardless. [source: Wikipedia, Damon Edge, as of 2026-06-10]

Dead Tech Compilations — Japan Connection

Dead Tech Vol. 1 (Dead Tech Sampler — No Wave From Japan, ST 7512, 1986): Recorded in 1985 in Tokyo, co-produced by Kazuyuki Kishino (K.K. Null) and Manfred Schiek. [source: MusicBrainz release entry https://musicbrainz.org/release/646a073e-1e5c-4998-99f0-9fc3730e2fb1 and search snippet metadata; Discogs release 597203]

Dead Tech Vol. II (ST 7541, 1988): Co-produced by K.K. Null and Schiek.

The mechanism of the Japan connection was K.K. Null / Kazuyuki Kishino, who served as the on-the-ground curator and co-producer for both compilations. Kishino joined the noise/progressive rock band YBO2 in 1984 and was already centrally placed in the Tokyo noise scene. [source: Wikipedia KK Null — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Null, as of 2026-06-10] No source explains how Schiek first made contact with Kishino specifically. The Discogs co-production credits are the primary documentation. Schiek’s pattern of direct transatlantic/international travel (documented for NYC) likely applied to Japan as well, or Kishino may have been introduced through the European industrial/noise network that Dossier was already embedded in.

Rhys Chatham — Die Donnergötter Recording Circumstances

Composed 1984–86. Recorded at BC Studio (Brooklyn, NY) in April 1986. [source: search snippet citing BC Studio session date, confirmed across multiple search results, 2026-06-10; Wikipedia Rhys Chatham confirms composition dates 1984–86 and Dossier/Homestead as original 1987 labels]

The Dossier release (ST 7538, 1987) is the European edition; Homestead Records handled the US release. This dual-release structure is consistent with Schiek’s practice of co-releasing or licensing NYC downtown sessions for European distribution.
