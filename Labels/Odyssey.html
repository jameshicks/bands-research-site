Overview

Odyssey launched in January 1967 as a US budget label under Columbia Records, focused predominantly on classical repertoire spanning Romantic, Baroque, and operatic styles. The catalogue was most dense in the 1960s and 1970s (150 releases in the latter decade), anchored by rosters such as the New York Philharmonic, the Philadelphia Orchestra, and Bruno Walter. The label shifted to a reissue function in the mid-1970s, reverted to budget status in 1987, and was relaunched a third time in 2002. Columbia’s Japanese affiliate independently introduced an Odyssey imprint in 1973 for modern folk music, a separate strand from the US classical line; sublabels Folk Odyssey and The Composer As Performer Series extended the domestic catalogue.
