Overview

Dossier was a German experimental and industrial music label founded in 1985 under the parent imprint Dossier Records (Label Code LC 8603). Its catalogue was most active during the 1980s, with 64 releases that decade spanning industrial, ambient, electro, and new wave — notably featuring Chrome members Damon Edge and Helios Creed alongside Canadian EBM artist Bill Leeb. Output slowed through the 1990s (33 releases) and dwindled to just three titles in the 2000s before the label folded. The Dossier name was later adopted by an unrelated entity.

Label Founder and Structure

Dossier was one of three labels launched in Berlin around 1984–1985 by record producer Manfred Schiek, who had previously run Vinyl Records. Schiek simultaneously founded Konnex Records (jazz focus) and an Atonal sublabel (electronic/classical avant-garde), with Dossier handling experimental, industrial, and electronic material. All three operated under the same Berlin umbrella organisation. Schiek is credited as executive producer on multiple Dossier releases, including the Z’EV album Schönste Muziek (1986) [source: coldspring.bandcamp.com] and the Dossiers compilation (1992) [source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dossiers]. He co-produced tracks on Chrome’s posthumous Angel of the Clouds (2002, DCD 9082) with Damon Edge [source: discogs.com/release/5566499]. The parent Konnex Records entry on Wikipedia (as of 2026-10-04) confirms Dossier as a sublabel operated by Schiek and notes that Konnex’s jazz roster included American avant-garde figures including Anthony Braxton and Cecil Taylor, indicating an existing transatlantic network of contacts in experimental and free-jazz circles.

How US Experimental Acts Came to Dossier — Web Sources

No single published source gives a direct first-person account of the mechanism, but the available evidence points to a combination of geographic proximity (Damon Edge’s relocation to Paris) and Schiek’s pre-existing transatlantic avant-garde network, rather than a formal licensing arrangement with a US label.

Damon Edge / Chrome: Edge moved to Paris in 1983 after marrying French singer Fabienne Shine, effectively becoming Europe-based. His first post-split Chrome recordings were made in France and released in 1984 on the French label Mosquito (Into the Eyes of the Zombie King, recorded at D.B. Studios, Rennes) [source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Eyes_of_the_Zombie_King]. Dossier reissued that album in Germany in 1986 (ST 7513) — the reissue pattern suggests Dossier licensed or acquired rights to existing French recordings rather than a direct US signing. From 1985 onward, new Chrome/Edge recordings appeared as Dossier originals (not reissues of US releases), suggesting a direct artist-to-label relationship established while Edge was already living in France. Helios Creed’s own account (Rank and Revue interview, helioschrome.com) confirms the Chrome/Dossier relationship lasted approximately 15 years, and after Edge’s death in 1995 Schiek’s label commissioned Creed to complete Edge’s unfinished tracks, resulting in Angel of the Clouds (2002) — a posthumous production arrangement that only makes sense in the context of a long-standing personal relationship between Schiek and the Chrome principals. A Stereo Embers interview with Creed (stereoembersmagazine.com) confirms “his German label asked me to finish the tracks,” corroborating the direct label-to-artist relationship.

New York avant-garde axis (Elliott Sharp, Wayne Horvitz, Z’EV, Bobby Previte, Controlled Bleeding, Zeena Parkins, etc.): The majority of the US experimental acts on Dossier were New York-based downtown avant-gardists. Schiek’s Konnex Records had already signed American free-jazz figures (Cecil Taylor, Anthony Braxton) by the mid-1980s, demonstrating an active channel between Schiek and the New York experimental scene. Elliott Sharp, Wayne Horvitz, Bobby Previte, and Zeena Parkins all overlap in the same downtown New York milieu. Dossier’s first catalog year (1985) includes multiple acts from this network simultaneously (Sharp, Horvitz, Controlled Bleeding), strongly suggesting Schiek had a single contact point or agent in the New York avant-garde community who facilitated multiple introductions. The Atonal sublabel ran parallel to the Berlin Atonal festival (founded separately by Dimitri Hegemann, 1982–1990), which drew international experimental acts; this shared scene context would have put Schiek in contact with touring US acts. No named intermediary or specific booking/A&R agent has been identified in any published source.

Helios Creed (solo): Creed’s solo Dossier releases followed the Chrome relationship and appear to be a natural extension of the existing label tie rather than an independent signing. The Rank and Revue interview confirms the relationship eventually wound down, with Creed expressing dissatisfaction that Dossier had moved on to more commercially viable acts.

Summary: The available evidence supports a model of (1) Edge’s physical move to France making him a de-facto European artist available to European labels, (2) Dossier acquiring rights to existing French Chrome recordings and then establishing a direct recording relationship with Edge from 1985, and (3) Schiek’s parallel jazz/avant-garde transatlantic network (via Konnex) providing the introductions that brought the broader New York experimental cohort to the label. No confirmed licensing deal with a US label or single named intermediary has been documented in public sources.
