Lust/Unlust Music

Production company / label umbrella. New York City. Active c.1978–c.1982. Discogs IDs: 19079 (Lust/Unlust Music), 320236 (Lust/Unlust Music Inc.), 1977849 (Lust/Unlust Ltd.).

Founded by Charles Ball. The core No Wave production operation of the downtown New York scene, 1978–1982. Ball had previously co-founded Ork Records with Terry Ork; Lust/Unlust was his independent follow-on. Roy Trakin: Ball “almost singlehandedly championed No Wave back at a time no one else got it.”

Operated as a production company, not a conventional label: Ball produced and funded releases but let artists choose their own imprint names — hence multiple imprints (Infidelity, Migraine, etc.) all flowing from one operation. The name comes from Lacan.

Production Notes

Ball was technically innovative: binaural recording on the Mars EP, early digital reverbs and delays. He maintained unreleased live and field recordings from the No Wave scene (unreleased as of 2003). Comparable to Martin Hannett in technical ambition within a D.I.Y. context.

Closure

Ball’s own explanation: “the label was ultimately stymied financially by the lack of really strong local support for No Wave.” — Charles Ball, interviewed by Simon Reynolds for Rip It Up and Start Again: Postpunk 1978–1984 (2005); extended excerpt published on the companion footnotes blog, 22 November 2008. http://ripitupfootnotes.blogspot.com/2008/11/footnotes-5-chapter-4-contort-yourself.html

Corroborating detail: Nina Canal recorded with Ball in 1980 and noted that “Charles suddenly left town without warning” — the Ut 7” single planned for that year was never released as a result. https://craigball.net/2012/08/14/charles-cameron-ball-1951-2012/

Robin Crutchfield (DNA, Dark Day): “He really didn’t get all the credit due him. I wish his creditors hadn’t outrun him on his vision.” https://www.noise11.com/news/label-owner-charles-ball-passes-away-20120816

The label was operationally wound down c.1981–1982, consistent with Ball’s abrupt departure from New York around 1980–1981.
