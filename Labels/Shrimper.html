Shrimper

Independent cassette and vinyl label founded in Upland, California by Dennis Callaci of Refrigerator. Discogs label ID: 31508. Website: http://www.shrimperrecords.com/ Mailing address: P.O. Box 1837, Upland, CA 91785-1837.

Sublabel: Bamboo Dart Press Special Projects (Discogs ID: 3494149).

Overview

Shrimper is one of the defining American DIY cassette labels of the 1990s, launched around 1990 out of the Upland/Pomona area of the Inland Empire, Southern California. Dennis Callaci — guitarist and vocalist with Refrigerator — ran the label as a vehicle for his own band and for the broader network of lo-fi cassette-culture artists he was connected to through tape trading and the underground fanzine circuit.

The label is best known outside its immediate scene as the primary early home for The Mountain Goats (and by extension John Darnielle): every major Mountain Goats cassette from 1991 through the mid-1990s came out on Shrimper, establishing the aesthetic template of boombox-recorded acoustic guitar that defined Darnielle’s early work. But Shrimper’s catalog extends far beyond that single relationship — at 245+ entries on Discogs it is a sprawling document of an entire underground cassette network spanning California, Nebraska, New York, and beyond.

The catalog runs from 1990 to the present (still active as of 2024), with the densest activity in the 1991–1999 period. Catalog numbers use an “SHR” prefix (SHR 001 onward for regular releases; SHR 701-series for compilations). Formats skew heavily toward cassette in the early years, with CD and vinyl entering the catalog later.

Founding and Cassette Culture Context

Shrimper’s launch coincides with the peak of the American cassette underground — a network sustained by dubbed tapes, hand-duplicated covers, mail-order distribution, and the fanzines that connected isolated musicians across the country. Labels in this ecosystem operated at very low cost: a cassette could be produced and distributed for nearly nothing, which enabled the kind of prolific, low-stakes output that Shrimper exemplified.

Callaci’s own band Refrigerator was the label’s anchor, releasing more titles on Shrimper (21 by Discogs count) than any other act. The label also drew in the Claremont/Inland Empire orbit that Callaci inhabited, including artists like Goosewind, Wckr Spgt, and Diskothi-Q — the latter featuring Peter Hughes (2) who would later become the Mountain Goats’ full-time bassist.

Lou Barlow of Sebadoh appears in the catalog both as Sentridoh (his solo alias) and under his own name — Lou B’s Wasted Pieces ‘87–‘93 (SHR 48, 1993) and The Original Losing Losers ‘82–‘91 (SHR 67, 1995) — cementing Shrimper’s connection to the New England lo-fi axis that crossed with the California underground through the tape network.

Key Releases by Era

Launch Period 1990–1991

The label opened with compilations and cassettes from the immediate Inland Empire orbit. SHR 001 was Winky Dog (1990), a Various Artists comp. The earliest catalog numbers document Callaci’s own bands (Paste (2) = Callaci solo alias, SHR 007), Buzzsaw (SHR 005), Wckr Spgt (SHR 08 — 5 Years (Aloha)), and Franklin Bruno’s Suggestion Box: Home Recordings 1984–1990 (SHR 09). Refrigerator’s first Shrimper release was Lonesome Surprize (SHR 13, 1991).

The Mountain Goats entered the catalog in 1991 with Taboo VI: The Homecoming (Shrimper 27), John Darnielle’s first widely circulated recording.

This period constitutes the label’s most prolific run, concentrated on cassette format. The Mountain Goats discography deepens significantly:

Alongside these, the label issued key releases by:

  • Lou Barlow / Sentridoh: Lou B’s Wasted Pieces ‘87–‘93 (SHR 48, 1993) and The Original Losing Losers ‘82–‘91 (SHR 67, 1995)
  • Soul-Junk: 1950 Free Shrimp (SHR 49, 1994) and 1951 (SHR 56, 1995)
  • Diskothi-Q: Harmless But Ultimately Unlovable / Loveless But Ultimately Unharmable (SHR 38, 1993), The Wandering Jew (SHR 55, 1994)

  • The Secret Stars: self-titled (SHR 60, 1995)
  • John Davis (2): multiple cassettes beginning 1993
  • Simon Joyner: Songs For The New Year (SHR 92, 1996)
  • Dennis Callaci + John Davis (2): collaborative Room For Space (SHR 74, 1996)

Compilations were central: Abridged Perversion (A Shrimper Compilation Of Shrimper Compilations) (SHR 98, 1993) collected material from earlier comp tapes, giving an overview of the ecosystem.

Transition to CD/Vinyl 1997–2003

The late 1990s saw Shrimper expand formats while maintaining its DIY character. Refrigerator’s self-titled LP/CD (SHR 101, 1997) marked a step toward vinyl. James McNew’s Dump project appeared with That Skinny Motherfucker With The High Voice? (SHR 106, 1998), a covers album. Simon Joyner continued with Blue Melody (Live From The South) (SHR 140, 2003). The label slowed somewhat after 2000 before picking back up.

Later Catalog 2007–Present

Woods (2) — the Brooklyn psychedelic folk band featuring Kevin Morby and Jeremy Earl — released their first two albums on Shrimper: At Rear House (SHR 150, 2007) and How To Survive In/In The Woods (SHR 152, 2007), before moving to Woodsist. The Babies (2), another Kevin Morby-affiliated project, appeared in 2011 (SHR 160). The label has continued into the 2020s with releases by Goosewind (SHR 215, 2024), Ben Woods (5) from New Zealand, and others.

The 20th-anniversary compilation Smooth Sounds: Various Artists Play The Future Hits Of Wckr Spgt (SHR 158, 2010) demonstrated the ongoing cohesion of the label’s community.

Geographic Orbit

The label’s core in the early period is the Inland Empire (Upland, Pomona, Claremont) — a suburban zone east of Los Angeles that produced a notable cluster of lo-fi artists in the early 1990s. Beyond that, the label drew artists from:

  • Nebraska (Simon Joyner, Omaha area)
  • Massachusetts / New England (Lou Barlow / Sentridoh)
  • New York (God Is My Co-Pilot, later Woods (2))
  • San Diego (Soul-Junk)
  • The broader national cassette underground tape-trading network
