Predecessor labels: Recommended Records France (ID: 183472) → AAA (ID: 65619) → Ayaa (SARL)

Overview

Ayaa was a small French independent label and distributor based in Reims, active from the mid-1980s to mid-1990s. It was run by Dominique Diebold, who had previously co-founded and directed the same entity under two prior names: first Recommended Records France (a regional affiliate of the UK’s Recommended Records network), then the association AAA (“A l’Automne Alité”) at the same Reims address. When the association changed its legal form to a SARL (limited liability company), it became Ayaa — the name appearing in various forms across releases (Ayaa Disques, Ayaa Distribution, Ayaa Records).

The label operated at the intersection of French and Swiss avant-garde/post-Rock In Opposition scenes. Its catalog crosses from angular post-punk and free improvisation through folk-inflected avant-garde and chamber music, and encompasses a notably international roster for a label of its size: British, Swiss, Estonian, Slovenian, and Italian artists all appear. The French acts on the label — particularly the Look De Bouk and Hellebore/Officer! orbits from Reims itself — overlap heavily with each other, suggesting a tight local scene. The Swiss acts (L’Ensemble Rayé, Nimal) likely arrived through Diebold’s pre-existing connections in the Rock In Opposition distribution network. Internationally distributed acts like The Work, Etron Fou Leloublan, and Ghédalia Tazartès give the catalog a broader avant-garde legitimacy.

Jean-Maurice Rossel of L’Ensemble Rayé and Nimal served as the most prolific engineer/producer figure across Ayaa releases, accounting for five engineering or mixing credits and production co-credits on key records.

Reims Local Scene

Several Ayaa artists appear to have been based in or near Reims, centered around a loose network of musicians that the label’s predecessor associations helped organize. The most prominent cluster:

Look De Bouk (ID: 495746) — three releases (1985–1990). Members: Alain Casari (sax, clarinet, flute), Denis Tagu (keyboards, accordion), Didier Pietton (saxophone), Dorian Feller, Etienne Himalaya (percussion), Kwettap Ieuw (keyboards, vocals). The personnel of Look De Bouk overlaps heavily with the next two groups.

Hellebore (ID: 543471) — two releases (1985). Members: Alain Casari, Antoine Gindt, Daniel Koskowitz, Denis Tagu, Jean Caël.

Officer! (ID: 722910) — three releases (1984–1985). Members included: Antoine Gindt, Bill Gilonis, Claudia Schmid, Daniel Koskowitz, Felix Fiedorowicz, Mick Hobbs, Patrick Q-Wright. The crossover with The Work (Gilonis, Hobbs) marks a UK-France axis.

NEO Museum (2) — vol. 1 of an “Ethnological Museum” series (1986). Musicians include Antoine Gindt, Daniel Koskowitz, Alain Casari — the same core.

The core Reims musicians recurring across these outfits:

  • Denis Tagu — keyboards/piano/accordion/organ/vocals: 7 releases across Look De Bouk, Hellebore, NEO Museum
  • Daniel Koskowitz — drums/percussion/keyboards: 6 releases (Look De Bouk, Hellebore, Officer!, NEO Museum); also mixing credits
  • Antoine Gindt — guitar/piano/synthesizer: 5 releases (Officer!, Hellebore, NEO Museum); also recorded multiple releases
  • Jean Caël — bass/guitar: 5 releases
  • Kwettap Ieuw — keyboards/vocals/percussion: 4 releases; also sleeve design
  • Etienne Himalaya — drums/percussion: 3 releases; also design concept
  • Alain Casari — saxophones/clarinet/flute: 4 releases

Key People

Dominique Diebold (Discogs ID: 765114)

French producer and distributor. Co-founded the label’s predecessor associations (Recommended Records France, then AAA) and directed the operation through its full run as Ayaa. Also credited as a musician on Nimal’s 1987 debut (drums on “La Marelle”; mix/co-production). Name variation: D.D.

Jean-Maurice Rossel (Discogs ID: 500700)

Swiss multi-instrumentalist and engineer, member of L’Ensemble Rayé and Nimal. Most prolific technical contributor on the label: engineered and mixed both L’Ensemble Rayé CDs and the Nimal Dis Tanz CD; also credited on the Octavo release. Founded LabelUsineS in Neuchâtel.

Fred Frith (Discogs ID: 78003)

British guitarist and composer. Produced Etron Fou Leloublan’s two Ayaa LPs and the Klimperei debut, and appears as performer on French Gigs (1991) with Lol Coxhill. Three total production credits on the label.

Ferdinand Richard (Discogs ID: 509991)

Bassist and founder of Etron Fou Leloublan. Producer of Les Batteries’ Noisy Champs (1986). Writer and performer across the EFL Ayaa releases.

Guigou Chenevier (Discogs ID: 488540)

Drums/saxophone/vocals; member of Etron Fou Leloublan and Octavo. Five releases on the label as performer and composer. Key figure in the French avant-rock scene.

Sophie Jausserand (Discogs ID: 544903)

Artwork and cover design on four Ayaa releases: Etron Fou Leloublan, Les Batteries, and Octavo.

Etienne Himalaya (Discogs ID: 544292)

Percussion and design on three Look De Bouk releases; sleeve and concept design recurring credit.

Nathalie Lucas (Discogs ID: 2166013)

Layout on three releases including Ne Zhdali’s Rhinoceroses And Other Forms Of Life.

Tatjana Hauptmann (Discogs ID: 2436771)

Artwork/cover design on three releases: both L’Ensemble Rayé CDs and one other.

Bratko Bibič (Discogs ID: 524571)

Slovenian accordionist; member of Nimal and Begnagrad. Mixed the Nimal Dis Tanz CD; accordion/piano on Begnagrad’s Ayaa release.

International Artists

Beyond the French and Swiss core:

  • The Work (UK): Post-punk/avant-rock (Tim Hodgkinson, Mick Hobbs, Bill Gilonis, Rick Wilson, Catherine Jauniaux). Rubber Cage LP (1989), also pressed in US and Europe. Gilonis and Hobbs also appear in Officer!.
  • Etron Fou Leloublan (France): Three releases (1985). Foundational French avant-rock; Ferdinand Richard and Guigou Chenevier as core. Fred Frith produced both Ayaa EFL records.
  • Ghédalia Tazartès (France/Israel): Two releases (1987, 1989). Idiosyncratic vocalist and composer; widely cited in the French experimental tradition.
  • Ne Zhdali (Estonia/Netherlands): Rhinoceroses And Other Forms Of Life CD (1992). Estonian experimental ensemble (members include Leonid Soybelman, Vadim Veeremaa, Oleg Davidovich, Vitaly Redchits, Victor Bitlovsky, Ilja Komarov); mixed at BeAt studio and by Dolf Planteijdt.
  • Begnagrad (Slovenia): Konzert For A Broken Dance CD (1990). Bratko Bibič’s chamber-folk group.
  • Lars Hollmer (Sweden): Vandelmässa CD (1994). Swedish keyboardist/accordionist, associated with Samla Mammas Manna.
  • Klimperei (France): Three releases (1991–1995). Christophe Petchanatz and Françoise Lefebvre; lo-fi toy-instrument domestic sound-world. Debut produced by Ayaa label.
  • Fred Frith / Lol Coxhill: French Gigs live CD (1991).
  • Les Batteries: Drum trio (Charles Hayward, Guigou Chenevier, Rick Brown); Noisy Champs (1986), produced by Ferdinand Richard.

Open Questions

  • Exact founding year of the AAA association and which specific releases (if any) appeared under it before the Ayaa name change — the first DT-numbered releases (1984) suggest the Ayaa name was in use from the start of the vinyl catalog, but the profile says AAA had “a few vinyl releases in 1982 and 1983.”
  • Dominique Diebold’s connection to Recommended Records UK — was this a formal licensing arrangement or informal?
  • The Douze Pour Un Vol. 2 (1987) compilation: what artists appear on it? Credits show only lacquer cut and photography; tracklist not confirmed from available data.
  • Hellebore’s two releases (Il Y A Des Jours 1985): same catalog number DT 0385 across two Discogs entries — may be two pressings of the same release.
  • Look De Bouk’s Bec Et Ongles (1990) uses cat no. T.I.C.0890 — different series from DT/CDT; significance unclear.
  • No profile or background information on Toupidek Limonade, Les I, or Larsen Rupin recoverable from Discogs alone. NEO Museum (2) now has a stub page.
  • Look De Bouk’s Bec Et Ongles (1990) catalog-prefix T.I.C. and …Avec Des Coussins Bleus (1988) prefix A.C.R. remain unexplained; may be internal project codes or acronyms for a production entity. The 2001 compilation confirms Look De Bouk had additional unreleased material beyond the three Ayaa releases.
  • Lars Hollmer’s Vandelmässa (1994) — an unusual late addition to the catalog; relationship unclear.
  • Whether Ayaa distributed releases for other labels beyond its own catalog (the label operated also as “Ayaa Distribution”).
  • Label appears to have ceased activity after the 1995 Klimperei CD; no confirmed closure date or reason found.
