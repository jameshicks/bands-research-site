Sunnyside Records

US independent jazz label based in New York. Founded 1982 by François Zalacain. Discogs label ID: 40726. Parent company: Sunnyside Communications, Inc. (Discogs ID: 269608).

History & Founding

Sunnyside was established in 1982 by François Zalacain, who has served as producer or executive producer on the majority of the label’s catalog ever since. The first release — Mirth Song (SSC 1001, 1982) by Harold Danko and Rufus Reid — set the template: inside-New York jazz, small-group acoustic, often piano-led.

The early catalog (SSC 1001–1030) was primarily produced at New York studios engineered by Chip Stokes, mastered by Alan Moy, and had sleeve design by Christopher Coffey and photography by Patxi (3). By the late 1980s, a number of the label’s releases were issued in France before the US, reflecting a distribution arrangement there, with Christopher Drukker taking over as the dominant in-house art director and designer — a relationship that has continued for over three decades (he is credited on more than 290 releases).

During the late 1980s and 1990s the label broadened its scope to include Afro-Cuban jazz (Jerry Gonzalez and the Fort Apache Band), vocal jazz (Meredith D’Ambrosio, Jay Clayton), and piano trio playing from Fred Hersch, Harold Danko, Kirk Lightsey, and Eddie Higgins. James Williams served as both artist and house producer through the 1980s and early 1990s.

Through the 2000s Sunnyside expanded its international reach significantly. The Israeli bassist/pianist Avishai Cohen made his debut on the label in 2003 (Lyla, SSC 4601) on a joint release with the RazDaz Recordz imprint. Brazilian vocalist Luciana Souza became one of the label’s most prolific artists, releasing a series of duet and solo albums. The label also began distributing catalog through partnerships, hosting reissues of Charles Mingus, Paul Bley / Steve Swallow / Jimmy Giuffre, Gil Evans, and Michel Petrucciani.

By the 2010s, Sunnyside was regularly releasing music that crossed jazz into contemporary classical (Dan Tepfer’s Goldberg Variations / Variations, SSC 1284, 2011) and electronics. Kurt Rosenwinkel’s Caipi (SSC 4618, 2017) — featuring Eric Clapton and Mark Turner (2) — was among the label’s highest-profile releases. The label has continued actively into the 2020s, with over 870 catalog entries in Discogs as of 2025.

Catalog Numbers

The main label uses the SSC prefix (Sunnyside Communications), followed by a four-digit number. Early LPs used SSC 1001–1035 (1982–1989). CDs through the 1990s–2000s ran roughly SSC 1040D–SSC 1200s. Sub-series prefixes observed:

  • SSC 1xxx — main catalog, solo/group releases
  • SSC 3xxx — compilations, reissues, world/French catalog
  • SSC 4xxx — RazDaz Recordz joint releases (e.g., Avishai Cohen, Kurt Rosenwinkel)

Open Questions

  • Exact founding date in 1982 (month): Discogs says “established in New York in 1982” but gives no month. The first release (SSC 1001) was issued that year.
  • Francois Zalacain’s biographical background: his national origin, prior industry experience, and relationship to the French distribution network are not documented in Discogs.
  • The precise nature of the Confluences Series sublabel and its release count are unclear — it shares a logo with Universal Music Jazz France.
  • Distribution deal details: several mid-1980s releases have France listed as the country of issue, suggesting a European distribution arrangement that is not formally documented in the Discogs entries.
  • Whether Sunnyside still hosts Anzic, CAM Jazz, Paquito Records, RazDaz, and Walrus (per parent company description) or if some of these have since departed.
