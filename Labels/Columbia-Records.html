Columbia Records

Major American record label. Founded January 1889, Washington DC. Parent: Sony Music Entertainment. Discogs label ID: 1866 (primary US/international imprint; also catalogued as “Columbia Records” ID 93330 under Sony). 381,000+ releases in Discogs. The oldest continuously operating brand name in recorded sound.

Origin: USA
Active: 1889–present

Overview

Columbia traces its origins to the Columbia Phonograph Company, incorporated January 1889 by Edward D. Easton to sell phonograph cylinders and, later, disc records. It opened a British operation shortly after founding; the UK branch was sold in 1922 and eventually became part of Electric and Musical Industries (EMI) in 1931. US anti-trust laws forced the divestiture of US operations from EMI at the same time, keeping the American and British Columbias separate for decades.

In the US, Columbia passed through CBS Inc. ownership from 1938 onward, operating under the parent structure of CBS Records until the late 1980s. It released its first stereo discs on July 28, 1958. On November 2, 1989, CBS sold its recording group to Sony for $2.1 billion. Sony rebranded CBS Records as Sony Music Entertainment and relaunched the Columbia name globally as of January 1, 1991. Columbia remains one of Sony Music’s four flagship imprints worldwide (except Japan, where the Columbia trademark is independently held by Nippon Columbia).

US offices:

  • 1965–1988: 51 West 52nd Street, New York
  • 1988–2016: 550 Madison Avenue, New York
  • From 2016: 25 Madison Avenue, New York

Label code: LC 0162 / LC 00162. Legacy/reissue sub-imprint: Columbia Legacy.

Key Eras

Pre-War and Early Recording Era (1889–1940s)

Columbia was one of the two dominant American labels of the 78 rpm era alongside Victor. Key artists of the early decades include Henry Burr (440 releases, 1902–1926), Bessie Smith (1923–), Benny Goodman (1931–), Duke Ellington (1933–), Louis Armstrong, and an extensive early classical/orchestral catalog.

Post-War and Golden Era (1950s–1960s)

A&R chief John Hammond shaped the label’s talent roster across multiple decades — signing Billie Holiday (1941 onward), Bob Dylan (1962), Aretha Franklin (1960–1966 before Atlantic), and Bruce Springsteen (1973). Mitch Miller led pop A&R in the 1950s.

Key signings and releases:

  • Miles Davis — from 1957; Kind of Blue (1959), Bitches Brew (1970), and much of the defining Columbia jazz catalog
  • The Dave Brubeck Quartet — Time Out (1959)
  • Thelonious Monk — 1963–
  • Bob Dylan — 1962–present (with some gaps); Freewheelin’, Blonde on Blonde, Blood on the Tracks, etc.
  • Simon & Garfunkel — Sounds of Silence (1966) through Bridge Over Troubled Water (1970)
  • Johnny Cash — 1958–1986

  • The Clash — UK punk/rock; Columbia in the US from 1977; London Calling (1979), Combat Rock (1982)

  • John McLaughlin — Mahavishnu-era and solo; 1971–
  • Wynton Marsalis — 1982–present
  • Branford Marsalis Quartet — late 1980s–1990s; Random Abstract (1988), Crazy People Music (1990), The Beautyful Ones Are Not Yet Born (1991), Bloomington (1993), The Dark Keys (1996), Requiem (1999). Producer on most Columbia dates: Delfeayo Marsalis.
  • The V.S.O.P. Quintet — V.S.O.P. (1977, live double)
  • Aerosmith — 1973–; Toys in the Attic (1975), Rocks (1976)
  • Judas Priest — from 1977 (US distribution)
  • Blue Öyster Cult — 1971–
  • The Psychedelic Furs — 1980–; US debut (1980, NJC 36791) had different tracklist from the European CBS version, including two Martin Hannett-produced tracks not on the UK edition. US catalog through World Outside (1991).
  • Translator (3) — 1982–1996; via 415 Records distribution and direct Columbia signing (Translator, 1985)
  • Fishbone — 1985–1996; produced by David Kahne; debut EP through Give A Monkey A Brain… (1993); Fishbone 101 retrospective (1996, Columbia/Legacy)

2010s–Present
