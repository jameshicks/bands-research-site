From Discogs

A British punk / new wave label formed in late 1977 by Martin Davis (managing director), along with former Liberty Records and United Artists Records UK MD Andrew Lauder. Label Code: LC 5454 / LC 05454 The label made its first releases in early 1978.

Funded and distributed by WEA, Radar immediately signed the Jake Riviera-managed Elvis Costello and Nick Lowe.

The Costello and Lowe releases were popular, as were records by three acts who were signed to US Polydor through a licensing deal: The Inmates (2), Bram Tchaikovsky and Yachts. However, most other Radar releases didn’t sell well enough to meet WEA’s expectations. At the end of 1979, Radar’s staff was dismissed, while Costello, Lowe, Riviera and Lauder formed a new label, F-Beat Records. However, WEA kept the Radar name alive for a few last releases through early 1981.

Between 1995 and 1998, Radar was revived as a semi-indie label under the leadership of Rob Collins (4) and Graeme Beattie. They assembled a new roster that included Midget (4), Prolapse and Pure Morning, the precursor to Clinic.

— Discogs label profile, ID 184057

Overview

A British punk and new wave label co-founded in late 1977 by managing director Martin Davis and Andrew Lauder, formerly of Liberty Records and United Artists Records UK, with funding and distribution from WEA. Its highest-profile signings were Elvis Costello and Nick Lowe, both managed by Jake Riviera; it also licensed recordings from The Inmates, Bram Tchaikovsky, and Yachts through US Polydor. Despite those successes, most of the roster underperformed against WEA’s expectations, and at the close of 1979 the staff was dismissed — Costello, Lowe, Riviera, and Lauder departing to launch F-Beat Records, while WEA kept the Radar name active for scattered releases into 1981. A second chapter ran from 1995 to 1998 under Rob Collins and Graeme Beattie, assembling a new indie roster that included Prolapse and Pure Morning, the latter a precursor to Clinic.

Notes (web) — WEA dismissal, end of 1979

Primary cause: roster underperformance against WEA sales expectations; mechanism: WEA buyout of the Radarscope 50:50 partnership.

Radar was structured as a joint venture between the Cladhurst company (renamed Radarscope), owned by Andrew Lauder and Martin Davis, and WEA, which funded and distributed the label. The original agreement, reported in Music Week (26 November 1977), described Radar as autonomous but able to draw on WEA’s marketing and promotional resources. (7tt77.co.uk)

By late 1979 the arrangement was visibly in trouble. Music Week (3 November 1979) reported that “the 50:50 partnership between Radarscope and WEA appeared to be floundering” with “comprehensive negotiations” about Radar’s future under way. Six weeks later, Music Week (22 December 1979) confirmed WEA had bought out Davis and Lauder and converted the licensed Radarscope company into a wholly-owned WEA operation. (7tt77.co.uk; search result snippets citing both issues)

Root cause as characterised by contemporary sources: Radar’s roster was highly eclectic and few acts besides Costello and Lowe met WEA’s sales expectations. The Inmates, Bram Tchaikovsky, and Yachts performed reasonably via their US Polydor licensing deal, but most other signings underperformed. No source names a specific numeric sales threshold or a formal contractual trigger. (7tt77.co.uk; Discogs label profile)

No personal falling-out documented. No source found — including the Terrascope interview with Lauder (terrascope.co.uk), the Wikipedia article on Lauder (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lauder_(music_executive)), or the F-Beat Wikipedia page (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-Beat_Records) — documents a personal or professional dispute with Andrew Lauder or Jake Riviera as a precipitating factor. The split is described consistently as a commercial/structural decision by WEA.

No WEA UK restructuring documented. No source identifies a broader WEA corporate restructuring as a cause. The buyout was specific to the Radarscope joint venture.

Post-buyout administration: WEA installed Stuart Hornall, an Elektra/Asylum manager, to administer the label. The plan was to retain the artist roster and continue the label’s identity, but Costello and Lowe moved to Andrew Lauder’s new F-Beat in early 1980, leaving the Inmates, Bram Tchaikovsky, and Yachts as Radar’s rump roster until the label’s last release in April 1981. (7tt77.co.uk)
