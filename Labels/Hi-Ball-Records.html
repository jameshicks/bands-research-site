Hi-Ball Records

Chicago indie label, active c. 1990–1999. Discogs ID: 61813. Parent label in Discogs listed as ID 212 (unresolved in database).

Overview

Hi-Ball Records was a small Chicago indie imprint whose catalog of roughly 15 releases (across all pressings; ~11 unique titles) is dominated almost entirely by The Coctails and their circle. The label launched in 1990 with the Coctails’ debut LP and remained active through 1999, when Archer Prewitt’s second solo album White Sky was the final entry. Beyond the Coctails, Hi-Ball released records by Louisville band Evergreen (5) (two releases, 1994–1996), solo work by Archer Prewitt (two records, 1997–1999), and a handful of one-off 7”s from adjacent Chicago acts: Brokeback (Douglas McCombs), Dump (James McNew of Yo La Tengo), M.O.T.O., Dame Darcy / The Coctails, and Dianogah split with The Log Letters.

The label’s visual identity was handled throughout by the design team of Kathy Moseley and Kim R. Kolb (art direction and layout on the majority of releases), with photography often by Giselle Simon. Archer Prewitt, himself an accomplished cartoonist and illustrator, contributed cover artwork to several releases beyond his own.

The label name may derive from the HiBall Lounge, suggested by the catalog entry The HiBall Lounge Sessions Volume 1 (cat# 1001), a sampler/compilation that has no year in Discogs.

The Coctails

The Coctails were the anchor act. Formed at the Kansas City Art Institute, they relocated to Chicago and were active 1988–1995. Core lineup: Archer Prewitt (guitar, drums, vocals, trumpet), Mark Greenberg (organ, accordion, piano, vibraphone, drums, vocals), John Upchurch (reeds, banjo, guitar, vocals — credited on Discogs as “John Upturch,” a common anv), and Barry Phipps (bass, drums, saxophone, vocals). The band’s sound moved across jazz, lounge, space-age pop, and art rock — frequently all within the same album.

Coctails Discography on Hi-Ball

Hip Hip Hooray! (1990, HBS-8255, LP) Debut album. Recorded by Jeff Johnson (5). Thirteen tracks including “Florence,” “Das Bakeria (The Donut Shoppe),” “Ghost Town,” and a version of “Waiting for Godot” (words by Samuel Beckett). Side B track “Gumshoe” features guest performers Jim Holmes (5), Mark Southerland, Kyle Dahlquist, and Christian Hankel. Styles: Art Rock, Cool Jazz, Lounge, Space-Age.

Evergreen (5)

See Evergreen (5) for the full page. Summary for Hi-Ball context:

Louisville-based act (also recording as Cinderblock (3)). Their two Hi-Ball releases mark a significant turn in sound — from the Self-Destruct Records hardcore phase toward garage rock and lo-fi indie. The Hi-Ball connection appears to run through Archer Prewitt, who drew the 7” cover illustration, and possibly through shared Chicago/Louisville scene contacts.

Pants Off / The Queen Song (1994, HBEP-0894, 7”) Limited, numbered pressing. Drums by Britt Walford. Cover illustration by Archer Prewitt. Engineered by Steve Good; mastered by Stan Getz II; tape transfers by Robert Weston. Art direction by Kathy Moseley / Kim R. Kolb.

Evergreen (1996, HBCD-0996 / HBLP-0996 / HBCS-0996, LP released on CD, LP, and cassette) Recorded by James Murphy (later LCD Soundsystem) and Nicolas Vernhes (later Rare Book Room studio). Mastered by Steve Rooke. All personnel uncredited on the pressing itself. An early recording credit for both engineers.

Archer Prewitt Solo

After the Coctails dissolved (c. 1995), Prewitt launched a solo career while also joining The Sea And Cake. Hi-Ball released two Prewitt LPs.

In The Sun (1997, HBLP0497 / HBEP0497, LP + bonus 7”) Self-produced by Prewitt with Ken Sluiter. Chamber-pop / acoustic record with strings, brass, and woodwinds. Notable guest: Chris Ware (the cartoonist / graphic novelist) plays piano on “Leave It Gone” and electric piano on “I’m All You Know.” Dave Max Crawford contributes trumpet on multiple tracks. John Upchurch and Mark Greenberg (Coctails bandmates) also appear.

Other Catalog Entries

Dame Darcy / The Coctails — Tardvark (1994, HBEP-0794) Split EP. Dame Darcy (the cartoonist and musician) appears with the Coctails. Archer Prewitt produced and recorded the session. Features bass clarinet (John Upchurch), musical saw (Upchurch), vibraphone (Greenberg), and various percussions. Additional participant Robert Nedelkoff (metal trough), Kaki Nedelkoff (shaker).

M.O.T.O. — Street Where Love Lives (1994, HBEP-0494, 7”) Paul Caporino (guitar, bass, vocals) plus Beck Dudley (drums). Engineered by Dan White (4) and Robert Weston. Art direction by Kathy Moseley / Kim R. Kolb.

The Log Letters — two releases:

  • Drum Set (1995, HBEP-1295) — guitar by Ann-Marie Riederer; guitar, drums, and recording by Mark Greenberg; mixed by Reza Ebrahimi; printed by Steve Walters
  • The Love Letters (1996, HBEP-6896) — guitar by Ann-Marie Riederer and Mark Greenberg

The Log Letters appear to be a Mark Greenberg side project.

Brokeback — Another Routine Day Breaks (1997, HBEP-1097, 7”) Douglas McCombs as sole performer. Three tracks: “Another Routine Day Breaks,” “The Airliner,” and a cover of “Brazil” (Ary Barroso / S.K. Russell). Recorded by Bundy K. Brown. Artwork by Sheila Sachs. Styles: Acoustic, Post Rock.

Dump — Phantom Perspective b/w The Lie (1997, HBEP-0297, 7”) Dump is the solo project of James McNew (Yo La Tengo). Loops, electronics (ring modulation), maracas, vocals by McNew alone.

Dianogah / The Log Letters — Super Tiny Electric Car / Split Song For Dianogah (1998, HBEP-0298, 7”) Split 7” between Dianogah (Chicago) and The Log Letters. No personnel detail in Discogs.

Open Questions

  • The parent label listed in Discogs (ID: 212) has no name resolved in the database — unclear what imprint or distributor Hi-Ball sat under.
  • The undated HiBall Lounge Sessions Volume 1 sampler (cat# 1001) has no year, no personnel, and no tracklist in Discogs. Could be a promo/distribution piece or an early release predating the 1990 debut LP.
  • Label founder / operator identity: Discogs carries no profile text identifying who ran Hi-Ball. Whether it was Barry Phipps, another Coctails member, or an outside party is not in the DB.
  • The Coctails issued releases on other labels (Carrot Top Records, Insipid Vinyl, Moll Tonträger) during the same period — the relationship between Hi-Ball and those is not documented here.
  • Archer Prewitt moved subsequent solo records to Carrot Top and then Thrill Jockey after Hi-Ball; the label appears to have closed after White Sky (1999) without announcement in Discogs.
