Overview

Lissy’s Records is a London-based independent label that operated through the 1990s, issuing mainly 7” singles and vinyl albums with a focus on synth pop, post rock, and experimental electronica. Its compact catalogue of around a dozen releases features a roster that includes Prolapse, New Bad Things, Wisdom of Harry, and Mandy Parnell, alongside releases by Stereolab and Bugger Sod. All 12 known releases fall within the 1990s, with the label’s first credit dating to 1995. The label’s corporate structure — any parent company or sublabels — has not been documented.

Notes (web)

Stereolab connection

Stereolab appear on Lissy’s Records in two capacities:

  1. Split 7” with Füxa (LISS 15, 1996) — “You Used To Call Me Sadness” (Stereolab) / “Skyhigh” (Füxa), pressed in a limited edition of 1,900 copies. (Wikipedia — Stereolab discography, as of 2026-11-04; grunnenrocks.nl — Lissy’s label page)

  2. Compilation track on “Godz Is Not A Put On” (LISS 5, LP+7”) — a Lissy’s Various Artists compilation that also featured Royal Trux and Comet Gain. (grunnenrocks.nl — Lissy’s label page)

Why Lissy’s, given Duophonic and Too Pure?

The Lissy’s split fits a well-documented pattern in Stereolab’s 1990s release strategy. Throughout that decade the band deliberately placed limited-edition 7” singles and split singles on a wide range of small indie labels independent of their primary homes — releases documented with Unrest (1993), Yo La Tengo (1995), Cat’s Miaow (1995), Tortoise (1996), Foetus/Faust (1996), and Fugu (1997), among others. (Wikipedia — Stereolab discography, as of 2026-11-04) Tim Gane described their original plan as putting out one small-edition single a month, spread across releases. The arrangement was enabled structurally by Duophonic operating two distinct imprints: Duophonic Ultra High Frequency Disks (for UK Stereolab releases licensed to other labels worldwide) and Duophonic Super 45s (for other artists). (Wikipedia — Duophonic Records, as of 2026-11-04) That licensing architecture meant individual tracks could be cleared for one-off indie releases like the Lissy’s split without conflicting with the Too Pure or Duophonic core catalogue.

The specific connection to Lissy’s appears to have been brokered through the Füxa relationship: Füxa (formed in Detroit by Randall Nieman and Ryan Anderson in 1994) performed several US shows with Stereolab around this period, and the split single grew from that touring association. (Wikipedia — Füxa, as of 2026-11-04) Lissy’s was already releasing material from US indie acts in the same experimental/post-rock orbit (The Sea and Cake on LISS 1, Further on LISS 4), which makes it a plausible label for a Stereolab/Füxa collaboration.

No source names the individual who initiated contact between Lissy’s and Stereolab/Duophonic for this release.
