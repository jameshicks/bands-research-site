Creed Records

Manchester-based UK independent label, founded in 1988 by Martin Price (owner of the Eastern Bloc record shop on Oldham Street) to release 808 State’s debut material. The label issued 808 State’s first album Newbuild (1988) and the Quadrastate mini-LP (1989) before expanding in 1991 into a broader catalog of house, techno, and indie-crossover singles. Around 27 releases total. Creed has a sublabel, F.R.O. Records.

History

Creed Records was set up by Martin Price in 1988 specifically to release the first 808 State album. At that point 808 State’s core trio was Price, Graham Massey, and Gerald Simpson (who also recorded as A Guy Called Gerald). The label’s address was care of Eastern Bloc Records, the influential Manchester record shop Price owned on Oldham Street — a central node in the city’s acid house underground.

Newbuild (STATE 002, 1988) was the debut LP: seven tracks of acid house, engineered by Massey and featuring A Guy Called Gerald. All writing credited to Simpson/Massey/Price. The single “Let Yourself Go / Deepville” (STATE 003, 1988) followed on the same catalog sequence. Quadrastate (State 004, 1989) was a six-track mini-LP that captured the expanded lineup — by this point Andy Barker and Darren Partington had joined, and the sound was shifting toward the Balearic/house/techno fusion that would define 808 State’s later ZTT years. Gerald Simpson appears credited only on “Pacific State” from Quadrastate before departing to pursue his solo career.

After the 808 State material moved to ZTT Records, Creed pivoted in 1991 to a wider Manchester dance roster. That run included K-Klass’s breakthrough “Rhythm Is A Mystery” (CREED 11T, 1991), which reached UK top five and became the label’s commercial high point. The label also put out indie-dance crossover singles from Mad Jacks (produced by Ronnie Stone) and remixed material from Justin Robertson. The F.R.O. Records sublabel was associated with this period — a scratched-out “FRO 105T” matrix on the French Connection (6) release suggests some releases were repressed or re-attributed between the two imprints.

Scene Connections

Creed Records sits at the intersection of two Manchester scenes. The 1988–1989 phase is pure acid house: it is one of the earliest UK acid house LP labels, the Eastern Bloc shop being a key distribution and culture point for Chicago imports and local productions. The connection to the Haçienda is indirect but structural — Martin Price and the Eastern Bloc orbit were embedded in the same Manchester underground from which the Haçienda drew its dance-floor energy, and 808 State (whose members also included the Lounge Jays alias) were early Haçienda residents.

By 1991 the label’s output reflects the post-rave fragmentation: K-Klass’s “Rhythm Is A Mystery” is polished bleep/house aimed squarely at the charts; Mad Jacks straddles indie rock and dance breaks; Sunkings lean ambient-techno; Justin Robertson (later of Lionrock and Spice, a Haçienda DJ) appears as remixer on two releases, placing Creed within the tightly networked Manchester dance production community of the period.

The F.R.O. Records sublabel (ID: 9823) appears related to the 1991 expansion phase, with matrix evidence suggesting some releases were re-attributed between the two imprints.
