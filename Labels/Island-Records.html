Island Records

Major independent label. Founded Jamaica 1959 by Chris Blackwell; relocated to UK 1962. Sold to PolyGram 1989; now part of Universal Music Group under Republic Corps. Discogs label ID: 8377.

Label Code: LC 0407 / LC 00407

History

1959–1966: Jamaican Roots and UK Launch

Island Records was named after the Harry Belafonte calypso hit “Island In The Sun” and founded in Jamaica in 1959 by Chris Blackwell, along with co-founders credited on Discogs as artist IDs 570526 and 282882. Blackwell’s initial focus was Jamaican music: ska, mento, and early rocksteady marketed to immigrant communities in London and Birmingham.

In May 1962, Blackwell relocated the label to the UK. For several years Island licensed its American rights to other labels — most frequently A&M Records and Atlantic Records — while building its UK independent base. The label’s Jamaican roster was eventually consolidated into the spinoff label Trojan Records.

1967–1972: British Rock and Art-Rock

In 1967 Island pivoted sharply to the British rock underground, becoming one of the key labels for the emerging British progressive and folk-rock scenes. Key signings of this era included:

  • Traffic — Steve Winwood’s post-Spencer Davis Group project; Mr Fantasy (1967), Traffic (1968), John Barleycorn Must Die (1970), The Low Spark Of High-Heeled Boys (1971)
  • Free — raw UK hard rock; Tons of Sobs (1969), Fire and Water (1970), All Right Now (1970 single)
  • Cat Stevens — folk-pop crossover; Mona Bone Jakon (1970), Tea for the Tillerman (1970), Teaser and the Firecat (1971)
  • Nick Drake — pastoral English folk; Five Leaves Left (1969), Bryter Layter (1971), Pink Moon (1972)
  • Steve Winwood — core throughout this era; also appeared on Island through Traffic and solo projects
  • Kevin Ayers — Canterbury scene; The Confessions of Dr. Dream (1974)
  • Brian Eno — post-Roxy Music solo work; Here Come the Warm Jets (1974), Another Green World (1975), and ambient collaborations with Robert Fripp (Fripp & Eno)
  • Robert Fripp / King Crimson — Earthbound (1972), Larks’ Tongues in Aspic (1973), Red (1974)
  • Emerson, Lake & Palmer — ELP (1970), Tarkus (1971)

Island also released early material by Roxy Music (1972–1976, before they moved to Polydor): Roxy Music (1972), For Your Pleasure (1973), Stranded (1973), Country Life (1974), Siren (1975). Brian Eno’s involvement with Roxy Music during this period deepened his relationship with Island.

1972–1976: Reggae Returns — Bob Marley

Island resumed its reggae commitments with the signing of Bob Marley & The Wailers in 1972, a landmark moment in bringing Jamaican music to mainstream international audiences. The partnership produced a run of essential albums:

  • Catch a Fire (1973)
  • Burnin’ (1973)
  • Natty Dread (1974)
  • Rastaman Vibration (1976)
  • Exodus (1977)
  • Kaya (1978)
  • Survival (1979)
  • Uprising (1980)
  • Confrontation (1983, posthumous)
  • Legend (1984) — the defining reggae compilation

Island’s deep roots in Jamaican music, and its ability to market reggae internationally, made this relationship uniquely productive. The label’s reggae infrastructure also supported the Antilles sublabel (ID: 998), which handled jazz and world music, and the Mango sublabel (ID: 10923) for reggae reissues and related releases.

Island also distributed the Black Ark productions of Lee Perry from 1976–1980 — including Super Ape (The Upsetters, 1976), War Ina Babylon (Max Romeo, 1976), Police & Thieves (Junior Murvin, 1977), and Colombia Colly (Jah Lion, 1976). These were among the most acclaimed reggae records of the period and were part of Island’s expanded Black music strategy under Blackwell alongside the Marley catalog.

1974–1979: Distribution, US Expansion, New Artists

In 1971 Island gained its first US identity through a distribution deal with Capitol Records; in 1973 it moved to independent US distribution. US headquarters relocated from Los Angeles to New York City in November 1977.

In the UK, Island was independently distributed until mid-1977, when Blackwell struck a deal with EMI Records Ltd.; the EMI name appeared on UK labels until the early 1980s.

New artists of this period included Robert Palmer — signed initially through the group Vinegar Joe, Palmer went solo on Island with Sneakin’ Sally Through The Alley (1974), Pressure Drop (1975), Double Fun (1978), Clues (1980, featuring Gary Numan and Nile Rodgers). Palmer’s work at Island ran until Riptide (1985, which contained the hit “Addicted To Love”).

1978–1984: Compass Point, Post-Punk, New Wave

Island’s mid-era sound was shaped substantially by Compass Point Studios in Nassau, Bahamas — an Island-owned facility that became one of the label’s most creatively fertile environments. Key personnel anchored to Compass Point include:

  • Alex Sadkin — engineer and producer, central to the Compass Point sound; produced and mixed many of Island’s key early 1980s releases
  • Chris Blackwell — regularly co-produced at Compass Point
  • Sly Dunbar and Robbie Shakespeare (collectively Sly & Robbie) — the defining Compass Point rhythm section
  • Wally Badarou — keyboardist central to the Compass Point recordings
  • Barry Reynolds — guitarist; wrote and played on Grace Jones’s albums
  • Steven Stanley — engineer/recording at Compass Point

The Compass Point rhythm team became an international session unit, appearing on records that bridged reggae, funk, post-punk, and disco.

U2 signed to Island UK in 1980 with Boy. Their Island tenure produced the band’s entire classic-era run: Boy (1980), October (1981), War (1983), The Unforgettable Fire (1984), The Joshua Tree (1987), Rattle and Hum (1988). Producer Brian Eno and Daniel Lanois worked on several of these later albums, maintaining the Eno/Island thread from the 1970s.

Tom Waits joined Island in 1982 for Swordfishtrombones — his first record after leaving Asylum and the beginning of his most experimental period. Island releases: Swordfishtrombones (1982), Rain Dogs (1985), Frank’s Wild Years (1987), Big Time (1988).

John Martyn maintained a long relationship with Island through the 1970s and 1980s.

1977–1985: Grace Jones and the Compass Point Trilogy

Grace Jones is the artist most central to this vault’s focus on Island Records. Originally a disco singer on Beam Junction and other labels, Jones signed to Island and made three landmark albums at Compass Point Studios in Nassau:

AlbumYearKey Credits
Warm Leatherette1980Prod. Chris Blackwell & Alex Sadkin; rhythm: Sly Dunbar & Robbie Shakespeare; keys: Wally Badarou; guitar: Barry Reynolds & Mikey Chung; photography: Jean-Paul Goude
Nightclubbing1981Same core team + Tyrone Downie (keys); Jack Emblow (accordion)
Living My Life1982Same core + Steven Stanley (engineer); written by Grace Jones, Barry Reynolds, Sly Dunbar, Melvin Van Peebles

These three albums represent a convergence of post-punk, Compass Point reggae-funk, and art-pop — a direct creative bridge between the New York/ZE Records scene and Blackwell’s Island infrastructure. Warm Leatherette opens with a cover of The Normal’s song, signaling explicit connection to UK industrial/post-punk. Jones’s visual identity during this period — created in collaboration with Jean-Paul Goude — became iconic.

Jones moved to Manhattan Records (an EMI subsidiary) after Living My Life, releasing Slave To The Rhythm (1985) on ZTT / Manhattan and Inside Story (1986) on Manhattan Records.

Island and ZE Records: The Distribution Relationship

Island Records had a significant distribution and licensing relationship with ZE Records, the New York no wave/mutant disco label founded by Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban. A large number of ZE releases — particularly from 1979 to 1982 — carried both ZE Records and Island Records as credited labels on the same release.

Late 1980s–1990s: Britpop and Sale to PolyGram

Blackwell sold Island to PolyGram in 1989 but remained as chairman of Island Entertainment Group until 1997. The label continued to sign major artists:

  • PJ Harvey — Dry (1992), Rid of Me (1993), 4-Track Demos (1993), To Bring You My Love (1995); among the most critically significant UK artists of the 1990s
  • Pulp — His ‘n’ Hers (1994), Different Class (1995), This Is Hardcore (1998), We Love Life (2001); Jarvis Cocker-led Sheffield band; signed via Gift Records 1993
  • Sugababes — Angels With Dirty Faces (2002), Three (2003), Taller in More Ways (2005); four UK #1 singles: “Round Round,” “Hole in the Head,” “Push the Button,” “About You Now”

Post-1999: Universal Era

Since 1999 Island has operated under Universal Music Group — as Island Def Jam Music Group (US) and Universal Island Records Ltd. (UK). In April 2014, Universal dissolved Island Def Jam, repositioning Island as a standalone label. In early 2024, UMG combined Island, Def Jam, and Republic Records under the unit name Republic Corps.

Compass Point Studios (Nassau)

Island’s recording facility in Nassau, Bahamas was central to the label’s early 1980s sound. The studio attracted artists from across the label’s roster and developed a distinctive hybrid of Caribbean rhythms, post-punk structures, and sophisticated engineering. The core Compass Point house band:

  • Rhythm section: Sly Dunbar (drums) and Robbie Shakespeare (bass)
  • Keys: Wally Badarou
  • Guitar: Barry Reynolds, Mikey Chung
  • Engineering: Alex Sadkin, Steven Stanley
  • Production: Chris Blackwell, Alex Sadkin

The Compass Point sound is audible across Grace Jones’s Warm Leatherette trilogy, Robert Palmer’s Clues (1980), and other Island releases of the period. The studio also attracted outside sessions.

  • Chris Blackwell — founder; producer; remained at Island until 1997
  • Alex Sadkin — defining Compass Point producer and engineer; worked across Island’s key 1980s releases
  • Sly Dunbar / Robbie Shakespeare — Compass Point rhythm section
  • Wally Badarou — Compass Point keyboardist
  • Barry Reynolds — Compass Point guitarist; co-wrote Grace Jones material
  • Jean-Paul Goude — visual collaborator on Grace Jones’s Island-era identity
  • Brian Eno — two-phase relationship: mid-1970s solo work and later U2 productions
  • Trojan Records — Island’s reggae spinoff for UK distribution
  • Antilles — Island’s US jazz/world music sublabel
  • Bob Marley & The Wailers — defining reggae partnership; Catch a Fire through Confrontation
  • Tom Waits — Island’s key experimental era signing, 1982–88

Open Questions

  • The exact contractual nature of the ZE/Island relationship (licensing vs. distribution vs. co-production) is not fully documented in Discogs metadata alone; the ZE Records article notes the arrangement but details would require web research.
  • Chris Blackwell’s founding partners (Discogs IDs 570526 and 282882) are not identified in this vault — their names are not visible in the Discogs profile text.
  • Compass Point Studios appears in release notes but does not have its own Discogs label entry; a Topics page could document it separately.
  • The Antilles sublabel’s relationship to ZE-adjacent releases (Suicide’s second album appears on Antilles) is worth mapping further.
