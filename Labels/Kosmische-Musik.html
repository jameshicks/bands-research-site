From Discogs

An early-mid 1970s German label, hosted by Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser and Gille Lettmann. It was a division of Ohr Records, manufactured and distributed by Metronome. The label was born as Die Kosmischen Kuriere with just 3 releases before becoming Kosmische Musik with distinctive Peter Geitner art/design. The music is mostly Space Rock, eclectic Krautrock and Electronic. The original label issued 14 albums.

French releases were issued via Barclay on Cosmic Music (2) with blue labels based on the same design. Italian releases were issued via PDU of which some had the Kosmische Musik label with silver print on black. Dutch releases 4 are known to exist, 2 on “Cosmic Music”, plus 2 more on “Kosmische Musik” (distributed by CNR) LP and CD reissues exist on various labels (sometimes with the original labels used), many from the current owners of the back-catalogue: Breeze Music Ltd., distributed by Pop Import, Bernhard Mikulski, ZYX Records, etc..

Overview

Kosmische Musik was a short-lived early-to-mid 1970s West German imprint run by Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser and Gille Lettmann as a subdivision of Ohr Records, with manufacturing and distribution handled by Metronome. It evolved from the predecessor imprint Die Kosmischen Kuriere — which had issued just three releases — taking on distinctive sleeve art by Peter Geitner from around 1973. The fourteen original LPs cluster firmly in the 1970s, spanning Space Rock, Krautrock, and electronics, with roster figures including Manuel Göttsching and Jürgen Dollase. International pressings were licensed to Barclay in France, PDU in Italy, and CNR in the Netherlands; the back-catalogue has since passed through several reissue hands, including ZYX Records and Breeze Music Ltd.

Notes (web) — Kaiser’s withdrawal and the label’s end

The collapse

The label’s end resulted directly from the Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser / Gille Lettmann Cosmic Jokers affair: Kaiser had recorded informal studio sessions (musicians paid small fees and given hallucinogens) and released the tapes as official albums without the participating artists’ knowledge or consent. Klaus Schulze and others brought legal action; West German courts voided Kaiser’s contracts with his bands, stripping him of his catalogue rights. The albums were withdrawn pending settlement of the musicians’ rights. musicinfluence.com

Kaiser’s withdrawal from the industry

The label’s collapse in 1975 did coincide with Kaiser’s complete withdrawal from the music industry, and the withdrawal appears to have been permanent. It is documented across several contemporary and retrospective accounts, though no interview with Kaiser himself has surfaced — his refusal to communicate with the outside world has made first-person documentation impossible.

What is documented:

  • Kaiser and Lettmann lost their Berlin office at the Europa-Center for unpaid rent after the bankruptcy; they retreated to their Cologne apartment and were subsequently evicted there too. An execution sale of Kaiser’s belongings was held, with some of his books later found at Cologne flea markets. They then moved to Lettmann’s mother’s house in Frechen-Königsdorf near Cologne, where they “abandoned the world completely.” Jan Reetze, “Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser & Gille Lettmann: The Cosmic Couriers Story,” 2011
  • In the late 1980s, when ZYX Records began reissuing the Ohr/Pilz/Kosmische Musik catalogue on CD, Kaiser made contact seeking his producer’s share of royalties, but was told he had lost all rights through the execution sales. This appears to be his last documented engagement with the industry. Jan Reetze, 2011; Wikipedia, Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser article (as of 2026-10-20)
  • Since the late 1980s Kaiser has maintained complete silence, refusing mail and making no public appearances. As of the early 2010s he and Lettmann reportedly resided in a Catholic Church facility near the Sauerland area. Lettmann, who identifies as “Sternenmädchen,” publishes an esoteric magazine for a select readership. Jan Reetze, 2011
  • The Studio Innocenti retrospective (2009) states Kaiser “ceased all artistic and commercial activities” after the last 1975 releases and “apparently disappeared from the country and quit the music industry for the rest of his life,” adding that without confirmed information “we have no final ending for the story of what truly became of Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser.” Studio Innocenti, 2009
  • Wikipedia’s Kaiser article notes that he and Lettmann “immediately retired completely from the public eye” after the legal fallout and that “journalistic investigations to trace their subsequent activities” have had “very little success.” Wikipedia, Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser (as of 2026-10-20)
  • Eurock, which had corresponded with Kaiser through the mid-1970s, reports them as “still alive and living out of touch with the world today by choice.” eurock.com

Reasons beyond the Cosmic Jokers scandal

Secondary accounts add context beyond the legal scandal alone: multiple sources suggest the breakdown was also personal and psychological. The DownTunedMag account (citing Klaus Schulze’s manager as an unnamed source) states Kaiser was reportedly “totally broke, begging for food and suffering from schizophrenia due to his excessive LSD intake” in Cologne after the collapse — this is weak secondary sourcing and should be treated with caution. downtunedmag.com, 2014 The Studio Innocenti piece characterises him as “a victim of his times, the drugs, and broken relationships,” suggesting that personal and chemical factors compounded the legal and financial ones. studioinnocenti.blogspot.com, 2009

No Kaiser interviews found

No interview or first-person statement by Kaiser post-1975 has been located by any source checked, including dedicated biographical research. His withdrawal from public life has been near-total; contemporaries and journalists who attempted to reach him have consistently reported failure.
