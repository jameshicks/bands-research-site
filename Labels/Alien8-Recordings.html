Alien8 Recordings

Discogs ID: 1095
Founded: 1996
Origin: Montreal, Quebec, Canada
Address: P.O. Box 666, Station R, Montreal, QC H2S 3L1
Active: 1996–2010s
Total releases: 167 (Discogs)
Sublabels: Substractif (ID 3905), Fancy (ID 46920)

Canadian independent label, co-founded by Sean O’Hara (ID 1879444) and Gary Worsley (ID 1133587). Launched as a noise and experimental outlet; evolved over its first decade into the primary home for the Montreal underground scene. Worsley later purchased Cheap Thrills record shop in Montreal (started there 1998, owner from 2018). O’Hara served as the label’s graphic designer and webmaster, handling artwork for 20 releases.

Founding Story

O’Hara and Worsley had been friends for roughly three years before founding the label — placing the start of their friendship around 1993. [source: exclaim.ca/music/article/alien_8, c. 2005] Neither had a background as a musician or scene organiser; both describe themselves as coming from the listener side. As they put it: “We decided to start a label because we were very much into music as listeners and wanted to become involved somehow in the music scene/industry.” [source: The Globe and Mail, Jason Anderson, 2001-03-29, https://www.theglobeandmail.com/arts/alternatives-to-alternative/article4145696/]

The label was entirely self-funded and run from their respective Montreal-area homes. The founding impulse was deliberately modest: “put out a few records and see how it goes.” [source: exclaim.ca/music/article/alien_8] Their entry point into releasing was a fax sent to Merzbow (Masami Akita) — already an established name in Japanese noise — requesting a release. Merzbow agreed, and Akasha Gulva (ALIEN CD1) became the debut, out in September 1996. [source: The Globe and Mail, 2001-03-29; McGill Daily, 2008-04, https://www.mcgilldaily.com/2008/04/we_come_in_peace_montreals_alien8_recordings/]

O’Hara attended Concordia University in Montreal. [source: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien8_Recordings, as of 2026-10-02 — Concordia detail sourced to LinkedIn/résumé data indexed by search engines] His role at the label was primarily graphic designer and webmaster; he built multiple iterations of the label’s website in Perl, PHP, and Ruby on Rails, and held the title President & Co-Founder from July 1996 to July 2012. [source: LinkedIn résumé data, indexed by search, domain linkedin.com]

Worsley’s pre-label background in the Montreal scene is not documented in any source found. His first documented post-label role is starting work at Cheap Thrills record shop in 1998 (two years after founding Alien8), under manager Guy Lavoie. He purchased the store in 2018. [source: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien8_Recordings; McGill Daily, 2008]

The label’s identity in its own words: “I think [our identity] is just the sum total of what we’ve done, who we’ve worked with and what we continue to do.” — O’Hara, c. 2005. [source: exclaim.ca/music/article/alien_8]

Catalog Overview — By Era

1996–2000: Noise and International Experimental

The founding years were anchored by Japanese noise. The debut release was Merzbow (Masami Akita, ID 12551 / 58248) — Akasha Gulva (1996, ALIEN CD1), a single 73:39 track; Merzbow personnel: Masami Akita (electronics: metal electronics, filters, tone works, devices), Tetsuo Sakaibara (action, mud, installation), Reiko Azuma (synthesizer, effects); visual concept by Abtechtonics (alias for Masami Akita). 2001–2003: Montreal Convergence — Set Fire To Flames / The Unicorns

The label’s most significant consolidation of the Montreal post-rock network happened around Set Fire To Flames and The Unicorns.

Set Fire To Flames (ID 65727): collective of 13 musicians from the Godspeed You! Black Emperor circle and related bands (Hanged Up, Hiss Tracts, HRSTA, Esmerine, Fly Pan Am, squintfucker press, Le Révélateur). Members: David Bryant, Sophie Trudeau, Roger Tellier-Craig, Rebecca Foon, Bruce Cawdron, Christof Migone, Aidan Girt, Jean-Sébastien Truchy, Mike Moya, Geneviève Heistek, Fluffy Erskine, Gordon Krieger, Speedy Weaver, Thea Pratt.

  • Sings Reign Rebuilder (2001) — co-released on 130701 / Alien8 / P-Vine Records; 15 tracks
  • Telegraphs In Negative / Mouths Trapped In Static (2003, ALIENLP 39) — 4 Discogs pressings

The Unicorns (ID 317587): Who Will Cut Our Hair When We’re Gone? (2003, ALIENCD 041) — 11 Discogs pressings; Indie Rock/Lo-Fi. Core members: Alden Penner (a.k.a. “Alden Ginger”, ID 1021164) — synth, percussion, organ, bass, guitar, vocals; Nick Diamonds (a.k.a. “Nichols ‘Niel’ Diamonds”, ID 420570) — same multi-instrument array; J’aime (Jamie) Tambour (ID 69289) — drums. Co-produced/mixed by Mark Lawson (2) (ID 3133636). Notable guest: Richard Reed Parry (ID 284431) — trumpet on “I Don’t Wanna Die”, bowed bass on “Sea Ghost”. The Unicorns had 12 Alien8 releases in total (2003–2011).

2002–2008: Tim Hecker and Electronic/Ambient Strand

Tim Hecker (ID 59906; alias: Jetone) became one of Alien8’s most prominent artists with 11 releases (first year 2002, last 2011). Real name: Timothy D. Hecker. Based in Montreal and Los Angeles.

  • Mirages (2004, ALIENCD47)
  • Radio Amor (2007, ALIENCD66)
  • Haunt Me, Haunt Me Do It Again (2010, alienlp89) — 4 pressings
  • Fantasma Parastasie (2008, ALIENCD81) — collaboration with Aidan Baker (ID 69673); 66-track album in five suites (“Phantom On A Pedestal”, “Hymn To The Idea Of Night”, “Auditory Spirits”, “Skeleton Dance”, “Gallery Of The Invisible Woman”, plus title track); styles: Abstract, Ambient, Drone, Experimental, Noise

Aidan Baker (ID 69673) also released solo material on Alien8 (Liminoid / Lifeforms, 2010, ALIENCD87).

2002–2011: Acid Mothers Temple

Acid Mothers Temple & The Melting Paraiso UFO (ID 53760) led the Japanese psychedelic strand with 12 releases (2002–2011), tying The Unicorns for the most releases on the label. Principal contact: Makoto Kawabata (ID 115177) — producer and engineer on all sessions. Key entries:

  • Electric Heavyland (2002, ALIENCD34)
  • Mantra Of Love (2004, ALIENCD44)
  • Lord Of The Underground: Vishnu And The Magic Elixir (2009, ALIEN8LP84)
  • Pink Lady Lemonade (You’re From Inner Space) (2011, ALIENLP90)

Acid Mothers Temple & The Cosmic Inferno (ID 461294) also appeared — Starless And Bible Black Sabbath (2006, ALIENCD62).

2004–2011: Montreal Indie Diversification

The label broadened considerably post-2004:

  • Duchess Says (ID 238100): art-punk/synth-punk Montreal quartet, 9 releases (2008–2011); debut Anthologie Des 3 Perchoirs (2008, ALIENCD 076)
  • Les Georges Leningrad (ID 132789): Deux Hot Dogs Moutarde Chou (2004, ALIENCD45) and Sur Les Traces De Black Eskimo (2004, ALIENCD49)
  • Lesbians On Ecstasy (ID 180674): 6 releases (2004–2007); also produced and mixed 3 of their own records
  • Nadja (5) (ID 299084): drone/doom duo; Truth Becomes Death (2005), Radiance Of Shadows (2007), Touched (2007)
  • Think About Life (ID 505942): 4 releases (2006–2009)
  • Sam Shalabi (ID 199971) and Shalabi Effect (ID 138827): 5 releases each; Shalabi is also credited as musician/oud player on numerous other Alien8 releases (10 total)
  • Menace Ruine (ID 1121283): 3 releases (2008)
  • Torngat (ID 978284): 5 releases (2007–2009)
  • Tanakh (ID 249087): 6 releases (2002–2006); debut Villa Claustrophobia (2002, ALIENLP 033)

Sublabels

Substractif (ID 3905): “devoted to electronic and digitally created forms of music.” Active 2001–2004; 5 releases (SUBSF01–07, not all numbers used). Artists: Mitchell Akiyama, Tomas Jirku, Tim Hecker (Haunt Me 2001 — later reissued on main Alien8 in 2010), Polmo Polpo, Daniel Menche.

Fancy (ID 46920): No stated remit; 2 releases only (1999–2001). Both more song-oriented than the experimental main catalog — Molasses (2) (You’ll Never Be Well No More, FANCYCD1, 1999) and Hrsta (L’Éclat Du Ciel Était Insoutenable, FANCYCD3, 2001). Effectively a very short-lived outlet for indie/post-rock acts.

Post-2011 Status — Effectively Dormant

After 2011 the label issued only two releases, both reissues of earlier catalog:

  • David Kristian — Cricklewood (2021, no catno) — reissue of the 1997 debut (master ID 2569463 = same as ALIENCD5)
  • Books On Tape — Dinosaur Dinosaur (2023, ALIENCD 060) — reissue of the 2005 original (same catno)

No new original material after 2011 in Discogs. The label appears to have wound down activity without a formal closure announcement, with Gary Worsley’s attention shifting to operating Cheap Thrills record shop (purchased 2018).
