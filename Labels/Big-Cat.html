UK label launched around 1990 that originally specialized in industrial and noise-rock releases. Operated a US office in NYC for a few years. Overview

Big Cat is a UK label with roots from 1988 that came into its own around 1990, initially focusing on industrial and noise-rock and running a New York City office for several years alongside its UK base. The catalogue is overwhelmingly a 1990s story — 148 of its 162 selected releases came from that decade — with a roster spanning Carter the Unstoppable Sex Machine, Pavement, Cop Shoot Cop, and Heather Nova across indie rock, alt-rock, and experimental styles. In 1996 the label’s more commercially successful acts were absorbed into parent V2 Records, broadening Big Cat’s remaining profile into more mainstream territory. The label wound down gradually: its remaining assets, including Carter USM’s debut, changed hands in 2011, and Jungle Records acquired the Big Cat (UK) imprint and residual recording rights in 2017.

Ownership and the V2 Integration (1996)

Big Cat was co-founded in 1989 by Steve Abbott, who ran it alongside a 50% business partner, Linda Obadiah. Abbott had previously been the frontman of post-punk band UK Decay before transitioning into label work. The label name derives from a line in the Big Youth reggae song “Lightning Flash.” (Wikipedia: Steve Abbott (musician), as of 2026-03-13)

The 1996 integration with V2 Records was triggered by a partnership split: Obadiah sold her 50% stake in Big Cat to Richard Branson’s newly formed V2 Records. V2 launched officially in December 1996 (with Branson founding it five years after selling Virgin Records to EMI), and it entered the deal with Big Cat in October 1996 — before V2 had even officially debuted. (Wikipedia: V2 Records; Grokipedia: V2 Records)

The move was part of a broader V2 launch strategy of acquiring stakes in established indie labels rather than building a roster from scratch. V2 made similar deals simultaneously with Gee Street Records (December 1996) and later Junior Boy’s Own (December 1997), creating a federated structure of semi-independent imprints under its umbrella. (Grokipedia: V2 Records)

The practical outcome was structural as much as artistic: Big Cat’s existing UK and US teams became the operational foundation for V2’s UK and North American offices. Abbott himself moved across, taking on the role of Head of International at V2 from 1998, then relocating to New York as Head of A&R North America (1999–2002). (Wikipedia: Steve Abbott (musician), as of 2026-03-13)

The Discogs profile’s framing — that “successful acts were integrated with V2 Records” — reflects the result of this ownership change rather than a deliberate A&R decision: the roster diversification that followed was a downstream consequence of V2 taking on the label infrastructure, not the stated goal of the deal.
