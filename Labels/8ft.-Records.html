8ft. Records

Discogs ID: 37241
Parent company: Eight Foot Records Inc. (Discogs ID: 748942)
Founded: 2003 (earliest catalog entry)
Primary artist / founder: Amanda Palmer
Total Discogs catalog: 115 releases (29 unique masters)
Twitter: https://twitter.com/8ftrecords

Self-run independent imprint founded by Amanda Palmer, initially as the release vehicle for The Dresden Dolls and subsequently for Palmer’s extensive solo catalog. The label’s first release was the Dresden Dolls self-titled debut (2003, cat: 8ft. 001). After the duo’s hiatus in 2008, 8ft. Records became Palmer’s primary outlet, releasing digital, Bandcamp, and limited physical editions, often in combination with larger distributors (Cooking Vinyl, Liberator Music). The label’s name derives from Palmer’s street-performance persona — the “Eight Foot Bride” living statue act she performed during her busking years in Cambridge, Massachusetts, Edinburgh, Berlin, and Adelaide.

The parent company Eight Foot Records Inc. (ID: 748942) is listed on Discogs as having no profile beyond its connection to Palmer (ID: 575536) and as the umbrella for the 8ft. Records sublabel. The parent was likely incorporated as the legal entity behind the imprint. The 8ft. Records label name appears on sleeves as 8ft. records (lowercase).

Key Releases — Personnel

The Dresden Dolls (2003, cat: 8ft. 001)

The label’s debut release and the Dresden Dolls’ debut LP. Recorded and produced by Martin Bisi at BC Studio, Gowanus, Brooklyn. The band self-produced initially, securing the Roadrunner distribution deal after recording was complete.

Theatre Is Evil (2012, cat: 8FT014CD / 8FT014LP)

Landmark LP, produced by John Congleton. Funded by a Kickstarter campaign that raised $1,192,793 from 24,883 backers — at the time the most ever raised for a music project on Kickstarter (per Wikipedia/Amanda Palmer). Featured artwork contributions from Frances Bean Cobain (credited as “Frances Cobain” on sleeve).

An Evening With Neil Gaiman & Amanda Palmer (2013, cat: 8FT015LP)

Live spoken word and music LP. Neil Gaiman (ID: 574063) and Palmer co-billed. No additional credits on the Discogs entry — stripped-down live recording.

You Got Me Singing (2016, cat: 8FT025LP)

Duet LP with Palmer’s estranged father Jack Palmer (2) (ID: 1223053). Recorded and mixed by Joe Costa. Liner notes by Maria Popova (Brain Pickings). Photography by Kyle Cassidy (cover). Pedal steel by Rob Stein.

Strung Out In Heaven: A Bowie String Quartet Tribute (2016, cat: 8FT028LP)

David Bowie tribute co-credited with Jherek Bischoff (ID: 422428). Bischoff arranged, conducted, and played double bass. Guest vocalists: Anna Calvi (vocals, guitar, track 1) and John Cameron Mitchell (vocals, tracks 4–5). String quartet: Alyssa Park, Serena McKinney (violin), Ben Ullery (viola), Jacob Braun (cello). Artwork by Sarah Beetson.

I Can Spin A Rainbow (2017, cat: 8FT035CD)

Full LP with Edward Ka-Spel (of The Legendary Pink Dots). Recorded summer 2016 at Hideaway Studio and Chez Dots, London (per Wikipedia). Mastered by Peter Van Vliet and Raymond Steeg. Artwork by Judith Clute. Violin by Patrick Q-Wright. Singing saw by Alexis Michallek.

There Will Be No Intermission (2019, cat: 8FT054CD)

Third solo LP. Produced by John Congleton (also drums, synth, sequencing). Jherek Bischoff arranged all inter-song instrumentals, played double bass/guitar/vibraphone/prepared piano/backing vocals, recorded strings. Jason Webley played accordion. Joey Waronker played drums. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Choir on “Baby Didn’t Die” track credited to “The Baby Didn’t Die Choir” (16 named members). Dillie Keane co-wrote one track.

Connections

  • Amanda Palmer — founder and primary artist; all creative decisions flow through Palmer; the label name derives from her Eight Foot Bride street performance identity
  • The Dresden Dolls — founding vehicle; debut release 8ft. 001 (2003); duo of Amanda Palmer and Brian Viglione
  • Eight Foot Records Inc. (ID: 748942) — parent company; no separate Discogs profile; legal umbrella for the imprint
  • Amanda Palmer & The Grand Theft Orchestra — post-hiatus touring/studio band; members include Jherek Bischoff, Michael McQuilken, Chad Raines
  • Evelyn Evelyn — Palmer / Jason Webley duo project (conjoined-twin fictional conceit); co-released with 11 Records (XI-13)
  • Edward Ka-Spel — The Legendary Pink Dots frontman; co-created I Can Spin A Rainbow (2017) and The Hands EP (2017) with Palmer
  • Martin Bisi — produced and engineered the label’s debut release (The Dresden Dolls, 2003) at BC Studio, Brooklyn
  • John Congleton — the label’s primary producer for its two most prominent post-Dresden Dolls LPs (Theatre Is Evil, There Will Be No Intermission)
  • Jherek Bischoff — most prolific instrumental contributor across the catalog; arranger, bassist, and conductor on multiple Palmer albums; member of Parenthetical Girls, Shudder To Think, and The Dead Science
  • Jason Webley — co-producer and performer; central to Evelyn Evelyn; Seattle-based accordion/guitar folk artist
  • Neil Gaiman — writer; An Evening With Neil Gaiman & Amanda Palmer (2013); married to Palmer 2011–2022; also member of 8in8 (with Palmer, Ben Folds, Damian Kulash Jr.)
  • Fred Kevorkian — mastering engineer on label debut and multiple pressings
  • Andrew Nelson (3) — the label’s primary package designer across 21 releases
  • Kyle Cassidy — the label’s recurring photographer (20 releases), including cover photography for Who Killed Amanda Palmer companion book and Theatre Is Evil
  • The Legendary Pink Dots — upstream connection via Ka-Spel collaboration; Palmer cited LPD as an influence on The Dresden Dolls

Research Queue

  • The Discogs entry for An Evening With Neil Gaiman & Amanda Palmer (2013) carries no additional credits at all — was this recorded live on a specific tour leg, and what was the recording venue or setlist? One contemporary concert review or press piece from the 2013 “Evening with” tour would answer this.
  • The Evelyn Evelyn LP (2010) is the only 8ft. Records release to carry a co-label credit (11 Records, cat XI-13). What is the relationship between 8ft. Records and 11 Records — are they legally separate, or was “11 Records” a one-off imprint designation for the Evelyn Evelyn project? The evelynevelyn.com press kit or a contemporary interview would clarify.
  • Jherek Bischoff appears in 19 credits across the 8ft. Records catalog in a wide range of roles (arranger, bassist, conductor, mixer). Was there a formal point at which he became a regular Palmer collaborator — e.g., did he join as a touring member before appearing in the studio? A contemporary interview with Palmer or Bischoff from the Theatre Is Evil era would identify when the working relationship began.
  • I Can Spin A Rainbow (2017) is credited as recorded at “Hideaway Studio and Chez Dots (London)” per Wikipedia. Did Ka-Spel and Palmer record live in the same room, or was it assembled across separate sessions with Palmer overdubbing? Either party’s liner note commentary or a 2017 interview would resolve this.
  • The There Will Be No Intermission (2019) credits include a “Library Department” crew role for Anthony Gaiman (credited as “Ash”), who shares Palmer’s ex-husband’s surname. Is this Neil Gaiman’s son? A single source confirming the relationship would settle this.
