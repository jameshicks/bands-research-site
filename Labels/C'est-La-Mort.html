Overview

C’est La Mort was an American independent label based in Baker, Louisiana, active from 1986 through the mid-1990s and run by label head Woodrow Dumas. It concentrated on gothic, ethereal, and “artsy” pop and rock — spanning synth-pop, goth rock, and indie rock — with its thirty-release catalogue split evenly across the 1980s and 1990s. The roster drew from a compact circle of artists including East Ash, Scott Rodger, John Golden, and James Pinker. The label is now defunct, and its corporate structure — any parent or sublabel relationships — remains unrecorded.

Notes (web)

How C’est La Mort came to operate from Baker, Louisiana

The label’s roots lie in a college radio programme rather than in any pre-existing local scene. Woodrow Huntley Dumas (born Baker, Louisiana, 27 August 1950; died Cottage Grove, Oregon, 18 September 2012) began hosting Dr. Death — a show specialising in cold/gothic sounds — on KLSU 91.1 FM, the student-run Louisiana State University station in Baton Rouge, in December 1983. nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2013/02/the-island-of-doctor-death.html; nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2015/05/cest-la-vie.html

The show built an audience by mail, which Dumas converted into a mail-order operation called Deadly Records, run from his home porch in Baker (a northern suburb of Baton Rouge). Deadly Records then evolved into the C’est La Mort label proper. The label effectively was Dumas’s house; it never had a commercial office. wikibin.org/articles/cest-la-mort.html; nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2013/02/the-island-of-doctor-death.html

Baker/Baton Rouge was the base of operations because that is where Dumas lived and had his radio access — there is no evidence of a pre-existing regional gothic or ethereal scene that attracted him there, nor of other contemporaneous gothic/darkwave labels or venues in the Baton Rouge area during this period. The label was geographically anomalous: it succeeded by mail order and later (in the 1990s) through distribution via Cargo, not because of local scene infrastructure. nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2013/02/the-island-of-doctor-death.html

Aesthetic model and national connections

C’est La Mort was consciously modelled on the UK’s 4AD label. nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2015/05/cest-la-vie.html Rather than being plugged into a regional goth circuit, Dumas built outward connections through his radio show and mail-order network:

  • The Dr. Death compilations introduced American audiences to experimental European acts including Legendary Pink Dots and Front Line Assembly. nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2025/04/cest-la-vie-de-nouveau.html
  • The roster included acts from outside Louisiana — Controlled Bleeding (New York), Psyche (2) (Canada), and Room Nine — suggesting the label recruited nationally and internationally via correspondence rather than through local proximity.
  • A forum post from a former KLSU listener notes meeting Dumas alongside a member of Skinny Puppy, indicating that by the mid-1980s he had personal contact with leading figures in the North American industrial/goth network. klsualumni.net/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?f=1&t=995 (single anecdotal forum post; treat as weak sourcing)
  • Several C’est La Mort releases were later reissued by Projekt Records’s Archive sub-label (Relic), cementing retrospective recognition within the US gothic/ethereal canon. nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2013/02/the-island-of-doctor-death.html

After C’est La Mort closed (~1994), Dumas relocated to the Pacific Northwest (Cottage Grove, Oregon) and ran an internet radio station called iPop Café. nathannothinsez.blogspot.com/2015/05/cest-la-vie.html

Sources checked / dead ends

  • No independent evidence of a Baton Rouge or Baker regional gothic/ethereal scene in the 1980s was found. Web searches for “Baton Rouge gothic scene 1980s” returned no relevant results. The label’s location appears to reflect Dumas’s personal biography rather than a scene.
  • The Ethereal wave Wikipedia article (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereal_wave) names C’est La Mort as the primary US label for the genre in the second half of the 1980s but provides no additional detail on regional context.
  • AllMusic, Rate Your Music, and Discogs artist pages were not fetched directly (blocked domains / policy); search snippets confirmed the Deadly Records → C’est La Mort chain without adding new detail.
