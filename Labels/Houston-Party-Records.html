Overview

Houston Party Records was a Barcelona-based booking agency and record label founded in 1997. Despite its Spanish base, the catalogue is dominated by American indie and alternative rock, with former Posies members Jon Auer and Ken Stringfellow, singer-songwriter Micah P. Hinson, and Ned Oldham among its roster. Release activity peaked sharply in the 2000s — 115 of its roughly 135 releases fell in that decade — before dropping to nine in the 2010s. By 2023 the label side had ceased operations entirely, leaving the company active only as a booking agency.

Notes (web) — American indie connection

Founder: Jaime Hernández, a member of Barcelona indie band Parkinson D.C., founded the label in 1997–1998. The original impetus was releasing Parkinson D.C.’s own music after a falling-out with their existing label over a broken promise. (Periférica academic journal, 2010)

Ken Stringfellow as personal bridge: The Periférica article quotes Hernández referring to Ken Stringfellow as “our good friend” — Stringfellow had been involved in a planned Parkinson D.C. album to be recorded in the USA, a project that fell through but left a personal relationship intact. That friendship appears to be the primary human bridge between Barcelona and the Seattle/American indie-folk scene. (Periférica, 2010)

Stringfellow’s own account: In a 2001 press statement on The Posies’ own site, Stringfellow described his years with Houston Party as “my fondest and most respectful recollections of any working relationship I have been in,” and recalled Hernández “ran the label out of his Barcelona flat, with a one-room office and stacks of CDs piled in the hallway.” He attributed the label’s credibility partly to Barcelona being “one of Europe’s most culturally aware cities.” (theposies.net Houston Party press page, 2001)

Live-to-label pipeline: The Posies’ live album Alive Before The Iceberg (HPR-012, 1999) was recorded at Luz De Gas, Barcelona, on 28 July 1998 — before the label was even a year old. The Barcelona show and the pre-existing friendship with Stringfellow likely set the template: build a relationship through live performance in Spain, then license studio recordings. (Wikipedia: Alive Before the Iceberg)

Structural reason for the American focus: Hernández’s model was explicitly dual-purpose — license albums and organize tours. Per a Neo2 interview, his starting logic was: “if you wanted to see certain groups live that weren’t touring here, the most reasonable way to get it done was to bring them yourself.” Releasing the album in Spain gave him leverage to book the tour; touring in Spain made the license worthwhile. This self-reinforcing model explains why the roster is almost entirely non-Spanish: he was curating what he personally wanted to see live. (Neo2: Houston Party Music)

The Posies’ decisive early role: A 2013 Alta Fidelidad piece on the label’s 15th anniversary noted that “The Posies tuvieron un papel decisivo en el despegue del sello” (The Posies played a decisive role in the label’s launch), and that for the anniversary Stringfellow and Auer agreed to perform two classic albums with their original lineups — an indication of ongoing personal warmth toward the label. (altafidelidad.org, 2013)

What was searched / not found: No source names a specific agent, manager, or third party who introduced Hernández to the broader American indie-folk network beyond Stringfellow. How Hernández came to sign Micah P. Hinson (first HPR release 2004), Damien Jurado, or the later folk-rock acts remains undocumented in publicly available English or Spanish sources checked. The 15-year interview in Fantastic Mag (fantasticmag.es, 2013) discusses these signings as career milestones but does not explain how those connections were formed.
