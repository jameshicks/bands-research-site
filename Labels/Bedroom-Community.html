Overview

Bedroom Community is an independent record label and collective founded in 2006 in Reykjavík, Iceland, by producer/engineer Valgeir Sigurðsson, composer Nico Muhly, and composer/producer Ben Frost. All three had converging reasons to be in Reykjavík — Valgeir ran the Greenhouse Studios there, while Muhly and Frost were drawn into the city’s experimental music orbit. The label sits at the intersection of modern classical composition, electronic music, drone, and experimental folk, with a strong curatorial identity built around acoustic-electronic hybrids produced at Greenhouse Studios.

The catalog uses the HVALUR (“whale” in Icelandic) catalog numbering series, running from HVALUR1 (2006) into the 2020s, with a secondary SELUR and HVALREKI series for selected releases. Across approximately 144 releases in the Discogs database, the label has remained a boutique operation where the roster artists frequently cross-produce and perform on each other’s records, giving the catalog a notable internal coherence.

History

Founding and early catalog (2006–2008)

The label launched simultaneously with its first two records: Nico Muhly’s Speaks Volumes (HVALUR1) and Ben Frost’s Theory Of Machines (HVALUR2), both issued in 2006. Speaks Volumes was produced by Valgeir Sigurðsson and featured chamber performers including Nadia Sirota (viola), Clarice Jensen (cello), and Antony (vocals on “Keep In Touch”). Theory Of Machines was assembled by Frost and Sigurðsson and included tracks featuring Amiina’s strings, Icelandic percussionist Siggi Baldursson, and Hildur Guðnadóttir on cello. Both albums were recorded at Greenhouse Studios, establishing the production approach that would define the label.

2007 brought Valgeir Sigurðsson’s own debut Ekvílibríum (HVALUR3) — a layered electronic-and-chamber record featuring Nico Muhly at the piano, Bonnie “Prince” Billy (Will Oldham) on vocals, Hildur Guðnadóttir on cello, and Warren Ellis on mandolin. The same year saw the US folk-sourced debut of Sam Amidon, All Is Well (HVALUR4), recorded under the name Samamidon and drawing on traditional American songs.

In 2008 the label released Nico Muhly’s second full-length, Mothertongue (HVALUR5), a song cycle for voice, chamber ensemble, and electronics, with Sam Amidon contributing banjo and guitar to one of its three multi-part movements, and Nadia Sirota playing viola on another. Valgeir Sigurðsson produced and engineered.

A digital-only sampler, Bedroom Community Sampler Volume 1 (2006–2008), collected key tracks from these opening years and became the label’s most-pressed single entry on Discogs (33 pressings/editions documented), reflecting widespread distribution and streaming adoption.

Expansion and key collaborations (2009–2013)

Ben Frost’s By The Throat (HVALUR, 2009) was a pivotal record — arguably his breakthrough into the experimental electronic mainstream. Dense with orca field recordings, prepared piano (credited to Muhly and Paul Corley), and contributions from Amiina, the album was produced and written by Frost entirely. Icelandic composer and conductor Daníel Bjarnason debuted on the label the same year with Processions (HVALUR7, 2009).

Valgeir Sigurðsson released Draumalandið (HVALUR8, 2009), and Sam Amidon returned with I See The Sign (HVALUR9, 2010) and later Bright Sunny South (2013). Nico Muhly’s I Drink The Air Before Me (HVALUR10, 2010) continued his sequence of vocal works.

The 2011 Frost/Bjarnason collaboration Sólaris (HVALUR12) — a response to Andrei Tarkovsky’s film — was a landmark of the label’s modern-classical-electronic intersection: orchestrated for the Sinfonietta Cracovia with Bjarnason conducting, produced jointly by Frost and Valgeir, and featuring Brian Eno as a visual contributor. Puzzle Muteson debuted that same year with En Garde (HVALUR11).

2012–2013 brought a run of chamber music records: Nadia Sirota’s Baroque (HVALUR17, 2013), organist James McVinnie’s Cycles (HVALUR19, 2013), and Valgeir Sigurðsson’s Architecture Of Loss (HVALUR13, 2012). The label also released a multi-artist DVD/concert document, Everything Everywhere All The Time / Whale Watching Tour (HVALUR14, 2013), documenting the BC roster on tour together.

Mid-period and new voices (2014–2019)

Ben Frost’s A U R O R A (HVALUR20, 2014) was the label’s highest-profile release in this period, bringing in collaborators Greg Fox, Shahzad Ismaily, and Thor Harris (of Swans), with additional production from Tim Hecker and Lawrence English. It reached audiences beyond the label’s usual chamber music base.

Frost’s The Wasp Factory (HVALUR25, 2016) — a ballet score — and subsequent Mute-affiliated work marked the beginning of his transition away from Bedroom Community as his primary home. The label continued releasing records from its extended roster: Emily Hall (2015), Jodie Landau (2015), Wild Up (2015), aYia (2016, 2018), Nakata7 (2018), and Sam Slater’s debut Wrong Airport Ghost (2018).

Daniel Pioro’s solo violin record Dust (2019) and the Donnacha Dennehy/Nadia Sirota collaboration Tessellatum (HVALUR29, 2017) extended the label’s classical-adjacent programming. Viktor Orri Árnason and Yair Elazar Glotman both debuted on the label in 2019.

Recent catalog (2020–present)

Lyra Pramuk’s Fountain (HVALREKI17, 2020) — a debut album built entirely from electronically processed vocal samples, self-produced and mastered by Valgeir Sigurðsson — became the label’s most talked-about release in this period. Her follow-up Delta (HVALUR39, 2021) continued that work.

The Manchester Collective began a run on the label from 2021, and Sam Slater’s second solo I Do Not Wish To Be Known As A Vandal (HVALUR41, 2022) appeared with contributions from Hildur Guðnadóttir and JFDR. Valgeir Sigurðsson’s own Dissonance (HVALUR28, 2017) and work by Edmund Finnis (2022) and Daniel Elms (2024) continued the label’s programming into the mid-2020s.

Connections

  • Valgeir Sigurðsson — founder; all production flows through Greenhouse Studios, Reykjavík
  • Ben Frost — co-founder; his catalog spans the full arc of the label (2006–2019)
  • Nico Muhly — co-founder; American composer embedded in the Reykjavík sessions
  • Hildur Guðnadóttir — not a BC artist herself but performed on early Frost and Valgeir releases; close to the network
  • Amiina — Icelandic string quartet; contributed to multiple BC releases
  • Sigur Rós — Daníel Bjarnason arranged strings for them; Valgeir Sigurðsson recorded/mixed Sigur Rós sessions
  • Tim Hecker — contributed additional production/sound design to A U R O R A (2014); Valgeir mixed Hecker’s Virgins
  • Lawrence English — contributed additional production to A U R O R A (2014)
  • Sam Slater — third-generation BC artist, himself a Hildur Guðnadóttir collaborator
  • Lyra Pramuk — 2020s voice of the label; Fountain most prominent recent release
  • Nadia Sirota — violist; solo label artist and ensemble performer across many credits
  • Mute Records — Ben Frost’s subsequent label home after BC; some overlap period
  • Daníel Bjarnason — Icelandic composer; Sólaris collab with Frost (2011)
