Editions EG

Discogs ID: 199
Parent: E.G. Records Ltd. (ID 291535)
Sublabel: Editions EG Collectors’ Edition (ID 1047301)
Active: 1980–c. 1992
Origin: London, UK

The prestige imprint of E.G. Records Ltd., formed in 1980 to house new experimental and ambient releases alongside an extensive reissue program drawn from the EG/E.G. catalog. Where the parent EG label dealt in rock and prog (Roxy Music, King Crimson, T. Rex), Editions EG defined itself by ambient, minimalist, new music, and experimental works — primarily the output of Brian Eno and his immediate circle, plus the EG management stable’s avant-garde adjacent artists. By release count it is one of the most significant experimental music imprints of the 1980s.

Parent Company and History

E.G. Records Ltd. was incorporated August 29, 1969, by David Enthoven and John Gaydon. Through the 1970s it served as both label and management company for King Crimson, T. Rex, Emerson Lake & Palmer, and Roxy Music. Gaydon left in 1971, Enthoven in 1977; Sam Alder and Mark Fenwick took over. Editions EG was spun off in 1980 as a dedicated imprint for more experimental material.

In the late 1980s Alder and Fenwick ran into severe financial difficulties through Lloyd’s of London investments. According to Robert Fripp, artist royalty streams were diverted by EG to cover debts, triggering lengthy legal battles with Fripp and others. E.G. Records Ltd. was eventually sold to Virgin Records Group Ltd (subsequently acquired by EMI predecessor companies). On May 9, 1991, the company was renamed Virgin EG Records Ltd. The Editions EG imprint went defunct around this time; final releases appear c. 1992.

Robert Quine / Fred Maher — Basic

Robert Quine and Fred Maher released Basic (1984, EGED 36) as a co-credited album: jazz-rock instrumentals, both musicians playing bass, guitar, and drum programming throughout. Mixed by Mario Salvati, mastered by Greg Calbi.

Killing Joke

Killing Joke had a substantial presence despite being sonically incompatible with most of the catalog — 18 releases including Killing Joke (EGS 109, 1980), What’s This For…! (EGM 111, 1981), Revelations (EGMD 3, 1982), “Ha” Killing Joke Live (EGMDT 4, 1982), Fire Dances (EGMD 5, 1983), Night Time (1985), and Outside The Gate (1988). EG was their label through the full first phase of the band.

The connection is straightforwardly managerial: EG was their label from the outset. The initial signing had friction — vocalist Jaz Coleman reportedly rushed the debut album to press before a label-controlled version could appear, and a lawsuit resulted over the release. The band stayed through Outside The Gate (1988), after which they moved to Noise Entertainment for Extremities, Dirt And Various Repressed Emotions (1990).

Edikanfo

The Pace Setters (1981, 2302 113) is not an anomaly — Brian Eno actually traveled to Accra and produced it there, recording at Studio One in Ghana. Edikanfo was an eight-piece ensemble managed by Faisal Helwani, an impresario based at the Napoleon Club in Osu, Accra. The album blended highlife and afrofunk. A coup d’état on December 31, 1981 derailed the band’s momentum, with subsequent curfews effectively shutting down Accra’s live music scene for years. The album went largely unheard until a 2020 reissue on Glitterbeat Records.

The Fripp Dispute

Robert Fripp’s legal battle with E.G. lasted six and a half years (April 1991 – September 1997), followed by further litigation with Virgin and EMI — 23 years of legal action in total by his account.

The root cause: Alder and Fenwick had invested heavily in Lloyd’s of London and attempted to enter real estate; major losses through 1988–1991 led them to have EG loan £4 million to prop up their failing personal businesses. Royalties to the roster were diverted to cover those loans. EG then sold the catalog to Virgin and the publishing to EMI without resolving the royalty debts.

Fripp’s response was to found Discipline Global Mobile (DGM) in 1992 with producer David Singleton — explicitly conceived as an ethical countermodel, with artists retaining all copyrights.
