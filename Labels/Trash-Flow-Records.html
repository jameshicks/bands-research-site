Trash Flow Records

Independent US record label. Discogs label ID: 98971.

Overview

Trash Flow Records is a small US independent label active between 1990 and 1993, issuing four confirmed releases across that span — three 7” singles and one 7” 45 RPM single. The Discogs label profile lists a Hoboken, New Jersey contact address (411 First Street Apt. #3, Hoboken, NJ 07030), though the label’s artistic and social context is firmly rooted in the Louisville, Kentucky underground of the same era: two of its four releases feature Louisville bands, one of those is the debut of King Kong (3) (whose founding members included the original Slint lineup), and the label’s house engineer is Howie Gano, the defining production figure of the mid-1980s to mid-1990s Louisville scene.

The known catalog runs TF02 through TF05. No release with catalog number TF01 has been located in Discogs — a TF01 search returns only unrelated labels — leaving TF01 as either an uncatalogued release, a promotional item, or a gap in the numbering. The fourth Discogs release, Seam’s Kernel 7” (TF05, 1993), comes three years after the label’s initial burst and connects the catalog to the Chicago post-rock orbit: Seam were a Chicago/Chapel Hill band led by Sooyoung Park, with members including Bundy K. Brown and John McEntire.

The label has no documented parent label, no sublabels, and no confirmed website.

Front cover art by Rudolph Grey (Blue Humans guitarist and noise scene figure). This is Love Child (3)‘s first vinyl release — their 1988 cassette preceded it on Not On Label. The band subsequently moved to Homestead Records for Okay? (1991) and City Slang for two 1992 releases.

The Babylon Dance Band was a Louisville group; Antietam’s guitarist Tara Key is credited here alongside the full BDB lineup. Engineered by Howie Gano, who also engineered TF04. Tammi Colichio designed both TF02 and TF03.

The band later appeared on Matador’s Four On One compilation (1994, release 1966518), also recorded by Gano.

7” EP, 33⅓ RPM, three tracks: “The Boy” / “The Man” / “Birdy Song.” Matrix: NR-18476 A/B (mastered by National Record Manufacturing). Personnel:

The label’s best-known release. King Kong (3)‘s debut on an outside label (following the self-released Movie Star 7”, 1989). The lineup here is Buckler’s first without the Slint-orbit four-piece — this is Buckler, Rappa, and Schuler as a trio. Pajo contributes uncredited artwork, maintaining his connection to the group even as he was no longer performing with them.

TF05 — Seam, Kernel (1993)

7”, 45 RPM, two tracks: “Kernel” (3:20) / “Sweet Pea” (3:32). Matrix: TF-05-A / TF-05-B. No additional credits listed in Discogs beyond primary artist. A master release exists (master 149653), with multiple pressings including a City Slang European version.

Seam were led by Sooyoung Park and at various points included Bundy K. Brown, John McEntire, Mac McCaughan, and Chris Manfrin. Their subsequent catalog was on Touch And Go (The Problem With Me, 1993; Are You Driving Me Crazy?, 1995; The Pace Is Glacial, 1998) and ché Records. Kernel predates the Touch and Go relationship and is among their earliest vinyl.
