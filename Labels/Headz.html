Overview

Headz is a Tokyo-based collective founded by Atsushi Sasaki that emerged from music journalism — most notably through the magazine Fader — before developing into a licensing label specialising in Japanese editions of international experimental, ambient, and post-rock releases. The catalogue is heavily weighted toward the 2000s (85 releases), with more modest activity continuing into the 2010s and 2020s, and operates across several sublabels including Leftbrain, Play, Twinkle, Unknownmix, and Vectors. The roster’s prominent Chicago names — John McEntire, Sam Prekop, and Jeff Parker — suggest a strong pipeline into the Thrill Jockey and post-rock orbit, which tracks for a collective that crossed the journalist/label divide.

Notes (web) — Headz / Thrill Jockey relationship

Short answer: both. A formal licensing contract with Thrill Jockey is confirmed, and all available evidence indicates it was brokered through — and ran alongside — Sasaki’s pre-existing role as a critic/journalist who wrote liner notes for the same releases and booked the same artists for Japanese concerts.

What is established

  • Sasaki co-founded HEADZ with Masaaki Hara in May 1995 in Udagawa-cho, Shibuya, initially as a cultural/journalism collective. The magazine Fader followed in 1997. (remaiin.eu, 2022-01; shinsho.kobunsha.com, Shibuya-kei article)
  • Discogs snippets and release credit data confirm that Headz “took on Thrill Jockey under contract” to publish Sea and Cake and Brokeback releases in Japan — indicating a formal licensing agreement, not purely an ad-hoc arrangement. (Discogs label profile 16930, via search snippet)
  • Sasaki wrote Japanese liner notes for at least the Headz/Thrill Jockey reissue of Tortoise’s Millions Now Living Will Never Die (HEADZ 40, 2004), demonstrating that the journalist and licensor roles were the same person on the same releases. (Discogs release 1738581, via search snippet)
  • HEADZ was also active in concert booking and production for overseas musicians in Japan from the late 1990s to mid-2000s — placing Sasaki in a direct working relationship with these Chicago-scene artists beyond just licensing their records. (shinsho.kobunsha.com)
  • The collective Sasaki assembled at HEADZ comprised “journalists, office workers, a graphic designer, and a translator” — consistent with a journalist-led operation that grafted on a formal label function. ([De:Bug Magazin snippet, search result for de-bug.de/mag/japan-headz/])

What is not established

  • No primary source — no interview with Sasaki, no Thrill Jockey/Bettina Richards statement, no contemporaneous press — explicitly describes how or when the initial Thrill Jockey licensing agreement was negotiated or who initiated it.
  • Whether the contact began before or after Headz started the label function (1995 collective; label releases date from 2003 on Discogs) is not documented in any source found.
  • The De:Bug Magazin article (de-bug.de/mag/japan-headz/) — the only dedicated English-language feature on Headz found — was unreachable during this research (connection closed; agent-browser also failed). It may contain detail about the founding of the label function and its Thrill Jockey relationship.
