Interscope Records

US major label. Discogs label ID: 2311. Parent: Interscope Geffen A&M (ID: 1465258), which in turn sits under Interscope Capitol Labels Group / Universal Music Group.

Founded in 1989–1990 by film producer Ted Field (through Interscope Communications, Inc.) and record producer Jimmy Iovine as a joint venture with Atlantic Records / Warner Music Group. Interscope differed from industry norms by giving A&R staff — and artists themselves — full creative control. The label achieved profitability in 1993, ahead of projections, and by the late 1990s had become one of the most commercially dominant labels in the US.

The label’s early roster combined alternative rock (Primus, Helmet, Nine Inch Nails, No Doubt) with hip-hop (2Pac, Snoop Dogg, Dr. Dre via Death Row). After Time Warner sold its stake in 1995 and MCA acquired 50% in 1996, Interscope expanded its hip-hop, R&B, and pop presence dramatically. The Universal/PolyGram merger (1998–1999) folded both Geffen Records and A&M Records into Interscope, creating the Interscope Geffen A&M group. From 1999 onward all three labels operate under the IGA umbrella.

Label codes: LC 06406 / LC 6406. Address (1999–): 2220 Colorado Avenue, Santa Monica, CA 90404. Pre-1999: 10900 Wilshire Blvd., Ste 1230, Los Angeles, CA 90024.

(Sources: Discogs label profile; Wikipedia — “Interscope Records”)

History

1989–1994: Founding and early alternative/hip-hop roster

Ted Field began building Interscope as a division of Interscope Communications in 1989. He hired John McClain (key figure in Janet Jackson’s success at A&M) and Tom Whalley (former head of A&R at Capitol). Jimmy Iovine — producer for U2, Bruce Springsteen, Stevie Nicks, John Lennon — joined as co-founder. The two were introduced by U2 manager Paul McGuinness; David Geffen helped broker the agreement.

Interscope launched formally in 1990 as a joint venture with Atlantic Records. First release: “Rico Suave” by Gerardo (December 1990), reaching #7 on the Billboard Hot 100. Primus debuted with Sailing the Seas of Cheese (May 1991), followed by Marky Mark and the Funky Bunch’s Music for the People (July 1991). Tom Whalley signed 2Pac two days after hearing his demo (August 1991); 2Pacalypse Now followed in November 1991.

In 1992, Interscope negotiated a $10m deal with Dr. Dre and Marion “Suge” Knight to finance and distribute Death Row Records. The Chronic was released December 1992 (via a complex arrangement with Priority Records) and sold nearly 3 million copies by end of 1993. That year also saw Snoop Dogg’s Doggystyle (first-week sales of 800,000+) and the launch of the label’s alternative rock presence: No Doubt’s self-titled debut, Helmet’s Meantime, 4 Non Blondes’ Bigger, Better, Faster, More!, and — through a joint venture with TVT/Nothing Records — the Nine Inch Nails EP Broken.

In 1994 Interscope invested $2.5m in a joint venture with Trauma Records (2) (Discogs ID: 47085), which produced Bush’s Sixteen Stone (three #1 Modern Rock tracks), Nine Inch Nails’ The Downward Spiral (#2 US), Marilyn Manson’s Portrait of an American Family, Toadies’ Rubberneck, and Helmet’s Betty.

1995–1999: Gangsta rap controversy, MCA acquisition, IGA formation

Vice President Dan Quayle (1992) and Senate Majority Leader Bob Dole (1995) both publicly attacked Interscope for its Death Row releases. Time Warner, under political pressure, sold its 50% stake back to Field and Iovine for $115 million in September 1995. MCA — then owned by Seagram — acquired 50% for a reported $200 million in February 1996, granting Interscope full creative autonomy.

Dr. Dre left Death Row in mid-1996 and founded Aftermath Entertainment (ID: 2310) as a new joint venture with Interscope. Death Row remained until 1997, when Suge Knight was imprisoned. By November 1996 Interscope held the top 4 positions on the Billboard pop chart simultaneously (Bush, Snoop Dogg, No Doubt, 2Pac) — first time any label had achieved this in 20 years.

In 1998, Interscope signed a joint-venture with Ruff Ryders. Jimmy Iovine’s assistant Dean Geistlinger saw Eminem perform at the Rap Olympics in 1997 and passed his CD to Iovine, who passed it to Dre. The Slim Shady LP (February 1999) entered the charts at #2 and won two Grammys. Later in 1999 Eminem and manager Paul Rosenberg founded Shady Records.

The Universal/PolyGram merger (1998) led to Geffen and A&M being absorbed into Interscope; by January 1999 the label operated as Interscope Geffen A&M.

2000–2010: Dominance and new ventures

Eminem’s The Marshall Mathers LP (May 2000) sold 1.76 million copies in its first week — then the fastest-selling rap album in history. Limp Bizkit’s Chocolate Starfish and the Hot Dog Flavored Water (October 2000) sold 1.05 million in week one, briefly the fastest-selling rock album in history.

Interscope began distributing U2 in the US after acquiring rights to All That You Can’t Leave Behind (2000). 50 Cent signed in 2002 with a $1 million advance; Get Rich or Die Tryin’ (2003) debuted at #1 and was certified 9x platinum. G-Unit Records followed as an Interscope joint venture.

DreamWorks Records was acquired by Universal in 2003 and merged into Interscope Geffen A&M in 2004, bringing Blink-182, The All-American Rejects, and Nelly Furtado.

In 2005, Martin Kierszenbaum launched Cherrytree Records with Interscope as a vehicle for international artists. Early Cherrytree acts included Feist and Robyn.

In 2006, Dre and Iovine co-founded Beats Electronics. Lady Gaga’s debut The Fame was released in August 2008; Interscope held all four top positions on the 2009 year-end Hot 100.

2010–2024: Madonna, Janick, ICLG

Interscope signed Madonna and Van Halen in 2011 (both previously Warner Bros.). John Janick — founder of Fueled By Ramen — was named president and COO in October 2012 and succeeded Iovine as chairman/CEO in May 2014 following Apple’s acquisition of Beats. The post-2012 decade brought Kendrick Lamar, Lana Del Rey, Imagine Dragons, Selena Gomez, Billie Eilish, and Tame Impala to the roster.

In 2023–2024 Interscope became the flagship of the newly reorganized Interscope Capitol Labels Group (ICLG), which unified UMG’s West Coast operations.
