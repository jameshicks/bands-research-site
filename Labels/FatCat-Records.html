FatCat Records

Discogs ID: 264
Origin: Brighton, UK (PO Box 3400, Brighton BN1 4WG)
Active: 1996–present
Parent company: FatCat Records Ltd. (Discogs ID: 265293)
Website: fatcat.online

Overview

FatCat Records is a Brighton-based UK independent label spanning electronica, noise, post-rock, folk, and experimental music. Its catalog is unusually wide-ranging — from abrasive noise (Merzbow, Black Dice) to delicate folk (Vashti Bunyan) to Scottish indie-rock (Frightened Rabbit, The Twilight Sad) — united primarily by an A&R sensibility that prizes idiosyncrasy over genre conformity.

The label began as a record shop founded in 1989 in Crawley, West Sussex by Dave Cawley (Discogs artist ID: 449571), Alex Knight (Discogs artist ID: 141590), and Andy Martin — each contributing £1,000 to open Crawley’s first dance music shop, specialising in Detroit and Chicago techno and house. It relocated to Covent Garden, London in 1990, stocking Aphex Twin, Black Dog, and the emerging British electronic scene. The shop closed in autumn 1997. When it did, Derek Birkett of One Little Indian offered the owners free office space to launch the label — logistical support rather than financial or A&R involvement. Björk’s connection is through her longstanding relationship with OLI/Birkett. The label’s first release — “The EVA EP” by Web (2), catalog 12 FAT 001 — appeared in 1996, suggesting limited label operations had already begun before the shop fully closed. FatCat relocated from London to Brighton in 2001. In June 2008, OLI and FatCat entered a joint venture deal.

FatCat is credited across 1,548 releases in the Discogs database as of 2025.

Eras

1996–2000: Electronica and noise foundations

The first releases were 12” singles under the 12FAT catalog sequence, oriented toward electronica, IDM, and experimental electronic music. Early signings included Grain, Transient Waves, Live Human, and Mice Parade (Adam Pierce). The label quickly established links to the outer edges of electronic music: splits with Merzbow, AMM, and Gescom (an Autechre alias), plus a remix 12” credited to Björk / Funkstörung (“All Is Full Of…” 12FAT022, 1998).

The first FatCat compilation, Across Uneven Terrain (FATCD01, 1999), surveyed this early period, featuring Process, Fonn, Live Human, Mice Parade, Transient Waves, Immense, and a Björk/Funkstörung collab alongside an Autechre remix.

The Sigur Rós relationship began in 1999 with the “Svefn-G-Englar” 12” (12FAT036) and “Ný Batterí” (12FAT039, 2000), leading to the UK release of Ágætis Byrjun (FATCD11, 2000) — the album that broke Sigur Rós internationally. FatCat held the UK license for their early catalog including ( ) (FATCD22, 2002) and the Angels Of The Universe OST (Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson / Sigur Rós, FATOST-CD01, 2001).

Also in this period: To Rococo Rot (12FAT007, 1998), HiM (Our Point Of Departure, FATLP12, 2000; New Features, FATLP15, 2001), and the early Emiliana Torrini E:RMX series.

2001–2004: múm, Animal Collective, and the 130701 sublabel

In 2001, FatCat launched its sublabel 130701 (named after its inception date: 13 July 2001), dedicated to orchestrated and instrumental material. The first 130701 release was Set Fire To Flames, Sings Reign Rebuilder (CD 13-01, 2001), followed by Sylvain Chauveau (CD 13-02, 2003) and Max Richter’s landmark The Blue Notebooks (CD13-04, 2004) — a record now considered a cornerstone of the modern classical revival.

On the main label, múm’s Finally We Are No One (FATCD18, 2002) arrived as one of the most critically praised experimental pop records of that year — hushed electronics, toy instruments, found sound, and Icelandic folk color. múm remained on FatCat through five more releases before departing after Go Go Smear The Poison Ivy (FATCD46, 2007).

Animal Collective began their FatCat relationship in 2003 with the reissue of their early double-album Spirit They’re Gone Spirit They’ve Vanished / Danse Manatee (FAT-SP07P) and continued with Sung Tongs (FAT-SP08, 2004), Prospect Hummer (FAT-SP09, 2005), Feels (FAT-SP11, 2005), and several singles. These are among the most-pressed FatCat releases in the database (16 pressings for Sung Tongs).

Vashti Bunyan’s Lookaftering (FATCD38P, 2005) — her second album, after a 35-year gap — was a significant cultural moment, reconnecting a lost British folk artist with a new audience. The album was produced with contributions from members of Devendra Banhart’s circle and Animal Collective.

David Grubbs had two albums on the label: Rickets & Scurvy (FATCD21, 2002) and A Guess At The Riddle (FATCD33, 2004).

Black Dice released Beaches & Canyons (FATCD24, 2003) and Creature Comforts (FATCD32, 2004) on FatCat — their most widely distributed period.

2005–2010: Scottish indie, folk, and the Glasgow pipeline

From around 2007 FatCat developed a particularly strong relationship with Glasgow-area acts, building what became the label’s most commercially successful era.

The Twilight Sad’s debut Fourteen Autumns & Fifteen Winters (FATCD55P, 2007) established the band as one of the label’s flagship acts — 18 pressings in the database across subsequent editions. They released five studio albums on FatCat through Nobody Wants To Be Here And Nobody Wants To Leave (FATLP132, 2013), plus numerous singles.

Frightened Rabbit’s FatCat catalog begins with the single “Be Less Rude / The Greys” (7FAT33, 2007) and continues through Sing The Greys (FATCD67, 2007), The Midnight Organ Fight (FAT CD 70, 2008), The Winter Of Mixed Drinks (2010), and beyond. The Midnight Organ Fight was produced by Peter Katis (who became a recurring FatCat producer) and is among the label’s most-pressed releases (20 pressings). Scott Hutchison of Frightened Rabbit also handled artwork across multiple FatCat releases (23 design credits in the database). The band remained one of FatCat’s key acts until Hutchison’s death in 2018.

We Were Promised Jetpacks. joined around 2009 with “It’s Thunder And It’s Lightning” (7fat75P) and released their debut These Four Walls (FATCD72P, 2009 — 17 pressings), followed by In The Pit Of The Stomach (FAT0097, 2010), The Last Place You’ll Look (2010), and Unravelling (2014).

Other notable acts from this period include Nina Nastasia (On Leaving, FATLP47, 2006; Outlaster, FATCD82, 2010), Vashti Bunyan (singles and the late compilation Some Things Just Stick In Your Mind), Vetiver (To Find Me Gone, FATLP43, 2006; Thing Of The Past, 2008), Tom Brosseau, Max Richter (via 130701), and Hauschka (straddling the main label and 130701).

2011–present: Post-punk, indie, and continued eclecticism

The 2010s saw FatCat continue across multiple registers. Shopping (3) (Why Choose, FATCD139, 2015; Consumer Complaints, 2015; The Official Body, 2017; All Or Nothing, 2020) became associated with the label’s post-punk revival corner. Honeyblood (Honeyblood, FATCD128, 2014; Babes Never Die, FATLP141, 2016) and Traams (Grin, FATLP122X, 2013; Modern Dancing, 2015; Personal Best, 2022) filled out the indie rock side.

C Duncan’s chamber-folk records (Architect, FATCD130, 2015; The Midnight Sun, FATLP144, 2016; H·E·A·L·T·H, FATLP151, 2019) are among the more distinctive solo artist projects of this era.

The 130701 sublabel continued growing through the decade with releases from Jóhann Jóhannsson (The Miners’ Hymns, LP13-13, 2011), Dustin O’Halloran (Vorleben, 2011; Lumiere, 2011), Ian William Craig (Centres, 2016; Slow Vessels, 2017; Red Sun Through Smoke, 2020), Resina (2) (Resina, 2016; Traces, 2018; Speechless, 2021), Clarice Jensen (The Experience Of Repetition As Death, 2020; Ainu Mosir, 2021; Esthesis, 2022), and Shida Shahabi.

Open Questions

Resolved (web research, 2026-05-07)

  • Shop founding year and location: 1989, Crawley, West Sussex — not Brighton. Third co-founder Andy Martin established alongside Cawley and Knight. Moved to Covent Garden, London 1990. Label moved to Brighton 2001.
  • Björk/OLI support: Derek Birkett (OLI) provided free office space in 1997 when the shop closed — logistical only. A separate OLI/FatCat joint venture was struck in June 2008.
  • Glasgow office: No evidence found. Confirmed second base is a small US office (New York area, opened ~2005). Glasgow references appear to be unfounded.
  • Dave Cawley and Alex Knight: Both confirmed active as co-founders and directors as of a 2015 interview. Label at that point had ~5 staff.
  • This Will Destroy You: No FatCat releases. Their catalog is on Magic Bullet Records. The connection mentioned in earlier research notes was incorrect.
  • Deaf Center: No FatCat releases. Deaf Center (Erik K Skodvin + Otto A Totland) released via Miasmah Recordings (Skodvin’s own label) and Type Records.
