Polydor

Discogs ID: 1610 Parent: Universal Music Group (via PolyGram, acquired 1998/1999) Label code: LC 0309 / LC 00309

Major multinational record label established in 1913 by the German Polyphonwerke A.-G. In the UK, a dedicated domestic operation (Polydor Records Ltd.) was established in 1954 and grew into one of the primary major-label homes for British rock and new wave throughout the 1970s and 1980s. After PolyGram’s merger into Universal Music Group (effective January 1999), Polydor continued as a frontline UK imprint under Universal.

Corporate Structure

Polydor’s corporate lineage runs: Polyphonwerke A.-G. → Deutsche Grammophon-Aktiengesellschaft (post-1932) → PolyGram (formed 1972, Philips/Deutsche Grammophon joint venture; Polydor placed under Polydor International from 1972) → PolyGram Label Group (1991) → Universal Music Group (1999).

For the vault’s purposes, the relevant entity is the UK arm operating from roughly 1977 onward. Discogs catalogs all of these under the umbrella Polydor (ID 1610), though individual releases may specify sub-regional labels such as Polydor France (ID 26531), Polydor International (ID 73924), etc.

PolyGram catalog number note: PolyGram’s seven-numeral catalogue system was introduced April 1970; any earlier release bearing such a number is a post-1970 reissue. A new six-digit-plus-suffix system replaced it in August 1982.

Key Post-Punk and New Wave Acts on Polydor UK

The Jam (1977–1982)

The Jam released their entire original catalog on Polydor — arguably the label’s most important post-punk relationship. From debut single In The City (1977) through the farewell single Beat Surrender (1982) and live album Dig The New Breed (1982), every Jam release was a Polydor UK release. Key albums:

AlbumYearNotes
In The City1977Debut; instant UK chart presence
This Is The Modern World1977Second album
All Mod Cons1978Widely considered their artistic breakthrough
Setting Sons1979Near-concept album; The Eton Rifles #1 single
Sound Affects1980Post-punk peak; Start! and Going Underground both #1
The Gift1982Final studio album; Town Called Malice #1
Dig The New Breed1982Live album; final release before breakup

After disbanding, Polydor continued issuing Jam compilations and reissues: Snap! (1983), Compact Snap! (1984), Direction, Reaction, Creation box set (1997), The Singles 1977-79 and The Singles 1980-82 (both 2001). Polydor remains the rights-holder for the catalog.

The Style Council (1983–1989)

The Style Council — Paul Weller’s post-Jam project with Mick Talbot — continued the Polydor relationship seamlessly. Entire catalog on Polydor UK, including:

The Style Council disbanded after Polydor rejected their house-music-influenced album Modernism: A New Decade in 1989.

Siouxsie & The Banshees (1978–1995)

Siouxsie & The Banshees signed to Polydor in 1978, with debut single Hong Kong Garden reaching UK #7. Their entire core discography was on Polydor UK (often via the Wonderland sublabel from 1983, Discogs ID 111105), with some later releases licensed to Geffen Records for the US market. Key records:

Orange Juice (3) (1981–1984)

Orange Juice — the Glasgow post-punk/jangle-pop band led by Edwyn Collins — started on the indie Postcard Records before signing to Polydor in 1981. Their Polydor period produced the bulk of their catalog:

That Petrol Emotion (1987)

That Petrol Emotion signed to Polydor for their second album Babble (1987), produced by Roli Mosimann. The Polydor period was brief — one album plus associated singles — before the band moved to Virgin.

Gang of Four’s 1991 reunion was signed to Polydor. The period produced one album and three singles, all recorded with the band’s Andy Gill producing:

Key Sublabels (Post-Punk / Indie Scope)

Fiction Records (ID 5810)

Fiction Records is the most significant sublabel for this vault’s scope. Founded 1978 by Chris Parry, it operated under Polydor’s manufacturing and distribution umbrella for its entire existence. Fiction is best known as the label home of The Cure for over twenty years. Also released The Passions (Hunted single 1979, Michael & Miranda LP 1980) and other UK post-punk acts. Sublabels of Fiction: Desire Records (ID 4082), Non Fiction Records (ID 29463).

Wonderland (3) (ID 111105)

Siouxsie & The Banshees’ dedicated sublabel under Polydor, used for most of their releases from 1983 through 1995.

Reaction (ID 20328)

An earlier Polydor sublabel; associated with 1960s acts (The Who had some early releases here).

Mother Records (ID 3103)

Another Polydor sublabel; associated with U2-adjacent releases in the 1990s.

Open Questions

  • Which specific UK catalog numbers did Polydor use for The Jam’s debut In The City and debut single of the same name? The Discogs data confirms Polydor UK but cat numbers need individual release lookup.
  • Was That Petrol Emotion’s Babble (1987) released on Polydor in all territories or only UK? Discogs shows 28 pressings across multiple countries; the US pressing label needs checking against specific release records.
  • Brian Eno appears with 47 Polydor UK releases — is this primarily early Roxy Music-era material licensed to Polydor, or direct Eno solo releases? Needs discography check.
  • The Polydor-period Gang of Four lineup for the Mall sessions (1991): Discogs release credits for the European LP (ID 1835757) list no additional credits. Who played on the record beyond Gill and King? Needs notes-check or web source.
  • Did Polydor UK ever release any material by the Buzzcocks? Discogs lists their top labels as United Artists/EMI — but their early period overlaps with Polydor’s punk signings and this should be confirmed.
