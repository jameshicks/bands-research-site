Guerilla Records

British independent record label. Founded 1990 in London by Disc O’Dell (Richard O’Dell) and William Orbit. Discogs label ID: 621 (listed as “Guerilla”; written as “Guerilla Records” on many releases). Contact address: Unit 32, Pall Mall Deposit, 126/128 Barbly Road, London W10 6BL (now defunct). Company dissolved 2003.

O’Dell’s follow-up venture after Y Records (his early-1980s Bristol post-punk label). Guerilla pivoted sharply toward progressive house and electronic dance music — one of the UK labels that helped define and spearhead that genre in the early 1990s. A new incarnation, Guerilla Recordings (2), appeared around 2008.

About / History

Guerilla was founded in 1990 by O’Dell and William Orbit, who had been on the Y Records roster (Orbit was a member of Bassomatic and co-ran their management through O’Dell Artists). The label launched with a run of 12-inches under the “GRRR” catalog prefix — deliberately energetic branding that matched the music’s tempo and attitude.

The inaugural release was Bassomatic’s “In The Realm Of The Senses” (GRRR 1, 1990), a William Orbit-produced house track that established the label’s sound immediately. Bassomatic — Orbit’s group, comprising also Laurie Mayer, Sharon Musgrave, Steve Roberts, Inna One Step, Sindy Finn, and John Gosling — became the label’s flagship act in its first year, with a string of singles culminating in the Virgin-distributed “Fascinating Rhythm” (VS 1274, 1990), which crossed over into the mainstream UK market.

The Guerilla/IRS deal was the label’s key commercial arrangement. I.R.S. Records had a prior relationship with Orbit as an artist, and when Guerilla launched, I.R.S. struck a US distribution deal. IRS executive Rob Di Stefano managed what Guerilla titles were released in the US, issuing them between 1991 and 1994 through his own TRIBAL America label. By 1994, Tribal was commercially ascendant and when the Guerilla deal came up for renewal, Di Stefano declined — partly due to high licensing costs. Without that US revenue stream, Guerilla experienced financial problems and eventually closed. The company was formally dissolved in 2003.

The label’s 1991–1994 peak produced around 100 unique releases (by master count), with a dense rotation of UK house, progressive house, and rave-era electronic music. The compilation series Dub House Disco (three volumes: GRLP 004, GRLP 007, GRCD 012) became the label’s most prominent catalog statement, surveying the Guerilla aesthetic from multiple angles. A US-market version, Dub House Disco & Dub House Disco 2000 (1993), was issued on IRS/Virgin.

Design across the catalog was handled primarily by Me Company (14 releases) and The Designers Republic (14 releases), giving the label a visually distinctive aesthetic rooted in early-1990s digital design and rave-era typography.

Key Artists

Bassomatic

The label’s flagship act in 1990–91. William Orbit’s group; members included Laurie Mayer, Sharon Musgrave, Steve Roberts, Inna One Step, Sindy Finn, and John Gosling. Discogs ID: 10357. Produced and co-written by Orbit throughout. Their single “Rockers Vs. Ravers” (GRRR 7) has 21 Discogs pressings — the most-pressed release on the label. “Fascinating Rhythm” (VS 1274, 1990) crossed over to Virgin distribution.

D.O.P.

Most prolific non-compilation act on the label: 34 releases (1991–1994). The duo Kevin Hurry and Kevin Swain, with The Chameleon Project as an alias. Discogs ID: 11055. Also recorded as Bliss (6) and Nostalgia Freaks. Artwork frequently by The Designers Republic.

React 2 Rhythm

18 releases (1990–1992). Quartet: Richard Dight, Richard Tappin, Andrew Charman, Ray Wagstaff. Discogs ID: 7632. “Intoxication” (GRRR 14, 1991) features a Leftfield remix and William Orbit vocal samples.

Spooky

17 releases (1992–1993). Duo: Charlie May and Duncan Forbes. Discogs ID: 68364. Released their debut LP Gargantuan (GRLP 006, 1993) on the label; engineered by Charlie May and Nick Hale. Collaborated with Billie Ray Martin on “Persuasion” (GRRR 58, 1993).

Drum Club

3 releases (1992–1993): “U Make Me Feel So Good” (GRRR31) and “Alchemy” (GRRR43). Discogs ID: 2284.

The Moody Boys

6 releases in 1994, including LP Product Of The Environment (GRLP 013). Solo project of Anthony Thorpe (also Tony Thorpe). Discogs ID: 5759.

William Orbit

Co-founder. 4 releases as primary artist (1993), including Water From A Vine Leaf (1993), which features vocals by Beth Orton and was managed through O’Dell Artists. Real name William Mark Wainwright. Discogs ID: 2728. Also produced across the label roster (Bassomatic, React 2 Rhythm, etc.) — 14 producer credits across the catalog.

Shape Navigator

10 releases, all 1994. Discogs ID: 29756. Peter Coyte was the primary writer/producer.

Lemon Sol

8 releases (1993–1994), including LP Environmental Architecture (GRCD 014). Discogs ID: 13995.

Tenth Chapter

8 releases (1993–1994), including “Prologue” (GRRR 66, 1994). Discogs ID: 11932.

Supereal

10 releases (1991–1993), including LP Elixir (GRLP005, 1992). Discogs ID: 15967.

Connections

  • Disc O’Dell — co-founder; also ran Y Records (1980–1984), post-punk predecessor label
  • William Orbit — co-founder; real name William Mark Wainwright; member of Bassomatic, Spill, Torch Song; later produced Madonna’s Ray of Light (1998)
  • Y Records — O’Dell’s predecessor label; Bristol post-punk (The Pop Group, The Slits, Pigbag, Shriekback)
  • I.R.S. Records — US distribution partner; Guerilla titles released in US via I.R.S. deal 1991–1994
  • TRIBAL America — Rob Di Stefano’s imprint within IRS; issued Guerilla catalog in US
  • Leftfield — remix credit on React 2 Rhythm’s “Intoxication” (GRRR 14, 1991)
  • Beth Orton — vocalist on William Orbit’s Water From A Vine Leaf (1993); early career appearance
  • Billie Ray Martin — collaborated with Spooky on “Persuasion” (GRRR 58, 1993)
  • The Designers Republic — Sheffield design studio; 14 design/artwork credits on D.O.P. and other releases
  • Me Company — London design studio; 14 credits including multiple Spooky releases

Open Questions

  • The Discogs label is listed as “Guerilla” (ID: 621) with “Guerilla Records” as an alternate writing — no separate label entry exists for “Guerilla Records” as the UK entity. The Czech label “Guerilla Records” (ID: 32450) is unrelated.
  • Rob Di Stefano’s role: Discogs credits him on 6 releases with roles including A&R, liner notes, sequencing, and “project development” — the exact nature of the IRS/Tribal America deal’s terms and when it lapsed (1994) is not in the DB.
  • Beth Orton’s 5 vocal credits on Guerilla: Water From A Vine Leaf is confirmed (1993). The other 4 credited releases are not individually verified here — worth checking if they are Orbit solo releases or others.
  • Anne Corr: 20 “Written By” credits on Guerilla releases but no Discogs artist ID — identity not resolvable from DB alone.
  • Jon Pearn: 19 keyboards credits; no further Discogs data available.
  • The label’s post-1994 wind-down and the 2008 Guerilla Recordings (2) revival: circumstances not in the DB.
  • A late outlier: The Troubadours (CHE0060, 2009) appears with Guerilla label credit — unclear if this is the original label or a revival/licensing anomaly.
