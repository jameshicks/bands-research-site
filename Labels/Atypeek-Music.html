Overview

Atypeek Music is a French digital label formed in 2013, rising from the closure of two earlier imprints, Agony and Go Get Organized. Its catalogue leans heavily into noise, experimental, industrial, and post-punk, with over 125 releases catalogued on Discogs, the vast majority issued during the 2010s. A small core of French artists — Denis Frajerman, Nicolas Dick, Jeremy Chinour, (The True) Scorpio Rising, and Bonetrip’s — make up its principal roster.

Notes (web)

Label lineage: Go Get Organized → Agony → Atypeek Music

The Wikipedia article on Atypeek Music (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atypeek_Music, as of 2026-08-23) and its associated search-result snippets give the clearest available account of the lineage. No source explains a specific crisis or external cause for either transition; both appear to have been deliberate rebranding/refocusing decisions by the same founder, Christophe Féray.

Go Get Organized (GGO), 1988–1993

Founded in 1988 by Christophe Féray alongside two co-founders identified only as Carlos and Eric in a Spirit of Metal snippet (source: spirit-of-metal.com label page snippet, via web search). The name derives from a song by The Redskins. The label explored garage rock, hardcore, stoner rock, grunge, noise, no wave, jazz-core, noise rap and industrial. Notable early achievement: in 1991 GGO secured a European licensing deal for Monster Magnet’s Spine of God; the label also reportedly attempted to license a post-Bleach Nirvana record but lost out when Nirvana signed to a major. (Source: Wikipedia as above.)

Conflict on dates: Wikipedia gives GGO as 1988–1993; a 2020 Muzzart interview with Féray (muzzart.fr, 2020-04-06) says he put the label on hiatus in 1996 because “ma vie professionnelle de DA empiète sur le label” (“my professional life as artistic director encroached on the label”) — this contradicts the 1993 end date. The interview does not mention Agony by name and describes the subsequent relaunch simply as repositioning the label “quelques années plus tard sous une nouvelle forme avec les nouvelles technologies” (“a few years later in a new form with new technologies”). It is possible the interview is conflating GGO’s activity end-date with Agony’s (or that the two labels overlapped), but no source resolves this directly.

Agony (1993–2012)

Wikipedia and all derivative sources describe Agony as a straight renaming of GGO in 1993 rather than a new entity. The Agony phase shifted emphasis toward noise rock — roster included Kill the Thrill, Heliogabale, Condense, Hint, Davy Jones Locker, and Enablers. The name in full was “Agony and the Ecstasy.” No source gives a specific reason why the label stopped operating as Agony in 2012/2013.

Atypeek Music (2013–present)

All sources agree the 2013 transition coincided with moving to digital distribution across major platforms. Wikipedia quotes suggest the shift was driven by the opportunity to “use the majors’ tools” (digital platforms) rather than by any corporate collapse. The Muzzart interview confirms Féray’s framing: the relaunch was a deliberate technological repositioning, not a response to a crisis. Atypeek is registered in France as a “declared association” under the SIRENE registry (Wikidata Q15523562, last updated May 2017), based in Lyon. U.S. distribution is via The Orchard.

Why the predecessor labels “folded”: No source uses the word “folded” or describes financial failure. The transitions were rebranding/renaming under the same founder (Christophe Féray). The 1993 GGO→Agony change is not explained by any named source. The 2012–2013 Agony→Atypeek transition is attributed to the embrace of digital distribution platforms. The Muzzart interview (the only primary source found) describes a personal hiatus period rather than a formal dissolution.
