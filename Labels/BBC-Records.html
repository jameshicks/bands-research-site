The British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC) first introduced its own record label in 1967. It has used various names through the years. Please be sure to use the correct label:

1967-70 BBC Radio Enterprises 1970-74 BBC Records 1974-89 BBC Records And Tapes 1990s- BBC Worldwide.

With a remit to release singles and albums connected to TV and radio programmes (plus royal souvenirs), its output has been extremely varied and takes in a large amount of spoken-word material as well as music in a wide range of styles including theme music released as singles. The BBC Radiophonic Workshop established in 1958 created experimental music and other sounds used for programmes.

Distributed in the United States by Gemcom Inc.

— Discogs label profile, ID 3380

Overview

BBC Records was the trading name used from 1970 to 1974 by the BBC’s in-house label — one phase of a longer run that began as BBC Radio Enterprises in 1967 and passed through BBC Records And Tapes (1974–89) before becoming BBC Worldwide in the 1990s. The label sat under BBC Enterprises and was mandated to issue recordings tied to television and radio programmes, including royal commemoratives, with output spanning comedy, theme tunes, sound effects, soundtracks, and spoken word. The catalogue as indexed on Discogs concentrates heavily in the 1970s (35 of 40 releases), the label’s most active decade, and encompasses the BBC Radiophonic Workshop alongside vocal and comedy artists. US pressings were distributed by Gemcom Inc.
