Dutch/German experimental and sound-art label, originally known as Staaltape (early catalog numbers begin ST 00X from this period). Founded by Geert-Jan Hobijn — also the initiator of the Staalplaat Sound System performance/installation project. In the 1980s Staalplaat co-founded the NL Centre concert space in Amsterdam; early shows were documented in the NL-Documentatieserie tape series (ST 00X catalog numbers).

Soleilmoon Partnership and Split

Staalplaat was Soleilmoon Recordings’s sister company throughout the 1990s. Each distributed the other in its home territory — Staalplaat handled European distribution of Soleilmoon releases; Soleilmoon handled US distribution of Staalplaat releases. The partnership ended in 2000 following the death of Bryn Jones (Muslimgauze) on January 14, 1999.

The Muslimgauze catalog was the central bridge between the two labels:

  • Staalplaat: 496 Muslimgauze releases (first year: 1988), with two dedicated sublabels — Muslimgauze Archive (ID 309009) and Muslimgauze Subscription (ID 309070)
  • Soleilmoon: 62 Muslimgauze releases (1988–2014)

Staalplaat was clearly the primary custodian of the catalog in Europe; the scale difference (496 vs 62) reflects Staalplaat’s role as the main release vehicle while Soleilmoon handled US licensing and distribution. With Jones dead, the commercial and creative basis for the cross-distribution arrangement — centered on continuously issuing new Muslimgauze material — dissolved within a year.

From web sources (Bidoun, 2009; Wikipedia): Geert-Jan Hobijn is described as the “de facto executor of Muslimgauze’s work” — not formally appointed, but acting as primary custodian. No public documentation of who legally controls the masters has surfaced. Jones’ recording practice — he “often inundated labels and collaborators with music” and mailed lengthy recordings with no instructions — left a diffuse archive spread across multiple parties.

Staalplaat ran a £50 subscription model for limited new Muslimgauze releases. By 2009 there were already 50+ posthumous releases; at least 180 total Muslimgauze titles existed by that point. By 2026 Staalplaat’s Muslimgauze Archive series had reached at least AR 74 (still active).

Origin of the connection: Staalplaat was reportedly introduced to Muslimgauze through Soleilmoon — meaning Soleilmoon found Jones first (1988), then connected him to Staalplaat. The partnership grew from there, with Staalplaat taking the European role.
