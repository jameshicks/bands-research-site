Touch

Experimental / sound art label and multimedia publisher. London. 1982–present.

Discogs label ID: 1498 Contact (historical): touch@touch33.net; 13 Oswald Road, London SW17 7SS (1990 address) Web: touch33.net, touch333.bandcamp.com, touchradio.org.uk

Overview

Touch was founded in 1981 as a multimedia publishing project — simultaneously an audio-cassette label and magazine operation — by Jon Wozencroft and Mike Harding (2). It became one of the most coherent and long-running experimental imprints in the UK, operating as a vertically integrated audio-visual publishing project rather than a conventional record label. Its releases are inseparable from their packaging: Wozencroft’s landscape photography and typographic restraint constitute the label’s visual identity as distinctly as any individual recording. As of 2026 the label remains active, with Fennesz releasing The Last Days Of May (V33.60) that year.

The label’s first release was Feature Mist (T1, 1982), a Various Artists compilation. Early releases were compilations and magazine/cassette hybrids. By the mid-1980s Touch had become associated with the experimental post-industrial network via The Hafler Trio; by the mid-1990s it had expanded to include field recording (Chris Watson), drone electronics (Biosphere, Mika Vainio), and laptop/glitch composition (Fennesz, Ryoji Ikeda). From the 2000s onward it became increasingly international in scope, adding Norwegian artists (Jana Winderen, BJNilsen, Deathprod), Icelandic modern classical (Jóhann Jóhannsson, Hildur Guðnadóttir), and in its most recent phase, artists from the Kenyan and Berlin experimental circuits (KMRU, 2024).

The label’s catalogue numbers follow a multi-series structure:

  • TO: — main album series
  • Tone — collaborative, live, and secondary releases
  • T33 — the older sub-series (magazine/compilation heritage)
  • Spire — sacred/spatial works in large architectural venues
  • V33 — vinyl-specific releases
  • Touch Radio — downloadable/streaming releases (some issued on physical formats)

Key Figures

Jon Wozencroft (co-founder, designer, visual author)

Jon Wozencroft (Discogs ID: 430994) co-founded Touch in 1981. He is credited on 367 Touch releases in design and photography roles — the dominant visual presence by an overwhelming margin. His aliases within Touch’s own release history are Touch 33 (ID: 229588) and AER (ID: 74876). He co-authored The Graphic Language of Neville Brody (with Neville Brody) and co-founded the FUSE typographic research project with Brody in 1990, of which he continues to serve as editor. He has also held senior positions at Central St. Martins and the Royal College of Art (Senior Tutor, Communication Art and Design). See Jon Wozencroft for full biography.

Mike Harding (co-founder, label operations)

Mike Harding (2) (Discogs ID: 208363) co-founded Touch and later launched Ash International (1993). His aliases include HM Ambassador UK, Neophyte (2), Pontifex Maximus, Serrata, and Souls On Board. He is a member of Drøne, Freq_Out, The Eternal Chord, The Freq_Out Orchestra, The Spire Ensemble, and ¡IT!. His own URL at mscharding.net and ashinternational.com anchor both the Ash International and Touch operations. Discogs lists him as primary artist on 2 Touch releases (2011–2013), primarily in the context of his ensembles.

Notes on selected roster entries:

  • Biosphere (Geir Jenssen, Norwegian): began on Touch in 2000 with Cirque (TO:46); the label became his primary home for two decades, encompassing Substrata² (2001), Shenzhou (2002), Dropsonde (2005), N-Plants (2011), and collaborative releases with Jon Wozencroft (Substrata 2.1, 2010, TD01). His roster dominance (72 releases) owes partly to reissues, compilations, CDR releases, and vinyl variants.
  • Fennesz (Christian Fennesz, Austrian): Touch since Plus Forty Seven Degrees 56’ 37” Minus Sixteen Degrees 51’ 08” (TO:40, 1999); 63 primary release credits covering Venice (2004), Black Sea (2008), Agora (2019), and the ongoing Fennesz + Sakamoto collaboration series (Cendre, 2007; Flumina, 2011). His most recent Touch release is The Last Days Of May (V33.60, 2026).
  • Philip Jeck: turntable artist; Loopholes (1995) through Stardust (2021, with Faith Coloccia); also the duo album Oxmardyke with Chris Watson (2023).
  • Chris Watson: field recording; see Chris Watson for full discography.
  • Sweet Exorcist: Sheffield bleep-techno duo (Richard H. Kirk / DJ Parrot) — Spirit Guide To Low Tech (T33.13, 1994). The Kirk connection threads back to Cabaret Voltaire and the Western Works network.
  • Jóhann Jóhannsson: Icelandic composer; Englabörn (TO:52, 2002) was the Touch debut; followed by Virðulegu Forsetar (TO:64, 2004). Jóhannsson later achieved wider recognition on Deutsche Grammophon and for film scores.

Note on Ash International: Discogs (and the Ash International Discogs profile itself) makes clear that Ash International is not a sublabel of Touch, but rather a separate label administered by the same organisation. Mike Harding launched Ash International in early 1993, initially with Robin Rimbaud (Scanner) as co-administrator. It focuses on electronic communications, radio emissions, and natural frequencies — a narrower, more concept-driven identity than Touch proper. See Ash International for its own roster and history.

Similarly, OR, The Tapeworm, and other affiliated releases are independently identitied imprints administered by the Touch organisation, not sub-labels.

TouchRadio is a streaming platform (touchradio.org.uk), not a label.

Production and Engineering

Mastering

Two engineers dominate the Touch mastering credits:

  • Denis Blackham (Discogs ID: 294328; real name Denis Blackham) — credited on 192 Touch releases, making him the label’s de facto house mastering engineer over several decades. Also performs as BilBo (3).
  • Jason Goz (Discogs ID: 815905; real name Jason Gosling) — 31 Touch releases, concentrated on vinyl mastering. Also credited under alias “Goz” in other contexts.

Design

Jon Wozencroft handles photography and design across virtually the entire catalogue — 367 credited releases on Touch. The visual approach is consistent: restraint, landscape photography (often bleak or elemental), and typographic economy. This aesthetic coherence over 40+ years is unusually systematic for any independent label.

Scene Connections

Post-Industrial / Early Network (1982–1990)

Touch launched within the same network that produced Industrial Records, L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords, and the Sheffield experimental scene. The Hafler Trio were the anchor act — Chris Watson and Andrew McKenzie had previously operated within Cabaret Voltaire, and the early Touch releases placed the label in dialogue with Doublevision, Fetish Records, and the cassette-trading underground.

Neville Brody contributed typography to several early Touch releases (Touch Travel, 1984; A Thirsty Fish, 1987 — The Hafler Trio). The collaboration with Wozencroft deepened into the Brody Studio (1987), the co-authored monograph The Graphic Language of Neville Brody (soundtrack by Hafler Trio as Kuklos, 1988), and eventually the FUSE typographic project (1990).

Electroacoustic / Field Recording (1993–)

From 1993 the label expanded toward electroacoustic sound art. Chris Watson’s Stepping Into The Dark (1996) is the watershed solo debut. Jana Winderen joined in 2006 with Utvær (Touch Radio 28); her profile grew throughout the 2000s and 2010s. The label developed a strong relationship with Norwegian experimental circles (Biosphere, BJNilsen, Jacob Kirkegaard, Jana Winderen).

Laptop / Digital Experimental (1996–)

Ryoji Ikeda (+/-, 1996), Fennesz (1999–2026), and Mika Vainio (1997–2019) anchored the laptop/digital strand. Fennesz remains the label’s most sustained long-term presence in this area with 63 primary release credits spanning 27 years.

Contemporary Expansion (2010–present)

The label has continued to expand its international scope in recent years: Claire M Singer (Scottish organist/composer, 2016–), Ipek Gorgun (Turkish electroacoustic composer, 2016–2025), Jacaszek (Polish composer, 2014–2025), Youmna Saba (Lebanese composer, 2023), and KMRU (Kenyan/Berlin, 2024). The Natur release (Tone 85) with KMRU — photography and design by Wozencroft — is the most recent example of the label’s curatorial extension beyond its European core.

Open Questions

  • The FUSE project (1990): Discogs does not have it as a label entry. Full publishing run and contributor list beyond Brody and Wozencroft are not traceable from the DB alone.
  • Exact founding date of Touch as a project vs. as a label: Discogs says “founded 1982”; the stub and Wozencroft’s biography note 1981 as the multimedia project start. Web confirmation would clarify.
  • Touch’s relationship to The Tapeworm and OR imprints: administered by the same organisation but independently identitied — full catalogue and editorial distinctions not fully mappable in the DB.
  • Mike Harding’s creative output under aliases (HM Ambassador UK, Pontifex Maximus, Souls On Board, etc.) — not fully explored in the DB.
  • Robin Rimbaud’s (Scanner’s) early role at Ash International (co-administrator, 1993–1995) — his departure to run the Scanner operation is documented in the Ash International Discogs profile but his exact contribution to the Ash International identity is not detailed in the DB.
