Ron Johnson Records

UK independent record label founded in 1983 by Dave Parsons (Discogs: Dave Parsons (20)), then aged 21, and based in Long Eaton, Nottinghamshire. The label ran until 1988, when it went bankrupt — a five-year run that produced one of the most concentrated catalogs of the British DIY post-punk underground. Ron Johnson was the connective tissue between several distinct strands of the mid-1980s UK indie scene: the scratchy Mancunian minimalism of Big Flame, the angular experimentation of A Witness and The Shrubs, the gonzo art-punk of Stump, and international collaborations including a co-release with Dutch anarchist collective The Ex. The catalog number scheme — ZRON for vinyl, RERON and ExRON for specific co-release variants — reflects an improvised, self-taught operation that nonetheless delivered consistently distinctive records. No sublabels; no parent label. The label’s full run spans catalog numbers RON 1 through ZRON 34.

Compilations

Two Ron Johnson compilation releases document the label’s ethos:

  • FLEXRON 1 — Solid Glod (1986): Flexidisc compilation.
  • ZRON 21 — The First After Epiphany (1987): Full compilation released in both UK and Germany pressings. Sleeve artwork and design by Susan De Banke.

Co-Releases and Label Connections

Ron Johnson operated independently but crossed paths with several allied labels:

  • Ex Records — co-released The Ex’s 1936, The Spanish Revolution (ZRON 11 / EX 028/029) and Too Many Cowboys (ZRON 25)
  • ReR Megacorp — Big Flame’s Two Kan Guru issued as RERON 8 (a ReR/Ron Johnson co-release variant)
  • ExRON 16 — the German pressing of Big Flame’s Cubist Pop Manifesto indicates distribution or licensing into Germany

Scene Context

Ron Johnson’s catalog maps directly onto the mid-1980s British DIY underground: scratchy guitar pop with political inflections (Big Flame, A Witness), noise-rock (The Shrubs, Stump), and a strand of wiry art-funk (Twang (2), Mackenzies). The label was not punk in a traditional sense — most acts had absorbed post-punk formalism (short-burst structures, angular rhythms, self-produced aesthetics) while pointing toward what would become the “shambling” or C86 moment. The Ex co-releases placed Ron Johnson in a pan-European anarchist DIY network; the Dale Griffin productions are an outlier that shows the label was willing to reach outside its immediate scene for craft. The label’s bankruptcy in 1988 corresponds almost exactly to when its core acts — Stump (to Ensign/Chrysalis), A Witness (ongoing) — either broke up or moved to larger platforms.
