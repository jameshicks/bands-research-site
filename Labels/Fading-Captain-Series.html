Overview

Fading Captain Series is an Ohio-based independent label founded in 1999, operating primarily as the release vehicle for Robert Pollard’s extensive catalog of solo and side-project recordings. The label’s 36-release catalog is concentrated almost entirely in the 2000s, spanning indie rock, lo-fi, and garage rock, with regular contributors including producer Todd Tobias and collaborator John Shough. Guided By Voices and the Wigtea Collective also appear on the roster, suggesting it served as Pollard’s broader personal imprint during and alongside GBV’s most prolific era.

Notes (web)

Why Fading Captain Series was launched in 1999

The label’s origin was directly tied to Guided By Voices’ transition from Matador Records to TVT Records for the Ric Ocasek-produced Do the Collapse. The sequence:

  1. Immediate trigger — the Do the Collapse limbo. Do the Collapse was recorded roughly a year before it was released; during that waiting period Pollard had already made Kid Marine. The Constant Bleeder’s “Robert Pollard-Mania!” series (Kid Marine instalment) describes it directly: “While Do the Collapse sat in what would be a yearlong state of limbo, Pollard went DIY for Kid Marine.” The label was not pre-planned; it was created to release that one record. theconstantbleeder.com

  2. TVT contract secured the ongoing right. Once Pollard secured the TVT deal he negotiated explicit contractual freedom for side releases. The Constant Bleeder “Do the Collapse” instalment quotes: “When Pollard went looking for a label with wider distribution than Matador, he also demanded the freedom to do whatever he wanted on the side. He put it in the contract that he can put out fifty-seven solo records on his own label if he wants and nobody in New York City can tell him to knock it off.” theconstantbleeder.com

  3. Pollard’s own framing (1999 Rolling Stone interview). He described FCS as satisfying a “split personality”: TVT would handle the polished commercial GBV sound while Fading Captain would preserve his love of “lo-fi hissy stuff” and four-track recording. “With TVT I get to satisfy both urges.” robertpollard.net

  4. No long-term plan at founding. The Constant Bleeder’s Crickets instalment (2025) adds that the series name was initially “a joke” with no expectation it would continue: “There were no plans for it as an ongoing thing, but that changed the moment that Pollard scored a deal with TVT Records for Guided by Voices that allowed him to do whatever he wanted on the side.” theconstantbleeder.com

Summary: FCS was not conceived as a long-term solo-overflow vehicle — it was created ad hoc to release Kid Marine while Do the Collapse was stuck in label limbo. The TVT contract (which replaced the Matador arrangement) then formalised Pollard’s right to release unlimited solo material independently, turning a one-off into an ongoing series. The label change from Matador to TVT is thus the direct structural cause, with the production delay on Do the Collapse as the immediate practical trigger.
