Radar Records

British punk and new wave independent label, active 1978–1981 (original run), revived 1995–1998. Founded in late 1977 by Martin Davis (managing director) and Andrew Lauder (A&R / label head), funded and distributed by WEA Records. Discogs label ID: 184057 (listed as “Radar Records (5)”; parent entity: Radarscope Records Ltd., ID 264702).

Label code: LC 5454 / LC 05454. Address: 60 Parker Street, London WC2B 5PZ (1978–79).

Founders and Key Executives

Andrew Lauder (Discogs ID: 1477914) — Previously MD of the UK divisions of Liberty Records and United Artists Records. Co-founded Radar with Martin Davis, then went on to co-found Demon Records and found Edsel Records and Silvertone Records. Born 22 May 1947; died 26 November 2025 in Seillans, France.

Martin Davis (Discogs ID: 269747) — Label executive, publisher, music and TV producer. Previously worked for the UA/Liberty UK division. Co-founder of Radar 1977. Later chairman of an unidentified entity (l282094) in the 1980s; founding member of the British Phonographic Industry Association.

Jake Riviera (Discogs ID: 755945) — Manager of both Elvis Costello and Nick Lowe. Had co-founded Stiff Records with Dave Robinson (3) in 1976, then departed to collaborate with Radar. When Radar collapsed at end of 1979, Riviera led the formation of F-Beat Records alongside Costello, Lowe, and Lauder.

Structure and Distribution

Radar was fully funded and distributed by WEA (Warner/Elektra/Atlantic), via the parent holding company Radarscope Records Ltd. — itself a WEA subsidiary. This gave the label major-label distribution reach while operating as an indie. The arrangement also created WEA’s expectation of commercial performance that Radar ultimately could not meet across its full roster.

Sublabel: Radarscope Records (ID 164161) — used for some releases in the revival era.

The two main catalog series in the original run:

  • ADA — singles (e.g. ADA 1, ADA 29, ADA 35)
  • RAD — albums (e.g. RAD 1, RAD 3, RAD 14, RAD 20)
  • RDR — used for a small number of compilation/special releases

Original Run, 1978–1981

Launch and Anchor Acts

Radar’s first releases appeared in early 1978. The label immediately signed Elvis Costello and Nick Lowe — both managed by Jake Riviera — as its commercial anchors.

Nick Lowe delivered Radar’s first single: “I Love The Sound Of Breaking Glass” (ADA 1, 1978), which reached the UK top 20. His debut album Jesus Of Cool (RAD 1 / RAD 56 466, 1978) — produced by Lowe himself — was the label’s first major LP. Labour Of Lust (RAD 21, 1979) followed with the crossover hit “Cruel To Be Kind” (ADA 43). Lowe was also the dominant in-house producer, overseeing records by Elvis Costello and others.

Elvis Costello (solo) and Elvis Costello & The Attractions supplied Radar’s biggest catalog. Key releases:

Barney Bubbles (real name Colin Fulcher, Discogs ID: 1651454) served as primary visual designer for the label’s most prominent releases, including the Costello and Lowe sleeves. He worked as “Assorted Images” or under various aliases; one Discogs alias is literally listed as “Radar Records (2).” His post-punk sleeve work for Radar is among the most cited visual design of the era. Collaborator Malcolm Garrett also contributed sleeve design across 46 Radar releases.

American Imports / Licensed Acts

Three acts were signed via a licensing deal with US Polydor, giving them UK/European Radar releases:

  • The Inmates (2) — pub-rock/R&B outfit; singles “Dirty Water” (ADA 44, 1979), “The Walk” (ADA 47, 1979), “Shot In The Dark” (RAD 28, 1980). Their most commercially successful Radar releases came in 1979–81.
  • Bram Tchaikovsky — UK new wave; “Girl Of My Dreams” (ADA 28, 1979), Strange Man, Changed Man (RAD 56 622, 1979).
  • Yachts — Liverpool new wave; singles “Look Back In Love (Not In Anger)” (ADA 23, 1978), “Yachting Types,” the LP Yachts (RAD 19, 1979), and the compilation-style Without Radar (RAD 27, 1980).

Post-Punk and Avant-Garde Acts

Radar’s more adventurous signings were commercially marginal but historically significant:

Pere Ubu — Datapanik In The Year Zero (RDR 1, 1978) was Radar’s first LP, a compilation of the Cleveland band’s early Hearthan singles. Radar provided their first proper UK distribution.

Red Krayola — Houston art-rock outfit led by Mayo Thompson. Radar released UK editions of The Parable Of Arable Land (RAD 12, 1978), God Bless The Red Krayola And All Who Sail With It (RAD 16, 1979), and Soldier-Talk (RAD 18, 1979). Mayo Thompson was in orbit around the Rough Trade world simultaneously.

The Soft Boys — Cambridge band fronted by Robyn Hitchcock; single “(I Want To Be An) Anglepoise Lamp” (ADA 8, 1978).

The Pop Group — Bristol post-punk/funk outfit. Radar released:

  • “She Is Beyond Good And Evil” (ADA 29, 1979) — produced (co-produced) by Dennis Bovell
  • Y (RAD 20, 1979) — the band’s debut album, also produced by Dennis Bovell

The Pop Group subsequently moved to Rough Trade and Y Records (run by their manager Disc O’Dell) for subsequent releases. The Radar-era records represent the band’s official debut on a distributed label. There is no evidence in the Discogs database of a Radar deal being dissolved before releases — the two records both appeared.

La Düsseldorf — German krautrock/new wave; UK editions of La Düsseldorf (RAD 7, 1978) and Viva (RAD 10, 1978).

Richard Hell & The Voidoids — “The Kid With The Replaceable Head” (ADA 30, 1979).

Rockpile — Dave Edmunds / Nick Lowe supergroup; one Radar release.

Visage — “Tar” (ADA 48, 1979), a pre-fame single featuring a remarkable lineup: Steve Strange (vocals), Midge Ure, Rusty Egan, Barry Adamson, Billy Currie, Dave Formula, and John McGeoch. Their debut single predates Visage’s commercial breakthrough on Polydor.

Tanz Der Youth — “I’m Sorry, I’m Sorry” (ADA 19, 1978).

Wayne Kramer — Former MC5 guitarist; “The Harder They Come” (ADA 41, 1979).

13th Floor Elevators and The Electric Prunes — Radar licensed and reissued mid-1960s US psychedelic acts, including The Psychedelic Sounds Of The 13th Floor Elevators (RAD 13, 1978) and Easter Everywhere (RAD 15, 1979), giving British audiences their first widely distributed access to this material.

Loudon Wainwright III — A Live One (RAD 56 711, 1979).

Clive Langer & The Boxes — “I Want The Whole World” (RDR 2, 1979). Clive Langer went on to be a key producer at Stiff.

Iggy Pop / James Williamson — Kill City (ADA 4, 1978) — a 1975 session released by Radar in the UK, with James Williamson producing and mixing.

Métal Urbain — French punk band; one Radar single (1979).

Collapse, 1979–1981

Most of the non-Costello/Lowe Radar releases failed to meet WEA’s commercial thresholds. At the end of 1979, Radar’s staff was dismissed. Costello, Lowe, Riviera, and Lauder regrouped and formed F-Beat Records (still distributed by WEA). WEA retained the Radar name and issued a handful of further records through early 1981 — primarily continuing The Inmates releases (through ADA 63, 1981) and Bram Tchaikovsky singles.

Revival Era, 1995–1998

Radar was briefly revived as a semi-indie under Rob Collins (4) and Graeme Beattie. The revival used “SCAN” prefixed catalog numbers and operated largely as a Scottish indie imprint. Roster included:

  • The Delgados — “The Lazarwalker E.P.” (SCANCS07, 1995) — early release by the Glasgow band
  • Pure Morning — precursor band to Clinic (per the label’s own profile note); multiple singles 1995–96
  • Midget (4) — “TINY” catalog series, multiple singles and albums 1997–98
  • Prolapse — Leicester post-punk act; several singles and The Italian Flag (SCANCD25, 1997)
  • Unsophisticates, Heave, Summit — further Scottish indie acts

The revival era has no significant overlap with the original 1978–81 roster or ethos and is best understood as a separate commercial venture using the Radar name.

Open Questions

  • The exact terms of the WEA/Radarscope commercial arrangement (sales thresholds, ownership split) are not documented in Discogs.
  • Whether The Pop Group’s Radar deal was an accident of timing (they were simply the next act to be signed before collapse) or a conscious curatorial choice by Lauder/Davis is not resolvable from Discogs alone.
  • The specific departure date of Jake Riviera from Stiff and the founding timeline of Radar (late 1977 vs. early 1978) could benefit from web/press corroboration.
  • Andrew Lauder’s precise A&R role at UA/Liberty UK that informed his Radar curation (psychedelic reissues, avant-garde acts) is referenced in his Discogs profile but details are not in the DB.
  • The Rob Collins and Graeme Beattie who revived the label in 1995 — whether they are the Rob Collins of The Charlatans (died 1996) or a different person — cannot be confirmed from Discogs alone.
