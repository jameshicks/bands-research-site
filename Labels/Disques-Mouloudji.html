Overview

Disques Mouloudji was a small French independent record label founded and operated by — or closely associated with — the chanson singer and actor Mouloudji (Marcel André Mouloudji, 1922–1994), one of the defining figures of postwar French chanson. The label served primarily as a vehicle for Mouloudji’s own extensive recording activity, releasing dozens of his singles and LPs through the mid-1960s to mid-1970s.

Within that context of chanson-centered output, Disques Mouloudji also issued two records that became founding documents of French free jazz: François Tusques’s debut LP Free Jazz (1965) and the co-led Le Nouveau Jazz (1967) with Barney Wilen. These releases sit outside the label’s mainstream chanson output and reflect the label’s brief, unusual engagement with the Parisian avant-garde.

Beyond jazz, the label released a cluster of experimental and literary spoken-word records in 1966 — most significantly Poésie De Cruauté (EMZ 13514), produced by electronic composer André Almuro with texts by Antonin Artaud, Jean Genet, Federico García Lorca, Jean Cocteau, and Olivier Larronde; and Le Condamné À Mort (EMZ 13518), Almuro’s setting of Genet’s long poem read by Mouloudji. These suggest a wider avant-garde program in 1965–1967.

The catalog numbering follows two main series: EM 12xxx M (mono 7-inch and LP singles), EMZ 13xxx / EM 13xxx S (LP series), and DNX 11xxx (a secondary series). The “S” suffix in the jazz catalog numbers likely denotes stereo pressings.

Key Releases: Free Jazz Documents

Free Jazz (1965) — EM 13507 S

Discogs release ID: 2033365 · Master ID: 239003

François Tusques’s debut LP, one of the first explicitly free jazz records made in Europe. Recorded in Paris, released on Disques Mouloudji. Four tracks across two sides, with extended improvisations in the Ornette Coleman / Albert Ayler mold filtered through a Surrealist visual/literary sensibility (track titles invoke desolate landscapes and Paris landmarks).

Le Nouveau Jazz (1967) — EM 13 517 S

Discogs release ID: 6144550 · Master ID: 768284
Format: LP, Mono

Co-led by Tusques and Barney Wilen, this album represents a step toward a more structured free jazz idiom while retaining harmonic freedom. Wilen appears on only three of the six tracks (the A3 and full B side), with two solo Tusques tracks opening the record. The bass chair was shared between Beb Guerin (solo tracks and A3–B) and J.-F. Jenny-Clark (A3–B). Liner notes by writer Yves Buin.

Connections

  • François Tusques — primary free jazz artist on the label; both his Mouloudji-era LPs are key documents
  • Barney Wilen — co-lead on Le Nouveau Jazz; also worked with Tusques on the Jean Rollin film score the same year
  • Michel Portal — bass clarinet on Free Jazz; both Portal and Vitet went on to lead major European improv formations
  • Bernard Vitet — trumpet on Free Jazz; later in Globe Unity Orchestra and Un Drame Musical Instantané
  • François Jeanneau — saxophone/flute on Free Jazz; also later in Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood of Breath
  • Beb Guerin — contrabass on both Tusques records; Tusques’s lifelong primary bassist
  • J.-F. Jenny-Clark — contrabass on Le Nouveau Jazz
  • Aldo Romano — drums on Le Nouveau Jazz; one of the central figures of French jazz drumming
  • Colette Magny — art direction on Free Jazz; politically engaged chanson singer; the link between Mouloudji’s chanson world and the free jazz circle
  • André Almuro — electronic/musique concrète composer; responsible for the label’s experimental poetry/concrète releases in 1966
  • Mouloudji — the label’s founder/proprietor; chanson singer and actor, most active on the label as primary artist

Open Questions

  • Who exactly founded and managed Disques Mouloudji — was Mouloudji himself the label owner/director, or did he license his name to a separate entity?
  • The catalog numbering (EM / EMZ / DNX series) suggests possible distribution through a larger company — the BIEM rights society notation appears on several records; parent distributor unconfirmed
  • Precise dates when the label stopped operating — the latest confirmed year in the Discogs catalog is 1975 (Bachir Touré), but some releases have no year
  • Whether the André Almuro experimental series (1966) was a deliberate program or coincidental to Mouloudji’s personal network connections
  • Any documented relationship between the Tusques/free jazz bookings and Mouloudji himself — did he personally commission these records?
  • The Graeme Allwright debut LP (EM 13506 S, 1965) sits adjacent to Free Jazz in catalog numbering — whether this is coincidence or indicates a deliberate “new music” push in 1965 is unclear
