Food

Discogs label ID: 6124 Parent: Food Ltd. (ID: 292643) → Parlophone (ID: 563997) Sublabels: Imminent (ID: 489541), Incoherent Records (ID: 36773) Founded: c.1984–85, London Founders: David Balfe and Andy Ross Active: 1984–2000 (EMI took full control 1994; label folded c.2000, absorbed into Parlophone)

UK independent label set up by David Balfe (ex-The Teardrop Explodes, ex-Zoo Records) and Andy Ross. Best known for signing Blur and Jesus Jones and launching several key strands of British alternative music from the late 1980s through the Britpop era. Distributed via Rough Trade initially, then WEA licensing, before being absorbed fully into EMI/Parlophone by 1994. The label’s catalog generated six number-one albums and over 120 consecutive Top 100 singles during its peak years.

History

Origins (1984–1988)

Food was established in London by David Balfe following the dissolution of The Teardrop Explodes, drawing on his earlier experience running Zoo Records in Liverpool. The earliest releases carried the SNAK catalog prefix; Brilliant — a group featuring Youth (later of Killing Joke), Jimmy Cauty (later of The KLF), and June Montana — was among the first acts through the door, releasing several singles on the label in 1984–86, produced variously by Chas Jankel and Stock Aitken Waterman. The Imminent sublabel functioned as a sampler series (four volumes: Imminent Episode One through Imminent 4, 1985–86).

Early signings established the label’s eclectic but guitar-forward sensibility: The Woodentops (Plenty, SNAK 2, 1984), Zodiac Mindwarp And The Love Reaction (1985–86), Voice Of The Beehive (1987), and Diesel Park West (from 1987). The hardcore-adjacent Crazyhead began their run in 1987 with “Baby Turpentine” (FOOD 10), delivering their debut LP Desert Orchid (FOODLP 1) in 1988.

The label had its first genuine breakthrough with Diesel Park West, whose debut LP Shakespeare Alabama (1989) appeared on the FOOD catalog prefix that would define the label’s output into the next decade.

Jesus Jones and the Alternative Dance Wave (1989–1992)

Jesus Jones arrived in 1989 and became Food’s first chart act. Fronted and primarily written by Mike Edwards (who self-produced most recordings), the band’s debut Liquidizer (1989) was produced by Craig Leon for most tracks, with Edwards taking over production across subsequent releases. The second album Doubt (FOOD CD 5, 1991) — engineered by Clive Goddard, mixed by John Fryer, produced by Mike Edwards — broke the band internationally, with “Right Here, Right Now” reaching number one in the US. Perverse (FOOD CD 8, 1993) and Already (FOODCD 22, 1997) followed the diminishing-returns arc typical of the era’s alternative crossover acts.

Jesus Jones’s catalog carried catalog numbers in the 12FOOD / CD FOOD series throughout, with later singles adopting the PERV prefix (The Devil You Know, 12PERV 1, 1992).

Blur and the Britpop Peak (1990–1997)

The label’s most consequential signing was Seymour, whom Andy Ross signed in March 1990. Renamed Blur, they debuted with “She’s So High” (FOOD 26, 1990). Their first album Leisure (FOODCD 6, 1991) was produced across tracks by Stephen Street (tracks 2–5, 9, 10), Mike Thorne (tracks 8, 11, 12), Steve Lovell and Steve Power (track 1), with Blur self-producing one track (track 6).

The band and the label found their footing together with Modern Life Is Rubbish (FOODCD 9, 1993), produced primarily by Stephen Street (tracks 1–7a, 12, 13) with John Smith (4) co-producing later tracks, and engineered throughout by John Smith (4). Parklife (FOODCD 10, 1994) — again produced by Stephen Street, with John Smith (4) engineering — was the commercial apex, reaching number one and anchoring the Britpop moment. Street contributed vintage keyboards, effects, and programming as well as production across all Blur’s major mid-period LPs, and Graham Coxon’s expanding artwork role (sleeve paintings, designs) was already visible by the Parklife era.

The self-titled Blur (FOODCD 19, 1997) maintained Street’s production role through tracks 1–9 and 11–14a, with John Smith (4) engineering. By 13 (1998/99), William Orbit replaced Street as producer, marking a deliberate sonic shift. William Orbit engineered and produced the bulk of 13, with Jason Cox assisting.

The Kick Horns (Simon Clarke (2) on saxophones, Tim Sanders on tenor saxophone, Roddy Lorimer on trumpet, Neil Sidwell on trombone) and the Duke Strings Quartet (John Metcalfe, Louisa Fuller, Rick Koster on strings) are recurrent session credits across Blur’s mid-period releases and appear on dozens of Food catalog entries.

Stylorouge served as the primary design studio across most of Blur’s Food releases (and much of the broader label catalog), credited on Leisure, Modern Life Is Rubbish, Parklife, and many associated singles. Paul Postle, Simon Fowler (2), and Bob Thomas (8)/Brunskill handled band photography across the Blur era.

Post-Balfe Expansion (1994–2000)

David Balfe sold his stake to EMI in 1994 (reportedly using the proceeds to buy a farm), leaving Andy Ross as sole director under EMI control. The label continued signing: Shampoo (bubblegum-pop duo, We Are Shampoo FOODCD 12, 1994); Strangelove (Bristol; see below); Dubstar; Octopus (2); The Supernaturals; Grass-Show (Swedish); Idlewild; Matthew Jay; Fungus.

Strangelove (Discogs ID: 92568; Bristol) signed in 1994 and released three albums: Time For The Rest Of Your Life (FOODCD 11, 1994), Love And Other Demons (FOOD CD 15, 1996), and Strangelove (FOODCD 24, 1997). Paul Corkett produced and engineered all three, co-producing with the band throughout. Personnel across the albums: Patrick Duff (vocals/lyrics), Alex Lee (3) (guitar, piano, organ, synth), Julian Pransky-Poole (guitar, piano, Hawaiian steel guitar), Joe Allen (4) (bass), John Langley (drums, backing vocals), Nick Powell (from the third album). Corkett mixed most tracks; Dave Bascombe mixed some tracks on Strangelove (1997). Design by Julian Pransky-Poole (and Greg Jakobek on the third LP).

Dubstar (2) (Newcastle) released Disgraceful (FOODCDX 13, 1995) and subsequent albums on Food. Produced by Stephen Hague (tracks 1, 2, 4, 7–9, 11) and Graeme Robinson (tracks 3, 5, 6, 10) on Disgraceful; Mike “Spike” Drake mixed. Sarah Blackwood (vocals), Steve Hillier (programming, writing), Chris Wilkie (guitar). The Attik Design Limited handled sleeve design; Robert Steel (2) photography.

Idlewild (Edinburgh) arrived in 1998 with “A Film For The Future” (CDFOOD 111) and debuted with Hope Is Important (FOOD CD 28, 1998), following with 100 Broken Windows (FOODCD 32, 2000).

Matthew Jay — final notable signing; released Draw (FOOD CD 34, 2001) with singles on Food through 2001.

Catalog Number Notes

Food used several catalog prefixes across its run:

  • SNAK — earliest singles (1984–87; e.g. SNAK 001, SNAK 2, SNAK 9, SNAK 11)
  • FOOD / 12FOOD / CD FOOD / CDFOOD / CDFOODS — main single series from mid-1980s onward (FOOD 10 = first Crazyhead single, 1987; FOOD 26 = Blur debut, 1990; CDFOOD 100 = label centenary release, 1997)
  • FOODLP / FOODCD / FOODCDX — LP/album series (FOODLP 1 = Crazyhead Desert Orchid; FOODCD 6 = Blur Leisure; FOODCDX 13 = Dubstar Disgraceful)
  • FOOD LP / FOOD CD (with space) — used for some mid-period albums (FOOD CD 5 = Doubt; FOOD CD 15 = Strangelove Love And Other Demons)
  • PERV / 12PERV — Jesus Jones later singles (1992–93)
  • DPW — Diesel Park West dedicated prefix
  • FOODLH — limited or special issues (FOODLH 117 = Blur “Tender”)
  • BLURBOX — Blur anniversary box sets
