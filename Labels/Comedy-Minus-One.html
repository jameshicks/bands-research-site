Overview

Comedy Minus One is an independent record label based in Princeton, New Jersey, founded in 2007 by Jon Solomon, who previously ran My Pal God Records. The name comes from Albert Brooks’s 1973 debut comedy album. Where My Pal God had a broader scope, Comedy Minus One operates with a tighter remit — a small roster of post-hardcore and noise rock acts, alongside distribution for select earlier releases. Its catalogue peaks in the 2010s with fourteen releases and has continued into the 2020s with six more, drawing on a core circle that includes Tim Midgett, Matthew Barnhart, Andy Cohen, Carl Saff, and Mint Mile.

Notes (web)

Why Comedy Minus One was started / My Pal God Records transition

The clearest explanation on record comes from Jon Solomon himself, quoted in a Bank Robber Music article:

“When I heard it [Bottomless Pit’s debut Hammer of the Gods] in full for the first time, I was overwhelmed and saw it as the impetus to launch a new label called Comedy Minus One.”

The context: Silkworm drummer Michael Dahlquist was killed in a car accident in August 2005. Surviving members Tim Midgett and Andy Cohen channelled their grief into new material under the name Bottomless Pit. Solomon heard the finished album and that experience directly prompted him to launch CMO, with Hammer of the Gods (CMO 001, 2007) as its debut release. (bankrobbermusic.com)

No source states an explicit reason why My Pal God Records was wound down rather than simply continuing alongside CMO. The framing across all sources — Discogs profile, Wikipedia, Bank Robber Music — treats CMO as a successor with “a more focused remit,” implying MPG’s broader, more eclectic scope gave way to a tighter post-hardcore / noise rock focus rather than any external event (financial, legal, etc.) forcing the closure. The MPG site’s history page (last updated September 2000) shows the label still active at that point, and its last Discogs-recorded release appears to predate CMO’s 2007 launch; no source bridges that gap with a stated reason.
