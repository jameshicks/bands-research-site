Overview

Gulcher Records is a long-running US independent label founded in Bloomington, Indiana by Bob Richert in 1975, making it one of the earliest small indie labels to combine a fanzine sensibility with actual releases. Its reputation rests largely on the first Gizmos EP, a raw punk artefact that cemented Gulcher’s place in the prehistory of lo-fi DIY culture before the term existed. The catalogue runs to punk, garage rock, and experimental sounds, with the majority of its output — around 18 of 31 releases — concentrated in a 2000s resurgence rather than the original 1970s run. A sublabel, Vulcher Records, extends the imprint, and recurring roster figures include Eddie Flowers, Kenne Highland, and Ted Niemiec.

2000s Revival

The 2000s reactivation was essentially both forces at once, but the decisive act was Richert’s own. In the mid-to-late 1990s, collectors and other labels began tracking him down seeking reissues of Gulcher’s classic material, but nothing materialised from those approaches. The turning point was personal: Richert retrieved the original records and master tapes from his mother’s attic in upstate New York — where they had sat for nearly two decades — and, hearing them again, was struck by how fun and exciting they sounded. He decided to revive the label himself. The Internet then made it practical, allowing him to reconnect with many original participants (including Eddie Flowers, who took on graphics and liner notes). Richert’s background in graphic arts and the availability of PC tools for digitising and mastering recordings made the transition to CD format feasible.

The revival opened with a Gizmos archival compilation in 2000 (1976/1977: The Studio Recordings). It then moved in two directions simultaneously: (a) systematic archival reissues of the original 1976–1981 catalogue, documented with outtakes, demos, live tracks, and liner notes; and (b) signing new artists who had no connection to the original label. By Richert’s own statement in a 2009 interview, Gulcher was “definitely not confined to the bands originally recorded by Gulcher, or the 1976–1981 period.” New signings included Kurt Vile (whose early release Constant Hitmaker appeared on Gulcher in 2008), Magik Markers, Home Blitz, Crawlspace, Tim Carroll, Meercaz, Mykal Xul, and Hypocrite In A Hippy Crypt. Some of the new roster (e.g. Mykal Xul) had connections to the original Gizmos scene; others were new discoveries. By 2009 the label had issued approximately 48 CDs total.

Richert eventually relocated from Indiana to Florida but continued operating the label from there.
