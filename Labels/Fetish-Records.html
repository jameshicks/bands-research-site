The Transatlantic Dimension

Fetish sat at an unusual intersection: UK industrial (TG, 23 Skidoo, Clock DVA, Z’EV) plus NYC downtown no-wave/post-punk (8 Eyed Spy, Bush Tetras, The Bongos). Rod Pearce evidently had strong US scene connections. The Bongos’ Wikipedia article notes they met Throbbing Gristle and Clock DVA through Fetish and performed with them in London.

Distribution and Relationship to Other UK Independents

Co-founder Steve Brown’s Faulty Products background

The label had a latent connection to the wider independent network through its co-founder Steve Brown. Brown was working at Faulty Products — Miles Copeland’s umbrella holding company for Step Forward, Illegal, and Deptford Fun City — handling the distribution side, around May/June 1978, when Genesis P-Orridge contacted him about repressing The Second Annual Report. Brown had previously worked at Charmdale Records and moved to Faulty Products after dealings with Copeland there. Lacking funds (having spent savings on Grateful Dead concerts in Egypt), he brought in Rod Pearce — then involved in the bootleg scene, living in Chiswick — who provided capital and named the label. Fetish was thus born out of a Faulty Products connection, not the Rough Trade/Cartel orbit. [source: https://www.negativeinsight.com/niblog/tgfetishrecords]

No confirmed distribution deal with Rough Trade, Factory, or The Cartel

No web-accessible source documents a formal distribution arrangement between Fetish Records and Rough Trade, Factory Records, Pinnacle, Spartan, or The Cartel co-operative. Wikipedia’s Fetish Records article (as of 2026-02-03) does not mention distribution at all. The Rough Trade Distribution Discogs label page is not searchable for Fetish catalog numbers without login. The Cartel’s founding members — Rough Trade, Red Rhino, Backs, Fast Forward, Nine Mile, Probe, Revolver — do not include Fetish in any documented account. [sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetish_Records (as of 2026-02-03), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cartel_(record_distributor)]

Indie chart position as indirect evidence

Fetish releases charted on the NME Indie Charts — Clock DVA’s Thirst (1981) knocked Adam and the Ants from the top spot; 23 Skidoo’s Seven Songs (February 1982) reached #1. Reaching the NME Indie Chart required retail stocking by indie-friendly outlets, implying some working distribution arrangement, but the specific distributor(s) are not named in any accessed source. [sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirst_(Clock_DVA_album), https://killyourpetpuppy.co.uk/news/23-skidoo-pineapple-products-1980/]

Clock DVA / Rough Trade Publishing (not a Fetish-era connection)

Clock DVA later appeared on the Rough Trade Publishing roster, but this reflects a contemporary (post-Fetish) publishing relationship for catalogue purposes, not a distribution deal during the Fetish period (1978–1983). [source: https://roughtradepublishing.com/artist/clock-dva/]

Physical pressing: Lyntone

Clock DVA’s Thirst was manufactured by Lyntone Recordings Ltd., a common UK pressing plant of the era, not indicative of any label alliance. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirst_(Clock_DVA_album)]

Connection to Dining Out Records

Via 23 Skidoo: 23 Skidoo members helped with sleeve finishing for Dining Out Records before signing to Fetish. The connection between the two labels is generational — Fetish was the next step up for bands in the same social orbit.

Additionally, Neville Brody (Fetish design director) was Tom Heslop’s squat-mate, placing him in the same physical/social world as the Dining Out scene.
