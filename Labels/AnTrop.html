AnTrop

Russian independent record label founded by Андрей Тропилло in Leningrad, initially operating through the state apparatus of Мелодия before emerging as an independent imprint in the late 1980s. One of the earliest independent labels in the Soviet and post-Soviet space, AnTrop was the primary commercial conduit for the Leningrad rock underground — releasing defining albums by Кино, Аквариум, Алиса, Зоопарк, and Телевизор at a pivotal moment when the underground was becoming official.

  • Discogs ID: 32108
  • Founded: active from at least 1981–82 (early tape releases); independent label operations formalised ~1988–1991
  • Based: Leningrad / St. Petersburg, Russia
  • Founder: Андрей Тропилло
  • Contact: Yasin@antrop.net (Ясин Тропилло, Андрей’s son, later manager)

History

The Young Technician Studio and the Underground Era (early 1980s)

Андрей Тропилло (b. 21 March 1951, Leningrad; d. 28 April 2024) ran a recording club housed in the Дом Юного Техника (House of Young Technicians) on the Okhta embankment in Leningrad. Working with minimal, often improvised equipment, he became the de facto house engineer of the Leningrad rock underground, recording bands that had no access to official Soviet studio infrastructure. The earliest AnTrop-associated recordings date to 1981–82 and were circulated as reel-to-reel tapes (магнитиздат) — Кино’s debut 45 (1982) and Зоопарк’s Уездный Город N (1983) both survive in AnTrop catalogue pressings from this era.

This activity predates AnTrop as a formal label — the early credits simply list the studio or engineer. Тропилло’s role was equal parts producer, sound engineer, session musician (flute, backing vocals), and gatekeeper: bands that passed through the Young Technician studio included virtually every significant act from the first decade of the Leningrad Rock Club scene.

Мелодия Co-releases and the Late Soviet Period (1987–1990)

As glasnost loosened restrictions on unofficial music, Тропилло brokered a working arrangement with the state label Мелодия through which AnTrop recordings were pressed and distributed officially. Releases including Аквариум’s Радио Африка (1988), Алиса’s Энергия (1988), Кино’s Ночь (1986), and Телевизор’s Шествие Рыб (1988) carry both the AnTrop and Мелодия catalogue numbers. This dual-credit arrangement allowed the material to reach wide distribution while preserving Тропилло’s authorship over the recordings.

The label’s early catalog number block (АНТС-9XX) marked the transitional moment: releases like Алиса’s Шестой Лесничий (1989, АНТС-904), Трилистник’s Матросская Тишина (1989, АНТС-907), Ноль’s Сказки (Детям) (1989, АНТС-908), and Юрий Морозов’s Красная Тревога (1990, АНТС-909) show the label establishing independent infrastructure.

Mass-Market Phase: Western Catalog Reissues (1991–1994)

From 1991 onward, AnTrop pivoted dramatically toward licensing Western catalog material for Russian release — a pattern common to early post-Soviet independent labels that needed volume to survive. Under the П91/П92/П93 and ATR catalog number series, the label issued dozens of canonical Western rock, blues, and classical titles:

  • The Beatles (68 releases — the label’s single largest artist by volume; titles confirmed: Abbey Road, White Album, Magical Mystery Tour, Help!, Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band, Revolver, Let It Be)
  • Led Zeppelin (confirmed: IV), Pink Floyd (confirmed: Wish You Were Here, Dark Side of the Moon), Deep Purple (confirmed: Stormbringer, П91 00127), The Rolling Stones
  • Bob Dylan (confirmed: Slow Train Coming) (searchingforagem.com)
  • Traveling Wilburys (confirmed: Volume One, П91 00223) (searchingforagem.com)
  • Black Sabbath (listed by Wikipedia on Andrei Tropillo article; specific titles not confirmed)
  • Jesus Christ Superstar (original cast recording) — listed in retail/eBay records
  • Sonic Youth (Нация Мечтателей / Daydream Nation, 1991, П91 00037)
  • The Stooges (multiple titles, 1991)
  • Tom Waits, John Cale / Lou Reed, Ornette Coleman
  • Classical and opera: Wagner, Bach, Verdi, Beethoven, Prokofiev

These releases were produced cheaply — the Discogs profile notes sleeves made from poor-quality paper, sometimes printed on the reverse side of other sleeves or maps, and sleeve designs that consistently differ from the original Western releases. This was characteristic of the early Russian CD and vinyl market’s constrained production economy.

Santa Records (1993–1995): Operational Split, Not Legal Restructuring

In 1993–1994, releases appeared under a parallel imprint, Santa Records (Discogs ID: 41889), which Discogs identifies as a sublabel of AnTrop active during that specific window.

Web research clarifies the nature of this imprint more precisely. By late 1992, label designer Nikolai Kibalchich was handling LP production himself. Тропилло and Kibalchich then discovered that presser Andrei Shendrik had been running unauthorized extra press runs at plants in Tashkent and Riga, profiting without permission and with declining quality control. Тропилло responded by shutting down new AnTrop LP production in spring 1993. Kibalchich then continued operations with Shendrik — but under the Santa Records name rather than AnTrop. The last known Santa Records LP was Paul McCartney’s All the Best (reportedly 1995), after which the market had shifted decisively to CD.

Santa Records was therefore not a legal restructuring of AnTrop as a corporate entity, nor a rebranding by Тропилло himself. It was a continuation by Kibalchich and Shendrik of the physical distribution operation — using press plants and catalog access that had been built under AnTrop — after Тропилло withdrew. Discogs correctly identifies it as a sublabel in the sense that it grew from AnTrop infrastructure, but operationally it functioned independently of Тропилло from spring 1993 onward. (Source: SovietRock, “AnTrop — Soviet Pirate Music Label” — account appears to draw on a 2024 interview with Kibalchich conducted by Artur Netsvetaev.)

Western Licenses: Legal Vacuum, Not Formal Licensing

AnTrop’s Western catalog activity spans two distinct phases with different legal frameworks.

Phase 1 — Melodiya era (1987–1990): the “radio broadcast” loophole. When Тропилло joined Leningrad Melodiya in 1987 (becoming branch director in 1989), he released Beatles and Led Zeppelin albums exploiting a provision of Soviet copyright law under which recordings credited as being reproduced “from a radio broadcast” were deemed exempt from commercial copyright claims. Records in this phase carried printed disclaimers stating the recording was used for broadcasting from the radio (“записано с радио” or equivalent), and Beatles releases additionally attributed provenance to the collection of Николай Васин (Beatles fan and Leningrad curator Nikolai Vasin), constructing a paper trail of individual collector-to-broadcast sourcing. In practice, the Beatles pressings in this phase were sourced from the 1987–88 CD masters, not from actual radio recordings. No formal license from Apple Records, EMI, or any Western rights holder was obtained; the mechanism was statutory exploitation, not contractual permission. (Sources: Wikipedia — Andrei Tropillo; sovietrock.com — AnTrop Soviet Pirate Music Label; AboutTheBeatles.com.)

Phase 2 — post-Soviet independent operations (1991–spring 1993): legal vacuum. After AnTrop operated independently from ~1991, the Melodiya institutional cover was gone and Russian IP law was in flux following Soviet collapse. Тропилло sourced masters through multiple channels: (a) original Western pressings (dubbed), (b) studio master tapes on professional reels at 38 ips — Warner Communications reportedly supplied a batch of masters for Russian release, (c) a Berlin intermediary firm named Zenzor, which supplied additional material including blues titles such as Zaideko and Solomon Burke recordings. No formal licensing agreement with any named Western rights holder (Apple, Atlantic, Warner, DGC/Geffen, etc.) has been documented in any source found. Тропилло’s stated position was that he routed royalties through Russia’s RAO (Российское авторское общество — the collecting society), but this claim has not been independently verified and RAO confirmation of any AnTrop account has not surfaced. Press run scale was substantial: individual titles reportedly saw runs of 200,000–500,000 copies, and the total across all plants was estimated at tens of millions of records by spring 1993 when LP production ceased. (Source: SovietRock — AnTrop Soviet Pirate Music Label.)

Тропилло’s own framing. In his interview for the BBC Storyville documentary How the Beatles Rocked the Kremlin, Тропилло stated: “I support not copyright but copyleft. Because I’m sure that in Russia we should support musical piracy, because musical piracy was the key to have freedom in Russia, to have free information.” He also invented what he described as “so-called licenses” to nominally legalize the releases within Russia’s transitional legal environment. (Sources: Wikipedia — Andrei Tropillo; Unearthing the Music — Andrei Tropillo.)

For Sonic Youth’s Нация Мечтателей (Daydream Nation) specifically (cat. П91 00037, 1991): the Russian pressing is an abridged single LP (8 tracks) with a sleeve that substitutes a different image for the original Gerhard Richter painting used on the Western release. Тропилло later cited this pressing — its appearance in premium Western collections and shops — as evidence that the releases had some legitimacy, but this is Тропилло’s own framing. No independent documentation of a formal license from DGC Records or Geffen for this specific title has been found. Daydream Nation was originally released on Enigma/Blast First in 1988; by 1991 Sonic Youth had signed to DGC/Geffen, and no sub-licensing arrangement with AnTrop for the Russian territory has been verified. The pressing should be treated as operating under the same unlicensed-but-tolerated framework as the rest of the AnTrop Western catalog of that period. (Sources: SovietRock; Afisha Volna interview with Тропилло; Russian Wikipedia on Тропилло as of 2026-06-08.)

Later Period and Legacy (1995–2025)

From the mid-1990s onward, AnTrop continued releasing Russian rock catalog — reissues and archival material — while also publishing new recordings by St. Petersburg-area artists. Notable later roster acts include Облачный Край (10 releases, 1999–2008), Вадим Курылев (4 releases, 2001–2003), and Корпус 2 (4 releases, 2001–2008). The label also released tribute albums to Борис Гребенщиков and compilations (Антропология, 2002; Антропология 2, 2004).

Тропилло himself released music under his own name on AnTrop as recently as 2025. A posthumous double album, Прекрасная Франшисса (2CD, cat. ATR 25998/9), was released by AnTrop in 2025, compiling Тропилло’s vocal recordings including Beatles covers. The Kommersant review (2025) and retail listings confirm this was a formal AnTrop release, indicating the label remained operationally active after Тропилло’s death on 28 April 2024. Who is running the label at this point — whether Ясин Тропилло or another party — has not been confirmed in any source found. (Sources: Kommersant review of Прекрасная Франшисса; Ozon retail listing.)

A late-career project, Андрей Тропилло И Новый Зоопарк (Discogs ID: 5389889), released three records on the label between 2015 and 2019.

Conflict — Ясин Тропилло’s relationship to Андрей: The existing vault entry (sourced from Discogs contact field “Yasin@antrop.net”) describes Ясин as Андрей’s son. Multiple Russian-language sources — including the 24СМИ biography and the Afisha Volna interview with Тропилло himself — consistently describe Ясин as Андрей’s nephew (племянник), not his son. Тропилло’s known children include daughter Polina (confirmed in multiple 2024 obituary sources). The Discogs contact field does not specify the relationship. Web sources are unanimous that Ясин is a nephew. The “son” attribution in this vault should be treated as an error. (Sources: 24СМИ biography; Afisha Volna interview; SovietRock on Yasin Tropillo.)

Scene Connections

AnTrop is the single most important archival label for the Leningrad Rock Club scene (est. 1981). The club’s founding acts — Аквариум, Кино, Алиса, Зоопарк, Телевизор, Странные Игры — all appear in the AnTrop catalog with their core studio recordings. Тропилло’s Young Technician studio was the technical backbone of this scene before any official infrastructure existed.

Key musicians who recur across AnTrop recordings as session contributors:

  • Борис Гребенщиков (Аквариум) — produced 45, played on Начальник Камчатки, credited on 21 releases
  • Сергей Курёхин — keyboards on Кино’s Начальник Камчатки, Алиса’s Энергия, Аквариум’s Радио Африка; 22 credits
  • Александр Титов — bass on Кино and Аквариум sessions; 29 credits
  • Всеволод Гаккель — cello on Аквариум and Кино recordings; 21 credits
  • Игорь Бутман — saxophone on Кино recordings; 24 credits
  • Виктор Цой — songwriter/vocalist, Кино; 19 writer credits
  • Константин Кинчев — Алиса vocalist/songwriter; 8 writer credits

The label also issued the compilation Рок На Костях 25+25 (2001, ATR 01031) and two-volume BG tribute Небо И Земля (2003), documenting the scene’s legacy into the 2000s.

Related labels in the Leningrad/St. Petersburg ecosystem: Мелодия (state co-release partner), Moroz Records (distributed later Кино reissues), Отделение ВЫХОД (later Аквариум releases), Feelee, SNC Records.
