Pan (3)

Discogs ID: 132937 Location: Berlin, Germany Founded: 2008 by Bill Kouligas Catalog prefix: PAN Genres: Experimental, Electronic, Avant-garde, Noise, Club Music, Contemporary Classical Website: p-a-n.org Sublabels: Codes (2) (ID: 912407), ENTOPIA (ID: 2664865)

Overview

PAN is a Berlin-based record label founded in 2008 by Bill Kouligas, a Greek-born artist and producer. The label began with an explicitly underground, noise-adjacent orientation — its first three releases were by harsh noise and experimental acts (Astro/Family Battle Snake split, Andy Ortmann, Mark Durgan) pressed in Greece. By 2009 the operation had moved to Germany, and through the early 2010s PAN developed a distinct editorial identity: electroacoustic music, musique concrète, free improvisation, and early-UK-bass-influenced electronic music coexisted under the same roof, united less by genre than by a shared interest in psychoacoustics and timbre.

The label’s most consistent calling card across its catalog is Rashad Becker’s mastering work — Becker appears on approximately 165 of the label’s releases, making him effectively PAN’s in-house mastering engineer and an integral part of the label’s sound identity. Becker also released two albums of his own on PAN (the Traditional Music Of Notional Species series, 2013 and 2016), underscoring the label’s practice of treating its technical collaborators as full artistic voices.

From the mid-2010s onward PAN became a central platform for a loosely affiliated scene of Berlin-based and internationally distributed artists working at the intersection of club music, ambient, and experimental electronics: Lee Gamble, M.E.S.H. (2), Objekt, Eartheater, Amnesia Scanner, Puce Mary, Pan Daijing, and others. The label also remained open to cross-disciplinary figures — visual artist and performance artist Anne Imhof released Faust (2019) on PAN; Mark Leckey collaborated with Hecker on Hecker Leckey Sound Voice Chimera (2015).

Bill Kouligas handles all artwork and design on the majority of PAN releases (credited on ~146 releases), alongside designer Kathryn Politis (credited on ~58 releases). The visual identity — minimal, often typographically led — has been as consistent a marker of the label as its mastering sound.

Founding and Early Catalog (2008–2010)

The label launched in Greece with a split LP between Astro and Family Battle Snake (PAN 1, 2008), followed immediately by releases from noise/experimental figures Andy Ortmann and Mark Durgan. The first years were dominated by LP-format releases in the noise/power-electronics vein, with early catalog entries also including Greek-born sound artist Ilios (Kenrimono, PAN 4, 2009), Swiss noise duo Joke Lanz / Rudolf Eb.er (Akustische Aktion – Zürich 1991, PAN 91, 2009), and Blood Stereo (Your Snakelike King, PAN 5, 2009).

By 2010, PAN had broadened to include the more formal experimental traditions: free-improv guitarist Evan Parker collaborated with noise artist John Wiese (PAN 9, 2010); composer Trevor Wishart’s Fanfare And Contrapunctus / Imago (PAN 12, 2010) brought electroacoustic composition into the catalog; and Keith Fullerton Whitman appeared with Disingenuity B/W Disingenuousness (PAN 13, 2010).

Consolidation and International Profile (2011–2014)

2011–2012 saw a surge of releases and a clear broadening of aesthetic scope. Key developments:

  • Electroacoustic / acousmatic: Valerio Tricoli and Thomas Ankersmit’s Forma II (PAN 16, 2011); Tricoli’s Miseri Lares (PAN 44, 2014)
  • Noise/harsh electronics: Hecker’s Sun Pandämonium (PAN 15, 2011); Kevin Drumm / Mika Vainio / Axel Dörner / Lucio Capece on Venexia (PAN 28, 2012)
  • UK-derived club/electronic: Lee Gamble’s Diversions 1994–1996 (PAN 33, 2012) established PAN as a home for a forensic, deconstructed approach to rave music. Gamble continued with Dutch Tvashar Plumes (PAN 36, 2012) and Koch (PAN 59, 2014)
  • Helm (2): Luke Younger’s Helm project released five times on PAN between 2012 and 2019, becoming one of the label’s defining long-term voices (Impossible Symmetry, PAN 27, 2012; Olympic Mess, PAN 63, 2015; Chemical Flowers, PAN 99, 2019)
  • Rashad Becker as artist: Traditional Music Of Notional Species Vol. I (PAN 34, 2013) — a landmark release of computer-generated musical entities, featuring no additional credited collaborators; Becker’s sole authorship of this material was a significant statement. Volume II followed in 2016 (PAN 74)
  • Improv: Free improvisation continued with SND/NHK (PAN 26, 2012); Aaron Dilloway / Jason Lescalleet (PAN 30, 2012); Marginal Consort’s INSTAL. Glasgow 2008 (PAN 25, 2013)

Club Music, Cross-Genre Expansion (2015–2018)

The mid-2010s brought PAN into active dialogue with Berlin’s club scenes without abandoning experimental rigor:

  • M.E.S.H. (2) — Scythians EP (PAN 52, 2014), Piteous Gate (PAN 66, 2015), Hesaitix (PAN 82, 2017)
  • Objekt — Flatland (PAN 60, 2014), Cocoon Crush (PAN 97, 2018)
  • Errorsmith — Superlative Fatigue (PAN 81, 2017)
  • Yves Tumor — Serpent Music (PAN 73, 2016), an early release before the artist’s wider recognition
  • Puce Mary — The Drought (PAN 87, 2018), mastered by Rashad Becker; photography by Torbjørn Rødland
  • Eartheater — IRISIRI (PAN 86, 2018), first in a long run of releases; Eartheater became PAN’s highest-volume artist (20 releases through 2025)

The Visionist release Safe (PAN 67, 2015) and ADR (3)‘s Deceptionista (PAN 68, 2015) extended PAN toward UK grime-adjacent electronics.

Current Era and Cross-Disciplinary Reach (2019–present)

From 2019 onward PAN moved into more explicit cross-disciplinary territory:

  • Anne Imhof — Faust (PAN 98, 2019): tie-in to Imhof’s performance/installation Faust at the German Pavilion, Venice Biennale 2017. Mastered by Rashad Becker; design by Zak Group; mixed by Ville Haimala and Nanni Johansson
  • Arca (4) — &&&&& (PAN 100 LP, 2020): artwork by Jesse Kanda; catalog milestone for the label
  • Amnesia Scanner — the duo (with Bill Kouligas as co-credited producer on Lexachast, PAN 83, 2019) became one of the label’s most prominent acts through releases including Another Life (PAN 96, 2018) and Tearless (PAN 108, 2020)
  • Pan Daijing — Lack 惊蛰 (PAN 79, 2017), Jade 玉观音 (PAN 113, 2021), Tissues (PAN 118, 2022)
  • Beatrice Dillon — Workaround (PAN 106, 2020), a significant release connecting PAN to a sophisticated rhythm-composition practice
  • Marina Herlop — Pripyat (PAN 128, 2022), Nekkuja (2023)
  • Honour (3) — a prolific recent addition (8 releases, 2022–2024), including the HBK Volume series
  • Lucy Railton / Peter Zinovieff — RFG Inventions for Cello and Computer (PAN90-COL, 2020)

Scene Connections

PAN sits at the intersection of several overlapping scenes:

  • Berlin experimental electronics / club: PAN is closely associated with the post-Berghain experimental dance scene — Objekt, M.E.S.H., Errorsmith, Amnesia Scanner all have roots in Berlin’s club infrastructure
  • UK deconstructed rave / post-grime: Lee Gamble, Visionist, Helm (2) connect PAN to a UK tradition of using rave materials as raw material for abstract composition
  • Electroacoustic / acousmatic tradition: Trevor Wishart, Valerio Tricoli, Thomas Ankersmit, Rashad Becker place PAN in dialogue with academic electroacoustic music
  • Berlin mastering scene: Rashad Becker’s centrality connects PAN to Ostgut Ton, Raster-Noton, and the broader ecosystem of high-resolution electronic mastering in Berlin
  • Cross-disciplinary art: Anne Imhof, Mark Leckey, Pan Daijing (also a visual/performance artist) push PAN toward gallery/institutional art contexts
