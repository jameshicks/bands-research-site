Piano (Label)

UK independent experimental music label. Discogs label ID: 18854. Parent entity: Piano Records (2) (Discogs ID: 477671, the ℗/© and “Produced For” credit entity associated with the same operation).

Overview

Piano was started in 1976 by David Cunningham, initially as an outlet for his first album of experimental systems music, Grey Scale (Piano 001, 1977). The label’s stated focus was “hybrids and minority music” with a tendency toward releases with connections to the visual arts and performance. Cunningham ran the label from Tyne & Wear (PO Box 50, Houghton-le-Spring) and operated it himself, sometimes routing releases onto other labels when distribution and administration demands exceeded what the imprint could handle.

The first phase ran from 1977 to approximately 1986, producing around eight catalog numbers including the landmark This Heat self-titled LP (THIS-1, 1979), two albums by Michael Nyman (the UK SHEET-1 pressing and the Japan VIP-6798 pressing, both 1981-82), and a 1980 batch of releases documenting Cunningham’s broader circle. The label then went dormant.

In the mid-1990s Cunningham relaunched Piano with distribution through Voiceprint (facilitated by Rob Ayling), using the opportunity to release both new material and archival recordings. The revival catalog begins at PIANO 501 and runs to at least Piano 511 by 2000. The 1996-2000 batch is the denser of the two eras in terms of releases per year.

A 2023 reissue of Grey Scale on Superior Viaduct (cat. SV175) also carries the Piano 001 catalog number — Cunningham’s label credit persists on the reissue.

Key Personnel Across the Catalog

Jon Wozencroft is the most consistent design presence on Piano after Cunningham himself, credited on twelve releases (cover artwork, design, and photography). He is the primary visual identity of the revival-era catalog. David Cunningham himself handled design on several first-phase releases.

Engineers / producers recurring across the label:

  • David Cunningham — produced 27 of 33 DB entries; engineered or mixed on five
  • Anthony Moore — co-produced the This Heat LP and subsequent This Heat releases
  • Dave Hunt — mixed/recorded the Nyman LP (SHEET-1) and other releases
  • Tim Young — mastered the Nyman LP and other releases

This Heat LP — Catalog Number Note

The This Heat self-titled debut carries the catalog number THIS-1, not a standard Piano NNN number. Whether THIS-1 was issued as a Piano release, a This Heat self-release, or some hybrid arrangement is not fully resolved by the Discogs data alone — the release is listed under Piano (label ID 18854) in the DB, but the numbering scheme is entirely separate from the Piano NNN sequence. The Discogs profile for the release lists Piano as the label. David Cunningham and Anthony Moore are both credited as producers alongside the band.

Design Identity

Jon Wozencroft (Touch label co-founder) designed virtually all revival-era Piano releases from 1996 onward. His involvement links Piano aesthetically to the Touch label world. For the first phase, design credits are minimal or handled in-house by Cunningham.

Open Questions

  • THIS-1 catalog number status: Is the THIS-1 number a Piano Records imprint number, a This Heat self-issued number, or a Cunningham-assigned number that stands apart from the Piano sequence? Discogs lists it under Piano but the numbering is inconsistent with the rest of the catalog. A copy of the original sleeve’s small print would resolve this.
  • Piano 004–007 gap: Discogs shows Piano 001, 002, 003, then jumps to Piano 008. Were catalog numbers 004–007 planned, announced, or otherwise documented but never released? Or were they assigned to releases that appeared on other labels (per Cunningham’s own description of routing releases elsewhere)?
  • SING-001 numbering: The 1979 Tony Sinden / Alan Baker / Insects release carries catalog number SING-001, not a Piano NNN number. Is SING-001 a separate sub-imprint, or a one-off?
  • Ghost Dance film: The Ghost Dance LP (PIANO 502) is credited as a film score. What film? The Discogs entry does not name the production. A single source (liner notes, interview, or filmography for Cunningham/Muir/Giles) would confirm this.
  • Piano 200 anomaly: Rude Mechanic (Pan Sonic / Hayley Newman / A. David Crawforth) carries catalog number Piano 200, which is entirely out of sequence with both the first-phase (Piano 001–008) and revival-phase (PIANO 501–511) numbering. Was this intentional (a separate sub-series for collaborative or performance-related releases)?
  • Voiceprint distribution deal: Cunningham’s own Discogs profile text mentions Rob Ayling of Voiceprint facilitating distribution for the revival. When did that arrangement end, and did it affect what Piano could release after 2000?
  • Martin Creed / owada: The 1997 owada release (Nothing, Piano 508) includes Martin Creed on guitar/vocals/keyboards. Creed won the Turner Prize in 2001. Is this the same Martin Creed, and was Piano aware of or connected to the art world context around this release?
