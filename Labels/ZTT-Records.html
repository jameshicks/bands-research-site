ZTT Records

Discogs label ID: 159 (display name: ZTT) Founded: 1983, London Founders: Trevor Horn, Paul Morley, Jill Sinclair Parent: ZTT Records Ltd. (Discogs ID 273979), owned by SPZ Group Distributed by: Island Records (1983–1988), WEA (1988–1997), then independent Catalog acquired by Universal Music: December 2017

Overview

ZTT — Zang Tumb Tuum — takes its name from Filippo Tommaso Marinetti’s 1914 Futurist sound poem “Zang Tumb Tumb,” an onomatopoeic evocation of artillery fire. The name also nods to Luigi Russolo’s 1913 manifesto The Art of Noises, from which The Art Of Noise drew their name directly.

Founded in London in 1983 by producer Trevor Horn, NME journalist Paul Morley, and label manager Jill Sinclair (Horn’s partner). The label was built around Horn’s maximalist studio vision — dense layered Fairlight CMI productions at Sarm Studios — and Morley’s conceptualist approach to pop iconography and packaging. ZTT operated as the music imprint of SPZ Group, the Horn/Sinclair umbrella company that also controlled Sarm Studios and Perfect Songs (publishing).

Over 3,500 releases documented in Discogs under label ID 159.

Eras

Island Era (1983–1988): ZTAS / ZTIS / ZTTIQ

The label launched with Into Battle With The Art Of Noise (ZTIS 100, 1983), a 12” EP that established the ZTT aesthetic: Fairlight CMI sample collages, found sound, and studio maximalism. The catalog used prefixes ZTAS (7” singles), ZTIS (12” singles), ZTPS (picture sleeve formats), and ZTTIQ (LPs); distributed by Island Records.

The debut single for Frankie Goes To Hollywood, “Relax” (ZTAS 1, 1983), was banned by BBC Radio 1, which drove it to #1 in the UK. All three FGTH singles in 1984 entered the chart at #1; their double LP Welcome to the Pleasuredome did the same — an unprecedented run. ZTT also released Propaganda’s A Secret Wish (1985) and Grace Jones’s Slave To The Rhythm (1985) during this period.

ZTT purchased Stiff Records in 1987, though Stiff was dissolved shortly after.

WEA Era (1988–1997): ZANG

After distribution shifted to WEA (Warner/Elektra/Atlantic), the catalog moved to the ZANG prefix. ZTT’s second commercial peak came with Seal, whose debut single “Crazy” (ZANG 8, 1990) — produced by Trevor Horn, with a William Orbit remix on the 12” — became a global hit. 808 State signed in 1989; their Ninety ZTT debut was followed by the critically acclaimed ex:el (1991). The label also released MC Tunes’s The North At Its Heights (1990), Shades Of Rhythm, and Shane MacGowan And The Popes (1994–1997).

Independent Era (1998–present)

From 1998 ZTT operated without major-label distribution. Catalog series issued from 2010 onward:

  • Element Series (2010–2019): archival and anthology releases
  • Definition Series (2020–present): commemorative reissues

The full ZTT catalog was acquired by Universal Music in December 2017. The imprint continues to issue archival releases under the Definition and related series.

Pivotal Releases

Into Battle With The Art Of Noise — The Art Of Noise (ZTIS 100, 1983)

The first ZTT release. 12” EP; Fairlight CMI collage aesthetics. Cover features a detail from Jan van Eyck’s Ghent Altarpiece. Written by Dudley / Langan / Jeczalik / Morley / Horn; sleeve design by XLZTT.

”Relax” — Frankie Goes To Hollywood (ZTAS 1, 1983)

FGTH debut 7”; produced by Trevor Horn; written by Holly Johnson, Mark O’Toole, Peter Gill. BBC Radio 1 ban drove it to UK #1. Illustration by Anne Yvonne Gilbert; sleeve by XLZTT.

Welcome to the Pleasuredome — FGTH (ZTT/Island, 1984)

Double LP; produced by Horn and Stephen Lipson. Covers: Bruce Springsteen’s “Born to Run,” Edwin Starr’s “War” (Whitfield-Strong), Burt Bacharach/Hal David’s “San Jose.” Steve Howe (Yes) plays acoustic guitar on “Snatch Of Fury (Stay).” Jean-Paul Gaultier and Bazaar costume design. Anne Dudley string arrangement on “The Power Of Love.” J.J. Jeczalik credited for “Software, Hardware, The Fifth Dimension.”

Who’s Afraid Of The Art Of Noise — The Art Of Noise (ZTTIQ 2, 1984)

Art of Noise debut LP. Contains “Beat Box (Diversion One)” (8:33), “Close (To The Edit)” (5:37), “Moments In Love” (10:17). Anton Corbijn front cover photography. Mastered at Townhouse.

A Secret Wish — Propaganda (ZTTIQ 3, 1985)

Propaganda debut LP; produced by Stephen Lipson. Covers: “Sorry For Laughing” (Paul Haig / Malcolm Ross of Josef K); “Dream Within A Dream” (lyrics from Edgar Allan Poe). Guests: David Sylvian, Glenn Gregory (Heaven 17), Ian Mosley (Marillion), Steve Howe (Yes), Andy Richards. Photography and sleeve: Anton Corbijn.

Slave To The Rhythm — Grace Jones (IS 206, ZTT, 1985)

Produced by Trevor Horn. Written by Bruce Woolley, Simon Darlow, Stephen Lipson, Trevor Horn. Ian McShane narrated “Blooded.” Front cover by Jean-Paul Goude. The release was packaged as The Strictly Unreasonable Zang Tuum Tumb Big Beat Colossus.

”Crazy” — Seal (ZANG 8 TX, 1990)

Seal debut 12”; produced by Trevor Horn. Remixes by William Orbit (additional production, mix) and Sean Chenery. Marked the start of the ZANG/WEA era and ZTT’s second commercial wave.

Notes

  • Jill Sinclair (1952–2014): co-founder and MD; co-founded Sarm Studios 1973; married Horn 1980; shot in 2006 air rifle accident by her son; died in a vegetative state 2014. No Discogs artist ID.
  • SPZ acronym: Sarm + Perfect Songs + ZTT. Reorganised as a group in 1984. Catalog sold to Universal December 2017.
  • Holly Johnson litigation: ruled in Johnson’s favour February 1988 (contracts = unreasonable restraint of trade; judge: “Mr. Johnson could be 70 years old and still be bound”). ZTT appeal rejected July 1989. Landmark precedent. Johnson went to MCA; Blast (1989, UK #1).
  • Paul Morley “Otto Flake”: pseudonym used mid-1980s for liner notes attacking the post-ZTT Art of Noise on a compilation. Otto Flake was a German Expressionist novelist (1880–1963). Style was deliberately ornate and pseudo-academic. Morley has never explained the choice.
  • Björk on ZTT: not a solo signing — she appeared as featured vocalist on 808 State’s “Ooops” (ZANG 19, 1991).
  • Tori Amos on ZTT: not a solo signing — she guested on Tom Jones’s “I Wanna Get Back With You” (ZANG 64, 1994), mixed by Bob Clearmountain, co-released on Atlantic/Interscope.
