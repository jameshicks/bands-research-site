Darla Records

Discogs ID: 3946

Twitter: @MrDarla

Independent US label, active from 1993 to present (~574 releases in DB). Based in Vista, CA. First release was a Grifters 7” (DRL 001, 1993). Built a catalog spanning noise rock, indie pop, shoegaze-adjacent drone (Bliss Out series), post-rock, ambient, and experimental electronic. Known for launching My Morning Jacket, nurturing Sweet Trip’s long career, and hosting late-career runs for Harold Budd and Robin Guthrie.

No Discogs profile text — founder identity not confirmed from Discogs alone.

Era I — Early Years and Knoxville Scene (1993–1996)

The first release was Grifters “Holmes” (DRL 001, 1993), a Memphis noise rock act. The early catalog had a distinct Knoxville, Tennessee thread: Superdrag debuted on Darla with a 7” (“Señorita,” Darla 002, 1994) before their full break on Arena Rock and then Elektra. 30 Amp Fuse released two early records on Darla (1995), with John Davis (15) (Superdrag’s frontman) playing bass on both.

Memphis also contributed Those Bastard Souls (Twentieth Century Chemical, DRL 011, 1996), produced by Stuart Sikes and Will David; Doug Easley (noted Memphis recording engineer) played bass.

Heartworms (Archie Moore, Trisha Roy, Chris Norborg) provided two of the earliest Darla records (DARLA 003 and DARLA 004, both 1995). Flowchart’s Tenjira (DRL 025, 1996) is an early example of the label’s shift toward ambient and electronic territory.

Era II — The Bliss Out Series (1997–2001)

The Bliss Out series — numbered ambient/drone volumes, primarily appearing on the Bliss Out (2) sublabel — is the label’s most distinctive editorial project. Ran at least through Vol. 17 (2001). Artists ranged from Michigan drone duo Windy & Carl to UK electronic act Amp to the early Sweet Trip. The Darla address in Vista, CA reflected the label’s California-based operation while its roster ranged globally.

My Morning Jacket’s debut The Tennessee Fire (DRL 089, 1999) was their first full-length record. The band (Jim James, J. Glenn, Johnny Quaid, Two Tone Tommy, Patrick Hallahan) self-recorded on 4-track. Follow-up At Dawn (2001) was mixed by Dave Trumfio and mastered by Mike Hagler; MMJ subsequently moved to ATO Records/Universal.

MMJ / Darla origin story: In February 1999 Jim James mailed Darla a handwritten love letter decorated with red foil and hearts, enclosing a 4-track cassette demo. Darla founder (“Mr. Darla”) discovered the tape, played it, and called James to sign the band; he later recalled hoping the debut might sell 1,500 copies. The 20th anniversary reissue (2019) reproduced artwork from that original letter. [source: darla.com product page, https://darla.com/products/my-morning-jacket-the-tennessee-fire-20th-anniversary-edition]

Why MMJ left Darla — web research note (2026): No interview or article found explicitly states why MMJ departed Darla after At Dawn (2001). Multiple sources confirm the timeline — signed to ATO Records in 2002 ahead of It Still Moves (ATO/RCA, 2003) — but none give a direct quote from Jim James or Darla about the reason. Jim James described the ATO/RCA deal as “our first real record deal” (Uproxx, https://uproxx.com/indie/jim-james-reviews-every-my-morning-jacket-album/), framing Darla implicitly as a pre-commercial stepping stone. ATO was co-founded by Dave Matthews and manager Coran Capshaw in 2000 and had distribution through RCA — a substantially larger commercial platform than Darla. The most probable inference is a natural career progression from a small boutique indie to a better-resourced label after the European success of At Dawn drew wider industry attention, but this is inference only: no contemporaneous press or band statement explaining the switch has been located.

Era III — Sweet Trip, European Voices, and Consolidation (2001–2006)

Sweet Trip (Roby Burgos and Valerie Cooper, Bay Area) became the label’s most prolific act. Velocity : Design : Comfort (DRL 136, 2003) — dense electronic/rock hybrid — is their landmark.

Piano Magic (UK, principally Glen Johnson) deepened their label relationship. Disaffected (DRL160, 2005) features John Grant and Angèle David-Guillou as guest vocalists — both would become longer-term Piano Magic members.

Mahogany (2) (New York, Andrew Prinz, Allysa Massais) appeared throughout this period; Prinz also produced Auburn Lull (Alone I Admire, DRL 122, 2002). Auburn Lull (Michigan) is a longstanding Darla act bridging shoegaze and slowcore.

International roster expanded: Mus (Asturias, Spain — Fran Gayo, Mónica Vacas) released multiple records; Entre Ríos (Argentina — Gabriel Lucena, Sebastián Carreras, Isol on vocals) brought South American post-rock to the label; Lali Puna (Munich) appeared; Saloon (UK) released three albums.

Unusual highlight: Crispy Ambulance (Manchester post-punk veterans, originally on Factory Records) returned with The Powder Blind Dream (DRL-138, 2004), produced by Graham Massey (of 808 State).

Era IV — Harold Budd, Robin Guthrie, and Ambient Focus (2006–2014)

Robin Guthrie (co-founder of Cocteau Twins) began an extensive solo run on Darla from 2006, releasing at least a dozen solo albums and several Harold Budd collaborations. Harold Budd (born 1936, LA; died 8 December 2020 of COVID-19 complications) found a prolific late-career home on Darla; his catalog there runs from 2007 through posthumous releases.

Their joint records (After The Night Falls, Before The Day Breaks — both 2007; Bordeaux, 2011; Another Flower, 2020) are among the most significant Budd catalog entries of the period. Harold Budd’s Avalon Sutra (DRL285, 2014) was produced by David Sylvian (with Jon Gibson as co-composer).

Mahogany’s Connectivity! (DRL 173, 2006) was mixed by Robin Guthrie, marking his first Darla connection before his solo run.

Piano Magic’s Ovations (DRL227-1, 2009) featured Brendan Perry and Peter Ulrich (both of Dead Can Dance) as guests.

Japancakes (Athens, GA) released Loveless (2007) — a full instrumental interpretation of My Bloody Valentine’s landmark album.

Era V — Contemporary Period (2015–present)

Continued releases from longstanding acts (Sweet Trip, Harold Budd, Robin Guthrie) alongside newer signings. Kestrels (Chad Peck, NYC) became a contemporary anchor; Sciflyer (shoegazey); Kitty Craft.
