Overview

Polyvinyl Record Co. is an independent label founded in 1996 in Champaign, Illinois by Matt Lunsford (Discogs artist ID: 671728) and Darcie Knight Lunsford. It grew out of the Champaign/Urbana post-hardcore and emo scene — the same milieu that produced Braid, Cap’n Jazz, and American Football — and remained one of the most visible US indie labels into the 2020s. The label is known for a broad stylistic range: Midwest emo and post-rock in its early years, experimental baroque pop (Of Montreal), noise pop and art rock (Deerhoof, Xiu Xiu), indie pop (Alvvays, Kero Kero Bonito, Jay Som), and post-punk/indie rock (Japandroids, Vivian Girls). The catalog on Discogs contains 2,431 releases across all pressings and formats.

History and Key Eras

1995–1998: Champaign Roots

The label’s earliest releases predate its formal 1996 founding — catalog number PVR-003 appears on a 1995 Walker / Back Of Dave split 7”. The first confirmed Polyvinyl era opens with Braid’s I’m Afraid of Everything (catalog: polyvinyl.#4 / number eight) and Rainer Maria’s debut EP (1996), both bands central to the Champaign scene. American Football’s debut EP (American Football, PRC-019 / CDSS001, 1998) and Braid’s landmark Frame & Canvas (PRC-018, 1998) established the label’s early identity: Midwest emo, math rock, and post-hardcore, often produced in-house (Braid self-produced Frame & Canvas with engineer J. Robbins).

The label ran several early sub-series including the Polyvinyl CD Single Series (CDSS001–005) and the 4-Track Singles Series, reflecting its DIY zine-distro roots.

1999–2003: Consolidation and Diversification

Rainer Maria’s Look Now Look Again (PRC-024, 1999) and Hell and High Water (PRC-038, 2000) cemented them as Polyvinyl’s flagship act of this period. Of Montreal’s first Polyvinyl release appeared in 1997 (The Bird Who Continues to Eat the Rabbit’s Flower, PRC-111-2), beginning one of the longest artist-label relationships in modern indie. Owen (4) (Mike Kinsella’s solo project) debuted on Polyvinyl in 2001 and remained on the label for two-plus decades. Mates of State joined in 2002 (Our Constant Concern, PRC-046). The label also briefly carried Burning Airlines (J. Robbins’ post-Jawbreaker project) on the CD Single Series.

2004–2009: Of Montreal’s Baroque Period and New Arrivals

Of Montreal’s rapid creative ascent — Satanic Panic in the Attic (PRC-069, 2004), The Sunlandic Twins (PRC-088, 2005), Hissing Fauna, Are You The Destroyer? (PRC-124, 2007), Skeletal Lamping (PRC-160, 2008) — made them Polyvinyl’s highest-profile act and the label’s most-released artist on Discogs (211 releases). Kevin Barnes, who writes, produces, and engineers all Of Montreal material, became the label’s most-credited producer.

Japandroids signed in 2009 with Post-Nothing (PRC-184). Architecture In Helsinki (Places Like This, PRC-139, 2007) and Asobi Seksu (Hush, PRC-170, 2009) extended the label’s international and experimental reach.

2010–2015: Indie Expansion

This era brought the label’s most commercially successful releases. Japandroids’ Celebration Rock (PRC-238, 2012) was widely acclaimed. Deerhoof joined the roster (from 2010 onward, 75 releases on Discogs). Xiu Xiu’s relationship with Polyvinyl began around 2011–2012 (Always, PRC-236; then back-catalog reissues of Fabulous Muscles, The Air Force, etc.). Starfucker (2) signed around 2010.

Vivian Girls joined in 2011; Someone Still Loves You Boris Yeltsin had been on the label since 2006.

2016–present: Broader Indie Pop and International Artists

Alvvays joined for reissue and new work (2014 onward, 60 Discogs entries). Kero Kero Bonito signed in 2018 (71 Discogs entries). Jay Som (Melina Duterte) joined 2016. Julia Jacklin in 2016. Jeff Rosenstock signed 2018. Laura Jane Grace (post-Against Me!) signed 2020. The Get Up Kids signed 2018. Japandroids remained through their final album cycle (2024). A major reissue program of the early catalog (Rainer Maria, Braid, Of Montreal) runs throughout this period.
