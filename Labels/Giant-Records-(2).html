Overview

Giant Records (2) is a US punk and hardcore label operating under the Giant Records, Inc. parent company, with the bulk of its 44-release catalogue concentrated in the late 1980s. Its roster reads like a who’s-who of the Washington DC hardcore scene: Government Issue and its individual members — vocalist John Stabb Schroeder, guitarist Tom Lyle, drummer Peter Moffett, and bassist J. Robbins — account for most of the label’s credits. The catalogue branches into alternative rock, synth-pop, and garage rock alongside the core punk and hardcore output. Activity tapered sharply after the decade’s turn, with just seven releases carrying into the 1990s. The label also operated a sublabel, Wishingwell Records.

Notes (web)

Founding and Government Issue affiliation

Giant Records was not founded by any Government Issue member. It was created in 1987 as an imprint of Dutch East India Trading, a Long Island, NY-based record distributor and importer run by Barry Tenenbaum. Tenenbaum offered Steev Riccardo (later a Metal Blade Records employee) the role of head of the new label, with full autonomy to sign bands. Riccardo described his brief as making Giant “mostly a punk label.” (Tony Rettman / No Idols — Steev Riccardo interview Pt. I, accessed 2026-11-17)

Government Issue’s connection to the label was initiated by Tenenbaum, not by any GI member: Tenenbaum specifically suggested GI as Giant’s first signing, and their 1987 album You became the label’s debut release. Riccardo built a close working relationship with guitarist Tom Lyle, but neither Lyle nor any other GI member had a founding or ownership role in the label. GI was a roster act, not a founder or affiliate. (same source)

Dutch East India Trading also held a licensing deal with the Belgian label Les Disques Du Crepuscule, which is why non-HC acts (Cabaret Voltaire, Anna Domino, Isabelle Antena, Blaine L. Reininger) appear in Giant’s catalogue alongside the punk/hardcore core. (Freeport Library — Historic Long Island Record Labels & Studios, accessed 2026-11-17)

Decline after 1990

Riccardo departed Giant after roughly two years (c. 1989) when Metal Blade Records offered him a position, and Tenenbaum did not attempt to retain him. At that point virtually all artists were released from their deals — Government Issue being a noted exception. (Tony Rettman / No Idols — Steev Riccardo interview Pt. II, accessed 2026-11-17)

The steeper structural cause of the label’s collapse was Barry Tenenbaum’s behaviour at Dutch East India Trading. Riccardo states Tenenbaum “disappeared off the face of the earth” and “screwed over a lot of people.” Because Tenenbaum held ownership of the masters and the Giant name — Riccardo had no ownership stake — the label became impossible to operate or reissue from. Tenenbaum eventually sold the Giant name alone to a Warner Brothers executive for approximately $75,000, keeping the proceeds. Dutch East India Trading as a whole ceased operations around 2000 following industry consolidation and a royalties dispute (with Uncle Tupelo, among others). (Rettman Pt. II; Freeport Library source above)

The catalogue evidence in Discogs is consistent with this account: releases drop sharply after 1990, with only a handful of titles (and a stray 1998 promo) appearing after the main 1987–1990 run.

Summary: Giant Records (2) was an independent label funded and owned by Dutch East India Trading / Barry Tenenbaum, headed operationally by Steev Riccardo. Government Issue were its anchor signing by Tenenbaum’s suggestion, not label founders or owners. The decline was driven by Riccardo’s departure c. 1989 and Tenenbaum subsequently abandoning the label and disappearing, leaving masters and the company name in limbo until the name was sold to Warner Bros.
