Cause And Effect

US cassette label, active primarily 1982–1988, with one archival release in 2013. Run jointly by Debbie Jaffe (also known as Deborah Jaffe) and Hal McGee out of the Chicago area. Discogs label ID: 12395. Bandcamp: causeandeffectk7.bandcamp.com.

No parent label. No sublabels on Discogs.

Overview

Cause And Effect operated squarely within the 1980s cassette underground — the international network of home-tapers, mail-art practitioners, and experimental musicians who circulated self-released tapes outside any commercial distribution channel. The label’s founders, Debbie Jaffe and Hal McGee, were themselves musicians: Jaffe performed as Master-Slave Relationship (solo) and as part of Viscera with McGee; McGee’s primary performance vehicle was Dog As Master as well as Viscera. This means Cause And Effect was as much a vehicle for its own founders’ output as it was a curatorial imprint.

The catalog spans industrial, experimental electronics, noise, minimal synth, and electro-acoustic improvisation. Genre diversity was part of the label’s character: the same roster that includes harsh noise from Master-Slave Relationship also carries ambient cassettes from Vox Populi! (France), text-driven minimal synth from Algebra Suicide (Chicago), and a Negativland live document. International reach was real — releases from French group Vox Populi!, French industrial act Le Syndicat (split with Pacific 231 (2)), and a Nurse With Wound cassette all appear in the catalog. The two known assigned catalog numbers are CAE 003 (Mirage, 1986 compilation) and CAE 004 (Suburban Nightmare, 1986 compilation); the vast majority of releases carry “none” as their catalog number, consistent with informal mail-order distribution.

The label had a strong Chicago-area core but was not exclusively local. Al Margolis (If, Bwana) was New York-based; John Duncan was operating out of Los Angeles at the time of Dark Market Broadcast; Negativland were from the Bay Area.

One Discogs outlier: a 2013 release by Theatre Of Ice, suggesting the label name was revived or the catalog was backdated for archival purposes decades after the main run.

Founders

Hal McGee (Discogs artist ID: 424754) — also performs as Dog As Master (realname: Hal McGee, confirmed on Discogs), Viscera (with Jaffe), and numerous smaller projects (Army Brats, Burnt Circuits, Bwana Dog, Straphangers’ Art Ensemble, The Concrete Etendue, Swamp Patrol). URLs: haltapes.com, halmcgee.bandcamp.com.

Debbie Jaffe (Discogs artist ID: 202289; realname Deborah Jaffe) — performs as Master-Slave Relationship, co-runs Viscera with McGee. Also credited on liner design and photography for Blue Faced Lust. Discogs aliases: Mipoola Palinga. Groups: Army Brats, Burnt Circuits, Dada Action Group, Swamp Patrol, The Concrete Etendue, Viscera.

Key Artists on the Label

Dog As Master (Discogs ID: 165492) — Hal McGee’s primary performance alias. Most prolific artist on the label with ~20 release appearances. Name variations: DAM, DaM.

Master-Slave Relationship (Discogs ID: 88471) — Debbie Jaffe solo project. ~9 releases. Industrial/electronic. Realname: Deborah Jaffe. Aliases: Debbie Jaffe, Mipoola Palinga. Has own website: masterslaverelationship.com.

Viscera (Discogs ID: 182636) — Debbie Jaffe + Hal McGee duo. ~9 releases. URL: haltapes.com.

If, Bwana (Discogs ID: 94868) — Al Margolis, New York-based. ~6 releases on the label. Aliases: Al Margolis, Sound Of Pig. Also collaborated with Dog As Master as Bwana Dog.

Bwana Dog (Discogs ID: 844925) — collaborative project, If, Bwana + Dog As Master. ~4 releases, 1987–1988.

F-i (Discogs ID: 94864) — Milwaukee experimental/space rock group. Members include Richard Franecki, Jan Schober, Tom Schnier, others. Alias: The Surfin’ Führers.

Algebra Suicide (Discogs ID: 118500) — Chicago minimal synth/spoken word duo: Don Hedeker (guitar, synth) and Lydia Tomkiw (voice, lyrics). One release on the label: Big Skin (1986).

Vox Populi! (Discogs ID: 210045) — French ambient/experimental group. La Cathédrale Morte (1985), their only Cause And Effect release.

Negativland (Discogs ID: 24120) — Bay Area culture-jamming group. Jamcon ‘84 (1986), co-released with their own Seeland label.

Nurse With Wound (Discogs ID: 26101) — UK avant-garde project of Steven Stapleton. Merzbild Schwet (1986).

Compilation Spotlights

Mirage (CAE 003, 1986) — 17-track compilation assembled by Hal McGee. Represents the international reach of the label: tracks from Master/Slave Relationship, Twilight Ritual, Rat (3), Psyclones, F/i, Controlled Bleeding, If, Bwana & Dog As Master, Human Flesh, and others.

Suburban Nightmare (CAE 004, 1986) — 9-track compilation, also produced by Hal McGee. Heavier NYC presence: If, Bwana (3 tracks), Alien Planetscapes, Nomuzic (Al Margolis project), Croiners, Thick Slimy Whisper (featuring Al Margolis, Cheryl Sobas, Paul Richards), Ron Anderson. Ron Anderson contributes a track with alto saxophonist Ken Claire and vocalist Kathy Life.

60 Minutes Of Laughter (1982) — the label’s earliest known release. A large Various Artists compilation featuring Viscera, Debbie Jaffe, Hal McGee, and many short-lived named acts (Trish & The Swishettes, Gabble Ratchet, Army Brats, Burnt Circuits, etc.) — essentially a document of the founders’ immediate local/social circle at the label’s inception.

Open Questions

  • Founding date unclear: Discogs lists the earliest release as 60 Minutes Of Laughter (1982) but label profile says “1980s” without a specific start year. Was 1982 the actual founding year or was there earlier activity?
  • Geographic base: listed as “Chicago-area” but Debbie Jaffe and Hal McGee’s address/city at operation time is not confirmed from Discogs data alone.
  • The 2013 Theatre Of Ice release is unexplained — whether the label was briefly revived, whether the catalog number was issued retrospectively, or whether it is a digitization/reissue under the same name is unknown.
  • No web search has been performed. Bandcamp (causeandeffectk7.bandcamp.com) and any surviving print catalogs or zine coverage (e.g. Op, Sound Choice) could fill gaps in the narrative and founding year.
  • Don Hedeker’s connection to the label beyond Big Skin: Discogs credits him only as a performer on that release, not as a label affiliate. The research brief mentioned him specifically — worth investigating whether there was a closer operational or curatorial relationship.
