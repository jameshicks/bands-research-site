Overview

ché was an independent London label co-founded by Vinita Joshi and Nick Allport in 1991, rising from the wreckage of Cheree Records — their earlier imprint with Paul May that collapsed after financial backers intervened. The label ran for roughly a decade, issuing 66 releases through the 1990s across indie rock, lo-fi, psychedelic rock, and post-rock, with Urusei Yatsura among its most prominent acts. It operated under parent company Ché Trading Limited and ran two sublabels, Ché Trading Limited Presents… and i. When Joshi grew frustrated with the label’s internal direction, she departed to found Rocket Girl, which carried forward a similar aesthetic.

Notes (web) — Vinita Joshi’s departure and founding of Rocket Girl

Two interviews with Joshi give the clearest account. No source names a personal falling-out with Nick Allport; her accounts centre on structural and directional frustration.

Drowned in Sound archive interview (drownedinsound.org): Joshi describes “a combination of reasons.” The immediate trigger was ché receiving funding from Sire Records/Seymour Stein, which brought in Coalition as a UK distribution partner — a mainstream-leaning operation she felt “would [not] understand where we were coming from.” She adds that there were “financial issues and personal stuff that affected my decision” and that she reached a point where she “could no longer put my hand on my heart and tell bands that we were the best label to work with.” She says she had “already decided to start my own label to release a Spacemen 3 tribute album. It made sense to go solo and I was no longer tied down to the constraints of others.”

KLOF Mag interview, Dec 2013 (klofmag.com): Joshi says she “was at Ché at the time, and they didn’t seem interested in the release [the Spacemen 3 tribute] and I really believed in it.” After she left, “Ché was going through so many changes,” and she brought Piano Magic — her first full-time Rocket Girl act — with her after their ché contract ended.

Summary: The departure was driven by ché’s shift in financial backing (Sire/Coalition involvement), Joshi’s loss of confidence in the label’s ability to serve its bands, and a specific creative disagreement over the Spacemen 3 tribute project that became Rocket Girl’s first release. No source documents a personal falling-out with Nick Allport or names him as the source of conflict; Joshi consistently frames it as structural incompatibility rather than an interpersonal dispute. The Discogs profile’s phrase “frustrated with the proceedings at ché” is consistent with her own account but no stronger than that.
