Hydra Head Records

Discogs ID: 25544 Parent: Hydra Head Industries Inc. (335800) Sublabel: Double H Noise Industries (ID 11897) — experimental/noise imprint Active: 1993–2012 (catalog releases continue post-dissolution) Origin: New Mexico (founded) → Boston, Massachusetts → Los Angeles (relocated 2011) Founded by: Aaron Turner (371617) Catalog prefix: HH666-XX

History

New Mexico origins and Boston years (1993–1998)

Founded in 1993 in New Mexico by Aaron Turner, then a high school student, initially as a distribution operation. In 1995, Turner relocated to Boston to attend art school; the same year he was handed a demo by local band Vent (7), whose 7” Long Lost Human (HH666-01, 1995) became the first Hydra Head release. The label grew around the Boston and broader US hardcore/metal underground — early signings included Roswell, Corrin, Piebald, and Converge (who appeared from HH666-07, 1997 onward). Turner graduated college in 1999 and the label became a full-time operation.

The “In These Black Days” Black Sabbath tribute split 7” series (HH666-12 through HH666-17, 1997–1999) — six volumes of split vinyl 7” singles, each featuring two bands covering Black Sabbath. Pairings: Vol. 1 Anal Cunt / EyeHateGod, Vol. 2 Brutal Truth / Converge, Vol. 3 Today Is The Day / Coalesce, Vol. 4 Cable / Cavity / Jesuit / Overcast, Vol. 5 Cave In / Botch, Vol. 6 Neurosis / Soilent Green. A 2013 box set (cat# none) collected all six 7”s together. These releases established HH’s identity as a heavy-network label where scene relationships were the organizing principle.

Peak era and flagship signings (1998–2007)

Cave In (286596, first release 1997) became the label’s primary flagship — 155 Discogs releases across 1997–2025, with Stephen Brodsky (434371) and Caleb Scofield (434370) as the core creative partnership. Key LPs: Until Your Heart Stops (HH666-31, 1998, 23 pressings), Jupiter (HH666-52, 2000, 22 pressings), Tides Of Tomorrow (HHR666-66, 2002, 14 pressings), Perfect Pitch Black (HH666-103, 2005, 10 pressings).

Botch (263605) arrived with American Nervoso (HH666-29, 1998, 17 pressings) and We Are The Romans (HH666-041, 1999, 20 pressings) — the latter widely considered one of the defining mathcore records. An Anthology Of Dead Ends (HH666-63, 2002, 18 pressings) collected rarities after their breakup.

Pelican (2) (335124) debuted with their Untitled EP (HH666-68, 2003) and delivered the label’s most-pressed proper album: The Fire In Our Throats Will Beckon The Thaw (HH666-91, 2005, 20 pressings), followed by City Of Echoes (HH666-124, 2007, 13 pressings) and Australasia (HH666-75, 2003, 18 pressings).

Daughters (514141, Providence RI) released Hell Songs (HH666-111, 2006, 17 pressings) and the self-titled Daughters (HH666-195, 2010, 11 pressings) — their two most-pressed HH albums.

Jesu (233796) — Justin Broadrick’s post-Godflesh project — had 54 releases on HH from 2004–2014, including the debut self-titled (HH666-79, 2004, 10 pressings) and Silver (HH666-110, 2006, 9 pressings).

Old Man Gloom (262038) — a supergroup featuring Aaron Turner alongside Cave In members and others — had 29 HH releases from 2011–2019.

Khanate (214036) — Stephen O’Malley and Alan Dubin’s extreme drone/doom project — contributed 28 releases to the catalog.

Expansion into noise and experimental (2004–2012)

The sublabel Double H Noise Industries (ID 11897) channeled experimental and noise work — Aaron Turner’s own label identity running parallel. Artists like Merzbow, Prurient (27 releases, 2011–2023), Lustmord, Xasthur, and Kayo Dot represented this strand.

Prurient (Dominick Fernow, 27012) became one of the most active HH artists of the later period with 30 releases including Bermuda Drain (HH666-220-5, 2011, 9 pressings).

Relocation and dissolution (2011–2012)

HH relocated from Boston to Los Angeles in 2011. The label ceased operations in 2012. Turner subsequently focused on Mamiffer (with partner Faith Coloccia) and Sumac (with Brian Cook (2) of Russian Circles). The catalog’s legal entity was Hydra Head Industries Inc. (335800). Some catalog reissues continued to appear under the HH name in later years (Cave In, Khanate, Isis reissues through 2018–2019).

Connections

  • Aaron Turner / Isis (6) — Turner ran HH while simultaneously fronting Isis; post-dissolution moved to Mamiffer and Sumac
  • Converge / Jacob Bannon — early HH signing; Bannon did 45 design credits; Bannon also founded Deathwish Inc., an adjacent label
  • Cave In — label’s longest-running flagship; Stephen Brodsky and Caleb Scofield appeared on Old Man Gloom as well
  • Botch — Brian Cook (2) later joined Russian Circles and Sumac; Dave Knudson also did extensive HH design work
  • Mamiffer — Turner + Faith Coloccia; active on HH in its final years
  • Double H Noise Industries — HH’s experimental/noise sublabel
  • Sargent House — parallel management/label relationship with several HH-adjacent bands (Russian Circles, These Arms Are Snakes)
  • Deathwish Inc. — Jacob Bannon’s label; overlapping rosters and aesthetic territory

No, they did not stay on HH after Jupiter. Following Jupiter, Cave In signed to RCA for Antenna (2002) and released Tides Of Tomorrow EP on HH that same year as a stop-gap/parting release.
