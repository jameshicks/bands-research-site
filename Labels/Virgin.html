Virgin

British major record label, founded by Richard Branson in the early 1970s. Started as an independent UK label, became a major player through the 1970s–80s, and was sold to Thorn EMI in 1992. Since 2012 the Virgin imprint has been licensed by various Universal Music Group companies.

Discogs label hierarchy:

  • Virgin (main imprint) — Discogs ID: 750 (parent: Virgin Records Ltd.)
  • Virgin Records Ltd. — Discogs ID: 26760 (parent: EMI Group Ltd.)
  • Virgin Records (sublabel, US-facing) — Discogs ID: 108701
  • Virgin UK — Discogs ID: 31795
  • Virgin America — Discogs ID: 26520

Label Code: LC 3098 / LC 03098

History

Virgin launched in 1972–73 as an independent UK label, initially signing progressive rock and experimental acts through distribution built around the Virgin mail-order and retail chain. The label’s first release and first massive success was Mike Oldfield’s Tubular Bells (1973), which funded the label’s early expansion. The iconic Virgin label design — Roger Dean’s snakes-and-dragons motif — ran through 1974–76 before transitioning through a series of twin-label and then green/red label designs (1978–1987).

Through the mid-1970s, Virgin’s catalog was built primarily around progressive rock, krautrock, and experimental music: Gong, Steve Hillage, Mike Oldfield, Henry Cow, Slapp Happy, Faust, Can (late period), Tangerine Dream. The label also operated the Caroline Records imprint in these years for heavier and more progressive material, though the Discogs record shows Caroline’s parent listed separately under Capitol Music Group (reflecting later ownership changes).

In the late 1970s, Virgin pivoted toward post-punk and new wave. Key signings in this period included Magazine (1978–1981), The Skids (1979–1981), Rip Rig & Panic (1981–1983), and The Human League (1979–). The label’s subsidiary Dindisc was used for new wave acts including OMD and Martha and the Muffins, and Blue Rondo À La Turk (1981–1986).

The 1980s broadened Virgin’s commercial reach considerably. Culture Club (1982–), Simple Minds (1979–), XTC (1977–1999), Scritti Politti (1982–), and The Human League (1979–) became flagship acts. Cabaret Voltaire signed in 1983, releasing their three key Some Bizzare/Virgin albums (The Crackdown, Micro-Phonies, The Covenant, The Sword And The Arm Of The Lord) before departing. Devo had US Virgin releases. Tangerine Dream extended their Virgin run into the 1980s.

Virgin was sold to Thorn EMI on 1 June 1992. Post-acquisition, the label continued operating under its own name while becoming part of the EMI structure. Notable post-acquisition activity in the vault’s range includes American Music Club’s Mercury (1993, see below), Asian Dub Foundation (2000–2007, including Enemy Of The Enemy, 2003), and Smashing Pumpkins.

In the US, Virgin’s activities were routed through Virgin America (ID: 26520) and later Virgin Records, Inc. (ID: 373790). The Vernon Yard Recordings imprint — parent: Virgin Records America, Inc. — was used for indie-adjacent signings including Low (I Could Live In Hope, 1994).

American Music Club — Mercury (1993)

Resolved: Virgin UK/Europe direct release; Reprise Records for US/Canada.

The UK/Europe LP pressing (Discogs release ID: 1864078, cat. no. V 2708) lists only Virgin (label ID 750) — no Reprise co-credit appears on the label or in the credits. The US and Canada pressings (e.g., ID: 367491, 7454113) are on Reprise Records. This is a split arrangement by territory: AMC signed to Reprise (Warner) for North America; Virgin handled UK/Europe independently, not as a license. There is no Reprise co-credit on the UK pressing.

Producer: Mitchell Froom. Engineer/Mixed By: Tchad Blake. Mastered By: Bob Ludwig. Members credited as performers: Mark Eitzel, Bruce Kaphan, Vudi, Tim Mooney, Daniel Pearson.
