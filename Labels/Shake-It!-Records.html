Shake It! Records is a record store and record label located in Cincinnati, Ohio.

The Shake It Records Label was started by Jess Hirbe & Doc Kalmus in 1978 as a way to issue records from local artists - mainly The Customs. It sat dormant for most of the 1980’s until it was revived to issue The Mortals “The One” 7’ in 1993. They specialized in releasing diverse music from Cincinnati artists but have put out records for many well known national acts as well; including The National, Easy Action, Greg Dullie, & The Drive-by Truckers. Shake It! has begun releasing forgotten/obscure/hard-to-find original music in its “Music From Ohio” series. The label also continues its constant relationship with its flagship band Wussy, who have garnered more praise and notoriety with each of their 5 proper studio albums. Shake It! was named one of the “Best Record Stores In America” by Rolling Stone magazine and has won the Cincinnati local weekly reader newspaper poll (City Beat) “Best Record Store” award for a dozen straight years.

Overview

Shake It! Records is a Cincinnati, Ohio record store and label co-founded by Jess Hirbe and Doc Kalmus in 1978, initially conceived as an outlet for local acts—chiefly The Customs. It sat largely idle through the 1980s before Hirbe and Kalmus revived it in 1993 with The Mortals’ “The One” 7”. Output accelerated through the 2000s and peaked in the 2010s, with indie rock band Wussy as the flagship act across five studio albums. The catalogue ranges across nationally known names—The National, Drive-By Truckers—alongside a dedicated sublabel, Music From Ohio, devoted to obscure and forgotten Ohio recordings. The store side of the operation won Rolling Stone’s recognition as one of the best record stores in America and took Cincinnati’s City Beat “Best Record Store” award for twelve consecutive years.

Notes (web)

End of initial run and 1992–1993 revival

Why the label went dormant. After releasing three singles — primarily for The Customs (3) — Hirbe and Kalmus shuttered the label to concentrate on their respective businesses. No single dramatic event is documented; the label simply stopped after those early releases. Hirbe later sold Mole’s Records (the Cincinnati shop he operated) and relocated to Arizona. Source: CityBeat cover story “Long Gone and Back Again” https://citybeat.com/home/article/13024445/cover-story-long-gone-and-back-again.

What prompted Darren Blase’s revival. The direct catalyst was The Mortals wanting to record a cover of “Long Gone” — the A-side of The Customs’ 1980 single (ST 004), written by Jim Cole. When Blase learned of this, he decided to frame the release under the old Shake It name: “When The Mortals wanted to cover ‘Long Gone,’ I thought, ‘I should just do this as a Shake It thing.’ You know, keep the joke alive.” He then tracked down Hirbe in Arizona and negotiated the transfer. The deal: a copy of Champion Jack Dupree’s Blues from the Gutter in exchange for the label’s remaining assets, described by Blase as “an envelope of pictures and a couple of negatives.” Blase formally acquired the label in 1992. Source: CityBeat “Long Gone and Back Again” https://citybeat.com/home/article/13024445/cover-story-long-gone-and-back-again.

The Mortals as first release. The revived label launched with The Mortals’ “The One” 7” (1993, catalog shake-705), which paired “The One” with their cover of “Long Gone” (credited to James Cole / Jim Cole). Source: Grunnen Rocks artist page https://www.grunnenrocks.nl/bands/m/mortalsthe.htm; CityBeat Mortals reunion article https://www.citybeat.com/music/renowned-cincinnati-garage-rockers-the-mortals-return-for-a-reunion-blowout-12173165/.

The National and Cherry Tree (2004)

The National are described as a New York band but all five core members are Cincinnati natives who formed the group in Brooklyn in 1999 after converging there from Cincinnati. Matt Berninger and Scott Devendorf met in 1991 at the University of Cincinnati’s College of Design, Architecture, Art, and Planning (DAAP) graphic design program. Aaron Dessner, Bryce Dessner, and Bryan Devendorf were childhood friends who had played in several bands together in Cincinnati before relocating.

The connection to Shake It! is therefore straightforward hometown loyalty: when the band recorded what became the Cherry Tree EP in 2004, they arranged for the initial vinyl pressing to be issued through their hometown record store/label. The release (catalog SHAKE-1040) was a limited run of approximately 500 copies on translucent red 10” vinyl — the only format Shake It! handled. The main CD release came through Brassland Records (catalog HWY-008, released 19–20 July 2004), which subsequently handled multiple repressings of the vinyl as well. The Shake It! 500-copy pressing has since become a sought-after collector’s item.
