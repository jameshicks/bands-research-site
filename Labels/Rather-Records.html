Rather Records

Discogs ID: 39208 Country: UK Type: Independent; self-funded Active: 1978–present (with a gap through the 1990s–2010s; revived 2022/2026) Catalog series: GEAR (singles/EPs), TROY (albums), RATHER (later releases)

Overview

Rather Records is a UK independent label founded in 1978 by members of Swell Maps — principally Nikki Sudden (real name Adrian Nicholas Godfrey) and his brother Epic Soundtracks (Kevin Paul Godfrey), with the band operating as its own releasing entity. It was one of the earliest fully self-operated DIY labels to emerge from the British post-punk moment, predating and running parallel to the network of independent distributors (notably Rough Trade) that coalesced around 1979.

The label launched with Swell Maps’ debut single “Read About Seymour” (GEAR ONE, 1978) and Steve Treatment’s “5A-Sided 45” (GEAR TWO, 1978), both recorded with engineer Mike Kemp at what appears to have been a small local studio. From 1979 onward, most releases were co-distributed through Rough Trade (appearing on both GEAR/TROY catalog numbers and Rough Trade’s RT/ROUGH series), and Rough Trade engineer/producer John A. Rivers became the house recording figure for the label through the early 1980s.

Rather Records issued the two Swell Maps albums — A Trip to Marineville (1979) and …In “Jane From Occupied Europe” (1980) — as well as the posthumous double-LP Whatever Happens Next… (1981, ROUGH 21), singles by Cult Figures (produced with Nikki Sudden and John A. Rivers), and Nikki Sudden’s first solo single and cassette album. Two “What A Nice Way To Turn Seventeen” compilation EPs/LPs (1983–84) documented the label’s ties to the early UK indie network. The label went dormant after the mid-1980s, then briefly re-emerged with a numbered CDr release in 2000, a 2022 Swell Maps archival single, and Swell Maps’ 2026 LP C21.

Founders / Operators

  • Nikki Sudden (Adrian Nicholas Godfrey, 1956–2006) — guitarist and vocalist of Swell Maps; principal songwriter; operated the label from inception. His solo releases on Rather Records include the “Back To The Start” single (1981, GEAR 11) and the cassette album Beau Geste (1983, RATHER 10). Discogs artist ID: 263346. Also credited under aliases Edrun Kübelwagen, Nikki Mapp.
  • Epic Soundtracks (Kevin Paul Godfrey, 1959–1997) — drummer of Swell Maps; contributed cover artwork for the earliest GEAR releases (GEAR ONE, GEAR TWO, GEAR THREE). Also credited as Amphibious Landing Craft. Discogs artist ID: 264772.
  • Phones B. Sportsman — guitarist/vocalist in Swell Maps; co-produced Nikki Sudden’s solo debut. Discogs artist ID: 541428.

The label appears to have been a collectively operated band vehicle rather than a formal business structure; individual members of Swell Maps produced other artists on the label (Nikki Sudden co-produced Cult Figures’ singles) and the band itself is credited as arranger/producer on all its own releases.

Distribution: Rough Trade Relationship

From 1979, Rather Records releases were co-distributed by Rough Trade and often assigned parallel Rough Trade catalog numbers:

  • Dresden Style (GEAR THREE = RT 012)
  • Zip Nolan (GEAR FOUR = RT 020)
  • A Trip To Marineville (GEAR FIVE / TROY ONE = ROUGH 2)
  • Let’s Build A Car (GEAR SEVEN = RT 036)
  • …In “Jane From Occupied Europe” (TROY TWO = ROUGH 15)
  • Whatever Happens Next… (ROUGH 21 — fully absorbed into Rough Trade’s catalog numbering)

The albums in particular were co-releases with Rough Trade carrying the primary marketing weight. This arrangement was typical of the Rough Trade distribution cooperative model that served many UK independent labels in the 1979–1984 period.

Connections

  • Nikki Sudden — co-founder; first solo releases here
  • Epic Soundtracks — co-founder; died 1997
  • Rough Trade — distribution and co-release partner from 1979 onward
  • Cult Figures — Birmingham group produced and issued by the Swell Maps circle

Open Questions

  • Operational/legal structure: Was Rather Records ever formally registered as a company, or entirely informal?
  • ROUGH 21 attribution: The Whatever Happens Next… double-LP is credited in Discogs as both ROUGH 21 and Rather Records — was this a Rough Trade release with Rather Records credit, or a full co-release?
