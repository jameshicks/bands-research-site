Warp Records

Warp Records

Oct 25, 20266 min read

Warp Records

Discogs ID: 23528
Parent company: Warp Records Limited (ID: 264514)
Founded: 1989, Sheffield, England
Founders: Steve Beckett, Rob Mitchell, Robert Gordon
Based: Sheffield (1989–mid-1990s); London (from late 1990s onward)

Independent British electronic label, one of the most influential of the last four decades. Founded in a Sheffield record shop in 1989, initially as an outlet for the local bleep-and-bass scene; by the 1990s expanded into IDM and experimental electronics; by the 2000s broadened further into post-rock, avant-pop, hip-hop, and R&B. The name originated as “Warped,” shortened to Warp; later backronym: “We Are Reasonable People.” Rob Mitchell died of cancer on 8 October 2001, aged 38.

Founders and Context

  • Steve Beckett — ran FON Records in Sheffield before co-founding Warp
  • Rob Mitchell — died of cancer, 8 October 2001, aged 38. Diagnosed approximately two weeks after his wedding; illness lasted around six months. NME, 10 Oct 2001; Music Week, 20 Oct 2001; Wikipedia: Warp Records (as of 2026-06-26)
  • Robert Gordon — third co-founder; later ran Lex Records (a Warp sublabel)

The label grew out of Sheffield’s late-1980s bleep-and-bass scene, which fused Detroit techno’s minimalism with British working-class electronics, heavy sub-bass, and a pronounced northern English aesthetic. Cabaret Voltaire and Richard H. Kirk were forerunners; the Designers Republic (Sheffield graphic studio) provided the label’s visual identity through the 1990s.

Era I — Bleep and Bass (1989–1991)

The first releases established the bleep sound and put Sheffield on the electronic map. Sweet Exorcist — Richard H. Kirk and Dean Thatcher — provided some of the earliest singles and the label’s first LP. LFO (Mark Bell and Gez Varley) scored an unexpected UK top-20 hit with “LFO.”

Nightmares On Wax (George Evelyn, Kevin Harper, Robin Taylor-Firth) bridged the Sheffield bleep scene and later trip-hop; they remained on Warp through multiple albums into the 2000s.

Era II — Artificial Intelligence / IDM (1992–1995)

The 1992 compilation Artificial Intelligence (WARP LP 6) named and codified a new genre — electronic music conceived for listening rather than dancing, drawing on ambient, techno, and Detroit influences. Key signings in this period: Aphex Twin (Richard David James), Autechre (Sean Booth, Rob Brown), B12 (Steve Rutter, Mike Golding), The Black Dog, Plaid.

Aphex Twin had been releasing on R&S (Analogue Bubblebath series) and Apollo before moving to Warp; his Warp catalog runs from 1994 through the 2010s and includes some of his most celebrated work (Selected Ambient Works Volume II, Richard D. James Album, Drukqs, Syro). Aliases Polygon Window, Caustic Window, GAK, and The Tuss also released on Warp or Warp-adjacent imprints.

Autechre debuted with Incunabula and have released exclusively on Warp ever since — among the longest and most consistent artist–label relationships in electronic music.

Plaid (Ed Handley, Andy Turner) originally recorded as The Black Dog before splitting off; signed to Warp from the mid-1990s through to the present.

Era III — Broadening (1995–2003)

Squarepusher (Thomas Jenkinson) debuted in 1997; his high-BPM drill’n’bass and jazz-fusion fusion became a Warp signature alongside Aphex Twin. Boards of Canada (Michael Sandison, Marcus Eoin — Scottish duo, not Canadian) joined with Music Has the Right to Children (1998), one of Warp’s most celebrated releases.

Broadcast (Trish Keenan, James Cargill, et al.) signed in 1999; their Radiophonic-influenced art-pop placed them somewhat apart from the label’s electronic mainstream. Seefeel (Sarah Peacock, Mark Clifford, Darren Seymour, Justin Fletcher) were earlier signings (mid-1990s), bridging shoegaze and ambient techno.

Richard H. Kirk continued solo on Warp (Virtual State, 1994; RetroActivity, 1995) alongside his Cabaret Voltaire work and other projects.

Prefuse 73 (Scott Herren) was a flagship signing of the early 2000s: glitchy sample-based hip-hop. Nine albums 2001–2011 on Warp; later moved to Lex Records.

Era IV — 2000s Expansion (2003–2012)

Warp diversified into post-rock (via Battles), indie (via !!!!!), and eventually hip-hop and R&B. Flying Lotus (Steven Ellison, nephew of Alice Coltrane) debuted in 2006; his Brainfeeder label became a parallel institution.

Battles (Ian Williams, Dave Konopka, John Stanier, Tyondai Braxton; Braxton departed after Mirrored) brought math-rock and time-signature complexity. Danny Brown (Daniel Dewan Sewell) joined later, bringing aggressive Detroit hip-hop.

Era V — 2010s to Present

Oneohtrix Point Never (Daniel Lopatin) signed mid-2010s; Yves Tumor (Sean L. Bowie) released Heaven to a Tortured Mind (2020); Kelela (Kelela Mizanekristos) brought R&B; Mount Kimbie, King Krule, Nala Sinephro, Squid, Jockstrap represent the label’s contemporary UK and international roster.
