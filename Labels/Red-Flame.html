Red Flame

UK independent record label. Discogs ID: 5173. Founded by Dave Kitson, who had previously been a promoter at the Moonlight Club, West Hampstead, London. Active 1982–1993, with the heaviest release schedule in 1982–1985. Operating company: Red Flame Ltd. (Discogs ID: 359654). Sublabel: Ink Records (Discogs ID: 5171).

Overview

Red Flame was a small London independent that emerged from the post-punk moment in 1982, positioning itself as a conduit for experimental and alternative acts from both the UK and internationally — notably Australia. Its catalog opens with a cluster of seven-inch singles in 1982 (catalog numbers RF 701 upward) and expands quickly to EPs and LPs.

The label’s founder, Dave Kitson, had come out of live music promotion rather than a recording background, which likely accounts for Red Flame’s roster diversity: the label signed acts as stylistically varied as jazz-punk agitators Blurt, soul-jazz vocalist Carmel McCourt (credited as Carmel (2)), Afrobeat-influenced Nigerian funk act Kabbala, Sheffield art-rock band Artery (2), and the Melbourne post-punk outfit The Moodists.

The year 1982 was the label’s launch year and its most concentrated burst: twelve distinct release titles appeared, almost all singles and EPs. 1983 continued the pace with another fourteen. By 1984–1985 the roster had thinned as some acts (Carmel, The Moodists) moved on to major or larger independent deals.

From 1986 onward the label reoriented around two anchors: the German art-rock group Phillip Boa & The Voodooclub (whose melodic chamber-pop filled five release slots 1986–1987) and London-based vocal duo Ruby Blue, who became the longest-running Red Flame act (26 releases, 1987–1993). Producer Victor Van Vugt became the key recurring studio presence in the label’s second phase, credited on 12 releases.

A late-period anomaly: the label issued Distant Plastic Trees (1991) by The Magnetic Fields — Stephin Merritt’s debut album proper — in the UK/Europe, making Red Flame one of the first labels to bring the Magnetic Fields to non-American audiences.

Corporate Structure

  • Red Flame Ltd. (Discogs ID: 359654) — the operating company; no releases of its own
  • Red Flame (Discogs ID: 5173) — the main imprint; 196 release entries, ~181 distinct masters
  • Ink Records (Discogs ID: 5171) — sublabel; home to Anne Clark’s most significant releases including “Our Darkness”

Connections

  • Ink Records — Red Flame sublabel; Anne Clark’s principal imprint
  • Red Flame Ltd. — parent operating company
  • Moonlight Club — West Hampstead venue where Dave Kitson worked as promoter before founding the label
  • Blurt — early key act; self-titled LP (1982) and single (1983)
  • The Moodists — Australian post-punk; Red Flame was their main UK outlet
  • Laughing Clowns — Ed Kuepper’s post-Saints project; UK releases here
  • Severed Heads — Australian industrial; Since The Accident (1983)
  • Victor Van Vugt — house producer for the label’s second phase (Ruby Blue era)
  • Vini Reilly — credited composer/performer on David Harrow releases
  • The Magnetic Fields — late anomaly; UK debut Distant Plastic Trees (1991)
  • Livingston Studios — North London studio where Harris and others recorded several Red Flame and Ink Records acts
