Dutch East India Trading

Discogs ID: 9986 Origin: Rockville Centre, NY, USA Active: ~1980s–c.2000 (went bankrupt ~2000) Founder: Barry Tenenbaum (began as Lord Sitar Records / LSR, a mail-order import operation)

US record distributor and importer. Parent company of Homestead Records and several other sublabels including Grass Records, Napalm Records (2), Rockville, Dutch East Wax Records, and Dutch East Tapes. Also distributed Peel Sessions titles in the US from January 1991.

Sublabels / Imprints (DEIT-owned)

  • Homestead Records (ID 11706) — founded 1983; managed successively by Sam Berger (–1984), Gerard Cosloy (1984–1990), Ken Katkin (1990–1992), Steven Joerg (1992–1996)
  • Grass Records (ID 74559)
  • Napalm Records (2) (ID 125970)
  • Rockville (ID 35901)
  • Dutch East Wax Records (ID 1799885)
  • Dutch East Tapes (ID 1042392)
  • Giant Records (independent) — hardcore/punk imprint founded 1987, run by Steev Riccardo; roster included Dag Nasty, Government Issue, Verbal Assault, Uniform Choice, Indestructible Noise Command, Big Wheel. Not listed among Discogs sublabels; confirmed as DEIT imprint via Wikipedia and Rettman interview. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Records_(independent)
  • LSR Records — Tenenbaum’s original imprint (Lord Sitar Records); catalog numbers prefixed DEI (e.g. DEI2064-2 on a 1993 DEIT sampler). Effectively a continued DEIT in-house imprint. https://www.discogs.com/label/34741-LSR-Records

Outside Labels Distributed (not DEIT-owned)

Strange Fruit (UK)

Strange Fruit Records (UK, founded 1986 by Clive Selwood and John Peel) licensed its Peel Sessions catalog to DEIT for US distribution from January 1991. DEIT released the US editions under its own catalog numbers (DEI prefix). The Freeport Library source describes this as a “partnership” for BBC John Peel Sessions distribution. Acts covered include The Cure, New Order, The Fall, Siouxsie & The Banshees, The Smiths, Syd Barrett, The Wedding Present, Doom. https://libguides.freeportlibrary.info/c.php?g=1500070&p=11208578; https://libguides.freeportlibrary.info/c.php?g=1500070&p=11208581

Les Disques du Crépuscule (Belgium)

DEIT had a licensing/distribution deal with the Belgian post-punk label Les Disques du Crépuscule. Releases by Cabaret Voltaire (e.g. Eight Crepuscule Tracks, 1988) were licensed from Crépuscule and released domestically — in at least some cases routed through the Giant imprint. Steev Riccardo confirmed: “Barry [Tenenbaum] wanted those [Crépuscule titles] to be released domestically through the Giant imprint.” https://rettman.substack.com/p/interview-steev-riccardo-pt-i

Fountain of Youth Records (DC hardcore)

Fountain of Youth Records (Washington DC, active 1983–1986, run by Derrick Hsu) entered into a distribution deal with DEIT that covered its entire catalog from at least 1983 through 1988 (including reissues/repress editions). From 1986 onward, DEIT is also credited as “Manufactured By,” indicating the arrangement became a full P&D (pressing and distribution) deal. Repress editions of earlier titles (Joy Ride, Boycott Stabb) carry the same credits through 1987–1988. The Discogs profile for Fountain of Youth notes the label “lost rights to all their recordings in a publishing and distribution swindle,” consistent with the deal turning adversarial. The label ceased operation in 1986, but DEIT continued pressing/distributing back-catalog through at least 1988.

Source: Discogs release_company credits, label ID 9986 (DEIT) on Fountain of Youth (label ID 41492) releases; Discogs profile for Fountain Of Youth Records.

Bar/None Records (Hoboken NJ)

Bar/None Records (founded 1986 by Tom Prendergast in Hoboken, NJ) used DEIT as one of its indie distributors alongside others (Systematic, Jem, Twin Cities). Bar/None shipped to “all the main indie rock distributors of the day,” with DEIT listed explicitly among them. Relationship described as standard wholesale/distribution, not P&D or ownership. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar/None_Records

Personnel Connections

Several people who later founded or ran significant labels worked at DEIT before starting those ventures:

  • Chris Lombardi — worked in sales at DEIT; founded Matador Records in 1989 (from his apartment, not as a DEIT venture). No confirmed evidence DEIT distributed early Matador releases — the connection is alumni, not a distribution deal. https://artists.spotify.com/en/blog/label-spotlight-matador-records
  • Gerard Cosloy — ran Homestead Records (DEIT) 1984–1990; co-founded Matador 1990.
  • Patrick Amory — also worked at DEIT before Matador.
