Overview

Thin Line was a Manchester label founded by producer Martin Hannett and Howard ‘Ginger’ Jones, a former employee of both the Hacienda and Factory Records. Its catalogue is compact — two releases in the mid-1980s — but the roster carries notable weight: The Stone Roses appear alongside Steve Diggle and Andy Couzens, placing Thin Line at the junction of Manchester’s post-punk legacy and the emerging indie acts of the mid-decade. The label sits squarely within the orbit of Hannett’s Factory-era connections while operating as a distinct venture from that parent scene.

Notes (web)

Why Hannett established Thin Line — relationship to Factory Records

Thin Line was neither a formal split from Factory nor a pre-planned solo imprint. It emerged as an independent parallel venture during Hannett’s period of estrangement from Factory Records, after their legal dispute but while Hannett remained loosely within the Factory orbit.

Timeline of the Factory dispute. Hannett filed suit against Factory Communications Limited in March 1982 (case No. 001356 of 1982; catalogued by Factory itself as FAC 61). His grievances included exclusion from management decisions, misappropriation of funds (citing £50,000 diverted to The Haçienda rather than the recording studio infrastructure he had pushed for), and unfavourable treatment of non-New Order artists. He sought either dissolution of Factory with proportional asset distribution, or a buy-out of his shares at a “just and reasonable” price. The suit dragged on for nearly two years, depriving Hannett of royalty income and new Factory commissions. It was settled in January 1984 — Hannett received a £40,000 payment. (factoryrecords.org/cerysmatic/fac61.php)

Thin Line came after the settlement, not during the dispute. The Stone Roses debut on Thin Line (THIN 001) was released September 1985 — roughly eighteen months after the Factory settlement concluded. Some secondary sources (LTM, Factory Benelux) erroneously date the release to 1983; Discogs, Wikipedia’s “So Young” article, and contemporary auction records all confirm 1985. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_Young_(The_Stone_Roses_song), as of 2026-11-24; betteronvinyl.co.uk — THIN 001 listing)

Thin Line was not a replacement for Factory; Hannett returned to Factory work. Despite the legal acrimony, Hannett eventually reconciled with Tony Wilson sufficiently to produce Happy Mondays for Factory — a reunion that would not have happened had Thin Line represented a permanent departure. This confirms the label was a one-off or small-scale independent outlet, not a rival imprint or formal successor to his Factory role. (sonicscoop.com/mad-genius-manchester-profile-producer-martin-hannett/)

Motivation. The label was created as a vehicle to release The Stone Roses by Jones (their manager) and Hannett (their producer), not as a broad independent label strategy. During the post-settlement period Hannett was working outside the Factory system and was available for independent projects; Jones needed a release mechanism for the band and had Factory-adjacent contacts including Hannett. The result was Thin Line — purpose-built, low-infrastructure, with only five total Discogs credits across its existence.

Note on date error in secondary sources. LTM Recordings (ltmrecordings.com/martin_hannett.html) and Factory Benelux (factorybenelux.com/martin_hannett.html) both state the Thin Line Stone Roses single was released September 1983. This would place it during the Factory lawsuit and before the settlement — a materially different story. However, all primary release evidence (Discogs, Wikipedia’s dedicated article, vinyl auction records) consistently gives September 1985. The 1983 date in those biography pages appears to be a shared transcription error. The correct date is 1985.

The Stone Roses connection — how the band came to Thin Line

Howard “Ginger” Jones had been working as director and general manager of the The Hacienda and was involved in Factory Records circles before parting ways with that scene. Sometime in 1984–85 Jones took on management of The Stone Roses after witnessing their early potential. Wanting to release the band’s debut material, Jones partnered with producer Martin Hannett — also a Factory Records veteran — and together (along with Tim Chambers) they set up Thin Line specifically to release The Stone Roses. The label was therefore not a pre-existing venture that the band approached; it was created by their manager as a vehicle for them. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stone_Roses, as of 2026-11-14; thestoneroses.co.uk/bio)

What was released

The band recorded their debut single at Strawberry Studios in Stockport in early 1985, with Hannett producing and engineer Chris Nagle assisting. The double A-side 12” “So Young / Tell Me” (cat. THIN 001) was pressed in a limited run of approximately 500 copies and released in September 1985. It is the first entry in Hannett’s catalogue for the label. The front cover artwork was made by John Squire, who smashed an old transistor radio and glued the parts together. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_Young_(The_Stone_Roses_song), as of 2026-11-14)

The Hannett sessions and the unreleased album

Beyond the single, Hannett oversaw further recording sessions at Strawberry Studios intended to yield a debut album. These sessions produced thirteen tracks. The band were unhappy with the results — Ian Brown later described them as “a disaster” and recalled Hannett as “only half there,” sometimes walking out mid-session. The LTM Records biography of Hannett notes these sessions fell during his difficult period following a legal dispute with Factory Records, when his drug addiction had significantly impaired his work. The album was shelved; the material eventually surfaced in 1996 on the posthumous release Garage Flower. (ltmrecordings.com/martin_hannett.html; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_Flower, as of 2026-11-14)

Break with Thin Line / Jones

The parting was personal rather than legal. Jones had no formal management contract with the band, so when they decided to leave — sometime in mid-1986, after the Thin Line single and the shelved Hannett album sessions — they could simply walk away. David Wood, writing in 1998, stated: “Howard Jones didn’t continue managing the band for long after the single and I believe he didn’t have a contract so they could walk away.” (thestonerosesarchive.co.uk/diary/1986, sourcing Wood 1998)

Ian Brown’s explanation, in the same archive source, was blunt: “Yeah. We reckoned he was a bit of a dick, full stop, proper.” This frames the break as a personal falling-out rather than any contractual or legal conflict.

The official Stone Roses biography characterises it as the band having “unceremoniously abandoned” Jones in favour of Gareth Evans. (confidentials.com) The transition date is placed around mid-1986: Evans offered the band a parental guardianship agreement (not a standard music management contract), signed 18 November 1986, with Evans promising more gigs than Jones had delivered. (thestonerosesarchive.co.uk/diary/1986)

No court case, injunction or financial settlement involving Jones has been found in any source. The legal battles that did follow the band were with Gareth Evans and with Silvertone Records — not with Jones. Wikipedia notes only that Jones “parted company” with the band, with no dispute recorded. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stone_Roses, as of 2026-11-15)

Jones died in 2007 following an accident. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stone_Roses, as of 2026-11-15)

Label name — possible drug reference

One source notes “Thin Line” was described as a drug reference, consistent with Hannett’s known habits during this period. This is a single uncorroborated claim and should be treated with caution until confirmed by a named interview or primary source.

Relationship to Thin Line Productions (Discogs ID 383451)

A separate Discogs entity, Thin Line Productions (ID 383451, first credited 1996), shares the same roster (Stone Roses members, Andy Couzens, The High). Whether it is a continuation, a differently registered entity, or a wholly separate venture is not confirmed by any source found in this research pass.
