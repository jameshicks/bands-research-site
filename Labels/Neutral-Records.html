Neutral Records

Independent label. New York City. 1982–c.1987. 415 Lafayette St., New York, NY 10003.

Discogs label ID: 145071 (note: IDs 27234 = French hard techno label, 550813 = counterfeit tracking entry — neither is this).

Direct spinoff from the collapse of the Branca / 99 Records relationship. Founded specifically to release Sonic Youth’s debut after Ed Bahlman refused to sign them. Ended up launching both Sonic Youth and Swans.

Key Figures

Glenn Branca — co-founder. Composer, guitarist, conductor. Had released his own work and The Ascension on 99 Records; left after the Bahlman/Sonic Youth dispute. Went on to run the label as a vehicle for No Wave and avant-garde composition, including his own Symphony No. 3.

Josh Baer — co-founder. Director of White Columns non-profit art space, 1979–1983. Left White Columns around the time the label’s main releases were clustered (1982–83). Later became a prominent contemporary art dealer. The White Columns connection explains the heavy crossover with New York visual artists throughout the catalog.

Discogs artist ID for Baer: 305055.

Origin

Glenn Branca had been instrumental in pushing Ed Bahlman to start 99 Records (first 99 release: Branca’s “Lesson No. 1,” 99-01, 1980). When Sonic Youth came to Bahlman looking for a deal, Bahlman passed. Branca co-founded Neutral with Josh Baer and put out the Sonic Youth debut EP as N-ONE in 1982.

Key Production Threads

Don Hünerberg — engineered N-ONE (SY EP), #3 (Native Dance), N-SIX (Vortex). House engineer for the early run.

Roli Mosimann — drummer on Filth (Swans), cover photographer on Confusion Is Sex (Sonic Youth), producer on Orchesography (David Linton). Appears on three separate releases in three separate roles — see also 23 Skidoo connections.

Wharton Tiers — produced and engineered Confusion Is Sex. First Sonic Youth album credit; he went on to produce many subsequent SY records.

Josh Baer — exec-produced #3 and N5, alongside Branca. His White Columns art world network explains Cindy Sherman (N5 cover), Jack Goldstein (N-7), Beth B & Scott B (N-SIX), and the Eric Bogosian connection.

Key Facts

  • Sonic Youth’s two earliest full studio works (debut EP + debut LP) came out here. Both records are foundational to noise rock and alternative music.
  • Swans’ debut Filth came out here. Neutral effectively launched both bands.
  • The label sits at the intersection of No Wave music and the downtown Manhattan visual art world — less “pure” No Wave than 99 Records, more deliberately art-institutional.
  • Jim Sclavunos appears on Confusion Is Sex — same period as his work with The Birthday Party and Crime & The City Solution.
  • Bob Bert (drums on Confusion Is Sex) replaced Richard Edson in Sonic Youth between the EP and the album.
  • Jack Goldstein (N-7 “Planets”) was known for vinyl records of sound phenomena (thunderstorm, dog bark, etc.) as artworks — a different mode than every other Neutral release.
  • 415 Lafayette St is in NoHo/Greenwich Village, a few blocks from the 99 Records store on MacDougal.
