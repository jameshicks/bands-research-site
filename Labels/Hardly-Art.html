Hardly Art

Independent record label. Seattle, Washington. Discogs ID: 121225. Parent label: Sub Pop Records (Discogs ID: 77343). Founded early 2007. Distributed by Alternative Distribution Alliance (ADA) and Sub Pop. Contact: info@hardlyart.com.

Label History

Hardly Art was founded in early 2007 as a fully independent subsidiary of Sub Pop Records, created to expand Sub Pop’s reach into more eclectic, lo-fi, and indie-pop territory beyond the parent label’s established identity. The label is run by three full-time employees and operates with meaningful autonomy from Sub Pop’s main operation, signing and developing its own roster. Sub Pop provides distribution infrastructure (via ADA) while Hardly Art handles A&R, releases, and day-to-day operations.

The inaugural release was Arthur & Yu In Camera (HAR-001, 2007), a folk-pop record that set a template for the early catalog: indie-leaning, song-based work with craft and personality rather than the noise-heavy or grunge-inflected material Sub Pop was historically associated with. The first years drew on the Seattle/Pacific Northwest region — The Moondoggies, The Dutchess And The Duke, Le Loup — but the label quickly became national in scope.

By the early 2010s Hardly Art had developed a clear stylistic identity around surf-inflected indie pop, garage punk, and Pacific Northwest DIY sensibility. The 2013 debut of La Luz (It’s Alive, HAR-076) became the label’s most prominent long-running artist relationship, spanning multiple lineup iterations and continuing into the 2020s. Protomartyr from Detroit joined in 2014 and became the label’s most critically acclaimed act in the post-punk/art-rock space. Chastity Belt (Seattle) and Tacocat (Seattle, palindromic name) were anchor artists through the mid-2010s, giving the label a strong feminist/DIY profile that distinguished it clearly from the parent.

Hardly Art also served as a catalogue steward for Carissa’s Wierd, the influential 1990s–early 2000s Seattle folk-rock band whose members include Ben Bridwell (later of Band of Horses) and Mat Brooke, issuing their archival discography in 2010. The label released The Julie Ruin — the band fronted by Kathleen Hanna of Bikini Kill/Le Tigre — in 2016 (Hit Reset, HAR-096), one of its highest-profile signings.

Into the 2020s the label has maintained a modest but steady release pace, continuing with La Luz, adding Marinero, Colleen Green, and Chris Cohen.

Stylistic Identity

Hardly Art’s catalog clusters around:

  • Surf and reverb-drenched indie rock — La Luz is the defining example; also Shana Cleveland’s solo work
  • Pacific Northwest DIY / garage — Tacocat, Chastity Belt, Jacuzzi Boys, Unnatural Helpers
  • Lo-fi indie pop and bedroom pop — Colleen Green, Seapony, La Sera, LALA LALA
  • Post-punk / art rock — Protomartyr (the label’s most critically lauded act in this vein), Black Marble (synth/post-punk)
  • Garage and power pop — Jacuzzi Boys, Hunx And His Punx, Shannon And The Clams
  • Archival / catalogue — Carissa’s Wierd reissues (2010)

The label has a notably strong women-led and feminist artist representation: La Luz, Chastity Belt, Tacocat, Colleen Green, La Sera, LALA LALA, Shana Cleveland, Julia Shapiro, Jenn Champion, Girl Friday.
