Eglo Records

Discogs ID: 143129 Origin: London, UK Founded: Late 2008 (first release 2009) Co-founders: Floating Points (Sam Shepherd) · Alexander Nut Sublabel: Ho Tep Website: eglorecords.com / eglorecords.bandcamp.com

Independent London label built around deep house, broken beat, future jazz, and neo-soul. Founded in late 2008 by producer/DJ Floating Points (Sam Shepherd) and selector/A&R Alexander Nut, Eglo became one of the defining UK underground labels of the 2010s, launching Floating Points’ recording career and providing a home for a tightly connected circle of London and international artists.

History

Founding (2008–2009)

Eglo was launched in late 2008 by Sam Shepherd (recording as Floating Points) and Alexander Nut, with the inaugural release arriving in 2009: For You b/w Radiality (LG001), a 7” credited to Floating Points. The early catalog number “LG001” (before settling on the “EGLO” prefix) reflects the label’s DIY origins. The label operated with Alexander Nut handling A&R and curation — he is credited in that role across multiple releases — while Shepherd served simultaneously as the label’s founding artist.

The second release, the Vacuum EP (EGLO002, 2009), established Floating Points as an artist of genuine depth: three tracks across a 12”, including the eight-minute “Argonaute II,” tagged as deep house but reaching into jazz-inflected texture. Artwork on the early singles was handled by Kay Shin (credited on LG001 and EGLO002).

Early Roster and Core Sound (2009–2012)

Within the first year Eglo added FunkinEven (EGLO003, Kleer, 2009) featuring vocalist Fatima (12), who quickly became one of the label’s most important artists in her own right. The Kleer EP introduced the label’s characteristic blend of broken beat funk and soulful vocals.

The early period, roughly 2009–2012, established the core sound: deep house rhythms with jazz harmony sensibilities, broken beat influence, vocal soul, and a consistent vinyl-first aesthetic. Key releases from this phase:

  • FunkinEven — Heart Pound / Another Space (EGLO10, 2010)
  • Fatima (12) — Follow You (EGLO14, 2011) — produced by Sam Shepherd and Fatima Bramme Sey; three tracks produced by Floating Points
  • Floating Points — Marilyn (EGLO13, 2011) — photography by Jamie Mack
  • Floating Points — Shadows EP (EGLO22, 2011) — 2×12” double pack
  • ARP.101 — U / Groove / True (EGLO21, 2011)
  • Mizz Beats — Pimpin (EGLO17, 2011)

Mastering across this period was handled predominantly by Matt Colton (credited on 21 releases across the label’s run — the single most recurring engineer on the catalog) and later by John Dent (7 releases).

Consolidation and Expansion (2012–2015)

The 2012–2015 period saw Eglo broaden its palette. The GB Presents: The Abstract Eye collaboration (EGLO23, 2012) brought the duo GB and The Abstract Eye into the fold. The label’s first compilation, Eglo Records Vol.1 (EGLO26, 2013), served as a catalog survey and introduction: a 2×CD collecting Floating Points, FunkinEven, Fatima, ARP.101, Mizz Beats, Shuanise, GB, and The Abstract Eye.

Strange U (UK rap duo) appeared with Scarlet Jungle (EGLO25, 2013) and EP #2040 (EGLO34, 2014), demonstrating the label’s willingness to host hip-hop alongside house.

In 2014, Dego and Kaidi Tatham — veterans of the 4hero/Reinforced world — debuted on Eglo with Dego And Kaidi EP (EGLO32), produced by both, mixing broken beat and future jazz. A second EP (EGLO42) followed in 2015. This was a significant acquisition: Dego and Kaidi brought with them credibility from the foundational broken beat scene.

Fatima (12) released Yellow Memories (EGLO36, 2014), her debut album on the label.

The most adventurous release of this phase was Marhaba (EGBC001, 2015), a co-release with Border Community credited to Maleem Mahmoud Ghania / Floating Points / James Holden. The 12” documents a recording session with Moroccan Gnawa master Maleem Mahmoud Ghania (guembri, vocals), Floating Points (keyboards), and James Holden (modular synthesizer), plus Ghania’s ensemble. Side A was recorded with Floating Points, Side B with Holden. The release is catalogued on Eglo as EGBC001.

Later Period (2016–2020s)

Steve Spacek joined the label in 2016 with singles Follow Me (EGLO49) and If U Wan 2 Find Me (EGLO50) and the 2018 LP Natural Sci-Fi (EGLO59) — seven releases total across 2016–2020, making him the fifth-most active artist on the label by release count.

Shafiq Husayn (of Sa-Ra Creative Partners) contributed On Our Way Home (EGLO52, 2016) and the LP The Loop (EGLO53, 2019).

Byron The Aquarius debuted on the label with Leaving This Planet (EGLO56, 2017), featuring Cropper (2) on “Song For A Friend.”

Fatima’s second LP, And Yet It’s All Love (EGLO61, 2018), was a sprawling double-album with production from The Purist, mndsgn, Natureboy Flako, JD Reid, Swarvy, Freckles Pecan, Taz Arnold, and Kirkis. Roc Marciano appeared as a guest. Alexander Nut is credited as A&R; mastered by Matt Colton. Artwork by Monica Kim Garza.

Natalie Slade became a key later-period artist with Control (EGLO71, 2020) and Control Remixes (EGLO80, 2021).

By the 2020s the label continued releasing, with artists including Destiny71z, Sticky Dub, and others continuing into 2024–2025.

Sound and Aesthetic

Eglo’s house style is easiest to hear in the early catalog: long-form, jazzy deep house built for listening as much as dancing — long tracks (the Vacuum EP runs past eight minutes on two of its three sides), sophisticated chord voicings, a warm analog palette. Floating Points brought classical training and jazz obsession; Alexander Nut’s DJ background (Rinse FM) shaped the curation toward a pan-genre late-night sensibility.

As the roster expanded, the common thread became harmonic depth and emotional directness: broken beat from Dego/Kaidi, neo-soul from Fatima, afro-jazz excursions with Maleem Mahmoud Ghania, deep house from Steve Spacek. The Gnawa session (Marhaba) is the furthest stylistic reach — but it fits the label’s core interest in extended, exploratory music with strong tonal personality.

Artwork was handled by a small recurring set of collaborators: Kay Shin (early releases), Jamie Mack (photography), Sassy J (design across several mid-period releases), Monica Kim Garza, and Will Hurt.

Ho Tep (Sublabel)

Discogs ID: 204496 Parent: Eglo Records

Ho Tep was Eglo’s sublabel, used for a small run of releases ca. 2010–2011. Three releases documented on Discogs:
