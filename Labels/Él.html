Él

Discogs ID: 31432 Founded by: Mike Alway (Discogs ID: 98257) Active: 1984–1989; revived 2005 as reissue label Location: Power Road Studios, 114 Power Road, London W4 5PY, UK Parent label: Cherry Red (from 1986; parent entity: Cherry Red Records Ltd., Discogs ID: 269699) Early distributor: Les Disques Du Crépuscule (first six releases via Él Benelux)

History

Founding and the Crépuscule Phase (1984–1985)

Él was founded in 1984 by Mike Alway after he left Blanco Y Negro, the WEA-distributed independent he had helped build. The name is rendered with the acute accent — “Él” — throughout the label’s life, a small act of Continental affectation entirely in keeping with its aesthetic.

The first six releases were manufactured and distributed through Les Disques Du Crépuscule, the influential Brussels-based label, via a dedicated imprint called Él Benelux (Discogs ID: 4353958). These early releases carry Belgian pressings and Él Benelux catalog numbers (EL 1, ELT 2, EL 4, FIN 1, ELT 5). Artists in this phase include Shock Headed Peters (the first Él release, I, Bloodbrother Be, EL 1, 1984), Klaxon 5 (Hothouse, ELT 2, 1984), Vic Godard (Holiday Hymn, EL 4, 1985), and Momus (The Beast With 3 Backs, ELT 5, 1985).

This Crépuscule connection was not incidental: the Brussels label had a near-identical sensibility — literary, European, beautifully designed, indifferent to mainstream chart ambition. Mike Alway shared with Crépuscule founders Michel Duval and Annik Honoré a faith in aesthetics as substance, and the early Él releases look and feel like Crépuscule product.

The Cherry Red Period (1986–1989)

From 1986, Él became a sublabel of Cherry Red (Discogs ID: 5174), itself part of the Cherry Red Records Ltd. group. This relationship gave the label manufacturing and distribution infrastructure while preserving its curatorial independence. Alway’s taste remained sovereign: the label expanded its roster but maintained a consistent aesthetic around orchestrated, pre-rock-referencing pop, chamber arrangements, wit, literary themes, and meticulous sleeve design.

The Cherry Red period produced the bulk of the original catalog. Catalog number series shifted from the early EL/ELT pattern to GPO (singles, 7” and 12”) and ACME (albums and compilation LPs/CDs). The label also made extensive use of a Japan distribution arrangement: most Él LPs were licensed for Japanese release with their own number series (85xxx-28/32 for LPs, 35xxx-28 for 12”s, and later 25xxx-12 for singles).

Key releases in this period include:

  • Louis Philippe’s three Él LPs: Passport To The Pogie Mountains (ACME … Japan 85602-32, 1987), Ivory Tower (ACME 15, 1988), Yuri Gagarin (ACME 23, 1989)
  • The King Of Luxembourg’s Royal Bastard (ACME 8, 1987) and “Sir” (ACME 16, 1988)
  • The Monochrome Set’s Westminster Affair (ACME 17, 1988) — the band’s final studio album
  • Anthony Adverse’s The Red Shoes (ACME 11, 1988) and Spin (ACME 22, 1989)
  • Would-Be-Goods’s The Camera Loves Me (ACME 14, 1988)
  • Simon Fisher Turner’s Caravaggio 1610 (ACME 6, 1986)
  • Felt’s Me And A Monkey On The Moon (ACME 24, 1989) — the Birmingham band’s final LP
  • Marden Hill’s Cadaquéz (ACME 13, 1988)
  • Always’s Thames Valley Leather Club (ACME 12, 1988)
  • Bad Dream Fancy Dress’s Choirboys Gas (ACME 18, 1988)
  • Compilation series: London Pavilion (Vols I–III), Girl Talk, 4-2-4 The Él Compendium Of Soccer
  • Abracadabra (The ‘Magnificent Triviality’ Of Él Records) — a label retrospective compilation (catno: monde 6cd)

Closure (1989)

Alway departed in 1989 following a disagreement with Cherry Red boss Iain McNay over a proposed release: a record of children singing songs written by their fathers. McNay reportedly refused to release it; Alway left, and the label closed. The dispute was one of principle as much as commerce, consistent with Alway’s history of treating curatorial taste as non-negotiable.

A handful of post-1989 Discogs entries on the label (including Anthony Adverse singles in 1990 and James Dean Driving Experience in 1990) suggest a brief wind-down period before the catalog became dormant.

Revival as Reissue Label (2005–present)

In 2005, Él was revived under the Cherry Red umbrella as a reissue label. The revival catalog is substantial — over 150 reissue CDs by the mid-2020s — and leans heavily on the label’s particular aesthetic enthusiasms: exotica (Les Baxter, Esquivel), Brazilian bossa nova and MPB (João Gilberto, Elis Regina, João Donato, Luiz Bonfá, Vinicius De Moraes), Indian classical (Ravi Shankar, Ali Akbar Khan, Vilayat Khan), French chanson and ye-yé (Serge Gainsbourg, Françoise Hardy, Juliette Gréco, Gillian Hills), Italian film music (Ennio Morricone, Nino Rota, Piero Piccioni), twentieth-century classical (Edgard Varèse, Erik Satie, John Cage, Karlheinz Stockhausen, Pierre Boulez), and British light entertainment (Peter Sellers, Frankie Howerd, Peter Cook). The ACMEM-prefixed catalog numbers (e.g., ACMEM 38CD onward) distinguish reissues from original catalog.

The reissue series also returned to some Él-era original artists: the Abracadabra compilation drew on the original label catalog, and several Louis Philippe reissues appeared (ACME 15CD for Ivory Tower, ACME 23CD for Yuri Gagarin).

Catalog Number Series

The label used several distinct catalog number series across its history:

Original UK (1984):

  • EL 1, EL TWO T etc. — earliest releases, plain sequential; 7” singles

Él Benelux (1984–1985):

  • EL 1, ELT 2, EL 4, FIN 1, ELT 5 — Belgium pressings via Les Disques Du Crépuscule; “T” suffix = 12”

Transition / Early Cherry Red (1985–1986):

  • ACME 2, ACME 3, ACME 4, ACME 5, ACME 6 — albums and key early singles (Momus Circus Maximus = ACME 2)

GPO (singles, 1986–1990):

  • GPO 6T through GPO 45T — 7” and 12” singles; “T” suffix = 12”
  • e.g. GPO 6T = Louis Philippe Red Roses & Red Noses; GPO 9T = Momus Nicky; GPO 10 = Bid Reach For Your Gun

ACME (albums/LPs, 1987–1989):

  • ACME 7 through ACME 26 (and CD variants: ACME 15CD, ACME 23CD, etc.)
  • Sequential LP/compilation numbering; CD suffix = compact disc
  • ACME 10 = London Pavilion Vol II; ACME 24 = Felt Me And A Monkey On The Moon

Japan (licensed pressings, 1987–1989):

  • 85xxx-28 / 85xxx-32 — LP-sized; first Japan batch (1987)
  • 35xxx-28 — second Japan 12” batch
  • 25xxx-12 — Japan single/mini-LP batch (1989)

Reissue period (2005–present):

  • ACMEM xxx CD — reissue mono/stereo CDs; sequential three-digit numbering
  • ACMExxx BOX — box sets
  • ACMExxx CDX / CDT — deluxe/expanded editions

Open Questions

  • The precise nature of Lord Cornelius Plum / Dave Gregory’s role at Él: the brief names him as active in the orbit but he has no primary-credit releases on Él in the Discogs DB; further research needed to establish specific contributions
  • What the proposed children’s album was: Discogs does not identify the act or the fathers whose songs were involved; the anecdote appears in the label profile text but the release was never made
  • The full circumstances of how Mike Alway negotiated the Les Disques Du Crépuscule manufacturing deal for the first six releases — whether this was a formal co-venture or a distribution-only arrangement
  • Post-Él activities of most of the label’s artists in the 1990s are underdocumented in the vault
  • The Abracadabra compilation (monde 6cd) has no released year on Discogs; unclear whether it was an original-era release or a later retrospective
  • Whether Alway had any creative role in the 2005 revival or whether Cherry Red drove the reissue program independently
