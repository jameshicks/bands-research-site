History

Large Records was founded in 1993 by Jeff Craven and Gregg Dickson in Chicago, Illinois. It is one of the earliest labels entered into Discogs (ID: 12), a marker of how well-established it became in the global house music database community from an early date.

The first release on the label was produced by Lil’ John Coleman, then working as a buyer at Loop Records in Chicago. Coleman also designed the original Large Records logo — the distinctive upturned-A wordmark that became a visual signature of the label’s early run. He produced the debut 12” under the name Ulysses (LJ-003, 1993).

The founding partners also co-founded two related imprints: 8th Bar and Swing Street, both of which operated as sublabels under the Large umbrella.

Jeff Craven is credited as Executive Producer across the bulk of the catalog, maintaining consistent creative oversight throughout the label’s active years. The label was rebranded Large Music in the 2000s, under which it continued to release and eventually moved into digital distribution.

Catalog Number Scheme

The early catalog ran under the prefix LJ- (e.g., LJ-003, LJ-005), transitioning to LAR- from around 1996 onward (LAR-019 is “Gabrielle” by Roy Davis Jr.). CD releases used LARCD- prefixes. The numbering was roughly sequential and continuous, reaching at least LAR-193 by 2014.

Sound and Aesthetic

Large Records is a foundational Chicago deep house label. The catalog is dominated by deep, jazzy house with strong influences from garage house, soul, and late-night loft sensibilities. Key aesthetic markers:

  • Live instrumentation woven into electronic productions: saxophone, trumpet, live guitar, strings. This is audible from the earliest Kerri Chandler releases (“Andromeda” on Digitalsoul Session One features string arrangements by Miri Ben-Ari) through Dennis Ferrer’s Soul Collectives series (saxophone by Talib Kibwe, trumpet by Fabio Morgera).
  • Long, hypnotic track structures: the Raw Grooves series tracks run 6–8 minutes with minimal arrangement changes — functional DJ tools built for extended play.
  • Soulful vocal house: Roy Davis Jr.’s “Gabrielle” (1996, featuring Peven Everett on vocals and trumpet) became a crossover hit and remains one of the best-known records ever associated with the label.
  • A consistent executive hand: Jeff Craven’s executive producer credit runs through releases by almost every major artist on the label, giving the catalog a coherent identity despite spanning different producers and styles within house.

The label’s sound sits squarely in the tradition of early 1990s Chicago house — influenced by the Trax/DJ International era but oriented toward a more polished, jazzy “deep house” aesthetic that would come to define the late-1990s house canon.
