Overview

Tomato was an independent New York label launched in May 1977 by Kevin Eggers as a successor to his earlier ventures Utopia and Poppy. Its catalogue centred on country blues, electric blues, and folk-rock — home to artists such as Albert King and Townes Van Zandt — with over fifty releases in its first decade and another hundred through the 1980s and 1990s. Ownership passed to Gramavision, with distribution handled by Rhino/Atlantic, before Eggers reclaimed independence in 2002 when Rykodisc absorbed Gramavision. Outside the blues world the label is best remembered for issuing the original 1977 pressing of a Philip Glass opera, an unlikely release for a roots imprint that speaks to Eggers’ eclectic connections.
