Parlophone

Discogs label ID: 2294 | Label Code: LC 0299 / LC 00299 Parent (current): Parlophone Records Ltd. (subsidiary of Warner Music International / Warner Music Group) Former parent: EMI Records Ltd. / Electric & Musical Industries Ltd. (EMI Group)

Major multinational pop/rock label. One of the oldest continuously operating record imprints in the UK, originating from the German Parlophon brand (1902) and arriving in Britain in 1923. Operated under EMI from 1931 until Warner Music Group acquired it in March 2013. Home to The Beatles in the 1960s, and across subsequent decades to Radiohead, Blur, Coldplay, Kylie Minogue, Talk Talk, and many others. Has marginal but real vault relevance: Talk Talk’s late career, Marc Almond’s post-Soft Cell solo work, and a 2013 digital single pairing Kylie Minogue with múm.

History

Origins (1902–1930) The name derives from the German Parlophon label, registered as a trademark for talking machines in 1902 by Max Straus and Heinrich Zuntz of Salon Kinematograph Co. GmbH, later absorbed into Carl Lindström A.-G. First Parlophon records pressed 1910. Parlophone proper appeared in London in 1923, administered by the English Lindström branch, pressing records domestically and importing recordings from Lindström imprints (Beka, Odeon) and American partner Okeh. Carl Lindström A.-G. was acquired by the Gramophone Company in 1926; following the 1931 merger of the Gramophone Company with Columbia Graphophone, Parlophone became a constituent of Electric & Musical Industries Ltd. (EMI).

EMI era — early decades (1931–1954) Within EMI, Parlophone occupied a secondary position, its catalog relying heavily on jazz, comedy, and imported recordings. A&R was led by Oscar Carl Preuss, a Lindström veteran, through the early 1950s.

George Martin era (1955–1965) In 1955 George Martin succeeded Preuss as head of A&R. Martin initially developed comedy and novelty talent, then pivoted the label sharply toward beat-group pop in the early 1960s. Parlophone signed The Beatles in 1962, along with Gerry & the Pacemakers, The Hollies, Billy J. Kramer & The Dakotas, and Cilla Black. The label moved from being regarded as “EMI’s dumping ground” to its dominant pop imprint almost overnight.

Post-Beatles decline and revival (1966–1979) Following The Beatles’ departure and the broader shift in the music industry, Parlophone’s profile declined through the late 1960s and early 1970s. EMI earmarked it for phasing out in 1973 in favor of the EMI label itself. The Parlophone imprint continued sporadically in certain markets through the decade, then was formally revived in 1980 for most European markets.

1980s–2000s: second wave The revived Parlophone became home to a wide range of British and international artists across pop, rock, and alternative music:

  • Pet Shop Boys (from 1985) — longest-running flagship act by release count
  • Duran Duran
  • Kylie Minogue (from 1988 via PWL transition, then independently from 2001)
  • Talk Talk (1986–1991) — late period including Spirit of Eden and Laughing Stock
  • Blur (1990–ongoing)
  • Radiohead (1992–2016)
  • Coldplay (from 2000)
  • Morrissey (post-Smiths solo, 1988 onward)
  • Marc Almond (solo, 1988–2002)
  • Supergrass
  • Hot Chip (2008–2010)
  • Gorillaz
  • Iron Maiden

Warner acquisition (2012–2013) When Universal Music Group acquired EMI’s recorded music interests in September 2012, Parlophone and the former EMI Records Ltd. were carved out as the Parlophone Label Group (PLG Holdco Ltd.) and placed for sale separately. Warner Music Group purchased PLG in March 2013. The Beatles’ catalog was not part of this transaction and went to Universal. Parlophone is now owned by Parlophone Records Ltd. (the renamed EMI Records Ltd.), itself a subsidiary of Warner Music International.

Vault-Relevant Artists

Talk Talk

Talk Talk recorded for Parlophone from 1986 onward. Their late, artistically adventurous period falls squarely in the label’s catalog: Spirit of Eden (1988) and Laughing Stock (1991) were both Parlophone releases. These albums — produced by Tim Friese-Greene — are landmark post-rock/ambient records, and Mark Hollis, Paul Webb, and Lee Harris all connect from there.

Radiohead

Radiohead signed to Parlophone in 1992 and released their full catalog — from Pablo Honey through A Moon Shaped Pool (2016) — on the label. Key personnel: Thom Yorke, Jonny Greenwood, Colin Greenwood, Phil Selway, Ed O’Brien; producer Nigel Godrich.

Marc Almond

Marc Almond (of Soft Cell) had a solo run on Parlophone 1988–2002. Soft Cell itself was on another label, but the Almond solo work is catalogued here.

Morrissey

Morrissey released solo material on Parlophone from 1988 onward (post-Smiths). The Smiths were on Rough Trade.

múm / Kylie Minogue — Whistle (2013)

A digital single co-credited to Kylie Minogue and múm, released on Parlophone in 2013 (catalog number: none; digital-only, UK and Europe). Both artists are listed as primary credits. Written by Gunnar Örn Tynes and Örvar Þóreyjarson Smárason (two of múm’s core members). Genres tagged Experimental / Folk / IDM — one of the more unusual releases in the Parlophone catalog. Release IDs: 13130909 (UK), 4509844 (Europe), 19386016 (undated pressing).
