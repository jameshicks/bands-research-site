Lowest Music & Arts

Discogs label ID: 16449 Country: Japan Address: 1-17-11 Higashitamagawagakuen, Machida-shi, Tokyo, Japan (now obsolete) Active: 1980–1985 (in DB; succeeded by ZSF Produkt from 1983) Operator: Masami Akita (Merzbow)

Overview

Lowest Music & Arts was a self-operated cassette imprint founded in 1980 by Masami Akita specifically to release his own work under the name Merzbow. It is the primary document of the original Merzbow duo period (1979–1989), when Akita worked alongside Kiyoshi Mizutani. The label’s entire 44-release catalog consists of Merzbow cassettes (plus one release crediting Reiko Azuma) recorded 1980–1985 in Tokyo.

In 1983, Akita spun off a successor label, ZSF Produkt (Discogs label ID 16450), to accommodate releases by other artists; Lowest Music & Arts continued to issue Merzbow’s own work in parallel through at least 1985. The recordings on this label document an early industrialist noise aesthetic — assemblage of tape, violin, guitar, electronics, and live performance — that predates Merzbow’s later pure harsh noise direction.

All releases carry catalog number “none” (no formal catalog numbering system was used).

Vinyl-on-demand 2012 Reissue Box Set

In 2012 German archival reissue label Vinyl-on-demand compiled the Lowest Music & Arts catalog as Lowest Music & Arts 1980–1983 (cat. VOD108; Discogs release IDs: 3993515, 3984669, 4000941). The set comprised 10 LPs + one 7”, limited edition, numbered, remastered. It reproduced material drawn from the original cassettes including Hyper Music, Metal Acoustic Music, Balance Of Neurosis, Merz Collection 007, Tridal Production, Mechanization Takes Command, Solonoise vols. 1 & 2, Yahatahachiman, Escape Mask, Fuckexercise, and Lowest Music vols. 1 & 2. Credits on the box set confirm Kiyoshi Mizutani playing various instruments on source live materials (tracks E1–H), while Masami Akita is credited with music, composition, and artwork.
