Delmark Records

Chicago-based independent record label founded by Robert G. Koester in 1953, originally under the name Delmar Records (named after Delmar Boulevard, the street of his first record store in St. Louis, Missouri). Relocated to Chicago in 1958 and renamed Delmark Records in 1963 after a legal dispute. One of the oldest continuously operating independent jazz and blues labels in the United States. Discogs label ID: 33470.

Overview

Delmark’s history spans more than seven decades of American vernacular music. Koester’s guiding principle was documentation: capturing blues and jazz artists working in Chicago often before they had any significant commercial profile. The label’s catalog divides broadly into two periods. The first era (1950s–1970s) focused on Chicago blues documentation and avant-garde jazz — most crucially the output of musicians connected to the AACM. The second era (1980s–present) continued the blues catalog, added hard bop and mainstream jazz, and gradually opened to experimental artists from Chicago’s post-1990s creative music scene, including Chicago Underground Trio, Rob Mazurek, and Jason Ajemian.

Founding and early history. Koester opened his first record store in St. Louis in 1953 and started the Delmar label to document regional jazz and traditional blues. When he moved to Chicago he established the Jazz Record Mart, which became a landmark institution for collectors and musicians alike. In 1963, renamed as Delmark Records, the label began systematically recording Chicago’s South and West Side blues scene. The label’s long-running address at 4243 N. Lincoln Ave. (later 4121 N. Rockwell) became a resource hub. Koester died in May 2021 at age 88; the label continued operating after his death.

AACM connection. From the mid-1960s onward, Delmark became the primary commercial outlet for AACM artists. The association was unusual: a blues label providing infrastructure for experimental jazz composers. Key releases in this vein began in 1966 with the Roscoe Mitchell Sextet’s Sound (DL-408), regarded as one of the founding documents of the AACM sound. This was followed by Joseph Jarman’s Song For (1967), Anthony Braxton’s debut 3 Compositions Of New Jazz (1968), and Muhal Richard Abrams’s Levels And Degrees Of Light (1968). The label also issued records by Sun Ra beginning in 1967 (Sun Song, DL-411), making it among Sun Ra’s earliest commercial distributors.

Blues catalog. Delmark’s blues documentation is among the most significant in the genre. The label’s landmark blues release remains Junior Wells’ Hoodoo Man Blues (1965, DL-612), recorded with Buddy Guy on uncredited guitar — one of the most important documents of Chicago electric blues. Magic Sam’s West Side Soul (1968) and Black Magic (1969) are comparably foundational West Side blues recordings. The label also archived folk blues artists including Sleepy John Estes, Big Joe Williams, and Roosevelt Sykes, and documented harmonica blues through Carey Bell, Junior Wells, and others.

Production infrastructure. Robert G. Koester was producer or supervising producer on nearly 900 releases across the label’s history — an extraordinary concentration of creative control in a single individual. Engineer Stu Black handled original recording duties on many of the 1960s–70s sessions. Producer Steve Wagner became Koester’s primary collaborator on later releases, credited on nearly 200 records. Designer Zbigniew Jastrzebski handled cover art and design on roughly 260 releases, and Al Brandtner on around 170, giving the label’s physical catalog a consistent visual identity.
