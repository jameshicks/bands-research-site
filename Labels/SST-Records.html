SST Records

Discogs label ID: 9335
Founded: 1978, Hermosa Beach, California
Founder: Greg Ginn
Sublabels: New Alliance Records (ID 35723), Cruz Records (ID 14892), Issues Records (ID 1571398), SST Video (ID 1398703)
Label Code: LC 46616

Overview

SST Records was founded in 1978 by Greg Ginn in Hermosa Beach/Lawndale, California, originally as an electronics company called SST (Solid State Tuners) Electronics. Ginn converted it to a record label to release Black Flag’s debut EP Nervous Breakdown (SST 001, 1979), with cover art by Raymond Pettibon — Ginn’s younger brother. The label quickly became the central institution of Los Angeles hardcore punk, home to Black Flag, Minutemen, Descendents, and Saccharine Trust in its early years.

From 1982 onward SST expanded its reach well beyond Southern California, signing Hüsker Dü (Minneapolis), Meat Puppets (Phoenix), Bad Brains (Washington D.C. via NYC), Sonic Youth (New York), Dinosaur Jr. (Massachusetts), Screaming Trees (Washington State), and Soundgarden (Seattle) — making it the de facto national distributor of American post-hardcore and alternative rock through the mid-1980s. By 1987 the catalog numbered into the hundreds and encompassed noise rock, experimental jazz, avant-garde, reggae, and fringe rock.

Spot (Glen Lockett) served as house producer/engineer on the majority of early releases (SST 001 through roughly SST 060), crediting over 254 SST releases by Discogs count. Joe Carducci served as label manager through much of the 1980s and produced a number of SST releases himself (Saint Vitus, Angst, D.C. 3). Raymond Pettibon provided visual identity via cover drawings on nearly every major release through 1986 (292 design credits on SST); his relationship with the label later became acrimonious.

The label’s catalog number sequence (SST 001 through approximately SST 280 by 1990, with parallel cassette series SSTC and CD series SST CD) provides the clearest map of the label’s scope. SST 001 (Nervous Breakdown, 1979) through SST 065 (I Against I, 1986) covers the classic hardcore era; SST 100 onward reflects the label’s broader expansion into alternative rock and experimental territory.

SST moved offices several times — Hermosa Beach (1978–79), Torrance (1980–81), Redondo Beach (1982–84), Hawthorne (1984–86), Carson (1987–92), Long Beach (1996–2007), Taylor, Texas (2007–present). The SST Superstore opened on Sunset Blvd, West Hollywood, in December 1992 and moved with the label to Long Beach in 1996.

Catalog Scope

The primary SST catalog ran from SST 001 (1979) to approximately SST 280+ (1990). Parallel series:

  • SSTC — cassette releases (e.g., SSTC 035, SSTC 052)
  • SST CD — CD-only or CD-first releases (e.g., SST CD 007, SST CD 066)
  • SSTV — video releases (e.g., SSTV 002)
  • PSST — promotional / special releases (e.g., PSST E39, PSST E58)
  • BOGUS — one-off series (BOGUS 001 = Black Flag Thirsty And Miserable)

Total Discogs release entries: 2,146 (including reissues and pressings across all series).

Research Queue

  • Joe Carducci’s exact tenure as label manager at SST — when did he join and when did he depart? One source (his book Rock and the Pop Narcotic or a contemporary interview) should resolve this.
  • Was the Raymond Pettibon / SST dispute over original artwork the primary reason for the mid-1980s break, or were there other contractual issues? One contemporaneous interview or documented account should resolve this.
  • Did Sonic Youth’s SST deal cover only Evol and Sister, or did it include additional unreleased material? The Discogs entries show Starpower (SST 080) as a separate single — was this covered under the same deal?
  • Damaged (SST, 1981) was reportedly originally intended for MCA/Unicorn distribution but MCA president Al Coury refused to release it — was SST’s eventual distribution of the record the label’s first nationally distributed release? One contemporaneous account (e.g. Michael Azerrad’s Our Band Could Be Your Life) resolves this.
  • Ethan James’ Radio Tokyo studio: was this the primary facility for Minutemen sessions (Double Nickels, What Makes A Man Start Fires?, Buzz Or Howl)? One studio-credit cross-check or interview resolves.
