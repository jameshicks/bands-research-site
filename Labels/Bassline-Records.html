Bassline Records

Brooklyn, New York deep house and garage house label founded in 1993, run by Victor Simonelli, Vincent Dimino, and Barry Koven under the parent umbrella Bassline Records Inc. One of the defining underground house imprints of the mid-1990s NYC scene, distributed through Colonel Records.

  • Sublabels: Big Big Trax (ID: 7531), Brooklyn Trax (ID: 2674)

History

Bassline Records launched in 1993 with NY’s Finest’s “Do You Feel Me” (BLR-01), produced and mixed by Simonelli with programming and engineering by Mac Quayle. The twelve received ten pressings on the main US edition and went on to significant European licensing, making it one of the most widely circulated soulful-house records of the era. The catalog numbers ran sequentially (BLR-01 through BLR-031 by 1999), giving the imprint a closely managed identity uncommon in the fractured mid-1990s underground market.

The label’s core aesthetic was gospel-tinged, soulful deep house grounded in the NYC/NJ garage tradition — lush vocals, live keyboards, and productions built around feel rather than pure dancefloor utility. Victor Simonelli served as executive producer across virtually the entire catalog (51 credited releases), while Dimino and Koven held the executive producer designation on the majority of releases as business partners. Creative production was parceled out to a small stable: Marc Pomeroy (7 releases as producer/arranger/mixer), Michael Hadzantonis (6), Brian Tappert of Soulsearcher (5), Gary Tutalo (5), Woody Bianchi (5), Jazz-N-Groove (5).

Through 1994–1995 the label expanded rapidly: Connie Harvey, Mimi Johnson, Northbound, Jazz-N-Groove, Children Of The Underground, and Wiwanyag Wachipi all appeared, cementing the label’s reputation for uplifting, vocally driven house. The Big Big Trax sublabel, also launching in 1994, handled a parallel tier of deeper and more percussively driven tracks. A second sublabel, Brooklyn Trax, briefly appeared in 1996 with four releases including a notable EP featuring Romanthony (BKN-001).

By 1996 the label had a coherent mid-catalog sound: BLR-018 through BLR-023 capture the peak — Peace (3), Desy Moore / Global Mind, Javier Perez / Strive For Jive, Colonel Abrams, Elation (2), and the Holiday House EP, all leaning on the same studio circle. The 1996 CD compilation Bassline — The Comp’ (BLRCD-3001-2), mastermixed by Simonelli, served as both a showcase and a document of that peak period.

1997 brought the highest-profile release in the label’s history: Tony Humphries’s Take Home The Club (BLRCD-3003-2), a DJ mix CD compiled from the Bassline catalog. Humphries’s involvement gave the label credibility with the broader garage/house audience beyond Brooklyn. Jocelyn Brown’s “Pickin Up Promises” (BLR-025, 1997), produced by Woody Bianchi, added a major-name vocalist to the catalog. The same year Laura Alford appeared on BLR-024.

The late catalog (1998–1999) thinned: DJ Duke / Inferno Reaction (BLR-028), Earl Bennett / Pound Boys (BLR-029), and Jordan Fields’s “The Ultra Chic EP” (BLR-031, 1999) are the final entries. The label went quiet after 1999 on its main series but the Discogs catalog notes scattered reissues and repress activity into the 2010s and beyond — NY’s Finest in particular has been reissued multiple times.

Mastering across the catalog was handled primarily by Duncan Stanbury (7 releases) and Chris Alexander (3 releases). Engineering was led by Charles Newman (4 releases) and Mike Mucci (3 releases).

Sublabels

Big Big Trax (ID: 7531)

Launched 1994 alongside the main Bassline series. Ran 19 releases (BBT-001 through BBT-019) through 1998, plus two compilation albums. Shares the soulful-house aesthetic of the parent label but leaned into more percussive and varied productions. Key releases:

  • “Your Love” — Insatiable / Moné (BBT-001, 1994)
  • “BoomBaata” — Blind Truth (BBT-002, 1994) — Blind Truth = Arthur Baker (Discogs artist 66219, realname confirmed); not a Simonelli alias
  • “No More Weeping” — Afrimerican Coalition (BBT-004, 1994) — Simonelli was a member of this group
  • “Everybody Clap Yo Hands” — A Church, A D.J. & A Sampler (BBT-005, 1994) — Simonelli alias
  • Force 1 (The Club Is Here) — Victor Simonelli (BBT / Koch Dance Force, 1995) — also credited to main Bassline
  • “The Aqua Booty Theme” — G.M.C. (BBT-009, 1995)
  • “Ya-Hey” — Native Rhythms (BBT-010, 1995)
  • Big Big Trax Vol. 1 — Various (BBT-4001, 1996) — label compilation
  • Europe Underground — Various (BBT-4002-1, 1996)
  • “Fight It (DJ Duke Remixes)” — The Music Makers (BBT-018, 1998)
  • Power Traxx EP — Various (BBT-019, 1998)

Has a Facebook page (facebook.com/Big-Big-Trax-Records-397865597037442).

Brooklyn Trax (ID: 2674)

Short-lived sublabel active only in 1996; four releases total. Catalog prefix BKN-001 through BKN-004. The most notable release is BKN-001, a joint EP by Romanthony and Eve Angel, produced and mixed by Romanthony and executive-produced by Simonelli, Dimino, and Koven. Other artists: DJs Brothers (“So Good,” BKN-002), Hot Issue! (“Hornet / Motion 96,” BKN-003), Radio 32 (“Drift Free,” BKN-004). Hot Issue! was itself a Simonelli group (per Discogs group_member data).

Scene Connections

Bassline Records operated squarely within the mid-1990s NYC/NJ soulful house and garage tradition. The label’s gospel-influenced vocal sound connected directly to the Sound Factory Bar and Club Shelter circuit, where Timmy Regisford and Larry Patterson championed the style known as “shelter music.” Simonelli’s roots at Shakedown Sound Studios in the late 1980s placed him among the first generation of Brooklyn house producers.

The Tony Humphries relationship is the clearest scene link: Humphries had long been the definitive DJ voice of the NJ garage tradition (his residency at Club Zanzibar, Newark), and his Take Home The Club CD on Bassline was a deliberate positioning of the label within that lineage.

Jazz-N-Groove (the duo of Roy Nathanson and others, active on the label as both artists and producers) brought a jazz-influenced orchestral layer. Jocelyn Brown — a veteran vocalist whose “Somebody Else’s Guy” (1984) remains a house touchstone — appeared on BLR-025, linking Bassline to the older NYC disco/garage vocal tradition.

Romanthony’s appearance on Brooklyn Trax BKN-001 (1996) connects the label to the NJ/NY gospel-house underground via Equal Vision / Glasgow Underground pathways Romanthony would later travel.

Colonel Abrams (BLR-022, 1996) brought another link to 1980s NYC dance music — Abrams had a series of UK house hits and was a regular on the underground circuit through the 1990s.

The label’s distributor, Colonel Records, was a specialist dance distributor serving the independent underground market in the Northeast.
