Overview

Paw Tracks launched in 2003 as a Carpark Records imprint dedicated to Animal Collective and the constellation of artists around them. Its experimental, psychedelic-leaning catalogue was densest in the 2000s — 26 releases — before tapering to 11 in the following decade. The roster drew from Animal Collective’s inner circle: Panda Bear, engineer and collaborator Rusty Santos, designer Rob Carmichael, and mastering engineer Joe Lambert, alongside outside signings such as Dent May.

Notes (web) — Animal Collective’s move to Domino Records

Why Animal Collective signed to Domino for major releases from 2007

The FatCat contract ended. Animal Collective had a two-album deal with FatCat Records covering Sung Tongs (2004) and Feels (2005). When that deal concluded after the Feels tour in summer 2006, the band chose not to renew. The Feels tour period was personally difficult — Deakin’s father had died unexpectedly — and the end of the contract provided a natural transition point. (Source: search snippet summarising The Skinny interview and Magnet Magazine, 2007; full Magnet URL https://magnetmagazine.com/2007/08/22/animal-collective-the-theory-of-evolution/ — paywalled, only snippet confirmed.)

Family circumstances were the decisive factor in choosing Domino. Geologist (Brian Weitz) stated: “one of the reasons we signed with Domino is that when we explained our family situations, they looked us in the eye and said, ‘OK.‘” Panda Bear (Noah Lennox), who had moved to Lisbon with his wife and child in 2004, said: “Part of it was financial. I’ve got a kid to worry about.” He noted the responsibility made him more careful professionally — he felt he could no longer afford to “screw up.” (Source: stübermania! Substack reprint of a 2007 interview, https://stubermania.substack.com/p/a-conversation-with-animal-collective)

Domino’s attitude toward creative autonomy mattered. Avey Tare (Dave Portner) told The Skinny: “[Domino] wanted to record us for who we are and what we have going on. They just wanted to help us progress in what we’re doing and that was pretty sweet.” He also told Drowned in Sound that the recording budget for Strawberry Jam was “a far cry” from the budget for Here Comes the Indian, and that having more resources allowed the band to plan rather than scramble. (Sources: The Skinny https://www.theskinny.co.uk/music/interviews/animal-collective-unsolved-mysteries; Drowned in Sound archived interview https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-sticky-fingers-animal-collective-discuss-strawberry-jam-2328861/)

Paw Tracks continued by design, not by default. In the same Drowned in Sound interview, Panda Bear described Paw Tracks as “more active than ever” at the time of Strawberry Jam. Avey Tare explained that Paw Tracks was started specifically to release side projects, and the label continued signing and releasing work by other artists (Ariel Pink, Eric Copeland, Black Dice, First Nation, Tickley Feather). The arrangement was not a co-release deal: Domino handled Animal Collective’s group studio albums; Paw Tracks (distributed by FatCat) remained the outlet for member side projects and roster artists. (Source: Drowned in Sound archived interview https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-sticky-fingers-animal-collective-discuss-strawberry-jam-2328861/)

Summary of motivation. The shift was driven by three overlapping factors: (1) the natural end of a fixed FatCat contract; (2) financial and family stability — Domino is a larger label and could offer better advances and touring support; (3) Domino’s reputation as “a pretty open minded and adventurous label” that promised no commercial pressure. There is no evidence of a falling-out with FatCat; Panda Bear expressed regret about leaving them: “It was really, really hard … they deserve a lot of credit for the situation we find ourselves in today.” (Source: stübermania! Substack https://stubermania.substack.com/p/a-conversation-with-animal-collective)
