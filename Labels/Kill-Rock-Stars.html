Kill Rock Stars

Discogs ID: 5661
From: Olympia, WA → Portland, OR
Active: 1991–present
Co-founded by: Slim Moon and Tinúviel (Tinúviel Sampson)
Parent: Exceleration Music Partners LLC (Discogs ID: 2411197)

Overview

Kill Rock Stars launched in Olympia, Washington in 1991 with a compilation that functioned simultaneously as an origin document and a declaration of intent — twelve acts from the Pacific Northwest underground including Bikini Kill, Bratmobile, Nation Of Ulysses, Unwound, Mecca Normal, Melvins, Jad Fair, and a then-unknown Nirvana, whose “Beeswax” is their only KRS release (recorded before Nevermind). Slim Moon co-founded the label with Tinúviel Sampson; in its first three years it became the primary label of the riot grrrl movement.

The label’s post-riot grrrl trajectory was shaped most durably by Elliott Smith (172 releases, 1995 onward) and, in the 2000s, by The Decemberists’ ascent from Olympia indie to college-radio staple. The Gossip’s Standing In The Way Of Control (2006) brought a rare mainstream crossover. Slim Moon left at the end of 2006 for Nonesuch Records; the label continued under Tinúviel and eventually became part of Exceleration Music Partners. From the 2010s onward, an ambitious reissue program added Delta 5, Essential Logic, The Raincoats, Liliput/Kleenex, and others to a catalog whose through-line is feminist and post-punk.

1991–1993: Olympia and the Riot Grrrl Moment

KRS 201 — Kill Rock Stars (1991) — was the founding document. Produced in Olympia with design by Tinúviel, lacquer by John Golden, and technical contributions from Calvin Johnson (K Records / Beat Happening), Pat Maley, and John Goodmanson. Tracklist: Bratmobile · Some Velvet Sidewalk · Courtney Love · Nation Of Ulysses · Unwound · Mecca Normal · Nirvana (“Beeswax”) · Bikini Kill · Witchypoo · Melvins · Kicking Giant · Jad Fair.

Bikini Kill — Kathleen Hanna (vocals), Kathi Wilcox (bass), Tobi Vail (drums), Billy Karren (guitar) — released Pussy Whipped (KRS-218, 1993). Produced and engineered by Stuart Hallerman. Twelve tracks including “Rebel Girl.” Cover photography by Tammy Rae Carland. Original format: Cassette.

Bratmobile’s Pottymouth (KRS 208, 1993) — Allison Wolfe (vocals/gas), Molly Neuman (drums/oil), Erin Smith (guitar/windows) — was recorded in two sessions: tracks 1–14 by Tim Green (2); tracks 15–17 (“Kiss & Ride,” “No You Don’t,” “Queenie”) by Conrad Uno and Pat Maley. “Cherry Bomb” is a Joan Jett / Kim Fowley cover, listed uncredited on the sleeve.

Unwound’s Fake Train (KRS-210, 1993) — Justin Trosper (guitar), Vern Rumsey (bass), Sara Lund (drums) — was co-produced by Steve Fisk and Unwound, with Slim Moon, Tim Green (2), and Stuart Hallerman as recording engineers. Mixed by Steve Fisk and Unwound.

Heavens To Betsy (2) — Corin Tucker and Tracy Sawyer, pre–Sleater-Kinney — also released on KRS in this period.

1994–2000: Elliott Smith, Sleater-Kinney, and After Riot Grrrl

Elliott Smith’s self-titled debut (KRS 246, 1995) was his first full-length after the Roman Candle cassette. Credits include Neil Gust (electric guitar on “Single File”), Rebecca Gates (harmony vocals on “St. Ides Heaven”), and JJ Gonson (photos). Either/Or (KRS 269, 1997) followed with minimal external credits. His run on KRS eventually grew to 172 releases including posthumous compilations and archival material.

Elliott Smith signing story

Smith was still an active member of Heatmiser when he signed with KRS as a solo artist. His solo debut, Roman Candle (1994), had been released on Cavity Search Records — not KRS — recorded on a four-track in the basement of the Portland house he shared with JJ Gonson, who was then Heatmiser’s manager.

The KRS connection came through a 1994 West Coast van tour of solo acts — variously described as “Pop Chord” — on which both Smith and Slim Moon were performing. The other participants included Tammy Watson, Carrie Akre, and Sean Croghan. Moon largely missed Smith’s opening night at the Crocodile in Seattle (the audience talked through the set), but was galvanized by Smith’s performance the following night at the Bottom of the Hill in San Francisco. Per Moon’s own account, he spent that show listening to Roman Candle in his car rather than watching the rest of the bill. He also cited a later acoustic set at Jabberjaw in Los Angeles — where the PA failed and Smith played unplugged in a courtyard to 50–75 people who “stayed dead silent for an hour” — as a formative moment.

Moon’s initial instinct was not to sign Smith to KRS but to connect him with Calvin Johnson at K: “He mentioned that he was interested in being on K Records. I knew Calvin, so over the next couple of months, I gave him a copy of the record, told him that Elliott was the real deal, and told him to come see him play. I never heard back from Calvin about that. To my knowledge, Elliott never heard from Calvin either.” (diffuser.fm)

When the K Records route stalled, Moon approached Smith directly, initially proposing a 7”. Smith reframed the offer: “Why don’t you put out my record?” Moon agreed, noting that KRS “did one record at a time, on handshake.” The result was Elliott Smith (KRS 246), released July 21, 1995 — Smith’s second solo album. Heatmiser did not formally disband until 1996, after their final album Mic City Sons (DreamWorks).

Moon described the music: “It was soft and gritty at the same time — a really rare combination.” He later expanded: “It completely blew my mind. I have never heard music as heartwrenchingly, gut-checkingly honest, intimate, and wise — before or since.” (grammy.com)

JJ Gonson’s precise role — Cavity Search, not KRS. Multiple sources confirm Gonson’s role was with Cavity Search only. Per Flavorwire, she “happened to play the tape for the folks at Portland’s Cavity Search Records while promoting Heatmiser” — Cavity Search then requested permission to release the full album. Wikipedia (Roman Candle article) and uDiscover Music corroborate: “after Gonson played the album for Cavity Search, they immediately requested permission to release it in its entirety.” No source — including the Flavorwire piece, the Grammy 30th-anniversary article, or diffuser.fm — places Gonson in the KRS negotiation. The KRS deal was initiated entirely by Slim Moon after the 1994 tour. The earlier conflict in this file (Flavorwire attributed to “KRS specifically”) was a misreading: Flavorwire correctly describes Gonson placing Roman Candle with Cavity Search, then separately describes Moon signing Smith to KRS for the self-titled second album — two distinct events, two distinct facilitators. Sources: Flavorwire — “Perfect in All Its Imperfections: Elliott Smith’s ‘Roman Candle’ at 20”; Wikipedia — Roman Candle (album) (as of 2026-10-11); uDiscover Music — “Photographer JJ Gonson Talks Elliott Smith And Capturing A Scene”.

Summary of brokers/facilitators: No formal broker. The deal was initiated by Slim Moon directly after the two met on a shared tour. The K Records gambit (Moon pitching Calvin Johnson) was an intermediary step that went nowhere. Smith himself proposed expanding the 7” idea to a full album. JJ Gonson (Heatmiser manager, Smith’s then-girlfriend) placed Roman Candle with Cavity Search Records — not KRS. She has no documented role in the KRS deal.

Sources: diffuser.fm — “The Story of Elliott Smith’s Self-Titled Second Album” (quotes from Slim Moon); KQED — “Two Decades After Its Release, Elliott Smith’s ‘Either/Or’ is Still Both”; Grammy.com — “Elliott Smith’s Debut Album Roman Candle: 30th Anniversary”; “so flawed and drunk and perfect still” (WordPress) — Slim Moon category (Moon quotes on acoustic sets and cultural context).

Sleater-Kinney’s Dig Me Out (KRS 279, 1997) — Carrie Brownstein (guitar/vocals), Corin Tucker (vocals/guitar), Janet Weiss (drums) — was produced and recorded by John Goodmanson. Jessica Lurie contributed saxophone on “It’s Enough.”

Kathleen Hanna’s solo project Julie Ruin (Discogs ID: 390236) released Julie Ruin (KRS 284, 1998) as a one-woman bedroom record. (Not to be confused with her later band The Julie Ruin, formed 2010.)

Other roster in this period: Free Kitten (Kim Gordon and Julie Cafritz), Huggy Bear (3) (UK riot grrrl), Liliput (Swiss post-punk, post-Kleenex), Lois Maffeo.

2000s: The Decemberists, The Gossip, and Expansion

The Decemberists began releasing on KRS in 2002 with Castaways And Cutouts (KRS 397). Picaresque (KRS 425, 2005) — co-produced by Chris Walla and the band, with illustration and design by Carson Ellis — was co-released with Jealous Butcher Records. Credits include Petra Haden (violin/voice), Sean Nelson (voice), Paul Brainard (trumpet/horn arrangement), and a Joanna Newsom cover (“Bridges And Balloons,” track D2). Mastered by Ed Brooks (CD); vinyl mastered by Roger Seibel at SAE Mastering. Their run on KRS continued through Her Majesty (KRS 375) and into 2012 reissues.

The Gossip’s Standing In The Way Of Control (KRS 422, 2006) — Beth Ditto (vocals), Brace Paine (guitar), Hannah Blilie (drums) — was recorded by Guy Picciotto (Fugazi) and Ryan Hadlock.

Deerhoof (31 releases), Xiu Xiu (19 releases), Marnie Stern (20 releases), and The Thermals (18 releases) all built long-running KRS affiliations in this decade. The Paper Chase’s Now You Are One Of Us (KRS 445, 2006) was produced by John Congleton.

Slim Moon left the label at the end of 2006 for an A&R role at Nonesuch Records.

2010s–present: Reissues, New Signings, and Continuity

The 20 Years Of Kill Rock Stars compilation (KRS 558, 2011) marked the label’s second decade.

Reissue program: An intentional archive of feminist and post-punk history — Liliput (2011), Kleenex/Liliput First Songs (2016), Delta 5 Singles & Sessions 1979–81 (2012), Essential Logic Fanfare In The Garden (2012), The Raincoats (2017), Team Dresch (2019), Frumpies (2020), Heavens To Betsy These Monsters Are Real (2018) and Calculated (2014).

New signings (selected): Filthy Friends (Corin Tucker + Peter Buck, 2017–2019), Cindy Wilson (B-52s, 2017/2022), Big Joanie (London, 2022), TEKE::TEKE (Montreal, 2021/2023/2025), Survival Knife (Justin Trosper post-Unwound, 2013), Wimps (2015/2018), Lithics (2018), Shamir (2023), Ron Gallo (2023/2025), Linda Perry (2025), Peaches (KRS860, 2026).

Comedy releases: Cameron Esposito (KRS-592, 2014), Hari Kondabolu (KRS587, 2014), Kurt Braunohler / Kristen Schaal (KRS595, 2016).

Research Queue

February 1991. Slim Moon obtained a business license in February 1991 — this is the formal founding date. The first product (spoken word 7”s) arrived in April 1991; the first KRS compilation was released in August 1991 at the International Pop Underground Convention. Wikipedia confirms “Founded: February 1991.” The Olympia Music History Project interview with Slim Moon (olympiamusichistory.org) corroborates the business-license date. Sources: Wikipedia – Kill Rock Stars (as of 2026-06-17); Olympia Music History Project – Slim Moon interview.

Evergreen State College / Olympia scene. Tinúviel moved to Olympia in 1989 to finish school at Evergreen State College, where she became part of the same indie/underground music community as Slim Moon. They were friends in the Olympia scene before KRS existed. Tinúviel came into the label “a few months after” Slim started it (so roughly mid-1991, per Slim Moon’s own account in the Olympia Music History Project interview). The Smith College Archives holds the “Tinuviel papers” (finding aid at findingaids.smith.edu/repositories/2/resources/977), which confirms the Evergreen connection — the finding aid returned a 403 on direct fetch, but the description was surfaced via search. Sources: Olympia Music History Project – Slim Moon interview; Smith College Finding Aids – Tinuviel papers.

Full co-operator; both creative and business. Slim Moon described Tinúviel as “my partner for a couple of years” and said she “was very important” to the label’s early operations. When others characterized her as handling only the creative side while Slim handled business, Tinúviel corrected this: “Actually, I do both.” No formal title (A&R, GM) has been documented in any published interview or profile. She was functionally a label co-operator — handling design, artwork, and business matters — from ~mid-1991 until she moved first to Seattle then to New York, where remote collaboration became impractical in the pre-internet era. She left KRS before Slim’s 2006 departure. After leaving she founded Villa Villakula (New York/Boston, 1994–1998). Sources: Olympia Music History Project – Slim Moon interview; search result summary from ExecReview – “It reeked of hope and ambition” (site returned connection error on direct fetch).

  • Timeline of the Olympia → Portland move

    Calvin Johnson (Discogs ID: 135968) is the K Records founder (K label ID: 886, “American label and distributor from Olympia, WA, initially started as a cassette-only label in 1982”). His connection to KRS is documented but limited. On the Discogs database, only one release appears on both K and KRS simultaneously: “Spring Tour ‘96” (1996). However, there is significant shared roster overlap — artists who released on both labels include Elliott Smith, Bratmobile, Mecca Normal, Sleater-Kinney, Mirah, The Gossip, Heavens to Betsy, Karp, and Cadallaca. Calvin Johnson’s personal KRS credits: “Technician [Technical Assistance]” on the KRS 201 compilation (1991) and engineer on “10 Million Hours A Mile” (1997, a KRS release). He is also a member of The Halo Benders with Steve Fisk. The K–KRS relationship is one of Olympia scene solidarity and shared roster rather than formal co-production infrastructure. See also: K.

    Stuart Hallerman (Discogs ID: 281169) owns and operates Avast! Recording Co. (Discogs label ID: 276755), a Seattle-based recording facility active from ~1990. The original “Avast! Classic” (1325 N. 46th St.) ran until 2008; a new studio opened 2005 at 601 NW 80th St. KRS credits: Pussy Whipped (Bikini Kill, 1993, producer/engineer), Fake Train (Unwound, 1993, recorded by), The Union Themes (Longstocking, 2000), Lend You A Hand (Longstocking, 2001). Wider Avast credits include Soundgarden (Louder Than Love 1989, Fell On Black Days 1994–95, A-Sides 1997), Death Cab for Cutie (Transatlanticism 2003, Plans 2005, Codes And Keys 2011), Earth (Earth 2, 1993), Tiger Trap, Evil Stig (Joan Jett + Bikini Kill). Full article: Stuart Hallerman.

    Tim Green (2) (Discogs ID: 302885) is a guitarist, producer, and engineer; runs Louder Studios (San Francisco → Grass Valley, CA). He was a guitarist in The Nation of Ulysses (Dischord, 1990–1992) before shifting to production work. His KRS run is among the densest of any non-Slim Moon figure in the early catalog: Yeah Yeah Yeah Yeah / Our Troubled Youth (Bikini Kill 7”, 1992), Pottymouth (Bratmobile, 1993, tracks 1–14), These Monsters Are Real (Heavens to Betsy, 1993), Tattle Tale (1993), Fake Train (Unwound, 1993), Thee Friendship Village E.P. (1993), Elephantitus Of The Night (Fitz of Depression, 1994), The Scientific Supercake L.P. (1994), Desperate, Scared, But Social (Long Hind Legs, 1995), and a string of 1997–1999 sessions. He also co-produced the Sleater-Kinney debut LP (1995, Villa Villakula). Full article: Tim Green (2).

    Steve Fisk (Discogs ID: 49143) is an American producer/engineer/musician based in Seattle, associated with K, Sub Pop, SST, and Kill Rock Stars across four decades. His KRS credits run from Groaning Machine (Wipers, 1991) through the full Unwound studio run (Fake Train 1993, New Plastic Ideas 1994, The Future Of What 1995, Repetition 1996, Challenge For A Civilized Society 1998, A Single History 1999) and continue into Deerhoof’s American Gong (2010) and Long Hind Legs (2023). He is also a member of The Halo Benders (with Calvin Johnson). Wider credits include Beat Happening (four K albums), Screaming Trees (SST), Low (The Curtain Hits The Cast, 1996), Soul Coughing (Irresistible Bliss, 1996), Minus The Bear, and Car Seat Headrest (Teens Of Denial, 2016, Matador). Full article: Steve Fisk.

    Guy Picciotto (Discogs ID: 279521, full article at Guy Picciotto) had multiple KRS touchpoints, not just the Gossip. Discogs credits: (1) Join Our Club (Heavens to Betsy, 1993, KRS) — Recorded By; (2) The Casual Dots (2004, KRS 404) — Producer, Mixed By; (3) Standing In The Way Of Control (The Gossip, 2006, KRS 422) — Recorded By (with Ryan Hadlock). He also mixed Revolution Girl Style Now (Bikini Kill archival, 2015, Bikini Kill Records — not KRS). His KRS relationship spans 1993–2006 and encompasses both the riot grrrl era and the mid-2000s peak. Not a staff relationship but a recurring outside presence across 13 years. The Gossip Music for Men (2009) and later records do not show Picciotto as credited — the Standing session appears to be his last KRS work.

Confirmed. Villa Villakula (Discogs label ID: 62260) was founded by Tinúviel and operated 1994–1998, initially from New York then Boston. It released 7 records (VVK 1–14): Sleater-Kinney’s debut 7” You Ain’t It! (VVK 2, 1994) and debut self-titled LP (VVK 6, 1995) — the latter co-produced by Tim Green (2); also Ruby Falls, Dame Darcy, Un Cuerpo Exquisito, and Victory at Sea. The label ran concurrently with Tinúviel’s Kill Rock Stars co-founder role. Full stub: Villa Villakula.

Bonus — James Murphy credit on VVK 1 and VVK 3: A “James Murphy” is credited as engineer on Ruby Falls “The Spirit Is Willing” (VVK 1, recorded Brooklyn, February 6–7, 1994) and on the Move Into The Villa Villakula compilation (VVK 3, 1994). The LCD Soundsystem James Murphy (born 1970, Princeton Junction NJ) was in the band Pony from 1992 until mid-1994 and immediately shifted to engineering work in New York in 1994, building his own studio around this time. The Brooklyn location and February 1994 date are consistent with his known movements. However, no source directly identifies this as the LCD Soundsystem James Murphy — it is plausible but unconfirmed. “James Murphy” is a common name and this could be a different person. Note for future research. Sources: Discogs – Ruby Falls “The Spirit Is Willing”; Wikipedia – James Murphy (electronic musician) (as of 2026-06-17).
