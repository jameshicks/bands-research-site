Ipecac Recordings

Independent avant-garde/experimental label. Discogs label ID: 48048. Founded 1999 by Mike Patton and Greg Werckman. Based in Orinda, CA. Tagline: “Making people sick since 1999.”

Overview

Ipecac was launched by Mike Patton (of Faith No More, Mr. Bungle) and his manager Greg Werckman as an outlet for music too idiosyncratic for the major-label system. The catalog reflects Patton’s omnivorous tastes: noise, metal, avant-jazz, experimental electronic, hip-hop, and film scores — but with a consistent refusal of genre safety. The label became one of the rare independents where the founder’s personal projects coexist with genuinely unrelated curatorial picks.

The first release was the self-titled Fantômas debut (IPC-1, 1999) — Patton’s avant-metal supergroup — setting the tone. The earliest catalog also introduced Melvins to the label, beginning a relationship that has generated more Ipecac releases than any other artist (over 300 pressings cataloged on Discogs by 2025).

Distribution shifted over time: Caroline Distribution (US) through ~2000; Southern Records (Europe, 2001–2006); Fontana (worldwide, 2006+).

Key Artists

Melvins (Discogs ID: 87652)

The label’s most prolific artist by far — over 325 pressings cataloged. A near-continuous presence from 1999 onward, with definitive Ipecac albums including Hostile Ambient Takeover (2002), (A) Senile Animal (2006), Nude With Boots (2008), Freak Puke (2012), and continuing through Tarantula Heart (2024) and Thunderball (2025). See Melvins.

Mike Patton’s Projects

Patton maintains several active projects on the label:

  • Fantômas (with Dave Lombardo, Trevor Dunn, Trey Spruance) — avant-metal/noise supergroup; four studio albums 1999–2004 + Suspended Animation (2005)
  • Tomahawk (6) (with Duane Denison, Kevin Rutmanis, John Stanier) — noise rock/alternative; active 2001–2023
  • Dead Cross (2017–present; with Justin Pearson, Gabe Serbian, Michael Crain) — hardcore punk; two albums on Ipecac
  • Mr. Bungle — returned to Ipecac for The Raging Wrath of the Easter Bunny Demo (2020) and The Night They Came Home (2021)
  • Faith No More — Sol Invictus (2015), after a 18-year gap; also singles/live
  • Peeping Tom (3) — Patton’s hip-hop/soul project, Peeping Tom (IPC-77, 2006)
  • Kaada/Mike Patton — experimental collaborations with Norwegian composer Kaada: Romances (2004), Bacteria Cult (2016)
  • Mike Patton solo — A Perfect Place soundtrack (2008), Mondo Cane (2010, Italian pop covers), The Solitude of Prime Numbers soundtrack (2011), 1922 (2018)

Post-Metal / Sludge

  • Isis (Discogs ID: 225447) — label’s dominant post-metal act; Oceanic (2002), Panopticon (2004), In the Absence of Truth (2006), Wavering Radiant (2009); catalog reissues continue through 2025. Key members: Aaron Turner, Jeff Caxide, Aaron Harris (2), Bryant Clifford Meyer, Clifford Meyer
  • Palms (4) — Isis-adjacent supergroup (post-Isis members + Chino Moreno); self-titled debut 2013
  • Old Man Gloom — adjacent via shared membership
  • Sumac — Aaron Turner post-Isis project (on other labels but connected)

Noise / Experimental

  • Dälek (Discogs ID: 26511) — noise-rap from Newark NJ; first major release From Filthy Tongue… (2002); Absence (2005), Abandoned Language (2007), Gutter Tactics (2009), Endangered Philosophies (2017), Precipice (2022), Negro Necro Nekros (2024), The Brilliance of a Falling Moon (2026)
  • Daughters (Discogs ID: 514141) — noise rock / post-hardcore; You Won’t Get What You Want (IPC-205, 2018) widely regarded as a late-career landmark
  • Human Impact (Discogs ID: 7531920) — NYC post-punk/noise; lineup includes Unsane and Cop Shoot Cop alumni; Human Impact (2020) recorded by Martin Bisi; Gone Dark (2024)
  • Unsane (Discogs ID: 138729) — Visqueen (IPC-85, 2007)
  • Oxbow (Discogs ID: 190161) — San Francisco avant-noise; Love’s Holiday (IPC-262, 2023)
  • Hella (Discogs ID: 218092) — Sacramento math-rock/noise duo; There’s No 666 in Outer Space (IPC-83, 2006)
  • Melt-Banana (Discogs ID: 190371) — Japanese noise-punk; split with Napalm Death (IPC-179, 2016)
  • The Locust (Discogs ID: 125425) — Safety Second, Body Last EP (IPC-61, 2005); produced by Alex Newport
  • Orthrelm (Discogs ID: 214040) — DC math/noise duo; OV (IPC-64, 2005)
  • Retox (3) (Discogs ID: 2260857) — San Diego post-hardcore; includes Justin Pearson; Ugly Animals (IPC-130, 2011)
  • Phantomsmasher (Discogs ID: 93269) — JG Thirlwell / Weasel Walter / Dave Weckl project; self-titled (IPC-31, 2002)
  • Steroid Maximus (Discogs ID: 262712) — JG Thirlwell orchestral project; Ectopia (IPC-28, 2002)

Dark Jazz / Doom / Ambient

  • Bohren & der Club of Gore (Discogs ID: 68140) — German doom-jazz; four albums: Black Earth (IPC-48, 2004), Dolores (IPC-108, 2008), Beileid (IPC-128, 2011), Piano Nights (IPC-155, 2014), Patchouli Blue (IPC-218, 2020)
  • Lustmord (Discogs ID: 8265) — dark ambient legend; Pigs of the Roman Empire (IPC-54, 2004, split with Melvins)

Experimental Hip-Hop / Electronic

  • Kid606 (Discogs ID: 552) — glitch/digital hardcore; Down With the Scene (ipc-7, 2000), Kill Sound Before Sound Kills You (IPC-46, 2003)
  • Omar Rodriguez-Lopez (Discogs ID: 274511) — released 23 solo albums on Ipecac in 2016–2017 as a dedicated catalog sub-series (IPC-ORL1 through IPC-ORL23); see full series breakdown below
  • General Patton / The X-Ecutioners — hip-hop/turntablist collaboration (IPC-60, 2005)
  • Deltron 3030 — adjacent; Dan The Automator released one album via Ipecac (2006)

Jazz / Avant

  • Trevor Dunn (Discogs ID: 273038) — bassist, Fantômas/Mr. Bungle member; Trevor Dunn’s Trio-Convulsant (IPC-52, 2004); later solo/duo work with King Buzzo
  • Eyvind Kang (Discogs ID: 174688) — avant-jazz violist/composer; Virginal Co Ordinates (IPC-050, 2004), Athlantis (IPC-87, 2007), The Narrow Garden (IPC-132, 2012)
  • Zu (Discogs ID: 212096) — Italian noise-jazz trio (sax/bass/drums); Carboniferous (IPC-110, 2009) produced by Mike Patton, Cortar Todo (IPC-168, 2015)
  • Ruins (Discogs ID: 60844) — Japanese avant-rock duo; Tzomborgha (IPC-35, 2002)
  • King Buzzo (Discogs ID: 344783) — Melvins frontman solo; This Machine Kills Artists (IPC-159, 2014); with Trevor Dunn as duo
  • J.G. Thirlwell — Oscillospira (IPC-221, 2020, with Simon Steensland)

Other Notable

  • The Jesus Lizard (Discogs ID: 282081) — reunited post-punk/noise rock legends signed to Ipecac; Rack (IPC-280, 2024) and Cost of Living (2024)
  • Crystal Fairy (Discogs ID: 5312262) — supergroup: Omar Rodriguez-Lopez, Teri Gender Bender (Le Butcherettes), Dale Crover (Melvins), Buzz Osborne; self-titled (IPC-180, 2017)
  • Queens of the Stone Age — Songs for the Deaf (IPC-41, 2003); Era Vulgaris (IPC-091, 2007); early relationship with label
  • The Desert Sessions — Josh Homme’s collaborative series; The Desert Sessions 9 & 10 (IPC-44, 2003)
  • Spotlights (3) (Discogs ID: 5068805) — Boston post-metal/shoegaze; Seismic (IPC-192, 2017), Love & Decay (IPC-210, 2019), Alchemy for the Dead (IPC-263, 2023)
  • Tētēma (Discogs ID: 4148559) — Mike Patton + Australian composer Marcus Reuter; Geocidal (IPC-167, 2014), Necroscape (IPC-219, 2020)
  • Human Impact (2) — see above
  • Nevermen (Discogs ID: 4850052) — Patton + Tunde Adebimpe (TV on the Radio) + Doseone; Nevermen (IPC-173, 2015)
  • Jean-Claude Vannier (Discogs ID: 150182) — French composer; Corpse Flower (2019, with Patton); later Ipecac reissues of his classic work
  • Ennio Morricone — Crime and Dissonance (IPC-66, 2005), a Morricone film score compilation
  • Dave Lombardo (Discogs ID: 251591) — Slayer/Fantômas drummer; Rites of Percussion (IPC-265, 2023) solo album
  • mclusky (Discogs ID: 109947) — Welsh noise-rock reunited; The World Is Still Here and So Are We (IPC-289, 2025)
  • Valve Studio Orchestra / Valve Corporation — video game soundtracks: Portal 2 (IPC-134, 2012), Team Fortress 2 (IPC-182, 2017), Half-Life: Alyx (2022), Dota 2 (IPC-196, 2017) — an unusual strand reflecting Werckman’s interest in game music

Omar Rodriguez-Lopez Series (IPC-ORL1–23, 2016–2017)

In 2016–2017, Omar Rodriguez-Lopez released 23 solo albums on Ipecac under a dedicated catalog sub-series using the prefix IPC-ORL (as opposed to the standard IPC-N sequence). All 23 are digital-only releases; each title exists on Discogs as two entries — one WAV and one MP3 — totaling 46 release records in the DB. There are no vinyl or CD pressings in the series.

Notes on Personnel

The series is internally heterogeneous in terms of documented credits:

  • Most albums (13 of 23) have no additional credits at all in the DB — sparse entries consistent with archival solo recordings or minimally documented sessions.
  • Several early-series albums (ORL1, ORL2, ORL9) share a recurring production team: Chris Common (engineer, mix, master), Jon Debaun (engineer), and Lars Stalfors / Marcel Rodriguez-Lopez appearing on ORL9. These are the same collaborators from the Rodriguez Lopez Productions / Mars Volta era, suggesting some recordings predate the Ipecac deal.
  • ORL21 (Birth of a Ghost) stands out as a full orchestral production with separate conductors, arrangers, and a string/wind ensemble — clearly a purpose-recorded project, not a solo home recording.
  • ORL6 (El Bien Y Mal Nos Une) credits ORL on guitar only, with no engineering/production credits, suggesting a bare archival session or live recording.

The catalog prefix structure (IPC-ORL vs. the standard IPC-NNN sequence) suggests this was treated as a discrete block within Ipecac’s catalog rather than integrated into the main running sequence. The standard IPC-NNN catalog was at approximately IPC-177 by mid-2016, and the ORL series ran concurrently without occupying those numbers.
