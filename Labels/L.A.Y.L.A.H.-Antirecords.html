L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords

Founded: 1984, Belgium Active: 1984–1989 Founder: Marc Monin Catalog prefix: LAY (vinyl); LAYCD (CD reissues) Parent label: Les Disques du Crépuscule (manufacturing and distribution only; A&R independent) Distributor: Initially Crépuscule; funding and distribution later passed to Himalaya

Overview

L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords was an independent industrial and experimental music imprint founded by Marc Monin in Belgium in 1984. It operated under the manufacturing and distribution umbrella of Les Disques du Crépuscule but maintained entirely independent A&R — Monin chose and signed the artists, and the label had its own identity distinct from Crépuscule’s art-pop and neo-classical roster.

The label became a central node for the UK and European post-industrial underground: it was the early home of Current 93, Coil, Nurse With Wound, Laibach, The Hafler Trio, Organum, Sol Invictus, 23 Skidoo, Robert Haigh, and Lustmord. Its catalog (LAY 01 through LAY 30, plus CD reissues) documented a network of artists who defined the intersection of industrial, ritual, neo-folk, and drone musics in the mid-1980s.

Funding initially came through Crépuscule, but control passed relatively quickly to distributor Himalaya — reflecting the label’s practical independence from its parent imprint despite the shared infrastructure. The label ceased new releases in 1989. Wikipedia: L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords (as of 2026-07-02)

Name

The name L.A.Y.L.A.H. is widely understood as a Thelemic reference. The word “Laylah” (لیلا) appears in Aleister Crowley’s The Book of the Law (Liber AL vel Legis, 1904), the central sacred text of Thelema, and in related Crowley writings as a name with occult significance. Given the roster — particularly Coil (whose founders John Balance and Peter Christopherson were openly engaged with Thelema and Chaos Magick, as was their former group Psychic TV) and Current 93 (whose David Tibet operated in the same milieu) — the Thelemic framing of the label name is consistent with the artists it signed.

Note: No documentary source has been located explaining what, if anything, the initials L.A.Y.L.A.H. stand for as an acronym, or whether Monin gave a formal statement on the naming. The Thelemic association is contextual rather than definitively sourced. Wikipedia: The Book of the Law

Notes (web) — LAY 11: Planned Coil LP The Angelic Conversation

What is documented

LAY 11 is documented as “Never Realized” in the esophagus.com LAYLAH discography, sitting between LAY 10 (The Fight Is On compilation, 1985) and LAY 12 (Organum, Tower of Silence, 1985). esophagus.com LAYLAH discography

The Discogs page for the L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords label identifies LAY 11 as a planned Coil LP titled The Angelic Conversation that was never released. Discogs: L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords label page

Source quality caveat

The attribution of LAY 11 to Coil rests on Discogs alone. No primary source — no Coil interview, no LAYLAH press release, no contemporary fanzine or advertisement, no liner note — has been located via web research that explicitly confirms the planned LP or names LAY 11 as a Coil release. Wikipedia’s L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords article does not list unreleased catalog entries, and the Coil discography Wikipedia article does not mention LAY 11. The esophagus.com discography lists LAY 11 as “Never Realized” without identifying the artist. The Discogs attribution is therefore the sole named source and is reproduced here with that caveat.

Context supporting the attribution

The attribution is circumstantially plausible:

  1. Coil was recording the score in 1984. A 1984 Coil interview in Unsound (archived at brainwashed.com) states: “We are recording a full length film soundtrack for the Derek Jarman film (35mm) called ‘Psychobilly’s Angelic Conversation’.” This confirms the material existed and was in production during the LAYLAH label’s active period. Brainwashed: Coil, Unsound Vol. 1 No. 5, 1984

  2. Coil had an active LAYLAH relationship. LAY 05 (How to Destroy Angels, 1984) was Coil’s debut release and the label’s fifth title. Coil contributing another release in the LAY 10–12 window (1985) is consistent with the relationship.

  3. The score was not released until 1994. When it finally appeared — as The Angelic Conversation CD on Threshold House (LOCI CD 06) — it came nine years after the film and five or six years after LAYLAH had ceased new releases. This long gap is consistent with an earlier release falling through.

  4. LAY 11 sits logically in sequence. Both its immediate neighbors (LAY 10, LAY 12) are 1985 releases, placing LAY 11 squarely in the mid-period when Coil’s film work would have been timely.

Why it did not materialize

No source found. Possible explanations consistent with the timeline:

  • Label infrastructure. Funding and distribution shifted from Crépuscule to Himalaya during this period; logistical disruption could have caused releases to fall through.
  • Material not ready / scope changed. The soundtrack may not have been in a form suitable for a standalone vinyl LP in 1985 — it eventually appeared as an 11-track CD.
  • Release deferred, then stranded. If the plan was to release the LP but it slipped, LAYLAH’s cessation of activity in 1989 would have stranded it entirely; the material was then held until Threshold House released it on CD in 1994.

None of these explanations are sourced; they are logical inferences from the timeline. No interview, label history, or other document has been located that addresses the specific fate of LAY 11.

Key Releases

LAY 05 — Coil: How to Destroy Angels (1984)

Coil’s debut release. Subtitled “Ritual Music For The Accumulation Of Male Sexual Energy.” The EP originated as material intended to be the B-side to Psychic TV’s “Silence & Secrecy,” but after John Balance and Peter Christopherson departed Psychic TV, it became the inaugural Coil release. A 12” of ritual ambient drone using gong, bullroarer, and other ceremonial instruments. Reissued in 1988. One of the most significant debut releases on the label and in the industrial/ritual canon. Wikipedia: How to Destroy Angels (Coil EP); Brainwashed: LAY 05

Context: The LAYLAH Network

L.A.Y.L.A.H. documents a specific subculture at the intersection of industrial music, occultism, and experimental composition. The core network — Current 93, Coil, Nurse With Wound — overlapped extensively with Psychic TV and the Temple ov Psychick Youth (TOPY), a network of artists and practitioners associated with Chaos Magick and Thelema. This is the genealogical reason for the label’s name.

Robert Haigh (LAY 09, 21) is a notable peripheral figure: a classically-influenced pianist who contributed to Nurse With Wound projects, and who later had a second career as Omni Trio, one of the founding drum & bass producers on Moving Shadow (from 1993). His LAYLAH releases — stripped-down solo piano — are the furthest thing from that later identity. Red Bull Music Academy: Robert Haigh interview

23 Skidoo’s appearance (LAY 23, The Culling Is Coming, 1988) is notable: this release is also cataloged as TWI 123 on Les Disques du Crépuscule. This is a dual-imprint release — the TWI number appears in the Crépuscule catalog, the LAY number situates it within the LAYLAH industrial imprint. The same release bridging both parents underscores how the two labels’ networks were physically adjacent even if aesthetically distinct.

Connections

  • Les Disques du Crépuscule — parent label for manufacturing/distribution; LAYLAH operated independently within that structure
  • 23 Skidoo — LAY 23 / TWI 123 (The Culling Is Coming) bridges both imprints
  • Coil — debut release LAY 05; grew directly out of the Psychic TV circle
  • Current 93 — most released artist on the label; David Tibet
  • Nurse With Wound — Steve Stapleton; three solo vinyl releases plus split with Current 93

Conflict Note — Crépuscule Vault Entry

The Les Disques du Crépuscule vault entry (sub-labels section) lists Death in June and Psychic TV as LAYLAH artists. The Wikipedia discography of L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords does not include either act. Death in June released primarily on their own New European Recordings (NER) imprint during 1984–88 (Burial 1984, Nada! 1985, The World That Summer 1986). Psychic TV released on Some Bizzare, Sordide Sentimental, and their own Temple Records. No LAYLAH release for either has been confirmed via web research. The Crépuscule vault page sub-labels section should be corrected. Wikipedia: Death in June discography
