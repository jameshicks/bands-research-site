Gold Standard Laboratories

Discogs ID: 12131
Abbreviation: GSL
Founded: 1993, Boulder, Colorado
Founder: Sonny Kay
Co-owner from 2001: Omar Rodríguez-López (The Mars Volta)
Relocated: San Diego, CA (2001) → Los Angeles, CA (c. 2003)
Closed: October 29, 2007
Sublabel: The Special 12 Singles Series (Discogs ID: 640479)
Total releases: ~430 (Discogs)

Independent record label. Founded by Sonny Kay while attending the University of Colorado at Boulder. In its first phase (1993–2001) it was the center of the San Diego post-punk/hardcore underground — The Locust, The VSS, The Rapture (pre-DFA), !!!, Out Hud, GoGoGo Airheart. In its second phase (2002–2007), The Mars Volta’s commercial breakthrough flooded the catalog numerically; GSL became the vehicle for early Mars Volta releases, with Rick Rubin producing and Storm Thorgerson designing. The label closed October 29, 2007 as The Mars Volta moved to Universal.

History

1993–1995: Boulder Origins

Sonny Kay founded GSL in 1993 in Boulder, Colorado, initially to release records by Angel Hair, the hardcore band he fronted. The first release (GSL-001, 1993) was Bunny Genghis. Early catalog also included Meanface, Woodpussy, ‘57 Lesbian, and Bare Minimum — small-run HC and punk 7”s.

1996–2001: San Diego Underground

Kay and GSL relocated to Berkeley in 1996, where he was also vocalist in The VSS (“futuristic” post-punk). He founded Bottlenekk Distribution in 1996, which expanded GSL’s reach and release pace. First VSS release on GSL: 21:51 (GSL 7, 1996).

Relocated to San Diego in 2001. The label’s underground phase centered on a network of interconnected San Diego and regional acts:

  • The Locust — the flagship act; multiple LPs, EPs, splits
  • The Rapture — pre-DFA, 5 releases 1998–2000 including The Chair That Squeaks 7” (GSL36, 1998)
  • !!! — 10 releases 1999–2004 including debut LP (GSL39, 2000)
  • Out Hud — 6 releases; Lab Series Vol. 2 split with !!! (GSL26, 1999)
  • GoGoGo Airheart — 15 releases; first GSL appearance Out Every Window (GSL31, 2000)
  • Arab On Radar — Providence noise-punk; split with The Locust (GSL29, 2000)
  • Le Shok — 8 releases including We Are Electrocution (GSL27, 1999)
  • The VSS — Kay’s own band
  • Year Future — Kay’s post-VSS band; 6 releases 2003–2006
  • Sunshine (6) — 9 releases 1997–2003
  • The Starlite Desperation — 9 releases 1997–2005; early split with Sunshine (GSL13, 1997)
  • Mohinder — Bay Area screamo; Everything (GSL34, 2001)
  • De Facto (2) — Omar Rodríguez-López’s pre-Mars Volta project; Megaton Shotblast (GSL45, 2001)
  • Chromatics — 10 releases 2002–2004 (pre-Italians Do It Better era, punk/post-punk phase); Chrome Rats vs. Basement Ruts (GSL59, 2003) and Plaster Hounds (GSL79, 2004)

2002–2007: The Mars Volta Era

The Mars Volta’s Tremulant EP (GSL54, 2002) launched the second phase. Omar Rodríguez-López became co-owner of GSL from 2001. The Mars Volta went on to release De-Loused In The Comatorium (GSL75, 2003) — produced by Rick Rubin, with cover art by Storm Thorgerson and Peter Curzon (the Pink Floyd design team); Frances The Mute (GSL95, 2005); Amputechture (GSL126, 2006). The Mars Volta account for 116 releases in Discogs across the GSL catalog, completely dominating the label’s later output.

The Special 12 Singles Series (GSLXYZ-) ran in 2005: limited 12” singles by Cedric Bixler-Zavala (GSLXYZ-12), Free Moral Agents (GSLXYZ-6), John Frusciante/Omar Rodríguez-López (GSLXYZ-11), and Sabertooth Tiger (GSLXYZ-7).

Final release: Damo Suzuki / Omar Rodríguez-López Please Heat This Eventually (GSL131, 2007). GSL closed October 29, 2007.
