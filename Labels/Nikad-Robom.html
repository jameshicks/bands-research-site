Operated by: Bojan Đorđević (Discogs artist ID: 1126369) and Aleksandar Konjikušić

Overview

Nikad Robom (“Never a Slave” in Serbian/Croatian) was a Yugoslav independent cassette label founded in 1984 and operating through at least 1992. Its eleven releases document a remarkably international roster for a self-operated underground imprint: alongside local Yugoslav acts, the label released concert tapes and studio recordings by visiting British, American, Canadian, and Italian avant-garde groups who performed in Yugoslavia during the 1980s.

The label’s name is an explicitly political statement. In the context of late Socialist Yugoslavia — where independent cultural production existed in a grey zone between state tolerance and official repression — a cassette label bearing the slogan “Never a Slave” signals alignment with the underground and counterculture currents of the era. Cassette culture more broadly was the main vehicle for independent music distribution in Yugoslavia during this period, enabling circulation outside the state-owned record industry.

The label was operated by Bojan Đorđević and Aleksandar Konjikušić. The surname Konjikušić appears on the label’s own releases: Predrag Konjikušić is credited for photography on Begnagrad’s Jodlovska Urška (cat. 010, 1990), suggesting the Konjikušić family had ongoing involvement in the label’s operation. The city of operation is not specified in the Discogs profile but the label’s focus on concerts in Beograd (Belgrade) and the David Thomas release titled April U Beogradu (“April in Belgrade”) points strongly toward Belgrade as the base.

The catalog shows strong connections to the Rock In Opposition and ReR network — artists such as This Heat, The Camberwell Now, Skeleton Crew (2), Elliott Sharp, Wondeur Brass, and Fred Frith (via Skeleton Crew) all appear on the label, and most of these artists had ties to Chris Cutler’s ReR Megacorp distribution network. The label also released the only known cassette release by Slovenian folk-avant group Begnagrad.

Key Artists

Skeleton Crew (2) (cat. 002, 1985) — Fred Frith and Tom Cora’s duo. Members: Fred Frith (guitar, violin, vocals, percussion) and Tom Cora (cello, vocals, bass, percussion). The Nikad Robom cassette Esta Es La Victoria is a live document from a Belgrade performance.

Joëlle Léandre / David Thomas And The Wooden Birds (cat. 003, 1986) — Split cassette. Joëlle Léandre (double bass, vocals) performs solo on Side A; David Thomas and The Wooden Birds occupy Side B. The Wooden Birds lineup: David Thomas (2) (vocals, harmonica), Tony Maimone (bass), Daved Hild (drums), George Cartwright (saxophone), Allen Ravenstine (EML synthesizer).

The Camberwell Now (cat. 004, 1987) — Post-This Heat group. Members on release: Charles Hayward (percussion, vocals), Trefor Goronwy (bass, vocals), Stephen Rickard (keyboards, tape), Maria Lamburn (saxophone, violin).

This Heat / Elliott Sharp Duo (cat. 005, 1987) — Split cassette, both recorded live in Belgrade. This Heat lineup: Charles Hayward (drums, vocals, keyboards), Charles Bullen (guitar, vocals, clarinet, tape), Gareth Williams (keyboards, vocals, bass). Artwork by Milenko Mihajlović.

Wondeur Brass (cat. 006, 1987) — Canadian all-female avant-jazz group. Members: Diane Labrosse (keyboards, vocals), Joane Hétu (saxophone, vocals, synthesizer), Danielle Palardy Roger (drums, vocals), Marie Trudeau (bass).

The Black Sheep (cat. 007, 1987) — Live in Beograd. Members: Chris Cutler (performer), Colin McLure (performer), Loek Van Saus (performer, artwork — credited as Aloysius Van Saus), Maggie Thomas (performer). Full group membership also includes Geoff Leigh, Graham Keatley, Ronnie Krepel, and Theo Olsthoorn, though only four appear on this cassette. Rooted in the Netherlands underground scene; Cutler connects them to the ReR/Rock In Opposition axis.

Tickmayer Formatio (cat. 008, 1987) — Yugoslav new music ensemble led by Stevan Kovacs Tickmayer. Large chamber ensemble including wind, strings, and percussion.

Buga Up (cat. 009, 1988) — No additional credits in Discogs.

Begnagrad (cat. 010, 1990) — Slovenian avant-folk group. See Begnagrad for full entry. Members: Bratko Bibič (accordion, melodica), Vlatko Špindler (bass guitar), Bogo Pečnikar (clarinet), Igor Muševič (drums), Boris Romih (band member; recorded by). Photography: Predrag Konjikušić.

Cirko Della Primavera (cat. 011, 1990) — Yugoslav experimental group. Members: Branko Brama (saxophone, trombone, zurla, harmonium), Apolon Česterton (bass, double bass, percussion, synthesizer), Celestin Hulahop (drums, percussion), Ištar Aerodinamik (guitar, pipe, tape), Kaspar Hauzer (xylophone, vocals; also artwork). Engineered by Boris Kovač.

Institut (2) (cat. 012, 1992) — Performance/concept art group. Members include Dejan Sretenović, Dragan Tomašević-Bačić, Srđan Aleksić, and others. Recorded by Goran Maričić.

Scene Connections

Nikad Robom operated at a nexus of the international avant-garde underground and the Yugoslav local scene. The label’s catalog maps a specific cultural moment: the late-1980s period when artists connected to the ReR Megacorp network and the Rock In Opposition orbit were touring Eastern Europe and Yugoslavia specifically.

  • ReR Megacorp — Chris Cutler’s UK distribution and label network; multiple Nikad Robom artists (This Heat, The Camberwell Now, Skeleton Crew (2), The Black Sheep) were ReR-affiliated
  • Chris Cutler (Discogs ID: 177209) — appears on both The Black Sheep cassette (cat. 007) and is a primary connection between many of these artists; member of This Heat, Henry Cow, Art Bears, Cassiber
  • Fred Frith (Discogs ID: 78003) — Skeleton Crew (2); central figure in New York / European experimental scene
  • Stevan Kovacs Tickmayer — leads Tickmayer Formatio (cat. 008); a major figure in the Novi Sad/Yugoslav contemporary composition scene
  • Boris Kovač — engineers Cirko Della Primavera’s cassette; significant Novi Sad composer and bandleader
  • Wondeur Brass — Montreal-based group later connected to Ambiances Magnétiques; the Nikad Robom cassette is an early document of their work

Open Questions

  • Cat. 001 is missing from Discogs — possibly an unrecorded first release, or the sequence began at 002
  • The city of operation is strongly implied to be Belgrade but not confirmed in the Discogs profile
  • Bojan Đorđević (Discogs ID: 1126369) has a sparse Discogs page with years active 1999–2022 — whether this is the same person as the label founder, or a disambiguation issue, is unclear
  • Aleksandar Konjikušić is listed as a label co-operator but has no Discogs artist page of his own; Predrag Konjikušić (credited on Begnagrad cassette, ID: 11884838) may be a relative
  • Buga Up (cat. 009) has no credits in Discogs beyond primary artist — origin and members unknown
  • Whether any Nikad Robom releases were officially distributed outside Yugoslavia, or whether they circulated purely via cassette trading networks
  • The gap between cat. 009 (1988) and cat. 010 (1990) and between 010/011 (1990) and 012 (1992) — whether there are unlisted releases or simply gaps in activity
  • Connection between Nikad Robom and the Slovenian scene (Begnagrad are Slovenian; whether the label had Ljubljana contacts as well as Belgrade ones)
