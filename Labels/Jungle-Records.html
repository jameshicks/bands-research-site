Jungle Records

Discogs ID: 14333 Founded: 1982 Location: Harrow, Middlesex (London), UK Address: Suite 2B, Livingstone Court, 55 Peel Road, Wealdstone, Harrow, Middlesex HA3 7QT Genres: Punk, Goth Rock, Indie Rock, New Wave, Proto-punk, Garage Rock Status: Active Parent label: None (independent) Sublabels: Fresh Records (14), Fallout Records, Skydog, Middle Earth Recordings, Goldtop Recordings, Mint (2), Mint Films, Mint Industries, Mint Sauce Records, Mint Tea (2), Obsession, Ministry Of Power, Antidote (3), Jungle Music, Jungle Visual Displays, Parole Records, Rifle, On Record (3), Tower Release

Jungle Records is a London-based independent label that has operated continuously since 1982, making it one of the UK’s longer-lived indie imprints. It built its catalog around punk, goth, and proto-punk acts — with particular depth in the New York underground (Johnny Thunders, The Heartbreakers) and the British goth scene (Christian Death, Play Dead, Fields of the Nephilim) — and has remained active into the 2020s through reissues and new signings.

Profile

Jungle Records launched in 1982 out of the London-area independent scene, entering the market in an all-vinyl era. Its opening catalog entries (JUNG 1 onward, plus the FREUD series on Fresh Records) immediately established its dual focus: British post-punk acts such as Play Dead (2) and Family Fodder, and American proto-punk alumni with strong UK cult followings.

The label absorbed Fresh Records (14) (Discogs ID 344059) in 1982 when that imprint ceased independent operation; Fresh became a Jungle sublabel and its catalog numbering (FREUD 1, FREUD 2, etc.) continued under Jungle’s umbrella. The FREUD series would go on to become Jungle’s primary album-format sequence, running well into the 2000s. Fresh Records (14) had been founded by Alex Howe and Alan Hauser; Hauser later worked as a compiler on Jungle releases including L.A.M.F. (The Lost ‘77 Mixes) (1994).

The label’s relationship with Johnny Thunders and The Heartbreakers (2) defined its early identity. The first release on the FREUD series was D.T.K. (Live At The Speakeasy) (FREUD 1, 1982), a live Heartbreakers set recorded by John “Speedy” Keen and mixed by Neil Brockbank. Subsequent Heartbreakers titles — Vintage 77 (JUNG 5), L.A.M.F. Revisited (FREUD P 4, 1984, a picture-disc edition with remixes credited to Tony James), and the definitive L.A.M.F. (The Lost ‘77 Mixes) (FREUD 044, 1994) — made Jungle the de facto custodian of the Heartbreakers’ recorded legacy in the UK. Johnny Thunders solo and collaborative material (with Patti Palladin) filled the catalog across the 1980s and 1990s, and the label released the Looking For Johnny documentary soundtrack in 2014.

In parallel, Jungle cultivated the British goth scene. Play Dead (2)‘s “Propaganda” (JUNG 2, 1982) was among the earliest UK goth singles on any label. Christian Death became Jungle’s most prolific goth signing, with releases spanning 1985 to 2001, including the All The Love All The Hate diptych (FREUD 33/34, 1989) and multiple compilations. The label’s three Gothic Rock compilation CDs (FREUD CD38, 1992; FREUD CD 051, 1995; FREUD CD059, 1998) drew on catalog by Bauhaus, The Southern Death Cult, X Mal Deutschland, Theatre Of Hate, Gene Loves Jezebel, Fields Of The Nephilim, Virgin Prunes, The Danse Society, Tones On Tail, Alien Sex Fiend, Sex Gang Children, Specimen, and others — positioning Jungle as an authoritative anthology label for the genre. Fields Of The Nephilim joined the roster in the 2000s, and Jungle issued their extensive back-catalog reissues and new material through 2024.

The Fallout Records sublabel (ID 33224) handled some Jungle catalog, particularly for UK Subs across the 1990s (FALL LP 050, FALL LP 052, FALL CD 055). The Skydog sublabel (ID 35054) connected Jungle to the French garage and proto-punk world, with ties to the late Marc Zermati’s Parisian operation; it had its own sub-imprints including Kind Of Groove, Skydog International, and Skydog Roots Of Garage Rock. Middle Earth Recordings (ID 5052) served as Jungle’s outlet for dub and ambient-adjacent material in the mid-1990s (MIDDLE 6, MIDDLE 7).

The Ministry Of Power sublabel (ID 15389) was used for Test Dept.’s 1990s catalog (MOP 5CD through MOP 8CD), covering albums Pax Americana, Pax Britannica, New World Order, and Bang On It! — an unusual signing that brought industrial percussion to Jungle’s roster.

Goldtop Recordings (ID 262479) operated as a recording studio and label imprint under the Jungle umbrella; it is associated with engineer/producer Neil Brockbank, who has been central to Jungle’s studio work and whose Goldtop Studio has handled much of the label’s recording from the 1980s onward.

From the 2000s onward, Jungle shifted toward a reissue-heavy model while continuing to sign new acts. The Eden House (atmospheric goth-rock) became a key 2010s act with multiple full albums on Jungle. The Hillbilly Moon Explosion (Swiss rockabilly) received consistent release support from 2011, spanning singles and albums into 2019. The Waldos (Johnny Thunders/Heartbreakers alumni) appeared on the roster in the 2010s, as did collaborations involving Wayne Kramer and members of MC5’s extended circle.

Jungle has also released archival material by Jimi Hendrix (early sessions / Curtis Knight material via the Skydog connection), MC5, The Stooges, The Slits, The Flamin’ Groovies, New York Dolls, Nico (3), Mercury Rev, and others — reflecting a broad proto-punk / psychedelic-rock archival mandate alongside its punk and goth catalog.

The label has maintained continuous operation into the 2020s, with releases through at least 2025 (Fields of the Nephilim, The Adicts, Urban Dogs).

Open Questions

  • Identity of Jungle Records’ founders: Discogs does not name the founding principals. The stub mentions Alex Howe and Alan Hauser as founders of Fresh Records (14); their precise role in establishing the Jungle parent label is not confirmed in Discogs data.
  • The stub associates “Mortarhate” and “Big Cat (UK) Records” and “Red Records (4)” as associated labels, but none appear as Jungle sublabels in the Discogs record. Mortarhate Records (ID 33215) is an independent entity with its own parent structure. These may be distribution or licensing relationships rather than ownership sublabel relationships.
  • The label’s website (jungle-records.net) and Wikipedia page may have founding and personnel details not captured in Discogs.
  • Neil Brockbank’s full role as in-house producer/engineer and his relationship to Goldtop Recordings/Studio deserves separate research.
