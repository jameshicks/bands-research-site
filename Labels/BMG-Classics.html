From Discogs

After reviving the RCA “lightning bolt” logo, the Victor imprint, and its vast back catalog of classical and jazz recordings, the BMG Classics label division was formed in 1987 by German-based Bertelsmann Music Group following the acquisition of the 50% stake in RCA/Ariola from General Electric.

A corporate restructuring of BMG in 2000 eventually led to the close of the BMG Classics division, with its associated labels being moved to RCA Victor Group through 2003 and the Arista Associated Labels group the following year. In 2004, BMG went into a joint venture with Sony, forming Sony BMG Music Entertainment, with classical music titles being released through the Sony BMG Masterworks label. In October 2008, Bertelsmann sold all its label holdings to its partner, completing the merger to becoming Sony Music Entertainment.

Overview

BMG Classics was a label division established in 1987 by Bertelsmann Music Group following its acquisition of a 50% stake in RCA/Ariola from General Electric; the label revived RCA’s “lightning bolt” logo and the Victor imprint, giving it custody of a deep back catalog spanning classical and jazz recordings. Its catalogue leans heavily Romantic and Classical, with a roster anchored by the Chicago Symphony Orchestra and Boston Symphony Orchestra under conductors including Fritz Reiner, and sublabels including BMG Classics UK, Catalyst, and CarClassics extending its reach. A corporate restructuring in 2000 wound down the division, its associated labels migrating first to the RCA Victor Group and then to Arista Associated Labels. The 2004 Sony–BMG joint venture brought classical releases under Sony BMG Masterworks, and Bertelsmann’s complete exit in 2008 folded everything into Sony Music Entertainment.
