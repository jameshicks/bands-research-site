Planet Mu

Discogs ID: 185
Parent entity: Planet Mu Records Ltd. (ID 264372)
Active: 1995–present
Base: UK
Founder: Mike Paradinas (as µ-Ziq)
Sublabels: Objects Limited, Timesig
Website: planet.mu

UK independent electronic label founded by Mike Paradinas in 1995, initially as a Virgin imprint and relaunched as a fully independent operation in 1998. Over three decades the label has moved from IDM and abstract electronic music through breakcore, grime, dubstep, footwork, and experimental club music — consistently following Paradinas’s ear rather than any fixed genre brief. By release count, Venetian Snares (Aaron Funk) is its most prolific artist at 122 releases; Jlin (Jerrilynn Patton) is arguably its most critically prominent act of the 2010s–2020s.

Origins and Virgin Era (1995–1997)

Planet Mu began as an imprint on Virgin Records on which Paradinas could release his own µ-Ziq material and sign similar artists. The first release under the Planet Mu name was µ-Ziq’s Dauphine (1995, catalog ZIQDJ1), a DJ-mix promo format that predates the independent ZIQ catalog. The Virgin era also produced µ-Ziq’s In Pine Effect (1995), Salsa With Mesquite (1995), Lunatic Harness (1997), My Little Beautiful (1997), and the label compilation Mealtime (1997).

The Virgin partnership ended because the major label proved unsuited to marketing this music. Paradinas set up the label independently in 1998 — the point at which the ZIQ… catalog numbers begin.

µ-Ziq before Planet Mu

Before launching the label, Paradinas had released on Rephlex (Richard D. James’s label): Tango N’ Vectif (1993, ZIQ’s first pressing) and Bluff Limbo (1994). µ-Ziq was originally a duo with Francis Naughton (ex-Blue Innocence, bass); Naughton left in late 1993 and the project became a Paradinas solo vehicle. The name is pronounced “music.”

Independence and Early ZIQ Catalog (1998–2001)

The independent era opens with Jega — the first non-Paradinas artist to anchor the catalog. Jega (Dylan J. Nathan) released Type Xer0 (ZIQ001, 1998) and Spectrum (ZIQ002CD, 1998), both breakbeat/IDM albums that established the label’s aesthetic outside its founder. Paradinas’s side project Horse Opera followed on ZIQ004, then Slag Boom Van Loon (Paradinas + collaborators) on ZIQ005.

Several early ZIQ slots went to Paradinas aliases — Tusken Raiders (ZIQ003, ZIQ006, 1999), Kid Spatula (ZIQ010, 2000), Frost Jockey (ZIQ013–014, 2000) — giving the catalog a house-label density before outside signings filled it out.

Luke Vibert arrived in 2000 (ZIQ016, ‘95–‘99) and remained a recurring presence through 2015 (28 releases total). Leafcutter John debuted in 2000 (ZIQ017) and produced three albums including The Housebound Spirit (ZIQ061CD, 2003). Venetian Snares entered the catalog via a split with Speedranch (Making Orange Things, ZIQ028, 2001), then immediately released the solo Songs About My Cats (ZIQ032, 2001).

Breakcore and IDM Expansion (2002–2005)

The early-to-mid 2000s are dominated by Venetian Snares. Aaron Funk produced a stream of albums for the label — Higgins Ultra Low Track Glue Funk Hits 1972-2006 (ZIQ056CD, 2002), 2370894 (ZIQ057CD, 2002), Winter In The Belly Of A Snake (ZIQ066CD, 2002), The Chocolate Wheelchair Album (ZIQ077CD, 2003), Huge Chrome Cylinder Box Unfolding (ZIQ088CD, 2004), Meathole (ZIQ099CD, 2005) — working at a pace no other artist on the label has matched. The landmark of this run is Rossz Csillag Alatt Született (ZIQ111CD, 2005), which grafted Venetian Snares’s breakcore against Hungarian classical orchestral music and reached a wider audience.

Funk also founded Timesig, a sublabel fully operated by Planet Mu, under his own name.

Other notable additions in this period:

  • Remarc (ZIQ084CD/085CD, 2003/2004) — archival reissues of drum and bass material
  • Hrvatski (ZIQ026CD, 2002)
  • Hellfish & Producer (ZIQ023CD, 2000; ZIQ058, 2002)
  • Datach’i (ZIQ087CD, 2004)
  • Vex’d debut (Degenerate, ZIQ115CD, 2005)
  • Label compilation Children Of Mu (ZIQ060CD, 2004)

µ-Ziq returned with Bilious Paths (ZIQ070CD, 2003) after a gap, and Ease Up (ZIQ130, 2005).

Grime and Dubstep Phase (2006–2009)

2006 saw 33 releases — the label’s most prolific single year. Planet Mu became a significant home for UK bass music: Vex’d, Boxcutter, Pinch, Benga, Terror Danjah, Mark One, Virus Syndicate, DJ Distance, and Neil Landstrumm all appeared. BBC Radio 1’s Mary Anne Hobbs compiled two releases for the label (2006–2009) that documented the emerging dubstep/grime/electro crossover.

Floating Points (Sam Shepherd) made his recorded debut on Planet Mu with the “J&W Beat” 12” (ZIQ242, 2009) before moving to his own Pluto imprint and later Ninja Tune.

Ital Tek (Alan Myson) first appeared in 2007 (ZIQ210) and became one of the label’s most durable signings, releasing 53 records through 2023. Kuedo (Jamie Teasdale, formerly one half of Vex’d) continued as a solo artist from 2009 (27 releases through 2022).

Footwork Pivot (2010–2013)

The most decisive curatorial move in the label’s history. Paradinas began licensing and releasing material from the Chicago footwork scene, introducing it to a global audience outside specialist Juke/footwork circles.

The Bangs & Works compilations are the key documents of this pivot — assembled with input from the Chicago scene and functioning as an introduction for listeners outside the city’s underground. RP Boo (ID 400544) and Traxman (ID 159923) became ongoing label artists.

Post-Footwork Diversification (2014–present)

Jlin (Jerrilynn Patton, Gary, Indiana) arrived in 2015 with Dark Energy (ZIQ356) — footwork-adjacent but with a distinct compositional voice — and became the label’s defining artist of the decade. Black Origami (ZIQ376, 2017), Autobiography (ZIQ399, 2018, music for Wayne McGregor’s Royal Ballet production), and Akoma (ZIQ460X, 2024) followed.

Other strands of this period:

  • Ekoplekz (Nick Edwards): four releases 2014–2017, analogue electronics
  • Machinedrum (Travis Stewart): Room(s) (ZIQ307CD, 2011)
  • Hieroglyphic Being: The Seer Of Cosmic Visions (ZIQ349, 2014)
  • Rian Treanor: Ataxia (ZIQ405, 2019), File Under UK Metaplasm (ZIQ420, 2020) — 10 releases through 2025
  • Speaker Music (DeForrest Brown Jr.): Of Desire, Longing (ZIQ416, 2019) through Techxodus (ZIQ457, 2023)
  • Gábor Lázár: Source (ZIQ419, 2020), Boundary Object (ZIQ442, 2022)
  • East Man (Joshua Bhatt): Red, White & Zero (ZIQ395, 2018) — grime instrumentals with political framing
  • Nondi_: Flood City Trax (ZIQ452, 2023)

µ-Ziq archival and new material continued throughout: Somerset Avenue Tracks (1992–1995) (ZIQ300, 2013), Chewed Corners (ZIQ333, 2013), Challenge Me Foolish (ZIQ400CD, 2018), Goodbye (ZIQ443, 2022), Hello (ZIQ447, 2022), Grush (ZIQ465, 2024).

The Mike & Rich album Expert Knob Twiddlers — originally a 1996 Rephlex release by Paradinas and Richard D. James (Aphex Twin) — was reissued on Planet Mu in 2016 (ziq369cd).

Anniversary compilations mark the label’s milestones: µ Allstars – Criminal (ZIQ025, 2000), Children Of Mu (ZIQ060CD, 2004), µ20 (ZIQ370CD, 2015), PlanetMµ25 (ZIQ425, 2020), Planet Mu 30 (ZIQ470, 2025).
