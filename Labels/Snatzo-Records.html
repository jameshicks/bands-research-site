Overview

Snatzo Records was a short-lived sublabel of Dindisc (Virgin’s new wave imprint) created specifically to release material by UK new wave act the Revillos. Its catalogue amounts to a single 1980 release in the New Wave, Power Pop, and Punk styles. The roster is essentially coextensive with the Revillos’ lineup — Eugene Reynolds, Rocky Rhythm, Hi-Fi Harris, and Tony Pilley — reflecting the label’s purpose as a dedicated vehicle for the band rather than a broader imprint.

Notes (web) — Snatzo imprint rationale

Question researched: Why did Dindisc/Virgin create a dedicated imprint (Snatzo) for the Revillos rather than releasing the 1980 material directly on Dindisc?

What the sources say:

The most direct account comes from the band’s own official biography at revillos.snatzo.com, which states: “Virgin Records were interested enough to fund a new label specifically for the band (Snatzo) which operated under Virgin’s DinDisc subsidiary.” This frames the imprint as a Virgin/label initiative, not the band’s alone — Virgin chose to fund and create it. The deal was signed in June 1979. revillos.snatzo.com/biography.htm

A review of the Stratoplay box set (Louder Than War) describes Rev Up! as “jointly issued by Dindisc and the band’s own Snatzo Recordi imprint,” which implies the band had an ownership stake or co-ownership in Snatzo — consistent with Virgin funding a dedicated vehicle that was contractually the band’s own imprint rather than a pure Dindisc sublabel. louderthanwar.com

A Vassar University music-urbanism study of the early DIN-series catalogue notes that the Snatzo branding on the debut single visually subordinated the Dindisc logo, and observes that the Revillos “had likely already negotiated with Virgin Records directly for their post-Rezillos re-launch” — suggesting the band’s team pushed for a distinct identity from the outset. The same source notes the band reformed specifically to escape a Sire Records contract (Sire agreed to release Eugene Reynolds and Fay Fife from the Rezillos deal on condition they not use the Rezillos name), which may have motivated establishing an entirely new imprint rather than appearing on an existing one. pages.vassar.edu/musicalurbanism/din-1-din-2-din-3-the-revillos-omd-peter-saville-duggie-campbell/

An interview with Eugene Reynolds (Louder Than War / Rezillos interview) confirms the Sire exit: “It took us a year” to get clear of the contract and launch as the Revillos. louderthanwar.com/interview-fay-eugene-rezillos/

Synthesis:

No source provides an explicit contractual document or named decision-maker explaining in their own words why Snatzo existed rather than a straight Dindisc credit. The available evidence points toward a hybrid arrangement: Virgin/Dindisc funded and backed the imprint, but it was constituted as the band’s own label (hence the “band’s own Snatzo Recordi imprint” framing), likely driven by the Revillos’ need to establish a clean identity after the Sire/Rezillos situation and to negotiate leverage with Virgin. The visual subordination of Dindisc on the sleeve supports the reading that this was meaningful to the band, not merely an administrative sublabel. Whether the specific choice of a separate imprint rather than a Dindisc credit was contractually required, a marketing call, or the band’s demand is not confirmed by any available source.
