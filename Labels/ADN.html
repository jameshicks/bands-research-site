From Discogs

Italian electronic/experimental/industrial label based in Milan. ADN was run by Marco Veronesi, Piero Bielli and Alberto Crosta (with one Carla Crotti also involved at some point), although Alberto Crosta was the main international contact.

ADN was established in 1983 as the first Italian fanzine for experimental new music published in English. The very first issue was called “L’Amore del Nipote” which set the trend for all labels as acronyms of ADN. The fanzine published 8 issues through to Spring 1986, with the later issues (co-released with “Skeletal Work”) including optional sampler cassettes. The booklet and cassette idea carried on with the series of various artist releases called “Out Of Standard”.

Most of their early releases were ADN Cassettes. For their vinyl releases, post new-wave and industrial music came out on the label ‘A Dull Note’, whilst avant-garde and “RiO” Rock In Opposition (left-field experimental rock) releases came out on ‘Auf Dem Nil’. There was also a short-lived CD label ‘Alma De Nieto’ and some other name variations. A few ‘Auf Dem Nil’ releases were also branded “Recommended Records Italia”.

In 2014 ADN has restarted with 4 labels: “Artisti del Novecento” (R.I.O. and experimental music) on vinyl, “Alma De Nieto” (R.I.O. and experimental music), “Apparémment des Notes” (re-editions and obscure artists) and
”Agnostic Dumplings Nursery” with the compilation dedicated to Marco Veronesi (who died in 2013) on CD

Overview

ADN is a Milan-based Italian experimental and industrial label founded in 1983 by Marco Veronesi, Piero Bielli, and Alberto Crosta — originally as a fanzine covering new experimental music, one of the first such publications in English from Italy. It grew from an eight-issue print run (with optional sampler cassettes) into a family of sublabels: A Dull Note handled post-new-wave and industrial vinyl, Auf Dem Nil covered avant-garde and Rock in Opposition material (some releases also carrying a “Recommended Records Italia” imprint), and Alma De Nieto was a short-lived CD offshoot. The catalogue is concentrated in the 1980s with 19 releases that decade, tapering sharply after; following Veronesi’s death in 2013 the label was revived in 2014 under four new imprints continuing the RiO and experimental focus.

Notes (web) — ADN and Recommended Records Italia

Origin of the relationship

The ADN official chronology (adnrecords.com/adn-chronology/, checked 2026-11-18) records a single entry under 1985: “Meeting with Chris Cutler and birth of Recommended Records Italia.” This is the only contemporary ADN-side statement on record about how the connection arose. No further detail about what was agreed, who initiated the meeting, or what preceded it is given on that page.

The meeting followed ADN’s organisation of an Italian tour for Étant Donnés and a concert at the Leoncavallo social centre in Milan — activities that placed ADN squarely in the European avant-garde circuit that Chris Cutler was building through the Recommended Records network.

Nature of the relationship

Recommended Records Italia appears to have been one of several independent European branches opened by the Recommended Records network in the mid-1980s. The Wikipedia article on Recommended Records (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recommended_Records, as of 2026-11-18) records that “independent branches were also opened in mainland Europe by Recommended Records Italia, Recommended Records (Switzerland), Recommended Music and Recommended Records France.” The comparable branches — RecRec (Switzerland), Review Records (Germany), AYAA (France) — are consistently described by Recommended Records sources as “fully distinct operations” that “share a common musical ethic” (Squidco ReR overview: squidco.com/rer/ReR.and.Related.html, checked 2026-11-18).

This framing — distinct but ethically aligned, using a Recommended Records name — is the clearest available model for what ADN/Auf Dem Nil became in Italy. The co-branding on select Auf Dem Nil vinyl releases (Discogs ID 334393) reflects this affiliation; Discogs records 11 releases under the Recommended Records Italia imprint (Discogs ID 87460, first credited 1986), all of which overlap with the ADN/Auf Dem Nil RiO-oriented catalogue.

What is not confirmed

No source located specifies:

  • Whether there was a formal written licensing or distribution agreement between ADN and ReR UK
  • Whether Chris Cutler held any editorial or financial stake in releases issued under the Recommended Records Italia name
  • Whether ADN received ReR distribution in the UK/internationally in return, or whether the relationship was purely name-affiliation
  • When and why the co-branding ceased (Discogs credits for Recommended Records Italia appear to stop in the early 1990s)
