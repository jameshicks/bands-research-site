Shredder

Discogs ID: 45630 Type: Independent record label Location: San Rafael, California, USA (Marin County, north of San Francisco) Founded: c. 1988 Active: 1988–c. 2002 (catalog peak 1988–1997) Founded by: Mel Cheplowitz

American independent punk label founded by Mel Cheplowitz, based out of San Rafael, California (address on record: 75 Plum Tree Lane #3, San Rafael, CA 94109 — now obsolete). Operating primarily from the East Bay / Bay Area punk scene, Shredder is best known for releasing the first two Jawbreaker records and running the World’s In Shreds regional punk compilation series.

Overview

Shredder launched in 1988 with The World’s In Shreds: Volume One: East Bay (SHRED TWO), a compilation documenting the Bay Area punk underground. The label’s first non-compilation release was a split 7” by the Mutants and (Impatient) Youth (‘78 On 45, SHRED ONE, 1989). Jawbreaker appeared early: their Busy EP (SH-50 / Shred Fifty, 1989) and debut LP Unfun (SH-07, 1990) were both Shredder releases. After Jawbreaker moved to Communion Label for Bivouac (1992), Shredder continued building a roster around the Parasites — the label’s most prolific act by release count — and power-pop adjacent acts like the McRackins and Jolt.

The label ran a companion compilation series (World’s In Shreds) with regional volumes covering East Bay, USA, Fresno, and New Jersey (1988–1991), plus a New Bands volume in 1991 and a surf-themed Hang 10 series from 1996.

Recurring engineers across the catalog: Michael James (2) (16 releases), Joel Badie and Vinny Fazzari (12 releases each), Richard Andrews (9 releases, also co-produced Unfun with Jawbreaker). John Golden mastered eight releases. Designer John Yates handled artwork and layout across five releases.

Notes (web) — Jawbreaker’s departure from Shredder

How the Communion/Tupelo signing happened

No interview or press source documents a deliberate departure from Shredder or any stated dissatisfaction with Mel Cheplowitz or the label. The available record suggests the move was driven by a new geographic connection rather than a break.

The key facts, sourced from Gary Held’s first-person account and the Wikipedia article on Bivouac:

  • After Unfun (1990), Jawbreaker relocated from Los Angeles to San Francisco — to the Mission District. Lance Hahn (of J Church) lived in the same apartment complex. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivouac_(album), as of 2026-10-26
  • In early 1992, Hahn told Gary Held — who ran Revolver Distribution (SF warehouse operation that handled Tupelo Recording Company and Communion Label) — that his neighbors were looking for a label to release a new album. Hahn’s exact words per Held: “some close friends of his were looking to release an album and thusly looking for a label, and that these guys liked a band I had released on Communion, Bitch Magnet. Would I be interested in funding recording of a new album as I wouldn’t be sorry if I did?” revolverusa.blogspot.com — Gary Held, “Memories of Lance Hahn,” Nov 2007
  • Blake Schwarzenbach’s stated reason for choosing Communion/Tupelo: they had previously released Bitch Magnet, one of his favorite bands. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivouac_(album), as of 2026-10-26
  • A practical factor: Revolver’s UK base was expected to give Jawbreaker better European distribution. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivouac_(album), as of 2026-10-26

Working interpretation (provisional, no direct citation)

The likeliest explanation — consistent with all available sources but not explicitly stated in any — is that this was a natural outgrow rather than a breakup: Shredder was a small Marin County label suited to 7”s and debut releases; after relocating to SF, the band found a new local connection (Hahn/Held) offering better infrastructure and a label with a roster they respected. No evidence of acrimony. Treat this as provisional until a contemporary interview surfaces.
