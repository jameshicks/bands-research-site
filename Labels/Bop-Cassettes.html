Bop Cassettes

Discogs ID: 83763 Location: Manchester, UK (Unit 128/129, 23 New Mount St., Manchester M4 4DE) Active: 1987–1990 Primary associated act: The Man From Delmonte Parent label(s): Scam (l21575) / Scam Records (l48079) — per Discogs profile

History

Bop Cassettes was a Manchester micro-label operating out of 23 New Mount St. in the Northern Quarter, active roughly 1987–1990. Per its Discogs profile it was run as a mail-order operation and is listed as a sublabel of Scam / Scam Records — a late-1980s Manchester electro label run by Sarah Champion and Alison Martin (operating as S.C.A.M.). The address and phone number (061 953 4067) place it squarely in central Manchester during the city’s post-Factory, pre-Madchester independent scene.

The label’s catalogue is broader than its reputation as a Man From Delmonte vehicle suggests. It opened in 1987 with a run of world-music and fusion cassettes on the “BIP” prefix (Sonjah Clegg, Inner Sense Percussion, Bip Bap Bop, Suns Of Arqa, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan), then expanded in 1988–1990 to include hip-hop (Mr. Spin), Brecht/Weill theatre (The Happy End), folk (Toss The Feathers), Black State Choir, Spaceheads, Social Kaos, and assorted compilation tapes. The Bop-specific “BC” prefix was reserved for the co-branded Manchester North Of England compilation.

The one co-release documented on Discogs is Manchester North Of England (1988), put out jointly with City Life (2) — the Manchester news, arts, and listings magazine published 1983–2005 (offices at 164 Deansgate). The compilation used the catalog numbers BC 001 (cassette) and BC V 001 (vinyl LP) and was compiled by Sarah Champion, who is also credited across several other Bop Cassettes releases. The relationship to City Life appears to have been a one-off, not an ongoing partnership.

The Man From Delmonte became the label’s flagship act from 1989 onward, accounting for 4 of the 5 substantive single/EP/album releases in the BIP 5xx–9xx range. Their manager Jon Ronson — credited as “Management” or “Svengali” on multiple releases — is the most frequently recurring non-performer name across the catalogue.

The Man From Delmonte

Manchester indie rock-pop group, active from at least 1987. Discogs artist ID: 300953. Name variations: “Man From Delmonte”, “The Man From Del Monte”.

Members (per Discogs):

  • Mike West (2) — guitar, vocals (credited on Big Noise LP)
  • Martin Vincent (2) — guitar, harmonica, backing vocals; also wrote liner notes for Catholic Boys On Mobilettes
  • Howard Goody — drums (credited on Big Noise LP and E.P.)
  • Sheila Seal — bass, backing vocals (credited on Big Noise LP and E.P.)
  • Roxy Walsh — trombone (credited on E.P. tracks B1, B2; Catholic Boys tracks A6, B3, B4)
  • Danny Ward — listed as member on Discogs, no specific release credit found in this period

Style: Indie pop / indie rock. The tracklists across their releases — song titles like “Australia Fair”, “Water In My Eyes”, “(Will Nobody Save) Louise”, “Armchair Valentino”, “I Don’t Go Out With Her Anymore, But” — suggest melodic, character-driven guitar pop in the mould of contemporaries such as James, The Railway Children, and Milltown Brothers, all of whom appeared alongside them on the Manchester North Of England compilation.

Manager: Jon Ronson (the future author/broadcaster), credited as manager on the E.P. (1989) and as “Svengali” on My Love Is Like A Gift You Can’t Return (1989), and as manager on Big Noise (1989).

Pre-Bop releases: Two singles on Ugly Man Records in 1987 (“Drive Drive Drive”, “Water In My Eyes”) and one in 1988 (“(Will Nobody Save) Louise”). They were also the first act on the Manchester North Of England compilation (track A1, “Australia Fair”).

Post-Bop: A 2025 release on a self-run “Not On Label” imprint suggests the band (or at least members) remained active decades later.

Recording credits across their Bop Cassettes releases:

  • Tim Oliver — recorded My Love Is Like A Gift You Can’t Return, track A1 of E.P., and several Catholic Boys tracks
  • Nick Garside — recorded E.P. tracks A2, B1, B2; most Catholic Boys tracks
  • Graham Massey — recorded My Love Is Like A Gift You Can’t Return (uncredited on that release, but credited as Engineer on related Bop Cassettes work); later became a core member of 808 State
  • Saul Davies — violin on My Love Is Like A Gift You Can’t Return and Catholic Boys B5; later known as a member of James

Manchester North Of England Compilation (1988)

Co-released by Bop Cassettes and City Life (2). Compiled by Sarah Champion (credited as “Tape Compiled By”). Cassette (BC 001) and limited-edition vinyl LP (BC V 001). Design on cassette release by Jetset (9); cover photography by A.J. Wilkinson.

Genres: Alternative Rock, Indie Pop, Indie Rock, Leftfield.

This compilation functions as a snapshot of the Manchester independent guitar-pop scene circa 1988, predating the “Madchester” explosion. It sits alongside Factory Records, Play Hard, and Playtime releases as a document of the city’s pre-baggy moment. Notably, Inspiral Carpets and James both went on to major-label deals and chart success; Bradford signed to Rough Trade.

Open Questions

  • Who owned / ran Bop Cassettes directly? The Discogs profile identifies a Scam sublabel relationship and Sarah Champion’s role as compiler, but no named founder or label head.
  • What is the relationship between the “BIP” prefix releases (world music, electro, hip-hop) and the Man From Delmonte-era “BIP 5xx–9xx” releases — was there a distinct editorial shift, or was it always eclectic?
  • Hit The North (BIP 806 CD, 1990) — tracklisting and full artists not retrieved; may be a significant Northern indie compilation.
  • The Man From Delmonte’s member Danny Ward appears on Discogs but carries no specific release credits in this period.
  • Catholic Boys On Mobilettes is tagged “Compilation” in its format — is it a retrospective of earlier material, a career-spanning collection, or a new-recordings album? The tracklist includes re-recordings of songs from earlier releases, suggesting it is a retrospective cassette compilation.
  • Jon Ronson’s precise relationship to Bop Cassettes (beyond managing the band) is not documented on Discogs.
