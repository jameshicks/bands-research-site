Overview

Idiosyncratics was a small American label active in the early-to-mid 1980s, concentrating on minimal electronic, experimental, and synth-pop sounds. Its catalogue of three releases is tightly contained within that decade, with a roster that spans American cassette-culture figure Gen Ken Montgomery and German electronic pioneer Conrad Schnitzler, pointing to transatlantic connections within the underground experimental network. No parent label or distribution structure is recorded.

Notes (web)

Label operator — Mark Lane and On-Slaught

Idiosyncratics was the label vehicle of Mark Lane, an LA-based electronic musician and editor. After the original founder Jody Quirk departed in 1983, Lane took over as editor of the On-Slaught cassette magazine and published four further issues under the Idiosyncratics name. The label thus grew directly out of the cassette-magazine/zine format that was central to the early-1980s underground: its first releases were the On-Slaught compilation tapes, which positioned it within “a by now flourishing worldwide cassette underground.” (Archaic Inventions blog, accessed 2026-11-08; 433rpm blog, accessed 2026-11-08; Cultural Amnesia, accessed 2026-11-08)

The LA Weekly has described Lane as a “seminal LA synthpop artist,” fixing the label’s base in Los Angeles. (LA Weekly, accessed 2026-11-08 — page did not load fully; label location inferred from other sources)

How Schnitzler came to release on Idiosyncratics — the Gen Ken bridge

The likeliest route is through Gen Ken Montgomery, the New York-based cassette-culture figure who is documented as the producer of Schnitzler’s Concert LP (Idiosyncratics I-86-103, 1986) and co-created the cover artwork. (tape-mag.com release page, accessed 2026-11-08)

Montgomery had been active in the international cassette underground since arriving in New York in 1978, self-releasing tapes from 1981 and exchanging them with experimenters worldwide. He and Schnitzler formed a close, documented working relationship that culminated in co-founding the label Generations Unlimited together (with David Prescott) in 1987 — the year after Concert appeared — and collaborating on the New Dramatic Electronic Music LP (1988). (Experimental Intermedia, accessed 2026-11-08; search result snippet for Generations Unlimited, accessed 2026-11-08)

Schnitzler also appeared on the Idiosyncratics On-Slaught No. 4 cassette compilation (1983) alongside his son, suggesting that the relationship with the label predated the 1986 vinyl LP and may have been initiated via the cassette-magazine network rather than a direct A&R approach. (Archaic Inventions blog, accessed 2026-11-08)

Summary of what can and cannot be confirmed: The cassette-network hypothesis is strongly supported: Idiosyncratics was itself a cassette-culture operation; Gen Ken Montgomery — the documented bridge between Schnitzler and the label — was a leading figure in the international cassette underground; and Schnitzler had material on the label’s compilation tape as early as 1983, three years before the full LP. No source directly states the precise moment or mechanism of introduction between Schnitzler and Mark Lane. It is not confirmed whether Lane and Montgomery knew each other independently, or whether Gen Ken was the sole conduit.

Lane–Montgomery prior working relationship (independent of Schnitzler)

Subsequent research confirms that Mark Lane and Gen Ken Montgomery had a documented working relationship that predates Schnitzler’s 1983 cassette appearance on Idiosyncratics, establishing Gen Ken as a plausible broker for the Concert LP deal.

The key evidence:

  • Gen Ken Montgomery released a flexi-disc, “Treat The Hell Out of It”, included with an issue of On-Slaught magazine (published by Artwerk, Lane’s label imprint) in 1982 — the year before Schnitzler first appeared on an Idiosyncratics compilation. (Vinyl-on-Demand artist page for Gen Ken Montgomery, accessed 2026-11-09)

  • Artwerk / Mark Lane is listed explicitly among the “respected labels” that released Gen Ken Montgomery’s cassettes, records and CDs in limited editions, alongside De Fabriek, Staalplaat, Tellus, Banned Productions and XI Records. (Vinyl-on-Demand artist page for Gen Ken Montgomery, accessed 2026-11-09)

  • Gen Ken Montgomery contributed synthesizer to Mark Lane’s own project Male Nymph (released 1987 on Artwerk/Idiosyncratics), showing the working relationship extended beyond one-off cassette distribution into direct studio collaboration. (scaruffi.com biography of Gen Ken Montgomery, accessed 2026-11-09; discogs.com search result snippet, accessed 2026-11-09)

Revised assessment: The prior Lane–Montgomery relationship is now confirmed. By 1982, Gen Ken had a track on an On-Slaught flexi — a year before Schnitzler appeared on the label. The sequence (1982: Gen Ken on Artwerk/On-Slaught → 1983: Schnitzler on Idiosyncratics comp → 1986: Schnitzler Concert LP produced by Gen Ken for Idiosyncratics) is consistent with Gen Ken having brokered or at minimum facilitated the Schnitzler connection, rather than Lane approaching Schnitzler directly after the 1983 cassette. No source states the exact mechanism in documentary terms, but the circumstantial chain is tight.
