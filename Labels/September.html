Overview

September was a small UK independent label founded by manager Paul Sutton following the closure of the Pink Label. It was set up primarily to release McCarthy’s debut album; Sutton managed both McCarthy and The Wolfhounds, and both acts appeared on the label. September was subsequently sold to Midnight Music, which handled the bulk of the later releases by both bands, with the exception of a single Wolfhounds release on Idea. The catalogue is concentrated in the late 1980s and spans indie rock, jangle pop, and art rock, with a sublabel, Instinct (7), also operating under the same umbrella.

Notes (web) — Pink Label closure

The Pink Label (Discogs ID 39693) was founded in 1984 by Simon Down, a friend of Paul Sutton’s from further education college in Lincolnshire, shortly joined by Simon’s brother Ed Down and by Sutton himself as a business partner. It was set up originally to release the debut recordings of The June Brides, and went on to put out a run of UK indie-pop singles and three LPs.

Cause of closure — financial collapse (1987). According to Simon Down in a TweeNet label history interview, the label’s difficulties were commercial: poor sales on Jamie Wednesday singles, Rumblefish’s debut, and finally the The Wolfhounds album Unseen Ripples from a Pebble (which Simon felt lost “the raw power of the earlier Wolfhounds”). Simon grew “very disillusioned with the music industry,” describing it as “more ruthless and cut throat than many other corporate environments.” He maintained his day job at London Underground throughout because the label could not sustain a salary. The label folded in 1987. (twee.net/labels/pink.html, fetched 2026-10-30)

Disputed financial aftermath. Simon Down states that the label had outstanding bank debt when it closed, and that he and his brother Ed paid it — adding: “we never saw a penny from Paul.” This suggests a breakdown in the business relationship between Sutton and the Down brothers at or around the point of closure. Paul Sutton then went on to found September Records immediately after. (twee.net/labels/pink.html, fetched 2026-10-30)

Summary. The closure was primarily commercial failure, not a formal dispute or event like a distribution deal falling through. The debt allegation introduces a conflict between Sutton and the Down brothers, but there is no independent corroboration of this claim beyond Simon Down’s own account on TweeNet — treat it as one-sided testimony.
