Overview

Empty Records (US) is a Seattle-based independent label founded in San Francisco in 1984. It operated in partnership with a German branch (trading as Musical Tragedies, later Empty Records Europe) from that founding until 1996, when the two split, prompting the American operation to adopt the “(US)” suffix and a new MTR catalogue prefix beginning at release #350. A legal dispute between the branches ran until 2001, after which the label formally became “Empty Records U.S.” (from #387 onward); it closed in 2007. Its 54 catalogue entries — split evenly across the 1990s and 2000s — lean toward punk, garage rock, and new wave, with its most prominent roster entries being Jay Reatard, Lost Sounds, Alicja Trout, and the Reatards.

Notes (web) — US/Germany split and lawsuit

Background

Empty Records was co-founded in San Francisco on 20 December 1984 by Joe Raimond and Volker Stewart. Joe quickly relocated to Nürnberg, Germany, where he was joined as half-owner by Florian Schück in 1989; this Nürnberg operation became the de facto German side of the label. Meanwhile, Blake Wright — a school friend of Joe’s — had returned to the US in 1987 and taken over the eMpTy P.O. Box address from Volker Stewart, releasing Seattle-scene records under the eMpTy name from 1989 onward.

The dispute (1996–2000)

By 1996, Joe Raimond and Florian Schück (the German side) considered Blake Wright to be mismanaging the US operation: accumulating debts and failing to cooperate. Joe cancelled Blake Wright’s licence to continue operating under the eMpTy name. The last officially sanctioned MT release on Blake’s side was The Hormones “Castaway” single (August 1996). Court proceedings against Blake Wright were begun that same year.

According to the 10 Things Zine blog, the core of the lawsuit was the right to the name “Empty Records” — Joe Raimond in Germany sued Blake Wright over ownership of the label name. The German branch also claimed the eMpTy logo.

The suit ran approximately 1996–2000. It ended in a settlement: Blake Wright was required to give up the eMpTy logo and any formal connection to MT/Musical Tragedies. The settlement is described by the German side as Blake losing both the logo and the name link to MT. The US branch’s subsequent name changes — “Empty Records US” from 1996 (catalog prefix MTR), then formally “Empty Records U.S.” from release #387 (2001) — reflect the progressive unwinding required by the settlement.

The German branch then resumed use of the original 1984 eMpTy logo and launched new punk releases under the name Empty Records Europe from 2000 onward.

Post-settlement — the “Bootleg Series”

After the settlement, the German side re-released what it characterised as “Blake Wright’s unauthorized eMpTy titles” under the banner “The Bootleg Series” — these were Empty Records US releases with label details covered by an Empty Records Europe sticker. This framing (calling them “bootlegs”) is the German branch’s perspective; it reflects their position that Blake’s mid-to-late 1990s releases were made without valid licence.

Blake Wright announced in February 2008 that Empty Records US had “called it a day”, which the German branch’s own history describes as “bringing to a final close one of the most difficult and unpleasant episodes in the history of MT.”

What was at stake

  • The right to use the name “eMpTy Records” / “Empty Records” in the US
  • The eMpTy logo (the German side retained/reclaimed it)
  • Implicit: ownership or legitimacy of the catalog of US releases made 1996–2000 under the eMpTy name without the German side’s licence (the German side later reframed these as a “bootleg series”)
