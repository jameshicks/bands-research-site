From Discogs

LTM Recordings was founded as Les Temps Modernes in Edinburgh in 1983 by James Nice. Its earliest releases championed the work of Section 25, The Happy Family and Crispy Ambulance, among others. In 1987 the label relocated to Brussels for four years, where Nice also worked for Les Disques du Crepuscule and later PIAS. Nice reactivated the label as LTM in 1997. This new iteration initially focussed on 1980s and 1990s CD reissues often related to the recently bankrupt Factory and its European cousin Les Disques du Crépuscule. It has since broadened its remit to cover more varied new wave, electronic and post punk artists, as well as new recordings and DVDs. In 2008 the compilation series Auteur Labels was launched, with volumes on New Hormones, Crepuscule, Factory, Factory Benelux, Independent Project and Object Music. An interest in 20th century experimental artists has also led to LTM publishing audio works from the likes of Erik Satie, Wyndham Lewis, Marcel Duchamp and William S. Burroughs alongside Futurist, Dada, Surrealist, Bauhaus and De Stijl compilations. The Boutique Label is a sub-division of LTM.

Overview

LTM Recordings — originally Les Temps Modernes — was founded in Edinburgh in 1983 by James Nice, with early output centred on post-punk and new wave acts including Section 25, The Happy Family, and Crispy Ambulance. The label relocated to Brussels in 1987, where Nice took roles at Les Disques du Crépuscule and later PIAS, before LTM went dormant. Nice revived the imprint in 1997, initially exploiting the catalogue gap left by Factory’s 1992 bankruptcy through CD reissues of Factory and Crépuscule-related material. The label has since broadened considerably, releasing audio works by experimental and avant-garde figures such as Erik Satie, Marcel Duchamp, and William S. Burroughs alongside Futurist, Dada, Surrealist, Bauhaus, and De Stijl compilations; 139 of its 179 selected releases date from the 2000s. Sublabels include Boutique, Avant-Garde Art, LTM Black Box, and Salon Recordings.

Notes (web)

LTM’s 1997 reactivation and the Factory bankruptcy

The connection between Factory’s 1992 bankruptcy and LTM’s 1997 revival is real but indirect: Nice had been doing Factory-related reissues on LTM since 1989/90 — before the bankruptcy — securing licences from Factory Records directly for artists whose back catalogue the label had no commercial interest in (Section 25, Crispy Ambulance, Josef K). Factory’s disinterest in its own non-Joy Division/New Order catalogue was the enabling condition, not the bankruptcy itself. louderthanwar.com interview

When Nice moved back to the UK around 1992 he stepped away from music and became a lawyer. Wikipedia notes he worked “as a lawyer (including representing some Factory artists against London Records in the mid-1990s)” — the legal work arising from the post-bankruptcy dispute over who controlled the Factory catalogue (London Records had acquired certain Factory assets out of receivership). This gave Nice unusually detailed knowledge of where rights sat and with whom. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTM_Recordings (as of 2025-01-01)

The 1997 reactivation was driven by a convergence of factors: (1) Nice’s mid-1990s legal work had mapped the post-bankruptcy rights landscape; (2) the CD format and early internet had by 1997 made operating a niche catalogue reissue label commercially viable in a way it had not been in 1992; (3) Nice already had established working relationships at both Factory and Crépuscule from his earlier career at those labels. The Louder Than War interview confirms he had met Tony Wilson before the bankruptcy to discuss a full Durutti Column reissue campaign on Factory itself — that project was killed by the bankruptcy; the 1997 LTM revival completed the work by a different route, via direct artist licences where Factory rights had reverted, and via London Records for assets it retained.

There is no evidence of a bulk catalogue acquisition: LTM’s method was (and remains) licensing individual artist catalogues, often directly from artists once rights had reverted. Nice has stated he does “a lot of Manchester and Brussels catalogue because the rights have often reverted to the artist.” louderthanwar.com

LTM did not become a full-time operation for Nice until 2001; the 1997 reactivation was a resumption of activity rather than an immediate full-time enterprise. theoriginalsinfanzine.blogspot.com
