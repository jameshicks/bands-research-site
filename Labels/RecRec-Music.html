RecRec Music

Swiss independent record label founded in Autumn 1979 by Daniel Waldner (Discogs artist ID: 1192958) and Veit Stauffer (Discogs artist ID: 1437638) in Zürich. Label codes: LC 7981 / LC 12366. Website: recrec-shop.ch.

Overview

RecRec Music was the central Swiss imprint for the European avant-rock and Rock In Opposition scene from 1979 through the late 1990s. Based in Zürich (Rotwandstrasse 64, 8004 Zürich), the label was co-produced on many releases by RecRec Music as a credited entity (Discogs artist ID: 2080025) and distributed initially by Recommended Records (Switzerland), later renamed to a separate distributor. RecRec ran a physical record shop alongside the label; after the label’s bankruptcy in 1997 the two functions were split — the distribution arm spun off as a separate company with two new imprints (ReR Megacorp-adjacent), while the shop continued under Veit Stauffer until the end of 2020.

The label’s catalog spans roughly 85 unique masters (with many pressing variants across Switzerland, Germany, France, Netherlands, Japan, the US, and the UK). Core artists include Fred Frith, Skeleton Crew (2), Tom Cora, The Ex, Nimal, Etron Fou Leloublan, Debile Menthol, The Camberwell Now, Negativland, UnknownmiX, and Die Knödel.

RecRec was closely networked with Ayaa (France), ReR Megacorp (UK), Ex Records (Netherlands), and the broader RIO/avant-rock circuit. The label’s house engineer Robert Vogel and recording engineers Tim Hodgkinson and Hairi Vogel recur throughout the catalog; mastering was frequently handled by Rico Sonderegger. Designer Peter Bäder (27 releases) and lithographer Busag (14 releases) gave the label a visual identity across its LP and CD era.

Fred Frith Catalog Reissues (1991 CD series)

In 1991 RecRec issued a run of CD reissues of Fred Frith’s back catalog under the “ReCDec 90x” and “ReCDec 9xx” numbering: Gravity (901), Speechless (902), Cheap At Half The Price (903), Guitar Solos (904). These were originally released on Virgin/Caroline and Ralph Records respectively.

Organik — Discogs ID: 42714

“Swiss new music, avant-rock and industrial music label, created by Marcus May.” Address: Magnustrasse 5, CH-8004 Zürich. The most substantial sublabel, documented across the mid-1980s through early 1990s with a focus on Swiss avant-rock and the first releases from The Young Gods.

Connections

  • Ayaa — French RIO label (Reims); co-released Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés (1985) with RecRec; Ferdinand Richard connection
  • Ex Records — Dutch label; co-released The Ex / Tom Cora albums Scrabbling At The Lock (1991), And The Weathermen Shrug Their Shoulders (1993), Mudbird Shivers (1995)
  • ReR Megacorp — UK; Recommended Records (Switzerland) was the early distributor of RecRec, later relationship continued via ReR
  • Rift (NYC) — co-released Learn To Talk (Skeleton Crew (2), 1984)
  • The Young Gods — launched via sublabel Organik (1986–1987) before international deals
  • Fred Frith — single most documented artist on the label; 9+ solo/collaborative releases; also provided back-catalog CD reissues via RecRec
  • Tom Cora — house presence as performer and co-producer across 14 releases
  • The Ex — Dutch post-punk group; three joint releases in early 1990s via Ex Records co-releases
  • Debile Menthol — Swiss RIO group; among the label’s first releases (1983–1984)
  • Nimal — Swiss RIO/folk-rock; two LPs (1987, 1990); Tom Cora co-produced debut
  • Etron Fou Leloublan — French RIO group; final LP Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés co-released 1985
  • Negativland — US media-culture critics; Escape From Noise (1987) and Helter Stupid (1989) were Swiss pressings on RecRec
  • Christian Marclay — Footsteps (1989)
  • Die Knödel — Swiss avant-cabaret; three CDs 1993–1995
  • Bob Ostertag — US composer/activist; four solo releases + collaborations with Frith, Zorn, Minton
  • John Zorn — appeared on Attention Span (1990)
  • Phil Minton — voice; Voice Of America (1994) and Say No More (1993)
  • Catherine Jauniaux — voice; Vibraslaps (1993) and guest on multiple The Ex / Tom Cora releases
  • Les Reines Prochaines — Swiss feminist performance/music collective; debut on BOY, then main label
  • Ferdinand Richard — EFL bassist; En Avant (1983), Dropera (1991, with Frith), …Enclume (Ferdinand Et Les Philosophes, 1991)
  • Guigou Chenevier — EFL drummer; A L’Abri Des Micro-Climats (1984, with Sophie Jausserand)

Research Queue

  • The Discogs profile for RecRec states the label was “founded in Autumn 1979” — is the first release (Debile Menthol, REC REC 01, 1983) really the first issued, or are there earlier cassette or private releases pre-dating the Discogs catalog?
  • The label profile references [a1192958] (Daniel Waldner) as co-founder — is Waldner documented in Swiss music press or interviews as an equal co-founder with Veit Stauffer, or did one of them have primary operational control?
  • After bankruptcy in 1997, the profile says “two new labels” emerged from the distribution arm — what are those two labels, and are they in Discogs? (The RecRec Music Switzerland sublabel ID 1372096 appears to be a publisher entity, not the new distribution labels.)
  • Plow! (Organik, 1985, ORG 85-1) is listed as a Various Artists compilation — which artists appear on it, and does it document an early Swiss avant-rock scene snapshot?
  • Kortatu’s A Frontline Compilation - Rock In The Basque Country (Organik, 1987, ORG 87/10) — is this a Kortatu-curated compilation or a Kortatu release? The primary artist credit is Kortatu, but the title implies a compilation format.
  • Sleeve credits on Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés (RecRec 08) list Fred Frith as producer — the Discogs RecRec Music entity (ID: 2080025) is also co-credited as producer. Does the sleeve explicitly credit both, or is the RecRec entity credit a Discogs data entry convention?
  • The Step Across The Border documentary film (dir. Nicolas Humbert / Werner Penzel) won awards — which festival(s) and year(s)? The Discogs entry does not carry this information.
  • Learn To Talk (RecRec 05) lists primary artists as both “Skeleton Crew (2)” alone and “Fred Frith / Skeleton Crew (2) / Tom Cora” across different pressings — which credit appears on the original Swiss pressing sleeve?
