Fonda Rae

Real name: Fonda Rae Wood. Born in Ossining, New York. American R&B, disco, boogie, and house vocalist. Active from the late 1970s through the 2000s. Discogs artist ID: 13811.

Overview

Fonda Rae entered the New York dance music circuit in the late 1970s through the ZE Records world: she sang on the 1979 debut LP by Don Armando’s Second Avenue Rhumba Band (produced by Andy Hernandez and exec-produced by August Darnell and Michael Zilkha), and carried that connection into Kid Creole And The Coconuts’ debut album Off The Coast Of Me (Island/ZE, 1980), where she appears as one of several vocal Coconuts alongside Adriana Kaegi, Lourdes Cotto, and Brooksie Wells.

Her solo breakthrough came on Vanguard in 1982 with “Over Like A Fat Rat,” produced by Bob Blank with arrangements by Leroy Burgess — a boogie/disco 12” that reached #22 on the U.S. Club Play chart and #75 on the R&B chart. It became one of the most-sampled records of the era: Eric B. & Rakim lifted its vocal for “Eric B. Is President” (1986), Grand Puba used it in “Fat Rat” on the Grand Puba LP (1991), SWV sampled it for their single “Right Here (Human Nature Remix)” (1996), and Kool G Rap & D.J. Polo sampled it on their 1996 single.

She followed up on her own Posse Records imprint (1983), co-writing and co-producing both “Heobah (Hey-O-Bah)” and “Live It Up” with collaborator Freddie Perez and executive producer Danny Weiss. The Streetwave single “Tuch Me (All Night Long)” (1984) — credited to Wish (3) — was produced and arranged by Patrick Adams and Greg Carmichael; it peaked at #70 on U.S. R&B and #49 in the UK, and later gave Cathy Dennis a major hit with her 1991 cover version.

Through the 1980s she was active as a session singer, contributing to releases by the Fat Boys, Kurtis Blow, Taka Boom, Deborah Harry, and others. She was also a member of Time Zone (John Lydon / Afrika Bambaataa) and Larry Joseph & Science, and appears on the Togetherness project.

Her 1996 return single “Living In Ecstasy” on Wave Music (produced by John Ciafone and Lem Springsteen, with a François K edit on the A-side) connected her to the New York garage/house circuit. The 2001 Nuphonic 12” Alternations, co-written and produced with Adam Goldstone, placed her in the UK downtempo-house world; it was engineered by Benno Hotz and mastered at Alchemy. Her 1993 house outing “Parade!” on Class X Recordings (featuring Community (Discogs)) had remixes by Tommy Musto and Victor Simonelli.

Name variations used across her credits: F. Rae, Fonda Raewood, Fonda Ray, Fonda Rey, Fondae Rae, Rae, Ronda Rae.
