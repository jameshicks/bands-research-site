Peter Gabriel

Discogs ID: 11136 Born: 13 February 1950, Chobham, Surrey, England, UK Real name: Peter Brian Gabriel Instruments: Vocals, keyboards, flute, recorder, piano Active: 1967–present (Genesis 1967–1975; solo 1977–present) Labels: Charisma, Virgin, Geffen Records, Real World Records

British singer, songwriter, producer, and entrepreneur. Co-founder and original vocalist of Genesis (1967–1975); prolific solo artist from 1977; founder of the WOMAD festival and Real World Records.

Biography

Genesis Years (1967–1975)

Gabriel co-founded Genesis at Charterhouse School in 1967 alongside Tony Banks, Mike Rutherford, Anthony Phillips, and Chris Stewart. He served as lead vocalist and theatrical frontman through the band’s progressive rock peak, culminating in the double concept album The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway (1974). He left the group in 1975, with Phil Collins taking over lead vocal duties.

Solo Beginnings: The Self-Titled Series (1977–1982)

Gabriel launched his solo career with four self-titled albums, each nicknamed after visual or thematic qualities:

Peter Gabriel I (“Car”) (1977, Charisma) — Produced by Bob Ezrin, recorded in Toronto with an eclectic US session band including Tony Levin (bass), Larry Fast (synthesizers), Allan Schwartzberg (drums), Robert Fripp (guitar), and the London Symphony Orchestra arranged by Michael Gibbs. “Solsbury Hill” was the breakthrough UK single.

Peter Gabriel II (“Scratch”) (1978, Charisma) — Produced by Robert Fripp, the album was rawer and more experimental. Core band: Levin, Jerry Marotta (drums), Larry Fast, Sid McGinnis (guitar), with Roy Bittan on keyboards. Fripp contributed Frippertronics tape-loop electronics on “White Shadow.”

Peter Gabriel III (“Melt”) (1980, Charisma) — Produced by Steve Lillywhite, with electronic production by Larry Fast; engineered by Hugh Padgham, who pioneered the gated reverb drum sound here before its use on Collins’s hits. Marotta on drums (tracks A4–B4); David Rhodes joined on guitar, becoming a long-term collaborator. Featured “Games Without Frontiers” and the anti-apartheid anthem “Biko.” Sleeve design by Hipgnosis (2).

Peter Gabriel IV (“Security”) (1982, Charisma/Geffen) — Co-produced with David Lord, who also engineered and performed. Extensive Fairlight CMI programming by Gabriel, Lord, and Larry Fast. Marotta remained on drums; Rhodes on guitar; Tony Levin on bass; Peter Hammill (of Van der Graaf Generator) contributed as a musician. The album leaned heavily into digital percussion and African rhythmic textures. “Shock the Monkey” was a US hit.

Birdy and the Bridge to So (1985)

Birdy (1985, Charisma/Virgin) — Score for Alan Parker’s film Birdy. Produced with Daniel Lanois (their first collaboration); core band of Levin, Marotta, Rhodes, Larry Fast, and Jon Hassell. Much of the material was reworked and recontextualized from earlier recordings.

So and the Mainstream Breakthrough (1986)

So (1986, Geffen/Virgin) — Produced by Gabriel and Daniel Lanois; engineered by Kevin Killen, with additional engineering by David Bottrill and David Stallbaumer. Overdubs at New York’s Power Station engineered by Bruce Lampcov. Design by Peter Saville (2) and Brett Wickens; photography by Trevor Key.

Personnel: Manu Katché replaced Marotta on drums; Tony Levin (bass), David Rhodes (guitar), Larry Fast (synths), with Wayne Jackson on horn arrangements and Youssou N’Dour appearing on “In Your Eyes.” The album produced five UK hit singles: “Sledgehammer,” “Big Time,” “Don’t Give Up” (duet with Kate Bush), “In Your Eyes,” and “Red Rain.” Global sales exceeded 5 million.

Passion and WOMAD/Real World (1989)

Passion — Music for The Last Temptation of Christ (1989, Geffen) — Score for Martin Scorsese’s film. Gabriel composed and produced entirely; engineered and co-mixed by David Bottrill; mastered by Ian Cooper. The album drew on field recordings and musicians from across North Africa, the Middle East, West Africa, and South Asia — among them Shankar (violin, track 10), Mahmoud Tabrizi-Zadeh (tar, track 14), and programming assistance by Stephen McLaughlin. A companion “sources” album (Passion Sources) was issued on Real World.

WOMAD (World of Music, Arts and Dance) was founded by Gabriel in 1980 (first festival 1982) to celebrate world music and arts. Real World Records was launched in 1989, distributed by Virgin until 2007, to release WOMAD-adjacent artists and cross-cultural collaborations. Real World Studios (Box, Wiltshire) became one of the UK’s premier recording facilities.

Us (1992)

Us (1992, Real World/Virgin) — Produced by Gabriel and Daniel Lanois; recorded/mixed by David Bottrill; mastered by Ian Cooper. Personnel: Katché (drums), Tony Levin (bass), Rhodes (guitar). Brian Eno credited with “Extra Brainstorming.” Sleeve by Malcolm Garrett / Assorted Images. Singles included “Digging in the Dirt,” “Blood of Eden,” and “Steam.”

Later Albums: Up, Scratch My Back, New Blood, I/O (2002–2023)

Up (2002, Geffen/Real World) — Produced by Gabriel alone; engineered and programmed by Richard Chappell; mixed by Tchad Blake (Blake’s first Gabriel album; he mixed from Los Angeles before relocating to England); mastered by Tony Cousins. Katché on drums (tracks 1–3, 5–7), Ged Lynch on drums (tracks 2, 6, 8); Levin and Rhodes returned. Guest performers included the Blind Boys of Alabama and The Dhol Foundation.

Scratch My Back (2010, Real World/Virgin) — Covers album featuring orchestral arrangements of other artists’ songs (Radiohead, David Bowie, Elbow, etc.) with no drums or guitars. Companion project And I’ll Scratch Yours (2013) invited those artists to cover Gabriel in return.

New Blood (2011, Real World) — Orchestral re-recordings of Gabriel’s back catalog with the New Blood Orchestra.

I/O (2023, Real World) — First studio album of original material since Up. Produced by Gabriel with guest producers Brian Eno (tracks 6, 18) and Richard Russell (tracks 5, 17). Mixed by Mark Stent (tracks 1–12) and Tchad Blake (tracks 13–24); mastered by Matt Colton. The Soweto Gospel Choir and Orphei Drängar (Swedish choral ensemble) contributed. Singles were released monthly throughout 2023 in “Bright-Side” and “Dark-Side” mixes — two stereo mixes per song — with a third “In-Side” Dolby Atmos mix.

Real World Records

Founded 1989 by Gabriel at Real World Studios (Mill Lane, Box, Wiltshire). Distributed by Virgin 1989–2007; subsequently independent / Rykodisc in US. The label became the primary home for WOMAD-connected artists and cross-cultural collaborations.

Discogs ID: 4066 | Parent: Real World Records Ltd. (ID 281512) Sublabels: Eclectic Art Records (829659); Peter Gabriel Full Moon Club (3978807); Real World X (2942420)

Key roster connections (partial): Youssou N’Dour, Shankar, Papa Wemba, Ayub Ogada, Sevara Nazarkhan, Michael Brook, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan.

WOMAD

World of Music, Arts and Dance — festival founded by Gabriel in 1980; first event 1982 at the Royal Bath and West Showground, Shepton Mallet, UK. Originally co-organized with Thomas Brooman and Bob Hooton. After the 1982 festival ran into financial difficulties, a reunion Genesis concert (Gabriel’s one-off return) bailed it out. WOMAD became a global touring festival, now running at multiple sites worldwide annually. Gabriel was listed in Discogs groups as appearing at “WOMAD” (as a label in the DB).

Scene Connections

Gabriel’s career traces a path from British progressive rock (Genesis, 1967–1975) through art rock (self-titled solo series, 1977–1982) into world music fusion (WOMAD, Passion, Real World Records, 1982–present), while achieving mainstream pop crossover with So (1986). His use of the Fairlight CMI (from Security onward) and his interest in African and Middle Eastern music influenced the broader world-music boom of the 1980s–1990s. The Real World recording studio became a significant hub for artists outside Western pop conventions.
