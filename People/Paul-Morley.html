Paul Morley

Paul Morley

Oct 04, 20265 min read

Paul Morley

Discogs ID: 231640

British music journalist and cultural critic, NME 1977–1983. Co-founder of ZTT Records (1983) alongside Trevor Horn and Jill Sinclair. Alias: Otto Flake. Married to Claudia Brücken (Propaganda vocalist).

Founding member of The Art Of Noise — credited as writer and “concept” on all early releases. His role at ZTT was primarily ideological and presentational: designing the label’s Futurist/modernist identity, writing sleeve texts, and shaping the visual grammar of FGTH and Art of Noise packaging. Credited on Welcome to the Pleasuredome for “Text By [Acted During The Bang]” and on Who’s Afraid Of The Art Of Noise as Written-By.

829 production credits and 20 producer credits on ZTT-related releases per Discogs.

Manchester Origins and Early Career

Born Paul Robert Morley, 26 March 1957, in Stockport, Greater Manchester. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-08-26.) He attended both Sex Pistols performances at Manchester’s Lesser Free Trade Hall in summer 1976 as a member of the audience — the gigs now legendary as the catalyst for Manchester’s post-punk explosion. (The Vinyl District interview.) He began writing for the NME in 1977, serving in practice as the paper’s Manchester stringer during the formative period of the Factory Records scene. (Joy Division: Piece by Piece, Goodreads.)

His father died by suicide in the late 1970s — a biographical fact Morley later connected in writing to his experience of Ian Curtis’s death in 1980. This personal thread runs through his 2000 memoir Nothing, which parallels the two losses and is set against the Manchester scene he was simultaneously documenting for the NME. (Goodreads.)

Factory Records and Joy Division

Morley was described by the Factory Records fan archive as “instrumental in spreading the word about Factory Records whilst working at the NME in the early days.” (factoryrecords.org.) His coverage began before the label’s name was established: in a 1977 NME live piece he noted the band (then called Warsaw) favourably. He reviewed a Joy Division live performance at The Ritz, Manchester, on 3 June 1978 for NME. (Source: Facebook post citing original NME tearsheet, https://www.facebook.com/groups/23844787597/posts/10164705035467598/.) He collected all his Joy Division and Warsaw writings in Joy Division: Piece by Piece: Writing About Joy Division 1977–2007 (Plexus, 2007/2015), which spans his NME reviews through to retrospective essays. (Amazon.)

NME reportedly barred him from reviewing Manchester band recordings or performances at some point during his tenure — the precise period and circumstances of this editorial restriction are not confirmed in available sources.

Morley was the only journalist permitted to view Ian Curtis’s body following his suicide in May 1980; he had also been present when Curtis suffered a serious epileptic seizure at a London concert in April 1980. (Goodreads summary of Piece by Piece.) He wrote Curtis’s NME obituary.

Factory Catalog Credits (FAC Numbers)

Three Factory catalog items list Morley directly:

  • FAC 104 — ‘The Tube’ from The Hacienda (1984): Morley appeared alongside Tony Wilson on an edition of Channel 4’s The Tube broadcast partly from the Haçienda on 27 January 1984. (factoryrecords.org; FAC 104 page.)
  • FAC 151 — The Festival of the Tenth Summer (1986): Morley compered the culminating all-day concert at G-Mex (Greater Manchester Exhibition Centre), 19 July 1986, organised by Factory Records to mark the tenth anniversary of the 1976 Sex Pistols Lesser Free Trade Hall shows. He also contributed to The Book, a 64-page publication accompanying the festival (alongside Jon Savage, Rob Gretton, Howard Devoto, Liz Naylor, and Cath Carroll). (Wikipedia: Festival of the Tenth Summer, as of 2026-08-26; factoryrecords.org FAC 151.)
  • FAC 211 — Wired Joy Division Feature (Channel 4 documentary, 18 minutes, on the release of Joy Division’s Substance): Morley was interviewed alongside Tony Wilson, Rob Gretton, Alan Erasmus, Peter Hook, Stephen Morris, and Bernard Sumner. (factoryrecords.org FAC 211.)

Tony Wilson Friendship and Biography

Morley and Wilson were long-standing friends; Wilson’s final request was that Morley write his biography. Morley did so: From Manchester with Love: The Life and Opinions of Tony Wilson (Faber & Faber, 2021/US 2022). The book was named a Book of the Year by the Sunday Times, New Statesman, MOJO, and Louder Than War. Morley discussed the friendship and the writing process at the Manchester Literature Festival. (NME announcement; Goodreads.)

Otto Flake Pseudonym

“Otto Flake” was a real person — a German Expressionist novelist (1880–1963). Morley adopted the name as a pseudonym in the mid-1980s following the acrimonious split with The Art Of Noise. He used it to write deliberately venomous, ornate liner notes for an Art of Noise compilation, attacking the new incarnation of the group after they left ZTT. The style was pseudo-academic and ironic — characteristic Morley. He has never publicly explained why he chose Flake’s name specifically.
