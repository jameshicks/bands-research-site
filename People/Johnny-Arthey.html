Johnny Arthey

Discogs ID: 346896 Real name: John Raymond Arthey Born: 1930 — Died: 2007 Aliases: Arthur Raymond (2) (Discogs ID: 7019625), Paul Windsor (Discogs ID: 2306369) Name variations: J. Arthey, J.A., John Arthey, John R. Arthey, Johnnie Arthey, Johnny Artey, Johnny Arthley, Jonny Arthey, Jean Arty, and others

British pianist, arranger, conductor, and bandleader. Active from the early 1960s through the mid-1970s, Arthey was one of the most prolific session arrangers in British pop, working across an extraordinary range of artists — from French chanson (Joe Dassin) to UK bubblegum (Bay City Rollers, The Piglets) to American country-noir (Lee Hazlewood). He conducted the British orchestra for three Eurovision Song Contest entries in the 1970s, and led orchestral releases under his own name and the names of his assembled groups.

Overview

John Raymond Arthey was a classically trained pianist who transitioned into orchestral arranging and conducting during the early British pop era. His working life was centered on London studios from approximately 1962 onward, and his range was unusually wide: he arranged soul-tinged ballads for Julie Rogers on Mercury, conducted strings for Joe Dassin’s CBS sessions in Paris, gave Barry Ryan’s theatrical pop its orchestral sweep, and arranged reggae crossover releases for Trojan Records. By the late 1960s he was one of a small group of British orchestral fixers — alongside contemporaries like David Whitaker and Keith Mansfield — who gave the British pop-orchestral sound its characteristic texture.

His most internationally prominent credit is the pair of Lee Hazlewood LHI albums recorded in London: Forty (1969) and Cowboy In Sweden (1970), where he arranged several tracks alongside Hazlewood himself and David Whitaker.

The Paul Windsor alias appears on a 1966 Canadian Columbia LP Classics With A Beat! and related pressings — an easy-listening adaptation set issued under that name. The Arthur Raymond (2) alias is attached to a 1961 Conroy single. Both aliases share the same Discogs real name (John Raymond Arthey) and are cross-linked to his main entry.

Career

Early Work (1962–1965)

Arthey’s first documented credits appear in 1962 on Columbia, working with the minor UK singer Peter Harvey (4). By 1964 he was the house arranger/accompaniment director for Julie Rogers at Mercury — a role that gave him his first international exposure, since Rogers’s single “The Wedding” (1964) became a major hit in multiple countries, requiring numerous international pressings. He also worked with John Hanson (3) at Philips on a series of Ivor Novello tribute recordings throughout 1964–1965, sharing those sessions with Christopher Hassall and Mary Bassano.

Major UK Pop Phase (1966–1970)

From 1966 onward, Arthey was in constant demand across British pop. Key relationships of this period:

  • Vince Hill (Columbia): A sustained run of arranging and conducting credits, including Hill’s 1967 Eurovision entry “Edelweiss” — one of the UK’s strongest placings of the decade. Arthey arranged and conducted the single and multiple subsequent pressings.
  • Joe Dassin (CBS): From 1966 onward Arthey was conductor/orchestrator for many of Dassin’s CBS recordings recorded in London, including “A New York” (1966) and the first CBS albums. The relationship continued until at least 1971.
  • Barry Ryan (MGM): Arthey was musical director and arranger on Ryan’s 1969 debut LP Barry Ryan Sings Paul Ryan — the album that contained Ryan’s hit “Eloise,” though the hit itself was arranged by David Whitaker. Arthey handled the majority of the LP’s other tracks.
  • Clodagh Rodgers (RCA Victor): Multiple arrangements from 1969 onward, culminating in the Rodgers & Heart LP (1971) — a Eurovision-related set where he shared arranging duties with Artie Butler and Keith Mansfield. Rodgers represented the UK at Eurovision 1971 with “Jack In The Box,” which Arthey arranged.
  • Lee Hazlewood (LHI Records): See dedicated section below.
  • Trojan Records: From 1970 Arthey was involved in a surprising strand of reggae crossover sessions — arranging and in some cases producing for Desmond Dekker (“You Can Get It If You Really Want”), Horace Faith (“Black Pearl”), Nicky Thomas (“Love Of The Common People”), and various others. These sessions gave the reggae-pop crossover its orchestral framing.

Lee Hazlewood / LHI Records (1969–1970)

Arthey’s most critically noted work outside the British mainstream came on two Lee Hazlewood LPs recorded partly in London for Hazlewood’s Los Angeles-based LHI Records:

Forty (LHI Records, 1969) Hazlewood’s bleak, country-noir meditation on middle age. The album had three arrangers: Big Jim Sullivan (track A4), David Whitaker (A1, A2, A5, B1, B2), and Arthey (credited as “Johnny Arthy”) on tracks A3, B4, B5. Producer: Shel Talmy and Hazlewood. A 2017 reissue on Light In The Attic brought the album renewed attention.

Cowboy In Sweden (LHI Records, 1970) Hazlewood’s album recorded during his Stockholm exile, with London orchestral sessions. Arthey was one of five arrangers alongside Clark Gassman, Craig Doerge, David Whitaker, and Hazlewood himself. Engineers: Eddie Brackett and Mickey Crofford. Associate producers included Donnie Owens, Jack Robinson, Larry Marks (3), and Shel Talmy. Again credited as “Johnny Arthy.”

The 2017 Light In The Attic box set The Cowboy & The Lady / Forty / Requiem For An Almost Lady / Demos ‘69 compiled both albums alongside the Ann Margret duet LP and unreleased demos, underscoring Arthey’s role in that LHI London cluster.

The Bay City Rollers and Bell Records (1971–1972)

Arthey arranged the Bay City Rollers’ debut single “Keep On Dancing” (Bell Records, 1971) — the song that launched their career. He also arranged “We Can Make Music” (Bell, 1972). These credits make him an unexpected participant in the early Rollers story, years before they became teenybopper stars.

He similarly arranged “Johnny Reggae” (Bell, 1971) for The Piglets — the novelty hit voiced by Jonathan King under a pseudonym — and The Piglets’ follow-up material.

Eurovision (1967, 1971, 1977)

Per his Discogs profile, Arthey conducted the orchestra for three Eurovision entries:

  • 1967: Conducted for Vicky Leandros’s Luxembourg entry “Colours Of Love (L’Amour Est Bleu)” — the Discogs credit (release 5883306) shows him as Conductor on the Philips pressing.
  • 1971: Arranged and directed Clodagh Rodgers’s UK entry “Jack In The Box.”
  • 1977: A third entry (details in the linked Arthey Eurovision blog post).

Later Career

Arthey’s production and arranging work extended into the mid-1970s. He produced several artists for Trojan and related reggae labels and worked on later Edward Woodward projects for DJM Records. He also served as music director for Jonathan King sessions at Decca in the early 1970s, including “Hooked On A Feeling” (1971). His orchestral releases under the Johnny Arthey Orchestra name continued to appear on RCA Victor.

Aliases and Name Usage

Johnny Arthey — primary name for the vast majority of his work, 1964 onward.

Arthur Raymond (2) (Discogs ID: 7019625) — used on a 1961 Conroy single (“Three Guitars Mood 1 / Mood 2”). The realname on this Discogs entry is “John Raymond Arthey,” confirming identity. Only one release documented under this alias in the database.

Paul Windsor (Discogs ID: 2306369) — used for the easy-listening LP Classics With A Beat! on Columbia (UK, 1966) and the Canadian Capitol Records pressing of the same album. The album is credited “The Artistry Of Paul Windsor” on one pressing. Arthey is credited as “Adapted By, Arranged By” on these releases. The Discogs Paul Windsor entry lists all three names (Arthey, Arthur Raymond (2), Paul Windsor) as aliases of each other, confirming they are one person.

Spelling variants — Arthey was frequently misspelled in liner notes. The LHI pressings of Forty and Cowboy In Sweden credit him as “Johnny Arthy.” Other variants include J. Artey, J. Arthoy, J. Artney, Johnny Artley, Johnny Hartey.
