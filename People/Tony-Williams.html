Tony Williams

Born: December 12, 1945, Chicago, Illinois Died: February 23, 1997, Daly City, California (heart attack following gallbladder surgery) Real name: Anthony Tillmon Williams Discogs artist ID: 261295

Jazz drummer. One of the most influential drummers in jazz history — redefined the role of the drum kit in small-group improvisation. Central to the Miles Davis second great quintet (1964–68), founding force of jazz-rock fusion via The Tony Williams Lifetime, and a consistent Blue Note leader from the mid-1980s onward.

Biography

Early Career (to 1963)

Williams began performing professionally in Boston as a teenager, working with saxophonist Sam Rivers. His precocity drew attention quickly: still 16, he appeared on Herbie Hancock’s Blue Note debut My Point of View (1963, BN 4126) and on Kenny Dorham’s Una Mas (1963). By mid-1963 he had joined Miles Davis’s working band, recording Seven Steps to Heaven (Columbia, 1963) — his first major Davis session — where his youth-age 17 — belied his technical command of the kit.

The Miles Davis Second Great Quintet (1963–1969)

Williams was the engine of what became known as the second great quintet: Davis, Wayne Shorter (tenor/soprano sax), Herbie Hancock (piano), and Ron Carter (bass). The band’s rhythmic concept — “time, no changes,” or playing “in time” while freely altering meter, density, and accent — was largely driven by Williams’s restless, conversational drumming.

Tony Williams Lifetime — First Phase (1969–1972)

Williams left the Davis band in 1969 to form The Tony Williams Lifetime — one of the founding documents of jazz-rock fusion. The initial lineup was a power trio: Williams (drums, vocals), John McLaughlin (guitar), and Larry Young (organ). Jack Bruce joined for Turn It Over (1970), replacing Young for that record.

Williams produced and directed the first three Lifetime records. Emergency! was produced by Jack Lewis and Monte Kay; Williams co-produced from Turn It Over onward. He also designed the Turn It Over jacket. Ego (1971) saw him write all the music and arrangements, with Ron Carter on bass and cello and Khalid Yasin on organ — a different direction from the McLaughlin power-trio format.

The New Tony Williams Lifetime (1975–1976)

After the original Lifetime disbanded, Williams reformed around a new lineup: Alan Pasqua (keyboards), Allan Holdsworth (guitar), and Tony Newton (bass). This version recorded Believe It (1975, Columbia) and Million Dollar Legs (1976). The Holdsworth connection made this group significant to the emerging fusion / prog-jazz community.

V.S.O.P. and Sideman Work (mid-1970s–early 1980s)

Through the mid-to-late 1970s Williams remained active as a sideman and in the V.S.O.P. Quintet — the acoustic quintet reuniting the Davis second-great-quintet rhythm section (Williams, Hancock, Carter) with Freddie Hubbard on trumpet and Wayne Shorter on saxophone. The group toured extensively and Williams appeared on V.S.O.P. live recordings.

Other sideman credits from this period include:

  • Stanley Clarke, Stanley Clarke (1974, Atlantic) — drums
  • Stanley Clarke, Journey to Love (session)
  • George Duke, The Golden Scarab (1974, Mercury)
  • Herbie Hancock, Herbie Hancock (Blue Note, 1975)
  • Herbie Hancock Trio, Third Plane (1978, JVC) — with Ron Carter

Williams also recorded in international contexts: Play or Die (1980, Switzerland, P.S. Productions) with Tom Grant and Patrick O’Hearn; New World (1979, Pausa Records) with European players including Niels-Henning Ørsted Pedersen, John Etheridge, Gordon Beck, Francis Lockwood, Didier Lockwood, and Jean-Michel Kajdan.

Solo Leader Work — Blue Note Return (1985–1996)

Williams re-signed with Blue Note in 1985 and released a string of leader dates with a consistent working band centered on trumpeter Wallace Roney, saxophonist Billy Pierce, and pianist Mulgrew Miller.

Wilderness (1996) was Williams’s final studio album and the most ambitious orchestral project of his career — featuring large chamber ensemble alongside a core band of Hancock, Brecker, Clarke, and Pat Metheny. Williams orchestrated several tracks himself.

Trio of Doom (1979 live, released 2007)

The Trio of Doom — Williams, John McLaughlin, and Jaco Pastorius — performed at the CBS Jazz Festival in Havana, Cuba, 1979. The recording went unreleased until 2007 (Columbia/Legacy). A one-off supergroup summation of jazz-rock; McLaughlin supervised the reissue.

A Tribute to Miles (1994)

Williams participated in A Tribute to Miles (Qwest/Reprise, 1994) — a memorial recording featuring the reunited second-great-quintet plus guests. Co-billed with Herbie Hancock, Ron Carter, Wayne Shorter, and Wallace Roney (in the Davis chair).

Death

Williams died February 23, 1997 in Daly City, California, of a heart attack following gallbladder surgery. He was 51. His last studio album Wilderness had appeared the previous year.
