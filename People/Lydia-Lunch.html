Lydia Lunch

Real name: Lydia Anne Koch Born: 2 June 1959, Rochester, New York Labels: ZE Records, 4AD, Doublevision, Widowspeak Productions (own), Triple X, Rykodisc, Atavistic, Rustblade Aliases: Honeymoon In Red, Stella Rico Active: 1977–present

Singer, musician, poet, novelist, filmmaker, spoken word performer. One of the central figures of No Wave — not just a participant but a connector across the full downtown NYC-to-global underground network. Her career spans nearly five decades and an enormous range of collaborators; the consistency is attitude and literary preoccupation rather than genre.

Origins: Teenage Jesus And The Jerks (1977–1980)

Left Rochester for New York City at 16. By 1977 she had formed Teenage Jesus And The Jerks — one of the four bands documented on Brian Eno’s No New York (Antilles, 1978). No Wave’s founding document. The Jerks were confrontational even by No Wave standards: Lunch on slide guitar and vocals, ultra-slow tempos, abrasive texture. The band appeared on ZE Records (one release, 1979) and was part of the same circle as James Chance/The Contortions, Mars, and DNA.

Beirut Slump — early side project, same period.

ZE Records: Queen Of Siam (1980)

Debut album. ZE Records, 1980. 26 pressings across territories — the most widely distributed of her early records. Produced with the Billy Ver Planck Orchestra — a jazz big band arrangement, entirely against No Wave expectations. Engineers included Bob Blank (Blank Tape Studios) and key contributors Robert Quine (Richard Hell & The Voidoids — 20 shared releases on this record), Jack Ruby (3), Dougie Bowne, Pat Irwin, all with 20 shared releases.

The album is extraordinary in context: a No Wave vocalist fronting a jazz orchestra, with lyrics drawn from a noir/pulp aesthetic. ZE’s “mutant disco” philosophy applied to an entirely different genre category.

The JG Thirlwell Thread

Thirlwell is her most prolific collaborator across the entire DB — appearing under both his own name (36 shared releases) and as “Clint Ruin” (22 shared releases), approximately 58 releases total. Key joint projects:

  • The Drowning Of Lucy Hamilton — Widowspeak, 1985
  • Hysterie — CD Presents, 1986 (later releases via Crippled Dick Hot Wax!)
  • York (First Exit To Brooklyn) — Blue Noise Japan, 1997; with The Foetus Symphony Orchestra
  • Stinkfist — Widowspeak, 1988 (as Clint Ruin — the “Death Valley 69” connection suggests Thurston Moore may also appear under this credit on some releases; see research queue)

Thirlwell had 6 releases on ZE Records in 1984, the same label as Lydia Lunch’s Queen Of Siam. Both were also part of the NYC industrial/post-punk underground in the early 1980s — operating at the point where No Wave’s confrontational energy intersected with European industrial.

The 4AD / Birthday Party Connection

Two 1982 4AD releases with Rowland S. Howard (Birthday Party guitarist):

  • “Drunk On The Pope’s Blood / The Agony Is The Ecstasy” — split EP with The Birthday Party; 4AD, 1982
  • “Some Velvet Morning” — Lee Hazlewood/Nancy Sinatra cover; duet with Howard; 4AD, 1982

Howard (26 shared releases total) is one of her most sustained musical relationships. Barry Adamson (4), Mick Harvey (4), Harry Howard (6), and Jim Sclavunos (18) all extend the Birthday Party network throughout her catalog.

”Drunk On The Pope’s Blood / The Agony Is The Ecstasy” (4AD, February 1982)

How it came about: Lunch met The Birthday Party in New York in October 1981 during their first US tour. She subsequently travelled to the UK to support them on their November 1981 UK dates. Both sets at The Venue, London on 26 November 1981 were recorded and became the split EP — a document of a shared bill rather than a deliberate studio collaboration. No specific account of Ivo Watts-Russell brokering the pairing has surfaced; both acts were already in the 4AD orbit, making the label the natural home for the document. (standupandspit.wordpress.com, Wikipedia as of 2025-06-25)

The split:

  • Side One (The Birthday Party): Nick Cave (vocals), Rowland S. Howard (guitar), Mick Harvey (guitar), Tracy Pew (bass), Phill Calvert (drums). Three of the four Birthday Party tracks were later included on their Live 1981–82 CD; their cover of “Loose” has never been officially reissued on CD.
  • Side Two (Lydia Lunch): Lydia Lunch (vocals), Steve Severin (guitar — on feedback; Siouxsie & The Banshees bassist, notably not Murray Mitchell), Murray Mitchell (guitar), Kristian Hoffman (drums). The Lunch side is a single continuous track recorded up to the run-out groove, causing it to play endlessly on manual turntables. Never officially reissued on CD until a 2016 Bandcamp reissue (The Agony Is The Ecstacy) paired it with a second live recording from De Effenaar, Eindhoven, 18 December 1981. (Wikipedia; Trouser Press via trouserpress.com)

The relationship: After the November 1981 UK dates, Lunch relocated to London/Berlin in early 1982. She and Cave co-wrote approximately 50 one-act plays during a two-week period (some later published in Cave’s King Ink and the comic AS-FIX-E-8). She and Rowland S. Howard became the closest creative partners — touring and recording through 1982, including the Honeymoon In Red sessions — while her relationship with Cave and the rest of the band eventually soured: “Basically, they did too many drugs and I didn’t.” (standupandspit.wordpress.com; fromthearchives.com)

“Some Velvet Morning” (4AD, September 1982)

How it came about: The seed was planted at The Birthday Party’s first New York show. Lunch recalls: “When they first played in New York I ran up to Rowland and pledged my devotion. He knew my work and immediately proposed covering ‘Some Velvet Morning’ together.” She notes that Howard’s knowledge of Lee Hazlewood was itself striking — very few people at that time knew Hazlewood’s work. (AnotherMan magazine, “Thurston Moore, Lydia Lunch and More Remember Rowland S Howard”)

So the collaboration was Howard’s proposal, made at their very first meeting — triggered by mutual recognition of each other’s work. The split EP on 4AD in February 1982 had already documented their joint orbit; the “Some Velvet Morning” session followed.

The recording: Recorded at Riverside Studios, London, March 1982. Personnel on the session included Howard on voice, guitar, and saxophone. The B-side, “I Fell in Love with a Ghost”, was an original composed by Lunch and Howard — the first song they wrote together. Released as a 12” single on 4AD in September 1982. (fromthearchives.com)

What followed: By June 1982 Lunch and Howard were in Berlin recording backing tapes with Einsturzende Neubauten for live performances, and recording early sessions for Honeymoon In Red at Game-Studio 36. They toured Europe as a duo across June–July 1982 (Eindhoven, Amsterdam, Paris, Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Bremen, Cologne, Frankfurt and others), performing a set they called “Thirsty Animal” using backing tapes. (fromthearchives.com)

The cover choice: “Some Velvet Morning” (Lee Hazlewood / Nancy Sinatra, 1967) was already an uncanny original — a duet with a lysergic, slightly menacing undertow. Lunch and Howard leaned into that quality, producing a version described as making it “even murkier,” amplifying its “fever dream” quality. (hit-the-north.uk)

The Sonic Youth Connection

“Death Valley 69” appeared twice in different forms:

  • Iridescence Records, 1984 (US)
  • Homestead Records / Blast First (UK), 1985

Blast First was the key UK conduit for downtown NYC noise/underground: Sonic Youth, Big Black, Dinosaur Jr., Butthole Surfers. Lunch appeared on their first major UK release alongside Sonic Youth (Thurston Moore, Kim Gordon, Lee Ranaldo all credited). Wharton Tiers engineered.

Widowspeak Productions

Lunch founded Widowspeak Productions (her own label) in 1985 and used it as the primary release vehicle through the late 1980s and intermittently ever since. Almost all her mid-period work appeared on it: Heart Of Darkness, The Drowning Of Lucy Hamilton, The Right Side Of My Brain, Oral Fixation, Stinkfist, Honeymoon In Red, Our Fathers Who Aren’t In Heaven, Brutal Measures (2016), The War Is Never Over (2021). Running your own label in 1985 was unusual; it gave her total editorial control across music and spoken word.

Re-Up (1999) — the collaboration record

Re-Up (Les Disques Du Soleil Et De L’Acier, France, 1999) is the clearest single document of her broader network: credited collaborators include Genesis P-Orridge, Alan Vega, Étant Donnés (Marc Hurtado), Marc Hurtado, Z’EV (via Twisted), Mark Cunningham (Mars). Effectively an index of the industrial/post-punk underground connective tissue she’d built since 1977.
