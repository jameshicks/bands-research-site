Maria Muldaur

American singer of folk, blues, gospel, jazz, and R&B. Born Maria Grazia Rosa Domenica D’Amato, 12 September 1943, New York City. Discogs artist IDs: 249836 (as Maria Muldaur), 3261334 (as Maria D’Amato). Best known for the 1973 hit “Midnight at the Oasis,” which reached #6 on the US Billboard Hot 100. Career spans six decades from early 1960s jug-band folk to contemporary New Orleans-flavored blues and early jazz.

Biography

Early Career: Jug Band Era (1963–1971)

Maria D’Amato came up in the Greenwich Village folk revival, gravitating toward the rowdy acoustic traditions of jug band music. In 1963 she appeared on the debut LP of The Even Dozen Jug Band (Elektra, ID 1582129) — a loose collective that also included David Grisman, John Sebastian, and Joshua Rifkin. She then joined the more prominent Jim Kweskin - The Jug Band (Discogs ID 1013031) — one of the defining outfits of the Cambridge/Boston folk scene — where she served as vocalist and fiddle player alongside Jim Kweskin, Geoff Muldaur, Fritz Richmond, and Bill Keith. During this period she was billed as Maria D’Amato, later Maria D’Amato Muldaur after her marriage to Geoff Muldaur.

The Kweskin band recorded three albums for Vanguard Records: Jug Band Music (1965), See Reverse Side For Title (1966), and Garden of Joy (1967, on Reprise). Maria’s contributions included lead vocals, fiddle, kazoo, and tambourine. After the Kweskin band dissolved around 1968–1969, she and Geoff recorded Sweet Potatoes (Reprise, 1972) as a duo.

Solo Breakthrough: Reprise Years (1973–1978)

Maria Muldaur’s self-titled debut solo album (Reprise, 1973; master 122044) was produced by Joe Boyd and Lenny Waronker, with engineering by Donn Landee and Lee Herschberg. It featured a cast of California studio heavyweights including Ry Cooder, Spooner Oldham, Mac Rebennack (Dr. John), Jim Dickinson, and saxophonist Jerry Jumonville. The album’s lead single “Midnight at the Oasis” — written by David Nichtern — became a top-10 hit and earned Grammy nominations. The album went gold.

The follow-up, Waitress in a Donut Shop (Reprise, 1974), was even more richly appointed: producers again Boyd and Waronker, with session players including Lowell George, Doc Watson, David Lindley, David Grisman, Paul Butterfield (harmonica), Amos Garrett (guitar), Elvin Bishop, Mac Rebennack, James Booker, Benny Carter (conductor on several tracks), and backing vocals from Linda Ronstadt, Kate McGarrigle, Anna McGarrigle, and Bobby King. The album showcased her range across folk, jazz, and New Orleans R&B.

Subsequent Reprise albums — Sweet Harmony (1976), Southern Winds (1978), and the Warner Bros. Make Love to the Music (1978) — continued in a mainstream pop-soul direction with arranger contributions from Nick DeCaro and session players including Mike Finnigan, Wendy Waldman, Bill Payne, and J.J. Cale. Commercial momentum faded through the late 1970s; Open Your Eyes (Warner Bros., 1979) was her last album for the label.

Gospel and Christian Phase (1980–1983)

Around 1980 Muldaur underwent a religious conversion and recorded in a gospel direction. Gospel Nights (Takoma, 1980) was recorded live at McCabe’s in Santa Monica with The Chambers Brothers as special guests, and featured backing musicians from Bob Dylan’s touring band including T-Bone Burnett, David Mansfield, and Stephen Bruton. She co-produced. There Is a Love (Myrrh, 1982) was an explicitly Christian record; Sweet and Slow (Spindrift, 1983) began a transition back toward jazz.

Return to Blues and Jazz (1985–2000)

After a period of smaller-label releases — including Live in London (Stony Plain, 1985) and the jazz-oriented Jazzabelle (Stony Plain, 1993) — Muldaur reconnected with her blues roots in the early 1990s. Louisiana Love Call (Black Top Records, 1992; master 812028) was produced by Hammond Scott and recorded in New Orleans, featuring Dr. John, Aaron Neville, Charles Neville, Amos Garrett, Dave Torkanowsky, and Zachary Richard. Meet Me at Midnite (Black Top, 1994) continued the New Orleans-inflected approach with Tony Braunagel on drums and Rick Vito on guitar.

She then moved to Telarc for a run of genre-themed albums: Fanning the Flames (1996, blues, with Bonnie Raitt, Huey Lewis, Mavis Staples, Johnny Adams, and Sonny Landreth); Southland of the Heart (1998); Meet Me Where They Play the Blues (1999); and Music for Lovers (2000).

Later Work and Tributes (2001–present)

From 2001 onward Muldaur produced most of her own records and leaned into roots and tribute formats. Richland Woman Blues (Grooveland, 2001) was a self-produced collection of classic blues from the repertoire of Memphis Minnie, Bessie Smith, and others. A Woman Alone with the Blues: Remembering Peggy Lee (Telarc Blues, 2003) was a stylistically sophisticated jazz tribute. Sisters & Brothers (Telarc, 2004) was a joint album with Rory Block and Eric Bibb. Sings Love Songs of Bob Dylan: Heart of Mine (Telarc, 2006) covered Dylan’s romantic catalog. She has continued releasing on Stony Plain Records (Canada) through the 2010s and 2020s, with albums including …First Came Memphis Minnie (2012), Steady Love (2011), and Let’s Get Happy Together (2021). Her 2025 release One Hour Mama: The Blues of Victoria Spivey (Nola Blue Records) is her most recent Discogs-catalogued record.

She also appeared on several Jerry Garcia Band live recordings — notably GarciaLive Volume Four (2014) and the 1978 Pure Jerry: Warner Theatre release — indicating a long-standing connection to the Grateful Dead circle.
