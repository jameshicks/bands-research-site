Arto Lindsay

Discogs ID: 10293
Real name: Arthur Morgan Lindsay
Alias: Tino Lytsardo
Born: 28 May 1953, Richmond, Virginia
Origin: Raised in Brazil (missionary parents); New York from 1974

Guitarist, vocalist, composer, and record producer. One of the central figures of the New York No Wave scene (1978–1982) as frontman of DNA; subsequently led Ambitious Lovers (with Peter Scherer) and a long solo career blending avant-garde downtown sensibility with Brazilian MPB. Also a major producer of Brazilian popular music from the late 1980s onward.

Early Life and New York Arrival

Born in Richmond, Virginia, to missionary parents who relocated to Brazil during his childhood. Lindsay grew up in Brazil, absorbing baião, bossa nova, and carnival traditions before returning to the United States for college. He graduated from Eckerd College in St. Petersburg, Florida in 1974 — the same institution that sent Mark Cunningham and China Burg to New York (both joined Mars). Lindsay moved to New York the same year, entering the downtown Manhattan art and music scene.

Brother: Duncan Lindsay (Discogs 410188).

No Wave Era: DNA (1978–1982)

Lindsay co-founded DNA in 1978. The original lineup — Lindsay (guitar, vocals), Robin Crutchfield (organ, vocals), and Ikue Mori (drums) — was among the most extreme units in the New York No Wave scene. Their four tracks on No New York (produced by Brian Eno, Antilles AN 7067, 1978) — “Egomaniac’s Kiss,” “Lionel,” “Not Moving,” “Size” — became the definitive document of their approach: atonal guitar, non-idiomatic drum patterns, deliberately wrong-headed playing.

Robin Crutchfield left after No New York; Tim Wright (2) (bass) replaced him. DNA continued into the early 1980s, releasing A Taste Of DNA (American Clavé AMCL1003 EP, 1981), then a final live set documented on DNA (Last Live At CBGB’s) (Avant AVAN 006, 1993, recorded 1982). The 2004 compilation DNA On DNA (No More Records No. 12) collects the full recorded output — 32 tracks — with Robert Quine producing the two earliest demos (“You & You,” “Little Ants”), Brian Eno producing the four No New York tracks, and the band self-producing the A Taste Of DNA material.

Downtown Period (1978–1984)

Beyond DNA, Lindsay was part of the broader downtown scene. He played with The Lounge Lizards (Editions EG, 1981 debut), and appeared on sessions with The Golden Palominos and various Downtown Music Scene projects. He was part of Locus Solus and other short-lived configurations. This period established him within the circle that included John Lurie, John Zorn, Bill Laswell, and Anton Fier.

Ambitious Lovers (1984–1991)

The most commercially visible phase of Lindsay’s career. With Peter Scherer (keyboards, production), he formed Ambitious Lovers — a New York-based duo that fused Brazilian percussion traditions with synth-funk, new wave production, and post-No Wave vocal style. Their records are distinguished by the contrast between Lindsay’s atonal, off-key singing and lush, highly arranged backing.
