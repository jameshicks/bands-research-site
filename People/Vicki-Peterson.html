Vicki Peterson

Full name: Victoria Anne Theresa Peterson
Born: 11 January 1958, Northridge, Los Angeles, California
Role: Lead guitarist, vocalist, songwriter, producer
Discogs artist ID: 328974
Alias: Vicki Peterson Cowsill (post-marriage to John Cowsill)

American musician — lead guitarist and co-founder of The Bangles, session guitarist, vocalist, and occasional producer. The principal lead guitarist on every Bangles studio album from the 1982 debut EP through the 2003 reunion record. Post-Bangles, she built an extensive parallel career as a freelance backing vocalist, a core member of the Continental Drifters, and one half of the Psycho Sisters vocal duo with Susan Cowsill. Her 2025 LP Long After The Fire (Label 51 Recordings), credited jointly to Vicki Peterson and John Cowsill, marks her first primary solo/co-credited release outside The Bangles.

Biography

Origins and The Bangs (1981–1983)

Vicki Peterson co-founded The Bangles in Los Angeles in 1981 alongside her younger sister Debbi Peterson and Susanna Hoffs. The band initially performed as The Bangs. Their earliest Discogs entry is the 1981 single “Getting Out Of Hand” (Down Kiddie Records), on which Vicki is credited for vocals, bass, and guitar — the lineup still fluid at this stage, with the three founders multi-tracking. The session was engineered by Ethan James; photographs were by David Roback.

The debut proper was The Bangs EP (1982, Faulty Products), produced by Craig Leon. Vicki played lead guitar throughout and held written-by credits across all four tracks (A1 through B1), making her the most prolific songwriter on the debut. Original bassist Annette Zilinskas played bass and harmonica.

The Bangles on Columbia: Classic Albums (1984–1989)

Annette Zilinskas departed in 1983, replaced by Michael Steele. The new four-piece signed to Columbia Records and recorded All Over The Place (1984), produced and engineered by David Kahne, with Joe Chiccarelli mixing. Vicki played lead guitar, sang, and held written-by credits on the majority of the tracklist — she is credited as writer on A1, A3 through B2, and B4 through B6, making her the album’s dominant composer. Michael Steele contributed bass and vocals; Debbi Peterson drums and vocals; Susanna Hoffs rhythm guitar and vocals.

Different Light (1985/1986, CBS/Columbia) was again produced by Kahne, with engineers Tchad Blake and Peggy McLeonard, and David Leonard mixing. Vicki’s role was lead guitar and vocals; no written-by credits are specifically attached to her on this release in Discogs, though “Walking Down Your Street” and “If She Knew What She Wants” carry Hoffs co-writes. The album reached #2 US Billboard 200 and yielded “Walk Like An Egyptian” and “Manic Monday.”

The third Columbia album, Everything (1988), was produced by Davitt Sigerson — not Kahne (a common misattribution). Vicki is credited for lead guitar (“Main Guitar”), mandolin, and percussion, in addition to vocals. The session featured an expanded cast: Phil Shenale (keyboards/programming), David Lindley (lute/dobro/bouzouki/classical guitar), Paulinho Da Costa (percussion), Tommy Morgan (harmonica), Jim Snodgrass (tabla). Mixed by Frank Filipetti; mastered by Doug Sax. “Eternal Flame” reached US #1.

The Bangles dissolved in 1989 following internal tensions around Susanna Hoffs’s increasingly prominent solo profile.

The Paisley Underground: Rainy Day (1984)

Alongside Hoffs, Vicki participated in Rainy Day (1984), the Paisley Underground collective produced by David Roback and engineered by Ethan James. The project drew on members of the Dream Syndicate, the Rain Parade, and The Three O’Clock alongside both Bangles representatives. Rainy Day released one LP on Llama Records and the single “I’ll Keep It With Mine” on Rough Trade — a set of cover versions serving as a primary sonic document of the LA scene’s shared influences. See Paisley Underground.

Post-Bangles: Psycho Sisters, Continental Drifters, Session Work (1990–2003)

After The Bangles’ breakup, Vicki joined two ongoing collaborative projects, both alongside Susan Cowsill (daughter of the Cowsills):

The Psycho Sisters (Discogs ID: 1465003) — a vocal duo of Vicki Peterson and Susan Cowsill. They released the single “Timberline / This Painting” (1994, Singles Only Label) as a primary release, with Vicki credited for guitar and vocals as well as design (she co-designed the sleeve). The Psycho Sisters also freelanced as backing vocalists-for-hire, providing celebrity backing vocals on several notable records. In 1993 they appeared (credited as “The Psycho Sisters”) on Steve Wynn’s Fluorescent album, singing on tracks 3, 4, 6, and 10. In 1992 they contributed backing vocals to Giant Sand’s Center Of The Universe (credited individually as “Psycho Sisters”). They also appeared on Steve Wynn’s 1993 Fluorescent LP (Brake Out Records). Both members are individually listed on these sessions.

The duo’s primary album is Up On The Chair, Beatrice (2014, RockBeat Records), self-produced by The Psycho Sisters. Vicki played acoustic guitar, electric guitar, and alto vocals and also handled additional recording; John Cowsill (Susan’s brother, drummer of the Cowsills) and Russ Broussard played drums; mixed by Jim Scott. The album is the fullest document of Peterson and Cowsill’s joint songwriting voice.

Continental Drifters (Discogs ID: 735159) — a New Orleans-rooted group including Susan Cowsill, Peter Holsapple (dB’s), Mark Walton, Robert Maché, Carlo Nuccio, and others. Vicki joined as vocalist, electric guitar, and acoustic guitar. The self-titled debut Continental Drifters (1994, A&M Records / Hypnotic Records) was self-produced by the band. Personnel on that record: Vicki (vocals, electric/acoustic guitar), Susan Cowsill (vocals, acoustic guitar), Peter Holsapple (vocals, keyboards, acoustic guitar, electric bass, mandolin, slide guitar), Mark Walton (acoustic guitar, electric bass), Robert Maché (electric guitar, slide guitar), Carlo Nuccio (vocals, acoustic guitar, drums). Vicki also contributed to the Continental Drifters’ later Blue Rose Records releases (Out Of My Way, 1997; Vermilion, 1998; Antic Ocean, 2000; Better Day, 2001; Listen, Listen, 2001).

The Bangles Reunion: Doll Revolution (1998–2004)

The Bangles reunited in the late 1990s. Doll Revolution (2003, Down Kiddie Records / Liberty EMI UK) was self-produced by The Bangles alongside engineer/mixer Brad Wood. Vicki played acoustic guitar, electric guitar, lead guitar, and mandolin across the album — an expanded and more varied role than on the classic-era records. She also sang throughout. Additional session players: Peter Holsapple (accordion, keyboards, mandolin), Greg Leisz (lap steel, pedal steel), Dillon O’Brian (electric guitar, Wurlitzer, acoustic guitar), R. Walt Vincent (harmonium), Greg Hilfman and Chick Wolverton (keyboards). The “Bangles Boys Choir” featured Dave Grohl, Chick Wolverton, and John Crooke. Mastered by Stephen Marcussen.

Long After The Fire: First Co-Primary Solo Release (2025)

Long After The Fire (2025, Label 51 Recordings) is credited jointly to Vicki Peterson and John Cowsill — marking her first appearance as a primary credited artist on a studio record outside The Bangles. She played electric guitar (soloist on A5), bass (A6), mandolin (B2), and harmony vocals throughout, and co-wrote the liner notes with Cowsill. Produced and engineered by Paul Allen (46); mixed by Mark Lonsway. Mastered by Howie Weinberg. Other personnel: Jimmy Calire (keyboards, A1/B1), Dave Pearlman (pedal steel), Will Cowsill (backing vocals). Cover photo by Henry Diltz.

Open Questions

  • Vicki’s exact involvement with Redd Kross, Action Skulls (5931769), and Lamps (2) (7904474) — listed as a member on Discogs but no release credits found in the DB for these projects.
  • The Hoodoo Gurus, Tom Petty, and Belinda Carlisle harmony vocal contributions mentioned in her Discogs profile are not individually traceable to specific releases within the current DB scan; the 1987 Blow Your Cool (Big Time / Chrysalis) entry with “Vocals [Guest]” is the best DB candidate for Hoodoo Gurus.
  • Vicki’s specific songwriting credits track-by-track on Different Light and Everything are not captured in this Discogs pressing; only the All Over The Place and The Bangs EP have granular per-track written-by credits visible.
  • The Go-Go’s 1994–95 reunion tour (replacing Charlotte Caffey) is not documented in Discogs (live shows / touring not in scope).
  • Per Discogs alias field, she is also known as “Vicki Peterson Cowsill” — indicating marriage to John Cowsill (of the Cowsills and co-primary on Long After The Fire), but wedding date is not in DB.
  • My Best Evil Friend (2023, Yep Roc) — Debbi Peterson’s page lists it; Vicki’s Discogs credits do not include it, suggesting it may be Debbi’s rather than a joint project.
