Graham Painting

Cellist, bassist, guitarist, and vocalist; core member of Family Fodder across multiple eras. Also billed as Henry Fodder. Real name: Grahame Painting. Also recorded under name variations including Graham Seviro, G Painting, and Grahame (G.D.) Painting. Discogs artist ID: 266396.

Overview

Graham Painting was a central multi-instrumentalist in the Family Fodder collective through their peak years, 1980–1983. His cello gives Monkey Banana Kitchen (1980) much of its textural strangeness; by ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy ! (1981) he was pulling the weight of the entire rhythm/guitar section (bass, guitar, percussion, voice) in a stripped-down lineup. He also contributed cello and tambourine to the “Coral” single (1982) — one of the earliest Jungle Records releases — and appeared as an uncredited vocalist, percussionist, and guitarist on the Belgian LP Greatest Hits (1981).

Outside Family Fodder, his credits span a broad and somewhat unexpected range: drums and vocals for A Popular History Of Signs (1981 single and 1983 release), cello and bass for Hermine Demoriane’s The World On My Plates (Crammed Discs, 1982), guitar on sessions for Principles Of Soul (1995), Sonia (1996), and Baby Fox (1997). He was also a member of the early-1980s new wave act Propaganda (12) alongside Gary Finch and Julian Macqueen, the pre-Family Fodder group The Passengers (6) (1979 self-released single), and the 2010s UK act Horseless Headmen, who released The Whole Nine Yards (North Circular Records, 2012).

The Henry Fodder alias (Discogs ID: 4114050) is used in writing credits on ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy ! alongside the band collective credit.

On Monkey Banana Kitchen, Painting appears alongside Alig, Felix Fiedorowicz, Ian Hill, Charles Bullen, Rick Wilson, Mick Hobbs, Martin Harrison, Mark Doffman, Dominique Levillain, Judy Carter, and Naresh Singh Kaul.

On ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy !, the lineup was a stripped-down four-piece: Alig (keyboards), Ian Hill (keyboards), Graham Painting (bass, guitar, percussion, voice), and Bazz Smith (drums). Produced by Chris Mansbridge and Family Fodder. Writing credits on that release go to Bazz Smith, Henry Fodder, Ian Hill, and John Pearce — confirming Graham Painting’s compositional contribution under his Henry Fodder alias.

The Hermine (3) session (The World On My Plates, Crammed Discs, 1982) is particularly notable: it connects Painting directly to the Crammed Discs world through Simon Brint (piano/organ) and Chris Mansbridge (engineer) — the same Mansbridge who produced ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy !. The 1983 “Lovely Rita” single for Martin Pig on Rough Trade shows his cello work continuing outside the Family Fodder frame.

The 1990s sessions for Principles Of Soul (Urban Groove label, UK soul/R&B) and Sonia (pop vocalist) demonstrate a working guitarist picking up diverse session work across genres.
