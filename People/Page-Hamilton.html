Page Hamilton

Guitarist, vocalist, producer, film composer. Born 18 May 1960, Portland, Oregon. New York City. Discogs artist ID: 9375.

Overview

Page Hamilton is the founder, principal songwriter, guitarist, and vocalist of Helmet (2). Before forming Helmet, he moved through two of the key guitar-ensemble acts in the late-1980s NYC experimental/noise scene: Glenn Branca Ensemble and Band Of Susans. His guitar work draws directly on the guitar-orchestra lineage of Glenn Branca and Rhys Chatham, filtered through a precision-metal sensibility that he forged into Helmet’s signature sound. Alongside his band work he has maintained a parallel career as a session guitarist, film score contributor, and outside producer from the mid-1990s onward.

Early Life and Education

Hamilton grew up in Portland, Oregon (born 18 May 1960) and studied music formally — he has a degree in jazz guitar — before relocating to New York City, where he inserted himself into the downtown experimental scene in the late 1980s.

Pre-Helmet: Glenn Branca Ensemble (1989)

Hamilton’s first significant NYC credit was as one of the guitarists on Glenn Branca’s Symphony No. 6 (Devil Choirs At The Gates Of Heaven) (Blast First, 1989; release ID: 590066). The ensemble lineup on that recording included Phil Kline, Jonathan Bepler, Evans Wohlforth, Carolyn Master, Eric Hubel, John Myers, and Algis Kizys on guitar/bass, with Stephan Wischerth on drums; Branca conducted and also played guitar. Wharton Tiers produced. Hamilton is listed as one of eight guitarists in the ensemble credit.

Pre-Helmet: Band Of Susans (1989)

Hamilton joined Band Of Susans for what became the band’s international breakthrough record: Love Agenda (Blast First / Mute, 1989; release ID: 393949). He replaced Susan Lyall in the guitar section alongside Karen Haglof, with Robert Poss (guitar, vocals, producer), Susan Stenger (bass, vocals), and Ron Spitzer (drums). Hamilton sang and played guitar. Poss produced; recording was split between Planet Sound and Platinum Island studios.

He also appeared on the promotional/touring-associated release Hard Light (Blast First, 1989; release ID: 2995722), credited again as Guitar, Vocals alongside the Love Agenda lineup. He left Band Of Susans shortly after to form Helmet.

Helmet: Formation and Amphetamine Reptile Era (1989–1992)

Hamilton formed Helmet in New York City in 1989. The founding lineup solidified around Hamilton (guitar, vocals), Henry Bogdan (bass), John Stanier (drums), and Peter Mengede (second guitar). They self-released a demo/self-titled cassette (1989) and quickly attracted Amphetamine Reptile Records, the Minneapolis noise-rock label run by Tom Hazelmyer.

Born Annoying (7”, Amphetamine Reptile, 1989; release ID: 1283651) was the first release. Strap It On (LP, 1990; release ID: 381575) followed, produced and engineered by Wharton Tiers, with all songs written by Hamilton. The album distilled the guitar-ensemble influences of Branca and Band Of Susans into a severe, riff-locked metal idiom. Tiers remained a key production figure for the next several releases.

Unsung (7”, Amphetamine Reptile, 1991; release ID: 830813) preceded the breakthrough LP.

Meantime (1992; release ID: 367025) was the band’s second full-length and their signing to the major-label network via Amphetamine Reptile / Atlantic / Interscope. Produced by Helmet themselves, with Tiers recording tracks 2–10 and Andy Wallace mixing. All songs written by Hamilton. The album broke Helmet nationally and drove heavy MTV rotation.

Helmet: Betty and Aftertaste (1994–1997)

Peter Mengede departed and was replaced by Rob Echeverria for Betty (1994; release ID: 367030). Produced by Helmet and T-Ray; recorded by Martin Bisi and Anton Pukshansky. Andy Wallace returned to mix. Hamilton wrote the bulk of the material, with Bogdan co-writing tracks 6 and 12. The lineup of Hamilton / Bogdan / Stanier / Echeverria is the one documented on this record.

For Aftertaste (1997; release ID: 367067), Chris Traynor was added as live guitarist. Dave Sardy produced and recorded alongside Greg Gordon; Terry Date mixed; Ted Jensen mastered. John Stanier and Henry Bogdan both departed after this album, effectively ending the classic Helmet lineup.

Solo: Zulutime with Caspar Brötzmann (1996)

During the Betty/Aftertaste gap, Hamilton co-led a guitar duo project with German guitarist Caspar Brötzmann. Zulutime (1996; release ID: 103680) was released on Blast First (and sub-licensed to Sub Rosa and Our Choice). Hamilton played guitar, produced, and co-wrote all material with Brötzmann. Wharton Tiers co-produced alongside Hamilton, Brötzmann, and Bruno Gebhard. Brötzmann also provided cover artwork/drawing. This was the only primary-credited release on which Hamilton appeared as a lead artist outside Helmet.

Session and Production Work (1990s–2000s)

Compilations and Collaborations

Hamilton contributed guitar vocals to Born Annoying (Amphetamine Reptile, 1989), a Helmet song that appeared on a compilation sampler. He appeared as a featured vocalist on Goldbug’s 1998 single (Antilles / Verve; release ID: 1963625). In 2007 he performed as part of the NY Band on the Across The Universe film soundtrack (Interscope; release ID: 1342516).

Helmet: Reunion and Later Records (2004–2023)

Helmet reformed in 2004. Size Matters (2004; release ID: 401091) featured Hamilton producing, with Chris Traynor and John Tempesta filling out the lineup; Charlie Clouser and Jay Baumgardner contributed additional production. Frank Bello (Anthrax) played live bass.

Monochrome (2006; release ID: 862449) was co-produced by Hamilton and Wharton Tiers — again bringing Tiers back into the fold. The lineup: Hamilton, Chris Traynor (guitar/bass), Jeremy Chatelain (bass), Mike Jost (drums).

Seeing Eye Dog (2010; release ID: 2540710) on Work Song (+1 Records) found Hamilton producing solo over a new lineup: Dan Beeman (guitar), Dave Case (bass), Kyle Stevenson (drums).

Dead To The World (2016; release ID: 9253680) on Ear Music maintained the Beeman / Case / Stevenson lineup; Hamilton produced and contributed additional engineering. Stevenson co-wrote tracks 3–5, 8.

Left (2023; release ID: 28845598) on Ear Music / Edel was the most recent studio album. Hamilton produced alongside Mark Renk and Jim Kaufman. Hamilton wrote all lyrics (tracks 1–10) and co-wrote all music with Stevenson (tracks 1–8, 10) and solo (track 9); track 11 covers John Coltrane. Strings were arranged by Hamilton and Paul Cartwright.
