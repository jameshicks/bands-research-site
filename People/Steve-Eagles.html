Steve Eagles

UK guitarist. Long-running member of Blurt and earlier of The Photos and Satan’s Rats. Discogs artist ID: 379581.

Overview

Steve Eagles is a UK guitarist whose career spans punk, post-punk, and experimental rock from the late 1970s onward. He first appeared on record with Satan’s Rats, a punk band whose debut single “In My Love For You” appeared on DJM Records in 1977. He then joined The Photos, contributing guitar and vocals to their 1980 self-titled debut LP on Epic and co-writing material on their 1983 single “There’s Always Work” on Rialto.

Eagles joined Blurt around 1984–85, replacing Peter Creese as the band’s guitarist. His first Blurt recording was the White Line Fever EP (1984, Another Side), credited as “Eagles” for the music. He was first individually named as “Steve Eaglet” on Friday The 12th (1985), a live album. From Poppycock (1986) onward he was credited as Steve Eagles or S. Eagles and took increasing co-writing credits alongside Ted Milton and various drummers. He has remained the band’s guitarist into the 2020s, making him one of Blurt’s longest-serving members after Ted Milton himself.

Concurrently with early Blurt activity, Eagles was also active in Bang Bang Machine, a UK alternative/indie rock group active through the early-to-mid 1990s. He played guitar and co-wrote material across their releases on Jimmi Kidd Rekordz and Parallel Records, which were produced by John Fryer on the album Amphibian (1995).

In 1996 he compiled and annotated What A Bunch Of Rodents, a Satan’s Rats retrospective on Overground Records, underscoring a continuing connection to his punk roots.

Name variations on Discogs: Eagles, S Eagles, S. Eagles, S.Eagles, Steve Eaglet.
