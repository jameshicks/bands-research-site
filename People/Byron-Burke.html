Byron Burke

Chicago house producer, keyboardist, programmer, and songwriter. Discogs artist ID: 19968. Real name Byron Ellis Burke; worked extensively as “Byron ‘B. Rude’ Burke.” Co-founder of Ten City alongside Byron Stingily and Herb Lawson, and the principal architect of the group’s sonic template — drum programming, synthesizer bass, acoustic piano, and synth arrangements — across their four Atlantic/EastWest-era albums (1989–1994).

Career

Early Chicago house scene (pre-1987)

Burke came up in the Chicago house scene around the DJ International / Trax Records axis. His earliest documented work is as producer and co-writer under the group name Ragtyme — the trio he formed with Stingily and Lawson before they were signed. A 1987 Ragtyme single, “Fix It Man” (Bright Star Records, featuring T.C. Roper), lists Burke and Stingily as both producers and writers. Also in 1987 Burke and Stingily co-produced and co-wrote “Love Emergency” for singer Rachel (2) on Atlantic — the same year Atlantic would sign the trio as Ten City.

Burke was also a member of the early Chicago house group Truth (2) (with Stingily and Marshall Jefferson), whose “Open Our Eyes” dates to 1988.

The Discogs profile notes Burke originally signed with DJ International under the name “B Rude, Inc,” with Stingily as lead singer — an early iteration of what became Ragtyme and then Ten City.

Ten City — Atlantic / EastWest era (1989–1992)

Atlantic originally wanted to sign Stingily as a solo artist, but Stingily insisted on bringing in Burke and Lawson. The trio signed as Ten City, with the production entity Burstinglaw (= Burke–Stingily–Lawson) handling their publishing through Burstinglaw Music Productions.

Foundation (Atlantic, 1989) — Ten City’s debut LP. Burke is credited as Drum Programming, Acoustic Piano, Synthesizer Programming, and Synthesizer Bass, plus Backing Vocals and horn arrangement (alongside Jefferson). Jefferson produced the majority of tracks; Ten City co-produced several. Burke co-wrote most of the tracklist. The single “That’s the Way Love Is” charted in R&B and dance markets.

State of Mind (Atlantic, 1990) — Second LP, produced by Jefferson and Ten City. Burke takes Additional Lead Vocals alongside Stingily’s lead role. He is also credited as co-writer on the singles “Whatever Makes You Happy,” “Superficial People,” and “I Should Learn to Love You.”

No House Big Enough (EastWest Records America, 1992) — Third LP. By this point Burke’s keyboard and programming workload had expanded significantly: credits include Keyboards, Drum Programming, Acoustic Piano, Organ, Synthesizer, Synthesizer Programming, Percussion, Strings (programmed), and Backing Vocals. Billy Beck and Tim Gant contributed additional keyboards; Herb Lawson handled lead guitar, bass, and further synthesis. Burke shares writing credits throughout.

Columbia era and dissolution (1993–1996)

Ten City signed to Columbia for their fourth album:

That Was Then, This Is Now (Columbia, 1994) — Burke, Stingily, and Lawson are listed as performers (“Ten City Is”); Burke did not take production credit on the album itself. The single “Fantasy” (Columbia, 1993), produced by Ten City and co-written with Masters at Work (Louie Vega and Kenny “Dope” Gonzalez), performed well in clubs. “Goin’ Up In Smoke” (Columbia, 1994) followed.

Also during 1994, Burke and Stingily co-produced and co-wrote “Nite Life” for Kim English on Nervous Records, later remixed by Armand Van Helden.

After the Columbia period, Burke pursued production under several aliases:

  • N-2-U — “Touch It” (House Jam Records, 1991); Burke credited as producer and writer
  • Komputer Kidz — “Rude Train” (Maxi Records, 1996); Burke on production and vocals
  • Byron Burke (solo) — “Time Plexx E.P.” (Continuemusics, 1996); the single solo release under his own name as primary artist
  • Vision (20) — “Somewhere” (MAW Records, 1997); a dance project released through Masters at Work’s label, with Burke producing and writing, executive-produced by Louie Vega and Kenny “Dope” Gonzalez
  • Bungie Boyz — active 1995 (Underground Construction label); “Touch Me” (1995) credits Burke as producer, mixer, arranger, and co-writer

Ten City’s retrospective output on Ibadan Records (2001) — the “Classics” compilations — draws from the Atlantic/EastWest-era catalog where Burke co-wrote the material.

Outside production (1990–1999)

Beyond Ten City, Burke’s standalone production credits include:

  • Truth (2) — “Life” (FFRR, 1990): co-produced and co-written with Stingily; a Chicago house recording under the alias the pair shared with Jefferson
  • Rachel (2) — “Love Emergency” (Atlantic, 1987): early outside production alongside Stingily
  • Kim English — “Nite Life” (Nervous Records, 1994/1995): co-wrote, co-produced, co-arranged original; Armand Van Helden remix
  • Groovin’ Ya (Defender Music, 1997): listed under the Komputer Kidz alias, produced and written by Burke
  • “Somewhere” as Vision (20) on MAW Records (1997)
  • “String Section / Harmattan” (Maxi Tracks, 1998): co-released under Burke’s name; two-track production release

Burke also co-wrote “Privately” (BPM King Street Sounds, 1995) and contributed lyrics to the Italian dance release “Heaven” (1998, Ariola/BMG Ricordi).

His writing credits have long tails: Ten City’s Atlantic material continued to be remixed and re-released through the late 1990s and into the 2000s (e.g., “Superficial People (The Shelter Remixes),” Soundmen On Wax, 2001; “That’s the Way Love Is,” multiple 1995–1999 re-releases on EXP Recordings, Nervous Records).

Post-Ten City and later credits (2015–2024)

Burke’s songwriting credits continued to appear on later releases not connected to Ten City’s 2021 reunion (which involved only Stingily and Jefferson):

  • “The Giver (Reprise)” (Virgin EMI Records, 2015, Europe) — credited as Written-By / Composed By
  • “Different Strokes” (TMRW Music, Australia, 2024) — credited as Composed By

These suggest ongoing licensing of earlier compositions rather than active new production.
