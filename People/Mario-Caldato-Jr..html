Mario Caldato Jr.

Discogs ID: 19134 · Real name: Mario G. Caldato Jr. · Also credited as: Mario C, Mario C., MCJ, Caldato Jr., Dato, M. Caldato

Brazilian-American producer, engineer, and mixer. Runs MCJ Studio (location unconfirmed from DB — likely Los Angeles). Active in Discogs credits 1987–2026. Best known as the primary studio engineer and co-producer on the Beastie Boys’ creative peak, and for a parallel career bringing American hip-hop production sensibilities to Brazilian MPB, rap, and pop.

Background

Mario G. Caldato Jr. is of Brazilian origin and based in the United States; his career bridges the two musical cultures throughout its entire span. His entry into the industry came through Delicious Vinyl, the Los Angeles hip-hop/R&B label, where he engineered nearly the entire early roster — Tone Loc, Young MC, Def Jef, and others — from 1987 onward. By 1989 he was tracking the Beastie Boys’ Paul’s Boutique at Capitol, which became the pivot point of his career.

Early Career: Delicious Vinyl (1987–1990)

Caldato Jr.’s first credited work is on Delicious Vinyl releases from 1987. He engineered or mixed a remarkable run of early West Coast hip-hop records on that label:

Beastie Boys (1989–2005)

The relationship with Beastie Boys is the defining thread of Caldato Jr.’s discography. He appears on every major album the group released from Paul’s Boutique (1989) through Solid Gold Hits (2005), and co-wrote tracks across several of them.

Paul’s Boutique (1989)

Engineer credit alongside the Beastie Boys and The Dust Brothers as producers. Paul’s Boutique is a landmark of sample-based production; Caldato Jr.’s role was capturing the technical side of an enormously complex recording. Discogs release ID: 31089 (Capitol Records).

Check Your Head (1992)

First full co-production credit and co-writing credit (tracks 1, 3, 6, 10, 12, 18). The album marked the Beastie Boys’ return to live instruments; Caldato Jr. handled both engineering and production alongside the band. Co-writes include tracks with Mark Ramos-Nishita (Money Mark). Discogs release ID: 53044. Label: Capitol Records / Grand Royal.

Ill Communication (1994)

Engineer and co-producer. Discogs release ID: 46508. Also appeared on the associated singles: Get It Together / Sabotage, Sure Shot, Body Movin’, Root Down EP — spanning engineering, production, and writing credits. Grand Royal / Capitol.

Hello Nasty (1998)

Engineer, producer, and co-writer (tracks 6, 7, 16, 17). Also appeared on the Intergalactic single (written-by, producer, engineer). Discogs release ID: 10665.

Later Beastie work

Co-produced Remote Control / 3 MCs & 1 DJ (1999). The Anthology: The Sounds Of Science compilation (1999) carries his production and engineering credits across the retrospective. Solid Gold Hits (2005) lists him as producer. The Aglio E Olio EP (1995) carried an engineer credit. He also recorded the punk side-project EP Dead Fucking Last (1993, Grand Royal) and the hardcore comp appearance America’s Most Hardcore (1994).

Songwriter credits on Beastie Boys material

Across Check Your Head and Hello Nasty, Caldato Jr. accumulated substantial co-writing credits — unusual for a producer/engineer of his type. The extent of his compositional involvement in those albums is rarely noted in mainstream writing.

Brazilian Music Work

Caldato Jr.’s parallel Brazilian career is as consistent as his American one, running from the mid-1990s through the 2020s and covering MPB, rap, tropicália-adjacent pop, and funk.

Planet Hemp — Os Cães Ladram Mas A Caravana Não Pára (1997)

Co-produced and recorded with Rio hip-hop/rock group Planet Hemp, featuring Marcelo D2 on vocals. Chaos Recordings, Brazil. Discogs release ID: 1203795.

Bebel Gilberto — Tanto Tempo (2000)

Produced, arranged, and mixed track 7, “So Nice (Summer Samba)” (music by Marcos Valle). The album was the principal Bossa Nova revival record of its era; the dominant producer was Suba, but Caldato Jr. handled this track solo. Co-producers across the album also include Thievery Corporation, Amon Tobin, and Arling & Cameron. Released on Ziriguiboom / Six Degrees Records. Discogs release ID: 12411038.

His work with Bebel Gilberto extended across 9 credits: the Tanto Tempo remixes album (2001, Ziriguiboom), and the 2003 UK reissue (Mixed By, Recorded By credits). Discogs artist ID for Bebel: 17090.

Marcelo D2 — À Procura Da Batida Perfeita (2003)

Producer, mixer, and recording engineer. A major record in Brazilian hip-hop. Chaos (3) / Sony. Discogs release ID: 7549248.

Marisa Monte — Universo Ao Meu Redor (2006)

Co-produced and mixed with Marisa Monte. EMI / Phonomotor Records. Discogs release ID: 903752.

Beck and Other Rock/Alternative Work

Co-produced and mixed “Deadweight” from the A Life Less Ordinary soundtrack (1997, A&M/Geffen) and the Devils Haircut single (1996, Bong Load/Geffen). Most significantly, co-produced and mixed track 9 of Beck’s Odelay (1996, Bong Load/Geffen, with The Dust Brothers handling most other tracks). Discogs release ID for Odelay: 553855.

Additional rock/alternative credits:

Solo / Own Projects

Listed as the sole member of Mario Brothers (Discogs artist ID: 871025) — a Discogs entry with no releases found. Also listed as a member of ABA All-Stars (Discogs ID: 77181), Jungle Bugs (ID: 12366622), Los Hooligans (3) (ID: 2608395), and The Bottletop Band (ID: 2815278) — all minor or non-primary credits.

He plays bass (upright bass is credited specifically on Comin’ Off, 1995, Grand Royal) and vibraphone (credited on Any Port In A Storm, 2010).
