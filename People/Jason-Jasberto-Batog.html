Jason Jasberto Batog

Chicago-based graphic designer and art director who served as the primary visual identity behind Large Records from 1997 through at least 2001, credited under his own name and under his studio imprint OpticaSENS. Responsible for the sleeve, label, and artwork design on nearly 40 releases across Large Records and its sister label Sfere.

  • Discogs ID: 1996145
  • Design imprint: OpticaSENS (Discogs label ID: 1279670 — zero-release label entry pointing to Batog as designer)
  • Roles: Artwork, Design, Sleeve, Label Design
  • Years active (Discogs): 1997–2001
  • Primary label affiliations: Large Records, Sfere

Career

Jason Jasberto Batog’s Discogs footprint is almost entirely concentrated within the Chicago deep-house world centred on Large Records (parent: Large Music, LLC), the city’s foremost deep-house imprint of the late 1990s. He is credited as the label’s dominant design figure across that entire window: out of 126 Large Records releases in the Discogs database, Batog holds design credits on 38 of them — three times the count of the next-most-credited designer (MG (18), at 12). This makes him the de facto house designer for Large during its commercial peak.

His credits begin in 1997 across four simultaneous releases including DJ Sneak’s “Blue Belly Bandit” and early entries in the Soul Searching compilation series mixed by DJ Rasoul. The bulk of his work runs through 1998–2000, covering most of Large’s catalogue from those years: releases by Kerri Chandler, DJ Rasoul, Solar House, Mateo & Matos, 95 North, Miguel Migs, and many others.

One credit on Kerri Chandler’s Digitalsoul (Session One) (1999, release ID 24) specifies “Design [For Opticasens]” — the only release where the imprint name appears explicitly in the role field. His Discogs artist profile describes him simply as “Designer at OpticaSENS,” and the OpticaSENS label page on Discogs (ID 1279670) reciprocally identifies him as the designer. This suggests OpticaSENS was a freelance studio identity Batog used for client work, rather than a separate credited artist name.

His work extends briefly to Sfere (subsidiary of Sfere Recordings Inc.), where he designed four releases in 1999–2001, including Kerri Chandler and Dennis Ferrer’s First Steps (Sfere, 1999) and “Peace In The Middle East” by One World (2) featuring Miri Ben-Ari (Sfere, 2001). The Sfere credits suggest Batog’s relationship with Jeff Craven — the executive producer behind virtually every Large Records release — extended across the related label network.

The most consistent creative pairing across his credits is with executive producer Jeff Craven (35 shared releases) and artists Kerri Chandler (24 shared releases) and DJ Rasoul (15 shared releases). Rick Essig (mastering, 9 shared releases) and Duncan Stanbury (mastering, 8 shared releases) round out the recurring technical team he worked alongside.

No credits appear in Discogs outside the Chicago house sphere during this window. His profile page has no external URLs, and Discogs lists no real name separate from his credited name.
