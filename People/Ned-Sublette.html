Ned Sublette

Born 1951, Lubbock, Texas (grew up in Portales, New Mexico). American composer, guitarist, vocalist, record producer, musicologist, historian, and author. Active in NYC downtown experimental scene from 1976; member of the Glenn Branca Ensemble; co-founder of Qbadisc (1992); 2005 Guggenheim Fellow.

Overview

Sublette studied Spanish classical guitar with Hector Garcia at the University of New Mexico and with Emilio Pujol in Spain. He studied composition with Kenneth Gaburo at UC San Diego before moving to New York City in 1976. There he entered the downtown experimental scene, working with John Cage, LaMonte Young, Pauline Oliveros, Glenn Branca, David Van Tieghem, Peter Gordon, and Arto Lindsay.

He is credited as guitarist on Glenn Branca’s The Ascension (99 Records, 1981) alongside Lee Ranaldo, David Rosenbloom, and Stephan Wischerth — a peer composer-performer in Branca’s guitar orchestra rather than a hired sideman. He also played on Branca’s Symphony No. 1 (Tonal Plexus) (ROIR, 1983), where he is credited with soprano guitar, tenor guitar, steel-wire guitar, and guitar tuning. He served as artistic advisor on Alvin Lucier’s Music For Solo Performer (Lovely Music, 1982).

His 1982 debut as Ned Sublette And The Southwesterners, Western Classics (Lovely Music), drew on country and old-time string-band traditions; the front cover was photographed by Robert Mapplethorpe. His 1984 single “Radio Rhythm (S-I-G-N-A-L S-M-A-R-T)” on Sleeping Bag Records, produced with David Van Tieghem, showed his post-punk/synth-pop crossover interests; mixes were done by David Van Tieghem and engineer Butch Jones, with remixes by Nicky Siano.

During the late 1980s–1990s Sublette deepened a collaboration with conceptual artist Lawrence Weiner. Their work together spans the cassette Ever Widening Circles Of Remorse (1989, self-released, with The Persuasions), the CD Ships At Sea, Sailors & Shoes (Excellent Records, 1993), and Monsters From The Deep (Triple Earth, 1997) — all with Weiner providing lyrics and artwork. Ships At Sea featured David Van Tieghem on percussion and electronic drums, Lenny Pickett on sarrusophone, and Armando Marçal on percussion.

In 1992 Sublette co-founded Qbadisc, a New York label devoted to releasing Cuban music in the United States. As executive producer he oversaw releases by artists including Ritmo Oriental and Isaac Delgado, and compiled/annotated Cuban and Latin compilations for Luaka Bop’s Cuba Classics series (volumes 2 and 3, 1991–1992) and World Pacific Records. He also co-produced PRI’s Afropop Worldwide radio program.

Cowboy Rumba (Palm Pictures / ZE Records, 1999) became his best-known solo album — a fusion of country-western and Afro-Caribbean styles (salsa, cumbia, rumba) featuring NG La Banda, Los Muñequitos de Matanzas, Yomo Toro, and Lloyd Maines; the title track reached #1 on World Music Charts Europe in December 1999. A second album, Kiss You Down South (Postmambo, 2012), continued this synthesis.

In 2006 Willie Nelson released Sublette’s song “Cowboys Are Frequently, Secretly Fond of Each Other” following the success of Brokeback Mountain. Sublette starred in Vidas Perfectas, a Spanish-language version of Robert Ashley’s 1983 television opera Perfect Lives, which premiered at Irondale Theater, Brooklyn, December 2011.

As a scholar, his book Cuba and Its Music: From the First Drums to the Mambo (2004) is a standard reference. He followed it with The World That Made New Orleans (2008) and The Year Before the Flood (2009), both from Lawrence Hill Books. In 2015 he and his wife Constance published The American Slave Coast: A History of the Slave-Breeding Industry.

(Sources: Wikipedia, Ned Sublette; Discogs artist 146475)
