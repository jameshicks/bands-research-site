Fred Kevorkian

Full name: Frederic Kevorkian
Discogs ID: 382969
Origin: New York City, USA
Website: kevorkianmastering.com
Also credited as: F. Kevorkian, FK, “Dr.” Frederic Kevorkian

NYC mastering engineer with credits spanning 1988–2026 across more than 2,000 release entries on Discogs. Known principally as a mastering engineer operating under the Kevorkian Mastering imprint, which Discogs lists as a sublabel/imprint within Avatar Studios (the New York studio complex at 441 W. 53rd St.). Earlier in his career he worked as a recording and assistant engineer at Sear Sound (353 W. 48th St., Walter Sear’s studio) and at Absolute Audio.

Not to be confused with François Kevorkian, the DJ and producer (also NYC-based).

Career Phases

Early career — assistant engineer, Sear Sound (1991–c.2000)

Kevorkian’s earliest Discogs credits are as assistant or additional recording engineer, predominantly at Sear Sound. This era spans jazz, avant-garde, and alternative rock sessions:

  • Sonic Youth — Dirty (1992, DGC): assistant engineer on overdubs; Experimental Jet Set, Trash And No Star (1994, DGC/Geffen): credited with the enigmatic “Arc/Weld Hyper-Assist” technician role, alongside standard assistant engineer credit, at Sear Sound. This credit appears only on Experimental Jet Set and its many pressings.
  • Cassandra Wilson — Blue Light ‘Til Dawn (1993, Blue Note): assistant engineer, digital editing, and sequencing; New Moon Daughter (1995/1996, Blue Note): assistant engineer (Sear Sound) and editor. Both albums were produced by Craig Street and are considered Blue Note’s commercial breakthrough of the era. Cassandra Wilson is the primary subject of these sessions.
  • Anthony Braxton — 4 (Ensemble) Compositions 1992 (1993, Black Saint): assistant engineer
  • Pharoah Sanders — Crescent With Love (1993, Venus Records) and Ballads With Love (1994): engineer
  • Julius Hemphill Sextet — Fat Man And The Hard Blues (1991/1992, Black Saint): assistant engineer
  • Juliana Hatfield — Only Everything (1995, Mammoth/EastWest): Sear Sound assistant engineer
  • Charlie Musselwhite — Signature (1991/1994, Alligator Records): assistant recording engineer
  • Peabo Bryson — Can You Stop The Rain (1991, Columbia): assistant engineer on select tracks
  • Lloyd Cole — Bad Vibes (1993, Fontana/Rykodisc): recording engineer
  • You Am I — Hi Fi Way (1995, WEA): assistant engineer and “cold edits”
  • Holly Cole — Temptation (1995, Metro Blue): assistant engineer, Sear Sound
  • Javon Jackson — For One Who Knows (1995, Blue Note): engineer
  • Muhal Richard Abrams — Familytalk (1993, Black Saint): engineer

Transition to mastering (mid-to-late 1990s)

By the mid-1990s Kevorkian was primarily mastering. His first mastering credits appear around 1995 on small NYC indie labels (Equal Vision, Personal Favorite, Jetset). By 1997–1999 the scope was widening:

  • Joan Osborne connection builds via ATO-adjacent labels
  • Firewater (2) — The Ponzi Scheme (1998, Jetset Records)
  • Congo Norvell — Abnormals Anonymous (1997, Jetset)
  • Buffy The Vampire Slayer (The Album) (1999, TVT/Columbia): major-label comp, one of his first highly-distributed soundtracks
  • Oneida — A Place Called El Shaddai’s (1997, Turnbuckle)
  • Oren Bloedow — Luckiest Boy In The World (1998, Knitting Factory Records)
  • Moe. — L (1999): early of many credits for this jam band

Around this period, the Absolute Audio facility appears as a credit; Kevorkian Mastering as a distinct imprint solidifies by the early 2000s, operating within or in association with Avatar Studios.

Peak indie rock era (2001–2010)

The 2000s mark Kevorkian’s most prominent period, with a dense concentration in indie rock, alt-country, and Americana:

The White Stripes — White Blood Cells (2001, Sympathy For The Record Industry / XL): mastered by Kevorkian across all pressings worldwide. This is one of the most-pressed albums in his Discogs credit list (~55 release entries). Only White Blood Cells appears; other White Stripes albums do not show his credit.

Iggy Pop — Skull Ring (2003, Virgin): mastered across a large worldwide pressing run.

The Dresden Dolls — self-titled debut (2003/2004, 8ft./Roadrunner): mastered.

Regina Spektor — Soviet Kitsch (2003/2004, Shoplifter Records): mastered.

The Walkmen — 19 release entries, including Bows + Arrows (2004, Record Collection), A Hundred Miles Off (2006, Record Collection), and the 2023 remaster of Bows + Arrows (Marcata Recording). The most consistent repeat-client relationship in the vault.

Ryan Adams & The Cardinals — Cold Roses, Jacksonville City Nights, Let It Ride (all 2005, Lost Highway): three albums credited in one year.

Jenny Lewis with The Watson Twins — Rabbit Fur Coat (2005/2006, Rough Trade / Team Love): mastered across many pressings.

Phish — Round Room (2002, Elektra) plus an enormous run of Livephish.com archival releases (2003–2008, Jemp Records / Round Records (3)): ~55 release entries total for Phish and related Trey Anastasio releases. The Round Records (3) / Jemp Records catalog accounts for the highest single-label concentrations in his mastering discography.

The National — Boxer (2007, Beggars Banquet): mastered across many pressings worldwide. One of the most widely-noted credits in this period.

Joan As Police Woman — the most numerically prolific single client in mastering: 57 release entries. Began with Real Life (2006) and continued through at least Cover Two (2020) and the Tony Allen / Dave Okumu / Joan As Police Woman collaboration (2021, [pias]).

Jesse Malin — The Fine Art Of Self Destruction (2002, One Little Indian); New York Before The War (2015, One Little Indian): long-running relationship.

The Jon Spencer Blues Explosion — remastering on Dirty Shirt Rock ‘N’ Roll and Year One (both 2010, Shove Records); new album Meat And Bone (2012, Shove/Bronzerat); Freedom Tower (2015, Shove).

VHS Or Beta — Bring On The Comets (2007, Astralwerks): mastered.

The Apples In Stereo — New Magnetic Wonder (2007), Electronic Projects For Musicians (2008), Travellers In Space And Time (2010): mastered.

2010s–2020s

Activity continued steadily into the 2020s. Notable later credits:

  • The Stooges — Ready To Die (2013, Fat Possum)
  • Peter Murphy — Ninth (2011, Nettwerk): mastered
  • Nina Persson (The Cardigans) — Animal Heart (2014, Sonet/Universal): mastered across European and US pressings
  • Joan Osborne — Bring It On Home (2012), Love And Hate (2014), Songs Of Bob Dylan (2017), Dylanology Live (2025)
  • Pokey LaFarge — 11 release entries
  • Dewolff, Triggerfinger, The Black Box Revelation — European rock bands with concentrated Kevorkian credits across 19–30 release entries each
  • Third Man Records — 21 release entries across various artists; his relationship with this label likely routed through White Stripes-era connections

Studios / Facilities

  • Sear Sound — primary engineering base in the early 1990s (Walter Sear’s NYC studio, 353 W. 48th St.)
  • Absolute Audio (Absolute Audio, Inc.) — appears in credits alongside early mastering work; defunct by mid-2000s
  • Kevorkian Mastering / Avatar Studios — primary mastering facility from mid-1990s onward. Discogs lists Kevorkian Mastering (ID: 296278) as a sublabel/imprint of Avatar Studios (ID: 264960). Personal website kevorkianmastering.com was active as of archival snapshots.

Connections

  • Sonic Youth — assistant engineer on Dirty (1992) and Experimental Jet Set (1994); the “Arc/Weld Hyper-Assist” credit on the latter is a Sonic Youth–specific studio role
  • Cassandra Wilson — significant early credit: engineering and digital editing on both Blue Light ‘Til Dawn and New Moon Daughter, two cornerstone Blue Note albums of the 1990s
  • François Kevorkian — NYC-based DJ and dance music producer; same surname, different person
