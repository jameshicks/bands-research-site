Born march 14, 1961. Was part of the London occult scene and a prolific poet. She played bass in Psychic TV during their Godstar era, and clarinet with Monte Cazazza. She was founding member of Fire+Ice. She passed away in 2022 aged 61.

“It’s a bit unfortunate that Mouse is remembered largely as a bass player in other peoples’ bands, as she was something of a Renaissance woman in what is now generally termed “magical artistry” - an intense and often catalytic personality who wrote poetry and prose, drew, played and wrote music and songs, and took photos - all in concert with her magical preoccupations and all of them rather well. She was one of those people who formed a link between the musical and occult “scenes” and apart from her own accomplishments was something of an “invisible influence”, responsible for many an introduction between people which would bear fruit elsewhere, later down the line.

After that she recorded and played live with her first husband Matthew Stevens (ex-Act of Faith) and Liverpool’s Royal Family And The Poor. Overview

Sharon Beaumont (1961–2022) was a bassist, clarinettist, and poet at the centre of the London occult-experimental scene from the mid-1980s onward. Best known for playing bass with Psychic TV through the Godstar era alongside Alex Fergusson, Genesis P-Orridge, and Monte Cazazza, she was also a founding member of the neofolk group Fire+Ice. Her catalogue runs 1985–2014, primarily on Temple Records, with later reissues on Cold Spring and Let Them Eat Vinyl; contemporaries described her as an “invisible influence” whose introductions between scene figures — straddling industrial, experimental, and magical-arts circles — shaped the broader milieu well beyond her credited roles.

Notes (web) — Fire+Ice connection

No source explicitly documents how Mouse and Ian Read met or the specific circumstances that brought her into Fire + Ice. What the available sources establish:

Shared occult-scene context. Ian Read was the leader of the English branch of the Illuminates of Thanateros (IOT) and was active in chaos magic circles in London circa 1991–92. Mouse gave a talk on magical creativity at the 1992 Chaos Magic Symposium in London (attended by, among others, Andrew Chumbley). Both were therefore operating in overlapping chaos magic / occult-arts circles at exactly the moment Fire+Ice was being formed. [source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk/news/mouse-a-renaissance-woman/ — snippet; Wikipedia on Ian Read https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Read_(musician) as of 2026-11-12]

Shared scene infrastructure. Read had already collaborated with David Tibet (Current 93) and Douglas Pearce (Death in June) since 1987, and it was Tibet and Pearce who encouraged him to release the Fire+Ice debut Gilded By The Sun (1992, New European Recordings). Mouse’s Psychic TV milieu (Temple Records / World Serpent distribution) overlapped substantially with the Current 93 / Death in June / Sol Invictus world. The Discogs profile describes Mouse as “responsible for many an introduction between people which would bear fruit elsewhere” — a cross-scene connector rather than a lateral recruit. [source: https://4ibrecords.com/2013/02/07/an-interview-with-fire-ice-ian-read-the-hidden-spirit/ ; Discogs artist profile 136295 (snippet from greengalloway.blogspot.com)]

What is confirmed. Mouse played bass, keyboards, and recorder for Fire+Ice from 1991–94, contributed the composition “Blood on the Snow” to the Blood on the Snow EP (1993, Asafoetida), and appeared on Gilded By The Sun (1992) and Hollow Ways (1994). These are Discogs-sourced credits.

Dead end. No interview with Mouse (none have surfaced online), no Ian Read interview, and no contemporary press source states how they met or who introduced them. The 1995 Ian Read interview at deathinjune.org and the 2013 interview at 4ibrecords.com both omit Mouse entirely. The blog pieces at greengalloway.blogspot.com and killyourpetpuppy.co.uk/news/ reference her Fire+Ice work without explaining its origins.

Plausible working conclusion (not sourced — inference only). The concurrence of overlapping chaos-magic scene involvement (IOT / 1992 Symposium), shared World Serpent-era social networks (Current 93, Death in June, Sol Invictus all intersecting with PTV’s label world), and Mouse’s documented role as a scene connector makes a shared social introduction in 1990–91 highly plausible. But this is inference; it should not be presented as established fact without a primary source.
