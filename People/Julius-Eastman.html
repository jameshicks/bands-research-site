Julius Eastman

Composer, pianist, vocalist. Full name: Julius Dunbar Eastman. Born October 27, 1940. Died May 28, 1990, Buffalo, NY — homeless, AIDS-related, age 49. Discogs ID: 343937.

Composer and pianist in the American experimental tradition; gay Black avant-garde figure whose work received almost no recognition during his lifetime. Part of the Creative Associates at SUNY Buffalo and the S.E.M. Ensemble. His scores were lost when his possessions were evicted from his apartment; he died two years before Arthur Russell (April 1992). Both have received substantial posthumous recognition. See Downtown Music Scene for broader context.

His compositions — Femenine (c.1974), Gay Guerrilla, Evil Nigger, Crazy Nigger — used deliberately provocative titles as artistic and political statements within the new music world. The posthumous reassessment began with Unjust Malaise (New World Records, 2005) and accelerated through the New Amsterdam Records series: Julius Eastman Vol. 1: Femenine (2021) and Vol. 2: Joy Boy (2022).

Buffalo Philharmonic Orchestra

Two distinct strands: Eastman as performer with the BPO, and BPO-affiliated musicians performing his compositions.

Eastman as BPO performer. The BPO archives list Eastman as one of four pianists (alongside Yuji Takahashi, Nils Vigeland, and Charles Bornstein) in a performance of Stravinsky’s Les Noces, conducted by Lukas Foss, on a program that also included Beethoven’s Symphony No. 9. The exact date is not recoverable from the live web (the archive page archives.bpo.org/bxr-6869.htm returned a socket error), but the Foss–Creative Associates connection places this likely in the early-to-mid 1970s while Eastman was resident at SUNY Buffalo. This is consistent with accounts of Eastman performing and touring works by prominent contemporary composers during his Buffalo years. [Source: BPO archives search result — https://archives.bpo.org/bxr-6869.htm; page unreachable but indexed by Google as of 2026-07-19; corroborating context from https://newmusicusa.org/nmbx/in-search-of-julius-eastman/]

BPO-connected musicians performing Eastman’s compositions. At the “Scores of a Man: A Revival of Julius Eastman” tribute (Burchfield-Penney Art Center, Buffalo, February 10, 2017), Janz Castelo — then a violist in the BPO — founded and led the Buffalo Chamber Players in a performance of Eastman’s Stay On It. The same event included Gay Guerrilla by the Buffalo Chamber Ensemble/BuffFluxus and Crazy Nigger by Amy Knoles. [Source: https://www.dailypublic.com/articles/02072017/julius-eastman-tribute; https://burchfieldpenney.org/about/news/article:02-09-2017-12-00am-julius-eastman-tribute-in-the-public/]

BPO as an institution performing Eastman’s own compositions. No documented performance found. Searches of BPO season announcements (2025-26, 2026-27), the BPO news archive, and the performing archives index return no mention of a BPO concert built around Eastman’s compositions. As of July 2026, the BPO has not publicly programmed his works as composer. Other orchestras have done so recently: the Philadelphia Orchestra performed his Symphony No. 2 in January 2026, and the LA Phil has programmed him. [Source: https://epgn.com/2026/01/06/philadelphia-orchestra-julius-eastman/; https://www.laphil.com/events/performances/3561/2025-03-29/julius-eastman-the-holy-presence]
