Vic Godard

Real name: Victor John Napper. Born: 26 August (year not publicly confirmed). London-based singer-songwriter, guitarist, vocalist. Discogs artist ID: 347629. Frontman and creative core of Subway Sect.

Overview

Vic Godard is among the most consistently unclassifiable figures to emerge from the first wave of UK punk. He formed Subway Sect in south-west London around 1976 as a loose collective of “soul boys” drawn into punk’s orbit via the Clash scene — the band debuted at the 100 Club Punk Festival in September 1976 alongside the Sex Pistols, the Clash, and Siouxsie and the Banshees. Despite this high-visibility launch, Subway Sect remained stubbornly peripheral: Godard’s writing resisted the genre’s anthemic conventions from the start, and the band went through multiple lineup shifts before breaking up, reforming, and breaking up again.

His career after punk is more difficult to categorize than most. He pivoted abruptly from post-punk noise toward jazz-inflected swing and pop in the early 1980s — Songs For Sale (1981, Island) is a fully-realized left-field jazz-pop record — and has continued recording and performing across idioms ever since. He was still releasing new material as recently as 2024.

Name variations across releases: “Vic Goddard,” “V. Godard,” “V.Godard,” “Godard,” “Goddard.”

Subway Sect: Formation and Punk Years (1976–1980)

Subway Sect came out of south-west London. The core early lineup comprised:

  • Vic Godard — vocals, guitar, harmonica, principal songwriter
  • Rob Symmons (Discogs: 2211076) — rhythm guitar
  • Paul Myers (Discogs: 1760044) — bass
  • Bobby Ward (Discogs: 477107) — drums

The band’s debut 7”, “Nobody’s Scared” / “Dontsplitit” (Braik Records, BRS 01, 1978), was produced by George Alexander (2) and written by Godard (A-side) and Rob Symmons (B-side). The performers were uncredited on the sleeve. This single has since become a collector’s piece — 17 pressings counted on Discogs.

The second single, “Ambition” (Rough Trade, RT 004, 1978), was produced by Mickey Foote and featured the same lineup. Foote had just produced the Clash’s debut; his involvement placed Subway Sect directly within the Clash’s production orbit. “Ambition” was one of Rough Trade’s earliest releases and remains Godard’s most well-known early document.

Bernard Rhodes — Clash manager — also managed Subway Sect during this period and produced the first proper album, What’s The Matter Boy? (MCA/Oddball Productions, 1980). By this point the lineup had changed considerably: Terry Chimes (Discogs: 289850) handled most of the drumming, Paul Chimes played bass, and Mike Dunne contributed guitar and engineering — all uncredited on the original release. The Black Arabs provided uncredited backing vocals and percussion.

The Early-1980s Pivot: Jazz, Pop, Swing

The most striking aspect of Godard’s trajectory was his wholesale move away from punk into jazz-influenced pop in 1980–1981.

“Hey Now (I’m In Love)” (London Records, 1981) — credited to Vic Godard & The Subway Sect — was produced by Alex Sadkin (Discogs: 30415), the Compass Point Studios producer behind Grace Jones’s records. That Sadkin was brought in for a Subway Sect single indicates the scale of the stylistic ambition.

Songs For Sale (Island Records, 1981) — the full-length debut — is the culmination of this pivot. It is an Island Records release; Island’s involvement (and by extension Chris Blackwell’s orbit) underscores how far removed this record was from Rough Trade / Braik punk. Credits on the Discogs entry are sparse, but it stands as one of the more unusual documents of early-1980s UK pop.

T.R.O.U.B.L.E. and the Working Week Connection (1986)

The solo LP T.R.O.U.B.L.E. (Another Side, 1986, Belgium) is Godard’s most elaborately produced record. It brought together a large ensemble including jazz and Latin musicians, arrayed across two distinct sessions:

Simon Booth was the guitar-and-production mastermind behind Working Week, a mid-80s jazz-pop collective with connections to the Young Turks jazz scene. His production here is a direct Working Week connection — Roy Dodds (drums) also appears on Working Week records. Robin Millar was then at the height of his commercial profile from the Sade connection.

Postcard Era (1993–1999)

Godard’s association with Postcard Records in the early 1990s produced some of his most focused solo work. All three Postcard-linked releases were produced or co-produced by Edwyn Collins:

Won’t Turn Back (Postcard, 1993)

Single. Produced by Edwyn Collins. Sleeve and photography by Alan Horne (Discogs: 605765), Postcard’s founder.

The End of the Surrey People (Postcard, 1993)

Produced by Edwyn Collins. This was a substantial collaborative session:

  • Edwyn Collins — vocals, guitar, piano, organ, slide guitar; engineer; producer
  • Paul Cook (Discogs: 250492) — drums, vocals (Sex Pistols drummer)
  • Clare Kenny — bass, vocals
  • Martin Duffy (Discogs: 328018) — piano, organ (Primal Scream)
  • Paul ‘The Wizard’ Baker — bass, drums, piano, organ, vocals
  • Graham Knibbs — violin, viola
  • Tony Treble-O — percussion
  • Nick Robbins — engineer (Discogs: 261168, already in vault)
  • Sebastian Lewsley — engineer
  • Jock Loveband — engineer
  • Alan Horne — sleeve design

Paul Cook’s appearance is notable: the Sex Pistols drummer playing on a Vic Godard record produced by Edwyn Collins, with Postcard’s Alan Horne doing the sleeve.

Twenty Odd Years – The Story Of… (Motion Records, 1999)

Retrospective compilation (2CD). Godard contributed liner notes and guitar (on selected tracks). Alan Horne also contributed liner notes. Mike Coe (Discogs: 392160) mastered.

Creeping Bent (1999)

Somewhere in the World E.P. (Creeping Bent, Singles Club #10, Bent 048, 1999) — credited to The Secret Goldfish, with Godard contributing a co-write (“Place We Used To Live,” credited “V. Godard”) to the A-side. The EP was produced by Douglas MacIntyre and Gregor Reid (Discogs: 143379). This is Godard’s sole Creeping Bent credit and situates him within the label’s wider UK post-punk curatorial reach.

Later Career: Returns and New Work (2008–2024)

We Come As Aliens (Gnu Inc. Recordings, 2010)

Vic Godard & Subway Sect. Co-produced by Godard and Jon Clayton (Discogs: 352157), who also recorded, mixed, handled percussion and keyboards. Lineup:

  • Gary Ainge — drums
  • Paul Cook — drums
  • Kevin Younger — guitar, organ, piano
  • Mark Braby — bass, acoustic guitar
  • Simon Rivers — backing vocals

Paul Cook again appears here, confirming a recurring presence in Godard’s later-era Subway Sect recordings.

1979Now! (AED, 2014)

Vic Godard & Subway Sect. Co-produced by Edwyn Collins and Sebastian Lewsley, with Godard arranging all tracks (Paul Cook arranged one track). Same core late-era lineup (Braby, Rivers, Ainge). A second substantial Godard–Collins co-production.

Commercial Suicide Man (Tiny Global Productions, 2018)

A collaboration with The Nightingales. Mixed by Andreas Schmid; recorded by Eddy Hewitt and John A. Rivers (Discogs: 282318). Artwork by Jon Langford (2) (Discogs: 289297, The Mekons).

Mums’ Revenge (Gnu Inc. Recordings, 2019)

Solo album; Godard played guitar, harmonica, keyboards, organ, piano, and backing vocals. Key personnel:

  • Ruth Tidmarsh — bass
  • Dave Morgan — drums, recorded by, mixed by
  • Kevin Younger — lead guitar
  • Mark Braby — bass
  • Simon Rivers — slide guitar, backing vocals
  • Johnny Britton — backing vocals
  • Douglas MacIntyre — bass
  • Mike Coe — compilation producer, mastered by
  • Andrew Paul Shaw — executive producer, sleeve design

Everybody’s Scared Now (Gnu Inc. Recordings, 2024)

Most recent release on Discogs. Sole primary credit; no additional credits listed.

The Long Decline

Godard is also a member of The Long Decline (Discogs: 595601), a group that includes:

  • Mark Perry (Discogs: 124281) — ATV founder
  • Kevin Younger
  • Andy Gwatkin, James Kyllo, Kenny Wisdom, Lee McFadden, Luci Bocchino, Rob Ugly

Mark Perry’s presence connects Godard to the ATV / Alternative TV orbit of first-wave UK punk.
