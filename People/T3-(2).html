American rapper, MC, and occasional producer from Detroit. Sole surviving founding member of Slum Village. Has steered the group through three decades, multiple lineup changes, and the deaths of both co-founders J Dilla (2006) and Baatin (2009).

Biography

Detroit Origins and J-88 (early 1990s–1996)

T3 grew up in Detroit’s Conant Gardens neighbourhood alongside Titus Glover (Baatin) and James Yancey (J Dilla). The three formed a rap/production unit that first operated under the name J-88 (Discogs group ID: 73959), with Yancey serving as the primary beatmaker. The name Slum Village emerged around 1996 and became the group’s primary identity going forward.

Fan-tas-tic Era (1996–2001)

Slum Village’s first release was Fan-tas-tic Vol. 1 (1997, Donut Boy Recordings, Discogs ID 10308103), a self-pressed cassette produced entirely by Dilla under his Jay Dee alias. It circulated among Detroit tastemakers and hip-hop producers nationally — a widely passed demo-quality record that established the group’s aesthetic before any commercial infrastructure existed.

Fantastic, Vol. 2 was recorded around 1997–98 and initially distributed as a promo through A&M Records before a proper commercial release through Good Vibe Recordings and Wordplay Records in 2000. The album is produced almost entirely by Dilla, with a track each by D’Angelo and Pete Rock. T3 and Baatin serve as the two MCs throughout; the album’s loose, meditative quality defined what hip-hop press later called “Detroit rap” and “neo-soul adjacent” hip-hop. It is the foundational SV record.

During this period T3 also wrote on the Carhartt compilation Best Kept Secret (2000, Germany) and appeared on the Wordplay Climax single (2000, UK).

The Tainted EP and Elzhi’s Arrival (2001)

J Dilla departed Slum Village around 2001 to pursue a solo deal with MCA Records. In his absence, Elzhi (Jason Powers, Discogs ID 1746431) joined as the third MC. The Tainted EP (2001, Barak Records / JCOR Entertainment, Discogs release ID 302358) documents the new lineup. T3 is credited as writer on all six tracks. Production split: Karriem Riggins produced the A-side (tracks A1–A3) and Scott Storch produced the B-side (B1–B3). Young RJ (Ralph J. Rice Jr., Discogs ID 782178) appears in mixing and recording credits on the A-side — his first documented SV involvement.

Trinity and the Dirty District Mixtape (2002)

Trinity (Past, Present And Future) (2002, Capitol Records / Barak Records, Discogs release ID 257762) was the group’s major-label full-length. T3 is credited with A&R, assistant engineer, mixing, and production on the European pressing (release ID 594613) — indicating his role had expanded well beyond pure MC duties by this point.

Dirty District (2002, Sequence Records, Discogs release ID 257761) is where T3’s production role becomes most explicit in the database: he is credited as producer on the majority of tracks (1–3, 6–8A, 10–12, 13B) and as mixer alongside Young RJ. Black Milk produced tracks 8B and 9 — an early appearance. This documents T3 not just as a lead MC but as the hands-on executive and production force keeping SV operational after Dilla’s exit.

Detroit Deli and the Self-Titled Album (2004–2005)

Detroit Deli (A Taste Of Detroit) (2004, Capitol Records, Discogs ID 577437) brought in B.R. Gunna (Discogs ID 350857) as primary producer. T3 is written-by credit throughout. Black Milk contributed additional vocals on tracks 1, 10, and 13 — an early major-label appearance from the Detroit underground peer who would later produce the self-titled.

The self-titled Slum Village (2005, Barak Records, Discogs ID 557551) was produced collaboratively: Young RJ produced tracks 1, 5, 8, and 10; B.R. Gunna produced 3, 4, 7, and 12; Black Milk produced 2, 9, and 11. Selfish (2004 single, Barak / Capitol, release ID 234686) appeared as a standalone release from this period with T3 on written-by credit.

Carrying On After Dilla and Baatin (2006–2009)

J Dilla died February 10, 2006, three days after his 32nd birthday and the release of Donuts. Baatin had left the group in the early 2000s due to mental health complications (he was later diagnosed with schizophrenia). T3 maintained Slum Village as a going concern through both events.

Fan-Tas-Tic Vol. 1 received an official US release in 2006 (Barak Records / Donut Boy Recordings, Discogs ID 986001) — T3’s written-by credits properly documented for the first time on an official pressing.

Baatin died July July 2009.

Villa Manifesto and the T3 / Young RJ Duo (2010–present)

Following Baatin’s death T3 restructured Slum Village as a duo with Young RJ. Villa Manifesto (2010, E1 Entertainment / Ne’Astra Music Group, Discogs release ID 2391166) documents this configuration: T3 is co-executive producer (alongside R.J. Rice Sr., Young RJ’s father), Young RJ handles mixing and recording. The archival J88 (2010, Barak Records / Donut Boy Records, Discogs ID 4791488) compiled J-88 era material.

Evolution (2013, Ne’Astra Music Group, Discogs ID 4800799) shows T3 producing track A4 himself and writing throughout. Young RJ produces the bulk of the album; collaborators include Illa J (J Dilla’s younger brother John Yancey, written-by credits across multiple tracks), Blu (2), Havoc (3), J. Ivy, and Earlly Mac.

Yes! (2015, Ne’Astra Music Group / Yancey Media Group, Discogs ID 7120130) continued the duo format. Slum Village Vol. 0 (2017, Barak / Ne’Astra, Discogs ID 10204074) and The Lost Scrolls Vol. 2 (Slum Village Edition) (2018, Ne’Astra) followed. Fantastic 2020 V.1 (2020, Barak / Ne’Astra) returned to the Fantastic Vol. 2 catalog. F.U.N (2024, self-released, Discogs ID 30665395) is the most recent release.
