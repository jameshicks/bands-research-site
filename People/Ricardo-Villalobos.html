Ricardo Villalobos

Chilean-born, Berlin-based minimal techno and microhouse DJ and producer. Discogs artist ID: 3057. Real name: Ricardo Villalobos. Born 6 August 1970, Santiago de Chile.

Biography

Origins and Relocation

Ricardo Villalobos was born on 6 August 1970 in Santiago de Chile, Chile. In 1973 — during a period of intense political upheaval following the coup — his family relocated to Darmstadt, Germany, where he grew up. He later moved to Berlin, where he has been based throughout his career as a producer and DJ.

Early Period (1993–1999)

Villalobos’s first documented release appeared in 1993 under the alias Minta Spacew — Sinus Poetry E.P. on Placid Flavour (PF01), an acid-leaning four-track 12” on which he is credited as Composed By and Arranged By. His first primary release under his own name was The Contempt (1995, Ladomat 2000), followed by N-DRA (1996, Hörspielmusik).

From 1998 he began releasing on Frisbee Tracks (Salvador, Pino Jet Explosion, Que Bel Epoque) and on Lo-Fi Stereo (Heike, 808 The Bassqueen). The Frisbee Tracks releases have a distinctly playful, micro-funk character. His debut on Perlon came in 1999 with Frank Mueller Melodram, and he also contributed to the Perlon compilation Sommerpause (1999) alongside the label’s core roster.

Perlon and Playhouse Era (2000–2005)

From 2000 onward Villalobos became central to two labels that defined the German minimal/microhouse sound of the early 2000s: Perlon and Playhouse. Playhouse (sublabel of Ongaku Musik) was the home of his most celebrated work of this period.

On Perlon he released Froebel 1792 (2000), Tomorrov Cocktail / Ananas (2000), Bredow / Damm3 (2001), then later Thé Au Harem D’Archimède (2004), Chromosul (2005), and What’s Wrong My Friends? (2006). The Perlon releases were consistently mastered by Rashad Becker at Dubplates & Mastering, and artwork handled by design studio Double Standards (alias: Standard Rad, Berlin; Discogs ID: 501407).

On Playhouse: Ibiza99 (2000), Luna (2000), Halma (2002), then the two landmark albums Alcachofa (2003) and its companion Alcachofa Tools (2003), followed by Alcachofa Remixes (2004), Fizheuer Zieheuer (2006), and What’s Wrong My Friends?. The Alcachofa double LP is widely considered his defining work: cover art by Saskia Randt (credited as “Saskia”), mastered by Rashad Becker.

He also released on Cadenza (Achso, 2005, Switzerland), and compiled and mixed Cocoon Recordings’ In The Mix: Taka Taka (2003) and In The Mix - Green & Blue (2005).

International Breakthrough and Mix Compilations (2006–2009)

Fizheuer Zieheuer (2006, Playhouse) — a 39-minute single-track 12” — became one of the most iconic extended dance-floor records in minimal techno history, mastered by Rashad Becker. That same year saw What’s Wrong My Friends? on Perlon.

Fabric 36 (2007, Fabric (2)) was his contribution to the long-running UK club mix series, cementing his international standing. He also remixed Depeche Mode (The Sinner In Me, 2006), Beck (Cellphone’s Dead, 2007), and Blood on the Dance Floor for Shackleton’s Skull Disco (Blood On My Hands, 2007).

The Vasco series on Perlon ran across 2008: Vasco EP Part 1, Vasco EP Part 2, and the combined Vasco LP.

Re: ECM and Expanded Collaborations (2010–2015)

Re: ECM (2011, ECM Records) was a landmark collaborative album with Max Loderbauer, on which both are credited as Electronics. The record reworks material from the ECM back-catalogue using electronics. Manfred Eicher handled both the original recordings production and mastering; Rashad Becker did pre-mastering; Christoph Stickel did mastering. Design by Sascha Kleis.

Also in 2011: Meet Tshetsha Boys (Honest Jon’s Records) — a three-way collaboration with Max Loderbauer and Peverelist — and RiRom on Raum…musik, the latter a project with others.

From 2012, the Dependent And Happy series on Perlon expanded his Perlon catalog across four numbered EPs (-One through -Four) plus a main LP (2012), all with Double Standards artwork. Any Ideas (2012, Perlon) and Baby EP (2012, raum…musik) followed.

Zug (2012, m=minimal) was a collaborative release with Max Loderbauer, on which Villalobos is credited with composition, mastering of originals, and remixing.

In 2015 he collaborated with Turkish ensemble Insanlar on Kime Ne (Honest Jon’s Records), a meeting of minimal electronics and Turkish traditional music featuring baglama, voice and synth contributions from Cem Yıldız and Baris K.

Also in 2015: Safe In Harbour on Perlon, Rawax Aira Series Vol 1 on Rawax.

Hubris / Avant-Garde Crossover and Later Work (2016–present)

Villalobos has consistently reached beyond the dance floor. In 2016 he contributed electronics and recorded engineering to Oren Ambarchi’s Hubris (Editions Mego) alongside Jim O’Rourke, Mark Fell, Crys Cole, Stephen O’Malley, and others. He also released Hubris Variation (Ricardo Villalobos Mix) (2016, Black Truffle).

Microgestures (2022, Arjunamusic) was a co-release with Samuel Rohrer (Rohrer on drums, percussion, modular synths, keyboards; Villalobos on modular synths, drum machine, keyboards), with design by The Designers Republic and mastering by Michelle Grinser.

The Changes EP trilogy on The Other Side (6) ran 2019–2021 (Parts 1, 2, 3). The Rawax label became a main vehicle from 2015 onward: Rawax Aira Series Vol 1 (2015), AsloHop EP (2020), Neunachi EP (2020), the Salvador trilogy (Parts I–III, 2025), and RAWAX Collectors Box (2025).

De Cada Uno EP (2022, Hearing Colours) and RTC002 (2022, Respect The Craft) round out recent work. His remix of Ela Minus & DJ Python’s ♡ appeared in 2023 on Smugglers Way.

Key Collaborations

Re: ECM (with Max Loderbauer)

Re: ECM (2011, ECM Records) — a reworking of ECM back-catalogue recordings using electronics. Both Villalobos and Max Loderbauer are credited as Electronics. Originally produced by Manfred Eicher; pre-mastering by Rashad Becker; mastering by Christoph Stickel and Eicher. Widely noted as a significant crossover between the ECM classical/jazz world and Berlin techno electronics.

The LoVil and Vilod projects (Discogs IDs 3981176 and 3479587) are further Villalobos–Loderbauer collaborative vehicles.

Meet Tshetsha Boys (with Max Loderbauer and Peverelist)

Three-way collaboration (2011, Honest Jon’s Records) between Villalobos, Loderbauer, and Bristol-based Peverelist.

Kime Ne (with Insanlar)

Kime Ne (2015, Honest Jon’s Records) pairs Villalobos with Turkish collective Insanlar, incorporating baglama, voice, synth (Cem Yıldız), and synth/sampler/production (Baris K). Design by Will Bankhead.

Hubris (with Oren Ambarchi)

Contributing electronics and engineering to Oren Ambarchi’s Hubris (2016, Editions Mego), alongside Jim O’Rourke, Mark Fell, Crys Cole, Stephen O’Malley, and others. He also released Hubris Variation Parts 2 & 3 (2020) on Oren Ambarchi Archive and the standalone remix Hubris Variation (Ricardo Villalobos Mix) (2016, Black Truffle).

Microgestures (with Samuel Rohrer)

Microgestures (2022, Arjunamusic) — co-production with percussionist Samuel Rohrer, both on modular synths and percussion. Design by The Designers Republic; mastered by Michelle Grinser.

Scene Context

Villalobos is a central figure in the Berlin/Frankfurt minimal techno and microhouse scene of the late 1990s and 2000s. Perlon (founded by Zip/Markus Nikolai, with close ties to Tiff Lacey and others) and Playhouse (Ongaku Musik) were the two key labels of this scene, alongside Kompakt (Cologne). The characteristic sound — lengthy, sparse tracks built around microscopic rhythmic variations, low-end bass lines, and deliberate slow-burn development — became highly influential across European club culture in the early 2000s.

His extended 2003–2006 run (Alcachofa through Fizheuer Zieheuer) is the period most often cited as his artistic peak, particularly Fizheuer Zieheuer’s 39-minute duration as a provocation against conventional dance-floor attention spans.

He has also moved fluently across more experimental territory — the Re: ECM project places him in dialogue with the ECM aesthetic (jazz, new music, silence as compositional element), and his Oren Ambarchi collaboration connects him to the improvised/experimental electronics community centered on labels like Editions Mego and Black Truffle.

His Chilean origins and sustained South American engagement appear in releases on Chilean label Plaza Independencia Música (as mixer on Libro, 2013), Barraca Music (Spain), and the recurring Salvador title (named for Salvador Allende’s Chile, though this is not confirmed in the DB — flagged as a gap).
