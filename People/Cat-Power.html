Cat Power

Solo artist. Real name Chan Marshall. Born 1972, Atlanta, Georgia. Discogs ID: 103195.

The performing name of Chan Marshall since her early-1990s Atlanta band. Signed to Matador Records for debut Dear Sir (1995) and Myra Lee (1996); first proper LP What Would The Community Think (OLE 202-2, 1996) established the fragile, stark aesthetic — solo voice and minimal guitar. Moon Pix (1998) remains her most celebrated early record. The Greatest (OLE 626-2, 2006) marked a turn toward Memphis soul — recorded with the Hi Rhythm Section. Jukebox (2008) was a covers album. 238 total releases on Matador.

Live Performance

Cat Power’s live reputation in the late 1990s and early 2000s was defined by erratic, often incomplete shows — a mythology widely circulated in music press and among fans. The pattern was consistent enough that the New Yorker’s Hilton Als wrote in a 2003 profile (“Wayward Girl,” 18 August 2003): “It is foolhardy to describe a Cat Power event as a concert,” citing “rambling confessions” and Marshall talking to “a friend’s baby from the stage.” Songs would begin and end abruptly, sets would be cut short without explanation, and performances frequently dissolved into extended, incoherent monologues from the stage. Wikipedia: Cat Power, as of 2026-07-12

One of her earliest documented performances illustrates how the unconventional approach predated any breakdown: opening for Man or Astro-man? in 1993, she played a two-string guitar and sang the word “no” for fifteen minutes. Wikipedia: Cat Power, as of 2026-07-12

The most granularly documented incident from the erratic-show era occurred at the Satellite Ballroom, Charlottesville, Virginia, 15 October 2005. Marshall asked the audience to sit, framing the evening as an intimate performance. Midway through the third song she became frustrated with the PA sound, struck the piano keys, and stopped. What followed lasted approximately two hours: incoherent rants directed at herself and the audience, complaints that the show was “only half of last night’s show,” and a declaration that she felt “lots of judgement coming from the crowd.” When she finally reached “I Don’t Blame You” she abandoned it before the first chorus, made cryptic references (including a mention of the KKK), said she felt “a weird energy,” and ran offstage. The audience departed without applause. Austin Kleon blog, eyewitness account, 16 October 2005

A 2012 performance at Grand Central, Miami (11 October 2012) shows the pattern persisting into her later career. She arrived carrying a lit candle, stage-left and visibly nervous, with a technical failure in the backing tracks escalating her agitation. At one point she addressed the audience directly: “This is the part where I collapse on the floor. This is the part where I collapse onstage, and I’m crazy, and it’s a train wreck.” Miami Rail, “Cat Power and the Tortured Artist Mystique”

Following the release of The Greatest, Marshall cancelled a spring 2006 tour entirely and was admitted to the psychiatric ward at Mount Sinai Medical Center, Miami Beach, leaving after a week. She has described the period immediately preceding as an extended, suicidal isolation: “I was just down in the hole, in the shit…my phone was on silent. Never even talked to my friends.” Magnet Magazine, “Life Is Beautiful,” February 2007

Retrospective: Marshall’s own account

Marshall has given varying accounts of what drove the chaotic shows, sometimes in tension with each other. To Drowned in Sound (c. 2006–07) she stated: “Even playing all my shows I was always intoxicated, always kind of not there, which led to the depression. It was more about the uncomfortableness with just being in my own skin, and that’s why the alcohol was always with me.” Drowned in Sound

To Harp magazine (December 2006, “Beauty Secrets,” by Jaan Uhelszki) she said simply: “I didn’t know I was messed up.” To the same interviewer she attributed the drinking not to alcoholism but to depression and nerves. She repeated this clarification to the Irish Times in 2018: “I was never an alcoholic. When I got really sick from drinking so much, I thought I was. But after doing years of therapy and understanding things about myself, I realised I was suffering from extreme depression.” Irish Times, 25 September 2018

By late 2006 she had assembled a new backing band (the Dirty Delta Blues band) and declared herself sober. Her performances shifted markedly: reviews from the The Greatest touring era describe her as “delivering onstage” and polished. Wikipedia: Cat Power, as of 2026-07-12
