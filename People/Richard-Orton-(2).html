Richard Orton (2)

Born: 1 January 1940, Derby, England
Died: 12 February 2013, Nottingham, England
Discogs ID: 580364
Name variations: R.O.

British electronic music composer, computer scientist, researcher, and educator. Founding director of the University Of York Electronic Music Studios (1968), the first academic electronic music facility in North England. Professor in the Department of Music at the University of York from 1967 to 1996. Founder of the live electronics ensemble Gentle Fire (1968–1975). Founding editor of Organised Sound: An International Journal of Music and Technology. Established the Composers Desktop Project (CDP) in 1986 and operated the private imprint Unique Music in the 2000s.

Biography

Early Life and Education

Richard Orton was born in Derby on 1 January 1940. He learned piano at the Birmingham School of Music, then won a choral scholarship to St John’s College, Cambridge, where he studied as a tenor vocalist and graduated with Bachelor’s degrees in Arts and Music.

University of York and Early Electronic Work (1967–1968)

In 1967 Orton joined the University of York’s Department of Music, where he remained until retirement in 1996. That same year he began improvising with his friend Hugh Davies, playing shortwave radios, an “amplified fireguard,” and toy piano. With Davies constructing DIY instruments from household items fitted with contact microphones, they toured Britain as one of the country’s first “live electronic music” duos, giving ten concerts.

In November 1968 Orton established the University of York Electronic Music Studios (EMS) — the first academic facility of its kind in North England — and invited Davies to join his newly-formed ensemble Gentle Fire the same month.

Gentle Fire (1968–1975)

Orton founded Gentle Fire as a live electronics and new music performance group based at the University of York. Members included Hugh Davies, Graham Hearn, Michael Robinson (2), Richard Bernas, and Stuart Jones. The group’s repertoire centred on works by John Cage, Earle Brown, Christian Wolff, and Karlheinz Stockhausen, alongside original group compositions. They were active 1968–1975 and documented on the EMI Electrola LP 4 Systems, Music For Amplified Toy Pianos, Music For Carillon, Edges (1974) and the retrospective Paradigm Discs box set Explorations (1970–1973) (2020).

The group’s live ICES 72 performance at the Roundhouse, London (14 August 1972) — playing their own Group Composition IV — is preserved on the Paradigm Discs retrospective.

Compositions and Performers

Orton was prolific as a composer throughout his career. Notable works include:

  • Icarus — for violinist Christopher Rowland of the Fitzwilliam String Quartet; Orton performed sound diffusion
  • Ambience — for trombonist James Fulkerson; Orton performed sound diffusion
  • Scatter — for James Fulkerson and Stephen Montague; Orton also played piano
  • Ennead — premiered at the Cheltenham Festival of Contemporary Music
  • Mythos (solo alto saxophone) — written for Jan Steele
  • Stellations — for the European Community Chamber Orchestra
  • Guitar Concerto “Lattices” — for Arthur Nestrovski (guitar) with the Anemone Festival Orchestra, conducted by John Godfrey, with Cathy Fells (obbligato solo cello); recorded 2002

His 1970 work Cycle, For 2 Or 4 Players — performed by Orton (piano, percussion) and Moray Welsh (cello) — appeared on the Mainstream Records compilation New Music From London (1970) alongside works by Peter Maxwell Davies, Harrison Birtwistle, and David Bedford.

York Pop Music Project and Trevor Wishart (1973)

In 1973 Orton served as producer on York Pop Music Project (York Electronic Studios, YES 1), a record by York student ensemble All Day (3) featuring a range of York University musicians including Jan Steele, Steve Beresford, Roger Marsh, and the Fitzwilliam String Quartet. He also appeared as a featured performer on Trevor Wishart’s large-scale electroacoustic piece Journey Into Space (1973, reissued by Paradigm Discs in 2002), which involved over forty York performers.

Technology Development and the Composers Desktop Project (1983–2013)

Orton developed the EMS at York from a classic tape studio into a facility with voltage-controlled synthesizers and digital systems. In the early 1980s, with his colleague Ross Kirk (Department of Electronics), he developed a “Music Technology” curriculum that became the template for other British universities.

He wrote his first software on a Sharp MZ-80A in March 1983. In 1986 he established the Composers Desktop Project (CDP), choosing the Atari ST/MIDI as the group’s platform — a collaborative software project for computer music composition. In the early 1990s he worked with Ross Kirk on the Musical Instrument Digital Array Signal Processor (MIDAS) project. He developed visual sound-representation programs for CDP Atari Systems and in 1992 presented Tabula Vigilans, an original algorithmic composition programming language for real-time performance.

Upon retirement in 1996, the University of York awarded him a lifetime honorary Emeritus Readership. He continued working on CDP software in later years, authoring ScoreBuilder (for algorithmically generated scores printed via Sibelius) and ProcessPack in collaboration with Tom Endrich.

Unique Music Imprint (2002–2003)

In the early 2000s Orton operated his private imprint Unique Music, on which he released two CDr albums of his own work:

  • Electronic And Computer Music (2002, CD IRUM 1003) — seven electronic works including Kiss, Clock Farm, Concert Music 5 (featuring Gentle Fire with tape-loop contributions from Hugh Davies, Graham Hearn, Michael Robinson, Richard Bernas, and Stuart Jones), For The Time Being, Astrorum Conscius, Timeshadows, and Ceci N’est Pas…
  • Instrumental Works (2002, CD IRUM 1002) — four works: Guitar Concerto “Lattices”, Icarus, Ambience, and Scatter
  • Timescape (2003, IRUM CD 1008) — a twelve-movement work with ensemble Estampie (sound diffusion by Orton); styles include musique concrète and contemporary composition

Shozyg I And II (1969) — Archival Release (2013)

In 2013, the year of Orton’s death, an archival recording of his and Hugh Davies’s earliest Shozyg experiments was released on Another Timbre as Shozyg I And II (1969) (AZ #21, release ID: 7655478). The recording — a single 10:22 track — captures both artists playing the Shozyg instruments Davies had constructed, made in 1969. It was issued as an MP3 download under Davies’s primary credit, with Orton credited as Electronics [Shozyg].

Later Years and Legacy

Orton remained active in academic musical life, regularly presenting at Audio Engineering Society conventions and the International Computer Music Conference, and contributing teaching materials for the Open University. He was a founding editor of Organised Sound (Cambridge University Press), the first academic journal dedicated to electroacoustic music and music technology.

He died in Nottingham on 12 February 2013. His work appears posthumously on the Persistence Of Sound (2) compilation Hemlock Stone (2023, PS010), which repackages several of his electronic works.
