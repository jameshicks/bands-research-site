Mick Glossop

English record producer and engineer. Real name: Michael Glossop. Discogs artist ID: 83382. Website: mickglossop.com

Overview

Mick Glossop is one of the most prolific British record producers and engineers of the post-punk, new wave, and alternative rock era. Born Michael Glossop, he began his career as an engineer at Wessex Sound Studios in the early 1970s, working as a tape operator and assistant on sessions for Virgin Records acts before rising to chief engineer. He was one of the original designers and chief engineers of both Manor Studios and The Town House — two of the most significant British recording facilities of the 1970s–80s (Wikipedia).

He first came to prominence producing and engineering the foundational post-punk records of the late 1970s: Wire’s trilogy on Harvest Records (Pink Flag, Chairs Missing, 154), The Ruts (The Crack, Danger Signs, Staring at the Rude Boys), Skids (The Absolute Game, Circus Games), Magazine (Fanfare, singles), Public Image Limited, and Penetration (2). This body of work established him as a key sonic architect of British new wave.

Through the 1980s he diversified into hard rock (UFO, Gillan, Waysted), Celtic-influenced rock (The Alarm, The Waterboys), and indie alt-rock (Furniture, The Wonder Stuff, Bronski Beat, The Bolshoi, The Southern Death Cult). He also worked with The Passions and produced records for Frank Zappa (re-mix engineering, live recordings) and John Lee Hooker.

His most sustained relationship is with Van Morrison, whom he began working with on Wavelength (1978) and with whom he recorded and/or mixed 21 albums across 35 years — including Into The Music (1979), Avalon Sunset (1989), Enlightenment (1990), Hymns To The Silence (1991), and Too Long In Exile (1993).

In 2009, Glossop was awarded a Visiting Professorship at Leeds College of Music. In 2010, he received Music Producers Guild (UK) awards for Recording Engineer of the Year and Live Album of the Year (Wikipedia). He also served as a director of the MPG.

Studio / Technical Background

Glossop was one of the original designers and chief engineers of Manor Studios (Richard Branson’s residential studio in Oxfordshire) and The Town House (Virgin’s flagship London studio). He worked extensively through the Manor Mobile unit for live and location recordings. The Town House closed in 2008. His early career included sessions at Wessex Sound Studios, where he is credited as assistant engineer on Wishbone Ash, Tangerine Dream (Ricochet mix), and Van Morrison’s A Period of Transition.

Zappa connections: Glossop re-mixed elements of Joe’s Garage (1979) and appears as recording engineer on multiple You Can’t Do That On Stage Anymore volumes and the 2023 Zappa ‘80 Mudd Club / Munich release.
