Overview

Jamie Gingell is a British musician active since 1990, best known as a member of the Bristol shoegaze group Secret Shine, which released on the celebrated Sarah Records between 1991 and 1994. Working across roles as vocalist, writer, producer, and performer, Gingell has accumulated 64 credits and has also been part of Collide (2) and Dreamscape (3). The core Secret Shine partnership with Scott Purnell (27 shared releases) and Dean Purnell (19) has been the sustained axis of Gingell’s career, with the band returning to activity on Clairecords in the mid-2000s and Saint Marie Records in the 2010s after a long gap following the Sarah Records era.

Notes (web)

Secret Shine: the gap years and return (1994–2005)

Secret Shine never formally disbanded after the Sarah Records era ended in 1994. According to a 2010 interview with Scott Purnell (“When The Sun Hits” blog, whenthesunhitsblog.blogspot.com), Gingell and the Purnell brothers paused Secret Shine activity partly because Scott Purnell, Dean Purnell, and drummer Tim Morris had “got into the whole Brit Pop thing (like millions of other bands at that time!) and just fancied doing something different.” The group formed a three-piece called Junkie, which played locally for a couple of years but did not achieve wider recognition. After Junkie wound down, the members assisted other bands without reviving Secret Shine. Their last confirmed live performance as Secret Shine before the hiatus was in Oxford in early 1996; they never officially announced a split. Wikipedia, Secret Shine article, as of 2026-11-27.

The 2003–2004 catalyst. In 2003 Clairecords (US, catalog fern 030) issued After Years, a CD compilation of the band’s Sarah Records output (minus the first single, but including the Untouched LP). A further edition appeared in 2004 distributed by Tonevendor. This retrospective renewed interest in the band and acted as the catalyst for the surviving members getting back together to rehearse. Wikipedia; search snippet from discogs.com release page 552683/534611.

Tim Morris’s death and the Morris tribute EP (2005). During the initial 2004 reunion rehearsals Scott Purnell temporarily stepped away from the band. The circumstances that brought the remaining members back permanently were devastating: Jamie Gingell and Dean Purnell arrived at Tim Morris’s factory unit one evening to find that Tim had suffered a fatal accident while operating one of his machines. Tim Morris (1974–2005) died as a result. The surviving members — including Gingell and Kathryn Smith — reformed Secret Shine specifically to record an eight-track acoustic tribute album, Morris (2005), featuring reworked Secret Shine songs alongside tracks from the Junkie project. All proceeds were donated to Médecins Sans Frontières (Doctors Without Borders), one of Tim’s favoured charities. Following the tribute recording and an accompanying performance, the band simply never stopped working together again. Scott Purnell interview, whenthesunhitsblog.blogspot.com, 2010; blog overview of Morris EP, loupgaruda.blogspot.com, 2011.

Summary of the gap. The band did not formally break up but drifted apart into Brit-pop adjacent work (the Junkie project) from roughly 1996. A US retrospective on Clairecords in 2003–2004 brought them back to rehearsal; the accidental death of their drummer Tim Morris in 2005 cemented the reformation as a tribute-driven act that then continued recording and releasing.
