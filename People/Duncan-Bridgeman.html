Duncan Bridgeman

Discogs ID: 69113

British musician, composer, and producer. Born in London. Double Grammy-nominated filmmaker and musician. Multi-instrumentalist (keyboards, synthesizer, bass, guitar, flute, percussion, drum programming) and prolific studio producer. Core member of I-Level and co-founder of 1 Giant Leap.

Overview

Bridgeman’s career spans from early-1980s UK post-punk and electro-pop production through major-label pop production in the late 1980s–early 1990s, into world-music multimedia projects. In the 1980s he was a founding member of I-Level (Virgin Records) and an in-house style producer for Spanish Ariola acts. His most internationally prominent work is the 1 Giant Leap project (2001–2008), a world-music collaborative documentary film/album conceived and directed with Jamie Catto, released on Palm Pictures and Grammy-nominated. He later composed film soundtracks (Hecho En México, 2012) and continued session and production work into the 2020s.

Name variations on Discogs: Bridgeman, D Bridgeman, D Bridgman, D. Bridgeman, Duncan, Duncan Bridgemann, Duncan Bridgman.

Key Projects

I-Level (1981–1992)

UK electro-soul trio with Joe Dworniak and Sam Jones (2), signed to Virgin. Their self-titled debut album (1983) was co-written, engineered, and performed by Bridgeman. Singles included “Give Me” (1982), “Minefield”, “Teacher”, “In The River”, and “Our Song”. Bridgeman is credited as performer, engineer, writer, and music composer across the catalogue.

1 Giant Leap (2001–2009)

Co-created with Jamie Catto. A world-music collaborative documentary project where Bridgeman and Catto travelled the world recording and filming musicians. The album (Palm Pictures, 2001) was Grammy-nominated. Bridgeman played guitar, bass, flute, and keyboards and handled production, recording, and mixing throughout. The follow-up film/album “What About Me?” (Channel 4 / One World Music, 2008–2009) continued the same approach. The project was also credited under aliases Odeon (3) and Worlds Collide.

Transvision Vamp (1988–1991)

Major production relationship: produced “Revolution Baby” (1987), “Pop Art” LP (1988), “Velveteen” LP (1989), and “Little Magnets Versus The Bubble Of Babble” LP (1991), all on MCA/UNI Records. Also remixed Duran Duran’s “Big Thing” (1988) and reprogrammed Salt-N-Pepa’s “Push It” (1988) in the same period.

Dido (1999–2002)

Co-produced and recorded tracks on Dido’s debut album “No Angel” (Arista / Cheeky Records, 1999), and produced the Brazilian pressing of “My Lover’s Gone” (2002).

Spanish Ariola work (1984–1987)

Extensive production, arrangement, and keyboard work for Spanish-language artists on Ariola and Plataforma Discos throughout the mid-1980s, including “De Un País En Llamas” (1985), multiple releases by the same acts, and “Carmina Burana” (1987).

Cinerama (1998–2002)

Played flute on three Cinerama (David Gedge) albums: “Va Va Voom” (1998), “This Is Cinerama” (2000), and “Torino” (2002).

Hecho En México (2012)

Solo film soundtrack, Mexico, released on Universal Music Group — his only Discogs primary artist credit outside of group work.
