Alan Licht

Guitarist, writer, and critic. Born in New Jersey. Based in New York. Discogs artist ID: 75589. A central figure in the NYC downtown experimental scene from the early 1990s onward, spanning art-damaged indie rock (Love Child, Run On), free improvisation (Blue Humans, Text Of Light), and long-form drone/ambient work. Also a prolific music critic and author.

Biography

Early Work — Love Child (1988–1992)

Licht’s first recorded project was Love Child (3) (Discogs ID: 197757), a New York noise-rock group with Rebecca Odes (bass, vocals), Will Baum (guitar), and a rotating cast. A cassette appeared in 1988 on Not On Label. Their Homestead Records debut Okay? (1991) was produced by the band with Ben Daughtrey. Love Child continued onto City Slang with the EP Stumbling Block (1992) and LP Witchcraft (1992) — the two German City Slang records positioned them alongside early-’90s American underground acts being licensed to European markets.

Simultaneously, Licht appeared as a sideman on Rudolph Grey’s Mask Of Light (Ecstatic Peace! / New Alliance, 1991), contributing guitar on side B alongside Grey, drummer Rashied Ali, and saxophonist Jim Sauter. Thurston Moore, Tom Surgal, and Don Fleming produced the B-side sessions.

Blue Humans (1992–1993)

Licht played right-channel guitar on two Blue Humans releases led by Rudolph Grey on New Alliance Records:

ReleaseYearLabelCo-credits
Clear To Higher Time1992New Alliance RecordsRudolph Grey (guitar), Tom Surgal (drums), Thurston Moore (guitar)

The dual-guitar setup (left/right channel split) was a Grey signature; Licht occupied the right channel position across both records.

Solo Debut — Siltbreeze (1994)

Licht’s first solo record, Sink The Aging Process (1994), appeared on Siltbreeze — the Philadelphia label associated with Philadelphia noise rock and early underground outsider releases. He played all instruments and handled the design himself; Fred Brockman recorded it.

Run On — Matador (1994–1997)

Run On (Discogs ID: 135317) was Licht’s principal band through the mid-1990s, on Matador Records. Members: Alan Licht (guitar), Sue Garner (guitar, bass, vocals), Rick Brown (drums, synthesizer, piano, vocals), David Newgarden (organ), Katie Gentile. Gene Holder produced their Matador records; the lineup had clear ties to the NYC post-no-wave downtown scene (Brown and Garner were veterans of Fish & Roses / ROIR-era scene bands).

Solo Recordings — Pure, Drag City (1997–1998)

Two significant collaborative solo records appeared in 1997–98:

Our Lips Are Sealed (1997, Pure) — a duo with saxophonist Tamio Shiraishi. Licht played guitar with effects chain (Twin Reverb, Digital Delay, Turbo Rat, Tube Screamer); Shiraishi played alto saxophone.

Hoffman Estates (1998, Drag City) — co-credited to Loren MazzaCane Connors and Alan Licht, this was a large Chicago-scene ensemble session produced by Jim O’Rourke. The full personnel:

Mandarin Movie (2005)

Mandarin Movie (Discogs ID: 318860) was a Chicago/New York crossover ensemble. Their self-titled 2005 album (Aesthetics label) featured Licht alongside Jason Ajemian (double bass, electronics), Rob Mazurek (computer, cornet, Moog, Pianette), Matthew Lux (bass, electronics), Frank Rosaly (drums), John Herndon (drums), Rick Rizzo (guitar), Steve Swell (trombone), and Jim Becker (banjo, fiddle). The record bridged Chicago improvised music and downtown New York avant-garde.

Text Of Light (2004–2006)

Text Of Light (Discogs ID: 288448) was a film-improvisation project that played live to experimental and avant-garde films, primarily associated with early American avant-garde cinema. Core members: Licht (guitar), Lee Ranaldo (guitar), Christian Marclay (turntables), Tim Barnes (drums), Ulrich Krieger (saxophone), William Hooker (drums), and Gregor Asch. Their releases include:

Everydays — with Aki Onda (2008)

Everydays (2008, Family Vineyard) — duo with Aki Onda (cassettes, electronics). Mixed by Jamie Saft (tracks 1–2) and Raz Mesinai (tracks 3–5). Artwork by Takehito Koganezawa.

Lee Ranaldo Projects (2012–2015)

Solo Work — Room40, Family Vineyard, Blank Forms (2021–2024)

Later solo releases span ambient and guitar-based work:

Three Chords And A Sword is a covers album spanning 1988–2020, including interpretations of Pere Ubu (“Heart of Darkness”), Van Halen (“Jump”), Palace (“Stable Will”), Suicide (“Rocket USA”), Sonic Youth (“Tom Violence”), Fred Neil (“Everybody’s Talkin’”), and Bob Dylan (“Last Thoughts On Woody Guthrie”).

At The Top Of The Stairs (2023, Family Vineyard) was recorded by Jonas Blank, mixed by Doug Graham, and mastered by Jim O’Rourke.

May 99 (2023, Blank Forms Editions) was a trio with Charles Curtis (cello) and Dean Roberts (electronics); mastered by Rashad Becker.

The Blue Hour (2024, Otoroku) was recorded by Billy Steiger and mixed by Oli Barrett.

Notes (web)

David Licht — family connection?

A search for a family connection between Alan Licht and David Licht (Bongwater/Klezmatics drummer) found an obituary for a different David Frederick Licht (1963–2023, Pigeon, Michigan; pest control worker) whose brother is named Alan Licht — clearly not the same Alan Licht. (ransfordfuneralhome.com)

Licht mentions in a Paris Transatlantic interview that he has a brother who liked Dixieland jazz, but does not name him. (paristransatlantic.com) No source links him to the Bongwater drummer David Licht as a relative. The shared surname appears to be coincidence; treat as unresolved.
