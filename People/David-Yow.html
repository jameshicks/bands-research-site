Overview

David Yow (born August 2, 1960, Las Vegas, Nevada) is an American musician, actor, and visual artist whose career in noise rock and punk stretches back to the mid-1980s. He co-founded Scratch Acid in Dallas before moving to Chicago to form The Jesus Lizard alongside guitarist Duane Denison and bassist David Sims — a core partnership reflected in upwards of 150 shared credits — with the band recording primarily for Touch And Go Records from 1987 onward. Beyond The Jesus Lizard he has contributed vocals to Pigface, Flipper, Qui, and Dumb Numbers, and Steve Albini figures as a recurring production presence with 69 shared credits. Light in the Attic’s sustained reissue programme from 2013 through 2024 (11 releases) has kept both the Scratch Acid and Jesus Lizard catalogues in circulation.

Notes (web)

The Jesus Lizard: 1999 dissolution and 2008 reunion

1999 dissolution

The breakup had two overlapping causes. First, drummer Mac McNeilly left the band in late 1996 — his stated reason was family: “I had two very young kids at home and I was finding we were gone so much of the time… I felt like I had to make a choice between family or the band, and I chose family.” (Exclaim!, 2009) The remaining members later acknowledged the departure was effectively the beginning of the end; as the Jim DeRogatis / jimdero.com piece (2009) puts it, “the other guys now say it should have ended then, though the group limped on until splitting after one last show in Sweden in March 1999.” (jimdero.com)

The second cause was the Capitol Records situation. The band signed to Capitol around 1995; disappointing sales on those major-label albums led Capitol to drop them, and the band announced the split the following June. Guitarist Duane Denison later reflected that “there comes a point when bands are no longer perceived as being as vital or important as they once were” and that “there simply wasn’t that much of a demand for our particular brand of rock anymore.” (search result snippet from MTV News, mtv.com — article text inaccessible, snippet only)

2008 reunion

Two things converged. Touch And Go remastered and reissued the back catalogue with bonus material, raising the band’s profile again. Separately, Duane Denison was asked by someone (attributed in one account to festival curator Mike Patton): “What would it take to get the Jesus Lizard back together for a show?” Denison relayed the question to the other members, starting the first real communication between them in years. The band had also been invited to play All Tomorrow’s Parties when Patton was curating but couldn’t get it together in time for that specific event — that invitation nonetheless “got the ball rolling.” (jimdero.com; Exclaim!)

McNeilly describes the momentum that followed: “Once that got going and the word got out, we started getting a lot of feedback from people that said, ‘Well, hey would you do this or this?‘” The band committed to a limited run rather than an open-ended reunion. Denison’s dry summary of the financial reality: “We were all broke.” (jimdero.com)

David Yow has attributed the sustained interest in the band to the internet enabling new generations of listeners to discover them. (Newsweek, 2009)
