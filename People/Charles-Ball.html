Charles Ball

Label founder, producer, A&R. Full name: Charles Cameron Ball. Born 1951. Died August 13, 2012, St. Petersburg, Florida (heart attack, age 61). Discogs ID: 265229.

Co-founder of Ork Records with Terry Ork (mid-1970s); founder of Lust-Unlust Music (late 1970s–c.1982). Roy Trakin described him as “a very important hidden figure who nurtured the scene and almost singlehandedly championed No Wave” at a time when no one else recognized it. Robin Crutchfield called him “generally a pretty nice and decent guy, but always a bit problematic in business.”

Biography

Educated at Mercersburg Academy and Sarah Lawrence College; pursued further study at Dartmouth and Columbia (near-completed master’s degree). Never married, no children. Substance abuse during his New York years took a toll; he later contracted Hepatitis C, which severely affected his health in his final years.

Moved to New York and entered the music world through Ork Records, co-founded with Terry Ork — the label that released Television’s first single and early recordings by Richard Hell and Alex Chilton. After leaving Ork, Ball founded Lust-Unlust Music as a production company: he produced and funded records but allowed artists to choose their own imprint names, creating the appearance of a stable of labels from a single operation.

His intellectual formation was in French theory and cinema: “I’d spent a year translating Jacques Revet’s film criticism… I read a lot of Adorno, Benjamin, Marcuse. Also the Situationists. And Lacan.” The name Lust/Unlust derives from Lacan. He was described by his brother as someone who was “never without a book” and who “loved music more” than almost anything else.

As a producer, Ball was technically innovative: on a Mars EP he used binaural recording mixed to simulate ear-spacing, combined with early digital reverbs and delays, creating distinctive spatial effects that sit alongside Martin Hannett’s productions of the period as a peak of No Wave recording technique.

He maintained extensive field recordings and live concert recordings from the No Wave scene — much of it unreleased as of a 2003 interview.

Ball did not receive recognition commensurate with his contribution during his lifetime. His final years were spent in St. Petersburg, Florida. He died of a heart attack on August 13, 2012, aged 61.
