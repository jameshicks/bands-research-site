Overview

Pal Shazar is an American singer, songwriter, and painter born December 24, 1952 in Los Angeles, California, and is married to fellow musician Jules Shear. She has recorded across a range of styles — New Wave, Synth-pop, Folk Rock, and Alternative Rock — with her earliest releases on Ensign in 1981–1982 and subsequent work on EMI America, Columbia, Enigma, and Funzalo Records stretching into the 2000s. She is a member of Shear Shazar and Slow Children (2), with whom she shares 19 credits. Her most sustained collaborations are with Jules Shear (44 shared releases), producer Stephen Hague, and engineer George Marino.

Slow Children: formation and dissolution

Slow Children (2) formed in Los Angeles in 1979. Shazar and Andrew Chinich were the core. Chinich began teaching Shazar his songs — it was her first time as a vocalist; she eventually took over lyric-writing. Their shared love of Television and Patti Smith was the creative bond that made the partnership click. [micki-steele-net.tumblr.com, 2021; search snippet via lostbands.blogspot.com]

To find a bassist, they advertised in the LA classifieds paper The Recycler. Michael Steele (later bassist of Michael Steele / The Bangles) answered the ad and joined. A manager connected to Jules and the Polar Bears funded rehearsal space in West Hollywood. The band cycled through several drummers. They played relatively few live shows before securing a UK deal. [micki-steele-net.tumblr.com (first-person account by Shazar, submitted 2010); Wikipedia — Michael Steele (musician), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Steele_(musician), as of 2026-10-12]

The band started as a full-sized LA club band before reducing to the duo of Shazar and Chinich. They relocated to England to record, signing with Ensign Records (UK), with US distribution through RCA. A pre-Ensign track, “Staring At The Ceiling,” was recorded in 1979 for Jet Records. [postpunkmonk.com Rock GPA part 2, https://postpunkmonk.com/2020/12/14/rock-g-p-a-slow-children-part-2/; Trouser Press, https://trouserpress.com/reviews/slow-children/]

Production on both albums involved Jules Shear and Stephen Hague. The debut (1981) was produced by Jules + The Polar Bears (with Chinich on guitar). [postpunkmonk.com Rock GPA part 1, https://postpunkmonk.com/2020/12/10/rock-gpa-slow-children-part-1/]

Dissolution: After Mad About Town (1982), RCA proposed a follow-up EP rather than a full album. Shazar and Chinich found the EP suggestion offensive — it signalled the label saw no commercial future for them. The band unravelled; material for a third album was written but never reached vinyl. Shazar and Chinich eventually settled in different countries and “grew up and away from one another.” [postpunkmonk.com Rock GPA part 4, https://postpunkmonk.com/2020/12/17/rock-g-p-a-slow-children-part-4/; lostbands.blogspot.com snippet]

Shazar subsequently married Jules Shear and moved to Woodstock, NY, pursuing a solo career across multiple labels. Around 2010 she and Chinich reconnected and began writing new material; Wikipedia records the band as active again 2015–2019. [postpunkmonk.com Rock GPA part 4; Wikipedia — Slow Children, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Children, as of 2026-10-12]
