Jake Milton

Jake Milton

Oct 30, 20267 min read

Jake Milton

UK drummer. Co-founder of Blurt and brother of frontman Ted Milton. Active as a recording musician from 1969 to at least 1985. Discogs artist ID: 379579.

Biography

Early Career: Quintessence (1969–1972)

Before Blurt, Jake Milton was the drummer for Quintessence (3), a London-based psychedelic-fusion group signed to Island Records. Quintessence blended jazz, progressive rock, Indian classical music, and “new age rock” — a distinctive hybrid whose members adopted devotional names and immersed themselves in Hindu philosophy. The core lineup included vocalist/keyboardist Shiva Shankar Jones, flautist Raja Ram, guitarist Allan Mostert, and bassist Shambhu Babaji, with Jake credited simply as “Jake” on the first two albums.

Quintessence (3) released four LPs on Island Records and RCA Victor between 1969 and 1972:

The band was produced by John Barham (who also arranged for George Harrison’s solo work), a detail that places Quintessence at the fringes of the late-Beatles cultural sphere. The 1969 debut featured artwork by Barney Bubbles, the designer later associated with Stiff Records and Elvis Costello. Jake’s drumming across these records is relatively understated, holding a steady rhythmic foundation beneath the band’s modal improvisations and devotional chanting.

The group dissolved around 1972–1973 after parting ways with their labels. Quintessence’s catalog was reissued repeatedly — Drop Out Records (UK) put out Epitaph For Tomorrow (1993) and Self / Indweller (1995), both with Jake credited on drums and percussion. A later anthology appeared on TIP.World (2007), and The Dutch Woodstock concert recording was issued by Gonzo Multimedia in 2013.

Interregnum (c.1973–1979)

No Discogs credits for Jake Milton appear between the last Quintessence LP in 1972 and the formation of Blurt around 1979–1980. The gap spans approximately seven years. The circumstances of this period — whether Jake was playing in other contexts, not recording, or working outside music — are not documented in the Discogs database.

Blurt: Co-Founding and Core Period (1979–1985)

Around 1979–1980, Jake Milton co-founded Blurt with his brother Ted Milton (poet, puppeteer, saxophonist) and guitarist Pete Creese (also credited as Peter Creese) in Stroud, Gloucestershire. Blurt’s sound was a radical departure from the cosmic expansiveness of Quintessence: tightly coiled sax-driven post-punk, abrasive and minimalist, with Ted’s spoken-word poetry delivering its lyrics over a stripped rhythm section.

Jake was the drummer and co-writer of the original material, credited as “Music By: Milton / Creese / J. Milton” across the early LPs. He also took a vocal role (credited “Drums, Vocals” on In Berlin).

The debut single — My Mother Was A Friend Of An Enemy Of The People / Get (1980, Test Pressings, cat. TPB1) — was the band’s first release, all three members credited as Written-By. Discogs ID: 816639.

A Factory Quartet (1980, Factory Records, FACT 24): Blurt’s biggest early exposure came on this double LP alongside The Durutti Column, Kevin Hewick, and Royal Family And The Poor. Jake produced the Blurt tracks (four sides: “Puppeteer,” “Dyslexia,” “Some Come,” “Benighted”), while Martin Hannett handled production for the other acts. This production credit is notable — it makes Jake not just the drummer but the sonic architect of Blurt’s Factory appearance. Discogs ID: 418113.

In Berlin (1981, Armageddon Records): A live LP recorded in Berlin. Mixed by Jo Julian. Jake credited for Drums, Vocals, and Music By. Discogs ID: 442774.

The Fish Needs A Bike (1981, Armageddon Records): The first proper studio LP. Jake on drums and Music By. Discogs ID: 717696.

Blurt (1982, Red Flame): Self-titled LP; Jake credited Music By alongside Ted and Peter Creese. Discogs ID: 463478. The band was simultaneously documented in Belgium: 13 Febr 1982 Zizni (Big Balumbas In Burundi label) captures a live performance.

Spill The Beans / The Ruminant Plinth (1983, Red Flame): A single, Jake on drums alongside Peter Creese on guitar and Ted on vocals/saxophone. Discogs ID: 745364.

Bullets For You (1984, Divine, France): LP; Jake on drums and Music By; self-produced by Blurt; engineered and mixed by Hugh Shewring. Discogs ID: 516646.

White Line Fever (1984, Another Side, Belgium): Jake credited Music By. Discogs ID: 10196978.

Friday The 12th (1985, Another Side, Belgium): A live LP with Jake on drums and Music By. Notably, this release features Steve Eagles on guitar rather than Pete Creese — Eagle’s credited as “Steve Eaglet” in the sleeve. Chris Vine handled the live mix (credited as “Dr. Vine”). This appears to be a transitional moment: the original Creese lineup giving way to Eagles, with Jake still present on drums. Discogs ID: 512810.

Jake’s last confirmed performing credit on a Blurt release is Friday The 12th (1985). From Poppycock (1986) onward, Paul Wigens is the credited drummer. The precise date of departure is not documented in the Discogs database, but the transition appears to have occurred sometime in 1985–1986.

A “Written-By” credit appears on The Body Live! (1989, Heute, Germany; EFA distribution) — this is a retrospective compilation drawing on earlier material and reflects authorship rather than a new performance.

Context: Factory Records and Post-Punk Scene

Blurt’s appearance on A Factory Quartet in 1980 placed the band — and Jake specifically, as the tracks’ producer — within the orbit of Factory Records. The LP was Factory’s FACT 24, a showcase for four acts at the experimental edge of the post-punk moment. Jake’s production of Blurt’s four tracks is a rare instance of a drummer taking sole production credit on a Factory release — for comparison, the other acts on the LP had their tracks produced by Martin Hannett, Factory’s principal studio architect.

Blurt’s sound at this point occupied an unusual position in UK post-punk: more rhythmically skeletal than Gang of Four, more abrasive and Dadaist than Magazine, and rooted in a jazz vocabulary that came naturally to musicians who had spent the early 1970s in an improvising ensemble. Jake’s pre-Blurt experience in Quintessence (3), with its open-form rhythmic approach, likely informed the stripped-down, trance-like groove quality that defined the early Blurt rhythm section.

The band’s European touring in the early 1980s gave them significant exposure on Belgian and French labels (Another Side, Divine) outside the UK independent sector, a pattern common to post-punk groups who found more continental enthusiasm than domestic commercial traction.

Open Questions

  • The seven-year gap between Quintessence (3) dissolving (c.1972–1973) and Blurt forming (c.1979–1980) is entirely undocumented in Discogs. Whether Jake was active in other musical contexts during this period is unknown.
  • Exact date of departure from Blurt (between 1985 and 1986) is not confirmed by Discogs. Friday The 12th is his last performing credit; Poppycock (1986) is the first Blurt LP with Paul Wigens on drums.
  • The Discogs URL (https://www.tedmilton.net/blurt/jake.html) on his artist page suggests a bio page exists on Ted Milton’s official website that may have biographical detail not captured in the DB. Web research would clarify the departure circumstances and any post-Blurt activity.
  • Quintessence (3) had a complex membership history and Jake’s role vs. Phil Jones (18) (another drummer listed on the Discogs group page) is unclear — possible Jake was replaced or supplemented at some point.
