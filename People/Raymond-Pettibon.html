Raymond Pettibon

Raymond Pettibon

Visual artist. Born Raymond Ginn, Tucson, AZ, 1957. Discogs artist ID: 601494. Real name: Raymond Ginn. Younger brother of Greg Ginn.

Known for pen-and-ink drawings with dense text — images combining figurative draftsmanship with fragmentary literary quotation. First gained wide notice through cover artwork for Black Flag and the SST Records milieu, late 1970s through mid-1980s. His professional name derives from a childhood nickname given by his father, “Petit Bon.” Based in Los Angeles.

Name variations used in credits: R. Pettibon, Ray Pettibon, R. Pettibone, Raymond Pettibone, Pettibon, Maestro Raymond Pettibon, The Artist.

Biography

SST Years (1979–1986)

Pettibon’s entry into the record-sleeve world was immediate and consequential. His artwork appeared on the very first SST release: Black Flag’s Nervous Breakdown EP (SST 001, 1979) — credited as “Raymond Pettibone.” From there he became the visual identity of Black Flag, contributing cover drawings to virtually every major release: Jealous Again (1980), Six Pack (1981), My War (1984), Slip It In (1984), Loose Nut (1985), In My Head (1985), and others. He also drew the insert illustrations for Spot’s Paganicons EP (SST, 1981) and the cover of The Blasting Concept SST compilation (1983). Minutemen’s Double Nickels on the Dime (SST, 1984) features his drawings throughout its insert.

The SST work was prolific but the relationship ended acrimoniously, reportedly over the treatment and return of his original drawings. After the mid-1980s he did no further work for SST and distanced himself from the label world Greg Ginn controlled.

During this period he also contributed covers to New Alliance Records releases (Minutemen-associated, San Pedro), including Cracks In The Sidewalk (1980) and others, and to Posh Boy Records (Posh Hits Vol. 1, 1983; The Future Looks Brighter, 1987).

Post-SST Work and Art World Crossover (1987 onwards)

After the break with SST, Pettibon’s profile in the fine-art world grew substantially — museum exhibitions, gallery representation, inclusion in major collections. He continued contributing album artwork selectively, but now on his own terms.

The most famous post-SST credit is the cover illustration for Sonic Youth’s Goo (DGC, 1990) — a pen-and-ink drawing of a couple (reportedly based on a Charles Starkweather reference), handwritten text included, which became one of the most recognized album cover images of the late twentieth century. He also contributed artwork to the associated Disappearer EP (DGC, 1990) and the Goo VHS (BMG Video, 1991).

He contributed ink drawings to Mike Watt’s Flyin’ The Flannel (Columbia, 1991) and provided the cover drawing for Watt’s Ball-Hog Or Tugboat? (Columbia, 1995), the sprawling collaboration record featuring Thurston Moore, Lee Ranaldo, J Mascis, Eddie Vedder, Flea, Dave Grohl, Henry Rollins, Krist Novoselic, and others.

Foo Fighters’ One By One (Roswell/RCA, 2002) carried his artwork across the album and multiple singles — a significant mainstream placement. His work appeared on the All My Life, Have It All, Low, and Times Like These singles from that campaign.

OFF!‘s self-titled 2012 LP (Vice Records) — the hardcore supergroup fronted by Keith Morris — carries his artwork, a return to the Black Flag-adjacent world.

He also contributed to Mike Watt’s The Man (Ecstatic Peace!, 1991) and various Ecstatic Peace! releases, including a 2010 compilation.

Musical Activity

Beyond visual work, Pettibon occasionally participated as a musician or vocalist, and maintained memberships in several largely informal groups logged by Discogs:

  • Sür Drone (Discogs ID: 3635837) — Pettibon as sole credited member; drone/noise context
  • Sock-Tight (Discogs ID: 3605085) — duo with Mike Watt
  • Psing Psong Psung (Discogs ID: 14238010) — includes Dirk Vandenberg (who shot photos for Double Nickels on the Dime), Ray Farrell (former SST/Matador), and Ricky Sepulveda
  • The Nichemakers (Discogs ID: 4221550) — seven-member group including Pettibon

In 2006 he contributed artwork and vocals to The Master’s Voice (Smog Veil Records). In 2015 and 2020 he appeared as primary artist on two Brigade Commerz releases (Wooden Heart, 2015; What We Know Is Secret, 2020), the latter credited with Lyrics By, Vocals, and Artwork By. On Smudge (ORG Music, 2020) he contributed vocals and words.

He provided lyrics and voice for Long Live The People Of The Revolution (Eventuell, Germany, 2005) and for Soundtrack To Red Tide Rising: Venice And Mars (2001), credited as songwriter, vocalist, and cover artist.
