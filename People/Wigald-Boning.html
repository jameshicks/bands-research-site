Wigald Boning

Born January 20, 1967, in Wildeshausen, Germany. Comedian, TV presenter, journalist, and multi-instrumentalist. Discogs artist ID: 238936. Website: boning.de.

Boning became nationally famous as a cast member of the RTL variety sketch show RTL Samstag Nacht (“RTL Saturday Night”) in the early 1990s, where he worked alongside Olli Dittrich. The two formed the comedy duo Die Doofen for a run of novelty singles and albums on Sing Sing / BMG. Outside the comedy vehicle, Boning has maintained an active life as a self-taught multi-instrumentalist — his Discogs credits span flute, saxophone (alto, soprano, tenor, bass clarinet), bassoon, oboe, tuba, trumpet, French horn, clarinet, piano, organ, violin, and glockenspiel.

Early Career: Bremen (5) and KIXX (1985–1995)

Boning’s first recorded appearances are with the Hamburg act Bremen (5), produced by Klaus Voormann (Polydor/ACT, 1988). The album Bremen features Boning on vocals, saxophone, flute, and keyboards. From the same period, his credit on the 1986 The Hidden Lover EP connects him to the group Kixx; the 1995 compilation Wigald Boning & KIXX on ITM Records (2) collects this material.

His solo catalog began in earnest with the 1991–1992 RCA period: Langsame Liebe (1991, RCA) and Wildeshausen (1991, RCA) were single releases produced by Ströer Bros. (Ernst Ströer, Hans Peter Ströer), with musicians including Lars Rudolph (guitar, trumpet), Jim Meneses (drums), and backing vocals from Bernadette La Hengst. Langspielplatte (1992, RCA) followed as a full-length.

Die Doofen (1992–2001)

As one half of Die Doofen with Olli Dittrich, Boning co-wrote and performed a string of parody/novelty singles and albums on Sing Sing and RCA throughout the mid-to-late 1990s. Key credits: Lieder, Die Die Welt Nicht Braucht (1995, Sing Sing) and Melodien Für Melonen (1996, Sing Sing / BMG). For these releases Boning played an extraordinary range of instruments: alto and soprano saxophone, clarinet, bassoon, recorder, alto horn, flute, trumpet, trombone, tuba, panpipes, basset horn, violin, piano, and electric razor. The duo’s compilation Das Dööfste (2001, Ariola Express) collects their work.

Other 1990s Work

Ein Freund, Ein Guter Freund / Das Bremserlied (1997, Sing Sing) was a Boning solo release for a film soundtrack, featuring Franka Potente. Geflügelpop Non Stop (2000, self-released) was a self-produced chicken-themed novelty record. Gimme More Huhn (2000, Hansa) featured Boning alongside the Moorhuhn video game character.

Zwei Stühle – Eine Meinung (1998, The Record Company/EMI Electrola) was a comedy spoken-word record co-credited to Boning, with compositions by and written-by credits including Frank Peterson, Francesco Sartori, and Lucio Quarantotto — placing Boning in orbit of the Eros Ramazzotti songwriting world in an unexpected crossover.

Jet Set Jazz (2008, Compost Records)

The centerpiece of Boning’s recorded output as a musician. Jet Set Jazz (Compost Records, CPT 308-2, 2008) is a 13-track comedy-jazz album credited to Wigald Boning as primary artist, with all creative labor shared across three writers/arrangers/producers:

  • Wigald Boning — Written-By, Arranged By; French horn, bassoon, flute (alto, nose), tenor saxophone, oboe, pocket trumpet, clarinet, bass clarinet, tuba, glockenspiel, vocals
  • Roberto Di Gioia — Written-By, Producer, Arranged By, Recorded By; piano, keyboards, guitar, bass, drums, percussion
  • Christian Prommer — Written-By, Producer, Arranged By, Mixed By; beats, sounds
  • Martine Rojina — Backing Vocals
  • Alexa De Puivert — Voice [Telephone]
  • Artwork: Christian Hundertmark (C100 Studio)

Genres: Electronic / Jazz. Styles: Easy Listening, Future Jazz, Latin Jazz, Leftfield. Label code LC 02518.

Hobby (5) / Hobby Musik Period (2013–2014)

Boning and Roberto Di Gioia formed the group Hobby (5) and established their own imprint Hobby Musik. Two releases followed:

Hobby (2013, Hobby Musik HOB 001–008)

An enormous 8-disc CDr box set (limited edition), credited to Hobby (5) / Wigald Boning & Roberto Di Gioia. The set spans baroque, jazz, new wave/synth-pop (original “Neon” section), folk/world, schlager, prog, punk, and spoken comedy. Boning plays flute, bass clarinet, oboe, and numerous unspecified instruments across the discs. Guest appearances include:

  • Tim Ries — Saxophone (taragato), discs 4-1, 4-2
  • Tony Marshall — Vocals, discs 5-1 to 5-21 (“21 Lieder Die Tony Marshall Nicht Singen Wollte”)
  • Maria Braun — Vocals, discs 8-1 to 8-10
  • Selina Sillanpää — Vocals, handclaps, discs 4-1, 4-2
  • Matthias Edlinger, Nina Braun — Vocals, discs 3-1 to 3-13
  • Marc Marshall — Photography
  • Recorded by Jeopard (Joachim Mezei)

Disc sections: Baroque / Die Band Mit Den Vergilbten Fotos / Neon / Good Old India, Good Old Ingria / 21 Lieder Die Tony Marshall Nicht Singen Wollte / Charthits / Bildpunk / Maria Braun

New Wave (2014, Hobby Musik HOB 010-2)

A 13-track CD, credited to Hobby (5) / Wigald Boning & Roberto Di Gioia. Produced by both Boning and Di Gioia. Mixed and mastered by Jan Krause. Track 11 (“Hi Robbe High”) features Afrob. Styles: Disco, New Wave.
