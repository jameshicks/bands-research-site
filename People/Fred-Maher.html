Fred Maher

Full name: Frederick J. Maher Born: 1964, Manhattan, New York Role: Drummer, music programmer, record producer, A&R executive Discogs ID: 69826 Alias: Freddy LaMarr

Overview

American drummer and record producer who emerged from the late-1970s New York avant-garde scene as a founding member of Material and Massacre (2). After a performing career that took him through New York Gong, Massacre (2), and Scritti Politti, he transitioned into production and became one of the more prolific rock producers of the late 1980s and early 1990s, with credits spanning Lou Reed, Matthew Sweet, Lloyd Cole, and others. Later served as VP of A&R at Elektra Records.

Biography

Early life and entry into the NYC scene

Maher grew up in Manhattan and began music lessons as a child — initially violin, which he abandoned early. He settled on drums at a music school, learning partly by playing along to progressive rock records, particularly Yes and Emerson Lake & Palmer. (rickrees.substack.com, as of 2026-06-16)

Around age 14 he was playing in a band called 1121, performing prog rock. Through that group he befriended Martin Bisi and Michael Beinhorn. The three frequented Pantasia Recorz, a Manhattan shop specializing in European avant-garde imports. After finding fliers advertising musicians interested in acts like Kraftwerk and Magma, they contacted Giorgio Gomelsky — a producer they recognized from record sleeves. At Gomelsky’s rehearsal space they encountered bassist Bill Laswell, whose skill immediately elevated the group. Guitarist Cliff Cultreri completed the lineup, which initially operated as the Zu Band. (rickrees.substack.com, as of 2026-06-16)

The Zu Band performed at Gomelsky’s Zu Manifestival, where they met Fred Frith, Chris Cutler, and Daevid Allen. At age 16 Maher dropped out of high school to tour in what he later described as a dilapidated school bus, completing his GED on return. The band recorded four songs with engineer Eddy Offord at Levon Helm’s Woodstock studio; released as “Temporary Music 1” (ZÙ Records, 1979). Shortly after, the band separated from Gomelsky — described as “an act of rebellion” — renamed themselves Material, and established their own studio in Brooklyn. (rickrees.substack.com, as of 2026-06-16)

At the October 1978 Zu Manifestival performance, Daevid Allen spontaneously adopted the name New York Gong for the set — leading to the band appearing under that name on the LP About Time (Charly Records, 1980). (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/About_Time_(New_York_Gong_album), as of 2026-06-16)

Material and Massacre

Maher was the drummer for Material from their earliest recordings through Memory Serves (Celluloid, 1981), playing on:

  • “Temporary Music 1” (ZÙ Records, 1979)
  • Discourse (Celluloid, 1980)
  • Memory Serves (Celluloid, 1981) — also played guitar and percussion

He co-founded Massacre (2) with Fred Frith and Bill Laswell in 1980. The trio recorded the album Killing Time (Celluloid, CEL 6597, 1981), which documented an abrasive, improvised guitar-bass-drums approach. The band performed live for just over a year before disbanding in mid-1981. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_(experimental_band), as of 2026-06-16)

During this same period he also drummed with The Dance, co-producing and co-arranging their debut Dance For Your Dinner EP (Go Go Records, 1980), and appeared on Chandra Oppenheim’s Transportation EP (Go Go Records, 1980).

Robert Quine collaboration

In 1984 Maher released Basic — an instrumental duo album with Robert Quine, formerly of Richard Hell and the Voidoids. Produced by the duo on Quine’s four-track Portastudio in his Manhattan living room (December 1983 – May 1984), the record features ten instrumentals built on Maher’s programmed electronic rhythms and Quine’s multitracked guitar. Mixed at Sorcerer Sound, released on E.G. Records (July 1984); reissued by Virgin/Charisma in 1996. The project followed their shared tenure in Lou Reed’s backing band. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_(Robert_Quine_and_Fred_Maher_album), as of 2026-06-16)

Scritti Politti (1984–1988)

Maher joined Scritti Politti as the drummer for Green Gartside’s refashioned commercial pop lineup alongside keyboardist David Gamson. His no-wave and funk background gave the group’s drum sound the rhythmic precision Gartside was seeking. He played on — and co-produced several tracks of — both major Scritti Politti albums of the era:

  • Cupid & Psyche 85 (Virgin, 1985) — drums on tracks 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10; co-producer on several tracks. Recorded at Atlantic Studios, Minot Sound, and The Power Station, New York. (hifinews.com, as of 2026-06-16)
  • Provision (Virgin, 1988) — continued as drummer

The Scritti Politti years yielded singles including “Wood Beez,” “Absolute,” “Hypnotize,” “The Word Girl,” and “Perfect Way.”

Massacre 1998 reunion — non-participation

When Frith and Laswell reformed Massacre in 1998 they asked Charles Hayward (of This Heat) to take the drum chair. Wikipedia’s article on the band does not give a reason for Maher’s absence. No published interview has surfaced explaining the decision from his side or theirs. Gap: reason for non-participation unconfirmed.
