Stephen Mallinder

Stephen Mallinder

Aug 24, 20266 min read

Stephen Mallinder

Born: 1 January 1955, Sheffield, South Yorkshire Based: Brighton, England (previously Perth, Western Australia ~1995–2006) Role: Vocalist, bass, electronics, tape — founding member of Cabaret Voltaire

Background

Grew up in Sheffield’s industrial landscape. Early influences: Dadaist aesthetics, William S. Burroughs, David Bowie (hearing “Space Oddity” in 1971 was formative). BA in History and Politics, Sheffield Hallam University.

Met Richard H. Kirk as teenagers — “old soul boys” bonding over record shops and sneaking into nightclubs. Kirk brought Mallinder in to provide vocals and bass for what became Cabaret Voltaire in late 1973. Chris Watson had been independently building tape loops and oscillators; he and Kirk were already collaborating.

At CV’s first public concert (13 May 1975), Mallinder was hospitalised with a chipped backbone after the audience threw objects at them. Hospitalised at least twice due to audience violence in the early years.

Cabaret Voltaire

See Cabaret Voltaire.

Primary vocalist and visual presence throughout. Also played bass guitar, percussion, keyboards, tape recorders, electronics. Work was always fully collaborative — nothing written individually and handed over.

Vocal approach was instrumental rather than conventional: manipulation, texture, Burroughs-style cut-up of media language without dogmatic messaging.

On CV vs. punk: “Punk was inherently quite normal. It was bass, drums, guitar, occasional keyboards and a vocalist. We made sound — that’s what we were trying to change.”

Met William Burroughs multiple times: at the Brussels show with Joy Division (1980) and at The Bunker, NYC (1983–84).

Left Sheffield with CV dissolved, 1994.

Solo Work (Fetish Records, 1981–82)

Recorded solo at night at the Western Works studio while CV used it during the day.

  • “Temperature Drop / Cool Down” 7” (Fetish Records FE 12, September 1981) — trippy, dub-influenced A-side; motorik B-side
  • Pow-Wow LP (Fetish Records FM2010, October 1982) — recorded entirely alone at Western Works; multi-tracked by jumping between instruments himself: “An experiment to play everything myself.” Mutant dub / funk / post-punk. Last Few Days appear credited as “Performer [Chance Element]” (as LFD). Reissued as Pow Wow Plus (Doublevision DVR 16 / Mute MAL 1CD, 1985) with extra tracks.

Mallinder also engineered two tracks on the Last Few Days / Laibach split cassette (Galerija ŠKUC Izdaja, 1983) — “Polar Vision” and “Blind Knives,” recorded at Western Works in May 1982.

These were his only solo releases for 37 years.

Side Projects (1980s)

  • Doublevision (1982) — UK’s first independent video label, co-founded with Kirk
  • Love Street (1988) — with Soft Cell members
  • Acid Horse (1989) — with Al Jourgensen (Ministry) and Chris Connelly; recorded in Chicago under pseudonyms to avoid label contracts. Abrasive industrial/electronic hybrid.

Perth Years (~1995–2006)

After CV dissolved, relocated to Perth, Western Australia — a deliberate break: “It was an opportunity to try things without feeling the weight of expectation on top of me.” Also single-parenting; his children grew up in Perth.

Off World Sounds label: co-founded with Pete Carroll, modelled on Rough Trade (50/50 splits, artist involvement). ~40 releases spanning dub, trance, hip-hop, house — global in scope despite Perth’s isolation. Commercial failure attributed to systemic Australian music policy problems.

Ku-Ling Bros: his own project on OWS — breaks/electro-oriented electronic music:

  • Coolin’ Brothers (OffWorld Sounds, 1998)
  • Creach (OffWorld Sounds / dPulse, 1999)
  • Here Come The Astronauts (Pública Records, 2008)

Sassi & Loco: collaborative project with a Perth-based partner.

Also: late-night and daytime radio at RTRFM (Perth community radio); music journalism for Ministry Magazine, The Sunday Times, The West Australian; DJing; festival promotion (including Vibes on a Summer’s Day, grew to 30,000 attendees).

Enrolled in PhD at Murdoch University during this period.

Academic Career

PhD: Movement: Journey of the Beat, Murdoch University, Perth, completed 2011

Now Dr. Stephen Mallinder — teaches Digital Music & Sound Art at the University of Brighton (Art Design Media Centre). Research interests: sound and moving image, live electronica, analogue technology, spoken word.

Academic publications include:

  • “Sounds Incorporated: Dissonant Sorties into Popular Music” (punk/post-punk anthology)
  • Preface to Assimilate: A Critical History of Industrial Music
  • Introduction to German Pop Music: A Companion
  • “Off World Sounds: Building a Collaborative Soundscape” (M/C Journal, 2006, with Tara Brabazon)
  • Remix chapter in Total State Machine: Test Dept.

Regular contributor to The Wire and Electronic Sound.

Wrangler

Formed on returning to UK. Members: Mallinder (vocals, bass) + Benge / Ben Edwards (analogue synthesis) + Phil Winter (keyboards; formerly of Tunng).

Ethos: exclusively analogue hardware, no samplers, no guitars, no chords — monophonic lines, sequencers, modular synthesis for drums. “I think it starts out as soup, we all throw a bit in and see how it tastes.”

  • LA Spark (MemeTune, 2014)
  • White Glue (MemeTune, 2016)
  • A Situation (Bella Union, 2020)

Creep Show

Wrangler + John Grant (American singer-songwriter). Origin: Grant encountered Mallinder at a Moloko aftershow in the late 1990s (huge for Grant; Mallinder doesn’t recall it). Reconnected at Sheffield’s Sensoria Festival 2014. Wrangler remixed Grant’s “Voodoo Doll” (2016). First performed together at Rough Trade’s 40th anniversary, Barbican, London (2016).

  • Mr. Dynamite (Bella Union, 2018)
  • Yawning Abyss (Bella Union, ~2021)

Solo Return

  • Um Dada (Dais Records, 2019) — first solo album since Pow-Wow, 37 years later
  • Clinker (Les Disques du Crépuscule TWI 1256, 2021) — with Benge and Julie Campbell (LoneLady)
  • Tick Tick Tick… (Dais Records, 2022)
  • “Contact / Cool Down” (Electronic Sound magazine vinyl, 2022) — rerecording of the 1981 Fetish single

Cotton Panic (2017)

Theatre production with actress Jane Horrocks, premiering at Manchester International Festival. Mallinder composed the music and performed live.

Cabaret Voltaire Redux (2025–)

Following Kirk’s death (21 September 2021), Mallinder stated he would not revive the CV name “out of respect.” Changed position for the 50th anniversary: 25 October 2025, Forge Warehouse, Sheffield — Mallinder and Chris Watson performed as Cabaret Voltaire. But What Time Is It Really? live album followed. Final World Tour announced through 2026, ending Octagon Centre, Sheffield, 25 October 2026.

Key Quote

“Music should be discovery not the realisation of expectation.”
