Phil Ranelin

Born: May 25, 1939, Indianapolis, Indiana
Real name: Phillip Arthur Ranelin
Discogs ID: 31167
Active: 1960s–present

Trombonist, composer, arranger, producer, educator, and bandleader. Co-founder of Tribe Records (Detroit, 1972–1976) alongside reed player Wendell Harrison. A central figure in the Detroit spiritual jazz and Black consciousness music scene of the early 1970s; his Tribe-era recordings have been heavily reissued and remixed since the 2000s. Continues to record and perform.

Early Career and Detroit Scene

Ranelin came up in Indianapolis before relocating to Detroit, where he became embedded in the city’s vibrant jazz community. His early collaborators included Marcus Belgrave (trumpet), Wendell Harrison (tenor saxophone/clarinet), Harold McKinney (piano), and Charles Moore (trumpet) — the core of what would become the Tribe collective.

He also worked extensively with touring and studio jazz artists, including Ella Fitzgerald, Freddie Hubbard, Art Pepper, Sarah Vaughan, Norman Connors, Teddy Edwards, Gerald Wilson, Roy Brooks, Larry Gales, and Tootie Heath, among others. His membership in The Freddie Hubbard Sextet brought him into the wider mainstream jazz circuit.

Tribe Records (1972–1976)

In 1972, Ranelin and Wendell Harrison co-founded Tribe Records at 81 Chandler St., Detroit, MI — a self-run label, publishing house, and production company operating under the motto “Music is the healing force of the Universe.” Alongside the record label, Tribe published a black awareness magazine. The collective included Marcus Belgrave, Harold McKinney, Charles Moore, Doug Hammond, and Faruq Z. Bey, among others.

Tribe released approximately five years of music before ceasing operations around 1976–1977. All releases are on Tribe (3) (Discogs label ID 100848).

Post-Tribe Years and Session Work

After Tribe’s run, Ranelin continued as a session trombonist. Notable credits include the debut album The Red Hot Chili Peppers (1984, EMI America / Enigma) and The Love Connection (1979, Columbia) among others. He appeared on sessions from Canada to the UK throughout the late 1970s and ’80s.

He was also a member of The Pan-Afrikan Peoples Arkestra and The Lexington Arts Ensemble in Los Angeles.

Rediscovery and the Hefty Remixes (2001)

Remixes (2001, Hefty Records, HEFTY 037 CD) brought Ranelin’s Tribe-era recordings to a new generation. Coordinated by John Hughes (Slicker), the album presented reworkings of tracks from Vibes From The Tribe and The Time Is Now! by a roster of electronic and experimental artists:

Build An Ark

Ranelin became a member of Build An Ark, a Los Angeles creative music ensemble founded immediately after September 11, 2001 by producer Carlos Niño and vocalist Dwight Trible. The large ensemble also included Nate Morgan, Adam Rudolph, Derf Reklaw, Alan Lightner, Joshua Spiegelman, Trevor Ware, and many others — a congregation of veterans from the Pan-Afrikan Peoples Arkestra lineage and LA underground jazz. Ranelin’s connection to Carlos Niño continued through subsequent solo projects.
