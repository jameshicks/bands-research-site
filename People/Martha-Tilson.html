Biography

London Years (1979–1980)

How Martha Tilson arrived in London’s post-punk scene appears to be via Pam Meyer — an American who met the Twilight Zoners while living in Berlin, and who designed their record sleeves. Tilson likely entered the scene through this New York/Berlin/London circle.

She sang backing vocals on the Twilight Zoners’ Hospital EP (1979), credited simply as “Tilli” in the acknowledgements. She wasn’t a full member but was clearly close to the group.

In 1980, Twilight Zoners member Glenn Carmichael (Gordon Twilight) financed a new record using Twilight Zoners alumni Tom Dixon and Justin Langlands, plus flautist Selima Guirey, with Tilson as lead vocalist. This was Occult Chemistry — releasing a four-track EP (the four elements) on Dining Out Records / Zip. The track “Water” also appeared on a flexi disc with Bikini Girl magazine (New York, 1980) — an early indicator of her New York connections.

New York Meeting (September 1980)

A Certain Ratio toured the US in September 1980, playing multiple nights at Hurrah and Tier 3 in New York alongside nascent New Order. Jez Kerr met Tilson there — she “came to see them, came back to Manchester to see Jez, and ended up doing the vocals.” She was already in New York at this point, suggesting she had returned there between the London post-punk scene and the ACR tour.

A Certain Ratio (1980–1982)

She joined ACR as a sixth member and live focal point. Appeared on Sextet (Factory Records, January 1982) — ACR’s first self-produced album, their most acclaimed record, featuring her on multiple tracks:

  • “Lucinda”, “Crystal”, “Gum”, “Skipscada”, “Knife Slits Water”

She co-wrote “Knife Slits Water” entirely. She was absent from the recording session due to illness; Jez and Pete brought back a cassette of the instrumental. She wrote the lyrics at the mixing session in about an hour. The title was inspired by Roman Polanski’s film Knife in the Water (1962). Her description: “It’s about sex, plain and simple — but also desire and sexual politics. It makes most sense when a woman sings it.”

Her voice was described by Martin Moscrop as “angelic.” Processed at times through an Eventide Harmonizer.

She departed ACR before I’d Like To See You Again (recorded Feb–March 1982). The November 1982 John Peel session was recorded without her.

Thick Pigeon / Stanton Miranda (December 1981)

Martha Tilson is credited as a performer on “Silhouettes” by Thick Pigeon — the first recorded appearance of Thick Pigeon, on the Les Disques du Crépuscule Christmas compilation Chantons Noël — Ghosts of Christmas Past (TWI-058, December 1981). The credit is confirmed by the official 2016 digital reissue metadata from The Orchard/Crépuscule: “Silhouettes · Thick Pigeon · Stanton Miranda · Martha Tilson.”

Thick Pigeon were a New York art-pop duo: Stanton Miranda (vocalist/bassist) and Carter Burwell (arranger/keyboards — later the Coen Brothers’ regular film composer). The Factory/Crépuscule connection is direct: Miranda’s partner Michael Shamberg ran Factory Records’ New York office. Les Disques du Crépuscule was the Belgian sister label to Factory. Tilson, still with ACR at this point, would have been squarely in this world.

The exact nature of her contribution (lead vocal, backing vocal) is not specified in available sources. This appears to be a single documented appearance; no other Thick Pigeon or Miranda Dali credits for Tilson have been found.

New York Years / Magnetic Pull (c.1982–1983)

After ACR she returned to New York with Pam Meyer. Together they co-led an all-female, unrecorded band called Magnetic Pull, which also featured Georgia Hubley (later of Yo La Tengo) on drums. No recordings were made. No further musical activity is documented after this.

Why She Matters in This Network

Martha Tilson is one of the most extraordinary connective figures in this entire graph:

  • London DIY (Twilight Zoners / Occult Chemistry / Dining Out Records)
  • → Manchester art-funk (A Certain Ratio / Factory Records / Martin Hannett)

A single person who passed through three cities and three distinct scenes, leaving almost no documented trace.

Notes (web research — 2026-07-04, supplemented 2026-07-04)

No standalone interview with Martha Tilson has been located anywhere online. ACR biographies (LTM Recordings, Factory Benelux, AllMusic, NostalgiaCentral) note her departure from the band before I’d Like To See You Again (early 1982) but say nothing about her subsequent activities. No source independently documents Magnetic Pull or her post-ACR life.

“Knife Slits Water” quote: The Factory Benelux 2014 Sextet reissue CD liner notes (source) include a direct quote from Tilson about “Knife Slits Water”: “It’s about sex, plain and simple — but also desire and sexual politics. It makes most sense when a woman sings it.” She explains she wasn’t present at the recording session, wrote the lyrics at the mixing session in about an hour after Jez and Pete brought back a cassette. These liner notes also include a comment from Jez Kerr that he “recently got back in touch with Tilly” — suggesting she is still alive and locatable as of the mid-2010s, but no further detail is given. (source)

Magnetic Pull source chain: The primary source documenting Magnetic Pull and Tilson’s involvement is the Messthetics #107 booklet (Hyped to Death, 2009) — a 24-page physical document not reproduced online. Discogs r15406580 draws from it. Tilson’s own voice on Magnetic Pull is absent from all located public sources.

Post-ACR trace: No evidence of any musical activity by Tilson after c.1983 has been located. No social media presence. The Jez Kerr note (“recently got back in touch”) from the mid-2010s liner notes is the only indication she remained contactable.
