Mick Buck

Guitarist and vocalist based in Austin, Texas (active there 1980–1998). Discogs artist ID: 2836154. Name variations: “M. Buck”, “Mick”.

Overview

Mick Buck was a fixture in the Austin underground across two decades and two distinct musical worlds: early-1980s Texas hardcore punk and late-1980s/1990s Americana-adjacent alt-country and cowpunk. He sang for the Offenders on their 1981 hardcore 7-inch, one of the earliest Texas hardcore releases, and a decade later became a key guitarist in the orbit of Jean Caffeine (2) — appearing on all three of her All-Nite Truckstop recordings — and a co-founder of Blind Willie’s Johnson, the Austin blues-punk outfit active through the 1990s. He also played guitar and co-produced the 1998 self-titled album for Texacala Jones & Her T.J. Hookers, an Austin cowpunk group led by Texacala Jones.

His Discogs profile notes he worked with Walter Daniels, Eric Hisaw, Davy Jones (2), and Jeff Smith (3) — all within the Austin scene — though these collaborations do not appear as separate documented releases in the database.

Career

The Offenders (1981)

Buck sang lead vocals on the Offenders’ debut 7-inch Lost Causes / Rockin’ The Town (1981, Suffering Sounds), one of the earliest Texas hardcore punk records. The Offenders were formed in Killeen, Texas in 1978 and relocated to Austin in 1980, becoming a touchstone of the Texas hardcore scene alongside D.R.I. and M.D.C. Buck is listed as a vocalist (not guitarist) on this release; other members included Anthony Johnson (4) on lead guitar, Mikey Donaldson on bass, and Pat Doyle on drums. His specific tenure with the band is not fully documented — the 2005 Belgian reissue We Must Rebel (Reflex Records) credits Buck on vocals for tracks B4–B8 only, suggesting he was the vocalist for one period of the band’s history rather than the primary frontman throughout.

Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop (1989–1992)

Buck’s most documented work is as lead guitarist and harmony vocalist in the ensemble around Jean Caffeine (2). He appears on all three of her documented releases:

  • Jean Caffeine (1989, Not On Label) — 4-track single-sided cassette demo. Buck plays lead guitar and backing vocals alongside JD Foster (bass, acoustic guitar, producer), Ponty Bone (accordion), Bobby Birdsong (lap steel), Fred KRC (drums), and others.
  • Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop (1990, Porch Music (2)) — 10-track cassette album. Buck: lead guitar, backing vocals, slide guitar. Also features Mark Rubin (acoustic bass), Lisa Pankratz (percussion), and JD Foster (mixed by, bass, acoustic guitar).
  • Hard Work And A Lot Of Hairspray! (1992, Porch Music (2)) — 12-track cassette album. Buck: electric guitar, slide guitar, backing vocals, and lead vocals. Produced by Gurf Morlix; also features Seth Tiven (lap steel), Dennis Ku (slide guitar, lead guitar), and Ponty Bone (accordion).

Blind Willie’s Johnson (1994)

Buck was a co-founding member of Blind Willie’s Johnson, the Austin blues-punk outfit described in their profile as “blues-wailing traffickers in narcotic electric mud.” The band’s sole documented Discogs release is Oops! (1994, Unclean Records, UR-028), a 4-track 7-inch EP in a garage rock style. Buck plays guitar and sings; other members: Jean Caffeine (2), Seth Tiven (bass), Dennis Ku (guitar), Jason Vargo (drums), Ron Polito (blues harp). The band also included Tall Tom Patton per Discogs membership records.

Texacala Jones & Her T.J. Hookers (1998)

Buck played guitar, co-produced, and mixed the self-titled debut album (1998, Honey Records (2), HON-CD03) by Texacala Jones & Her T.J. Hookers, an Austin cowpunk band led by Texacala Jones. Co-producer: Jeff Smith (3). Other players: Roger Prescott (guitar), Tim Kinard (bass), Rob Gaines (2) (drums), Walter Daniels (saxophone, harmonica). This was a CD release on Honey Records (2), one of only a handful of Discogs-documented releases on that label.

Open Questions

  • Buck’s profile states he was in Austin “from 1980 to 1998” — his whereabouts and activities after 1998 are undocumented in Discogs.
  • His exact tenure in the Offenders is unclear: the 1981 7-inch and the 2005 reissue both credit him, but the band’s formation period (1978–1980, pre-Austin) is not accounted for.
  • Collaborations with Eric Hisaw and Davy Jones (2) mentioned in his Discogs profile have no corresponding releases in the database — these may be undocumented live/session work or releases not yet entered in Discogs. (Walter Daniels and Jeff Smith (3) do appear on the documented T.J. Hookers release.)
  • No solo releases found in Discogs.
  • The 1989 demo also credits “Andy Dyken” (Andrew Dyken) on lead guitar — unclear whether Buck and Dyken were co-lead guitarists or each appeared on different tracks.
