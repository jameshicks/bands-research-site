Aug 07, 20264 min read

Eric Bogosian

Performance artist, monologist, actor, playwright. New York City. Discogs ID: to confirm.

Self-titled LP (N-10, Neutral Records, 1983) — produced by Chris Lycett (London) and Leroy Sound; photography by Paula Court. His inclusion on Neutral connects him to the downtown Manhattan art/performance scene that overlapped with No Wave, via Josh Baer’s White Columns network.

White Columns / Josh Baer Connection

Josh Baer served as director of White Columns (then at 325 Spring Street) from 1979–83. He took the position at age 23, with no prior administrative experience, after being encouraged by the space’s founder Jeffrey Lew. (thebaerfaxt.com; Leslie Rankow Fine Arts blog, 2013-06-11)

While director, Baer co-organized Noise Fest (June 16–24, 1981) at White Columns alongside Kim Gordon and Thurston Moore — a nine-night festival featuring 40+ performers including Rhys Chatham, Glenn Branca, UT, and Sonic Youth. Baer served as executive producer of the cassette release. (Wikipedia: Noise Fest, as of 2026-08-02)

After his White Columns directorship, Baer co-founded Neutral Records with Glenn Branca (~1982), releasing 14 records total. One source describes the label as “document[ing] early performances by monologuist Eric Bogosian” alongside early Sonic Youth, Swans, and other downtown figures. (Leslie Rankow Fine Arts blog, 2013-06-18)

Bogosian performing at White Columns: Multiple secondary biography sources (Film Reference, search engine synthesis from same) state that “The Ricky Paul Show” was produced at White Columns, New York City, in 1979 — placing Bogosian at the space during the first year of Baer’s directorship. The Film Reference biography page (filmreference.com) is the attributed source for this claim; the page was inaccessible for direct verification during this research pass (socket hang up). The claim is consistent with Bogosian’s known 1979 activity (he performed “Slavery” at Hallwalls, Buffalo, April 1979) and with White Columns’ role as a performance venue in that period.

LP release party at White Columns: A 4×6″ announcement card (catalogued by J.N. Herlin, Inc.) confirms a party celebrating the release of the LP “Eric Bogosian Live in London” was held at White Columns Gallery, July 3, 1983 — the same day also marking the upcoming premiere of his solo “Fun House” at Joseph Papp’s New York Shakespeare Festival. (J.N. Herlin ephemera listing) This is a primary document and confirms White Columns was used to launch the Neutral Records LP.

The relationship in summary: Bogosian’s connection to White Columns operates on two levels: (1) he apparently performed there during Baer’s directorship (1979, “The Ricky Paul Show” — per Film Reference, not directly verified); and (2) the LP release party was definitively held there in 1983. Baer’s co-founding of Neutral Records with Branca after his White Columns tenure is the structural link that put Bogosian’s performances on record.

White Columns is listed on Wikipedia as having had Josh Baer among its directors, but Bogosian is not mentioned in the White Columns Wikipedia article. (Wikipedia: White Columns, as of 2026-08-02)

N-10 Production Credits

The LP was recorded live at the Institute of Contemporary Art, London, October 29, 1982, as part of an ICA/NY performance season directed by Michael Morris. The sleeve credits two producers with distinct roles:

  • Producer [London] — Chris Lycett: BBC Radio 1 staff producer and engineer; John Peel’s long-running producer (1966–2000) and head of BBC Radio 1 live music. His credit indicates hands-on recording/production of the ICA live capture, not a label or business role. (British Entertainment History Project interview)
  • Producer [Post-production] — Leroy Sound: handled the post-production/mixing phase.

Josh Baer’s role on N-10: Baer is not credited on the sleeve in any production capacity. No source found credits him as producer, engineer, or in any creative role on N-10 or on any other Neutral Records release where production credits are documented (e.g., the Sonic Youth EP and Confusion Is Sex credit only the band and engineers — Baer absent). His role at Neutral Records was as co-founder and label operator alongside Glenn Branca, not as a hands-on producer. The Bogosian LP’s London production was handled entirely by Chris Lycett; post-production by Leroy Sound.
