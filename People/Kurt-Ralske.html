Overview

New York City-based video and performance artist, musician, producer, songwriter, and engineer, Kurt Ralske has been active since 1986 primarily in indie rock, shoegaze, and experimental contexts. He is most prominently associated with Ultra Vivid Scene, which accounts for the bulk of his 373 Discogs credits, and has also been a member of 242.pilots, Crash, Dissipated Face, Nothing But Happiness, and Todd Capp’s Mystery Train. His production and engineering work spans a broad range of labels — 4AD and Rough Trade chief among them — alongside repeated collaborations with Byron Guthrie, Guy Fixsen, and Hugh Jones. He also owns the Zabriskie Point label, based in New York City.

Notes (web) — Los Planetas connection

How the relationship formed

No accessible source documents the precise moment of first introduction between Ralske and Los Planetas. The circumstantial evidence points toward a shared orbit around Mercury Rev: Los Planetas drew heavy influence from early Mercury Rev, and Ralske was close friends with Mercury Rev vocalist/co-founder David Baker (singer). On the Pop album’s song “db” (named after Baker), Ralske tried — unsuccessfully — to get Baker to contribute vocals, attempting to reach him by phone; Baker was unavailable because he was producing another band at the time. (Source: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_(álbum_de_Los_Planetas), similarrock.com)

The Spanish music press noted Ralske as “líder de la banda neoyorquina Ultra Vivid Scene” when the Pop collaboration was announced, suggesting Los Planetas sought him out at least partly on the strength of his UVS reputation in the indie-rock world they admired. (Source: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Planetas_(banda))

Albums produced and timeframe

1. Pop (1996) Recorded October–November 1995 at Red Led studio, Madrid. Ralske traveled to Spain for these sessions. Released 29 January 1996. This was the working relationship’s first outing. Ralske’s Spain visit around this time also prompted a profile/interview in Spanish music magazine Factory (issue 9, Jan–Mar 1996), conducted by Guillermo Z. de Águila. (Source: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_(álbum_de_Los_Planetas), cuandoeramosalternativos.blogspot.com)

2. Una semana en el motor de un autobús (1998) Recorded winter 1997–98 (predominantly January 1998) at Ralske’s own Zabriskie Point studio, New York. The band traveled to NYC for these sessions. Ralske’s equipment — vintage compressors, a vintage mixing desk, unusual instruments — was part of the appeal; the studio building also contained rehearsal rooms and instrument shops which the band used during their stay, per guitarist Florent’s account to Rolling Stone. Released 13 April 1998.

Ralske was not the first-choice producer for this album: Brad Wood was initially approached but became unavailable due to scheduling conflicts with The Smashing Pumpkins’ Adore sessions, leading the band to return to Ralske. (Source: es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Planetas_(banda), notedetengas.es)

Una semana was named Rockdelux’s album of the year for 1998 and ranked among the greatest Spanish-language albums of the 20th century; it is widely regarded as the album that broke Los Planetas to a mainstream audience. (Source: europopmusic.eu)

Span of the working relationship

Documented credits span 1995–1998 across the two albums. No further Ralske production work for Los Planetas is documented after Una semana. Discogs shows 22 shared releases between Ralske and Los Planetas, likely accounting for original pressings, reissues, and singles from both albums through later years (including reissues on Octubre 2013–2015 and BMG España / Sony Music noted in Ralske’s label credits).

What could not be confirmed

  • The precise mechanism of first introduction (who introduced them, or whether Los Planetas cold-approached Ralske) is not stated in any accessible Spanish or English source. The Mercury Rev / David Baker shared orbit is documented but the causal link is circumstantial.
  • The Factory magazine interview text (Jan–Mar 1996) is only available as scanned images in the blog post above; Ralske’s own words about how the collaboration began are therefore inaccessible.
