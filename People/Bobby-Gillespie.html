Bobby Gillespie

Discogs ID: 8979 Real name: Robert Bernard Andrew Gillespie Born: 22 June 1962, Springburn, Glasgow, Scotland (family moved to Mt. Florida, Glasgow, 1973) Active: 1981–present Roles: vocalist, lyricist, drummer (early career), producer, bass guitar (earliest work)

Biography

Glasgow origins and early bands (1981–1984)

Gillespie grew up in Springburn, a working-class district of Glasgow, moving with his family to the southside suburb of Mt. Florida in 1973. His first instrument was bass guitar; he later shifted to voice as his primary instrument, though he retained the ability to play drums, guitar, and bass throughout his career.

In 1982 he joined The Wake as bassist — a post-punk band from Glasgow on Factory Records. He left in August 1983 after one year with the group. His earliest Discogs credits are as sleeve designer on two 1981 releases on Autonomy Records: The Ha Ha Hee Hee! E.P. and Participate! — indicating his proximity to the Glasgow DIY scene even before formal band membership.

Also in 1982, Gillespie co-founded what would become Primal Scream with guitarist Jim Beattie, initially operating under the name “The Primes (2)” and experimenting with a loose “elemental noise” format before settling into guitar-band form by 1984.

The Jesus and Mary Chain: drums (1984–1985)

In 1984 Gillespie joined The Jesus And Mary Chain as drummer — the East Kilbride band fronted by Jim Reid and William Reid that was about to sign with Blanco Y Negro. His drumming appears on three landmark early JAMC singles: Never Understand (1985), You Trip Me Up (1985), and Just Like Honey (1985), all on Blanco Y Negro. He also drums throughout Psychocandy (1985, Blanco Y Negro), the band’s debut album — now widely considered one of the defining UK albums of the decade, recorded with John Loder engineering and the Reid brothers producing. The lineup for Psychocandy also included bassist Douglas Hart and backing vocalists Karen Parker and Laurence Verfaillie.

Gillespie’s drumming style on Psychocandy was notably primitive and propulsive, matching the album’s wall-of-noise approach. He left the band to concentrate on Primal Scream — his departure was amicable and the connection between the two bands persisted through the Creation Records scene for years afterward. A further French pressing of You Trip Me Up (1986, Keri) documents his credit. His JAMC drum credits were compiled on The Power Of Negative Thinking: B-Sides & Rarities (2008, Rhino/Blanco Y Negro) and the Strange Fruit Peel Sessions collections.

Primal Scream: indie origins (1985–1989)

While still drumming for JAMC, Gillespie was writing and recording for Primal Scream alongside Beattie and Robert Young. The debut Primal Scream single, All Fall Down (1985, Creation Records), credited Gillespie as vocalist and co-writer — the first Creation release. Further early singles followed: Crystal Crescent (1986), Gentle Tuesday and Imperial (both 1987, on Elevation/WEA).

The debut album, Sonic Flower Groove (1987, Elevation/WEA), established the band’s early jangly, Byrds-influenced sound. Gillespie is credited as vocalist and co-writer (alongside Beattie); production on most tracks was by Mayo Thompson (of Red Krayola), with Clive Langer and Colin Fairley producing one track. The lineup at this stage was Gillespie (vocals), Andrew Innes (rhythm guitar), Jim Navajo (12-string lead guitar), Robert Young (bass), with Dave Morgan (drums) and Martin Duffy (piano) as helpers.

Ivy Ivy Ivy (1989, Creation) and the self-titled second album Primal Scream (1989, Creation) saw the band deepen their Creation Records identity, with Gillespie now fully focused on fronting and writing.

Screamadelica and the rave crossover (1990–1992)

The pivotal moment in Gillespie’s career came with the commission of Andrew Weatherall to remix I’m Losing More Than I’ll Ever Have into Loaded (1990, Creation) — a transformation of a country-soul B-side into a landmark acid-house record. The encounter with Weatherall opened an entirely new creative direction.

Weatherall produced most of Screamadelica (1991, Sire/Warner; Creation/UK — Discogs release ID 6295), handling tracks 3, 5–7, and 9–11, with Hugo Nicolson as assistant producer. Gillespie and the core writing team of Innes and Young wrote tracks 1 and 3–11; track 2 (“Slip Inside This House”) was a cover of 13th Floor Elevators (written by Roky Erickson and Tommy Hall). Denise Johnson sang lead on Don’t Fight It, Feel It and contributed vocals across the album. The album synthesised gospel, soul, acid house, and psychedelic rock into a sound that won the inaugural Mercury Music Prize (1992) and has remained a landmark of the era.

Singles from Screamadelica included Movin’ On Up, Higher Than The Sun, and Don’t Fight It, Feel It (all 1991). The Dixie-Narco EP (1992, Creation) extended the campaign.

Memphis: Give Out But Don’t Give Up (1993–1994)

The follow-up was recorded in Memphis with legendary soul/R&B producer Tom Dowd — a sharp pivot from rave electronics to American roots rock, funk, and soul. Give Out But Don’t Give Up (1994, Creation — Discogs release ID 339112) was engineered by Jeff Powell and credits Gillespie/Innes/Young as writers across tracks 1–11 (with a George Clinton co-write on track 10). Photography was by William Eggleston. Denise Johnson again contributed vocals.

Singles included Rocks, Jailbird, and (I’m Gonna) Cry Myself Blind (all 1994, Creation). The album’s rawer sound divided listeners but established another mode for the band.

Vanishing Point and the dub/krautrock period (1997)

Vanishing Point (1997, Creation/Reprise — Discogs release ID 12568) marked a return to experimental electronic territory, this time influenced by dub, krautrock, and soundtrack music. Produced by Gillespie, Innes, Young, Duffy, and Brendan Lynch, engineered by Martin “Max” Heyes, with design by Julian House. The associated Echo Dek (1997, Creation) was a full dub remix album of Vanishing Point material. Singles: Burning Wheel, Kowalski, Star, If They Move Kill ‘Em.

By this point the band’s expanded lineup included Gary Mounfield (bass) and Darrin Mooney (drums), who joined after the Memphis period.

XTRMNTR and the political turn (2000)

Exterminator (XTRMNTR) (2000, Epic/Creation — Discogs release ID 12567) was the band’s most abrasive and politically explicit statement — a noise-rock/electronic album co-produced by Brendan Lynch, recorded by the expanded lineup (Gillespie, Innes, Young, Duffy, Mooney, Mounfield, Jim Hunt, Duncan McKay) with guest musician contributions from Bernard Sumner, Kevin Shields, Phil Mossman, Marco Nelson, Gay-yee Westerhoff, Greg Knowles, and Zach Danziger. Kill All Hippies was the lead single.

Evil Heat and the Kevin Shields collaboration (2002)

Evil Heat (2002, Columbia/Epic — Discogs release ID 75836) was produced substantially by Kevin Shields (tracks 1–01, 1–04 to 1–07, 1–09) and Two Lone Swordsmen (Weatherall/Tenniswood, tracks 1–03, 1–08, 1–10, 1–11), with Brendan Lynch and Jagz Kooner also engineering. Design by Julian House. The core lineup was Gillespie, Innes, Young, Duffy, Mooney, Mounfield. Miss Lucifer and Autobahn 66 were singles.

Riot City Blues and later albums (2006–2016)

Riot City Blues (2006, Columbia — Discogs release ID 709401) was co-produced by Youth, with Tim Bran programming, Dave Sardy mixing, and Stephen Marcussen mastering. Lineup: Gillespie (vocals), Innes (guitar, mandolin, banjo, Moog), Young (guitar, harmonica), Mounfield (bass), Mooney (drums), Duffy (piano, organ, harmonium). Singles: Country Girl, Dolls.

More Light (2013, First International — Discogs release ID 4566032) was produced by David Holmes, engineered by Holmes, Innes, Brendan Lynch, and others. Gillespie is credited with lead vocals, backing vocals, mellotron, handclaps, tambourine, electric piano, drums, and percussion — an unusually broad instrument list for him. Mixed by Rich Costey; artwork by Jim Lambie.

Chaosmosis (2016, First International — Discogs release ID 8256120) was co-produced by Gillespie, Innes, and Björn Yttling (of Peter Bjorn and John), who also co-wrote and produced several tracks. Gillespie also handled design.

Utopian Ashes: collaborative solo debut (2021)

Utopian Ashes (2021, Silvertone — Discogs release ID 19351063) is Gillespie’s first record under his own name as a primary artist (outside compilation curations and guest credits). A collaborative album with Jehnny Beth (Camille Berthomier, formerly of Savages), co-written with Beth, Andrew Innes, and Johnny Hostile (Nicolas Congé). Mastered by John Davis (4). The record was a chamber-pop/indie song cycle strikingly different in register from anything in the Primal Scream catalog.

Come Ahead (2024)

Come Ahead (2024, BMG — Discogs release ID 32230896) is the most recent Primal Scream studio album, engineered by Michael Harris (10).

Curatorial and compilation work

Gillespie served as curator and sleeve-note writer on several important reissue projects:

  • Sunday Mornin’ Comin’ Down (2015, Ace — Discogs release ID 7893811): a soul/gospel compilation for which he supplied artwork, design, sleeve notes, and curation. Co-produced with Ace’s Dean Rudland and Nick Robbins.
  • I Still Can’t Believe You’re Gone (2023, Ace — Discogs release ID 28987933): presenter/compiler of a further Ace compilation.

He also wrote liner notes for the Russian issue of Can’s Tago Mago (1999) and for the Brand Neu! compilation (2009, Feraltone).
