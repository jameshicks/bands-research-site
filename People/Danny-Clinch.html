Danny Clinch

American music photographer and film director, born 1964 in Toms River, New Jersey. Discogs artist ID: 329877. Active 1991–present. One of the most prolific rock and hip-hop photographers of the 1990s–2020s, with photography credits on over 4,600 releases across nearly every major label. Also known for his harmonica playing on select recordings and his work as a music video / DVD film director.

Overview

Clinch began his career in the early 1990s in New York, shooting hip-hop artists on the indie and major-label scene before establishing himself as the go-to photographer for rock and alternative acts on Columbia, RCA, Roswell, and Elektra. His client list reads as a near-complete roster of 1990s–2010s rock royalty: Bruce Springsteen, Foo Fighters, Pearl Jam, Metallica, Alice In Chains, Rage Against The Machine, and Audioslave are all major repeat clients. On the hip-hop side he shot early work for Nas, Redman, Public Enemy, and Fugees.

He has maintained a particularly close relationship with Phish and their label Jemp Records, contributing photography to over 60 Phish releases across the Elektra era and the Jemp back-catalog reissue program. He also appears occasionally as a harmonica player on Foo Fighters studio sessions (In Your Honor, 2005) and on releases by Nicole Atkins, among others.

Beyond still photography, Clinch has directed music video content and live concert films, including directing the DVD component of Bruce Springsteen’s Devils & Dust (2005) and the concert film Immagine In Cornice (2007) for Pearl Jam.

Career Narrative

1991–1994: New York Hip-Hop and Metal

Clinch’s earliest Discogs-documented credits are from 1991, shooting the band photos for Corrosion of Conformity’s Blind (Relativity) and Public Enemy’s Shut Em Down single (Def Jam). Over the next few years he worked extensively in New York’s hip-hop scene — Lords of the Underground (Here Come The Lords, Pendulum/Elektra, 1993), Nas (Illmatic, Columbia, 1994), Redman (Dare Iz A Darkside, Def Jam/Rush, 1994), Buckshot LeFonque (Buckshot LeFonque, Columbia, 1994), and Fugees (Blunted On Reality, Ruffhouse/Columbia, 1994). The Def Jam and Columbia connections from this period became foundational to his later career.

His rock work in this period includes Ween (Chocolate And Cheese, Elektra, 1994), and early sessions for Blind Melon (Soup, 1995).

1995–2000: Alternative Rock Ascent

The mid-to-late 1990s were defined by alternative rock clients. His relationship with Phish began with A Live One (Elektra, 1995) and continued through Billy Breathes (1996), Stash (1996), and Farmhouse (2000). Live (The Beauty Of Gray single, 1992; then extensively) and Blind Melon (all photography on Soup and Nico, 1995–96) were also major clients. Johnny Cash’s American III: Solitary Man (2000) and Neil Young’s Road Rock (2000) marked expansion into roots and country.

2001–2010: Rock Superstardom

This decade established Clinch as one of the most in-demand photographers in rock. His biggest ongoing clients in this period:

  • Bruce Springsteen: The Rising (Columbia, 2002) — his most widely replicated credit; also Devils & Dust DVD director (2005), We Shall Overcome – The Seeger Sessions (2006), and continuing into the 2020s
  • Foo Fighters: There Is Nothing Left To Lose (Roswell/RCA, 1999), Everywhere But Home DVD/photography (RCA, 2003) — also plays harmonica on the DVD — and In Your Honor (RCA, 2005, harmonica)
  • Bob Dylan: contributing photographer on “Love And Theft” (Columbia, 2001), and continuing through the Bootleg Series
  • Pearl Jam: additional photography on Riot Act (2002); directed Immagine In Cornice concert film (2007)
  • Audioslave, Rage Against The Machine, Alice In Chains, Metallica — repeat credits across this decade

2011–present: Sustained Dominance

Clinch’s output has not slowed. He continued to photograph Bruce Springsteen through multiple reissues and new albums (Tracks II / The Lost Albums, Columbia, 2025). New clients include Chvrches (Every Open Eye, 2015/2022), My Morning Jacket (multiple), John Mayer, and Eminem (Curtain Call 2, 2022). His Phish/Jemp work expanded with the back-catalog reissue program throughout the 2010s–2020s.

He also plays harmonica on Nicole Atkins’ Mondo Amore (New West, 2011) and on Rachel Ana Dobken’s When It Happens To You (2018).
