Overview

Charles Blackwell (20 May 1940 – August 2024) was an English pianist, arranger, composer, conductor, music director, and producer who became one of the most prolific studio arrangers in British pop during the 1960s and 1970s. He entered the industry at 19 as an arranger for record producer Joe Meek, quickly building a discography of hit records that included “Johnny Remember Me” by John Leyton and “I’ll Never Fall in Love Again” by Tom Jones. He worked with Burt Bacharach on the film scores for What’s New Pussycat? (1965) and After the Fox (1966), and as a conductor he represented British interests at international events including the San Remo Festival and the Eurovision Song Contest. His closest working relationships were with producer Peter Sullivan (643 shared releases), arranger Johnny Harris (319), engineer Bill Price (312), and songwriter Les Reed (310), with Decca his primary label across six decades of activity.
