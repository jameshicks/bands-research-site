Jean Caffeine (2)

Real name: Jean Leider Aliases / Name variations: Jean Leider, J. Caffeine, Jean Roles: Drummer, vocalist, guitarist, producer Active: 1981 – present (Discogs credits span 1982–2025) Discogs artist ID: 2491625

Drummer and vocalist who emerged from the Manhattan downtown percussion scene as a founding member of Pulsallama (1981–1983), then relocated to Austin, Texas, where she built a career as a solo artist and bandleader through the 1990s and 2000s. Discogs lists an IMDB page and a personal website, placing her also in acting/film work.

Biography

NYC Downtown Scene: Pulsallama (1981–1983)

Jean Caffeine (born Jean Leider) was a member of Pulsallama, the short-lived, legendary all-female bass-and-percussion ensemble that ruled Manhattan nightlife in 1981–1982. She is credited with drums and backing vocals. Pulsallama recorded two singles for Y Records — “Ungawa Pt. II (Way Out Guiana)” / “The Devil Lives in My Husband’s Body” (1982) and “Oui-Oui (A Canadian in Paris)” (1983), both produced by Disc O’Dell. The band, originally a 12-piece that trimmed to 7, was known for theatrical stage antics, awkward time signatures, and volatile performances; in 1982 they opened for The Clash at a 6,000-capacity show. The band dissolved around 1983.

For full Pulsallama context and member roster, see Pulsallama.

Austin Years: Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop (Late 1980s–1990s)

After Pulsallama, Jean Caffeine moved to Austin, Texas, and formed Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop, a rotating ensemble of Austin roots musicians. Their sound was country-rock/folk-rock with blues inflections — a sharp stylistic pivot from her downtown NYC percussion work. She played acoustic and electric guitar, sang lead, and produced her own records.

1989 self-titled demo cassette (Not On Label): A four-track, single-sided cassette featuring a core band of JD Foster (bass, acoustic guitar, producer, backing vocals), Fred KRC (drums), Mick Buck (lead guitar), Ponty Bone (accordion), Bobby Birdsong (lap steel), Wash Hamilton (acoustic guitar, bass), Julie Grady (backing vocals), Matt Dunlap (drums), Scott Blesener (fiddle, violin), Andrew Dyken (lead guitar). Four tracks overlap with the subsequent LP.

1990: Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop (cassette LP, Porch Music (2)): The first proper release, self-produced, featuring 10 tracks. Credited under the band name “Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop” rather than her solo name. The band on this record included a rotating cast of Austin players — Ponty Bone (accordion), Mark Rubin (acoustic bass, backing vocals), Mick Buck (lead guitar, slide guitar, backing vocals), Bobby Birdsong (lap steel), Fred KRC (drums), Julie Grady (backing vocals), Scott Blesener (fiddle, violin), JD Foster (mixed by, acoustic guitar, bass, backing vocals), Lisa Pankratz (percussion), Brent Wilson (6) (co-producer, acoustic guitar), with bongos by “Bongo Don” Stroud.

1992: Hard Work And A Lot Of Hairspray! (cassette, Porch Music (2)): Second All-Nite Truckstop LP. Produced by Gurf Morlix, who was also producing Lucinda Williams at this time and was a central figure in the Austin Americana scene. Band: Mick Buck (guitar, vocals), Dennis Ku (guitars), Seth Tiven (lap steel, bass), Ponty Bone (accordion), Michael Charles (10) (drums), Shawn Devlin (drums), Denny DeGorio (bass), Amy Tiven (violin).

Solo Career (1996–2000s)

1996: Knocked Down 7 Times Got Up 8 (Joe Records (3)): Solo record under her own name. Self-produced with Lars Göransson co-producing tracks 1, 2, 5, 8, 12. Mastered by Göransson and Scot Reynolds. Engineers: Ben Blank, Lars Göransson, Mark Phillips (4). Gurf Morlix contributed mixing on tracks 4, 6, 7. Acoustic guitar and vocals only — a stripped solo direction. No Discogs personnel listing beyond crew.

2000: Idée Fixe (Innerstate Records LLC / Joe Records (3), CD): Her most fully produced record — 10-track CD in an indie rock / alternative rock vein. No additional credits in Discogs beyond the primary artist.

2011: Geckos In The Elevator (Joe Records (3)): Multi-contributor record with an Austin-centric lineup including Thor Harris (drums), Mike Rittenhouse (drums), Rusty Miller (drums, keyboards), Tom Altobelli (bass), Ned Doherty (bass), Mike Trebilcock (guitar), Derek Morris (3) (keyboards), J. J. Wiesler (keyboards).

Blind Willie’s Johnson (Austin, 1990s)

Jean Caffeine was a member of Blind Willie’s Johnson, an Austin-based band described as playing “prime-evil blues and swampy, proto-punk” with slide guitar, mouth harp, and overloaded tones. Band members included Mick Buck (guitar, vocals), Dennis Ku (guitar), Seth Tiven (bass, lap steel), Ron Polito (harp), Jason Vargo (drums), and Tall Tom Patton. The band’s only Discogs-catalogued release is Oops! (1994, Unclean Records).

Late Career (2017–present)

2017: Sadie Saturday Nite (Not On Label, self-released digital): 13-track album including spoken-word pieces (“High School Was A Drag,” “I Needed To Play Those Drums”), suggesting a mix of song and autobiographical narrative. San Francisco references in the tracklist (“Neon Adventure / Mission (District) Statement,” “Diamond Heights,” “Winterland (Talking Blues)”) previously implied she may have been based in San Francisco at that point. However, a 2019 Daily Texan profile describes her as a UT alum and “countercultural Austinite,” and all subsequent recording activity is in Austin — so the SF tracklist geography may reflect earlier life history (she was in the SF punk scene in the 1970s, pre-NYC) rather than a 2017 relocation. [source: https://thedailytexan.com/2019/11/21/jean-caffeine-ut-alum-countercultural-austinite-still-going-strong/]

2025: Live At/On WFMU (Not On Label, CD, with Thyroids): Recorded live on The Evan “Funk” Davies Show at WFMU (Jersey City, NJ) on 14 May 2025. Jean Caffeine performed a live set backed by Dallas punk band Thyroids, who then also played a set of their own. Personnel for the Jean Caffeine set: Jean Caffeine (guitar, vocals, drums), Kenny (guitar), Connie (bass), Mark (drums); engineered by Mario Santana. The show also featured Tuff Sunshine. [source: https://www.wfmu.org/playlists/shows/152068]

2025: Generation Jean (Flak Records, CD/digital, released September 5, 2025): Full-length studio album on Austin-based Flak Records — her most recent LP. 10-track record spanning retro pop, Americana, punk-tinged blues, power pop, and art rock. Recorded primarily at Sounds Outrageous studio (Austin) with Lars Göransson; some tracks at the Wheel Studio with Cris Burns. Credited musicians include Josh Robins (Invincible Czars), Jon Notarthomas, Shannon Rierson (Utley 3), and Zack Humphrey (drums). The album was preceded by a drip campaign of singles including “Love What Is It?”, “I Know You Know I Know” (on Flak Records), “Another Crying Christmas” (November 2024), “Mammogram,” “I Don’t Want to Kill You Anymore,” and “You’re Fine.” [source: https://www.indiemusicdiscovery.com/jean-caffeine-delivers-the-goods-on-new-generation-jean-lp-featuring-youre-fine/ ; https://poprockrecord.com/2025/09/09/generating-jean-caffeine/]

Film / Acting

Jean Caffeine has two separate IMDB entries — one as Jean Leider (nm2630326) and one as Jean Caffeine (nm0128414) — which appear to represent the same person under her real name vs. stage name. Combined, these list the following credits:

  • Population: 1 (1986, dir. René Daalder) — credited as Jean Leider. Punk rock musical / post-apocalyptic fantasy starring Tomata du Plenty (The Screamers) as the sole survivor of nuclear war. Cast also included Beck (early appearance), El Duce, Maila Nurmi (Vampira), Penelope Houston, K.K. Barrett, and Los Lobos members. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population:_1 as of 2026-06-28; https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0091781/]

  • Slacker (1990, dir. Richard Linklater) — credited as Jean Caffeine; played “Roadkill,” an old woman lying in the street after being run over, encountered by Linklater’s own character early in the film. A key Austin, TX indie film that launched Linklater’s career. [source: https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0102943/characters/nm0128414; televisionstats.com]

  • Waking Life (2001, dir. Richard Linklater) — animation department credit (not acting). Linklater’s rotoscoped philosophical fantasy film; Jean Caffeine contributed to the animation production. [source: https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0128414/]

  • Six in Austin: Out of Bounds (2002) — acting credit on IMDB (nm0128414). An Austin-made anthology short film; details are sparse — it appears to be an indie/local project screened at SXSW 2002 or similar. [source: https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0128414/]

The Slacker credit is the most significant: she plays an early cameo in Richard Linklater’s breakthrough film, set and shot entirely in Austin. Her appearance as “Roadkill” is a brief scene. The Population: 1 credit (as Jean Leider) predates her Austin move and places her in the LA underground punk film scene in 1986. The two Linklater connections (Slacker, Waking Life) are consistent with her deep embeddedness in the Austin creative community through the 1990s–2000s.
