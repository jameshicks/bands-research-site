Martha Johnson

Canadian vocalist, keyboardist, and songwriter. Born Martha Helen Johnson, 18 December 1950, Toronto, Canada. Discogs artist ID: 47731. Co-founder and lead singer of Martha And The Muffins and the M+M duo.

Overview

Martha Johnson is the central voice and co-writer of one of Canada’s most distinctive new-wave acts. She co-founded Martha And The Muffins with guitarist Mark Gane in Toronto circa 1977; the pair have been the band’s songwriting nucleus ever since, and from 1983 operated the streamlined duo M + M (Discogs ID: 79790) as a parallel and eventually primary identity.

Her instrument across all recordings is vocals and keyboards. She also plays percussion on many later sessions. On the Dindisc LPs she is the lead vocalist; on the Current/RCA albums she co-produces alongside Gane and Daniel Lanois. In the 1990s she and Gane moved into television and film scoring. The birth of their daughter inspired the 1995 children’s album Songs From The Tree House, which won the 1996 Juno Award for Best Children’s Album. Her 2013 solo album Solo • One (on the band’s own Muffin Music label) was co-written partly with Ron Sexsmith. The band reunited under the Martha And The Muffins name for the 2021 archival set Marthology: In And Outtakes.

She also appears as a member of Northern Lights (6) (Discogs ID: 425454), the Canadian charity supergroup that recorded “Tears Are Not Enough” for the 1985 Africa famine relief effort.

Biography by Era

1977–1979 — Formation and First Single

Martha Johnson and Mark Gane co-founded Martha And The Muffins in Toronto around 1977. The initial lineup also included Martha Ladly (vocals, keyboards, trombone), Andy Haas (saxophone), Carl Finkle (bass), and Tim Gane (2) (drums). The band signed to the British indie Dindisc (a Virgin imprint), and in 1979 released the debut single “Insect Love” (Discogs release 186203). Johnson is credited with Vocals on that pressing.

1980 — Breakthrough: Metro Music and Trance And Dance

The debut album Metro Music (1980, Dindisc DID.1, release 59814) was produced by Mike Howlett and engineered by Richard Manwaring. Johnson wrote tracks A2 (“Paint By Number Heart”), A3 (“Saigon”), A4 (“Indecision”), and B2 (“Monotone”). The album contains “Echo Beach,” written by Gane, which became an international hit (UK #10). Johnson plays Vocals and Keyboards throughout; Martha Ladly plays Vocals, Keyboards, and Trombone.

The second album Trance And Dance (1980, Dindisc DID.5, release 125815), also produced by Howlett, expanded the lineup’s sound. Johnson wrote “Symptomatic Love” (co-written with Andy Haas), “Am I On?”, and the title track “Trance And Dance.”

1981 — This Is The Ice Age

This Is The Ice Age (1981, Dindisc DID 10, release 98076) marked the first full collaboration with Daniel Lanois, who co-produced alongside the band and engineered at Grant Avenue Studio (Hamilton, Ontario) and Nimbus 9 (Toronto). Jocelyne Lanois (Daniel’s sister) joined on bass, backing vocals, and keyboards. Johnson is credited: Vocals, Keyboards, Percussion. The band self-co-produced this LP (credited to Martha And The Muffins, Daniel Lanois).

1983 — Danseparc and the M+M Identity

Danseparc (1983, RCA AFL1-4664, release 125810) was the first release credited jointly to Martha And The Muffins / M+M. Lanois produced with Gane and Johnson as co-producers. The core M+M live unit on the record was Johnson, Gane, Jocelyne Lanois, and Nick Kent (drum machine/percussion). Ron Allen (2) contributes saxophone on several tracks; John Oswald plays alto saxophone on “Boys In The Bushes”; Glenn Schellenberg contributes Casiotone 202. “Danseparc (Every Day It’s Tomorrow)” was the lead single (master 105630).

A companion single, “Black Stations / White Stations” (master 105633), was released in 1983/84 — a pointed commentary on US radio segregation — and became the duo’s most internationally known track.

1984 — Mystery Walk

Mystery Walk (1984, Current WAVE 3, release 41576) was the second M+M o.k.a. Martha And The Muffins album, again produced by Lanois, Gane, and Johnson. Sessions drew on a wide array of New York and Toronto players: The Brecker Brothers on horns for “Black Stations / White Stations,” Yogi Horton and Fred Maher on drums, David Piltch and Tinker Barfield on bass, Rufus Cappadocia on cello, and Sharon Lee Williams / Shawne Jackson on backing vocals. David Bottrill appears as an assistant engineer — an early credit for the future Manic Street Preachers and Tool producer. The record was released on the band’s Canadian label Current (Canadian label).

1986 — Song In My Head and The World Is A Ball

Two M+M albums followed on RCA in 1986: Song In My Head (master 105636) and The World Is A Ball (master 105637). Both were produced by Johnson and Gane. A companion M+M single, Someone Else’s Shoes (master 2553917), was released in Canada on Current the same year. Producer credit on “Song In My Head” is noted as Johnson and Gane; a dance remix was released in Europe.

A different producer, David Lord, handled two tracks on the later compilation Then Again — “Song In My Head” and “By The Waters Of Babylon.”

1985 — Northern Lights

Johnson participated in the Canadian charity supergroup Northern Lights (6) (“Canada For Africa”), recording “Tears Are Not Enough” (1985) alongside Bryan Adams, Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Gordon Lightfoot, Anne Murray, Bruce Cockburn, Geddy Lee, and dozens of other Canadian artists.

1992–1996 — Modern Lullaby and Children’s Music

Modern Lullaby (1992, Intrepid Records/Rebel Rec., master 275346) is credited as a Johnson performer/writer/producer credit. The 1995 children’s album Songs From The Tree House (Muffin Music ARK 1, release 2274167) was produced and performed by both Johnson and Gane; Johnson wrote all but one track (Gane wrote track 2, “Everybody Needs A Home”). Johnson also provided the cover illustrations (drawings). Peter Moore mastered the CD. The album won the 1996 Juno Award for Best Children’s Album.

The children’s album was also preceded by the 1997 compilation All About Our World (Oak Street Music), which featured Johnson alongside Canadian children’s music artists Norman Foote and Fred Penner.

1990s–2000s — TV/Film Scoring and Retrospective

Through the 1990s Johnson and Gane composed music for television and film (specific credits not in Discogs database — see Open Questions). In 1998 the retrospective Then Again: A Retrospective (EMI Music Canada / Muffin Music, release 857180) compiled 18 tracks spanning the full MatM/M+M catalog, with producer credits distributed across Howlett (tracks 1–3), the band (4–6), and Lanois/Johnson/Gane (7–18).

The 2002 tribute/remix compilation Select Cuts From Echo Beach (Germany, Select Cuts label, release 208002) featured various artists reworking MatM/M+M material, with Johnson credited for original vocals.

In 1998, “Black Stations / White Stations” elements were sampled in “The Beginning (No Further Delay)” by a Profile Records artist (release 303808), with Johnson credited as Written-By.

2013 — Solo • One

Solo • One (2013, Muffin Music ARK 6, release 4663684) is Johnson’s first album under her own name. Co-produced with Gane and Ray Dillard; mixed and engineered by Dillard; mastered by Peter Moore. The songwriting is split between Johnson/Gane compositions and three co-writes with Ron Sexsmith (“See Saw Eyes,” “Show Me How,” “I Wouldn’t Change A Day”). “No Man’s Land” also credits Hill Kourkoutis. Johnson wrote the liner notes; cover photography by Eve Gane (Gane’s daughter).

2017–2021 — Reunion and Archival Projects

“Where Blue Meets Green” was reworked into a Balearic edit in 2017 (Echo Beach label, Germany, master 1324771). In 2019, “Do You Ever Wonder?” received a remix by The Confidence Emperors (Canada, master 2375443).

Marthology: In And Outtakes (2021, popguru POP2111, release 21008284) is a 14-track archival compilation of outtakes and alternate versions, credited to Martha And The Muffins, produced and compiled by Johnson and Gane, with Peter Moore handling restoration and remastering.

Open Questions

  • Specific TV and film scoring credits from the 1990s are not in the Discogs database. The brief references this work but no individual production titles are documented there.
  • The 1979 debut single “Insect Love” release number 186203 appears with Dindisc label but no catalog number in the data — worth confirming.
  • Modern Lullaby (1992, Intrepid/Rebel Rec., master 275346) appears to be a German release — unclear whether this is a separate children’s project or a European edition of Songs From The Tree House material. The Juno Award was given in 1996 for Songs From The Tree House (released 1995 in Canada).
  • The 1931 release “Going Away Blues / Rolling Log Blues” (Superior label, US) appears in Johnson’s Discogs discography attributed to a 1931 release — this is almost certainly a data error/coincidence (blues 78 from 1931 cannot be the same person). Discogs ID 30975784.
  • Someone Else’s Shoes (1986, Current, master 2553917) appears as an M+M single — release details are sparse in the database.
  • No information on M+M albums Song In My Head and The World Is A Ball track-level credits available from this Discogs pull; full personnel not documented there.
