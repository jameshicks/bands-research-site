Melton Mustafa

American jazz trumpeter, flugelhorn player, arranger, educator, and bandleader. Born November 23, 1947, Miami, Florida. Died December 28, 2017, Miami, Florida. Discogs artist ID: 577645.

Name variations on record: Melton Jones, Melton Mustava, Melton S. Mustafa, Melton S. Mustaga, Melton Shakir Mustafa, Milton Mustapha.

Brother of saxophonist Jesse Jones, Jr. (Discogs ID: 1208839).

Career Overview

Mustafa was a Miami-based jazz trumpeter and educator who operated across two distinct but overlapping worlds: the national big-band circuit and a South Florida regional scene that he organized into his own ensemble, the Melton Mustafa Orchestra.

His earliest traceable session work dates to 1980, when he appeared on The People’s Champ Featuring Muhammad Ali (Amour Records) playing trumpet. Through the early 1980s he turned up on R&B and pop-adjacent sessions — horn arrangements for a funk date (Sittin’ On It, 1981, Tempus) and the Boneroo Horns horn section on Anne Murray’s How Old Are You? (1983, Polydor).

The most consequential thread in his career was his integration into the Count Basie Orchestra during the Frank Foster directorship. Mustafa appears in the trumpet section on Long Live The Chief (1986, Denon), Diane Schuur And The Count Basie Orchestra (1987, GRP), Live At El Morocco (1992, Telarc Jazz), and the live Telarc recordings Live At Orchestra Hall, Detroit (1993) and Basie’s Bag (1994). Alongside him in the trumpet section across these dates: Byron Stripling, Sonny Cohn, and Bob Ojeda. The Schuur/Basie album was a high-profile release — GRP, Grammy-winning, widely distributed — giving Mustafa his widest national exposure.

In the hard-bop vein, Mustafa worked with Bobby Watson & Horizon, the group led by alto saxophonist Bobby Watson. He appears on the band’s Blue Note albums The Inventor (1990) and Post-Motown Bop (1991, co-led with drummer Victor Lewis). On Post-Motown Bop he is credited as both trumpeter and composer (contributing one original, track 10). The rhythm section across both sessions featured Edward Simon on piano and Carroll V. Dashiell Jr. on bass. Post-Motown Bop was produced by Watson and Matt Pierson.

He also worked with other artists name-checked in his Discogs profile: pianist John Hicks, arranger and composer Mercer Ellington, pianist James Williams (2), and conductor Gunther Schuller. He is listed in The Horizon Quintet ensemble.

From the mid-1990s onward, Mustafa increasingly focused on his own orchestra. The Melton Mustafa Orchestra released Boiling Point (1995, Contemporary Records) — a large ensemble date with Lonnie Smith on organ, Billy Ross on tenor saxophone and flute, and a full brass section. Executive producer was Bob Weinstock (the founder of Prestige Records). The orchestra followed with St. Louis Blues (1997, Contemporary Records), co-billed with Mustafa’s brother Jesse Jones, Jr. on alto saxophone and soprano saxophone. On St. Louis Blues Mustafa composed, arranged, conducted, produced, and played — a comprehensive bandleader credit. Guest soloists included Ira Sullivan (trumpet, alto flute) and Pete Minger (trumpet). His son Melton R. Mustafa appears on baritone saxophone.

His only solo release under his own name in the DB is The Traveling Man (2002, Not On Label) — released independently in Miami.

Mustafa died December 28, 2017 in Miami. He was 70. He had been active as a musician and educator in South Florida for decades.
