Saul Davies

Saul Davies

Oct 02, 20265 min read

Saul Davies

Discogs ID: 273100 Name variations: Davies, Delvies, S. Davies, S. Davis, Saul, Saul Davis, S·Davies Instruments: Violin, guitar (acoustic, electric, baritone), percussion, drums, backing vocals Groups: James, Celtic Cross

Multi-instrumentalist, songwriter, and producer from Manchester. Core member of James since 1989–90, contributing violin as a defining texture alongside Andy Diagram’s trumpet across the band’s Fontana and later catalog. Outside James, collaborated with Simon Posford and Martin Glover in the psychedelic-electronic project Celtic Cross, and has undertaken external production and session work.

Career

Pre-James (1989)

Before joining James, Davies appeared as a session violinist on The Man From Delmonte’s 12” single My Love Is Like A Gift You Can’t Return (1989, Bop Cassettes, BIP 701), a Manchester indie act whose sessions were recorded by Graham Massey (of 808 State) and Tim Oliver, and managed by Jon Ronson. Also in 1989, he played violin on an unreleased or non-primary James session that would be captured on the BBC Transcription Services recording In Concert-495 (1990, master 1263382).

Joining James / Gold Mother era (1990)

Davies joined James in spring 1989 alongside keyboardist Mark Hunter, both recruited in the same period (Hunter had been recommended via Mick Armistead). Davies made his impact felt on Gold Mother (1990, Fontana 846 189-1), the band’s first major-label LP — playing violin, guitar, and cowbell. The album was self-produced by the band (Glennie, Gott, Garside, Booth) and featured Andy Diagram on trumpet. The track “Gold Mother” featured backing vocals from Inspiral Carpets.

Seven and commercial peak (1992)

Seven (1992, Fontana) — which reached #2 in the UK — had Davies on guitar, violin, percussion, and backing vocals. His contribution to the live document Seven - The Live Video (1992, PolyGram Video) was also credited. That same year he played violin and percussion on a session for Sweetest Child (Geffen Records), the dance/electronic project featuring Maria McKee, produced by Youth.

Laid / Wah Wah (1993–1994)

Laid (1993, Fontana) — produced by Brian Eno, who also played additional bass and keyboards — had Davies on violin and guitar. The concurrent ambient twin Wah Wah (1994, Fontana) listed him as band credit. A live document, England 1993 (1994, Rarities & Few), also carries his violin and guitar credit alongside a Written-By credit, indicating active contribution to setlist and arrangements.

Whiplash and production role (1997–1999)

Davies’s profile within James shifted from player to co-producer. On the single Waltzing Along (1997, Fontana) he is credited as “Produced With,” and Millionaires (1999, Mercury) gives him co-producer credit across the album. The promo sampler Millionaires | 4 Track Sampler (1999, Mercury) lists him as engineer, producer, and co-writer. He also co-wrote the single I Know What I’m Here For (1999, Mercury).

Pleased To Meet You and first breakup (2001)

Pleased To Meet You (2001, Mercury) lists Davies as producer (with) and written-by. He co-wrote and co-produced the single Getting Away With It (All Messed Up) (2001, Mercury). The band broke up in 2001; Davies contributed liner notes and mixing credits to the live album Getting Away With It…Live (2002, Sanctuary).

Celtic Cross (1998)

Alongside keyboardist Mark Hunter, Davies collaborated with Simon Posford (Hallucinogen) and Martin Glover (The Orb) in the psychedelic-electronic project Celtic Cross. The group’s sole studio album Hicksville (1998, Dragonfly Records / LSD – Liquid Sound Design, BFLCD 31) blends downtempo, dub, and Goa trance influences; Davies is credited Written-By. The album was produced by Youth and Simon Posford, with sleeve design by Mark Neal (also associated with Posford’s Shpongle albums). A Hicksville Remastered + And Expanded Deluxe Edition appeared in 2009 on LSD.

Production and session work (2002–2007)

During the James hiatus Davies worked as producer and engineer for other artists. He and Mark Hunter produced and played all instruments on the acoustic version of Ainslie Henderson’s single Keep Me A Secret (2003, Mercury, 077981-4), a British pop act. South African releases under EMI (Mosaic, 2005; Staring At The Ceiling, 2008) carry his producer/recorded-by/mixed-by credits. He also took sole production/engineering credit on an untitled Portuguese release for Polydor (In N’ Out, 2003). The Celtic Cross profile identifies his work with Mark Hunter on a project called “Watershed,” details otherwise not confirmed in Discogs.

Reunion and later James albums (2008–present)

James reunited in 2007 and Davies returned to full membership. Hey Ma (2008, Fontana) has him on guitar (acoustic, electric, baritone) and violin; he also plays additional drums on “Waterfall” and “Semaphore.” Subsequent albums continued his multi-instrumental role: La Petite Mort (2014, BMG/Cooking Vinyl — guitar, drums, percussion, violin, backing vocals), Girl At The End Of The World (2016, BMG), Living In Extraordinary Times (2018, Infectious Music), All The Colours Of You (2021, Virgin Music), Be Opened By The Wonderful (40 Years Orchestrated) (2023, Virgin Music), and Yummy (2024, Virgin Music — guitar, liner notes, co-writer).

The Nothing But Love // The Definitive Best Of compilation (2025, EMI/Mercury/Universal) lists him in an exhaustive role spanning five decades of involvement: backing vocals, co-producer, drums, engineer, guitar, liner notes, percussion, tambourine, violin.
