Jody Harris

American guitarist, singer, songwriter, and composer. Born 1951, Kansas. Moved to New York City in 1976. Central figure in the No Wave scene and its aftermath. Discogs artist ID: 228350.

Name variations on records: Harris, J. Harris, Joel Harris.

Biography

Arrival and The Contortions (1976–1979)

Harris arrived in New York in 1976 and embedded himself in the downtown Manhattan scene gathering around CBGB and the nascent No Wave milieu. By 1977 he was playing guitar in James Chance & The Contortions, James Chance’s abrasive funk-punk-free jazz outfit. He was present at the Contortions’ first documented show (4 December 1977) and remained with the band through their run on ZE Records.

Harris appears on No New York (1978, Antilles AN-7067) — the Brian Eno-produced compilation that documented the No Wave scene, with the Contortions occupying Side A. His guitar underpins “Dish It Out,” “Flip Your Face,” “Jaded,” and the James Brown cover “I Can’t Stand Myself.” The band’s slide guitarist on those tracks was Pat Place, who left the Contortions in spring 1978 following a dispute over Paris gig money. Harris remained.

With the Contortions he recorded Buy (1979, ZE Records) and contributed to the parallel James White and the Blacks project (Off White, ZE-Buddah, 1979). Harris also reportedly taught Pat Place the ninth chord that became foundational to her later guitar work.

SPY Records and The Lester Bangs Single (1979)

In 1979 Harris played guitar on the SPY Records single “Let It Blurt” / “Live” by Lester Bangs (SPY 003). The session brought together a notable crew: Jay Dee Daugherty (drums, producer), David Hofstra (bass), Harris and Robert Quine (both on guitar), and John Cale (mixer). It was a one-off encounter at the margins of the No Wave and punk-rock worlds, uniting figures who otherwise moved in adjacent but distinct orbits.

The Raybeats (1979–c.1983)

Harris co-founded The Raybeats in 1979, alongside George Scott (2) (bass), Don Christensen (drums), and Pat Irwin (saxophone, guitar). The Raybeats drew from the same Contortions nucleus but pivoted away from No Wave’s noise toward cleaner melodic surf-influenced instrumental rock. George Scott III’s death from a heroin overdose on 5 August 1980 ended the original lineup; Danny Amis replaced him on bass.

Harris co-wrote tracks 1, 3, 4, 5, and 7 with Christensen and Irwin.

Implog (1980–1982)

Parallel to the Raybeats, Harris contributed to Don Christensen’s solo project Implog (alias impLOG). He appears on the “Holland Tunnel Dive” 12” (1980, Infidelity (2) / Lust-Unlust Music, JMB-231), playing guitar on the B-side track “On B’Way.” This was one of his rare appearances outside his core bands.

The Harris / Quine Collaboration: Escape (1981)

Harris and Robert Quine released a collaborative LP together: “Escape” (1981, Infidelity (2), Discogs ID 329050). Both are credited as primary artists. They each composed and played guitar, bass, and percussion [electronic], and co-produced. Howie Weinberg (HW) mastered. Photography: Marcia Resnick. Tracklist: A1 “Flagpole Jitters” (12:30), A2 “Don’t Throw That Knife” (7:48), B1 “Up In Daisy’s Penthouse” (5:44), B2 “Termites Of 1938” (9:06), B3 “Pardon My Clutch” (11:02) — five extended improvisational guitar duets totaling approximately 46 minutes. Genre: Experimental / New Wave / No Wave. This is a rare instance of Harris as a named primary artist rather than a band member. The two had already shared a session on the Lester Bangs single and moved in overlapping circles — Quine was simultaneously the guitarist in Richard Hell & The Voidoids.

The Golden Palominos (1985–1989)

From 1985 onward Harris was a core guitarist in The Golden Palominos, the shifting collective assembled by drummer Anton Fier for Celluloid Records. He appears on three albums in the Celluloid era:

Visions Of Excess (1985) — Harris plays guitar on tracks A1, A2, A4, B1–B4. The album is a guest-vocalist showcase: Michael Stipe (A1–A3), John Lydon (A4), Jack Bruce (B1), Syd Straw (B2–B3), Arto Lindsay (B4). Harris co-wrote five tracks with Fier and Syd Straw:

Animal Speaks (1986) — French single/release (Celluloid). Harris plays guitar alongside Fier (drums), Laswell (bass), and Worrell (organ).

Blast Of Silence (1986) — Harris plays guitar throughout. The album features Peter Blegvad as the dominant voice-and-guitar presence across six tracks, with Syd Straw handling most lead vocals. Other personnel include T-Bone Burnett, Matthew Sweet, Don Dixon, Carla Bley.

After the Celluloid run, Fier restructured The Golden Palominos around different personnel for the 1989 A Dead Horse (though the Discogs credits list Harris on the compilation Thundering Herd in 1990). Harris does not appear on the post-1989 Restless Records albums.

Other Session Work (1983–1990)

Desire Develops An Edge (1983, American Clavé) — Harris plays electric guitar on tracks A3, C3, D3 on this release (Discogs ID 557684). Primary artist is Kip Hanrahan — a Latin/avant-jazz producer/composer central to the downtown NYC scene. The album features Arto Lindsay, John Scofield, Jack Bruce, Steve Swallow, Anton Fier, John Zorn, and a large Latin percussion ensemble.

The Big Gundown (1986, Nonesuch/Elektra) — John Zorn assembled an enormous cast of downtown musicians for this Ennio Morricone interpretations project. Harris appears in the “featuring” list alongside Robert Quine, Anton Fier, Arto Lindsay, Bill Frisell, Fred Frith, Diamanda Galás, Vernon Reid, Wayne Horvitz, Bobby Previte, and others.

Happy Come Home (1987, Geffen) — Victoria Williams debut album, produced by Anton Fier. Harris is credited as “Musician” (no specific instrument listed). The session was effectively a Golden Palominos reunion: Fier, Laswell, Worrell, Peter Blegvad, T-Bone Burnett, Syd Straw (background vocals), and others from the same network.

The Trouble Tree (1990, Bar/None Records) — Harris plays guitar on track 5 (“Fun Ride”) only (Discogs ID 1382868). Primary artist is Freedy Johnston — a Kansas-born singer-songwriter based in New York; debut album, produced by Chris Butler (The Waitresses). A peripheral downtown-adjacent session rather than a core scene credit.

Later Credits (1998–2024)

Music For Films (1998, Tzadik, Discogs ID 1571310) — Harris plays electric guitar on tracks 1–9 of this Phillip Johnston album. A large ensemble cast (David Hofstra on bass, Dave Douglas on trumpet, Mark Feldman on violin, Erik Friedlander on cello, John Zorn as executive producer). This is the only confirmed new recording session Harris appears on in the 1990–2009 window; all other appearances in those years are reissues.

American Saturday Night (2009, Arista Nashville) — Harris has a written-by credit. This is likely a song he co-wrote that was recorded by another artist.

I Really Want To Bomb You: 1972–1984 (2022, Modern Harmonic, Discogs ID 24513233) — A retrospective compilation of Jill Kroesen’s work. Harris plays guitar on track 1-3, alongside Bill Laswell (bass), Don Christensen (drums), Larry Saltzman (guitar), Peter Gordon (organ/sax), and Arthur Russell (cello). This effectively documents the downtown NYC avant-garde scene’s personnel circa 1979–1984.

Duka 1 1979–1988 (2024, Modern Harmonic, Discogs ID 32669139) — Primary artists are Julia Heyward and T-Venus. Julia Heyward is a New York performance/video artist active 1979–1988; this is an archival document of her work from that period. Harris plays guitar and has written-by credits on tracks 4 and 6, alongside Don Christensen (drums/electronics) and Pat Irwin (guitar/sax/keys) — effectively the Raybeats circle in a different context.

Confirmed: Harris played electric guitar on tracks 1–9 of Music For Films (1998, Tzadik, Discogs ID 1571310), a Phillip Johnston album. This is a genuine new recording session in the gap window, not a reissue. All other appearances under his name in the 1990–2009 window are reissues or repressings of earlier material (Blast Of Silence Japan 1992, Desire Develops An Edge 1994, Off White 1995, Visions Of Excess 1995, Guitar Beat 1997, No New York Japan 1997, The Big Gundown Russia 1999, Buy France 2004).
