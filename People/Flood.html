Flood

Full name: Mark Ellis Role: Engineer, producer Born: 1960, London

Overview

British engineer and producer. Major figure in 1980s–90s British alternative and electronic production. Associated with Cabaret Voltaire’s mid-period records and U2, Depeche Mode, Nine Inch Nails, Nick Cave, PJ Harvey, Foals.

Early Credit: Martin Hannett Session

Flood’s earliest Discogs credit is as assistant engineer on New Order’s Movement (FACT 50, 1981), produced by Martin Hannett at The Townhouse, London. Also assistant engineer alongside John Leckie on that session.

Goldfrapp

Co-produced Goldfrapp’s Seventh Tree (Mute, 2008): co-producer tracks 3, 5–7, 9; additional production tracks 1, 2, 4, 8, 10; also played guitar (tr. 7) and keyboards/keys (tr. 3, 7, 9); mixed track 7 and mixed additional stems on track 9. Flood’s involvement was extensive across the whole album — not limited to specific tracks — distinguishing his role from typical co-production cameos. Tony Hoffer mixed the remaining tracks (1–6, 9, 10); Stephen Marcussen mastered the album.

Depeche Mode

Produced Violator (1990, Mute) and Songs Of Faith And Devotion (1993, Mute) — the band’s commercial and critical peak. Violator was mixed by François Kevorkian (most tracks). On Songs Of Faith And Devotion, Flood co-produced and co-mixed with the band; Mark Stent (Spike) handled additional mixing. Returned as mixing producer on Delta Machine (2013, Columbia), mixed alongside Rob Kirwan.

Interpol

Produced Interpol’s The Other Side Of Make-Believe (2022, Matador), recorded in London. Also appeared as guest vocalist on track “A” alongside Richie Kennedy. Alan Moulder contributed additional production — Flood and Moulder are longtime studio collaborators across the Depeche Mode/NIN orbit. 9 shared releases with Interpol in Discogs.

Sigur Rós

Produced and mixed Sigur Rós’s Með Suð Í Eyrum Við Spilum Endalaust (EMI / Krúnk, 2008). Sessions took place at Assault and Battery (London), Sear Sound (NYC), Egrem Studio (Havana), and Sundlaugin (Iceland). 23+ shared Discogs entries with Sigur Rós. Flood’s approach brought a more open, structured production style compared to the Ken Thomas-era albums.
