Lee Harris

Drummer. Born Lee David Harris, 20 July 1962, Essex, England. Founding member of Talk Talk (1981–1991) and .O.Rang. Discogs ID: 60624.

One of the most influential drummers in the transition from synth-pop to post-rock and ambient music. His playing on Talk Talk’s final two albums — Spirit of Eden (1988) and Laughing Stock (1991) — is definitive: sparse, intuitive, deeply dynamic, often more about what he doesn’t play than what he does.

Biography

Grew up in Essex. Pre-Talk Talk: member of Eskalator, a Southend reggae band alongside future Talk Talk bassist Paul Webb. Joined Talk Talk in 1981 alongside vocalist Mark Hollis, Webb, and keyboardist Simon Brenner.

Talk Talk (1981–1991)

Talk Talk began as a synth-pop act on EMI, scoring UK hits with “Talk Talk,” “It’s My Life,” and “Such a Shame.” From The Colour of Spring (1986) onwards the band shifted toward experimental territory, driven by Hollis’s increasing interest in space, silence, and improvisation — with Harris and Webb as the rhythm section holding it together. Producer Tim Friese-Greene became the de facto fourth member from the second album onward.

Spirit of Eden (1988) and Laughing Stock (1991) are the canonical endpoints: large ensemble recordings built from improvised sessions, heavily edited in post-production. Both are foundational references for post-rock and ambient music. The band disbanded in 1991; Mark Hollis died in 2019.

.O.Rang (1990s)

After Talk Talk, Harris and Webb formed .O.Rang — originally called Shwaa, never released under that name. Two albums of abstract, percussion-forward electronic music.

Bark Psychosis / Graham Sutton connection

Harris appears on Bark Psychosis’s Codename: Dustsucker (Fire Records, FIRECD90, 2004) — drums and percussion across the album, co-writing “Miss Abuse” (track 3) and “INQB8TR” (track 7) with Graham Sutton. His Discogs ID (60624) also appears as assistant engineer on Hex (1994), suggesting a working relationship with Sutton reaching back to the original Bark Psychosis era.

Recent work

Collaborated with Beth Gibbons (Portishead) on her 2024 solo album Lives Outgrown (Domino).
