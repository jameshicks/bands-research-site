John Williams (4)

American film and television composer, conductor, and pianist. Born John Towner Williams, February 8, 1932, Floral Park, Long Island, NY. Discogs artist ID: 273394.

One of the most decorated film composers in history, with five Academy Awards (from 53 nominations as of 2023 — second only to Walt Disney’s 59), four Golden Globe Awards, seven BAFTA Awards, and 21 Grammy Awards. A career spanning six decades encompasses some of the most recognizable orchestral scores in cinema. Inducted into the Hollywood Bowl Hall of Fame in 2000; Kennedy Center Honors recipient 2004; Richard Kirk Award at 1999 BMI Film and TV Awards.

Father of Joseph Williams (singer/songwriter) and Mark T. Williams (musician). His musical style is broadly Neo-Romantic with heavy use of leitmotifs; he has also worked in Impressionist, Expressionist, and progressive jazz idioms (his father was a jazz drummer and Williams began as a jazz pianist, working with Red Norvo).

(Discogs uses the “(4)” disambiguation suffix to distinguish from other artists named John Williams.)

Biography by Era

Early Career: Jazz Pianist and TV Composer (1950s–1960s)

Williams began as a session pianist and jazz musician in New York, often working with the Red Norvo Quartet (he appears under the alias “Johnny Williams” and “Little Johnny Love Williams” in this period). He relocated to Hollywood and entered the studio music world as an arranger and bandleader, notably working with singer Frankie Laine. He joined the TV world in the early 1960s, composing themes for series including Lost in Space and Land of the Giants (both released on GNP Crescendo in the 1990s).

Early film credits include Diamond Head (Colpix, 1963), How to Steal a Million (20th Century Fox Records, 1966), Valley of the Dolls (20th Century Fox Records, 1967), and Goodbye, Mr. Chips (MGM Records, 1969). His first major orchestral showcase was Images (1972) for Robert Altman, and The Reivers (Columbia Masterworks, 1969) was an early prestige credit.

Breakthrough: 1970s Blockbusters

The Fiddler on the Roof soundtrack (United Artists Records, 1971, 108 pressings across editions) gave Williams his first wide album reach. The true turning point came with the MCA Records trio: Jaws (1975), Close Encounters of the Third Kind (Arista, 1977), and Star Wars (20th Century Records, 1977). The Star Wars album became one of the best-selling classical/soundtrack LPs ever, credited to Williams and the London Symphony Orchestra (recorded at Anvil Studios, London, with engineer Eric Tomlinson). Producer credit on Star Wars went to George Lucas.

Superman The Movie (Warner Bros. Records, 1978) extended Williams’s streak with another large-scale LSO recording, again engineered by Eric Tomlinson. The Jaws score — recorded in Hollywood with engineer John Neal — is credited as a landmark in using a short two-note motif as a leitmotif for dread.

1980s: Indiana Jones, E.T., Empire of the Sun

Raiders of the Lost Ark (CBS, 1981) and its sequels consolidated Williams’s partnership with Steven Spielberg. The score was recorded with the London Symphony Orchestra, engineered by Eric Tomlinson (recording) and John Neal (remixing), with liner notes by Spielberg himself. Music editor Ken Wannberg began his long association with Williams here.

E.T. The Extra-Terrestrial (MCA Records, 1982) was co-produced by Williams and Bruce Botnick — the start of Botnick’s extensive run as Williams’s album producer (~157 shared releases on Discogs). Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom (Polydor, 1984) and Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade (Warner Bros. Records, 1989) followed, with the latter featuring engineer Dan Wallin and music preparation by JoAnn Kane Music Service.

From 1980 onward Williams served as conductor/music director of the Boston Pops Orchestra, recording numerous pops and classical programs for Philips through the 1980s–90s. These include Pops In Space (1980), Peter and the Wolf (1984), By Request (1987), and the Gershwin and Bernstein tribute albums.

Empire of the Sun (Warner Bros. Records, 1987) was another Spielberg collaboration and reflected Williams’s growing range with impressionist textures alongside his orchestral grandeur.

1990s: Schindler’s List, Jurassic Park, Prequel Trilogy Preparation

Home Alone (CBS, 1990) and Hook (Epic Soundtrax, 1991) maintained Williams’s commercial presence, but the definitive 1990s work was the back-to-back Jurassic Park and Schindler’s List (both MCA Records, 1993), for which he won consecutive Academy Awards. The Schindler’s List album was mixed by Shawn Murphy (scoring mixer) and engineered with assistance from Sandy De Crescent (music contractor) and music editing by Ken Wannberg. The violin soloist on the score was Itzhak Perlman.

Saving Private Ryan (DreamWorks Records, 1998) — another Spielberg film — was followed by Angela’s Ashes (Sony Classical, 1999) and the first Star Wars prequel, The Phantom Menace (Sony Classical, 1999), which introduced the “Duel of the Fates” motif performed with the LSO and New London Children’s Choir.

JFK (Elektra, 1991), Nixon (Hollywood Records, 1995), and Amistad (DreamWorks Records, 1997) demonstrated his work with directors beyond Spielberg.

2000s: Harry Potter, Prequel Sequels, Later Spielberg

Harry Potter and the Philosopher’s Stone (Atlantic/Nonesuch/Warner Sunset, 2001) opened a new franchise. The recording featured the London Symphony Orchestra with London Voices chorus (director Terry Edwards (2)), recorded and mixed by Simon Rhodes, and mastered by Patricia Sullivan Fourstar. Williams composed scores for all three Columbus-directed Harry Potter films.

He continued the Star Wars prequel trilogy through Attack of the Clones (Sony Classical, 2002) and Revenge of the Sith (Sony Classical, 2005). Catch Me If You Can (DreamWorks Records, 2002), Minority Report (DreamWorks Records, 2002), Memoirs of a Geisha (Sony Classical, 2005), Munich (Decca, 2005), and War of the Worlds (Decca, 2005) filled the decade alongside concert works including the Five Sacred Trees (bassoon concerto, Sony Classical, 1997) and Violin Concerto No. 1 (Sony Classical, 2002, performed by Itzhak Perlman and Yo-Yo Ma on their respective featured recordings).

The cellist Yo-Yo Ma recorded a full album of Williams themes, Yo-Yo Ma Plays The Music Of John Williams (Sony Classical, 2002).

2010s–Present: Star Wars Sequel Trilogy and Deutsche Grammophon Era

Williams returned to Star Wars for the Disney sequel trilogy: The Force Awakens (Walt Disney Records, 2015), The Last Jedi (Walt Disney Records/Lucasfilm, 2017), and The Rise of Skywalker (Walt Disney Records/Lucasfilm, 2019). He also scored The BFG (Walt Disney Records, 2016) for Spielberg.

A significant shift in prestige label came with Deutsche Grammophon, where Williams signed for concert recordings and new works: Across the Stars (2019, featuring violinist Anne-Sophie Mutter), John Williams in Vienna (2020), The Berlin Concert (2022), Williams: Violin Concerto No. 2 & Selected Film Themes (2022, with Anne-Sophie Mutter), and In Tokyo (2024).

Late film credits include Lincoln (Sony Classical, 2012), War Horse (Sony Classical, 2011), The Post (Sony Classical, 2017), The Fabelmans (Sony Classical, 2022), and Indiana Jones and the Dial of Destiny (Walt Disney Records, 2023).
