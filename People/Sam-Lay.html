Sam Lay

American blues drummer. Born March 20, 1935, Birmingham, Alabama; died January 29, 2022. Discogs artist ID: 683574

Name variations in Discogs: Lay, Sam Ley, Sammy Lay, Sammy Layhorne, Sammy Lewis, Samual Lay, Samuel Lay, Samy Lay.

One of the defining drummers of postwar Chicago blues — a key member of Howlin’ Wolf’s band in the early 1960s and the original drummer on The Paul Butterfield Blues Band’s debut. His credited groups span five decades: Paul Butterfield Blues Band, James Cotton’s Blues Band, The Siegel-Schwall Band, Chicago Blues Reunion, Rob Stone & The C-Notes, and his own Sam Lay Blues Band.

Biography

Birmingham to Chicago (early career)

Lay came up through the Alabama rhythm-and-blues circuit before migrating to Chicago, where he entered the tight-knit network of Chess Records session players. By 1961 he was drumming for Howlin’ Wolf, appearing on the landmark single “Shake For Me” / “The Red Rooster” (Chess, 1961) alongside Willie Dixon on bass, Hubert Sumlin and Jimmy Rogers on guitar, and Henry Gray on piano. Those two sides became some of the most-covered tracks in the Wolf catalog.

Howlin’ Wolf (1961–1964)

Lay is credited on the Howlin’ Wolf sessions compiled across multiple Chess reissues: His Greatest Sides, Volume One (1983), Muddy & The Wolf (Muddy Waters sides: 1960 session, released 1982), The Real Folk Blues, The Chess Box (1991), and Howlin’ Wolf (Second Album, AKA Rockin’ Chair). The 1982 Muddy & The Wolf album documents two separate London sessions — Lay drummed on the Muddy Waters half (with Mike Bloomfield on lead guitar and Otis Spann on piano); Charlie Watts drummed the Wolf half. Paul Butterfield also appears on harp on the Muddy sessions — the same creative circle that would converge in Butterfield’s band.

The Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1964–1965)

Lay and bassist Jerome Arnold were recruited out of Howlin’ Wolf’s band by Paul Butterfield, an unusual move that placed two Black veterans of the electric Chicago blues circuit at the rhythm core of what became one of the most important integrated blues-rock bands of the decade.

The self-titled debut The Paul Butterfield Blues Band (Elektra, 1965 — Discogs master 76225, 111 pressings) features Lay on drums alongside Jerome Arnold (bass), Mark Naftalin (organ), Elvin Bishop (rhythm guitar), and Mike Bloomfield (slide guitar). Produced by Paul A. Rothchild (credited as Paul Rothchild). The album was recorded and released in 1965 and has remained continuously in print.

Lay left the band after the debut; Billy Davenport replaced him from East-West onward.

Newport Folk Festival 1965 (Bob Dylan connection)

Lay appears in Discogs credits on The Other Side Of The Mirror (Live At The Newport Folk Festival 1963–1965) (Columbia/Legacy, 2007 — master 347016), credited on tracks 17–19. The full band on those Newport tracks: Jerome Arnold (bass), Sam Lay (drums), Mike Bloomfield (guitar), Al Kooper (organ), Barry Goldberg (organ). This is the infamous July 25, 1965 set where Bob Dylan “went electric” — the Butterfield Blues Band rhythm section provided the backbone.

Lay also appears on a 1976 bootleg/unofficial release Passed Over And Rolling Thunder (The Amazing Kornyfone Record Label, 1976 — master 1260103), credited on tracks A1–A5, alongside Mike Bloomfield (guitar), Jerome Arnold (bass), and Al Kooper (organ): an earlier Dylan session or rehearsal from the same 1965 era.

James Cotton Blues Band (1967)

After Butterfield, Lay drummed on The James Cotton Blues Band (Verve Forecast, 1967 — master 526342, 17 pressings). Personnel: James Cotton (harmonica/vocals), Luther Tucker (lead guitar), Alberto Gianquinto (piano), Bob Anderson (bass/vocals). Produced by Mike Bloomfield, Barry Goldberg, and Norman Dayron — Bloomfield again as a producer connects the Butterfield and Cotton Chicago orbits.

Siegel-Schwall Band (late 1960s–1970s, and 1988 reunion)

Lay became the permanent drummer for The Siegel-Schwall Band (Corky Siegel on harmonica/piano, Jim Schwall on guitar). Their catalog spans late-1960s releases on Vanguard and appearances on Lake Michigan Ain’t No River (pläne, 1979, Germany — master 415157) and Chicago – London (Roots, 1990). The 1988 Reunion Concert (Alligator Records — master 380421) captures their reformed lineup with Lay on drums, produced by Alligator’s Bruce Iglauer.

Muddy Waters – Fathers and Sons (1969)

Lay is credited on Fathers And Sons (Chess, 1969 — master 425218, 70 pressings), the landmark Muddy Waters album pairing him with a younger generation: Mike Bloomfield and Paul Butterfield alongside Muddy’s own band. Personnel with Lay: Donald “Duck” Dunn (bass), Otis Spann (piano), Mike Bloomfield (guitar), Paul Butterfield (harmonica). Produced by Norman Dayron; engineered by Ron Malo. A direct through-line from the Wolf sessions to the late-1960s “blues revival” context.

Sam Lay In Bluesland (1969)

His sole album as primary leader: Sam Lay In Bluesland (Blue Thumb Records, 1969 — master 760754, 5 pressings). Produced by Nick Gravenites; Jack Walroth on harmonica (uncredited). The only moment in Discogs where Lay carries the primary artist credit outside of the 1998 Howlin’ Wolf tribute project.

Chicago Session Work — 1960s compilations

Appears on Goin’ To Chicago (Testament Records, 1969 — master 3997612) in a vocal role on tracks A1–A3, alongside Jerome Arnold (bass) and Jeff Carp (harmonica). A snapshot of the Chicago small-club circuit ca. 1969.

Magic Sam Live (Delmark, 1981 — master 733083): Lay on drums for the final set (tracks C1–D5) of this posthumous archival release documenting Magic Sam in concert.

Howlin’ Wolf Tribute (1998)

A Tribute To Howlin’ Wolf (Telarc Blues, 1998 — master 1497585): Lay is a primary-credited artist on this ensemble album alongside James Cotton, Hubert Sumlin, Henry Gray, Lucinda Williams, Taj Mahal, Ronnie Hawkins, Debbie Davies, and others. One of his few late-career primary credits, circling back to the Wolf circle he’d come from in 1961.

Late Career

Lay remained active into the 2000s through APO Records (APO Records (2)): credits on Legacy (1998), Three Days Walkin’ (1999), and Sho’ ‘Nuff (2001), all for that imprint. Also appears on Buried Alive In The Blues (Out Of The Box Records, 2005) and the Siegel-Schwall-adjacent Flash Forward (Alligator, 2005 — master 1926936).
