Richard Hell

Singer, bassist, poet, novelist. Born Richard Lester Meyers, 2 October 1949, Lexington, Kentucky. New York City from early 1970s.

Overview

Co-founded Television with Tom Verlaine; left to form The Heartbreakers (with Johnny Thunders) and then Richard Hell & The Voidoids. His torn-clothing, safety-pin aesthetic influenced the British punk look. Also a published novelist.

NYC Connections

  • Lizzy Mercier Descloux — romantic partner during NYC years; Hell later based a female protagonist in one of his novels on her; co-created Desiderata (1977, poetry/image book with Patti Smith preface)
  • Patti Smith — Desiderata contributor
  • Robert Quine — lead guitarist, Richard Hell & The Voidoids; also on SPY 003 (Lester Bangs single, mixed by John Cale)
  • Lizzy Mercier Descloux and Esteban encountered Hell on their first New York trip (1975, via Rock News)

Key Projects

  • Television (with Tom Verlaine) — co-founded as The Neon Boys (1972); left 1975
  • The Heartbreakers (2) (with Johnny Thunders, Jerry Nolan) — May 1975 to May 1976; see below
  • Richard Hell & The Voidoids — Blank Generation (Sire, 1977); Robert Quine on guitar

The Heartbreakers (May 1975 – May 1976)

Hell joined Thunders and Nolan immediately after leaving Television — the same week the Dolls split, Thunders and Nolan had quit that band, and Hell had just walked out on Verlaine over a songwriting-control dispute. First Heartbreakers show: 30 May 1975, the Coventry, Queens. Hell played bass and shared vocal duties. Walter Lure joined on second guitar in July 1975 (debut CBGB, 4 July 1975) — Lure was an addition, not Hell’s replacement.

Departure: At a rehearsal in early 1976 Hell issued an ultimatum — he would sing most songs per set, with Thunders cut to one or two. Thunders, Nolan, and Lure all sided against him. Hell’s final show was 14 May 1976, Rathskeller, Boston. He then formed Richard Hell & The Voidoids, taking “Blank Generation” and his other songs with him.

What Hell said about Thunders: In his 2013 memoir I Dreamed I Was a Very Clean Tramp Hell was unsparing, writing that Thunders’ lyrics “were half-assed in never having an original idea or turn of phrase.” (Kirkus Reviews summary)

Recordings: No studio material released during his tenure. Four demos recorded at SBS Studios, NYC, 23 January 1976 (Hell on bass) appeared on the L.A.M.F. Definitive Edition box set (Jungle/2012) and separately on The L.A.M.F. Demo Sessions (Jungle/2019). Live recordings from CBGB (7 July 1975) and Mother’s (16 November 1975) released on What Goes Around… (Bomp!, 1991). Ork Records

Another World EP (cat. 81976, 1976) — his first recordings under the Hell name. Three tracks: “(I Could Live With You) (In) Another World,” “You Gotta Lose,” “(I Belong To The) Blank Generation.” Personnel: Hell, Robert Quine (guitar), Ivan Julian (guitar), Marc Bell (drums). Produced by Craig Leon and Hell. Photo Roberta Bayley. “(Blank Generation)” would anchor his debut LP on Sire the following year.
