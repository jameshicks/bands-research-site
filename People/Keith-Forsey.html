Keith Forsey

Real name: Keith Forsey
Born: 4 January 1948, London, England
Discogs ID: 53801
Roles: Drummer, producer, songwriter
Labels: Chrysalis, Columbia, A&M, MCA

British drummer, producer, and songwriter who moved from krautrock session work in Germany to become one of the most commercially prominent producers of the 1980s. Session drummer for Giorgio Moroder in Munich in the 1970s; later the primary producer for Billy Idol’s solo career; produced The Psychedelic Furs’ Mirror Moves (1984); co-wrote “Don’t You (Forget About Me)” for Simple Minds and “Flashdance…What a Feelin’” for Irene Cara (Academy Award, 1984).

Biography

Early Career: London and Munich (1960s–1976)

Forsey began in the London psychedelia scene in the late 1960s with The Spectrum (Discogs ID: 339425). Around 1970 he moved to Germany, joining Giorgio Moroder’s touring/session band Motherhood (ID: 804721). He played drums on the Amon Düül II album Vive La Trance (1973, United Artists), and was part of the supergroups Niagara (ID: 56047) and 18 Karat Gold (ID: 728947). Through Niagara he connected with Udo Lindenberg, becoming drummer in Udo Lindenberg und das Panikorchester (ID: 921853) until 1976. Other German bands from this era include Hallelujah (3), Gaz, Munich Machine, and Ralf Nowy Group.

He also played on a large number of German Schlager and pop records throughout 1971–1977 as a session drummer — Discogs lists credits on well over 100 releases from this period, spanning Ariola, Polydor, CBS, Atlantic, and smaller German imprints.

He co-wrote “Hot Stuff” (1979) for Donna Summer with Moroder and Pete Bellotte — the track reached #1 in the US. He also co-wrote other Moroder-associated songs including “Watch Out for the Boogie Man” and several Silver Convention tracks. His writing credit appears on Donna Summer’s “Cold Love” (1980) as well.

Production Career Begins: Generation X / Billy Idol (1980–1986)

Forsey began producing in earnest starting with Generation X. His earliest production credits are the Generation X Chrysalis singles Dancing With Myself and Don’t Stop (1980–1981), and the album Kiss Me Deadly (1981). When the band split, he continued with Billy Idol’s solo career, producing essentially every major Idol release through the mid-1980s:

The Idol partnership was the most sustained of Forsey’s career, spanning from Dancing With Myself in 1980 through at least Devil’s Playground (2005) and Scream (2005).

The Breakfast Club, Simple Minds, and the Oscar (1983–1985)

Forsey co-wrote “Flashdance…What a Feelin’” with Irene Cara and Giorgio Moroder for the Flashdance soundtrack (1983). The song won the Academy Award for Best Original Song in 1984.

He served as producer and performer on The Breakfast Club original motion picture soundtrack (1985, A&M Records), on which he played drums and keyboards. He co-wrote “Don’t You (Forget About Me)” — originally conceived for Billy Idol, it was instead recorded by Simple Minds and became that band’s biggest global hit and one of the signature songs of the decade. On Discogs the Simple Minds release (master 58693) credits Forsey as both Producer and Words By/Music By.

He also produced and co-wrote “Fire in the Twilight” (Wang Chung, for The Breakfast Club), “All The Things She Said” (Wang Chung), and other tracks tied to John Hughes films. His credit as Music By appears on “Love Theme From The Breakfast Club” as well.

In 1984 he produced Harold Faltermeyer’s “Axel F” (from the Beverly Hills Cop OST) and “The Heat Is On” (Glenn Frey) for the same soundtrack (MCA Records), and co-wrote “The NeverEnding Story” (theme for the 1984 German film by the same name).

Icehouse and Australasian Work (1982–1993)

Forsey produced Icehouse (the Australian new wave act, also known as Flowers), including their breakthrough single “Hey Little Girl” (1982, Chrysalis) — their first major UK chart entry. He also produced Great Southern Land (1982, Regular Records), Street Café (1983), Primitive Man (1982), Fresco (1983), and continued with Icehouse into the late 1980s–1990s:

Mirror Moves and The Psychedelic Furs (1984)

Forsey produced The Psychedelic Furs’ fourth album Mirror Moves (1984, Columbia), also playing drums and percussion across the record. Vince Ely had departed; Forsey covered most of the drum duties, with Thommy Price on two tracks (A2, A3). Mars Williams joined on saxophone. The album contains “The Ghost In You” and “Heaven,” two of the band’s most enduring tracks. Forsey also produced and mixed both the UK and US single versions of “The Ghost In You” (CBS/Columbia, 1984), “Heaven” (CBS, 1984), and “Here Come Cowboys” (Columbia, 1984).

Further Production Work (1987–2005)

Forsey continued producing and writing through the late 1980s and beyond:

  • Simple Minds — produced Good News From The Next World (1995), Hypnotised (1995 single), She’s A River (1995), Glittering Prize 81/92 (1992 compilation, credited as producer/co-writer on included tracks)
  • Jennifer Rush — produced and co-wrote tracks on The Power of Jennifer Rush (1991, Columbia)
  • Harold Faltermeyer — Harold F (1988, MCA); Prophecy (1988); also co-produced Axel F / The Heat Is On (1984)
  • Various — New York New York (Gerard Kenny, 1983, Columbia); Angstlos (Peter Maffay, 1983, CBS); In Deep (Cherie, 1987); Wide Awake In Dreamland (Pat Benatar, 1988); Young Einstein OST (1988, A&M); Passion (Gary Morris, 1988)
  • Billy Idol continued — Charmed Life (1990), Devil’s Playground (2005), Scream (2005)
  • Rooney (2003, Geffen) and Blueside (2003) — indie rock productions in a later career shift
