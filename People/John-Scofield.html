John Scofield

Real name: John Leavitt Scofield Born: December 26, 1951, Dayton, Ohio Instrument: Guitar Discogs artist ID: 12628 Active: 1974–present

Grammy Award-winning American jazz guitarist and composer. One of the central voices in post-bop and jazz-fusion guitar from the 1980s onward, known for a tone that blends bebop fluency with blues and funk. Studied at Berklee College of Music.

Overview

Early career included work with Gerry Mulligan and Chet Baker, membership in The Billy Cobham - George Duke Band for two years, sessions with Charles Mingus, and a stint with the Gary Burton Quartet. The pivotal association was with Miles Davis, whom he toured and recorded with in the early-to-mid 1980s. From 1984 onward Scofield led his own groups with consistent critical and commercial success.

Career-spanning label relationships: early albums on Enja Records and Arista Novus; the electric-funk Gramavision run in the mid-to-late 1980s; the Blue Note period circa 1990–1995; a long stretch on Verve Records through the 2000s; and a late-career alignment with ECM Records from 2006 onward (with returns in 2020, 2022, 2023, 2025).

He is a member of Medeski Scofield Martin & Wood (ongoing groove/improv project with John Medeski, Chris Wood (3), and Billy Martin (2)) and Hudson (10) (with Jack DeJohnette, Larry Grenadier, and John Medeski).
