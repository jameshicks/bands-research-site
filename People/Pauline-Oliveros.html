Overview

Pauline Oliveros (1932–2016) was a Houston-born American composer, performer, and author whose practice centred on experimental electronics, extended techniques, and what she termed “deep listening” — a philosophy of focused, whole-body sonic attention. She developed the Extended Instrument System, a suite of digital signal processors built for live performance. Her Discogs catalogue runs from the late 1960s through the early 2020s (including posthumous releases), with the heaviest output in the 1980s on Lovely Music, Ltd. and again in the 2010s on Important Records (2), which became her primary label home in later years. She was a founding member of Deep Listening Band and collaborated most frequently with Stuart Dempster and Panaiotis; she also established both the Deep Listening label and the Pauline Oliveros Foundation to support her practice and archive.

Notes (web)

Lovely Music, Ltd. — label context

Lovely Music, Ltd. was founded in 1978 by Mimi Johnson in New York City as an outgrowth of her nonprofit management organisation Performing Artservices, Inc. (founded 1972). The label was dedicated to releasing work by living composers in the contemporary avant-garde. Johnson’s original roster centred on Robert Ashley (her husband), David Behrman, Meredith Monk, Jon Hassell, and Peter Gordon; Oliveros appeared later. Wikipedia — Lovely Music, as of 2026-09-26 / OperaWire — Mimi Johnson interview

Note: the brief that prompted this search named “Robert Goldstein” as Lovely Music’s founder — this is incorrect. The founder is Mimi Johnson.

UCSD departure and Lovely Music — temporal correlation, no documented causal link

Oliveros left her tenured position at UCSD in 1981 and relocated to Kingston, New York, to work as an independent composer. Her first Lovely Music release, Accordion & Voice (LMR 1901), appeared in 1982 — the year immediately following. The temporal proximity is clear, but no accessible source explicitly frames her Lovely Music relationship as a direct consequence of leaving UCSD. The IRCAM biography presents the two events in sequence without connecting them (IRCAM biography). The New Music USA interview mentions the Lovely Music accordion LP only in passing, with no reference to how the recording relationship began (New Music USA). Mimi Johnson’s OperaWire interview does not name Oliveros at all.

Performing Artservices, Johnson’s nonprofit, had already managed artists in Oliveros’s immediate circle (David Behrman was an early Lovely Music artist; Sonic Arts Union, which included Behrman, overlapped with Oliveros’s San Francisco/UCSD milieu), suggesting a pre-existing professional network that the 1982 recording may have drawn on rather than a post-departure new arrangement. This remains speculative without a primary source.

Bottom line: The 1981–1982 sequence is confirmed by multiple sources; a documented causal or contractual link is not established in any publicly available source found in this search.
