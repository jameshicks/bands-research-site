Natalie Prass

Natalie Prass

Discogs ID: 2551952
Born: 1986, Virginia Beach, Virginia, USA
Based: Nashville, TN (early career); Richmond, VA (Spacebomb era)
Genres: Soul, Pop, Indie Rock
Labels: Spacebomb Records (2015), Caroline International (2015), ATO Records (2018–2019), Domino (2016, 2021)

American singer-songwriter with roots in Virginia. Her self-titled 2015 debut, produced by Matthew E. White on Spacebomb Records, drew wide critical attention for its lush orchestral soul arrangements. The follow-up, The Future And The Past (2018, ATO Records), moved toward synthesizer-forward indie pop and carried a more politically engaged tone.

Biography

Early Years and Nashville Period (1986–2013)

Natalie Prass was born in 1986 in Virginia Beach, Virginia — the same hometown as Matthew E. White — and relocated to Nashville, Tennessee during her formative years as a working musician. Her earliest Discogs credit is a guest vocal appearance on Distractions (2006, Evo Recordings). In 2009 she self-released Small & Sweet, a six-track EP recorded in Nashville with producer and multi-instrumentalist Mike Odmark (Iron Mike Odmark). That record featured a small chamber ensemble — cello (Hannah Schroeder, Markus Midkiff), violin (Lyndsay Pruett, Zach Casebolt, Tyler Andal), French horn (Jordan Brooke Hamlin), trombone (Aaron Roche), trumpet (Aron Wright) — and was arranged by Matthew Smith. The EP was self-released and had limited distribution, but it established the orchestrated pop-soul sound she would later develop with Spacebomb Records.

Around 2013 Prass contributed harmony vocals to the debut self-titled album by Torres (2) (Torres, 2013, self-released) and lead vocals to the Communicating Vessels LP Rabbit Runs A Destiny (2013).

Spacebomb Debut (2014–2015)

A pre-release promo single, Bird Of Prey, appeared on Caroline Records in 2014 (Netherlands pressing, CDr promo). The full debut album Natalie Prass was released in 2015 on Spacebomb Records (cat. SB006) and Caroline International Ltd. The record was produced by Matthew E. White, with Trey Pollard serving as co-producer, engineer, mixer, and string arranger/conductor. The album’s sound is built around a full ensemble drawn almost entirely from the Spacebomb collective: The Spacebomb Strings, The Spacebomb Horns, and The Spacebomb House Band all contributed. String players included Amber Blankenship, Naomi Steckman, and Stephanie Barrett on cello; Anna Bishop, Faith Hofma, Jessica Blanks, Katie Wooldridge, and Melissa Sunderland Jones on violin; Garrett Pederson and Matt Jewell on viola. Harp on two tracks (A4 “Christy” and B5 “It Is You”) was played by Keety Dolfe. The horn section (clarinet/flute/saxophone: Jason Scott, Lauren Serpa, Venessa Lopez; brass: Bob Miller, Marcus Tenney, Bryan Hooten, Reggie Pace, Reggie Chapman, Marcus Redden) was arranged by Matthew E. White for most of the album, with Trey Pollard arranging the horns on “It Is You.” Piano on tracks A1–B3 was Daniel Clarke. Cameron Ralston played bass and double bass; Pinson Chanselle played drums and percussion; Giustino Riccio contributed congas. Matt Coyle played vibraphone on two tracks. Mastering was by Gene Paul at Gateway Mastering. Lacquer cutting by Jörg Peters.

Most of the songs were co-written with Kyle Ryan Hurlbut (credited across six of the nine tracks), with co-writers including Peter Barbee (“My Baby Don’t Understand Me”), Jabe Beyer (“Why Don’t You Believe In Me”), and Evan P. Donohue (“Christy”). The sole solo Prass composition was “Violently.”

A European promo single Why Don’t You Believe In Me followed in 2015 on Caroline Records/Spacebomb, with radio edit, album version, and instrumental.

Live EP and Matthew E. White Collaboration (2016)

Side By Side (Live At Spacebomb Studios) (2016) was a five-track 12” EP released on Columbia/Spacebomb Records/Startime International (cat. 88875177621). It featured a stripped touring band lineup: Daniel Clarke (piano/Wurlitzer), Trey Pollard (guitar), Michael Libramento (bass), Scott Clark (drums). The set included two originals (“My Baby Don’t Understand Me,” “Christy”), a cover of Anita Baker’s “Caught Up In The Rapture,” Paul Simon’s “The Sound of Silence,” and Grimes’s “REALiTi.” Mixed by Chad Wahlbrink; photography by Andrea Behrends.

In 2016 Prass featured on Matthew E. White’s single “Cool Out” (Domino, cat. RUG741CDP), a promo CDr single; she appears in the primary artist credit alongside White.

The Future And The Past (2018)

The second album, The Future And The Past (2018, ATO Records, cat. ATO0421), represents a stylistic pivot. Genres on Discogs are listed as Rock / Alternative Rock / Indie Rock — a departure from the debut’s Funk/Soul/Vocal categorization. Matthew E. White produced the majority of the album (tracks A1–A5, A7–B5); Prass self-produced “Lost” (A6). Trey Pollard returned to arrange and conduct strings on “Lost,” while Taylor Barnett conducted strings on all other string-bearing tracks. Cameron Ralston played bass; Pinson Chanselle played drums and percussion; Devonne Harris (alias DJ Harrison) played keyboards; Alan Parker played guitar throughout and lap steel on “Nothing To Say.”

A full choir appears on three tracks (“Hot For The Mountain,” “Sisters,” “Ain’t Nobody”), directed by Joseph Clarke. The choir members included Alana Nichols, Amber Bethea, Ashleigh Banks, Brandi Wellman, Briana Vaughn, Charnise Archie, Danielle Nichols, Kionna Harris, and Raven Worlds. A new string section replaced the Spacebomb Strings: Beth Judson, Jason McComb, Peter Greydanus, and Schuyler Slack on cello; HyoJoo Uh, Kim Ryan, and Molly Sharp on viola; Adrian Pintea, Anna Bishop, Ellen Cockerham-Riccio, Stacy Matthews, and Treesa Gold on violin.

Mixing was split across three engineers: Adrian Olsen handled most tracks; Michael H. Brauer mixed “Lost”; Mikaelin Bluespruce mixed “Short Court Style” (A2). Mastered by Bob Ludwig. Photography by Tonje Thilesen; graphic design by Na Kim; creative direction and ceramic sculpture artwork by Erica Prince.

Co-writers on the album include Matthew E. White (“Oh My,” “Hot For The Mountain,” “Ship Go Down,” “Sisters,” “Ain’t Nobody”), Trey Pollard (“Interlude: Your Fire”), Mikky Ekko (“The Fire”), Amy Wadge and Adj Buffone (“Lost”), Kyle Ryan (“Short Court Style,” “Nothing To Say”), Peter Barbee (“Nothing To Say”), Dillon O’Brian and Steve Lindsey (“Never Too Late”), and Fiona Bevan (“Far From You”).

Later Work (2019–Present)

In 2019 Prass and Dylan LeBlanc released a split 7” single on ATO Records (Wild Horses / Never Tear Us Apart, cat. 0882387419). Prass’s side — “Never Tear Us Apart” (originally INXS) — was produced by Dave Cobb. LeBlanc’s “Wild Horses” (originally Prefab Sprout’s Paddy McAloon) was produced by Andrew Rendazzo and Devonne Harris (DJ Harrison).

Prass contributed vocals to the release Wiseacre (2020, Slick Records) and to K Bay (2021, Domino) as a guest vocalist.
