Robert Fripp

Oct 21, 202614 min read

Robert Fripp

Discogs artist ID: 10355
Born: 16 May 1946, Wimborne, Dorset, England, UK
Role: Guitarist, keyboards, composer, producer, label co-founder
Active: 1960s–present
Married: Toyah Willcox (Toyah), 16 May 1986

British guitarist and the founder and only continuous member of King Crimson (Discogs ID: 70828), from their 1969 debut through to their final live performance in Tokyo on 8 December 2021. Co-founded Discipline Global Mobile (DGM) with David Singleton in 1992. Originator of Frippertronics (tape-loop guitar system) and Soundscapes (digital delay/synth variant). Creator of Guitar Craft, a teaching method first practised from March 1985 at Claymont Court, West Virginia, USA.

Biography

Early career and Giles, Giles And Fripp (1966–1969)

Fripp took up guitar in Wimborne, Dorset, and came up professionally through the local dance-band circuit. In 1967 he formed Giles, Giles And Fripp with brothers Michael Giles (drums) and Peter Giles (bass). Discogs ID: 414613. The trio recorded one album — The Cheerful Insanity Of Giles, Giles And Fripp (1968, Deram) — before disbanding. The Giles brothers briefly joined the nascent King Crimson formation.

King Crimson: Mark I (1969–1972)

King Crimson formed in London in January 1969 with Fripp, Greg Lake (bass, vocals), Michael Giles (drums), Ian McDonald (reeds, keys, mellotron), and Peter Sinfield (lyrics, illumination). Their debut In The Court Of The Crimson King (Island Records, October 1969) was completed in under a week and entered the UK charts at no. 5. The album’s personnel:

The band underwent rapid lineup changes: Lake departed to form Emerson, Lake & Palmer; McDonald and Giles left; Gordon Haskell, Mel Collins, Andy McCulloch, Boz Burrell, and Ian Wallace passed through. The Mark II period produced In The Wake Of Poseidon (1970), Lizard (1970), and Islands (1971).

King Crimson: Mark III (1972–1974)

A complete lineup rebuild brought John Wetton (bass, vocals), Bill Bruford (drums, ex-Yes), David Cross (violin, keys), and briefly Jamie Muir (percussion). This lineup produced Larks’ Tongues In Aspic (1973), Starless And Bible Black (1974), Red (1974). Fripp disbanded King Crimson on 25 September 1974, citing his view that the large rock band format was a “dying force.”

After a period of study with the philosopher J.G. Bennett’s Gurdjieff foundation (1975–1977), Fripp moved to New York where he developed Frippertronics — solo performances using a continuous tape-loop system. This led to the 1979 solo album Exposure (EG/Polydor):

Exposure is a collage album — fragments of dialogue, multiple vocal styles, guests from across the New York rock and pop scene. Fripp co-wrote much of it with Daryl Hall, Peter Gabriel, and Peter Hammill.

He also performed at Washington Square Church (NYC) and recorded the Frippertronics live document Let The Power Fall (1981, Editions EG). The companion release God Save The Queen / Under Heavy Manners (1980, Polydor) documented further solo Frippertronics performances.

King Crimson: Discipline era (1981–1984)

Fripp reformed King Crimson in 1981 under the working name “Discipline” (later also used as an alias for the band). The new lineup was radically different: Adrian Belew (guitar, vocals), Tony Levin (bass, Chapman Stick), and Bill Bruford (drums). The band made three studio albums:

  • Discipline (1981, EG)
  • Beat (1982, EG)
  • Three Of A Perfect Pair (1984, EG)

Fripp then again dissolved King Crimson, in 1984, spending the following years teaching Guitar Craft and issuing solo releases.

Guitar Craft and The League Of Crafty Guitarists (1985–1994)

Fripp founded Guitar Craft seminars in March 1985 at Claymont Court, West Virginia. The performance arm — The League Of Crafty Guitarists (Discogs ID: 488720) — toured internationally through the late 1980s and early 1990s, issuing:

Members of these ensembles formed the seed of the California Guitar Trio and other DGM-adjacent projects. From 2010 Guitar Craft became “Guitar Circles” in the Americas and Europe; Fripp continued to direct The Orchestra Of Crafty Guitarists (Discogs ID: 2967489).

Founding DGM and soundscape period (1992–2003)

In 1992 Fripp and David Singleton founded Discipline Records (later renamed Discipline Global Mobile / DGM in 1994) as a direct-to-fan independent after the protracted dispute with E.G. Records Ltd. See Discipline Global Mobile for full catalog.

Fripp’s own DGM output in this period centred on solo Soundscapes recordings — live performances using digital delay and synthesizers in the Frippertronics tradition:

Later King Crimson and A Scarcity Of Miracles (2008–2021)

A Fripp-led quartet and later septet took shape in the 2010s, with Jakko M. Jakszyk joining as vocalist/guitarist alongside original-era members Mel Collins (saxophone), Gavin Harrison (drums), Tony Levin, and Pat Mastelotto.

A Scarcity Of Miracles (2011, DGM) — credited as a “King Crimson ProjeKct” to Jakszyk, Fripp, and Collins:

King Crimson performed live extensively 2014–2021 with a seven- and eight-piece lineup. Their final performance was Tokyo, 8 December 2021. Fripp has described the band as “in a state of rest” since then.

Music For Quiet Moments and Leviathan (2021)

Leviathan (2021, DGM/Panegyric) collected Fripp’s Soundscapes recordings with The Grid (Richard Norris / Dave Ball). Music For Quiet Moments (2021, DGM) presented curated soundscape recordings.

Exposures (2022, DGM/Panegyric) was a major boxed retrospective, and The Complete Recordings 1981–1984 (2025, Panegyric) compiled the Fripp/Summers duet period.

His first widely published book, The Guitar Circle, was released 1 September 2022 by Anthology Editions in association with DGM.

DGM Label Co-Founder

Fripp co-founded Discipline Global Mobile with David Singleton in 1992 (initially named Discipline Records; renamed DGM 1994). The label was created explicitly as a response to the royalty dispute with E.G. Records Ltd., and founded on the principle that artists retain copyright and the label operates transparently.

Key facts:

  • Fripp credited on approximately 806 DGM releases across all roles (producer, mastering, performer, writer, etc.)
  • 79 of those credits are as primary artist
  • Production entity Tone Probe (Discogs ID: 527247) — Fripp and Singleton jointly credited — appears on compilations and reissue productions throughout the catalog
  • DGM is the exclusive home of King Crimson from 1992; sublabels include The King Crimson Collectors’ Club (CLUB prefix), DGMLive.com (downloads), and multiple anniversary series
  • Fripp’s book The Guitar Circle (2022, Anthology Editions in association with DGM) is the first widely published memoir
