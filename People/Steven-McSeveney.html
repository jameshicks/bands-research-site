Overview

Steven McSeveney is a bassist whose credits span both the mid-1990s Britpop era and the 2010s indie underground. He played in Octopus (2), a band that released on Food and Parlophone in 1996, and in The Secret Goldfish, which resurfaced on Glasgow’s Creeping Bent label in 2017. His recorded work is compact at three releases, but his two bands together place him across indie rock, Britpop, and psychedelic rock territory, alongside recurring collaborators John Morose, Katy McCullars, and Paul Turnbull (3).

Notes (web)

Octopus (2) after 1996

Octopus (2) released their only album From A To B (Food/Parlophone) in September 1996 and split up in 1997. Their final single was “Jealousy” (November 1996). The band did not continue recording after the split. McSeveney played bass on the album; he was replaced by Cameron Miller for live work after the album was recorded. Other members went on to separate projects: Marc Shearer worked with Adem and later recorded solo as Marc Meon; Cameron Miller joined Blowpipe and later played with Ben Christophers and Adem. Source: Octopus (Scottish band) — Wikipedia (checked 2026-11-06).

McSeveney and The Secret Goldfish

The framing of “twenty years later” in the research question somewhat overstates the gap. The Secret Goldfish were active 1994–1999, and McSeveney’s Discogs credit on Jet Streams (Marina Records, 1997) — described in search results as an album of B-sides and outtakes — places him in the band during their original 1990s run. The Wikipedia article confirms McSeveney as bassist in the band’s “final line-up,” alongside vocalist Katy McCullars, guitarist John Morose, and drummer Paul Turnbull; the original rhythm section (from The Mackenzies) featured Graham Lironi on bass, so McSeveney joined as a replacement at some point before or around the 1997 release. The gap before his return to recording activity was therefore roughly 1999–2017 (~18 years), not 20. Source: The Secret Goldfish — Wikipedia (as of 2026-05-31).

The band returned to live performance in January 2016, their first shows in approximately 16 years, with added guitarist James Kirk (ex-Orange Juice). Their third album Petal Split (Creeping Bent, April 2017) had a long gestation: recording reportedly began around 2000 and was completed in late 2016. No published interview or press piece found explains what specifically prompted the 2016 return; the available reviews and label descriptions focus on the music rather than the reunion’s circumstances. Sources checked: Louder Than War review; I Don’t Hear A Single review; Creeping Bent Bandcamp (404); Last.fm; Wikipedia (as above).
