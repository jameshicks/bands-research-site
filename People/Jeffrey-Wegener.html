Jeffrey Wegener

Jeffrey Wegener

Oct 27, 20267 min read

Jeffrey Wegener

Discogs ID: 381696 Alias: One & A Half Sticks Name variations: J. Wegener, Jeff Wegener, Wegener

Australian drummer active in the Sydney underground from the late 1970s. Founding member and sole constant drummer of Laughing Clowns, and a credited member of The Saints (2). One of only two musicians — alongside Ed Kuepper — present across all 51 Laughing Clowns releases in the Discogs database.

Biography

Pre-Laughing Clowns: The Saints and Young Charlatans (1976–1979)

Wegener appears as a member of The Saints (2), the Brisbane-born punk band fronted by Chris Bailey and featuring Ed Kuepper on guitar. The Saints were one of the first punk acts globally, releasing “(I’m) Stranded” in 1976 well ahead of the British wave. Wegener does not appear on any Saints studio releases in the Discogs database as a primary performer (his sole Saints credits are liner notes on the 2024 anthology (I’m) Stranded, In The Red Recordings), suggesting his tenure with the group was brief or live-only — a gap the DB cannot resolve.

More substantially, Wegener was a member of Young Charlatans, the Melbourne post-punk outfit that also included Rowland S. Howard and Ollie Olsen. The band’s archival LP 1978 (Eminent Vinyl, 2026) — collecting demos and live recordings from that year — credits Wegener on drums alongside Howard and Olsen. This places him at the very genesis of what would become the Melbourne underground, shortly before the formation of Laughing Clowns and The Birthday Party.

Laughing Clowns: The Core Years (1979–1986)

Laughing Clowns were formed in Sydney by Kuepper and Wegener after both left The Saints. The band is widely regarded as one of the key Australian post-punk/jazz-rock acts of the era, fusing free-jazz horn arrangements with angular guitar and Kuepper’s literary songwriting. Wegener was the band’s sole drummer for the entirety of their recording career.

First recordings (1980): The debut single Sometimes, The Fire Dance… (Prince Melon Records, PM-01 EP, 1980) established the core lineup: Kuepper (guitar, vocals), Wegener (drums), Ben Wallace-Crabbe (bass), Dan Wallace-Crabbe (piano), Bob Farrell (saxophone). Engineered by Tony Cohen.

The debut LP The Laughing Clowns (Missing Link, MLB 001, 1980) followed immediately, with the same lineup and Cohen again engineering and mixing. This remains one of the key documents of the early Australian post-punk scene.

Prolific middle period (1981–1983): The band released a string of EPs and LPs on Prince Melon Records, including 3 (1981) and the full-length Mr Uddich-Schmuddich Goes To Town (Prince Melon, PM5000, 1982). On Mr Uddich-Schmuddich, Wegener shared production credit with Kuepper and engineer Peter Doyle (2) — his most prominent behind-the-board role in the discography. The lineup shifted during this period: Leslie Millar replaced Ben Wallace-Crabbe on bass, and Peter Doyle (2) added trumpet.

A UK compilation LP Laughing Clowns (Red Flame, 1982) brought the band to a European audience.

Hot Records period (1983–1985): Moving to Hot Records (2), the band issued their most polished work. The 12” single Eternally Yours (HOT 12001, 1984) featured Peter Milton Walsh on bass and Louise Elliott on saxophone. Law Of Nature (HOT 1004, 1984) — the band’s most cited LP — brought in Chris Abrahams on piano. Ghosts Of An Ideal Wife (HOT 1013, 1985) added Louis Tillett (piano), Diane Spence (saxophone), and Glad Reed (trombone), representing the band’s most orchestrated sound. Both LPs were mastered by Don Bartley.

The band dissolved around 1986. A best-of, History Of Rock N’ Roll - Volume One (Hot Records, 1984), and the career retrospective Golden Days - When Giants Walked The Earth (Hot Records, 1995) document the arc.

Post-Clowns: The Lost Domain, Slawterhaus, and Late Work (1990s–2010s)

After the Clowns, Wegener worked across several projects:

The Lost Domain — an experimental/drone outfit with Eugene Carchesio, Leighton Craig, and others, active from the late 1990s through the 2010s on small labels including Shy Tone, Cook An Egg, Digitalis Recordings, and PseudoArcana. The group released prolifically — over a dozen albums — placing Wegener within Melbourne/Sydney experimental scenes well into the 2000s.

Slawterhaus Band — an improvised music ensemble that included violinist Jon Rose, saxophonist Jim Denley, and Richard Vella. Released The Last Waltz (Fringe Benefit Records, 2022), indicating the group was still active into the 2020s.

The Sump — self-released False Turbines (1995).

Ed Kuepper And The Kowalski Collective — Wegener re-united with Kuepper in this ensemble, releasing Jean Lee And The Yellow Dog (Hot Records, 2007) and related singles. Track-level guests included Warren Ellis (violin on “Miracles”), Chris Bailey (vocals on “That Depends Pt 3”), and John Willsteed (banjo, dobro on “Real To Me”). Kuepper produced; Don Bartley mastered.

Mick Turner session (2013): Wegener appeared on Don’t Tell The Driver (Drag City, DC569, 2013) by Mick Turner of Dirty Three, one of multiple drummers used across the record.

Young Charlatans archival release (2026): Wegener is credited on 1978 (Eminent Vinyl, EMV0001, 2026), the official archival LP of the Young Charlatans’ 1978 recordings, alongside Rowland S. Howard and Ollie Olsen.

The Birthday Party connection

The DB flags a single shared release with The Birthday Party: A Fuckin’ Rotten Business This! (Tuff Monk Records, TF-391, 2007). This is an unofficial bootleg LP on which Wegener is credited as the drummer while Nick Cave, Mick Harvey, Rowland S. Howard, and Tracy Pew are listed as uncredited. This appears to be a live recording — almost certainly predating the formation of The Birthday Party proper, from a period when Wegener was moving in the same Melbourne underground circles as that band’s future members.
