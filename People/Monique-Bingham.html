Biography

Monique Bingham is a Brooklyn-based singer-songwriter and producer who has been one of the most consistently credited vocalists in the international deep house circuit since the mid-1990s. Her work spans both sides of the Atlantic — she has a substantial body of credits with UK-based producers and labels (notably Curious (3), Sir Piers, and R2 Records) as well as US-rooted collaborations with figures such as Kerri Chandler, Louie Vega, Quentin Harris, and Karizma. She runs her own imprint, Bigga Sounds, from which she has released her own productions.

Early Credits (1996–2000)

Bingham’s earliest traceable Discogs credit is the 1996 US release “Get Another Plan” on Streetwave Music, on which she is credited for vocals. That same year the same song surfaced on the UK label Talkin’ Loud in a remixed form (Rob Playford and Flytronix), where she receives a written-by credit. Her first significant feature came in 2000 via the San Francisco deep house label Naked Music Recordings — she appears on the track “Pure” with a featuring, vocals, and written-by credit. This placed her on the radar of the US soulful house underground at the point that Naked Music was attracting international attention.

UK Deep House Scene and Curious Records (2002–2006)

The early 2000s saw Bingham establish a strong foothold in the UK deep house world. The anchor collaboration was with producer Sir Piers (Piers Penfold): their 2002 single “Don’t You Love Me” on Curious (3) (UK) became a foundational release in that label’s catalog, spawning three rounds of remix packages (“The Remixes Part 2,” “Part 3”) and a run of follow-up singles. Sir Piers was the most significant production relationship of this phase — he produced, arranged, programmed, and mixed the tracks; Bingham contributed lead and backing vocals and co-wrote. The extended “Don’t You Love Me” series ran through at least 2003 and totalled 20 shared releases between them across Curious (3), Not On Label, and OM Records.

Also in 2002, she appeared on the Astralwerks/Naked Music Recordings release “Beautiful Tomorrow.” In 2003 the “After Hours” remix package appeared (Not On Label), again with Sir Piers.

Her 2004 Japan release Flight (New World Records (4)) brought a new production partner: Studio Apartment, the Japanese house duo, who are co-credited across the Flight single and the Found Him (2007) follow-up. Frankie Feliciano remixed “Flight” for the US market (BPM King Street Sounds, 2004).

In 2004 she also worked with UK producer Matthias Heilbronn on “Go Getter” (Papa Records, 2004), which was co-written by both and produced by Heilbronn. That same year the “Fools Got Nothin’” series appeared on Curious (3), produced with additional personnel including Reel People.

She released her own production “Glance, Look, Stare” (2005, MN2S) on which she is credited as arranger, writer, and producer in addition to vocalist — a signal of her production ambitions. The same year brought “Poor People” (Symple Soul, 2005), a collaboration with New York producer Quentin Harris.

“In The Morning” (2006, Bigga Sounds) was co-credited with Kerri Chandler — one of the most prominent names in deep house — with Chandler producing and Bingham on vocals. The release confirmed she had built connections across both the UK and US deep house elite. In 2006 she also released “Now What” on Z Records (UK), produced with Matthias Heilbronn.

Consolidating the Solo Profile (2007–2012)

“They Forgot It” (2007, OM Records) was co-credited with UK duo Tarantulaz (Piers Penfold / Toni Economides), with co-writing by Mark De Clive-Lowe. “Won’t Stop” (2008, Jellybean Soul) continued solo-oriented releases. The Japan album Found Him (2007, New World Records (4)) gathered earlier material and Studio Apartment collaborations.

From 2009, German label Gogo Music became a significant home for her work. Little W. 12th St. (Gogo Music, 2009) was co-credited with South African producer Ralf GUM and marked the start of a lasting partnership — “Take Me To My Love” (Gogo Music, 2012) followed, again with Ralf GUM, who also appears credited as co-writer. Gogo Music, run by Ralf GUM, gave Bingham a consistent European platform through this period.

The Karizma collaboration “Good Morning” (2010, R2 Records) is among her most prominent single credits — a co-headlined release on Karizma’s home label, with a follow-up EP.

Later Period and Ongoing Activity (2015–present)

“Elevator (Going Up)” (2015, Vega Records) was co-headed with Louie Vega, one of New York house music’s most prominent figures. Bingham sang lead and backing vocals and arranged the vocal parts; Vega produced. The Louie Vega Remixes version appeared the same year.

Best Of The Last (Anthology 1995–2015) (2016, House Afrika Records, South Africa) collected two decades of output and signaled her breadth of international reach — including the South African market.

A cameo credit on “Black Stereo Faith” (2018, Def Mix Music / Blufire / Deep Sugar Music / Peace Bisquit / We Got The Beats) placed her alongside house royalty on a multi-label release.

Over Him, Under Him (2020, Sunclock, Italy) was co-credited with Italian producer DJ Vivona, generating four pressings including remixes by Enoo Napa and Jon Cutler.

Her own label Bigga Sounds has served as an outlet for self-produced material, including “In The Morning” (2006).
