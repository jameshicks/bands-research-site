Laura Nyro

Discogs ID: 370500 Real name: Laura Nigro Born: October 18, 1947, The Bronx, New York City, USA Died: April 8, 1997, Danbury, Connecticut, USA (ovarian cancer, aged 49)

Singer-songwriter, pianist, and producer. One of the most influential American songwriters of the late 1960s, responsible for a string of hit songs covered by major acts while her own records sold modestly. Inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame (2010) and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (2012).

Biography

Early Career and Verve Forecast Years (1966–1967)

Nyro was the daughter of Louis Nigro, a jazz trumpeter and piano tuner, and Gilda Mirsky. She grew up in the Bronx absorbing gospel, soul, doo-wop, and jazz alongside folk and classical music. Her debut single “Wedding Bell Blues” / “Stoney End” appeared on Verve Folkways in 1966.

Her debut album More Than A New Discovery (Verve Folkways FTS 3020, 1967) was produced by Milton Okun and arranged/conducted by Herb Bernstein. It contained virtually all the songs that would become massive hits for other artists: “Goodbye Joe,” “And When I Die,” “Stoney End,” “Wedding Bell Blues,” “He’s A Runner,” “Blowin’ Away.” The album sold poorly but the songwriting attracted immediate attention from other artists and management.

Her performance at the 1967 Monterey Pop Festival was widely reported as a disaster — she was booed — but it put her name in front of the industry. Shortly after, she signed to David Geffen’s management operation, a relationship that would prove formative for both of them.

Columbia Years, Peak Songwriting Era (1968–1971)

Nyro moved to Columbia Records for Eli And The Thirteenth Confession (Columbia CQ 1069, 1968), on which she self-produced alongside Charlie Calello, who also arranged. The album was an artistic breakthrough: dense, soul-gospel-inflected pop with complex vocal performances. It contained “Sweet Blindness,” “Eli’s Comin’,” “Stoned Soul Picnic,” and “Emmie.” Within months, these songs were being recorded by others:

  • “Stoned Soul Picnic” and “Sweet Blindness” — The 5th Dimension
  • “Eli’s Comin’” — Three Dog Night
  • “Wedding Bell Blues” — The 5th Dimension (No. 1 pop hit, 1969)

New York Tendaberry (Columbia KCS 9737, 1969) was starker and more personal, largely piano-and-voice with orchestration. The Discogs entry credits show no additional musicians on this Canadian pressing — a reflection of the album’s stripped-back character in places. It contained “Save The Country” (later covered by The 5th Dimension) and “Time And Love.”

Christmas And The Beads Of Sweat (Columbia KC 30259, 1970) marked a major expansion of her studio palette. The album split sessions between two distinct bands: Side A used the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section (Roger Hawkins, drums; David Hood, bass; Eddie Hinton, guitar; Barry Beckett, vibraphone; Felix Cavaliere, organ) while Side B deployed a New York group including Chuck Rainey (bass), Dino Danelli (drums), Cornell Dupree (guitar), Alice Coltrane (harp), and Ashad Garabedian (oud). Musical arrangements were split between Arif Mardin and Nyro herself. Duane Allman played uncredited electric guitar on “Beads Of Sweat” (Side B, track 3). Engineer: Tim Geelan. Cover portrait by Beth O’Brien.

Gonna Take A Miracle (Columbia KC 30987, 1971) — see dedicated section below.

Gonna Take A Miracle (1971)

A soul covers album recorded in Philadelphia with LaBelle (Patti LaBelle, Nona Hendryx, Sarah Dash), produced by Gamble & Huff at their Philadelphia studio. All three LaBelle members appear uncredited on the sleeve, listed only as “Vocals [Uncredited]”; Nyro herself is also listed as “Vocals [Uncredited], Piano.”

The tracklist draws on Shirelles, Marvin Gaye, Curtis Mayfield, Holland-Dozier-Holland, Smokey Robinson, and Leiber/Spector — a tour through the doo-wop and soul songbook Nyro grew up on. The title track “It’s Gonna Take A Miracle” was written by Bobby Weinstein, Lou Stallman, and Teddy Randazzo. Catalog: Columbia KC 30987; mastering by Frankford/Wayne, Philadelphia.

Retirement and Return (1972–1975)

After Gonna Take A Miracle, Nyro largely withdrew from recording and public performance, marrying carpenter David Bianchini. She did not release another album until Smile in 1976. She contributed backing vocals to Barbra Streisand’s recording of “Stoney End” (Columbia, 1970), and the 1971 Stoney End album, which Nyro produced, was a major hit for Streisand.

Later Columbia Period (1976–1984)

Smile (CBS S 81171, 1976) was produced by Charlie Calello with Nyro co-producing. The band included John Tropea (guitar), Will Lee (bass), Chris Parker (drums), Michael Brecker (saxophone), Randy Brecker (trumpet), Richard Davis (bass on several tracks), and Joe Farrell (saxophone). Nyro performed vocals, piano, and acoustic guitar. Engineer: Don Puluse, Tim Geelan.

Season Of Lights…Laura Nyro In Concert (Columbia PC 34786, 1977) documented a live tour with a working band: John Tropea (electric guitar), Richard Davis (bass), Mike Mainieri (clavinet, vibraphone, balophone), Andy Newmark (drums), Nydia Mata (percussion/congas), Carter C.C. Collins (2nd percussion), Jean Fineberg (flute/saxophone), Ellen Seeling (trumpet), Jeff King (saxophone). Mixed by Don Puluse and Ron Johnsen.

Nested (Columbia PC 35449, 1978) was a more intimate studio album featuring Will Lee (bass), Andy Newmark (drums), Vincent Cusano (guitar), and Felix Cavaliere (keyboards/piano on tracks 7 and 10). Nyro: vocals, piano, songwriter.

Mother’s Spiritual (Columbia FC 39215, 1984) — self-produced (the first album she fully self-produced), with co-production assistance from Todd Rundgren and Nydia Mata. Personnel included Jan Nigro (acoustic guitar — her brother), Lisa Sunshine (bass), John K. Bristow (electric guitar), Nydia Mata (percussion). Engineers: Arthur Kelm, Chris Andersen. Mastered by Greg Calbi (Sterling Sound). Rundgren played synthesizer on tracks A7 and B2. A political and ecological record, thematically concerned with feminism, ecology, and animal rights.

Later Work and Posthumous Releases (1988–2001)

Laura (Laura Nyro Live At The Bottom Line) (Cypress Records, 1989) — self-produced live album.

Walk The Dog & Light The Light (Columbia 474296 2, 1993) was her final studio album released in her lifetime. Producer: Gary Katz (of Steely Dan fame). String arrangements by Carlos Franzetti. Nyro also took string arrangement credit. Band: Bernard Purdie (drums), Freddie Washington (bass), Elliott Randall, Ira Siegel, Michael Landau (guitar), Michael Brecker (saxophone solo), Randy Brecker, Lou Marini, Lawrence Feldman, Roger Rosenberg (horns), Ellen Uryevick (harp), Bashiri Johnson (percussion). It closed with Nyro covers of Curtis Mayfield’s “I’m So Proud” and the Shirelles’ “Dedicated To The One I Love.”

Angel In The Dark (Rounder Records ROUNDER 3176, 2001) — posthumous, assembled from recordings made in the 1990s. Produced by Nyro (posthumous release overseen by Eileen Silver-Lillywhite and Scott Billington). Features John Tropea, Will Lee, Freddie Washington, Bernard Purdie, Chris Parker, Randy Brecker, Michael Brecker, Bashiri Johnson. Mixed by Steve Rosenthal.

Go Find The Moon (The Audition Tape) (Omnivore Recordings, 2021) — pre-debut demo recordings.
