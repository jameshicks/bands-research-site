Holger Czukay

Bassist, multi-instrumentalist, producer, composer. Born Holger Schüring, 24 March 1938, Danzig (now Gdańsk, Poland). Died 5 September 2017, Weilerswist, Germany, age 79. Discogs artist ID: 3480. Known alias: Ghost Angel.

Co-founder and bassist of Can, and one of the central figures of the Krautrock movement. Studied composition under Karlheinz Stockhausen in Cologne in the 1960s; the influence of tape manipulation, collage, and found-sound shortwave radio permeated his solo work throughout his career.

Early Life and Stockhausen Studies

Czukay studied under Karlheinz Stockhausen at the Hochschule für Musik in Cologne in the mid-1960s, and subsequently taught at the same institution. There he met Michael Karoli, one of his guitar students, which led directly to Can’s formation.

Can (1968–1978 and beyond)

Czukay co-founded Can in Cologne in 1968 with Michael Karoli, Jaki Liebezeit, and Irmin Schmidt. Early vocalist Malcolm Mooney was replaced by Damo Suzuki, whom Czukay discovered busking in Munich in 1970.

Within Can, Czukay played bass and handled recording and engineering — the band self-recorded at their Inner Space Studio in Weilerswist. He was credited as “Technical Laboratory Chief” on the debut. He departed in 1977 as creative tensions grew over the funk direction of the later albums. Can briefly reunited in 1986, producing Rite Time (1989, Mercury).

Canaxis 5 (1969)

Before Can’s public profile solidified, Czukay and Rolf Dammers released Canaxis 5 (1969, Music Factory) — a tape-collage work layering Vietnamese folk recordings over electronics. Reissued 1982 on Spoon Records under Czukay’s name. Discogs master ID: 23135.

Solo Discography

Movies (1979)

Debut solo album. EMI Electrola / Harvest. A landmark of tape-collage pop: shortwave radio transmissions, found audio, Czukay multi-tracking all main instruments. Jaki Liebezeit on drums and congas; Conny Plank “Adviced By.” Czukay produced, arranged, played everything. Discogs release ID: 1281400.

On The Way To The Peak Of Normal (1981)

EMI Electrola / Welt-Rekord. Czukay on guitar, organ, vocoder, bass, French horn, flute, radio, mouth organ, congas. Liebezeit on drums; Conny Plank co-wrote and performed on one track. Discogs release ID: 72008.

Der Osten Ist Rot (1984)

Virgin. Co-written and performed with Liebezeit (drums, harmonium, trumpet, piano, organ). Conny Plank contributed Emu synthesizer. Czukay: dictaphone, vocals, guitar, bass, organ, mixed and processed by, recorded by. Cemented his shortwave-radio aesthetic. Discogs release ID: 81673.

Rome Remains Rome (1987)

Virgin. Czukay reunites with Can personnel and extends his collaborator network: Liebezeit (drums, trumpet, piano, percussion), Michael Karoli (guitar), Jah Wobble (bass, vocals). Czukay: guitar, organ, abs-bass, French horn, synthesizer, radio sounds, vocals, producer. Discogs release ID: 66780.

Plight & Premonition (1988) — with David Sylvian

Venture (Virgin sub-label). Co-equal collaboration: David Sylvian (piano, prepared piano, harmonium, vibraphone, synthesizers, guitar) and Czukay (electronics/radio, organ, sampled piano, orchestral and environmental treatments). Czukay produced; Liebezeit contributed infra-sound effects. Discogs release ID: 403968.

Flux + Mutability (1989) — with David Sylvian

Venture. Second Sylvian–Czukay album. Co-composed, co-produced; Czukay engineered. Discogs release ID: 55655.

Radio Wave Surfer (1991)

Virgin. Recomposition of existing material. Discogs release ID: 355847.

Moving Pictures (1993)

Mute. Czukay: bass, double bass, radio, French horn, guitar, synthesizer, produced. Guests: Jah Wobble (bass, track 6), Michael Karoli (guitar, track 6), Sheldon Ancel (vocals). Discogs release ID: 159821.

Good Morning Story (1999 / reissued 2010)

Originally Tone Casualties (US, 1999). Reissued by Claremont 56 as A Good Morning Story (cat. C56LP02, 2010). Czukay: guitar, bass, sampler (forged ethno samples), shortwave electronics, synth VS60, trumpet mouthpiece, vocals. Discogs release ID: 2112291 (C56 press).

The New Millennium (2003)

FünfUndVierzig. Czukay: music, instruments, producer. Discogs release ID: 1312695.

Der Osten Ist Rot / Rome Remains Rome (2014)

Grönland Records reissue compilation. Discogs release ID: 5943736.

Eleven Years Innerspace (2015)

Grönland Records. Archival compilation. Discogs release ID: 6912452.

Grönland Records. Posthumous. Discogs release ID: 11759742.

Grönland Records reissue. Discogs release ID: 12173170.

Key Collaborations

Snake Charmer (1983) — Jah Wobble, The Edge, Holger Czukay

Island Records, 1983. Three-way primary credit: Jah Wobble, The Edge (of U2), and Czukay. Produced by François Kevorkian. Wobble’s Invaders of the Heart as backing band. Stephen Street as second engineer. Arthur Russell is associated with this release in the stub (noted as lyricist), though the Discogs written-by credits on the primary pressing (ID: 31585) list Kevorkian, Czukay, Wobble, and Marland — Russell’s precise credit role remains to be confirmed (see Open Questions). Discogs release IDs: 31585 (main), 2618785 (second pressing).

David Sylvian (1984–1989)

Four shared releases:

  • Brilliant Trees (1984, Virgin) — Czukay: French horn, voice, guitar; contributor only
  • Words With The Shaman (1985, Virgin) — four-way credit with Jon Hassell, Steve Jansen; Czukay: electronics/radio
  • Plight & Premonition (1988, Venture) — co-headlined; co-produced
  • Flux + Mutability (1989, Venture) — co-headlined; co-produced, engineered

Jah Wobble (1983–1993)

Wobble appears across three Czukay releases: Snake Charmer (1983), Rome Remains Rome (1987, bass and vocals), Moving Pictures (1993, bass on track 6).

Conny Plank

Long-running studio partnership. Confirmed shared credits include:

  • Movies (1979) — Plank “Adviced By”
  • Phew (1981, Pass Records) — Czukay and Plank co-produced, co-wrote, co-edited with Liebezeit for Japanese artist Phew
  • On The Way To The Peak Of Normal (1981) — Plank co-wrote and performed
  • Der Osten Ist Rot (1984) — Plank on synthesizer
  • After The Heat (1978, Sky Records) — Czukay plays bass on B5; Plank engineered and co-produced the Eno/Cluster album (Discogs release ID: 116501)

Note: The stub states Czukay appeared on Cluster & Eno (1977), but Discogs release 8860305 shows no Czukay credit on that record. His confirmed appearance is on the follow-up After The Heat (1978).

Brian Eno / Cluster

After The Heat (1978, Sky Records). Primary artists: Eno, Dieter Moebius, Hans-Joachim Roedelius. Czukay: bass on track B5. Conny Plank: engineer and co-producer. Discogs release ID: 116501.

Open Questions

  • Arthur Russell and Snake Charmer: the stub says Russell wrote lyrics, but the Discogs written-by credits on the main pressing (ID: 31585) list Kevorkian, Czukay, Wobble, and Marland — no Russell. A second pressing exists (ID: 2618785), not yet checked. Russell’s specific credit role needs web confirmation or second-pressing lookup.
  • Cluster & Eno (1977): stub stated Czukay was guest bassist on this album (“Ho Renomo”). Discogs release 8860305 shows no Czukay credit. His confirmed appearance is on the follow-up After The Heat (1978). The track name “Ho Renomo” cited in the original stub is not visible in the Discogs credits — possible confusion or requires the full tracklist check.
  • Can catalog numbers for UA-era LPs not pulled in this session; available from Discogs if needed.
  • Exact nature of some Claremont 56 entries (e.g., Salmon Spungcake, 2019) — whether these are Czukay originals, remixes by others, or posthumous compilations using his material.
