Overview

David Smythe (Dr. D. K. Smythe) was the bass player who co-founded The Rezillos, the Edinburgh punk/new wave group, but departed after their debut single in 1977. He subsequently appeared with The Revillos, the successor outfit assembled around Fay Fife and Eugene Reynolds — the same core collaborators he shared the most credits with. His catalogue is sparse but long-spanned, with releases touching the late 1970s, the 1990s, and the 2000s–2010s through labels including Damaged Goods and Cherry Red, largely within punk and post-punk contexts.

Notes (web)

Departure from The Rezillos — voluntary, career-driven

Smythe’s departure was voluntary and amicable, not a falling-out. The trigger was the band’s decision to go professional after the Craigmillar Free Festival in July 1977. Other members who were art students obtained sabbaticals, but Smythe — already 30 years old and working as a research geophysicist — chose a different path.

In his own words: “I already had a promising career as a research geophysicist and had turned thirty, so I decided to quit.” (davidsmythe.org)

He also notes his practical contributions during the early period: he subsidised the band by providing his Volvo saloon and a long-wheelbase Transit van for touring Scotland. Hi-Fi Harris (Mark Harris, rhythm guitar) departed at the same time. Angel Paterson (Ali Donaldson) took over full-time on bass for the now-professional five-piece. (punk77.co.uk)

No conflict with the Discogs entry (“left the band in 1977 after the first single”) — this web source confirms the timing and adds the reason.

Post-Rezillos musical activities

After leaving The Rezillos, Smythe pursued his geophysics career. He returned to music later, spending roughly 15 years focused on singing (choirs, lieder, classical song as a tenor, with weekly private lessons) before taking up bass again. By around 2020 he was playing fretless bass in a Big Band jazz ensemble at a local conservatoire. (davidsmythe.org)
