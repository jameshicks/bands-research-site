Alan Vega

Born: June 23, 1938, Brooklyn, New York Died: July 16, 2016, New York City Full name: Boruch Alan Bermowitz Discogs ID: 54918 (also listed as Alan Bermowitz: 9009820) Role: Vocalist, visual artist Groups: Suicide, Vainio Väisänen Vega (VVV)

Overview

Co-founder and vocalist of Suicide alongside Martin Rev. Pioneer of electronic minimalist rock; Suicide’s confrontational 1977 debut (produced by Craig Leon) is a foundational proto-punk/synth-punk record. Solo career ran concurrently with Suicide from 1980 onward.

Solo debut Alan Vega (ZE Records/Celluloid Records, 1980) — a co-release on both labels. Further solo work on ZE through the mid-1980s.

Pre-Suicide: Visual Art Career

Bermowitz studied physics and fine art at Brooklyn College under Ad Reinhardt and Kurt Seligmann, graduating in 1960. Through the 1960s he was active in the Art Workers’ Coalition, a radical artists’ group that protested major museums. In 1969, with New York State Council on the Arts funding, he co-founded MUSEUM: A Project of Living Artists — a 24-hour multimedia gallery at 729 Broadway, Manhattan. Working as “Alan Suicide,” he moved from painting to light sculptures assembled from electronic debris: discarded televisions, radios, and fluorescent tubes plugged in so that whatever lit up became the piece. He held a residency at the OK Harris Gallery in SoHo (exhibiting through 1975; Barbara Gladstone continued to show his work into the 1980s). His signature works included neon crucifix assemblages from found metal in an arte povera style. [source: Wikipedia/Alan Vega, as of 2026-06-06; ArtNews]

A pivotal moment came in August 1969 when he saw The Stooges at the New York State Pavilion: “It showed me you didn’t have to do static artworks, you could create situations.” He met Martin Rev in 1970; with Rev and initially guitarist Paul Liebgott, he formed what became Suicide. Liebgott departed in 1971. Vega coined the term “punk music” for their sound, adopting it from a Lester Bangs article on the Stooges. [source: Wikipedia/Alan Vega, as of 2026-06-06]

Death (2016)

Vega died in his sleep on July 16, 2016, in New York City, aged 78. No cause of death was officially stated. He had suffered a stroke and a heart attack in 2012, which curtailed his live activity and led him to focus on painting. His death was announced publicly by Henry Rollins via an official statement from Vega’s family. [source: NPR; Variety]

Relationship with Henry Rollins / 2.13.61

Rollins first heard Suicide as a teenager c.1977 and has described “Frankie Teardrop” as “the single most intense song I’ve ever heard.” He met Vega in person on July 15, 1991, in NYC — cold-calling Vega through his agent and visiting Vega’s apartment, which was packed with paintings, sculptures, and notebooks. They spent approximately four hours together, and Rollins later wrote that “within a few minutes, I knew I was going to be friends with Alan.” Two summers later (c.1993), Rollins was living in NYC and visited regularly, helping Vega assemble a manuscript of his writing and sketches. This became Cripple Nation (2.13.61, 1994), a 128-page collection of Suicide-era lyrics, prose, and drawings — Vega’s first book, edited by Rollins.

The 2.13.61 label also issued the US edition of Dujang Prang (1996, licensed from Saturn Strip, Ltd.; the original was on Musidisc, France, 1995), and co-released the archival 7” Murder One with In The Red Records (original pressing 200 copies; later reissued). The relationship was one of genuine friendship and mutual admiration: Rollins was a self-described evangelist for Suicide and Vega’s solo work, and used his label as a platform for Vega’s output.

When Vega died on July 16, 2016, Rollins published the family’s official statement and dedicated a KCRW radio broadcast to him. His LA Weekly tribute was titled “Alan Vega Was One of the Few True Artists I’ve Ever Met.” He told Pitchfork that Vega had completed an unreleased album called It before his death. [source: LA Weekly; Exclaim!; Goodreads/Cripple Nation; In The Red Records]

Note on Dujang Prang dates: The solo discography table lists this as 1995 on Musidisc — that is the original French pressing. The 2.13.61 US edition is a separate 1996 release.
