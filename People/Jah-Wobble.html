Jah Wobble

Born: John Joseph Wardle, 11 August 1958, Stepney, London Discogs artist ID (solo): 3476 Discogs artist ID (Invaders Of The Heart): 37723

Overview

Jah Wobble is the original bassist of Public Image Limited (PiL), whose deep, dub-anchored bass work on Metal Box (1979) set a template for post-punk’s engagement with reggae and dance music. The name “Jah Wobble” originated as a slurred mispronunciation of “John Wardle” by Sid Vicious. After leaving PiL in 1980 Wobble built one of British music’s more expansive solo careers, releasing prolifically under his own name and as Jah Wobble’s Invaders Of The Heart from 1983 through the 2020s. His music spans dub, world music, ambient, dance, and jazz, and he ran his own imprint, 30 Hertz Records, from the mid-1990s. He is married to harpist Zi Lan Liao.

Early Career and PiL (1978–1980)

Wobble’s recording career began with PiL alongside John Lydon, Keith Levene, and Jim Walker. He appeared on Public Image (First Issue) (1978) and the landmark Metal Box (1979, Virgin) — the latter’s side-long bass-driven tracks remain among the most cited post-punk recordings. He parted with Lydon and PiL in 1980.

His earliest solo material appeared on Virgin. The debut solo LP The Legend Lives On… Jah Wobble In Betrayal (1980, Virgin, V 2158) features Martin Atkins on drums and covers “Blueberry Hill” alongside originals. A further single, V.I.E.P. Featuring Blueberry Hill (1980), and the collaborative EP Steel Leg V The Electric Dread (1978, with Don Letts) were also Virgin releases.

Full Circle and Island Records Phase (1981–1985)

Wobble’s pivotal early collaboration came with Can veterans Holger Czukay and Jaki Liebezeit. The LP Full Circle (1981, Japan/Trio-Kenwood) was a joint billing with Czukay, and its UK Island Records single How Much Are They? (1981) brought his bass into contact with the Can rhythm axis.

Snake Charmer (1983)

The EP Snake Charmer (Island Records, 1983) is the key document of this period and one of the more remarkable record credits of the era. Primary artists: Jah Wobble, The Edge (U2), and Holger Czukay. Backing band: Jah Wobble’s Invaders Of The Heart. Personnel:

Arthur Russell wrote lyrics for the record. The result is an intersection of PiL bass aesthetics, Can krautrock, NYC disco production (François Kevorkian), and Russell’s lyrical sensibility.

Island Records singles (1984–1985)

Further Island releases include Love Mystery (1985, featuring Shara Nelson on vocals) and Neon Moon (1985). Singles were engineered by Stephen Street and Rico Conning.

Lago Records and Independent Output (1983–1989)

The debut Invaders Of The Heart LP Invaders Of The Heart (1983, Lago Records) established the group name as a separate entity. Further Lago material includes Voodoo (1984), Blow Out (1985), and Tradewinds (1986). Psalms (1987, Wob Records) and Island Paradise (1987, Wob Records) followed on his own imprint.

The Legend Lives On… Jah Wobble In Betrayal (1980) had already set the dub-new wave template; the Lago and Wob years developed it toward world music and ambient directions.

The Invaders Of The Heart album Rising Above Bedlam (1991, Atlantic/Oval) is the major commercial statement of this period. Personnel:

The single “Visions Of You” (1992, EastWest) reached the UK charts. The period also produced Erzulie (1991), The Ungodly Kingdom EP (1992), and Without Judgement (1990, KK Records).

Radio Morocco (1990, 4th & Broadway) is a separate co-credited release: Pulse 8 featuring Jah Wobble and Justin Adams, produced by David Harrow.

Take Me To God and Island Records Return (1994)

Take Me To God (1994, Island Records) is the Invaders Of The Heart full-length of this period, produced by Wobble. Key personnel:

Singles from this cycle included Amor (1994), Becoming More Like God (1994), and The Sun Does Rise (1994), all on Island.

Spinner and the Eno Collaboration (1995)

Spinner (1995, All Saints Records / Gyroscope) is a co-headline album with Brian Eno. It is the most prominent one-off collaboration of this period. Personnel:

Jaki Liebezeit appears on drums; Justin Adams contributed on earlier tracks in the Invaders cycle around this time. Heaven & Earth (1995, Island) is the solo/Invaders LP from the same year.
