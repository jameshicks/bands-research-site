Overview

James Endeacott began his music career playing guitar with Loop (3) — by his own admission without much technical ability — before moving into A&R at Rough Trade Records. There he made his name signing The Strokes and The Libertines, among the most acclaimed rock acts of the early 2000s. In 2006 he launched 1965 Records, a London-based subsidiary of SonyBMG, which was active through at least 2011. His credits span from 1987 into the mid-2020s across labels including Rough Trade, XL Recordings, Blanco Y Negro, and Drag City.

Notes (web)

How Endeacott came to sign The Strokes to Rough Trade

The documented sequence, drawn from multiple sources:

Step 1 — The demo reaches Rough Trade via Matt Hickey. Matt Hickey, a booker at New York’s Mercury Lounge who was also working as a scout for Rough Trade, received a three-track demo from The Strokes. Hickey phoned Geoff Travis at Rough Trade early one morning and played roughly ten seconds of “The Modern Age” down the phone. Travis recalled: “Matt phoned up at about seven o’clock in the morning and played about ten seconds of the EP over the phone, and that was it.” (Yahoo/Guardian feature)

Step 2 — Travis hears the demo and commits. Travis was convinced by as little as 15 seconds of “The Modern Age” — its opening guitar riff — without hearing a full song. He flew to New Jersey to see the band live, then agreed to release the demo as the The Modern Age EP in January 2001. (Far Out Magazine)

Step 3 — Travis plays the demo to Endeacott. Endeacott’s own account: “Geoff Travis played me ‘The Modern Age’ one morning and it was like hearing all your favourite records in one. It was exhilarating.” He had not heard the band independently; Travis brought the demo to him. (NME, “Is This It at 20” feature, 2021)

Step 4 — Endeacott becomes the band’s dedicated A&R. Following the signing Endeacott handled day-to-day A&R for The Strokes at Rough Trade, covering their first two albums (Is This It and Room On Fire). He travelled to New York during the Is This It recording sessions with Gordon Raphael. When RCA in the US pushed back on the “unprofessional sound” and lobbied for big-name producers, Endeacott’s enthusiasm for Raphael’s work was a key counterweight: producer Raphael recalled that on hearing the first playback Endeacott “threw his hat up in the air.” Endeacott: “I went to New York for a few days when they were recording the album. The first thing I heard was New York City Cops. I screamed with joy when I heard it. Gordon was the right person for the job and the band knew what they were doing.” (NME, 2021)

His A&R philosophy on the signing: “At the time, The Strokes were against everything else in guitar music. It was dead and awful with record companies just churning out rubbish band after the next.” He signed them for an initial single and looked after them for two years. (ICMP blog, undated)

Summary: Endeacott did not discover The Strokes independently — the initial discovery path ran through Matt Hickey (Mercury Lounge/Rough Trade NYC scout) to Geoff Travis, who then played the demo to Endeacott. Endeacott’s documented role was as the Rough Trade A&R who then championed and managed the band’s UK career, serving as the primary label contact through Is This It (2001) and Room On Fire (2003).
