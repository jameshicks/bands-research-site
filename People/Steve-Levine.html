Steve Levine

British Grammy Award-winning record producer, engineer, songwriter, and musician. Discogs artist ID: 91282. Real name: Steven Mark Levine. Operates under his own production imprint, Do Not Erase Productions. Brother of David Levine (11).

Overview

Steve Levine came up as an assistant engineer in the mid-1970s at CBS’s London studios, working on sessions for Epic and Columbia artists including Gary Moore, The Clash, and The Dooleys. By the late 1970s he was co-producing and performing with Dance People (alongside Simon Humphrey) on the Satril label.

His career-defining work began in 1982 when he took on Culture Club as producer and engineer. He helmed every major Culture Club release through the band’s commercial peak: the debut Kissing to Be Clever (1982), the breakthrough Colour by Numbers (1983, featuring the UK and US no.1 “Karma Chameleon”), and Waking Up With the House on Fire (1984). He also co-produced the Believe It All album credited to a solo project. Together with Jon Moss, Levine handled digital mixing on the Colour by Numbers campaign — an early adoption of digital mixing technology that he championed publicly.

Beyond Culture Club he maintained a broad client list across the 1980s: China Crisis (debut album Difficult Shapes & Passive Rhythms, 1982); Gary Moore (Corridors of Power, 1982; singles 1983–84); David Grant (debut album, 1983, on Chrysalis); Helen Terry (Back into Blue, 1985); The Beach Boys (the 1985 self-titled LP on Caribou); French band Téléphone; Simply Red; Ziggy Marley and the Melody Makers; and the Honeyz (1998, Mercury). He had a writing and co-producing credit on the Beach Boys’ “Getcha Back” (Caribou, 1985).

Levine’s production work has been extensively reissued, with Culture Club catalog appearing on Virgin, Epic, SBK, and Caroline into the 1990s and 2000s.

Scene Connections

Levine sits at the centre of early 1980s UK commercial pop and new wave:

  • Culture Club orbit: worked closely with all four members of Culture Club — Boy George, Jon Moss, Roy Hay, Michael Craig — as well as backing vocalist Helen Terry and additional keyboardist Phil Pickett. The Culture Club records remain his most commercially visible work.
  • Virgin Records: the bulk of the Culture Club and China Crisis catalogue was on Virgin, making him a key creative figure in that label’s early-1980s pop roster.
  • Chrysalis / UK soul-pop: David Grant’s Chrysalis debut and singles tied Levine into the emerging UK funk-soul scene alongside Grant’s work with Jaki Graham.
  • AOR / US crossover: The Beach Boys 1985 LP and the Caribou connection placed him briefly in American rock/pop territory.
  • Reggae / Caribbean: Ziggy Marley and the Melody Makers (EMI-Manhattan, 1988) and the Reggae Philharmonic Orchestra extended his reach.
  • Early engineering apprenticeship: The Clash (Give ‘Em Enough Rope, credited as assistant engineer), Gary Moore, and The Dooleys were among his formative sessions at CBS London.
  • Dance People (Satril, 1979): His own artist project co-created with Simon Humphrey, for which he performed (drums, percussion, synthesizer) as well as producing and writing.
