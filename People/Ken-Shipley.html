Ken Shipley

Ken Shipley

Nov 16, 20264 min read

Ken Shipley

Discogs ID: 618240

Co-founder of Numero Group (2003, Chicago) alongside Tom Lunt and Rob Sevier. 549 producer credits on Numero Group — the label’s most-credited individual. Previously worked as A&R manager at Rykodisc; founded the label after losing that job, bringing with him an Antena reissue plan (Camino Del Sol, NUM002, 2004). Also previously founded his own label (Discogs ID 42747, 1995–2000). Member of the band September (10). Credited as producer on the very first Numero release (Everybody Down by Matthew (2), Numero 1, 2002) and as reissue producer on Savage Young Dü (Hüsker Dü, 2017), When I See The Sun (Codeine, 2012), and compilation producer on Eccentric Soul: The Capsoul Label (001, 2004).

Background and origin story

Ken Shipley — early life and career

Shipley grew up east of San Francisco as a skate-punk in the mid-1980s, listening to Minor Threat and Dead Kennedys cassettes and ordering 7-inch records from distant labels. [https://indyweek.com/news/culture/music/archival-record-label-numero-group-excavates-hidden-artistic-histories/] He lived in San Jose, California, in the early 1990s — later noting the irony that he was unaware of Ozeon, a local band he would eventually work with, despite living there at the time. [https://wxyc.org/blog/numero-group-in-conversation-with-cofounder-ken-shipley]

He founded Tree Records (1995–2000, San Jose) and moved through a series of record industry jobs before landing at Rykodisc as A&R manager, where he developed initial ideas for archival projects that never materialized at the label. [https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/labels-we-love-numero-group/]

Meeting Rob Sevier

Shipley and Rob Sevier crossed paths at Groove Distribution in Chicago, where Sevier had got Shipley a job. [https://indyweek.com/news/culture/music/archival-record-label-numero-group-excavates-hidden-artistic-histories/] By one account in a 2009 interview, Shipley says they met in a bar after he lost his Ryko job, and Sevier had already pitched an initial compilation concept called “Eccentric Soul.” [https://nerdtorious.com/2009/03/25/anonymous-exuberance-part-2-interview-with-ken-shipley/] The two hatched the Numero Group plan while both working at Groove Distribution. Shipley later said: “Rob and I were really fortunate to find each other because we both had a lot of drive to do it and to do it right.” [https://www.insidehook.com/music/the-best-record-label-in-america-doesnt-release-new-music] Sevier introduced Shipley to a DIY music universe he hadn’t previously recognised, while Shipley brought operational expertise in royalties and label management. [https://floodmagazine.com/126405/numero-group-at-20-feature/]

Meeting Tom Lunt

Tom Lunt was a former VP/creative director at Leo Burnett Worldwide advertising agency (1988–2000) who had also worked as a buyer at Streetside Records in St. Louis. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Lunt (as of 2025-11-16)] After a career in advertising, Lunt was unemployed when he crossed paths with Shipley at a grocery store in Chicago — both men were out of work at the time. Lunt subsequently provided roughly $23,000 in seed money to fund the initial phase of Numero Group. [https://nerdtorious.com/2009/03/25/anonymous-exuberance-part-2-interview-with-ken-shipley/] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Numero_Group (as of 2025-11-16)]

Founding Numero Group

Numero Group’s earliest conceptual form was an artist development imprint Shipley had in mind while at Rykodisc in 2002. After losing that A&R job in 2003, he and Sevier recast the idea as a direct-to-consumer CD company focused on rediscovering lost and forgotten music, modelled on UK reissue labels such as Soul Jazz Records and Honest Jon’s. [https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/labels-we-love-numero-group/] [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Numero_Group (as of 2025-11-16)]

The label was co-founded in February 2003. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Lunt (as of 2025-11-16)] Operations began in Shipley’s fourth-floor walkup apartment in Chicago’s Bucktown neighbourhood. [https://www.insidehook.com/music/the-best-record-label-in-america-doesnt-release-new-music]

The founding concept was partly shaped when Sevier discovered that soul artist Bill Moss had recorded dozens of tracks — not just the two Sevier already knew — for the Columbus, Ohio, Capsoul label. That discovery shifted their vision from a random 45s compilation toward label-focused narratives: “Labels would be much more interesting and could tell a nice tightly wrapped story,” Shipley said. [https://nerdtorious.com/2009/03/25/anonymous-exuberance-part-2-interview-with-ken-shipley/] Because Capsoul’s master tapes had been destroyed, Shipley and Sevier compiled the first release, Eccentric Soul: The Capsoul Label (2004), from clean rips of pristine vinyl sourced through crate-digging and eBay. [https://indyweek.com/news/culture/music/archival-record-label-numero-group-excavates-hidden-artistic-histories/]

Shipley’s immersion in Chicago’s soul music scene — particularly the Sheer Magic soul nights — helped him identify an underserved audience of collectors and connect with the community the label would serve. [https://nerdtorious.com/2009/03/25/anonymous-exuberance-part-2-interview-with-ken-shipley/]

Tom Lunt served as co-producer and art director for the label’s first 32 releases, receiving two Grammy nominations (2011 as art director; 2012 as co-producer alongside Shipley and Sevier), before retiring from Numero Group in April 2013. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Lunt (as of 2025-11-16)]

Note on co-founder name

The brief that commissioned this research referred to “Mark Lunt.” All sources — Discogs, Wikipedia, the existing page, and every web source consulted — consistently name the third co-founder as Tom Lunt, not Mark Lunt. No source was found for a “Mark Lunt” connection to Numero Group.
