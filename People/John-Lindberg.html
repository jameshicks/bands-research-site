John Lindberg

Discogs ID: 314873 Real name: John Arthur III Lindberg Born: 16 March 1959, Royal Oak, Michigan, USA Instrument: Double bass

American free-jazz and avant-garde bassist. Co-founder of the String Trio of New York (1977) alongside Billy Bang (violin) and James Emery (guitar). One of the most consistently active bassists in the post-AACM New York downtown scene from the late 1970s onward, with a parallel career as a composer and leader of multiple ensembles. Extensive long-term association with Wadada Leo Smith’s groups from the 2000s through the 2010s.

Name variations on record: J. Linberg, J. Lindberg, John Linberg, Lindberg, John Lindberg’s Tripolar.

Early Career and The Human Arts Ensemble (1977–1978)

Lindberg arrived in the New York scene in the late 1970s. One of his earliest documented credits is on Lowe And Behold (1977, Musicworks), recorded with Frank Lowe’s Orchestra — a large ensemble session that also included Billy Bang, John Zorn, Butch Morris, Joseph Bowie, Philip Wilson, and Eugene Chadbourne. This was an early meeting point for the downtown free-jazz generation.

He appears on Junk Trap (1978, Black Saint), credited to Charles Bobo Shaw / The Human Arts Ensemble / Joseph Bowie. The session also features James Emery on guitar and Luther Thomas on alto saxophone — the first documented recording shared by Lindberg and Emery. The Human Arts Ensemble (HAE) was a St. Louis free jazz collective active 1971–1977, connected to the Black Artists Group (BAG) orbit, including Julius Hemphill, Oliver Lake, Hamiet Bluiett, Baikida Carroll, Joseph Bowie, and Charles Bobo Shaw.

String Trio of New York (1977–c.1997)

Lindberg co-founded the String Trio of New York in 1977 with Billy Bang and James Emery. The trio was one of the defining chamber-free-jazz ensembles of the era, recording a sustained run for Black Saint from 1979 onward.

Own Ensembles and Leader Sessions (1982–2016)

Early Leader Work (1982–1985)

Lindberg led or co-led several sessions in the early 1980s, many on the Italian CECMA Records label:

  • In Duet Unison (1982, CECMA) — co-led session
  • Six Duets (1982) (1982, CECMA) — leader
  • Team Work (1982, CECMA) — leader
  • Dimension 5 (1982, Black Saint) — John Lindberg Quintet, with Marty Ehrlich (alto saxophone, flute), Billy Bang (violin), Hugh Ragin (trumpet, piccolo trumpet), Thurman Barker (drums). Lindberg wrote, produced, and wrote liner notes.

Trilogy Of Works For Eleven Instrumentalists (1985)

European Collaborations (1987–1993)

Lindberg was active in European free improvisation circuits from the late 1980s. Key sessions:

  • The Amiens Concert (1987, Label Bleu) — trio with Eric Watson (piano) and Steve Lacy (soprano saxophone)
  • Transition (1988, FMP) — trio with Louis Sclavis (bass clarinet, clarinet, soprano sax) and Heinz Becker (trumpet, flugelhorn)
  • Dodging Bullets (1992, Black Saint) — trio with Eric Watson (piano) and Albert Mangelsdorff (trombone)
  • The Fool School (1993, AA Records) — Eric Watson Trio: Watson, Lindberg, Bill Elgart (drums)

The Watson / Lindberg duo was a sustained working relationship across multiple formats. Discogs also lists a dedicated Watson/Lindberg Duo entity (ID 12528205).

Black Saint Leader Sessions (1996)

  • Resurrection Of A Dormant Soul (1996, Black Saint) — Lindberg leading quartet: Albert Mangelsdorff (trombone), Eric Watson (piano), Ed Thigpen (drums). Giovanni Bonandrini produced.

NoBusiness Records (2015)

  • Juggling Kukla (2015, NoBusiness Records, Lithuania) — duo with cellist Anil Eraslan. Lindberg co-composed, produced, played double bass.

Anthony Braxton Association

Lindberg appears on multiple Braxton large-ensemble and quartet sessions across the 1970s–80s:

  • Creative Orchestra (Köln) 1978 (recorded 1978, issued 1995, hat ART)
  • Seven Compositions 1978 (1980, Moers Music)
  • Four Compositions (Quartet) 1983 (1983, Black Saint)
  • Six Compositions (Quartet) 1984 (1985, Black Saint)
  • Various Braxton Bootleg releases: Performance 9/1/79, Orchestra (Paris) 1978, Quartet (Sweet Basil) 1985, Quartet (Salzburg) 1985

Anthony Braxton also conducted the Trilogy Of Works For Eleven Instrumentalists session (above).

Wadada Leo Smith Association (2000s–2016)

Lindberg became a core member of multiple Wadada Leo Smith ensembles from the early 2000s. Discogs lists him as a member of:

  • Wadada Leo Smith’s Golden Quartet (ID 1034747)
  • Wadada Leo Smith’s Golden Quintet (ID 4378479)
  • Wadada Leo Smith’s Great Lakes Quartet (ID 10277485)
  • Wadada Leo Smith’s Organic (ID 2364717)
  • Wadada Leo Smith’s Silver Orchestra (ID 10756051)
