Sjón

Lyricist, poet, novelist, and performer. Born 27 August 1962, Reykjavík, Iceland. Real name: Sigurjón Birgir Sigurðsson. Discogs artist ID: 149879. Also recorded and performed under the alias Johnny Triumph. Name variations in Discogs: Sjon, Sjorn, Sjòn, Sjón Sigurdsson, B. Sjon, Sijon.

Sjón is Björk’s primary lyrical collaborator and, for many years, her personal partner. He has co-written lyrics across her solo catalog from Post (1995) onward, with continuous work through the Selmasongs, Homogenic, Medúlla, and Volta cycles, and extending through the 2022 film score The Northman. Outside music he is an internationally published novelist — The Blue Fox, The Whispering Muse, Moonstone: The Boy Who Never Was, among others — and was nominated for an Academy Award (Best Original Song, with Lars von Trier and Björk) for “I’ve Seen It All” from Dancer in the Dark (2000). He also contributed to the Reykjavík avant-garde arts scene in the 1980s, including liner notes for releases by Þeyr-adjacent groups and the collective Smekkleysa.

Early Reykjavík Scene

Sjón’s earliest Discogs appearance is as lyricist on the 1993 retrospective “Ekki enn” by Purrkur Pillnikk (Smekkleysa, SMCD…, Iceland), where he wrote liner notes. Purrkur Pillnikk was one of the key post-punk groups in the Reykjavík scene that also spawned Kukl, The Sugarcubes, and the Smekkleysa collective. The Sugarcubes’ Discogs member list does not credit Sjón directly, but he is documented as having performed with them under the alias Johnny Triumph — an alias listed on his Discogs artist page, though the specific credits do not appear in the release database.

He wrote liner notes for the Motorlab #2 compilation (2000, Iceland) — a Reykjavík avant-electronics document featuring tracks by múm, Apparat Organ Quartet, and others, with text credited to him on tracks 1–5 (the múm set).

In 2006 he appeared as a primary artist on “Bergmál = Echo” (Smekkleysa, Iceland), a collaborative release with harpist Ragnhildur Gísladóttir and Japanese percussionist Stomu Yamash’ta. Sjón wrote lyrics; Valgeir Sigurðsson mixed and mastered. This was a live-installation work.

Also in 2006 he released “Ljóð” (Dimma, Iceland) — an album of spoken word/poetry with music composed by Agnar Már Magnússon, on which he appears as both primary artist and narrator.

Björk Collaboration — Core Discography

The Sjón/Björk lyric-writing partnership is among the most sustained in contemporary pop. The first Discogs-confirmed credits appear on Post (1995) and have continued through the 2020s.

Post (1995, One Little Indian / Mother Records)

Sjón is credited Written-By across multiple pressings of Post. The specific tracks co-credited to him on this album include “Isobel” — confirmed via the contemporaneous “Isobel” single (1995, One Little Indian, master 36157, 11 pressings). He also co-wrote “Hyperballad,” confirmed via the 1996 single (One Little Indian, master 34664). Telegram (1996), the remix album derived from Post, also carries his written-by credit (Mother Records, master 37287).

Homogenic (1997, One Little Indian)

Sjón’s most granular documented contribution to a Björk album from this period. On the UK LP (Discogs release 74263):

TrackSjón’s credit
A2Lyrics By, Written-By
A4Lyrics By, Written-By

Remaining tracks are credited solely to Björk. He is thus confirmed on at minimum two of the album’s nine tracks — likely “Jóga” (A2) and “All Is Full Of Love” (A4), though track mapping would require the tracklist confirmation. The “Bachelorette” single (1997, One Little Indian, master 34409, 16 pressings) also carries his Written-By credit, placing it alongside Björk. The “Jóga” single (1997, One Little Indian, master 36353) likewise credits him.

Selmasongs (2000, One Little Indian)

Soundtrack to Lars von Trier’s film Dancer in the Dark. Sjón is credited Written-By on tracks 2–7 alongside Björk, Mark Bell, and Lars von Trier. The “I’ve Seen It All” single (2000, Belgium, Universal) credits him “Lyrics By [Uncredited]” — the Academy Award-nominated song.

Medúlla (2004, Polydor / One Little Indian)

On the Japan pressing (Discogs release 315356), Sjón is credited Written-By on track 9 (“Miðvikudags…” — later released as “Where Is the Line” on various pressings). Other songwriters across the album include Olivier Alary (track 8), Jakobína Sigurdardottir (track 4), Tanya Tagaq (track 11), and E. E. Cummings (lyrics, track 10). The vocal-intensive album is primarily Björk’s compositional territory; Sjón’s contribution is focused here.

Biophilia (2011, One Little Indian / Nonesuch)

The UK pressing (Discogs release 3150578) does not credit Sjón on the album-level credits. The “Virus” single (2011, Nonesuch, USA & Canada, master 360029) carries his Written-By credit. The “Cosmogony” single (2011, One Little Indian, Europe, master 354393) also credits him Written-By. The “The Comet Song” single (2010, One Little Indian, UK, master 272777) credits him Lyrics By.

Several Biophilia remix releases also carry his Lyrics By credit: Biophilia Remixes | Part One (2012), Part Three (2012), Part Six (2012), all One Little Indian. The Biophilia Live release (2014, One Little Indian) credits him both Lyrics By and Script By [David Attenborough Script].

Volta (2007, Polydor)

The USA/Canada LP pressing (Discogs release 1192350) does not include Sjón in the additional credits, which list only production, mix, and design roles. His lyric contributions to Volta are documented externally but are not reflected in the main Discogs credits examined. The “Wanderlust” single (2008, One Little Indian, master 5414) does credit him Written-By.

Vulnicura (2015, One Little Indian)

The primary US pressing (Discogs release 6949425) does not credit Sjón in its additional credits — the album is credited exclusively to Björk as writer. Per Discogs data, Vulnicura appears to be a sole-authored Björk lyric album; the credited role “Folie à Deux” (2015, release ID in the DB) does show “Lyrics By” for Sjón — this appears to be a single or related release from that period carrying his credit. The main album pressing in the DB does not include him.

Utopia (2017, One Little Indian) and Fossora (2022, One Little Indian Independent)

The UK pressing of Utopia (Discogs release 11154831) and the worldwide pressing of Fossora (Discogs release 24616424) do not credit Sjón in their Discogs additional credits. These albums’ lyric credits in the database are attributed to Björk alone. Whether Sjón contributed uncredited or in a consultative capacity on these later albums is not established by Discogs.
