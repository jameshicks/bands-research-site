Malachi Favors

Real name: Malachi Favors Maghostut. Discogs artist ID: 255027.

Born August 22, 1927, Lexington, Mississippi; died January 30, 2004, Chicago, Illinois. American jazz bassist. One of the founding members of The Art Ensemble Of Chicago and a central figure of the AACM (Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians). Favors was almost without exception the bassist in every major AACM ensemble context from 1966 onward. Known for painted face, elaborate ceremonial costumes, and an approach to the instrument that covered the full range from deep arco drones to hard percussive pizzicato — and extended to banjo, zither, balafon, log drums, and “little instruments” (a category used by the AACM to describe non-standard found-sound objects). He held a compositional credit on the foundational AEoC record People In Sorrow (1969) and was the sole credited composer on his solo LP Natural & Spiritual (1978, AECO Records). He worked with the AEoC until his death in 2004.

Early Career — Chicago R&B and the Pre-AACM Circuit

Before free jazz, Favors worked Chicago’s commercial circuit. He is credited (as uncredited bass) on two 1956 singles on Ping Record Co. His first documented substantial jazz credit is on Andrew Hill’s debut album So In Love (1960, Warwick), a trio session with drummer James Slaughter. He also appears on Jazzville Chicago Volume 1 (1960, Top Rank International) and Stand By (1962, Argo), suggesting sustained activity in Chicago’s mainstream jazz scene before his orientation shifted with the AACM.

The Roscoe Mitchell Art Ensemble — Origins 1966–68

The immediate precursor to the Art Ensemble was Roscoe Mitchell’s working group on the South Side of Chicago. Favors was its bassist from the start. Two albums document this period:

Sound (1966, Delmark Records) — the debut of the Roscoe Mitchell Sextet. Personnel: Mitchell (alto sax, clarinet, recorder), Favors (bass), Lester Bowie (trumpet, flugelhorn, harmonica), Maurice McIntyre (tenor sax), Lester Lashley (trombone, cello), Alvin Fielder (percussion). Produced by Robert G. Koester for Delmark; supervised by Chuck Nessa.

Congliptious (1968, Nessa Records) — the ensemble now billed as the Roscoe Mitchell Art Ensemble. Personnel: Mitchell, Favors, Bowie, Rob Crowder (drums). Favors credited on bass, electric bass, and little instruments.

The Paris Sessions 1969–71 — BYG Records

The Art Ensemble (by now including Joseph Jarman) relocated to Paris in 1969 and recorded extensively for BYG Records in a burst of sessions that produced most of the foundational AEoC catalog. Favors’s multi-instrumental range is fully documented here — his credits across these records extend beyond bass to Fender bass, banjo, log drums, zither, cittern (cythar), sitar, and percussion.

People In Sorrow is notable for Favors sharing the writing credit alongside Jarman, Mitchell, and Bowie — rare for this period, and a mark of compositional standing within the group.

Les Stances À Sophie was the soundtrack to Moshe Mizrahi’s film, with vocalist Fontella Bass joining the ensemble. The original AEoC quartet of Mitchell, Jarman, Bowie, and Favors played the session; all four shared writing credits.

Archie Shepp sessions, BYG

During the same Paris period Favors appeared on two major sessions led by Archie Shepp:

Blasé (1969, BYG Records) — Favors on bass alongside Shepp (tenor sax), Dave Burrell (piano), Lester Bowie (trumpet/flugelhorn, one track), Jeanne Lee (voice), Philly Joe Jones (drums). A central document of the BYG era.

Yasmina, A Black Woman (1969, BYG Records) — Favors on bass.

Other BYG/Paris credits

  • Tarik (1970, BYG Records) — Favors on bass
  • Certain Blacks (1970, America Records) — Favors on percussion and bass

Early 1970s — Return to the US, Atlantic Records

Upon returning to the United States the AEoC signed to Atlantic:

Bap-tizum (1973, Atlantic) — a live recording. Favors: bass, electric bass, gong, log drums, whistle, vocals. Full five-piece: Favors, Mitchell, Jarman, Bowie, Famoudou Don Moye. Don Moye had joined the ensemble in Paris (1969–70) as the fifth member; Bap-tizum is the first widely distributed US recording with the classic quintet lineup. Produced by Jimmy Douglass, Michael Cuscuna, and Tunç Erim.

Fanfare For The Warriors (1974, Atlantic) — Personnel: Favors (bass), Mitchell, Jarman, Bowie, Moye, with Muhal Richard Abrams (piano) guesting. One of the AEoC’s most considered studio albums.

The Spiritual (1972, Freedom/Spiegelei) — recorded in Germany. Favors: banjo, bass, Fender, percussion, sitar.

Live At Mandel Hall (1974, Delmark Records) — live at the University of Chicago. Favors credited across bass, Fender, zither, banjo, bells, gong, log drum, whistle, horns, balafone, and vocals — one of the most complete instrument lists on any single AEoC record.

The Paris Session (1975, Arista/Freedom) — Favors: bass, electric bass, banjo, zither.

Solo Work — Sightsong (1976) and Natural & Spiritual (1978)

Sightsong (1976, Black Saint) — a duo album with Muhal Richard Abrams (piano). Favors credited as co-leader (primary artist, featured alongside Abrams). His roles: bass, miscellaneous percussion, vocals. He composed one track (B1); Abrams composed the remaining five. This is one of the most intimate documents of Favors’s playing — the only major duo recorded under his name.

Natural & Spiritual (1978, AECO Records) — Favors’s sole proper solo LP. All compositions credited to him. Credited as “Brother Malachi Favors Magoustous.” Bass and (uncredited) percussion. Released on AECO Records, the AEoC’s own imprint. One of the rarest entries in his catalog.

ECM Period — Nice Guys through The Third Decade (1979–85)

The AEoC moved to ECM Records for four studio albums, their most commercially successful:

ReleaseYearFavors’s credits
Nice Guys1979Bass, Percussion, Melodica
Full Force1980Bass, Percussion, Melodica, Vocals
Urban Bushmen1982Bass, Percussion, Melodica, Vocals
The Third Decade1985String Bass, Percussion (Bass Pan Drums, Cans), Electric Bass, Cymbal, Melodica, Balafon

All four produced by Manfred Eicher. Favors’s use of melodica (introduced by this period) became one of his signature timbral additions to the AEoC’s ensemble sound.

Kahil El’Zabar Collaborations

Favors maintained a long working relationship with drummer Kahil El’Zabar, who led the Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio (which Favors was a member of per his Discogs group listing).

The Ritual (1986, Sound Aspects Records) — co-credited to El’Zabar (featuring) / Lester Bowie / Malachi Favors. Favors: bass and composed by (some tracks). El’Zabar: drums, earth drum, shekere, voice, producer.

Sacred Love (1988, Sound Aspects Records) — listed as primary artist. Favors: bass, edited by.

Another Kind Of Groove (1987, Sound Aspects Records) — primary artist.

Alika Rising At Leverkusener Jazztage (1990, Sound Aspects Records) — primary artist.

Dennis Gonzalez Sessions — Silkheart Records (1987–88)

Favors appears on several records led by Dallas trumpeter Dennis Gonzalez for Swedish label Silkheart:

  • Namesake (1987, Silkheart) — double bass and voice
  • Liquid Magic (1987, Silkheart)
  • Bannar (1987, Silkheart)
  • Debenge-Debenge (1988, Silkheart)

These sessions (collectively: “Dennis Gonzalez New Dallas Sextet” and related configurations) placed Favors alongside musicians of a younger generation.

Later Career (1990s–2004)

Favors remained active with the AEoC through the final decade of his life. Key late-period releases:

Coming Home Jamaica (1998, Atlantic) — AEoC plus Jamaican musicians; Favors on bass, percussion, bells, whistle.

Live In Berlin (1998, West Wind) — AEoC live.

The Meeting (2003, Pi Recordings (2)) — one of the last AEoC studio sessions with Favors.

Urban Magic (2003, Musica Jazz) — AEoC live recording.

Sirius Calling (2004, Pi Recordings) — released in 2004; likely recorded shortly before Favors’s death.

Tribute To Lester (2003, ECM Records) — AEoC tribute to Lester Bowie (who died in 1999). Favors: double bass, bells, whistles, gongs, liner notes.

Name Variations

Discogs lists many variations of the name in print:

  • Brother Malachi Favors Magoustous / Magoustout / Magoustut / Magoustof
  • Malachi Favors Maghostut / Maghostus / Magostus / Magoustous / Meghismus / Megistus / Moghostut
  • Malachi „Maghostut” Favors
  • M. Favors / N. Favors (typos/OCR artifacts)
  • mf (uncredited abbreviated form)
  • マラカイ・フェイヴァース (Japanese transliteration)

The suffix “Maghostut” (also spelled variously) was a spiritual name adopted as part of his cultural and religious practice.
