Overview

Phil Wilson is a guitarist and vocalist best known as a member of The June Brides, an indie pop act that recorded for The Pink Label and In Tape in the mid-1980s before a brief stint on Creation Records in 1987. After a long hiatus, he re-emerged in the 2000s with The Granite Shore, releasing through Edition 59, Occultation Recordings, and Slumberland Records into the 2010s. His catalogue of 77 credits clusters in the 1980s and again from the late 2000s onward, with Frank Sweeney, Jon Hunter, Simon Beesley, and Arash Torabi as his most consistent collaborators across both bands.

The June Brides: Label History and Break-Up

Label sequence

The June Brides moved through three labels during their initial run:

  1. The Pink Label (1984–1985) — Founded by Simon Down specifically to release the band’s debut material after Alan McGee declined to sign them to Creation Records, reportedly because it would have been “too obvious.” Two Joe Foster-produced singles (“In The Rain”, “Every Conversation”) and the mini-LP There Are Eight Million Stories… (1985, produced by John O’Neill of That Petrol Emotion) came out here. The album reached no. 1 on the UK Indie Chart, charting for 38 weeks. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-10-03)

  2. In Tape (1985–1986) — The band grew dissatisfied with The Pink Label and moved to Marc Riley’s In-Tape label, believing its commercial orientation better matched their ambitions. Two further singles followed: “No Place Like Home” and “This Town.” (Wikipedia, as of 2026-10-03); Wilson quote on In-Tape’s appeal: palebloomsandbeyond.wordpress.com

  3. Creation Records (1987) — Wilson’s solo releases only; the band had already split by this point (see below).

Why the band broke up (1986)

The band dissolved in 1986 — not 1987. The proximate cause was Go! Discs withdrawing a previously-offered recording contract as the band “fell out of favour with critics and some of their early fans.” (Wikipedia, as of 2026-10-03) Wilson’s own account adds exhaustion and diminishing momentum: “We were tired and poor…it felt as if continuing would only produce diminishing results.” palebloomsandbeyond.wordpress.com

The Pink Label itself collapsed financially around the same time — by late 1986 mounting debt (partly from disappointing sales on Jamie Wednesday singles) made continuation impossible — but this was a parallel event, not a direct cause of the band’s split. The June Brides had already left the label for In-Tape before the Pink Label failed. (twee.net Pink Label history)

Phil Wilson solo on Creation Records (1987)

After the band split, McGee signed Wilson as a solo artist — Wilson suggested this was McGee’s way of “rectifying his earlier daftness” in not signing the band. Wilson released two solo singles on Creation in 1987: Waiting For a Change and 10 Miles. His third 1980s single, Better Days (1989), was on Caff Corporation, run by Bob Stanley of Saint Etienne, and was co-written with Primal Scream guitarist Andrew Innes. Official site; palebloomsandbeyond.wordpress.com

Summary: The Creation Records connection in 1987 is Wilson solo, not The June Brides. The label change sequence was Pink Label → In Tape (due to dissatisfaction with Pink Label), then band break-up in 1986 (Go! Discs deal collapse + exhaustion), then Wilson solo on Creation in 1987. The Pink Label’s own financial collapse in late 1986/1987 is a related but distinct event.
