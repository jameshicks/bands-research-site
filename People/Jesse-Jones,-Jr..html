Jesse Jones, Jr.

American jazz saxophonist and multi-instrumentalist based in South Florida. Discogs artist ID: 1208839. Name variations on record: Jesse Jones Jr., Jesse Jones, J. Jones, Jr.

Brother of trumpeter Melton Mustafa (Discogs ID: 577645).

Biography

Jesse Jones, Jr. is an alto saxophonist, soprano saxophonist, and flutist rooted in the South Florida jazz scene. He is the younger brother of Miami trumpeter and bandleader Melton Mustafa (1947–2017), and the two worked closely together through the mid-1990s and late 1990s on the Melton Mustafa Orchestra recordings for Contemporary Records.

Jones’s earliest traceable credit is an arranger role — specifically horn arrangement — on a 1980 funk 45 by Rivage, “Sittin’ On It / Strung Out (On Your Love)” (Tempus), where he is credited as “J. Jones, Jr.” A 1981 follow-up single release on Tempus carries the same credit. These early appearances show him active as both a performer and arranger in South Florida before the jazz orchestra work.

In 1982, Jones appears on a gospel album, There Is A God Somewhere by Andrew Wartts And The Gospel Storytellers (Champ Records), credited to drums — a reminder that his documented musicianship extends beyond saxophone. Notably, a Jesse Jones, Sr. is also credited on that same album (bass guitar, backing vocals), suggesting a family musical context.

By the mid-1990s, Jones was a full member of the Melton Mustafa Orchestra, the big band his brother had built in Miami. He plays saxophone on the orchestra’s debut album Boiling Point (1995, Contemporary Records), a large-ensemble session produced by John Goodwin, conducted and arranged by Melton Mustafa, with executive production by Bob Weinstock (founder of Prestige Records). Guests included Lonnie Smith on organ and Billy Ross on tenor saxophone and flute.

His most prominent credit is his co-billing on St. Louis Blues (1997, Contemporary Records), issued as “Melton Mustafa Orchestra With Jesse Jones, Jr.” On this record Jones plays alto saxophone, soprano saxophone, flute, percussion, and vocals — a comprehensive contribution. Melton Mustafa composed, arranged, conducted, and produced. Guest soloists were Ira Sullivan (trumpet, alto flute) and Pete Minger (trumpet). The album’s executive producer was again Bob Weinstock.

Jones also has two solo-leader releases in the database on Contemporary Records: Soul Serenade (1996) and After Hours (1997). After Hours is co-billed as “Eric Allison with Dr. Lonnie Smith, John Bailey (2) [and] Jesse Jones, Jr.” — a quartet/small-group session produced by Eric Allison (2), with Jones on alto saxophone alongside Lonnie Smith on piano and Hammond B-3, John Bailey (2) on trumpet, Turk Mauro on baritone saxophone, Dennis Marks on bass, and Danny Burger on drums. Executive producer was Bob Weinstock; mastered by George Horn.

A 1994 credit places Jones on saxophone for Anthony Watson (2)‘s R&B album Ain’t No End To The Rainbow! (Tashamba / Vision Records), with Betty Wright among the backing vocalists — situating him in Miami’s contemporary R&B recording world outside of jazz.

His personal website is listed on Discogs: jessejonesjrmusic.com.

Scene Context

Jones operated within the South Florida — primarily Miami — jazz community of the 1980s–1990s. That scene’s center of gravity in the DB is the Melton Mustafa Orchestra, which drew on a core of local players (John McMinn, James Cotmon, Milton Omar Dailey, and others recurring across both orchestra albums) while booking national guests such as Lonnie Smith, Ira Sullivan, and Pete Minger. Bob Weinstock, who ran Prestige Records in its earlier era, served as executive producer on all the Contemporary Records output in this cluster — an unusual late-career involvement linking the orchestra to bebop-era institutional memory.

Ira Sullivan, who moved from Chicago to Miami in the mid-1960s, represents the longer South Florida jazz tradition Jones and Mustafa were working within. Sullivan’s guest appearance on St. Louis Blues is a generational handshake within the local scene.
