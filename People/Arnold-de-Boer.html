Arnold de Boer

Discogs ID: 507996 Also credited as: Arnold Zea Nationality: Dutch (Netherlands) Roles: Vocalist, Guitarist, Sampler, Producer, Engineer, Trumpet Years active: c.1999–present Groups: Zea (primary project), The Ex (2009–present), The Ex Guitars

Biography

Origins: Zea (1999–present)

Arnold de Boer emerged from the Dutch independent scene via Zea, a one-man “breakpop” project based in Amsterdam. Zea first appeared on the 1999 split LP Last Laugh Is Guffaw alongside Birdskin, Seedling, and Zoppo on The Drowning Man/Transformed Dreams. The project operates as a solo vehicle — de Boer writes, plays all instruments, records, mixes, and masters himself — and extends to collaborating with an evolving cast of guests.

The early Zea records (Today I Forgot To Complain, 2003; Insert Parallel Universe, 2006) were released on Transformed Dreams. From 2009 onward, Zea moved to Makkum Records, which has remained the project’s primary home. The shift coincided with de Boer joining The Ex: the Split 7” on Makkum (2009) was the first release under the new label arrangement.

Zea has maintained a consistent output alongside The Ex work, releasing full-lengths roughly every two to three years: The Beginner (2010), Bourgeois Blues (2012), The Swimming City (2014), Moarn Gean Ik Dea (2017), Summing (2020), Witst Noch Dat D’r Neat Wie (2021), We Are Still Each Other’s Only Hope (2023), and In Lichem Fol Beloften (2025). The project is also a vehicle for de Boer’s production and engineering work — he handles recording, mixing, and mastering himself and has extended those services to other Makkum Records releases by outside artists.

De Boer’s instrument range expands on Zea records: on We Are Still Each Other’s Only Hope (2023) he is credited with guitar, vocals, trumpet, synth, and sampler. Witst Noch Dat D’r Neat Wie (2021) lists him on cuica, gyil (marimba), various percussion, piano, synth, and trumpet in addition to guitar and vocals. Minimal Guitar (2021) is a solo release credited to Arnold de Boer directly rather than Zea, with de Boer handling all performance, recording, mixing, mastering, and artwork entirely alone.

Joining The Ex (2009)

GW Sok had been the primary vocalist of The Ex since the band’s formation in 1979, departing after roughly three decades. De Boer joined as vocalist and guitarist in 2009 and made his debut on Catch My Shoe (2010, Ex Records), recorded at Electrical Audio in Chicago with Steve Albini engineering and mastered by Bob Weston. On that record de Boer is credited for vocals, guitar, and sampler alongside Andy Moor (2) (guitar, baritone guitar), Terrie Ex (guitar, baritone guitar), and Katherina Bornefeld (drums, vocals). The album also features Italian trumpeter Roy Paci on two tracks.

De Boer’s second Ex studio album was Enormous Door (2013, Ex Records), a joint record with the brass ensemble Brass Unbound that brought in Mats Gustafsson, Ken Vandermark (clarinet, baritone sax, tenor sax), Wolter Wierbos (trombone), and Roy Paci (trumpet). De Boer contributed guitar, sampler, and vocals, and is also credited for partial recording duties on Gustafsson’s parts. The record was produced collectively, with mastering by Gilius Kreiken.

27 Passports (2018, Ex Records) was a more stripped-down Ex record — the core quartet without guests. De Boer is credited with vocals, guitar, and handclaps; Andy Moor handled photography as well as guitar.

If Your Mirror Breaks (2025, Ex Records) is the most recent Ex studio album. Mastered again by Bob Weston, recorded by Jan Schenk (2). De Boer is credited with vocals and guitar.

The Ex Guitars

The Ex Guitars (Discogs ID: 15903296) is a sub-unit of The Ex consisting of the three guitarists: Andy Moor (2), Arnold de Boer, and Terrie Hessels. The project name is used when the guitar trio participates with other artists, per the Discogs profile. The unit appears linked to at least one documented release (r=33499205).

Production and Engineering Work

Alongside performance, de Boer functions as a producer and engineer, primarily for the Makkum Records orbit. Notable engineering/production credits outside his own work include:

  • Baro 101 (2012, Terp Records) — Photography By, Recorded By
  • Y’Anbessaw Tezeta (2012, Terp Records) — Trumpet
  • Wicked Leaders (2014, Makkum Records) — Recorded By, Mixed By, Mastered By
  • Six Dances (2016, Makkum Records/De Platenbakkerij) — Artwork, Recorded By, Mixed By, Mastered By
  • 1000 Can Die (2017, Glitterbeat) — Edited By, Producer, Mixed By
  • We Have One Destiny (2018, Makkum Records/Rebel Up Records) — Artwork, Mixed By, Producer, Mastered By
  • Sycophantic Friends (2017, Makkum Records) — Mixed By, Mastered By
  • Billow (2018, Terp Records) — Mastered By
  • Thirsty Ears (2018, Terp Records) — Mastered By
  • Abflug (2019, Terp Records) — Mastered By
  • Godverdomme Godverdomme De Liefde Godverdomme De Dood (2021, Katzwijm/Makkum) — Edited By, Mastered By, Recorded By, Mixed By
  • Energy Flashback (2022, Lianne Hall self-released) — Mixed By, Mastered By, Producer

He also supplied lyrics for Atomic (2)‘s Exit! (2013, Rune Grammofon).

Scene Connections

De Boer sits at the intersection of the Dutch post-punk/experimental tradition (The Ex lineage) and a looser Amsterdam DIY/improv scene centered around Makkum Records. The Ex connection brings him into contact with the international free-jazz and improvised music world — Mats Gustafsson, Ken Vandermark, Wolter Wierbos, Roy Paci — while Makkum Records situates him in a Dutch independent milieu that includes Fendika (Ethiopian collaborators, co-credited on Lale Guma / Addis Hum) and broader global music crossovers. His engineering role on Terp Records releases further connects him to the Dutch world music and Afrobeat-adjacent scene.

The Ex’s original post-punk context (1979 Amsterdam squatter scene, anarcho-punk, DIY politics) is the lineage he stepped into in 2009; he inherited that legacy from GW Sok.
