Dan Catsis

Bassist, guitarist, vocalist. Real name: Daniel Bernard Catsis. Bristol, England. Discogs ID: 652160. Name variations: Catsis, D. Catsis, Dan Katsis.

Co-founder of Glaxo Babies (1977); bassist with The Pop Group (1979–80); founding member of Maximum Joy (1981–83); session player with The Blue Aeroplanes (1983); rejoined The Pop Group for reunion (2010–ongoing).

Era 1 — Glaxo Babies, Co-Founder (1977–1980)

Catsis founded Glaxo Babies in Bristol in late 1977 with Geoff Alsopp (drums) and Tom Nichols (bass), taking the guitar and vocals role himself. The band recorded prolifically for Heartbeat Records across 1979–80, releasing four EPs and a single before dissolving.

Era 2 — The Pop Group (1979–1980)

Catsis joined The Pop Group as bassist in 1979, overlapping with the final stages of Glaxo Babies. He appears on two of the band’s key releases from this period.

Era 3 — Maximum Joy (1981–1982)

Catsis was a founding bassist of Maximum Joy alongside Charlie Llewellin, Tony Wrafter, Janine Rainforth, and John Waddington — all former Glaxo Babies and Pop Group associates. Their releases leaned into funk, dub, and post-punk dance music.

Catsis’s tenure covered the band’s earliest recordings (1981–early 1982), before Kev Evans took over on bass for Station M.X.J.Y. and the later singles. The 2017 singles compilation I Can’t Stand It Here On Quiet Nights: Singles 1981–82 (Silent Street SSR001) credits Catsis on Bass for tracks A, B1, C1, and D1 — corresponding to:

  • “Silent Street / Silent Dub” (track A) — the B-side of the “Stretch” 12”
  • “White And Green Place (Extraterrestrial Mix)” (track B1)
  • “Building Bridges / Building Dub” (track C1) — an otherwise uncredited single
  • “Stretch (7″ Mix)” (track D1)

Kev Evans is credited on the remaining tracks (B2, C2, D2: “In The Air,” “Simmer Til Done,” “Do It Today”), confirming the bassist changeover happened between these sessions. Catsis does not appear in the credits for Station M.X.J.Y. (1982) or later Maximum Joy releases.

Both Catsis and Tony Wrafter played saxophone on this early Blue Aeroplanes debut, with Charlie Llewellin on bells — the Maximum Joy rhythm section appearing as Bristol scene guests. Steve Street (also from the Glaxo Babies orbit) engineered.

Era 5 — Gap Years (c.1983–2010)

No Discogs credits appear for Catsis between the 1983 Bop Art session and the 2006/2007 Cherry Red retrospective compilations (Dreams Interrupted, The Porlock Factor), on which he holds artwork, guitar, bass, piano, vocals, and written-by credits for archival Glaxo Babies recordings. The interim period (c.1983–2010) is undocumented in Discogs.

Era 6 — The Pop Group Reunion (2010–2016)

Catsis rejoined The Pop Group for their 2010 reunion tour and remained for two studio albums.
