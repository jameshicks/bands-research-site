Overview

Lawrence Livermore (born Larry Hayes, 1947) is an American musician, producer, and music journalist best known for co-founding Lookout! Records with David Hayes — the label that defined the East Bay pop-punk scene of the late 1980s and early 1990s and launched Green Day. Before the label existed, he ran the Lookout! fanzine and fronted The Lookouts, a California punk band whose drummer was a teenage Tre Cool. Between 1987 and 2007 he was a prolific music journalist, writing for Maximum Rocknroll and later Punk Planet; he stepped back from running Lookout! in 1997, handing ownership to Chris Appelgren. His 94 Discogs credits run from 1987 into the 2020s across roles as producer, guitarist, vocalist, and songwriter, with the bulk of his work through Lookout! Records and later appearances on Asian Man, Don Giovanni, and Hellcat; his tightest collaborator cluster is the Screeching Weasel circle — Ben Weasel, Danny Vapid, Jughead, and B-Face — alongside engineers Andy Ernst, Kevin Army, Mass Giorgini, and John Golden.

Notes (web) — Departure from Lookout! Records, 1997

Livermore’s exit was not a clean planned handover but the product of converging personal and professional pressures, as he describes in detail in his own writing and interviews.

Post-Green Day commercial drift. After Green Day’s 1994 major-label breakthrough on Reprise, Lookout’s profile and income grew sharply, and that brought new strain: bands increasingly expected major-label-style advances, tour support, expensive videos, and promotional budgets. Livermore described the feeling as “bands that wanted the benefits — fame and fortune — of being on a major label but wanted to retain the credibility of being on an indie label” — a shift in expectation that clashed with his founding ethos. larrylivermore.com/the-end-of-lookout/

The Screeching Weasel crisis as proximate trigger. The breaking point involved Ben Weasel’s demands for unrealistic budgets and a subsequent contract dispute. When Livermore pursued legal action to prevent Screeching Weasel from reclaiming their catalog, Chris Appelgren threatened to leave the company, creating an internal conflict that made Livermore’s position untenable. Livermore was clear that the dispute was “from start to finish, a Ben Weasel dispute” rather than a band-wide breakdown. jgtwo.com interview, 2013

Personal burnout and mental health. Alongside these external pressures, Livermore was experiencing depression and described his own emotional instability as “a big part of my reason for leaving Lookout when I did.” He had grown exhausted by the shift from passion project to commercial operation — resenting that he was spending nearly all his time promoting others’ work — and was drinking heavily as a coping mechanism. The Willamette Week profile (2016) quotes him: “I was depressed and angry about how everything had turned out.” wweek.com, 2016

The handover. Livermore signed ownership over to Chris Appelgren — then 24 — in early 1997 and flew to London. He has stated that he structured the exit so definitively that he had no mechanism to intervene later, even as the label’s decline became visible. He has expressed regret that Appelgren “took the label in a new direction that eventually became a dead end,” while also acknowledging that his own state at the time made continued stewardship unlikely. Lookout! stopped releasing albums in 2005 and ceased operations around 2012.
