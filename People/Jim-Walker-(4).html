Overview

Jim Walker (born James Simmers Walker, 1955, Edmonton, Alberta, Canada) is a drummer best known as the original member behind the kit for Public Image Ltd., the post-punk group John Lydon assembled after the Sex Pistols’ collapse. He joined PIL at its formation in May 1978 and appears on the band’s debut album First Issue, but departed by late January 1979 — the exit was reported in Melody Maker on 27 January 1979. His core PIL collaborators — John Lydon, Keith Levene, and Jah Wobble — account for the bulk of his 75 Discogs credits, all rooted in punk, post-punk, dub, and new wave. After PIL he drummed for The Pack (3), Kirk Brandon’s pre-Theatre of Hate outfit, joining in April 1979, then for The Straps from July 1980; he also appears under The Furies and The Human Condition.

Notes (web)

Walker’s departure from PIL — 27 January 1979 Melody Maker and later interviews

The 27 January 1979 Melody Maker report

No digitised or transcribed text of the 27 January 1979 Melody Maker item has been found online. The date is referenced on Discogs (and therefore in the “From Discogs” section above) but the article itself does not appear to have been reproduced anywhere on the open web. The specific wording of that report, and whether it quoted Walker or Lydon or merely announced the departure, could not be confirmed. Sources checked: Wikipedia (Jim Walker and PIL articles), fodderstompf.com, Louder Than War, a proboards.com fan thread; none reproduce the article text. The Melody Maker archive (paywalled, not web-accessible) would be the only route to the primary source.

Walker’s own account of why he left

Walker addressed his departure in at least two interviews, both available online:

  • Fodderstompf.com undated interview (fodderstompf.com/INTERVIEWS/walkint.html): Walker cited lack of professionalism and inactivity: “When I left PiL it was because we never rehearsed, or played any gigs, we didn’t conduct ourselves like the businessmen we were always bragging about being.” He also noted the financial reality: “two years earlier I’d been earning two and a half times more in wages as a construction surveyor. PiL hardly seemed like a profitable enterprise.” On the December 1978 Rainbow gigs he said: “I knew there and then it was time to pull the plug once and for all.” He described the band as having become “the Johnny Rotten Band” rather than the collaborative unit he had joined, and said: “I’d have never joined to start with had I known what was going to happen.”

  • October 2002 fan-forum interview (fuggle77.proboards.com/thread/591/jim-walker-interview-october-2002): Walker stated: “Getting ripped off, business wise was behind my decision to leave; that and the fact PiL under Lydon soon degenerated into something not unlike Col. Kurtz’ camp from Apocalypse Now…No Method.” He also described people around Lydon as “Parasites posing as employees, bleeding the PiL organisation white.” He added: “As soon as it became absolutely clear that PiL was never going to function as a proper band it was time to leave.” Note: source is a fan forum; treat with appropriate caution.

Both accounts consistently attribute the departure to financial grievances (feeling ripped off on the business side) and disillusionment with the band’s failure to operate as a functioning, rehearsing, gigging unit — not primarily musical/stylistic disagreement.

Fodderstompf.com member page (fodderstompf.com/MEMBERS/walker.html) also carries a quote from Walker that he had “simply become increasingly jaded and disillusioned with life in and around PiL,” and from Jah Wobble that they were “an extremely formidable rhythm section that never got to realise its full potential” and that things “came a bit too quick for Walker” — which Walker disputed. Sources for those quotes are not cited on that page.

Lydon’s account

John Lydon characterised Walker’s exit as happening “in a flurry of solicitors letters” — cited on fodderstompf.com member page (no source date given). Lydon also said of Walker generally: “We had a fuckin’ good drummer called Jim Walker, but he fucked off after a few months.” Source for this quote appears in search results attributed to Lydon but without a specific interview date or publication; it has not been traced to a primary source here. Walker was also interviewed for the 2017 documentary The Public Image is Rotten, where he recalled “a huge undercurrent of potential danger at all times” around the early band — the documentary does not appear to address his departure directly in available press coverage of it.

Wikipedia conflict note

Wikipedia (as of 2026-11-03, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Walker_(drummer)) describes Walker as “dissatisfied with the band’s direction” — a characterisation Walker himself does not use in either interview found. His stated reasons are financial grievance and the band’s failure to function as a working unit. The “direction” framing may be Wikipedia’s own summary rather than a direct quote. No conflict with Discogs data.
