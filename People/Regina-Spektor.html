Regina Spektor

Russian-American singer-songwriter and pianist. Discogs artist ID: 413427. Born Regina Ilyinichna Spektor on 18 February 1980 in Moscow, USSR; emigrated to New York City as a child; raised in the Bronx.

Overview

Spektor’s career divides cleanly into two phases: a grassroots self-release period (1999–2004) rooted in the NYC anti-folk scene, and a major-label period (2004–present) on Sire Records within the Warner system. Across both phases she remained a consistent co-producer of her own records and the sole author of her material. Her piano-driven songwriting, idiosyncratic vocal technique (glottal pops, beatboxing, deliberately “wrong” notes), and theatrical storytelling set her apart from most singer-songwriter contemporaries.

She is often associated with the Sidewalk Café anti-folk scene in Manhattan, where she played alongside artists like The Moldy Peaches and Adam Green in the early 2000s.

Early Self-Released Work (1999–2004)

Spektor’s first recordings were distributed informally at shows and online. The 1999 Demo (Not On Label) is the earliest Discogs entry. Two proper self-released albums followed on her own imprint Shoplifter Records: 11:11 (2001) and Songs (2002).

11:11 (2001) was co-produced with Richie Castellano, who also engineered and mixed; Spektor played piano and guitar and co-designed the packaging with David Panarelli. Mastering by Eric Helmuth.

Songs (2002) is an even more stripped-down document: Spektor alone on piano and vocals, engineered and mastered by Joe Mendelson.

Soviet Kitsch (2003) was the self-released album that generated industry attention. Produced by Spektor, Gordon Raphael, and Alan Bezozi; engineered in New York City by Toshi Yoshioka (with Josh Stoddard and Will Kelly assisting) and in London by Matt Hyde; mastered by Fred Kevorkian. Spektor also arranged the strings. The album was picked up by Sire Records and commercially re-released in 2004 (also credited to Shoplifter Records on the co-release).

A limited UK EP, Your Honor & The Flowers (2004, Shoplifter Records), appeared around the same time with no additional production credits beyond Spektor herself.

Her 2004 duet Modern Girls & Old Fashion Men with The Strokes’ Julian Casablancas was released on BMG/RCA and appears on the Strokes’ side of the Discogs graph as well.

Transition to Sire / Transgressive (2005–2006)

The Carbon Monoxide EP (2005) was released on Sire in the US and Transgressive Records in the UK. Produced by Raphael, Bezozi, and Spektor; side B engineered, mixed, and mastered by Pier Giacalone.

Mary Ann Meets The Gravediggers And Other Short Stories (2005, Sire / Transgressive) is a UK/Europe/US compilation collecting earlier material. Tracks 3, 4, and 11 produced by Raphael, Bezozi, and Spektor; tracks 1, 6–9 engineered and mastered by Joe Mendelson; tracks 2, 5, 10 engineered by Richie Castellano. Mastering on select tracks by Bob Ludwig. The album features illustrations by Julie Morstad.

Us (2005, Sire) — EP; no additional credits in DB.

Live At Bull Moose (2005, Sire) — live release, Spektor credited for piano, voice, liner notes, and written-by.

Begin To Hope and Breakthrough (2006)

Begin To Hope (2006, Sire) was produced by David Kahne, who also engineered and mixed. Spektor co-produced. Drums on six tracks by Shawn Pelton. Mastered by Bob Ludwig. Photography by Adria Petty. This album produced the singles “Fidelity,” “Better,” “Samson,” and “On The Radio,” all of which generated significant streaming and licensing attention.

Singles from the album were issued across Europe and the UK on Sire throughout 2006.

Far (2009)

Far (2009, Sire) was Spektor’s most production-intensive album to date, with five separate producers handling different tracks:

Spektor co-produced tracks 1, 2, 5, 7, 12–14 herself. Mastered by Bob Ludwig. Executive producer: Tom Whalley. Engineer: Adam Hawkins (tracks 1, 2, 5, 13, 14). The Jeff Lynne involvement (ELO, Traveling Wilburys) on five tracks is the most unusual production credit on any Spektor album.

What We Saw From The Cheap Seats (2012)

What We Saw From The Cheap Seats (2012, Sire) produced by Mike Elizondo, with Spektor co-producing. Engineered and mixed by Adam Hawkins; mastered by Bob Ludwig. The album yielded the single “You’ve Got Time” (2013), which became the theme for the Netflix series Orange Is the New Black.

Remember Us To Life (2016)

Remember Us To Life (2016, Sire) produced by Leo Abrahams, with Spektor co-producing. Mixed by Dan Grech-Marguerat; mastered by Bob Ludwig (CD) and Chris Bellman (vinyl). Engineered by Samur Khouja.

Live On Soundstage (2016, BMG) — concert recording.

Home, Before And After (2022)

Home, Before And After (2022, Sire) produced by John Congleton, who also engineered and mixed. Spektor co-produced and wrote all songs. Mastered by John Davis. Features the FAME’S Skopje Studio Orchestra conducted by Oleg Kondratenko — Spektor’s most orchestrally ambitious record. Photography by Shervin Lainez.
