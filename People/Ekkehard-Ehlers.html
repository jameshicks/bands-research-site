Ekkehard Ehlers

Ekkehard Ehlers

Aug 24, 20269 min read

Ekkehard Ehlers

Discogs ID: 15493
Born: 1974, Frankfurt am Main, Germany
Based: Berlin, Germany

Sound artist and producer working primarily in the digital manipulation and re-composition of existing music. One of the central figures in the German microsound/electroacoustic scene of the early 2000s, closely affiliated with the Staubgold and Mille Plateaux labels. Ehlers builds works that process and deconstruct recorded source material — blues, free jazz, contemporary classical — while keeping the referent audible, creating a kind of critical citation through sound.

Aliases: Auch, Betrieb, Ferdinand Fehlers
Groups: Autopoieses, März, Schwabinggrad Ballett

Biography

Frankfurt / Late 1990s — Origins in the Force Inc. Network

By the late 1990s Ehlers was working at the Force Inc / Mille Plateaux Frankfurt office, curating the Mille Plateaux label series. This positioned him at the heart of the German click-and-cut and microsound movement — a network that included Stephan Mathieu, Marcus Schmickler (aka Pluramon), and Sebastian Meissner (aka Klimek). During this period he launched the Auch alias (Discogs ID: 5729) for club-oriented, dancefloor-adjacent work released on Force Inc. Music Works (five EPs/singles, 2000–2001), signaling that his interests spanned both the conceptual and the functional.

His debut under his own name, Betrieb (Mille Plateaux, 2000; cat. MP85), consists of 17 short tracks titled in bilingual German–English pairs (“On = Auf”, “Inside = Innen”, etc.). The album sits squarely in the abstract/minimal digital territory Mille Plateaux was then defining, with no additional credited contributors.

2000–2002 — The “Plays” Series and Staubgold

Ehlers’ signature mode crystallised almost immediately on Staubgold: a series of works titled “Plays [proper name]”, each reprocessing recordings associated with a specific figure from music, cinema, or literature. The releases unfold as critical homage — not cover versions or sampling in the hip-hop sense, but extended digital re-reads in which the source is audible yet transformed.

  • Plays Robert Johnson (Bottrop-Boy, 2001; 7”) — earliest instalment; two untitled sides, processed Delta blues
  • Plays Albert Ayler (Staubgold, 2001; staubgold 19; 12”) — liner notes by Diedrich Diederichsen
  • Plays John Cassavetes (Staubgold, 2001; staubgold 21; 12”) — Side B uncredited production by Stephan Mathieu
  • Plays (Staubgold, 2002; staubgold 30; CD) — compilation gathering the Cardew, Fichte, Cassavetes, Ayler, and Johnson sides; liner notes by Diedrich Diederichsen; guitar on the Hubert Fichte pieces by Joseph Suchy; cello on the Albert Ayler pieces by Anka Hirsch. Mastered by Suchy and Peter Fey. This remains the definitive document of the “Plays” series and was reissued on Keplar in 2022.
  • Plays Hubert Fichte (Staubgold, 2002; 12”) — literature-derived piece

The Plays records established Ehlers’ position as an intellectual figure in the experimental scene: the source material (Ayler’s free jazz, Johnson’s blues, Cardew’s political music, Cassavetes’ cinema) carried embedded critiques that extended into his processing choices.

He simultaneously contributed as writer/producer to a run of releases on Klang Elektronik for other artists (Balthus EP 2001, Betty Ford remix 2001, Harmolodic House 2001, Schlechte Vorbilder 2002, Bubifunk EP 2003, Sekt 2003) and compiled the FrequencyLib compilation (Ritornell, 2001).

Also in 2001: Heroin (Staalplaat; cat. brombron 02), a full-length collaboration with Stephan Mathieu — electroacoustic/glitch, with organ overdubs on two tracks by Katja Mathieu. One of the period’s key examples of German microsound meeting drone and electroacoustic approaches.

White Cube Jazz (En/Of, 2001) — a conceptual release co-credited with visual artist Liam Gillick.

2002–2004 — März, Institutional Commissions, and Expansion

During this period Ehlers co-formed März (Discogs ID: 37063) with Albrecht Kunze. The duo released four albums on Karaoke Kalk (2001–2004): One From The Heart (2001), Love Streams (2002), The River (2004), and Wir Sind Hier (2004).

Politik Braucht Keinen Feind (Staubgold, 2003; staubgold 41) is a departure into chamber music: two commissioned pieces for Schirn Kunsthalle Frankfurt (Mäander, for bass clarinet) and Ballett Frankfurt (Woolf Phrase, a 21-minute work) plus Blind for cello quintet. Credits include bass clarinet by Burkard Kunkel, cello by Anka Hirsch, compositional collaboration on Mäander by Markus Weisbeck, Nikolaus Hirsch, and Michel Müller. Mastered by Joseph Suchy. The album marks a move toward notated/performed music that would continue.

Soundchambers (Staubgold, 2004; staubgold 49) — trio recording with Franz Hautzinger (trumpet) and Joseph Suchy (guitar, balafon). Five untitled tracks of electroacoustic improvisation. Additional sound contributions: noise recordings from Stuttgart’s heating rooms by Alexander König, piano recordings by Adam Butler (aka Vert). Engineered by Marcus Schmickler; graphics by Markus Weisbeck.

Ehlers also co-produced La Beat (Trikont, 2005) and was credited as songwriter/producer on releases through Karaoke Kalk.

Childish Music (Staubgold, 2005) — Ehlers compiled and contributed sleeve notes.

2005–2009 — Schwabinggrad Ballett, Ferdinand Fehlers, and A Life Without Fear

The mid-to-late 2000s expanded Ehlers’ work into explicitly political-collective terrain. Schwabinggrad Ballett (Staubgold, 2005; staubgold 61) is the debut LP of the theater/music collective Schwabinggrad Ballett — a large ensemble including Bernadette La Hengst, Roberto Ohrt, Thomas Butteweg, and others — produced by Pascal Fuhlbrügge, Peta Devlin, and the collective itself. The group later released Beyond Welcome! (Buback, 2016), with lyrics and music credited to Ehlers.

Under the alias Ferdinand Fehlers (Discogs ID: 412121), Ehlers issued Hearth (Meteosound, 2005), a 12” with abstract dub/house coloring, with a B-side remix by Pole. The Meteosound label (Meteo 018) placed this firmly in the post-dub electronic milieu.

A Life Without Fear (Staubgold, 2006; staubgold 66) is arguably the most ambitious single Ehlers release: an LP drawing on gospel, African mbira, Appalachian folk, and Zimbabwean music, processed and arranged for an ensemble including Franz Hautzinger (trumpet), Joseph Suchy (guitar, balafon), Reinhold Friedl (piano), Björn Gottstein (viola), and vocalists Howard Katz Fireheart and Charles Haffer Jr.. Written-By credits acknowledge Ralph Stanley (B5), Dumisani Abraham Maraire (B2), and Traditional (A1). Mastered by Rashad Becker.

Ballads (Staubgold, 2009; staubgold digital 1) — recorded with percussionist Paul Wirkus; additional players Kai Fagaschinski (clarinet), Achim Tang and Werner Dafeldecker (double bass). Track titles in Polish. Staubgold’s first “digital 1” catalog number marks the label’s move into digital releases.

2010s — Adikia and Continued Practice

Adikia (Staubgold, 2012; staubgold digital 20) — a single 26-minute work. Ensemble: Kai Fagaschinski (clarinet), Werner Dafeldecker (double bass), Paul Wirkus (percussion), Eva Reiter (viol, subcontrabass recorder), Björn Gottstein (viola), Todosch (vocals). Mastered by Rashad Becker.

Ehlers was sampled on Live @ De Appel, November 2nd 2002 (Nostalgie De La Boue, 2014).

Mikado (Amplify 2020, 2020) — digital single with Dean Roberts; classified as Sound Art / Field Recording.

Groups

Autopoieses

Duo with Sebastian Meissner (aka Klimek / Random Industries). Discogs ID: 3831. Website: autopoieses.de. No albums found in Discogs; primarily a live/installation project.

März

Duo with Albrecht Kunze. Discogs ID: 37063. Website: maerzmusik.net. Four albums on Karaoke Kalk, 2001–2004, ranging from pop-song structures to abstract electronics.

Schwabinggrad Ballett

Hamburg-based political theater/music collective. Discogs ID: 714406. Members also include Bernadette La Hengst, Roberto Ohrt, Thomas Butteweg, Patricia Wedler, Christine Schulz, Carol Gaier, Chara Ganotis. Two Staubgold/Buback releases (2005, 2016).

Connections

  • Mille Plateaux / Force Inc — Ehlers worked at the Frankfurt office in the late 1990s; Betrieb was his debut on Mille Plateaux
  • Staubgold — primary label home; 15 primary-credit releases 2001–2012; shares the Force Inc. / microsound milieu
  • Stephan Mathieu — key early collaborator; Heroin (Staalplaat 2001) and uncredited production on Plays John Cassavetes
  • Diedrich Diederichsen — cultural critic; wrote liner notes for Plays Albert Ayler and Plays, situating Ehlers’ work in critical discourse
  • Rashad Becker — mastering engineer at D&M; appears on Plays, A Life Without Fear, and Adikia; the recurring D&M credit links Ehlers to the Berlin experimental mastering community
  • Liam Gillick — visual artist; White Cube Jazz (2001) collaboration points to art-world adjacency
  • Schirn Kunsthalle Frankfurt / Ballett Frankfurt — institutional commissions on Politik Braucht Keinen Feind (2003) demonstrate access to mainstream cultural institutions
