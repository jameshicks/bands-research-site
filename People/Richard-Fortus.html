Richard Fortus

Jul 26, 20266 min read

Richard Fortus

Discogs ID: 265003
Born: November 17, 1966, Saint Louis, Missouri, USA
Name variations: Fortus; R. Fortus

American rock guitarist, session musician, and producer. Best known as a member of Guns N’ Roses (2002–present), but with a substantial pre-GNR career spanning post-punk, indie rock, and alternative through The Eyes (9), Love Spit Love, and The Psychedelic Furs. Also carries a small producer’s catalog including Voltaire’s debut and the Furs’ Made Of Rain.

Biography

Early Work: The Eyes (9) (1989)

Fortus’s earliest Discogs credit is lead guitar on Freedom In A Cage (1989, self-released), by The Eyes (9) — a Saint Louis band that he apparently played in before moving to New York.

Love Spit Love (1992–1997)

After The Psychedelic Furs went on hiatus following World Outside (1991), Richard Butler formed Love Spit Love in New York, recruiting Fortus as guitarist alongside Tim Butler (bass) and Frank Ferrer (drums).

Love Spit Love (1994, Imago): Produced by Dave Jerden. Fortus played guitar, cello, and mandolin. Guest appearance by Jon Brion (Chamberlin, keyboards, Optigan, piano).

Trysome Eatone (1997, Maverick): Fortus co-produced with Richard Butler and Ben Grosse, and also co-mixed and co-wrote. The lineup shifted — Chris Wilson (8) replaced Tim Butler. The album is remembered chiefly for their cover of “How Soon Is Now?” (The Smiths), which became the theme for the TV series Charmed.

Session and Production Work (1998–2001)

After Love Spit Love dissolved, Fortus did session and production work:

  • Voltaire — The Devil’s Bris (1998, Projekt): Fortus produced the debut album by Cuban-American singer-songwriter Voltaire (Aurelio Voltaire Hernandez), a darkly theatrical alt-rock/gothic act.
  • Jimmie’s Chicken Shack — Smartbomb (2000, Nettwerk): Live guitar credit.
  • B-52’s / Beautiful Chaos / Live From House Of Blues (2001): Guitar on various Psychedelic Furs live releases from the band’s reunion.
  • Backstreet Boys — Pop (2001, Jive): Bass and electric guitar — a notable session credit outside his usual rock context.
  • Jesse Malin — The Fine Art Of Self Destruction (2002, One Little Indian): Lead guitar on track 1. The album was primarily produced by Ryan Adams; Paul Garisto played drums throughout. This is a one-track guest credit, not a full album role.
  • Gravity Kills — Superstarved (2002, Sanctuary): Co-wrote track 10 (as R. Fortus). Produced by Gravity Kills and Martin Atkins.

Joining Guns N’ Roses (2002)

Fortus joined Guns N’ Roses in 2002, replacing Robin Finck as rhythm/lead guitarist, and has remained a member. He appears on:

  • Chinese Democracy (2008, Geffen): Fortus is one of several guitarists on the long-delayed album.
  • Appetite For Democracy 3D (2014, Geffen): Live Las Vegas concert film/album.
  • Various GNR live releases and compilations through 2025.

His GNR role became his primary identity, but he continued studio session work alongside it.

Jesse Malin — The Heat (2004)

Jesse Malin — The Heat (2004, One Little Indian): Electric guitar, alongside Paul Garisto (drums), Ryan Adams, Tommy Stinson, and others.

Psychedelic Furs — Made Of Rain (2020)

Fortus’s most prominent production credit outside his own bands. The Psychedelic Furs — Made Of Rain (2020, Cooking Vinyl): Fortus produced the album in its entirety (David Maurice co-produced tracks 4, 6, 8, and 12) and played guitar. The album was the Furs’ first studio record in 29 years. Mixed by Tim Palmer.

Other Credits (2008–2022)

  • Repo! The Genetic Opera (2008, Lions Gate): Guitar on the film soundtrack.
  • The Dead Daisies (supergroup, member; Revolución 2015, Rock ‘N Roll Ain’t Easy 2018, and others): Hard rock supergroup in which Fortus is listed as a member on Discogs (ID: 3543811).
  • Pale Divine — Lavatorium (2021, Cleopatra): Lead guitar and co-writer — a doom/heavy psych act.
  • I Can’t Give Everything Away [2002–2016] (2025, ISO/Parlophone): Guitar credit on what appears to be a David Bowie-related compilation.
