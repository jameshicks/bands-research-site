Jamie Muir

Scottish percussionist, painter, and visual artist. Born James David Muir, 30 November 1942; died 17 February 2025, Cornwall, UK, aged 82. Discogs artist ID: 252062.

Overview

Jamie Muir was one of the most confrontational percussionists to emerge from the British experimental scene. His Discogs credits span free improvisation (The Music Improvisation Company, Company), avant-garde jazz (Derek Bailey collaborations on Incus Records), a brief but influential tenure in King Crimson, and a late return to credited work on the film score Ghost Dance (1996, Piano). He was also a visual artist: several Discogs credits list him as painter, artwork designer, and photographer. Reportedly he withdrew from music after c.1973 to pursue a path as a Buddhist monk, making the 1996 Ghost Dance soundtrack a rare reappearance on record.

Early Work: The Music Improvisation Company (c.1966–1971)

Muir was a founding member of The Music Improvisation Company, a free improvisation ensemble that also included Derek Bailey (guitar), Evan Parker (soprano saxophone), Hugh Davies (live electronics), and Christine Jeffrey (voice). The group recorded for ECM Records:

The 1976 Incus compilation drew on archival recordings from the group’s active years. Muir’s dual listing (percussion and painting) on 1968–1971 indicates that visual art ran alongside his musical work throughout this period.

King Crimson (1972–1973)

Muir joined King Crimson in 1972 as part of the radical lineup reorganisation that Robert Fripp assembled following the end of the Islands-era band. The 1972–73 lineup — Fripp (guitar, mellotron), John Wetton (bass, vocals), David Cross (violin, mellotron), Bill Bruford (drums), and Muir (percussion/“allsorts”) — was the most experimental and sonically violent incarnation of the group. Muir departed before the lineup completed its run, reportedly in early 1973.

His sole studio appearance with Crimson is on Larks’ Tongues in Aspic (1973), listed as “Percussion [Allsorts]” — the “allsorts” tag was used consistently across all subsequent Crimson reissues to describe his contributions. The album has generated an exceptionally large Discogs footprint (149+ pressings across releases tracked).

Derek Bailey / Company Associations (1981–1986)

After the Crimson years, Muir appeared in Bailey’s orbit across two distinct projects:

Dart Drug (1981, Incus INCUS 41) — A primary co-credit with Derek Bailey. Four tracks of free improvisation: Bailey on guitar, Muir on percussion, with Muir also credited for artwork. Bailey produced. Released on Bailey’s own Incus Records imprint.

Company (2) — Muir is listed as a member of Bailey’s long-running improvisation collective Company, which at various points included Evan Parker, Hugh Davies, Peter Brötzmann, Joëlle Léandre, Han Bennink, Fred Frith, John Zorn, and many others. His specific Company appearance on Discogs is Trios (1986, Incus), credited on tracks B2 and B4 alongside Davies, Parker, Brötzmann, Léandre, and others.

Other Session and Visual Credits (1980s–1989)

Beyond the Bailey-affiliated work, Muir accrued a scattering of credits across the 1980s:

  • Aida (1980, Incus) — Typography credit only; this is a Bailey solo/Incus release where Muir contributed lettering/typography.
  • Fables (1980, Incus) — Cover design credit.
  • Damascus (1986, Another Side) — Percussion on two tracks (A1, B1) for Richard Strange & The Engine Room, a post-punk art-rock outfit led by Richard Strange.
  • Conceits (1987, Acta) — Artwork credit only, for the trio Russell, Durrant, Butcher (John Russell, Phil Durrant, John Butcher).
  • Music From New Demons (1989, Editions EG) — Engineering credit on a dance/electronics record by West India Company. No percussion role.
  • Lament (1990, Great Dane Records) — Percussion; likely a Crimson-related compilation.
  • Cat Food (1991, Living Legend Records) — Percussion; another Crimson-related Italian release.

The visual-art credits (typography, cover design, artwork) across multiple Incus releases indicate that Muir’s post-Crimson presence in the London free-music world was partly as a visual contributor as well as occasional performer.

Ghost Dance (1996, Piano)

Ghost Dance is a film soundtrack, directed by Ken McMullen, released on Piano (cat. PIANO 502). It is the most substantial post-Crimson project under Muir’s primary credit. The three co-credited performers are Michael Giles, Jamie Muir, and David Cunningham.

Open Questions

  • Was Muir credited on the original Island Records pressing of Larks’ Tongues in Aspic, or did his credit first appear on Atlantic/EG pressings? Discogs’s earliest tracked pressing is the Atlantic Canada 1973 edition (release 367620) — the UK original pressing record would clarify this.
  • Did Muir depart King Crimson before or after the Larks’ Tongues in Aspic studio sessions were completed? His absence from live recordings prior to certain dates would help pin this down, but Discogs live dates (The Beat Club Bremen, October 1972; Zoom Club, October 1972; Guildford, November 1972; Oxford, November 1972; Newcastle, December 1972) show him active through at least December 1972. The exact departure month is not determinable from Discogs alone.
  • Was the Ghost Dance film released before or after the soundtrack CD (both 1996)? The film’s production timeline relative to the recording sessions is not documented in Discogs.
  • The Rainbow (2012, Discipline Global Mobile) credit lists Muir as “Performer [Uncredited]” — what material from the Larks era does this release contain, and why is he listed as uncredited?
  • Muir’s Music From New Demons (1989, Editions EG / West India Company) credit is “Engineer” only, with no percussion role. The circumstances of his engineering involvement with this dance/electronics record are unclear — single-source question for a West India Company or Édouard Lock biography.
  • No Discogs credits appear between Lament (1990) and Ghost Dance (1996) under Muir’s own name beyond compilations. Whether he recorded anything during the reported Buddhist monastery period (c.1973 onwards) that was never commercially released is not answerable from Discogs.
