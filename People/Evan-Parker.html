Evan Parker

Discogs ID: 42708 Real name: Evan Shaw Parker Born: April 5, 1944, Bristol, England Instruments: Soprano saxophone, tenor saxophone Active: 1960s–present

British saxophonist and central figure of European free improvisation. Co-founder of Incus Records with Derek Bailey. Developed a radical extended technique on soprano saxophone — circular breathing, multiphonics, harmonics — that became one of the defining sounds of free improvisation. A prolific collaborator and institution-builder across six decades, Parker’s work spans intimate duos to large-scale electro-acoustic ensembles and orchestras.

Biography

1960s — Origins and the first London free scene

Parker moved to London in the mid-1960s and entered the emerging British free jazz scene through contact with John Stevens (2), Derek Bailey, and Tony Oxley. He became a core member of the Spontaneous Music Ensemble (SME), the collective formed by Stevens that was one of the earliest British free-improvisation groups. In the SME milieu Parker developed an austere, non-idiomatic approach that rejected jazz convention. During this period he also worked with Trevor Watts, Paul Rutherford (2), and the loosely organized scene around the Little Theatre Club in London.

He also joined The Music Improvisation Company alongside Bailey, Hugh Davies, and others, a group that was documented on ECM Records in 1970.

1970 — Incus and the first recordings

In 1970 Parker co-founded Incus Records with Derek Bailey and Tony Oxley — one of the first artist-run improvised music labels in Europe. The label’s first release was The Topography Of The Lungs (Incus 1, 1970), a trio with Bailey and Han Bennink that remains a landmark of European free improvisation.

Parker’s early Incus solo work — Saxophone Solos (1976), Monoceros (1978) — established him as an innovator on soprano saxophone, deploying circular breathing and multiphonics in a way that created an almost continuous, texture-based sound unlike anything in the existing saxophone tradition.

1970s — European networks and large ensembles

Through the 1970s Parker became increasingly embedded in the pan-European free improvisation network. He joined Globe Unity Orchestra, Alexander von Schlippenbach’s large ensemble that united British and German improvisers; Parker appears on its key FMP recordings from 1973 onward. He also appeared regularly with the London Jazz Composers Orchestra (LJCO), the orchestra organized by Barry Guy, which released on Incus and later on Intakt Records.

Parker established a deep working relationship with the South African exile community in London through Ogun and Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath, playing tenor saxophone alongside Parker’s Louis Moholo, Dudu Pukwana, and others. He was also active on the German FMP circuit, connecting with Peter Brötzmann, Paul Lovens, Jost Gebers, and the Wuppertal network.

The trio with Paul Lytton (percussion) and Barry Guy (double bass) became a central formation of this period and continued for decades — all three appear consistently across Parker’s most important recordings.

1980s–1990s — Solo consolidation and the Electro-Acoustic Ensemble

Parker’s solo soprano work reached a new level of refinement through the 1980s. Chirps (FMP, 1986) is widely considered the definitive document of his extended-technique solo practice. He continued to record prolifically for FMP (The Hearth 1989, Duets, Dithyrambisch 1990, Process And Reality 1991, Three Blokes 1994, Nailed 2000) and for Emanem imprint psi (3).

In the mid-1990s Parker expanded into electro-acoustic collaboration. Toward The Margins (ECM, 1997) — the debut of the Evan Parker Electro-Acoustic Ensemble — brought together live electronics processors (Walter Prati, Marco Vecchi, Philipp Wachsmann) alongside Guy and Lytton. The ensemble was a deliberate extension of the improvisation ethos into real-time signal processing. Two further ECM releases followed: Drawn Inward (1999) and Memory / Vision (2003).

He also released Time Will Tell (ECM, 1995), a celebrated trio with Paul Bley and Barre Phillips — one of his most accessible and widely heard recordings.

2000s–present — psi, global touring, continuing output

Parker founded the psi label (a sublabel of Emanem run by Martin Davidson) to document his ongoing work, releasing a large part of his post-2000 catalogue there. Alongside psi, he has recorded extensively for Intakt Records, Leo Records, Clean Feed, and the London venue-label Otoroku (Cafe Oto).

He co-founded Trance Map with John Coxon (Spring Heel Jack), which recorded for Treader. From the early 2000s he has collaborated with musicians from very different traditions — including Craig Taborn, Matthew Shipp, Spring Heel Jack, Otomo Yoshihide, and Nate Wooley — demonstrating an ongoing willingness to work outside the European free-improvisation core.

His label appearances in this period include Rune Grammofon (Norway), Fundacja Słuchaj! (Poland), Aguirre Records (Belgium), Byrd Out (UK), and numerous others, confirming his continued centrality to improvised music internationally.

Key Groups and Ensembles

Spontaneous Music Ensemble

Founded by John Stevens (2); Parker was a core member from the mid-1960s. Documented on Marmalade, Emanem, and other labels.

The Music Improvisation Company

Late 1960s–early 1970s group with Derek Bailey, Hugh Davies, and others. ECM Records debut in 1970.

Company (2)

Derek Bailey’s rotating improvisation collective; Parker appeared in various configurations documented on Incus.

Globe Unity Orchestra

(Discogs ID: 284214) Alexander von Schlippenbach’s large ensemble. Parker a member across its FMP catalog from Live In Wuppertal (1973) through the 50th anniversary release on Intakt (2018). Also recorded for Japo Records and Po Torch.

London Jazz Composers Orchestra

(Discogs ID: 1311998) Barry Guy’s large ensemble. Released on Incus (Ode, 1972), FMP, and Intakt Records across releases from 1972–1998. Parker is a member alongside Dave Holland, Paul Lytton, Kenny Wheeler, and many others.

Evan Parker Electro-Acoustic Ensemble

(Discogs ID: 620511) Formed mid-1990s. Core personnel: Parker (soprano sax), Barry Guy (double bass), Paul Lytton (percussion/live electronics), Philipp Wachsmann (violin/electronics), Walter Prati (electronics), Marco Vecchi (electronics), Lawrence Casserley (signal processing instrument). Extended roster listed on Discogs includes Agustí Fernandez, Joel Ryan, Ko Ishikawa, Ned Rothenberg, Paul Obermayer, Peter van Bergen, Richard Barrett, and Peter Evans. Released three albums on ECM Records (1997–2003) and one later on Fundacja Słuchaj! (Warszawa 2019, 2021).

Evan Parker Electro-Acoustic Quartet

(Discogs ID: 593415) Smaller electro-acoustic formation.

Evan Parker / Barry Guy / Paul Lytton (trio)

(Discogs ID: 1465414) The core trio documented across multiple labels over several decades.

Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath

Parker on tenor saxophone; documented on Ogun — Live At Willisau (1974), Procession (1978).

The Dedication Orchestra

South African exile tribute ensemble; Parker credited on Spirits Rejoice (Ogun, 1992) and Ixesha (Time) (1994).

Trance Map

Ongoing project with John Coxon. Documented on Treader.
