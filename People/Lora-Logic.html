Lora Logic

Born: Susan Carena Whitby, 9 September 1961
Discogs ID: 47971
Name variations: L. Logic, Laura Logic, Logic, Lora

UK vocalist and saxophonist. Founding member of X-Ray Spex (1976–1977), then formed Essential Logic (1978–1981, reformed 2001). Session saxophonist for The Raincoats, Red Crayola, Kollaa Kestää, and others in the British post-punk and DIY orbit. Her two solo albums — Wonderful Offer (1981) and Pedigree Charm (1982) — were released on Rough Trade. Returned to recording with Essential Logic in 2022, releasing Land of Kali and the retrospective Logically Yours.

Biography

X-Ray Spex (1976–1977)

Lora Logic was a founding member of X-Ray Spex alongside vocalist Poly Styrene (born Mari Elliott), joining the band in 1976 when she was still a teenager. The band formed in London and became one of the defining voices of British punk, distinguished by Poly Styrene’s confrontational lyrics and Logic’s abrasive saxophone work. Logic appeared on the group’s earliest material — she is credited as “Laura Logic” on her saxophone contribution (track A5) to The Stranglers’ Black and White (A&M, 1978), recorded around this period.

She was fired from X-Ray Spex in 1977 before the band recorded their debut LP Germfree Adolescents. The firing is attributed to Poly Styrene feeling threatened by audience and critical attention focussed on Logic’s saxophone playing — Logic was described in contemporary accounts as “arguably stealing the show” on the band’s single “Oh Bondage Up Yours!” Despite her dismissal, her saxophone arrangements were used uncredited on Germfree Adolescents, with a different player (Rudi Thompson, temporarily preceded by Glyn Johns) executing them. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lora_Logic as of 2025-10-23; https://www.furious.com/perfect/essentiallogic.html]

She rejoined the band in the 1990s for their reformation.

Essential Logic (1978–1981)

After leaving X-Ray Spex, Logic immediately formed Essential Logic in 1978. The founding lineup comprised:

  • Lora Logic — vocals, saxophone
  • Phil Legg — guitar, vocals (also credited as Ashley Buff)
  • William Bennett — guitar
  • Mark Turner (4) — bass guitar
  • Rich Tea — drums
  • Dave Wright (2) — saxophone

The band debuted with the single “Aerosol Burns” (Cells Records, 1978), produced entirely by the band. This was followed by their key EP Beat Rhythm News – Waddle Ya Play? (Rough Trade, 1979), co-produced by Logic and engineer Hugh Jones, a playful, jagged post-punk document with two saxophones, motorik rhythms, and Logic’s distinctive “warbling” vocal style.

Further singles appeared on both Rough Trade and Virgin:

  • “Wake Up” (Virgin, 1979) — produced by Essential Logic themselves
  • “Eugene” (Rough Trade, 1980) — produced by Essential Logic
  • “Music Is A Better Noise” (Rough Trade, 1980) — Logic co-wrote both sides with Phil Legg

Logic’s own imprint, Logic Records (2), also appeared on some pressings of “Popcorn Boy Waddle Ya Do?” (1979).

The band split in 1981 after a run of UK singles, with Jon Oliver and Ben Annesley having taken over bass duties from Mark Turner at various points. A 1981 Italian compilation (Essential Logic, Base Record) collected key tracks, later superseded by Fanfare in the Garden: An Essential Logic Collection (Kill Rock Stars, 2003) and the career-spanning Logically Yours (Hiss and Shake Records / Cargo Records, 2022).

Session Work (1979–1981)

During the Essential Logic years Logic was active as a session player and collaborator across the British DIY scene:

  • The Raincoats — saxophone on track A4 of The Raincoats (Rough Trade, 1979). The album was co-produced by Geoff Travis and Mayo Thompson.
  • Red Crayola — listed as musician on Soldier-Talk (Radar Records, 1979), alongside members of Pere Ubu including David Thomas (2), Allen Ravenstine, and Tony Maimone; produced by Geoff Travis and Mayo Thompson. Also musician and co-producer on Kangaroo? (Rough Trade, 1981) alongside Epic Soundtracks, Ben Annesley, and Gina Birch.
  • Kollaa Kestää — tenor saxophone and backing vocals across the entire debut LP Jäähyväiset Aseille (Johanna, 1979). The album was recorded at Finnvox studio in Helsinki. How Logic came to participate has not been confirmed in any source, Finnish or English. Solo Career (1981–1982)

Logic released two solo albums on Rough Trade, both co-produced with Phil Legg:

Wonderful Offer (Rough Trade, 1981) — her debut. Credited as primary artist; Lora handled voice, soprano and tenor saxophone, glockenspiel, and synthesizer. Phil Legg played bass, guitar, and rototrons, and co-produced. A small ensemble record, intimate and exploratory.

Pedigree Charm (Rough Trade, 1982) — the more fully-realised solo statement. Added Charles Hayward on drums (alongside Rich Tea on selected tracks) and Ben Annesley on bass. Co-produced with Phil Legg and Stephen Rickard, who also engineered and mixed. Cover photography by Anton Corbijn. Logic wrote all songs and is credited as sole primary artist.

Hare Krishna Period and Hiatus (c. 1982–1995)

After Pedigree Charm (1982), Logic left the music industry. In interviews she has described the circumstances directly: “I had a meltdown during the making of Pedigree Charm. I was living in a squat. There was no kitchen, no running water. I had a weekly bath at the Victorian bath house in Ladbroke Grove. I didn’t eat properly; I took too many intoxicants. And it got too much. I went to live in the ashram.” [source: https://www.furious.com/perfect/essentiallogic.html]

Around 1983 she took formal initiation into the Hare Krishna movement, adopting the spiritual name Syama-manjari-devi dasi. She lived at Bhaktivedanta Manor — the property donated to the English Krishna community by George Harrison — and travelled to temples in India and Europe. In 1984 she had an arranged marriage at the Krishna temple and had two children. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lora_Logic as of 2025-10-23; https://back2godhead.com/every-town-village-50/]

During this period she reconnected with Poly Styrene, who had also joined the movement. Together with other devotees they formed a reggae-influenced group called Juggernaut and performed at Glastonbury Festival in June 1983, playing to an audience estimated at 30,000. [source: https://back2godhead.com/every-town-village-50/; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A42lOppEtwo]

The furious.com interview also states that Logic sang for Boy George on “Bow Down Mister” (1991) and appeared on Top of the Pops for that performance. Conflict: the Wikipedia article on “Bow Down Mister” credits Indian playback singer Asha Bhosle as the female vocalist (uncredited on the single); Lora Logic is not mentioned there. [Discogs says: Asha Bhosle per Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_Down_Mister; Logic’s own account: https://www.furious.com/perfect/essentiallogic.html — unresolved]

Later Activity and Return (1990s–2022)

Logic rejoined X-Ray Spex for their 1990s reunion, appearing on their 1995 LP Conscious Consumer (Receiver Records) and live recordings from the Roxy Club.

Essential Logic reformed in 2001 and began playing live again. By the 2020s, the band was active around a new studio album and career anthology:

  • Land of Kali (Do Yourself In Records / Hiss and Shake Records, 2022) — Essential Logic studio album co-produced by Logic and Youth (Martin Glover of Killing Joke). Logic co-engineered and mixed. Celeste Bell (daughter of Poly Styrene) served as executive producer. The album includes a track co-written by Logic and Poly Styrene.
  • Logically Yours (Hiss and Shake Records / Cargo Records, 2022) — a large retrospective compilation. Features contributions from Poly Styrene (written-by credits), Vice Cooler, Gary Valentine, William Bennett, and many other collaborators spanning the band’s full history. Liner notes by Celeste Bell.
  • Rekalibrated (Hiss and Shake Records, 2024) — a remix album of Land of Kali (2022). Features 10 reworkings by various producers including Dave Audé (Grammy-winning dance remixer), Youth (who co-produced the original), Adamski, bis, Skylab, and others. Released 29 November 2024 in an ultra-limited 200-copy pressing on fluorescent yellow vinyl via Cargo Records Direct, with a hand-signed art card. This is not new studio material — it is entirely remixes of the 2022 LP. [source: https://essentiallogic.bandcamp.com/album/rekalibrated; https://cargorecordsdirect.co.uk/products/essential-logic-rekalibrated]
  • Beat Rhythm News (Waddle Ya Play?) (Hiss and Shake Records, 2024) — reissue compilation of the 1979 EP material.
