Reginald Veal

Jazz bassist, born 5 November 1963 in Chicago, Illinois, USA. Discogs artist ID: 253758. Name variations: R. Veal, Redginald Veal.

Biography

Reginald Veal came up through the New Orleans jazz infrastructure despite being Chicago-born — he was active in the late-1980s New Orleans-adjacent scene centered on the Marsalis family orbit and the Young Lions neoclassical revival that was reshaping acoustic jazz’s relationship with its own hard-bop and swing heritage.

His first recorded appearance under his own steam dates to 1981 (In Command, Chekmate Records), where he handled bass guitar, keyboards, synthesizer, organ, and scoring duties. By 1987 he had settled into his role as a straight acoustic bassist and landed simultaneous berths in two of the decade’s most visible hard-bop units: the Donald Harrison / Terence Blanchard co-led quintet (credited on Discogs as Harrison-Blanchard) and the expanding Wynton Marsalis band on Columbia Records.

His decade-long tenure with Marsalis (roughly 1989–1999) placed him on over two dozen Columbia and Sony Classical releases. He was a listed member of the Wynton Marsalis Septet, the working small-group unit that toured internationally and recorded the large-scale projects of the early 1990s. He continued appearing on Marsalis productions through the late 1990s, including the Pulitzer Prize-winning oratorio Blood On The Fields (1997) and the multi-disc Village Vanguard live set.

Outside the Marsalis world Veal contributed to a notably wide range of leaders: guitarist Mark Whitfield (Warners debut The Marksman, 1990), pianist Harry Connick Jr. (self-titled Columbia debut, 1987), a cross-over unit called Buckshot LeFonque co-led by Branford Marsalis, the Branford Marsalis Trio, the Ellis Marsalis Trio, and Japanese pianist Junko Onishi’s trio on Blue Note. From the mid-1990s onward he also logged credits for Impulse!, Verve, Criss Cross, and Jazz Village.

His sole release as a named primary artist in the Discogs data is Gold Sounds (2005, Brown Brothers Recordings), a quartet date co-led with Cyrus Chestnut (piano), Ali Jackson (drums), and James Carter (3) (saxophones).

Harrison / Blanchard Quintet

The quintet co-led by alto saxophonist Donald Harrison and trumpeter Terence Blanchard was one of the defining Young Lions units of the late 1980s. Veal anchored the rhythm section alongside drummer Carl Allen and pianist Cyrus Chestnut on both Columbia albums produced under the act name Harrison/Blanchard.

Scene Context

Veal is a product of the Young Lions neoclassical hard-bop revival that coalesced around Wynton Marsalis and the Jazz at Lincoln Center program in the late 1980s and 1990s. The movement was characterized by a self-conscious return to bebop and post-bop acoustic jazz vocabulary, heavy Columbia Records backing, and a generational statement against fusion and free jazz. Key ideological allies included Stanley Crouch (liner notes on many of Veal’s most prominent dates) and producer Steve Epstein, who appears repeatedly across the Marsalis catalog Veal anchored.

The Harrison/Blanchard quintet was emblematic of this moment: both leaders were Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers alumni (Blakey being the direct institutional ancestor of the movement), and their Columbia albums landed Veal in the precise center of the neoclassical turn.

His later Blue Note work with Norah Jones (2003–2014) and the Gold Sounds session with James Carter and Cyrus Chestnut represent a second career phase reaching beyond the Marsalis orbit.
