Craig Silvey

Craig Silvey

Oct 13, 20269 min read

Craig Silvey

Discogs artist ID: 231071 Role: Mixer, engineer, producer Name variation on Discogs: Craig Silwey

UK-based record producer, sound engineer, and mixer. Closely associated with Arcade Fire as their primary mixer from The Suburbs (2010) through WE (2022) — a run of four consecutive studio albums and 112 shared Discogs credits. Also widely active as a mixer for UK guitar acts (Arctic Monkeys, The Horrors, The Coral) and major international albums (Portishead’s Third, Florence + The Machine’s Ceremonials, The National’s Trouble Will Find Me, New Order’s Music Complete).

Career Overview

Early Career: Skywalker Ranch and Nettwerk (1988–1997)

Silvey’s earliest Discogs credits are as an assistant engineer, beginning in 1988 at Skywalker Ranch (the Lucasfilm complex in Marin County, California). This period accounts for a dense cluster of classical and film-music engineering credits on Nonesuch and Elektra Nonesuch releases — Kronos Quartet sessions, John Adams catalogue work, early-music and chamber recordings — plus work on pop and rock records for Nettwerk Records (Skinny Puppy adjacent releases, Grapes of Wrath) and several Windham Hill new-age projects.

The Skywalker Ranch connection threads through multiple credits in the early 1990s. By 1991–1994 he had moved into mixing roles on individual tracks, including mixing work on some Nettwerk releases. A 1994 Consolidated album (Business Of Punishment) lists him as both engineer and mixer, a sign of increasing seniority.

In 1997 he co-produced a Kill Rock Stars release (Games People Play — artist unspecified on Discogs), his first listed solo production credit.

London Break-in: Mo Wax, Go! Beat, Rough Trade (1998–2003)

By the late 1990s Silvey had relocated to (or was heavily working in) London. A cluster of credits on Mo Wax and Go! Beat dates from 1998–2003:

  • Money Mark, Push The Button (1998, Mo Wax) — Additional Production
  • Money Mark, Maybe I’m Dead (1998, Mo Wax) — Additional Production
  • Gabrielle, Rise (1999, Go! Beat) — Mixed By (platinum UK album)
  • Zoe, McKay / Tell Him (2003, Go! Beat) — Engineer, Mixed By
  • R.E.M., Up (1998, Warner Bros.) — Digital Editing (San Francisco)

The Gabrielle Rise credit is notable: the album went to number one in the UK, carried by the Dylan-sampling title track. This mainstream commercial success likely underpinned Silvey’s move into high-profile UK pop and rock.

Early 2000s also includes production work on Baxter Dury’s debut album Len Parrot’s Memorial Lift (2002, Rough Trade) — engineer, mixer, and co-producer on most tracks, alongside Geoff Barrow on one track and featuring Adrian Utley on bass.

UK Guitar Band Years: Deltasonic and The Magic Numbers (2004–2008)

The mid-2000s sees Silvey consolidate as a go-to mixer/producer for British guitar bands, particularly through the Deltasonic Records roster:

  • The Coral — The Invisible Invasion (2005), various singles (2005–2007, 2012, 2013, 2015): Mixed By, and frequently Produced By and Recorded By across Roots & Echoes (2007) and surrounding singles.
  • The Magic Numbers — self-titled debut (2005, Capitol/Heavenly): Producer, engineer, and mixer on most tracks. The album reached number 7 in the UK charts.
  • The Magic Numbers — singles (Forever Lost, I See You You See Me, Love Me Like You, Love’s A Game) (2005, Heavenly): Mixed By, Producer.

The Coral work runs deep: he produced, recorded, and mixed Roots & Echoes (2007) and a string of associated singles. His connection to Deltasonic owner Alan Wills placed him at the centre of the early-2000s Liverpool guitar revival.

Outside the Deltasonic world:

  • Portishead, Third (2008, Island): Mixed By (alongside Geoff Barrow). One of the decade’s most critically acclaimed returns-from-hiatus.

The Horrors Trilogy (2009–2014)

A sustained production and mixing partnership with The Horrors yielded three albums:

  1. Primary Colours (2009, XL Recordings): Producer, Mixed By, Engineer — alongside Geoff Barrow on most tracks. Mercury Prize nominee.
  2. Skying (2011, XL Recordings): Co-producer, Mixed By, Recorded By [Additional Recording] — sole co-producer credit, produced by the band.
  3. Luminous (2014, XL Recordings): Produced By, Recorded By, Mixed By.

These three records span a total stylistic reinvention of the band from gothic punk to psychedelic shoegaze. The Geoff Barrow connection is prominent on Primary Colours; by Luminous Silvey is the primary external hand.

A related credit: The Horrors live release Sea Within A Sea (Live 2019) (2020) — Producer, Mixed By.

Arcade Fire (2010–2022)

Craig Silvey is the consistent mixer for Arcade Fire across their most successful commercial period. His assistant on several of these projects was Eduardo de la Paz (Mixed By [Assisted By]).

On The Suburbs, he is the sole credited mixer. The album’s other key production figures are co-producer Markus Dravs and recording engineer Mark Lawson (2); Owen Pallett handled string arrangements.

On Reflektor, mixing was split between Silvey, James Murphy (DFA), Tom Elmhirst, and the band/Mark Lawson.

On WE, Nigel Godrich mixed “Lightning I”, the album’s centrepiece; Silvey handled the rest.

The band and Silvey have also collaborated on singles and EPs outside the main album sequence, accounting for the high shared-release count (112).

Wider 2011–2022 Mixing Career

Running parallel to the Arcade Fire relationship, Silvey mixes a broad catalogue of indie and alternative rock from British and international artists:

UK acts:

  • Arctic Monkeys, Suck It And See (2011, Domino): Mixed By — produced by James Ford; mixed at The Garden, London
  • Anna Calvi, self-titled debut (2011, Domino): Mixed By
  • Florence + The Machine, Ceremonials (2011, Universal Republic): Mixed By (tracks 3, 8, 12, 14, 15) — shared with Mark Rankin, Spike Stent, Tom Elmhirst
  • Bombay Bicycle Club, A Different Kind Of Fix (2011, Island): Mixed By, Producer
  • The Vaccines, What Did You Expect From The Vaccines?-era singles
  • Frightened Rabbit, Pedestrian Verse (2013, Atlantic): Mixed By
  • Noel Gallagher’s High Flying Birds, Chasing Yesterday (2014/2015, Sour Mash): Mixed By; also associated singles (In The Heat Of The Moment, Ballad Of The Mighty I, Lock All The Doors, The Dying Of The Light, Riverman, Black Star Dancing) across 2014–2019
  • New Order, Music Complete (2015, Mute): Mixed By (all tracks except one)
  • Jack Garratt, Album (2016) [not listed on Discogs but see Painting Of A Panic Attack]

  • Declan McKenna, What Do You Think About The Car? (2017, Columbia): Mixed By

  • Wet Leg — later 2020s credits
  • The Coral, Butterfly House era (2019), Lovers’ Landscape (2023): ongoing relationship

International acts:
