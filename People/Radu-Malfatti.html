Radu Malfatti

Austrian trombonist and composer (born Innsbruck, 16 December 1943) who moved from the free jazz and large-ensemble improvisation scenes of 1970s London and Europe into the extreme reductive/minimal improvisation associated with the Wandelweiser Komponisten Ensemble from the 1990s onward. He also founded his own CDr label, B-Boim Records, to release primarily his own compositions.

Biography

Free Jazz Period (1973–1983)

Malfatti came up through the London free jazz scene of the early 1970s, playing in large orchestral ensembles that drew on both British and South African jazz in exile. His earliest Discogs credit is on the Ogun label with Irene Schweizer, Rüdiger Carl, Paul Lovens, and Harry Miller — Ramifications (1975) — a quintet of major European free improvisers. He also performed with Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath, appearing on the live document Live At Willisau (Ogun, 1974) and Procession (Ogun, 1978), alongside Evan Parker, Dudu Pukwana, Harry Beckett, Mark Charig, and Louis Moholo. He was a listed member of the London Jazz Composers Orchestra (Barry Guy’s large ensemble) and the Louis Moholo Octet.

In 1979 he contributed as a guest trombonist on track B2 (“Fara”) of Soft Heap’s self-titled LP (Charly Records), alongside Mark Charig on trumpet and the Soft Heap core of Alan Gowen, Elton Dean, Hugh Hopper, and Pip Pyle.

His FMP period produced key duo and trio records: Zwecknagel (FMP, 1981) with Harry Miller (his last major album — Miller died later that year), and Ach Was!? (FMP, 1981) with Ulrich Gumpert and Tony Oxley, all three credited as composers and editors. He also appeared on Joe McPhee’s Topology (Hat Hut Records, 1981), playing trombone, percussion, and electronics in a large group with Irene Schweizer, Pierre Favre, and others. His solo/leader work in this period includes the solo LP Ramifications (Ogun, 1975) — the only primary-credited LP issued on Ogun — and appearances on FMP compilations and records for Bead, Verlag Adolf Hürlimann, and Intakt.

Transition and Ensemble Work (1984–1994)

Through the late 1980s and into the 1990s Malfatti remained active in free improv contexts, now increasingly working from London and Vienna. News From The Shed (Acta, 1989) is a key document from this transitional period: a quintet with John Butcher, Phil Durrant, Paul Lovens, and John Russell, recorded by Marc Hunt. He also appeared on Intakt Records releases during this period, including Songs And Variations (hat ART, 1989) and Harmos / Double Trouble / Theoria (Intakt, 1989–1992).

In 1992 he led Ohrkiste (ITM Classics), a large-ensemble record he composed and conducted, featuring a broad cast including John Butcher, John Russell, Peter van Bergen, Melvyn Poore, Wolfgang Fuchs, Alfred Zimmerlin, Fred Van Hove, and others — essentially a survey of his European free improv network.

He was a member of the Austrian group Polwechsel, whose debut self-titled LP appeared on Random Acoustics in 1995, with core members Werner Dafeldecker, Burkhard Stangl, and Michael Moser; later Polwechsel rosters would include John Butcher and Burkhard Beins.

Reductive/Minimal Improvisation (1995–present)

From the mid-1990s Malfatti moved decisively toward the extremely sparse, silence-heavy improvisation associated with the Wandelweiser circle. He became a member of the Wandelweiser Komponisten Ensemble, alongside Antoine Beuger, Jürg Frey, Michael Pisaro, Burkhard Schlothauer, and others, and this network generated the bulk of his later Discogs credits. His primary collaborators in this period include Jürg Frey (7 shared releases), Michael Pisaro (7), Antoine Beuger (6), Taku Unami (5), and Mattin (5).

He founded B-Boim Records (Discogs ID: 754989) in Vienna to release primarily his own compositions on CDr, issuing at least two titles: Wechseljahre Einer Hyäne (B-Boim 007, 2007), a composed work for the saxophone ensemble Intersax (with Ulrich Krieger, Martin Losert, Tobias Rüger, Reimar Volker), and Das Pelzige M (B-Boim 008, 2007).

Building Excess (GROB, 2004) with Klaus Filip, Mattin, and Dean Roberts is a key reductive improvisation document from the mid-2000s. Later Discogs credits include collaborations with Taku Sugimoto, Lucio Capece, and Ernesto Rodrigues, and appearances on the Confront label with the 10tet (2016).

Scene Connections

Malfatti’s trajectory maps the full arc of European free improvisation from its roots in the early 1970s through to the post-Wandelweiser reductive scene of the 2000s–2010s.

1970s free jazz / large ensemble: His formative period overlaps directly with the South African jazz-in-exile network anchored in London — Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath brought together players like Louis Moholo, Dudu Pukwana, Johnny Dyani, Mongezi Feza, and British improvisers including Evan Parker, Mark Charig, and Harry Beckett. Ogun and FMP were the two labels most central to his work in this period.

1980s British/European free improv: The FMP Berlin axis (Jost Gebers producing) and the Incus/Ogun London network. Key partnerships with Harry Miller (who died in 1983), Tony Oxley, and the broader London improv scene represented on News From The Shed (Acta, 1989).

Wandelweiser and reductive improv (1990s–): The shift toward extremely sparse, low-dynamic improvisation — long silences, minimal gesture — connects him to the Wandelweiser circle based in Düsseldorf/Haan, whose composers include Antoine Beuger and Jürg Frey, as well as to the Tokyo reductive scene through Taku Unami and Taku Sugimoto, and to Vienna-based figures like Klaus Filip and Werner Dafeldecker.
