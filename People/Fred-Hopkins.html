Fred Hopkins

Born: Frederick J. Hopkins, 10 October 1947, Chicago, IL, USA Died: 7 January 1999, Chicago, IL, USA Discogs artist ID: 268348 Role: Double bassist

American double bassist, a central figure in the AACM and the New York loft jazz scene of the 1970s–90s. Hopkins appeared on well over 60 releases across three decades, working almost exclusively in free jazz and avant-garde contexts. He is best known as the bassist in Air (4) (with Henry Threadgill and Steve McCall), New Air (same trio with Pheeroan Aklaff replacing McCall), and the Henry Threadgill Sextett. He was also a prolific sideman across the broader constellation of artists associated with AACM, Black Saint, India Navigation, and DIW.

Overview

Hopkins grew up in Chicago and came up through the AACM circle, where he first worked alongside Henry Threadgill. The two formed Air around 1971, recording first in Japan in 1975 and subsequently establishing a decade-long run through Nessa, Arista Novus, and Black Saint. When drummer Steve McCall departed in the early 1980s, the group reconvened as New Air with Pheeroan Aklaff, recording two albums for Black Saint before disbanding.

Throughout the Air years and beyond, Hopkins maintained a heavy sideman schedule — appearing on key early records by David Murray, Arthur Blythe, Oliver Lake, Hamiet Bluiett, and Jemeel Moondoc, among many others. He was a consistent presence in the Henry Threadgill Sextett through its entire run (1982–89), appearing on all five of the group’s proper studio/live documents. In the early 1990s he led or co-led a handful of sessions, including a notable duo with cellist Diedre Murray and a trio date with Peter Brötzmann and Rashied Ali on FMP.

Hopkins died in Chicago in January 1999 at age 51.

Henry Threadgill Sextett — Discography

The Sextett was a larger ensemble built around Threadgill’s writing, with a rotating cast of brass, woodwinds, cello, and two drummers. Hopkins anchored the bass chair throughout.
