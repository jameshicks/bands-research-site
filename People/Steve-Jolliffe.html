Steve Jolliffe

British multi-instrumentalist, composer, and vocalist. Born Steven Ernest Jolliffe. Active from the late 1960s across blues-rock, progressive, electronic, ambient, and new age. Discogs artist ID: 129451.

Overview

Jolliffe’s career spans an unusual arc: from blues-rock (Steamhammer) through early Krautrock (the proto–Tangerine Dream lineup), to a brief return as TD’s vocalist and woodwind player on Cyclone (1978), and then a long solo career in electronic and ambient music running from the 1980s through the 2020s.

He first met Rick Davies in 1967 and formed a group called The Joint, which — after Jolliffe departed — evolved into Supertramp. In 1969 he enrolled at the Berlin High School for Music, becoming the first student accepted who could not read notation. In Berlin he met Edgar Froese and Klaus Schulze, and the three toured Germany together in one of Tangerine Dream’s earliest incarnations. He left after roughly a year and returned to England.

Back in England he joined Steamhammer, a blues/rock band, and toured Europe with them across the early 1970s. He appears on MK II (CBS, 1969), playing alto saxophone, tenor saxophone, alto and soprano flute, harpsichord, and vocals. In 1973 he left Steamhammer and began scoring films, including the award-winning documentary Tattoo (the original score was finally released by Trunk Records in 2023).

In 1978 Jolliffe rejoined Tangerine Dream — by this point comprising Edgar Froese, Christopher Franke, and drummer Klaus Krüger — to record Cyclone (Virgin). He wrote lyrics, sang, and played an extensive list of instruments: bass flute, alto flute, C-flute, piccolo, cor anglais, bass clarinet, Hohner Clavinet, Elka String Synthesizer, Roland System 100, grand piano, Fender Rhodes, tenor horn, soprano horn, and the Computone Lyricon (an electronic wind instrument). The album remains controversial among TD fans for its incorporation of lyrics, conventional drums, and vocals — a sharp break from the purely electronic sound of Rubycon and Phaedra. The quartet toured Europe that year, playing to audiences of up to 100,000. Jolliffe left TD a second time shortly after.

He then pursued a solo career in England, recording a run of albums through the 1980s and 1990s. Journeys Out of the Body (1983) was co-produced with members of Jade Warrior — guitarist Richard Brunton and synthesizer player Tony Duhig (credited as Jade Warrior on the release). In 1986 he contributed guest flute to the Klaus Schulze alias project Richard Wahnfried’s Miditation album. He also worked in library music through the late 1980s, composing for sync use (TV, film, advertising).

In 1990 Jolliffe relocated to the United States and signed with Horizon Music, releasing Escape (1991), Warrior (1992), and Deep Down Far (1999) among others, as well as a compilation Invitation (1999) drawing across his catalog. His 1995/96 album Zanzi was selected as one of Musical Starstreams’ Top Ten albums of 1996. During this period he collaborated with Eat Static members Merv Pepler and Joie Hinton (formerly of Ozric Tentacles) on their album Science of the Gods. He returned to England in 1994 and continued recording into the 2010s and 2020s, mostly self-releasing through Not On Label.

In 2015, following Edgar Froese’s death, Jolliffe released a tribute album Magical (In Memory Of Edgar Froese 1944–2015). His most recent Discogs entry is Zankintera (2024).
