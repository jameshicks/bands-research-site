Richard H. Kirk

Born: 21 March 1956, Sheffield, England Died: 21 September 2021 Role: Guitar, wind instruments, synthesizer, electronics, tape, vocals — co-founder of Cabaret Voltaire Discogs ID: 2238

Overview

Richard Harold Kirk was the primary instrumentalist and electronics architect of Cabaret Voltaire, the Sheffield group he co-founded in 1973 with Stephen Mallinder and Chris Watson. Where Mallinder was the vocalist and Watson the early tape manipulator, Kirk was the multi-instrumentalist: guitar, clarinet, saxophone, synthesizers, sequencers, and eventually Fairlight CMI. He also ran a parallel solo career from 1980 onward, releasing under his own name and under a proliferating set of aliases — most prominently Sweet Exorcist (with Richard Barratt / DJ Parrot, pioneering bleep techno on early Warp Records) and Sandoz (dub-inflected electronics, on Touch). After Cabaret Voltaire dissolved in 1994, Kirk continued under his own name and aliases through the 2000s and 2010s. In 2014 he revived the Cabaret Voltaire name as a solo vehicle; Shadow of Fear (Mute, 2020) was the final release. He died the following year.

Cabaret Voltaire

See Cabaret Voltaire for the full group history.

Kirk met Stephen Mallinder as a teenager in Sheffield. Chris Watson was independently building tape loops and oscillators; Kirk and Watson began collaborating first, then brought in Mallinder as vocalist and bassist. The group’s earliest performances were Dada-influenced noise events — at their first public concert (13 May 1975), Mallinder was hospitalised with a chipped backbone after the audience threw objects. Cabaret Voltaire signed to Rough Trade in 1978.

Kirk’s instrumental contributions spanned the entire CV discography: guitar and wind instruments in the Rough Trade era, expanding to synthesizers and sequencers as the band grew more electronic, then Fairlight CMI and computer-generated instrumentation in the Virgin period.

After Chris Watson left at the end of 1981, Cabaret Voltaire was a duo. By the early 1990s — when Mallinder relocated to Perth, Australia — Kirk became the sole working member, releasing fully instrumental CV albums on Plastex, Instinct, and Apollo through 1994.

Solo Career

1980–1986: Industrial and Rough Trade

Kirk’s first solo album, Disposable Half-Truths (Industrial Records IRC 34, 1980), appeared the same year as CV’s The Voice of America. Industrial Records — Throbbing Gristle’s label — was a deliberate placement: Kirk’s solo work from the start inhabited a harder, more abstract space than CV’s increasingly pop-oriented direction.

Two more solo records on Doublevision (the UK’s first independent video label, co-founded with Mallinder in 1982):

  • Time High Fiction (Doublevision DVR 2, 1983) — all writing, production, and instrumentation by Kirk
  • Leather Hands (Doublevision DVR 13, 1985) — collaboration with vocalist Peter Hope

Rough Trade solo cluster: Black Jesus Voice (ROUGH 99, 1986), Hipnotic (1986), Ugly Spirit (1986) — released as the CabVolt/Virgin relationship wound down.

1989–1994: Bleep Techno and Warp Records

In 1989 Kirk linked up with Sheffield DJ Richard Barratt (alias Parrot) and formed Sweet Exorcist. “Testone” (Warp Records, 1989/1990) was among the first Warp releases; Clonk’s Coming was Warp’s first album. The Sweet Exorcist name came from the Curtis Mayfield LP. Together they are a foundational document of Sheffield bleep-and-bass.

Warp Records was co-founded by Steve Beckett, Rob Mitchell, and Robert Gordon — who had previously worked at FON Studio in Sheffield with Mark Brydon as FON Force. Kirk’s pre-existing Sheffield network made Warp a natural home.

Kirk also released solo material on Warp as himself: Virtual State (Warp CD19, 1994) and The Number of Magic (Warp CD32, 1995).

1992–: Sandoz

From 1992, Kirk produced dub and electronic music as Sandoz — named after the Swiss pharmaceutical company that synthesised and first marketed LSD. The Sandoz work drew on two decades of listening to Jamaican music, processed through electronic minimalism. Primary outlet: Touch label. Key releases: Digital Lifeforms (Touch, 1993), Spirit Guide To Low Tech (Touch, 1994), In Dub — Chant To Jah (Touch, 1998), Live In The Earth: Sandoz In Dub Chapter 2 (Soul Jazz Records, 2006).

2003–2021: Intone and late solo period

Kirk ran his own imprint Intone from the early 2000s for a sustained output of electronic and ambient works: TWAT v4.0: The War Against Terror (Intone, 2003), Dasein (Intone, 2017), Black&White Medicine (Wrong Revolution, 2018). Late-period collaborators included Peter Hope (vocals) and DJ Ping Pong.

2014–2021: Cabaret Voltaire revival

Kirk revived the Cabaret Voltaire name for a solo performance at the Atonal Festival, Berlin, 2014 — playing exclusively new material. European live activity followed through 2015–2019. Shadow of Fear (Mute, November 2020) was the final Cabaret Voltaire album; Dekadrone and BN9Drone (Mute, 2021) followed in the last months of his life.

Kirk died 21 September 2021.
