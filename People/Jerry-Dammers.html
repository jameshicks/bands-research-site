Jerry Dammers

Discogs ID: 91314
Real name: Jeremy David Hounsell Dammers
Role: Keyboards, producer, songwriter, label founder
From: Coventry, England

Founder of The Specials and Two-Tone Records. Wrote “Ghost Town” (UK #1, 1981). After the Specials split, continued as The Special AKA (1982–85) with John Bradbury and others, releasing In The Studio (1984) and the anti-apartheid anthem “Free Nelson Mandela” (UK #9). Produced The Higsons’ “Tear The Whole Thing Down” (Two-Tone CHS TT 21, 1982). Later formed the Spatial A.K.A Orchestra and the Visionistics collective. Did not participate in the 2008 or 2019 reunions.

Absence from the 2008–09 and 2019 Reunions

2008–09 reunion

The Specials’ 30th-anniversary reunion was organised without Dammers. According to his own December 2008 public statement, he was not invited to the initial planning meeting and only learned of it because drummer John Bradbury tipped him off. Shortly after attending that meeting, Lynval Golding phoned him to say his participation was not required — Dammers quoted Golding as comparing him to “Bill Wyman in The Stones.” Terry Hall’s manager then circulated internal emails from which Dammers was excluded, and rehearsals were held without him. Dammers described the whole enterprise as “a takeover, rather than a proper reunion” driven by Hall’s manager and financier Simon Jordan (who confirmed in The Sun, 8 Dec 2008, that he had helped assemble the band starting with Hall and then Golding). (Exclaim!, Dec 2008; The Quietus, Dec 2008; Clash Magazine)

Dammers also objected to the reunion’s first public outing — a set at Bestival, September 2008 — on the grounds that dedicated Specials fans could not get tickets to a sold-out festival. He attended as a spectator and criticised the performance’s emotional register in Mojo (Nov 2008). (Marco on the Bass blog, Oct 2008)

The band disputed this account. Bradbury told journalists he spoke to Dammers “night after night all through 2008” trying to find common ground and that “there just wasn’t a meeting of the ways.” Hall said the core sticking point was format: Dammers wanted a single large-scale show — one date in Coventry at the football stadium for 30,000 people — whereas the reunited band wanted a proper tour of 2,000–3,000-capacity venues. Hall suggested to The Guardian that Dammers “doesn’t like the idea of touring” and had obscured that in his public statement. The band’s position, stated in multiple Feb 2009 pre-tour interviews, was: “We did not kick Jerry Dammers out of the band.” (NME, Feb 2009; Marco on the Bass blog, Feb 2009)

No evidence of formal legal disputes over band-name ownership, trademark, or publishing rights has surfaced in press coverage of this period; the documented disputes were over creative direction, format, and management influence.

2019 reunion (Encore)

The three-member core of Hall, Golding, and Horace Panter that had been touring since 2009 carried forward into the Encore recording sessions (released February 2019). The album was produced by Hall and Golding; longtime touring keyboardist Nikolaj Torp Larsen performed on the record. No public statement from Dammers addressing Encore specifically has been located. Terry Hall’s 2019 interviews around the album did not discuss Dammers’ absence beyond acknowledging he had not been part of the reunion since 2008. The omission for Encore appears to be a continuation of the 2008–09 estrangement rather than a fresh dispute. (uDiscoverMusic, Jan 2019; Wikipedia, Encore album article, as of 2026-06-28)
