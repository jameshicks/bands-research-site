Overview

Icelandic cellist, composer, and singer. Classically trained from age five, Hildur studied composition and new media at the Iceland Academy of the Arts and the Berlin University of the Arts. She comes from a musical family: her father is composer and clarinettist Guðni Franzson, her mother is opera singer Ingveldur Guðrún Ólafsdóttir, and her brother is Þórarinn Guðnason of Agent Fresco.

Her career divides into two overlapping phases. The first (roughly 2000–2014) was rooted in the Reykjavík experimental and Bedroom Community networks: she contributed cello to early múm recordings, performed with Pan Sonic, Throbbing Gristle, and Sunn O))), collaborated with Jóhann Jóhannsson, Ben Frost, Valgeir Sigurðsson, Skúli Sverrisson, BJNilsen (as part of BJ Nilsen & Stilluppsteypa), and Hauschka, and released four solo albums on Touch under her own name (and one as Lost In Hildurness on 12 Tónar). During this period she also played the halldorophone, an electro-acoustic bowed string instrument, on releases by The Knife, Sunn O), and others.

The second phase (2015–present) is dominated by film and television scoring. She scored Sicario: Day of the Soldado (2018), Mary Magdalene (2018, co-composed with Jóhann Jóhannsson), the HBO miniseries Chernobyl (2019, Emmy + Grammy winner), Joker (2019, Academy Award + Golden Globe + BAFTA winner — making her the first solo woman composer to win all three for a single film), Tár (2022), Women Talking (2022), A Haunting in Venice (2023), and Joker: Folie à Deux (2024). She signed to Deutsche Grammophon in 2019. She lives in Berlin; her husband is composer Sam Slater, her closest ongoing collaborator. (Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildur_Guðnadóttir)
