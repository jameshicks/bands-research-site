Mike Fellows

Real name: Michael Fellows. Washington DC bassist, artist, and engineer. Discogs artist ID: 228502. Alias: Miighty Flashlight (Discogs artist ID: 916648).

Born into the DC hardcore orbit, Fellows played bass in two of the most influential Revolution Summer bands — Rites Of Spring and Happy Go Licky — before drifting through indie rock, country-adjacent, and experimental scenes in the 1990s and 2000s. His post-DC career spans Government Issue, Air Miami, Royal Trux, Silver Jews, solo recordings as Miighty Flashlight, and more recent work with Prison (11). He also acquired engineering and production credits alongside his performance work.

DC Hardcore Origins: Government Issue (1984)

Fellows’ earliest Discogs credit is as bassist on Government Issue’s Joy Ride LP (1984, Fountain Of Youth Records). Government Issue were a long-running DC hardcore band, active from 1981, with a rotating membership that included Brian Baker, Tom Lyle, and vocalist John Stabb Schroeder. Joy Ride was produced by Baker, Lyle, and Tom Scott. Fellows’ tenure with GI overlapped with his entry into the Revolution Summer circle.

Rites Of Spring (1984–1986)

Fellows was the bassist in Rites Of Spring, the DC hardcore band whose emotional intensity and melodic restlessness made them a foundational post-hardcore act. The core lineup was Fellows (bass), Guy Picciotto (guitar, vocals), Eddie Janney (guitar), and Brendan Canty (drums).

Happy Go Licky (1986–1988)

After Rites Of Spring dissolved, the same four members — Fellows, Picciotto, Janney, Canty — reconvened as Happy Go Licky, a more improvisational and deliberately loose project. The sole studio release is the self-titled cassette / LP Happy Go Licky (1988, Peterbilt Records).

Air Miami (1994–1998)

Fellows was a member of Air Miami, the DC-area indie pop band formed around Mark Robinson and Bridget Cross after the breakup of Unrest. He contributed as a songwriter on Airplane Rider (1994, Teen Beat), credited alongside Robinson, Cross, and Lauren Feldsher. Air Miami released on Teen Beat and 4AD through approximately 1998.

Royal Trux (member, dates uncertain)

Fellows is listed as a member of Royal Trux on Discogs but carries no release-level performance credits in the database. Royal Trux’s membership list is extensive and includes several musicians who passed through the live or rehearsal lineup without appearing on studio records. His involvement likely predates or coincides with the early Neil Hagerty / Jennifer Herrema recordings. No specific release or year is confirmed by the Discogs data.

Silver Jews (1998–2001)

Fellows appeared as a performer on two key Silver Jews albums during the band’s Nashville-era incarnation under David Berman:

American Water is widely considered Silver Jews’ finest record. Fellows appears in the Performer credit alongside Malkmus (who had appeared on earlier Silver Jews records). His assistant production credit on Bright Flight and Tennessee suggests a sustained working relationship with the Nashville sessions around Berman and Nevers.

Bonnie “Prince” Billy (2001, 2005)

Fellows played bass on Bonnie “Prince” Billy’s Ease Down The Road (2001, Palace Records), a Will Oldham LP with David Pajo handling much of the mixing and instrumentation alongside a large ensemble including Jon Theodore, Bryan Rich, Matt Sweeney, and others.

He returned as additional performer and recording engineer (tracks 3–4) on I Gave You (2005, Drag City), a collaboration between Bonnie “Prince” Billy and Matt Sweeney.

Prison (11) (2020s)

Fellows is a member of Prison (11), an indie/experimental group whose only documented release is Upstate (2023, Drag City). The lineup includes Fellows (bass, vocals), Matthew Lilly (drums, percussion), Paul Major (guitar, vocals), Sam Jayne (guitar, vocals), and Sarim Al-Rawi (guitar, vocals). Mastered by Carl Saff; engineered and mixed by Kevin McMahon.

Getaway Car

Fellows is listed as a member of Getaway Car on Discogs alongside Guy Picciotto and Kim Thompson. No releases appear in the Discogs database for this project.
