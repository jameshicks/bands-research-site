Sylvia Robinson

Born Sylvia Vanderpool (née Vanterpool). Singer, songwriter, producer, and co-founder of Sugar Hill Records (hip-hop). Discogs artist ID: 100584. Also credited as Sylvia Inc. (Discogs entity ID: 48684, her production company).

Overview

Often called the “Mother of Hip-Hop” for her role in founding Sugar Hill Records and producing the first major hip-hop records. Prior career as recording artist (Mickey & Sylvia, “Love Is Strange,” 1957; solo “Pillow Talk,” 1973). Co-founded Sugar Hill in 1979 with her husband Joe Robinson.

As Sylvia Inc., she produced or co-produced the core Grandmaster Flash & The Furious Five catalog including “Freedom,” “Scorpio,” and “The Message.” Co-wrote “The Message” with Duke Bootee, Melle Mel, and Clifton “Jiggs” Chase. Sugar Hill declared bankruptcy in 1985, partly due to artist royalty disputes and the $660,000 White Lines judgment won by 99 Records.

Earlier groups: Mickey & Sylvia (Discogs ID: 287108), Bobby & Sylvia (ID: 2677175).

Production Infrastructure

As Sylvia Inc. (Discogs entity ID: 48684), Robinson was credited as producer on 419 Sugar Hill releases — the dominant production entity on the label. She also engineered and mixed on 77 releases. Her personal writing credits total 235 releases.

The house production team she led included staff producers Joey Robinson Jr. (son, 226 credits), Clifton “Jiggs” Chase (59 co-producer credits), Nate Edmonds (52 credits), Cheryl Cook (50 credits), Billy Jones (47 credits), Reggie Griffin (43 credits), and Duke Bootee / Ed Fletcher (29 credits). Key engineers were Steve Jerome (109 credits) and Eric Thorngren (106 credits).

The house rhythm section — Keith LeBlanc (drums), Doug Wimbish (bass), Skip McDonald (guitar) — played on virtually all Sugar Hill recordings before leaving for On-U Sound c.1983.
