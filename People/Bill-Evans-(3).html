Bill Evans (3)

Overview

William D. Evans is an American jazz-fusion saxophonist born in Clarendon Hills, Illinois. He joined Miles Davis’s comeback band at age 22, appearing on the Columbia albums The Man With The Horn (1981), We Want Miles (1982, live), Star People (1983), and Decoy (1984) — the core documents of Davis’s electric 1980s period. Concurrently he was a founding member of Elements (6), the fusion group co-led with bassist Mark Egan, drummer Danny Gottlieb, and keyboardist Clifford Carter.

In 1984 he joined John McLaughlin’s revived Mahavishnu Orchestra, playing tenor and soprano sax and flute on Mahavishnu (WEA, 1984) and Adventures In Radioland (CBS, 1986). He contributed the composition “Clarendon Hills” (named for his birthplace) to the Mahavishnu album. That same year he released his first solo record, Living In The Crest Of A Wave (Elektra Musician, 1984), produced and written by Evans with the Elements rhythm section.

From the late 1980s onward Evans pursued an active solo career on labels including Blue Note, ESC Records, BHM Productions, and Jazzline, while continuing to guest across fusion, smooth jazz, and big band contexts. He has worked with Herbie Hancock, John Scofield, Mike Stern, Randy Brecker, Marcus Miller, Béla Fleck, Victor Wooten, Darryl Jones, and Steps Ahead, among many others. As of the mid-2020s he continues to record and tour actively.

Elements (6) (1982–)

Elements (6) (Discogs ID: 349830) formed in 1982 as a fusion quartet:

Additional members listed in Discogs at various points include Gil Goldstein, Steve Khan, Joe Caro, and David Mann.

The debut album Elements (Philo, 1982) established the group’s acoustic-fusion sound. Forward Motion (Antilles, 1984) followed with Steve Khan co-credited. A film soundtrack, Blown Away (Victor Japan, 1986), and Illumination (Novus, 1988), Liberal Arts (Novus, 1989), and Spirit River (Novus/RCA, 1990) constitute the main catalog before a long gap. Evans, Egan, and Gottlieb reunited for Wouldn’t It Be Nice (A Jazz Portrait Of Brian Wilson) (Blue Note Contemporary, 1997, arranged by Egan).

Mahavishnu Orchestra (1984–1986)

See Mahavishnu Orchestra for full coverage of the revival lineup. Evans’s specific contributions:

  • Mahavishnu (WEA, 1984): tenor sax, soprano sax, flute on all tracks; sole composition credit “Clarendon Hills” (track B1). Personnel: John McLaughlin (guitar/producer), Billy Cobham (drums), Jonas Hellborg (bass), Mitchel Forman (keyboards), Danny Gottlieb (additional percussion). Guest uncrediteds on “When Blue Turns Gold”: Hariprasad Chaurasia (flute) and Zakir Hussain (tabla).
  • Adventures In Radioland (CBS, 1986): saxophone; same core lineup plus drum programmer Max Costa.
