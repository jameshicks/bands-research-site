Jonathan Poneman

Co-founder and continuing leader, Sub Pop Records. Seattle, Washington. Discogs ID: 341518.

Joined Bruce Pavitt as co-founder when Sub Pop formalized as a label in 1988. Continued as sole leader after Pavitt’s departure around 1995. Oversaw the label’s survival through near-bankruptcy and the Warner Bros. 49% stake deal, and its subsequent expansion into folk, indie pop, and hip-hop in the 2000s–2010s. Discogs credits list him as producer or A&R on numerous early Sub Pop releases.

2000s Signings — The Shins, Iron & Wine, Fleet Foxes

The Shins

The Shins were brought to Sub Pop’s attention via two routes: Isaac Brock of Modest Mouse passed cassette demos (including “New Slang”) to the label, and James Mercer also sent material directly. Poneman saw The Shins open for Modest Mouse at a show in San Francisco and was impressed enough to offer them a spot in Sub Pop’s Singles Club; “New Slang” was released as their Singles Club entry in February 2001. The strong press response to that 7” led Sub Pop to sign the band fully, and their debut album Oh, Inverted World followed in June 2001. Multiple secondary accounts credit Poneman personally seeing the band live as the direct trigger for the Singles Club offer, which then converted into a full album deal. [source: https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/the-shins/25-years-ago-the-shins-oh-inverted-world-changed-our-lives — paywalled, details corroborated via search snippets; https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20030425/nite25/shins-sub-pop-bridge-gap-between-bad-and-good-times]

Iron & Wine

Sam Beam’s home recordings were shared by his childhood friend Ben Bridwell (later of Band of Horses) with Mike McGonigal, editor of Yeti magazine, who included Beam’s track “Dead Man’s Will” on a Yeti compilation CD. Jonathan Poneman heard the recording and contacted Beam directly to propose a record deal. Beam signed and released his debut The Creek Drank the Cradle on Sub Pop in 2002. Poneman’s personal outreach to Beam — rather than Beam approaching Sub Pop — is documented in the Wikipedia article on Iron & Wine. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_%26_Wine as of 2026-07-30]

Fleet Foxes

Fleet Foxes signed to Sub Pop on 18 January 2008 after a bidding war with larger labels; the band chose Sub Pop in part because of its Seattle roots and independent identity. The Sub Pop A&R credited with the Fleet Foxes signing is Sue Busch, not Poneman directly — she is named in a HitQuarters interview (4 May 2009) as the A&R representative for Fleet Foxes. Poneman was the label head during this period but the specific internal champion was Busch. [source: HitQuarters interview with Sue Busch, 4 May 2009, listed at https://www.songquarters.com/archive.php; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Foxes as of 2026-07-30]

Summary: Poneman’s personal A&R involvement is confirmed for The Shins (attended live show, initiated Singles Club offer) and Iron & Wine (contacted Beam directly after hearing the Yeti compilation). For Fleet Foxes, the credited A&R is Sue Busch; Poneman’s personal role in that signing is not documented in available sources.
