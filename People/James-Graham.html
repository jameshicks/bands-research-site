James Graham

Scottish vocalist and lyricist. Real name James Alexander Graham. Born 29 June 1984. Discogs artist ID: 708628. Name variations on Discogs: Graham, James, James Alexander Graham, NJG.

Lead vocalist and lyricist of The Twilight Sad (Kilsyth, Scotland; Discogs ID 578828). Also a member of Out Lines, Gentle Sinners, and The Fruit Tree Foundation.

Biography

The Twilight Sad — Formation and FatCat Era (2003–2017)

The Twilight Sad formed in Kilsyth, North Lanarkshire around 2003, founded by Graham (vocals) and Andy MacFarlane (guitar), with Mark Devine (drums) and Craig Orzel (bass) completing the original lineup. Graham’s thick Scottish accent and emotionally direct lyric writing became a defining feature of the band’s sound, described by critics as “folk with layers of noise.”

Their debut album Fourteen Autumns & Fifteen Winters (2007, FatCat Records) was produced by Andy MacFarlane and generated substantial critical attention. It ran to 18 pressings. The follow-up mini-album Here, It Never Snowed. Afterwards It Did (2008) was produced and engineered by Iain Cook, with MacFarlane, Devine, and Cook all taking mixing credits. Violin was contributed by Laura McFarlane.

For the second album Forget The Night Ahead (2009), production shifted to a collaborative model: MacFarlane produced, with Mark Devine and Paul Savage both taking co-producer credits, and Savage handling mixing and recording at his Chem19 studio. Glasgow-based Martin Doherty contributed noise strings. Mastering was by Alan Douches. Graham is credited for lyrics and co-writing throughout this period; music is credited to MacFarlane.

No One Can Ever Know (2012) marked a stylistic departure into industrial and krautrock territory. The band self-produced, with Jim Anderson (4) as engineer, mixer, and additional producer. Andrew Weatherall received an unusual “Anti-Produced By” credit. Mastering again by Alan Douches. Artwork and illustrations by DLT (3).

Nobody Wants To Be Here And Nobody Wants To Leave (2014) was produced primarily by MacFarlane, with Peter Katis and Andrew Bush (3) handling additional production and mixing. Johnny Docherty joined the lineup on bass. Graham is credited for lyrics by name (as “James Alexander Graham”) on both this album and No One Can Ever Know.

Craig Orzel departed in early 2010 after The Wrong Car EP; Mark Devine left amicably in January 2018.

Side Projects and Scottish Scene Involvement (2010–2018)

The Fruit Tree Foundation (Discogs ID 2583880) — a Scottish mental health awareness project founded in 2010 by Rod Jones (Idlewild) and Emma Pollock (The Delgados) in partnership with the Mental Health Foundation. Graham was a member alongside Scott Hutchison (Frightened Rabbit), Alasdair Roberts, Jill O’Sullivan, and Rod Jones. The project released one album, First Edition (2011, Chemikal Underground / self-released). That record was recorded and mixed by Paul Savage at Chem19. Jonny Scott played drums. Aidan Moffat contributed package design and photography.

Out Lines (Discogs ID 6082449) — a trio formed with producer Marcus MacKay and Glasgow singer-songwriter Kathryn Joseph. Their single album Conflats was released in 2017 on Rock Action Records. MacKay wrote, recorded, and produced; Graham and Kathryn Joseph both contributed vocals and writing. Mixed by Tony Doogan, mastered by Frank Arkwright at Abbey Road. Artwork was co-credited to DLT (3) and Scott Hutchison. See Out Lines for the full release detail.

Aidan Moffat — Where You’re Meant To Be (2016, Kiss My Beard Productions) — Graham sang on this soundtrack album for Paul Fegan’s documentary film about Moffat’s touring of Scottish traditional folk songs. Graham is credited as “Vocals [Singing]”; Moffat wrote the lyrics (bar one traditional piece) and sang and played drums.

The Twilight Sad at Tiny Changes (2019) — Graham contributed liner notes to Tiny Changes: A Celebration of Frightened Rabbit’s ‘The Midnight Organ Fight’ (Atlantic Records), the tribute album released in the wake of Scott Hutchison’s death in May 2018.

Gentle Sinners (Discogs ID 11157959) — a duo with Aidan Moffat (Arab Strap). They released a double A-side single Killing This Time / DTM (2022, Rock Action Records) and the full album These Actions Cannot Be Undone (2022, Rock Action Records), on which both Graham and Moffat are credited as performers and producers — uncredited on the sleeve. Graham also contributed photography, credited under the alias NJG. Paul Savage mastered the album.

Rock Action Era and It Won’t Be Like This All The Time (2019)

The Twilight Sad moved to Rock Action Records — the Glasgow label run by Mogwai — for their fifth album It Won’t Be Like This All The Time (January 2019). By this point the lineup consisted of Graham, MacFarlane, Johnny Docherty (bass), Brendan Smith (2) (keyboards), and Jonny Scott (drums; credited on the album, though Sebastien Schultz is also listed as a Discogs group member). MacFarlane produced and provided all music; mixed by Chris Coady, mastered by Greg Calbi. Robert Smith (The Cure) appeared under the “Musical Assistance [Vambo]” credit — the same credit he held on the 2015 split single with The Twilight Sad. DLT (3) handled artwork.

It’s The Long Goodbye (2026)

The Twilight Sad’s sixth studio album, released in 2026 on Rock Action Records. Robert Smith returns with on-record credits for guitar, 6-string bass, and Mellotron on tracks 4, 6, and 9, in addition to the recurring “Musical Assistance [Vambo]” credit. MacFarlane produced and plays guitars, synths, and programming. Graham is credited for lyrics and vocals. Mixed by Chris Coady, mastered by Matt Colton. Engineered and additional production by Andy Savours. New lineup members: Alex Mackay (bass, synths, piano), David Jeans (drums).

Production and Writing Credits

Across all Twilight Sad albums, Graham holds lyrics/writing credits. MacFarlane composes the music; Graham writes all the words. On These Actions Cannot Be Undone (Gentle Sinners), Graham is co-producer (uncredited on sleeve) alongside Aidan Moffat. On Conflats (Out Lines), he shares co-writing with MacKay and Kathryn Joseph.

Graham also used the alias NJG for photography credits on These Actions Cannot Be Undone (Gentle Sinners, 2022).
