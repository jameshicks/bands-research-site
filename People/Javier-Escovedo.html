Javier Escovedo

Discogs ID: 549087
Discogs name variations: Escouedo, Escovedo, J. Escovedo, Javier

American guitarist and singer-songwriter from the Escovedo musical family. Brother of Alejandro Escovedo; the wider family includes Pete Escovedo and Sheila E. Best known as co-founder, lead guitarist, and primary vocalist/songwriter of The Zeros (Chula Vista, 1976–1980 and various reunions), and as co-founder of The True Believers (Austin, 1983–1990) alongside Alejandro. Also played in The Sacred Hearts (2) and Chariot (2); solo releases on Folc Records (2012) and Saustex Media (2016).

The Zeros

Javier was a founding member of The Zeros alongside Hector Penalosa (bass), Karton “Baba” Chenelle (drums), and Robert Alan Lopez (rhythm guitar). He is the primary songwriter on the band’s original BOMP! singles and later reunion records, and sang lead on the majority of the band’s catalog.

The debut single “Don’t Push Me Around / Wimp” (BOMP!, 1977) was self-produced by the band; Javier wrote both sides and sang lead. The follow-up “Wild Week-End / Beat Your Heart Out” (BOMP!, 1978) was co-produced with BOMP! founder Greg Shaw (2). A third single appeared on Test Tube Records in 1980 before the initial break-up.

After the Zeros’ first run, Javier co-produced the 1991 BOMP! retrospective Don’t Push Me Around (Rare & Unreleased Classics From ‘77) — a 12-track LP collecting the singles, outtakes, and unreleased recordings, with separate production credits going to Greg Shaw (A-side originals), Craig Leon (one track), and the band (remaining tracks). He wrote the liner notes for the 1994 reunion album Knockin’ Me Dead (Gasatanka Records / Rockville), co-produced it alongside Bill Bartell, and handled lead guitar and lead vocals across most of the record; guest vocalists included Jeff McDonald, Kim Shattuck, and Steven McDonald of Redd Kross. The 1999 reunion album Right Now! (BOMP!) followed. The most recent Zeros release is the 2019 single “In The Spotlight / Nowhere To Run” on Munster Records (MR 7318), produced by The Zeros with Victor Penalosa on bass.

The True Believers

Javier co-founded The True Believers in Austin, Texas with brother Alejandro Escovedo, Jon Dee Graham, JD Foster, Rey Washam, and Kevin Foley. The band’s sole studio album, True Believers (EMI America / Rounder Records, 1986), was produced by Jim Dickinson and mixed by Joe Hardy. Javier is credited Vocals, Guitar and is a co-writer on several tracks (with Alejandro and bassist Denny DeGorio): “Tell Her,” “Ring The Bell,” “Hard Road,” “We’re Wrong,” and “I Get Excited.” Jon Dee Graham wrote “Lucky Moon” and “Sleep Enough To Dream.”

The 1994 Rykodisc compilation Hard Road assembled previously unreleased material across two lineup eras; Javier appears on the first eleven tracks (the original lineup sessions, produced by Jim Dickinson / Mike Stewart (2)), with later tracks produced by Jeff Glixman.

The Sacred Hearts (2)

Javier played guitar and co-wrote material for The Sacred Hearts (2), an early 1990s Chula Vista/LA garage band also featuring Karton “Baba” Chenelle (drums), Bill Dusha (guitar/vocals), and Chris Trent (2) (bass). Their sole Discogs release is the “Temptation” 7” (Sympathy For The Record Industry, SFTRI 214, 1992) — three tracks, with Javier credited Guitar, Vocals, and Written-By. Recorded and mixed by Geoff Siegel and Rick Parker.

Chariot (2)

Chariot (2) was a late-1990s band whose Discogs membership includes Ken Stringfellow (Posies), Brian Young (5), Bill Bartell, and Javier Escovedo. They released two records on Munster Records in 1998: I Am Ben Hur (produced by Chariot (2), with Javier on guitar) and What If I Run Out Of My Pills?. A third entry, Winter Rodeo 1999, appeared on a not-on-label European release in 1999.
