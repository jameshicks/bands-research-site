Smokey Hormel

Jazz and rock guitarist, born 1959 in Los Angeles, California. Real name: Gregory Hormel. Discogs artist ID: 293886. One of the most widely-employed session guitarists in Los Angeles from the 1990s through the 2010s, with credits ranging from Beck and Johnny Cash to Adele, Neil Diamond, Norah Jones, and the Dixie Chicks. Equally active as a bandleader and ensemble player: founder of Smokey & Miho (with Miho Hatori), member of Forro In The Dark, former member of The Blasters, The John Doe Thing, and The Million Dollar Bashers.

Overview

Hormel came up in the Los Angeles Americana and roots-rock underground in the late 1980s, joining The Blasters for a period and playing on the 1994 Merle Haggard tribute Tulare Dust (Hightone Records) alongside Dave Alvin, John Doe, and Joey Waronker. By the mid-1990s he had become a core member of The John Doe Thing — the band fronted by John Doe (2) (of X) — appearing on their releases on Kill Rock Stars.

His profile expanded sharply in 1998 when he became part of Beck’s core recording band, appearing on Mutations (1998), Tropicalia (1998), Midnite Vultures (1999), Sea Change (2002), the Odelay expanded reissue (2008), and The Information (2006, intro sounds). The partnership with Beck was anchored by the same rhythm section across most of these records: Justin Meldal-Johnsen on bass and Joey Waronker on drums.

Alongside session work, Hormel formed Smokey & Miho with vocalist Miho Hatori (of Cibo Matto), releasing a self-titled album (2002, Afro Sambas) and The Two EPs (2003, Afro Sambas), with Hormel producing. He also became a member of Forro In The Dark, a New York-based Brazilian forró group also featuring Mauro Refosco, Guilherme Monteiro, and Davi Vieira.

His session calendar through the 2000s and 2010s was extraordinarily broad: he played on Johnny Cash’s final American Recordings albums (IV through VI), Norah Jones’s The Fall and Chasing Pirates, Neil Diamond’s 12 Songs (2005) and Home Before Dark (2008), the Dixie Chicks’ Taking The Long Way (2006), and Adele’s 21 (2011). He appeared in the Leonard Cohen: I’m Your Man concert film band (2006), and contributed guitar to Mike Patton and Jean-Claude Vannier’s Corpse Flower (2019, Ipecac Recordings) and Bettye LaVette’s Blackbirds (2020, Verve).
