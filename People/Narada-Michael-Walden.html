Narada Michael Walden

Discogs ID: 49530 Born: April 23, 1952, Kalamazoo, Michigan, USA Real name: Michael Walden (né Michael Walden; spiritual name “Narada” given by Sri Chinmoy in the early 1970s) Based: San Rafael, California Roles: Drummer, multi-instrumentalist, vocalist, record producer, arranger Active: 1974–present

American drummer and record producer who moved between two very different worlds: a fusion sideman and solo artist in the jazz-rock vanguard (1974–1984), then a dominant force in mainstream pop and R&B production (1985–1995 and beyond). He replaced Billy Cobham in John McLaughlin’s Mahavishnu Orchestra second lineup (1974–1976), appeared on Tommy Bolin’s Teaser and Jeff Beck’s Wired, played a stint with Weather Report and briefly with Journey, launched a solo career on Atlantic from 1976, then shifted to production work that defined the sound of Whitney Houston, Aretha Franklin, and others. He owns Tarpan Studios and Tarpan Records in San Rafael, California. Some of his compositions are published through his own Gratitude Sky Music imprint.

Biography

Jazz-Fusion Era (1974–1976)

Walden’s first major credit was as the replacement for Billy Cobham in the Mahavishnu Orchestra’s second lineup. The group was rebuilt entirely by McLaughlin after the original four-piece dissolved in late 1973; Walden joined alongside electric violinist Jean-Luc Ponty, electric bassist Ralphe Armstrong, and keyboardist/vocalist Gayle Moran.

Apocalypse (CBS, 1974) — the first album Walden appeared on — was the most orchestrally ambitious release in the band’s catalog: a collaboration with the London Symphony Orchestra, conducted by Michael Tilson Thomas and orchestrated by Michael Gibbs. Produced by George Martin at AIR Studios London, engineered by Geoff Emerick. Walden is credited under his birth name “Michael Walden” on this album — the “Narada” name came from Sri Chinmoy around this time. He plays drums, percussion, and contributes vocals.

Visions Of The Emerald Beyond (Columbia, 1975) — Walden is credited on drums, percussion, vocals, and clavinet. He also receives a writing credit for the track “Cosmic Strut” (B1), his only composition credit on a Mahavishnu record. Produced by John McLaughlin and Ken Scott; Ponty still in the lineup on this album.

Inner Worlds (CBS/Columbia, 1976) — Keyboardist Stu Goldberg replaced Moran; this was the last second-lineup album. Walden is credited with drums, timpani, congas, marimba, shaker, piano, lead vocals, and organ — his most expansive instrument list. Co-produced by Dennis MacKay and McLaughlin.

Alongside the Mahavishnu work, Walden appeared on several other significant jazz-fusion and rock records in 1975–1976: Tommy Bolin’s Teaser (Nemperor, 1975) on drums; Jeff Beck’s Wired (Epic, 1976) on drums and piano; Larry Coryell’s Velvet Darkness (CTI, 1976); Chick Corea’s My Spanish Heart (Polydor, 1976) with handclap credits; and Moonshadows (Epic, 1976) playing drums and keyboards.

Solo Career and Continued Sideman Work (1976–1984)

Walden launched his solo career on Atlantic Records, beginning with Garden Of Love Light (Atlantic, 1976). Produced by Tom Dowd, with engineering by Dennis MacKay and assistant engineering by Jimmy Douglass. This is his debut solo album and marked the beginning of a long Atlantic relationship.

I Cry, I Smile (Atlantic, 1977) — Walden produced, composed, and arranged; he plays vocals, keyboards, and drums.

Awakening (Atlantic, 1979) — produced by Walden (co-production on individual tracks by Patrick Adams, Sonny Burke (2), and Wayne Henderson). The album features an extraordinary cast: Carlos Santana (guitar), Hiram Bullock (guitar), Jay Graydon (guitar), Pat Thrall (guitar), Pointer Sisters (backing vocals), Patti Scialfa (backing vocals), Airto Moreira (percussion), The Brecker Brothers (horns), Greg Phillinganes, Bobby Lyle, and Clifford Carter (keyboards). Sri Chinmoy contributed the poem on the back cover.

The Dance Of Life (Atlantic, 1979) — Walden is lead vocalist, drummer, pianist, percussionist, and arranger (horn and rhythm arrangements) in addition to producer. Regular session collaborators Bob Clearmountain (mixing), Frank Martin (keys), Corrado Rustici (guitar synth), and Vicki Randle begin appearing in this period.

Further solo albums followed: Victory (Atlantic, 1980), The Real Thing (Atlantic, 1980), Confidence (Atlantic, 1982), Looking At You, Looking At Me (Atlantic, 1983), Reach Out (Atlantic, 1983), The Nature Of Things (Warner Bros., 1985), Divine Emotions (Reprise, 1988).

During this period Walden also continued session work: drums on Jaco Pastorius (the debut solo album, Epic, 1976, confirmed by Discogs credits), drums on Jeff Beck Group’s output, drums on Rick James’s Street Songs (Gordy, 1981), drums on the Clarke/Duke Project II (Epic, 1983), and additional percussion on Teena Marie and others.

He also played on Robert Fripp’s Exposure (EG/Polydor, 1979) — listed as “Featuring” — one of the more unlikely pairings.

A brief stint with Weather Report appears in his group credits on Discogs; he plays drums on Black Market (CBS, 1976). He also appears as a member of Journey, although his recording credit with them in the primary discography is sparse — his tenure was brief and predated their commercial breakthrough.

He was a member of the Tommy Bolin Band.

Mainstream Pop Production Peak (1985–1995)

Under contract with Arista Records under Clive Davis, Walden became one of the most prolific producers in mainstream American pop and R&B. His sound in this era — dense drum programming (LinnDrum/Simmons) mixed with live percussion, synthesizer layers, and horn sections — defined the commercial radio sound of the late 1980s.

Aretha Franklin — Walden produced and co-wrote “Freeway Of Love” (Arista, 1985), which features Clarence Clemons on saxophone and Dez Dickerson on guitar. He produced and co-wrote many tracks on Who’s Zoomin’ Who? (Arista, 1985) and the full album Aretha (Arista, 1986), which was arranged and produced by Walden with string arrangements by Michael Gibbs. He also co-wrote “I Knew You Were Waiting (For Me)” (1987), recorded with George Michael.

Whitney Houston — Walden is credited on the debut Whitney Houston (Arista, 1985) as “The Players.” More substantially, on Whitney (Arista, 1987), he produced and arranged tracks A1, A2, A5, B2, B4–B6 — more than half the album, including “I Wanna Dance With Somebody (Who Loves Me)” and “So Emotional.” Key session team on those tracks: Corrado Rustici (guitar synthesizer), Michael Gibbs (conductor and string arranger on A2, B5, B6), David Frazer (recorded and mixed). He also produced “Where Do Broken Hearts Go” (Arista, 1988), “All The Man That I Need” (Arista, 1990), and tracks on I’m Your Baby Tonight (Arista, 1990). Clive Davis was executive producer across all these albums.

Mariah Carey — Walden has a writing credit on the debut Mariah Carey (CBS, 1990) and co-production on Emotions (Columbia, 1991) and There’s Got To Be A Way.

Later Career (1995–present)

Walden continued production work into the late 1990s and 2000s, including tracks for George Benson, Pia Zadora, Sheena Easton, and Kylie Minogue (Junction Seven, Virgin, 1996). He produced Aretha Franklin’s A Rose Is Still A Rose era and collaborated on Higher Love (Kygo/RCA, 2019), a posthumous Whitney Houston duet that charted globally.

He released further solo material including Sending Love To Everyone (Walden Records, 1995), Thunder 2013, and Evolution. He plays drums on the Mohini Dey collaboration (2023) and received a producer credit on Sentient (Candid, 2025).

His studio Tarpan Studios in San Rafael remains active.

Scene Connections

  • John McLaughlin — shared Mahavishnu context; both disciples of Sri Chinmoy; McLaughlin gave Walden the path into the band
  • Billy Cobham — the drummer Walden replaced in Mahavishnu; Cobham had simultaneously established the template for fusion drumming that Walden inherited
  • Jean-Luc Ponty — bandmate on Apocalypse and Visions; both departed the Mahavishnu second lineup around the same time
  • Jeff Beck — Walden played drums and piano on Wired (1976), bridging his jazz-fusion period into mainstream rock
  • Tommy Bolin — Walden drummed on Teaser; Bolin died in December 1976, close to when Walden was pivoting to his solo career
  • Tom Dowd — Atlantic house producer who supervised the debut LP; Dowd’s Atlantic network likely facilitated Walden’s broader production career
  • Clive Davis — the Arista/Atlantic relationship was central to the 1985–1995 production peak
  • Carlos Santana — Sri Chinmoy disciple (credited on Awakening as “Devadip Carlos Santana”); his guest appearance on Walden’s 1979 album reflects the spiritual community’s crossover into music
  • Patti Scialfa — appeared on Awakening as a backing vocalist years before her public profile as a Springsteen collaborator
  • Whitney Houston — the relationship ran from 1985 to at least 1992; Walden produced the majority of Whitney (1987), her biggest-selling album
  • Aretha Franklin — Walden co-wrote and produced “Freeway Of Love” and substantial portions of her 1985–1987 Arista comeback; the Franklin/Walden sound was a defining element of her late career
  • Walter Afanasieff — co-writer on later sessions; Afanasieff went on to produce Mariah Carey’s peak work, a chain traceable partly through the Walden circle
  • Bob Clearmountain — 98 shared releases; Clearmountain’s mixing presence ties the Walden production catalog to the wider 1980s mainstream sound

Open Questions

  • Exact dates and recording details of the Weather Report stint are not resolvable from Discogs alone (he plays on Black Market but the extent of live/touring involvement is unclear)
  • Journey tenure: he is listed as a member on Discogs but no primary recording credits with Journey are visible in the discography — may have been a touring period before Perry/Gregg Rolie era albums
  • Inner Worlds (1976): the Discogs Japan pressing (id 2160513) has the fullest credit list; the US/Columbia pressing (id 7565030) has only Dennis MacKay and McLaughlin as producers with no performer credits — fuller personnel may only appear on the Japan or UK sleeve
  • Whether Walden played on any unreleased or abandoned Mahavishnu material beyond the three second-lineup studio albums is unknown
  • The exact Whitney Houston tracks on Whitney he is credited for: Discogs lists A1, A2, A5, B2, B4–B6 but does not name individual tracks — track identification requires either the release liner notes or a web source
  • Extent of co-writing credit on the Mariah Carey debut beyond Discogs listing is unclear
