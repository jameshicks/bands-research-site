Sylvie Courvoisier

Swiss-American pianist, composer, and bandleader. Born 30 November 1968, Lausanne, Switzerland. Based in New York City since 1998. Discogs artist ID: 369026.

Overview

Sylvie Courvoisier studied classical music at the Conservatory of Lausanne and jazz at the Conservatory of Montreux. She won Switzerland’s Prix des Jeunes Créateurs in 1996 and relocated to Brooklyn in 1998. Her work spans avant-jazz improvisation, chamber music, and contemporary classical composition, with a career built across both European and New York scenes. She teaches at the New School’s Jazz and Contemporary Music program in New York City. (Wikipedia)

Her primary instrument is piano, and she also plays spinet on some recordings. She has been credited as composer, producer, and arranger across most of her own releases. Name variations on Discogs: Courvoisier, S. Courvoisier, Sylvie Curvoisier.

Awards include: Switzerland’s Prix des Jeunes Créateurs (1996), Grand Prix de la Fondation Vaudoise pour la Culture (2010), SUISA Prize for Jazz (2017), Foundation for Contemporary Arts Grants to Artists (2018), Swiss Music Prize (2018), United States Artist Fellow (2020), Germany’s International Jazz Piano Prize / Deutsche Jazzpreis (2022), American Academy of Arts and Letters Music Award (2025), Swiss Music Grand Prix (2025). (Wikipedia)

Early Career and European Period (1994–1997)

Courvoisier led Swiss ensembles before her New York move. Her debut recording, Sauvagerie Courtoise (Unit Records, 1994), features a quintet with Guglielmo Pagnozzi (sax), Lauro Rossi (trombone), Pascal Portner (drums), and Banz Oester (bass). Her second, Ocre: Music for Barrel Organ, Piano, Tuba and Percussion (Enja, 1997), is a quintet with Michel Godard (tuba), Pierre Charial (barrel organ), Mark Nauseef (drums), and Tony Overwater (bass). A third Enja record, Y2K (2000), reduces the ensemble to a trio with Godard and Charial.

New York Period and the Feldman Duo (1998–2009)

After moving to Brooklyn, Courvoisier formed a long-term partnership with American violinist Mark Feldman. The duo debuted with Music for Violin & Piano (Avant, 1999) — released on John Zorn’s Avant label in Japan, with Zorn and Kazunori Sugiyama as executive producers. The duo subsequently recorded Masada Recital (Tzadik, 2004) and Malphas: Book of Angels Volume 3 (Tzadik, 2006), both albums of John Zorn compositions; Obligia (Tzadik, 2010); and Live at Theatre Vidy-Lausanne (Intakt, 2013). Their final duo album was Time Gone Out (Intakt, 2019), composed with support from a Chamber Music America New Jazz Works commission.

The duo also co-led the Sylvie Courvoisier–Mark Feldman Quartet (Discogs ID: 1843582), whose lineup included bassist Thomas Morgan (4) and drummer Gerry Hemingway, recording To Fly To Steal (Intakt, 2010) and Hotel du Nord (Intakt, 2011); a later incarnation with Scott Colley and Billy Mintz recorded Birdies for Lulu (Intakt, 2014).

Courvoisier’s landmark release as solo leader from this period is Abaton (ECM, 2003), a double CD: disc one contains her original compositions performed by the trio of Courvoisier, Feldman (violin), and Erik Friedlander (cello); disc two presents the same trio in free improvisation. Produced by Manfred Eicher at Rainbow Studio, Oslo.

Other early New York collaborations: duo-piano album Deux Pianos with Jacques Demierre (Intakt, 2000); appearances on John Zorn’s Cobra (Tzadik, 2002), Femina (Tzadik, 2009), and Dictée/Liber Novus (Tzadik, 2010); and duo Every So Often and trio As Soon As Possible with saxophonist Ellery Eskelin and cellist Vincent Courtois.

Mephista (2002–2004)

Courvoisier was a founding member of Mephista (2) (Discogs ID: 229612), a trio with Ikue Mori (electronics) and Susie Ibarra (drums). Mephista recorded two albums for Tzadik, both produced by the band and with John Zorn as executive producer:

  • Black Narcissus (Tzadik TZ 7704, 2002) — cover art by Salvador Dalí; recorded by Jim Anderson; mastered by Scott Hull
  • Entomological Reflections (Tzadik, 2004)

Solo Piano and Quintets (2007–2015)

Signs and Epigrams (Tzadik, 2007) was Courvoisier’s first solo piano release. For Lonelyville (Intakt, 2007), she composed a suite for a quintet with Feldman, Ikue Mori (electronics), Vincent Courtois (cello), and Gerald Cleaver (drums).

Courvoisier played piano on Erik Friedlander’s Claws & Wings (Skipstone, 2013) alongside Ikue Mori. She released the duo album Either Or And with Evan Parker (Relative Pitch, 2014) and Salt Task with Parker and Chris Corsano (Relative Pitch, 2016).

She is also a member of the Phantom Orchard Ensemble (Discogs ID: 3903427) — the 6-piece formation featuring Zeena Parkins, Ikue Mori, Maja S. K. Ratkje, Margaret Parkins, and Sara Parkins — appearing on Through The Looking-Glass (Tzadik TZ 4003, 2014).

Trio with Drew Gress and Kenny Wollesen (2014–present)

Courvoisier’s long-running trio with bassist Drew Gress and drummer Kenny Wollesen has produced multiple critically acclaimed albums, all primarily on Intakt:

  • Double Windsor (Tzadik, 2014) — named best album of the year by Slate and NY City Jazz Record; awarded “CHOC” by Jazz Magazine
  • D’Agala (Intakt, 2018) — four stars in DownBeat; ranked best of year by NYT and LA Times
  • Free Hoops (Intakt, 2020)
  • To Be Other-Wise (Intakt, 2024) — solo piano
  • Éclats – Live In Europe (Intakt, 2026) — trio, live

Chimaera Ensemble (2023–present)

Chimaera (Intakt CD 410, 2023) is a double CD presenting a new large ensemble: Courvoisier (piano), Wadada Leo Smith (trumpet), Fennesz (guitar/electronics), Nate Wooley (trumpet), Drew Gress (bass), Kenny Wollesen (drums/vibraphone). Commissioned by the Sons D’Hiver Festival. Recording produced by Courvoisier and David Breskin; recorded by Ryan Streber; mastered by Denis Blackham.

Collaborations with Mary Halvorson (2017–present)

A key latter-career duo partnership with guitarist Mary Halvorson:

  • Crop Circles (Relative Pitch, 2017) — four stars in DownBeat
  • Searching for the Disappeared Hour (Pyroclastic, 2021) — praised in NYT
  • Bone Bells (Pyroclastic, 2025)

Other Projects

  • VWCR (Discogs ID: 6636410): quartet with Ken Vandermark, Nate Wooley, Tom Rainey. Recorded Noise of Our Time (Intakt CD 310, 2018).
  • Lockdown (Clean Feed CF560CD, 2021): trio with Ned Rothenberg and Julian Sartorius.
  • Transatlantic Trance Map (Discogs ID: 15093314): large ensemble also including Evan Parker, Ikue Mori, Craig Taborn, Mat Maneri, Peter Evans, Sam Pluta, Ned Rothenberg, and others.
  • Winged Serpents (Discogs ID: 6685438): group membership noted on Discogs; no releases yet documented.
  • TST (7) (Discogs ID: 3815990): group membership noted on Discogs.
  • Ocre (2) (Discogs ID: 1739971): group membership noted on Discogs; recorded Y2K (Enja, 2000).
  • Sylvie Courvoisier Quintetto (Discogs ID: 2119483): recorded Sauvagerie Courtoise (Unit Records, 1994).
  • Herb Robertson NY Downtown Allstars (Discogs ID: 1743234): group membership noted on Discogs.
  • Michel Godard Quartet (Discogs ID: 2650755): group membership noted on Discogs.

Collaboration with Israel Galván (Spanish dancer/choreographer) on La Consagración de la Primavera, combining a two-piano interpretation of Stravinsky’s Rite of Spring with Courvoisier’s own piece Spectre d’un Songe, premiered 2019 with co-pianist Cory Smythe. Album released 2021 on Pyroclastic.
