Butch Morris

Cornetist, composer, and conductor. Born Lawrence Douglas Morris, February 10, 1947, Long Beach, California; died January 22, 2013, Brooklyn, New York. Discogs artist ID: 268350. Brother of bassist Wilber Morris. Core figure in the New York downtown and loft jazz scenes, and the originator of “conduction” — a system of real-time compositional direction for improvising ensembles using hand signals and gestures.

Overview

Morris moved to New York in the mid-1970s and quickly embedded himself in the emerging loft jazz scene. His longest creative partnership was with tenor saxophonist David Murray: Morris appeared on Murray’s records from 1978 onwards, first as a cornetist and later as conductor of the David Murray Big Band. Through the 1980s he expanded his practice beyond the cornet, developing conduction as an autonomous art form — he could direct groups of any size and instrumentation, specifying texture, dynamics, tempo, and pitch contour in real time without pre-written scores.

His conduction practice eventually took him to Europe, Japan, and the Middle East, where he assembled ensembles from local musicians and directed them using the same vocabulary of signals. The system was documented in a ten-CD box set, Testament: A Conduction Collection (New World Records, 1995), covering fifty conductions recorded between 1985 and 1995.

Morris died of lung cancer in January 2013.

Early Career and European Sessions (1977–1979)

Morris’s earliest documented recordings were made in France in 1977. He was a co-leader on Europamerica (Palm, 1977) alongside Jean-Charles Capon, Philippe Maté, and Serge Rahoerson — a four-way co-lead session. The same year he appeared on several Paris dates: The Other Side and Tricks Of The Trade (both Marge), and the two-volume Vol. I: Penthouse Jazz / Vol. II: Holy Siege On Intrigue (Circle Records) with what appear to be European free-jazz ensembles.

He also appeared in 1977 on Frank Lowe’s Lowe And Behold (Musicworks) — a large-ensemble recording billed to The Frank Lowe Orchestra, with John Zorn (alto), Billy Bang (violin), Phillip Wilson (drums), Eugene Chadbourne (guitar), and Joseph Bowie (trombone). This was an early sighting of the downtown NYC network on a single record.

In 1978, Morris was a sideman on David Murray’s Interboogieology (Black Saint), an early Black Saint date with Johnny Dyani and Oliver Johnson. He was also the primary billed artist on Last Of The Hipman (Red Record, 1978).

The David Murray Octet records Ming (1980) and Home (1982) are central Black Saint documents. Morris was the cornetist alongside a lineup that also included Wilber Morris on bass, Henry Threadgill on alto, George Lewis on trombone, and Olu Dara on trumpet — a concentrated gathering of the AACM/NYC downtown lineages. By 1985’s Live At “Sweet Basil” records, Morris had transitioned from player to conductor of Murray’s large ensemble.

Own Leader Sessions and the Conduction Series (1982–2008)

Morris’s leader recordings moved through several phases:

Sound Aspects Period (1986–1989)

The German label Sound Aspects (run by Pedro De Freitas) was the primary home for his early conduction documentation:

Current Trends In Racism In Modern America (1986) was his first major conduction document as a leader. The ensemble was an unusual mix of downtown NYC improvisers and AACM-adjacent figures; Morris composed, orchestrated, conducted, and produced. Christian Marclay’s turntables alongside John Zorn’s game calls and Frank Lowe’s saxophone indexed the range of experimental practice Morris was drawing from.

Nine Below Zero was a more intimate trio record with Wayne Horvitz (piano, synth) and Bobby Previte (drums) — a downtown NYC grouping that ran alongside Horvitz’s other collaborations of the period.

Todos Santos expanded that trio into a six-piece including Robin Holcomb (whose compositions are the basis) and Bill Frisell.

New World Records / Conduction Documentation (1991–1995)

ReleaseYearLabelNotes
Dust To Dust1991New World RecordsLarge ensemble: Andrew Cyrille, Marty Ehrlich, Zeena Parkins, Wayne Horvitz, Jason Hwang, Jean-Paul Bourelly, J.A. Deane, John Purcell, Myra Melford

Dust To Dust (New World Records, 1991) brought a large conduction ensemble into a formal documentation setting; the personnel represent a cross-section of downtown NYC: AACM, free jazz, and post-punk experimentalists.

Testament: A Conduction Collection (1995) remains the definitive document — a ten-disc retrospective of fifty conductions spanning 1985–1995, issued on Lawrence D. Morris’s own CounterCurrents imprint distributed by New World. The fifty-item conduction was conducted in Japan with musicians from the Japanese new music world.

Open Questions

  • No Discogs connection found between Morris and Kip Hanrahan / American Clavé despite the biographical brief mentioning Hanrahan. No co-credited releases in DB. Possible the connection was on stage or in print only, or credited under a different name variation.
  • Arthur Blythe connection cited in brief; Discogs shows Morris and Blythe in the same Black Saint / Soul Note orbit but no directly shared release found in this trawl. May require closer check of Blythe’s discography.
  • In Touch… But Out Of Reach (Kharma, 1982) — a US release credited as a Morris leader date; 33 pressings listed in Discogs but release data is sparse (no label full info pulled). May be a private press or very limited issue.
  • The conduction system is described in detail on Morris’s website (conduction.us) and in his liner notes; web research would add significantly to the biography of the methodology itself.
  • Discogs has no credits linking Morris to hat ART despite that label being cited in the brief. Hat ART is closely associated with FMP-adjacent European free improvisation; Morris did appear on FMP, but no hat ART releases found.
