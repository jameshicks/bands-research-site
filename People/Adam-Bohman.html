Adam Bohman

Adam Bohman

Oct 13, 202612 min read

Adam Bohman

Discogs artist ID: 75355
Role: Improviser, instrument builder, composer, visual artist
Location: London, England
Years active (Discogs): 1984–2019+
Bandcamp: adambohman.bandcamp.com

British improviser and instrument builder. Active in London’s free improvisation scene from the mid-1980s onward. Known primarily for constructing and performing on homemade string instruments, prepared violin and balalaika, and for deploying amplified found objects — a practice with close affinities to Hugh Davies’s Shozyg instrument work. Bohman applies contact microphones to everyday objects to generate raw, textural sound; he also uses unamplified objects when context demands. His visual art practice intersects with his music: he produced collage artwork for his own and others’ releases, and contributed cover paintings.

Bohman is a long-standing member of Morphogenesis, the London-based large ensemble, and a founding member of The Bohman Brothers duo (with brother Jonathan Bohman). He is also a member of the London Improvisers Orchestra and numerous other collaborative projects including Conspiracy (12), Diastolic Murmurs, Ideal Morphogenesis, and Second Violin.

Biography

Early Activity and Cassette Networks (1984–1992)

Bohman’s first documented appearance is on the 1984 LMC cassette LMC 18/7/84 (London Musicians’ Collective) — credited with “Music By” — and a further 1984 tape 24/11/84, both on the self-released Cockpit Improvised Music imprint. From the mid-to-late 1980s he appeared extensively on US cassette network releases, particularly through Zan Hoffman’s ZH27 label — contributing “audio sources” (i.e., tape-traded material used as raw collage by Hoffman) to many ZH27 releases between 1986 and 2004. These include Infringe Upon Your Privacy (ZH27 190, 1988), E & R #4 (ZH27 385, 1990), and many others.

His early musical group activity includes Diastolic Murmurs, which self-released cassettes from 1987 onward (Electro-Medicine, ‘Live’ Electronic Dissections 1985–1986, Purgations Flushed 1X10). He appeared on the first Morphogenesis release, the 1987 Sound Of Pig tape (SOP 77), and on MG1 and MG2 (Generations Unlimited, 1988) with the same group.

In this period Bohman also appeared on releases from If, Bwana (Al Margolis’s Sound Of Pig label): A Graceful And Ancient Art (SOP 161, 1988), Whispering Wendy (SOP 180, 1988), and Picasso’s Chicken (SOP 293, 1990).

Morphogenesis and Conspiracy (1990–1994)

Morphogenesis released Stromatolites (Direction Music, 1991; cat: dmc 19) — Bohman credited with prepared violin, stringed instrument, and cover painting. The group also contributed to the Spanish Improvisaciones concert documentation (Modisti, 1998; later reprinted as Improvisations on CEDI/Hurly Burly, 1999).

The Conspiracy (12) project (with members including Nick Couldry, Paul Obermayer, Furt, Richard Crow, and John Greenough) documented an extended performance period. Their 1990 cassette The Beaufort Scale credits Bohman for prepared strings, found objects sounds, and uncredited artwork. The Institution of Rot events (London, 1993) produced multiple releases: the 1993 self-released audio document and The Institution Of Rot, June 1993 (Institution of Rot / Vintage Productions, 1994).

Paradigm Discs and Solo Emergence (1996–2003)

Paradigm Discs became Bohman’s primary UK home from the mid-1990s. His first Paradigm release was Charivari Music (PD 02, 1996), followed by Music And Words (PD 09, 1999) — his main solo statement, with production and artwork by Clive Graham and back-collage artwork by Bohman himself. Paradigm also released Morphogenesis’s In Streams (Volume 2) (PD 17, 2001) and the The Bohman Brothers album Bunhill Row (PD 19, 2006). Music And Words 2 (PD 30, 2014) collected further material, and the original Music And Words was reissued (2013).

Sounds Heard (FMR Records, FMRCD104-0402, 2002) was Hugh Davies’s anthology documenting his performance practice across decades. Bohman appears as joint composer on track 15 alongside Johannes Bergmark, Martin Klapper, and Mats Lindström; Clive Graham handled mastering and engineering, with Trevor Taylor (2) producing. This is the “Sounds Heard” connection flagged in the existing stub.

For Hugh Davies and Another Timbre (2008)

For Hugh Davies (Another Timbre, at11, 2008) is a memorial release for Hugh Davies, who died on 1 January 2005. Bohman is a primary-credited artist alongside Davies (archival recordings), Lee Patterson, and Mark Wastell. Bohman plays prepared balalaika and amplified objects; Patterson plays amplified objects; Wastell plays cello. The release was recorded and mastered by Simon Reynell (Another Timbre’s founder). Design by grayscaling@hotmail.com.

Davies and Bohman shared a fundamental aesthetic: using contact microphones on invented or found objects as instruments in their own right rather than as pick-ups for conventional instruments. Davies’s Shozyg series (amplified found-object assemblages) and Bohman’s own homemade string and object instruments represent parallel approaches to the same problem.

The Bohman Brothers and London Scene (2001–2016)

The Bohman Brothers — Adam and Jonathan Bohman — released A Twist For All Pockets (Rossbin, RS003, 2001) and Live At Wiltons Music Hall (777 Was 666, 777-019, 2011; with Blood Stereo). Jonathan plays objects and radio; Adam plays prepared violin, home-made strings, objects, wine glass, and voice.

Other significant releases from this period:

  • Emanem appearances: Proceedings (Emanem 4201, 2000) with the London improvised music community; Freedom Of The City 2001 - Large Groups (Emanem 4206, 2002); Freedom Of The City 2002 (Emanem 4090, 2003); Reality Fandango (Emanem 4135, 2007; with Roger Smith, Martin Davidson, and others).
  • Adhesives And Grout (Korm Plastics, KP 3041, 2011): with If, Bwana (Al Margolis), Jos Smolders, Jeremy Jansen, and Isabelle Vaverka.
  • Granite, Silk & Soldering — Solo And Duo Recordings 2010–2011 (Audition Records, ar040, 2011): with Adrian Northover and Aniana Heras.
  • Adam Bohman With Dylan Nyoukis & Friends (Scumbag Relations SR183 / Sleepy Cobalt Sounds SC003, 2012).
  • The Car Boot Sales (Kukuruku Recordings, 2013): duo with Tasos Stamou; Bohman on prepared violin, balalaika, amplified objects; Stamou on sampling keyboard, prepared zither, circuit-bent electronics.
  • Bagpipes And Blackberries (Confront, ccs 57, 2016): duo with Jean-Michel Van Schouwburg; Bohman on unamplified objects; Van Schouwburg on voice.
  • 10.9.15 (Otoroku, DS068, 2016): Morphogenesis live at Cafe Oto; Bohman credited with prepared violin and objects.
  • Bakers Of The Lost Future (Inexhaustible Editions, ie-004-2, 2016): international improvisation collective including Katalin Ladik, Peter Strickland, and others.
  • Four Perfect Balls (Migro Records, MIG009, 2016): with Richard Thomas (Discogs ID 433), Vincent Oliver, and Panos Ghikas.

Text Pieces and Later Work (2016–2019)

Bohman has also worked in text-based composition — Discogs credits include Text Pieces + Compositions for Prepared Strings and Objects with Grant Richardson (2) and Rory O’Brien (3). He appeared on the German Fluxus anthology Keine direkten Erlaeuterungen zu geschlossenen Organismen in der FLUXUS-Musik (---) (Psych.KG, 2017; with violin and objects), and on Hospital Of The Soul #1 (Psych.KG, 2019; electronics and noises).

‘Live’ Electronic Dissections #1 & #5 (Nostalgie De La Boue, ndlb#127, 2019) documents early Diastolic Murmurs performances.
