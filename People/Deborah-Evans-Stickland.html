Deborah Evans-Stickland

Discogs ID: 139443 Also credited as: Deborah Evans Stickland, Deborah Evans Strickland, Evans Origin: UK Role: Vocalist, songwriter

UK vocalist best known as the singer on The Flying Lizards’ self-titled debut LP (1979, Virgin) and the group’s UK #4 hit “Money (That’s What I Want).” After the Flying Lizards she retrained as a psychotherapist, formed her own group Deborah And The Puerto Ricans, and continued occasional session and guest vocal work into the 2010s.

The Flying Lizards (1979)

Deborah was the vocalist on The Flying Lizards (1979, Virgin VA 13137), the debut LP produced and engineered by David Cunningham. The group had a rotating, collective lineup rather than fixed membership; additional personnel on the record included David Toop, Steve Beresford, and others. “TV” (A3) is co-written by Deborah (credited as “Evans”) alongside Cunningham, Vivien Goldman, and General Strike. General Strike (Discogs ID 156118) is a distinct entity — a trio of Steve Beresford, David Toop, and David Cunningham, founded 1979, with releases on Canal (4), Touch, and Sub Rosa. Since all three were also Flying Lizards collaborators, the co-writing credits reflect the overlapping collective nature of both groups rather than a pseudonym. The simultaneous crediting of Goldman and General Strike on the same track is therefore two separate credits, not one person under two names.

“Money (That’s What I Want)” was released as a 7” single (Virgin VS 276, 1979) and reached #4 on the UK Singles Chart. Deborah’s flat, deadpan vocal delivery — half-sung, half-announced — over Cunningham’s sparse electronic arrangement became the record’s defining quality. A separate “TV” 7” (Virgin VA 67006) was released for the US market in the same year.

The Flying Lizards’ subsequent LPs used different vocalists: Patti Palladin on The Fourth Wall (1981) and Sally Peterson on Top Ten (1984). Deborah’s Discogs profile notes she “left the band’s ever-changing line-up” after the debut.

After The Flying Lizards

Per her Discogs profile, after leaving the Flying Lizards Deborah retrained as a psychotherapist and had a child. She formed Deborah And The Puerto Ricans (Discogs ID 2783881; no releases in Discogs). She was also working helping people with learning difficulties at the time the profile was written, and had “recently” appeared on an album by Richard X (no release traceable in Discogs).

The 2003 “Insanity” is a house/tech house 12” produced by Robbie Rivera (written by Rivera and Ned Bigham).

The Richard X album — the Discogs profile cryptically mentions she “recently” appeared on “an album by Richard X”; this is Presents His X-Factor Vol. 1 (2003, Astralwerks ASW 92883). She is credited as “Deborah Evans Strickland” (note the alternate spelling) on two tracks, both billed as “Richard X In Collaboration With Deborah Evans-Stickland”:

  • Track 8: “Walk On By” — Bacharach/David cover; credited “Vocals, Other [Extended Courtesy Credit By]”; produced by Richard X (who plays piano), engineered/mixed by Pete Hofmann
  • Track 9: “Lemon / Lime” — original written by Richard X; Vocals; additional bass drum by Steve Brown (3)

The 2018 “Money” is a Cid & Bahary remix of the Flying Lizards original, produced by David Cunningham, with Deborah providing vocals — presumably a re-recorded or repurposed take.
