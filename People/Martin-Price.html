Martin Price

Discogs ID: 251562 Real name: Martin Price Origin: Manchester, England

Manchester producer, DJ, and music entrepreneur. Owner of the Eastern Bloc record shop in Manchester — a pivotal hub of the city’s rave and independent music scene — and founder of Creed Records, the label attached to the shop. Co-founded 808 State in 1988 with Graham Massey and Gerald Simpson (A Guy Called Gerald); left the group in late 1991 at the peak of their success.

Eastern Bloc and Creed Records

Price’s Eastern Bloc record shop (Manchester) was a focal point for the emerging acid house and techno scenes in the late 1980s. His Creed Records issued the first 808 State releases — Let Yourself Go / Deepville (1988), Newbuild (1988), and Quadrastate EP (1989) — before the group signed to ZTT. Eastern Bloc Records (the shop’s imprint) later released Switzerland’s 2 (1993).

808 State (1988–1991)

Price co-founded 808 State with Graham Massey (formerly of Biting Tongues) and Gerald Simpson. The trio self-produced and wrote all material on Newbuild (1988), with Price, Massey, and Simpson sharing written-by credits. Gerald Simpson departed in 1989 to pursue A Guy Called Gerald; the Spinmasters — Andy Barker and Darren Partington — joined as the replacement rhythm section.

Under Price’s tenure the group crossed into the UK mainstream: Pacific (1989) reached the top ten, and the ZTT run of albums and singles — Ninety (1989, 40 pressings), Olympic State (1990, 79 pressings — the group’s most-pressed release), and ex:el (1991, 51 pressings) — established them as one of the UK’s major electronic acts. ex:el featured collaborations with Björk and New Order vocalist Bernard Sumner.

Price left in October 1991 when the group scheduled a US tour; he chose to remain in Manchester to manage local talent (including Rochdale rappers Kaliphz) and develop his own projects. From Gorgeous (1992) onwards, 808 State continued without him.

Post-808 State: Switzerland

After leaving 808 State, Price formed Switzerland with Neale Johnson (585109) and Steve Caton (2) (1125144). The group released two records: Inflight (The Correct Use Of Dope) (1992, ZTT) and 2 (1993, Eastern Bloc Records). The ZTT connection suggests continuity from the 808 State deal.
