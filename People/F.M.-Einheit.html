F.M. Einheit

Real name: Frank Martin Strauß Born: 18 December 1958, Dortmund, Germany Discogs ID: 48077 Name variations: FM Einheit, Die Einheit, F.M., F M Einheit, F. M. Einheit, F.M.Einheit, F.M.Enheit, FC Einheit, fm Aliases: Mufti (2)

German percussionist, electronics musician, and composer. Born Frank Martin Strauß, he adopted the stage name “Einheit” (German: “unit” or “unity”). One of the defining figures in the European industrial and experimental music underground from the early 1980s through the 1990s. Best known as a core member of Einstürzende Neubauten from 1981 until his departure in the mid-1990s, and as a founding member of Hamburg new wave/NDW act Palais Schaumburg (1979–1981). After leaving Neubauten he became a prolific solo and collaborative artist, working primarily in radio-art, theatrical soundtrack, and noise-experimental contexts with long-term collaborators including Andreas Ammer, Caspar Brötzmann, and Gry.

Early Career: Abwärts and Palais Schaumburg (1979–1981)

Einheit’s earliest documented Discogs credit is with Hamburg punk/experimental group Abwärts (Discogs ID: 58780), on their debut LP Amok Koma (Zickzack, 1980). On that record he is credited with synthesizer, effects, and vocals alongside other players including Marc Chung and Axel Dill. The Abwärts connection was significant: Marc Chung later joined Einstürzende Neubauten, and Einheit’s own path overlapped with theirs throughout the Hamburg underground.

Simultaneously, Einheit was a founding drummer for Palais Schaumburg (1979–1981), the Hamburg neue deutsche Welle group formed around Holger Hiller and Thomas Fehlmann. He played on their early Zickzack singles. He departed in 1981, before the group’s first LP, and was replaced by Ralf Hertwig. A later Abwärts release, Der Westen Ist Einsam (Mercury, 1982), credits him under “Keyboards, Percussion; Performer [Abwärts],” indicating his continued connection to that group even after joining Neubauten.

Einstürzende Neubauten (1981–1996)

Einheit joined Einstürzende Neubauten (Discogs ID: 38794) in 1981 and appears as performer on their debut LP Kollaps (Zickzack, 1981), alongside Blixa Bargeld and N. U. Unruh. He remained a core member through the band’s most celebrated period, performing on every major LP from Kollaps through Ende Neu (1996).

On ½ Mensch (1985) the lineup was Bargeld, Einheit, Alexander Hacke, Marc Chung, and N. U. Unruh, co-produced with Gareth Jones; Thomas Stern and Raymond Watts (Nainz Watts) engineered. By Fünf Auf Der Nach Oben Offenen Richterskala (1987), also produced with Jones, Einheit shared writing credits with the full band. On Ende Neu (1996) Einheit contributed instruments and noises to tracks 1, 3, 4, and 6, and co-wrote those tracks; he also appears as a writer across the Ende Neu Remixes EP (1997), described as “[All Original Versions].”

He left Neubauten in the mid-1990s after Ende Neu. Neubauten continued without him; Einheit was not on any subsequent studio records.

Solo and Collaborative Career (1990–present)

Einheit launched his solo discography alongside his Neubauten tenure, with the first primary-credited release in 1990. His solo and duo work is principally on the German experimental label Our Choice (and its associated Reihe EGO sublabel) and later his own FM 4.5.1 imprint.

Radio-Art and Theatrical Work

A distinctive strand of Einheit’s solo output is his collaboration with radio dramatist and author Andreas Ammer. Their joint work fuses found text (Dante’s Divine Comedy, Aeschylus, Homer) with electronic percussion and noise, often created as Bayerischer Rundfunk radio plays before release as albums. Key releases in this strand:

  • Radio Inferno (1993) — Dante’s Inferno as radio drama; primary voices include Blixa Bargeld (Dante), Phil Minton (Vergil), Caspar Brötzmann, Yvonne Ducksworth, and John Peel; text adapted by Herbert Kapfer; Einheit provides music and production.
  • Apocalypse Live (1995) — live document of the Ammer/Einheit theatrical radio project.
  • Odysseus 7 (1998) — theatrical soundtrack for a Staatsschauspiel Munich production; Das Space Orchestra; theremin by Lydia Kavina.
  • Hammerschlag (2020) — Ammer/Einheit collaboration with MusicAeterna Chorus, conductor Teodor Currentzis, featuring the texts of Soviet poet Aleksej Gastev.
