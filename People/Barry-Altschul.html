Barry Altschul

American jazz drummer and percussionist. Born January 6, 1943, in The Bronx, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 260591.

One of the defining drummers of avant-garde jazz from the mid-1960s through the 1980s, Altschul operated across the full spectrum of free improvisation and post-bop jazz — from intimate piano trios to large-scale orchestral works. He is best known for his work with Circle (5) (1970–71), as a member of the Anthony Braxton Quartet across the mid-1970s, and through a sustained trio partnership with Dave Holland and Sam Rivers that extended from 1973 into the 2010s.

Biography

Early Career and Paul Bley (1960s)

Altschul came up in the New York loft scene of the 1960s, moving in free jazz circles from an early age. His first significant recording association was with pianist Paul Bley. He appears on the Paul Bley Trio’s Closer (ESP-Disk’, 1966) alongside bassist Steve Swallow — one of the foundational free jazz piano trio documents of the era.

Through the late 1960s Altschul also recorded with the Paul Bley Trio for Radio Canada International (Paul Bley Trio, 1972, recorded live), and appeared on sessions that circulated in Europe, including on the French BYG label. The Bley connection continued well into the 1970s: Paul Bley & Scorpio (Milestone, 1973), featuring Altschul on percussion alongside bassist Dave Holland, was a key release; followed by Virtuosi (Improvising Artists Inc., 1976) and Japan Suite (Improvising Artists Inc., 1977), both trio recordings with Bley and bassist Gary Peacock, on which all compositions were credited to Annette Peacock.

Circle and ECM (1970–73)

In 1970, Altschul joined Circle (5) alongside two alumni of Miles Davis’s electric band — pianist Chick Corea and bassist Dave Holland — plus reedist Anthony Braxton. The group committed to radical collective free improvisation. Circle released two live CBS/Sony albums for the Japanese market in 1971 (Circle-1 Live In German Concert, Circle 2: Gathering), as well as the ECM trio record A.R.C. (1971, ECM), a Corea/Holland/Altschul date produced by Manfred Eicher. The group’s main document is the double LP Paris - Concert (ECM, 1972), recorded live and featuring all four members. Circle dissolved when Corea departed in 1971–72 to form Return to Forever.

Altschul’s most celebrated sideman credit of this period is Dave Holland’s Conference of the Birds (ECM, 1973), credited to the David Holland Quartet. The session paired Holland with Sam Rivers and Anthony Braxton on reeds and flute, with Altschul contributing percussion and marimba. Produced by Manfred Eicher, the record became a cornerstone of the ECM catalog and of 1970s avant-garde jazz. Altschul is also listed as a primary credited artist on that release.

Anthony Braxton Quartet (1974–77)

Altschul became a central member of the Anthony Braxton Quartet for much of the mid-1970s, appearing on several of Braxton’s key Arista albums. This included Five Pieces 1975 (Arista, 1975) alongside Dave Holland and Kenny Wheeler, and Creative Orchestra Music 1976 (Arista, 1976), a large-ensemble work where Altschul contributed bells, chimes, gong, percussion, and snare drum alongside players including Roscoe Mitchell, George Lewis, Muhal Richard Abrams, Wadada Leo Smith, and others. Earlier live documentation of the Braxton Quartet with Altschul appeared on Dortmund (Quartet) 1976 (hat ART, 1991) and the Braxton Bootleg series of quartet concert recordings (Quartet (Avignon) 1974, Quartet (Bremen) 1975, Quartet (Graz) 1976). The Anthony Braxton Quartet group is listed in Altschul’s Discogs memberships (group ID: 361203).

He also appeared on Braxton’s The Montreux / Berlin Concerts (Arista, 1977).

Solo Albums as Leader (1977–84)

Altschul led his own sessions on the Muse label in the late 1970s. You Can’t Name Your Own Tune (Muse, 1977) was co-produced with Michael Cuscuna. Another Time / Another Place (Muse, 1978) was more ambitious — Altschul composed half the material and produced the date, bringing in Arthur Blythe on alto saxophone, Anthony Davis (2) on piano (who also arranged and co-composed), Ray Anderson on trombone, and Dave Holland on bass for some tracks. Give And Take (Black Saint, 1983) was credited to the John Lindberg Trio (2) with George Lewis and Barry Altschul — Altschul playing drums, berimbau, marimba, and tympani. No Idea Of Time (Red Record, 1984) was a quartet co-credit with Italian pianist Franco D’Andrea, Mark Helias on bass, and Tino Tracanna on tenor and soprano saxophone.

Broader Sideman Work (1975–84)

Beyond the core Braxton and Bley partnerships, Altschul appeared across a wide range of 1970s avant-garde sessions:

  • Spiral (Freedom, 1975) — Andrew Hill, alongside Lee Konitz and others
  • ‘Coon Bid’ness (Arista/Freedom, 1975) — Julius Hemphill, with Arthur Blythe and Hamiet Bluiett
  • Flexible Flyer (Arista/Freedom, 1975) — Roswell Rudd, featuring Sheila Jordan
  • Drum Ode (ECM, 1975) — Paul Bley (percussion)
  • Circling In (Blue Note, 1975) — Chick Corea archival recordings from the Circle period
  • The Quest (Red Record, 1976) — with European improvisers
  • Circulus (Blue Note, 1978) — Chick Corea
  • The Live Trio Sessions (ABC Impulse!, 1978)
  • The Song Of Singing (Blue Note, 1971) — Bobby Hutcherson, with Dave Holland
  • Kinshasa, Washington D.C. Paris (Celluloid, 1983) — with African percussionists

Sam Rivers Trio and Later Work (1970s–2010s)

Altschul’s partnership with Sam Rivers and Dave Holland is one of the longest-running in his career. It dates to Conference of the Birds (1973) and extended to recordings under the Sam Rivers Trio name and, eventually, reunion sessions. Sam Rivers Con Schiano Altschul E Holland (Gong, 1977) documented an Italian concert. The trio reconvened for Reunion: Live In New York (Pi Recordings, 2012), a fully improvised performance on which all three were primary credited artists, recorded shortly before Rivers’s death. A posthumous archival release, Ricochet (NoBusiness Records, 2020), credited to the Sam Rivers Trio featuring Holland and Altschul, extended the documentation of this partnership.

3Dom Factor and Recent Activity (2010s–2020)

Altschul’s most recent extended ensemble project is Barry Altschul’s 3Dom Factor, a trio with alto/soprano saxophonist Jon Irabagon and bassist Joe Fonda (Discogs group ID: 4904536). The group recorded It Takes All Kinds (Jazzwerkstatt/Irabbagast, 2013) in a trio format with Mark Helias substituting on bass, and Reunion: Live In New York as a Rivers reunion side-project. The trio also appeared documented under related groupings.
