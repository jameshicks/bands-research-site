Mike Thorne

British record producer, engineer, musician, and label owner. Born 25 January 1948 in Sunderland, England. Discogs artist ID: 53664.

Overview

Mike Thorne began his career as an A&R man and in-house producer at EMI/Harvest in the mid-1970s, where he signed and produced Wire’s first three albums — Pink Flag (1977), Chairs Missing (1978), and 154 (1979) — establishing himself as one of the key figures in British post-punk production. He also recorded early Buzzcocks live tracks before moving to New York around 1980, where he built a career producing mainstream new wave and pop acts. His most commercially successful period came in the early 1980s producing Soft Cell (Non-Stop Erotic Cabaret, The Art Of Falling Apart), Bronski Beat (The Age Of Consent), and The Communards. He founded two studios — Rimshot Studio in Kent (label ID: 547837) and The Stereo Society in New York (label ID: 298434) — and his own label, The Stereo Society (label ID: 48484), on which he released recordings as “Thorne.”

Early Career: EMI and the Roxy

Thorne joined EMI in the mid-1970s, working out of the Harvest imprint. One of his earliest production credits is on Wire’s debut single “Mannequin” (Harvest, 1977). That same year he produced The Roxy London WC2 (Jan–Apr 77) — a landmark early punk live compilation on Harvest capturing acts at the Roxy Club. He also produced Buzzcocks’ live recordings of “Breakdown” and “Love Battery” (featuring Garth Smith on bass, and Howard Devoto/Pete Shelley songwriting credits), which surfaced on the German compilation Total Pop 1977-80: Rare, Live And Great (Weird System, 1987). His other early Harvest work included Téléphone (French rock group, Columbia/Harvest, 1977–78) and The Shirts (Harvest, 1978).

The Wire Trilogy (1977–1979)

Thorne’s most celebrated production work is Wire’s first three albums, often cited as a defining sequence of post-punk minimalism:

New York: Post-Punk and Synth-Pop (1980–1984)

Relocating to New York, Thorne worked across a widening range of new wave and synth-pop acts:

  • Urban Verbs (Warner Bros., 1980) — DC post-punk group, self-titled debut
  • Soft Cell — Produced Non-Stop Erotic Cabaret (Some Bizzare, 1981), including “Tainted Love” and “Bedsitter”; continued with Non-Stop Ecstatic Dancing (Sire, 1982), The Art Of Falling Apart (Some Bizzare, 1983), and associated singles including “Say Hello, Wave Goodbye,” “Torch,” “Numbers/Barriers”
  • Nina Hagen — Nunsexmonkrock (Columbia, 1982); band included Chris Spedding (guitar), Paul Shaffer (synthesizer), Paul Roessler (piano)
  • Bronski Beat — The Age Of Consent (MCA/Forbidden Fruit, 1984), including “Smalltown Boy”; Jimmy Somerville (vocals), Larry Steinbachek, Steve Bronski; choir by The Pink Singers
  • John Cale — Honi Soit (A&M, 1981); Thorne credited as Producer and Engineer (Computer Processing); Andy Warhol concept cover

During this period Thorne also produced the Some Bizzare compilations material that helped define the early UK synth scene, and worked consistently with engineer Harvey Goldberg and mastering engineer Jack Skinner (Sterling Sound, NYC).

The Communards and Jimmy Somerville (1986–1996)

After the success of Bronski Beat, Thorne followed Jimmy Somerville into The Communards:

  • The Communards — Communards (London Records, 1986), including “Don’t Leave Me This Way” (Gotham City Mix); Richard Coles (piano/organ/synthesizer), Sarah Jane Morris (additional vocals)
  • Jimmy Somerville — solo work through London Records into the 1990s, including compilations The Singles Collection 1984/1990 and The Very Best Of Jimmy Somerville, Bronski Beat And The Communards

’Til Tuesday, Laurie Anderson, and US Work (1985–1992)

Thorne produced ‘Til Tuesday’s debut Voices Carry (Epic, 1985) — featuring Aimee Mann — and continued working with them. He also co-produced tracks on Laurie Anderson’s Strange Angels (Warner Bros., 1989) and had a co-producer credit on two tracks in the Talk Normal: The Laurie Anderson Anthology (Rhino, 2000).

Other notable US credits in this period:

  • China Crisis — Diary Of A Hollow Horse (Virgin, 1989), co-produced with Walter Becker
  • Holly And The Italians — Holly And The Italians (Virgin, 1982); Thorne plays keyboards
  • Human League — produced and remixed work on Harvest/Virgin releases

As a Musician

Thorne performed as a member of two acts:

  • Plaza (4) (Discogs ID: 640630) — with Karl Jenkins and Mike Ratledge (both of Soft Machine); released on the French Sidewalk label, 1979: “(Got My) Dancing Shoes,” “Never Gonna Change My Mind,” “Plaza.” Thorne wrote and produced these sides.
  • Thorne (Discogs ID: 535764) — his own recording project, released on his label The Stereo Society: Sprawl (1999) and Dancing With B (2006).

He appears uncredited as keyboards/synthesizer on Colin Newman’s A-Z (1980).
