Jóhann Jóhannsson

Jóhann Jóhannsson

Aug 16, 20268 min read

Jóhann Jóhannsson

Discogs ID: 52220
Real name: Jóhann Gunnar Jóhannsson
Born: 19 September 1969, Reykjavík, Iceland
Died: 9 February 2018, Berlin, Germany (aged 48)
Labels: Touch, 4AD, Deutsche Grammophon, NTOV, 130701, Kitchen Motors
Groups: Apparat Organ Quartet, Ham, Dip, Lhooq, Ekta, Hljóðmúrinn, The Dirac Quartet, Daisy Hill Puppy Farm, Evil Madness

Icelandic composer, producer, and multi-instrumentalist. One of the central figures of Reykjavík’s experimental/avant-garde scene in the 1990s, Jóhannsson moved outward across two decades into contemporary classical composition, film scoring, and eventually mainstream prestige releases on Deutsche Grammophon. His solo catalog is defined by austere orchestral writing layered over electronics, often grounded in specific historical and conceptual subjects (a room-sized 1960s computer, an abandoned Ford utopia in Brazil, Durham coalfield brass bands). He was Denis Villeneuve’s primary film composer during the peak of Villeneuve’s Hollywood career.

Died unexpectedly in Berlin on 9 February 2018, cause attributed to an accidental drug interaction. Several major works were released posthumously.

Early Career and the Reykjavík Scene (1988–2001)

Jóhannsson came up through the Icelandic rock band Ham (active 1988–1994, reunited 2001), one of the most prominent bands of the early-1990s Reykjavík underground. He also contributed to a number of other Icelandic groups across the 1990s: Dip, Lhooq, Ekta, Hljóðmúrinn.

Lhooq — his primary pre-solo project — released a debut on Echo in 1998. Around the same time Jóhannsson contributed keyboard programming to Barry Adamson’s Stranger Things (2001, XIII BIS Records) and appeared on recordings by Marc Almond.

Kitchen Motors: Jóhannsson co-founded the Reykjavík experimental label and collective Kitchen Motors (Discogs ID 3934), which released the Motorlab series of compilations documenting the Icelandic avant-garde scene. Motorlab #3 (2001) credits Jóhannsson as mixer, producer, and liner note writer.

Apparat Organ Quartet: Founding member of Apparat Organ Quartet (est. 1999 in Reykjavík), an all-keyboards ensemble using vintage analogue machinery — Russian synthesizers, Italian transistor organs, Hammonds, vocoders, circuit-bent Casios. Self-described “Machine Rock and Roll.” Jóhannsson left the band in 2012 to focus on solo work. Other members included Hörður Bragason, Úlfur Eldjárn, and later Arnar Geir Ómarsson.

Touch Period (2002–2005)

Englabörn (2002, Touch TO:52)

Debut solo album. Jóhannsson played piano, glockenspiel, harmonium, organ, and electronics. The Eþos String Quartet (Auður Hafsteinsdóttir, Bryndís Halla Gylfadóttir, Greta Guðnadóttir, Guðmundur Kristmundsson) provided strings. Mixed by Aron Þór Árnason and Viðar Hákon Gislason. Photography by Jon Wozencroft (Touch’s in-house photographer).

Virðulegu Forsetar (2004, Touch TO:64)

65-minute work in four parts for brass, organ, piano, and electronics; released as a CD+DVD-Audio set. The Caput Ensemble performed; brass players (horns: Anna Sigurbjörnsdóttir, Einar St. Jónsson, Emil Friðfinnsson, Stefán Jón Bernharðsson, Þorkell Jóelsson; trumpets: Eiríkur Örn Pálsson, Ásgeir H. Steingrímsson; tuba: Sigurður Már Valsson) and organists (Hörður Bragason, Guðmundur Sigurðsson). Conducted by Guðni Franzson. Bass and electronics by Skúli Sverrisson; glockenspiel and bells by Matthías M.D. Hemstock. Recorded and mastered by Sveinn Kjartansson.

4AD Period (2006–2008)

Jóhannsson signed to 4AD for his two major studio albums of this era, both expanding his orchestral palette via the City of Prague Philharmonic Orchestra (a key ongoing partner).

IBM 1401, A User’s Manual (2006, 4AD CAD 2609 CD)

Conceptual album inspired by Jóhannsson’s father, who worked as a technician for the IBM 1401 computer system in Iceland in the 1960s. Actual recordings of the IBM 1401 operating (made by Elías Davídsson, Jóhann Gunnarsson, and Örn Kaldalóns) are incorporated into the music; a musical fragment from the Icelandic hymn “Ísland Ögrум Skorið” played by the computer is credited to its original composer Sigvaldi Kaldalóns. Track 5, “The Sun’s Gone Dim And The Sky’s Turned Black,” quotes Dorothy Parker. Track 4, “IBM 729 II Magnetic Tape Unit,” features voice by Erna Ómarsdóttir.

Fordlandia (2008, 4AD CAD 2812)

Named after Henry Ford’s failed 1920s rubber plantation in the Amazon basin. The title track alone runs nearly 14 minutes; the album ends with a 15-minute closer, “How We Left Fordlandia.” The full personnel credits on this promo copy are sparse in Discogs, suggesting a similar orchestral lineup to the preceding album.

NTOV and 130701 Period (2009–2012)

And In The Endless Pause There Came The Sound Of Bees (2009, NTOV)

Film score for Varmints (2008), directed by Marc Craste, produced by Studio AKA. A 24-minute animated short based on the picture book by Helen Ward (illustrated by Craste); BAFTA-nominated for Best Animated Short and Oscar-shortlisted. City of Prague Philharmonic Orchestra; conducted by Miriam Němcová (who also served as Chorus Master); orchestration co-credited to Nicklas Schmidt; soprano soloist Michaela Šrůmová. Recorded by Jan Holzner. Won Best Original Score at the Rhode Island International Film Festival. (IMDb; official site as of 2026-06-28)

The Miners’ Hymns (2011, 130701 LP13-13)

Score composed for Bill Morrison’s documentary film about the Durham coalfield mining communities. Released as a double LP. Jóhannsson composed, arranged, produced, mixed, and performed electronics. Conducted by Guðni Franzson. Featured a large colliery brass ensemble: cornets (Niall Thompson, Tony Thompson), French horns (Alan Tokeley, Callum Mackay, David Tollington, Graham Tedd), trombones (Alex Trotter, Brian Gibson, John Bell, Steve Baxter), trumpets (Alex Maynard, Ellie Lovegrove, Russell Jackson, Thomas Glendinning), tuba (Eric Leckenby, Jeff Winter, Owen Wallage). Organ: Robert Houssart. Live sound engineer: Audun Strype. Recording engineer: Sveinn Kjartansson.

Copenhagen Dreams (2012, NTOV)

Score for a Danish film. Piano, organ, celesta, electronics by Jóhannsson.

Free The Mind (2012, NTOV)

Documentary score. Directed by Phie Ambo (Danish director; film follows neuroscientist Richard Davidson’s study of meditation/mindfulness for PTSD veterans and children with ADHD). source

Film Scoring Career (2013–2018)

Jóhannsson became one of the most in-demand film composers of the 2010s, with a string of acclaimed scores for prestige films.

Prisoners (2013, WaterTower Music)

Score for Denis Villeneuve’s psychological thriller. Jóhannsson credited on electronics, percussion, organ, guitar. Key session collaborator: Hildur Guðnadóttir (cello). Mastering: Mandy Parnell.

The Theory of Everything (2014, Back Lot Music)

Score for James Marsh’s Stephen Hawking biopic. BAFTA-nominated; Golden Globe-nominated. Jóhannsson played keyboards and electronics; orchestrations by Anthony Weeden.

Sicario (2015, Varèse Sarabande / Cutting Edge)

Score for Denis Villeneuve’s border thriller. Prominent use of low brass and bass frequencies. Jóhannsson: electronics, synthesizer, piano, keyboards; again worked with Hildur Guðnadóttir and a large ensemble including Skúli Sverrisson (bass).

Arrival (2016, Deutsche Grammophon 479 6782 GH)

Score for Denis Villeneuve’s sci-fi first-contact film. Jóhannsson’s most complex score in terms of ensemble: City of Prague Philharmonic Orchestra (conductor Anthony Weeden); Theatre Of Voices choir (conductor Paul Hillier); Hildur Guðnadóttir (cello, Halldorophone, voice); Colin Stetson (saxophone, lyricon); Adam Wiltzie (guitar); Robert Lowe (2) (vocals, modular synthesizer); Joan La Barbara (co-wrote track 2). Sound design by Simon Ashdown and Wil Slater; modular synth programming by Phillip Barth; additional programming by Rutger Hoedemaekers.

Note: Jóhannsson’s score was substantially replaced during post-production by director Denis Villeneuve, who brought in additional music — including material that later formed the basis for Hildur Guðnadóttir’s work. The released album is Jóhannsson’s score as originally composed.

Mandy (2018, Lakeshore Records) — posthumous

Score for Panos Cosmatos’s horror film. Collaborators: Randall Dunn (producer), Stephen O’Malley (guitar), Pepijn Caudron / Kreng, Úlfur Eldjárn, Kjartan Holm, Matt Chamberlain (drums), Yair Elazar Glotman.

Late Solo Work (2016–2020)

Orphée (2016, Deutsche Grammophon 479 6021)

Non-film album, conceived as an abstract meditation on the Orpheus myth. Theatre Of Voices (cond. Paul Hillier); AIR Lyndhurst String Orchestra (cond. Anthony Weeden); Hildur Guðnadóttir and Clarice Jensen on cello; Caleb Burhans (violin); Yuki Numata Resnick (violin); Ben Russell (4) (viola); ACME ensemble. Recorded by Francesco Donadello and Geoff Foster (among others); mixed by Donadello; mastered by Calyx Mastering. Jóhannsson wrote the liner notes himself.

12 Conversations With Thilo Heinzmann (2019, Deutsche Grammophon) — posthumous

Score for / in dialogue with the work of German painter Thilo Heinzmann. Performed by Echo Collective.

Last And First Men (2020, Deutsche Grammophon) — posthumous

Score for Sturla Brandth Grøvlen’s film adaptation of Olaf Stapledon’s 1930 science-fiction novel. Narrated by Tilda Swinton. Instrumentation includes ondes Martenot, tape loops, and electronics alongside orchestra. Collaborators: Yair Elazar Glotman, Wouter Rentema, Samuli Kosminen, Viktor Orri Árnason, Sam Slater.

Drone Mass (2022, Deutsche Grammophon) — posthumous

Performed by Theatre Of Voices, ACME (American Contemporary Music Ensemble), Clarice Jensen, and Simon Goff.
