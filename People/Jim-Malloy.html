Jim Malloy

American recording engineer. Born Dixon, Illinois, 1931; died July 5, 2018, age 87. Discogs artist ID: 322818.

Nashville-based engineer on the Nancy & Lee LP (1968, Reprise RS 6273) — the 2022 LITA reissue credits him specifically as “Engineer [Nashville],” distinguishing his role from Eddie Brackett’s Hollywood sessions. 89 shared releases with Nancy Sinatra & Lee Hazlewood on Discogs.

RCA Studio B Affiliation (confirmed)

Malloy was a staff engineer at RCA Nashville from January 1965 through November 1968, working at both RCA Studio B and RCA Studio A. He was recruited by producer Chet Atkins after guitarist Duane Eddy — whose records Malloy had engineered in Los Angeles — recommended him as “the best engineer in town.” Before Nashville, Malloy had worked in Los Angeles since 1954 (Radio Recorders and elsewhere), winning a Grammy in 1964 for engineering Henry Mancini’s Charade soundtrack.

During his RCA tenure he engineered sessions for Elvis Presley (including “How Great Thou Art”), Dolly Parton, Waylon Jennings, Charley Pride, Eddy Arnold, Hank Snow, Connie Smith, John Hartford, and Chet Atkins himself. Presley reportedly called him “the best engineer anywhere.” After leaving RCA in late 1968, he moved to Monument Records.

Nancy Sinatra / Lee Hazlewood Sessions — Web Research

Web research confirms that Malloy’s two known credits (Country, My Way and the Nashville portions of Nancy & Lee) appear to be the full extent of his work on Hazlewood-produced Nancy Sinatra recordings. The other Hazlewood-produced Nancy Sinatra albums in the 1965–1968 window were all Hollywood sessions engineered by Eddie Brackett:

  • Boots (1966, Reprise RS-6202) — engineer: Eddie Brackett; recorded Hollywood (Boots (album) - Wikipedia)
  • How Does That Grab You? (1966, Reprise) — engineer: Eddie Brackett; recorded Hollywood (Discogs credits)
  • Sugar (1967, Reprise RS 6239) — engineers: Eddie Brackett, Ben Jordan (track 11), Winston Wong; recorded Hollywood (sessiondays.com)
  • Movin’ with Nancy (1967, Reprise) — engineer: Eddie Brackett; recorded Hollywood (Wikipedia)
  • Nancy & Lee Again (1972, RCA) — recorded at Poppi Recording Studios, Hollywood; no Malloy credit (Discogs)

Key singles from this period — “Some Velvet Morning” (1967) — were recorded at Capitol Studios in Los Angeles, not Nashville. No Malloy credit found.

Nancy & Lee (1968) spanned three studios: RCA Nashville (Malloy), United Recorders “B” in Hollywood (Brackett), and Pye Studios in London. The split-engineer arrangement reflects the album’s geographically distributed sessions.

Conclusion: Malloy’s Nashville role on Hazlewood/Sinatra material appears limited to the two Reprise titles already in the vault. The dividing line is simple: Hollywood sessions = Brackett; Nashville sessions = Malloy. No evidence found of additional Nashville Hazlewood/Sinatra sessions during Malloy’s RCA tenure (1965–1968).
