Bela B.

Bela B.

Oct 20, 20269 min read

Bela B.

Real name: Dirk Felsenheimer Discogs ID: 201185 (also credited as Dirk Felsenheimer, ID: 655101) Also known as: Alberto Rocca, Bert Brandes, Charlie Pulkig, Dirk Geröllheimer, Julia Laterne, Paul Kersey (2) Born: 14 December 1962, Berlin, Germany Active: 1982 – present Origin: Berlin, Germany Roles: drummer, vocalist, guitarist, songwriter, producer, actor, author

Bela B. (born Dirk Felsenheimer) is the drummer, co-vocalist, and co-songwriter of Die Ärzte, one of Germany’s most commercially successful and culturally durable punk/rock bands. He co-founded Die Ärzte in Berlin in 1982, broke up with the band in 1988, and reunited in 1993 for a second continuous run that continues to the present. Outside Die Ärzte he has released several solo albums under his own name, produced other artists, acted in film and theatre, and collaborated with figures ranging from German cabaret writer Wiglaf Droste to country legend Lee Hazlewood.

Biography

Early bands (1979–1982)

Felsenheimer moved through several Berlin punk outfits in the late 1970s and early 1980s: Empire, Wild In The Streets, Kawumm, and Soilent Grün (1979–1982). These groups gave him his live grounding before he and Jan Vetter (later Farin Urlaub) founded Die Ärzte in 1982.

Die Ärzte — first run (1982–1988)

Die Ärzte formed in Berlin in 1982 with Felsenheimer (drums/vocals, stage name Bela B.) and Jan Vetter (guitar/vocals, stage name Farin Urlaub), initially with Hans Runge (stage name Sahnie) on bass. They signed to CBS and issued their debut LP Debil in 1984. The band became fixtures of the emerging German Neue Deutsche Welle / punk scene, releasing six studio albums between 1984 and 1988 (Debil, Im Schatten Der Ärzte, Die Ärzte, Ab 18, Ist Das Alles?, Das Ist Nicht Die Ganze Wahrheit…). Sahnie was fired in 1986 and replaced by Hagen Liebing (stage name The Incredible Hagen). The band split in 1988 after their farewell live album Die Beste Band Der Welt (…Und Zwar Live!).

Post-split side projects (1989–1992)

During the hiatus Felsenheimer stayed prolific. He released the satirical punk single “Grönemeyer Kann Nicht Tanzen” (1989) co-credited with cabaret writer Wiglaf Droste on Weser Label — a jab at mainstream rock and one of his best-known side releases. He also co-produced and performed on “Pogo Dancing” (1989, Weser Label) as a primary artist. He was a performer on the S.U.M.P. album Get Wise, Get Ugly, Get Sump (Weser Label, 1989), played with Depp Jones (1990–1992), and appeared with Crashland (1992). The 1991 single “Wir Brauchen… Werner” (Polydor) was a primary credit under his own name.

Die Ärzte — reunion (1993–present)

Die Ärzte reunited in 1993 with Rodrigo González (stage name Rod, later Rod Gonzalez) replacing Liebing on bass. Their comeback album Die Bestie In Menschengestalt (Metronome, 1993) was a substantial commercial and critical success, with the anthemic “Schrei Nach Liebe” becoming one of their signature tracks. The band went on to release a string of major albums: Planet Punk (Metronome, 1995), Le Frisur (Metronome, 1996), 13 (Hot Action Records, 1998), Runter Mit Den Spendierhosen, Unsichtbarer! (Hot Action Records, 2000), Geräusch (Hot Action Records/Universal, 2003), Jazz Ist Anders (Hot Action Records, 2007), Auch (Hot Action Records, 2012), Hell (Hot Action Records, 2020), and Dunkel (Hot Action Records, 2021). The band operates largely on their own Hot Action Records imprint from the late 1990s onward.

Solo work (2001–present)

Bela B. began a parallel solo career in 2001 with the 6” split single Are You Ready For Some Darkness? (with Turbonegro and Denim Girl/Blümchen, Zhengaya Records). His first full solo album was Bingo (BPX1992, 2006), a pop-punk record co-produced with Olsen Involtini and Wayne Jackson (2), featuring guest vocals from Lee Hazlewood on track 12 (“Lee Hazlewood & Das Erste Lied Des Tages”) and Charlotte Roche on track 8. He followed with a series of EPs and singles on BPX1992 — the single “Gitarre Runter” (2007) and the EP cluster “1. … 2. … 3. …”, “Bingo”, “Sie Hat Was Vermisst”, “Tag Mit Schutzumschlag” (all 2006). His second album Code B (BPX1992, 2009) was issued on double vinyl. He also released Altes Arschloch Liebe (2009), Schwarz / Weiss (BPX1992, 2009, arranged and co-produced), and continued releasing on his own label B-Sploitation in the 2010s: Abserviert (2013), Bye (2013), Immer So Sein (2014), Bastard (2017).

The 2016 single “You Spin Me Round (Like A Record)” (Halb 7 Records) — a cover of Dead or Alive — charted. Der Graf Vs. Horrorpunks (Gan-Shin, 2007), a collaboration with Japanese horror-punk acts including Balzac, saw Felsenheimer credited as lyrics, music, and producer.

Lee Hazlewood collaboration

In 2006 Felsenheimer played drums on track 7 (“The First Song Of The Day”) of Lee Hazlewood’s final studio album Cake Or Death (BPX1992, 2006), with Claudia Stülpner on vocals, Olsen Involtini on guitar, Wayne Jackson (2) on guitar, Michael O’Ryan on surf guitar, and Davide Rossi on strings. He co-wrote the song’s words and music (credited as Dirk Felsenheimer). The track also appeared in a different version on his own Bingo album the same year. Hazlewood was dying at the time of recording; Cake Or Death was released months before his death in August 2006.

Acting, voice work, and written projects

Felsenheimer pursued an acting career alongside his music, with voice roles in audio dramas: Gabriel Burns 6 – Die Totenmaschine (2004, Universal Family Entertainment), Vampira series (2006, Lübbe Audio), and Captain Berlin Versus Dracula (2022, Klabautermann Records/WDR). He narrated or read Das Handbuch (Deutsche Grammophon Literatur, 2003) — an audio guide to making a #1 hit (a comedic collaboration involving Bill Drummond and Jimmy Cauty of The KLF). He read the audiobook Kill Your Friends (Deutsche Grammophon Literatur, 2009) by John Niven. He played Mephisto in the Goethe adaptation Faust Vs. Mephisto (Deutsche Grammophon Literatur, 2004).
