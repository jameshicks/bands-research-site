Overview

American jazz drummer and percussionist born in California who relocated to Amsterdam around 1980, where he became embedded in the Dutch free improvisation and avant-garde jazz scene. His recorded output runs from 1984 into the 2020s, with work spanning free improvisation, free jazz, and contemporary jazz, and credits that extend beyond percussion to hammered dulcimer and composition. He is a member of several ensembles — 1000, 1000+1, 2000, 3000, and 4Walls — and has worked most extensively with saxophonist Michael Moore, trombonist Wolter Wierbos, and bassist Wilbert De Joode across dozens of shared releases. Ramboy Recordings has been his primary label since the early 1990s, with earlier appearances on hat ART, Ear-Rational, and Claxon.

Early Career and Move to Amsterdam

Born November 12, 1954 in Eureka, California. Began on vibraphone and snare drum as a child. Before Amsterdam he performed in California with reedist Michael Moore (4), John Handy, and Terry Gibbs. At twenty-two he moved to New York City, where he worked as a painter, wallpaper hanger, and dishwasher before becoming a dance class accompanist; he received drum tuition from Joey Baron but was largely self-taught. (Jazzhelden portrait, accessed 2026-08-24)

What brought him to Amsterdam: Vatcher arrived in the Netherlands in the summer of 1979 — not 1981 as some bios state — when his American band Available Jelly came to Europe as the backing group for the Great Salt Lake Mime Troupe, a company of theater makers, dancers, and clowns performing at Amsterdam’s Festival of Fools. He found the Dutch musical climate appealing and saw better employment prospects there than in New York; the Available Jelly members subsequently settled in Amsterdam. (Jazzhelden portrait, accessed 2026-08-24; DOEK — Available Jelly, accessed 2026-08-24)

Available Jelly was founded by multi-instrumentalist Gregg Moore and saxophonist Stuart Curtis and also featured trumpeter Jimmy Sernesky and reedist Michael Moore (4); its style was described as “an infectious mix of jazz, folk, pop and theater.” (Jazzhelden portrait, accessed 2026-08-24)

He relocated back to New York in 2017 while continuing European collaborations. (Jazzhelden portrait, accessed 2026-08-24)

Date conflict: Discogs and most English-language bios give his arrival as “ca. 1980” or “1981”; Jazzhelden (jazzarchief.nl, Dutch jazz archive) gives the Festival of Fools visit as “summer of 1979.” DOEK’s Available Jelly page places the troupe’s Amsterdam visit in “the mid-1970s.” The 1979 date from Jazzhelden is the most specific and carries the most contextual detail; the 1981 figure may reflect when he formally established residency or when he began appearing on recordings. The DOEK “mid-1970s” figure appears to be an error.

Hammered Dulcimer

Vatcher’s instrument range extends beyond drums and percussion to include hammer dulcimer, marimba, zither, harmonica, and violin. The earliest confirmed recording credit for hammer dulcimer is the Available Jelly album In Full Flail (Ramboy 17, recorded June 1988 at Orkater Studio, Amsterdam; released 1989), where he is credited on percussion, hammer dulcimer, and marimba. (Ramboy Recordings — ramboy17, accessed 2026-08-24; Discogs — Available Jelly: In Full Flail)

No source consulted traces precisely when or from whom he learned the instrument, nor connects the dulcimer to a specific compositional project distinct from Available Jelly’s broad multi-instrument approach.
