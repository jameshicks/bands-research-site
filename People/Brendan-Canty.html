Brendan Canty

Discogs ID: 339856. Drummer, guitarist, vocalist, producer, and filmmaker. Born March 9, 1966, Teaneck, New Jersey. Based in Washington DC since the early 1980s.

Brother of James Canty (Nation of Ulysses, Make-Up).

Biography

Early DC Scene — Deadline, One Last Wish (early–mid 1980s)

Canty arrived on the DC hardcore scene in the early 1980s. He was a member of Deadline (5) and One Last Wish before the bands that made his name. The Flex Your Head (1982, Dischord Records) compilation — the definitive document of early DC hardcore — carries a drums credit for him, placing him inside the scene at its inception.

Rites Of Spring (1984–1986)

With Guy Picciotto (vocals, guitar), Eddie Janney (guitar), and Mike Fellows (bass), Canty was the drummer for Rites Of Spring — widely credited as the originating band of “emo.” Their self-titled LP (1985, Dischord 16), produced by Ian MacKaye, is one of the defining documents of the DC post-hardcore scene. The band dissolved in 1986, but Canty and Picciotto would reunite in Fugazi within two years.

Interim: He also played in Happy Go Licky (1987–1988) alongside Picciotto, a transitional outfit that bridged Rites Of Spring and Fugazi.

Fugazi (1987–2003)

Canty was a founding member and the drummer for Fugazi — with Ian MacKaye (guitar/vocals), Guy Picciotto (guitar/vocals), and Joe Lally (bass). Fugazi’s first show was September 3, 1987 at the Wilson Center, Washington DC.

Fugazi went on indefinite hiatus in 2002–2003. The Fugazi Live Series (from 2004 onward) released archival concert recordings including the 9-3-87 first show and dozens of others; Canty is credited on all of them.

Canty also served on several Fugazi releases as recording engineer and contributed to production.

Instrument (1999) — Film Work

Canty co-directed Instrument (1999) with Jem Cohen, a feature-length documentary on Fugazi spanning 1987–1999. Cohen is credited for photography/cover stills on the Instrument Soundtrack LP (Dischord, 1999); the film itself sits outside the DB’s release catalog. This collaboration established Canty as a filmmaker as well as a musician.

Post-Fugazi — Engineering, Production, Session Work (2001–2012)

After Fugazi’s hiatus, Canty remained active across DC and broader indie circles as a producer, engineer, and drummer-for-hire.

Garland Of Hours (2003–2012)

Garland Of Hours was Canty’s primary band project in the post-Fugazi decade. The lineup included Jerry Busher, Amy Farina (or Amy Domingues at various points), Devin Ocampo, Mary Timony, and Jonah Takagi. Released on Noble Task and Arrest Records.

Deathfix (2009–2013)

Deathfix was a band Canty founded in 2009, in which he switched to guitar and vocals. Lineup: Canty (guitar, vocals), Devin Ocampo (drums, backing vocals), Mark Cisneros (bass, backing vocals), Richard “Rich Morel” Morel (keyboards, vocals). Self-titled LP released on Dischord Records (2013). Canty recorded the album himself and shot the photography.

Lois Maffeo Collaboration

Canty co-credited on The Union Themes (2000, Kill Rock Stars) with Lois Maffeo; played virtually every instrument (acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, piano, drums, mellotron) and produced and mixed the record. One of the more unusual entries in his discography — a full multi-instrumentalist/production role rather than a drum credit.

The Messthetics (2018–present)

The Messthetics reunites Canty and Joe Lally — the Fugazi rhythm section — with jazz guitarist Anthony Pirog. An instrumental trio working at the intersection of post-hardcore and free jazz.

Fake Names (2019–present)

Canty is an uncredited drummer on the Fake Names EP (2021, Epitaph) and the Expendables LP (2023, Epitaph). Fake Names is a DC supergroup including Brian Baker (Minor Threat/Dag Nasty/Bad Religion), Michael Hampton, and others.

MC50 and Other Recent Work

Canty is listed as a member of MC50 — the 50th anniversary touring band for the MC5. Also a member of The Foreign Correspondents (2) — Lovin’ You Ain’t Easy (2024, Outer Battery Records) credits him as a member. The Second LP (2025, Balmat) shows him as primary artist, playing drums, bass, and piano and credited as writer.
