Fennesz

Real name: Christian Fennesz. Born: 1962, Vienna, Austria. Discogs artist ID: 3837. Guitarist, computer musician, composer. Active: 1990s–present.

Roles: performer, composer, producer, remixer. Primary instruments: guitar, laptop/computer, electronics, synthesizer.

Aliases: C. Fennesz, Ch. Fennesz, Christian Fennesz (as namevariation on releases).

Groups: Fenn O’Berg, Fennesz + Sakamoto, MIMEO, Orchester 33 1-3, Maische, Golden Tone, 4g, Regenorchester XII, Till The Old World’s Blown Up And A New One Is Created.

Key labels: Mego, Editions Mego, Touch, Ritornell, Commmons (Ryuichi Sakamoto’s label), Samadhisound (David Sylvian’s label).

Biography

Vienna Underground — Early Career (late 1980s–mid 1990s)

Christian Fennesz began as singer, guitarist, and songwriter in Maische, a band active in the Viennese underground in the late 1980s. His trajectory shifted sharply in the mid-1990s when he began working with computers and software to process and disintegrate guitar sounds — a move that aligned him with the nascent experimental electronic scene forming around Vienna’s Mego label, run by Peter Rehberg (Pita) and Ramon Bauer.

His first releases as Fennesz appeared in 1995: the debut Instrument (Mego, 1995) and the two-track 5/6 (Syntactic, 1995). These early recordings announced a sound built from fractured, stuttering digital textures layered over guitar residue. He also contributed guitar and production to other Vienna-area projects during this period, including Orchester 33 1-3 and production work on releases for Burkhard Stangl’s milieu.

Hotel Paral.lel and the Mego World (1997–1999)

Hotel Paral.lel (Mego, 1997) was Fennesz’s first fully realised statement: a dense, self-contained solo work in which he credited himself as doing everything. The album established the aesthetic that would define Mego’s mid-period — hyperactive DSP processing, glitch artifacts deployed melodically rather than as confrontation, a latent pop sensibility under the digital noise.

Plays (Mego, 1998) followed, a more abstract set. His first release for Touch — Plus Forty Seven Degrees 56’ 37” Minus Sixteen Degrees 51’ 08” (1999) — marked the beginning of a long relationship with the UK label run by Jon Wozencroft and Mike Harding, and showed a more atmospheric, geographical approach to electronics.

Laptop Music and the Broader Scene (1999–2001)

Around 1999–2001 Fennesz was visible throughout the European improvised and electronic scenes. He participated in MIMEO (Music In Movement Electronic Orchestra), a large-scale improv ensemble that drew in figures including Keith Rowe, Kassel Jaeger, Phil Minton, and Christian Marclay. He also contributed to the Afternoon Tea (Ritornell, 2000) five-way laptop/guitar improvisation with Oren Ambarchi, Pimmon, Peter Rehberg, and Keith Rowe — one of the key documents of the era’s Vienna/Sydney/free-improv overlap.

He appeared at the 2001 Sonic Youth “Live At L’Olympia” performance, credited as playing laptop.

Endless Summer (2001)

Endless Summer (Mego, 2001; Discogs release ID: 11078) is Fennesz’s most celebrated album and a landmark of early 2000s electronic music. Entirely solo, it processes guitar into warm, oceanic masses of digital distortion and hiss — the surface is corroded but the emotional register is almost naively lyrical, referencing Beach Boys melodicism through a filter of laptop noise. The album has been reissued multiple times on Editions Mego and remains the central reference point for “glitch-ambient” as a genre.

Credits: cover design by Tina Frank (a frequent Mego/Editions Mego collaborator); mastered by Street Romance; photography by Hertha Hurnaus.

Venice and Touch (2003–2007)

With Mego folding after financial difficulties in the early 2000s (it would be relaunched as Editions Mego by Rehberg in 2006), Fennesz moved his solo work fully to Touch. Venice (Touch, 2004; release ID: 252767) was a more intimate, string-toned record: where Endless Summer was abrasive beneath its beauty, Venice is warmer, slower, more openly romantic. Guest contributors on specific tracks included Burkhard Stangl (guitar, co-composer, track 5 and 10) and David Sylvian (co-composer, track 8). Engineering for the duo tracks by Christoph Amann. Mastered by Denis Blackham; sleeve by Jon Wozencroft.

During this period Fennesz collaborated extensively with Ryuichi Sakamoto, initially performing together live (including the 2005 Sala Santa Cecilia Touch release). The duo was formalised as Fennesz + Sakamoto; their studio collaboration Cendre (Commmons, 2007; release ID: 939790) documented guitar/laptop/piano interplay recorded and mixed by Fennesz and Sakamoto jointly, with additional mixing by Fernando Aponte.

Fennesz also contributed guitar and laptop to David Sylvian’s Manafon (Samadhisound, 2009; release ID: 1925789), a large-ensemble improvised-text album also featuring Keith Rowe, Evan Parker, John Tilbury, Toshimaru Nakamura, Franz Hautzinger, Otomo Yoshihide, and Werner Dafeldecker.

Black Sea (2008)

Black Sea (Touch, 2008; release ID: 1487500) is Fennesz’s darkest solo album — electric and acoustic guitar, synthesizer, electronics, and computer (using the Lloopp software environment). Entirely self-composed, performed, recorded, and mixed. It extended the palette of Venice into more turbulent, storm-driven territory. Mastered by Denis Blackham; sleeve and photography by Jon Wozencroft.

Fenn O’Berg

Fenn O’Berg — Fennesz, Jim O’Rourke, and Peter Rehberg — was an ongoing laptop trio active in live and studio contexts through the 2000s and 2010s. Releases on Mego/Editions Mego: The Return Of Fenn O’Berg (Mego MEGO 054, 2002; release ID: 36643), Magic & Return (Editions Mego eMego 3154, 2009; 2×CD compilation pairing new recordings with the remastered debut), In Stereo (eMEGO 103, 2010), Live In Japan Part One (eMEGO 110.1, 2010) and Live In Japan Part Two (eMEGO 110.2, 2010), and In Hell (eMEGO141, 2012; 2×LP of November 2010 Japan tour recordings). The trio became inactive with Rehberg’s death in July 2021.

Sparklehorse and Wider Collaborations (2009–2011)

In The Fishtank 15 (Konkurrent, 2009; release ID: 1901697) paired Fennesz with Mark Linkous (Sparklehorse) for an unusual atmospheric guitar-and-electronics session, with all songs co-written by the two. The album was produced and mixed by Zlaya Hadzich.

Seven Stars (Touch, 2011; release ID: 2896237) continued the Touch solo run — acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, synths, and computers, recorded by Christoph Amann.

Bécs and Editions Mego Return (2014)

Bécs (Editions Mego, 2014; release ID: 5606903) — the name is the Hungarian word for Vienna — marked Fennesz’s return to Editions Mego (which had been issuing Fenn O’Berg material). An album of solo compositions, with cover design by Tina Frank and mastering by Rashad Becker.

Also in 2014: There’s A Light That Enters Houses With No Other House In Sight (Samadhisound; release ID: 6222396) — a David Sylvian project featuring Fennesz on guitar and laptop alongside poet Franz Wright and pianist John Tilbury.

Agora and Recent Work (2019–2025)

Agora (Touch, 2019; release ID: 13406995) was Fennesz’s most recent solo Touch album before Senzatempo. Released also through P-Vine in Japan. Further Touch releases followed: Station One (2018), Umbrella (2019), Senzatempo (2023).

Mosaic (P-Vine, 2024) signalled continued activity on the Japan-facing label, and The Last Days Of May (Longform Editions, 2025) extended the catalog. A 2024 reissue of Venice was marked as Venice 20 for the album’s 20th anniversary.

Ongoing collaborations continue; a 2026 release Kin (Editions Mego) credits Fennesz as producer and co-writer.

Scene Connections

Vienna laptop/experimental scene (Mego ecosystem, late 1990s–2000s): Fennesz is a central node. Mego gathered Peter Rehberg, Mika Vainio, Florian Hecker, Kevin Drumm, and numerous others around a shared interest in harsh computer music, glitch, and digital synthesis. Fennesz was unusual within this context for foregrounding lyricism and guitar heritage alongside the computer noise. His success with Endless Summer helped define “laptop music” as a legitimate form in mainstream critical reception.

British ambient/post-industrial via Touch: Touch (London) sits adjacent to industrial and experimental traditions (it has issued work by Chris Watson, Biosphere, Coil and others). Fennesz’s sustained presence on Touch connected the Vienna scene to that tradition. Jon Wozencroft’s characteristic Touch visual language appears across Fennesz’s Touch catalog.

Free improvisation crossovers: Through MIMEO and the broader improv circuit, Fennesz worked alongside figures from British and European free improvisation — Keith Rowe (AMM), Evan Parker, John Tilbury, Toshimaru Nakamura, Franz Hautzinger. This gave his work a conceptual rigor beyond ambient electronics.

Japanese ambient/art music: Via Sakamoto’s Commmons label and the Sylvian/Samadhisound axis, Fennesz’s work was deeply embedded in Tokyo art music circles through the 2000s–2010s.

4g: A lesser-documented group shared with Oren Ambarchi (Discogs artist ID: 367199 for the group).
