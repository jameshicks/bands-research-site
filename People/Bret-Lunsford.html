Overview

Bret Lunsford is a guitarist, songwriter, singer, author, and historian based in Anacortes, Washington. He is best known as a co-founder of Beat Happening, the Olympia indie/lo-fi trio he formed with Calvin Johnson and Heather Lewis — a partnership that accounts for more than 90 shared credits from 1984 onward. Beyond Beat Happening he has worked within D+ (2) and The Microphones’ Singers, and founded his own Knw-Yr-Own imprint. His catalogue touches K, Sub Pop, Domino, and P.W. Elverum & Sun, with recurring collaborators including Steve Fisk, Karl Blau, and Phil Elvrum placing him firmly at the centre of the Pacific Northwest underground.

Knw-Yr-Own Records

Lunsford founded Knw-Yr-Own in 1988 to document and promote music being made in his hometown of Anacortes, Washington. According to the Olympia Music History archive, the explicit motivation was “to highlight the music and culture of his hometown.” Karl Blau’s biography, written from inside the Anacortes scene, frames the label’s premise as “to honor the music being created in Anacortes with a common badge and help to create a ‘thing’” — meaning a shared identity for the local scene rather than a commercial enterprise. (karlblau.com/about, olympiamusichistory.org/people/bret-lunsford)

In its early years the label concentrated on local compilations. Phil Elvrum became a significant figure at Knw-Yr-Own in the mid-1990s, releasing work by The Microphones (including cassettes Microphone and Wires and Cords) as well as Pirhanas, Bronwyn, Khaela Maricich, Dennis Driscoll, and James Moffit. Elvrum subsequently relocated to Olympia and became associated with K, which provided Knw-Yr-Own with national distribution support and a press-contact list for the compilations Remote Wing (2001) and Shipwreck Day (2002). (anacortesmusic.substack.com) The label also played an early role in Karl Blau’s career, releasing his early cassettes and organising a 1997 community spaghetti dinner to fund the pressing of his debut CD Shell Collection. (karlblau.com/about)

How Knw-Yr-Own differs from K Records. The two labels share overlapping personnel and a DIY Pacific Northwest ethos — D+ released early material jointly on both imprints — but their editorial scope is distinct. K is centred on the Olympia underground and operates as a nationally distributed label with an international profile. Knw-Yr-Own is explicitly hyperlocal: its founding premise was to give Anacortes musicians a “common badge,” and its catalogue — dominated by Anacortes compilations and individual releases by artists rooted in that town — reflects that. One characterisation from a contemporary review describes the Knw-Yr-Own circle as “retaining the DIY culture of the ’90s” while “sidestep[ping] some of the pretension associated with their K records cousins” (source: search snippet, exact article unlocated). That framing should be treated with caution as the originating article has not been verified. What is clear from multiple sources is that the geographic and community focus is the defining distinction: K speaks for the broader Olympia scene; Knw-Yr-Own speaks for Anacortes specifically.

The label’s activities have extended beyond music: Lunsford also uses the Knw-Yr-Own imprint for his book publishing, including Sounding for Harry Smith (Knw-Yr-Own). He also runs What the Heck Fest, an annual Anacortes music festival whose inaugural edition (2001) produced the Remote Wing compilation and whose second edition (2002) produced Shipwreck Day. (anacortesmusic.substack.com)

Note — “sublabel” claim. Grunnenrocks.nl describes Knw-Yr-Own as “a sublabel of K Records.” This conflicts with Discogs and multiple other sources, which treat Knw-Yr-Own as an independent label owned and operated by Lunsford. The K connection appears to be a distribution/promotional relationship rather than an ownership one. The Grunnenrocks source is a hobbyist directory and is likely incorrect.
