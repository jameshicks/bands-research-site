Douglas MacIntyre

Guitarist, bassist, vocalist, songwriter, and label founder from Strathaven, Scotland. Discogs artist ID: 893550. Founder of Creeping Bent. Name variations: Douglas Macintyre, Douglas McIntyre, D. MacIntyre, D. McIntyre, D.MacIntyre, MacIntyre, McIntyre.

Biography

Origins and Early Glasgow Scene (1981–1984)

MacIntyre emerged from the early-1980s Glasgow post-punk ferment alongside better-known contemporaries Orange Juice, Josef K, and Aztec Camera. His first documented credit is on Article 58’s debut 7” Event To Come (Rational Records, 1981), where he played guitar under the name “Douglas McIntyre.” Article 58 also featured Stephen Lironi on drums and Gerry McLaughlin on vocals. The single was produced by Alan Horne (Postcard Records founder) and Malcolm Ross. It was reissued on Optic Nerve Recordings in 2022.

Around the same time he was involved with Jazzateers, the Glasgow art-pop group that briefly signed to Rough Trade — the connection running through shared membership with Lironi. Jazzateers’ core recordings (1981–82) included sessions produced by Edwyn Collins; MacIntyre wrote liner notes for the 2014 Cherry Red archival release Don’t Let Your Son Grow Up To Be A Cowboy (Unreleased Recordings 1981–82). He is not listed as a performer on the Jazzateers’ Rough Trade output, suggesting his involvement was primarily in the 1981–82 pre-label phase. The later Jazzateers 1983 self-titled LP (Rough Trade) was self-produced by the band (Ian Burgoyne, Keith Band), without MacIntyre in the primary credits.

Love And Money (1986–1993)

MacIntyre joined Love And Money, the Glasgow soul/pop band led by James Grant, providing guitar and backing vocals. The band released several albums on Mercury/Fontana including Strange Kind Of Love (1988) and Dogs In The Traffic (1991), which reached the UK charts. MacIntyre appears credited on Dogs In The Traffic for guitar and backing vocals. The band also shared members with what became Sugartown — a side project featuring MacIntyre, Gordon Wilson, and Paul McGeechan (Discogs ID: 893549), who released Swimming In The Horsepool on Marina Records (Germany, 1995).

Creeping Bent — Label Founding (1994–present)

MacIntyre founded Creeping Bent in Glasgow in 1994, building it into the city’s primary home for post-punk, art-rock, and leftfield independent music. The label’s first decade centered on Glasgow scene output and archival releases. His role was both curatorial and hands-on: he produced, played on, and co-wrote material for artists on the label throughout its history.

Key early production work includes the Secret Goldfish EP Somewhere In The World (Creeping Bent, 1999), co-produced with Gregor Reid, which featured a Vic Godard co-write.

The Sexual Objects (2007–present)

MacIntyre’s primary ongoing band. The Sexual Objects debuted with the 7” Full Penetration (Creeping Bent, BENT 093, 2007) and have released steadily on Creeping Bent and the German labels Aufgeladen Und Bereit and Aktion Und Spass. The lineup: MacIntyre (bass, vocals), David Henderson (guitar, vocals, written-by), Ian Holford (drums, vocals), and Simon Smeeton (guitar, vocals). The 2025 LP Orangutang (Creeping Bent, BENT 093 — catalog number reused as a statement) extended the lineup with Graham Wann on guitar/vocals, with production split between MacIntyre, Marshall Craigmyle, and John Disco (3).

Port Sulphur (2018–2022)

A later-period project on Creeping Bent. Port Sulphur released Paranoic Critical (2018), Compendium (2020), Speed Of Life (2022), and Meta Guru (2024) on Creeping Bent / Last Night From Glasgow. Members: MacIntyre, Sam Smith (4) (who also produced, recorded, mixed, and played synth/guitar/percussion), Paul Turnbull (3) (drums), Gavin Fraser (2), and Grant McPhee.

The Skinner Group (2014–2016)

A reunion project centered on Jazzateers singer Grahame Skinner (Discogs ID: 893545), also including MacIntyre, Campbell Owens (ex-Aztec Camera), Gordon Wilson, and Michael Slaven. Released Back On The Horse (Not On Label, 2014) and Watertoun (Not On Label, 2016).

The Kingfishers (4) (2023)

A project with Ewan McLennan (ex-Article 58), Kenny Blythe, and Robert McCormick (2). Released Reflections In a Silver Sound (Creeping Bent / Last Night From Glasgow, 2023).

Production and Session Credits

Beyond his own bands, MacIntyre has produced and co-produced for Creeping Bent artists:

  • The Secret Goldfish — Somewhere In The World E.P. (1999, Creeping Bent), Petal Split (2017, Creeping Bent)
  • The Magic Circles — Kirk Street (2008, Creeping Bent), co-produced and co-written
  • Monica Queen — Stop That Girl (2022, Creeping Bent / Last Night From Glasgow), co-produced; MacIntyre played guitar, bass, Wurlitzer, Hammond organ; Monica Queen on vocals
  • Marina Records releases — credited on multiple releases including Life As A Dog (1994, producer and guitar), Kelvingrove Baby (1997, bass), Endless Soul (1998, liner notes), In Bed With Marina compilation (1996, compiled by)
