Jangil Callas

Vocalist, songwriter, and visual artist; frontman of Electric Callas. Real name Jean-Gilles Posada. Discogs artist ID: 1118349.

Overview

Jangil Callas (a.k.a. J.-G. Posadas, Jangli) was the creative core of Electric Callas, writing all credited material on both of the band’s releases. He sang lead vocals on the 1981 Winner 12” and handled graphic design and co-design on the 1979 So Chic EP alongside Federico Cimatti. The “Callas” surname is a nod to opera singer Maria Callas, given a punk charge by the “Electric” prefix.

Identity and origin

Jean-Gilles Posada adopted “Jangil Callas” as his stage name; the “Callas” surname is a reference to opera singer Maria Callas, and the band name plays on that reference with punk electricity. He was the sole permanent member of Electric Callas — the wider lineup fluctuated around him throughout the group’s existence. (rockmadeinfrance.com, fr-academic.com)

Musical influences cited in contemporary press: David Bowie, T. Rex, Iggy Pop / The Stooges. (rockmadeinfrance.com)

Post-Electric Callas activity

Two post-band projects have been documented:

  1. Posada — a formation assembled specifically for the 1993 Johnny Thunders tribute compilation I Only Play R&R For Kids To Dance – Tribute to Johnny Thunders. Posada (the project) contributed a track; Jangil Callas performed “Chatterbox” on the release. (rockmadeinfrance.com; release confirmed at amazon.com)

  2. Slogan — a later band credited with a six-track EP. Minimal documentation exists; mentioned in liner notes as a subsequent project. (rockmadeinfrance.com)

A 2008 recording also exists: Electric Callas re-recorded “W.S.B.” (the B-side of So Chic, 1979) featuring Patrick Vidal of Marie Et Les Garçons. This track appeared on the Des Jeunes Gens Mödernes compilation connected to an Agnès B exhibition in Paris (April–May 2008). The credit “JG Callas” is noted on the Talk Talk single with Howie B (date unconfirmed). (senscritique.com; last.fm)

Name usage

“Jangil Callas” remains the name used for Electric Callas-related contexts. The surname “Posada” appears in the tribute-band context. No evidence found of significant activity under either name after the mid-1990s, though compilations continued to reference and re-license the original recordings.
