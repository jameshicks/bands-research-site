Lydia Tomkiw

American poet, vocalist, and lyricist. Born August 6, 1959, Chicago, Illinois. Died September 4, 2007, Phoenix, Arizona. Discogs artist ID: 283278.

Co-founder and vocalist/lyricist of Algebra Suicide (1982–1994), alongside Don Hedeker. Her contributions were always credited as words/text/lyrics rather than conventional songwriting — the spoken-word dimension was central to the duo’s identity.

Biography

Early Poetry (pre-1982)

Tomkiw was an active poet in the Chicago literary underground before any recorded output existed. Three early collections appeared via MOD Distribution, Chicago:

  • Ballpoint Erection (1978; 2nd edition 1979)
  • Obsessions (1979)
  • Popgun Sonatas (1980)

Her brother John Tomkiw appears on early Algebra Suicide sleeve credits as photographer and graphic designer (confirmed on True Romance At The Worlds Fair and An Explanation For That Flock Of Crows).

Algebra Suicide Era (1982–1994)

Tomkiw and Don Hedeker formed Algebra Suicide in Chicago in 1982. The project ran for roughly twelve years and produced seven releases under the name. The sound was built on Hedeker’s guitar, tape, and synthesizer work underneath Tomkiw’s spoken and sung text — delivered with minimal performance affect, which became a recognizable quality of the duo.

The 1982 cassette True Romance At The Worlds Fair (self-produced, Buzzerama Records) was engineered by Jerry Soto, who remained the primary engineer through 1985. The debut established the template: Tomkiw credited for “Words And Voice,” Hedeker for “Music And Vice,” both jointly as “Written-By.”

Big Skin (1986, Cause And Effect) marked a significant step — it was packaged as an audio/text object pairing the cassette with a printed text volume, foregrounding Tomkiw’s poetry as equal content to the recorded material. Credits are minimal on Discogs (voice and lyrics only), suggesting the duo produced it themselves without outside engineering.

The Secret Like Crazy (1987, Dom Elchklang / RRRecords) saw Tomkiw take an additional credit for cover design — the only release in the catalog where she is credited for visual work.

By Alpha Cue (1990, Body Records, Belgium) and Swoon (1991, Body Records), the duo had moved to a Belgian label and the ensemble briefly expanded: Dave Riley (later of Big Black) contributed bass, piano, and percussion on two tracks of Swoon, and multiple engineers (Scott Steinman, Benj Kanters, David Appelt) appear across the two records.

In 1993, Tomkiw appeared uncredited as voice on the A-side of Evidence, a 7” by Tag Team (3) (Crosstalk). The record was co-produced uncredited by Phillip Hertz and Steve Fisk.

Post-Algebra Suicide (1995–2007)

After Algebra Suicide dissolved around 1994, Tomkiw worked through 1993–1994 assembling a new set of poems for music. The result was Incorporated (1995, Widely Distributed Records, cat. WDRU 038), a CD-only solo album recorded and mixed at Flat Iron Studios, Chicago. She collaborated with a different set of musicians on each track rather than a fixed band: Chuck Uchida (of No Empathy) on three tracks, Reality Scare on three tracks, Sosumi on one track, Robert Coddington on one track, Dirk Ivens (of The Klinik / Dive) on one track, and Edward Ka-Spel and the Legendary Pink Dots on two tracks; Martin Bowes of Attrition also contributed one track. Producers were Mark Schwarz, Chuck Uchida, Lydia Tomkiw (most tracks), and Edward Ka-Spel (“Sometimes…” and “Pretty Something”). The record extended her Algebra Suicide template while incorporating industrial, techno, and ambient elements. [sources: continuo.wordpress.com; brainwashed.com; wikipedia.org]

After Incorporated, Tomkiw largely withdrew from public activity. Dan Shepelavy, who edited her posthumous collected poems, described the trajectory plainly: “she craved connection and an audience and as that began to slip away, she struggled. The solace of booze comes with a heavy price and it undid her.” [source: modernsoulrecordsco.com, In Conversation with Dan Shepelavy]

In 2004, following what Wikipedia describes as “a period of aimlessness, false starts & dissipation,” she moved to Sun City, Arizona to be with her widowed mother Zenovia. She had no public presence from 2004 onward. Paul Hoover, who had been her teacher and later wrote the introduction to her collected poems, noted on his blog after her death that the news “is a shock to those of us who knew her, but as someone has commented, not a complete surprise.” [source: paulhooverpoetry.blogspot.com]

She died September 4, 2007, in her apartment in Phoenix, aged 48, of natural causes — a friend’s blog cited heart problems as a possible factor (noted by savetier.eu). She was buried at Saint Nicholas Ukrainian Catholic Cemetery, Chicago. No poetry or other published work appeared after Incorporated (1995). [source: wikipedia.org, accessed 2026-05-16; savetier.eu]

Posthumous Releases

Two posthumous Algebra Suicide compilations appeared on San Francisco reissue label Dark Entries:

  • Feminine Squared (2013) — remastered by George Horn, tape transfer by Alberto Hernandez; includes a video credit for Mark Giangrande
  • Still Life (2019) — final posthumous collection; engineered by Jerry Soto

Both releases list Tomkiw with “Lyrics By, Vocals” / “Vocals, Words By,” maintaining the same credit language used during the active years.

A posthumous poetry collection, Lydia Tomkiw Poems, was published in 2020 by Universal Exports of North America (Philadelphia / Jim Thorpe, Pennsylvania; ISBN 978-1-7345347-0-2; 409 pp.), edited and designed by Dan Shepelavy. It contains facsimile editions of all her published chapbooks, over 180 previously uncollected poems, a comprehensive biography, an introduction by Paul Hoover, a recollection by Sharon Mesmer, and a consideration of Algebra Suicide by Ira Robbins (Trouser Press). Each copy included a download of Algebra Suicide’s Big Skin (1986), issued in partnership with Dark Entries. The book was also listed for sale by Modern Soul Records at $15.00 (sold out). [sources: universalexportsna.com; modernsoulrecordsco.com; books.google.com; thewombwellrainbow.com, accessed 2026-05-16]

Note: the Modern Soul Records listing dates this to 2019; Google Books and the Universal Exports website give 2020 copyright. The Propeller Books review (October 2020) and copyright date of 2020 are taken as authoritative; 2019 may be a pre-release/advance listing date.
