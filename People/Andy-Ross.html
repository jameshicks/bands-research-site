Andy Ross

Andrew Ian Ross (31 January 1956 – 25 January 2022). UK music executive, journalist, and musician. Confirmed as the same Andy Ross in Club Tango (1981) who later co-ran Food Records and signed Blur (1990). Died aged 66.

Early Career — Music and Journalism

Ross worked in record shops before joining the Inland Revenue for a period to secure a mortgage. By the early 1980s he was playing with The Disco Zombies (the Camden post-punk band co-founded by Dave Henderson). After the Disco Zombies, Ross was a member of Club Tango (1981), a short-lived post-punk outfit also featuring Dave Henderson, Alex Turnbull (later of 23 Skidoo), Paul Hood, and Nigel Lackey.

He then moved into music journalism, writing for Sounds magazine alongside figures including John Robb. Around 1986, Ross left a stint at the Inland Revenue to work full-time at Food Records in Camden.

Sources: Systems of Romance blog, April 2022 (as of 2026-06-25); Wikipedia — Andy Ross (music executive) (as of 2026-06-25); Louder Than War tribute (as of 2026-06-25)

Food Records

Food Records was founded in the mid-1980s by David Balfe. Ross joined as Balfe’s partner and became the label’s operational head, based in Camden. He continued part-time journalism at Sounds simultaneously.

In November 1989, Ross saw Blur perform at the Islington Powerhaus. He signed the band on 7 March 1990 for a small advance; the signing celebration was held at a pizza restaurant on Beak Street. Blur became the label’s defining act: Parklife (1994, Food/EMI) debuted at UK #1 and established the band as central figures in Britpop alongside Oasis and Pulp. Ross considered Blur’s 1993 Reading Festival set “the turning point of Blur’s career.”

EMI invested in Food and gained full control in early 1994, subsequently folding it into Parlophone in 2000. Ross continued running Food as an EMI sub-label. Other acts on Food under Ross included Jesus Jones, Dubstar, Strangelove, Idlewild, The Supernaturals, Octopus, Grass Show, and Fungus (Sweden). The label’s final Blur release was 13 (1999).

Ross was also closely involved in the logistics of Blur’s 1995 “Country House” / Oasis “Roll With It” chart battle, working directly with Damon Albarn on the release strategy.

Sources: Wikipedia — Andy Ross (as of 2026-06-25); Billboard obituary; NME tribute

Death

Ross died on 25 January 2022, aged 66, six days before his 66th birthday. Tributes came from Blur, Damon Albarn, and numerous figures in the British music industry.
