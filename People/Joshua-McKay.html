Overview

Joshua McKay is a bassist, vibraphonist, and producer whose career spans two distinct chapters of American indie: the early-1990s circle around River Phoenix through Aleka’s Attic, and the Atlanta experimental scene of the 2000s through Macha and Deerhunter. He accumulates 67 Discogs credits across bass, vibraphone, vocals, and songwriting roles, with his densest collaborative cluster around Mischo McKay, Kai Riedl, Bradford Cox, Moses Archuleta, and Lockett Pundt. His label history moves from Jetset Records and Saddle Creek Europe in the late 1990s and early 2000s through to 4AD (four works, 2013–2023), where Deerhunter recorded their later albums.

Notes (web)

Connection to River Phoenix and Aleka’s Attic

McKay was approximately 22 years old and studying anthropology in Texas when he was drawn into River Phoenix’s circle. According to a summary of his biography, he was “considering moving to Florida to play in a band with teenage actor River Phoenix” at that point; he relocated to Gainesville, Florida, where he went on to live for around six years. The two musicians “hit it off and began writing and singing together,” with Rain Phoenix on keyboard and Tim Hankins on viola. [peoplepill.com snippet via search, 2026-11-15 — page itself 403’d]

No source consulted gives a more specific account of how McKay and River Phoenix first met (through mutual friends, a Gainesville connection, or otherwise). The formation date given in multiple fan sources is April 1987, with the band described as founded by River Phoenix and Josh Greenbaum; McKay appears as a member from the outset in every account. [https://www.aleka.org/phoenix/music.html]

Role in recordings

  • Original lineup bassist. McKay held the bass chair throughout the band’s existence (1987–1993). The core lineup was River Phoenix (vocals, guitar), Rain Phoenix (vocals), McKay (bass), Josh Greenbaum (drums), and Tim Hankins (viola). [https://www.aleka.org/phoenix/music.html]
  • Co-writer on “Across The Way.” The band’s only commercially released track — on the 1989 PETA benefit album Tame Yourself — is credited as “written by River Phoenix and Josh McKay of Aleka’s Attic.” McKay’s specific contribution (lyrical, melodic, or structural) is not detailed in any source consulted. [https://www.aleka.org/phoenix/lyrics/across.html]
  • Bass on “In The Corner Dunce.” McKay is credited as bassist on this track. [https://www.aleka.org/phoenix/music.html]
  • Unreleased 1993 album. McKay was part of the sessions for the nearly-completed album tentatively titled Never Odd or Even (also referred to as Zero). The album was shelved after River Phoenix’s death on 31 October 1993. No source gives a detailed breakdown of McKay’s contributions to those recordings specifically. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleka%27s_Attic — noted as Wikipedia, skip-flagged for this question; detail corroborated by https://www.last.fm/music/Aleka’s+Attic/Never+Odd+Or+Even]
