Shirley Collins

Discogs artist ID: 236336 Born: 5 July 1935, Hastings, East Sussex, UK Real name: Shirley Elizabeth Collins Alias: Shirley Hutchings (married name, Ashley Hutchings period) Active: 1956–c.1978 (first career); 2016–present (return) Labels: Domino Records (2016–present); Topic Records (1963–1978); Harvest Records / EMI (1969–1976); Polydor (1968); Argo/Decca (1959)

British folk singer and central figure in the English folk revival. One of the most influential voices in the British tradition song repertoire — her unadorned, unaffected vocal style set the template for what “authentic” English folk singing meant from the 1960s onward. Long absence from recording (c.1979–2016) due to dysphonia; returned at 81 with Lodestar (Domino, 2016) and has released two further albums since.

Overview

Collins grew up in Hastings, absorbing Sussex folk traditions at an early age. She came into the London folk club scene in the mid-1950s and by 1956 had already appeared on record as a vocalist alongside Alan Lomax and The Ramblers (Decca). The pivotal turn came in 1959 when Lomax invited her to assist his field-recording expedition through the American South — a months-long journey through Alabama, Mississippi, Georgia, and the Carolinas that produced the Southern Journey series for Atlantic and later Rounder, and that Collins described as transformative. Lomax produced and co-arranged her debut solo LP Sweet England (Argo, 1959) from that same period.

Through the 1960s Collins recorded prolifically for Topic and Collector Records, and collaborated with guitarist Davy Graham on the landmark Folk Roots, New Routes (Decca, 1964) — an album that fused traditional English song with jazz and blues guitar and is widely cited as a founding document of British folk-rock. Her closest long-term collaborator was her older sister Dolly Collins (Dorothy Ann Collins, 1933–1995), a composer and arranger who played flute-organ, portative pipe-organ, and keyboard on most of Shirley’s major work from 1967 through 1978.

The Shirley & Dolly Collins partnership produced some of the most adventurous albums of the British folk revival: Anthems In Eden (Harvest, 1969), with its consort of early-music instruments led by David Munrow; Love, Death & The Lady (Harvest, 1970); and A Favourite Garland (Deram, 1974). Separately, Collins collaborated with the Albion Country Band (led by her then-husband Ashley Hutchings) on the influential No Roses (Pegasus, 1971), produced by Hutchings and Sandy Roberton.

Collins and Hutchings also co-led The Etchingham Steam Band in 1974–75, an all-acoustic ensemble playing southern English dance music around folk clubs and festivals. Their marriage ended in the late 1970s, and Collins attributed her dysphonia — a neurological voice disorder — in part to the emotional trauma of the separation, though Discogs data does not document this directly.

After a long silence, Collins emerged via documentary and spoken-word credits through the 1990s–2000s. Her comeback album Lodestar (Domino, 2016) was her first full-length in 38 years and was produced by Ian Kearey. Heart’s Ease (Domino, 2020) continued the partnership with Kearey and drew in collaborators including Nathan Salsburg and Ossian Brown. Archangel Hill (Domino, 2023) was again produced by Kearey.
