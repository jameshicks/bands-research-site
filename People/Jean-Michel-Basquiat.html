Jean-Michel Basquiat

Discogs artist ID: 79325

American painter and neo-expressionist artist. Born December 22, 1960, in Park Slope, Brooklyn, New York; died August 12, 1988, of a heroin overdose in SoHo, Manhattan, age 27. Active in the NYC downtown scene from the late 1970s onward, first as street-art persona SAMO (with Al Diaz), then as a painter whose work moved rapidly from East Village galleries into international museum circuits. He was also a practicing musician — co-founder of the noise/industrial band Gray (2) and producer of the 1983 hip-hop single “Beat Bop.” His visual art credits appear on numerous record sleeves, and his Discogs footprint spans sleeve art, music production, and songwriting. [Sources: Discogs; Wikipedia/Jean-Michel_Basquiat (as of 2026-06)]

Biography

SAMO and the downtown scene: 1978–1980

In May 1978, Basquiat and schoolmate Al Diaz began spray-painting enigmatic slogans under the tag SAMO (for “same old shit”) across Lower Manhattan. The Village Voice covered the campaign in December 1978. Around this time Basquiat became a regular on the downtown club circuit — Max’s Kansas City, CBGB, the Mudd Club, Hurrah — and appeared on Glenn O’Brien’s TV Party public-access show from 1979 onward.

In April 1979, Basquiat met Michael Holman at the Canal Zone Party. They founded a band first called Test Pattern, shortly renamed Gray. Original members alongside Basquiat and Holman included Nicholas Taylor (guitar/bass), Wayne Clifford (aka Justin Thyme), and Shannon Dawson. Vincent Gallo joined for a brief period nearer the end of the first incarnation. Basquiat named the band after Gray’s Anatomy, a book his mother had given him during a childhood hospital stay. Gray performed noise/industrial/ambient sets at Max’s Kansas City, CBGB, Hurrah, and the Mudd Club; their sound has been described as “haunting, machine-like ambient music.” Gray recordings appear on the soundtrack to Edo Bertoglio’s film Downtown 81 (shot 1980–81, released 2000). [Wikipedia]

Rise as a painter: 1980–1983

In June 1980 Basquiat appeared in The Times Square Show and was noticed by curators and dealers. In February 1981 he appeared in Diego Cortez’s landmark New York/New Wave exhibition at P.S.1, which brought him mainstream gallery attention. He left Gray sometime around 1981 to concentrate on painting full-time.

In 1983, while preparing a show at the Gagosian Gallery in Los Angeles, Basquiat produced the hip-hop single “Beat Bop” for Rammellzee and K-Rob. He booked a studio, arranged and produced the track, and released it himself on his Tartown Inc. imprint in a limited pressing of approximately 500 copies, with his own artwork on the cover. Profile Records distributed a version without the artwork. The original pressing, combining Basquiat’s cover art with experimental hip-hop, became among the most valuable rap records ever made; Rolling Stone ranked it no. 79 on its list of the 100 Greatest Hip-Hop Songs of All Time. [Wikipedia/Beat_Bop]

Session players on “Beat Bop” included Al Diaz (percussion), Sekou Bunch (guitar), and Eszter Balint (violin). [Wikipedia/Beat_Bop]

International success and death: 1983–1988

Basquiat collaborated extensively with Andy Warhol in 1984–1985. He also maintained a cover-art relationship with music: he designed sleeves for The Offs’ First Record (1984) and was photographed by James Van Der Zee for Warhol’s Interview. He died on August 12, 1988. Just after his death, the former Gray members — Holman, Taylor, Thyme, and Gallo — reunited and performed at a memorial at St. Peter’s Lutheran Church. In 2010 Michael Holman and Nicholas Taylor regrouped as Gray to produce the band’s first proper album, Shades Of…, released on their own Plush Safe Records. [Discogs; Wikipedia]
