V. Selvaganesh

Real name: Vinayakram Selvaganesh Discogs ID: 959762 Role: South Indian Carnatic percussionist — kanjira, mridangam, ghatam, udu; also konnakol (rhythmic vocal) Family: Son of Vikku Vinayakram; brother of V. Umashankar and V. Umamahesh; together they form The Vinayakrams

Biography

V. Selvaganesh (Vinayakram Selvaganesh) is a Chennai-born South Indian percussionist specialising in Carnatic instruments, primarily the kanjira (a small frame drum central to South Indian classical music) but also mridangam, ghatam, and udu. He is the son of ghatam master Vikku Vinayakram, one of the founding members of the original Shakti (2) with John McLaughlin in the 1970s.

Selvaganesh’s earliest documented recordings date to 1994, when he appeared on a traditional ensemble recording for JVC Japan (Percussion Music Of Madras) and a Tips India release (Tabla), both in a supporting kanjira role.

Remember Shakti and Verve Records (2000–2001)

By 2000 Selvaganesh had taken his father’s place in the revived Remember Shakti formation alongside John McLaughlin, Zakir Hussain, and mandolinist U. Srinivas. He plays kanjira, ghatam, and mridangam on both Remember Shakti albums released on Verve Records:

  • The Believer (2000) — his highest-profile appearance at the time
  • Saturday Night In Bombay (2001) — live document of the same band

Remember Shakti is catalogued on Discogs under Shakti (2) (using the name “Remember Shakti” as a name variation). The Shakti profile on Discogs explicitly identifies Selvaganesh as “son of T. H. ‘Vikku’ Vinayakram.”

Jonas Hellborg / Bardo Records (2000–2002)

Selvaganesh’s most prominent billing as a primary artist came through three records on Bardo Records, Jonas Hellborg’s label. These releases document a sustained Indo-classical fusion trio:

  • Good People In Times Of Evil (2000, Bardo Records) — trio with Jonas Hellborg (bass) and Shawn Lane (guitar); Selvaganesh plays kanjira, udu, and vocals; produced by Hellborg; recorded and mixed by Scud Noonan and Danny Kadar; Ustad Sultan Khan adds sarangi on one track
  • Icon - A Transcontinental Gathering (2002, Bardo Records) — quintet adding V. Umashankar (ghatam, konnakol) and V. Umamahesh (vocals); Selvaganesh plays kanjira and konnakol; credited as co-writer alongside Hellborg

Selvaganesh also appears on Hellborg’s Paris (2004, Bardo Records) in association with V. Umamahesh (vocals credit on that release).

Solo and Co-leader Work (2005–2007)

  • Soukha (2005, Naïve) — solo/leader credit; no additional credits listed in Discogs
  • The Way Of Beauty (2006, Universal Music Group) — primary artist credit, with Shankar Mahadevan and a large ensemble including Christian Pégand and Daniel Richard
  • The Gateway To Rhythm (2007, Abstract Logix / Mediastarz Monaco) — co-leader release with John McLaughlin, billed as “John McLaughlin And S. Ganesh Vinayakram” (a name variation used for this release)

Film Work

  • Kama Sutra: A Tale Of Love OST (1997, TVT Soundtrax) — kanjira
  • Life of Pi OST (2012, Sony Classical) — kanjira

Later Collaborations (2009–2023)

  • Land Of The Freaks (2009, Thunderstruck Productions) — frame drum / kanjira
  • Still Point: Turning World (2019, Whirlwind Recordings) — guest appearance for composer Joel Harrison (2); plays kanjira, udu, konnakol on tracks 1, 7, 8
  • This Moment (2023, Abstract Logix (2)) — kanjira, mridangam, ghatam, konnakol vocals, co-producer

Other Groups

Discogs lists Selvaganesh as a member of:

  • Shakti (2) (Remember Shakti)
  • The Vinayakrams — family percussion ensemble with V. Umashankar and V. Umamahesh
  • Deep Energy Orchestra
  • Art Metal — Scandinavian fusion project with Jonas Hellborg, Anders Johansson, Jens Johansson, and Mattias Ia Eklundh; release: Art Metal (Vyakhyan-Kar)

Family Connections

All three Vinayakram siblings carry the surname prefix “V.” for their father Vikku Vinayakram (born Thetakudi Harihara Vinayakram, 1942), the renowned ghatam player and original Shakti member. Together they perform and record as The Vinayakrams (Discogs ID 1101312).

V. Umashankar is described on Discogs as a ghatam specialist in South Indian classical Carnatic music. He and Selvaganesh both appear on the 1994 JVC Percussion Music Of Madras ensemble recording, suggesting early collaborative documentation. V. Umamahesh has sparser documentation — vocals on Icon and Paris (Bardo Records, 2004) are the primary credits.

Name Variations (Discogs)
