Alva Rogers

Vocalist, playwright, actress, composer. Born 1959, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 611527.

Overview

Alva Rogers is a New York City-based vocalist, playwright, composer, actress, and arts educator. In the late 1980s she was a founding-lineup vocalist with Band Of Susans, appearing on their debut EP Blessing And Curse (1987) and their first full-length album Hope Against Hope (1988), both produced by Robert Poss. Before that, she sang backing vocals on Poss’s solo debut Sometimes (1986, Trace Elements Records) — making her one of the earliest contributors to the Poss/Band Of Susans circle. She also narrated a track on flutist Marion Brandis’s The Absolute Truth (1986, Nisus Recording Co.) alongside pianist Myra Melford and violinist Leroy Jenkins, and contributed vocals to Gary Lucas & Gods and Monsters’ The Ordeal Of Civility (2011, 5nakefork). She is represented in Band Of Susans’ career retrospective Wired For Sound: 1986–1993 (Blast First / Mute / World Service) on tracks from the Blessing And Curse and Hope Against Hope period.

Beyond music, Rogers is best known for playing Eula Peazant in Julie Dash’s landmark 1991 film Daughters of the Dust, and for playing Doris Witherspoon in Spike Lee’s School Daze (1988). She was a founding member of Rodeo Caldonia (1985–1989), a Black women’s art collective in the Fort Greene neighborhood of Brooklyn whose members included Lorna Simpson, Chakaia Booker, and Sandye Wilson (Wikipedia). She received a 2026 Guggenheim Fellowship.

She has advanced degrees from NYU’s Tisch School of the Arts (MFA in musical theater writing, 1995), Brown University (MFA in literary arts, 1998), and Bard College (MA in teaching/history, 2013). She graduated from The High School of Music & Art in New York City with a concentration in vocal music.

Band Of Susans Founding Lineup

Rogers was part of Band Of Susans’ original formation. The founding lineup through Hope Against Hope was:

By Love Agenda (1989), Rogers and the three Susans (Lyall, Tallman) had left; the lineup shifted to Karen Haglof and Page Hamilton on guitars.
