Will Gregory

Discogs ID: 35204 Real name: William Owen Gregory Born: 1959, Bristol, UK Role: Saxophonist, Keyboardist, Composer, Arranger, Producer Active: 1985–present Associated Acts: Goldfrapp, The Will Gregory Moog Ensemble, Loggerheads, Rhythm Riot, The London Saxophonic

Biography

Early Session Work (1985–1999)

Will Gregory studied classical music at York University and emerged in the mid-1980s as a session saxophonist working across a wide range of UK pop and rock recordings. His earliest Discogs credit is a saxophone part on the Tears for Fears single I Believe (A Soulful Re-Recording) (Mercury, 1985, Netherlands) and the album Songs From The Big Chair (Mercury, 1985) — including a saxophone solo on the latter. He also appears on Scenes From The Big Chair, the associated VHS/home video release. These credits place him squarely in the premium-budget, major-label session world of mid-1980s British pop.

Through the late 1980s he worked across a diverse range of sessions:

  • Peter Gabriel sphere: Appeared on Gabriel’s OVO (Real World, 2000) as brass arranger, and on Scratch My Back (Real World, 2010) as arranger. The OVO credit and later Scratch My Back and New Blood credits (2011) make him a recurring Gabriel collaborator.
  • Louis Philippe — Yuri Gagarin (Él, 1989): Played baritone, alto, and sopranino saxophone. This was a significant Él Records production, and Gregory’s appearance in that orbit connects him to the South London chamber-pop scene around Mike Alway’s label.
  • Spiritualized — Lazer Guided Melodies (Dedicated, 1992): Credited on baritone, tenor, alto, and soprano saxophone. This is one of the period’s defining psychedelic rock albums; Gregory’s multi-register saxophone work was a textural element across the whole record.
  • The Cure — Wild Mood Swings (Fiction/Elektra, 1996): Arranged the brass section.
  • Portishead — Roseland NYC Live (Go! Beat/Polydor, 1998): Baritone saxophone. Also appears on a Russia-released version Roseland New York (2002) credited for horns.
  • John Harle — The Ship: Music From The BBC Television Series (BBC Records, 2002): Keyboards (Oberheim), piano, programmed by, and synthesizers (ARP, Moogamin, VCS3). This credit is notable because it already shows Gregory functioning as a synthesizer specialist — including specifically Moog instruments — before the Moog Ensemble was formalized.
  • Olga Bell / Devi (Naked Voice Music, 1996): Engineer credit — an unusual role, showing his studio versatility.
  • Sax Pax For A Sax (Kopf, 1994, Germany): Mixed By and Saxophone — demonstrating mixing capability alongside performance.
  • He also appears on releases by Tears for Fears’ Breakaway (Parlophone, 1993), the Factory Classical release Crossing The Border (1992), and Getting The Holy Ghost Across (Portrait, 1986).

In 1998 he produced Towards The Light (Eclectic Music Productions, UK).

Co-founding Goldfrapp (1999–2017)

Gregory co-founded Goldfrapp with vocalist Alison Goldfrapp in 1999. Within the duo he has occupied the production, arrangement, and composition role: the instrumental infrastructure behind Alison Goldfrapp’s vocal presence. On Discogs his credits shift decisively from session sax/brass to songwriter, producer, and synthesizer player.

Felt Mountain (2000) is the most fully documented production credit: Gregory is listed as co-writer, performer, and producer alongside Alison Goldfrapp, and specifically credited for keyboards and for the string and brass arrangements — work that defines the album’s lush orchestral character. On Tales Of Us (2013) he took on string arrangements and played all other instruments alongside Goldfrapp, indicating a more stripped-back but hands-on production role.

On Supernature (2005), production was credited to Goldfrapp as a unit rather than individuals, with Nick Batt contributing additional programming and synthesizer. The same applies to Seventh Tree (2008), where Flood co-produced several tracks and Adrian Utley appeared on bass and guitar (track 9). Silver Eye (2017) brought in The Haxan Cloak as additional co-producer on several tracks.

The duo released on Mute throughout their existence (1999–2017 active period; compilations and reissues extended to 2025). Gregory also appeared on related Goldfrapp singles in roles including Synthesizer (Synths): “Ooh La La” (2005), “Ride A White Horse” (2005), “Fly Me Away” (2006), “Number 1” (2005), and others. He mixed Wonderful Electric (Live In London) (Mute, 2004) and mixed live recordings from the 2005 Brixton show.

Goldfrapp was among the more critically and commercially successful British acts of the 2000s, crossing art-pop, glam electronica, and pastoral folk across their album sequence.

BBC Work and Television Scoring

Gregory’s 2002 credit on the John Harle-led score The Ship: Music From The BBC Television Series (BBC Records, 2002) is a significant synthesizer and keyboard appearance outside of Goldfrapp. He played Oberheim, piano, ARP, VCS3, and notably a synthesizer listed as “Moogamin” — a specialized keyboard instrument — alongside Harle’s ensemble which included Adrian Utley, Andy Sheppard, Guy Barker, and Sarah Jane Morris.

The ensemble has two primary releases:

  • Heat Ray (2024, Mute) — co-produced by Gregory and Utley, featuring the BBC National Orchestra of Wales; orchestrated by Ian Gardiner and Peter Riley (5); mastered by Stefan Betke (Pole); Gregory played Minimoog and Mellotron and co-wrote the material; Daniel Miller (Mute founder) contributed Eurorack synthesizer on one track.

Arcadia Film Score (2018)

Gregory and Adrian Utley co-composed the score for Arcadia (2018), a documentary film directed by Paul Wright (not Nick Abrahams — the Discogs record credits only the two musicians). The score was released on Invada Records (the label founded by Geoff Barrow of Portishead). Artwork was by Stanley Donwood (longtime Radiohead collaborator).

Both Gregory and Utley are credited with “Original Music By” as co-primary artists on the release. This is Gregory’s only Discogs release as a primary artist outside of Goldfrapp, making it a rare standalone credit.

Peter Gabriel Collaborations

Gregory appears on three Peter Gabriel releases as arranger:

  • OVO (Real World, 2000): Arranged By [Brass]
  • Scratch My Back (Real World, 2010): Arranged By
  • New Blood – Live In London (Eagle Vision/Real World, 2011): Arranged By
  • Live Blood (Real World/Virgin, 2022): Arranged By
  • Play: The Videos (Real World, 2004): Arranged By [Brass]

This is a substantial recurring relationship with the Real World / Peter Gabriel operation spanning over two decades.

Steve Martland Tribute (2015)

Gregory appears as a musician on the Martland tribute compilation (NMC, 2015), a tribute to the British composer Steve Martland. This is consistent with his classical music training at York University and positions him within the contemporary classical/new music world alongside his pop career.
