Johnny Lee Schell

American guitarist, vocalist, songwriter, producer, and recording engineer. From Farwell, Texas. Discogs artist ID: 408456. Active on Discogs from 1974 to 2025. Owner and operator of Ultratone Studios in Los Angeles.

One of the busiest session guitarists in the American roots-rock, blues-rock, and Americana orbit from the late 1970s onward. Best known as a core member of The Bump Band (Ian McLagan’s backing group) and as a session guitarist on major records by Bonnie Raitt, Lucinda Williams, John Fogerty, Lyle Lovett, Taj Mahal, Eric Burdon, and Little Feat, among many others. Also carries producer and engineer credits extending into the 2010s–2020s.

Biography

Early career and Baby (7) (mid-1970s)

Schell’s earliest Discogs credit is the 1974 album Baby (Lone Starr, LP-3714), on which he plays acoustic guitar, organ, harmonica, lead guitar, and sings — a multi-instrumentalist from the start. He was a member of the band Baby (7) (Discogs ID: 1246900), which also included John Mark Camp (guitar, vocals), Stephen G. Crane (bass, vocals), and Woodie Putman (drums). The album was produced by Norman Petty at his Clovis, NM studios. Schell wrote six of the ten tracks. The album Where Did All The Money Go? (Chelsea Records, CHL 517, 1976) followed under producer Wes Farrell, with Schell credited for lead, rhythm, and acoustic guitar, organ, and vocals, and with co-writing credits on six of nine tracks — including the title track. Session horn player Tom Scott joined for the second album. See Baby (7) for full details.

Ian McLagan — Troublemaker (1979)

By 1979 Schell was in the orbit of Ian McLagan’s solo work. McLagan’s debut solo LP Troublemaker (Mercury, 1979) brought together an extraordinary circle: Keith Richards, Ron Wood, Ringo Starr, Jim Keltner, Bobby Keys, and Stanley Clarke. Schell appears as guitarist and vocalist, and shares co-writing credits on two tracks (“B1” and “B3,” the latter shared with Ron Wood). The album was recorded in Los Angeles and produced by Geoff Workman.

The Bump Band and Bump In The Night (1981)

McLagan’s second album, Bump In The Night (Mercury, 1981), introduced The Bump Band as a named unit. Schell was a founding member of the Bump Band, credited as “Band [Bump Band], Lead Guitar, Vocals.” Other Bump Band members on this record were Ricky Fataar (drums, percussion, vocals, piano, bass) and Ray Ohara (bass). Guests included Ron Wood (lead guitar, duelling with Schell on “A1”), Bobby Keys (horns), and Renée Geyer (vocals). The album was produced by Rob Fraboni.

Los Angeles session work (1981–1993)

Through the 1980s and into the 1990s Schell maintained a busy session schedule, mostly from a Los Angeles base:

  • Green Light (Bonnie Raitt, Warner Bros., 1982) — vocals and guitar
  • Renee Geyer (Portrait, 1982) — guitar, piano, backing vocals
  • Nine Lives (Bonnie Raitt, Warner Bros., 1986) — percussion
  • Melissa Etheridge (Island Records, 1988) — guitar (additional overdubs); executive producer was Rob Fraboni
  • Seven Year Itch (Lynsey De Paul, Island Records, 1988) — lead guitar, rhythm guitar
  • John Prine Live (Oh Boy Records, 1988) — electric bass, harmony vocals
  • Rubbin’ Me The Right Way (Doris Allen, EMI USA, 1989) — bass and guitar

Luck Of The Draw and the Bonnie Raitt peak (1991)

Schell appears on Bonnie Raitt’s career-defining album Luck Of The Draw (Capitol Records, 1991), playing electric guitar on track 5. The album was produced by Don Was and also featured Ian McLagan (Hammond B3, track 5), Benmont Tench, Richard Thompson, John Hiatt, Bruce Hornsby, Jeff Porcaro, and Ricky Fataar. Schell’s track-5 placement puts him directly alongside McLagan’s Hammond part on the same song — a Bump Band-adjacent pairing in a major-label context.

Austin Americana and roots-rock (1992–1999)

The early-to-mid 1990s produced a dense stretch of Americana and blues-rock session credits:

  • Joshua Judges Ruth (Lyle Lovett, MCA/Curb, 1992) — electric guitar (track A5)
  • Racine (Rita Chao, Impact American, 1992) — guitar, backing vocals
  • Feels Like Rain (Buddy Guy, Silvertone, 1993) — guitar
  • Dancing The Blues (Etta James, Private Music, 1993) — guitar (Leslie), backing vocals
  • Ain’t Enough Comin’ In (Bobby Blue Bland, This Way Up, 1994) — guitar
  • Meet Me At Midnite (B.B. King, Black Top Records, 1994) — guitar
  • Phantom Blues (Taj Mahal, Private Music, 1996) — guitar (multiple tracks), rhythm guitar, backing vocals (track 6), guitar [Vib] (track 10); album also features Eric Clapton and Bonnie Raitt
  • Señor Blues (Taj Mahal, Private Music/BMG, 1997) — guitar
  • Car Wheels On A Gravel Road (Lucinda Williams, Mercury, 1998) — electric guitar on tracks 4, 5, 7, 11, 12; co-produced by Roy Bittan and The Twangtrust; also featured Buddy Miller, Steve Earle, Charlie Sexton
  • Premonition (John Fogerty, Reprise Records, 1998) — guitar and backing vocals (track 11); live album

Blues Plate Special (2002) — sole primary-credit solo release

The sole release on Discogs where Schell is the primary artist: Blues Plate Special (5 Alarm Music, 2002), a library/sync music album. He is credited as composer, writer, and performer. This appears to be catalogue music for licensing rather than a commercial artist album.

The Phantom Blues Band (1996–onward)

Schell is a member of The Phantom Blues Band (Discogs ID: 365807), the house band centred around Tony Braunagel and Joe Sublett that backed Taj Mahal on Phantom Blues (1996) and Señor Blues (1997) and issued their own records. This circle overlaps substantially with the broader Los Angeles/Austin session world he inhabited.

Martin Scorsese’s The Blues and later Americana (2003–2012)

Schell appeared on the Martin Scorsese Presents The Blues compilation (Columbia/Legacy, 2003). Other credits in this period:

  • Have Love Will Travel (2003) — guitar and editing
  • Rooster Rag (Little Feat, Rounder Records, 2012) — vocals, guitar, and engineer; a significant multi-role credit on a major band’s record
  • Soul Shot (Ruthie Foster, Alligator Records, 2012) — guitar
  • Slipstream (Bonnie Raitt, Redwing Records, 2012) — special guest

The Rooster Rag credit is notable: for Bill Payne and Paul Barrere’s Little Feat he contributed guitar, vocals, and engineering from his Ultratone Studios, indicating his studio role was by now fully integrated with his performance work.

Engineering and production work (2004–2025)

From the mid-2000s onward, Schell increasingly appears in engineer and recording-engineer roles alongside (or instead of) guitar credits. Key engineering/production credits:

  • My Secret Life (Jann Arden, SPV, 2004) — engineer
  • Soul Of A Man (Eric Burdon, SPV, 2006) — guitar and backing vocals (session, not engineer)
  • High Wide And Handsome: The Charlie Poole Project (Roseanne Cash, Proper Records, 2009) — recording engineer (Ultratone)
  • 10 Songs For The New Depression (Janis Ian, Proper Records, 2010) — recorded by
  • Older Than My Old Man Now (Mark Knopfler, Diverse Records, 2012) — engineer
  • Badlands (Telarc, 2013) — recorded by
  • The Beautiful Lowdown (Coco Montoya, Alligator, 2016) — engineered and guitar
  • Hard Truth (Tinsley Ellis, Alligator, 2017) — recorded by; rhythm and slide guitar
  • Cry No More (Concord Records, 2018) — guitar and recorded by
  • Rough Cut (Alligator, 2018) — mixed by
  • Coming In Hot (Laura Chavez, Alligator, 2019) — engineered and rhythm guitar
  • LA Sessions (Bullseye Records, 2019) — engineer
  • Damage Control (Alligator, 2021) — engineered by

Ultratone Studios is the consistent venue for the recording-engineer credits; Alligator Records is the label that recurs most heavily in the post-2015 Ultratone work, suggesting a preferred working relationship with that Chicago blues imprint.

2019–2025

Continued session and engineering credits through the early 2020s: Memphis Moonlight (Vizztone Label Group, 2021, guitar/bass/keyboards/backing vocals), The Love You Bleed (Forty Below Records, 2024, editing and percussion engineering), and guitar on the Live At Electric Ladyland release (BMG, 2025).
