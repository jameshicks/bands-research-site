James Blood Ulmer

American guitarist, vocalist, and composer. Discogs artist ID: 149965. Born James Ulmer, February 8, 1940 (some sources say February 2, 1942), St. Matthews, South Carolina, USA. Also recorded under the name Damu Mustafa Abdul Musawwir.

Biography

Early Career (pre-1979)

Ulmer arrived in New York in the late 1960s and began working across the boundary between jazz and soul-funk. His earliest documented session credit on Discogs is an uncredited guitar appearance on a 1968 King Records release. In 1969 he appeared on an Accent On The Blues session for Blue Note. His 1973 credits situate him in the orbit of the loft jazz generation: guitar on Larry Young’s Lawrence Of Newark (Perception Records), on the Multiple anthology (Milestone), and on the Rashied Ali Quintet LP (Survival Records) — placing him alongside core avant-jazz figures from early in his New York career.

He is listed as a member of Juma Sultan’s Aboriginal Music Society and The Frank Wright Quartet during this period.

The Ornette Coleman Connection (1973–1979)

Ulmer’s defining early relationship was with Ornette Coleman, whose harmolodic theory — a compositional and improvisational system in which melody, harmony, and rhythm are treated as equal voices — became the framework for Ulmer’s entire mature output. An undated Ornette Coleman self-released recording (Lonely Woman Trio ‘66 – Quartet ‘74) credits Ulmer on guitar for the Quartet ‘74 portion, alongside Sirone (bass), Billy Higgins (drums), and Coleman on alto, trumpet, and violin — placing Ulmer inside Coleman’s working unit in 1974.

The most important outcome of the Coleman association was the debut album Tales Of Captain Black (1979, Artists House), credited to “James Blood” — a recording produced by Coleman and John Snyder, with Coleman on alto saxophone, Denardo Coleman on drums, and Jamaaladeen Tacuma on electric bass. All compositions are credited to Ulmer. Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder.

Harmolodic Guitar — The Rough Trade / CBS Period (1980–1984)

Are You Glad To Be In America? (Rough Trade, 1980) established the Music Revelation Ensemble lineup — Amin Ali (electric bass), Calvin Weston and Ronald Shannon Jackson (drums), David Murray (tenor), Oliver Lake (alto), and Olu Dara (trumpet) — and placed Ulmer at the centre of the New York loft jazz/no-wave intersection. Co-produced by Mayo Thompson (Rough Trade UK) and Roger Trilling; Ulmer produced, arranged, mixed, and wrote all songs.

The CBS/UK deal (brokered through the US arm as Columbia) produced a run of major-label albums with more accessible production values:

  • Free Lancing (CBS, 1981) — core rhythm section (Ali, Weston), with Murray, Lake, and Olu Dara on horns; Ronald “Head” Drayton on rhythm guitar on several tracks.
  • Black Rock (CBS, 1982) — the rhythm section again, with Cornell Rochester on second drums; Drayton back on rhythm guitar. Notable for Ulmer recording some tracks as “Damu Mustafa Abdul Musawwir,” his Muslim name.
  • Odyssey (CBS, 1983) — slimmed to guitar, bass, drums (Warren Benbow), and Charles Burnham on violin. Co-produced by PatTricia Samuel and Joe McEwen for CBS. The most stripped-back and blues-adjacent of the CBS records.
  • Eye Level (Rough Trade, 1984) / Part Time (Rough Trade, 1984) — two releases back on Rough Trade in the same year; Ulmer credited on words and music.

Music Revelation Ensemble and Moers Music (1980–1994)

Music Revelation Ensemble released No Wave on Moers Music in 1980 — the earliest MRE recording on Discogs — with Murray, Jackson, and Ali. Produced by Burkhard Hennen. Ulmer composed everything. The MRE’s Moers Music run extended to at least four albums (1980–1982); the Discogs entry for the label credits at least the No Wave LP. A 1981 solo release on Moers Music is also documented (details thin in the DB).

The MRE shifted to DIW Records in Japan from 1988 onward, with four DIW releases:

  • Music Revelation Ensemble (DIW, 1988) — Murray, Jackson, Tacuma; produced by Kazunori Sugiyama.
  • Elec.Jazz (DIW, 1990) — Murray, Cornell Rochester (replacing Jackson), Ali; co-produced by Ulmer and Sugiyama.
  • After Dark (DIW, 1992) — Murray, Rochester, Ali.
  • In The Name Of… (DIW, 1994) — Murray, Rochester, Ali; guest spots from Arthur Blythe (alto), Hamiet Bluiett (bari sax), and Sam Rivers (flute, soprano, tenor).

Phalanx (4) (1986–1988, reunion 2013)

Phalanx (4) was Ulmer’s co-led project with tenor saxophonist George Adams, documented on three studio albums across Moers Music and DIW, plus a 2013 live reunion. Two lineups: the funk-oriented Ali/Weston rhythm section (Moers debut) and the open-territory Sirone / Rashied Ali combination (DIW). See the Phalanx (4) page for full documentation.

Blue Note and the Mid-Career (1986–1988)

Live At The Caravan Of Dreams (Caravan Of Dreams Productions, 1986) — with Ali and Warren Benbow, plus Charles Burnham on violin. Recorded live at the Fort Worth jazz venue.

America – Do You Remember The Love? (Blue Note, 1987) — produced with Bill Laswell, who plays bass (4-, 6-, and 8-string); Ronald Shannon Jackson on drums; Nicky Skopelitis on 12-string guitar and banjo. The most commercially accessible production Ulmer had released, and his only Blue Note album.

Dance Romance (In+Out Records, 1988) — Germany; Ulmer on guitar, vocals; produced and mixed by Ulmer.

Blues Pivot (1990–2003)

From the early 1990s Ulmer increasingly foregrounded his blues and Southern roots alongside the harmolodic approach. Key releases:

  • Revealing (In+Out Records, 1990) — Germany.
  • Blues Preacher (DIW, 1993) — with Ronald “Head” Drayton on guitar, Aubrey Dayle on drums, Mark Peterson on bass.
  • The James Blood Ulmer Blues Experience released Blues Allnight (In+Out Records, 1990) — Ali, Weston, Drayton; produced by Frank Kleinschmidt and Ulmer.
  • Plays The Music Of Ornette Coleman: Music Speaks Louder Than Words (DIW, 1996) — explicitly tribute to the harmolodic source; Rashied Ali on drums for some tracks, Amin Ali on bass, Aubrey Dayle on drums for others; Michael Mustafa Ulmer on keyboards.
  • Harmolodic Guitar With Strings (Koch Jazz, 1997) — Ulmer with strings (Quartette Indigo: Gayle Dixon, John Blake, Ron Lawrence, Akua Dixon); produced by Ulmer; engineered and mixed by Joe Ferla.
  • Forbidden Blues (DIW, 1998) — Japan.
  • Blue Blood (Innerhythmic, 2001) — US.
  • The Sun Sessions (Label M / PDSE Records, 2001) — US.
  • Guitar Music (Dernière Bande, 2003) — France.
  • No Escape From The Blues: The Electric Lady Sessions (Hyena Records, 2003) — US.

Odyssey The Band Reunion (1998)

Odyssey The Band released Reunion (Knitting Factory Records, 1998), bringing back the Odyssey lineup with Charles Burnham on electric violin and Warren Benbow on drums. Ulmer produced.

Later Period (2005–2017)

  • Birthright (Hyena Records, 2005) — produced by Vernon Reid.
  • Out Of Nowhere (Live At The Blue Note) (Half Note Records, 2005).
  • Bad Blood In The City: The Piety Street Sessions (Hyena Records, 2007) — recorded in New Orleans with Vernon Reid on guitar, Charles Burnham on electric fiddle, Leon Gruenbaum on keys, Aubrey Dayle on drums, Mark Peterson on bass. Reid also produced.
  • In And Out (In+Out Records, 2009) — Germany; with Aubrey Dayle and Mark Peterson; produced by Frank Kleinschmidt and Ulmer.
  • Baby Talk (Live At Molde International Jazz Festival 2015) (The Thing Records, 2017) — European release.

Open Questions

  • Birth date discrepancy: Discogs profile states February 8, 1940; the brief given for this research states February 2, 1942. The Wikipedia URL linked from Discogs likely has the canonical date but cannot be confirmed without web access.
  • The 1981 solo release on Moers Music: catalog number and title not confirmed in DB (only documented via a Dutch flexi/sampler release, cat no momu 01072 area, and a secondary credit on Street Priest extracts / Untitled, Netherlands, Moers Music + Flying Disc Company Delft).
  • Moers Music MRE releases beyond No Wave (1980): the DB shows MRE had 4 Moers releases 1980–82, but only No Wave is individually credited in Discogs — full catalog numbers for the other three need confirmation.
  • Early group membership dates for The Frank Wright Quartet, Juma Sultan’s Aboriginal Music Society, and the 52nd Street Blues Project are not confirmed in DB.
