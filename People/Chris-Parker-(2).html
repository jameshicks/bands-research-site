Chris Parker (2)

Christopher “Toph” Parker — session drummer and percussionist, born Chicago, Illinois. Discogs artist ID: 311107. Credited on sleeves as “Christopher Parker” or “Christopher A. Parker.” Leader of The Chris Parker Trio. Father was a jazz drummer; Parker started drumming at age three, first paid gig at eleven. Made his recording debut with country-rock group Holy Moses (4) while in college. Member of the Saturday Night Live band 1986–1992.

Overview

Parker is one of the most prolific New York session drummers of the 1970s–1990s. His career falls into three overlapping phases. In the early 1970s he was embedded in the Bearsville/Woodstock circle, serving as the core drummer for Paul Butterfield’s Better Days alongside Bill Rich on bass, and appearing on Geoff Muldaur’s first solo album. From the mid-1970s onward he became a top-call New York funk and R&B studio man, most visibly as a founding member of Stuff (2) — the Warner Bros. session collective built around Steve Gadd, Richard Tee, Cornell Dupree, Eric Gale, and Gordon Edwards. Across those same years he appeared on records by Esther Phillips, Joe Beck, the Brecker Brothers, Barry Manilow, Sinead O’Connor, Donald Fagen, and dozens more. He also served as SNL house-band drummer and later as a producer (credited on the 1994 Stuff tribute album Made In America).

Stuff (2) — Membership

Parker was a founding member of Stuff (2), the New York session collective that recorded for Warner Bros. from 1976. The core lineup:

  • Steve Gadd — drums (Parker shared the kit role)
  • Richard Tee — piano, organ
  • Cornell Dupree — guitar
  • Eric Gale — guitar
  • Gordon Edwards — bass
  • Chris Parker (2) — drums, percussion

Other members who appeared: Alex Foster (sax), Mike Clark (2), James Alan Smith, Mick Gaffney (2).

Bearsville / Woodstock Circle Connections

Parker entered the New York session world through the Bearsville orbit. On the two Paul Butterfield’s Better Days records (1973) and Paul Butterfield’s 1975 solo LP Put It In Your Ear, he shared stages and studios with:

  • Geoff Muldaur — co-produced Better Days; Parker later appeared on Muldaur’s Is Having A Wonderful Time (1975)
  • Maria Muldaur — vocals on Better Days and It All Comes Back
  • Amos Garrett — guitar throughout
  • Ronnie Barron — keyboards, Better Days
  • Bill Rich — bass, Better Days (Parker’s rhythm-section partner)
  • Levon Helm — co-drum credit on Put It In Your Ear
  • Garth Hudson — keyboards/reeds, Put It In Your Ear
  • Nick Jameson — engineer/producer, both Better Days albums
  • Bobby Charles — vocals, It All Comes Back

No Discogs credits link Parker directly to Todd Rundgren or other Bearsville core acts.
