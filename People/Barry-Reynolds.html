Barry Reynolds

British guitarist, songwriter, composer, and producer. Discogs artist ID: 139229.

Overview

Barry Reynolds is a British guitarist and songwriter whose career spans three distinct phases: early-1970s British rock with Pacific Drift and solo releases; a decade-long creative partnership with Grace Jones as the core guitarist and principal co-writer of her Compass Point trilogy (1980–82); and an ongoing role as producer and musical director for Marianne Faithfull stretching from 1979 through the 2000s. He is a founding member of the Compass Point All Stars, the Nassau-based studio collective assembled by Chris Blackwell at Compass Point Studios. Reynolds co-wrote the majority of the Grace Jones Compass Point material, appeared on Joe Cocker’s Sheffield Steel (1982), co-produced Marianne Faithfull’s A Child’s Adventure (1983), played on Cristina’s Sleep It Off (Mercury, 1984), and produced Baaba Maal’s Television album (2009). His name appears variously credited as “Barry (White) Reynolds” on Island sleeves.

Early Career: Pacific Drift and Solo Work (1970–1981)

Reynolds first appears on record as lead vocalist, lead guitarist, and songwriter for Pacific Drift, whose debut LP Feelin’ Free was released on Deram (SDN 13) in 1970 — a progressive rock album on which he co-wrote the majority of tracks. Under his own name he released singles in Italy and Germany in the early 1970s (Wild World / Share The Land on Clan Celentano, 1971; Outsider’s Point Of View on EMI Electrola/RAK, 1974) and a single on Epic in 1975. In 1982 he released an Island Records solo LP, I Scare Myself, featuring the entire Compass Point rhythm section — Sly Dunbar (drums), Robbie Shakespeare (bass), Mikey Chung (guitar), Wally Badarou (keyboards), and Uziah “Sticky” Thompson (percussion) — with Reynolds on guitar and vocals.

Marianne Faithfull: Broken English (1979)

Reynolds played on Marianne Faithfull’s landmark comeback album Broken English (Island Records, 1979), co-writing the title track (one of the album’s key songs) alongside Faithfull. The album was produced by Mark Miller Mundy. Reynolds is credited as “Barry Reynalds” (a sleeve name variation he carried across multiple Island releases).

Compass Point All Stars and Grace Jones Trilogy (1980–1982)

Reynolds joined the Compass Point All Stars — the studio collective assembled by Chris Blackwell and Alex Sadkin at Compass Point Studios, Nassau — as the group’s primary guitarist and chief songwriter for the Grace Jones sessions. He co-wrote and played guitar on all three albums of the Compass Point trilogy:

Warm Leatherette (1980, Island Records)

Personnel: Grace Jones (vocals), Barry Reynolds & Mikey Chung (guitars), Wally Badarou (keyboards), Sly Dunbar (drums), Robbie Shakespeare (bass), Uziah “Sticky” Thompson (percussion). Produced by Chris Blackwell and Alex Sadkin.

Nightclubbing (1981, Island Records)

Same core Compass Point lineup plus Tyrone Downie (keyboards) and Jack Emblow (accordion). Produced by Blackwell and Sadkin.

Living My Life (1982, Island Records)

Core lineup again; Reynolds co-wrote the entire B-side (B1–B4). Additional engineering by Steven Stanley. Mastered by Ted Jensen.

Reynolds also had a writing credit on Jones’s 1981 single “I’ve Seen That Face Before (Liber Tango).”

Joe Cocker: Sheffield Steel (1982, Island Records)

Reynolds played guitar alongside Mikey Chung on this Sly Dunbar / Robbie Shakespeare–produced album, engineered by Alex Sadkin. Also featuring Wally Badarou on keyboards. The full Compass Point rhythm section was deployed on a non-Grace Jones Island album.

Marianne Faithfull: Dangerous Acquaintances (1981) and A Child’s Adventure (1983)

Reynolds returned as musician and co-writer on Faithfull’s Dangerous Acquaintances (Island, 1981), credited with six writing co-credits. He then co-produced her follow-up A Child’s Adventure (Island, 1983) alongside Wally Badarou, with Harvey Goldberg also receiving a producer/engineer credit.

Cristina: Sleep It Off (1984, Mercury)

Reynolds played guitar on Cristina’s second and final studio album, produced by Don Was, with Michael Zilkha as executive producer. Cristina wrote all lyrics; Reynolds co-wrote music on “What’s A Girl To Do” (B2) and “The Lie Of Love” (B3). Other musicians included Doug Fieger (bass, lead guitar on B4), James Chance (saxophone on B4), David Was (saxophone on B4), Howard Wyeth (drums), and Andy Hernandez (marimba). Mastered by Bob Ludwig.

Black Uhuru: Red (1981, Island Records)

Reynolds received a lead guitar credit on Black Uhuru’s reggae landmark Red, produced by Sly Dunbar and Robbie Shakespeare, with engineering by Steven Stanley.

Marianne Faithfull: Blazing Away (1990, Island Records)

Reynolds served as associate producer and guitarist (on all main tracks) for Faithfull’s live album Blazing Away, produced by Hal Willner. The band also included Marc Ribot (guitar), Garth Hudson (keyboards), Mac Rebennack (piano), Lew Soloff (trumpet), Fernando Saunders (bass), and Dougie Bowne (drums).

Hubert Félix Thiéfaine: Chroniques Bluesymentales (1990, Justine)

Reynolds produced and played guitar, keyboards, and harmonica on this French rock album. Mikey Chung and Fernando Saunders also appeared.

Marianne Faithfull: Continued Collaboration (2000s)

Reynolds continued playing in Faithfull’s live band through the 2000s, appearing on Before The Poison (2008, Naïve) and live release Live In Hollywood (2005, Eagle Vision) as guitarist and backing vocalist, and is credited as a member of “Marianne’s Band.”

Baaba Maal: Television (2009, Because Music/Palm)

Reynolds co-produced (alongside Baaba Maal and John Leckie) and played guitar and co-wrote five of the eight tracks on Television, a collaboration between Senegalese singer Baaba Maal and Brazilian Girls. Also co-written with Sabina Sciubba and Didi Gutman of Brazilian Girls.
