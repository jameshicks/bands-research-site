Biography

Early career and The Village Recorder (1973–1975)

California-born, Rob Fraboni came up as a studio engineer in Los Angeles in the early 1970s. His earliest Discogs-documented credits are from 1973, associated with The Village Recorder in West Los Angeles — a studio he is explicitly credited at on several releases of that period. His first notable work was engineering the The Beach Boys’ Holland (1973, Brother/Reprise), where he served as Director of Engineering on key tracks. That same year he did engineering assistance on jazz releases for ABC/Impulse! — including an Ornette Coleman retrospective and assisting on The Best Of B.B. King.

The pivotal early relationship was with The Band, whose studio work at The Village Recorder he engineered from 1973 onwards. He recorded and mixed Northern Lights-Southern Cross (1975, Capitol) — one of The Band’s most critically regarded later records — and continued through their farewell period.

His most consequential early credit came on two 1974 Bob Dylan releases: he engineered Planet Waves (Asylum Records), Dylan’s first studio album since New Morning (1970) and the recording that formally reunited Dylan with The Band; and he served as both recording and mixing engineer on Before The Flood (Asylum), the live double album from their joint 1974 tour. Both records feature The Band’s full lineup — Robbie Robertson, Rick Danko, Richard Manuel, Garth Hudson, Levon Helm — in the studio and on stage. He also mixed The Basement Tapes (CBS/Columbia, 1975), the long-delayed Dylan/Band recordings from 1967.

The Shangri-La Studio and The Band’s farewell (1975–1978)

Through the mid-1970s Fraboni became closely identified with The Band and their home studio, Shangri-La, in Malibu. He engineered Northern Lights-Southern Cross (1975) and Islands (Capitol, 1977) — The Band’s final original-material album before their retirement. He served as consulting engineer on The Best Of The Band (Capitol, 1976).

The Band’s formal farewell, The Last Waltz (Warner Bros., 1978), is his most widely-known credit. He co-produced the triple live album alongside Robbie Robertson and John Simon, the Band’s long-time collaborator. The concert film was directed by Martin Scorsese; the album co-production was Fraboni’s most prominent billing to that point.

Eric Clapton and the Shangri-La circle (1976)

Fraboni produced Eric Clapton’s No Reason To Cry (RSO, 1976), recorded at Shangri-La with an extraordinary guest roster: Bob Dylan, Robbie Robertson, Rick Danko, Richard Manuel, Ron Wood, and Georgie Fame. Carl Radle co-produced. The album represented the apex of the loose Shangri-La/Malibu rock community of which Fraboni was a central figure.

Solo production career: late 1970s–1980s

Moving into full production work, Fraboni produced a string of releases for artists connected to the Clapton/Stones orbit and beyond:

  • Rick Danko — Rick Danko (Arista, 1977), Fraboni co-produced with Danko and contributed horn arrangements and background vocals.
  • Blondie Chaplin — Blondie Chaplin (Asylum, 1977); Fraboni produced and mixed.
  • Jennifer Warnes — engineering on her self-titled 1976 Arista debut; later served as producer on her Shot Through The Heart (Arista, 1979), Best Of Jennifer Warnes (1982), and related singles.
  • Joe Cocker — produced tracks appearing on A&M compilations.
  • Ian McLagan — produced Bump In The Night (Mercury, 1981), with guests Ron Wood, Renée Geyer, and Bobby Keys, and The Bump Band as McLagan’s backing group. Engineer was Tim Kramer; studio credited was Shangri-La.
  • Bonnie Raitt — produced Green Light (Warner Bros., 1982); The Bump Band served as Raitt’s backing band on the record.
  • Milton Nascimento — produced Milton (A&M, 1977), a landmark in his introduction of Brazilian MPB to North American audiences.
  • Wayne Shorter — engineering on Native Dancer (CBS, 1975), featuring Nascimento, Herbie Hancock, and Airto Moreira.

He also produced the Australian singer-songwriter Renée Geyer through a series of Mushroom Records releases (1981–1983), the country artist Jennifer Warnes, the vocal group Joe Cocker (A&M anthology), and the supergroup KGB (MCA, 1976). The Joe Cocker production connection yielded I Can Stand A Little Rain (A&M, 1974) and associated singles; and Stingray (A&M, 1976) for the Cocker-adjacent act.

Island Records and the 1980s–1990s

From the mid-1980s Fraboni moved into a long association with Island Records, serving largely as executive producer and in supervisory roles. Key credits include:

  • Executive Producer on Melissa Etheridge’s self-titled debut (Island, 1988), alongside label head Chris Blackwell.
  • Executive Producer on Trouble (4th & Broadway, 1987) and related Trouble Funk releases.
  • Producer on Trouble Funk’s Still Smokin’ (4th & Broadway, 1985) — a Washington D.C. go-go release on the Island subsidiary.
  • Producer on Good To Go (Island, 1986) — the soundtrack to the go-go film, which he also compiled.
  • Various Island Records releases for Seven Year Itch, Buckwheat Zydeco (Taking It Home, 1988; Best Of, 2006), and others.

Bob Marley remaster project (1990–1993)

One of Fraboni’s most commercially significant bodies of work in the early 1990s was digitally remastering the entire Island/Tuff Gong Bob Marley & The Wailers back catalogue. He is credited as “Remastered By” on the 1990 CD reissues of Catch A Fire, Burnin’, Natty Dread, Live!, Rastaman Vibration, Exodus, Kaya, Survival, Uprising, Babylon By Bus, Rebel Music, and the Legend compilation — releases that collectively became the definitive CD-era Marley catalogue, selling tens of millions of copies through the 1990s. He also remastered Confrontation (Island, 1983) and continued this role through subsequent Tuff Gong reissue campaigns.

Keith Richards and Wingless Angels (1996–2010)

A late-career highlight was his collaboration with Keith Richards on Wingless Angels (Island Jamaica / Mindless Records, 1996), a field recording project documenting Rastafarian Burru drumming from Richards’ estate in Jamaica. Fraboni produced, recorded, mixed, and edited the album; Richards served as executive producer and added guitar, bass, keyboards, and vocals. A second volume appeared on Wingless Angels Volumes I & II (Mindless Records, 2010).

Rolling Stones (1997)

Fraboni recorded and mixed tracks on the Rolling Stones’ Bridges To Babylon (Virgin/Rolling Stones Records, 1997), co-produced by Don Was and The Glimmer Twins.

Later work and mastering (2000s–2025)

From the mid-2000s onwards, Fraboni appears increasingly in mastering credits across a range of artists — including Hubert Sumlin (About Them Shoes, Artemis/Tone-Cool, 2005, produced by him), North Hills (ATO Records, 2009), and various smaller releases through 2025. He remained active in production as well: the Hughes/Thrall album (Epic, 1982), and as late as 2021 produced Universe Of Dreams for Ear Music.

Scene Connections

Fraboni was a connective tissue figure across several interlocking 1970s rock communities:

  • The Band / Shangri-La circle — The Band, Robbie Robertson, Levon Helm, Rick Danko, Richard Manuel, Garth Hudson
  • Dylan/The Band touring world — Bob Dylan, Phil Ramone (co-engineer on Before The Flood)
  • Malibu rock community (mid-1970s) — Eric Clapton, Ron Wood, Keith Richards, Robbie Robertson
  • Island Records world — Chris Blackwell, Melissa Etheridge, Buckwheat Zydeco, Trouble Funk
  • Tuff Gong/reggae — Bob Marley & The Wailers, remaster collaboration with Tuff Gong
  • Keith Richards / Stones orbit — Keith Richards, Blondie Chaplin, The Rolling Stones
  • Ian McLagan / Bump Band world — Ian McLagan, The Bump Band, Ricky Fataar, Johnny Lee Schell
  • Brazilian MPB crossover — Milton Nascimento, Wayne Shorter, Airto Moreira
