Björk

Vocalist, composer, producer, multi-instrumentalist. Reykjavík, Iceland. Discogs ID: 1373. Real name: Björk Guðmundsdóttir. Born 21 November 1965, Reykjavík.

One of the most singular figures in popular music since the early 1990s: a vocalist of extraordinary range and technique; a composer who has moved between art pop, electronic music, chamber music, noise, and experimental song; and a conceptual artist whose albums are often conceived as unified works with visual and theatrical extensions. Formed her first band at age 11; fronted The Sugarcubes through their international career (1986–1992); then launched a solo career that has now spanned more than 30 years and 11 studio albums.

Early Groups and Reykjavík Underground (1977–1986)

Guðmundsdóttir released a self-titled album in Iceland in 1977 as a child prodigy (age 11). Through the early 1980s she was active in the Reykjavík post-punk underground, passing through several groups:

  • Exodus — early teenage band
  • Tappi Tíkarrass — punk group; released records in Iceland (1981–1983)
  • KUKL — avant-garde post-punk collective with close ties to Crass Records UK; she was the lead vocalist. Discogs ID: 217096. Released The Eye (1984) and Holidays in Europe (1986) on Crass Records.
  • Rokka Rokka Drum — short-lived project
  • Sykurmolarnir — another Reykjavík band

Kukl is the most documented of these early formations in the Discogs catalog.

The Sugarcubes (1986–1992)

The Sugarcubes (Discogs ID: 9452) were formed in Reykjavík in 1986, growing out of the Kukl circle plus others. Signed internationally to One Little Indian (UK) and Elektra (US). Their three albums brought Björk’s voice to a mass international audience, with “Birthday” (1987) a landmark UK indie hit.

Album-by-Album

Debut (1993) — One Little Indian TPLP31 / TPLP31CDL

Discogs release IDs: LP 72028 (UK vinyl), CD 28709 (UK CD)

The solo debut. Produced predominantly by Nellee Hooper, who contributes beats, programming, and engineering throughout. A landmark in 1990s art pop: Hooper’s lush drum-machine constructions dominate Side A and much of Side B, while the lone Björk self-production (“The Anchor Song”) establishes her as a formidable solo producer. Massive commercial and critical success launching her international career.

Note on track-level credits: the UK vinyl (72028) carries album-level production credits only (no per-track breakdown in Discogs). The assignment below is derived from the album-level credit listing: Nellee Hooper produces A1–A4, A6–B10; Björk self-produces B11; Björk and Hooper share co-production credit on A5 (“Like Someone In Love”). Writing credits: Björk writes all tracks except A5, which is by Jimmy Van Heusen and Johnny Burke. Hooper has co-writing credits on A1, A2, A4, A6, and B10.

Post (1995) — One Little Indian TPLP51CDX (Limited) / TPLP51CD

Discogs release ID: 28724 (UK CD Limited)

A major expansion of scope: big band brass (“It’s Oh So Quiet”), electronic hip-hop (“Enjoy”, “Headphones” — both Tricky productions), orchestral balladry (“Hyper-Ballad”), and Howie B’s dub-inflected production. The album is genuinely multi-producer, with each track largely assigned to a different producer/collaborator. Nellee Hooper handles the orchestral and pop material; Tricky handles the more unsettling tracks; Graham Massey provides industrial edge; Howie Bernstein (Howie B) produces “I Miss You”. Björk is listed as overall producer.

Homogenic (1997) — One Little Indian TPLP71CD

Discogs release ID: 9244846 (UK CD)

The first album conceived as a unified sonic statement: the Icelandic String Octet and a London chamber orchestra (Eumir Deodato, orchestrator) set against compressed, distorted drum programming by Mark Bell of LFO and Markus Dravs. Mark Bell co-produces the majority of tracks; Guy Sigsworth co-produces “Unravel”; Howie B produces only the closer, “All Is Full Of Love”. Often cited as her masterpiece.

Note — Evelyn Glennie / Homogenic credit resolved (web research 2026-06-27): The widespread secondary-source attribution of Glennie to Homogenic is a probable misattribution — she does not appear in Discogs credits for release 9244846, nor in the Wikipedia personnel section for Homogenic (checked 2026-06-27), nor in any named-byline source found. Her actual Björk collaboration is on Telegram (1996), the remix/B-sides companion to Post: she co-wrote and performed percussion (exhaust pipes, played with glockenspiel mallets) on “My Spine” and co-produced that track with Björk; the session took place at Glennie’s home in Oxford. She is additionally co-composer on “Oxygen”, later released on her own Her Greatest Hits compilation. Both credits are documented on Glennie’s own discography page at evelyn.co.uk and confirmed by the Wikipedia Telegram article. The likeliest mechanism for the error: secondary writers conflated Telegram (a Post-era release, 1996) with Homogenic (1997), both released in close proximity and in the same Björk creative period. Sources: evelyn.co.uk/discography/bjork-telegram/ (Glennie’s own site); Wikipedia: Telegram (album) (as of 2026-06-27); Wikipedia: Evelyn Glennie (as of 2026-06-27); Wikipedia: Homogenic (as of 2026-06-27 — no mention of Glennie).

Sjón co-writes “Jóga” (credited as Sigurjón Birgir Sigurðsson, also separately as Sjón on tr. 4 “Bachelorette”). “Bachelorette” additionally credits a London brass and string orchestra not identified as the Icelandic String Octet.

Vespertine (2001) — One Little Indian TPLP101CDL

Discogs release ID: 28725 (UK CD Limited)

Intimate, microscopic: built from harps, music boxes, choir, and laptop electronics. Björk primarily self-produces, with Matmos credited for programming on several tracks and Thomas Knak (Opiate) producing/co-writing two tracks. A deliberate turn inward after Homogenic’s orchestral bombast.

Medúlla (2004) — One Little Indian TPLP358CD

Discogs release ID: 9244725 (UK CD)

Almost entirely a cappella — constructed from voices, beatboxing, and choral arrangements, with only minimal programmed percussion beds. Radical in its rejection of electronic instrumentation. All tracks written by Björk except: tr. 4 “Vökuró” (Jórunn Viðar, traditional-derived); tr. 8 “Desired Constellation” (co-written Olivier Alary); tr. 9 “Oceania” (Sjón, lyrics); tr. 11 “Ancestors” (Tanya Tagaq, co-written).

Volta (2007) — One Little Indian tplp460cd

Discogs release ID: 962517 (UK CD)

Extroverted turn: brass bands (all-female Icelandic brass ensemble), African rhythms (Konono Nº1), hip-hop production (Timbaland on two tracks), and political content (“Declare Independence”). ANOHNI (Antony Hegarty) appears on “The Dull Flame Of Desire” and “My Juvenile”. Björk and Mark Bell are primary producers on the non-Timbaland tracks; Damian Taylor engineers and edits throughout. Nellee Hooper credited under “Paternal Musical Advice” — an advisory/consultant role, not full production.

Biophilia (2011) — One Little Indian tplp1016cd

Discogs release ID: 3186742 (UK CD)

Conceived as a multimedia app-album exploring relationships between music and natural phenomena. Each track is associated with a custom app and a specific natural or scientific concept. Features a 24-voice Hamrahlíð Choir; a custom “gameleste” instrument (midi-controlled gamelan-celeste hybrid); a pendulum harp; a Sharpsichord. Damian Taylor co-produces and co-mixes throughout; Matthew Herbert contributes beat programming on two tracks; 16 Bit (2) co-produces the two most club-leaning tracks.

Fossora (2022) — One Little Independent Records tplp1485cd1

Discogs release ID: 24688199 (Europe CD)

Named after the Latin for “she who digs”. Bass clarinets replace the flutes of Utopia — a six-piece bass clarinet sextet provides a dense low texture. Features her son Sindri Eldon (on “Ancestress”) and daughter Ísadóra Bjarkardóttir Barney (on “Her Mother’s House”). Produced in large part with Gabber Modus Operandi (Kasimyn, ID: 7426129) — an Indonesian gabber/noise duo providing the most abrasive beats in her catalog. Also features Serpentwithfeet and Emilie Nicolas as vocal collaborators. Mixed and mastered by Heba Kadry.
