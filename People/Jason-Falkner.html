Oct 31, 202611 min read

Jason Falkner

American guitarist, vocalist, and multi-instrumentalist. Born 2 June 1968 in Agoura Hills, California. Discogs artist ID: 325726.

Known for highly melodic power-pop songwriting and complete command of the recording studio as a one-man band. Original guitarist in Jellyfish (2) (1989–1991), co-member of The Grays (1993–1994) with Jon Brion, and long-running session guitarist/bassist for Beck. His three primary solo LPs on Elektra (Presents Author Unknown, 1996; Can You Still Feel?, 1999) and Make It Be (2017, with R. Stevie Moore) are the core of his own catalog. As a session player he appeared on records by Beck, AIR, Charlotte Gainsbourg, Daniel Johnston, Brendan Benson, Chris Cornell, Soulwax, Noel Gallagher, Aimee Mann, and Travis, among many others.

Name variations on Discogs: Falkner, J. F., J. Falkner, J.Falkner, JF, Jason, Jason Faulker, Jason Faulkner, jf. No aliases listed.

Era 1: Early Bands — Three O’Clock and Jellyfish (1988–1991)

Falkner joined The Three O’Clock as guitarist on their final album Vermillion (1988, Paisley Park Records), contributing guitar, vocals, and one co-written track. The band dissolved shortly after release.

Roger Joseph Manning Jr. then convinced him to move to San Francisco to join his new band, Jellyfish (2). Falkner played most guitars and basses on the debut album Bellybutton (1990, Charisma) and sang background vocals. Creative tensions with frontman Andy Sturmer — primarily around songwriting credit and artistic direction — led to his departure after the world tour in support of Bellybutton. He did not appear on Spilt Milk (1993). Eric Dover replaced him as guitarist.

Falkner’s Jellyfish credits also include the UK EP The Scary-Go-Round E.P. (1991).

Era 2: The Grays and Session Work (1992–1995)

After Jellyfish, Falkner worked as a session player and met Jon Brion through Fabulon-related work. Breaking his own vow against joining bands, he co-founded The Grays with Brion, Buddy Judge, and Dan McCarroll — three songwriters/multi-instrumentalists sharing lead vocal duties. They released one album, Ro Sham Bo (1994, Epic), produced by Jack Joseph Puig, before disbanding due to musical differences. Falkner played guitar, bass, keyboards, and sang. Brion played the same range of instruments.

Around the same period he appeared as a featured guest on Eric Matthews’s Sub Pop debut It’s Heavy In Here (1995), credited as engineer. He returned as a featured guest playing electric guitar, bass, piano, and drums on Matthews’s second album The Lateness Of The Hour (1997, Sub Pop).

He also co-wrote seven songs on Brendan Benson’s debut One Mississippi (1996, Virgin) — his first major co-writing credit outside his own bands — and played on Susanna Hoffs’s second solo album Fanfare (1995, Sub Pop).

Era 3: Solo Career — Elektra Period (1996–1999)

Presents Author Unknown (1996)

Falkner’s debut solo album on Elektra (cat. 61941-2). Written, produced, and performed almost entirely alone — all instruments except a string section on several tracks and one guitar overdub by Alain Johannes on “Miracle Medicine.” String arrangements co-handled with Charlie Barnett; strings section included Suzie Katayama, Dave Stone, David Stenske, Larry Corbett, Nancy Roth, Norman Hughes, Peter Kent, and Scott Haupert. Mixed by Falkner and Jim Ebert (tracks 2, 4, 6–12) and Jack Joseph Puig (tracks 1, 5). Mastered by Doug Sax and Gavin Lurssen.

Well-reviewed but not commercially successful. Received press attention in power-pop circles for its confident studio craft and melodic density.

Can You Still Feel? (1999)

Second solo album on Elektra (cat. 62205-2). Again written and performed entirely by Falkner — keyboards, guitar, bass, drums, percussion — with added horns and strings (string arrangements by Suzie Katayama). Co-produced by Nigel Godrich on tracks 1–3 and 6–12; Falkner produced, engineered, and mixed all tracks. Woodwind by Jim Akimoto (flute, saxophone). Mastered by Alan Yoshida.

The Godrich co-production credit connects Falkner to the same producer behind Beck’s Sea Change and Radiohead’s records — a notable alignment of aesthetics.

Era 4: Sessions and Collaborations (1998–2004)

Following his Elektra albums, Falkner became a sought-after session player in Los Angeles, with a concentration of credits in the Beck orbit.

Falkner appeared on several Beck albums in varied roles:

On Sea Change (2002, Geffen/DGC, produced by Nigel Godrich) he is credited alongside Roger Joseph Manning Jr. (synthesizer, Wurlitzer, piano, percussion), Justin Meldal-Johnsen (bass), Joey Waronker (drums), and Smokey Hormel (guitar) — Beck’s core recording band of the era.

On The Information (2006) he appears across the bulk of the album in a wide instrumental spread.

AIR — 10 000 Hz Legend (2001)

Falkner sang vocals on tracks 3 (“How Does It Feel?”) and 8, and appeared as a featured vocalist on track 6. He later toured with AIR as their live bassist. This led to further French connections — while in France with AIR, he, Roger Joseph Manning Jr., and Brian Reitzell performed as TV Eyes (2) (Discogs ID: 2920326), releasing the TV Eyes album in 2006 on Noise McCartney Records (Japan).

On Lapalco (Benson’s second album, V2) Falkner co-produced and co-wrote half the tracks — a more substantial collaboration than a typical session.

Bliss Descending EP (2004)

A five-track EP on his own Wreckchord Records label (CD-WRK-001). Produced, performed (all instruments), and mixed by Falkner throughout. Drums on tracks 1 and 4 by Jeremy Stacey; mixing on those same tracks by Justin Stanley. Falkner used this EP as his opening-act material on Travis’s 2004 European tour.

He produced, recorded, and mixed Daniel Johnston’s album Is And Always Was (2009, Feraltone) — a high-profile production credit on a critically valued outsider-music figure.

For Charlotte Gainsbourg’s Beck-produced IRM (2009, Because Music) he played acoustic guitar on track 4 and electric guitar on track 8.

He played guitar and sitar on Stax / Go Funk Yourself artist Jonatha Brooke’s Pebble To A Pearl (2008).

For the Ocean’s Thirteen soundtrack (2007, Warner Sunset) he contributed bass and guitar.

Era 7: Noel Gallagher, R. Stevie Moore, and Later Sessions (2013–present)

Noel Gallagher’s High Flying Birds

Falkner played bass guitar across the majority of tracks on Who Built The Moon? (2017, Sour Mash), produced by David Holmes, alongside Jeremy Stacey (drums) and Johnny Marr (guitar, harmonica, on “If Love Is The Law”). He also contributed bass guitar on Back The Way We Came: Vol. 1 2011–2021 (2021).

Make It Be (2017) — with R. Stevie Moore

A collaborative LP with lo-fi DIY pioneer R. Stevie Moore (Discogs ID: 220114), released on Bar/None Records (BRN-LP-242). Both credited as primary artists; both credited as “Instruments, Voice, Noises” and co-producer. Covers of “Don’t You Just Know It” (Huey “Piano” Smith) and “Another Day Slips Away” (I Roger Ferguson) appear alongside originals split between the two writers.

On Matthew Sweet’s Bird Streets (2018, Omnivore Recordings) Falkner served as full producer and performed all main instruments — the most complete production role documented in his later session work.

For Pyjama Days (Excelsior, 2016) and earlier Dutch releases (In Another Life, 2009; Anne Soldaat, 2012), he worked repeatedly with the Excelsior Recordings roster as producer and engineer.

Wikipedia (June 2026) notes Falkner played with the Foo Fighters in January 2026 as a substitute for Pat Smear after Smear broke his leg; he was also St. Vincent’s touring guitarist on the 2024 All Born Screaming Tour.
