Overview

Kevin March (born Kevin Daniel March) is an American drummer whose career in indie and alternative rock stretches back to 1992. He is best known as a member of Guided By Voices, appearing across multiple eras of the band — from the early spinART years (1992–93) through the EastWest major-label period and deep into the prolific Guided By Voices Inc. run (2014–2025, his densest concentration of credits at 29 releases). He has also been a member of Shudder To Think, Ricked Wicky, and Mind Science Of The Mind. His closest working relationships are with Robert Pollard and Doug Gillard, with whom he shares roughly 85–100 releases each; he has additionally built a substantial body of work alongside Bobby Bare Jr. and, in a different vein, singer Joan Wasser and Nathan Larson.

How Kevin March was recruited into Shudder to Think

Searched extensively for interviews or press accounts explaining how March crossed from the Dayton/New England indie scene (the Dambuilders) into DC post-hardcore Shudder To Think. No source found that directly addresses the recruitment mechanism.

What the searches established:

  • March replaced Adam Wade as Shudder to Think’s drummer prior to the recording of 50,000 B.C. (1997); Wade had joined the band for Pony Express Record (1994). Wikipedia: Shudder to Think
  • The 2004 Modern Drummer feature on March (moderndrummer.com) does not discuss the Shudder to Think recruitment.
  • The 2021 Stereogum “We’ve Got A File On You” interview with Craig Wedren (stereogum.com) confirms March as “the final drummer in Shudder to Think” and notes he later “spearheaded making Lapland” (Wedren’s 2009 solo album), but says nothing about how March was originally brought in.
  • The AV Club interview with Wedren (avclub.com) describes the 2007 reunion origin: “Kevin was playing with both A Camp and me, so we were just like, ‘Let’s do a few Shudder songs’” — establishing March’s continued close ties to Wedren but not touching the 1995–96 recruitment.
  • A 2012 Adam Wade interview at Floodwatch Music (floodwatchmusic.wordpress.com) covers only the Pony Express Record era and does not address his departure or March’s arrival.
  • The Anti-Matter Substack conversation with Wedren (antimatter.substack.com) mentions March only as a collaborator on Nathan Larson’s Mind Science of the Mind project (with Joan Wasser and Mary Timony) — suggesting March and Larson had overlapping New York-based networks by the mid-1990s, but no explicit link to how he joined the band is given.
  • The Head Medicine blog’s two-part Shudder to Think history (headmedicine.blogspot.com) notes March is “New York based” but does not account for his recruitment.
  • The GBV drummer interview at Excited Ones Substack (excitedones.substack.com) only confirms March’s membership without detail.
  • DC Rock podcast episode on Shudder to Think (dcrockpod.podbean.com) episode description does not mention March at all.

Partial circumstantial picture: March, Larson, Wasser, and Timony were all part of overlapping New York indie circles in the mid-1990s. The Mind Science of the Mind collaboration (Larson + March) appears to predate or coincide with March joining Shudder to Think, suggesting a Larson connection may have been the bridge — but no source states this. Cross-scene proximity (New York as common ground rather than DC vs. Dayton) is consistent with the available evidence but unconfirmed.

Dead end: No interview with March, Wedren, or Larson found that explicitly describes how March was approached, auditioned, or agreed to join. The recruitment circumstances are not documented in any accessible online source as of October 2026.
