David Toop

Born 1949. UK musician, writer, curator, and sound artist. Discogs artist ID: 391. Professor of Audio Culture and Improvisation at London College of Communication.

Overview

David Toop has maintained a practice crossing free improvisation, field recording, electronic sound, installation, and writing since 1970. As a performer he plays guitar (electric, acoustic, lap steel, pedal steel), flute (C, alto, bamboo, bone, piston, stopped), percussion, glockenspiel, tape/cassette, and found materials including bones, stones, paper, leaves, and feedback devices. As a writer he is one of the most influential anglophone critics of experimental and electronic music, publishing eight books including Rap Attack (1984), Ocean of Sound (1995), Sinister Resonance (2010), Into the Maelstrom (2016), and Flutter Echo (2019). His parallel career as a label essayist has spread his critical voice across releases on Warp, Columbia, Sub Rosa, Confront, Room40, and many others.

Name variations on record: D. Toop, D.T., Dave Toop, Toop. Aliases used: Iron Monkey, Steve Topp.

Career by Era

1970–1975 — Early Improvisation and the Obscure Debut

Toop began performing in London in the early 1970s, gravitating toward improvised music and experimental approaches to sound. By 1975 he had recorded New And Rediscovered Musical Instruments with sculptor and kinetic artist Max Eastley, released on Brian Eno’s Obscure Independent Classics label (Island Records, 1975). The album showcased handmade and found-sound instruments, with Eno producing and Rhett Davies engineering. It remains a touchstone of British speculative instrument building.

1977–1980 — Quartz, Bead, and Free Improvisation Networks

A dense cluster of releases across small UK labels:

  • Cholagogues (Bead Records, 1977): trio with Paul Burwell and Nestor Figueras. Toop plays an elaborate array of flutes (C, alto, water, piston, stopped end-blown, New Guinea initiation), whistles, bone trumpet, panpipes, and balloon. One of the most fully realised documents of the London free-improvisation scene of the era.
  • Burwell / Toop (Quartz-Mirliton Cassettes, 1977): cassette duet with Burwell; self-released.
  • Wounds (Quartz Publications, 1979): duo with Paul Burwell. Electric guitar, flute, cassette tapes, water, rubbish, small explosives. Toop handles tape editing, production, and cover design; Max Eastley appears as recording engineer. Field recording from Continamo Falls in Venezuela feeds into the material.
  • Whirled Music (Quartz Publications, 1980): solo.
  • Improvised Music & Sound Works (Audio Arts, 1980): solo LP.

Toop also appears on several peripherally related releases from this period — dub sessions (flute on Prince Far I’s Cry Tuff Dub Encounter Chapter III, Daddy Kool, 1980), ensemble pieces for the London Musicians’ Collective (Spanish Guitar, 1980), and archival field recordings from his 1978 trip to Amazonas, Venezuela, documenting Yanomami shamanism (eventually released as Lost Shadows on Sub Rosa, 2016).

1979 — The Flying Lizards

Briefly a member of David Cunningham’s post-punk pop project The Flying Lizards in 1979 — a cameo within a wider circle that included Steve Beresford.

1977–1984 — Alterations and General Strike

Alterations was an iconoclastic improvising quartet formed in 1977 with Steve Beresford, Peter Cusack, and Terry Day. The group has been intermittently active into the 2020s, with Toop describing a revival in recent years.

General Strike — the duo/project of Toop and Beresford — released Danger In Paradise on Touch (1984), the key document of their collaboration. David Cunningham produced; the album captures Toop playing guitar, percussion, flute, alto flute, glockenspiel, vocals, and tape against Beresford’s multi-instrumental pile-up (bass, piano, glockenspiel, organ, trumpet, synthesizer, flugelhorn, toy piano, euphonium, melodica, percussion, vocals, noises).

1984–1989 — Frank Chickens, Deadly Weapons, and Nato

Toop produced several records for Frank Chickens (Kaz Records, 1984–1987), the Japanese performance duo based in London. He arranged and played on We Are Frank Chickens (1984) and Get Chickenized (1987), contributing guitar, flute, drum programming, synthesizer, sampler, and trumpet.

Deadly Weapons (Nato, 1986) is a landmark free-jazz/noise record: a quartet of Toop, Beresford, John Zorn, and actress/vocalist Tonie Marshall. Produced by Jean Rochard; engineered by Dave Hunt. Toop plays guitar, pedal steel, flute, alto flute, percussion, keyboards, drum programming, and sampler; Zorn adds alto saxophone, keyboards, clarinet, guiro, and choir.

Also credits from this era: Dancing The Line (nato, 1985, flute and guitar); Privilege (Rough Trade, 1983, alto flute, banjo, vocals, percussion, Amazonian field recordings, producer).

1990–2000 — Writing, Spirit World, and Needle In The Groove

Toop’s output as a performer thinned during this period while his writing output peaked. Ocean of Sound: Aether Talk, Ambient Sound and Imaginary Worlds (1995) became a landmark text on ambient music, connecting Brian Eno, Debussy, hip-hop, musique concrète, and global sound practices. Earlier, Rap Attack: African Jive to New York Hip Hop (1984, revised editions 1991, 2000) established him as among the first serious critics of hip-hop outside the US.

Discographic credits in this era lean heavily toward liner notes: Warp’s Artificial Intelligence II (1994); Miles Davis’s Panthalassa (Columbia, 1998); Tommy Boy anniversary sets; dub compilations; electronic music anthologies.

Spirit World (Virgin, 1997) — a solo record produced and performed by Toop; largely a conceptual/sound-art piece.

Needle In The Groove (Sulphur Records, 2000) — a collaboration with novelist Jeff Noon, matching Toop’s electronic environment and sound to Noon’s voice and words. Toop performs and records; engineer Dave Hunt returns.

Buried Dreams (Beyond, 1994) — ambient field-recording/sound work; Toop contributes ambient recordings and liner notes/stories.

2004 — Breath Taking with Akio Suzuki

Breath Taking (Confront, 2004): duo with Japanese sound artist Akio Suzuki, performing kikkokikiriki, ireba, analapos, stone-flute, panpipes, and small stones against Toop’s flute, bone whistle, dog whistles, stones, and feedback devices. Recorded by Akinori Yamasaki. A defining document of the Toop–Suzuki ongoing collaboration.

2014–2016 — Mondo Black Chamber, Lost Shadows, Entities Inertias Faint Beings

Mondo Black Chamber (Sub Rosa, 2014): a major compilation/exhibition document curated by Toop.

Entities Inertias Faint Beings (Room40, 2016): first of several collaborations with the Brisbane label run by Lawrence English. English masters and provides field recording; featured players include Roger Turner (drums), John Butcher (soprano and tenor saxophone), Rie Nakajima (machine objects on paper), Sylvia Hallett (sarangi), and Emi Watanabe (transverse flute). Toop plays all other instruments and produces.

Lost Shadows: In Defence Of The Soul — Yanomami Shamanism, Songs, Magic And Funeral Rites, 1978 (Sub Rosa, 2016): the long-delayed release of Toop’s 1978 Amazonas field recordings.

2017–2020 — Room40, Ken Ikeda, Ryuichi Sakamoto

Skin Tones (Home Normal, 2017): duo with Ken Ikeda, Toop on steel guitar, flute, laptop, and objects; artwork by Rie Nakajima.

Live In Porto (Noise Precision Library, 2017): trio with percussion and tape.

Dirty Songs — a project featuring Toop on bass, guitar, electronics, and VCS3 synth; Dirty Songs Play Dirty Songs (Audika, 2017).

Apparition Paintings (Room40, 2020): solo album; Toop also contributes the cover paintings. Additional compositions by Keiko Yamamoto and Yifeat Ziv.

No Relation (Amplify 2020, 2020): live/improv set — Toop plays percussion, lap steel, bass recorder, African fiddle, cassette tape.

2021–2025 — Sakamoto, Bang/Wastell, Room40 Continuations

Garden Of Shadows And Light (ThirtyThree ThirtyThree Records, 2021): duet with Ryuichi Sakamoto. Mixed by Dave Hunt; mastered by Noel Summerville.

Compound Full Of Bones, Translucent Thousands (Confront, 2021): trio with Jan Bang (live sampling) and Mark Wastell (tam-tam, gongs, autoharp). Mark Wastell produces; Rupert Clervaux mixes and masters.

Breathing Spirit Forms (Room40, 2021): trio with Akio Suzuki and Lawrence English. English contributes electronics (shortwave radio) and percussion; Suzuki plays analapos, pocket bottle, sponge, and hand mirror.

Pink Spirit, Noir World (Foam On A Wave, 2022): solo.

The Shell That Speaks The Sea (Room40, 2023): duo with Lawrence English.

Untitled / Logos Improvisations (Sub Rosa / Logos, 2023): solo flutes, home-made reeds, and percussion.

Thought Forms (Shrike Records, 2023): solo — lap steel, alto flute, astro-electronics, bone conduction, cassettes, voice.

Wunderkammer (Confront, 2024): quintet with Jan Bang, Michael Francis Duch (double bass), Erik Honoré, and Mark Wastell. Group named Wunderkammer (2).

Music For Voilà (Quartz Publications, 2024): assembly sculptures, percussion, recordings — returning to his own imprint.

Is Spring A Sculpture? (Room40, 2025): duo with Rie Nakajima.
