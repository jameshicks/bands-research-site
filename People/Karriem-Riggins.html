Karriem Riggins

Karriem Riggins

May 18, 20269 min read

Karriem Riggins

Discogs ID: 80688 Born: Detroit, Michigan, USA Active: 1995–present Instruments: Drums, percussion Roles: Drummer, hip-hop producer, beatmaker, arranger Labels (as leader): Stones Throw Records, Madlib Invazion, Smoke Sessions Records

American jazz drummer and hip-hop producer from Detroit. Son of keyboardist/producer Emmanuel Riggins. Came up playing drums for vocalist Betty Carter and trumpeter Roy Hargrove, then joined the Ray Brown Trio in the late 1990s. Simultaneously built a reputation in Detroit hip-hop circles, producing tracks for Common, The Roots, and becoming a close associate of J Dilla. Co-executive produced Dilla’s posthumous The Shining (BBE, 2006). As a solo beatmaker, released Alone Together (2012) and Headnod Suite (2017) on Stones Throw Records. Member of supergroup August Greene (with Common and Robert Glasper) and the duo Jahari Massamba Unit (with Madlib).

Biography

Early Life and Jazz Training (pre-1995)

Karriem Riggins grew up in Detroit in a musical household — his father is Emmanuel Riggins (Discogs ID: 356441), a keyboardist and producer. He began his career as a jazz drummer and worked with vocalist Betty Carter and trumpeter Roy Hargrove in the early-to-mid 1990s, absorbing the bebop tradition at a high level.

By 1995 he was appearing on records. His first documented Discogs credits are on two Verve Records sessions: Mulgrew Miller’s Getting To Know You (1995, Novus/BMG; also listed with Mulgrew Miller personnel Richie Goods on bass) and the Verve albums Family and Renaissance (both 1995).

Ray Brown Trio and Jazz Sideman Peak (1995–2003)

Riggins became a member of the Ray Brown Trio (Discogs ID: 296953), the long-running band led by bassist Ray Brown. He is listed as a trio member alongside pianists including Benny Green, Geoff Keezer, Cedar Walton, and Monty Alexander, and fellow drummer Jeff Hamilton. Key Ray Brown Trio credits include:

  • The Very Tall Band (1999, Telarc Jazz) — with Oscar Peterson, Ray Brown, and Milt Jackson
  • Some Of My Best Friends Are…The Trumpet Players (2000, Telarc)
  • Some Of My Best Friends Are…Guitarists (2002, Telarc Jazz)
  • Ray Brown Monty Alexander Russell Malone (2002, Telarc Jazz)
  • Walk On – The Final Ray Brown Trio Recording (2003, Telarc Jazz)

Other jazz sideman work in this period includes sessions for Diana Krall (Quiet Nights, 2009, Verve — drums on DVD bonus tracks with Robert Hurst and Anthony Wilson), and appearances on Children Of The Light (DIW, 1996), Hidden Kingdom (DIW, 1997), Fragile and Funky! (both Somethin’ Else, 1998), and Musicale (Impulse!, 1996). He also played on the Tribe (8) / Carl Craig Detroit jazz-electronics crossover Rebirth (2009, Planet E), alongside Marcus Belgrave, Amp Fiddler, Phil Ranelin, Wendell Harrison, and producer Carl Craig.

Detroit Experiment and Cross-Scene Work (2001–2005)

Detroit’s convergence of jazz, hip-hop, and electronic music was embodied in The Detroit Experiment (Discogs ID: 80686), a large collective curated by Carl Craig. Riggins played drums and percussion on their self-titled 2002 album (Ropeadope Records), sharing the session with Geri Allen, Amp Fiddler, Bennie Maupin, Marcus Belgrave, Regina Carter, and Francisco Mora Catlett. The project stands as a defining document of Detroit’s jazz-hip-hop fusion moment.

He also appeared on Welcome 2 Detroit (BBE, 2001) — the J Dilla-associated Detroit hip-hop showcase — listed for “Drums [Live].”

Hip-Hop Production: Common, The Roots, J Dilla Circle (1997–2010)

Riggins was producing hip-hop concurrently with his jazz work throughout the late 1990s and 2000s:

  • Common One Day It’ll All Make Sense (1997, Relativity) — credited as “Karriem ‘Mad Rhythm’ Riggins,” produced one track (D5). Other producers on the album include No I.D., James Poyser, and The Roots.
  • Common Electric Circus (2002/2009 reissue, MCA Records) — produced one track. Also wrote on the single Come Close Remix (Closer) (MCA, 2003).
  • Common Black America Again (2016, Def Jam) — co-executive producer alongside No I.D.
  • The Roots Phrenology (2002, MCA Records) — produced track C97 (credited as “Kareem Riggins”). The album also features producers DJ Scratch, Scott Storch, and Questlove.
  • J Dilla The Shining (2006, BBE/Okayplayer) — served as executive producer alongside Dilla’s estate and Maureen Yancey (Ma Dukes). This was a posthumous curation of Dilla’s recordings.
  • Erykah Badu New Amerykah: Part One (4th World War) (2007, Universal Motown) — written-by credit.
  • Mochilla Presents Timeless: Suite For Ma Dukes (2010) — uncredited drums on this tribute to J Dilla’s music.

Stones Throw: Solo Beatmaker (2010–2017)

Riggins signed to Stones Throw Records as a beatmaker, releasing:

  • Classic (2010/2011, Stones Throw) — produced by Riggins, two pressings
  • Alone/ (2012, Stones Throw) — solo instrumental hip-hop, produced, written, and mixed by Riggins; mastered by Kelly Hibbert, photography by Gerard Victor
  • Alone Together (2012, Stones Throw) — expanded companion to Alone/; same core credits, design by Jeff Jank
  • Headnod Suite (2017, Stones Throw) — produced by Riggins; design again by Jeff Jank, mastered by Jake Viator

These releases established his identity as a beatmaker within the Stones Throw aesthetic — sample-heavy, jazz-inflected, rooted in Detroit.

City EP with Madlib and Freddie Gibbs (2013)

City (2013, Madlib Invazion) was a three-way primary credit: Freddie Gibbs, Madlib, and Karriem Riggins. This placed Riggins inside Madlib’s extended universe alongside Gibbs, intersecting with the Piñata era collaborations.

August Greene Supergroup (2018)

August Greene (Discogs ID: 6419053) — the trio of Riggins, Common (Lonnie Rashid Lynn), and Robert Glasper — released their self-titled debut in 2018 (August Greene LLC). Riggins co-produced and co-wrote throughout. Guest writers and performers included Bilal, Brandy Norwood, Estelle, and Samora Pinderhughes. Mixed by Manny Marroquin, mastered by Michelle Mancini.

Jahari Massamba Unit (2020–2024)

Jahari Massamba Unit (Discogs ID: 1892481) is Riggins’ duo with Madlib (Otis Jackson Jr., Discogs ID: 346740). Their debut Pardon My French (2020, Madlib Invazion) was fully co-produced, co-written, co-mixed, and co-recorded by both. Their second album YHWH Is LOVE (2024, Law Of Rhythm) continued the partnership. The project is described in the Discogs profile under the alias “The Jahari Massamba Unit.”

Continued Jazz Work (2009–present)

Riggins continued as an in-demand jazz drummer after his Stones Throw period:

  • Diana Krall Kisses On The Bottom (2012, Concord)
  • Norah Jones Day Breaks (2016, Blue Note) — drums
  • Esperanza Spalding Emily’s D+Evolution (2016, Concord) — drums
  • Erykah Badu A Beautiful Revolution Pt 2 (2021, Loma Vista) — producer
  • Junko Onishi Presents Jatroit (2019, DIW) — primary artist alongside Junko Onishi, Robert Hurst, Shinya Matsushita, and others; a Japan-based session bridging Detroit and Tokyo jazz
  • To The Jungle (2024, Madlib Invazion) — solo leader release
  • TRIUNE (2025, Smoke Sessions Records) — primary artist, with Nicholas Payton, Esperanza Spalding, Ivan Neville, Nikki Glaspie, and Erica Falls
