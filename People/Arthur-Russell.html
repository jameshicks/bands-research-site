Arthur Russell

Cellist, composer, vocalist, producer. Born Charles Arthur Russell Jr., 21 May 1951, Oskaloosa, Iowa. Died 4 April 1992, New York City (AIDS-related illness), age 40.

Discogs artist ID: 23524. Aliases: Dinosaur, Dinosaur L, Loose Joints, Indian Ocean, Killer Whale (used posthumously as producer/mix credit on archival releases).

One of the most anomalous figures in the NYC downtown scene. Operated simultaneously in avant-garde composition, disco/dance production, and intimate singer-songwriter territory — without apparently resolving the contradiction. Died before most of this work received wide recognition; posthumous releases from 2003 onwards built a second-generation cult.

Biography

Iowa → San Francisco (early 1970s) Raised in Oskaloosa, Iowa. Learned cello. Early 1970s moved to San Francisco; studied Indian classical music at the Ali Akbar Khan School (Ravi Shankar’s fellow student under Allauddin Khan; Ali Akbar Khan is not Ravi Shankar’s brother — see Research Queue). Met Allen Ginsberg; began accompanying him on cello while Ginsberg sang or read poetry.

New York City (mid-1970s onwards) Moved to NYC mid-1970s. Connected immediately to the loft music / minimalist avant-garde and simultaneously to the emerging disco underground. Both scenes at once, no separation.

Formed The Flying Hearts — ensemble that included David Byrne, Rhys Chatham, Jon Gibson, Peter Gordon, Jerry Harrison, Glenn Iamaro, Bill Ruyle, Jon Sholle. Performed and recorded Instrumentals between 1975 and 1979 (released Les Disques du Crépuscule, 1984; reissued Audika Records, 2017). Peter Gordon (Flying Hearts) also produced Michael Byron’s Tidal (N5, Neutral Records, 1983) — a direct thread between Russell’s circle and the Branca/Neutral world.

Key Projects & Releases

Dinosaur — “Kiss Me Again” (Sire, 1978)

First disco single on Sire. Co-produced with Nicky Siano (one of the original NYC disco DJs; co-ran The Gallery with Steve D’Aquisto). David Byrne on guitar. Peter Gordon on saxophone. Myriam Valle on vocals. Wilbur Bascomb on bass. Russell: producer, written-by.

The combination of Byrne (Talking Heads, then at peak of their No Wave adjacency) and Siano (architect of NYC’s original disco sound) on one Russell record in 1978 encapsulates what he was doing — sitting at intersections nobody else was even visiting.

Loose Joints — “Is It All Over My Face?” (West End Records, 1980)

Co-produced and co-written with Steve D’Aquisto (Nicky Siano’s DJ partner at The Gallery). François Kevorkian edited — uncredited. Became a foundational NYC dance track; Larry Levan played it regularly at the Paradise Garage. Later widely sampled. 11 pressings in DB.

Also: “Pop Your Funk” (West End, 1980).

The Necessaries (SPY Records, 1979; Sire, 1981–82)

Joined Ed Tomney’s band replacing Chris Spedding, mid-1981 — see SPY Records. Keyboards, guitar, cello, vocals. Big Sky (Sire, 1981) was withdrawn without the band’s consent (Sire released unfinished mixes). Event Horizon (Sire, 1982) is the authorised album. Completely Necessary (Anthology 1978–1982) reissued on Omnivore Recordings, 2026. Ernie Brooks (Necessaries bassist, ex-Modern Lovers) later co-compiled Love Is Overtaking Me.

Dinosaur L — 24→24 Music (Sleeping Bag Records, 1981)

The first Sleeping Bag Records release. Co-produced by Russell. Personnel:

  • Ed Tomney — guitar (The Necessaries; SPY Records bandmate)
  • Peter Gordon — tenor saxophone
  • Peter Zummo — trombone (long-term collaborator; Zummo With An X, Loris, 1985)
  • Julius Eastman — keyboards, vocals
  • Jill Kroesen — vocals
  • Ingram family (Butch, Timmy, John, William, Jimmy Ingram) — bass, congas, drums, guitar, keyboards
  • Wilbur Bascomb — bass; Rome Neal — percussion, vocals; Denise Mercedes — guitar

Dinosaur L — “Go Bang! #5” (Sleeping Bag Records, 1982)

Later remixed by François Kevorkian into a canonical dance track. “Go Bang” became foundational for house and electronic music DJs; it surfaces in DJ sets decades later.

Allen Ginsberg — First Blues (John Hammond Records, 1983)

Ginsberg’s album; Russell arranged, played cello, tambourine, provided vocals. Note: First Thought Best Thought (Audika Records/Rough Trade, 2006) is Russell’s own posthumous LP, not a release of the Ginsberg sessions — Ginsberg does not appear in its Discogs credits.

Tower Of Meaning (Chatham Square Productions, 1983)

Orchestral composition. Produced and written by Russell. Conducted by Julius Eastman — the gay Black minimalist composer who was a repeated Russell collaborator. Eastman died homeless in Buffalo in 1990, two years before Russell, age 49. A remarkable pairing of two figures whose full recognition came decades posthumously. Give It To The Sky: Arthur Russell’s Tower Of Meaning Expanded (Erased Tapes, 2023) is the expanded edition.

”Snake Charmer” (Island Records, 1983)

Jah Wobble and Invaders of the Heart. Arthur Russell wrote the lyrics. Produced by François Kevorkian. Co-written by Holger Czukay and Jaki Liebezeit (both Can). The Edge (U2) appears. An intersection of PiL-adjacent bass, Can krautrock, U2, NYC disco production, and Russell’s words.

World Of Echo (Upside Records, 1986)

The definitive solo statement. Entirely self-performed: effects, hand percussion, vocals, cello. No band, no collaborators. Mastered by Greg Calbi (Sterling Sound). Melody Maker Top 30. 20 pressings in DB.

Also: “Let’s Go Swimming” (Rough Trade, 1986) — 12 pressings.

Peter Gordon — Innocent (CBS/FM Records, 1986)

Russell appears as featured guest on Peter Gordon’s major-label album alongside Gary Lucas, Tony Levin, Elliot Easton (The Cars), Lenny Pickett, and the Blind Boys of Alabama. See Peter Gordon file for full personnel.

Jerry Harrison: Casual Gods (Fontana/Phonogram, 1988)

Backing vocals and songwriting for Talking Heads guitarist’s solo album. A mainstream-adjacent pop record; 12 pressings globally. Demonstrates the range of contexts Russell moved through.

Sleeping Bag Records

Co-founded 1981 with William Socolov and Juggy Gales. Label ID 3379. Eclectic dance label (1981–1992). Kurtis Mantronik (Mantronix) acted as A&R executive and recorded for the label. Sub-label: Fresh Records (founded 1984). After closure in 1992, catalog sat in limbo until Warlock Records purchased it in 1996. Now controlled by Phase One Network.

Key Sleeping Bag releases in Russell’s orbit: 24→24 Music (1981), “Go Bang! #5” (1982), “School Bell / Treehouse” (1986).
