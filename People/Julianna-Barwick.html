Julianna Barwick

Julianna Barwick

Oct 19, 20266 min read

Julianna Barwick

Discogs ID: 584243
Origin: Missouri, USA (later based in Brooklyn, NY)
Active: 2006–present
Instruments / roles: Voice, piano, electric piano, electric organ, loops, production

American vocalist and experimental musician. Barwick’s practice is built around layered loop recordings of her own voice — often wordless, sometimes choral — processed and stacked into dense, spiritual-sounding ambient compositions. She plays and produces much of her solo work herself, recording and mixing with collaborators as needed. Her technique was largely established before her first proper release and has remained consistent across labels and production contexts.

Biography

Barwick grew up in Missouri and is based in Brooklyn. She began releasing music under her own name in 2006 with the self-released cassette Sanguine. The full-length Florine (2009, Florid Recordings) established her sound on a proper imprint — all vocals and piano, recorded and performed by Barwick alone. Both releases circulated quietly before she broke wider with The Magic Place (2011, Asthmatic Kitty Records), her most widely distributed record to that point.

In 2011 she also collaborated with No Wave drummer and electronics artist Ikue Mori on FRKWYS Vol. 6, a split/collaboration release on Rvng Intl. — part of that label’s ongoing series pairing artists across generations.

The collaborative project OMBRE (4) (with Roberto Carlos Lange / Helado Negro) yielded Believe You Me in 2012 on Asthmatic Kitty. Lange handled recording and mixing; both contributed music.

Nepenthe (2013, Dead Oceans) was recorded in Iceland with producer and engineer Alex Somers (known from Sigur Rós productions). The record brought in the Icelandic string quartet Amiina and a Reykjavik teen girls’ choir. Barwick’s mother contributed vocals. This was Barwick’s most collaborative and orchestral record to that point.

Pacing (2013, Suicide Squeeze Records) was a shorter EP / mini-album. Rosabi EP (2014, Dead Oceans) continued the Dead Oceans relationship.

Will (2016, Dead Oceans) introduced more live drumming (Jamie Ingalls) and cello (Maarten Vos), with vocal contributions from Thomas Arsenault (Mas Ysa) and engineering / mixing by Eric Elterman alongside Barwick herself. Healing Is A Miracle (2020, Ninja Tune) was mastered by Heba Kadry and represents her move to that larger label. An extended versions edition followed the same year.

Tragic Magic (2026, Infiné) is a co-billed LP with harpist Mary Lattimore, Barwick’s most prominent recent collaboration. No additional credits appear in the Discogs record beyond the two primary artists.
