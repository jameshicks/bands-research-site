Matt Goldman

Audio engineer, producer, and musician. Real name Matthew J. Goldman. Discogs artist ID: 373970. Based in Atlanta, Georgia. Owner and operator of Glow In The Dark Studios. Member of Christian rock band Smalltown Poets (Discogs artist ID: 343547). Active in recorded music from approximately 1995 through the mid-2020s, primarily in the Christian/alternative and post-hardcore scenes.

Overview

Matt Goldman is an Atlanta-based audio engineer and producer whose career spans roughly three decades, predominantly within the American Christian music ecosystem — particularly the post-hardcore, emo, metalcore, and indie-rock subgenres that flourished under labels like Solid State Records, Tooth & Nail Records, and Facedown Records from the late 1990s through the 2010s.

Goldman operates Glow In The Dark Studios in Atlanta, which became a hub for Christian alternative and post-hardcore recording. He is also a performing musician — member of pop/rock act Smalltown Poets, who released on ForeFront Records from 1997 to 2004. He played drums, percussion, and guitar on Smalltown Poets’ Third Verse (2000) and It’s Later Than It’s Ever Been (2004), the latter of which he also directed.

His earliest engineering credits date to the mid-1990s (a 1995 Speedy West/Jimmy Bryant compilation). A signal early production credit is the band Luxury’s self-titled 1999 record (Luxury, Bulletproof Music), where he is credited as engineer and auxiliary musician — a working relationship that evidently ran deep: Goldman appears as interviewee and mixer on the 2020 documentary Parallel Love: The Story Of A Band Called Luxury (Awake Productions).

The early 2000s established Goldman as a sought-after producer in the Christian alternative circuit. He produced and recorded Copeland’s Beneath Medicine Tree (2003, The Militia Group) — one of the label’s signature records — contributing string arrangements, additional guitars, and additional percussion alongside production and engineering duties. He also worked pre-production on Underoath’s Blueprints For The Black Market (2003, Tooth & Nail) and recorded additional overdubs for Casting Crowns’ self-titled debut (2003).

The 2004–2008 period saw Goldman working across multiple Solid State/Tooth & Nail acts simultaneously. His highest-profile credit from this era is Underoath’s Define The Great Line (2006, Solid State) — the album that brought the band mainstream crossover attention. He also produced As Cities Burn’s Come Now Sleep (2007, Solid State) and engineered/produced the band’s The Fiancée (2007). He produced and engineered Haste The Day’s In Motion (2005) and Anberlin-adjacent acts. He also contributed to Norma Jean’s O God, The Aftermath era releases, including Everything Is Alive, Everything Is Breathing, Nothing Is Dead And Nothing Is Bleeding (2004, Solid State).

A notable outside-the-Christian-scene credit: Goldman produced the Dead Oceans release Freedom Wind (2008) by The Appleseed Cast — a post-rock/emo act not typically associated with the Solid State world. This suggests his reputation extended beyond strictly faith-based markets.

From the 2010s forward, Goldman continued to work steadily across the Christian/post-hardcore world, producing August Burns Red’s Leveler (released as Long Live, 2010, Good Fight Music), and maintaining a long-running relationship with My Epic across multiple albums. He also worked on Norma Jean’s Wrongdoers-era material. International credits appeared — a 2011 Sony Music Australia release (All Through Winter) and a 2016 Australian self-released record (Songs Of Soulitude, Dream On Dreamer).

By the mid-2010s Goldman’s role shifted somewhat toward mixing — engineering and mixing credits appear more frequently than sole producer credits, suggesting a mature positioning in the studio ecosystem. He mixed Haste The Day’s Coward (2015) and contributed to releases on Epitaph, Equal Vision, Pure Noise, Spinefarm, and Cooking Vinyl through the late 2010s and early 2020s.

Goldman also played session drums and percussion on a number of records over his career, and contributed keyboards on several productions — making him a musician-producer in the tradition of the working studio player.
