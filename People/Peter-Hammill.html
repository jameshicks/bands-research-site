Peter Hammill

Jul 29, 20266 min read

Peter Hammill

Discogs ID: 279473
Born: 5 November 1948, Ealing, West London, UK
Real name: Peter Joseph Andrew Hammill

British singer-songwriter, multi-instrumentalist, and producer. One of the most prolific and uncompromising figures in British progressive rock. Co-founded Van der Graaf Generator in 1967 and has remained its central figure through every incarnation. His parallel solo career, launched with Fool’s Mate in 1971, now stretches to over 40 studio albums released across six decades. Self-produced and increasingly self-released (via his own Sofa Sound and later Fie! Records imprints), his solo work moves freely between art rock, post-punk aggression (Nadir’s Big Chance, 1975), intimate piano confessional (Over, 1977; And Close As This, 1986), and electronic experimentation (Enter K, 1982; Sonix, 1996). He also contributed as a featured musician to Peter Gabriel’s Security (1982) and appeared on Robert Fripp’s Exposure (1979), co-writing material with Fripp.

Van der Graaf Generator

Formation and classic lineup

Formed 1967 in Manchester by Hammill, Chris Judge Smith (Discogs: 405626), and Nick Pearne (Discogs: 3517345, now deceased). The classic lineup that defined the band’s most celebrated period (1970–1978) was:

Nic Potter (bass, Discogs: 326044) played on early releases (The Aerosol Grey Machine, The Least We Can Do Is Wave To Each Other) and returned for later work. Graham Smith (violin, Discogs: 297530) replaced Jackson in the 1977 lineup (The Quiet Zone / The Pleasure Dome). Chas Dickie (Discogs: 344001) also contributed cello in later phases. The band also recorded without Jackson at several points; from September 2006 Van der Graaf Generator has operated as a trio (Hammill / Evans / Banton), without David Jackson.
