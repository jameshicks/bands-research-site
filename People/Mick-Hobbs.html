Mick Hobbs

English multi-instrumentalist, vocalist, and songwriter; born London, died 3 January 2025. Discogs artist ID: 307762. A central figure in the British post-punk / avant-rock underground from 1979 onwards, Hobbs participated in The Work, Officer!, Family Fodder, 1-2 Japanese, The Momes, The Lo Yo Yo, The Lowest Note, Strobe Talbot, and The Paddock. His solo work was released under his own name and credited to Officer! on several key records.

Overview

Hobbs entered the London post-punk scene around 1979–1980, appearing first as a photographer on Crass’s Te Deum (Small Wonder Records, 1979) before joining as a multi-instrumentalist and songwriter. In interview (2022), Hobbs described his pre-Work background: “I’d borrowed my brother’s double bass, and got playing in various jazz situations, as well as rocking out whenever the opportunity presented itself.” Bill Gilonis has stated that Hobbs came to The Work after answering a bass-player ad placed in the Melody Maker around 1978–79, which Gilonis and Tim Hodgkinson placed after they “started playing together in 1978.” No specific pre-Work groups or formal training are documented in any web source to date. [source: https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html, accessed 2026-06-20] He became a member of the loose ensemble Family Fodder, playing bass and percussion on their debut LP Monkey Banana Kitchen (Fresh Records, 1980) and bass and organ on Greatest Hits (Crammed Discs, 1981). Family Fodder was a large rotating collective around frontman Alig (Fiedorowicz) and operated in the post-punk / art-pop zone.

Simultaneously — or shortly after — Hobbs became a core member of The Work, the avant-rock quartet completed by Tim Hodgkinson (Henry Cow), Bill Gilonis, and Rick Wilson. The Work debuted with the single I Hate America (Woof Records, 1981) and produced the LP Slow Crimes (Woof Records, 1982), on which Hobbs played bass, drums, guitar, tape, ukulele, and vocals — a testament to his multi-instrumental range. The band was distributed and championed by Recommended Records, placing them squarely in the RIO / ReR orbit. The Work continued releasing through the 1980s, with Rubber Cage appearing in 1989 (Woof/Ayaa/In-Poly-Sons), a production they handled entirely themselves.

In parallel, Hobbs led or co-led the ensemble Officer!, an ever-shifting collective of European and UK improvisers and composers. Officer!‘s first major document was 8 New Songs By Mick Hobbs (AAA, France, 1983), credited to Officer! but explicitly announcing Hobbs as composer. Ossification (Ayaa, 1984) followed. The 1988 self-released cassette Yes Yes No No Yes No Yes drew on a broad cast of participants including Antoine Gindt, Bill Gilonis, Claudia Schmid, Daniel Koskowitz, Felix Fiedorowicz, and Keisuke Matsui. Officer!‘s 2014 release Dead Unique (Blackest Ever Black) was a compiled retrospective of unreleased material, with Hobbs credited for vocals, guitar, bass, words, music, and engineering — essentially operating as a solo project under the Officer! umbrella.

Hobbs’s only strictly solo-credited release on Discogs is Bandagen (No Records (9), Switzerland, 1989), a cassette on which he played all instruments and wrote all material, with guest contributions from Tim Hodgkinson, Joey Stack, Andy Wake (2), and members of a Swiss musical circle including the Kalahari Surfers’ Warrick Sony (credited via that project’s writing credit). This record stands as the clearest single-authored statement in his discography.

During the late 1980s and early 1990s Hobbs was also active with The Momes, a trio with Hodgkinson and Andy Wake (2) that released Spiralling (Woof Records, 1989), with Hobbs on bass, guitar, sequencer, and voice.

From the 1990s onward Hobbs contributed to a wide range of projects: bass and guitar on Jad Fair / 1-2 Japanese recordings (We Are They Who Ache With Amorous Love, Psycho Acoustic Sounds, 1990); guitar on Maximum Joy-adjacent releases; bass on The Work compilations and live documents. He appeared on Murphy’s Law / Murphy Working Stiffs releases in the 2010s, contributing to multiple Fire Records albums. His late-period work concentrated around the Austrian label Klanggalerie, which released Paragraphs And Principles (2021) and Dead Right (2025), the latter credited to him as composer, voice, instruments, mixer, and field-recording artist. Hobbs died on 3 January 2025, with Dead Right appearing posthumously.

Throughout his career Hobbs operated largely outside the mainstream, working across self-released cassettes, small European labels (Ayaa, No Records (9), Klanggalerie), and on releases that circulated primarily within the international avant-rock / RIO underground.

Open Questions

  • The extent of Hobbs’s involvement with 1/2 Japanese beyond the one documented release. (Note: the Dead Unique Baltimore sessions took place while Half Japanese were there recording Hot in early 1995, which confirms a sustained connection.)
  • Whether Hobbs had any direct ties to the core RIO/ReR institutional network (Chris Cutler, Henry Cow) beyond Hodgkinson’s membership in The Work — Cutler appears as a guest on Slow Crimes but no deeper administrative role documented in Discogs.
  • The “Savoir Faire: The Best Of” release listed in the prior stub has no Discogs match; may be incorrect or a different edition.
  • Context for the later Fire Records era (2017–2025) — which projects these credits belong to is unclear from the data available without web sources.
  • Pre-1979 background: only self-described as playing “in various jazz situations” and “rocking out” before The Work. No pre-Work groups documented.
  • Hobbs’s relationship with Gareth Williams of This Heat / Flaming Tunes: Hobbs was involved in the 2009 Flaming Tunes CD reissue and produced music for Williams’s memorial concert; Officer! later covered multiple Williams / Mary Currie songs. The depth of personal connection during Williams’s lifetime (d. 2001) is not documented.
