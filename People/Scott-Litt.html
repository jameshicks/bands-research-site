Overview

Scott Litt is an American record producer and engineer from New York. He came up through The Power Station studio in the late 1970s, where he accumulated an unusually wide range of engineering credits — disco, pop, rock, film soundtracks — before pivoting to production in the early 1980s. His entry point to the New York underground came through a shared network: in 1981 he co-mixed the studio side (Side A) of 8 Eyed Spy’s self-titled Fetish Records LP alongside Chris Stamey, Jim Sclavunos, and Pat Irwin, with Bob Blank producing those sessions. That same year he produced The dB’s’ second album Repercussion (Albion Records) — one of his earliest full production credits — reinforcing a connection to the New York/Athens power-pop axis that also included Chris Stamey.

Litt’s major commercial breakthrough came in 1987 when R.E.M. hired him to produce Document (I.R.S. Records). He went on to co-produce every R.E.M. album through New Adventures In Hi-Fi (1996), a run of six studio albums and several singles and compilations. The partnership was always a shared credit — every album is produced by “R.E.M. and Scott Litt” — and he also performed harmonica and clarinet on Automatic For The People (1992). Beyond R.E.M. his most significant production work includes the Indigo Girls’ self-titled Epic debut (1989), The Juliana Hatfield Three’s Become What You Are (1993), and Incubus (2)‘s Make Yourself (1999) and Morning View (2001). He also mixed key tracks on Nirvana’s In Utero (1993) and the full Counting Crows debut August And Everything After (1993).

The Sylvain Sylvain credit is notable: Sylvain Sylvain was a former New York Doll, and the session was produced by Tony Bongiovi (Power Station co-founder) and Lance Quinn. Litt’s “vocal arrangements” co-production role on that LP predates the 8 Eyed Spy session by two years and shows early crossover between the Power Station world and the New York underground.

He also engineered the Star Wars Christmas Album (RSO, 1980) and contributed additional recording to Ian Hunter’s You’re Never Alone With A Schizophrenic — neither underground, both significant commercial credits. By 1982 he was mixing and recording for Chic (Tongue In Chic) and engineering the debut Bon Jovi album in 1984.

New York Underground Connections (1981)

In 1981 two credits stand out against the mainstream Power Station work:

8 Eyed Spy (Fetish Records, 1981)
The studio side of 8 Eyed Spy’s posthumous self-titled album (Side A, tracks A1–A5) was co-mixed by Scott Litt, Chris Stamey, Jim Sclavunos, and Pat Irwin. Bob Blank produced and engineered those tracks at Blank Tape Studios. The live side (Side B) was recorded by soundmen Steve Cohen and Neil Catchura and edited/reprocessed by Chris Stamey. Lydia Lunch is the vocalist throughout.

The dB’s — Repercussion (Albion Records, 1981)
Litt produced and recorded The dB’s’ second album, written entirely by Chris Stamey and Peter Holsapple. This is his earliest outright “Producer” credit on a rock album of note. The connection to Stamey appears twice in the same year — a meaningful overlap between his Power Station work and the New York/Carolinas underground-pop world.

R.E.M. Production Run (1987–1996)

Litt co-produced all six R.E.M. studio albums from Document through New Adventures In Hi-Fi, always sharing the credit with the band collectively. He also engineered Document and Green, and mixed Automatic For The People and New Adventures In Hi-Fi.
