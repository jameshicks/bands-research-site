Deborah Scroggins

Deborah Scroggins

Discogs ID: 689442
Role: Bassist, vocalist
Active with ESG: 1981–1983 (99 Records era); 2000 (reunion material)

American bassist and vocalist. One of the founding instrumentalists of ESG, the South Bronx art-funk group built around the Scroggins family. Sister of Renee Scroggins (vocals, guitar), Valerie Scroggins (drums), and Marie Scroggins (congas, vocals).

Career

ESG — 99 Records era (1981–1983)

Deborah played bass on all three studio releases ESG made for 99 Records, forming the rhythmic bedrock alongside Valerie’s stripped drum patterns and Tito Libran’s congas.

The 1981 EP is the most historically significant of the three. The A-side — “You’re No Good,” “Moody,” “UFO” — was produced by Martin Hannett and stands as some of his most spare work; the rhythm section’s locked-in minimalism predates much of what would become post-punk dance music. The B-side — “Earn It,” “ESG,” “Hey!” — was a live mix by Ed Bahlman. The release carried dual catalog numbers, pressed jointly by 99 Records (New York) and Factory Records (Manchester), making it one of the few direct document-links between the No Wave and Factory post-punk scenes.

Written-By credits (1988–1993)

Deborah holds a co-writing credit (alongside other ESG members) on “Party Music / Moody (A New Mood)” (1988, Popular Records) and on a 1993 pressing of “Moody” (C.T. Records / Emerald Sapphire & Gold Records). These written-by credits confirm her compositional stake in ESG’s most-sampled track.

Reunion — A South Bronx Story (2000)

Deborah appears as a performer on A South Bronx Story (2000, Universal Sound), the comprehensive retrospective compiled by Stuart Baker. The compilation draws on multiple ESG eras: the Hannett A-side material, Bahlman-produced B–C-side tracks, and a D-side of newer 1990s recordings by Renee Scroggins. Leroy Glover (bass, an earlier ESG lineup member) is also credited separately, reflecting the compilation’s coverage of different periods. Lorraine Scroggins is credited on “Erase You” (D1). David Miles (3) plays lead guitar. Remasters by Duncan Cowell and Pete Reilly.

Departure and Later Life

Sources conflict on the exact timing of Deborah’s departure. Exclaim! places it around 1984, coinciding with 99 Records closing. She Shreds gives 1987. A MusicianGuide biography states she left “after a battle with drugs” following 1991 activity. The clearest indicator is the Bam-Bam Jam (1987) lineup: that release (credited to ESG II) lists Leroy Glover on bass — Deborah is absent. When ESG reformed after 1987, Renee Scroggins took over bass duties. Deborah did not return for the later lineup, which eventually added daughters Nicole and Chistelle.

Her appearance in the 2000 A South Bronx Story credits appears to reflect the original 1981–83 recordings included on that compilation rather than new recording activity.
