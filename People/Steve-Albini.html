Steve Albini

Recording engineer, musician, writer. Born July 25, 1962, Pasadena, CA. Died May 7, 2024, Chicago, IL. Discogs ID: 59373.

Operated Electrical Audio (formerly Albini’s home studio on Touhy Ave, then purpose-built facility on Belmont Ave, Chicago). Engineered thousands of records on a flat-fee basis, refusing royalty arrangements as a matter of principle. Among his most prominent engineering credits: Pixies Surfer Rosa (1988), Nirvana In Utero (1993), PJ Harvey Rid Of Me (1993), Robert Plant / Jimmy Page No Quarter (1994).

Also a musician in his own right:

  • Big Black (guitar, vocals; 1981–1988; Touch And Go)
  • Rapeman (guitar, vocals; 1987–1989; Touch And Go)
  • Shellac (guitar, vocals; 1992–2024; Touch And Go)

Appeared on Slint’s Tweez (1989) credited as “Some Fuckin’ Derd Niffer” (engineer), and on the Untitled 10” (1994, Touch And Go).

Key Credits (added from Melt-Banana research)

  • Melt-Banana — recorded Speak Squeak Creak (1994) and Scratch Or Stitch (1995, Meldac)

Key Credits (added from Arnold de Boer research)

  • The Ex — recorded Catch My Shoe (2010, Ex Records), at Electrical Audio; the debut album featuring Arnold de Boer as vocalist

Key Credits (added from H-Burns research)

  • H-Burns — recorded and mastered Off The Map (2013, Vietnam (2)); H-Burns is among a small group of French artists to record at Electrical Audio. Bob Weston (Robert Weston) also co-credited on mastering.

Key Credits (added from Head Of David research)

  • Head Of David — produced Dustbowl (1988, Blast First, BFFP 18CD). Lineup: Stephen R. Burroughs (vocals), Eric Jurenovskis (guitar), Justin K Broadrick (drums), Dave Cochrane (bass). Credited as “Steven Albini” on the release.

Key Credits (added from Ut research)

  • Ut — engineered Griller (1989, Torso / Blast First). Charlie D. played drums on most tracks; Albini handled engineering throughout.

Key Credits (added from Black Midi research)

  • Black Midi — recorded live track “953 (Live At Electrical Audio),” included as track C on Hellfire (Rough Trade, 2022); the only track on any of their three studio albums with an Electrical Audio credit.

Death (May 7, 2024)

Albini died of a heart attack at his home in Chicago on May 7, 2024, aged 61. Rolling Stone · NPR

His death came ten days before the scheduled release of Shellac’s sixth (and final) studio album Shellac - To All Trains (Touch and Go, May 17, 2024) — the band’s first record in a decade. The album had been completed and announced in March 2024; Albini had given interviews about it shortly before his death. Wikipedia — To All Trains (as of 2025-06-26) · Consequence of Sound

Impact on Shellac: Wikipedia lists Shellac’s active years as 1992–2024, indicating the band ended with Albini’s death. No public statements from Bob Weston or Todd Trainer about continuing were found. Primavera Sound festival, where Shellac had been scheduled to perform, held a listening party of To All Trains during their slot as a memorial, and renamed a stage in Albini’s honour. Wikipedia — Shellac (as of 2025-06-26)

Writing

Albini was an active music journalist and polemicist from the early 1980s through the 1990s, contributing to several underground and independent publications alongside his engineering and band work.

Matter (Chicago, c. 1983–1986)

Matter was a Chicago-based punk/indie music magazine. Albini was a regular contributor, reportedly writing for it from around 1983 to 1986. crackmagazine.net · chicagomag.com

Known contributions:

  • “Tired of Ugly Fat?” — regular column, ran in each issue. Matter #11 carried an entry expressing frustration at the state of music and calling for “some new blood and some new noise.” crackmagazine.net
  • “Hüsker Dü? Only Their Hairdresser Knows For Sure” — article, Matter Sept. 1983 (Vol. 1, No. 3); included an interview with Bob Mould. crackmagazine.net
  • “The Moron’s Guide to Making a Record” — appeared in Matter #8 and possibly #10. fanzinehemorrhage.com — Matter #10
  • “Good Guitar” — column or feature listing favoured guitars, Matter Sept./Oct. 1984. rateyourmusic.com
  • Full pieces on Breaking Circus and Man-Sized Action appear in Matter #10 (Sept./Oct. 1984); Albini contributed more content to that issue than any other writer. fanzinehemorrhage.com — Matter #10
  • Round-robin record reviews (grading releases A–F). Matter #8 also records reader complaints about his style, to which he responded in the letters section. fanzinehemorrhage.com — Matter #10

Note: Contemporaneous contributor at Matter included Gerard Cosloy (later co-owner of Matador Records). chicagomag.com

Forced Exposure (Boston, c. 1985–1986)

Forced Exposure was a Boston-based independent music fanzine (1982–1993) known for its sardonic, opinionated stance. Albini is listed among its contributors. Wikipedia — Forced Exposure (as of 2024-05-10)

Known contributions:

  • “I like noise” — a short manifesto / evocation of the Big Black sound, published in Forced Exposure #9 (Winter 1986). The widely-quoted passage reads: “I like noise. I like big-ass vicious noise that makes my head spin. I wanna feel it whipping through me like a fucking jolt. We’re so dilapidated and crushed by our pathetic existence we need it like a fix.” Described by multiple sources as “hugely influential.” rollingstone.com · chicagomag.com
  • Article on Patti Pezatti — Forced Exposure #9; a piece about a local Chicago fanzine editor and sister of Jeff Pezatti (singer of Naked Raygun); described by Fanzine Hemorrhage as “horndog” content that would not have survived contemporary scrutiny. fanzinehemorrhage.com
  • “Eyewitness Record Reviews” — record-review feature attributed to Albini in Forced Exposure; includes scathing assessments of bands. Notably his review of the Pixies in a post-Surfer Rosa issue called them “blandly entertaining college rock” and criticised their willingness to be managed. crackmagazine.net · loudersound.com
  • Forced Exposure #10 also has a connection to Albini’s circle (Byron Coley reviewed Big Black in that issue), but Albini’s own direct writing credits in #10 are not confirmed by available sources.

Conflict note: A search-result summary attributed the “I like noise” manifesto to Forced Exposure #10, but the Fanzine Hemorrhage review of #9 (Winter 1986) places Big Black content there and the manifesto matches that context. Issue assignment for #10 remains uncertain — see Research Queue.

The Baffler (1993)

  • “The Problem with Music” — The Baffler, No. 5 (December 1993). Albini’s most widely read essay: a detailed economic breakdown showing how a band receiving a $250,000 major-label advance would still end up roughly $14,000 in debt after selling 250,000 copies, while the label and its ancillaries netted over $3 million. Became a canonical text of DIY/indie ethics in the era of major labels courting punk bands. thebaffler.com

Maximum Rocknroll (1994)

  • “The Problem with Music” — reprinted in Maximum Rocknroll No. 133 (June 1994, “Major Labels” themed issue), which extended the essay’s reach into the hardcore/punk readership. archive.org — MRR #133

Chicago Reader (1994)

  • “Three Pandering Sluts and Their Music-Press Stooge” — open letter published in Chicago Reader, January 27, 1994, directed at critic Bill Wyman after his year-end “Hitsville” column praised Smashing Pumpkins, Liz Phair, and Urge Overkill. Albini called Phair “a fucking chore to listen to,” the Pumpkins “ultimately insignificant,” and Urge Overkill “weiners in suits playing frat party rock.” The letter sparked weeks of reader-response debate. music.newcity.com · crackmagazine.net

Style and reputation

Albini’s written voice was characterised by lacerating wit, confrontational ethics, and a systematic critique of industry co-option of underground music. His writing consistently targeted: the financial exploitation of artists by labels; the complicity of mainstream press in hyping sellout acts; and the standards of recording practice. Multiple obituary pieces described his prose as “fervent, flamboyant, cuttingly funny and often pissed off.” He later acknowledged that some early writing (particularly in Forced Exposure) used language and made personal attacks he came to regret.
