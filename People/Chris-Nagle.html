Biography

Apprenticeship and Pre-Factory Work (1977–1978)

Chris Nagle’s earliest Discogs credits are as an assistant or tape op from 1977–1978, working on sessions for major and independent labels including Polydor and Chrysalis. These include:

  • Almost Alive… (Nova (6), Germany, 1977) — Mix assistant
  • XII — Tangerine Dream (Polydor, Germany, 1978) — Tape Op
  • The Eye of Wendor: Prophecies (Chrysalis, Europe, 1978) — Assistant engineer
  • Tim Hart (Chrysalis, 1979) — Assistant engineer

This apprenticeship period established Nagle’s technical foundation before his Factory Records work began.

The Factory Sound: Primary Engineer under Martin Hannett (1979–1982)

Nagle’s defining contribution is his role as primary engineer on Martin Hannett’s Factory Records sessions from 1979 to 1981 — the recordings that established the signature Factory aesthetic of cavernous drum separation, digital delay treatments, and extreme spatial depth.

His first major credit in this context was Joy Division’s debut album Unknown Pleasures (FACT 10, Factory, 1979), where he is credited as “Engineered By.” Hannett produced; Peter Saville (2) designed the sleeve. The same year Nagle engineered OMD’s “Electricity” single (Dindisc, 1979) — one of Hannett’s key outside-Factory credits in this period.

Through 1980–1982, Nagle was the constant engineering presence across the core Factory catalog, including:

  • The Return of the Durutti Column (FACT 14, Factory, 1980) — shared engineer credit with John Brierley; Hannett produced
  • Flight — A Certain Ratio (FACT 16, Factory, 1980)
  • Pauline Murray and the Invisible Girls (Illusive, 1980) — Hannett’s production vehicle for The Invisible Girls, with Vini Reilly as guest
  • A Factory Quartet (FACO 4, Factory, 1980) — Nagle engineered the Durutti Column and Kevin Hewick sides (tracks A and D)
  • Lips That Would Kiss — Section 25 (Factory Benelux, 1980)
  • To Each… — A Certain Ratio (FACT 22, Factory, 1981)
  • Still — Joy Division (FACT 40, Factory, 1981) — posthumous double LP
  • Movement — New Order (FACT 50, Factory, 1981) — with assistant engineers Flood and John Leckie
  • The Double 12” — New Order (Factory Italy, 1981)
  • Dolphin’s Spurt — Section 25 (Factory, 1981)
  • Harmony — The Wake (Factory, 1982) — co-produced with The Wake; John Leckie assistant
  • Go Exciting / Tingle — Section 25 (Factory, 1982)

On Movement, Nagle’s credit sits alongside two names who would become significant producers in their own right: Flood (then an assistant) and John Leckie.

By 1982 Nagle had also begun taking sole or co-production credits independently of Hannett: Harmony (The Wake, Factory, 1982) credits him as both engineer and producer. That same year he produced The Plateau Phase for Crispy Ambulance on Factory Benelux — another Factory-adjacent act.

Post-Hannett Independence: Cherry Red, Clay Records, and Beyond (1982–1986)

After Hannett’s effective exit from Factory in 1981, Nagle’s engineering career broadened considerably beyond the Factory orbit. He became a recurring presence on Cherry Red Records through 1982–1983:

  • Degenerates (Cherry Red, 1982)
  • Epic Sound Battles Chapter One (Cherry Red, 1982)
  • XOYO (Cherry Red, 1982)
  • Threat to Creation (Cherry Red, 1981) — mixing
  • Enflame (Cherry Red, 1983) — recording

He also built a production relationship with Clay Records (primarily a punk/rock label), producing and engineering for G.B.H. over several releases 1983–1986:

  • City Baby’s Revenge (Roadrunner Records, 1983) — engineer and producer
  • Invasion of the Porky Men (Clay Records, 1984) — producer and engineer
  • Into the Fire — Live (Clay Records, 1985) — mix
  • Oh No It’s G.B.H. Again! (Rough Justice, 1986) — producer
  • The Clay Years — 1981 To 84 (Clay Records, 1986) — engineering retrospective

Other notable credits in this period:

  • Magic, Murder and the Weather — Magazine (Virgin, 1981) — assistant mix
  • War of Words (99 Records, 1981) — mixing engineer
  • Voice of a Generation (No Future Records, 1982) — engineer
  • Temple of Love — The Sisters of Mercy (Merciful Release, 1983) — recorded by
  • Second Empire Justice / Telecommunication — Future Records acts (1983) — producer and writer
  • Another Setting — The Durutti Column (Factory, 1983) — co-produced with Vini Reilly
  • The Guitar and Other Machines — The Durutti Column (Factory, 1987)
  • Valuable Passages — The Durutti Column (Factory, 1986) — co-produced tracks 10–11

Factory Return: Biting Tongues and Late-Period Credits (1986–1989)

By the mid-to-late 1980s Nagle had returned to the Factory fold and was also operating out of Strawberry Studios and Yellow Two Studios in Stockport — the base for What? Noise.

His highest-profile sole production credit in this phase is Compressor — Biting Tongues (FAC 188, Factory, 1987). The sleeve misspells his name as “Chris Nagel.” He produced sides A and B2; Dave Pringle produced B1. The album features Graham Massey, Paddy Steer, Phil Kirby, Howard Walmsley, and Tom Barnish.

Other Factory credits from this period:

  • Night Time Shadows — Section 25 (Factory, 1986) — engineer
  • Freaky Dancin’ / The Egg — Happy Mondays (Factory, 1986) — engineer
  • Bad News Week — Section 25 (Factory, 1987) — engineer
  • Flyaway — The Railway Children (Factory, 1989)
  • Deep Sleep — The Durutti Column (Factory, 1985) — uncredited mix

Also produced Contenders (Transmedia, 1986) including remixing work, and Midnight Madness and Beyond… for G.B.H. (Combat Core, US, 1986). In 1987–1988 he produced multiple releases on the Bodybeat label (Bodybeat, Help, and the combined reissue).

What? Noise (Late 1980s)

Nagle was a founding member of What? Noise alongside Tim Harris and Julia Adamson. The group were sound engineers working out of Strawberry and Yellow Two Studios in Stockport. They also performed live: a UK support tour with Fishbone in 1989 and a university tour with label-mates Finitribe in 1990. They played instruments including stylophone, guitars, and whirly sound hoses, programming drums and sequencers using C Lab software on Atari computers. Concerts in Manchester included performances with Dub Sex and Flatback Four.

The Charlatans and Madchester (1990–1995)

Nagle’s most commercially successful production work came in the early 1990s, when he produced key early Charlatans records:

  • The Only One I Know single (Dead Dead Good / Situation Two, 1990) — their UK hit debut
  • Then EP (Dead Dead Good / Situation Two, 1990)
  • Sproston Green (Dead Dead Good / Situation Two / Virgin, 1991)
  • Over Rising (Situation Two, 1991)
  • Please Be Cruel (Cow / Mute, Japan, 1991)
  • Some Friendly (Situation Two, 1990) — their debut album
  • The Beast Inside (Cow / Mute, 1991) — second album
  • Caravan Remix (Cow / Mute, 1991)
  • Island Head EP (Cow / Mute, 1990) — mix
  • Hit Parade 1 (RCA, 1992) — producer

He also mixed Intastella’s Intastella and the Family of People (MCA, 1991).

Also in this period: Indian Rope (Dead Dead Good, 1990) — produced, being the pre-Only One I Know Charlatans single, and Melting Pot (Beggars Banquet, 1998) — career compilation, producer credit.

1992–Present: Continuing Work and Discogs Archive

Nagle’s production and engineering work continued through the 1990s and beyond, across Decoy Records, Creation Records, and various independent labels:

  • I Call Your Name (Creation Records, 1992) — producer
  • Shaped Up, Shipped Out (Creation Records, 1994) — producer and mix
  • Garage Flower — The Stone Roses (Garage Flower Records, 1996) — uncredited engineer (early session material)
  • The Complete Stone Roses (Silvertone, 1995) — engineer credit
  • Compulsion (Factory Benelux, 2015) — recorded at Strawberry, Stockport

Later credits extend through the 2000s–2020s on smaller labels, with occasional archival engineering work on reissue projects including:

  • Always Now (Factory Benelux, 2019)
  • M24J (Anthology) — Biting Tongues (Factory Benelux, 2018) — producer credit on retrospective
  • Circuses and Bread (Factory Benelux, 2021) — engineered
  • Wadada Magic (Lantern Rec., 2024) — engineer

Connections

  • Joy Division — engineer on Unknown Pleasures (1979) and Still (1981)
  • New Order — engineer on Movement (1981), the transition album following Joy Division’s dissolution
  • A Certain Ratio — recurring engineer on Factory releases
  • The Durutti Column / Vini Reilly — long relationship; engineer through Hannett period, co-producer from 1983 (Another Setting, Valuable Passages, The Guitar and Other Machines)
  • Biting Tongues — produced Compressor (FAC 188, 1987); credited as “Chris Nagel” on sleeve; also producer on M24J (Anthology) (2018)
  • Graham Massey — co-credited on Compressor; Massey later formed 808 State
  • Paddy Steer — Biting Tongues bassist, also Discogs-linked to What? Noise-adjacent Manchester scene
  • The Charlatans — produced debut album Some Friendly and several hit early singles; key commercial relationship

Open Questions

  • Exact studios used for the 1979–1981 Factory sessions: Strawberry Studios (Stockport) and Cargo Studios (Rochdale) are documented for Hannett-era work; Nagle’s specific studio assignments within those sessions are not broken out by Discogs
  • The “Chris Flash Nagle” and “Chris Iceman Nagle” name variants suggest possible roles in DJ or club contexts — not traceable through Discogs alone
  • What? Noise recorded and toured; Discogs lists the group (ID 159347). Julia Adamson now has her own vault page. Tim Harris has no Discogs artist page and remains unresolved
  • The Stone Roses Garage Flower uncredited engineer credit: full context of those early sessions (early 1980s recordings first released 1996) may have additional documentation outside Discogs
  • Whether Nagle’s credit on Temple of Love (Sisters of Mercy, 1983) as “Recorded By” reflects a single session or ongoing relationship with that scene
  • Any web presence or interview material about his working methods with Hannett
