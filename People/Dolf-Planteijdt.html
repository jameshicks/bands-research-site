Dolf Planteijdt

Dutch guitarist, producer, and recording engineer (Haarlem). Discogs artist ID: 638764. Founding member of The Ex, credited on their releases from the 1979–80 debut period through to production credits deep into the 1990s and beyond. Owner and operator of two successive recording studios in the Amsterdam/Haarlem area — ADM’s Koeienverhuurbedrijf (late 1980s–early 1990s) and Koeienverhuurbedrijf in Purmerend (mid-1990s onward, also known as Rent-a-Cow Studio). Elder brother of guitarist/producer Wouter Planteijdt.

Overview

Dolf Planteijdt is one of the founding members of The Ex, appearing on the band’s debut as guitarist and quickly assuming the role of primary studio producer. His fingerprints are on virtually the entire early-to-mid Ex catalog: he produced Disturbing Domestic Peace (1980), History Is What’s Happening (1982), Dignity Of Labour (1983), Blueprints For A Blackout (1984), Pokkeherrie (1985), 1936, The Spanish Revolution (1986), Aural Guerrilla (1988), Joggers & Smoggers (1989), Scrabbling At The Lock (1991, with Tom Cora), and And The Weathermen Shrug Their Shoulders (1993, with Tom Cora).

He maintained a deliberate low public profile, using rotating humorous pseudonyms for tax reasons or as in-jokes: Dolf Anonymousfortaxreasons (ID: 2907446; used on Tumult, 1983), Dolf Koeienverhuur (referencing his studio), Dolf Morzel, and Dolf Zimmerman. Name variations on record include D. Planteijdt, Dolf P., Dolph, and Planteijdt.

Beyond The Ex, he produced and engineered for a wide network of Dutch and international underground bands — Italian hardcore acts Negazione (their first two LPs, 1985–86), Dutch artists on Vögelspin Records, and experimental projects connected to the Amsterdam squatter/ADM scene. His studio — first at ADM, then in Purmerend — was a hub for the Dutch punk/experimental underground through the 1980s and 1990s. He remained active as an engineer and occasional guitarist into the 2010s and 2020s, with credits as late as 2024.

Studios

Dolf Planteijdt owned and operated two successive recording studios:

  • ADM’s Koeienverhuurbedrijf (Discogs label ID: 381331) — located in Amsterdam; active from c.1988 into the early 1990s. Part of the ADM squat/cultural space. Successor to an earlier studio [l362214]. Credited as “ADM’s Koeienverhuurbedrijf”, “Koeienverhuurbedrijf”, or “Koeienverhuur”.
  • Koeienverhuurbedrijf (Discogs label ID: 438122) — in Purmerend, near Amsterdam; successor to ADM’s after the squat was shut down. Active from the mid-1990s onward. Also known as Rent-a-Cow Studio, Soundbound Rent A Cow Studio.

The studio name translates roughly as “cattle rental company” — consistent with his broader taste for absurdist pseudonyms.
