Hugh Davies

Full name: Hugh Seymour Davies
Born: 23 April 1943, Exmouth, Devon, England
Died: 1 January 2005, London, England
Discogs ID: 119006
Name variations: Davies, HD

British electronic music composer, improviser, and instrument builder. The first UK composer to perform “live electronic music.” Renowned for constructing unique DIY electroacoustic instruments — most famously the “Shozyg” series — out of amplified found objects. A central figure in the British free improvisation scene of the late 1960s and 1970s, Davies worked across avant-garde electronics, academic composition, and free jazz, while maintaining a parallel career as a musicologist and archivist of electronic music. His instruments are held in the Science Museum’s permanent collection in London; one Shozyg is in MoMA’s collection in New York.

Biography

Education and Early Training (1961–1966)

Davies studied music at Worcester College, Oxford (1961–64), where fellow students included future members of Monty Python’s Flying Circus. His introduction to electronic music came through Daphne Oram of the BBC Radiophonic Workshop, for whom he produced his first electronic composition at Oram’s Tower Folly studio in London.

From 1964 to 1966 Davies worked as an assistant to Karlheinz Stockhausen in Cologne, Germany. This immersion in Stockhausen’s methods exposed him to live electronic processing — real-time transformation rather than pre-assembled tape music. He appeared with Stockhausen’s ensemble on the landmark 1967 CBS LP Mikrophonie I / Mikrophonie II (release ID: 2117497; reissued 1995 on Stockhausen-Verlag as Vol. 9 of the complete edition), operating filters and potentiometers on the Mikrophonie I side.

Instrument Building and the Shozyg (1967–1970)

Returning to England in 1967, Davies began constructing his own instruments using contact microphones on household objects: egg slicers, fretsaw blades, furniture wheels. His most celebrated creation was the Shozyg — an assemblage of amplified tiny found objects housed inside the hollow cover of an encyclopedia volume (named after the “SHO–ZYG” alphabetic range on the volume’s spine). Davies considered the Shozyg the first device he explicitly designated an “instrument for live electronic performance.”

In 1969 he produced ten hand-numbered Shozygs for an issue of Henri Chopin’s avant-garde art magazine OU. One of these is now in MoMA’s permanent collection.

From the 1970s onward Davies expanded his construction practice to include the Springboard series: arrays of long springs stretched on woodblocks and amplified by electromagnetic pickups.

Goldsmiths, Free Improvisation, and the EMW (1968–1986)

Davies taught at the Electronic Music Workshop (EMW) at Goldsmiths College, London, from 1968 to 1986 — one of England’s earliest academic electronic music studios. During this period he performed in several key British free improvisation ensembles:

  • Gentle Fire (1968–75): founded by Richard Orton at the University of York; performed John Cage, Earle Brown, Christian Wolff, and other post-Cage composers. Davies appeared on their 1974 EMI Electrola LP 4 Systems, Music For Amplified Toy Pianos, Music For Carillon, Edges (release ID: 813554).
  • The Music Improvisation Company (1969–71): co-founded with Derek Bailey (guitar), Evan Parker (saxophone), and Jamie Muir (percussion). Documented on The Music Improvisation Company (ECM 1009, 1970; release ID: 560235) and 1968-1971 (Incus, 1976; release ID: 1508481). The ECM record was produced by Manfred Eicher.
  • Naked Software (1968–73): parallel ensemble active in the same period.
  • Artist Placement Group (APG) (mid-1970s): alongside Ian Breakwell, John Latham, and other British conceptual artists.

Shozyg Music and FMP (1980–1987)

Davies issued his major solo statement, Shozyg Music For Invented Instruments, on FMP in 1982 (release ID: 1059619; master ID: 865792). Composed, produced, and with artwork by Davies; additional production and artwork by Jost Gebers; recorded by Dick Lucas. The album documents the Shozyg instruments in their mature form and remains his most widely circulated solo record. A reissue appeared on BE! Jazz in 2018.

An archival companion, Shozyg I And II (1969), was released on Another Timbre in 2013 (release ID: 7655478), presenting the earliest Shozyg recordings made with Richard Orton (2).

He participated in Company (2) improvisations in May 1983, playing in several trios with Joëlle Léandre, Vinko Globokar, and Evan Parker, documented on Trios (Incus, 1986; release ID: 1786668).

In 1986 Davies recorded with Alan Tomlinson, Phil Minton, and Roger Turner as The Ferals on Leo Records: Ruff (Leo LR 138, 1987; release ID: 1279709). Produced by Leo Feigin. Davies contributed live electronics and co-composition.

Spirit of Eden and Later Career (1988–2005)

Davies appeared on Talk Talk’s Spirit of Eden (1988; release ID: 74337; master ID: 26436), credited as performer on Shozygs. The album was produced by Tim Friese-Greene and Mark Hollis and recorded at Wessex Sound Studios. Davies was one of a large cast of session contributors — including Nigel Kennedy (violin), Danny Thompson (double bass), Henry Lowther (trumpet) — many of whom played without hearing the other parts.

From 1999 until his death, Davies was a part-time researcher and lecturer at Middlesex University’s Centre for Electronic Arts in London.

He died on 1 January 2005 in London. A memorial release, For Hugh Davies, appeared on Another Timbre (at11, 2008; release ID: 1416732), with Adam Bohman (prepared balalaika, amplified objects), Lee Patterson (amplified objects), and Mark Wastell (cello) performing alongside archival Davies material. Recorded and mastered by Simon Reynell.

Musicology and Archivism

Parallel to his performance career, Davies compiled one of the most comprehensive early catalogs of electronic music: International Electronic Music Catalog (Répertoire International Des Musiques Electroacoustiques), co-authored with Pierre Schaeffer (credited at INA-GRM) and published by The MIT Press in 1967. He wrote liner notes for several key releases, including Anna Lockwood’s Glass World (Tangent Records, 1970) and the Sub Rosa anthology An Anthology Of Noise & Electronic Music.
