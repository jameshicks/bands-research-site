Jun 28, 20268 min read

Quin Ivy

Discogs artist ID: 351181
Real name: Quinon Ray Ivy
Born: June 3, 1937, Oxford, Massachusetts
Died: December 10, 2022

Disc jockey turned producer, studio owner, and label entrepreneur. A central though often overlooked figure in the Muscle Shoals / Sheffield, Alabama recording ecosystem of the 1960s. Ivy built his own studio and labels in Sheffield, distributed through Atlantic/Atco, and produced the bulk of Percy Sledge’s Atlantic catalog — including the landmark “When A Man Loves A Woman” (1966). His studio, Quinvy Recording Studio, was an independent alternative to FAME Studios in nearby Muscle Shoals, and his labels — South Camp (sublabel of Quinvy) and Quinvy Custom — documented a range of regional soul and R&B artists throughout the late 1960s.

Biography

Origins: Radio and Songwriting (pre-1965)

Ivy grew up the son of a sharecropper and came up through radio rather than music performance. He worked as a disc jockey at WMPS in Memphis and at WLAY in Muscle Shoals, Alabama — positions that put him close to both the record business and the emerging Muscle Shoals studio scene. During this period he began writing songs and formed a working relationship with producer Rick Hall of FAME Studios (Discogs artist ID: 366660; label ID: 299088). The nature of that collaboration — shared songs, productions — established Ivy’s connections to the infrastructure he would later work alongside, and against, with his own studio.

Quinvy Recording Studio and the Atlantic/Atco Deal (1965–1967)

In 1965 Ivy opened Quinvy Recording Studio in Sheffield, Alabama (address: 1307 Broadway, Sheffield, Alabama 35660 — shared with the South Camp label office). The studio gave him a base independent of FAME. He launched two labels distributed through Atco/Atlantic:

  • Quinvy (label ID: 457359) — parent label, 1960s–1970s
  • South Camp (label ID: 341214) — sublabel of Quinvy, 1960s soul singles
  • Quinvy Custom (label ID: 1590250) — second sublabel

The Atlantic distribution arrangement meant Ivy’s studio work could funnel directly into the major-label pipeline. It also made him a natural producer for Atlantic’s own Percy Sledge sessions: those recordings were made at Quinvy but issued on Atlantic proper.

A music publishing arm, Quinvy Music (later Quinvy Music Corp. / Quinvy Publishing Co., Inc.), ran alongside the labels.

Percy Sledge and Atlantic Records (1966–1974)

Ivy’s most consequential production relationship was with Percy Sledge. He co-produced (almost always alongside Marlin Greene) the entirety of Sledge’s Atlantic output from the debut single through the early 1970s:

Ivy is credited as producer or co-producer on every major Sledge Atlantic LP and the majority of the singles. On later releases he is sometimes credited “Recording Engineer” alongside producer — confirming that Quinvy was the recording site.

The 1974 Capricorn sessions mark the end of the Sledge-Ivy relationship in Discogs records; by then Ivy had sold the studio.

The South Camp singles particularly reveal the Quinvy studio as a hub for multiple independent producers: Spooner Oldham produced two South Camp sides (Bill Brandon, June Edwards), and Eddie Hinton co-produced several Don Varner and Mickey Buckins sessions alongside Paul Ballenger and Robert Gregg.

The Charly Compilation Evidence

A 1989 Charly R&B compilation, You Better Believe It — Rare Soul From The Quinvy/Broadway Sound Studio, Volume 3, documents Ivy’s production share vs. Hinton’s directly:

  • Ivy produced tracks A1–A7, B1–B2, B4–B6, B8
  • Hinton produced A8 and B3
  • David Johnson (7) produced B7

The “Broadway Sound Studio” in the title refers to a later iteration of the recording space, with David Johnson (7) as engineer/producer — suggesting the studio passed through different hands or identities as Ivy wound down his involvement.

Other Sessions at Quinvy Studio

The studio hosted a range of Atlantic / Atco-distributed sessions beyond Sledge:

  • Feed The Flame / She Knows What To Do For Me — artists on ATCO Records, 1967; Ivy as producer
  • Cheaters Never Win (Revue, 1968)
  • Rainbow Road (Tower, 1968) — Ivy producer
  • Lonely Weekend (Uni Records, 1969)
  • Polly Wally / Gentle On My Mind (Revue, 1969) — Ivy producer
  • The Bluest Blues (Excello, 1971) — Ivy credited Producer and Arranged By

Marlin Greene (as engineer/guitarist) was also involved in recording the debut Boz Scaggs album (Atlantic, 1969) at Muscle Shoals — Ivy not credited on that record, but Greene and the same session players link the worlds.

Later Career and Exit (1970s)

Ivy sold Quinvy Recording Studio sometime in the early 1970s. He then pursued an M.A. degree from Mississippi State University and transitioned to teaching accounting courses. No Discogs production credits appear after the 1974 Capricorn Sledge sessions, confirming a clean break from the music business.

He died on December 10, 2022.

Connections

  • Rick Hall — pre-1965 songwriting and production collaboration; ran adjacent FAME Studios in Muscle Shoals
  • Jeanie Greene — background vocalist on Sledge sessions; wife of Marlin Greene
  • FAME Studios — parallel Muscle Shoals recording infrastructure; Ivy’s studio was an independent alternative
