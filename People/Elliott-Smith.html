Elliott Smith

Born: 1969, Omaha, Nebraska
Died: 2003, Los Angeles, California
Active: early 1990s–2003
Real name: Steven Paul Smith
Discogs artist ID: 243300

Singer-songwriter. One of the defining voices of 1990s indie and lo-fi rock. Released early singles on Suicide Squeeze: “Division Day / No Name #6” (S-005, 1997) — one of his first 7” releases — and the posthumous “Pretty (Ugly Before)” b/w “A Distorted Reality Is Now A Necessity To Be Free” (S-028, 2003), sleeve designed by Jesse LeDoux. Died October 2003; SSQ has continued to release archival material. 25 releases on the label as of 2025.

Also recorded for Kill Rock Stars, DreamWorks, and ANTI-. Co-founder of Heatmiser (Portland, 1991–1996); Sam Coomes joined Heatmiser as bassist for their final album Mic City Sons (1996, Caroline Records). Coomes also contributed bass to Smith’s solo album Figure 8 (2000, DreamWorks) and Pretty (Ugly Before) (2004, Domino).

Suicide Squeeze Records — origin of the relationship

David Dickenson founded Suicide Squeeze in August 1996 in Seattle. He had been a devoted fan of Smith since Smith’s debut, Roman Candle (1994, Cavity Search Records): “I was just a big fan of his ever since he put out his first record on Cavity Search, Roman Candle.” (David Dickenson interview, “so flawed and drunk and perfect still” fan archive, 2017-01-11) The Seattle Met interview adds context on where that devotion formed: Dickenson was living in Hawaii at the time, making a cassette with Roman Candle on one side and OutKast’s Southernplayalisticadillacmuzik on the other, which he played on his work commute. Smith was “at the top of my [artist] wish list” — Dickenson’s words — before the label even launched. (Seattle Met, “A Fiendish Conversation with Suicide Squeeze’s David Dickenson”, August 2016)

Dickenson attended many early Smith performances in Seattle — shows with audiences of around 20–25 people — but when he came to ask Smith about releasing a single on his fledgling label, nerves held him back. His wife Polly ultimately made the direct approach; Smith agreed enthusiastically (“Yeah, I’d love to”). (Bandcamp Daily, Suicide Squeeze 20th anniversary label profile, c. 2016; so flawed and drunk and perfect still, 2017-01-11)

The result was “Division Day / No Name #6” (S-005, 1997) — the label’s fifth release, following debuts from 764-HERO (S-001), The Scenic Vermont (S-002, S-004), and Modest Mouse (S-003). Dickenson described the collaboration as “such an honor.” The single has remained in print continuously. (Bandcamp Daily profile; catalog chronology via Wikipedia, Suicide Squeeze Records discography, as of 2025-07-28)

Dickenson’s wife Polly is the same Polly Johnson who drummed for 764-HERO, the band whose single (S-001) launched the label — the personal and musical networks overlapped closely. The Wikipedia article on 764-HERO confirms Dickenson was “Atkins’s coworker and Johnson’s partner” when the label was established; the Seattle Weekly profile further identifies Johnson as a “founding force behind the label during its early years.” (Wikipedia, 764-HERO, as of 2026-07-29; Seattle Weekly, “No Sleaze, All Squeeze”)
