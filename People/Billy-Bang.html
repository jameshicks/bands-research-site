Billy Bang

Billy Bang

Discogs ID: 174749 Real name: William Vincent Walker Born: 20 September 1947, Mobile, Alabama, USA Died: 11 April 2011, The Bronx, New York, USA (aged 63) Name variations: B. Bang, Billy “Bang” Walker, Billy Bang Walker, Billi Bang

American free jazz violinist and composer. One of the few violinists to establish a sustained presence in the post-Coltrane avant-garde, Bang brought a raw, blues-inflected attack to the instrument, resisting the instrument’s classical associations entirely. He co-founded the String Trio of New York in 1977, led his own groups across five decades under his name and various ensemble names, and recorded for Soul Note, Delmark Records, hat ART (hat Hut/hat MUSICS), Celluloid, and Justin Time. He is best known for two late-career Vietnam War suites — Vietnam: The Aftermath (2001) and Vietnam: Reflections (2005) — which drew on his own service in Vietnam.

Biography

Early Life and Vietnam Service

William Vincent Walker was born on 20 September 1947 in Mobile, Alabama. He grew up in Harlem, New York, and took up violin as a child. He served in the US Army in Vietnam in the 1960s — an experience that haunted him for decades and eventually became central to his most significant work as a bandleader.

1970s: Entering the New York Avant-Garde

By the mid-1970s Bang was working in the New York free jazz scene. His earliest Discogs-documented appearance is on Sound Craft ‘75 Fantasy For Orchestra (1975, Anima Productions), a large-ensemble recording that placed him alongside other avant-garde players circulating through New York.

In 1977 he co-founded the String Trio of New York with guitarist James Emery and bassist John Lindberg — a pioneering all-strings improvising trio that would record extensively for Black Saint through the 1980s. The String Trio’s debut First String (1979, Black Saint) established the group’s chamber-free sound. Bang remained a core member through the group’s classic period.

Also in this period he appeared on Eye On You (1980, About Time Records) as a sideman.

1980: First Solo Record

Distinction Without A Difference (1980, hat Hut Records) was Bang’s first solo release — violin alone, issued on Werner X. Uehlinger’s Swiss label. The hat Hut imprint (ID: 27173) was a focal point for European-circuit free improvisation, and the release placed Bang in that context early.

1981–1985: Soul Note, Celluloid, and the Quintet Years

The early-to-mid 1980s were Bang’s most prolific period as a bandleader. Rainbow Gladiator (1981, Soul Note) was the first Billy Bang Quintet album, featuring Charles Tyler (alto and baritone sax), Michele Rosewoman (piano), Wilber Morris (bass), and Denis Charles (drums). Soul Note producer Giovanni Bonandrini documented many of Bang’s best groups across this decade.

Outline No. 12 (1982, Celluloid) was a large-ensemble date that reads like a downtown NYC summit: David Murray (bass clarinet), Charles Tyler (clarinet), Butch Morris (conductor), Frank Lowe (soprano sax), Khan Jamal (vibraphone), Jason Hwang (violin), Sunny Murray (percussion), recorded by Martin Bisi. The album was issued on the Celluloid label, with Bang also credited for liner notes and composition.

Bangception (1983, hat MUSICS) was a duo with drummer Denis Charles — spare, focused, and issued on Uehlinger’s Swiss imprint again.

Sideman appearances in this period include:

  • Butch Morris’s Coup De Tête (1981, American Clavé)
  • Fred Frith’s Memory Serves (1981, Celluloid)
  • Ronald Shannon Jackson And The Decoding Society’s Decode Yourself / Mandance sessions
  • David Murray’s Dimension 5 (1982, Black Saint) and Live In Berlin (1984, Black Saint)
  • Ronald Shannon Jackson sessions

Another Kind Of Groove (1987, Sound Aspects Records) was a collaboration with Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio — the beginning of a long association with percussionist Kahil El’Zabar and AACM-connected players; that session also featured Malachi Favors (bass).

1987–1991: Material, Live Work, and Sideman Credits

Bang appeared as string soloist on Bootsy Collins’s What’s Bootsy Doin’? (1988, CBS) — a rare mainstream pop credit. He also played on Less More (1991, Circulasione Totale) and Valve No.10 (1991, Soul Note).

His connection to the Material project appears in his group affiliations list, placing him in Bill Laswell’s orbit.

1993: A Tribute To Stuff Smith (with Sun Ra)

A Tribute To Stuff Smith (1993, Soul Note) is one of Bang’s most unusual records: a trio with Sun Ra (piano, synthesizer), John Ore (bass), and Andrew Cyrille (drums). It was Sun Ra’s final studio recording before his death in May 1993. Bang’s homage to Stuff Smith — the 1930s–40s jazz violinist who established a template of hot, blues-rooted violin — also implicitly positioned Bang within that lineage. Produced by Giovanni Bonandrini for Soul Note.

1995: Big Cliff (Delmark, with Kahil El’Zabar)

Big Cliff (1995, Delmark Records) was a co-billed date with Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio, featuring Kahil El’Zabar (drums, percussion, thumb piano), Malachi Favors (bass), and Ari Brown (tenor sax, piano). Produced by Robert G. Koester for Delmark.

1997–2000: Justin Time and CIMP

Bang On! (1997, Justin Time) was Bang’s first release on the Montreal jazz label run by Jim West. Bang is credited as producer as well as performer.

The Power (2000, CIMP) was a three-way co-bill with Kahil El’Zabar’s Tri-Factor and Hamiet Bluiett, released on Bob Rusch’s Cadence Improvised Music Productions imprint. CIMP specialized in unedited, unprocessed live-to-two-track recordings of improvised music.

2001: Vietnam: The Aftermath

Vietnam: The Aftermath (2001, Justin Time) was the first of two suites Bang wrote drawing on his Vietnam War service. The ensemble was large and deliberately assembled from players who had also served: Bang (violin, composer, producer), Frank Lowe (tenor sax, tracks 4, 5, 8), Ted Daniel (trumpet), John Hicks (piano), Curtis Lundy (bass), Michael Carvin (drums), Ron Brown (percussion), Sonny Fortune (flute, tracks 7–8), and Butch Morris (conductor). Recorded and engineered by Jim Anderson; produced by Bang and Jean-Pierre Leduc (Justin Time).

The album was a critical landmark in late-career free jazz — not just as political statement but as compositional achievement, with Morris’s conduction holding the ensemble together across long arcs.

2003: Above & Beyond

Above & Beyond: An Evening In Grand Rapids (Radio Edits) (2003, Justin Time) was a co-billed live date with Frank Lowe. It was among Lowe’s final recordings before his death in 2003. See Frank Lowe for full context.

2005: Vietnam: Reflections and Live At The River East Art Center

Vietnam: Reflections (2005, Justin Time) was the second Vietnam suite, with a similar large ensemble: James Spaulding (alto sax, flute), Henry Threadgill (flute), Ted Daniel (trumpet), John Hicks (piano), Curtis Lundy (bass), Michael Carvin (drums), Ron Brown (percussion), Butch Morris (conductor), plus Vietnamese musicians Co Boi Nguyen (vocals) and Nhan Thanh Ngo (dan tranh zither). Bang wrote, arranged, produced, and mixed the record; some tracks draw on traditional Vietnamese material. Recorded by Jim Czak; mastered by Renée Marc-Aurèle.

Live At The River East Art Center (2005, Delmark Records) was another Kahil El’Zabar Ritual Trio collaboration, this time with Ari Brown, Yosef Ben Israel (bass), and Bang on electric violin.

Later Career and Legacy

Bang continued to perform and record into the late 2000s. He appeared on records by William Parker, Ahmed Abdullah, and others in the New York free jazz community. The posthumous compilation (Music From The Film) Lucky Man (2021, BBE / IF Music) brought some of his work to a wider audience after his death.

He died on 11 April 2011 in The Bronx at age 63.
