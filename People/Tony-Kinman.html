Tony Kinman

Tony Kinman

Discogs ID: 408498
Also credited as: Tony Nineteen; A. Kinman; T. Kinman; Kinman
Born: 1956
Died: May 4, 2018 (pancreatic cancer)

American bassist, vocalist, and songwriter. Co-founder of The Dils and Rank & File, two of the defining bands of the American punk and cowpunk movements respectively. Throughout his career he worked almost exclusively in close partnership with his brother Chip Kinman.

Biography

Formation and Early Years — The Dils (1977–1980)

Tony Kinman and his brother Chip Kinman formed The Dils in Carlsbad, San Diego County, California, before the band relocated first to San Francisco and then to Los Angeles. The Dils cycled through several lineups over their active years, with Tony on bass/vocals and Chip on guitar/vocals providing the consistent core in every configuration. Early drummers included Rand McNally, Endre Alqover, Bill Chobotar (aka Zippy Pinhead), and John Silvers.

The Dils were part of the first wave of the LA punk scene and were sometimes dubbed “California’s The Clash” — a comparison driven by their overtly political lyrics. Their debut 7” “I Hate The Rich” / “You’re Not Blank” (What Records?, 1977) is one of the essential documents of that moment. The follow-up Dangerhouse 7” “198 Seconds of The Dils” (1977), featuring “Class War” and “Mr. Big,” consolidated the reputation. Both Kinman brothers shared all songwriting credits throughout.

The final Dils release was the three-sided 12” EP Made in Canada (Rogelletti Records, 1980), recorded in Vancouver with producers Bob Rock and Gerry Barad. The record marked a significant stylistic shift toward melody and country-tinged rock — a direct preview of where the brothers were heading next. The Dils disbanded in 1980.

Note: during the San Francisco period Tony Kinman was also briefly a member of an unnamed local group (referenced in The Dils’ Discogs profile but not identified by name in the database).

Cowpunk Pioneer — Rank & File (1981–1987)

After the Dils dissolved, Tony and Chip Kinman relocated to Austin, Texas and founded Rank & File in 1981 alongside Alejandro Escovedo, who had previously played in The Nuns. Rank & File are widely credited as one of the founding acts of the cowpunk genre — marrying the energy and DIY ethos of punk with the instrumentation and sensibility of classic country.

Their debut LP Sundown (Slash Records, 1982) was produced by David Kahne with the full classic lineup: Tony (bass, vocals), Chip (guitar, vocals, harmonica), Alejandro Escovedo (guitar, vocals), and Slim Evans (drums). The bulk of the songwriting was credited to “Kinman and Kinman,” with a handful of tracks co-credited to Escovedo. The title track “Rank and File” (A3) carries a writing credit shared between Escovedo, Tony Kinman, and classic country songwriters Don Sessions and Ned Miller.

By the time of the second album Long Gone Dead (Slash / Metronome, 1984), Escovedo had departed. The record was produced by Jeff Eyrich with an expanded session lineup including Stan Lynch (drums, percussion), Richard Greene (fiddle), Jeff Ross (guitar), and Pete Grant (pedal steel, slide, autoharp, banjo). Tony and Chip remained the songwriting core.

Rank & File continued releasing material through 1987, including a live album (Live!, Triple X Records) and a German compilation (Rank And File, Zensor). The band also appeared on the 1985 Rhino compilation Tales of the New West, for which Tony contributed backing vocals.

Electronic Detour — Blackbird (1987–1992)

Following the close of Rank & File’s active period, Tony and Chip Kinman formed Blackbird (295001), an electronic duo that represented a sharp stylistic break from their previous work. The Blackbird debut EP (Iloki Records, 1987) was co-produced by the Kinmans and Ric Wallace; it was followed by further releases on Iloki (1989), Fundamental (1990, European release), and Scotti Bros. Records (1992). The Scotti Bros. era produced Class War (1992) — a direct callback to the Dils track — which received remixes by industrial outfit KMFDM, released as Class War: The KMFDM Remixes (Scotti Bros., 1992). A single “Take Me” also appeared on Fully Radical / Scotti Bros. in 1992.

The Blackbird phase found the Kinmans exploring lo-fi indie rock and electronic textures; the brothers self-produced most of the material and held all writing credits.

Later Projects — Cowboy Nation and The Tonys (1990s)

By the mid-1990s Tony and Chip Kinman had shifted again, this time toward a stripped-back country and folk sound under the name Cowboy Nation (1557960). The self-titled Cowboy Nation album (Cortex, 1996, Australian release) was produced by the band themselves; members included Tony (bass, dobro bass, vocals), Chip (guitar, harmonica, vocals), ‘Taco’ John Norman (drums), and Jamie Spidle. The record mixed originals with traditional arrangements (“Old Paint,” “Cowboy’s Lament”).

Tony and Chip also performed as The Tonys (2037663), a duo vehicle that appears in the Discogs credits for the 2021 European release Some Things Never Change (Restless Empire) — the last Kinman Brothers release in the database, drawing on Dils-era material.

Retrospective and Legacy

The Dils’ back catalog was compiled on Dils Dils Dils (Lost Records, 1990), a career-spanning LP drawing on studio material from 1977–79 and live recordings from 1978–79 across multiple drummer lineups. Tony and Chip oversaw reissue production on The Slash Years (Rhino Handmade, 2003), which collected the Rank & File Slash Records output. A later compilation re-release of Dils Dils Dils appeared on Bacchus Archives in 2001, alongside a Bacchus reissue of Class War.

Tony also contributed additional vocals to the 1994 Sympathy for the Record Industry comp Graciasland.

Tony Kinman died on May 4, 2018, of pancreatic cancer.
