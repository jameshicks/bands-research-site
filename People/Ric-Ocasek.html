Ric Ocasek

Singer, guitarist, keyboardist, and record producer. Born Richard Theodore Otcasek, 23 March 1944, Baltimore, Maryland. Died 15 September 2019, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 113688.

Frontman, principal songwriter, and self-producer of The Cars, one of the most commercially successful new wave bands of the late 1970s and early 1980s. In parallel with The Cars, Ocasek built a substantial outside production career that cut across punk, new wave, post-punk, and alternative rock — beginning with Suicide in 1979–80 and extending through the mid-2010s with clients ranging from Bad Brains and Bad Religion to Weezer and Motion City Soundtrack.

Biography

Early Life and Milkwood

Ocasek grew up in Baltimore and Cleveland. In the early 1970s he formed an acoustic folk duo, Milkwood (2), with Benjamin Orr, recording one album, How’s The Weather? (Paramount Records, 1973). Ocasek played acoustic guitar and sang. The partnership would persist into The Cars.

Cap’n Swing and the Formation of The Cars

Before The Cars, Ocasek and Orr played in an interim Boston band, Cap’n Swing, which served as the direct evolutionary step. The Cars formed in Boston circa 1976–77 with Ocasek (vocals, rhythm guitar), Benjamin Orr (bass, vocals), Elliot Easton (lead guitar), Greg Hawkes (keyboards), and David Robinson (2) (drums).

The Cars (1978–1987)

The Cars’ debut album The Cars (Elektra, 1978) was produced by Roy Thomas Baker and launched the band immediately with “Just What I Needed” and “My Best Friend’s Girl.” Occasek wrote or co-wrote virtually the entire catalog. The band released six studio albums on Elektra through 1987:

The Cars reunited in 2010 without Benjamin Orr (who died of pancreatic cancer in 2000) and recorded Move Like This (Hear Music, 2011), their final studio release. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2018. Ocasek died in September 2019.

Solo Career

Ocasek began issuing solo material in 1982. His first three solo albums were on Geffen Records:

Beatitude (1982) was produced and performed almost entirely by Ocasek, with him playing guitar, keyboards, and vocals. The single “Jimmy Jimmy” was released separately on Geffen/Trouser Press. This Side Of Paradise yielded “Emotion In Motion” (1986), his most successful solo single. Nexterday (2005, Sanctuary) saw him credited additionally for illustration and playing all other instruments.

Production Work

Ocasek’s production career is one of the more eclectic in American rock. He worked across punk, post-punk, indie rock, and new wave from the late 1970s onward, and continued producing commercially significant records into the 2010s.

Notable Production Notes

Suicide (1979–1988). The longest-running outside production relationship in Ocasek’s career. He produced Suicide’s three studio associations: the Dream Baby Dream single (Island, 1979), the Alan Vega · Martin Rev LP (Island/ZE, 1980), and A Way Of Life (Chapter 22, 1988). The 1980 LP was a sharp departure from Suicide’s raw debut — synthetic, metronomic, heavily influenced by Giorgio Moroder’s disco productions. Engineer on that session was Larry Alexander; assistants included Garry Rindfuss and James Farber. Ocasek also photographed the A Way Of Life cover.

Bad Brains — Rock For Light (1983). Ocasek produced the original PVC Records release of Rock For Light, the Bad Brains’ second full-length and their defining document of ROIR-era hardcore/reggae fusion. Engineer Ian Taylor; assistant Walter Turbitt. The album went through multiple reissues and became one of the most heavily pressed releases in Ocasek’s production catalog (62 pressings counted).

Weezer (1994–2014). Ocasek produced Weezer’s debut Blue Album (DGC, 1994) — one of the defining alternative rock LPs of the decade — and returned for the Green Album (Geffen, 2001) and Everything Will Be Alright In The End (Republic, 2014). The 1994 album’s engineer was Chris Shaw; personnel: Rivers Cuomo (vocals/guitar), Matt Sharp (bass), Brian Bell (guitar), Pat Wilson (drums). The 2014 album was mixed by Tom Lord-Alge and marks Ocasek’s last major commercial production.

Bad Religion — The Gray Race (1996). Co-produced with Bad Religion themselves. Engineer: Bruce Calder. Personnel included Greg Graffin (vocals), Greg Hetson, Brian Baker (both guitar), Jay Bentley (bass), Bobby Schayer (drums).

Guided By Voices — Do The Collapse (1999). Ocasek served as producer, recording engineer, mixer, and keyboardist on this TVT Records LP — a highly produced departure from Robert Pollard and GBV’s lo-fi home-recording aesthetic that proved divisive with the band’s fanbase. String arrangements by David Soldier.

ZE Records Connection

Ocasek’s outside production work in the early 1980s ran largely through or alongside ZE Records, the New York new wave / mutant disco label co-founded by Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban. The Alan Vega productions Saturn Strip, Video Babe, and Just A Million Dreams all appeared on ZE. The Suicide Alan Vega · Martin Rev LP was co-released on ZE and Island. This places Ocasek in close proximity to the ZE roster (Suicide, Was (Not Was), Kid Creole, etc.) during that label’s peak creative period. Note: Earlier versions of this file incorrectly attributed these Alan Vega albums to Wayne County / Jayne County — the Discogs database contains no Wayne County or Jayne County releases on ZE Records.
