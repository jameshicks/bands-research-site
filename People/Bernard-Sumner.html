Bernard Sumner

Discogs IDs: 33928 (Bernard Sumner), 535777 (Bernard Albrecht)
Born: 4 January 1956, Broughton, Salford, Manchester
Real name: Bernard Sumner (mother’s maiden name, on birth certificate). Adopted by stepfather James Dickin; sometimes credited as Bernard Dicken.
Also known as: Bernard Albrecht (Joy Division era), Be Music (production alias), Barney Rubble (2), B.A. Digham

Singer, guitarist, keyboardist, producer. Co-founder of Joy Division and New Order; co-founder of Electronic. One of the central figures of the post-punk and Madchester eras. Before music, worked at Cosgrove Hall Productions (animation studio, Chorlton, Greater Manchester) — an animators’ credit on a TV adaptation of The Wind in the Willows.

Warsaw / Joy Division (1976–1980)

Sumner was a founding member. The group began as Stiff Kittens (named by Tony Wilson), performed their first gig as Warsaw, and renamed themselves Joy Division at the start of 1978 before releasing their debut EP. Sumner played guitar and keyboards throughout, credited as Bernard Albrecht.

Joy Division lineup: Ian Curtis (vocals, occasional guitar), Sumner (guitar, keyboards), Peter Hook (bass), Stephen Morris (drums). Produced primarily by Martin Hannett for Factory Records.

Key releases (all Factory):

  • An Ideal For Living EP (1978, Enigma Records — pre-Factory)
  • Unknown Pleasures (1979, FACT 10)
  • Closer (1980, FACT 25)
  • Love Will Tear Us Apart / Atmosphere (1980, FAC 23)

Ian Curtis died by suicide on 18 May 1980. The three remaining members — Sumner, Hook, and Morris — had agreed before Curtis’s death that if any member left, the band would change its name. They became New Order.

New Order (1980–present)

Sumner stepped into the vocalist role despite no prior experience as a lead singer. Gillian Gilbert (keyboard, guitar) joined in late 1981 — her presence on New Order’s debut Movement (1981) was limited, but she became integral from Power, Corruption & Lies (1983) onward.

The band’s pivot to electronic dance music — most decisively with Blue Monday (1983, FAC 73) — was largely driven by Sumner and Morris’s interest in synthesizers, sequencers, and drum machines. Sumner co-wrote and co-produced across all New Order studio albums.

Peter Hook left New Order in 2010 after a prolonged acrimonious split; replaced for live work and recording by Tom Chapman (2). Phil Cunningham had joined on guitar/keyboards in 2001. The current lineup is Sumner, Morris, Gillian Gilbert (returned 2011), Cunningham, and Chapman.

Be Music

Production alias used by individual New Order members for outside work. Sumner used “Be Music (Bernard Sumner)” as producer and composer on Section 25’s From The Hip (Factory FACT 90, recorded at Rockfield Studios, Monmouthshire). The Be Music credit on Discogs is shared with Peter Hook, Stephen Morris, and Gillian Gilbert — each member used the alias independently. Two “Be Music” remix credits appear on the From The Hip 1998 LTM reissue (Discogs 247465), remixing “Looking From A Hilltop” alongside Donald Johnson.

Electronic (1988–1999)

Side project with Johnny Marr (formerly of The Smiths). Initially conceived as a Sumner solo vehicle before becoming a duo. Signed first to Factory, then to Parlophone.

Debut single: Getting Away With It (1989, Factory) — featured Neil Tennant on vocals.

Self-titled debut album Electronic (1991, Warner Bros.):

  • Sumner: vocals, keyboards, programming, production
  • Johnny Marr: guitar, keyboards, programming, production
  • Guest: Neil Tennant (vocals, co-writer on “Getting Away With It” and “The Patience Of A Saint”); Chris Lowe (keyboards on two tracks)
  • Guest: Denise Johnson (vocals on “Get The Message”)
  • Guest: Donald Johnson (drums, “Tighten Up”, “Feel Every Beat”)

Second album: Raise The Pressure (1996, Parlophone). Third: Twisted Tenderness (1999, Parlophone). On indefinite hiatus since 1999. Compilation: Get The Message: The Best Of Electronic (2006, EMI).

Bad Lieutenant (2008–2011)

Band formed with members overlapping with New Order: Phil Cunningham and Tom Chapman (2) were both involved, alongside younger musicians Jake Evans (vocals, guitar, co-writer), Jack Mitchell (2) (drums), Matt Evans (5) (guitar, backing vocals, co-writer on some tracks). Sumner as co-vocalist and co-producer.

One studio album: Never Cry Another Tear (2009, Triple Echo). Notable for guest bass contributions from Alex James (2) (Blur) on four tracks (“Walk On Silver Water”, “Shine Like The Sun”, “Falling Trees”, “Split The Atom”). Stephen Morris contributed additional drums and programming on several tracks. Andy Robinson (a recurring New Order/Electronic associate) handled additional recording.

Solo and Collaboration Notes (verified from DB)

“This Time I’m Not Wrong” (1997)

Discogs release ID 95525 / 62731. Credited as Sub Sub with Bernard Sumner (and variant billing Bernard Sumner / Sub Sub). Released on Robs Records (a Manchester independent). Written by Sumner, Jimi Goodwin, Andy Williams (4), and Jez Williams — the core Sub Sub lineup that became Doves. Produced and recorded by Sub Sub; Jim Spencer recorded tracks 1–2. Four tracks total.

”Crystal” (2001)

Discogs master 2216260. Primary credit “Crystal” on MFS (Germany) and master 460796 on Flesh label — both as primary Bernard Sumner release. Distinct from New Order’s “Crystal” (2001).

”Riptide” (2023)

Discogs release 26941154. Collaboration with Mella Dee (electronic producer). Bernard Sumner: lead vocals. Released on Three Six Zero Recordings.

Miracle Cure (2008)

Discogs release 1350836. Sumner: “Vocals [Sung By]“. Primary credit. Maelstrom Records.
