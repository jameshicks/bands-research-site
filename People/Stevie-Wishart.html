Aug 07, 20265 min read

Stevie Wishart

Discogs ID: 255733 Name variations: SW, Steve Wishart, Wishart

English composer, improviser, and performer on hurdy-gurdy and medieval violin. Based in the UK, active from at least 1980 through the 2010s. One of the rare figures who bridges medieval early music scholarship and free improvisation / experimental electronics.

Biography

Wishart was educated at Cambridge, Oxford, and the Guildhall School of Music. She studied composition and electronic music at the University of York with composer Trevor Wishart and Richard Orten, then studied improvised and aleatoric music with John Cage and David Tudor.

Her career operates on two distinct but intersecting axes:

Medieval and early music (Sinfonye): She is the founder and director of Sinfonye, a medieval music ensemble specialising in repertoire c.1000–1400, with a focus on female poet-composers. Sinfonye released its catalog on Hyperion Records in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and on Glossa (Spain) in 1997. Sinfonye’s core members over the years included Jim Denley (percussion), Paula Chateauneuf (lute), Bonnie Shaljean (harp), Jocelyn West (vocals), Vivien Ellis (vocals), Pedro Estevan (percussion), Guy Sigsworth, and Moira Smiley.

Experimental / sound-art (Machine For Making Sense): She is a core member of Machine For Making Sense, the Australian sound/text group alongside Jim Denley, Amanda Stewart, Chris Mann, Rik Rue, and Shane Fahey. The group released On Second Thoughts (1994, Tall Poppies) and represents Wishart’s engagement with text-sound, improvisation, and aleatoric composition.

She was also a member of the Welsh folk/early music ensemble Aberjaber in the 1980s, where she played violin, viola, crwth, and hurdy-gurdy alongside Delyth Evans (harp) and Peter Stacey (flute/pipes). Their self-titled LP came out on Sain (1985). She also appears on two earlier Sain releases from 1980 and 1982 alongside Welsh musicians.

Her sound-art practice draws on digital sound technologies for creating, processing, and visualising sound in space, in dialogue with the hurdy-gurdy’s medieval technology and iconography. Sound installations include Ut v.2 with artist Joan Grounds (Contemporary Arts Centre of South Australia). She has worked with Margie Medlin on film sound.

She has also edited and recorded the complete works of Hildegard of Bingen.

Additional group affiliations: Chris Burn’s Ensemble (free improv, UK), Cromlech (2), and The Little Red Spiders.

The Medieval/Experimental Crossover

Wishart is a key figure in the overlap between medieval early music performance and experimental improvisation — a relatively rare axis. The hurdy-gurdy is central to this: as both a medieval instrument and a drone-based sound source amenable to electronics processing, it sits naturally in both worlds. Her studies with John Cage and David Tudor placed her in the American experimental lineage while her Sinfonye work positioned her in the European early music revival. Machine For Making Sense connected her to Australian avant-garde circles (Sydney free improv scene, Fish of Milk label via The Necks collaboration). The 2006 Compass, Log, And Lead on Intakt brought these threads into the European jazz/improv world via Fred Frith and Carla Kihlstedt.
