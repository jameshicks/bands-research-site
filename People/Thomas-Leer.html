Overview

Thomas Leer is a Scottish electronic musician and vocalist, born Thomas Wishart in 1953 in Port Glasgow. He was among the earliest UK artists to self-release home-recorded electronic-influenced music with a DIY punk ethos, predating the synthpop mainstream by several years. His debut 7”, “Private Plane” b/w “International” (Oblique Records, ER101, 1978), was entirely home-recorded using vocals and instruments performed and produced by Leer alone — a landmark of UK DIY electronics. Despite sounding electronic, the record used no synthesizers: guitar, bass, a Roland organ rhythm box, and a Stylophone were the instruments, processed through tape echo and effects pedals. [source: Electronic Sound “Bedroom Bedlam” feature; Electronics & Music Maker Sep 1984 interview]

In 1979, Leer collaborated with fellow Scottish electronic musician Robert Rental on The Bridge (Industrial Records, IR0007), a split LP of minimal synth, ambient, and experimental electronics. The record was recorded on home equipment and was instrumental in establishing the possibilities of home studio production in the UK independent scene. The two sides of the LP divide along the artists’ distinct approaches: Leer’s contributions favour synthesizer melody and processed vocals; Rental’s tend toward loops, tape manipulation, and layered voice.

Leer moved to Cherry Red for his solo work in the early 1980s, releasing the “4 Movements” EP (1981) and the double-LP Contradictions (1982) — the latter noted for its blend of electronic pop, industrial texture, and synth-funk. The compilation album All About You (Cherry Red, 1982) collects period material. His LP The Scale Of Ten (Arista, 1985) marked a move to a major label and a more polished synth-pop sound, with string arrangements by Anne Dudley and production by Paul Hardiman.

In 1986, Leer formed the duo Act with Claudia Brücken of Propaganda, releasing on ZTT. The group made three charting singles (“Snobbery & Decay”, “Absolutely Immune”, “I Can’t Escape From You”) and one album, Laughter, Tears And Rage (1988), produced variously by Act themselves, Stephen Lipson, Trevor Horn, and Greg Walsh. The project satirised the music industry’s glitz while operating within it; it dissolved after the album without a significant chart breakthrough.

From the 2000s Leer released material through his own label, Future Historic, including archival EP series drawing on 1979, 1982, and 1983 recordings. He also recorded as Reel Mothas (a hip-hop-inflected project featuring MC Earl Mathis) on Future Historic. His 2003 album Conversation Peace was released on Avatar Records / NMC (Israel), co-produced with Avichay Avigal. His 2019 vinyl debut Saving, Drum appeared on Emotional Rescue.

Notes (web research)

Conversation Peace (2003) — Avatar Records / NMC and Avichay Avigal

Avatar Records is a Tel Aviv-based independent electronic music label, founded in 1997 by Avichay Avigal and Peter Eibenschutz. When it launched, Avatar operated as a subsidiary of NMC Music, an established Israeli label (est. 1964, originally a CBS subsidiary, independent from 1988); this accounts for the “Avatar Records / NMC” dual credit on the Conversation Peace sleeve. Avatar later collaborated with BNE before becoming fully independent around 2008–2009. The label specialises in psytrance, Goa trance, ambient, chillout, and lounge — Leer’s downtempo/ambient direction on Conversation Peace fits squarely within that catalogue. [source: Wikipedia — Avatar Records (electronic music) as of 2026-10-17; Wikipedia — NMC Music as of 2026-10-17]

Avichay Avigal is therefore the co-founder of the releasing label, not an outside collaborator brought in via a prior personal connection to Leer. He is also a recording artist and producer in his own right — he is a core member of the psytrance project Asia 2001 — and the Conversation Peace liner notes credit him as co-producer. [source: PsyDB — Conversation Peace; Wikipedia — Avatar Records (electronic music) as of 2026-10-17]

How the album was made: Per PsyDB credits, Conversation Peace was recorded at Thomas Leer Studio (Scotland) and mastered at Space Cat Studios. Leer therefore recorded in Scotland; Avigal’s co-production credit suggests remote or session collaboration rather than Leer relocating. The album (cat. no. AVA 019) was released 1 May 2003 and is classified as Electro / Downtempo / Ambient — a departure from the psytrance core of Avatar’s catalogue, but consistent with Avatar’s ambient/chillout sub-strand and their “Pro File” classics arm. [source: PsyDB — Conversation Peace; Avatar Bandcamp]

Why an Israeli label? No interview or press source has been found explaining the initial introduction between Leer and Avigal. The most plausible reconstruction from available evidence: Avatar/Avigal was actively seeking established European electronic artists for an ambient/downtempo strand of the label around 2001–2003 (Leer’s Parts Of A Greater Hole on Future Historic, 2001, preceded this); Leer’s name and early-80s reputation would have been visible to any collector in that scene. The digital catalogue remains live on Avatar’s Bandcamp as of 2026.

Rights reversion: Leer later reclaimed digital distribution of the album; it is currently streamed via the Future Historic Bandcamp page with the note “Released on CD in 2003 by Avatar records. Now Bandcamped by Future Historic.” [source: futurehistoric.bandcamp.com/album/conversation-peace]

Avigal’s pattern with European electronic artists: Asia 2001, Avigal’s own recording project, includes Martin Cooper (formerly of Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark) as a collaborator, indicating Avigal had an established practice of recruiting veteran UK/European electronic artists. This contextualises the Leer connection as part of a broader label strategy rather than a unique anomaly. [source: avatarmusic.bandcamp.com/album/contact — Asia 2001 Contact album credits; Wikipedia — Avatar Records (electronic music) as of 2026-10-24]

No source found explaining how first contact was made between Leer and Avigal/Avatar. This remains an open question; an interview with Leer or Avigal would likely be the only way to resolve it.

”Private Plane / International” — instrumentation and the “first UK synth self-release” claim

Research conducted October 2026 found no source that makes the claim that “Private Plane / International” (1978, Oblique ER101) was the first UK self-released synth record. The claim does not appear in music press, AllMusic, Wikipedia, Electronic Sound, Rate Your Music, or any interview with Leer. The existing vault Overview described it as “a landmark of UK DIY electronics,” which is supportable — the stronger version (“first UK synth self-release”) is unsupported and should not be stated as fact.

More importantly, multiple sources confirm that “Private Plane” did not use synthesizers. Leer himself, in both the Minimal Wave interview and the Electronic Sound “Bedroom Bedlam” long-read, states he and Robert Rental had no synthesizers in 1978. The instruments on “Private Plane” and “International” were:

  • Guitar
  • Bass
  • Roland organ rhythm box (drum machine)
  • Stylophone (a more elaborate model than the Rolf Harris consumer toy, with two styluses and a wah-wah pedal) — per the 1984 Electronics & Music Maker interview, the Stylophone was “the main instrument on the record”
  • Watkins Copycat tape echo and effects pedals
  • TEAC A3440 4-track recorder / ALICE mixing board (recording equipment)

Sources: Electronic Sound, “Bedroom Bedlam” — Leer quoted directly: “there were no synthesisers involved in ‘Private Plane’, even though it sounds like there were”; Minimal Wave interview; Electronics & Music Maker, Sep 1984 via muzines.co.uk.

Contemporaneous 1978 UK self-releases that did use synthesizers:

  • Robert Rental — “Paralysis / A.C.C.” (Regular Records, 1978): used an EDP Wasp synthesizer, Stylophone, Roland drum machine. Leer produced this record.
  • The Normal (Daniel Miller) — “T.V.O.D. / Warm Leatherette” (Mute 1, 1978): used a Korg miniKORG 700S synthesizer, recorded at home, distributed via Rough Trade. Released November 1978 per Wikipedia (exact month contested across sources: some give Oct, some Nov).
  • The Human League — “Being Boiled / Circus of Death” (Fast Product FAST 4, 30 June 1978): all-electronic, but released on Fast Product (an existing independent label run by Bob Last), not self-released by the band.

Leer’s single is well-documented as a landmark DIY release of 1978 and attracted significant attention (NME “Single of the Week,” 650 copies sold out). Classic Pop Magazine (classicpopmag.com, “birth of synth-pop”) groups Leer, Rental, and The Normal together as collectively emerging “around the same time (1978)” without assigning priority to any one of them. Piero Scaruffi’s music history (scaruffi.com) describes it as pioneering “bedroom music production using drum-machines and electronic keyboards” — but not as the first of its kind.

Conclusion: The “first UK synth self-release” framing is not attributable to any identified source and is factually complicated by the absence of synthesizers on the record itself. The vault Overview should be revised to drop the implicit priority claim. “Private Plane” is legitimately notable as an early UK DIY home-recorded electronic-influenced single (1978), but the closest parallel self-released synth record in the same period is The Normal’s Mute 1.

Vault Overview revision (per above)

The Overview sentence “He was among the earliest UK artists to self-release fully synthesizer-based music on an independent label” is inaccurate — “Private Plane” used no synthesizers. A more accurate description: Leer was among the earliest UK artists to self-release home-recorded electronic-influenced music with a DIY punk ethos, predating the mainstream synthpop wave.
