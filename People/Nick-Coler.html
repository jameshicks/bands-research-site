Overview

Nick Coler is a British musician, keyboardist, guitarist, programmer, producer, and songwriter whose career spans punk/ska roots in the late 1970s through to a central role in one of the most commercially successful British pop production operations of the 2000s. He came up as a session keyboards player in the 1980s, became a key programmer and instrumentalist for The KLF at the height of their success (1988–1992), and then joined Xenomania — the writing and production collective led by Brian Higgins — where he contributed to hit records by Girls Aloud, Sugababes, Alesha Dixon, and others throughout the 2000s. He also has a recurring presence in the extended world of Saint Etienne, co-writing tracks on Finisterre (2002) and appearing on Words And Music By Saint Etienne (2012). He left Xenomania around 2010.

Biography

Early career and punk/ska roots (late 1970s–1985)

Coler’s earliest Discogs-documented credit is as a member of The Tigers (4), a little-known British punk/ska band. He also played keyboards and was involved in a cluster of early-1980s rock and pop productions — appearing on Blackhearts & Jaded Spades (Steamhammer, 1985) as keyboardist, and on Scarred (Scotti Bros., 1984). These credits mark him as an active session player in the British rock and new wave circuit.

Bill Drummond and early KLF connections (1986–1989)

In 1986, Coler played piano and organ on Bill Drummond’s solo album The Man (MNW, 1986). This was a formative connection: Drummond was about to co-found The KLF with Jimmy Cauty, and this session established the working relationship. By 1988, Coler was programming for the KLF and the Timelords, appearing on foundational early releases:

  • Shag Times (KLF Communications/Rough Trade, 1988) — Programmed By
  • Doctorin’ The Tardis (1988, as The Timelords) — Programmed By — a UK number one
  • Kylie Said To Jason (KLF Communications, 1989) — Programmed By, Keyboards (Additional)
  • The History Of The Jams Aka The Timelords (TVT Records, 1989) — Programmed By

The KLF peak period (1990–1992)

Coler was a recurring presence on KLF releases during their commercial peak:

  • Uptight (KLF Communications, 1989) — Engineer
  • 3 A.M. Eternal (Live At The S.S.L.) (1990) — Keyboards
  • The White Room (Arista, 1991) — Programmed By (Additional Programming) — the group’s breakthrough album
  • Justified & Ancient (KLF Communications, 1991) — Keyboards, Programmed By — one of the biggest-selling UK singles of 1991, featuring Tammy Wynette
  • Last Train To Trancentral (1991) — Keyboards
  • America: What Time Is Love? (1992) — Arranged By (Orchestra), Keyboards, Programmed By
  • It’s Grim Up North (1991) — Conductor (Orchestra & Choir Conducted By)
  • ***k The Millennium (Blast First, 1997, as 2K) — Arranged By / Conductor (Choir And Orchestra)

He also sang backing vocals on Alice Cooper’s Hey Stoopid (Epic, 1991), listed under the “British Backing Vocal Gang” credit.

Session work and production (1991–1999)

Alongside the KLF work, Coler maintained an independent production and songwriting practice through the 1990s. Notable credits:

  • Infamy! Or How I Didn’t Get Where I Am Today (Siren, 1991) — Producer
  • Lemmings (Parlophone, 1993) — Producer, Written-By
  • Spiders (Gut Records, 1996) — Producer
  • The Next Big Thing (Food/EMI, 1997) — Producer (with Food Records acts)
  • On The Soft (Fontana/Mercury, 1997) — Producer
  • Various Columbia releases around 1997–1998 (Science Fiction EP, Shine, Pink & Blue, Start Again, That’s Why We Lose Control, Where Do I Stand?) — Producer/Engineer

He also formed the production duo Mr. Joshua with Brian Higgins (Discogs ID: 44870), which produced tracks on Saint Etienne’s Finisterre. Mr. Joshua credits include production of tracks 1 (“Action”), 4 (“Soft Like Me”), 7 (“Shower Scene”), and 9 (“New Thing”) on that album.

Xenomania and the pop hits era (2000–2010)

Coler is listed as a member of Xenomania (Discogs ID: 93682), the Brian Higgins-led production collective. Through Xenomania he contributed keyboards, programming, guitar, and songwriting to a string of major UK pop hits:

  • What Will The Neighbours Say? (Polydor, 2004) — Guitar (the album)
  • Chemistry (Polydor, 2005) — Guitar (multiple tracks); Keyboards; Mixed By (track 4); Programmed By; Written-By
  • Taller In More Ways (Island, 2005) — Guitar
  • Red Dress (Island, 2006) — Guitar
  • The Sound Of Girls Aloud – The Greatest Hits (Polydor, 2006) — Guitar; Keyboards; Programmed By; Written-By
  • Tangled Up (Fascination, 2007) — Guitar; Keyboards, Programmed By
  • Out Of Control (Polydor/Fascination, 2008) — Guitar; Keyboards, Programmed By

Saint Etienne connections (2002, 2005, 2012)

Coler (via Mr. Joshua with Brian Higgins) co-wrote and contributed to Saint Etienne’s Finisterre (Mantra, 2002): co-wrote and co-produced “Action” (track 1) and “Shower Scene” (track 7) alongside Higgins and Tim Powell. He is credited as “Featuring (The Musical Talents Of)” alongside other contributors to the album.

For Tales From Turnpike House (Sanctuary, 2005), Xenomania (as a production unit) contributed to tracks 1-3 and 1-8, with Coler in the “With A Little Help From” credit alongside Higgins, Shawn Lee, and others.

For Words And Music By Saint Etienne (Heavenly/Universal UMC, 2012), Coler played Guitar, Bass, Keyboards, and Programmed By on tracks 1-4, 1-6, 1-9, and 1-13 of the double album — a substantial contribution.

Post-Xenomania (2010–present)

After leaving Xenomania around 2010, Coler’s Discogs credits thin out considerably. Documented post-Xenomania appearances include:

  • One Touch (Geffen Records, 2010) — Keyboards, Programmed By
  • Words And Music By Saint Etienne (2012) — Guitar, Bass, Keyboards, Programmed By (tracks 1-4, 1-6, 1-9, 1-13)
  • Shuffle The Cards (Zealand Records, 2015) — Lyrics By
  • Hole In My Soul (Embody Remix) (Virgin Music, 2016) — Keyboards, Programmed By
  • Knights Of The Blade (Hear No Evil Recordings, 2017) — Keyboards (compilation/remaster)
  • Andrew Lloyd Webber’s Starlight Express (The Other Songs, 2024) — Written-By
  • Can’t Speak French (Girls Aloud reunion, Universal UMC, 2024) — Guitar, Keyboards, Written-By
  • You Freak Me Out (Joe Goddard Freakier Friday 2025 Remix) (Universal Music Catalogue, 2025) — Guitar

The 2024 Girls Aloud credit indicates he remained connected to that legacy catalog.
