Conrad Schnitzler

German electronic composer and performance artist. Born 17 March 1937 in Berlin; died 4 August 2011 of stomach cancer. Real name: Konrad Schnitzler. One of the foundational figures of the Krautrock and early electronic music scenes, active from 1969 until days before his death. Father of Gregor Schnitzler. Discogs artist ID: 17135.

Biography

Berlin Origins and Tangerine Dream (1969–1970)

Schnitzler studied with Joseph Beuys at the Düsseldorf Art Academy in the mid-1960s, an influence that shaped his conceptual approach to music as a vehicle for ideas rather than entertainment. In the late 1960s he joined the fledgling experimental rock band Tangerine Dream, contributing cello, violin, guitar, and noise-making devices (notably an Addiator, a mechanical calculator). His presence is documented on the band’s debut album Electronic Meditation (Ohr, 1970), recorded alongside Edgar Froese and Klaus Schulze. He left after that single record.

Kluster (1970–1971)

Immediately after departing Tangerine Dream, Schnitzler co-founded Kluster (3) with Hans-Joachim Roedelius and Dieter Moebius. The trio operated out of Berlin’s commune scene and recorded two albums for Schwann AMS Studio — Klopfzeichen (1970) and Zwei-Osterei (1971) — engineered by Conny Plank and produced by Oskar Gottlieb Blarr. These records are among the most radical documents of the era: freely improvised, confrontational, and largely atonal. Schnitzler left after these two records; Roedelius and Moebius continued as Cluster.

Solo Beginnings and Self-Release Era (1973–1980)

With no label willing to release music on his terms, Schnitzler began self-releasing from 1973 onward. The earliest solo works — Rot (1973), Blau (1974) — were entirely self-produced: composed, performed, recorded, and manufactured by Schnitzler alone, distributed via mail order and personal networks. This DIY model, radical for the time, defined his practice for decades. The color-coded titling system (Rot, Blau, Gelb, Grün, Gold, Silver, etc.) became a signature, creating a loose conceptual taxonomy across an ever-expanding discography.

In 1978 the French label Egg released Con, produced by Peter Baumann (then of Tangerine Dream). This was one of his few commercial-label releases of the period and introduced a harder, more minimal synthesizer sound that would define his 1980s work.

Auf Dem Schwarzen Kanal (RCA, 1980) was a brief major-label excursion that went nowhere commercially. Schnitzler quickly returned to independent channels.

Sky Records and the 1980s Productivity Peak (1981–1989)

Con 3 (Sky Records, 1981) was his main Sky Records release — one of only a handful he placed with that Hamburg-based Krautrock imprint. The bulk of his 1980s output remained self-released or placed on tiny imprints: Edition Block for Gelb and Grün, Discos Esplendor Geometrico (Spain) for Consequenz II (1986) and Congratulacion (1987), and the US cassette label Generations Unlimited for several works in 1988.

Consequenz II (1986) was a significant collaboration with Wolf Sequenza (also known as Wolfgang Seidel), a partnership that produced 27+ shared releases across their careers.

Schnitzler also maintained unusual international connections: collaborations with the Dutch collective De Fabriek (Fabrieks Conseqensus, 1982), and with US new music figures through the Auricle label (Face On Radio, 1986; Contrasts, 1988).

1990s Through 2000s: Sustained Prolificacy

The pace never slackened. Through the 1990s and 2000s, Schnitzler continued self-releasing and working with small specialist labels worldwide: Spalax (France), Artgallery (France), Marginal Talent, Ricochet Dream (US), Qbico (Italy), Karisma Records (Norway), Sagittarius A-Star. Works like Con Brio (Spalax, 1993), Blue Glow (Artgallery, 1994), Computer Jazz (self-released, 1999), and Aquafit (Karisma, 2009) document a composer continually probing new textures without radically altering his core approach.

He also maintained a long partnership with Gen Ken Montgomery, with whom he shared 29 releases — primarily the Concert, New Dramatic Electronic Music, and Gencon Dramatic series on Generations Unlimited.

His last work was completed four days before his death on 4 August 2011.

Posthumous Legacy: Bureau B Archive (2011–present)

After Schnitzler’s death, Bureau B became the primary custodian of his archive. With 31+ unique master releases issued under his name, Bureau B is by far his most active label in terms of unique titles — significantly ahead even of his own self-releases (17 unique masters). Releases include reissues of the classic early works (Blau, Consequenz, Con 3, Gold, Silver, Con), live archival material, and previously unreleased sessions (Paracon - The Paragon Session Outtakes 1978–1979, 2021). The annual Con-Struct series (Bureau B, 2015–2022+) presents ongoing compilations of Schnitzler’s solo electronic material. Thomas Fehlmann compiled Kollektion 05 for the Bureau B Kollektion series in 2015.
