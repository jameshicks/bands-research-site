Oct 19, 20268 min read

Jason Ajemian

Born: 5 August 1976, Calf Mountain, Virginia
Role: Double bass, bass guitar, electronics, vocals; improviser, composer
Discogs ID: 506968
Name variations: Ajemian; Jason; Jason (Michelangelo)

Jazz-trained bassist based primarily in Chicago who became a key figure in the city’s post-AACM free-improv scene before extending into Brooklyn/New York circuits. Long-running member of Exploding Star Orchestra (Rob Mazurek’s large ensemble on Thrill Jockey) and a frequent sideman on International Anthem Recording Company releases. Also a co-leader on two albums under his own name — Let Me Get That Digital (2010) and Folklords (2014) — and founding member of a string of Chicago-rooted groups: Born Heller, Mandarin Movie, Triage (5), Dragons 1976, Lay All Over It, Who Cares How Long You Sink, and others.

Biography

Early Work and Chicago Entry (2001–2004)

Ajemian surfaces on Discogs as early as 2001, playing bass on Premium Plastics (Solitaire Records), an early-career record. The first significant marker is Born Heller, a duo project with singer/multi-instrumentalist Josephine Foster, documented on a 2004 LP on Locust Music. On that record Ajemian plays bass, contributes voice, and co-writes several tracks alongside Foster; recording was split between Devin Davis and Will Oldham affiliate Paul Oldham, with mastering by Ernst Karel. The Born Heller LP is an intimate folk-improv hybrid very different from his later large-ensemble work, and signals his comfort moving across genre lines.

Chicago Underground Scene and Exploding Star Orchestra (2005–2009)

By 2005 Ajemian was embedded in the Chicago underground, co-founding Mandarin Movie — a sextet with Rob Mazurek, Alan Licht, Matthew Lux, Frank Rosaly, and Steve Swell that released a self-titled album on the Aesthetics label. The ensemble mixes electric and acoustic improv with electronics and covers a wide sonic palette.

The biggest institutional affiliation in this period is Exploding Star Orchestra, Mazurek’s large-format group. Ajemian is listed as a member on Discogs, appearing on two Thrill Jockey releases:

  • We Are All From Somewhere Else (2007) — the core ESO lineup; produced by Mazurek and John McEntire; other members include Jeff Parker, Nicole Mitchell, Chad Taylor, Matt Bauder, Josh Berman, Jeb Bishop, Corey Wilkes, Matthew Lux, Jim Baker, John Herndon, Jason Adasiewicz, Mike Reed (2)
  • Bill Dixon With Exploding Star Orchestra (2008) — collaborative session with free-jazz trumpet legend Bill Dixon; same core ensemble

Also in this period, Ajemian joined Triage (5), a trio with saxophonist Dave Rempis and drummer Tim Daisy — three musicians central to the Chicago improvised-music world.

A guest credit of note: Chicago Underground Trio (Mazurek / Taylor / Ajemian) issued Chronicle on Delmark Records in 2007, with Ajemian on acoustic bass and electronics. This is effectively a Mazurek-led small unit rather than Ajemian’s own project, but it shows how tight the Mazurek collaboration ran.

Leader Work and New York Connections (2010–2014)

Two leader records define this period:

Let Me Get That Digital (2010, Sundmagi — his own imprint) is a co-credited release with The HighLife (2), a duo or small-group formation that appears on two releases with Ajemian. The release is minimal in credits — no additional performers listed on Discogs beyond the two primary artists.

Folklords (2014, Delmark Records) is the more substantial solo statement. Personnel on record: Ajemian (bass, electronics, vocals), Kid Bliss (3) (alto saxophone), Jason Nazary (drums), Owen Stewart-Robertson (guitar, electronics, vocals), Frank Corpus (congas); produced by Robert G. Koester, the veteran Delmark founder. The album positions Ajemian’s voice as a composer and bandleader in a free-improv-plus-electronics space.

Also active in New York orbit during this stretch, Ajemian played bass on Hush Arbors (Keith Wood) sessions: Yankee Reality (2009, Ecstatic Peace!) — where he also plays electric piano, vibraphone, and tuba — and 2 Of Us Riding Nowhere (2012, New Atlantis Records).

He joined the very large collective Who Cares How Long You Sink, a Chicago-anchored rotating ensemble with dozens of members including Jaimie Branch, Jeff Parker, Frank Rosaly, Tim Daisy, Aram Shelton, Matt Bauder, Josh Berman, and many others.

He also became a member of Lay All Over It (a trio with Noritaka Tanaka and Tim Haldeman) and Dragons 1976 (with Aram Shelton and Tim Daisy).

Jaimie Branch / International Anthem (2017–2023)

Ajemian became a core member of Jaimie Branch’s fly or die band, the quartet that released three records on International Anthem Recording Company and earned wide critical recognition. He plays double bass (and occasionally electric bass, marimba, percussion, vocals) on all three studio documents and the live album, and shares composition credits:
