Jimmy Sutton

Chicago-based upright/electric bassist, multi-instrumentalist, record producer, and label founder. One of the central figures in the Chicago roots/rockabilly/Western swing scene. Discogs artist ID: 1976189. Name variations: J. Sutton, James E. Sutton, Mr. Bones, Sutton.

Overview

Jimmy Sutton has operated as a bassist, producer, engineer, and label head in the Chicago roots underground since at least the mid-1990s. He is the founder of Hi-Style Records (Discogs label ID: 242960, sublabel of Hi-Style Enterprises, Inc.) and co-founder of Hi-Style Studio (Discogs label ID: 719626), a recording facility at the centre of the Chicago roots scene that has tracked many of the records he produced. Sutton plays double bass, electric bass, baritone guitar, electric and acoustic guitar, and contributes vocals on many projects; as a producer he handles recording, mixing, and art direction as well as music production.

His most sustained band projects are:

  • The Four Charms (Discogs ID: 2209778, also credited as “Jimmy Sutton’s Four Charms”) — his own Chicago Western swing/jump blues outfit with Joel Paterson (guitar), Jonathan Doyle (saxophone), and various drummers
  • JD McPherson (Discogs ID: 2213341) — as a founding member/touring bassist alongside Alex Hall (4), Doug Corcoran, Jason Smay, and Raynier Jacildo
  • The Moondogs (11) (Discogs ID: 6269258)

Early Career (mid-1990s)

Sutton’s earliest credits in the database place him in the Chicago blues/roots circuit in the mid-1990s:

  • Ronnie Dawson — Monkey Beat (1994, Crystal Clear Sound): bass on tracks 1–5 and 7–10
  • Willie “Big Eyes” Smith with Kim Wilson and Pinetop Perkins — Bag Full Of Blues (1995): upright bass
  • The Mighty Blue Kings — Meet Me In Uptown (1996, R-Jay Records): bass
  • The Big Barn Combo — Comin’ All The Way From Detroit City (2000, Woodward Records): producer, recorded by, mixed by

The Four Charms (2000–2005 and beyond)

Sutton’s own band, The Four Charms, appears to have been active from around 2000. The core lineup was Sutton (bass, vocals), Joel Paterson (guitar, vocals), Jonathan Doyle (saxophone), and Jim Barclay (2) (drums).

JD McPherson (2010–2018)

Sutton co-produced and played bass and guitar on JD McPherson’s debut single:

  • JD McPherson / Jimmy Sutton / Alex Hall (4) — North Side Gal (2010, Witchcraft International): co-credited primary artist; Sutton played bass, guitar, backing vocals, and produced. Also on the session: Scott Ligon (piano), Jonathan Doyle (tenor sax), Josh Bell (baritone sax).

He then produced McPherson’s debut full-length Signs & Signifiers (2010, Hi-Style Records; later widely reissued on Rounder Records in 2012), which launched McPherson internationally. Sutton is credited producer on all pressings; on later pressings he is also credited with bass, electric/acoustic/baritone guitar, backing vocals, design, and layout.

Pokey LaFarge (2015–2020)

Sutton’s highest-profile session work came through Pokey LaFarge on two Rounder/New West Records albums, as part of an overlapping Chicago crew that also included Alex Hall and Scott Ligon:

ReleaseYearSutton’s role
Something In The Water2015Producer (co-produced with LaFarge), double bass (upright, select tracks), percussion, handclaps, backing vocals
Rock Bottom Rhapsody2020Electric bass, double bass

The Something In The Water session was a large ensemble affair featuring Beau Sample (bass on other tracks), Adam Hoskins (guitar), Ryan Koenig (guitar banjo, harmonica), Chloe Feoranzo (reeds), Andy Schumm (cornet), Paul Asaro (piano), and others.

Hi-Style Studio

Sutton is listed as co-founder of Hi-Style Studio (Discogs label ID: 719626, hi-stylerecords.com/studio-about/), which served as the recording location for many of the above sessions — particularly those engineered by Alex Hall.
