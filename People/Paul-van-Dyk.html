Paul van Dyk

Real name: Matthias Paul Born: 16 December 1971, Eisenhüttenstadt, East Germany Discogs ID: 1049 Origin: Berlin, Germany Active: 1992–present

German trance DJ and producer; one of the defining figures of the Berlin techno/trance scene in the 1990s. Resident DJ at Tresor club in Berlin in the early 1990s, where he helped shape the sound of the first wave of Berlin techno. Founded his own imprint Vandit Records (est. c.1999), which remains his primary label home. Also recorded as part of The Visions Of Shiva (with Harald Blüchel) and Dolfin’. Aliases include Matthias Paul (2).

Married twice; first wife Natascha van Dyk (appears on several releases), later married to Margarita Paul.

Career Overview

Early Years & MFS (1992–1998)

Paul van Dyk began DJing in East Berlin clubs before the Wall fell, and became a resident at Tresor club in the early 1990s — at the time one of the most important techno clubs in the world. His label home during this period was MFS (Berlin), run by Mark Reeder, where he released his debut album and developed his melodic, trance-leaning style within a harder techno context.

Deviant Records & Mainstream Crossover (1997–2002)

From 1997 onwards PvD moved to UK-based Deviant Records, escalating his international profile. Key single “For An Angel” (1998, Deviant/MFS) became his signature track and one of the definitive trance records of the era — it was later re-released multiple times and has over 50 pressings on Discogs. The 2000 album Out There And Back (Deviant Records, cat. DVNT37DCD) featured collaborations with Saint Etienne (Sarah Cracknell, Bob Stanley, Pete Wiggs on “Tell Me Why (The Riddle)”), BT (“Namistai”), and vocalist Natascha van Dyk.

During this period he also signed to Mute for US distribution (via Mute Corporation), which handled releases including the Columbia EP (2001) and the DJ mix album Global (2002).

The Ministry Of Sound relationship yielded The Politics Of Dancing (2001), his best-selling DJ mix compilation.

Vandit Records Era (1999–present)

PvD launched Vandit Records (ID: 2351, parent: Vandit Records GmbH ID: 269266) around 1999, initially as a vehicle for self-releases. Vandit became his dominant label from 2003 onwards and is still active (1,700+ releases on Discogs). Sublabels include Vandit Digital, Vandit Alternative, and Vandit Next Generation.

From 2009 onwards PvD also released an ongoing Vonyc Sessions series on Vandit (annual DJ mix compilations).

Positiva / EMI / Major Label Period (2003–2009)

The Reflections album (2003, Positiva) was his highest-profile UK release, licensed to EMI. It featured collaborations with Jan Johnston (multiple tracks), Vega 4 (“Time of Our Lives”), and hip-hop features (Atomek Dogg, Trooper Da Don on “Knowledge”). Later albums White Lies (2007) and The Best Of: Volume (2009) were released through EMI / Positiva.

2010s–2020s

Moved to Ultra Records for US releases (2009–2015), then back to Vandit. The Politics Of Dancing 3 came in 2015 (New State Music). More recent output continues annually on Vandit; collaborated with Armada Music for We Are Tonight (2013). Still releasing on Vandit Alternative from 2023.

Remixography

Extensive. Discogs credits PvD with remix roles on at least 152 releases (across ~70+ unique masters as primary remixer, plus numerous additional-credit remixes). Remixes span New Order (“Spooky”), Inspiral Carpets (“Saturn 5”), Dance 2 Trance, U2 (“Elevation”), Radiohead (“Knives Out” implied by Polydor/2005 credit “Celice”), Gorillaz (“Dare”, Parlophone 2005), and many trance/electronic acts. A curated selection was compiled on Vorsprung Dyk Technik - Remixes 92–98 (1998) and Paul Van Dyk Remixes 1 (2005, Not On Label).

Notable Other Works

  • Zurdo (2002, Asterisco/Universal Mexico): Scored the soundtrack for the Mexican film Zurdo, an unusual crossover.
  • “Wir Sind Wir” (2004, Urban): German-language anti-fascist/unity anthem with Peter Heppner; became a cultural statement in Germany.
