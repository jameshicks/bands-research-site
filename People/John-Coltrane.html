John Coltrane

John Coltrane

Aug 15, 202611 min read

John Coltrane

Discogs ID: 97545 Born: September 23, 1926, Hamlet, North Carolina Died: July 17, 1967, Huntington, Long Island, New York (aged 40, liver cancer) Instruments: Tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, bass clarinet, flute, bells

American saxophonist and jazz composer. One of the defining figures in jazz history, Coltrane moved from bebop and hard bop through the modal revolution of his classic quartet period to the free-jazz and spiritual explorations of his final years. He was posthumously proclaimed a saint in 1969 by the African Orthodox Saint John Coltrane Church in San Francisco, and received a posthumous Pulitzer Prize Special Citation in 2007.

Biography by Era

Early Years and Philadelphia (1926–1954)

Coltrane grew up in High Point, North Carolina, then relocated to Philadelphia after his father’s death in 1939. In Philadelphia he was a high school contemporary of Benny Golson, both coming up through the city’s jazz scene in the mid-1940s. After a stint in the US Navy (1945–46, where he played in a navy band), he worked with local rhythm-and-blues acts before his jazz career began in earnest.

His earliest significant jazz work was as a sideman — with Dizzy Gillespie’s orchestra and sextet in the early 1950s, then with Earl Bostic (where he studied altissimo technique), then briefly with Johnny Hodges and His Orchestra. These years gave him experience in big bands, small combos, and R&B, but relatively little survives on record until his Prestige debut.

Prestige Period (1955–1958)

Coltrane signed with Prestige and began recording prolifically from 1955 onward, with nearly all sessions engineered by Rudy Van Gelder at his Hackensack, NJ studio. His first credited Prestige leader date was Mating Call (1956/57, with Tadd Dameron). During this period he was simultaneously working as a sideman with the Miles Davis Quintet (1955–57, then 1958), appearing on a string of Columbia releases alongside Davis that he did not lead but that shaped his reputation enormously.

He also recorded Tenor Conclave (1957, Prestige) — a gathering of tenor saxophonists including Hank Mobley, Al Cohn, Zoot Sims, and Eddie “Lockjaw” Davis — and collaborated with Mal Waldron on Mal/2 (1957). The Prestige catalog was reissued so extensively that the label’s Discogs entry credits Coltrane on well over 1,200 releases, the highest count of any artist associated with Prestige.

Thelonious Monk stint (1957): Between his two Miles Davis stints, Coltrane joined Thelonious Monk’s quartet for a residency at the Five Spot Café in New York. The pairing was formative — Monk’s harmonically dense compositional approach pushed Coltrane toward new approaches to chord substitution. He recorded with Monk for Riverside and appeared on Thelonious Monk With John Coltrane (Jazzland, 1961).

Blue Note Date (1957–1958)

Coltrane’s sole leader album for Blue Note was Blue Train (1957), produced by Alfred Lion and engineered by Rudy Van Gelder. The personnel was a who’s-who of hard bop:

ReleaseYearPersonnel
Blue Train1957/58Lee Morgan (trumpet), Curtis Fuller (trombone), Kenny Drew (piano), Paul Chambers (bass), Philly Joe Jones (drums); produced by Alfred Lion

Blue Train remained his only Blue Note leader session. He later appeared on several Blue Note sideman dates, and a 2014 compilation Sideman: Trane’s Blue Note Sessions collected these appearances.

Atlantic Period (1959–1961)

Coltrane moved to Atlantic in 1959, where he recorded under the supervision of Nesuhi Ertegun. The Atlantic years produced some of his most celebrated and commercially successful recordings. Sessions were engineered by Tom Dowd and Phil Iehle, departing from the Van Gelder sound of his Prestige work.

Giant Steps (1960) — his Atlantic breakthrough. All compositions were Coltrane originals, introducing the harmonic sequence known as “Coltrane changes” (cycling through three key centers a major third apart). The recording used two different piano/drum combinations:

PersonnelTracks
Tommy Flanagan (piano), Paul Chambers (bass), Art Taylor (drums)most of album
Wynton Kelly (piano), Paul Chambers (bass), Jimmy Cobb (drums)one track

My Favorite Things (1961) — introduced Coltrane’s soprano saxophone to the broader public via the Rodgers & Hammerstein standard. This is the first documented Discogs appearance of McCoy Tyner (piano) and Elvin Jones (drums) alongside him, with Steve Davis on bass:

ReleaseYearPersonnel
My Favorite Things1961 (recorded Oct 1960)McCoy Tyner (piano), Steve Davis (bass), Elvin Jones (drums); produced by Nesuhi Ertegun

Other Atlantic albums from this period include Coltrane Jazz (1961), Coltrane Plays The Blues (1962), Coltrane’s Sound (1964, recorded 1960), and Olé Coltrane (1962) — the latter featuring Freddie Hubbard (trumpet), Reggie Workman and Art Davis (dual basses), alongside Tyner and Jones.

The Heavyweight Champion: The Complete Atlantic Recordings box (Rhino, 1995) is the comprehensive reissue of this period.

Classic Quartet at Impulse! (1961–1965)

Coltrane signed with Impulse! in 1961 under producer Bob Thiele, who would oversee almost all of his Impulse! work. The label became the home of what is often called his greatest sustained body of work.

A Love Supreme (Discogs ID 12649262, recorded December 1964) is Coltrane’s most celebrated single album — a four-part suite that he composed entirely and that has been reissued in over 333 pressings on Discogs. Note: the original Discogs pressing lists the primary artist as “Ken Coltrane” — an anv (artist-name-variation) used on that specific release.

Eric Dolphy was a frequent collaborator through this period, appearing on Impressions (bass clarinet on one track) and the 1961 Village Vanguard live recordings. He was part of Coltrane’s working group for portions of 1961–62 before his own career trajectory pulled him away.

Bob Thiele produced essentially all of the Impulse! classic-quartet work and the subsequent late-period recordings. Rudy Van Gelder continued to engineer the sessions, giving the Impulse! records continuity with the Prestige sound while the music moved in new directions.

Transition and Late Period (1965–1967)

Beginning in 1965, Coltrane expanded the quartet and moved toward freer, more extended music. Two key additions:

  • Pharoah Sanders (tenor saxophone) — joined in 1965; first major appearance on Ascension (1965, recorded June); continued through Om, Meditations, Live At The Village Vanguard Again!, Expression, and Kulu Sé Mama
  • Rashied Ali (drums) — joined alongside Elvin Jones in late 1965; Elvin Jones departed by early 1966 (last appears on Meditations, which uses both Jones and Ali simultaneously); Ali became the sole drummer for the final period

Alice Coltrane (John’s second wife, pianist; Discogs artist ID 42058) replaced McCoy Tyner in late 1965/early 1966 and appears on Live At The Village Vanguard Again! (1966) and Expression (1967). Their son Ravi Coltrane (born 1965, Discogs artist ID 316502) also became a saxophonist.

Interstellar Space (recorded February 1967, released 1974) was a duo session with Rashied Ali alone — no other instruments. Coltrane plays tenor and bells. Alice Coltrane and Ed Michel produced it for release after John’s death; Van Gelder engineered uncredited.

Coltrane died July 17, 1967 from liver cancer, aged 40. His final recorded public performance was at the Olatunji Concert in New York on April 23, 1967, released as The Olatunji Concert: The Last Live Recording (Impulse!, 2001).

Posthumous Releases

Impulse! and its successor labels (ABC Records, MCA Impulse!, GRP/Impulse!) continued issuing vault material for decades:

  • Transition (1970) — quartet sessions from 1965
  • Sun Ship (1971) — quartet, 1965
  • First Meditations (For Quartet) (1977) — pre-Meditations quartet version, 1965
  • Interstellar Space (1974) — duo with Rashied Ali, 1967
  • Stellar Regions (1995) — 1967 sessions
  • Living Space (1998) — 1965 sessions
  • Both Directions At Once: The Lost Album (Impulse!/Verve, 2018) — 1963 studio recordings discovered in Ravi Coltrane’s possession; regarded as a significant posthumous discovery
  • Blue World (2019) — 1964 soundtrack sessions for the Canadian film Le chat dans le sac
  • A Love Supreme: Live In Seattle (2021) — 1965 live performance of the suite
  • Evenings At The Village Gate (Impulse!/UMe, 2023) — 1961 live recordings with Eric Dolphy

Connections

  • Benny Golson — Philadelphia high school contemporary; same 1940s Philly scene
  • McCoy Tyner — came to Coltrane via the The Jazztet (Argo, 1960); joined the classic quartet
  • Miles Davis — sideman in Davis’s quintet/sextet 1955–57, 1958; major period of musical development
  • Thelonious Monk — transformative 1957 collaboration at the Five Spot Café
  • Eric Dolphy — close musical associate 1961–62; multi-instrumentalist who appeared on several Impulse! sessions
  • Pharoah Sanders — protégé who joined the late group; carried Coltrane’s influence into the following decade
  • Alice Coltrane — second wife; pianist in final group; steward of his posthumous legacy
  • Rashied Ali — sole rhythm partner in Coltrane’s final phase; duo recordings on Interstellar Space
  • Impulse! — primary label 1961–67; produced by Bob Thiele
  • Prestige — formative early period; over 1,200 Discogs entries credited to him
