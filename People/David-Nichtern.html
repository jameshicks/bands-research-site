David Nichtern

David Nichtern

American songwriter, guitarist, television composer, and music producer. Discogs artist ID: 346223. Best known for writing “Midnight at the Oasis,” a Top 10 hit for Maria Muldaur (Reprise Records, 1973), and for his long-running role as Muldaur’s session and touring guitarist across multiple albums in the 1970s–80s. Also a senior teacher in the Shambhala Buddhist lineage of Chögyam Trungpa Rinpoche, which has shaped a second strand of his career in devotional and world music production.

Overview

Nichtern emerged in the early 1970s in the Los Angeles session world orbiting Reprise Records and producer Lenny Waronker. His composition “Midnight at the Oasis” — recorded for the 1973 Maria Muldaur debut — became a radio staple and remains his most-licensed songwriting credit. Through the mid-to-late 1970s he remained central to Muldaur’s studio band, contributing guitar to Waitress in a Donut Shop (1974) and Sweet Harmony (1976), and then stepping up to co-produce Open Your Eyes (1979) and Fall in Love Again (1979). He also produced both Kate & Anna McGarrigle LPs for Warner Bros. during this period (Pronto Monto, 1978; French Record, 1980) and contributed Synclavier programming to Paul Simon’s Hearts and Bones (1983).

From the late 1990s onward Nichtern’s production work shifted toward Hindu devotional (kirtan) and world-fusion music. He was a primary collaborator on recordings by Krishna Das, including producing Kirtan Wallah (2014, KDM/Krishna Das Music). He also played guitar alongside Krishna Das, Ustad Sultan Khan, and Ty Burhoe on the collaborative world-music album A Drop of the Ocean (2003, Artemis/Triloka) — his only primary-credited release as a group artist outside the band context. Sideline group credits include The Great American String Band (with Jerry Garcia, David Grisman, Taj Mahal, Richard Greene, and Buell Neidlinger) and The Beyman Bros (with Christopher Guest and Jeffery CJ Vanston).

His television composition work is noted in his Discogs profile but no releases are indexed there.

Connections

  • Maria Muldaur — central professional relationship from 1973 through at least the early 2000s; Nichtern wrote her signature song, played guitar on multiple albums, and co-produced three
  • The Great American String Band — early-career ensemble credit linking Nichtern to the Grateful Dead/Bay Area folk-rock world
  • Krishna Das — sustained collaboration in American kirtan/Hindu devotional music sphere, reflecting Nichtern’s Buddhist practice
  • Paul Simon — Synclavier programming on Hearts and Bones (1983); Lenny Waronker was the connective tissue between these worlds
