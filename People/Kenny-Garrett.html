Kenny Garrett

American alto saxophonist and flutist, born Detroit, Michigan, October 9, 1960. Discogs artist ID: 55729.

One of the most prominent jazz alto saxophonists of his generation. Came up in the early 1980s through the Blue Note–associated group Out of the Blue, spent several years as a sideman in Miles Davis’s electric band (c. 1986–1991), then built a substantial solo career across Atlantic, Warner Bros., and Mack Avenue.

Early Career (1978–1985)

Garrett’s recorded debut appears on Donald Byrd’s Take A Look At Yourself (Monument, 1978) — he was around 17 or 18 at the time. Through the early 1980s he worked with Bob Brookmeyer’s ensemble (recorded 1982) and began appearing on records as a sideman around the New York scene.

He was a founding member of Out of the Blue (3) (Discogs ID: 1386813), the Blue Note collective also featuring Ralph Peterson, Michael Philip Mossman, Robert Hurst, Renee Rosnes, Steve Wilson (2), Billy Drummond, and others. OTB released O.T.B. (Blue Note, 1985), Inside Track (Blue Note, 1986), Live At Mt. Fuji (Blue Note, 1987), and several more through the late 1980s.

His first session as a leader was Introducing Kenny Garrett (Criss Cross Jazz, 1985) — in Discogs this is listed as a sideman credit (release 2509202) rather than primary, suggesting it may have been a co-billed or label-credited session. The first confirmed primary-credited release is Moon Alley (Criss Cross Jazz, 1986), billed as “Tom Harrell Quintet Featuring Kenny Garrett And Kenny Barron,” with Tom Harrell, Kenny Barron, Ray Drummond, and Ralph Peterson, engineered by Rudy Van Gelder, produced by Gerry Teekens.

Miles Davis Years (c. 1986–1991)

Garrett joined Miles Davis’s band in the mid-to-late 1980s, serving as the saxophonist in Davis’s final electric group. His primary documented studio appearance with Davis is on Amandla (Warner Bros., 1989) — the most prominent post-comeback Davis studio album — where he plays alto and soprano saxophone on six of the eight tracks. The album was produced by Marcus Miller, who also played most of the instruments. Garrett is listed alongside Ricky Wellman, Don Alias, Mino Cinelu, and Foley among others.

On Miles In Paris (Warner Music Vision, 1990) — a live document of the late-period band — Garrett is credited alongside Ricky Wellman, Kei Akagi, Foley, and John Bigham. He also appears on various Montreux and live documents from 1987–1991 (captured in the Rhino box The Complete Miles Davis At Montreux 1973–1991, 2002, and subsequent DVD collections).

On his own leader debut Prisoner of Love (Atlantic Jazz, 1989), Garrett plays trumpet on two tracks — Miles Davis appears, playing trumpet on B1 and B2. This is the clearest personal overlap: Davis guesting on Garrett’s record while Garrett was still in his band.

After Davis’s death in 1991, Garrett paid tribute with Sketches of MD: Live at the Iridium Featuring Pharoah Sanders (Mack Avenue, 2008).

Key Sideman Credits

Beyond Miles Davis, Garrett appeared as a sideman on:

  • Bob Brookmeyer: Make Me Smile & Other New Works (Finesse, 1982) — early big-band credit
  • Out of the Blue (3) (Blue Note, 1985–1988 period) — founding member
  • Mulgrew Miller: Wingspan (Landmark, 1987); Hand in Hand (Novus, 1993) — 12 shared releases total
  • Herbie Hancock: Gershwin’s World (Verve, 1998) — featured guest
  • Chick Corea / John McLaughlin: Five Peace Band Live (Concord, 2009)
  • Sting: If On A Winter’s Night… (Deutsche Grammophon, 2009) — soprano saxophone
  • Various Marcus Miller, Lenny White, Elvin Jones sessions
