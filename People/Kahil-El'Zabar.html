Kahil El’Zabar

Born Clifton Blackburn, November 11, 1953, Chicago, IL. American percussionist, composer, and multi-instrumentalist. Discogs artist ID: 309759. Core member of the AACM (Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians), Chicago-based throughout his career.

Instruments: drums, earth drum, shekere, balafon, kalimba/sanza (thumb piano/mbira), berimbau, ankle bells, ashiko drum, bamboo bass flute, voice.

Name variations on records: El’Zabar, Kahil El Zabar, Kahil El’ Zabar, Khalil El’Zabar.

Biography

Early Career and AACM (late 1970s–early 1980s)

El’Zabar founded the Ethnic Heritage Ensemble as one of his primary ongoing vehicles. The Ethnic Heritage Ensemble debuted on record with Three Gentlemen From Chikago (1981, Moers Music) and Impressions (1982, Red Record), capturing the trio format of El’Zabar with Joseph Bowie (trombone) and Edward Wilkerson (saxophones). These early recordings established the ensemble’s pan-African percussion vocabulary mixed with free jazz structures.

He also appeared as a sideman: a 1980 congas credit on Live Without Fear (Ancient Afrika Records) shows him active on the Chicago scene from the very start of the decade.

Sound Aspects and the Ritual Trio (1986–1990)

In 1986 El’Zabar launched Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio (Discogs ID: 322784) with a self-titled debut on Sound Aspects Records — The Ritual — featuring himself, Lester Bowie (trumpet), and Malachi Favors (bass). The album was produced by El’Zabar, recorded digitally by Scott Pollard, and executive-produced by Pedro De Freitas (Sound Aspects label head). This lineup directly connected the Ritual Trio to the Art Ensemble Of Chicago axis via Bowie and Favors.

The Ritual Trio’s next three Sound Aspects records followed rapidly:

In parallel, Golden Sea (1989, Sound Aspects) appeared under El’Zabar’s own name as a duo with David Murray (tenor saxophone, bass clarinet), with El’Zabar contributing original compositions alongside a Miles Davis tune. This was the first of a sustained El’Zabar–Murray partnership.

The Ethnic Heritage Ensemble also recorded Ancestral Song (Live From Stockholm) (1988, Silkheart) during this period — the lineup here was El’Zabar, Joseph Bowie, and Edward Wilkerson.

Delmark Era and the Ritual Trio (1992–2007)

From 1994 onward El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio shifted to Delmark Records, releasing a concentrated run of albums there over more than a decade. The core trio settled into the lineup of El’Zabar (drums, percussion, kalimba), Malachi Favors (bass), and Ari Brown (saxophones, piano). Robert G. Koester supervised production at Delmark; Paul Serrano engineered most sessions.

El’Zabar’s Juba Collective project (Discogs ID: 984027) — a larger ensemble — produced Juba Collective (2002, Premonition Records).

Spiritmuse Era (2019–present)

From 2019 El’Zabar shifted to Spiritmuse Records (ID: 1625713) for both his solo work and the Ethnic Heritage Ensemble. This period saw an expansion into large-ensemble and orchestral settings as well as continued free jazz trio work.

Groups

Ethnic Heritage Ensemble (Discogs ID: 222935) — El’Zabar’s primary ensemble, active from c. 1981. Original core: El’Zabar, Joseph Bowie, Edward Wilkerson. Later lineup (1990s–2000s): El’Zabar, Ernest “Khabeer” Dawkins (saxophones), Joseph Bowie or Corey Wilkes. Current (2019–): El’Zabar, Corey Wilkes, Alex Harding.

Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio (Discogs ID: 322784) — Formed 1986. Shifting lineup; foundational members were Lester Bowie and Malachi Favors; settled into El’Zabar / Favors / Ari Brown for the Delmark years; later Yosef Ben Israel on bass.

Kahil El’Zabar’s Tri-Factor (Discogs ID: 933684) — El’Zabar, Billy Bang, Hamiet Bluiett. Two releases 2000–2002.

Other groups (Discogs member credits): Bright Moments (3), David Murray Quartet (ID: 941170), Juba Collective (ID: 984027), Kahil El’Zabar Trio (ID: 933734), Kahil El’Zabar’s Ethnics (ID: 3977579), Kahil El’Zabar’s Infinity Orchestra (ID: 934953), Shadow Vignettes (ID: 3735172), The Kahil El’Zabar Quartet (ID: 4564704).
