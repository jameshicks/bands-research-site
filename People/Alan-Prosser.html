Alan Prosser

British folk musician — guitar, fiddle, bowed psaltery, bones, mandolin, banjo, bouzouki, kantele. Founding member and principal songwriter of Oysterband (active 1976 to present). Also a member of Fiddler’s Dram. Runs his own imprint, Rafting Dog Records. Discogs artist ID: 506428. Name variations: A. Prosser, Alan. Alias as electronic act: 12 Inch Thumpers. Early-1990s solo alias: The Riggler (Discogs ID 262916).

Overview

Prosser is from Canterbury, Kent. He was among the founding members of the Oyster Ceilidh Band in the mid-1970s, which evolved through various configurations — including the dance-band offshoot Fiddler’s Dram — before settling as Oysterband by the mid-1980s. He is the band’s primary guitarist and a co-writer of most of its original material.

1977–1983: Fiddler’s Dram and early Oyster Ceilidh Band

Prosser’s first significant recording appearances are as an accompanist on folk solo albums: Cold Snap (1977, Blackthorne Records) and Penelope Isn’t Waiting Anymore (1977, Rounder Records). With the full Canterbury group, he appeared on To See The Play (1978, Dingle’s Records), playing a wide array of instruments including guitar, harmonium, electric bass, bones, dulcimer, and fiddle.

Fiddler’s Dram’s self-titled LP (1980, Dingle’s Records) features Prosser on bass, bones, guitar, and vocals alongside Ian Telfer, John Jones (4), Cathy Lesurf, and others; the album was produced by David Foister. The concurrent Oyster Ceilidh Band LP Jack’s Alive (1980) shows Prosser already mixing, in addition to performing. A further Dingle’s release, 20 Golden Tie-Slackeners (1984, Pukka Records (2)), documents the ceilidh-band strand.

1986–1997: Cooking Vinyl years

After the group became Oysterband, they signed to Cooking Vinyl, releasing a string of studio albums that showed Prosser’s increasingly prominent guitar and songwriting roles:

  • Step Outside (1986) — produced by Clive Gregson; core lineup also includes Ian Telfer, John Jones (4), Ian Kearey, Russell Lax
  • Wide Blue Yonder (1987) — UK/US release on Cooking Vinyl and Polydor
  • Ride (1989) — US release on Polydor
  • Freedom And Rain (1990) — collaboration with June Tabor; produced by John Ravenhall; brass section Metal Fatigue
  • Deserters (1992) — US release on Rykodisc; lineup now includes Chopper (3) and Lee Partis
  • Holy Bandits (1993, Rykodisc) — Prosser contributes guitar, mandolin, banjo, viola, vocals, and songwriting
  • Trawler (1994) — Spanish release; also issued on Cooking Vinyl
  • The Shouting End Of Life (1995) — German release on Alternation (2) / Cooking Vinyl
  • Deep Dark Ocean (1997)

During this period Prosser also appeared as a session musician on Michelle Shocked’s Labour Day (1988, Stony Plain Records), and contributed electric guitar to a release on Fuse Records (1988).

1996–2006: Solo and smaller-label work

Prosser launched his own imprint, Rafting Dog Records (cat series RD001 onwards), releasing his debut solo album Hall Place (1996, RD001). The album is multi-instrumental and self-produced, with contributions from Chopper (3), Lee Partis, and Alaric Neville. Makerfield (2003, RD002) followed as a solo release with no additional credits listed in the DB.

In 1993–94 he briefly recorded under the alias The Riggler for Recent Future Music, releasing a single (I’ll Give My Love To You / Keeping Me High, 1993) and an EP (A Better Day, 1994).

Prosser also co-wrote material appearing on June Tabor’s Gone West (1993, Cooking Vinyl) and contributed bass guitar and double bass to Recollections (1993, Shanachie).

1999–2015: Running Man, Westpark, and later Oysterband

Here I Stand (1999, Running Man Records) is a substantial Oysterband album on which Prosser co-produces and mixes several tracks alongside Alaric Neville; guest vocals come from Canadian band Great Big Sea.

Rise Above (2002, Pläne) was produced by Alan Scott (credited as Al Scott), who became a recurring producer. Lineup includes Benji Kirkpatrick and Lee Partis.

Nomads – Cellar Sessions May 2006 (Rafting Dog Records, RD003) is a trio recording with harmonica player Brendan Power and percussionist Lucy Randall (2), with Prosser recording, mixing, and playing guitar.

Oysterband moved to Westpark Music for Meet You There (2007) and Rising Road (2009). The second collaboration with June Tabor, Ragged Kingdom (2011, Topic Records), was produced by Alan Scott and brought the band back to a major folk label; Prosser plays guitar, kantele, and fiddle.

Diamonds On The Water (2014, Navigator Records (2)) continued the Oysterband run. At Wild End (2015, Wild End Records) is documented in the vault; Prosser is credited on guitar.

2018–2024: Continued solo recording

Prosser released 5/4 AP (2018, Rafting Dog Records, RD054) as a solo instrumental, performing and mixing himself, mastered by Alan Scott. Living In Clover (2019, RD055) is a duo release with Al Clarke (5) — both play, write, and sing. Folkzone (2024, RD056) continues the Rafting Dog solo series.

A compilation This House Will Stand — The Best Of Oysterband 1998–2015 (2016, Westpark Music) documents the later-period Oysterband catalog.
