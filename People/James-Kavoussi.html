Overview

James Kavoussi is a New York musician and recording engineer active since the late 1980s, with over 100 Discogs credits spanning indie rock, punk, lo-fi, and psychedelic rock. He is a member of Fly Ashtray, Dustdevils, Coloring Book, S!t!o!i!n!k!, and The Geeky Dorks, and operates a solo alias — pHoaming Edison — for murky, low-structure sound experiments. His engineering work threads through the New York indie underground; his top collaborators, Jim Fourniadis, Wm. Berger, and Michael Anzalone, each appear across more than a dozen shared releases. His label credits run from early 1990s work on Shimmy Disc and Nur Sch. Rec! through to Drag City and Fuzzy Warbles Cassettes in the 2020s.

Notes (web)

Toxic Shock Studio and the Reagan Youth connection

The 14 shared Discogs credits between Kavoussi and Reagan Youth trace back to a single studio relationship. According to a Big Takeover profile of Fly Ashtray, Kavoussi ran a recording studio in Manhattan called Toxic Shock from approximately 1988 to 1992, which gave Fly Ashtray unlimited studio time during that period. Big Takeover, “Fly Ashtray — Four Decades of Warped Pop… and still going. Part 1”

Reagan Youth recorded their second studio album Volume 2 (New Red Archives, 1990) at Toxic Shock/Toxic Studios. Kavoussi and Jim Fourniadis are credited as recording and mixing engineers on that session, with guitar overdubs by Joey Turk also noted in credits. The 14 shared credits likely represent: the Volume 2 LP itself, its various pressings/reissues, and the tracks drawn from Volume 2 that appear on the 1994 compilation A Collection of Pop Classics (New Red Archives) — where tracks 11–22 are the Volume 2 material, with Kavoussi and Fourniadis credited engineers for that portion. The Volume 1 / early material on that same compilation (tracks 1–10, recorded at High 5 Studios, Spring 1983–Spring 1984) has separate credits: engineer Jerry “Boss” Williams, with a Jeff Lin remix at C&J Studios.

These credits are all sourced from Discogs release pages (search result snippets, domains discogs.com — not directly fetched). The Wikipedia articles for Youth Anthems for the New Order, A Collection of Pop Classics, Reagan Youth, and Dave Rubinstein contain no engineering credits.

Scene bridge: The Reagan Youth connection represents a clear instance of Kavoussi’s studio serving as a meeting point between the NYHC world (Reagan Youth were a Queens-formed hardcore band, active since 1980) and the downtown indie/art-rock scene Kavoussi moved in via Fly Ashtray and Uncle Wiggly. The studio relationship appears to be the mechanism — Reagan Youth came to Toxic Shock as clients, not via any prior band or personal overlap documented in sources found. No interview or narrative source found explains who specifically connected them or why Reagan Youth chose Toxic Shock over more typical hardcore-adjacent studios (e.g. Don Fury’s, which handled much of the NYHC scene).

Studio name variants: Discogs credits list the facility variously as “Toxic Studios,” “Toxic Shock Studios,” and “Toxic NYC.” All appear to refer to the same Manhattan location. The Big Takeover article uses “Toxic Shock.” [Big Takeover as above]

What was not found: No interview with Kavoussi, Fourniadis, or any Reagan Youth member that describes how the Reagan Youth–Kavoussi relationship began. No documentation of how Kavoussi came to co-run or staff the studio with Fourniadis. No address or precise location for Toxic Shock.
