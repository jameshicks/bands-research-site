Patrick Gauthier

Patrick Gauthier

Oct 13, 20267 min read

Patrick Gauthier

Discogs ID: 252639 Born: 27 February 1953, France Real name variants: Patrick Antisthène Gauthier, Patrick Thémistocle Gauthier Instruments: Piano, synthesizer (Moog, Minimoog, Polymoog, VCS3, ARP)

French keyboardist and composer. A central figure in the French progressive and electronic underground of the 1970s, Gauthier moved between the rock-meets-electronics world of Richard Pinhas and Heldon and the Zeuhl cosmos of Magma (6), then helped found the post-Magma splinter group Weidorje. His only solo album, Bébé Godzilla (1981), was co-produced by Pinhas. He continued working with Pinhas into the 1990s and 2000s.

Biography

Early Career — Schizo (4) and the Pinhas Circle (1972–1974)

Gauthier’s earliest Discogs credits place him in the orbit of Richard Pinhas before Heldon had fully crystallised. In 1972 he played piano and Moog synthesizer on the Schizo (4) single Le Voyageur / Torcol (Disjuncta), alongside Pinhas (guitar, VCS3), Coco Roussel (percussion), and Pierrot Roussel (bass), with Gilles Deleuze contributing uncredited spoken words and Georges Grünblatt on guest Mini-Moog. The same year he contributed piano to a single track (A2) on Relating A Message To You by the US soul group Cane And Able (Epic, France).

Heldon — Electronique Guerilla (1974)

Gauthier appears on side B of Heldon’s debut album Electronique Guerilla (Disjuncta, 1974), playing piano and VCS3 alongside Pinhas, Coco Roussel (drums), Pierrot Roussel (bass), and Georges Grünblatt (VCS3), with Gilles Deleuze again on spoken words. All compositions on the credited tracks were by Pinhas. The album is a foundational text of French electro-rock; Gauthier’s VCS3 contributions appear on the B-side suite.

He is listed as a member of Heldon in Discogs but does not appear in the credits of Heldon 2 – Allez-Teia (1975), Third (“It’s Always Rock’n’Roll”) (1975), Heldon IV “Agneta Nilsson” (1976), Un Rêve Sans Conséquence Spéciale (1976), or Heldon.6. Interface (1977). His next confirmed appearance on a Heldon record is Stand By (1979).

Magma — Üdü Wüdü (1977)

Gauthier is credited as “Performer” on Üdü Wüdü (Tomato, 1977), Magma’s seventh studio album. The full performer roster for that record: Christian Vander, Klaus Blasquiz, Jannick Top, Bernard Paganotti, Benoît Widemann, Michel Graillier, Stella Vander, and Gauthier. This is his only confirmed album-credit within Magma’s main discography. Üdü Wüdü is generally considered the peak of the Zeuhl style, dominated by Jannick Top’s bass compositions.

A 1994 live compilation Théâtre Du Taur – Concert 1975 – Toulouse (AKT (3)) credits Gauthier on keyboards, suggesting live appearances with Magma predate the Üdü Wüdü sessions by at least two years. Similarly, Concert 1976 – Opéra De Reims (AKT (3), 1996) credits him on keyboards, and the 1977 US pressing of Üdü Wüdü confirms studio involvement. His Magma tenure likely ran approximately 1975–1977.

Weidorje — Self-Titled Album (1978)

After Üdü Wüdü, bassist Bernard Paganotti departed Magma and formed Weidorje, taking Gauthier with him. The Weidorje lineup on their sole studio album (Cobra, 1978) was: Paganotti (bass, vocals), Gauthier (keyboards), Jean-Philippe Goude (keyboards), Kirt Rust (drums), Michel Ettori (guitar), Yvon Guillard (trumpet, vocals), and Alain Guillard (saxophone). Producer: Frédéric Leibovitz; recorded by Laurent Thibault.

Weidorje’s discography is essentially two releases: the 1978 album and Live At Toulon 1978 (Noizu! Records, 2023), a posthumous release of concert recordings from their active year.

Heldon — Stand By (1979) and the Return to the Pinhas Axis

Gauthier reappears extensively on Stand By (Egg, 1979), Heldon’s final studio album. He is credited with piano, Polymoog, and Minimoog — both solo Minimoog parts across the A-side (tracks A1–A8) and piano/synthesizer on B1 and B2. He also co-wrote B1. The album also features François Auger (percussion, producer), Didier Batard (bass), and Klaus Blasquiz (voice).

Solo Album — Bébé Godzilla (1981)

Gauthier’s sole album as primary artist. Released on Cy Records (France). He is credited as Composer and Arranger on all tracks. The album was co-produced by Richard Pinhas and Yves Chamberland, with engineering by Roger Roche (mixed by Roche on most tracks), assisted by Philibert Rossi and Loïc Reissmann. A side project track (B1) was produced and mixed by Jean-Louis Rizet and Laurent Peyron.

1982 and Richard Pinhas’s L’Ethique

Gauthier contributed “Additional Minimoog” to two tracks (A2, B2) on Pinhas’s solo album L’Ethique (Pulse (10), 1982). Other contributors included Bernard Paganotti (bass guitar), Jean-Philippe Goude (Minimoog, percussion), and Clément Bailly (drums).

Session and Film Work (1979–1986)

Through the early-to-mid 1980s Gauthier worked extensively as a session keyboardist:

  • Higelin À Bercy (Pathé, 1986) — synthesizer; Higelin live

Pinhas Collaborations — 1990s and 2000s

Gauthier returned to Pinhas’s projects several times in the CD era:

  • DWW (Cuneiform, 1992) — Minimoog solo (track 4), Additional Polymoog (track 8), synthesizer/piano/drums (track 6), co-wrote track 6
  • It’s Always Rock ‘N’ Roll / Electronique Guerilla (Cuneiform, 1993) — keyboards (reissue/compilation)
  • Rhizosphere / Live, Paris 1982 (Cuneiform, 1994) — Minimoog
  • East West (Cuneiform, 2005) — Polymoog (tracks 1 and 9)
  • Metatron (Cuneiform, 2006) — Minimoog

Later Activity — Clinamens, Le Morse, Dans L’Entrelacs Des Roses-Pierres (2010s–2020s)

The collaborators table shows Gauthier continuing to work with Bernard Paganotti and others. He appears on later releases credited to projects with Paganotti (Sur Les Flots Verticaux, Le Morse, Clinamens), alongside Antoine Paganotti, Julie Vander, Stella Vander, Himiko Paganotti, Philippe Bussonnet, and François Laizeau. These are effectively Magma extended-family recordings.

His 2022 album Dans L’Entrelacs Des Roses-Pierres (Assai Records) is credited to him as Executive Producer (primary artist), suggesting a late-career return to personal projects.
