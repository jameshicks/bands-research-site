John Zorn

John Zorn

John Zorn

Discogs ID: 99731
Born: September 2, 1953, New York City, USA
Active: 1977–present
Instruments: Alto saxophone, clarinet, reeds, game calls, duck calls, saw, piano, guitar, flute
Aliases: Dekoboko Hajime, Rav Tzizit, The Composer (3)
Label: Tzadik (owner/founder)

American saxophonist, composer, and label founder; central figure of the downtown NYC avant-garde. Works across free jazz, hardcore, klezmer, classical, noise, film music, and improvisation. One of the most prolific recording artists in the experimental music world — typically releasing several albums a year under his own name and through dozens of side projects and groups.

Early Life and Formation

Grew up in New York City, studying piano, guitar, and flute as a child. Attended college in St. Louis, where he encountered free jazz, then dropped out and returned to Manhattan. In his early years he gave concerts in his apartment playing reeds, duck calls, tapes, and whatever was at hand — proto-performances that prefigured the game-piece compositional approach he would develop in the late 1970s.

Early NYC — Game Pieces and Parachute (1977–1984)

Zorn’s first recordings appeared on Parachute Records, a downtown NYC imprint. His “game pieces” — structured improvisations named after sports: Pool (1980), Archery (1981), Cobra (1987) — gave improvisers a set of graphic/verbal rules rather than a score, generating unpredictable music within controlled parameters. Cobra became his most enduring compositional framework.

Yankees (1982, Celluloid Records) was an early transatlantic summit: Zorn with Fred Frith and Derek Bailey, three figures from distinct free-improv traditions.

Locus Solus (1983, Rift) documented his group of the same name.

Elektra Nonesuch Era (1985–1992)

In the mid-1980s Zorn signed to Elektra Nonesuch (executive producer Robert Hurwitz), which gave him a significant platform and distribution.

The Big Gundown (1986)

The Big Gundown (Elektra Nonesuch 7559-79139-2) is an album of Ennio Morricone arrangements (plus one Zorn original, “Tre Nel 5000”). Produced by Yale Evelev. Rather than a single ensemble, each track assembles a different cast — the full performer list constitutes a snapshot of the downtown NYC scene:

Spillane (1987)

Spillane (Elektra Nonesuch 7559-79172-2) is a three-part suite:

  1. “Spillane” (25:12) — film-noir-influenced piece for a large ensemble including Zorn (sax/clarinet), Bill Frisell (guitar), David Hofstra (bass/tuba), Bobby Previte (drums), Carol Emanuel (harp), Anthony Coleman (piano), Bob James (3) (tape/CDs), Arto Lindsay (texts), Robert Quine (voice, as “Mike Hammer’s Conscience”), John Lurie (voice, as “Mike Hammer”)
  2. “Two-Lane Highway” — country/blues suite with Albert Collins (guitar/voice), Ronald Shannon Jackson (drums), Melvin Gibbs (bass), Robert Quine (guitar), Wayne Horvitz (piano)
  3. “Forbidden Fruit” — for voice, Kronos Quartet, and turntables: Christian Marclay (turntables), Hiromi Ohta (voice), Kronos Quartet

Recorded and mixed by Don Hünerberg; mastered by Bob Ludwig.

Spy Vs. Spy (1989)

Spy Vs. Spy—The Music Of Ornette Coleman (Elektra Musician) — a hardcore-inflected Ornette Coleman tribute played at extreme speed and volume.

Naked City formed in 1988. Lineup:

The debut Naked City (1990, Elektra/Nonesuch 79238-2) mixes originals, film themes (Morricone, Mancini, John Barry, Georges Delerue), Ornette Coleman covers, and grindcore blasts — often within seconds of each other. Photography by Weegee; illustration by Suehiro Maruo; mixed/recorded by Roger Moutenot; mastered by Bob Ludwig. The band recorded its final album in December 1992.

Japan/DIW/Avant Era and Masada (1990–1994)

Zorn’s ties to the Japanese avant-garde scene deepened through the early 1990s — releases on DIW and Avant (Japanese labels), and collaborations with Yamatsuka Eye, Ground Zero, and others.

The Masada songbook — original compositions in a klezmer/free jazz idiom drawing on Jewish modes and the Ornette Coleman quartet model — began in 1994 with the first DIW releases (Alef, Beit, Gimel, Dalet…). The classic Masada (3) quartet:

The Hebrew-alphabet series ran through Yod (1998) on DIW, with later volumes on Tzadik.

Tzadik Records and Post-1995 Expansion

Zorn founded Tzadik in 1995, which became the primary vehicle for his own output and for releasing avant-garde/experimental music by others. From this point his discography proliferates enormously:

  • Filmworks series — soundtrack music for film and television, ongoing from 1990 (collected and released chronologically on Tzadik)
  • Book of Angels series — a second Masada songbook, with different artists interpreting each volume
  • Electric Masada — electric expansion of the Masada concept; ID 272954
  • Masada String Trio, Bar Kokhba Sextet, Masada Chamber Ensembles — chamber variations
  • Classical/orchestral works — string quartets, piano music, choral work
  • PainKiller (2) — power trio with Bill Laswell and Mick Harris; noise/metal/dub
  • The Dreamers — exotica-inflected septet
  • Ongoing game piece events

The Book of Heads (1995, Tzadik) — 35 études for solo guitar, performed by Marc Ribot.
