Overview

Bill Walsh (William Charles Walsh) is an Australian musician best known as the drummer and co-vocalist of Cosmic Psychos, the Melbourne pub-punk outfit whose core lineup also includes Ross Knight and Peter Jones. His Discogs footprint runs from 1985 to at least 2024 across 63 credits, with styles spanning garage rock, punk, grunge, and alternative rock. The catalogue moves from early Australian releases on Survival and Rattlesnake Records into US exposure through Amphetamine Reptile Records in the early 1990s, and resurfaces in the 2010s–2020s via Goner Records. Beyond the Cosmic Psychos axis, notable production connections include Butch Vig (8 shared credits) and Melbourne-based producer Mikey Young (6 shared credits).

Notes (web)

Cosmic Psychos / Butch Vig connection

Short answer: Amphetamine Reptile Records is the most plausible conduit, though no source states it explicitly.

What the sources establish:

  1. Go the Hack (1989, Survival Records, Australia) was licensed to Sub Pop Records for North American release in 1990. This was the band’s Sub Pop connection — a licensing deal, not a signing. (subpop.com, retrieved 2026-11-24)

  2. The Amphetamine Reptile connection came through a personal relationship between band members and label head Tom Hazelmeyer: they “became drinking buddies with label head Tom Hazelmeyer,” which led to the AmRep deal for Blokes You Can Trust (1991). This personal/social route is consistent with AmRep’s ethos at the time. (Wikipedia snippet / search result, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_Psychos, as of 2026-11-24)

  3. Blokes You Can Trust was recorded at Butch Vig’s Smart Studios, Madison, Wisconsin, with Vig producing (co-credited with Mr. Colson). The Beat Magazine article on the band notes: the band “were signed to Amphetamine Reptile Records and sent into the studio to record Blokes You Can Trust (1991) with none other than Nevermind producer Butch Vig” — wording that implies AmRep arranged or directed the session. (beat.com.au, retrieved 2026-11-24)

  4. Crucially: Vig had already produced another Australian band, King Snake Roost, at Smart Studios for Amphetamine Reptile Records — recorded October–November 1989, released June 1990 (ARR 89189). This establishes that the Vig/AmRep/Australian-band pipeline existed before Cosmic Psychos entered it. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Into_the_Dirt, as of 2026-11-24)

  5. No source names the individual who specifically brokered the Vig session. Ross Knight recalled of Vig: “He is just such a good bloke. When he heard me fire up the bass for the first time, he came running in saying ‘what’s that fucking noise?!’ And from there we just got along so well.” Knight also noted he “never realized he was such a big, respected producer” at the time. (beat.com.au, emptybottle.com, both retrieved 2026-11-24)

Conclusion: The balance of evidence points to Amphetamine Reptile, not Sub Pop, as the conduit to Vig. The sequence is: (a) Sub Pop licenses Go the Hack (1990), establishing US presence; (b) Cosmic Psychos befriend Tom Hazelmeyer and sign to AmRep; (c) AmRep, which had already used Vig for King Snake Roost, arranges the Smart Studios session. The broader US grunge-era interest in Australian punk (the band is cited as a significant influence on Mudhoney, Pearl Jam, and others) created the receptive environment, but the specific routing appears to be AmRep’s doing.

Sources checked, no specific brokering detail found: Wikipedia (Cosmic Psychos article), Sub Pop artist page, Beat Magazine, Empty Bottle interview, Guitar World interview, ihrtn.net AmRep retrospective, Wikipedia (Butch Vig production discography — Cosmic Psychos not listed, possibly a gap in that article).

Note: The 2013 documentary Cosmic Psychos: Blokes You Can Trust (dir. Matthew Weston) features interviews with Butch Vig and may contain direct testimony about how the session was arranged. Not checked — the film itself was not accessible via web research. (imdb.com)
