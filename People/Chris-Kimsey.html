Chris Kimsey

Oct 07, 20267 min read

Chris Kimsey

Real name: Christopher Kenneth Kimsey
Discogs ID: 85730
Alias: Mr Crimson
Born: 3 December 1951, Battersea, London
Website: chriskimsey.com

English sound engineer and producer. Best known for his deep working relationship with The Rolling Stones (1,722 shared Discogs credits across 1971–1995), where he rose from tape operator to associate and then co-producer. Equally significant as producer of Marillion’s defining mid-1980s run and The Psychedelic Furs’ biggest US hit album. Career spans from tape operator at Olympic Studios in 1970 through to production and mixing work into the 2020s.

Early Career: Olympic Studios (1970–1977)

Kimsey began his career at Olympic Studios in London (Discogs label ID: 264021), one of the UK’s premier facilities during the era. Early credits are as tape operator, second engineer, and assistant engineer on major sessions:

  • Soft Machine — Third / Fourth (1970–1971, tape operator / assistant)
  • Led Zeppelin — Led Zeppelin III (1970, assistant engineer, uncredited)
  • Ten Years After — A Space In Time (1971, engineer), Rock & Roll Music To The World (1972), Recorded Live (1973)
  • Emerson, Lake & Palmer — Brain Salad Surgery (1973, engineer)
  • Bad Company — Burnin’ Sky (1976, engineer)
  • Peter Frampton — Frampton’s Camel (1973), Frampton (1975, chief engineer / associate producer), Frampton Comes Alive! (1975, live recording), I’m In You (1977, engineer/mix)

By the mid-1970s Kimsey was producing independently, with credits for acts including Frankie Miller, Savoy Brown offshoot tracks, and various A&M Records acts.

The Rolling Stones: Full Arc (1971–1995)

Kimsey’s relationship with the Stones spanned nearly 25 years. His first Stones credit is as engineer on Sticky Fingers (1971). His most intensive period runs from Some Girls (1978) through Flashpoint (1991), encompassing six studio albums:

Some Girls (1978)

Engineer and mixer. The Glimmer Twins produced. Kimsey handled the recording and final mix — a lean, urgent record widely regarded as the band’s strongest in years. Session players: Sugar Blue (harmonica). Mastered by Ted Jensen. Design: Peter Corriston.

Emotional Rescue (1980)

Engineer and associate producer. Additional personnel included Nicky Hopkins and Ian Stewart (piano), Bobby Keys (saxophone), Michael Shrieve (percussion), and Ron Wood on pedal steel. Mastered by Ted Jensen.

Tattoo You (1981)

Associate producer and recording engineer. This album was assembled from outtakes across multiple sessions rather than new recordings; Kimsey’s role involved compiling and preparing the material alongside the Glimmer Twins. Bob Clearmountain handled remixing; Bob Ludwig mastered. Art direction again by Peter Corriston.

Undercover (1983)

Full co-producer (alongside the Glimmer Twins) and recording/mixing engineer. Featured Chuck Leavell on keyboards, percussion from Martin Ditcham and Sly Dunbar, saxophone by David Sanborn. Also notably featured Stephen Lipson as assistant engineer — an early credit for Lipson before his own production career took off.

Steel Wheels (1989)

Co-producer and mixer (tracks A2, B4). Mixing split with Michael H. Brauer and Christopher Marc Potter. Matt Clifford played keyboards and additional instrumentation; Bernard Fowler and Lisa Fischer provided backing vocals; The Kick Horns on brass. Mastered by Ted Jensen.

Flashpoint (1991)

Co-producer and mixer on this live compilation from the Steel Wheels/Urban Jungle tours.

Kimsey also co-produced or mixed numerous Stones singles, B-sides, and compilation tracks through 1994–1995 (including Forty Licks source material, Jump Back). He provided executive production for the Symphonic Music Of The Rolling Stones (1994, RCA Victor). His credit also appears on Honk (2019) as original session producer for the tracks sourced from his era.

Bill Wyman Solo Work (1982)

Produced, engineered, mixed, mastered, and co-wrote one track (“A New Fashion,” A5) on Bill Wyman’s self-titled 1982 solo album (A&M Records). Kimsey appears throughout as a creative partner rather than hired hand — the album was cut close to the Undercover Stones sessions.

Marillion (1985–1988)

Kimsey produced the core of Marillion’s EMI catalog during the Fish era, making him the primary studio architect of the band’s commercial peak:

Also produced individual singles across this run including “Kayleigh,” “Lavender,” “Heart Of Lothian,” “Incommunicado,” “Sugar Mice,” “Warm Wet Circles,” and “Hooks In You.” Engineer on Misplaced Childhood was Thomas Steihler; mixing engineer Mark Freegard assisted.

Ian Mosley (drums), Steve Rothery (guitar), Mark Kelly (4) (keyboards), Pete Trewavas (bass), Fish (vocals/lyrics) — all credited on Misplaced Childhood.

The Psychedelic Furs: Midnight To Midnight (1987)

Produced and mixed (tracks B3, B5) The Psychedelic Furs’ fifth album. Steve Thompson & Michael Barbiero mixed the remaining tracks. Personnel:

The album’s biggest track, “Heartbreak Beat,” reached the US Top 30. The note in the Furs’ vault page listing the Discogs release year as 1986 (master 82380) reflects the UK pressing date; the album came out in 1987.

Killing Joke: Brighter Than A Thousand Suns (1986)

Produced tracks A1–A4 and B2–B4 of Killing Joke’s seventh studio album (EG Records). Julian Mendelsohn mixed those same tracks. Engineer: Thomas Stiehler. Killing Joke lineup: Jaz Coleman (voice, keyboards), Geordie Walker (guitar), Paul Raven (bass), Paul Ferguson (2) (drums). The album is notable as the closest Killing Joke came to a mainstream sound; Kimsey’s production is cleaner and more polished than their earlier EG records.
