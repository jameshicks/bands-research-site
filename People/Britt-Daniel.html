Overview

Britt Daniel is the vocalist and guitarist at the centre of Austin indie rock band Spoon, with whom he has accumulated over 500 Discogs credits stretching back to 1993. His two longest label relationships—Matador (1996–2024) and Merge Records (1998–2022)—trace Spoon’s rise from indie cult act to critical consensus favourite across the 2000s and 2010s. His most consistent creative partners are Spoon drummer Jim Eno (171 shared credits) and producer Mike McCarthy (106). Outside Spoon he has worked in Divine Fits, Golden Millennium, Sally Crewe & The Sudden Moves, and The Alien Beats, cutting a catalogue that touches indie rock, art rock, garage rock, and punk.

Divine Fits — formation and intent

Daniel and Boeckner had been friends and “mutual admirers for five years or so” (Globe and Mail, 2012 — source). Daniel first saw Boeckner at a Handsome Furs concert around 2007; their bands later shared shows. Around 2010, Daniel floated the idea of starting a band together. In February 2011 he called Boeckner directly — “Do you have time to start a new project, like a rock band?” — and Boeckner “jumped at it.” (Cult MTL, Aug 2012)

The songwriting process started with Daniel writing “What Gets You Alone” and sending it to Boeckner, who returned it with his own vocal contributions. Daniel said of his motivation: “I wanted to try some new things and go some places I haven’t gone before” — framing it as creative experimentation after nearly two decades in Spoon. He added: “I didn’t know what it was going to be like — there was no vision.” (Globe and Mail, 2012)

Boeckner cited creative compatibility as the draw: he, Daniel, and drummer Sam Brown were all “merciless editors of music” with no ego friction, making songwriting “fast and fun; it feels very economical and focused.” (Cult MTL, Aug 2012) Boeckner also noted he did not have time for a project that wouldn’t commit: “I don’t feel like I have time to put aside three months for a band that isn’t going to commit to something.” (Globe and Mail, 2012)

One-off vs. ongoing: Both members were explicit that Divine Fits was intended as a real, continuing band. Boeckner stated that “all of its members have made clear that the band is a band and not some one-off project.” Their model was for each member to return to their respective main projects between Divine Fits releases: “With Divine Fits, we always thought we’d do our thing and then obviously Britt would make a Spoon record, I would do something, and then we’d come back and do another Divine Fits record.” (Little Village, 2013) Around the time of the debut album they were already planning singles and a second record. In practice the band went quiet after their final show in Austin in October 2013 and did not release further material.
