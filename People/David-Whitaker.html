David Whitaker

David Whitaker

Aug 23, 202610 min read

David Whitaker

Full name: David Sinclair Whitaker Discogs ID: 16375 Born: 6 January 1931, Kingston upon Thames, England Died: 11 January 2012 Role: Composer, arranger, conductor, music director Nationality: British

Not to be confused with video game composer David Whittaker (born 1957).

Overview

David Sinclair Whitaker was one of the most versatile British orchestral arrangers of the 1960s–2000s. Based primarily in London, he straddled the worlds of pop, film, television, and art music — credited variously as arranger, conductor, music director, composer, and orchestrator across a career spanning at least 1964 to 2025 (posthumous reissues). His most concentrated period of activity ran from 1964 to the early 2000s.

He is best known in distinct spheres: as a key arranger for the London sessions of Lee Hazlewood (contributing to Forty and Cowboy In Sweden); as an orchestrator on Serge Gainsbourg’s Initials B.B.; as the composer/conductor of the 1982 cult sword-and-sorcery film score The Sword and the Sorcerer; and late in his career as the orchestral arranger on AIR’s landmark Moon Safari (1997). His credit also appeared on reissues as recently as 2025 (AIR’s Blue Moon Safari).

He worked under numerous name variants in his credits — “D. S. Whitaker,” “David Sinclair Whitaker,” “David Whittaker,” “D. Sinclair Whitaker,” among more than two dozen documented variants — consistent with a career spread across multiple countries and labels with inconsistent sleeve crediting.

Career by Era

1964–1966 — London Pop Sessions (Decca, CBS, Pye)

Whitaker’s earliest Discogs-documented credits are from 1964–1965, appearing on singles and albums for Decca and Pye Records. He worked as music director on Marianne Faithfull’s Blowin’ In The Wind (Decca, 1964, the single that launched her career) and arranged for numerous British artists including Billie Davis and a string of CBS acts. This era established him as a reliable London session arranger working alongside the active pool of mid-1960s UK pop production.

Notable early credits:

  • Blowin’ In The Wind — Marianne Faithfull (Decca, 1964) — Music Director
  • Some Things Just Stick In Your Mind — Marianne Faithfull (Decca, 1965) — Arranged By
  • This Little Bird — Marianne Faithfull (Decca, 1965) — Arranged By

1965–1967 — The Andrew Loog Oldham Connection / Rolling Stones Adjacent

In 1965, Whitaker arranged The Rolling Stones Songbook (London Records, US) for the Andrew Loog Oldham Orchestra — credited as “David Whittaker.” This was an orchestral treatments record produced by Andrew Loog Oldham, the Stones’ original manager. The connection to Oldham’s world placed Whitaker within the core of mid-1960s British pop production.

He also arranged for the French market during this period, working with Sylvie Vartan (RCA Victor, 1967) and on Gainsbourg’s Johnsyne Et Kossigone collaboration.

1967 — Serge Gainsbourg: Initials B.B. and Comic Strip

A critical year for high-profile international work. Whitaker orchestrated three tracks on Serge Gainsbourg’s Initials B.B. (Philips, 1968 — recorded 1967), sharing orchestration duties with Arthur Greenslade (eight tracks) and Michel Colombier (one track). He also served as arranger and conductor on Gainsbourg’s “Comic Strip” single (Philips, France, 1967) — the notorious duet with Brigitte Bardot, featuring an uncredited session band including Madeline Bell on chorus, Clem Cattini on drums, and Victor Briggs on guitar and sitar. Producer: Giorgio Gomelsky.

1967 — Cat Stevens: New Masters

Whitaker was one of five music directors on Cat Stevens’ second album New Masters (Deram, 1967), alongside Alan Tew, Des Champ, Ivor Raymonde, and Phil Dennys. Producer was Mike Hurst. This was a significant Deram LP, reflecting the label’s then-current approach of using multiple in-house arrangers across different tracks.

1968 — Film Work Begins: Hammerhead

Whitaker composed, produced, and conducted the score for the spy film Hammerhead (Colgems, 1968) — his first major film credit as composer rather than arranger. The soundtrack featured Madeline Bell as a featured vocalist. This marks an important transition: from session arranger to film composer.

He also continued French-language work, orchestrating on Gainsbourg’s Initials B.B. sessions and conducting for Sylvie Vartan and various Philips acts.

1969 — Run Wild, Run Free and the Lee Hazlewood Albums

Two major threads converge in 1969:

  1. Film: Whitaker composed, conducted, and produced the score for Run Wild, Run Free (SGC/CBS-Sony, 1969), a UK film starring Mark Lester. This established him as a credible British film score composer.

  2. Lee Hazlewood: He arranged the majority of tracks (A1, A2, A5, B1, B2) on Lee Hazlewood’s Forty (LHI Records, 1969), produced by Shel Talmy and Lee Hazlewood. Co-arrangers on the album were Big Jim Sullivan (A4) and Johnny Arthey (A3, B4, B5).

1970 — Cowboy In Sweden

Whitaker was one of three arrangers on Lee Hazlewood’s Cowboy In Sweden (LHI Records, 1970), alongside Craig Doerge and Johnny Arthey. Producers included Hazlewood, David Anderle, and associate producers Shel Talmy, Donnie Owens, Jack Robinson, and Larry Marks (3). Vocalists beyond Hazlewood included Nina Lizell and Suzi Jane Hokom. Engineers: Eddie Brackett and Mickey Crofford.

This was a hybrid London/Stockholm/LA production — Whitaker’s involvement likely represents the London string/orchestral overdubs that Hazlewood’s UK recordings routinely used during this period.

1971–1973 — Ongoing Session Work

Continued as arranger on compilation releases and new recordings, including work with Italian pop artists (Anna Oxa, later in the decade) and French acts. Also credited on the Marianne Faithfull Rarities (See For Miles, 1984) as a “Featuring” credit, suggesting some archival tracks reaching formal release.

1973 — Shady Lady

Arranged Shady Lady for Nico Fidenco or related artist (Polydor, Germany/Italy, 1973) — this appears to be Italian pop/lounge work.

1982 — The Sword and the Sorcerer

A cult landmark. Whitaker composed and conducted the full score for The Sword and the Sorcerer (Varèse Sarabande, 1982), performed by the Symphonie-Orchester Graunke. Producers: Scot Holton and John Sievers. The film was a low-budget US sword-and-sorcery picture; the score became notable among cult film music collectors.

He also contributed one composed track (B1) to Music From The Hammer Films (Silva Screen, 1989), sharing the LP with James Bernard (2) and Christopher Gunning, performed by the Philharmonia Orchestra conducted by Neil Richardson.

1989–1996 — Hammer Films, Saint Etienne, French Pop

The 1989 Hammer compilation and ongoing French-language work mark a continued dual career. By the mid-1990s, Whitaker was working with a new generation of artists:

  • Tiger Bay — Saint Etienne (Warner Bros., 1994) — Orchestral Arrangements
  • He’s On The Phone — Saint Etienne (Heavenly, 1995) — Strings
  • Reserection — St. Etienne Daho / Saint Etienne + Etienne Daho (Virgin, 1995) — Orchestra/Conductor
  • Eden — Etienne Daho (Virgin, 1996) — Orchestra Arrangements, Director
  • French pop: Vanessa Paradis’s Jungle Pulse (Virgin, 1995)

His involvement in the Saint Etienne world is a notable late-career bridge between the British orchestral pop tradition and the 1990s indie/Francophile scene centred on Heavenly and Bob Stanley’s circle.

1997–2002 — AIR and the French New Wave Pop Era

Whitaker’s highest-profile late-career work came as arranger and director on AIR’s Moon Safari (Source, 1997) — the defining French electronic-pop album of the era, produced by Jean-Benoît Dunckel and Nicolas Godin. His orchestral arrangements are woven throughout the album’s signature lush sound. He continued with AIR into the 2000s (appearing on the 2025 Blue Moon Safari reissue, suggesting ongoing archival involvement).

Other late 1990s–early 2000s orchestral credits:

  • Shakira — Oral Fixation Vol. 2 (Sony BMG, 2005) — Strings/Conductor
  • Simply Red — Blue (EastWest, 1998) — Orchestra/Conductor (with Pro Arte Orchestra Of London)
  • Kings of Leon — tangential (not confirmed from this data)
  • Annie Lennox — Bare (RCA, 2003) — Strings
  • Robbie Williams — work not confirmed in these credits

He also received his own compilation: The David Whitaker Songbook (Tricatel, 2002, France), compiled with Bertrand Burgalat, presenting arrangements and compositions from across his career.

Open Questions

  1. The Evil of the Daleks credit: Discogs assigns a “Screenwriter [Written By]” credit on The Evil of the Daleks (BBC/Demon, 2019) to this David Whitaker (ID 16375). There was a notable Doctor Who writer named David Whitaker (1928–1980) who wrote The Evil of the Daleks in 1967. These are almost certainly different people; the Discogs attribution may be an error. Web research needed to confirm.

  2. Scope of Lee Hazlewood connection: Discogs logs 22 shared releases but the person_credits tool only surfaced two albums directly (Forty, Cowboy In Sweden). The additional 20 releases likely include reissues, compilations, and individual track credits on Reprise-era Hazlewood material — not all visible in the primary credits list. Worth querying the raw DB.

  3. French session work extent: Credits show extensive French-language work (Gainsbourg, Sylvie Vartan, France Gall, Vanessa Paradis, Etienne Daho) but not all sessions may be documented in Discogs. His Paris-based work is probably underrepresented.

  4. Hammer Films involvement: He composed one track (B1) on the 1989 Music From The Hammer Films compilation but the nature of his original Hammer connection (if any direct scoring credit exists) is unclear from Discogs alone.

  5. Career as conductor vs. composer: The credit pattern suggests he spent more time arranging/conducting than composing original scores. The Hammerhead, Run Wild, Run Free, and Sword and the Sorcerer scores are clear exceptions.
