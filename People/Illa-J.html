Illa J

Detroit rapper and producer, real name John Derek Yancey, younger brother of J Dilla. Active as a solo artist since 2007 and a founding member of the duo Yancey Boys with Frank Nitt.

  • Discogs ID: 1021038 (primary alias entry); also indexed as John Yancey (ID: 1458577)
  • Real name: John Derek Yancey
  • Aliases: John Regal; name variations Illa, Illa J., Illa Jay, Illa-J, Illaj
  • Roles: Rapper, Producer (Co-producer)
  • Groups: Slum Village (member, post-2006); Yancey Boys (duo with Frank Nitt)
  • Years active: 2007–2024 (Discogs range)
  • Top labels: Delicious Vinyl, Jakarta Records, Bastard Jazz Recordings

Biography

Origins and family

John Derek Yancey grew up in Detroit, Michigan, younger brother of James Dewitt Yancey — the producer who became internationally known as J Dilla and Jay Dee. Dilla’s mother Maureen Yancey (known in Detroit hip-hop circles as Ma Dukes) remained an important figure for the family’s musical legacy after his death. Illa J began recording under his own name in the mid-2000s, crediting himself as both MC and co-producer on his early material.

Slum Village membership (2006–2008)

After J Dilla’s death in February 2006, Illa J joined Slum Village — the Detroit rap group his brother had been a founding member of alongside Baatin and T3 (2). The Slum Village Discogs entry lists John Yancey alongside members T3, Baatin, and others as part of the group’s extended post-Dilla lineage. His tenure overlapped with the development of the Yancey Boys project.

Yancey Boys (2008)

The debut album Yancey Boys (2008, Delicious Vinyl, cat. DV 9045) is the central release in Illa J’s catalog. Billed under Illa J’s name, it is built almost entirely around posthumous J Dilla beats — J Dilla (credited as Jay Dee, Discogs ID 33373) holds the Producer credit across the record, with Illa J and Mike Floss serving as Co-producers. Mike Floss also handled mixing and executive production alongside Stacey Kim. Geoff Schroer recorded and mixed. The album features track-level appearances from Guilty Simpson (rapping on “R U Listenin’?”), J Rocc (scratches on “R U Listenin’?” and “DFTF”), Frank Nitt (voice on “Alien Family (Interlude)”), Debi Nova (featured vocalist on “Sounds Like Love”), and comedian Affion Crockett. Paul Powell plays bass on “Sounds Like Love.”

The album saw extensive reissue: Delicious Vinyl pressed it across at least seven distinct configurations 2008–2023, plus a Japanese edition on P-Vine Records (2008). An instrumentals version (Yancey Boys Instrumentals) was released concurrently and also went through multiple pressings.

Note: the brief that initiated this research described the label as Mello Music Group — Discogs records it as Delicious Vinyl (catalog DV 9045). Mello Music Group is a separate label with no documented Illa J releases in the DB.

We Here single and Sounds Like Love EP (2008–2009)

A pre-album single “We Here” (2008, Delicious Vinyl) featured production credited to Jay Dee, with Illa J and Mike Floss as co-producers and J Rocc on scratches. Sounds Like Love (2009, Delicious Vinyl) followed as a standalone single/EP, with Illa J as primary artist.

Mochilla Suite For Ma Dukes tribute (2010)

Illa J contributed vocals to Mochilla Presents Timeless: Suite For Ma Dukes — The Music Of James “J Dilla” Yancey (2010, Mochilla label), a live orchestral tribute to his brother. The release went through three pressings (one as early as 2010, another in 2021).

Yancey Boys duo and Delicious Vinyl singles (2013)

Under the group name Yancey Boys — a duo of Illa J and Frank Nitt (Discogs: Frank Bush, ID 346744 / Frank Nitt, ID 1607052) — two releases appeared on Delicious Vinyl in 2013: “Quicksand” and the single/EP Sunset Blvd. (master 616070, 8 pressings across multiple formats).

Self-titled album (2015)

Illa J (2015, Bastard Jazz Recordings) was produced by Potatohead People (the Canadian duo of Nick Wisdom and AstroLogical (2) / Nate Drobner), who also handled recording and mixing. The album marked a shift toward West Coast-influenced production aesthetics. It was issued in US and Japan editions (including a Bastard Jazz Japan pressing).

Low Key (2016)

“Low Key” (2016, Salute The Sun Records) was released as a collaboration featuring Vanessa White, produced by Chloe Martini.

Home (2017)

Home (2017, Jakarta Records, Germany/US) was produced, arranged, mixed, and recorded entirely by Calvin Valentine, who also contributed vocals to two tracks. Guest vocalists included Danielle Henderson and Dank (2) (Derrick Harvey). Moka Only (Canadian rapper/producer Daniel Denton) contributed writing on one track. Celly handled scratches. The album appeared in multiple pressings across Germany and the US.

John Yancey (2018)

John Yancey (2018, Jakarta Records) continued the collaboration with Calvin Valentine as sole producer/engineer/mixer. Celly (3) handled scratches. This was issued in a European pressing.

The Bakery (2019)

The Bakery (2019, Inner Ocean Records, Canada) was a collaborative album with Atamone (a.k.a. Tam Kadjulik). Jesse Mancini played bass; mastered by Dave Cooley.

French Kiss series and later releases (2021–2024)

The French Kiss series spans three iterations. The original “French Kiss” appeared on Illa J’s self-titled 2015 LP (Bastard Jazz Recordings), produced by Potatohead People, as an Illa J solo track. In 2016, Phife Dawg contributed a verse to the track while he was still alive: Illa J recalled that Phife rang him and rapped the verse over the phone. That version was pressed on a limited Serato 2×12” in 2016 (1,000 copies, Smokin’ Needles), released shortly after Phife’s death in March 2016 — described at the time as “one of his final recordings.” [source: https://ambrosiaforheads.com/2016/08/phife-dawg-illa-j-french-kiss-audio/]

Two sequels followed in 2021 — “French Kiss Deux” and “French Kiss Trois” (the latter also featuring Redman) — both released under Phife Dawg’s name via AWAL Recordings / Smokin’ Needles, and drawn from the posthumous Forever album (released March 22, 2022). Both tracks also credit Illa J and use Potatohead People production. Illa J’s quote on the 2021 release: “I really wish he was here to celebrate the release of this song. But I know he’s here in spirit, and I’ll never forget him spittin’ his verse over the phone to me. That will always be a special moment.” [source: https://consequence.net/2021/05/phife-dawg-new-single-french-kiss-deux-stream/; https://www.okayplayer.com/music/phife-dawg-illa-j-french-kiss-deux-forever.html]

In sum: Phife Dawg’s vocals across the French Kiss series are not samples from existing ATCQ recordings. They are purpose-recorded verses — delivered by Phife over the phone to Illa J before his death — which were archived and released posthumously across the 2016 Serato vinyl and the 2021 Forever singles.

Also in 2021: “Love Games” (with Frank Nitt, S.electY.our.Entertainment), a collaboration with O.S.T.R. on “West Ice / Czas I Pieniądz 2” (Asfalt Records, Poland).

“No Traffic” (2023, BBE) and “Off Days In London” (2024, BBE) mark a relationship with the UK label BBE. “What It Feels Like (Carrtoons Remix)” (2023, Bastard Jazz Recordings) and “New Levels / Chartreuse” (2024, Four Flies Records, Italy) round out the most recent documented activity.

Scene Connections

Illa J is embedded in the Detroit hip-hop lineage through his family connection to J Dilla, who produced nearly the entire Yancey Boys LP from stockpiled beats. The album was overseen by the Delicious Vinyl infrastructure that had already managed late-Dilla-era releases, with Michael “Mike Floss” Ross in the executive producer role.

His membership in Slum Village placed him inside the original Detroit crew that J Dilla co-founded with T3 (2) and Baatin.

The Suite For Ma Dukes tribute (Mochilla, 2010) drew Illa J into a wider circle of Dilla devotees including orchestral arrangers and musicians organized around honoring his brother’s catalog.

Later work moved further from the Detroit enclave: the Illa J LP (2015) connected him with the Canadian neo-soul/funk production scene through Potatohead People and Kaytranada, while the Jakarta Records albums (2017–2018) linked him to the Hamburg-based international hip-hop label roster. The BBE releases (2023–2024) situate him within the UK/European boom-bap circuit.

The posthumous Phife Dawg collaborations on the French Kiss singles (2021) connect him to the A Tribe Called Quest legacy alongside his own family’s place in hip-hop history.
