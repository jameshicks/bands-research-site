Overview

Me Company is a London-based graphic design studio founded in 1985 by Paul White (born 1959, South London). White studied graphics and illustration at West Sussex College of Design, ran his own design company in the early 1980s, and then spent 18 months with a studio called The Clinic before going freelance and eventually establishing Me Company. He was involved in setting up One Little Indian and the studio became the label’s primary visual house from its earliest releases in 1986–87.

The studio’s initial work was rooted in flat-colour, high-contrast sleeve design typical of the mid-1980s British independent scene — a style partly dictated by the economics of small-label print runs. Paul White designed the sleeve for The Sugarcubes’ debut album Life’s Too Good (One Little Indian, 1988), using colour variations derived from a character figure he had been drawing, with flat backgrounds kept deliberately minimal to control printing costs (per Wikipedia, Life’s Too Good article). This connection to the Sugarcubes brought a sustained working relationship with Björk that carried through her entire early solo career.

As access to digital tools expanded in the late 1980s and early 1990s, Me Company became identified with pioneering the use of 3D computer-generated imagery in record sleeve design. The studio’s profile grew substantially through commissions across the British independent and major-label worlds simultaneously: Erasure and Depeche Mode releases on Mute, pop and dance records on Mercury, Cooltempo, and FFRR, and a sustained run of work with The Shamen on One Little Indian through the early 1990s.

The three Björk solo albums Me Company designed — Debut (1993), Post (1995), and Homogenic (1997) — are among the most visually discussed records of the decade. For Post, Paul White surrounded Björk with giant postcards at Piccadilly Circus to represent communication with friends back in Iceland; the photograph was shot by Stéphane Sednaoui (per Wikipedia). For Homogenic, Me Company produced the distinctive Icelandic-inspired geometric digital portrait. The Post design is explicitly credited in Wikipedia as the work of “Paul White of Me Company, who had collaborated with Björk since her time with the Sugarcubes.”

The studio also completed significant volume work for ZTT (Frankie Goes To Hollywood reissues and compilations), React, and numerous One Little Indian acts including Frostbite (2), Spooky, and Utah Saints.

Into the 2000s Me Company continued selectively, designing Garbage’s Beautifulgarbage (2001) and its singles campaign on Mushroom/[PIAS], and contributing to the 2006 Björk box set Surrounded. Paul White also contributed occasional photography and video direction credits.

Personnel

Me Company is structured as a studio rather than a named ensemble. The Discogs listing has one confirmed member:

NameRoleDiscogs ID
Paul WhiteFounder; principal designer347686

Paul White’s own Discogs page (ID 347686) carries pre-Me Company sleeve credits under his own name from 1984–86 — work for Mute (Nick Cave, Fad Gadget, Lydia Lunch) and Some Bizzare — before the studio was established. His name variations include “The Man With No Name” as an occasional alias. He is also listed as a member of Republican High Life (Discogs ID 2277274).
