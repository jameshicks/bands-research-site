Sadie Benning

Video artist and musician, Chicago-born. Discogs artist ID: 1057466. Co-founder of Le Tigre.

Biography

Early Life and Video Art

Sadie Benning began working as a video artist in adolescence, making lo-fi diary films using a Fisher-Price Pixel Vision camera. The work was explicitly queer and autobiographical, drawing attention in underground and gallery circuits through the early-to-mid 1990s. Her visual art practice pre-dates her music career and is the context she returned to after leaving Le Tigre.

Le Tigre (1998–2001)

Benning co-founded Le Tigre in 1998 with Kathleen Hanna (ex-Bikini Kill) and Johanna Fateman. The project fused feminist performance and DIY punk with electronic dance music — drum machines, samples, synth hooks — and articulated a post-riot-grrrl politics explicitly oriented around queer visibility.

She appears as a named performer on the debut self-titled LP (Le Tigre, 1999, Wiiija Records) and the “Hot Topic” single (1999, Wiiija Records), both credited under the Discogs formulation “Performer [Le Tigre Is]” — the band’s standard collective credit convention. The debut was co-produced with Chris Stamey and issued first in the UK on Wiiija, then in the US on Mr. Lady.

Benning left Le Tigre in 2001, before the recording of the second album Feminist Sweepstakes (2001, Mr. Lady). That record credits JD Samson as Performer in her place — the transition is cleanly visible in the Discogs credits, with no overlap between Benning and Samson across the studio discography.

After Le Tigre

Benning returned to visual art after 2001. There is a 2003 Chicks On Speed single “Wordy Rappinghood” that lists Le Tigre as “Vocals [Guest]” — but this is a group credit (Le Tigre as an entity, post-Benning lineup) rather than an individual credit to Benning. Her Discogs page inherits this listing as a band member, but she was not the Le Tigre participant on the track.

Her post-2001 activity (gallery exhibitions, video installations, curatorial work) is outside Discogs scope.

Scene Context

Le Tigre emerged from the aftermath of riot grrrl — specifically the dissolution of Bikini Kill in 1998. Where Bikini Kill had been guitar-driven and confrontational in a punk mode, Le Tigre shifted toward electronic production and dance music, retaining the feminist and queer politics but changing the sonic register significantly. Benning’s background as a video artist shaped the visual and performance dimension of the project; Le Tigre’s live shows integrated projected visuals and choreography.

The band’s founding lineup (Hanna/Fateman/Benning) recorded only one studio album before Benning’s departure — making the 1999 self-titled LP the sole document of the original configuration.
