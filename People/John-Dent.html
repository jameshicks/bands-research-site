John Dent

John Dent

Oct 06, 20266 min read

John Dent

Discogs ID: 147827 Born: unknown Died: 29 December 2017 Based: London, UK Runout signature: “JONZ” (also: JoNZ, Jonz., Jonzzzz, JONZ Loud, Junz, J-nz, and numerous other variants) Name variations: Dent, J. Dent, Jon Dent, John D., John “The Don” Dent

British mastering engineer with one of the most extensive credit trails in UK music history. Active from 1973 until his death in 2017 — a 44-year career spanning reggae, punk, post-punk, industrial, new wave, pop, goth, dub, jungle, drum and bass, and electronic music. His mark appears in the runout grooves of thousands of records under the “JONZ” signature. In 2014 he won Mastering Engineer of the Year at the Music Producers Guild Awards.

Era 1 — Trident Studios (1973–1978)

John Dent’s early career was defined almost entirely by Island Records and its reggae/roots roster, alongside the first wave of UK punk.

Reggae and roots

The Trident period made Dent the principal mastering engineer for Island’s entire UK reggae output. Key credits:

  • Bob Marley & The Wailers — Exodus (1977), Jamming / Punky Reggae Party (1977), Waiting In Vain (1977); multiple pressings across all three
  • Burning Spear — Dry & Heavy (1977), Live (1977)
  • Augustus Pablo — King Tubby Meets The Rockers Uptown (Island, 1977)
  • Grace Jones — Portfolio (Island, 1977)
  • John Martyn — One World (Island, 1977)
  • John Cale — Guts (Island, 1977); Animal Justice (Illegal Records, 1977)
  • Klaus Schulze — Mirage (Brain/Island, 1977); Body Love (Island, 1977)
  • Linton Kwesi Johnson — early singles; Island catalogue through the early 1980s
  • Junior Murvin — Police & Thieves (Island, 1977)
  • Steel Pulse — Nyah Luv (Anchor, 1977)
  • Rico Rodriguez — Dial Africa (Island, 1977)
  • Max Romeo — Reconstruction (Mango, 1977)

Era 2 — The Sound Clinic (1978–1986)

The Sound Clinic years show Dent moving into the post-punk and early industrial/goth scenes alongside continued Island catalogue and new mainstream commissions.

Reggae continuing

Bob Marley’s Legend (Island, 1984) — Dent cut multiple lacquers of the compilation on its original release. Continued Island reggae work throughout the period.

Era 3 — The Exchange (1987–1996)

The Exchange period brought Dent into the centre of the UK’s 1990s alternative mainstream via Mute Records, which became a dominant client.

Mute Records

Near-total coverage of Mute’s vinyl output in this period:

  • Erasure — Chorus (1991); Abba-Esque EP (1992); I Say I Say I Say (1994); many singles and extended mixes
  • Depeche Mode (select credits via Mute subsidiary and reissue work)
  • Moby — Everything Is Wrong (Mute/Elektra, 1995); earlier singles
  • Inspiral Carpets (Cow/Mute, 1991–1993)
  • Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds (Mute, select credits)

Beggars Banquet and related

  • Gary Numan — Premier Hits remaster (Beggars Banquet, 1996)
  • Death Cult — Ghost Dance remaster (Beggars Banquet, 1996)
  • Global Communication — “The Way / The Deep” (Dedicated, 1996)
  • Bill Janovitz — Lonesome Billy (Beggars Banquet, 1996)

PJ Harvey

The Dance Hall At Louse Point album (Island Records, 1996) — collaborative album by PJ Harvey and John Parish — appears in Dent’s credits on both the 1996 vinyl cut and across more than a dozen subsequent pressings, making it one of his most widely distributed later credits.

Era 4 — Loud Mastering (1996–2017)

Co-founded with Jason Mitchell after leaving The Exchange. Loud Mastering served both the electronic music world and the continuing legacy of his roots-music and rock clients.

Drum and bass / jungle / techno

  • Danny Breaks — You Ain’t Down (Evolution, 1996); multiple Evolution label releases
  • Decoder — Circuit Breaker / Life (Tech Itch Recordings, 1996)
  • Carl A. Finlow / Domenic Cappello — “Shielded EP” (20:20 Vision, 1996)
  • Soma Quality Recordings — multiple releases (Carl A. Finlow, Envoy)
  • Norman Jay — “Message In A Dream” (High On Hope, 1996)

Jazz and roots

  • Ernest Ranglin — Below The Bassline (Island Jamaica Jazz, 1996)
  • Monty Alexander — Yard Movement (Island Jamaica Jazz, 1996)
  • Fairport Convention — Old New Borrowed Blue (Woodworm, 1996)
  • John Cale — Artificial Intelligence remaster (Beggars Banquet, 1996)

Scale of credits

John Dent’s Discogs credits exceed 2,000 unique releases (each 500-row query caps out before reaching the full span of any single decade). Within the Discogs database alone:

  • 1973–1980: 500+ credits, Island Records accounting for ~200 (~40%)
  • 1981–1986: 500+ credits; Island, Illuminated Records, and Midnight Music are top labels
  • 1987–1996: 500+ credits; Mute dominates (44 credits in the sample alone), followed by Beggars Banquet (39)
  • 1996–2017: 500+ credits; evolution of Loud Mastering into d&b, techno, jazz, and remastering

Total estimated Discogs credits: well in excess of 3,000, making him one of the most-credited mastering engineers in the UK independent music record.
