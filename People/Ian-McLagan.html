Ian McLagan

British keyboard player (organ, piano, Hammond B3). Discogs artist ID: 306714. Born Ian Patrick McLagan, 12 May 1945, Isleworth, Middlesex, England; died 3 December 2014, Austin, Texas, after a stroke, aged 69. Universally known as “Mac.” Best remembered as the organist and pianist of the Small Faces (1965–1969) and the Faces (3) (1969–1975), though he spent four decades as one of rock’s most sought-after session and touring keyboardists.

Biography

Early life and the Muleskinners (early 1960s)

McLagan grew up in West London (Hounslow/Isleworth) and began playing in bands in the early 1960s. His first documented group was The Muleskinners (Discogs ID: 1277318), a West London R&B outfit. He also passed through a pre-fame group called the Boz People. These apprentice years placed him squarely in the London R&B scene that was feeding the mod movement.

Small Faces (1965–1969)

In late 1965 McLagan replaced original keyboardist Jimmy Winston in the Small Faces (Discogs ID: 152682). The other members were vocalist/guitarist Steve Marriott, bassist/vocalist Ronnie Lane, and drummer Kenny Jones. The band recorded for Decca (1965–1967) and then Immediate Records (1967–1969), the latter period producing their most ambitious work.

McLagan contributed organ, piano, mellotron, harpsichord, and other keyboards across the Small Faces catalogue. His writing credit appears on several Immediate singles and albums, including Ogdens’ Nut Gone Flake (1968) and the compilation There Are But Four Small Faces (US, 1968). He also took a songwriting co-credit on “Tin Soldier” (1967) — one of the band’s most celebrated singles.

Steve Marriott left the band in early 1969 to form Humble Pie, effectively ending the original Small Faces.

Faces (1969–1975)

The remaining Small Faces — McLagan, Lane, and Jones — recruited vocalist Rod Stewart and guitarist Ron Wood (both previously of the Jeff Beck Group) to form the Faces (3) (Discogs ID: 289040). The lineup was completed later by bassist Tetsu Yamauchi (who replaced Ronnie Lane in 1973).

McLagan played piano, organ, harmonium, electric piano (Wurlitzer), and clavinet across the Faces catalogue. He also co-wrote “Cindy Incidentally” (1973) and “You Can Make Me Dance, Sing Or Anything” (1974). He is credited as Compilation Producer on the 1999 Rhino anthology The Best Of Faces: Good Boys… When They’re Asleep… and as Box Set Producer on the 2004 Rhino set Five Guys Walk Into A Bar…, demonstrating his ongoing curatorial role in the Faces legacy.

He was also a featured musician on concurrent Rod Stewart solo albums recorded during the Faces years (see Session Work below).

Rolling Stones touring and New Barbarians (1978–1979)

After the Faces split in 1975, McLagan became a touring keyboardist for the The Rolling Stones, appearing on the Some Girls sessions (1978) — he plays Hammond organ on “Shattered” (track A3) and electric piano on “Miss You” (track A1). He continued as a Stones touring musician, appearing on the live album Still Life (American Concert 1981) (1982).

In 1979 McLagan co-founded The New Barbarians (Discogs ID: 1709529) with Keith Richards, Ron Wood, Bobby Keys, Stanley Clarke, and Joseph Modeliste. The New Barbarians were assembled primarily to support Ron Wood’s touring commitments and performed a short North American tour.

Solo career — Los Angeles era (1979–1994)

McLagan relocated to Los Angeles in 1979 to begin a solo career. His debut album Troublemaker (Mercury, 1979) featured an extraordinary cast: Keith Richards (guitar), Ron Wood (guitar, vocals), Ringo Starr (drums), Jim Keltner (drums), Bobby Keys (horns), Stanley Clarke (bass), and Joseph Modeliste (drums) — effectively a supergroup session. Producer was Geoff Workman; McLagan wrote the majority of the songs.

His second album Bump In The Night (Mercury, 1981) introduced The Bump Band as his backing group. Ron Wood again appeared as a guest. Producer was Rob Fraboni.

A third album Last Chance To Dance (Barking Dog, 1985) followed.

Austin era and The Bump Band (1994–2014)

McLagan relocated again in 1994, this time to Austin, Texas, where he became a fixture of the city’s roots-rock and Americana scene. He assembled and maintained The Bump Band as his regular touring and recording group. Bump Band core members across various periods included:

  • Jud Newcomb (guitar) — “Scrappy Jud Newcomb”
  • Johnny Lee Schell (guitar)
  • Conrad Choucroun (drums)
  • Don Harvey (bass/guitar)
  • Mark Andes (bass)
  • Ray Ohara (bass)
  • Ricky Fataar (drums)
  • George Reiff (bass)
  • Gurf Morlix (guitar)
  • Sarah Brown (7) (bass)

From Austin, McLagan sustained a prolific session career (see below), appearing on records by Bonnie Raitt, Ryan Adams, Billy Bragg, Bruce Springsteen retrospectives, Lucinda Williams, and many others. His final studio album as a primary artist, United States (Yep Roc Records, 2014), was released the year he died.

Personal life

McLagan was married to Sandy Sarjeant (1968–1972). His second wife was Kim Mclagan (née Kim Kerrigan, previously Kim Moon — widow of Keith Moon of The Who), whom he married in 1978. She died in 2006. McLagan’s daughter-in-law Dermot Kerrigan appears in credits on Rise & Shine! (2004). McLagan died in Austin on 3 December 2014, following a stroke.

Scene Context

McLagan spans several distinct scenes:

British Invasion / Mod rock (1965–1969). The Small Faces were a core mod band from the East End/West London scene. Their Immediate-era records pushed psychedelia, but McLagan’s Hammond work kept the R&B and soul foundation intact.

Early 1970s British rock (1969–1975). The Faces occupied a looser, more working-class boozy-rock register than their contemporaries. McLagan’s piano and organ gave the band much of its warmth; his Wurlitzer is particularly distinctive on the later Faces records.

Rolling Stones touring circle (1978–1982). McLagan was among the cohort of extra musicians who supplemented the Stones in the late 1970s/early 1980s, alongside Ian Stewart, Bobby Keys, and others. The Some Girls appearance is his most widely-heard contribution in this role.

Austin Americana / roots-rock (1994–2014). In Austin, McLagan became a fixture of the city’s session culture, recording with local and visiting artists across blues, country-rock, and Americana. He mentored younger Austin musicians and kept The Bump Band active as a live draw until close to his death.
