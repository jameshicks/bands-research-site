Ian Caple

UK recording engineer and producer. Discogs artist ID: 78315. Active from 1982 onward, principally based in England. Caple is one of the most consistently credited engineers in the 1980s–2000s British independent music scene, with a particular long-term relationship with Shriekback (104 shared releases) and Tindersticks (both self-titled albums, Can Our Love…, and related singles). Also a member of Noise Abroad (Discogs ID: 86322) alongside Dave Allen (5), Luc Van Acker, and Brian Nevill.

Overview

Caple’s credit history spans six decades of releases — from 1982 Y Records post-punk to 2025 self-released Shriekback material. His roles shift fluidly between engineering, recording, mixing, producing, and occasionally co-writing; by the early 1990s he was routinely taking full producer credit on major-label records as well as indie releases. His client list ranges from early-80s Belgian and Yugoslav art-pop to Tricky’s 4th & Broadway albums, Tindersticks’s first two LPs, Mansun’s debut, JJ72’s debut, and a long parallel run of French pop and chanson through the 2000s–2010s.

He also appears as a performer on the 1984 Cherry Red release “Baltimore [Behold The Precious Stupa] Positive Paranoia!” (release ID: 1064134), suggesting limited session work as a player in addition to his studio roles.

Noise Abroad

The group Noise Abroad (Discogs ID: 86322) was a four-piece comprising:

  • Dave Allen (5) — bass, keyboards, drum programming
  • Luc Van Acker — guitar, vocals, keyboards, percussion, drum programming
  • Brian Nevill — saxophone, vocals, keyboards, percussion, drum programming
  • Ian Caple — engineering, tape loops, vocal treatments, drum programming, co-writer

Their sole documented release on Discogs is the 1983 single “Vent That Spleen” (EMI, Belgium; release ID: 110408). All four members share a co-writing credit (credited as I. Caple, D. Allen, L. Van Acker, B. Nevill). Caple’s role is unusual here — “Flight Deck Engineering” alongside tape loops and vocal treatments marks him as a creative contributor rather than a pure engineer. The Belgian EMI release places the group in the overlap between the UK post-punk diaspora and the Belgian industrial/new wave scene (Front 242, Luc Van Acker’s solo career, Arbeid Adelt!).

Shriekback

Caple’s most sustained client relationship. He is credited on releases spanning from 1982 (My Spine Is The Bassline, Y Records) through to 2025 (Thunder Among The Lilies, self-released), making him present across every major phase of the band’s activity.

First album credits (release ID: 389316): Caple produces and engineers alongside Tindersticks themselves; Terry Edwards (trumpet) is the only other outside contributor credited. Second album credits (release ID: 389323): Caple recorded and mixed; the album carries a large string section for the orchestrated tracks. His Claire Denis film score work (the Tindersticks-scored films): the 2011 compilation Claire Denis Film Scores 1996–2009 (Constellation) credits Caple as recorded/mixed at Eastcote Studios and at La Ferme.

Goat (5)

Co-produced the debut single “Can’t Get By” (1989, Situation Two; release ID: 2426158) and engineered the B-side remix “Kissin Me” on “Your Love Isa Mine” (1990, Beggars Banquet). See Goat (5) for full personnel detail.

Mansun

Caple recorded (but did not produce) Attack of the Grey Lantern (1997, Parlophone; release ID: 463522), the debut album by Mansun — a Number 1 UK chart album. Producer credit went to the band’s Paul Draper; Caple is listed as “Recorded By” on tracks 1, 3–7, 9–11. Mix credits are split between Clif Norrell and Mark “Spike” Stent.

JJ72

Caple produced the debut self-titled album by Irish rock band JJ72 (2000, Lakota; release ID: 424360), distributed in the UK. The record also credits Wired Strings for orchestration. Related singles on Lakota include October Swimmer, Oxygen, and Snow — all produced by Caple.

Broken Records

Caple co-produced Until the Earth Begins to Part (2009, 4AD; release IDs: 1762052 and 1796353) by Edinburgh post-rock group Broken Records. Both pressings credit Caple as producer (or co-producer / mixed by) alongside the band.

French Pop and Chanson (2000s–2020s)

A significant strand of Caple’s later career involves French artists. The Discogs record documents recurring production and mixing work across a range of French acts and labels, most prominently:

  • Kat Onoma — Kat Onoma (2001, EMI; release ID: 606526), full producer credit
  • Cousteau (2) — debut LP Cousteau (2000, Palm Pictures; release ID: 263166), producer alongside Robin Brown and Davey Ray Moor
  • Têtes Raides / Le Ours — Le Ours (2005, Naïve; release ID: 6099842), co-producer and mixed
  • Bikini Machine — Daily Music Cookin’ With Bikini Machine (2006, Platinum); Let’s Party With Bikini Machine (2012)
  • Florent Marchet — Faux Mouvement (2000, Small / Le Village Vert; release ID: 921096), producer and mixed
  • Camille — Le Fil (partial credits), Mue (2014, Barclay; release ID: 5503177), co-producer and Recorded By
  • Cali (French singer-songwriter) — multiple releases on Barclay/Sony BMG in the mid-2000s
  • Various French pop and chanson releases through 2020s (mixing credits)

Work for Globe Style in 1986–1987 gives an earlier international strand: Caple engineered and mixed several world music / African compilations and artist albums for that London-based label.

Open Questions

  • Caple’s base of operations (studio affiliation) in the 1980s is not confirmed from Discogs — he is referenced as “Flight Deck Engineering” in the Noise Abroad credit, but what studio or facility this denotes is unclear. The King Swamp / Tindersticks work likely used multiple London facilities including Angel Studios (cited in Tindersticks credits) and Eastcote Studios (cited in the Claire Denis film scores compilation).
  • Noise Abroad: only one release documented in Discogs. Whether the group had further activity (live performances, unreleased recordings) is not resolvable from the DB.
  • The Brian Nevill who appears in Noise Abroad (Discogs ID: 367723) has no other substantial discography in Discogs — his background and connection to the others is unclear.
  • The 1991 4AD “Queer” credit is anomalous — the Discogs entry attributes it to Garbage, but the timeline predates Garbage’s formation; this may be a mis-tag or a different act.
  • Caple’s French-language client base is large and largely undocumented in the vault; a dedicated research pass on his French work (Camille, Cali, Kat Onoma, Florent Marchet, Bikini Machine) would surface a distinct career strand from the 2000s onward.
  • Web sources would be needed for: biographical background, studio affiliations, interviews about working methods, Noise Abroad live activity.
