Biography

Early Career and Blondie (1974–1982)

Jimmy Destri (born James Mollica) grew up in Brooklyn and became one of the founding members of Blondie (Discogs ID: 16655), the New York new wave band that emerged from the CBGB scene in the mid-1970s. As the band’s keyboardist, Destri was central to their sound across the classic run of albums on Chrysalis Records. His instrument credits shift across releases — Farfisa organ, RMI electric piano, grand piano, Polymoog synthesizer, Roland synthesizer, and electronic keyboards — reflecting the range of tones he brought to the band’s material.

He also contributed as a songwriter. His most prominent composition credit is “Presence Dear” — “(I’m Always Touched By Your) Presence Dear” (Chrysalis, 1978) — a UK hit single drawn from Plastic Letters. He co-wrote “Dreaming” and “Heart of Glass” (both credited on singles pressed in multiple territories), and contributed songwriting to The Hunter (1982). His song “Maria” (co-written with Blondie) appeared on the 1999 reunion album No Exit (Beyond Records) and became the band’s final UK number one, its writing credits confirmed across many international pressings (master ID 120609).

Solo Work (1981)

In 1981, Destri released a solo single on Chrysalis: “Heart On A Wall” (Discogs ID: 1867029; master ID: 261350). He is the primary artist and wrote the song, played vocals, guitar, and keyboards, and co-mixed it. The session featured an unusually strong cast of collaborators:

The B-side and surrounding 1981 sessions also generated a second solo release: “If It’s The Last Thing I Do” (Next Plateau Records, 1984; Discogs ID: 1160521), on which Destri served as producer and co-wrote with Keith Forsey. The recording was credited to Risqué (3).

Outside Production Work (1979–1984)

During and immediately after the Blondie peak years, Destri was active as a producer for other downtown New York artists. His production credits in this period:

Student Teachers — “Channel 13” / “Christmas Weather” (Ork Records, 1979; Discogs ID: 1606712)
The debut single by the Student Teachers, released on Ork Records — the Terry Ork label that had also pressed Television’s first single. Destri produced; Ray Bardani (Discogs ID: 132144) engineered. The session personnel: Phillip Shelley (guitar), Lori Reese (bass, credited “Basic Black Bass”), Laura Davis (drums, “Pink Drums”), Bill Arning (keyboards, “After Eight Keyboards”), Jan David Cruel [David Scharff] (vocals). Jerry Silber served as executive producer.

Joey Wilson — Going Up (Modern Records, 1980; Discogs ID: 3200360)
Destri produced and played keyboards on this release, alongside a large session cast that included Michael Kamen (string arrangements, conductor), Roland Alphonso (saxophone), and Ellie Greenwich (backing vocals on two tracks).

Joey Wilson — Hold On Girl (Modern Records, 1980; Discogs ID: 6261693)
Destri credited as producer.

Joey Wilson — If You Don’t Want My Love (Modern Records, 1980; Discogs ID: 5396460)
Destri credited as producer.

Marty Thau Presents 2x5 (Red Star Records, RED 100, 1980; Discogs ID: 1093880)
Destri produced this key compilation curated by Marty Thau, placing two tracks each from five New York acts: The Fleshtones, Revelons, Bloodless Pharaohs (Brian Setzer’s pre-Stray Cats band), Comateens, and Student Teachers. Engineers were Cliff Hodsdon (credited as Cliff Hodson) and Jim Bonnefond. Cover design was by Peter Zaremba of The Fleshtones. Howie Weinberg mastered.

Student Teachers retrospective — Invitation To… The Student Teachers: 1978–1980 — The Complete Syllabus (Nacional Records, 2013; Discogs ID: 5138218)
Destri is credited as producer for the first batch of tracks (tracks 1–4/5) on this career-spanning retrospective, encompassing both the 1979 Ork single and additional early recordings. Later-period tracks were produced by Antone DeSantis and Jay Burnett.

Session Work and Peripheral Credits (1980–1994)

Outside of Blondie and his own solo/production work, Destri appears in several other session contexts:

  • Jah Malla — Jah Malla (Modern Records / WEA, 1981; Discogs ID: 20336020): Destri plays Polymoog synthesizer on this reggae-influenced project. The session also featured David Sanborn and Hank Crawford (alto saxophone), Jackie Mittoo (organ), Roland Alphonso (tenor saxophone), and Dick Wagner (guitar). Michael Kamen produced and arranged.
  • The Colors — The Colors (Dirt Records, 1982; Discogs ID: 3191565): Additional keyboards.
  • The Shape Of Things To Come (Fuz Records, 1983; Discogs ID: 22268734): Keyboards.
  • Brace Yourself! A Tribute To Otis Blackwell (Shanachie, 1994; Discogs ID: 1562617): Organ and piano.
  • Ovulation (American Empire Records / Priority Records, 1994; Discogs ID: 5231534): Mixed By; Producer.

Blondie Reunion (1997–2003)

Destri participated in the Blondie reunion, which coalesced around 1997 under the umbrella of the pseudonym Adolph’s Dog (Discogs ID: 600843) — a name adopted after the band discovered that Hitler’s dog had been named Blondie. Under that name, with the pre-reformation lineup (Destri, Chris Stein, Deborah Harry, Gary Lachman, and Clement Anthony Bozewski), they released a single track: “Ordinary Bummer,” for the Iggy Pop tribute compilation We Will Fall: The Iggy Pop Tribute (Royalty Records, 1997; Discogs ID: 772000).

The official Blondie reunion followed in 1999 with No Exit (Beyond Records), on which Destri is credited as performer and has lyric/music writing credits. Livid (Beyond Records, 1999) features his keyboards in a live context. The next reunion studio album, The Curse of Blondie (Epic, 2003), credits him as musician. Destri did not participate in subsequent Blondie activity — he is not credited on Panic of Girls (2011) or later releases.

The Jah Trio

Destri was a member of The Jah Trio (Discogs ID: 771068), alongside Frank Infante (also a Blondie member; alias “Frank Freak”; Discogs ID: 422777) and Mike Chapman (Discogs ID: 74105) — the Australian-born producer who produced Blondie’s run of commercial peaks (Parallel Lines, Eat to the Beat, Autoamerican, The Hunter). No releases are documented on Discogs under the Jah Trio name; the group appears to be a informal project uniting three people from the Blondie production circle.

Scene Connections

Destri’s production work in 1979–1980 was concentrated in the downtown New York post-punk and new wave circuit — the same world that surrounded Ork Records (Television, Student Teachers) and Red Star Records (Suicide, Real Kids). His position as keyboardist of Blondie gave him access as a producer: the Student Teachers specifically sought him out because of the Blondie connection, and Marty Thau’s choice of Destri to produce the 2x5 compilation reflects the same social network. The bands on 2x5 — The Fleshtones, Comateens, Bloodless Pharaohs, Revelons, and Student Teachers — were all working the CBGB/Max’s Kansas City circuit.

The Joey Wilson sessions on Modern Records (1980) represent a different strand — a more commercial or reggae-adjacent orbit (Michael Kamen, Roland Alphonso, Ellie Greenwich) that is less well documented.
