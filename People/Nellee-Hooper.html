Nellee Hooper

Nellee Hooper

Oct 19, 20266 min read

Nellee Hooper

Producer, DJ, percussionist. Bristol, UK. Discogs ID: 11831. Real name: Paul Andrew Hooper. Born 15 March 1963, Bristol.

One of the most consequential UK producers of the 1990s. Started as a teenage percussionist in Maximum Joy and a DJ in The Wild Bunch (the Bristol collective that became Massive Attack); transitioned into record production and spent the decade at the centre of some of the most important pop and electronic records made in Britain. Co-produced Soul II Soul’s first two albums; produced Sinéad O’Connor’s “Nothing Compares 2 U”; produced or co-produced four Björk albums; co-produced Madonna’s Bedtime Stories and wrote “Bedtime Story” (with Björk and Marius de Vries); produced Tina Turner’s “GoldenEye” (Bond theme, written by Bono and The Edge); co-produced Massive Attack’s Protection; produced the Romeo + Juliet soundtrack. Won the Brit Award for Best Producer in 1995.

Early Bristol: Maximum Joy and The Wild Bunch (1979–1987)

Founding member of Maximum Joy (1979), playing percussion and backing vocals; credited on “Why Can’t We Live Together” (1983) as Syndrums/percussion. Simultaneously a key DJ in The Wild Bunch, the Bristol sound system / collective that laid the groundwork for Massive Attack and the Bristol sound of the 1990s.

Also played percussion on Give! (Zimdiscz, UK, 1982) — a Zimbabwe music release, reflecting the Bristol scene’s early engagement with African and Caribbean sounds.

Soul II Soul (1988–1991)

Co-produced both of Soul II Soul’s studio albums with founder Jazzie B (Beresford Romeo).

Contained “Keep On Movin’” and “Back To Life (However Do You Want Me)” — major UK hits. Hooper is co-composer on multiple tracks. Engineered at Brittania Row by Arabella Rodriguez.

Vol. II (1990 – A New Decade) (10 Records, 1990)

Co-produced with Jazzie B. Singles “A Dream’s A Dream”, “Missing You”, “People”, “Wish”. Also produced and mixed by Hooper and Jazzie B.

Also produced Soul II Soul adjacent singles for other artists during this period: “Fair Play” and “Feel Free” (both 10 Records, 1988).

Sinéad O’Connor (1990)

“Nothing Compares 2 U” (Chrysalis / Ensign, US, 1990)

Co-produced with O’Connor. The lead single from I Do Not Want What I Haven’t Got (which Hooper produced in full); the song — written by Prince — became one of the most iconic singles of the decade. Also produced: “The Chimes” and the Tracy Chapman single “How Long” (both 1990).

Massive Attack (1991–1994)

Blue Lines (Wild Bunch / Circa, 1991) — Mixing

Mixed “Safe From Harm” and “Unfinished Sympathy” from Massive Attack’s debut album — the two most celebrated tracks on the record. His mixing role (rather than full production credit) reflects the collaborative authorship of the Wild Bunch circle.

Björk (1993–1996)

The most sustained creative partnership in Hooper’s discography. Produced key tracks across Debut, Post, and their overlap.

Hooper plays percussion on the record as well as producing and engineering. “Human Behaviour” and “Big Time Sensuality” are among his productions. Bruce Smith (ex-The Pop Group) on drums — a direct Bristol connection back to Maximum Joy’s world. Also features Talvin Singh (tabla).

Post (One Little Indian, 1995)

Further productions; Hooper credited on “Isobel”, “It’s Oh So Quiet”, “Venus As A Boy” (remix). The Brit Award win included this album.

”Bedtime Story” — Madonna (Maverick / Sire, 1995)

The Björk-Hooper collaboration extended to writing: “Bedtime Story” from Madonna’s Bedtime Stories was written by Björk, Nellee Hooper, and Marius de Vries — the only Madonna single co-written by Björk.

Madonna: Bedtime Stories (Maverick / Sire / Warner, 1994)

Full co-production credit on the album, including “Bedtime Story” and the album’s overall sound. His contribution deepened the trip-hop and ambient influences that defined the record.

The James Bond GoldenEye theme, written by Bono and The Edge (U2), produced by Hooper. A major Hollywood/pop convergence moment; Craig Armstrong arranged the orchestral score.

Romeo + Juliet Soundtrack (Capitol Records, 1996)

Executive producer and composer across both soundtrack volumes for Baz Luhrmann’s film. The Vol. 1 soundtrack (featuring Garbage, The Cardigans, Radiohead, Des’ree, Gavin Friday, Butthole Surfers, and One Inch Punch) was a major commercial success. Vol. 2 features additional original material. Also produced the Kym Mazelle single “Young Hearts Run Free” (Capitol, 1996) for the film.

Meanwhile… Imprint (Virgin, late 1990s)

Founded the Meanwhile imprint as a Virgin subsidiary in the late 1990s, with the group Furslide as an early project. Also released his own productions: “All Over My Face”, “OVERMYHEAD”, “Love Song ©”, “Payback Time” (all 1999-2003, Meanwhile… / Virgin).
