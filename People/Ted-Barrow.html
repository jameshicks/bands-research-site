Discogs ID: 741343

Origin: UK

Overview

Ted Barrow is a UK drummer and percussionist operating in London’s experimental underground. His Discogs footprint is small but spans nearly four decades and covers markedly different scenes: a 1983 guest appearance on The Lowest Note’s The Lowest Note On The Organ (on Woof Records, the label run by members of The Work and closely associated with Tim Hodgkinson); a 1989 drums credit on one track of Matfield & The Pond’s Not Quite ‘It’ (Distortion Records (4)); and a 2019 percussion credit on Unhappy Fly by the Unhappy Fly ensemble. His most central association is Die Trip Computer Die, which he joined in 1994 — completing the trio of himself, Lepke Buchvalter, and Jim Welton (alias Xentos, among many others). Barrow’s alias cluster (Bongo Bongo Barrow, Ted, Ted Mantee) reflects the playful pseudonym culture common to the wider post-punk / RIO-adjacent London underground.

Context: Release Environments

The Lowest Note On The Organ (1983, Woof Records)

The Lowest Note (members: Bill Gilonis, Mick Hobbs, Rick Wilson, Tim Hodgkinson) was a project on Woof Records, the label associated with This Heat/Work-adjacent musicians. The release’s “Very Special Guest” credit is a loose one — Barrow is listed alongside several others including Véronique Vincent (of Aksak Maboul / Honeymoon Killers) and Charles Bullen (This Heat). The 1983 date places Barrow in that Woof circle well before any Die Trip Computer Die involvement.

Not Quite ‘It’ (1989, Distortion Records (4))

A 15-track compilation LP. Primary artist is Matfield & The Pond — a UK experimental/lo-fi project with no further Discogs profile data (no members listed, no other releases in the DB). Barrow, credited as “Ted,” plays real drums on track A3 (“Cloned”) only. The label Distortion Records (4) (ID: 246070) has only two releases in Discogs: She’s Like Heroin (The Morticians (3), 1988) and this LP. The label appears to be a small, short-lived UK indie imprint.

Sibling Family Research (1995, self-released)

Die Trip Computer Die’s only Discogs entry. Credited performers are Lepke Buchvalter (as “Lepke Buchwater”) and Xentos (as “Xentos (Flycraken) Jones”) — Barrow is absent from the Discogs credit list despite joining the group in 1994 per the band’s own Discogs profile.

Unhappy Fly (2019, Emotional Response (2))

Unhappy Fly is a Jim Welton-led ensemble. Discogs lists three core members: Jim Welton, Richard Dudanski, and Sarah Washington (2). The album on Emotional Response (Emotional Response (2), ID: 604742 — 264 releases) reconnects Barrow with Welton/Xentos 25 years after Die Trip Computer Die.
