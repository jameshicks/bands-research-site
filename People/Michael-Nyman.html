Michael Nyman

Composer, pianist. UK. Born Michael Laurence Nyman. Discogs artist ID: 39574.

Overview

Leading British minimalist composer, pianist, and music critic. One of the key figures connecting the UK experimental classical world to post-punk and art-rock. Led The Michael Nyman Band (Discogs ID: 595202) throughout his career, and later The Michael Nyman Orchestra (ID: 711060). A member of The Scratch Orchestra in the early 1970s alongside Cornelius Cardew, Brian Eno, John Tilbury, Keith Rowe, and Eddie Prévost.

He coined the term “minimalism” applied to music in a 1968 article on Cornelius Cardew. His decade-long collaboration with filmmaker Peter Greenaway, all produced by David Cunningham, produced some of the most distinctive British film music of the 1980s. The 1993 soundtrack to Jane Campion’s The Piano was his biggest commercial crossover, selling millions worldwide.

Early Career (1974–1980)

Nyman contributed organ to Gavin Bryars’s The Sinking of the Titanic (1978, Obscure (2)), a key document of early British experimentalism produced by Brian Eno on his Obscure label. He played piano, marimba, and glockenspiel on Tom Phillips’s Irma (1977, Obscure (2)) and contributed liner notes to other releases in the Obscure catalog.

His debut as primary artist was Decay Music (1976, Island Records / Obscure (2)), produced by Brian Eno — a solo work of sustained, decaying piano tones that established his minimalist approach. He also contributed liner notes to early American minimalist releases (Frederic Rzewski’s Voices and Instruments, Antilles, 1976).

He appeared as a live euphonium player on the Cornelius Cardew-adjacent recording Hallelujah (1974, Transatlantic Records).

The Scratch Orchestra

Member of The Scratch Orchestra (Discogs ID: 115798), the radical ensemble founded by Cornelius Cardew in 1969. Other members included Brian Eno, John Tilbury, Keith Rowe, Eddie Prévost, Howard Skempton, and Christopher Hobbs. The Scratch Orchestra was fundamental to British experimental music’s political and aesthetic radicalism in the early 1970s.

The Greenaway Scores (1982–1993)

The core of Nyman’s catalog from 1982–1991 is the series of film scores for director Peter Greenaway, all produced by David Cunningham. Cunningham’s role varied across releases — producer, co-producer (recording), editor, mixing engineer — but he is credited on every major score in the series. The ensemble performing these scores was The Michael Nyman Band.

On The Draughtsman’s Contract, Nyman played harpsichord and piano. On A Zed & Two Noughts, he conducted and played harpsichord, sharing production credit with Cunningham. On Drowning By Numbers and The Cook, The Thief, he co-produced alongside Cunningham.

The Nyman/Greenaway Soundtracks (1989, Venture) compiled highlights from the collaboration.

The post-Greenaway score Prospero’s Books (1991, Decca) was the last with Cunningham producing; from this release Nyman began handling production himself.

The Kiss And Other Movements (1985, Editions EG)

Released on Editions EG/Polydor, produced jointly by Nyman and Cunningham. Notable for including vocalist Dagmar Krause, bassist John Greaves (of Henry Cow), and vocalist Omar Ebrahim. Nyman played piano and Kurzweil electric piano.

The Piano (1993)

Nyman’s best-known work internationally: the soundtrack to Jane Campion’s The Piano (1993). Released on Venture/Virgin (UK) and Delabel (France, as La Leçon de Piano). Nyman composed, arranged, produced, conducted, and played piano. The album sold millions of copies globally and is credited with bringing contemporary classical music to mainstream pop audiences.

The release on Virgin (US) spawned a single, The Heart Asks Pleasure First / The Promise, issued on Virgin Movie Music (1993).

The score did not involve David Cunningham as producer; Nyman handled production himself at this stage.

The Flying Lizards Connection

Nyman appeared as guest pianist on the The Flying Lizards album Fourth Wall (1981, Virgin), specifically on the track “Hands 2 Take.” The single release of “Hands 2 Take” credits Nyman on piano alongside David Cunningham (guitar, keyboards, percussion; producer), Patti Palladin (vocals), Stephen Saunders (trombone), and John Harle and Keith Thompson on reeds — overlapping almost exactly with the Michael Nyman Band personnel of the period.

Fourth Wall was produced and mixed by David Cunningham, linking the Flying Lizards and the Nyman Band as essentially the same network of post-punk/experimental musicians.

Ravenous (1999, EMI Soundtracks) was co-composed with Damon Albarn — an unusual crossover credit with the Blur and Gorillaz frontman.

Chamber and Concert Music

Alongside film scores, Nyman maintained a parallel catalog of concert works:

  • String Quartets 1–3 (1991, Argo (2)) — Kronos Quartet-era string writing
  • The Suit and the Photograph: String Quartet No. 4 / 3 Quartets (1998, EMI Classics)
  • The Piano Concerto / MGV (1994, Argo (2)) — Nyman as pianist with orchestra
  • Concertos: Double Concerto for Saxophone & Cello, Harpsichord Concerto, Trombone Concerto (1997, EMI Classics)
  • Noises, Sounds & Sweet Airs (1994, Argo (2)) — vocal work
  • The Man Who Mistook His Wife For A Hat (1988, CBS) — chamber opera after Oliver Sacks
  • Facing Goya: An Opera in Four Acts (2002, Warner Classics)
  • Sangam (Michael Nyman Meets Indian Masters) (2002, Warner Classics) — cross-cultural collaboration

Early 2000s and MN Records

Nyman founded his own label, MN Records, in the mid-2000s. Key releases:

  • Nyman/Greenaway Revisited (2005) — re-recordings of the Greenaway scores
  • The Libertine (2005) — film score
  • Nyman Brass (2006)
  • 8 Lust Songs: I Sonetti Lussuriosi (2008)
  • Mozart 252 (2008)
  • The Glare (2009)

Later major label releases include Man On Wire (2008, Decca), Chasing Pianos (2014, Decca), and Nyman Complete Piano Music (2016, Brilliant Classics).

Unusual Crossover Credits

  • Kate Bush’s The Sensual World (1989, EMI Japan pressing) — Nyman credited for “Arranged By [Orchestra]”
  • Alva Noto’s Xerrox Vol. 2 (2009, Raster-Noton) — Nyman’s recordings sampled
  • Pet Shop Boys’ Electric (2013) and Love Is A Bourgeois Construct (2013, x2 (2)) — Nyman credited as “Written-By” (likely a song or theme co-write)
  • Pet Shop Boys’ Smash (The Singles 1985–2020) (2023, Parlophone) — Written-By credit persists across compilation
