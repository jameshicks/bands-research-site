Christine Hahn (No Wave)

Full name: Christine Hahn Born: June 17, 1951, Lakewood, Ohio, USA Died: May 28, 2025, Middletown, Maryland, USA Role: Drummer, percussionist, visual artist Discogs artist ID: 375281 Name variations: Hahn

Downtown NYC No Wave drummer and visual artist. Founding member of The Static and Malaria!. Studied fine arts, graphic design, and art history at Hunter College (New York), the Art Academy in Cincinnati, and Montgomery College (Maryland).

Overview

Hahn became involved in the downtown New York art and music scene in the late 1970s while studying in the city. She played drums and percussion and was a founding member of three bands: Daily Life (c.1978, with Glenn Branca and Barbara Ess), The Static (1979), and CKM Introject. She performed at Mudd Club, Max’s Kansas City, The Kitchen, and Artists Space.

After a European tour with The Static (1979), she moved to Berlin, where she collaborated with artist Martin Kippenberger (and filmmaker Eric Mitchell) on Luxus — a square photo booklet with two 45 rpm records, released on S.O.36 Records (1979). This was also Kippenberger’s debut recording and an early artifact of the Berlin punk/art intersection; the S.O.36 label took its name from the club Kippenberger briefly managed. She subsequently recorded an LP at Studio Christoph Franke with electronic musician/drummer Klaus Krüger, and went on to become a founding member of Malaria!, the influential Berlin post-punk/new wave group, with whom she made numerous recordings and performances.

She also worked as a visual artist in painting, photography, video, and print, with solo and group exhibitions across her career.

Note: Not to be confused with a Berlin-based synthesizer player of the same name documented separately in the Discogs database. The two may overlap in the vault’s existing Christine Hahn page, which should be treated with caution until disambiguated.

CKM Introject

Active: 1980 Members: Christine Hahn (No Wave) (bass), Kim Gordon (guitar, vocals), Stanton Miranda (drums) Name variant: Also referred to as simply “CKM” in most sources; “CKM Introject” appears in Christine Hahn’s own biographical materials (MSAC directory) but not in press accounts or other members’ accounts.

Formation and context

CKM was formed at the instigation of conceptual artist Dan Graham, who approached Kim Gordon to put together an all-girl band to perform alongside his piece “Performer/Audience/Mirror” — a work in which Graham would stand before a large mirror, describe the audience’s gestures and movements, then turn and describe himself in the mirror. Gordon, then 27 and with no prior instrumental experience, made her first attempt at playing guitar for the project. Graham introduced her to Hahn (described in some accounts as a member of The Static / Glenn Branca’s circle) and Stanton Miranda (then a member of Rhys Chatham’s group Arsenal). [Sources: Kim Gordon Wikipedia article (as of 2026-09-26) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Gordon; Stereogum interview https://stereogum.com/2254662/kim-gordon-the-collective-sonic-youth-career-spanning/interviews/weve-got-a-file-on-you; Wisconsin/Caw thesis summary via search]

Songs

The band wrote satirical songs described by Gordon as “making fun of girl talk”: “Soft Posh Separates” and “Cosmopolitan Girl,” in which Gordon sang advertising copy from Cosmopolitan magazine. [Source: search result summary drawing on the Caw thesis and Kim Gordon Wikipedia article]

Performance

CKM gave one known public performance at Eventworks, a festival organized by artist and musician Christian Marclay at the Massachusetts College of Art, Boston, 1980. Marclay organized Eventworks to explore the relationship between rock music and the visual arts; other participants included DNA, Rhys Chatham, Karole Armitage, Johanna Went, Boyd Rice, and Zev, with films by Eric Mitchell, Jack Smith, and Vivienne Dick. [Source: Christian Marclay interview, Perfect Sound Forever, https://www.furious.com/perfect/christianmarclay.html; Sonic Youth Mustang site CKM page, http://www.sonicyouth.com/mustang/cc/ckm80.html (confirmed URL, page not retrievable)]

The Sonic Youth fan archival site (“Mustang”) records the show as: CKM — ??/??/80 — Boston, MA @ Massachusetts College of Art. [http://www.sonicyouth.com/mustang/cc/ckm80.html]

Recordings

No recordings by CKM / CKM Introject are known. No releases appear in Discogs.

Legacy

The CKM performance was the occasion on which Stanton Miranda introduced Kim Gordon to Thurston Moore (and Lee Ranaldo), directly leading to the formation of Sonic Youth later in 1981. [Source: Kim Gordon Wikipedia article https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Gordon; Stanton Miranda Wikipedia article https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanton_Miranda]

Conflict / discrepancy note

The existing Groups table in this vault listed CKM Introject as “c.1978–1979.” All independently sourced accounts place CKM in 1980 (Kim Gordon Wikipedia, Stanton Miranda Wikipedia, Eventworks/Marclay sources). The “c.1978–1979” date has been corrected to 1980 in the Groups table above.

Some sources describe Hahn as playing “bass” in CKM; this conflicts with her primary instrument (drums/percussion) in every other project. The claim that she played bass comes from the Caw thesis summary and related secondary sources; it is plausible as a one-off role-swap in a conceptual art context, but should be treated with caution.

Some sources (including the earlier search-result summary) describe Hahn as a member of “Glenn Branca’s band The Static.” This is imprecise: Branca was a collaborator in Daily Life (c.1978), a separate band. The Static was Hahn’s own band (with Barbara Ess). The confusion likely arises from Branca’s close association with the No Wave scene.
