Overview

Andy McCluskey is a British singer, songwriter and bassist born 24 June 1959 in Heswall, Wirral, who co-founded Orchestral Manoeuvres In The Dark with Paul Humphreys after earlier stints in The ID, Dalek I, and Engine (3). His catalogue across more than 1,300 Discogs credits spans synth-pop, new wave, and experimental electronics, the overwhelming majority of them shared with Humphreys and long-serving OMD members Malcolm Holmes and Martin Cooper. He debuted on Virgin and its Dindisc imprint in 1979, later moved to A&M Records through the late 1980s, and has remained active on Virgin-affiliated and independent labels into the 2020s.

Notes (web)

1989 split with Paul Humphreys — circumstances and tone

The split was acrimonious. Classic Pop Magazine describes it plainly as such. Paul Humphreys’ account (Sound on Sound, 1996 interview; omdweb.com interview c. 2000) describes a structural breakdown: Humphreys wanted to stop touring and take time to reinvent the band, but Virgin Records — backing McCluskey as the public face — opposed a break. Humphreys felt pushed to the wall: “Andy was the face of OMD and the record company…backed that poll. So at that point, I had my back up against the wall and I had to sell the name ‘OMD’ to Andy.” Mal Holmes and Martin Cooper sided with Humphreys; McCluskey aligned with the label. A legal settlement followed: Humphreys was released from his Virgin contract and McCluskey retained the OMD name. (Sound on Sound; Classic Pop Magazine)

McCluskey’s own retrospective account (Sound on Sound, 1996) is softer in tone but confirms the emotional weight: “I was traumatised when he left, because he was the guy I’d written songs with since I was 16. But looking back, I realise that it had to happen… I think we both decided that OMD, as it was, could not have continued — it would have completely self-destructed.” He added: “I’m glad it ended when it did.” Classic Pop quotes McCluskey on the wider exhaustion: “We were run-down, had lost sight of our roots, the well was dry of ideas and we didn’t know what to do. It was a lack of communication and outside influences.” (Sound on Sound; Classic Pop Magazine)

A notable conflict exists between the two accounts: Humphreys describes the split as driven by a genuine dispute over strategy, with a legal element (selling the name); McCluskey frames it as mutual recognition of an impasse — “we both decided.” See Conflicts section below.

After the split, McCluskey disclosed in 2011 that the three departing members subsequently fell out among themselves: “Paul and Malcolm and Martin sort of had a falling out with each other over Telegraph Records and so everybody just sort of atomized into different directions.” (omd-messages.co.uk, 2011 interview part 2)

2005/2006 reunion — what prompted it

The catalyst was an invitation from a German TV programme (The Ultimate Chartshow, recorded 30 May 2005 in Cologne). McCluskey and Humphreys had not been in the same room for approximately 16 years. The TV production paid their travel and hotel. The decisive moment, per McCluskey, happened at the hotel bar in Cologne after the taping:

“I can remember we sat at the bar in Cologne and I just said ‘Right guys now listen. Look at this picture here, we’ve been here for two days and somebody else has paid for our airfare, and somebody else has paid for our hotel, and we’re sitting here drinking beer and yesterday we did six minutes work and today we’ve done 3 minutes work. Would you like to do this again for a living?‘”

McCluskey was explicit that no earlier conversations had been pointing toward a reunion: “It wasn’t something that we were already kind of tossing about in your head. That was the thing where we just thought ‘Ah, this is fun.‘” (omd-messages.co.uk, 2011 interview part 2)

The 2005 taping thus served as both the reconciliation and the trigger. OMD officially reformed in 2006; a planned nine-date UK tour expanded to approximately 50 shows on the strength of public demand. Classic Pop notes that once they stopped, “that’s finally when we made a lot of money because the costs of running OMD was so massive that it was consuming us” — suggesting the reunion was entered without financial pressure from the lean years. (Classic Pop Magazine)

Conflicts

  • Tone of the 1989 split: Classic Pop Magazine describes the split as “acrimonious.” Humphreys (omdweb.com interview c.2000) describes being forced to sell the OMD name after the label backed McCluskey. McCluskey (Sound on Sound, 1996) frames it as a mutual decision and says “I think we both decided.” The underlying facts (legal settlement, name transfer) support Humphreys’ account of genuine conflict rather than a fully consensual parting — but McCluskey’s retrospective tone is reconciliatory rather than bitter.
