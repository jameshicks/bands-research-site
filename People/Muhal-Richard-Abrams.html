Muhal Richard Abrams

American pianist, composer, bandleader, and educator. Born Richard Lewis Abrams, September 19, 1930, Chicago, IL; died October 29, 2017. Discogs artist ID: 295402.

Founder and first president of the AACM (Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians), established on Chicago’s South Side in 1965. One of the defining organizing forces in American avant-garde jazz of the 20th century.

Overview

Abrams grew up in Chicago and began his career as a working bop and hard bop pianist in the 1950s. By the early 1960s he had co-founded the Experimental Band — an informal rehearsal group that became the seedbed for the AACM — and was functioning as a mentor to the younger generation of Chicago experimentalists. The AACM’s founding in 1965 formalized this community, and Abrams served as its anchor: composer, bandleader, theorist, and institutional president.

His own recordings range from solo piano through duo, chamber ensemble, and large orchestra formats. He worked consistently with the Italian jazz imprint Black Saint from 1975 through the early 1990s, which became the primary documentary record of his mature output. He also recorded for Delmark Records (early Chicago work), Arista/Arista Novus (mid-1970s), and ECM Records (a late-career session). He relocated to New York in 1976 and was a founding member of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians’ New York chapter.

As a composer he wrote for the full range of forces — solo keyboard, string quartet, chamber group, jazz ensemble, orchestra — and occasionally incorporated poetry, voice, and synthesizer. He also painted and created collages, several of which appear on his own album covers. He received a MacArthur Fellowship in 2010.

Open Questions

  • Pre-AACM sideman work (1950s–early 1960s) is sparse in Discogs; the 1963 Half And Half (Vee Jay, uncredited piano) is the only early credit surfaced — likely more sessions exist
  • Roots Of Blue (1986, RPR Records) has 34 pressings listed in Discogs but very thin credits — personnel unknown from DB
  • UMO Plays The Music Of Muhal Richard Abrams (1989, Finland) — Abrams credited as composer, conductor, and producer; personnel of the UMO ensemble not pulled
  • Rejoicing With The Light (1983, Black Saint) shows Abrams as composer/cover painter/conductor but not as primary artist — which ensemble this documents is unclear from credits alone
  • The Hearinga Suite (1989) and Blu Blu Blu (1991) personnel not fully pulled
