Crys Cole

Canadian sound artist and vocalist based in Berlin. Discogs artist ID: 1638665

Biography

Crys Cole is a Canadian sound artist who relocated to Berlin. Her practice spans composition, live performance, sound sculpture, and installation. She works primarily in electro-acoustic improvisation, generating sound through haptic gestures — physical contact with objects — and mundane materials to produce texturally nuanced results. Her own description foregrounds the “subtle and imperfect”: haptic process over conventional instrumental technique.

She is a member of the duo Ora Clementi (with James Rushford), which operates as a separate project alongside her solo work and her long-running collaborative partnership with Oren Ambarchi.

Cole has performed across Canada, Japan, Australia, Thailand, Singapore, the USA, UK, and throughout Europe. Her installation and exhibition work has shown in Canada, Russia, Switzerland, Spain, Czech Republic, Germany, Denmark, Sweden, the UK, and Thailand.

Listed collaborators in her profile: Oren Ambarchi (AU), James Rushford (AU), Francis Plagne (AU), Leif Elggren (SW), Tetuzi Akiyama (JP), Seiji Morimoto (DE), Jessika Kenney (US), David Rosenboom (US), Keith Rowe (UK), Annea Lockwood (US), David Behrman (US), Lance Austin Olsen (CA), Jamie Drouin (CA), Mathieu Ruhlmann (CA), Tim Olive (JP/CA).

Scene Context

Cole occupies a specific niche within the international free-improvisation and electro-acoustic scene centred around Black Truffle and its associated networks. Her primary collaborative gravity is Oren Ambarchi’s orbit — both as a duo partner (Hotel Record, Gallivant) and as a session contributor to his records (Hubris, Live Hubris, Works For The Ever Present Orchestra). Joe Talia is the recurring production anchor across her discography, appearing as mixer and/or mastering engineer on nearly every release.

The Ora Clementi project with James Rushford is her other sustained creative outlet, operating on a more austere, text-oriented axis via Penultimate Press, Otoroku, and Black Truffle.

Her work on Gallivant (The Black Dot, 2021) documents a wider instrumental palette: contact microphones, small electronics, Casio SK-1, and Amazonian bird whistles alongside Ambarchi’s guitar/electronics. The solo releases (Beside Myself, Other Meetings, A Piece Of Work) represent a more recent consolidation of her solo voice, separate from the Ambarchi partnership.
