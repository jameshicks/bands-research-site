Oct 12, 20265 min read

Malachi Ritscher

Discogs ID: 585187 Real name: Mark David Ritscher Born: 1954 Died: November 3, 2006 (self-immolation in protest of the US-led Iraq War) Name variations: Malachai Ritcher, Malachi Richter, Malachi Ritscher Jr.

Chicago recording engineer, musician, and archivist whose tape recorder captured the city’s improvised music scene across roughly a decade. He recorded hundreds of concerts at venues including the Empty Bottle, creating one of the most systematic primary-source archives of Chicago jazz and experimental music in the 1990s and 2000s. He died in a public act of political protest.

Biography

Early Career — Arsenal (2) (1988–1990)

Before he became primarily known as an engineer and archivist, Ritscher played bass in Arsenal (2), a Chicago post-punk/hardcore group on Touch And Go. Arsenal included Santiago Durango (vocals, guitar, drum machine) and Pierre Kezdy (bass). Ritscher played bass on both of their Touch And Go releases:

ReleaseYearLabelRole
Manipulator1988Touch And GoBass
Factory Smog Is A Sign Of Progress1990Touch And GoBass (tracks 6–9)

On Factory Smog Is A Sign Of Progress, Pierre Kezdy covered bass on tracks 1–5 (uncredited); Ritscher took over on the latter half. The band’s profile links to Touch And Go’s Chicago hardcore/post-punk roster.

Recording Engineer and Scene Archivist (mid-1990s–2006)

From the mid-1990s Ritscher shifted to documenting Chicago’s improvised music scene. His work as a location recording engineer appears across a wide range of labels and artists central to that world:

  • Atavistic — multiple releases for Vandermark 5 and others
  • Okka Disk — Brötzmann/Sharrock Fragments (2003), McPhee/Brötzmann/Kessler/Zerang Guts (2006)
  • hatOLOGY — Misha Mengelberg Two Days In Chicago (1999, disc 2)
  • Thrill Jockey — Isotope 217 Who Stole The I Walkman? (2000)
  • Emanem — John Butcher Fixations (14) (2001, tracks 2–5)
  • Corbett vs. Dempsey — The Bottle Tapes (2025, archival compilation)
  • Conundrom — F-O’R (2020, archival)

His 2025 posthumous credit on The Bottle Tapes: Selections From The Empty Bottle Jazz And Improvised Music Series 1996–2005 is perhaps the clearest evidence of the scope of his archival project: a full compilation drawn from recordings he made at the Empty Bottle across nearly a decade.

He also worked under or alongside Savage Sound Syndicate as a recording entity on several releases (notably the Vandermark 5’s Acoustic Machine, 2001 and Guts, 2006).

Guts (Okka Disk, 2006)

The release most associated with Ritscher’s name is Guts, recorded live in Chicago and released on Okka Disk in 2006 — the year of his death. He received two credits: Recorded By and Text By [Quote In Booklet], the latter placing his own words inside the release.

Want Not

Ritscher was also a member of Want Not, a Chicago group with limited Discogs documentation (Discogs ID: 1831982). No release credits appear under this group in the database.
