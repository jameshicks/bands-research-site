Allen Ginsberg

Poet, chanter, and spoken-word recording artist. Born 3 June 1926, Newark, New Jersey. Died 5 April 1997, New York City, age 70. Discogs artist ID: 105832. Real name: Irwin Allen Ginsberg.

Central figure of the Beat Generation, Buddhist practitioner, and advocate for free speech and individual liberty. Son of poet Louis Ginsberg. Member of The Plastic Ono Band (Discogs group credit). His recording career spanned spoken-word, harmonium-accompanied chanting, and full-band folk/blues albums, often in collaboration with downtown New York musicians.

Overview

Ginsberg’s recording career runs parallel to his literary one. His earliest records were poetry readings — Howl And Other Poems (Fantasy, 1959), Reads Kaddish (Atlantic, 1966) — but through the 1970s and 1980s he moved toward music-accompanied performance. He played harmonium and finger cymbals on his own recordings and in live settings. His central musical collaboration was with Arthur Russell, who accompanied him on cello at poetry readings in the early 1970s San Francisco scene and went on to arrange and perform on First Blues (John Hammond Records, 1983). Other major musical ventures include the Philip Glass opera Hydrogen Jukebox (1993) and the single The Ballad Of The Skeletons (Mercury/Mouth Almighty, 1996) co-credited with Paul McCartney, Philip Glass, and Lenny Kaye. He contributed liner notes and guest appearances to Beat-adjacent and avant-garde releases through the 1990s. Posthumous archival releases continue to appear, most recently on Shimmy Disc (2026) and Cadiz Music (2024).

Connections

  • Arthur Russell — cello accompanist from early 1970s San Francisco; arranger and performer on First Blues (1983); co-credited on Ballad Of The Lights (Audika, 2010). Note: First Thought Best Thought (Audika, 2006) is Arthur Russell’s own album, not a Ginsberg release — Ginsberg does not appear in its Discogs credits.
  • Hal Willner — produced The Lion For Real (1989), Holy Soul Jelly Roll (1994), The Ballad Of The Skeletons (1996), Die On Me (2026, posthumous). Primary creative producer of Ginsberg’s later career recordings.
  • Philip Glass — co-created Hydrogen Jukebox opera (1993, Elektra Nonesuch); co-performer on The Ballad Of The Skeletons (1996)
  • Paul McCartney — co-primary credit on The Ballad Of The Skeletons (1996); provided guitar, drums, organ, maracas
  • Lenny Kaye — bass and arranger on The Ballad Of The Skeletons (1996)
  • William S. Burroughs — Beat-generation peer; shared Giorno Poetry Systems appearances; both produced by Hal Willner in later career
  • Gregory Corso — Beat-generation peer; co-credited on posthumous Die On Me (Shimmy Disc, 2026)
  • The Fugs — wrote liner notes for The Fugs (ESP-Disk’, 1966)
  • The Clash — guest backing vocals on Combat Rock (1982)
  • The Plastic Ono Band — Discogs group credit
  • Audika Records — released Ballad Of The Lights (2010)
  • ESP-Disk’ — liner notes on The Fugs LP (1966)
  • Shimmy Disc — Die On Me (2026)
  • Anne Waldman — vocalist on First Blues (1983); Naropa Institute co-founder
  • Peter Orlovsky — vocalist on First Blues (1983); longtime companion
  • Bob Dylan — arranger and guitarist on First Blues (1983) tracks A1–A3; Ginsberg wrote inner sleeve liner notes for Desire (1976)
  • David Amram — multi-instrumentalist on First Blues (1983)
  • Ann Charters — produced First Blues: Rags, Ballads & Harmonium Songs (Folkways, 1981)
  • Harry Smith — recorded the 1981 Folkways LP
