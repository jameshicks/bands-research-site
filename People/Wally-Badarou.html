Wally Badarou

Discogs ID: 9051
Real name: Waliou Jacques Daniel Badarou
Alias: The Chief Inspector
Born: 1955, Paris, France (Beninese descent)
Role: Keyboards, synthesizer, composer, producer

French musician of Beninese descent; one of the key session keyboardists of the late 1970s–1980s, and the signature synthesizer voice of the Compass Point All Stars. Active across reggae, post-punk, electro-funk, pop, and ambient. Top labels: Island Records, 4th & Broadway.

Biography

Paris Origins and Early Session Work (1977–1979)

Badarou was born in Paris in 1955 into a family of Beninese origin. He came up as a session keyboard player in the French pop-soul circuit in the late 1970s, most notably with the Gibson Brothers — the Guianese-French disco trio who scored international hits including “Cuba” (Island, 1979), on which he played synthesizer. He also appeared on the Tumblack album (Barclay, 1979), an early Franco-African fusion project. His early session credits reflect deep roots in the Paris music community around producer Daniel Vangarde and the Barclay label axis.

He also contributed synthesizer to the M project’s New York, London, Paris, Munich (MCA, 1979) — the album containing the international hit “Pop Muzik” — which put him in contact with the UK post-punk/new wave circuit.

Compass Point Studios and the All Stars (1980–1984)

By 1980 Badarou had relocated to Nassau, Bahamas, to work at Chris Blackwell’s Compass Point Studios facility. He became a founding member of the Compass Point All Stars, the informal house band assembled by Blackwell to record artists on his Island Records roster.

The group were usually credited individually rather than as a collective. Their template — driving reggae-influenced rhythms with Badarou’s synthesizer color on top — became one of the defining sounds of early-1980s British pop and rock produced at Island.

Badarou’s synthesizer work in this period was notable for its control of texture and space: he deployed the Prophet 5, Minimoog, Korg Polyphonic, and later the Yamaha DX-7 and Synclavier, adapting to each artist’s register without dominating the track. Sessions were engineered by Alex Sadkin and Steven Stanley.

Solo Career (1982–1989)

Badarou developed a parallel solo career on Island, initially with “Theme From Countryman” (1982) — a single and score co-composed and produced for Chris Blackwell’s Jamaican film Countryman. His first full solo album was:

  • Echoes (Island, 1984) — entirely self-produced, written, and performed (synthesizers including Mini Moog, Prophet 5, Synclavier; acoustic piano; Linn Drum; acoustic guitar; voices). Exec-produced by Chris Blackwell; mixed by Andy Lyden (most tracks) and Steven Stanley (one track). A landmark in ambient/electronic music, drawing on African melodic sensibility filtered through European synthesis.

  • Kiss of the Spider Woman (Island Visual Arts, 1985) — original soundtrack with music and dialogue for Héctor Babenco’s film.

  • Words of a Mountain (Island, 1989) — second solo album; also self-produced on Island. Less credited in DB (sparse additional credits).

  • Colors of Silence: Musical Poetry for Yoga (La Bande Son/Universal, 2001) — later ambient project.

A single from the Compass Point era, “Padlock” (Garage Records, 1983/1985), credited him as a featured artist and keyboardist on remixes by Larry Levan — linking him to the Paradise Garage axis.

Key Session Credits

Grace Jones — Compass Point Trilogy (Island, 1980–1982)

Badarou played keyboards across Grace Jones’s entire Compass Point trilogy, all produced by Alex Sadkin and Chris Blackwell:

AlbumYearBadarou Role
Warm Leatherette1980Keyboards
Nightclubbing1981Keyboards
Living My Life1982Keyboards

All three feature the full Compass Point All Stars lineup alongside Jones. Nightclubbing is the most celebrated — containing “Pulling Up to the Bumper,” “Walking in the Rain,” and “I’ve Seen That Face Before (Libertango).”

He also appears on later Grace Jones releases on Island, including Anthem (1984) and compilations.

Level 42 (Polydor, 1981–1989)

Badarou had one of his most sustained non-Island collaborations with Level 42, the London jazz-funk group. He played synthesizer (Prophet 5, Korg Polyphonic, Minimoog) on their 1981 debut self-titled album (produced by Mike Vernon), and went on to co-write and produce several of their most successful albums:

He co-wrote multiple tracks including “The Chinese Way,” “Hot Water,” “The Chant Has Begun,” “Something About You,” “Lessons in Love,” and “Children Say.” The Level 42 partnership represented his deepest creative investment outside his own solo work.
