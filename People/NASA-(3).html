NASA (3)

Discogs ID: 104853 Real name: Paul Loverro Aliases / stage names: Uncommon Nasa, Adam Warlock, Paul Loverro Name variations on records: NASA, NASA (Of The Presence) Role: Recording engineer, mixer, mastering engineer, producer, MC Label founded: Uncommon Records (Discogs ID: 32317)

Paul Loverro — credited on records as NASA (3) or later Uncommon Nasa — was the house recording and mixing engineer at Definitive Jux during the label’s peak years (2001–2005). Working alongside El-P and the wider Def Jux infrastructure, he recorded and mixed the majority of the label’s most significant releases: Cannibal Ox’s The Cold Vein, El-P’s Fantastic Damage, Mr. Lif’s I Phantom, Aesop Rock’s Bazooka Tooth, and C-Rayz Walz’s Ravipops (The Substance), among others. In all, Discogs credits him on 50 releases in the Definitive Jux catalog.

Beyond engineering, Loverro appears as an additional vocalist on early Mr. Lif releases and holds a production credit on one release. Outside the Def Jux orbit, he engineered records for Big Dada and Matador-adjacent artists around the same period. In later years he shifted focus to his own MC career under the Uncommon Nasa name, founded Uncommon Records, and co-hosts the Dope Sh!t Podcast. He is also described in his Discogs profile as a published author and poet.

Career by Era

Pre-Def Jux: First Credits (1999–2000)

Loverro’s earliest Discogs credits place him in the Boston hip-hop scene, recording and engineering for Mr. Lif’s Farmhand EP (1999, Grand Royal) alongside Vassos and Insight (2). He also recorded the A-sides on Mr. Lif’s Front On This 12” (2000, Ozone Music). On Farmhand, he appears not only as engineer but with additional vocal credits on two tracks — one of the few instances of his voice on record.

He also appears as assistant mixing engineer on a Hi-Rise Records release (Echo Leader / Charlie / Rap Tyranny, 1999) and as assistant engineer on Matador’s This Morning (1999) — suggesting early studio work in NYC and/or Boston before his Def Jux run.

Around the same time (2000) he received a recording credit on Big Dada’s Gun Hill Road release and a mixing credit on Hero Theme (Big Dada), extending his network into the UK-linked hip-hop underground.

Definitive Jux House Engineer (2001–2005)

The bulk of Loverro’s recorded career falls in this window. He functioned as one of the primary studio engineers for Definitive Jux, frequently co-crediting recording sessions with El-P himself or with Vassos. His presence on a release typically indicates he was in the room for tracking and subsequent mix sessions.

His work on The Cold Vein is the most prominent single credit: he recorded the entire album and mixed the majority of tracks (El-P, Matt Quinn (2), Phil Painson, and Vassos divided the remaining mix duties). On I Phantom he shared recording and mixing with El-P, Insight (2), Fakts One, and Ray Fernandes. On Bazooka Tooth and Fantastic Damage he is listed wholesale alongside El-P with no per-track breakdown — suggesting those were full co-session situations.

Also in 2005, he received a Mixed By credit on a NIN/Interscope release: Only (Remixes By Richard X And EL-P) — reflecting El-P’s outside-world production profile at that time.

After Def Jux (2012–present)

After the label wound down around 2010, Loverro largely ceased appearing as an engineer on major releases. His later Discogs credits shift to mastering engineer on smaller independent hip-hop releases:

Label — Uncommon Records

Loverro founded Uncommon Records (Discogs ID: 32317), which released his Uncommon Nasa solo work and has a sublabel, Uncommon Restoration. The label’s website matches his uncommonnasa.com domain.
