Sun Ra

Born Herman Poole Blount, May 22, 1914, Birmingham, Alabama; died May 30, 1993, Birmingham, Alabama. Legal name changed to Le Sony’r Ra on October 20, 1952. American jazz musician, composer, pianist, bandleader, and philosopher; leader of The Sun Ra Arkestra from the mid-1950s until his death. Pioneer of free improvisation, modal jazz, and Afrofuturism; among the earliest users of electronic keyboards and synthesizers in jazz. Claimed publicly to have been transported to Saturn and charged with preaching cosmic peace through music.

Discogs artist ID: 35328. The Sun Ra Arkestra (separate entity): ID 2219395.

Biography

Early Life and Chicago Years (1914–1956)

Herman Poole Blount grew up in Birmingham, Alabama, where he was a prodigious pianist from childhood — able to sight-read and compose by age 11. He played semi-professionally in local jazz and dance bands as a teenager. His first full-time band job came in 1934 when Ethel Harper, his biology teacher, organized a touring ensemble; when she moved to New York, Blount took over and renamed it the Sonny Blount Orchestra. The band disbanded, but Blount continued working steadily in Birmingham.

Between 1936 and 1937, Blount left college after one year following a visionary experience he described as being transported to Saturn by extraterrestrial beings who commanded him to preach peace through music. He converted his family’s home into a conservatory-workshop open to musicians and esoteric thinkers at any hour.

In 1945, Blount moved to Chicago, where African-American political and cultural ferment shaped his thinking. He began abandoning his birth name, taking “Le Sony’r Ra” (shortened to Sun Ra, after Ra, the Egyptian sun god), and formalized the legal change on October 20, 1952. In Chicago he worked and performed as a sideman and arranger for Fletcher Henderson, Coleman Hawkins, Stuff Smith, Wynonie Harris, and Andrew Tibbs, among others — Discogs shows him credited as arranger or pianist on Okeh and Columbia sides from 1950–1953.

In 1952, Sun Ra assembled a trio with drummer Tommy Hunter and saxophonist Pat Patrick, forming the first nucleus of what would become the Arkestra. When Patrick moved to Florida (he would later rejoin multiple times), saxophonists John Gilmore and Marshall Allen joined. Around the same time Sun Ra met Alton Abraham, a precocious teenager who became his closest collaborator and de facto business manager. Together, in the mid-1950s, they founded El Saturn Records — initially pressing 45 rpm singles by Sun Ra and Arkestra-adjacent artists. A separate Discogs entry (ID 356555) lists Abraham as co-founder.

Sun Ra’s first album as a leader, Jazz By Sun Ra Vol. 1 (1957, Transition Records), was produced by Tom Wilson (2) (ID 253021). That same year, the Arkestra began self-releasing on El Saturn with Super-Sonic Jazz (cat SR-LP-0216).

New York and the Saturn Years (1956–1961)

In late 1961 Sun Ra and the Arkestra relocated collectively to New York City, living communally to pool resources. Through the early 1960s the band issued a string of Saturn LPs — Jazz In Silhouette (1961), When Sun Comes Out (1963), Art Forms Of Dimensions Tomorrow (1965), Secrets Of The Sun (1965) — that documented a transition from hard bop toward free-form modal improvisation and exotic timbres. Sun Ra handled cover art, liner poems, and catalog administration himself.

By the mid-1960s the Arkestra was performing regularly at Slug’s Saloon on the Lower East Side. The beat generation and early psychedelic audiences discovered them around this time, and jazz critics began paying serious attention. Alton Abraham remained the Arkestra’s business infrastructure throughout this period.

Sun Ra’s first release on a major independent label came in 1965: The Heliocentric Worlds Of Sun Ra, Vol. I on ESP-Disk’ (cat 1014), followed immediately by Vol. II (cat 1017) in 1966, and Nothing Is… (cat 1045) in 1966. These three albums, recorded in New York with the full touring Arkestra, brought the group to a wider avant-garde audience and documented the orchestra’s fully developed free-jazz language. Sun Ra is credited on Heliocentric Vol. I as composer, arranger, pianist, bass marimba, electronic celesta, timpani, and cover artist.

Touring Collective / Avant-Garde Peak (1961–1978)

In 1968, when their New York building was sold, Sun Ra and the Arkestra relocated to the Germantown section of Philadelphia, on Morton Street — their base of operations until Sun Ra’s death. That same year they toured the US West Coast for the first time, and Sun Ra appeared on the cover of Rolling Stone.

The Arkestra signed briefly with Impulse!/ABC for the landmark Astro Black (1973, cat AS-9255), co-produced by Alton Abraham and Ed Michel, the primary document of the early-1970s electric period with Moog synthesizer, vibraphone, and a large percussion section. Around this time the Arkestra also recorded Space Is The Place (1973, Blue Thumb BTS-41) — the soundtrack to the film of the same name — one of the defining statements of cosmic jazz, with a 21-minute title track featuring the full Arkestra including vocalist June Tyson and an extended roster of horn players.

In 1970, the Arkestra began touring internationally — France, Germany, and the UK. The 1971 Nuits De La Fondation Maeght double LP (Shandar, France) documented a major performance at the Fondation Maeght, Saint-Paul-de-Vence, and became one of the most praised live documents of the group. The 1971 MPS/BASF LP It’s After The End Of The World captured concerts at the Donaueschingen and Berlin festivals for the German market.

During 1971–72 the Arkestra also recorded two Vols. of The Solar-Myth Approach for BYG Records in France during the Actuel sessions. The Thoth Intergalactic label (a Saturn sublabel) released the Live In Egypt recordings (1972).

The late 1970s saw stylistic expansion: Lanquidity (1978, Philly Jazz PJ666) was an unusually accessible, groove-heavy record with guitars and a tight rhythm section — Sun Ra played Fender Rhodes, Hammond B3, ARP synthesizer, Moog Minimoog, Crumar keyboard, and piano simultaneously — while still maintaining an experimental edge. The album featured James Jacson on bassoon and June Tyson on vocals.

Late Career and Legacy (1978–1993)

From 1978 onward the Arkestra’s touring and recording activity continued without pause. Releases appeared on Improvising Artists Inc. (solo piano records with Paul Bley), Horo Records (Italy), SteepleChase (Denmark), hat Hut Records / hat ART (Sunrise In Different Dimensions, 1981), Rounder Records (Strange Celestial Road, 1980), Y Records (Nuclear War, 1982), and Black Saint (multiple albums 1987–1992).

The Black Saint relationship produced several significant late-career records: Reflections In Blue (1987, BSR 0101), Hours After (1989), Mayan Temples (1992), and A Tribute To Stuff Smith (1993, Soul Note). These showcase a tighter small-ensemble format with Sun Ra on piano and synthesizer alongside Marshall Allen, John Gilmore, Pat Patrick, and June Tyson — the core long-term members.

For the wider market, Blue Delight (1989, A&M Records) and Purple Night (1990, A&M Records) were produced by John Snyder and offered Sun Ra’s most polished commercial-label recordings. Evidence Records (5) (ID 358208) reissued a large portion of the Saturn catalog through the 1990s, making it widely available for the first time.

Sun Ra suffered a stroke in 1990 but continued performing and leading the Arkestra. In late 1992 he returned to Birmingham to live with his sister Mary Jenkins, and died on May 30, 1993. After his death, Marshall Allen assumed leadership of the Arkestra, which continues performing under his direction as of 2025.

The Sun Ra Arkestra

Discogs entity: The Sun Ra Arkestra (ID 2219395). Active 1956–present; top labels: El Saturn Records, Enterplanetary Koncepts, Saturn Research.

The Arkestra was a large communal collective, not a fixed band — membership fluctuated constantly, but a core of long-term members defined the group’s identity over decades. El Saturn Records

Discogs ID: 86748. Founded mid-1950s in Chicago by Sun Ra and Alton Abraham, along with Abraham’s brother Artis. Address: 4115 S. Drexel, Chicago, IL.

El Saturn was one of the earliest artist-run independent record labels in jazz. Sun Ra and Abraham handled every aspect of production: recording (often direct to acetate or minimal studio setups), mastering, pressing, cover design, and distribution — primarily hand-to-hand at concerts. Catalog numbers were inconsistent and often functioned as date codes (e.g., “11-1-79” = November 1, 1979). The label appeared under multiple name variants: “El Saturn,” “Saturn,” “Saturn Records,” “Saturn Research,” “El Saturn Research.”

Discogs sublabels of El Saturn Records include:

  • Saturn Research (ID 54200) — the most frequently used variant for 1970s releases
  • Thoth Intergalactic (ID 163151) — used for the Live In Egypt recordings (1972)
  • Enterplan (ID 351472)
  • Repeto (ID 165059)

The label issued around 389 releases on Discogs across all variants. After Sun Ra’s death, Alton Abraham retained control of the Saturn masters; reissues have been licensed to Evidence (5), Art Yard, Strut, Enterplanetary Koncepts, and others.
