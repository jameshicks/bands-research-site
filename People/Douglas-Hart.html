Douglas Hart

Bass player, producer, and music video director. Born 2 May 1965, Glasgow, Scotland. Discogs artist ID: 222130

Founding member and original bassist of The Jesus And Mary Chain, active with the band from their East Kilbride origins through approximately 1990. Subsequently established himself as a music video director and record producer, working with artists including Primal Scream and The Stone Roses. Also recorded as Acid Angels and was a member of late-2000s band Cristine.

Biography

Origins and The Jesus And Mary Chain (1983–1990)

Douglas Hart grew up in East Kilbride alongside Jim Reid, William Reid, and Bobby Gillespie, forming the nucleus of what became The Jesus And Mary Chain. Hart played bass on the band’s debut single “Upside Down” (Creation Records, 1984), produced by Alan McGee; the B-side “Vegetable Man” was produced by Joe Foster. This was the founding lineup: Jim and William Reid, Hart on bass, and Murray Dalglish on drums.

By 1985, Bobby Gillespie had replaced Dalglish on drums, and the band signed to Blanco Y Negro. Hart played bass on all three of the band’s 1985 singles — “Never Understand,” “You Trip Me Up,” and “Just Like Honey” — as well as on the debut album Psychocandy (1985), engineered by John Loder. The band self-produced the album.

Hart’s last confirmed Discogs credit as a JAMC performer is on the Peel Sessions tracks recorded for Side One of the 1991 Strange Fruit release The Peel Sessions — those sessions (tracks A1–A3) feature the classic Psychocandy-era lineup of Hart, Gillespie, and the Reid brothers, suggesting the sessions predate Gillespie’s departure. He does not appear in the credits for Darklands (1987), confirming that he had left the performing lineup by the time that album was recorded.

The 1991 compilation Blackpool Live (The Stone Roses) credits Hart with “Post Production Direction,” hinting he had already shifted toward production/directorial work by this point.

Production Work (1986–1990)

Concurrent with his JAMC role, Hart was active as an independent producer in the mid-to-late 1980s, working primarily with Scottish indie bands on small UK labels:

  • Splashing Along (1986, Narodnik) — produced a single for an unidentified act
  • Billy The Whizz E.P. (1987, Narodnik) — produced Jesse Garon & The Desperadoes
  • Secret Goldfish / Real World (1987, Narodnik) — produced
  • The Adam Faith Experience (1987, Velocity Records (2)) — produced Jesse Garon & The Desperadoes LP
  • The Rain Fell Down (1987, Narodnik) — produced
  • Mad Not Mad Party (1986, Geebo Geebo Records) — engineered
  • You’ll Never Be That Young Again (1988, Velocity Records (2)) — produced
  • A Cabinet Of Curiosities (1989, Velocity Records (2)) — produced (a compilation)

Jesse Garon & The Desperadoes was an Edinburgh indie-pop group; Hart produced two of their records for Velocity Records. These sessions place him in the Scottish indie underground during precisely the period between Psychocandy and Darklands.

Acid Angels (1988)

Under the alias Acid Angels (Discogs ID: 49268), Hart released the single “Speed Speed Ecstasy” (1988, Product Inc.) — the only Acid Angels release in the database. It was co-produced and co-mixed with Ken Thomas, who also worked on JAMC-adjacent and indie projects throughout this era. Hart also handled sleeve design. The project appears to be a solo or pseudonymous endeavour rather than a band proper.

The Screamadelica credit is as compiler on a VHS/video release (M.C.E.G. Virgin Music Video), not a performing credit. The Complete Stone Roses (1995) is a VHS retrospective for which Hart directed the early-career segment (tracks 1–11) alongside Geoff Wonfor’s live footage from Blackpool. By 2009 he was directing music videos for The Hours (on Is Good records) and Pet Shop Boys (“Did You See Me Coming?”, Astralwerks).

His company, Pretty Bird EU (listed in his Discogs profile URLs), places him within the commercial and music video production world.

See See Rider (1990)

Hart also played guitar (on track AA2 only) on the See See Rider EP (1990, Lazy Records) by the band See See Rider, produced by Paul Sampson. This is a minor guest appearance and likely a one-off session.

Cristine (2005–2007)

Cristine was formed in London in 2005/2006 by Stephane Bejean (aka Stephane B.L.), releasing the debut single “Piggy In The Middle / I Freak” on Cassette Records in 2005 — credited as Simian Mobile Disco vs. Cristine. A second single, “Cross The Line / Use,” followed on Mute Irregulars/Mute in 2007.

Hart joined Cristine on bass sometime in 2007, alongside drummer Robert Schultzberg and guitarist/vocalist Tree Carr. Hart is not credited on the 2005 Cassette Records single (only Schultzberg is credited on the 2007 Mute single, under “Robert Schulsberg”); Hart’s membership is documented in the artist profile but not in release credits in the Discogs database.

Members: Stephane Bejean (vocals/writing, Discogs 343315), Robert Schultzberg (drums, Discogs 521304), Tree Carr (guitar/vocals, Discogs 475168), Douglas Hart (bass)

Open Questions

  • Exact year Hart left The Jesus And Mary Chain — Discogs places his last performer credit on Peel Sessions (Side 1, likely recorded 1985), and he has no credit on Darklands (1987). The actual departure year is not determinable from Discogs alone; most accounts say c.1990 but the performing work ended earlier.
  • Full list of music videos directed — Discogs is not a comprehensive video database; only releases that include a video component and credit the director have been captured. Hart’s Pretty Bird profile and IMDB (linked in Discogs URLs) would have the full filmography.
  • Nature of the “Acid Angels” project — is this purely Hart solo, or a collaboration? Ken Thomas co-produced but the Discogs alias records it as Hart’s pseudonym.
  • Cristine album/further material — the two singles are the only Cristine releases in Discogs. Whether Hart appeared on any unreleased sessions is unknown.
  • Specific Primal Scream videos directed by Hart — the Screamadelica VHS credit is as compiler, not director. The known JAMC/Primal Scream connection suggests earlier directing work that may predate Discogs coverage.
  • “The Cleansing” (2024, Domino) — the primary artist for this release was not established from available data; Hart is credited for gatefold photography.
