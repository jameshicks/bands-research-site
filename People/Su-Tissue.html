Su Tissue

Vocalist, keyboardist, and composer; frontwoman and primary songwriter of Suburban Lawns (Long Beach, CA, 1978–1983). Real name: Susan M. McLane (Discogs artist ID 1757973 confirms this; born August 1958). The name “Susan Farthing” appears in some outside sources but has no Discogs entry with any connection to Su Tissue or Suburban Lawns — see Open Questions. Discogs artist ID: 1132149.

Biography

Background

Sue McLane was born in August 1958. She is the daughter of James Price McLane Jr., a competitive swimmer who represented the United States at the Olympic Games. She adopted the stage name Su Tissue — sometimes credited simply as “Tissue” in songwriter credits. The origin of the stage name is undocumented in primary sources; one informal account suggests it is a play on the Kleenex/tissue brand, but Su Tissue herself never gave interviews explaining it (she was by all accounts extremely reticent in press situations — bandmate Chuck Roast confirmed in 2015 that she “never spoke in band interviews”).

Suburban Lawns (1978–1983)

Su Tissue was the most recognisable face of Suburban Lawns, the post-punk/new wave group she co-founded in Long Beach, California in 1978. Vocal duties on the band’s recordings were distributed across multiple members — Frankie Ennui (real name Richard Whitney), Vex Billingsgate (real name William Ranson), and John McBurney all sang lead — but Su Tissue’s slightly dissonant, flat-affect delivery on tracks such as “Janitor,” “Gossip,” “Green Eyes,” and “Baby” defined the band’s public image.

Beyond vocals, she contributed keyboards extensively across the 1981 LP (credited on A2, A4, A6, B2, B3, B5, B7), played bass guitar on one track (A3), and handled percussion. As a songwriter she co-wrote the majority of the band’s material, sharing credits with Ennui, Billingsgate, and McBurney across most of the LP and the 1983 Baby EP.

The band released three primary records during their lifespan:

  • 1979 — “Gidget Goes To Hell / My Boyfriend” single on Suburban Industrial Records (produced by E.J. Emmons)
  • 1981 — Suburban Lawns LP on I.R.S. Records (produced by E.J. Emmons and Troy Mathisen)
  • 1983 — Baby EP on I.R.S. Records (produced by Richard Mazda)

Wikipedia and multiple secondary sources confirm that the band was co-founded by William “Vex Billingsgate” Ranson and Sue McLane, both CalArts students — the band was not a pre-existing group she joined. The other members (Richard “Frankie Ennui” Whitney, Charles “Chuck Roast” Rodriguez, John “John Gleur” McBurney) joined subsequently. Note: Discogs credits McBurney as “John McBurney”; Wikipedia and press sources consistently use the stage name “John Gleur” — both refer to the same person. McBurney/Gleur left the band before the 1983 Baby EP was recorded, according to the 2015 Something Else interview.

Suburban Lawns dissolved in 1983.

Post-Suburban Lawns: Salon De Musique (1984)

After the band split, McLane attended Berklee College of Music in Boston to study piano. In 1984 she made a sharp stylistic break with a solo LP, Salon De Musique, co-credited to herself and Arspenlian Reeves (Discogs ID 9096340; no other releases or profile information). Multiple reviewers consider Arspenlian Reeves a pseudonym for Su Tissue herself — the cyberinsekt LiveJournal review (2014) calls it “surely a pseudonym,” and The Sentinel review (2025) states it is “clearly a pseudonym for Su” — on the grounds that the name appears nowhere else and the piano on the record aligns with her Berklee studies. No primary source has confirmed this; it remains the most plausible interpretation but is not verified. The album was released on Adversity LTD (cat. SMLP 3263), a small 1980s New York City label (Discogs ID 263193). A self-released cassette version also appeared the same year.

The record is filed under Classical / Minimal / Post-Modern on Discogs — a long-form, three-movement work far removed from the band’s new-wave punk. The three sides run to roughly 33 minutes total:

  • A1: “2nd Movement” (11:45)
  • A2: “Train Station” (11:54)
  • B: “Salon De Musique” (10:16)

Su Tissue is credited as Composed By; no other personnel credits appear on the Discogs entry.

The record attracted renewed interest decades later. A bootleg cassette reissue was issued by Savers (a bootleg cassette label) in 2021 (cat. SAVERS 03), and a further Savers pressing appears in 2026 — suggesting the album has maintained a cult following. An unofficial self-released cassette predated the Adversity LP pressing.

After 1984

In 1986, McLane made a final documented public appearance: a small acting role as “Peggy Dillman” in Jonathan Demme’s comedy film Something Wild (1986), starring Melanie Griffith and Jeff Daniels. This appears to be her last verified public activity.

After Something Wild she withdrew entirely from public life. Multiple sources note that she changed her name (first and last) at some point after 1984, and that she now lives privately on the US east coast. Circulating rumours among fans include that she became a lawyer in Boston or Los Angeles, or a piano teacher in the Boston suburbs, or a housewife — none confirmed. Chuck Roast stated in the 2015 Something Else interview that he had not seen or heard from her since the band split, but said “I thought the stuff she was doing afterwards was very cool.”

As of 2026, no new recordings, public appearances, or interviews under any name have been documented. A 2019 Outline article and a 2021 Polyester piece both concluded by respecting her apparent wish to remain private.

No further releases under the Su Tissue name are documented in Discogs beyond pressings of Salon De Musique. The Suburban Lawns LP and Baby EP have received multiple reissues: UK pressings on Futurismo (2015, 2018), a 2021 US pressing on Superior Viaduct, and a 2024 reissue of Baby by Rubellan Remasters. Su Tissue appears in the credits of these reissues in her original performing role. No new recordings or projects are documented in Discogs.

Open Questions

  1. Real name conflict — partially resolved. All credible sources (Wikipedia, The Outline, Polyester, Something Else interview with Chuck Roast, Our Mind On Music 2026) give her name as “Sue McLane” or “Susan McLane.” “Susan Farthing” is a separate person entirely: a British actress (IMDb nm2572518) known for The Comic Strip Presents (1982), with no connection to Su Tissue. The confusion is likely a web misattribution. Multiple sources note she changed her name (first and last) after leaving public life — her current legal name is unknown and deliberately not publicised. Discogs’s “Susan M. McLane” is correct for her birth/stage-era name.

  2. Arspenlian Reeves. No primary source confirms this is a pseudonym, but no evidence of a real separate person named Arspenlian Reeves has ever surfaced. The most plausible interpretation — that it is a pseudonym for Su Tissue herself, possibly to credit her piano role separately from her vocalist/composer credit — is the consensus among reviewers. Still unverified from a primary source.

  3. Post-1984 activity. Confirmed: Berklee College of Music (piano studies), Salon De Musique (1984), role in Something Wild (1986). After 1986 she withdrew entirely from public life and changed her name. No further public activity documented. Location rumoured as east coast US. Profession unknown.

  4. Suburban Lawns break-up circumstances — partially resolved. John McBurney (Gleur) departed during the Baby EP recording sessions due to internal disputes (“business vs. art” conflicts per Frankie Ennui). The remaining four-piece attempted to continue but “the band dynamic had changed; it ran its course” (Chuck Roast, 2015). McLane’s role in or response to the dissolution is not documented in any surviving interview — she did not give press. See Suburban Lawns “Dissolution and Aftermath” section.

  5. Stage name origin. No primary source. Informal suggestion it is a tissue/Kleenex brand pun, but not confirmed by Su Tissue or any band member in documented interviews.
