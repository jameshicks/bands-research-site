Brian Paulson

Musician, record producer, and audio engineer from Minnesota. Discogs ID: 252689. Aliases: James Bunchberry Lane, Sir James Bunchberry.

One of the key recording engineers and producers of the American indie rock underground from the late 1980s through the 2000s. Best known for engineering Slint’s Spiderland (1991) and producing Uncle Tupelo’s Anodyne (1993), Son Volt’s Trace (1995) and Straightaways (1997), and Wilco’s A.M. (1995). Also produced and mixed one track on Beck’s Odelay (1996). A member of the Minneapolis post-punk/hardcore band Man Sized Action in the early 1980s.

Biography

Minnesota Origins and Man Sized Action (early 1980s)

Brian Paulson grew up in Bemidji, Minnesota — a small city in the northern part of the state. After graduating high school, he moved south to Minneapolis and began playing in Man Sized Action, a post-punk/hardcore band formed in the early 1980s. The band released two records on Bob Mould’s Reflex Records label: Claustrophobia (1983) and Five Story Garage (1984), on which Paulson is credited as guitarist, co-producer, and cover designer. Other members included Kelly Linehan, Pat Woods, Tippy, and Tony Pucci.

Man Sized Action placed Paulson within the Minneapolis underground scene and, critically, within the Chicago–Minneapolis touring circuit that would shape his engineering career.

Chicago Pipeline and Steve Albini (mid-1980s)

Through the Minneapolis–Chicago underground network, Paulson began touring with Steve Albini — at the time an engineer of growing reputation in the Chicago underground (Big Black, Rapeman). The connection with Albini introduced Paulson to the Chicago recording scene. Via Albini, Paulson met David Grubbs and worked with him on a record for Grubbs’s band Bastro.

Paulson’s earliest engineering credits in the Discogs database include work on the 1986 Twin/Tone Records release While You Were Out (mixed by Paulson), and producing and engineering several releases on Treehouse Records in the 1986–1988 period, including records by Cows (Taint Pluribus Taint Unum, 1987; Chow, 1988).

Spiderland and Breakthrough (1989–1992)

Paulson’s career-defining moment came when he was brought in to engineer Slint’s second and final album, Spiderland, recorded in Chicago in late 1990. According to the Discogs profile and band lore, the album was tracked in only four days, with Paulson and the band working through the night to meet a deadline. Spiderland was released on Touch And Go in March 1991. The album’s unconventional dynamics, near-silence passages, and spoken-word vocal style demanded precise engineering; Paulson’s work captures the spatial precision that made the record influential.

In the immediate aftermath, Paulson remained busy across the Chicago–Louisville–Minneapolis axis:

  • Engineered Babes In Toyland’s Fontanelle (1992, Reprise) — co-produced by Lee Ranaldo of Sonic Youth
  • Engineered the Slint Untitled 10” (1994) mixing sessions (uncredited)
  • Producer/engineer credits on Arcwelder’s Pull (1993, Touch And Go)
  • Engineered and mixed Unrest’s Perfect Teeth (1993, 4AD)

Uncle Tupelo, Wilco, and Son Volt (1993–1998)

The most commercially significant phase of Paulson’s production work ran from 1993 to 1998, centered on the extended Uncle Tupelo family.

Uncle Tupelo — Anodyne (1993, Reprise/Sire): Paulson served as sole producer, engineer, and mixer on Uncle Tupelo’s final album. The session featured the full band — Jay Farrar and Jeff Tweedy on guitars/vocals, Ken Coomer on drums, John Stirratt on bass, and Max Johnston on fiddle and lap steel.

After Uncle Tupelo’s 1994 dissolution, both successor bands came to Paulson:

Wilco — A.M. (1995, Reprise): Paulson produced and recorded the album (co-produced with Wilco), mixing three tracks (8, 11, 13). Richard Dodd mixed the remainder. The band at this stage was Tweedy, Stirratt, Coomer, Johnston, and Brian Henneman.

Son Volt — Trace (1995, Warner Bros.): Paulson co-produced with Son Volt (Jay Farrar) and mixed four tracks (4–6, 11). Trace was Son Volt’s debut and one of the flagship alt-country records of the period.

Son Volt — Straightaways (1997, Warner Bros.): Full producer, engineer, and mixer on the follow-up, recorded at Pachyderm Studio and Echo Park. This is the most complete Paulson production credit on a Son Volt record.

The Jayhawks — Sound Of Lies (1997, American Recordings): Paulson produced and mixed two tracks (“Sound Of Lies” and one other) at Smart Studios, working alongside the main album team at Ocean Way Studios. He has an extended relationship with The Jayhawks, also credited on the 2009 anthology Music From The North Country.

Other notable 1990s production/engineering credits:

  • Archers of Loaf — All The Nations Airports (1996, Alias): Producer and engineer
  • Archers of Loaf — White Trash Heroes (1998, Alias): Producer, engineer, mixed by
  • Superchunk — Foolish (1994, Merge): Mixed by
  • Polvo — Corvette Summer (1994): Engineer, mixer, producer
  • Squirrel Nut Zippers — The Inevitable (1995, Mammoth): Producer, engineer, mixer
  • Beck — Odelay (1996, Geffen/DGC): Producer and mixer, track 9 only (alongside The Dust Brothers, Mario Caldato Jr., Rob Schnapf, and Tom Rothrock)
  • Golden Smog — Weird Tales (1998, Rykodisc): Producer

Studio Work and Later Career (1999–2025)

Paulson’s workload through the 2000s remained substantial but increasingly diversified. He continued working with alt-country and indie rock artists, including Something For Kate (Australian alternative, late 1990s–early 2000s), and the broader Merge Records roster — with credits on releases by Crooked Fingers, The Rosebuds, and Mount Moriah. For The Rosebuds specifically, he produced, recorded, and mixed Birds Make Good Neighbors (2005, Merge/Goodnight Records) and contributed additional recording to Night Of The Furies (2007, Merge/Goodnight Records).

Notable later credits:

  • Bonnevill — Pelican (2001, Instinct Records): Recorded by (credited as “Brian Poulsen” on the “Ides” track)
  • Superchunk — Here’s To Shutting Up (2001, Matador): Producer
  • US Maple — The Suntanama (2002, Drag City): Engineer
  • Crooked Fingers — Bringing On The Snakes (2001): Recorded by, producer
  • Future Islands — In Prism (2009, Merge): Producer, engineer, recorder, mixer
  • Drag City — Acre Thrills (2025): Producer (uncredited)

Paulson also contributed to archival and reissue projects, including audio transfers for Voices Of Mississippi (Dust-to-Digital, 2018) and tape transfers for Yep Roc Records reissues.

Aliases

Paulson has used two known pseudonyms on Discogs, both under the “James Bunchberry” alias family:

  • James Bunchberry Lane — appears on select releases
  • Sir James Bunchberry — appears on select releases

The context or rationale for these aliases is not documented in the Discogs database. Name variations in the database include “Brian Poulsen,” “Brian Paulsen,” “B. Paulson,” “Brian ‘The Fixer’ Paulson,” and “Brian G. Paulson.”

Band Memberships

Man Sized Action

Post-punk/hardcore band from Minneapolis, MN. Formed in the early 1980s. Discogs ID: 1661804.

Members: Brian Paulson (guitar, production), Kelly Linehan, Pat Woods, Tippy, Tony Pucci.

Releases:

  • Claustrophobia (1983, Reflex Records)
  • Five Story Garage (1984, Reflex Records) — Paulson credited as guitarist, producer, and cover designer

The band’s label, Reflex Records, was run by Bob Mould (Hüsker Dü). Paulson’s work on Five Story Garage represents his earliest known production credit.

  • The Jayhawks — production and mixing credits across two records; part of the same Minneapolis alt-country axis
