Adrian Oxaal

Adrian Oxaal

Oct 17, 20267 min read

Adrian Oxaal

Discogs ID: 691978
Instruments: Guitar (lead, rhythm), cello, bass, keyboards, marimba, backing vocals
From: Brighton, UK
Name variations on record: Adrian, Adrian “Odd” Oxaal, Oxaal, Oxall

Multi-instrumentalist and guitarist, best known as a long-serving member of James. First appears in the Discogs record on the 1988 Lenna And The Snakemen single before moving through early-1990s Brighton projects — notably the Goat (5) singles on Beggars Banquet and Sharkboy (2) on Nude Records — and then joining James in time for Whiplash (1997). He has remained a member through the 2007 reformation, the 2018 album Living In Extraordinary Times, the 2021 album All The Colours Of You, and the 2023 40th-anniversary orchestrated album, and contributed liner notes to the 2025 definitive best-of. He is also listed as a member of Oysterband, playing cello on their 2014 album Diamonds On The Water.

Pre-James Activity (1988–1996)

Lenna And The Snakemen (1988)

Oxaal played lead and rhythm guitar on the single “Something’s Cooking” (1988, Wizard Records (8)) for Lenna And The Snakemen, a Brighton-based group also featuring drummer Dil Davies and vocalist Lenna Santamaria. This is the earliest Discogs-documented credit.

Goat (5) (1989–1991)

Goat (5) was a Brighton indie outfit on Beggars Banquet releasing several singles between 1989 and 1991. Oxaal is credited as a songwriter and guitarist/noise-maker across their catalog. He is listed in the Discogs member roster alongside Ambrose Lovejoy, Marcus Thirtle, and Mark Browning (2). On the album Medication Time (1991) he is billed as “Adrian ‘Odd’ Oxaal” with credits for “Guitar, Noises, Other [Yellch]” and Songwriter. The band also self-produced the record alongside Mark Flannery.

Key Goat (5) releases:

  • “Can’t Get By” (1989, Situation Two) — produced by Goat (5)
  • “Your Love Isa Mine” (1990, Beggars Banquet) — Oxaal: Written-By
  • “Good Times” (1991, Beggars Banquet) — Oxaal: Guitar
  • “Everybody Wants To Be There” (1991, Beggars Banquet)
  • Medication Time (1991, Beggars Banquet) — Oxaal: Guitar, Noises, Songwriter

Sharkboy (2) (1993–1995)

Sharkboy (2) was a mid-1990s indie-pop group on Nude Records, fronted by vocalist Avy. Oxaal played bass, cello, guitar, keyboards, and marimba across their output, and has a writing credit on The Valentine Tapes (track 7). The lineup included Dil Davies (drums), Alan Stirner (guitar), Nick Wilson (2) (trumpet/keyboards), and Gavin Cheyne (bass/guitar). The 1995 album The Valentine Tapes was produced by Phil Wright (2) and also featured Dickon Hinchliffe on violin and Steve Hewitt on drums on one track.

Key Sharkboy (2) releases:

  • “Crystaline” (1993, Nude Records) — Bass, Cello, Guitar, Keyboards
  • “Matinee” (1994, Nude Records) — Guitar, Cello, Bass, Keyboards
  • “Razor / Bright Things Lie / Dear Gilda / Show Me Now” (1994, Nude Records) — Bass, Cello, Keyboards, Banjo, Guitar
  • “The Valentine Singles / Big Black Jaguar - Diamonds Are Forever” (1995, Nude Records) — Guitar, Cello, Keyboards
  • The Valentine Tapes (1995, Nude Records) — Cello, Guitar, Keyboards, Marimba; Written-By (track 7)

James (1997–present)

Oxaal joined James no later than the recording of Whiplash (1997, Fontana), on which he is listed in the “James Are:” band credit alongside Tim Booth, Jim Glennie, Saul Davies, Mark Hunter, David Baynton-Power, and Larry Gott. That album was produced by Stephen Hague with co-production interference from Brian Eno.

He continued with the band through the late-1990s albums — appearing on the compilation The Best Of (1998, Fontana/Mercury) with credits for Guitar and Cello — and the live release Getting Away With It…Live (2002, Sanctuary/Warner Music Vision) as guitarist.

After the band’s 2007 reformation (he is confirmed in the Discogs member list for James, Discogs ID 12094), he appears on all subsequent studio and live output:

  • Living In Extraordinary Times (2018, Infectious Music) — Guitar, Cello, Backing Vocals. Produced by Charlie Andrew with Brian Eno on synth programming. The sessions also included Andy Diagram (trumpet), Adam Betts (drums), and a full brass section.
  • Many Faces (Edit) (2018, Infectious Music) — Electric Guitar
  • Live In Extraordinary Times (2020, Nothing But Love Music) — Guitar, Cello
  • All The Colours Of You (2021, Virgin Music) — Performer [James]; produced by Jacknife Lee (Garret Lee). Music written by Jacknife Lee, Jim Glennie, Mark Hunter, Saul Davies, Tim Booth. The 2021 lineup also includes Andy Diagram, Chloe Alper, David Baynton-Power.
  • Be Opened By The Wonderful (40 Years Orchestrated) (2023, Virgin Music) — Lead Guitar; orchestrated by Joe Duddell with the ORCA22 orchestra and Manchester Inspirational Voices choir.
  • Yummy (2024, Virgin Music) — Liner Notes
  • Nothing But Love // The Definitive Best Of (2025, EMI/Mercury/Universal) — Guitar (track D4), Backing Vocals (track D4), Liner Notes

Oysterband

Oxaal is listed as a member of Oysterband (Discogs ID: 89461) and credited with Cello on their 2014 album Diamonds On The Water (Navigator Records (2)). He also appears on the 2016 compilation This House Will Stand - The Best Of Oysterband 1998–2015 (Westpark Music). The Oysterband lineup on Diamonds on the Water also includes Dil Davies on drums — a collaborator going back to the Lenna And The Snakemen and Sharkboy days — as well as Alan Prosser, Ian Telfer, and John Jones (4).

Open Questions

  • Exact year Oxaal joined James: Discogs confirms by Whiplash (1997) but does not document any earlier James recording credit. Web sources would be needed to confirm if he joined for touring before the album or was recruited specifically for it.
  • Brighton background: The profile notes “Multi instrumentalist from Brighton, UK” — Goat (5) was a Brighton act on Beggars Banquet. Whether Oxaal was Brighton-based throughout or relocated to Manchester with James is not resolvable from Discogs alone.
  • The “Hey Ma” (2008) credit in the old stub is not supported by Discogs data — no release credit for Oxaal on that title appears in the DB. Likely a touring-only period; the 2002 live album is the last Discogs credit before 2014.
  • Goat (5) vs James timeline: Goat (5) were active 1989–1991; Sharkboy (2) 1993–1995; James credit starts 1997. The gap 1995–1997 is undocumented in Discogs.
