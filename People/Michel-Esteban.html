Michel Esteban

Discogs ID: 259194
Full name: Michel Antoine Gaston Esteban
Born: Paris, 7 May 1951

French record producer, label co-founder, designer, and cultural impresario. Co-founder of SPY Records Ltd. (1977, New York) and ZE Records (1978, New York). Key figure in the Paris–London–New York punk triangle of 1975–1982; long-term partner of Lizzy Mercier Descloux and her exclusive record producer through Mambo Nassau (1981). Subsequently based in France and then Salvador da Bahia, Brazil, where he revived ZE Records as a reissue label.

Biography

Education, 1968–1974

Esteban studied graphic arts at L’École d’Arts Graphiques, Paris, from 1968. In 1974 he traveled across the United States and returned to New York, where he studied under graphic designer Milton Glaser at the School of Visual Arts. During this New York period he frequented CBGB and made early connections with Patti Smith and Robert Mapplethorpe before returning to Paris.

Background — Paris, 1973–1976

In 1973, while still commuting between Paris and New York, Esteban opened Harry Cover at 12, Rue des Halles (1st arrondissement), a Paris shop selling imported US/UK rock records, merchandise, magazines, and books — described as Paris’s first concept record store for rock culture. The basement served as a rehearsal space that became a hub for French punk. (Lizzy Mercier Descloux lived at number 11 opposite; they met when Esteban left a note at her door.) Wikipedia — Lizzy Mercier Descloux, as of 2025-06-01

Co-published Rock News (1975–76; 7 issues, January–September 1976) with Lizzy Mercier Descloux as Artistic Director — Esteban served as Publication Director. The magazine’s first issue carried Iggy Pop on the cover and featured The Velvet Underground, New York Dolls, and the nascent NYC punk scene. Issue 4 (May 1976) was a Special Punk issue featuring the Sex Pistols (described as “still little known to the general public”) and Stinky Toys. One of the first European magazines to cover the parallel punk/new wave emergence in New York, London, and Paris simultaneously. Through Rock News he connected with US critics and figures including Lester Bangs, Richard and Lisa Robinson, and Danny Fields, as well as Malcolm McLaren and Vivienne Westwood in England. Galerie Babylone — Rock News complete set listing Galerie Babylone — Rock News n°4 Special Punk

In 1977, Esteban published a small run of books through his own Paris imprint: Patti Smith’s poetry collections Witt and The Night, and Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s first book of poems, Desiderata (written in New York in winter 1975, with drawings, collages, and photographs by Descloux and collaborators including Smith and Richard Hell).

In 1975, traveled to New York via Rock News; connected with Patti Smith, Richard Hell, Tom Verlaine of Television, Robert Mapplethorpe, Lizzy Mercier Descloux, and Malcolm McLaren. Helped organize the Sex Pistols’ first French concert (September 1976) and promoted French band Stinky Toys in England. Relocated permanently to New York in 1977.

SPY Records Ltd. — 1977–1978

Esteban’s first production credit: Marie Et Les Garçons’ Rien À Dire (Rebel Records, 1977), co-produced with the band. Through John Cale, he became a partner in SPY Records Ltd. (Discogs ID 98626), which Cale launched in 1977 with manager Jane Friedman and, later, Michael Zilkha. Esteban designed the SPY logo and introduced Zilkha to the label. Catalog ran to three documented releases: Harry Toledo & The Rockets (SPY 001, 1977), Marie Et Les Garçons Attitudes (SPY 305, 1978), and John Cale Sabotage/Live (SP 004, 1979). Distributed by I.R.S. Records.

In 1978 Esteban and Zilkha left SPY to found their own label.

ZE Records — 1978–1985

Founded 1978, New York. The name ZE inverts the founders’ initials (Zilkha + Esteban). Michael Zilkha handled business and finance; Esteban handled design, creative direction, and European connections.

During the founding period Esteban was in a relationship with fashion editor Anna Wintour. Wintour had stepped back from work in late 1978 and for approximately two years divided her time between Paris and New York with him; she returned to work in 1980 as fashion editor of Savvy magazine (subsequently moving to New York magazine in 1981), placing the relationship roughly late 1978–1980. Wikipedia — Anna Wintour, as of 2025-06-01 Wintour introduced Esteban to Chris Blackwell of Island Records, which led directly to the Island worldwide distribution deal. ZE Wikipedia describes Blackwell as “a friend of Esteban’s then-girlfriend Anna Wintour.” Wikipedia — ZE Records, as of 2025-06-01 No public statements by Wintour about Esteban have been located; Esteban has not discussed the relationship in identified interviews. ZE operated with Island Records as worldwide distributor; records were marketed via Island in the UK and Ariola in continental Europe. Profile: mutant disco, No Wave, funk, Latin, and New Wave — home to James Chance & The Contortions, Kid Creole And The Coconuts, Alan Vega, Suicide, Was (Not Was), Lydia Lunch, Material, and others. Esteban was primarily the label’s art director and European liaison.

ZE shut down in 1984 (per ZE Records official history) or 1985 (per Discogs). Esteban had left New York and the label in 1982.

Departure from ZE (1982): The Guardian (via ZE Books) quotes Zilkha describing the split as over “a financial matter.” ZE Books — Guardian cover story The ZE official about page and Wikipedia describe it simply as “differences” or “a personal dispute.” Esteban’s own account (ZE Records official interview page) does not address the specific reason for his departure. The nature of the dispute remains vague in all verified primary sources.

Conflict — ZE shutdown date: ZE official site bio for Zilkha states he “stopped ZE Records in 1984”; the Discogs entry and the existing article say 1985. zerecords.com/about

Lizzy Mercier Descloux Productions

Esteban was the executive or sole producer of all three LMD albums recorded while they were partners:

1. Press Color (ZE Records, 1979) — LMD’s debut LP (ZEA 33-004). Produced, arranged, and mixed by the trio of Descloux, her brother Didier Esteban (under alias D.J. Banes), and Erik Elliasson. Esteban executive-produced. Genres: No Wave, Free Funk, New Wave.

2. Mambo Nassau (Philips, 1981) — LMD’s second LP. Exclusively produced by Esteban; recorded in Nassau with engineer/producer Steven Stanley and synthesizer work by Wally Badarou. Full band session with Philippe Le Mongne (bass), Bill Perry (drums), Yann Lecker (guitar), Yvan Noe (percussion). Esteban contributed backing vocals on “Lady O K’Pele.”

3. Zulu Rock (CBS, 1984) — Co-produced with Adam Kidron; Esteban handled photography and design. Their personal and professional partnership ended before the next LMD album.

From One For The Soul (Polydor, 1986) onwards, Esteban remained involved at arm’s length — credited as coordinator, design, management, photography — but was no longer sole producer.

The Rosa Yemen connection: Esteban produced the 1979 Rosa Yemen 12” (ZE 12003, later Celluloid) featuring Descloux on guitar and vocals, with his brother Didier Esteban on rhythm guitar.

Lio (Wanda Maria Ribeiro Furtado Tavares de Vasconcelos) became the primary artist of Esteban’s post-ZE career — a Portuguese-Belgian pop star for whom he functioned as producer, manager, and art director through the late 1980s. Esteban and Lio had a daughter together — Nubia Esteban, born 15 September 1987 — and separated in 1989. Purepeople — “Lio maman de 6 enfants : Qui est Michel Esteban, le père de sa fille Nubia” Nubia is Lio’s eldest child. She pursued music and performance, appearing in 2012 at the RFM Party 80 Paris concert tour and contributing vocals to a 2019 track (“Sweet Harmony”). She subsequently worked as a DJ, touring internationally. As of reporting c.2024–2025 she is based in Thailand, where Esteban also settled after relocating to Koh Phangan in 2013 to open a small recording studio. Purepeople — “Lio : Artiste français, un des pères de ses 6 enfants réapparaît avec sa fille aînée, en Thaïlande”

1985–2002 — Brazil Discovery, Retreat, and Cuba

Esteban first encountered Brazil in 1985, while recording with Lizzy Mercier Descloux in Africa for what became the Zulu Rock project — the Brazil connection developed in parallel with those sessions. ZE Records official interview, zerecords.com/about-interview-5.html Following his separation from Lio in 1989, Esteban stepped back from the commercial music business. During the 1990s he studied esoteric disciplines, principally Theosophy and Sufism. Later in the decade he traveled to Cuba, where he recorded local musicians. Wikipedia — Michel Esteban, as of 2025-06-01 This period produced no significant commercial releases but fed into his later interest in Brazilian and Afro-diasporic music. Around 2006 he relocated from Paris to Salvador da Bahia, Brazil. [search result citing ZE Records interview, 2009] A subsequent relocation: by 2013 Esteban had moved to Koh Phangan, Thailand, where he opened a small recording studio. Purepeople — “Lio : Artiste français, un des pères de ses 6 enfants réapparaît avec sa fille aînée, en Thaïlande” The Salvador da Bahia address for ZE Records may reflect a corporate registration rather than his current residence.

ZE Records Revival — 2002–present

Esteban relaunched ZE Records in 2003 from France (per ZE official site; the existing article said 2002/2003 — web sources consistently say 2003), and later from Salvador da Bahia, Brazil (current registered address: Av. Tancredo Neves, Salvador), where he operates a cultural arts center called A Fabrica — described by Esteban as “a sort of Sundance film institute, but for all arts,” housed in a restored colonial factory building and comprising galleries and recording studios. ZE Records official interview The revival also recorded new artists including Glasgow band Michael Dracula. Light in the Attic reissue campaign — 2015–2016

Separately from the ZE self-releases above, Light in the Attic (Seattle reissue label) licensed and reissued the complete Lizzy Mercier Descloux discography in 2015–2016. These are LITA releases, not ZE releases; the label on each is Light in the Attic, with ZE Records noted as original label. Esteban is credited as executive producer on Press Color (LITA) and cooperated with the campaign; liner notes by music journalist Vivien Goldman appear across the series.

Esteban in his own words

Selected direct quotes from identified interviews:

On founding ZE with Zilkha (ZE Records official interview, zerecords.com): “Michael was very shy but very passionate, he was speaking french perfectly, we immediatly get along fine.”

On ZE’s founding philosophy (ZE Records official interview): “No clear vision, just instinct.” He describes the label’s personality in one word: “Freedom.” Artists had “caractere, personality and tallent” and “something that other artists did not had.”

On ZE’s lasting appeal (ZE Records official interview): “we had good taste in music and personalities” — and gave artists creative freedom.

On ZE’s art philosophy (via web source, attributed to Esteban): “music, graphic art, movies, and to a certain extent literature or even painting are the same.” He cited Edgard Varèse: “An artist is never ahead of his time, but most people are far behind theirs.”

On Lizzy Mercier Descloux (quoted in AnOther tribute piece, 2017): “Lizzy never thought she had something to prove. I don’t think she was ambitious in the pejorative sense. She didn’t have that big ego to become a superstar.” AnOther — Tribute to Lizzy Mercier Descloux

On Zulu Rock sourcing controversy (attributed via web source): “Some songs are covers. The rest were studio jams. I made the deal with the musicians there and did the publishing there, everything was normal.”

Lizzy Mercier Descloux — death 2004

Descloux died 20 April 2004 in Saint-Florent, Corsica, from ovarian and colorectal cancers diagnosed in 2003, aged 47. No public statement from Esteban at the time of her death has been located in web sources. His subsequent actions — producing ZE’s own reissue series of her work (2004–2005) and cooperating with Light in the Attic’s full-catalog reissue campaign (2015–2016; see section below under ZE Records Revival) — document continued stewardship. The AnOther tribute quotes him directly; the quote was likely given for the 2015/2016 reissue campaign, not at the time of her death. Lizzy Mercier Descloux Wikipedia — as of 2025-06-01
