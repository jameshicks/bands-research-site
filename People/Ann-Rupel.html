Ann Rupel

Instruments: Bass, organ, vocals
Active: 1983 – at least 1996
Groups: Curlew, No Safety, God Is My Co-Pilot, V-Effect (2)

US avant-garde bassist, vocalist, and composer. Active in the New York downtown scene from the early 1980s. Best documented as the bassist and occasional vocalist on Curlew’s Cuneiform-era recordings (Bee, A Beautiful Western Saddle, Paradise), where she also contributed original compositions. Prior to Curlew she was a founding member of V-Effect (2), a trio with drummer Rick Brown and saxophonist David Zonzinsky. Her parallel activities include membership in the noise-rock/avant ensemble No Safety and the queercore collective God Is My Co-Pilot.

Early Career: V-Effect (2) (1983)

Rupel’s earliest documented recording credit is on Stop Those Songs (1983, Rift Records), the sole release by V-Effect (2) — a trio completed by Rick Brown on drums/glockenspiel/vocals and David Zonzinsky on alto saxophone/vocals. Rupel plays bass, organ, and sings. The album was engineered in part by Martin Bisi and mixed by Fred Frith alongside Bisi, Vogel, and the band collectively — placing the session squarely in the downtown NYC network around BC Studio. The Bandcamp reissue URL associated with V-Effect (2) identifies the project as “brownoutspassed.”

No Safety (1985–1993)

Rupel is a member of No Safety, the New York avant-rock ensemble led by guitarist Chris Cochrane. The group’s self-titled 1985 cassette (CNLF) co-credits No Safety, Cochrane, and Robert Hinrix as primary artists. By the time of Spill (1992, Knitting Factory Works), the lineup includes Rupel on bass and vocals, Zeena Parkins on keyboards/accordion/electric harp/vocals, Doug Seidel on guitar, Tim Spelios on drums/percussion, with David Shea guesting on turntables and samples. Produced by David Weinstein and the band; engineered by John Siket and Jon Rosenberg. Live At The Knitting Factory (1993, Knitting Factory Works) captures the same core with Tim Spelios added on “traps.”

Curlew (1991–1996)

Rupel joins Curlew for Bee (1991) on bass, filling the slot previously held by Fred Frith and before him Bill Laswell. She remains through three studio albums and various live recordings.

Bee (1991, Open Minds / Sabiton Records)

Produced, recorded, and mixed by Hahn Rowe (with John Siket assisting). Rupel on bass. Compositions by George Cartwright (tracks 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11) and Tom Cora (tracks 3, 6, 9). Mastered by Roger Seibel.

A Beautiful Western Saddle (1993, Cuneiform Records)

Co-credited: Curlew with Amy Denio (lyrics/vocals) and Paul Haines (lyrics). Rupel plays bass throughout and contributes backing vocals on tracks 1, 7, 9, 10, 13. She is the sole composer of track 2 (credited as “Music By”). Produced by Bob Appel; mixed by Hugo Dwyer (with Scott Gilfix assisting); recorded by Jon Rosenberg; mastered by Roger Seibel. Design by Ruth Peyser.

Paradise (1996, Cuneiform Records)

Samm Bennett replaces Pippin Barnett on drums; Chris Cochrane (also her No Safety bandmate) joins on guitar and vocals; Tom Cora is absent. Jim Spake guests on baritone/soprano saxophone. Rupel plays bass, sings, and composes tracks 1 and 9 (credited as “Written-By”). George Cartwright writes tracks 3–8. Produced by Cartwright; recorded and mixed by Rusty McFarland. Executive-produced by Steven Feigenbaum.

The Peel Sessions (1996, Strange Fruit) — God Is My Co-Pilot

Though listed under God Is My Co-Pilot, Rupel appears on tracks 2, 5, 6, 9, 12, 15 playing bass, vocals, and Hammond organ. This documents her overlap between Curlew and GodCo during 1996.

Archival Curlew releases

Rupel appears on both archival live releases: Live At The Knitting Factory 1991 / Live At D.C. Space (2018, Cuneiform Records) and CBGB’s 1987 (2022, Cuneiform Records). The latter captures the band at CBGB before her tenure; the former is from the Bee era lineup.

God Is My Co-Pilot (1990s)

Rupel is a member of God Is My Co-Pilot, the prolific New York queercore/avant-rock collective centered on Sharon Topper and Craig Flanagin. Her specific tracks on their Peel Sessions (1996) are documented above. The group’s catalog includes a large number of releases on labels including The Making Of Americans, Les Disques Du Soleil Et De L’Acier, Blackout! Records, and Dark Beloved Cloud.
