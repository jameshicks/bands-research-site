Chick Corea

American jazz pianist, composer, bandleader, and keyboardist. Born Armando Anthony Corea, June 12, 1941, Chelsea, Massachusetts; died February 9, 2021, Tampa, Florida. Discogs artist ID: 37731. Of southern Italian descent; acquired the nickname “Chick” from an aunt. Married to vocalist Gayle Moran. Won 25 Grammy Awards across his career.

Biography

Early Career (1960s)

Corea began his professional career in the early 1960s, working as a sideman in New York. He appeared with Latin-inflected groups — including Mongo Santamaria And His Afro-Latin Group — and recorded his debut album Tones For Joan’s Bones in 1966 (Vortex, released 1968). His breakthrough as a leader came with the post-bop trio album Now He Sings, Now He Sobs (Solid State, 1968), recorded with drummer Roy Haynes and bassist Miroslav Vitous — a session that remained a touchstone for pianistic invention. He also recorded The Song Of Singing (Blue Note, 1971) in this trio configuration.

From 1968 to 1970 he was a member of Miles Davis’s electric band, recording on a series of landmark electric sessions (In a Silent Way, Bitches Brew) that defined jazz-rock fusion. He and Dave Holland both departed Davis’s group to pursue more collectively oriented approaches.

Circle (1970–1972)

After leaving Davis, Corea co-founded Circle (5) with Dave Holland (bass), Anthony Braxton (reeds), and Barry Altschul (drums). The quartet committed to free collective improvisation with no fixed harmonic center. The group recorded two live double-LPs in Germany for CBS/Sony Japan (Circle-1 Live In German Concert, SOPL-19-XJ; Circle 2: Gathering, SOPL-20-XJ, both 1971). A trio variant — Corea, Holland, Altschul — recorded A.R.C. (ECM 1009 ST, 1971), produced by Manfred Eicher. The full quartet’s Paris concert was later released by ECM as Paris - Concert (ECM 1018/19 ST, 1972). Early Circle (Blue Note, 1992) collected earlier Corea trio/quartet material. Corea departed Circle in early 1972, finding the purely free approach creatively exhausting; he sought a context that allowed melody and tonality back in.

Return To Forever — First Lineup (1972–1973): Latin-Acoustic

The first Return To Forever album (ECM, 1972) introduced a lyrical Latin-acoustic fusion: Corea on electric piano, Joe Farrell (flutes, soprano sax), Stanley Clarke (electric and double bass), Airto Moreira (drums, percussion), and Flora Purim (vocals, percussion). Lyrics on several tracks by Neville Potter. Produced by Manfred Eicher. Light As A Feather (Polydor, 1973) — co-credited to Chick Corea & Return To Forever — kept the same lineup and produced the standard “Spain,” with mastering by Bob Ludwig.

Return To Forever — Electric Fusion Lineup (1973–1977)

With Hymn Of The Seventh Galaxy (Polydor, 1973), RTF pivoted hard into amplified jazz-rock. Farrell, Moreira, and Purim were replaced by guitarist Bill Connors and drummer Lenny White. Clarke remained. This lineup recorded one studio album; Connors departed and was replaced by Al Di Meola for the remainder of the electric period.

The classic rock-inflected RTF quartet — Corea, Clarke, Di Meola, White — recorded Where Have I Known You Before (Polydor, 1974), No Mystery (Polydor, 1975), and Romantic Warrior (Columbia, 1976), the latter their most prog-influenced work (Corea on piano, Fender Rhodes, Minimoog, ARP Odyssey, PolyMoog, Clavinet, Yamaha organ, marimba). Corea self-produced all these records with Clarke, Di Meola, and White as assistant producers. RTF also recorded Musicmagic and Live before disbanding around 1977.

Solo Polydor Period (1975–1980)

Running parallel to RTF, Corea maintained an active solo career under Polydor. Key releases:

  • The Leprechaun (Polydor, 1976) — orchestral fusion
  • My Spanish Heart (Polydor, 1976) — double album with strings, brass, Stanley Clarke (acoustic bass), Steve Gadd (drums), Gayle Moran (vocals/choir); Corea arranged and produced; mastered by Bob Ludwig
  • The Mad Hatter (Polydor, 1978) — concept album
  • Secret Agent (Polydor, 1978)
  • Friends (Polydor, 1978) — with Steve Gadd, Joe Farrell, Eddie Gomez
  • Delphi 1, 2 & 3 Solo Piano Improvisations (Polydor, 1979–1980)

He also recorded piano duets with Herbie Hancock: An Evening With Herbie Hancock & Chick Corea In Concert 1978 (Columbia, 1978) and An Evening With Chick Corea And Herbie Hancock (Polydor, 1979).

1979–1980 Live Quartet

Corea assembled a short-lived electric quartet for live work in 1979–1980 with Al Di Meola (guitar), Tony Williams (drums), and Bunny Brunel (bass). Documented on Summer Jam 1979 (rec. 1979, released 1994) and Live In Tokyo ‘79 (Den-En Coliseum, rec. 1979, released 2021 on Hi Hat/Inter Art Committees). Corea also contributed keyboards to tracks A2 and A4 on Brunel’s Ivanhoe (1982).

Trio Music / Acoustic Return (1981–1985)

Three Quartets (Warner Bros., 1981) featured Corea with Michael Brecker, Eddie Gomez, and Steve Gadd — one of his most acclaimed acoustic post-bop statements.

Trio Music (ECM, 1982) reunited Corea with Miroslav Vitous and Roy Haynes — the Now He Sings rhythm section — performing original compositions and Thelonious Monk covers. Produced by Manfred Eicher. Trio Music, Live In Europe (ECM, 1986) documented the same trio on tour.

Septet (ECM, 1985) and Children’s Songs (ECM, 1984) extended his acoustic ECM work.

He also appeared on Gary Burton collaborations (Like Minds, Concord, 1998) through their long-running duo partnership ( Chick Corea).

Elektric Band Era (1986–1991)

Corea founded the Chick Corea Elektric Band for the GRP Records period, centering the group around a stable core:

  • John Patitucci — electric and acoustic bass
  • Dave Weckl — drums and electronics
  • Frank Gambale — guitar (joining later, also a GRP artist)

First album The Chick Corea Elektric Band (GRP, 1986): Patitucci, Weckl, Corea (keyboards, synthesizer), produced by Corea, engineered and mixed by Bernie Kirsh. GRP executive producers: Dave Grusin and Larry Rosen.

The Elektric Band released several albums through GRP (1986–1991), exploring contemporary fusion and synthesizer-driven jazz-rock.

Simultaneously, Corea ran the Chick Corea Akoustic Band (GRP, 1989) — an all-acoustic counterpart with the same Patitucci/Weckl rhythm section plus Corea on piano. The Akoustic Band debut was also produced by Corea, with Grusin and Rosen as executive producers.

Stretch Records and Late Career (1990s–2021)

Corea founded Stretch Records (Discogs ID: 95840; parent: Concord Music Group) as his own imprint for later career work, releasing most of his recordings through it from the 1990s onward. Key Stretch releases include Rendezvous In New York (Stretch/Universal, 2003), The Ultimate Adventure (Concord/Stretch, 2006), The Vigil (Concord/Stretch, 2013), The Musician (Concord/Stretch, 2016), and Chick Corea Plays (Stretch, 2018).

Notable late-career projects:

  • Chick Corea Trilogy — with Christian McBride and Brian Blade; Orvieto (ECM, 2011); Trilogy 2 (Universal, 2018); Trilogy 3 (Universal, 2025, posthumous)
  • The Spanish Heart Band — ensemble exploring Spanish-themed repertoire; Antidote (Concord, 2019)
  • Five Peace Band — with John McLaughlin, Kenny Garrett, Christian McBride, Brian Blade; Five Peace Band Live (Concord, 2009)
  • The Continents (Deutsche Grammophon, 2012) — concerto for jazz quintet and chamber orchestra
  • Further Explorations (Universal, 2011) — Horace Silver tribute
  • Remembrance (Thirty Tigers, 2024) — posthumous solo recordings

He maintained an ECM association across multiple eras, returning for Universal Syncopations (ECM, 2003) and Orvieto (ECM, 2011).
