Kerri Chandler

Real name: Kerri Camar Chandler Born: East Orange, New Jersey Discogs artist ID: 59 (one of the lowest-numbered IDs on the platform — assigned early in Discogs’s history) Nickname: “Kaoz 6:23” — derived from a series of chaotic events that occurred on June 23 each year early in his career.

DJ and producer from East Orange, New Jersey; one of the foundational figures of the New York/New Jersey deep house scene from the early 1990s onward. Founded Madhouse Records in 1992 and co-founded Sfere with Dennis Ferrer in 1998. Also founded Madtech Records (2012, a Madhouse sub-label) and Kaoz Theory (2015, an independent imprint). His total Discogs credit footprint spans over 1,000 release appearances (primary and additional).

Biography

Origins and Early Career (1990–1992)

Kerri Chandler’s recorded output begins with “Super Lover” (Express Records (2), 1990), a house track credited to him as primary artist. He followed this in 1991–92 with a run of production and engineering work for other acts: credited as producer on releases for Champion Records (UK), Movin’ Records (US), and Strictly Rhythm. His production credits from this period include releases for acts on Champion and Movin’ Records, often working alongside engineer and co-producer Yahya McDougald.

In 1992 Chandler launched Madhouse Records (Discogs label ID: 186095, parent: Madhouse Records, Inc.) out of the New York/New Jersey area. The label’s inaugural releases — catalog numbers KCT 1000 and KCT 1001 — established its character: deep, soulful NJ house. KCT 1000 was Get Out by Matrix (15) (Chandler’s own alias); KCT 1001 was I Got That Feelin by Dreamer G. Also in 1992 Chandler co-produced and executive-produced the Madhouse compilation A Basement, A Red Light, And A Feelin’ (KCLP 623), alongside co-producer Dee Dee Brave, which became a foundational document of the NJ deep house sound. The same year the Panic E.P. (KCT 1002) appeared under his own name — a debut statement on his own label that would frame his career.

His grandfather Calvin Reed Sr. (a.k.a. Grampa; Discogs ID: 21895, real name Calvin C. Reed Sr.) was a musician; Chandler produced and co-wrote “She’s Crazy” (Movin’ Records, 1993) with his grandfather on vocals. His father Joseph Chandler (Discogs ID: 479780; real name Joseph E. Chandler Jr.) and uncle Arnold Chandler (Discogs ID: 479779) were DJs in New York and were credited on the 2002 Madhouse release A Basement, A Red Light & A Feelin’ Vol. 2 (master ID 170483). Chandler’s daughter is Kiki Willows (Discogs ID: 5250606).

The NJ Deep House Peak (1993–1998)

By 1993 Chandler was deeply embedded in the New York/New Jersey house production circuit, placing tracks on Strictly Rhythm, BPM King Street Sounds, and Nott-Us Records. His Atmosphere E.P. Vol. 1 (Shelter Records (3), 1993) was an early touchstone of the atmospheric deep house subgenre. He remixed Ten City’s “Whatever Makes You Happy” (Ibadan, 1996) and co-produced “Say Something” on Ten City’s Columbia LP That Was Then, This Is Now (1994), linking his work to that of Jerome Sydenham and Joe Claussell’s Ibadan orbit.

His relationship with BPM King Street Sounds was prolific across this period: releases including “Hallelujah” (1996), the Trionisphere EP series (1996–2003), “Kaoz On King Street” (1997), and multiple compilations appeared there. The Trionisphere series eventually culminated in a live album (Trionisphere Live, 2003) and a full studio LP (Trionisphere: The Album, 2003).

In 1995 Chandler began his most enduring label relationship, signing to Large Records (ID: 12), where his Raw Grooves series (1995–1998, four volumes, LJ-016 / LJ-018 / LAR-023 / LAR-033) made him the label’s flagship artist. He would go on to place at least 10 primary release masters on Large Records through 2005, including the four-volume Digitalsoul LP series (1999–2002) and the Street Musiq series (2003). He remains the label’s most prolific primary artist in the Discogs catalog.

Also in 1997, Chandler began releasing on Ibadan (label ID: 836, the imprint operated by Jerome Sydenham): Escravos De Jo (1997), Atmosphere (The Lost Dubs) (1998), and ongoing collaborative work there through 2018. Ibadan also housed the Jerome Sydenham And Kerri Chandler duo catalog (18 releases on Ibadan through 2019).

Sfere and the Duo Era (1998–2003)

In 1998 Chandler and Dennis Ferrer co-founded Sfere (Discogs label ID: 1146, under parent Sfere Recordings Inc.). The label’s home address was reportedly 304 Hudson St., #503, New York NY 10013. Their joint debut on the label, First Steps (SF-005, 1999), was a 2xLP co-produced and arranged by both. Sfere placed releases by both Chandler and Ferrer through the early 2000s (total catalog of 40 releases), including “Brooklyn (Where I Live)” (2001), and housed the One World (2) / UFP collaboration “Peace In The Middle East” (2001). Sfere also received releases from the Jerome Sydenham And Kerri Chandler duo (e.g., “The Lost Tribes Of Ibadan 3,” 2001).

The formal duo Jerome Sydenham And Kerri Chandler (Discogs group ID: 9482) — which also records as Afro Elements (Discogs ID: 1508) — released their first track together in 1997 (“Espirito Du Tempo,” Ibadan) and built a catalog through to a 2019 remix project. Their releases span Ibadan, Madhouse, and Sfere, making the Sydenham/Chandler axis central to the Ibadan label’s roster.

Chandler also produced the Cesaria Evora By Kerri Chandler project (BMG France / Lusafrica / RCA, 2003), an unusual intersection of his house production sensibility with the catalog of Cape Verdean morna singer Cesaria Evora.

Madhouse Decade and Beyond (2001–2015)

The tenth anniversary of Madhouse Records produced the compilation 10 Years Of Madhouse (2001). The label continued releasing new Chandler material — Good Vibrations (with Roy Ayers, KCT 1118, 2003), and label singles from the wider Madhouse roster (Christopher McCray, Arnold Jarvis, Tony Lionni, Kamar, Lafayette, Dennis Ferrer).

In the mid-to-late 2000s Chandler moved into a deeper relationship with the French label Deeply Rooted House, anchoring his ambitious Computer Games LP project (2007–2009), issued in multiple “Expansion Pack” installments. This project extended the NJ deep house aesthetic into a more studio-conceptual framework.

He also released through Soul Heaven Records (co-compiled with Dennis Ferrer), NRK Sound Division (UK, 2006–2007, “The Promise,” “Coast 2 Coast”), and Watergate Records (Germany, 2014–2017, “The Watergate Files,” “Checkmate”). His BPM King Street Sounds series Trionisphere continued through a fifth volume in 2003.

The 25th anniversary of Madhouse was marked with 25 Years Of Madhouse Sampler (2018) and the double retrospective sets Remixed (2018) and Remixed Vol. 2 (2019).

In 2012 Chandler launched Madtech Records (Discogs label ID: 427720), a Madhouse sub-label, focused on contemporary house and UK bass-leaning sounds (artists included Voyeur (4), Citizen (15), Krystal Klear, Waze & Odyssey). Madtech had 62 releases through approximately 2014.

Kaoz Theory and Recent Work (2015–present)

In 2015 Chandler launched Kaoz Theory (Discogs label ID: 893599) as a standalone imprint. The label’s inaugural release was a self-titled EP (KT001, 2015). Kaoz Theory has since released Chandler’s own output alongside a small roster of associates: Jamie Jones (2), DJ Deep, Chris Stussy, DJ Steaw, Tuccillo, and others (80 releases through Discogs data).

The major statement of this era is Spaces And Places (KTLP001, 2022), Chandler’s first solo full-length in many years, issued in four vinyl samplers plus a full LP across 2022. He also contributed to the “!K7 Records” DJ-Kicks mix series (DJ-Kicks, 2017). Recent Kaoz Theory releases include “Caged Bird” (2024, with Nae (SA)) and “You Are In My System” (2023, with Troy Denari and Dennis Quin).
