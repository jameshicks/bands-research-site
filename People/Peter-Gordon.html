Peter Gordon

Composer, saxophonist, producer, multi-instrumentalist. Born Peter Laurence Gordon, 20 June 1951, New York City.

Discogs artist ID: 155981. Name variations: P. Gordon, P.L. Gordon, Peter Laurence Gordon.

Leader of Love of Life Orchestra (LOLO). Longtime collaborator of Arthur Russell, Robert Ashley, Laurie Anderson, Kathy Acker, Lawrence Weiner, and David Van Tieghem. Trained composer who moved fluidly between downtown minimalism, dance music, jazz-inflected pop, film/theatre scores, and art music — the LOLO records in particular sit at a rare intersection of all of these.

Biography

Early life Born June 20, 1951, New York City. Grew up in Virginia, Munich, and Los Angeles. Began piano at age 7 and clarinet in early childhood; took up saxophone at 14. In Munich, studied saxophone with Don Menza and music theory with Peter Jona Korn. During high school in Los Angeles, befriended Captain Beefheart and reportedly visited his studio during the Trout Mask Replica (1969) recording sessions — an early contact with radical compositional thinking in rock.

Education BA in composition, University of California San Diego — studied with Kenneth Gaburo and Roger Reynolds. MFA, Mills College Center for Contemporary Music — studied with Robert Ashley and Terry Riley. The UCSD and Mills pedigree placed him in the American experimental music lineage (minimalism, text-sound, avant-garde composition) before he arrived in New York.

Relationship with Kathy Acker, 1971–1977 In a relationship with writer/novelist Kathy Acker for roughly six years. Acker later contributed lyrics to his Leningrad Xpress (New Tone, 1990). The creative and personal overlap was formative.

New York City — downtown scene (mid-1970s onwards) Arrived in the NYC downtown scene and entered the orbit of the loft music / minimalist community. Formed The Flying Hearts ensemble with Arthur Russell — the group included David Byrne, Rhys Chatham, Jon Gibson, Jerry Harrison, Glenn Iamaro, Bill Ruyle, and Jon Sholle. Performed and recorded Instrumentals between 1975 and 1979 (released Les Disques du Crépuscule, 1984; reissued Audika Records, 2017).

Appeared on Arthur Russell’s “Kiss Me Again” (Sire, 1979) as saxophonist — the Dinosaur single with David Byrne and Nicky Siano.

Love of Life Orchestra (LOLO) — founded April 1977 Gordon was asked to perform at a benefit concert for Performing Artservices in spring 1977 and formed LOLO for the occasion. Founding lineup: Arthur Russell, Peter Zummo, Jill Kroesen, “Blue” Gene Tyranny, David Van Tieghem, Scott Johnson, Ernie Brooks, Ed Friedman, and Kathy Acker. The ensemble was conceived as “democratic music” — a space where people from classical, rock, poetry, and visual art backgrounds could play together.

Ensemble-based with a rotating cast thereafter; core long-term collaborators included David Van Tieghem (drums/percussion/electronics), Larry Saltzman (guitar), Rhys Chatham, and Tim Schellenbaum. The group appeared on Infidelity Records (distributed via Lust/Unlust Music, 1980), Beggars Banquet UK (1981), Expanded Music/Italy (1982), Artservices (1984), and New Tone Records (1992–1995), plus a 2010 DFA compilation reissue.

Robert Ashley — Perfect Lives (1980–1991) Gordon served as producer and co-composer of instrumental music beds for Robert Ashley’s landmark video opera Perfect Lives. The “score” — referred to in TV industry terms as “music beds” — was largely the creation of Gordon and pianist “Blue” Gene Tyranny. Gordon spent hundreds of hours in a 24-track analog studio with Ashley discovering new sounds and processes, cutting his teeth as a producer. The collaboration spans Lovely Music releases beginning with Perfect Lives (Private Parts): The Bar (1980) through the full Perfect Lives LP (Lovely Music, 1991). Gordon also returned as music producer and live electronics player for the Spanish-language version Vidas Perfectas (Alex Waterman, director).

Awards — 1985 Two major recognitions in the same year: Village Voice Obie Award for his score for Otello (theatre), and a Bessie Award for composing Secret Pastures — the Bill T. Jones/Arnie Zane Dance Company ballet that premiered at Brooklyn Academy of Music, November 14, 1984, with Keith Haring designing the set and Willi Smith creating costumes. Score recorded at Media Sound, NYC, November 18, 1984; only released on cassette (Artservices).

Kit Fitzgerald Married to video artist Kit Fitzgerald, a frequent creative partner. Return of the Native (1988) was described as “the first fusion of live orchestra and live video projection,” created with Fitzgerald. De Dode (The Deadman) on Still Life and the Deadman (1994) was composed for Fitzgerald’s film De Dode.

DFA connection (2010) James Murphy and Pat Mahoney of LCD Soundsystem used LOLO’s “Beginning of the Heartbreak” and “Don’t Don’t” to open and close their mix FabricLive.36 (2007). DFA then released remixes of those tracks in 2010 (DFA2238) and reissued the LOLO back catalog (DFA2229, DFA2263). Jonathan Galkin served as executive producer on the 2010 LOLO album.

Later work and teaching Collaborated with David Cunningham (The Flying Lizards) on The Yellow Box (Piano, 1996). Ongoing work with Tim Burgess of The Charlatans resulted in Same Language Different Worlds (O Genesis, 2016) and subsequent releases. Scored theatre (The Talking Band’s “The Necklace”), opera (The Society Architect Ponders the Golden Gate Bridge with Lawrence Weiner, 2008, for Bridge Records Crosstalk: American Speech Music). Work on the Desperate Housewives TV soundtrack is noted in his Discogs profile. Professor emeritus of Music Technology at Bloomfield College, New Jersey.

Compositional Context: Downtown Music

Gordon belongs to the downtown music scene — a distinct tradition from the “uptown” classical establishment centered on Lincoln Center and Juilliard, and equally distinct from commercial pop. Downtown music emerged in lower Manhattan (Greenwich Village, East Village, SoHo) from the 1960s onward, defined by its rejection of both institutional classical convention and commercially-defined pop. Its venues were lofts, art spaces, and clubs: The Kitchen, CBGB, Max’s Kansas City, the Mudd Club, Judson Memorial Church. Its peer community crossed freely between composed music, free improvisation, performance art, no-wave, disco, and rock.

Formation: Post-Minimalism at Mills and UCSD

Gordon’s compositional training placed him in the American experimental lineage, not the European serialist one. At UCSD he studied with Kenneth Gaburo (text-sound, language as musical material) and Roger Reynolds (electroacoustic composition). At Mills College he studied with Terry Riley — one of the originators of minimalism, whose In C (1964) directly shaped Gordon’s interest in “simple patterns and the layering of simple patterns” — and Robert Ashley, a pioneer of electronic opera and text-sound who became his most sustained creative partner. This formation made Gordon a post-minimalist: he inherited minimalism’s interest in process, pattern, and duration but was never bound by its asceticism.

What Downtown Was Not

By the time Gordon arrived in New York (1975), Glass and Reich had already moved toward “uptown” presentation — concert halls, formal programs, European touring. The downtown scene Gordon entered was explicitly pluralist and anti-hierarchical. John Zorn, Elliott Sharp, Glenn Branca, Rhys Chatham, and Laurie Anderson were peers; Arthur Russell connected the scene to disco and dance music; no-wave (DNA, the Contortions) brought punk abrasion. Gordon’s own description of what he wanted LOLO to be — “a band in which musicians and even artists, non-musicians from all sorts of genres, could create music that had a shared core” — is a precise statement of the downtown ethos.

Disco as a Structural Discovery

Gordon has specifically named early Donna Summer records as a compositional revelation: “if you could keep that groove going, then you can do whatever you want on top of it” — breaking “that verse-chorus hegemony which had been really dominating music for so long.” This is why LOLO’s early records sit at an intersection of minimalist pattern-work and dance music: the groove functions like a Riley phase-process, except it’s also a dancefloor.

Influences (in Gordon’s own words)

Simultaneous listening to James Brown, the Rolling Stones, John Coltrane, and Sun Ra. Saxophonists: Albert Ayler, King Curtis, Junior Walker (“that gutbucket type of tenor sound”). Beefheart as “someone doing rock music, influenced by blues, but really treating it as an artist.” Terry Riley’s In C as the model for pattern-layering. His own method: “if you have just a four-note pattern and you start shifting its rhythmic components, you will start suggesting a whole different range of genres.”

Key Institutions

  • The Kitchen (NYC) — primary venue; Perfect Lives commissioned here
  • Brooklyn Academy of Music — Secret Pastures premiered here (1984)
  • Performing Artservices — LOLO formed for a benefit concert for this organization (1977)
  • Mills College Center for Contemporary Music — institutional origin of the American experimental tradition Gordon came from
  • Bloomfield College — where he taught as professor of Music Technology (professor emeritus)

Key Projects & Releases

Star Jaws (Lovely Music LML 1031, 1977)

Peter Gordon’s debut album on Lovely Music — the label run by Ashley collaborators. Gordon as composer, producer, multi-instrumentalist (alto sax, tenor sax, clarinet, piano, poly-moog synth, vocals). Key personnel:

  • “Blue” Gene Tyranny — producer, clavinet, RMI electric piano, poly-moog synth, recorded by
  • Steve Bartek — guitar, acoustic guitar, bass (Oingo Boingo guitarist; frequent Gordon collaborator)

  • Steven Mackay — baritone saxophone
  • Karl Young — tenor saxophone, clarinet
  • Chuck Clark — tenor saxophone
  • Jane Sharp — vocals, backing vocals
  • Rich Gold — kazoo

The album connects the Mills/UCSD composition world (Tyranny, Payne) to the downtown New York scene Gordon was simultaneously inhabiting.

Dinosaur — “Kiss Me Again” (Sire, 1979)

Gordon plays saxophone on Arthur Russell’s disco single under the Dinosaur alias. Personnel: Arthur Russell (producer), Nicky Siano (producer), David Byrne (guitar), Larry Saltzman (guitar), Myriam Valle (vocals), Peter Zummo (trombone), Wilbur Bascomb (bass), Sammy Figueroa (percussion), Allan Schwartzberg (drums), Henry Flynt (violin), Frank Owens (piano). A direct connection between downtown minimalism/no wave and NYC disco production.

A collaboration between conceptual artist Lawrence Weiner (words) and Peter Gordon (music) on the Brussels label Les Disques du Crépuscule. Gordon has “music by” credit; Weiner has “words by.” An extension of the Weiner design collaboration begun on the Geneva EP.

Wim Mertens / Soft Verdict — At Home / Not At Home (Les Disques du Crépuscule TWI047, 1982)

Gordon plays tenor sax and synthesizer on Wim Mertens’s album — primary artist listed as Soft Verdict, the Belgian composer’s ensemble. Gordon appears as a Crépuscule session contributor rather than a LOLO release.

Laurie Anderson — Big Science (Warner Bros., 1982)

Gordon plays clarinet and tenor saxophone on Laurie Anderson’s major-label debut. David Van Tieghem also appears. The LOLO/Anderson orbit is well established by this point — Anderson did the cover concept for Extended Niceties in 1980 and appears in LOLO’s collaborator network repeatedly.

Tidal — Michael Byron (Neutral Records N5, 1983)

Gordon produces Michael Byron’s Tidal on Glenn Branca’s Neutral Records (catno N5). Personnel credited in DB: Michael Byron (composed by), Peter Gordon (producer), Glenn Branca (executive producer), Josh Baer (executive producer), Jo Bonney (design), Cindy Sherman (cover photo), Peter Garland (liner notes), Philip Lamantia (text). Cindy Sherman doing cover photography for a Neutral Records release is notable. Branca as executive producer situates this in the same Neutral world as Sonic Youth (N-ONE), Swans (N11), Branca’s Symphony No. 3 (NEUTRAL 4), and Y Pants (N-TWO).

Love of Life Orchestra — Secret Pastures (Ballet Suite) (Artservices, 1984)

Commissioned score for the Bill T. Jones/Arnie Zane Dance Company ballet Secret Pastures. World premiere: Brooklyn Academy of Music, November 14, 1984. Keith Haring designed the set; Willi Smith created costumes. Score recorded at Media Sound, NYC, November 18, 1984. Only released on cassette (C90). Peter Gordon won the Bessie Award for this score in 1985.

Peter Gordon — Still Life And The Deadman (New Tone Records RDC5017, 1994)

With the Balanescu String Quartet. 24 pieces, 38 minutes. Three commissions documented: “Awareness” — for the theatrical production Bataille, Bataille (based on writings of Georges Bataille); “De Dode (The Deadman)” — for the film De Dode directed by Kit Fitzgerald (Gordon’s wife); “Rembrandt Suite” — recorded May 1992 at Tokyo Broadcasting System Studios, Tokyo. A chamber-oriented record, distinct from the LOLO ensemble sound.
