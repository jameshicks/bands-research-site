Don Was

Real name: Donald Edward Fagenson. Discogs ID: 62920. Born Detroit, MI. Co-founder of Was (Not Was) with David Was.

Overview

Bassist, keyboardist, vocalist, and producer. With David Was formed Was (Not Was) circa 1979 in Detroit. Served as primary recording engineer, bassist, and co-producer on all Was (Not Was) studio albums (1981–2008). Also performed as alias “A Thousand Points Of Night.”

Post-Was (Not Was), became one of the most in-demand producers in popular music through the 1990s and 2000s. Co-produced The Rolling Stones’ Voodoo Lounge (1994), Bridges to Babylon (1997, executive producer), A Bigger Bang (2005), and Hackney Diamonds (2023), in each case alongside The Glimmer Twins. 657 shared Discogs credits. Also produced Bonnie Raitt, Bob Dylan, Elton John, and others. Has been president of Blue Note Records since 2012.

Key Credits (Was (Not Was))

  • Bass, keyboards, vibraphone, clavinet on debut LP (1981)
  • Recording engineer and mixer across all albums
  • Co-producer: Was (Not Was) (1981), Born To Laugh At Tornadoes (1983), What Up, Dog? (1988), Are You Okay? (1990), Boo! (2008)

Production Career

After Was (Not Was)‘s mainstream breakthrough on What Up, Dog? (1988), Don Was rapidly became one of the most sought-after producers in mainstream American music. His first major breakout outside Was (Not Was) was producing The B-52’s Cosmic Thing (1989, Reprise) — a massive commercial comeback that included “Love Shack” and “Roam,” both of which he also produced as singles. The same year he produced Bonnie Raitt’s Nick Of Time (Capitol, 1989), which won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year at the 1990 ceremony and launched Raitt’s commercial peak; he produced every subsequent Raitt album through the 1990s (Luck Of The Draw 1991, Longing In Their Hearts 1994, Road Tested 1995, Fundamental 1998).

Blue Note Records

Was became President of Blue Note Records in 2012. This is consistent with his long relationship with Blue Note-adjacent artists — Norah Jones (Last Days At The Lodge, 2008 was on Blue Note proper). He supervised Sweet Pea Atkinson’s late-career solo album Get What You Deserve (2017, Blue Note).
