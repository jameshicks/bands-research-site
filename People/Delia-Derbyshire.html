Delia Derbyshire

Discogs ID: 131041 Born: 5 May 1937, Coventry Died: 3 July 2001, Northampton Real name: Delia Ann Derbyshire Alias: Li De La Russe (used for Standard Music Library credits) Active: 1962–1973 (BBC Radiophonic Workshop); 1960s–1970s freelance

British electronic music composer. Member of the BBC Radiophonic Workshop (1962–1973). Realised Ron Grainer’s Doctor Who theme in 1963 — building it from a plucked string, 12 oscillators, and extensive tape manipulation — one of the first television themes created entirely with electronics. Grainer heard the result and asked “Did I really write this?”; he attempted to credit her as co-composer but was blocked by BBC policy. She was not credited on-screen until the 50th anniversary special (2013).

A prolific contributor to BBC programmes across almost 200 productions in 11 years, Derbyshire also worked extensively in parallel outside the Workshop. With Brian Hodgson and Peter Zinovieff, she co-founded Unit Delta Plus (1966), an electronic music promotion collective. She and Hodgson then co-founded the Kaleidophon studio with David Vorhaus, producing White Noise’s debut An Electric Storm (Island Records, 1968) — a landmark of British electronic music. Under the alias Li De La Russe she contributed pseudonymously to the Standard Music Library; those recordings were used in ITV programmes The Tomorrow People and Timeslip in the 1970s.

She left the BBC in 1973, worked briefly at Hodgson’s Electrophon studio, then retired from music in 1975, subsequently working in gas pipelines, art galleries, and a bookshop. She died in 2001. After her death, 267 reel-to-reel tapes from her attic were entrusted to Mark Ayres and later deposited at the University of Manchester.
