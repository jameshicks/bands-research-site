Perry Haines

Role: Fashion stylist, art director, record label associate, band manager, recording artist Discogs artist ID: 47967 Active on Discogs: 1981–2025 (credits span across releases)

Overview

Perry Haines is a British fashion figure best known as co-founder of i-D magazine — the London style bible launched in 1980 alongside Terry Jones (7). His Discogs footprint places him squarely in the early-1980s intersection of UK post-punk style culture and the music industry: photography/concept credits on early releases, a solo funk single on Fetish Records’ sublabel, and then a sustained run co-managing King (Coventry) through their CBS years.

His Discogs profile note: “Early 80’s co-founder of UK style-bible ‘ID Magazine‘“

On The Last Testament (1983)

Perry Haines appears on Fetish Records compilation The Last Testament (FR2011, 1983) with his track “What’s Funk?” — a reuse of the 1981 Fetish Funk Rox single. Track A3 on the LP. He is the only figure on the compilation who was also producing commercial pop management work within a year.

The Haines Gang

Discogs lists The Haines Gang (artist ID: 503168) as a group with Perry Haines as sole credited member. Their only known release is “So Hot (The Razor’s Edge Mix)” (1983, London Records), produced by Steve Brown (Fetish Records co-founder). This is a direct Fetish connection — Brown produced the Haines Gang record after the label wound down.

Web Sources

Biography

Perry Haines graduated from St. Martins Fashion College, London, and became an influential figure in London’s early 1980s style circuit. No birth year has been confirmed by any source found. From around 2006 to 2015 he lived on an island in the marshlands near Saint-Omer, northern France, close to the natural park Le Rommelaëre. [source: Duran Duran fan wiki / shapersofthe80s.com — see conflict note below]

i-D Magazine — Founding Role

i-D was founded in August 1980. The co-founders were Terry Jones, his wife Tricia Jones, and Perry Haines. Wikipedia’s i-D article credits Terry Jones as sole founder and editor-in-chief (1980–2012) and does not mention Haines — likely because his involvement was early and brief rather than sustained. [Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-D, as of 2026-06-29]

The Terry Jones Wikipedia article and multiple secondary sources confirm all three co-founders. [Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Jones_(i-D), as of 2026-06-29]

Haines’s specific role: he co-edited the inaugural issues and helped shape the magazine’s focus on subcultural street style. He is described variously as “co-editor” of the early issues and as one of its “cub editors.” The first issues were photocopied fanzines — banned from magazine retailers for ungainly staples — so Haines and Jones distributed copies from the boots of their cars. [sources: shapersofthe80s.com https://shapersofthe80s.com/revolution/key-to-the-blitz-cults-lift-off/; themagazineclub.co.uk https://www.themagazineclub.co.uk/i-d-magazine-2/]

The magazine became a commercial newsstand product in 1984 when Jones enlisted Time Out publisher Tony Elliott’s help. Haines’s involvement appears to have been concentrated in the early fanzine phase (1980–1981); he is not referenced in accounts of the magazine’s post-1982 development. No specific departure date confirmed.

i-D’s launch manifesto phrase — “Style isn’t what you wear but how you wear it” — is associated with Haines. [shapersofthe80s.com]

“New Romantic” Term

Multiple sources attribute coinage of the term “New Romantic” partly to Haines, via i-D’s autumn 1980 launch issue. The claim is contested: Richard James Burgess (who also produced King) is credited with coining the term in a separate context (an interview about Spandau Ballet). Haines is described as one of the “motormouth spokesmen” alongside Bob Elms and Steve Dagger who articulated the movement’s philosophy in print. [shapersofthe80s.com; classicpopmag.com https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/the-new-romantics/]

Fashion and Styling Career

Haines worked across several parallel roles simultaneously: club promoter/DJ, image consultant, fashion stylist, and magazine editor. Key documented fashion credits:

  • Duran Duran stylist (early career): From the beginning of Duran Duran’s career, Haines styled the band, incorporating Anthony Price silk suits with New Romantic street fashion. He also directed their “Careless Memories” video (1981) — his directorial debut, funded by the band. [duranduran.com https://duranduran.com/2025/malcolm-garrett-on-designing-for-duran-duran/]
  • Spandau Ballet and Visage: Identified as an associate / image collaborator; precise credits not confirmed by primary sources found.
  • Dr. Martens / King: Haines introduced King to Doc Martens and persuaded the brand to produce multi-coloured boots; this resulted in Dr. Martens sponsoring the group. This is the documented fashion input into his management work. [coventrygigs.blogspot.com https://coventrygigs.blogspot.com/2014/11/king-rise-and-fall-of-this-coventry-band.html]

No named designer brand (Westwood, McQueen, etc.) credits found in web sources. Haines’s fashion career appears to have been more stylist/image-consultant than designer in his own right.

Connection to Rod Pearce and the Fetish World

The Pearce–Haines connection is confirmed: “Rod Pearce and Perry Haines (onetime founder of i-D magazine) managed the successful pop stars King together.” [keithsneuroblog.blogspot.com http://keithsneuroblog.blogspot.com/2012/06/rhythm-of-cruelty-rod-pearce-fetish.html] The management of King is described by that source as “a total reversal” of what Pearce had been doing at Fetish Records.

Fetish Records had a strong design/fashion crossover via Neville Brody (art director for The Face, later Arena magazine), which positioned the label at the intersection of experimental music and high-fashion aesthetics. Haines’s earlier Fetish releases (“What’s Funk?” 1981, and the Last Testament compilation 1983) are the connective tissue: he was a Fetish recording artist before becoming Pearce’s management co-partner.

Dolphin Lovers Ltd.

No web source identifies the principals or ownership structure of Dolphin Lovers Ltd. It appears on Discogs credits as a management co-credit alongside Haines’s name on King releases. Given that Pearce and Haines are documented as the co-managers, Dolphin Lovers Ltd. is likely the trading vehicle for one or both of them, but which one (or both) cannot be confirmed from sources found.

Other Management Work

Haines’s confirmed first management venture was King. The Duran Duran fan wiki claims he went on to manage Massive Attack in the late 1980s, “breaking the band with Blue Lines.” This claim is not corroborated and is likely false: Wikipedia’s Massive Attack article identifies Cameron McVey as Massive Attack’s first manager and executive producer of Blue Lines, with no mention of Haines. [Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massive_Attack, as of 2026-06-29] The Discogs credits on this page show management extending to Paul King’s solo work through 1990 (Siren Records), which is the limit of confirmed management credits.

Later Life

From approximately 2006 to 2015, Haines lived on an island in the marshlands near Saint-Omer, northern France, close to the natural park Le Rommelaëre. No confirmed current location or activities. [source: Duran Duran fan wiki — weak fan source, treat as low confidence]

Conflict Notes

  • The Duran Duran fan wiki (duranduran.fandom.com) is the most detailed single source for Haines’s biography but is an unvetted fan wiki. Individual claims from it that are corroborated by other sources (i-D founding, Duran Duran styling, King management) are treated as reliable. The Massive Attack management claim from the same source is contradicted by Wikipedia and is flagged as likely erroneous.
