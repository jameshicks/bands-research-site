Biography

Early Work and Hungry Ghosts (1997–2000)

J.P. Shilo is a Melbourne-based multi-instrumentalist whose primary instruments are guitar and violin, though his Discogs credits also span bass, accordion, organ, autoharp, drum machine, and piano. His earliest documented work is with Hungry Ghosts, the Australian band he co-founded alongside Jason G. Boneham, Tim Howden, Nancy Lam, Steve Boyle (2), and — listed separately in the Discogs member roster — “John Brooks (2)”, which is his own alias (Discogs confirms realname “J.P. Shilo” for that entry).

Hungry Ghosts released two albums: a self-titled debut in 1997 (cassette/self-released) and a second self-titled album in 1999 (Fiido / Reliant Records). Shilo co-composed the material on the 1999 album, alongside Boneham and Howden. Notably, the 1999 record was produced by Rowland S. Howard — the Birthday Party guitarist and Melbourne underground pillar — with engineer Lindsay Gravina. The band’s final release was the self-released Alone, Alone (2017), on which Shilo is credited as Composer and Performer. That release also featured Steve Shelley (Sonic Youth drummer) as a guest performer and co-producer alongside Luc Suèr and the band.

Solo Work (2006–2019)

Shilo has released several records under his own name. As Happy As Sad Is Blue (2006, Smells Like Records, US) was his first solo album, entirely self-written, performed, and produced. The 2012 EP/cassette S L E E P (Not On Label, self-released) is a short guitar-and-violin statement featuring cover photography by Casper Shilo (presumably a family member; the only other artist credit on that release besides Shilo himself).

Invisible You (2019, Ghost Train Records) is his most recent documented solo release on a label. It was produced, recorded, mixed, and mastered by Lindsay Gravina, with engineering assistance from Brenton Conlan — Gravina’s longtime role as Shilo’s main production collaborator is evident across multiple projects.

Session and Collaborative Work

Outside his own projects, Shilo has played as a sideman and session contributor across the Melbourne and broader Australian underground:

  • Rowland S. Howard — Pop Crimes (2009, Liberation Music): Shilo played bass on several tracks and contributed guitar, violin, and “General Strangeness.” Also co-credited with “The Golden Age Of Bloodshed” (2010, Liberation Music), where he handled guitar, violin, production, mixing, and slide guitar. Howard died in December 2009; “The Golden Age Of Bloodshed” was a posthumous collection.
  • Mick Harvey — Sketches From The Book Of The Dead (2011, Mute) and Four (Acts Of Love) (2013, Mute): Shilo played accordion, guitar, and violin on both records. Harvey is another Melbourne underground elder (Birthday Party, Bad Seeds).
  • Amaya Laucirica — Rituals (2018, Opposite Number): electric guitar, autoharp, vocals. Laucirica is a Spanish-Australian artist.
  • The Drones (2) (via Braindrops, 2019, Joyful Noise Recordings): violin, instruments credited as “Happy Apples,” nylon string “Chalk Guitar,” and “Soviet Space Organ.”
  • David McComb tribute — Truckload Of Sky: The Lost Songs Of David McComb Vol 1 (2020, Lost Records And Tapes): credited as a performer “The Band” — connecting directly back to the Triffids/Blackeyed Susans lineage.
  • Mick Harvey (again) — Waves Of Anzac / The Journey (2020, Mute) and Phantasmagoria In Blue (2023, Mute): acoustic guitar and violin.
  • Mick Harvey — Five Ways To Say Goodbye (2024, Mute): looped guitar.

There is also a 2014 compilation Six Strings That Drew Blood (Liberation Music) where Shilo served as Music Consultant, and a 2019 release The Wisdom Line (Not On Label) with a guitar credit.

The Ensemble Of Shades

The Ensemble Of Shades is a duo listed in Discogs as a group of which Shilo is a member, alongside Zac Keiller (Discogs ID: 449329). The group has no releases on file in Discogs as primary artist, so no further discographic detail is available.

Blackeyed Susans (2017)

Shilo joined the Blackeyed Susans as a full credited member for Close Your Eyes And See (2017, Teardrop (2), BS699), the band’s final studio album. He is credited with guitar, violin, and backing vocals. The album was produced and mixed by Dan Luscombe, with Brenton Conlan recording. The core lineup for the album was Rob Snarski (vocals), Phil Kakulas (bass/guitar/keyboards), Mark Dawson (2) (drums), Evil Graham Lee (pedal steel/guitar), Kiernan Box (keyboards/harmonica), Dan Luscombe, and Shilo.
