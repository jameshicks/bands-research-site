Stewart Lupton

Discogs ID: 696453 Real name: Stewart Lupton Origin: Washington DC, USA Born: c. 1974–1975 Died: May 27, 2018 (aged 43)

American singer/songwriter and guitarist. Vocalist and frontman of Jonathan Fire*Eater (1993–1998), the most prominent DC/NYC indie band of the mid-1990s hype cycle. After the band’s dissolution he led two further projects: The Beatings (5) (with Carole Wagner Greenwood) and The Child Ballads. Died May 27, 2018. The JFE catalog was reissued on Third Man Records the following year.

Biography

Jonathan Fire*Eater (1993–1998)

Lupton formed Jonathan Fire*Eater in Washington DC in 1993 alongside bassist Thomas Frank, guitarist Paul Maroon, drummer Matt Barrick, and keyboardist/organist Walter Martin. The band quickly relocated to New York City and became one of the most-hyped underground acts of the era — a swaggering, organ-driven post-punk five-piece whose theatrical energy generated intense press attention.

Their recorded output began with a self-titled album on the DC micro-label Third World Underground (1995) and two singles on PCP Entertainment and Deceptive. The UK’s Deceptive Records became their primary release partner for the EP Tremble Under Boom Lights (1996) — a six-track statement recorded with John Siket (engineer/mixer) and Bil Emmons (basic tracks) that established their sound internationally.

The signing to DreamWorks Records for the full-length Wolf Songs For Lambs (1997) was unusual for an American underground act of this profile; the album was recorded by Jim Waters and mastered by Howie Weinberg. Its commercial underperformance led directly to the band’s breakup in 1998.

Three members — Barrick, Maroon, and Martin — went on to form The Walkmen. Bassist Thomas Frank (5) did not continue with them. Lupton’s path diverged entirely from the rest of the band.

The Child Ballads (mid-2000s)

By the mid-2000s Lupton was fronting The Child Ballads, a six-piece outfit that included Judah Bauer (lead guitarist — also of The Jon Spencer Blues Explosion), Betsy Wright (vocals, viola, keyboards), Jesse Rivkin (drums), John Melville, and Luke Wyatt. The band released a single EP: Cheekbone Hollows (Pop 1 / 2 Life) (2006, Loog), a UK-released six-track effort. Lupton is credited on vocals and acoustic guitar throughout; Bauer plays lead guitar on tracks 1 and 5.

The Beatings (5) (c. 2007–2010)

The Beatings (5) was a duo (or small ensemble) with Carole Wagner Greenwood, who contributed vocals, marimba, piano, xylophone, organ, electric upright bass, and additional recordings. The project released two records:

  • A Little Give And Take (2009, Civilian Art Projects) — Lupton played and produced nearly everything himself: vocals, rhythm guitar, lead guitar, acoustic guitar, electric upright bass, electric bass, and harmonica; he also mixed the album. Additional contributors included Brendan Canty (additional recordings), Cynthia Connolly (banjo on A1), Jesse Rivkin (drums), Paul Thornley (lead guitar on A1 and B1), and Justin Long (engineer/mastering/mixing). Photography by Colby D. Caldwell.
  • Edward (2010, self-released on “Not On Label”) — a smaller release; Lupton on guitar, Carole Wagner Greenwood on acoustic bass and vocals, and Ryan Hunter Mitchell on vocals, banjo, and guitar.

A Little Give And Take is the most fully realized document of Lupton’s post-JFE solo work, credited under the Beatings name but essentially a solo production.

Death (2018)

Lupton died on May 27, 2018, aged 43. Cause of death is not documented in the Discogs database. The following year, Third Man Records reissued both the Tremble Under Boom Lights EP (TMR-576, with five bonus tracks including early singles and the Lee Hazlewood cover “When Prince Was A Kid”) and Wolf Songs For Lambs (TMR-612).
