Amy Denio

Discogs ID: 270152
Real name: Amy Eliott Denio
Alias: Ya Mid One
Based: Seattle, Washington, USA
Label: Spoot Music (own imprint; Discogs ID: 91136)

Composer, singer, multi-instrumentalist, audio engineer, and international collaborator. Main instruments: voice, accordion, saxophones (alto, baritone), clarinet, electric guitar, bass. Founder of her own record label and publishing company, Spoot Music. Active from the mid-1980s across avant-garde, free improvisation, Balkan-inflected folk-rock, and electroacoustic music, with an unusually wide collaborative footprint spanning the US, Europe, and beyond.

Early Work and Solo Origins (1984–1991)

Denio’s earliest documented release is Spootones 1984 (1984, Not On Label), a self-produced cassette. No Bones (1986, Not On Label) followed as a further self-released recording. Both are credited as conceived, arranged, performed, and recorded by Denio alone — establishing the self-sufficient approach that defines her solo catalogue.

Her first label releases appeared on the German cassette imprint SHM Tapes: Never Too Old To Pop A Hole (1988) and No Elevators (1988). No Elevators in particular bears the full weight of Denio’s production philosophy: credited “Music By [Conceived], Performer, Edited By [Reformed], Recorded By, Mixed By, Producer [Produced By], Other [Abused By, Packaged, And Intended By].” The LP is a solo multi-track work of free improvisation and experimental composition, spanning jazz, electronic, and free improv styles.

Birthing Chair Blues (1991, Knitting Factory Works / Spoot Music, cat# KFWCD-111) was her breakthrough release on a distributed label. Entirely written, performed, produced, and engineered by Denio — playing fretted and fretless bass, electric guitar, slide guitar, voice, alto sax, harmonica, alto recorder, bass mbira, organ, and drum machine. One track (“BUNT”) has lyrics by Alma Larsen; all others by Denio. Ben Goldfarb engineered two tracks. The album spans an eclectic range of song styles with a DIY home-recording sensibility.

Tone Dogs (1988–1992)

Tone Dogs was a Seattle-based group featuring Denio alongside Fred Chalenor (bass, guitars, keyboards, voice), Matt Cameron (drums — later of Soundgarden / Pearl Jam), and Will Dowd. Their album Ankety Low Day (1990, Z Records, CZ016) was their main release, with notable guests Fred Frith (guitars, violin) and Hans Reichel (handmade guitars, daxophone). Engineering by Drew Canulette. A second release, The Early Middle Years (1991, Soleilmoon Recordings), followed.

A Beautiful Western Saddle (1993, Cuneiform)

Co-credited primary artists: Curlew with Amy Denio (Lyrics By) and Paul Haines. Denio sings on the album and is credited as composer of the music for track 7 specifically; all other music is composed by George Cartwright (tracks 1, 3–5, 9, 10, 12, 14), Tom Cora (tracks 3, 6, 8, 13), Ann Rupel (track 2), and Davey Williams (track 11). Paul Haines supplies lyrics throughout; Denio supplies lyrics as well (her specific lyric contributions are not separated track-by-track in the Discogs credit).

Produced by Bob Appel. Mixed by Hugo Dwyer (assisted by Scott Gilfix). Recorded by Jon Rosenberg. Mastered by Roger Seibel. Digital editing by Ted Orr. Cover design by Ruth Peyser.

Collaborative Projects and Groups

Denio has sustained membership in numerous groups across her career. Key ones documented on Discogs:

The Billy Tipton Memorial Saxophone Quartet / The Tiptons

Discogs ID: 821896. All-saxophone ensemble named in tribute to jazz musician Billy Tipton. Also known as The Tiptons Sax Quartet (and variants). Members include Denio, Jessica Lurie, Barbara Marino, Sue Orfield, Annelise Zamula, Marjorie De Muynck, Maya Johnson, Tina Richerson, Tobi Stone. A long-running Seattle institution.

Tone Dogs

See above. Seattle quartet with Matt Cameron pre-Soundgarden. Discogs ID: 196585.

Kultur Shock

Discogs ID: 340245. Seattle-based band fusing Balkan music with punk, metal, and experimental rock. Members include Denio plus Srđan Jevđević (also credited as Gino Jevđević), Matty Noble, Paris Hurley, Chris Stromquist, and others. Releases on Koolarrow Records (Bill Laswell’s label): Fucc The I.N.S. (2001), Kultura-Diktatura (2004), Ministry Of Kultur (2011), IX (2014).

Voice & Electronics (2002, Spoot Music)

Solo CDr — loops, voice processing, and electronic composition. Seven tracks including “Epic Loop,” “Dreamy Loop” (9:33), “Rhythmic Pingpong,” “English As A Second Language.”

Pandemonium (2021, Spoot Music)

16-track album composed, performed, recorded, produced, and mastered by Denio. Instruments: voice, accordion, clarinet, alto saxophone, electric guitar, 12-string acoustic guitar, electric bass, baglamadita (upcycled baglama), American Tourister briefcase. Guest contributions: Matty Noble (co-composer, algorithm-created drums/bass/violin on track 8), Chris Littlefield (flugelhorn, trumpet, trombone, bass trombone on track 14), Gino Jevdjevic (trash percussion), Nonda Trimis (surdo, pandeiro, tamborim on track 5), Jon-Paul Pachenker (guiro/fish on track 5). Mastered jointly by Denio and Steve Turnidge. Artwork by Anne Marie Grgich.

The track titles name cities (Detroit, Dublin, Colorado Springs, Trieste, etc.) with numerical codes, likely referencing pandemic statistics.

Varieté (2024, Spoot Music)

20-track soundtrack commissioned by Seattle Theater Group. Denio composed, produced, played electric guitar, accordion, and voice. Mixed by Denio and Floyd Reitsma; recorded by Reitsma. Mastered by Steve Turnidge. Conducted by Chelsea Natseway. Ensemble cast: Lamar Lofton (bass), David Wall (7) (bassoon), Gabriela Garza (cajón), Rachael Beaver (cello), Eric Likkel (clarinet), Matt Anderson (15) (horn), Janet Putnam (2) (oboe, English horn), Marina Albero (psaltery), Brianna Atwell (viola), Matty Noble (violin), Rachel Nesvig (violin, Hardanger fiddle). Artwork by Anne Marie Grgich.
