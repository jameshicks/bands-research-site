Manuel Göttsching

German guitarist, composer, and electronic musician. Born 9 September 1952, Berlin; died 4 December 2022, Berlin. Discogs artist ID: 29855.

Overview

Göttsching was the co-founder and primary creative force of Ash Ra Tempel (1970–1976), one of the founding texts of Berlin School kosmische music and krautrock. When that project dissolved he reconstituted it as Ashra (1977–1980s), which brought in a rotating cast including Harald Grosskopf and Lutz Ulbrich. His solo recordings — particularly Inventions For Electric Guitar (1974) and E2-E4 (recorded 1981, released 1984) — became foundational to ambient and electronic dance music. E2-E4, a single hour-long piece of sequenced synthesizer recorded in one improvised take, was influential on Chicago house and Detroit techno producers and is one of the most discussed proto-electronic records of the 20th century.

He also founded his own label, MG.ART (Discogs label ID: 20277), through which he released much of his later work.

Biography

Early Years: Steeple Chase Bluesband (late 1960s)

Göttsching began playing music in Berlin in his teens. In the late 1960s he played in the Steeple Chase Bluesband (Discogs ID: 207837) alongside Hartmut Enke, an early partnership that would carry directly into Ash Ra Tempel. Klaus Schulze also shared roots in this Berlin rock underground through his early stint in Tangerine Dream.

Ash Ra Tempel (1970–1976)

Ash Ra Tempel was formed in 1970 by Göttsching (guitar, electronics, vocals), Schulze (drums, electronics), and Enke (bass). Their debut album Ash Ra Tempel (Ohr, 1971) — produced by James McRiff and engineered by Conny Plank — featured two extended side-long improvisations (“Amboss” and “Traummaschine”), setting a template of free-form space rock with electronics. Schulze departed after Join Inn (1973), and Enke effectively withdrew from active music after the same period.

From Starring Rosi (1973) onward, Göttsching was the dominant force, surrounded by shifting collaborators. Starring Rosi featured singer/harpist Rosi Müller and drummer Harald Grosskopf — both returning figures. Seven Up (1973) was a collaboration with LSD advocate Timothy Leary, co-credited to Leary and Ash Ra Tempel, with lyrics by Brian Barritt and Leary; Schulze and Enke did not appear on this record.

New Age Of Earth (1976, Isadora / RCA) was the pivot point: credited jointly to Ash Ra Tempel and Manuel Göttsching, it is effectively a solo album — Göttsching played and arranged everything himself (guitar, keyboards, synthesizer). It launched the ambient Berlin-School sound that defined Ashra.

All early ART albums were produced or supervised by Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser for Kosmische Musik / Ohr. Engineers notably included Dieter Dierks.

Live archival releases document early ART performances: Bern, Switzerland (September 10, 1971) and Akademie Der Künste, Berlin, 1971 (Anökumena, 2014). The film soundtrack Le Berceau De Cristal (Spalax, 1993) was an ART-credited release from a 1975 Philippe Garrel film.

The Cosmic Jokers (1973–1974)

Göttsching participated in sessions at Dieter Dierks’ studio that were assembled into the The Cosmic Jokers albums released on Kosmische Musik in 1974. These sessions also involved Schulze, Grosskopf, keyboard player Jürgen Dollase, and Dierks. The releases — The Cosmic Jokers, Galactic Supermarket, Planeten Sit-In, Sci Fi Party, and Starring In Tarot — were controversial in that the participating musicians were reportedly unaware the jams were being prepared for commercial release by Kaiser and Dierks. Starring In Tarot (1975, Cosmic Music) appeared under Göttsching’s name as a solo release in some pressings.

Ashra (1977–1982)

After ART’s dissolution, Göttsching relaunched as Ashra, working with a more stable lineup: Harald Grosskopf (drums), Lutz Ulbrich (also known as Lüül, guitar/keyboards), and Steve Baltes. The Ashra era was on Virgin Records internationally.

Blackouts (Virgin, 1977) was the debut Ashra album. Correlations (Virgin, 1979) featured co-writing from Grosskopf and Ulbrich and was co-produced with Mick Glossop. Belle Alliance (Virgin, 1980) was the last Ashra studio album, a fully collaborative band effort with Grosskopf and Ulbrich sharing writing credits. The project went dormant through the early 1980s before Göttsching pivoted to solo work.

Later archival Ashra releases include Walkin’ The Desert and Tropical Heat (Navigator, 1989/1991) and the retrospective Sunrain (The Virgin Years) (Virgin, 1996). A 1997 live recording was eventually released as Live At The Open Air Festival Herzberg 1997 (TempelRecords, 2001).

Solo Work and E2-E4 (1981–1984)

Göttsching’s landmark E2-E4 was recorded on 2 December 1981 in a single improvised session, using sequencers, synthesizers, guitar, and electronics. The track title refers to the first move in a chess game, and the individual track titles on the album reference chess terminology. The original 1984 release was on Inteam GmbH (catalog number ID 20.004), not “Private Records” as sometimes cited. The album was credited entirely to Göttsching as composer, producer, and performer (Guitar, Electronics).

The record received limited attention on its 1984 release but gained enormous retroactive recognition after being sampled and cited by house and techno producers in the late 1980s and 1990s. It has since been reissued extensively (31+ pressing variants on Discogs).

In the early 1980s Göttsching also appeared as a guest guitarist on Klaus Schulze’s alias Richard Wahnfried’s album Tonwelle (Innovative Communication, 1981), alongside Schulze himself (keyboards) and Michael Shrieve (percussion). He also appeared on Schulze’s Zwischenmischung (1982).

MG.ART Label and Later Period (1984–2022)

Göttsching founded his own label MG.ART (Discogs ID: 20277) in the 1980s, through which he released Die Mulde (2005), Joaquin Joe Claussell Meets Manuel Göttsching (2006), Live At Mount Fuji (2007), Blackouts (2008 reissue), Live In Melbourne (2017), and a number of other titles.

The extensive Private Tapes series (Manikin Records, 1996) — six volumes plus a best-of — collects home recordings and studio works from across his career. These were also released as an omnibus The Private Tapes set.

He continued performing E2-E4 live in later years: Performing E2 E4 (DFC, 2015) documents one such concert performance. Volcano Extravaganza (The Vinyl Factory, 2016) was a live recording. Live In Melbourne (MG.ART, 2017) captured his Australian performances.

Göttsching died in Berlin on 4 December 2022.
