Scott Solter

Record producer, mixer, engineer, and session performer. Based in San Francisco / Bay Area. Discogs artist ID: 207793. Website: boxharp.com.

Name variations in credits: Scott (Celtar) Solter; Solter; Solter, Scott.

Overview

Scott Solter is a Bay Area-rooted producer, engineer, and mixer whose career spans the late 1990s through the 2020s. He emerged from the San Francisco post-rock scene — early credits on Tarentel’s Temporary Residence releases — and built a long-running partnership with John Vanderslice that ran through five albums across Barsuk Records, anchoring Vanderslice’s studio sound at Tiny Telephone from the early 2000s onward. He is also the primary behind-the-boards figure for The Mountain Goats through their mid-career transition from 4AD to Merge Records, contributing from The Sunset Tree (2005) through Goths (2017).

Solter is a member of two groups that served as vehicles for his own compositional and ambient work: Boxharp (a large Bay Area collective including members of The Court and Spark, among others) and The Balustrade Ensemble (an ambient/chamber ensemble led by Grant Miller). He has also released solo material under his own name.

Groups

  • Boxharp (Discogs ID: 1435460) — Bay Area collective; members include M.C. Taylor, Scott Hirsch, Wendy Allen, Pat Campbell, Tom Heyman, Carrie Bradley, Zach Gill, and Patrick Main — overlapping heavily with The Court And Spark
  • The Balustrade Ensemble (Discogs ID: 957759) — ambient/chamber ensemble; members: Grant Miller (2), Liam Singer, Matt Henry Cunitz, Rich Vaughan, Ryan Rosenberg, Wendy Allen, Scott Solter

John Vanderslice Partnership

Solter’s most sustained single-artist partnership. He engineered, mixed, and/or produced five consecutive Vanderslice studio albums and a remix EP:

On Pixel Revolt, Solter received a co-Music By credit alongside Vanderslice. On Emerald City, he was credited both as Performer/Arranged By and as producer. John Darnielle contributed lyric editing to Pixel Revolt, making it a three-way creative intersection. John Vanderslice operates Tiny Telephone studio in San Francisco, where much of this work was recorded.
