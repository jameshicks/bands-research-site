Martin Hannett

Role: Record producer, sound engineer, bassist, songwriter Born: 31 May 1948, Manchester Died: 18 April 1991, Manchester (heart failure, age 42) Real name: James Martin Hannett Also known as: Martin Zero (alias, Discogs ID 227772; used on early Factory releases and Rabid Records work) Discogs ID: 80561 | Alias ID: 227772 Groups: The Invisible Girls (277394), The Curious Yellows (3392156)

Biography

Pre-Factory: Rabid Records and the Manchester Scene (1975–1978)

Hannett’s production career began in the mid-1970s on the Manchester independent label Rabid Records, where he worked with a cluster of local acts. His earliest credits (as Martin Zero) include the Buzzcocks’ debut EP Spiral Scratch (New Hormones, 1977), one of the founding documents of British punk. On Rabid he produced Slaughter and the Dogs’ Cranked Up Really High (1977) and John Cooper Clarke’s first records — the Innocents E.P. (1977), Disguise In Love (CBS, 1978), and Snap, Crackle & Bop (Epic, 1980) — where he also played bass, programmed synthesizers, and took writing credits. He co-wrote with Clarke and performed as a member of The Invisible Girls, the backing group assembled for Clarke’s studio and live work.

The Martin Zero pseudonym was his working name through this period. Discogs lists it as a separate artist entry (ID 227772) cross-linked to Martin Hannett. Credits under Zero include the early OMD single “Electricity” (Dindisc, 1979) and the two Joy Division tracks on A Factory Sample (FAC-2, 1979).

The Factory Sound: In-House Producer (1978–1981)

Tony Wilson and Factory Records gave Hannett near-total creative latitude over the label’s sound from its first release onward. He produced almost every Factory act through 1981: Joy Division, New Order, A Certain Ratio, Section 25, The Durutti Column, and OMD. His production approach — heavy use of digital delay (AMS units), extreme separation of drum elements (each drum and cymbal treated individually), large amounts of reverb and space, and the blending of live drums with drum machines — defined the austere, cavernous quality that became synonymous with the label.

Key studios: Strawberry Studios, Manchester; Cargo Studios, Rochdale; The Townhouse, London (for Movement and Bummed).

Outside Factory he continued to work broadly: Magazine (The Correct Use of Soap, Virgin, 1980), Buzzcocks (“Strange Thing” / “Are Everything”, United Artists, 1980), The Psychedelic Furs (Columbia, 1980), and U2’s first single “11 O’Clock Tick Tock” (Island, 1980) — one of his most commercially significant outside credits.

His relationship with Tony Wilson and the other Factory directors deteriorated over finances. Hannett held a 20% stake in Factory Communications Ltd. alongside Wilson, Alan Erasmus, Rob Gretton, and Peter Saville. The breaking point was the £50,000 commitment to The Haçienda (FAC 51) made without his knowledge — he had wanted a Fairlight synthesizer (£30,000) instead. He was also effectively frozen out of producing for Factory from August 1981.

FAC 61 — FCL vs Hannett (filed April 1982): Hannett petitioned to either wind up Factory Communications Ltd. and distribute assets proportionately, or have his shares bought out at a fair price. His specific claims included: a £22,000 share purchase from Peter Saville’s stake recorded as “directors’ fees” without his knowledge; £13,000 spent on video equipment at Wilson’s home; a £10,000 advance to a record company (“M.V.S.”) without proper documentation; being excluded from directors’ meetings; and Rob Gretton’s dual role as FCL director and New Order/Joy Division manager creating a conflict of interest. Factory gave the lawsuit its own FAC number in their cataloguing system.

The case settled out of court in January 1984 — a £40,000 payment to Hannett; he stepped down as director. He subsequently spent most of the settlement on heroin.

Outside and Decline (1982–1987)

Post-Factory, Hannett produced Cabaret Voltaire and worked with acts on Les Disques du Crépuscule and other European labels. His drug use — primarily heroin — worsened through the mid-1980s and his output became sporadic. He produced occasional records including a session with ESG (1981, 99 Records) and Pauline Murray and the Invisible Girls (Illusive, 1980), the latter as a performer and writer rather than purely a producer.

Return to Factory and Death (1988–1991)

Wilson brought Hannett back to Factory to produce Happy Mondays’ second album Bummed (FACT 220, 1988), recorded at Strawberry Studios and The Slaughterhouse. The record is sonically rawer than his Joy Division work but retains his characteristic use of space and drum treatment. He also produced the Wrote For Luck single (FAC 242, 1988) and contributed to subsequent Mondays releases.

Hannett died on 18 April 1991 of heart failure in Manchester, aged 42. Factory issued the compilation Martin (FAC 307, 1991) shortly after his death, collecting key productions. A fuller retrospective, Zero (A Martin Hannett Story 1977–1991) (Big Beat, 2006), was issued posthumously and is the primary Discogs entry where he appears as primary artist.

The Invisible Girls

Hannett’s backing group for John Cooper Clarke, functioning as a studio band. Members shifted but included Steve Hopkins on keyboards and various Manchester session players. Hannett played bass and contributed synth programming. The group also performed on Pauline Murray’s 1980 album. The Curious Yellows (Discogs ID 3392156) is another group Hannett is listed as a member of.
