ANOHNI

Vocalist, composer, visual artist. Born 1971, Chichester, West Sussex, England. Based New York City. Discogs primary ID: 268047 (as “Antony (3)”). Also indexed as Antony Hegarty (ID: 462766) and Anohni (ID: 4792245). Group IDs: Antony And The Johnsons (185134); Anohni And The Johnsons (13220847).

Trans artist and one of the most distinctive voices of the 2000s–2020s. Began performing as Antony Hegarty / Antony and the Johnsons in New York in the 1990s; Mercury Prize winner 2005 for I Am A Bird Now. Adopted the name ANOHNI for solo work from 2015 onward; revived the Johnsons collective name in 2023 as Anohni and the Johnsons.

Overview

ANOHNI grew up in England and the Netherlands, moving to New York City as a young adult. In the early 1990s they became embedded in the downtown Manhattan performance and cabaret scene, performing at venues including the Pyramid Club and working within a community that included drag performers, experimental composers, and artists in the orbit of the Warhol legacy. The debut recordings appeared on David Tibet’s Durtro label (ID: 4572), a UK imprint associated with Current 93 and esoteric / freak-folk adjacencies: the self-titled album Antony And The Johnsons (2000) and the EP I Fell In Love With A Dead Boy (2001) were released in editions of a few hundred copies.

The project’s aesthetic — ANOHNI’s high, wavering voice over spare piano and chamber arrangements — attracted attention in the downtown New York experimental community. Early collaborators included the cellist Julia Kent, arranger Doug Wieselman, and drummer Todd Cohen. The first album also featured William Basinski (clarinet) and Baby Dee (2) (harp).

US distribution came through Secretly Canadian (ID: 35538) starting in 2004 with The Lake EP and I Am A Bird Now. The latter became the pivotal release: released on Secretly Canadian in the US and licensed to Rough Trade (ID: 51167) for the UK, it won the 2005 Mercury Prize and placed ANOHNI internationally. Featured guests on the album included Lou Reed, Rufus Wainwright, Boy George, and Devendra Banhart.

Following international recognition, ANOHNI was drawn into collaborations with peers across pop, avant-garde, and experimental music. They appeared on Björk’s Volta (2007) and Vulnicura (2015), on Lou Reed’s The Raven (2003) and Animal Serenade live album (2004), and on Hercules & Love Affair’s debut single “Blind” (2008, DFA Records). They contributed to Hal Willner’s large-scale productions and to Rufus Wainwright’s Want Two (2004).

Albums as Antony And The Johnsons continued: The Crying Light (2009, Secretly Canadian), Swanlights (2011, Secretly Canadian), and the live orchestral set Cut The World (2012). The project also produced a film/performance work, Turning (2014), released on Rough Trade.

Around 2015, ANOHNI began releasing work under their own name, adopting the spelling ANOHNI as a new identity. The debut solo album Hopelessness (2016, Secretly Canadian / Rough Trade) was produced in collaboration with Oneohtrix Point Never (Daniel Lopatin, Discogs ID: 1191704) and Hudson Mohawke (Ross Birchard, Discogs ID: 812652), marking a sharp shift toward electronic beats, political anger, and drone — addressing drone warfare, surveillance, environmental collapse, and capital. The Paradise EP followed in 2017. In 2023, ANOHNI revived the Johnsons name for My Back Was A Bridge For You To Cross (Anohni And The Johnsons), a return to the orchestral chamber-pop register co-produced with Jimmy Hogarth and featuring William Basinski on saxophone.

ANOHNI is also known as a visual artist and has collaborated with filmmaker and performance artist Charles Atlas (2) (Discogs ID: 816340) on multiple video and installation works.
