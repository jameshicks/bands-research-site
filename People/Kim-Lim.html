Kim Lim

Kim Lim

Born: 16 February 1936, Singapore Died: 23 October 1997, London (aged 61) Occupation: Sculptor, printmaker Connection to this network: Mother of Johnny and Alex Turnbull of 23 Skidoo

Biography

Born in Singapore of Chinese descent; spent early childhood in Penang and Malacca. Trained in ballet as a teenager. Moved to London aged 17/18 in 1954 — part of the first generation of post-WWII Asian diaspora artists emigrating to the UK.

Studied at:

  • St. Martin’s School of Art (1954–56)
  • Slade School of Fine Art (graduating 1960)

Married sculptor William Turnbull in 1960. They traveled extensively together (China, Indonesia, Cambodia, Egypt, Malaysia, Turkey). Alex and Johnny Turnbull (23 Skidoo) are their sons. She died in London in 1997; her estate is now managed by her sons.

Artistic Practice

Kim Lim worked simultaneously in sculpture, printmaking, and drawing across three phases:

  • 1960s–70s: Abstract carved wood sculptures exploring “the relationship between art and nature” — vertical, architectonic forms influenced by Brancusi. Also worked with industrial materials: blockboard, steel, aluminium, fibreglass with industrial paint. Serial, modular approaches treating light, space, and rhythm as sculptural materials.
  • 1980s onward: Transition to stone and marble carving; organic forms, more metaphorical content.

Her aesthetic drew on both European and Asian visual traditions — a genuinely transcultural practice at a time when that was rare in British institutional art.

Significance — Why She Was Overlooked

Kim Lim was a significant but largely marginalized figure in postwar British sculpture:

  • In the mid-1960s she appeared on British television to discuss her monumental steel sculpture Day, displayed in Battersea Park alongside works by Henry Moore, Barbara Hepworth, and Anthony Caro.
  • In 1977 she was the only female and only non-white artist featured in the Hayward Annual at the Hayward Gallery.
  • Close associates — and her own sons — contested any assumption that William Turnbull’s career dominated hers; the creative exchange was mutual.

She has been extensively re-evaluated recently:

  • Tate Britain acquisition and display (2020–21)
  • Hepworth Wakefield — Kim Lim: Space, Rhythm & Light (2023–24), first major museum exhibition since 1999
  • National Gallery Singapore — Kim Lim: The Space Between. A Retrospective (from September 2024), 150+ works; described as “the first rigorous attempt to reposition Lim as a major figure in 20th-century sculpture and printmaking”

Connection to 23 Skidoo

No direct documented music connection beyond being the mother of Alex and Johnny Turnbull. The household’s diverse record collection — “spanning jazz, ethnic, and classical music” — is cited by both brothers as formative. The transcultural dimension of Kim Lim’s practice likely fed directly into 23 Skidoo’s own incorporation of non-Western musics.
