Byard Lancaster

Discogs ID: 18142 Born: August 6, 1942, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania Died: August 23, 2012, Wyndmoor, Pennsylvania Real name: William Byard Lancaster Instruments: Alto saxophone, bass clarinet, soprano saxophone, tenor saxophone, baritone saxophone, flute (all registers), piccolo flute, trumpet, piano, electronics (Octavoice), vocals

Overview

Byard Lancaster was a Philadelphia-born multi-reedist and flautist working across free jazz, avant-garde improvisation, and Black spiritual music for five decades. He came up in the New York free scene in the mid-1960s, appearing on foundational ESP-Disk’ and RCA Victor sessions before establishing himself as a prolific bandleader across his own recordings for Vortex, Dogtown, and the French label Palm in the 1970s. Lancaster spent formative years working in Paris in a collaborative alliance with French producer Jef Gilson, cutting several albums in the 1973–74 period that are now considered rare-groove touchstones. Returning to Philadelphia in the late 1970s and 1980s, he continued leading groups and recording under self-run imprints before a late-career resurgence on Black Fire Records in the 1990s. He died in 2012 in Wyndmoor, Pennsylvania, near Philadelphia.

His Discogs name variations include “Byard ‘Thunderbird’ Lancaster,” “Sunny Byard,” and “Philly Sax,” testifying to the range of contexts — spiritual, funk, jazz — he inhabited.

Early Career: New York Free Scene (1965–1968)

Lancaster entered the recording world as a sideman on the New York avant-garde circuit. His first documented session was Sunny Murray’s self-titled ESP-Disk’ date (1966), on which he contributed alto saxophone alongside trumpeter Jacques Coursil and bassist Alan Silva.

First Leader Dates: It’s Not Up To Us (1968, Vortex)

Lancaster’s debut as a primary recording artist appeared on Atlantic’s Vortex subsidiary — an imprint that issued important avant-garde and jazz-rock crossover records in this period.

BYG Sessions: Paris Free Jazz (1970)

Lancaster traveled to France for the BYG Actuel sessions, appearing on Sunny Murray’s An Even Break (Never Give A Sucker) (1970, BYG Records), playing alto saxophone, soprano saxophone, bass clarinet, and flute in a quartet with Malachi Favors on bass and Kenneth Terroade on tenor saxophone and flute.

Sounds Of Liberation: Philadelphia Community Music (1970–1972)

Back in Philadelphia, Lancaster was a founding member of Sounds Of Liberation, a politically engaged free-jazz collective rooted in the Germantown and Mt. Airy neighborhoods. The group was both a band and a social movement. Its recorded output appeared on the small local label Dogtown Records.

Their release New Horizons (1972, Dogtown Records) was co-credited to the group and co-produced by the band.

Around the same time, Lancaster was co-leading sessions with drummer J.R. Mitchell, resulting in Live At Macalester College 72 (Dogtown Records), captured live in Minnesota. The record documents Lancaster’s multi-instrumentalism — he plays alto and tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, bass clarinet, trumpet, and flute across the concert.

Palm Records / France: Core Recordings (1973–1979)

Lancaster’s most celebrated body of work as a bandleader came during his extended collaboration with French pianist and producer Jef Gilson and the Palm label in the 1973–74 period. Three albums were recorded in France, released on Palm in 1974:

  • Exactement (with Keno Speller) — a largely solo and duo recording featuring Lancaster’s multi-layered compositions on alto, soprano, bass clarinet, piano, flute, and Octavoice electronics
  • Mother Africa (with Clint Jackson III) — co-led with trumpeter Clint Jackson III; engineered by Jef Gilson
  • Us — further collaborative recordings on the Palm imprint

Philadelphia Recordings (1980–1981)

Back in Philadelphia, Lancaster documented a decade of work on two self-released albums:

Documentation: The End Of A Decade (1980, Bellows Records)

Lancaster’s own label Bellows Records issued this summary of his late-1970s activity, with Lancaster serving as producer and playing alto sax, soprano sax, flute, and bass clarinet. The ensemble included cellist David Eyges, Michael Ray on trumpet, and Joan Hansom on vocals.

Personal Testimony Vol. I (1980, Concert Artists)

A solo overdub recording on which Lancaster plays all instruments — alto sax, soprano sax, bass clarinet, flute, piccolo flute, piano, and vocals — functioning as music director and composer. Michael Cuscuna wrote the liner notes.

Later Career (1981–2012)

Lancaster continued recording and performing through the 1980s and 1990s. Significant later credits include:

  • Eye On You (1980, About Time Records) — with unspecified ensemble; alto and soprano sax
  • Copeland Special (1981, Demon Records) — with the Ronald Shannon Jackson And The Decoding Society; alto and tenor sax
  • Coup De Tête (1981, American Clavé) — flute and wooden flute, tenor sax
  • Spaces (1982, Idibib) — alto sax and flute
  • Infinity (1984, Jambrio) — alto sax and flute
  • Thinking Of You (1987, Storyville) — alto sax, flute, clarinet, bass clarinet
  • A Lover’s Question (1990, Les Disques Du Crépuscule) — saxophone, flute, percussion, voice
  • My Pure Joy (1992, Black Fire Records) — producer and composer, marking a return to Philadelphia recording
  • Arc Of The Testimony (1997, Axiom) — alto sax and bass clarinet
  • “A” Heavenly Sweetness (2005, Isma’a / Discograph) — final major leader session; alto sax, flute, vocals, composer

He also appeared on Bill Laswell’s Axiom label in 1997 (Arc Of The Testimony) and on Rashied Ali’s Postones, and contributed soprano saxophone to Van Morrison’s Nagual Site (1998, Wicklow).
