Roli Mosimann

Roli Mosimann

Oct 30, 20269 min read

Roli Mosimann

Discogs ID: 50643 Born: 7 November 1955, Thurgau, Switzerland Died: 15 September 2024, Wrocław, Poland Active: 1981–2024 Origin: Switzerland / New York City Roles: drummer, producer, engineer, photographer

Swiss-born drummer and record producer who played on the founding recordings of Swans before pivoting to become one of the most distinctive producers of the post-industrial/dance crossover era. His production credits span The Young Gods, The The, That Petrol Emotion, Faith No More, and Hair & Skin Trading Company. From around 2021 until his death in 2024 he lived in Wrocław, Poland.

Biography

Swiss Beginnings (1955–1982)

Roli Mosimann was born on 7 November 1955 in Thurgau, in northeastern Switzerland. His earliest documented credit is as drummer on a 1981 Swiss release, Drum Sing I Grad Drum, on Meteor Records — a record that placed him within Switzerland’s small but active experimental music scene at the turn of the decade.

By the early 1980s he had relocated to New York City, landing in the same downtown Manhattan world that generated No Wave and its aftershocks.

Swans Period (1982–1985)

Mosimann joined Swans — then a recently-formed no wave band led by Michael Gira — in May 1983, according to his Discogs profile. He was not the sole drummer on their debut album: the 1983 German pressing of Filth (Neutral Records / Zensor) credits both Mosimann and Jonathan Kane on drums, percussion, and tapes, alongside Norman Westberg on guitar and Harry Crosby on bass.

By the 1984 EP Cop (K.422), Mosimann was co-producing alongside Gira — a significant step that positioned him as a creative partner, not just a sideman. The same year, the Young God EP continued the Gira/Mosimann co-production pairing. Both were recorded partly in Zürich and partly in New York City, with J.G. Thirlwell among the engineers on Cop. These records mark the transition from Filth’s raw no wave assault toward the slower, more monolithic sound Swans would pursue through Greed and Holy Money — a transformation Mosimann helped engineer before leaving the band.

He is also credited on Swans’ Ten Songs For Another World (1990, Young God Records, a compilation of early material) and the Filth (L.P.#1, E.P.#1) 1982/83 retrospective, placing him firmly on the band’s foundational records.

Photography and NYC Scene (1983)

In parallel with his Swans work, Mosimann contributed cover photography to Sonic Youth’s debut album Confusion Is Sex (1983, Neutral Records). He is also credited for photography on Sonic Death (Sonic Youth Live) (1988, Blast First). These credits place him within the overlapping social world of early-1980s NYC avant-garde — the same network around Neutral Records that connected Swans, Sonic Youth, and related acts.

Wiseblood (1985–1991)

After leaving Swans, Mosimann formed the industrial/noise duo Wiseblood with JG Thirlwell (also credited as Clint Ruin). The collaboration produced three releases:

  • Motorslug (1985, Wax Trax! Records) — debut, engineered by Ian North and Steve Peck
  • Dirtdish (1987, K.422) — full LP; Mosimann co-wrote most tracks; sleeve designed by Thirlwell
  • P T T M (1991, Big Cat) — final release

Wiseblood occupied the harder end of the industrial/dance spectrum, occupying similar sonic territory to Throbbing Gristle and Cabaret Voltaire but with a particular heaviness that drew on Mosimann’s rhythmic background.

The Young Gods (1986–1995)

The most sustained production relationship of Mosimann’s career was with The Young Gods, the Swiss industrial/rock group built around singer Franz Treichler and sampler player Cesare Pizzi. Mosimann produced or co-produced virtually their entire catalog across nearly a decade:

  • Envoyé! (1986, Wax Trax! / Organik) — debut EP; engineered and produced
  • The Young Gods (1987, Product Inc.) — debut album; engineered and produced
  • L’Amourir (1988, Wax Trax!) — single
  • L’Eau Rouge – Red Water (1989, Play It Again Sam) — arranged and produced
  • T.V. Sky (1992, Play It Again Sam) — produced; also wrote music
  • The Young Gods Play Kurt Weill (1991, Play It Again Sam) — produced
  • Skinflowers (1991, Play It Again Sam) — produced; writing credit
  • Only Heaven (1995, Play It Again Sam / Interscope) — produced, recorded, mixed
  • XX Years 1985–2005 (2005, [PIAS]) — archival compiled, mixed

This body of work is considered among the most important in European industrial/alternative music. Mosimann’s production gave the Young Gods their characteristic density — the collision of metal-influenced dynamics with sampler-driven textures.

The The and UK Alternative (1986–1989)

Through the late 1980s Mosimann became embedded in the UK alternative mainstream. He contributed music and co-produced tracks on The The’s Infected album (1986, Epic) — an album that is one of the landmark records of British alternative pop — and continued with production and engineering roles on Mind Bomb (1989), where he produced tracks 3, 6, 7, and 8 alongside Matt Johnson and Warne Livesey. The Mind Bomb lineup included Johnny Marr on guitar.

He also produced That Petrol Emotion’s Babble (1987, Polydor) — the Irish post-punk group’s second album — and their singles “Big Decision” (1987) and “Cellophane” (1988), as well as their 1988 LP End Of The Millennium Psychosis Blues (Virgin).

Hair & Skin Trading Company (1992)

Mosimann produced both of the Hair & Skin Trading Company’s Situation Two albums: Jo In Nine G Hell (1992) and Ground Zero (1992). The Hair & Skin Trading Company were a UK noise/industrial group, and these records represent the extension of Mosimann’s industrial production aesthetic into the early 1990s.

23 Skidoo

Mosimann produced material for 23 Skidoo, the London-based avant-funk/industrial group: the Did You Miss Me? single (1987, Organik / Product Inc.) and other work in the same period.

Faith No More (1997)

Mosimann mixed and co-produced Faith No More’s Album of the Year (1997, Slash / London Records) alongside the band and Billy Gould. This was one of the more unexpected late-career collaborations, connecting him to the mainstream US alt-metal world.

Lydia Lunch (1985)

Mosimann engineered, alongside Thirlwell, the first side of Lydia Lunch and Lucy Hamilton’s The Drowning of Lucy Hamilton (1985, Widowspeak Productions).

Later Career (1993–2024)

Mosimann continued working as a producer and mixing engineer well into the 2000s and 2010s, primarily in Switzerland and Poland. Notable later work:

  • Butterfly Wings (1993, Atlantic/Mammoth) for Machines Of Loving Grace
  • Concentration (1993, Mammoth) — production
  • Vanity / Nemesis (1990, RCA/Noise) — Swans live album, producer
  • Recurring production work for Swiss acts through Qilin Records, Gig Ant, and related labels
  • Engineering and mixing credits on Polish experimental releases (Gig Ant label) through the 2010s
  • 50 (2022, Intakt Records) — produced, recorded, mixed, mastered

He died on 15 September 2024 in Wrocław, Poland, where he had been based since 2021.
