Overview

Richard Adams is a Leeds-based musician best known as one of the two core members of Hood, alongside his brother Chris Adams. His career spans from early 1990s lo-fi indie — first appearing on the small Fluff label in 1992–93 — through a peak period on Domino in the late 1990s and 2000s, and continuing into the mid-2020s across labels including Signal Records, Home Assembly Music, and Sound In Silence. Beyond Hood, he has worked under or within several other projects: Boyracer, Great Panoptique Winter, Memory Drawings, and Northern Exchange, with his styles running from post-rock and experimental to acoustic. His most consistent collaborators are The Declining Winter (61 shared releases), Andrew Johnson, Sarah Kemp, Antony Ryan, Richard Formby, and Craig Tattersall.

Notes (web)

Hood’s move from Fluff to Domino (late 1990s)

Hood’s early output — the “Sirens” and “Opening Into Enclosure” singles (1992–93) and the debut album Cabled Linear Traction (1994) — appeared on Fluff (2), a Leicester-based noise-pop operation run as a part-time concern. Fluff was typical of the micro-labels Hood used through the mid-1990s: owner-operated, small-run, and built on personal relationships rather than industry infrastructure.

The path to Domino ran through an intermediate figure and an aborted deal. A third album (eventually Rustic Houses, Forlorn Valleys) was recorded in the summer of 1996 and originally slated for release on Planet Records, a small label run by someone named Rick. According to a 1999 interview with Chris Adams, the project stalled at Planet. When Rick subsequently left Planet and took a role at Domino, he played Hood’s unreleased tape in the Domino office — and then called the band to say Domino would want to put it out instead. Chris Adams: “one night he phoned us up and said that Domino would want to do it instead.” (Luna Kafé interview with Chris Adams, c. 1999)

Crucially, Hood had not been actively seeking a larger deal. As Chris Adams told Luna Kafé: “We never ever sent out demos to people and we never really tried in that capacity.” The Domino signing was therefore not the result of a conventional campaign but of an insider introduction by a label person who already knew the band’s work. Domino released the single “Useless” in 1997, then Rustic Houses, Forlorn Valleys (1998) and The Cycle of Days and Seasons (1999), with Matt Elliott (of Third Eye Foundation) producing the latter two.

The scale shift was significant. Chris Adams described Domino as “very different from any label we’ve ever been on. They all were part-time concerns of the people who ran them.” (Luna Kafé) This framing applies directly to Fluff and the other micro-labels of the early period: they were side projects of their owners, whereas Domino operated as a full-time label business.

A c. 1996 Terrascope feature on Hood also corroborates that Rustic Houses was originally planned for Planet Records, consistent with the Luna Kafé account. (Terrascope, c. 1996)
