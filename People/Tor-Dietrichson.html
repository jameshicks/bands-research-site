Tor Dietrichson

Percussionist (tabla, congas, bongos, timbales, and assorted hand percussion), based in the US Pacific Northwest. Discogs artist ID: 1418152. Website: tordietrichson.com. Name variations on record: “Tor”, “Tor Deitrichson”.

Overview

Tor Dietrichson’s career spans five decades as a tabla specialist and percussionist working across remarkably diverse contexts — from 1970s Grateful Dead-orbit world-music projects to 1980s new age recordings, and into 2000s–2010s avant-rock and psychedelic sessions in the Pacific Northwest. His earliest documented credit is the 1976 Diga Rhythm Band album Diga, a landmark percussion ensemble album produced by Mickey Hart featuring Zakir Hussain. That record placed him in a high-profile world of fusion between North Indian classical percussion and rock/jazz sensibility early in his career.

His primary release as a leader is Global Village (1987, Global Pacific Records), a new age / world fusion album produced, arranged, engineered, and mixed by Steven Kindler. He co-led Spirit Of The Heart (1998, Flashback Records) with Kindler, Rebbe Soul, Endangered Species (10), and Bob Kindler — a long-standing working relationship with that crew.

From the mid-2000s onward his credits gravitate toward the Pacific Northwest experimental and psychedelic rock scenes. He contributed tabla to Six Organs Of Admittance’s Luminous Night (2009, Drag City), produced by Randall Dunn, and appeared as a guest tabla player on Master Musicians Of Bukkake’s Totem Three (2011, Important Records), also produced by Randall Dunn. He played congas and tabla on Midday Veil’s This Wilderness (2015, Beyond Beyond Is Beyond Records), again produced by Randall Dunn — making Dunn the recurring axis of his experimental-scene work. His most unusual credit is “Recorded By” on Stephen O’Malley’s Fuck Fundamentalist Pigs (2015, iDEAL Recordings), where he is credited as “Tor”.

He is or was a member of three groups: Diga Rhythm Band, Battlestar Kalakala, and Children Of The Revolution (2).

Scene Connections

Dietrichson bridges two distinct worlds:

1970s Grateful Dead / world-music orbit — Diga Rhythm Band was Mickey Hart’s percussion collective, and the 1976 Diga album brought together Zakir Hussain, Ray Spiegel, Jordan Amarantha, Aushim Chaudhuri, and others in an ambitious cross-cultural percussion project on Round Records (distributed via United Artists).

2000s–2010s Pacific Northwest experimental / psych — The Randall Dunn axis connects him to Six Organs Of Admittance (Ben Chasny), Master Musicians Of Bukkake, and Midday Veil (which also featured Bernie Worrell, Skerik, Eyvind Kang, Steve Moore (5)). This cluster is heavily interconnected around the Seattle experimental scene. The Totem Three session also featured Alan Bishop, Faith Coloccia, and Timb Harris.

New age / world fusion — the Global Village (1987) and Bridge Of Dreams (1991, with David Lanz and Paul Speer featuring the Seattle Symphony Orchestra) credits show a parallel career thread in the 1980s–90s new age market through Global Pacific Records and Narada Equinox.
