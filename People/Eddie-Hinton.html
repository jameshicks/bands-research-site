Eddie Hinton

Eddie Hinton

Aug 05, 202611 min read

Eddie Hinton

Discogs artist ID: 316562
Real name: Edward Craig Hinton
Born: June 15, 1944, Jacksonville, Florida
Died: July 28, 1995, Birmingham, Alabama

Guitarist, singer, and songwriter; an early member of the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section and one of the most prolific session guitarists of the Muscle Shoals recording scene from the mid-1960s through the mid-1970s. Also pursued a solo career as a blue-eyed soul singer and recording artist from the late 1970s onward. Known for co-writing “Breakfast In Bed” (covered by Dusty Springfield and later a UK hit for UB40 featuring Chrissie Hynde, 1988).

Biography

Origins and Muscle Shoals Entry (mid-1960s)

Hinton was born in Jacksonville, Florida in 1944 and came up as a guitarist in the Deep South. By the mid-1960s he had relocated to the Muscle Shoals area of northern Alabama, plugging into the recording infrastructure around FAME Studios and the network of producers and musicians who made the region a national center for rhythm and blues and soul recording.

His earliest Discogs-traceable session credits date to 1967. He appears on Percy Sledge’s The Percy Sledge Way (Atlantic, 1967) — an early major soul record from the Muscle Shoals orbit, produced by Quin Ivy and Marlin Greene. The same year he was in the studio on sessions for French releases on Barclay, demonstrating how quickly Muscle Shoals players were being hired across the Atlantic for European soul productions. He was also producing at this period: Discogs credits him as producer on several South Camp and Quinvy label singles in 1967–68.

The FAME / Atlantic Era (1967–1970)

Through the late 1960s Hinton was a core participant in the FAME Studios / Atlantic Records production pipeline that Jerry Wexler had established. He appears as guitarist on the Muscle Shoals tracks that make up some of the best-known recordings of the era:

  • Aretha Franklin, Spirit In The Dark (Atlantic, 1970) — Hinton played guitar on tracks A3, A5, B1, B5 with the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section (alongside Roger Hawkins on drums, David Hood on bass, Barry Beckett on keyboards, and Jimmy Johnson (4) on guitar). Produced by Jerry Wexler, Arif Mardin, and Tom Dowd.
  • Laura Nyro, Christmas And The Beads Of Sweat (Columbia, 1970) — Hinton played electric guitar on all five Side A tracks for the Muscle Shoals sessions (Roger Hawkins, David Hood, Barry Beckett also present; Felix Cavaliere on organ; produced by Arif Mardin and Cavaliere). Duane Allman appeared uncredited on Side B.
  • Cher, 3614 Jackson Highway (ATCO, 1969) — Hinton on lead guitar; produced by Wexler, Mardin, and Dowd; rhythm section of Hawkins, Hood, Beckett plus Jimmy Johnson on rhythm guitar. The album title references the address of Muscle Shoals Sound Studios.
  • Boz Scaggs, Boz Scaggs (Atlantic, 1969) — Guitar alongside Duane Allman, Jimmy Johnson, Hawkins, Hood, Beckett; produced by Jann Wenner, Marlin Greene, and Scaggs.
  • Wilson Pickett — Discogs credits include Right On (Atlantic, 1970) and Muscle Shoals Nitty Gritty (Embryo, 1970).

He also appears on sessions for Ronnie Hawkins (Cotillion, 1970), Odetta (Polydor, 1970), and King Curtis’s Get Ready (Atlantic, 1970).

The Broader Session Career (1969–1976)

Hinton’s session work extended well beyond Muscle Shoals mainstays. He played on:

  • Willis Alan Ramsey (Shelter Records, 1972) — the only album by the Texas singer-songwriter
  • Waylon Jennings, Honky Tonk Heroes (RCA, 1973) — rhythm guitar; the Willie Nelson-dominated album considered one of the foundational outlaw country records
  • Barry Goldberg (ATCO, 1974) — guitar
  • Ronnie Milsap (Warner Bros., 1971) — early solo outing by the country piano star

He also turns up on notable compilations of the period that draw from Muscle Shoals sessions, including Muscle Shoals Nitty Gritty (Embryo, 1970).

His producer credits in the late 1960s include sessions for smaller R&B labels: Diamond Records, South Camp, Cotillion, Quinvy. On the South Camp label he co-produced several Don Varner singles and the Mickey Buckins & The New Breed sides with Paul Ballenger and Robert Gregg (1967). He arranged strings on Solomon Burke’s Moments (ATCO, 1970), taking a broader production role.

First Solo Single and the Move to Solo Artist (1969–1978)

Hinton’s first documented primary solo credit is the 1969 Pacemaker Records (5) single A Railroad Trestle In California / Dreamer, produced by Dan Penn and Spooner Oldham — two central figures in the Muscle Shoals songwriting and production world. This was an early indicator that he was developing a solo voice, though a full album would not come for another nine years.

His solo debut album, Very Extremely Dangerous (Capricorn Records, 1978), was produced by Barry Beckett and recorded with the core Muscle Shoals unit: Roger Hawkins (drums), David Hood (bass), Jimmy Johnson (guitar), Barry Beckett (piano/organ/synthesizer), Harvey Thompson (saxophone), Dennis Good (trombone), Harrison Calloway (trumpet, horn arrangements), Ronald Eades (baritone saxophone), and the Muscle Shoals Horns. Hinton played guitar, piano, and sang. The record was a blue-eyed soul statement, placing Hinton’s raw vocal style against the polished Muscle Shoals rhythm section.

Letters From Mississippi and the European Market (1986)

After the Capricorn debut, Hinton’s second album arrived eight years later: Letters From Mississippi (Instant Records / Line, Germany, 1986). It was recorded in two separate sessions: the Muscle Shoals tracks with Roger Hawkins, David Hood, Jimmy Johnson, Clayton Ivey, and Ronald Eades, and a second set with a different group (Owen Brown on bass and engineer, Jeffrey Simpson on drums, John Wyker producing). Hinton produced the Muscle Shoals session himself, Jimmy Johnson recorded and mixed those tracks. All songs credited to Edward Craig Hinton. The album found a warmer reception in Germany and Europe than in the US.

Cry And Moan (1991) and Very Blue Highway (1993)

The early 1990s were Hinton’s most productive solo period. Cry And Moan (Bullseye Blues, 1991) was co-produced by Hinton and Ron Levy; the band was stripped down — Owen Brown on bass, Johnny Sandlin on drums and bass, Joe Walk on organ, Ron Levy on organ and percussion, Harvey Thompson on saxophones. Hinton played vocals, guitar, Wurlitzer electric piano, and percussion throughout.

Very Blue Highway (Bullseye Blues / The Network, 1993) expanded the lineup: Marvell Thomas on organ, Ron Levy on organ (track 3), Jeffrey Simpson on drums, Owen Brown on bass, the Memphis Horns (Andrew Love on tenor saxophone, Wayne Jackson on trumpet/trombone), with co-production and engineering from Terry Manning and Owen Brown. Robert Gordon (5) wrote the liner notes. This is generally considered the peak of Hinton’s solo output.

Later Recordings and Death (1993–1995)

Hard Luck Guy (Zane Records, 1999; recorded materials from Hinton’s final period, released posthumously) appeared four years after his death. Lost And Found (Breathe Easy Music, 1995) — a project where he took producer, engineer, vocalist, and multi-instrumentalist roles — was completed near the end of his life.

Hinton died in Birmingham, Alabama on July 28, 1995, at age 51.

Posthumous Legacy

Zane Records issued two further volumes of Hinton’s songwriting sessions: Playin’ Around - The Songwriting Sessions Vol.2 (2004) and Beautiful Dream - Sessions Vol.3 (2005). A compilation Cover Me (The Eddie Hinton Songbook) has also circulated. A live album, Live Smokin’ Soul (New Shot Records, Italy, 2023), documents a later performance.

UB40’s 1988 cover of “Breakfast In Bed” (written by Hinton and Donnie Fritts) reached No. 1 in the UK, providing Hinton’s most commercially prominent songwriting credit. Earlier the song had been recorded by Dusty Springfield (1969) and Millie Jackson.

Open Questions

  • The exact circumstances of Hinton’s exit from full-time session work after the mid-1970s, and the nature of the “troubled later life” described in press accounts, cannot be documented from Discogs alone. Web sources would be needed for biographical detail on the gap years between Very Extremely Dangerous (1978) and Letters From Mississippi (1986).
  • Lost And Found (Breathe Easy Music, 1995) credits Hinton as producer and engineer but the Discogs entry has no additional credits — unclear who else performed on those recordings.
  • “Breakfast In Bed” co-writer: Discogs credits Hinton as “Written-By” on the UB40 recording and various other pressings, but does not clarify whether Donnie Fritts co-wrote it — this is commonly stated in music journalism but not confirmed in this Discogs pass.
  • The Hard Luck Guy (Zane Records, 1999) entry has no additional credits in Discogs — session players unknown from this data.
  • Whether the Live Smokin’ Soul (New Shot Records, 2023) is a genuine posthumous concert recording or uses newly released archival material is unclear from Discogs metadata alone.
  • The Coleman-Hinton Project (Discogs artist ID: 9048709) and The Duck And The Bear (Discogs artist ID: 1312419) — two group credits Hinton carries — are not further documented in this pass.
