Jerry Goldsmith

American composer and conductor. Born February 10, 1929, Pasadena, California. Died July 21, 2004, Beverly Hills, California. Real name: Jerrald King Goldsmith. Discogs artist ID: 208267.

One of Hollywood’s most prolific and acclaimed film and television composers, active from the early 1960s through the early 2000s. Associated with the Jerry Goldsmith Orchestra. Father of composer Joel Goldsmith.

Biography

Early Career — 1960s

Goldsmith began releasing soundtrack albums in 1963–64, with early work for MGM and Epic. His first period of sustained output was 1964–1969, when he scored dozens of films including Lilies of the Field (Epic, 1964), In Harm’s Way (RCA Victor, 1965), Our Man Flint (20th Century Fox, 1966), In Like Flint (1967), Planet of the Apes (Project 3, 1968), and Patton (20th Century Fox, 1970). In this era he worked primarily on UA, 20th Century Fox, and MGM releases.

He also wrote TV themes throughout the 1960s, including Dr. Kildare (1962), Room 222 (1969), and music from The Man from U.N.C.L.E.. Several Twilight Zone episodes from this era later appeared on Varèse Sarabande compilations.

1970s — Hollywood Mainstream

The 1970s were Goldsmith’s breakthrough decade. Key scores include Papillon (Columbia, 1973), Chinatown (EMI, 1974), The Wind and the Lion (Arista, 1975), Logan’s Run (MGM, 1976), The Omen (Tattoo Records, 1976 — Academy Award winner), MacArthur (MCA, 1977), The Boys from Brazil (A&M, 1978), Alien (20th Century Fox, 1979), and Star Trek: The Motion Picture (Columbia, 1979).

On The Omen, Goldsmith composed and produced; Lionel Newman conducted. The National Philharmonic Orchestra served as the primary ensemble for his London-recorded work throughout this decade. Orchestrations were handled by Arthur Morton (his most frequent orchestrator across 535 shared releases on Discogs).

Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979) marks the beginning of the long-standing collaboration with Bruce Botnick (418 shared releases), who served as executive producer and editing engineer.

1980s — Action and Horror

The 1980s saw Goldsmith scoring a range of blockbuster and genre films: First Blood (Regency, 1982), Poltergeist (MGM, 1982), Psycho II (MCA, 1983), Twilight Zone: The Movie (Warner Bros., 1983), Gremlins (Geffen, 1984), Legend (Filmtrax, 1985), Rambo: First Blood Part II (Varèse Sarabande, 1985), Rambo III (Scotti Bros., 1988), Innerspace (Geffen, 1987), and Star Trek V: The Final Frontier (Epic, 1989).

Varèse Sarabande became his primary label during this decade; the relationship continued through the rest of his career. Alexander Courage was his regular orchestrator from the mid-1980s onward (228 shared releases). Robert Townson served as executive producer on his Varèse releases (192 shared releases).

Goldsmith also scored the Twilight Zone television revival — multiple volumes of original TV scores appeared on Varèse Sarabande in 1983–85.

1990s — Peak Varèse Period

The 1990s saw continued high output for Varèse Sarabande. Notable scores: Total Recall (1990), Basic Instinct (1992), Medicine Man (1992), Rudy (1993), The River Wild (RCA, 1994), The Shadow (Arista, 1994), Congo (Epic Soundtrax, 1995), First Knight (Epic Soundtrax, 1995), Star Trek: First Contact (GNP Crescendo, 1996), Air Force One (1997), L.A. Confidential (1997), Mulan (Walt Disney Records, 1998 — composed the instrumental score alongside song collaborators Matthew Wilder and David Zippel), The Mummy (Decca, 1999), and The 13th Warrior (1999).

The Mulan score credits show his typical 1990s studio model: Bruce Botnick recording and mixing; Alexander Courage orchestrating; Sandy de Crescent as contractor; Lois Carruth as assistant; Ken Hall as music editor.

Also notable in this period: Goldsmith conducted the world premiere recording of Alex North’s unfilmed 2001 score for Varèse Sarabande (1993).

2000s — Final Years

Output continued through 2003: Along Came a Spider (2001), Star Trek: Nemesis (Varèse Sarabande, 2002), The Sum of All Fears (Elektra, 2002), Looney Tunes: Back in Action (Varèse Sarabande, 2003). He died in July 2004. Posthumous reissues through Intrada, Varèse Sarabande, Film Score Monthly, and other specialty labels have continued to appear since.

Connections

  • Bruce Botnick — executive producer, editing engineer, mixer on ~418 releases (1979–2004); the central production relationship of his later career
  • Arthur Morton — orchestrator, 535 shared releases; Goldsmith’s most frequent collaborator overall
  • Alexander Courage — orchestrator from mid-1980s onward; also known for Star Trek TV theme
  • Lionel Newman — conductor on The Omen and numerous 1970s scores
  • National Philharmonic Orchestra — primary ensemble for London sessions
  • Robert Townson — Varèse Sarabande executive producer; produced dozens of Goldsmith albums
  • Joel Goldsmith — son; film composer
  • Alex North — Goldsmith conducted the premiere recording of North’s unused 2001 score
  • Varèse Sarabande — primary label from mid-1980s through 2004 (283 releases)
  • Jerry Goldsmith Orchestra — named ensemble used on some concert/crossover recordings
