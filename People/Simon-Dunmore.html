Simon Dunmore

British DJ, A&R executive, and label founder. Discogs artist ID: 72329. Name variation: S. Dunmore. Member of Touchdown Productions (with Steve McCutcheon).

Overview

Simon Dunmore is one of the central figures in UK house music infrastructure — the A&R and executive behind the careers of numerous American house artists in the UK market from the late 1980s through the 2010s. His career runs from club promoter and Blues & Soul journalist through label A&R at Cooltempo and AM:PM, to co-founding and directing Defected for over two decades.

He DJed from 1982 and promoted events in west London before joining The Record & Disco Centre in Rayners Lane, Pinner in 1988. In December 1989 he moved to Cooltempo, the Chrysalis-owned dance imprint, as club promotions manager under Ken Grunbaum. At Cooltempo he championed American house artists including Adeva, Tribal House, and Urban Soul, and commissioned club remixes. His own production work at this period was released under the Touchdown Productions alias, co-produced with engineer Steve McCutcheon (also known as Steve Mac (2)).

In his A&R capacity at Cooltempo, Dunmore forged a licensing relationship with the New York label Strictly Rhythm, bringing Aly-Us, Djaimin, The Boss, and River Ocean to UK release. He also pushed the label toward house-oriented signings including Juliet Roberts and the Zoo Experience.

In 1994 A&M Records offered him the chance to develop their dance imprint, relaunching it as AM:PM with Dunmore as A&R and Janet Bell as club promotions manager. In 1998, facing the imminent Universal/Polygram merger and likely closure of A&M, he and Bell launched Defected with Ministry Of Sound funding.

Dunmore was Director of Defected from 1999 until July 2022, when he sold the company to former MD Wez Saunders. He is the father of Louie and Lucas Dunmore, who record as the Dunmore Brothers.

Scene Context

Dunmore’s career maps the arc of US house music’s penetration into the UK market. At Cooltempo he was instrumental in licensing the Strictly Rhythm catalogue for UK release in the early 1990s — a direct conduit between the New York underground (Aly-Us, The Boss, River Ocean) and British dancefloors. The AM:PM years (1994–1998) overlapped with the UK’s progressive house peak. Defected, launched in 1998, became one of the defining UK house labels of the 2000s and 2010s, known for the Eivissa/Ibiza compilation series and later the Glitterbox disco-revival strand.

His deep-house stewardship of Osunlade — an artist whose releases crossed BBE (the London jazz-influenced imprint of Peter Adarkwah) and Strictly Rhythm — connects the Defected/commercial house circuit with deeper Afro-spiritual house currents.
