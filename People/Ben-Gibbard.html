Ben Gibbard

Discogs ID: 221561 Real name: Benjamin D. Gibbard Born: 1977, Bremerton, Washington Active: 1996–present Alias: ¡All-Time Quarterback!

Singer, guitarist, and songwriter. Primary frontman of Death Cab For Cutie and co-founder of The Postal Service. One of the defining voices of Pacific Northwest indie rock in the 2000s. Based in Seattle.

Career

1996–2002 — Bellingham / early Death Cab

Gibbard formed Death Cab For Cutie in Bellingham, WA in 1996, initially as a solo project/cassette release (You Can Play These Songs With Chords, Elsinor, 1997; 6 pressings). Guitarist/producer Chris Walla (ID 226217) joined quickly; bassist Nicholas Harmer (ID 401139) and drummer Jason McGerr (ID 401140) completed the lineup that recorded the Barsuk-era albums.

Something About Airplanes (Sonic Boom Recordings, 1998; 19 pressings) and We Have The Facts And We’re Voting Yes (Barsuk, 2000; 19 pressings) established the band’s reputation in Pacific Northwest indie circles. The Photo Album (Barsuk, 2001; 16 pressings) widened their reach.

2003 — Breakout: Give Up and Transatlanticism

Two landmark releases in the same year:

  • The Postal Service — Give Up (Sub Pop, 2003; 32 pressings — most-pressed release associated with Gibbard). Collaborative long-distance project with programmer Jimmy Tamborello (ID 153505). Backed vocally by Jenny Lewis (ID 288772) on tracks 1, 3, 5–7, 9. Recorded by exchanging tracks via mail. Became one of Sub Pop’s best-selling records of the decade.

  • Death Cab — Transatlanticism (Barsuk/Fierce Panda, 2003; 23 pressings). Produced by Chris Walla. Title track featured backing vocals by John Roderick (ID 288778), Phil Wandscher (ID 288777), and Sean Nelson (ID 280269).

2005–2008 — Atlantic signing, mainstream peak

Death Cab signed to Atlantic Records; Plans (Atlantic/Barsuk, 2005; 24 pressings) was their major-label debut. Sean Nelson sang harmony on “Crooked Teeth.” “I Will Follow You Into The Dark” became the band’s most-recognised track.

Narrow Stairs (Atlantic/Barsuk, 2008; 16 pressings) followed. Gibbard also composed and performed the score for the documentary Kurt Cobain About A Son (Barsuk, 2008; 2 pressings) with engineer Steve Fisk (ID 49143).

2009 — One Fast Move Or I’m Gone (Kerouac project)

Collaborative soundtrack album with alt-country songwriter Jay Farrar (ID 400816), adapting Jack Kerouac’s Big Sur. Recorded across multiple studios (Hyde St., SF; Prairie Sun, SF; Camp Street, Cambridge; The Ship, LA). Pedal steel by Brad Sarno (ID 969122); mixed by Paul Q. Kolderie (ID 264726) and Adam Taylor (ID 120531). Released on Atlantic / F-Stop Music. Accompanied a documentary film directed by Curt Worden; DVD included interviews with Tom Waits, Patti Smith, Lawrence Ferlinghetti, Lenny Kaye.

2012 — Former Lives (solo debut)

First proper solo album under his own name (Barsuk, BARK130LP; 12 pressings). Folk-rock/indie; sparse credits — mastered by Ed Brooks (ID 39276). Featured Aimee Mann (ID 11068) on “Bigger Than Love” and Trio Ellas on “Something’s Rattling (Cowpoke).“

2015–present — Kintsugi, Asphalt Meadows, ongoing

Kintsugi (Atlantic/Barsuk, 2015; 17 pressings) was Chris Walla’s final album with Death Cab before his departure; subsequent albums used Dave Depper (ID 615354) and Zac Rae (ID 339639) as additional members. Asphalt Meadows (Atlantic/Barsuk, 2022; 8 pressings) is the most recent studio LP. Gibbard also curated Ocean Child: Songs Of Yoko Ono (Atlantic/Canvasback, 2022) and collaborated with Tycho (Scott Hansen, ID 71827) on Only Love (Mom+Pop/Ninja Tune, 2021; 2 pressings).
