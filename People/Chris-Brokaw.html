Chris Brokaw

Discogs ID: 53516
Real name: Christopher Jon Brokaw
Born: August 1, 1964, New York City
Alias: Chris Burkoff
Name variations: -cb, CB, C. Brokaw, Christopher Brokaw

American drummer, guitarist, and singer-songwriter. A pivotal figure in 1990s American slowcore and noise rock through his roles in Codeine and Come (2), and in post-rock through The New Year and Pullman. Simultaneously maintained a prolific solo and collaborative output from the late 1990s onward, releasing on labels including Atavistic, 12XU, Capitan Records, JellyFant, and Damnably. His credits span drummer, guitarist, vocalist, composer, and producer.

Biography

New York and Codeine (1989–1994)

Brokaw was born in New York City in 1964. His first documented recording is the Codeine demo cassette All Writhe (1989, Not On Label), a self-released tape credited to 7 Or 8 Worm Hearts — an early Codeine-related project. Codeine’s proper catalog began with the Frigid Stars LP (1990, Glitterhouse / Sub Pop), a three-piece of Brokaw (drums, guitar), Stephen Immerwahr (bass, vocals), and John Engle (guitar), produced by themselves and Mike McMackin. The album established the American slowcore template: drastically slowed tempos, minimal distortion, major-key melancholy run at funeral pace.

Codeine’s Sub Pop run continued with the Barely Real EP (1992), the Tom EP (1993, Sub Pop UK), and the final LP The White Birch (1994, Sub Pop) — by which point Doug Scharin had replaced Brokaw on drums. The When I See The Sun compilation (Numero Group, 2012) and the box set Dessau (Numero Group, 2022) collected the complete catalog. David Grubbs is also listed in Codeine’s Discogs member roster, reflecting session or associated involvement.

Come (2) (1991–1998)

Brokaw co-founded Come (2) with Thalia Zedek in the early 1990s — a Boston noise-rock/post-punk duo (with rotating rhythm section) signed to Matador. The debut Eleven : Eleven (Matador, 1992) had a four-piece lineup: Brokaw and Zedek on guitar/vocals, Sean O’Brien on bass, Arthur Johnson on drums, with Carl Plaster producing. Don’t Ask Don’t Tell (Cortex, 1994, with Bryce Goggin and Carl Plaster co-producing) followed, then the String EP (1995), Near Life Experience (Matador, 1996), and the final record Gently Down The Stream (Matador, 1998) — by which point Daniel Coughlin had replaced Johnson on drums and Winston Braman had replaced O’Brien on bass. Paul Q. Kolderie recorded Gently Down The Stream.

Come also released Secret Number (Domino, 1996, UK), on which Brokaw played guitar, bass, synthesizer, tambourine, and slide guitar. A reunion album, AKA The Come Club, appeared on Chunklet Industries in 2021, along with Peel Sessions (Fire Records, 2022).

Solo Work: First Period (2001–2005)

While still active with Come into the late 1990s, Brokaw began releasing solo material. Red Cities (Atavistic, 2001) was his first major solo album: guitars, percussion, and vocals, with Paul Q. Kolderie and Pete Weiss mixing and Carl Plaster and David Michael Curry on preproduction. Wandering As Water (Return To Sender, 2003) was an acoustic guitar / vocals / tambourine record, followed by I Was Born, But… (Original Film Score) (12XU, 2004), a score for Yasujirō Ozu’s 1932 silent film — composed, performed, and annotated by Brokaw, mastered by Jeff Lipton.

Incredible Love (Acuarela, 2005, Spain) was a solo LP, and the compilation Wandering As Water gathered his Return To Sender material.

The New Year and Pullman (2001–2017)

Brokaw drummed on all three studio albums by The New Year (the post-Bedhead project of Matt Kadane and Bubba Kadane): Newness Ends (Touch And Go, 2001), The End Is Near (Touch And Go, 2004), and the self-titled The New Year (Touch And Go, 2008). Steve Albini engineered all three. Newness Ends and The End Is Near also featured bassist Mike Donofrio and (on the latter) Peter Schmidt (9) as an additional member. A fourth album, Snow, appeared on Grand Hotel Van Cleef in 2017.

Pullman — a guitar quartet with Douglas McCombs, Doug Scharin, and Geoff Farina — released Turnstyles & Junkpiles (Thrill Jockey, 1998) and Viewfinder (Thrill Jockey, 2001). The lineup reunited for III (Western Vinyl, 2026). Brokaw contributed guitar to both studio albums.

Consonant and Collaborations (2002–2010)

Consonant (Fenway Recordings) released two records: Consonant (2002) and Love And Affliction (2003), both featuring Brokaw on guitar.

Guest and collaboration credits from this period include: guitar on Karate’s Pockets (Southern Records, 2004) — second guitar on “Cacophony” and “Concrete”; electric and slide guitar on Tara Jane O’Neil’s Viewfinder (Thrill Jockey, 2001); performer on The Willard Grant Conspiracy history compilation There But For The Grace Of God (Glitterhouse, 2004); performer on Alien8 recordings (2004); participant in Yoshimi’s 77 Boa Drum (Commmons, 2008); musician on Rhys Chatham’s A Crimson Grail (Nonesuch, 2010).

The Dirtmusic self-titled album (Glitterhouse, 2007) found Brokaw as primary vocalist/multi-instrumentalist alongside Hugo Race, Chris Eckman, and Milan Cimfe.

Capitan Records and Increased Solo Output (2008–2016)

Brokaw’s relationship with Capitan Records (Austin, TX) produced a sequence of solo releases: Canaris (2008), Gracias, Ghost Of The Future (2009), Tundra (2010), Tidal Mud (2011), The Coyote of Deadhorse Canyon (2013), The Hand That Wrote This Letter (Songs By David Bowie + Prince) (2017). The 12XU label released Gambler’s Ecstasy (2012) — a more band-oriented record featuring Douglas McCombs (bass), John Herndon (drums), Howard Draper (organ), David Michael Curry (viola), and Wussy (written-by on one track), recorded by Matt Brown, Matthew Barnhart, and Tim Iseler.

Other releases in this period: The Angel’s Message To Me (JellyFant, 2010), The Boarder’s Door (Damnably, 2010), Stories (Limited Appeal, 2011).

The Periscope Twins (12XU, 2015) was a produced project. All Out & Down (Landland Colportage, 2016) appeared as a more minimal release.

Film Scores and Continued Work (2014–present)

Now, Forager (Original Soundtrack) (Dais Records, 2014) showed the breadth of Brokaw’s instrumental range: acoustic guitar, electric guitar, slide guitar, bass guitar, melodica, vibraphone, piano, percussion, and electronics — all composed and performed by him, mastered by Josh Bonati.

End Of The Night (Vin Du Select Qualitite, 2019) was a producer/guitarist/co-writer credit on a full album. Ghost Ship (12XU, 2025) is his most recent solo release, with guitar and vocals.

Additional notable credits: slide guitar on Gramercy Arms (2008); guitar on Dave Derby and the Norfolk Downs (2007); guitar swells on Loose Records release Untethered (2018); electric guitar on Ba Power (Glitterbeat, 2015); guitar on Xiu Xiu / Tara Jane O’Neil Night Of Your Ascension (Thrill Jockey, 2015); guitarist on 2 Meter Sessions Live On 2 Meter Sessions (2025).

Steve Wynn

Brokaw’s most extensive guest relationship. The Melting In The Dark sessions (1996) brought together essentially the Come band (Brokaw, Thalia Zedek, Sean O’Brien, Arthur Johnson) plus Steve Wynn; subsequent Wynn albums continued the connection through the early 2000s.
