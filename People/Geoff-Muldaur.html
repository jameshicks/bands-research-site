Geoff Muldaur

Blues singer and multi-instrumentalist. Born Geoffrey Muldaur, 12 August 1943, Pelham, New York. Discogs artist ID: 611748.

Central figure in the Cambridge folk/jug band revival of the 1960s as a core member of Jim Kweskin - The Jug Band; co-leader and co-producer of Paul Butterfield’s Better Days (1972–1973); and a prolific solo recording artist, arranger, and occasional sideman from the mid-1970s onward. Ex-husband of Maria Muldaur; father of Jenni and Clare Muldaur (both of whom appear on his recordings).

Biography

Cambridge Folk and Jug Band Era (1963–1971)

Muldaur came up in the early-1960s Cambridge/Boston folk scene and joined Jim Kweskin - The Jug Band (Discogs ID: 1013031) from the outset. The band’s debut LP Jim Kweskin And The Jug Band (Vanguard, 1963) listed him as contributing vocals, guitar, and kazoo. He was also on record early as a solo presence: Sleepy Man Blues (Prestige Folklore, 1963), a solo LP, was his first primary-credited release and lists him on guitar and vocals.

In parallel, he appeared as a sideman on early sessions for Cambridge folk stalwart Eric Von Schmidt: The Folk Blues Of Eric Von Schmidt (Prestige Folklore, 1963) and Pickin’ The City Blues (Prestige Folklore, 1964), the latter credited to Von Schmidt with Muldaur on guitar and washboard.

The Kweskin band ran for six studio albums across two labels:

The full membership across this period included Jim Kweskin (vocals, guitar, leader), Maria Muldaur (vocals, fiddle; then billed as Maria D’Amato / Maria D’Amato Muldaur), Fritz Richmond (washtub bass, jug), and Bill Keith (banjo, pedal steel). After the Kweskin band dissolved c.1969–1970, a Greatest Hits! compilation appeared on Vanguard in 1970.

Transition and the Woodstock Scene (1972)

After the Kweskin band, Muldaur relocated to the Woodstock, NY orbit and became part of the loose community around Bearsville Records. In 1972 he and Maria Muldaur recorded Sweet Potatoes (Reprise, master 454137) as a duo under the billing “Geoff & Maria Muldaur” (Discogs group ID: 2589220). The album featured Amos Garrett on guitar and vocals, Bill Keith on pedal steel, and Joe Boyd producing one track (“B5”). Muldaur arranged the horn and string charts.

That same year he contributed guitar and arranged the horns and strings on Eric Von Schmidt’s 2nd Right 3rd Row (Poppy, 1972) — the album’s credits are an early snapshot of the emerging Woodstock-adjacent circle: Paul Butterfield on harmonica, Amos Garrett on guitar/trombone, Maria Muldaur on backing vocals and percussion, Milton Glaser on jacket design, Nick Jameson on engineering. He also appears as guitarist on Stories We Can Tell (RCA Victor, 1972).

Paul Butterfield’s Better Days (1972–1973)

Muldaur co-produced and co-led Paul Butterfield’s Better Days with Paul Butterfield. The band recorded two albums for Bearsville, both produced by Butterfield and Muldaur, with Nick Jameson engineering:

YearTitleLabelNotes
1973Better DaysBearsvilleCredited to “Paul Butterfield”; Muldaur co-produced
1973It All Comes BackBearsvilleCredited to “Paul Butterfield’s Better Days”; Nick Jameson added as co-producer

Core band: Amos Garrett (guitar), Ronnie Barron (piano, vocals), Bill Rich (bass), Chris Parker (2) (drums). Guest vocalists across the two albums included Maria Muldaur and Bobby Charles. Milton Glaser designed both sleeves; Bob Ludwig mastered.

A Bearsville single, New Walkin Blues (1973), was pulled from the debut. The band dissolved in 1974.

Solo Career: Reprise and Flying Fish (1975–1980)

After Better Days, Muldaur made two solo albums for Reprise and then moved to Flying Fish (2).

Is Having A Wonderful Time (Reprise, 1975, master 425332) is his most ambitious solo record. Produced by Joe Boyd and engineered/mixed by John Wood, it drew on an extraordinary cast across distinct stylistic sections: a Duke Ellington/Benny Carter-inflected New York big-band session with Duke alumni (Doc Cheatham, Taft Jordan, Quentin Jackson, Benny Morton on trombones, Seldon Powell, Russell Procope), arranged by Benny Carter; British folk-rock tracks with Richard Thompson on lead guitar and John Cale on viola; and New Orleans-flavored material with James Booker on organ and piano. Amos Garrett contributed guitar and vocal arrangements; Maria Muldaur played fiddle on one track; Fritz Richmond rejoined for jug; Bill Keith played pedal steel; Bernard Purdie and Chris Parker (2) split drum duties; Ron Carter played bass on two tracks; Cornell Dupree on guitar. Stephen Bruton also contributed guitar and vocals.

Motion (Reprise, 1976, master 486860) followed. A single, Since I’ve Been With You Babe (Reprise, 1976), was also issued.

The Geoff Muldaur & Amos Garrett LP (Flying Fish, 1978, master 486867) was a duo record with Amos Garrett, produced by Michael Melford. Personnel included Bill Rich on bass, Fritz Richmond on jug, and Jenni Muldaur on vocals. Muldaur played guitar, piano, clarinet, alto saxophone, and arranged the horns.

Blues Boy (Flying Fish, 1979, master 835109) was his next solo LP, again on Flying Fish. He also released Live In Japan (Yupiteru Records, 1979).

I Ain’t Drunk (Hannibal Records, 1980, master 810577) was recorded with The Nite Lites and co-produced with Joe Boyd and John Wood. It featured Tom Principato on lead guitar, Mark Kazanoff on harmonica and tenor saxophone, and Sarah Brown on bass.

Later Solo Work (1987–present)

Muldaur returned to jug band roots with Jug Band Blues (Mountain Railroad Records, 1987), contributing guitar, mandolin, clarinet, washboard, kazoo, and vocals to a reunion-style project with Kweskin-era bandmates.

The Secret Handshake (Hightone Records, 1998, master 2995715) and Password (Hightone Records, 2000, master 2439382) were his main late-period solo statements — both self-produced and self-arranged; Password included sleeve notes by critic Peter Guralnick.

Private Astronomy — A Vision Of The Music Of Bix Beiderbecke (Edge Music, 2003, master 1052608) was recorded under the name Geoff Muldaur’s Futuristic Ensemble (Discogs ID: 3874105) and was arguably his most ambitious later project: a large ensemble tribute to Bix Beiderbecke, executive-produced by Joe Boyd, with an NYC woodwind/brass orchestra, guests including Loudon Wainwright III, Martha Wainwright, Greg Prestopino, Jenni Muldaur, and Clare Muldaur (in the vocal group “The Harmony Girls”), and solo slots for cornetist Randy Sandke and guitarist Matt Munisteri.

He contributed to Texas Sheiks sessions around 2009 (via Geoff Muldaur And The Texas Sheiks, Discogs ID: 2423205, on Tradition & Moderne), appearing on the Texas Sheiks album (2009).

His Last Letter (The Amsterdam Project) (Moon River Music, 2021) was his most recent solo record in the DB.

In 2025 he appears as musician on Bearsville Studio Sessions (Bearsville / Rhino), a retrospective archival release from the Bearsville catalog.
