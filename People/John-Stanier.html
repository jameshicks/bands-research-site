John Stanier

Drummer. Born August 2, 1968, Baltimore, Maryland. Grew up in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania and Florida. Discogs artist ID: 251525

Overview

John Stanier is one of the more recognizable drummers in American alternative and experimental rock — identified visually by a hi-hat mounted on an unusually tall riser, and sonically by wide-interval, high-force patterns that favor space and weight over density. His career has three main chapters: a formative early run in hardcore/noise-rock bands in the late 1980s and early 1990s, an extended tenure as drummer for Helmet through the band’s commercial peak (1989–1997), and an ongoing role as drummer/co-founder of Battles (2002–present). Across those chapters he also served as a core member of Tomahawk, Mike Patton’s post-industrial rock project, from its 2001 formation through the 2021 album Tonic Immobility.

Name variations on record include J. Stanier, John D. Stanier, John D. Stanier Jr., and Stanier.

Early Career: Pittsburgh / Crawlpappy (c. 1988–1992)

Stanier’s earliest documented band is Crawlpappy, a hardcore/noise-rock group from Pittsburgh (Discogs artist ID: 328209). Members alongside Stanier included Alan Peters, Brian Childers, Mike Sentkewitz, Rick Roy, Scott Colombo, and Zippy (6). Crawlpappy released a self-titled 7”/LP on Blackout! Records in 1990 (ID: 472014), appeared on a split with Sheer Terror that same year, and contributed to a split single Temple Body / Mind’s Eye (Blackout!, 1991) — a release Stanier also appears on separately via his Helmet credit. The German label We Bite Records issued a compilation of theirs, Deluxe, in 1992.

The Pittsburgh/Baltimore underground context connects Stanier to the wider AmRep-adjacent noise-rock scene of the era; Crawlpappy released on Blackout! Records, the same label that handled some of that regional output.

Helmet (2) — 1989–1997

Stanier joined Helmet early in the band’s formation (or at its founding), appearing on the self-released 1989 demo cassette (labeled “Helmet,” Discogs ID: 8413982) and the band’s first official release, Born Annoying (7”, Amphetamine Reptile Records, 1989). Helmet’s lineup for the classic period was:

  • Page Hamilton — guitar, vocals, primary songwriter
  • Peter Mengede — guitar (through Meantime)
  • Henry Bogdan — bass (through Aftertaste)
  • John Stanier — drums

The band recorded with downtown New York engineer Wharton Tiers on early releases. The key labels across Stanier’s Helmet tenure were Amphetamine Reptile Records (early material) and Interscope Records / Atlantic (major-label period).

Tomahawk (6) — 2001–2021

Tomahawk was formed in 2001 by Mike Patton (Faith No More, Mr. Bungle) and guitarist Duane Denison (The Jesus Lizard), with Kevin Rutmanis (Melvins) on bass and Stanier on drums. All four Tomahawk albums were released on Ipecac Recordings, Patton’s label. Rutmanis was replaced by Trevor Dunn (Mr. Bungle, Fantômas) for the fourth album.

Tomahawk ran concurrently with Battles through much of Stanier’s career — the band was active during the same years he was recording and touring with Battles, making Tomahawk effectively his primary “other” band for two decades.

Battles — 2002–present

Battles is Stanier’s primary ongoing band. Founded in New York City in 2002 by Ian Williams (ex-Don Caballero), Dave Konopka, Tyondai Braxton, and Stanier. The band’s approach built on Williams’s math-rock background (finger-tapping, looped guitar), Braxton’s processed falsetto, and Stanier’s physically dominant, wide-interval drumming — a style described at the time as machine-like in its regularity but physically large in scale.

The band signed to Warp Records and released a pair of early EPs before the debut album Mirrored (2007) established their international profile.

Mirrored (2007)

The debut album. Lineup: Stanier (drums), Ian Williams (guitar, keyboards), Dave Konopka (guitar, bass), Tyondai Braxton (guitar, keyboards, vocals). Self-produced and self-performed as a band unit; recorded and mixed by Keith Souza; assistant engineers Mike Viele and Seth Manchester; mastered by Jeff Lipton and Jessica Thompson. Art direction and design: Dave Konopka.

Stanier’s drumming on Mirrored — exposed, physically dominant, with no drummer’s kit concealed by studio layering — was widely cited as defining the album’s sound alongside Braxton’s processed vocals.

Gloss Drop (2011) — trio era begins

Tyondai Braxton departed Battles in 2010 before recording began. Remaining trio: Williams, Konopka, Stanier. Rather than replacing Braxton, the band recruited guest vocalists for individual tracks:

La Di Da Di (2015)

Fully instrumental — no guest vocalists. Produced by Battles, Keith Souza, Seth Manchester. Mastered by Greg Calbi and Steve Fallone. Art direction/design by Konopka.

Juice B Crypts (2019)

Returned to the guest vocalist model. Discogs credits list Stanier (drums, percussion) and Williams (guitar, keyboards) as “Uncredited” — credits added editorially to the DB entry; the original sleeve omits individual credits. Guest vocalists included Xenia Rubinos, Jon Anderson, Sal Principato, Shabazz Palaces, and Tune-Yards.

Other Projects and Credits

Cologne Tape (Discogs ID: 1850152): A German experimental collective with a large roster (including Axel Willner / The Field, Jörg Burger, and others). Stanier is listed as a member. The band released Render (Magazine, 2010) and Welt (Magazine, 2017). Stanier appears on Palace (Magazine, 2016) and Welt (2017) with Drums and Performer credits respectively, and on Mirapolis (Infiné, 2017) with Composed By, Drums credits.

The Mark Of Cain (Discogs ID: 271359): Listed as a group on Stanier’s Discogs page; the discography entry Songs Of The Third And Fifth (Feel Presents, 2012, Australia) credits him for Drums, Percussion and Written-By. Australian post-hardcore band originally formed in Adelaide.

Auf der Maur (2004, EMI, Discogs ID: 1209085): Stanier appears credited as “Musician [Cast Of Musicians And Lovers Of Sound]” on Melissa Auf der Maur’s debut solo album.

LP5 / Autechre (Mute, 2019, Discogs ID: 13374437): Credited as “Sampler [Additional Drum Samples]” on the Autechre album LP5 (2019 reissue/pressing) — a minor appearance indicating session drum samples were sourced.

Yesterday and Today (Anti-, 2009, Discogs ID: 1746152): Credited as Drums, Producer, and Written-By — a rare production credit outside his primary bands. Suggests broader involvement in this release.
