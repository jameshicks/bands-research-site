Simon Crab

Electronic musician, producer, and label founder. Born in Liverpool, England. Founder and constant member of Bourbonese Qualk. Discogs artist ID: 457858.

Real name: Simon Crab. Also credited as: Simon Crabbe, Simon Crabtree, Crab.

URLs: bourbonesequalk.net · simoncrab.com · simoncrab.bandcamp.com

Biography

Origins and early Liverpool activity (late 1970s – 1981)

Simon Crab grew up in Liverpool and founded Bourbonese Qualk in the late 1970s with his brother Ted (Ted Crabtree, Discogs ID: 5660842). According to his profile, the name was chosen without a fixed meaning — “good as any.” The duo began making experimental music in a DIY anarchist spirit, rooted in Crab’s ongoing resistance to authority.

In the early 1980s, Crab used local Liverpool radio stations to promote the nascent UK electronic and power electronics scenes. He ran ads in magazines soliciting submissions and founded the Recloose Organisation (Discogs label ID: 13231) with a debut compilation, Sudden Departure (1982, listed in Recloose records as informal first entry). The label ran from 1981 to 1986 and was conceived as a collectivist imprint — proceeds from releases would fund new artists and recordings. Not all artists who joined shared the collectivist ethos; the label folded circa 1986 after approximately 13 releases.

Bourbonese Qualk: first phase at Recloose (1982–1986)

After moving to London into a squat, Crab was joined around 1982 by Julian Gilbert (vocals, theatrics) and Steven Tanza (percussion). The three ran the Ambulance Station as an open cultural space — studio, rehearsal room, exhibition and performance venue, printer workshops — and published under the Recloose Organisation umbrella.

By the time the first Bourbonese Qualk album, Laughing Afternoon (Recloose, LOOSE 004, 1983), was released, Crab’s brother Ted was no longer in the group. Crab played guitar, bass, clarinet, violin, and tape; Steven Tanza played drums and percussion; Julian Gilbert provided voice and selected instruments. The album’s thirteen tracks — “God With Us,” “Idiot Pain,” “Barcelona Telephone Exchange,” “Freefall,” “Blood Orange Bargain Day,” and others — document an industrial/experimental sound with acoustic and tape elements.

Hope (LOOSE 007, 1984) followed, with Crab on guitar. Preparing For Power (LOOSE 010, 1985) added voice and electronics alongside guitar, bass, and violin — the lineup was Crab and Tanza, with Julian Gilbert contributing vocals on individual tracks and US poet Craig Runyon providing words on two tracks. Julian Gilbert departed around 1985; Steven Tanza left around 1986 to pursue art and music under the alias The State.

The Spike (Dossier, ST 7504, 1985) was a German release, Bourbonese Qualk’s first on a foreign label, with Crab credited for instruments, voice, and tape; Tanza for drums, percussion, and voice; Gilbert (now departing) for voice on three tracks.

Recloose Organisation as label

During this period, the Recloose Organisation catalogue was broader than just Bourbonese Qualk. Key releases included:

  • Triptych E.P (LOOSE 002, 1982) — E.g Oblique Graph
  • Buddhist On Fire (LOOSE 008, 1984) — Muslimgauze (Bryn Jones)
  • A three-cassette box series (Loose 3 A/B/C, 1984) — Observation Clinique, Pierpaolo Zoppo, Laxative Souls
  • Live Series Number One (LOOSE 005, 1984) — various artists
  • Songs From The New International (LOOSE 012, 1986) — various artists compilation
  • Live 1983–85 (LOOSE 013, 1986) — Royal Family And The Poor

The label also had sublabels: Provision Video, Recloose Studios, London, and The Recloose Organisation Live Series. After Recloose folded, operations evolved into New International Recordings in 1987.

Crab’s connection to Bryn Jones / Muslimgauze via the label is notable — the Chasings The Shadow Of Bryn Jones 1983-1988 compilation (Vinyl-on-demand, 2014) credits Crab with book design.

New lineup and crossover work (1986–1989)

From 1986 onwards, with Tanza gone, the core Bourbonese Qualk lineup shifted to Crab plus Owen If Rossiter (Discogs: 478437, percussion) and multi-instrumentalist/guitarist Miles Miles (Discogs: 442917), with significant contributions from Kif Cole (Discogs: 478436).

The self-titled LP Bourbonese Qualk (New International Recordings, NIR871, 1987) documented this era. The lineup was confirmed as Crab and Tanza (with Tanza still credited on that release), and reflected the group’s shift toward a more electro-industrial and rhythm-driven sound across fifteen tracks. The label New International Recordings was Recloose’s successor imprint.

In 1987, Crab joined Royal Family And The Poor as a member. He contributed vocals, keyboards, and Mirage sampler programming, and co-produced the LP In The Sea Of E (Gaia Communications, Phase 3, 1987) with Michael Anthony Keane. Side A was engineered by Keith Hartley; Side B by Frank Sparks. Crab’s Mirage sampler work is specifically credited. He also co-wrote and arranged the material alongside Keane. The band toured Germany and Belgium during this period with Merlin Shepherd (alto and tenor saxophone, strings) and Andy Frizell (bass, flute).

Crab also received an “assistance” producer and mix credit on the obscure 1985 Atonal Records release Moral Rearmament (Germany), and an engineer/producer/shenai/vocals credit on In This Morbid Ecstasy A Release Is Needed (I Live In Hell Records, Ireland, 1987).

My Government Is My Soul (FünfUndVierzig, 1989) was the pivotal late-1980s Bourbonese Qualk LP, reflecting the new lineup’s full integration. Crab is credited for voice and electronics; Miles Miles for guitar and saxophone; Owen Rossiter for drums and percussion; Kif Cole for live sound engineering. The group wrote and produced the record themselves.

Praxis period and techno convergence (1992–1993)

Bourbonese Qualk moved into techno and hard trance territory in the early 1990s, releasing on Praxis (UK):

  • Knee Jerk Reaction EP (Praxis 2, 1992) — Crab as written-by, producer, recorded by. Four tracks: “Digital Domain,” “Take Control,” “Knee Jerk Reaction,” “Technophobia.”
  • Qual EP (Praxis, 1993) — two-track EP
  • Autonomia (Praxis 5 CD, 1993) — the full-length CD, with twelve tracks including “Traffic,” “Logic Bomb,” “Qual,” “Knee-jerk-reaction,” “50 Years Of Acid.” Produced, recorded, and written by Bourbonese Qualk.

The convergence of industrial, tribal, and techno on these releases places Bourbonese Qualk in the early UK rave-adjacent underground alongside acts on the Praxis network.

Dissolution and legacy (late 1990s – 2001)

The band officially disbanded in late 2002 in the wake of the death of Miles Miles. Crab remained the sole constant member throughout the entire active period from 1979 to 2001.

Solo and collaborative work (2013–present)

Crab has remained active as a solo artist and collaborator:

  • Sunseastar (Discogs: 975429) — collaborative project with Andy Wilson (5) (Discogs: 974903), described as “the first location recording / remix project” by Wilson and Crab. Andy Wilson subsequently used the name for solo recordings. The project released Fjærland (Not On Label, 2007).

  • Saccades (Thisco, 2013) — solo release credited to Simon Crab, with Dave Smith (6) on drums and Geoff Leigh on flute.

  • Sabaturin (Discogs: 7828774) — duo with Charles-Emile Beullac. Released Kenemglev (Holuzam, ZAM008, 2020) — nine-track LP in abstract/dub/glitch territory recorded in Portugal. Crab co-produced and created the artwork.

  • Invisible Cities (Space Ritual (2), SR006, 2022) — solo album, sixteen tracks in ambient/downtempo/dub territory. Additional percussion from Dave Smith (6), Fritz Catlin, and Ksenia Sadovski (vocals). Mastered by Justin Drake.

  • Big Daddy (37) (Discogs: 12850523) — duo with Fritz Catlin (Discogs: 448649). Released Bomb Culture (Offen Music, OFFEN 025, 2023) — digital album in dub/leftfield territory. Catlin played drums, percussion, bass, guitar, and melodica; Crab handled electronics, synthesizers, drum programming, and co-wrote, recorded, and produced. Crab also did the graphic design.
