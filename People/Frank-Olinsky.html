Frank Olinsky

Frank Olinsky

Aug 15, 20269 min read

Frank Olinsky

Discogs artist ID: 265503
Website: frankolinsky.com
Name variations: Frank Olinksy, Frankie Baby, Olinsky

American graphic designer and art director. Co-founder of Manhattan Design, the New York-based design collective also comprising Pat Gorman (2) and Patti Rogoff. Active as a freelance designer from the early 1970s through the 2010s, with peak label work at Elektra, A&M Records, Geffen Records, and Nonesuch through the late 1980s–2000s.

Career Overview

Manhattan Design and Early Work (1970s–mid-1980s)

Olinsky’s first confirmed Discogs credit is a 1974 cover design for Blues From the Apple on Oblivion Records, followed by a label design (uncredited) on Spring Of Two Blue-J’s on Unit Core Records (also 1974). These early jazz-adjacent credits predate his mainstream commercial period.

By the early-to-mid 1980s he was working alongside Pat Gorman (2) under the Manhattan Design banner (Discogs group ID: 1894759). Manhattan Design is best known outside music circles for creating the original MTV logo and visual identity in 1981 — that connection is not directly documented in the Discogs DB but is widely attributed to the collective. Within music, Manhattan Design credits appear on releases through the late 1980s and into the 1990s.

Mid-1980s work shows Olinsky active across multiple labels — ZE Records, Mercury, RCA, Chrysalis, Capitol, EMI, Polydor — predominantly in a graphic design and illustration capacity. Notable early credits include:

  • John Cale Comes Alive (Island/ZE, 1984) — Art Direction
  • Whiplash Smile — Billy Idol (Chrysalis, 1986) — Art Direction, Illustration
  • Notorious — Duran Duran (EMI, 1986) — Cover design
  • The Power Station 33⅓ (Capitol, 1985) — Design

A&M Records and Stay Awake (1987–1989)

Olinsky’s most celebrated single credit from this era is co-art directing Stay Awake: Various Interpretations Of Music From Vintage Disney Films (A&M, 1988) with Jeff Gold. The album was produced and conceived by Hal Willner (who also served as executive producer and liner notes author), with cover painting and drawings by Rodney Alan Greenblat.

He also co-art directed A Very Special Christmas (A&M, 1987) with Gold, a charity compilation for Special Olympics that became a long-running franchise. He later contributed original art direction credit to A Very Special Christmas 2, A Very Special Christmas 3 (1997, with design), and A Very Special Christmas Live (1999) and A Very Special Christmas 5 (2001).

Smashing Pumpkins (1995–1998)

Olinsky co-art directed and co-designed Mellon Collie And The Infinite Sadness (Virgin, 1995) with Billy Corgan himself and illustrator John Craig (3). This is among the highest-profile commercial releases he worked on. He continued through much of the Pumpkins’ Virgin run:

  • Mellon Collie And The Infinite Sadness (1995) — Art Direction, Design (w/ Corgan)
  • The Aeroplane Flies High box set (Virgin, 1996) — Art Direction, Design, Box Concept
  • Adore (Virgin/Hut, 1998) — Art Direction, Design
  • Ava Adore single (Virgin/Hut, 1998) — Art Direction, Design
  • Perfect single (Virgin/Hut, 1998) — Art Direction, Design
  • Various Pumpkins singles 1996 (1979, Thirty-Three, Tonight Tonight, Zero)

The Grammy Nominees 1997 compilation (PolyTel, 1997) credits him specifically for “Mellon Collie” art direction.

R.E.M. (1988)

Olinsky designed the packaging for Green (Warner Bros., 1988) alongside Jon McCafferty and Michael Stipe. This was part of R.E.M.’s transition to Warner Bros.
