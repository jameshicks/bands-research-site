Tony Cook (2)

Discogs ID: 266379
Real name: Anthony Malcolm Cook
Born: 30 March 1954
Name variations: Sir Anthony M. Cook, Tony

UK studio engineer and producer. Worked in recording studios in Iceland from 1975 to the mid-1980s, placing him at the centre of the nascent Icelandic post-punk scene and building a parallel UK post-punk engineering credit list that includes Bauhaus’s debut album. His Icelandic period overlaps directly with Björk’s earliest recordings — he engineered and co-produced her first significant band work.

Career Summary

1974 — Early UK Work

Engineered Neil Sedaka Live At The Royal Festival Hall (1974, Polydor, ID 950643 — 17 pressings). The session featured Andy Summers on guitar (pre-Police) and Michael Giles on drums (original King Crimson).

1975–1982 — Iceland

Relocated to Reykjavík and became the primary English-speaking studio engineer in Iceland’s small but active recording scene. Credits span Icelandic folk, pop, and rock on local labels (Steinar, Júdas, Spor, Gramm):

1980–1981 — UK Post-Punk

During or around this Icelandic period Cook also accumulated significant UK post-punk credits, suggesting regular travel between Iceland and Britain:

Bauhaus — In The Flat Field (1980, 4AD, ID 379701 — 43 pressings)
Engineer. Bauhaus self-produced; personnel: Peter Murphy (vocals), Daniel Ash (guitar), David J (bass), Kevin Haskins (drums).

UK Decay — For Madmen Only (1981, Fresh Records, ID 929680 — 5 pressings)
Engineer alongside John Loder and Simaen Skolfield. Produced by John Loder and UK Decay.

Also engineered UK Decay’s Hobby For A Day and Unexpected Guest / Dresden (both 1981, Fresh Records).

1982–1985 — Icelandic Scene, Björk Connections

Tappi Tíkarrass — Miranda (1983, Gramm, ID 1088305 — 3 pressings)
Engineer and co-producer (with the band). This is the clearest Björk connection: Björk Guðmundsdóttir (ID 517951) performed keyboards and lead vocals (tracks A2–B5); Eythor Arnalds provided additional vocals. Tappi Tíkarrass members: Björk Guðmundsdóttir, Eyjólfur Jóhannsson (guitar), Jakob Smári (bass), Oddur F. Sigurbjarnason (drums), Eythor Arnalds, Guðmundur Þór Gunnarsson.

Kukl — The Eye (1984, Crass Records, ID 309652 — 7 pressings)
Engineer. Kukl was the post-punk/anarchist collective that directly preceded The Sugarcubes. Personnel: Björk Guðmundsdóttir (woodwind, voice), Einar Örn (metal-winds, voice), Einar Melax (bagpipes, bells, harmonium, strings), Birgir Mogensen (bass), Trix (11) (drums), Godkrist (2) (electric strings). Artwork by Dada Nana.

Frank Chickens — We Are Frank Chickens (1985, Les Disques Du Soleil Et De L’Acier, ID 970734 — 3 pressings)
Engineer (track B4 only). Produced by David Toop and Steve Beresford; guests included Alexander Balanescu, Annie Whitehead, Lol Coxhill, Tony Coe.

Also engineered Hana Nú (1987, Skífan) and Í Sjöunda Himni (1992, Skífan) during continued Iceland work.

1996 — Björk Debut Reissue

Co-produced the 1996 Australian reissue of Björk’s self-titled debut album (Parrot Records, ID 395567 — 11 pressings) alongside Pálmi Gunnarsson and Sigurður Karlsson. This debut was originally recorded in Iceland in 1977, when Björk was around 11 years old.
