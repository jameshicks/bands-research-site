Eszter Balint

Hungarian-American singer, songwriter, violinist, and actress. Born 7 July 1966, Budapest. Discogs artist ID: 230540.

Overview

Eszter Balint grew up immersed in avant-garde performance: her father Stephan Balint founded the Squat Theatre collective in New York City, and Eszter was living with the troupe when she first met Jean-Michel Basquiat. In 1983 Basquiat brought her into the studio to play violin on the influential hip-hop single “Beat Bop” by Rammellzee & K-Rob — a session that placed her in the orbit of downtown New York’s experimental scene before she had released a solo record.

A year later she made her film debut as Eva in Jim Jarmusch’s Stranger Than Paradise (1984), a role that established her screen presence internationally. Further film and TV work followed across decades: Chantal Akerman’s American Stories (1989), Woody Allen’s Shadows and Fog (1991), Steve Buscemi’s Trees Lounge (1996), Downtown 81 (2000), a recurring role as Amia in Louis C.K.’s Louie (season 4, 2014), and another Jarmusch collaboration, The Dead Don’t Die (2019). She received an Independent Spirit Award nomination for Best Female Lead for Bail Jumper (1990). (Wikipedia)

As a recording artist, Balint released three solo albums — Flicker (1998), Mud (2004), and Airless Midnight (2015) — all produced by JD Foster. A fourth album, I Hate Memory! (2022), was self-released. Her songwriting was praised by the New York Times, The New Yorker, and Billboard; Jon Pareles wrote of Mud that she “puts arty twists into back-alley Americana” and has “her own film noir sensibility.” (Wikipedia)

As a session and ensemble player she contributed to Michael Gira’s Angels of Light on two studio albums (Everything Is Good Here / Please Come Home, 2003; We Are Him, 2007), and appeared as a special guest on the Swans album The Seer (2012). She also appears on Marc Ribot’s group Los Cubanos Postizos, toured Europe with Marc Ribot’s Ceramic Dog in 2009, and contributed fiddle to Larkin Grimm’s Parplar (2008, Young God Records). Her cover of Serge Gainsbourg’s “Un Poison Violent, C’est Ca L’amour” appears on Great Jewish Music: Serge Gainsbourg (Tzadik, 1997). (Wikipedia)

Name variations on Discogs: Esther Ballet, Ezster Balint, Ezther Ballant, eb.

Connections

  • Angels of Light — violinist and vocalist on Everything Is Good Here (2003) and We Are Him (2007); part of Gira’s rotating downtown New York ensemble
  • Michael Gira — led Angels of Light and Swans; Balint also guested on The Seer (2012)
  • Swans — guest violin on The Seer (2012)
  • Jean-Michel Basquiat — brought Balint to the “Beat Bop” session (1983); she has publicly criticized commercialization of his work
  • JD Foster — produced all three early solo albums (Flicker, Mud, Airless Midnight)
  • Marc Ribot — appeared together on downtown projects including Flicker sessions (per Chris Cochrane vault entry); Balint toured with Ribot’s Ceramic Dog (2009) and appears on Your Turn (2013)
  • Larkin Grimm — Balint played fiddle on Parplar (2008, Young God Records)
  • Stew — co-writer and vocalist on I Hate Memory! (2022)
  • Chris Cochrane — guitarist/co-writer on Flicker (1998); also together on I Hate Memory!
  • Stephan Balint — father; founder of Squat Theatre (New York avant-garde collective)
  • Young God Records — label for Angels of Light and Larkin Grimm credits
  • Scratchie Records — Flicker (1998)
  • None Records — Mud (2004)
  • Jim Jarmusch — Stranger Than Paradise (1984) and The Dead Don’t Die (2019)
