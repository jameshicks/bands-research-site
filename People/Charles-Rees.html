Charles Rees

Discogs ID: 381623

UK recording engineer, producer, musician, and studio manager. From 1992 to 1999 he worked at Gallery Studios in London. Since 1999 he has been based at — and manages — Black Barn Studios, Paul Weller’s private facility in Surrey. Over three decades he has accumulated credits on well over 200 releases, with Weller’s solo catalog as the backbone of his career. He also contributed early in his career as an assistant engineer on sessions for Héroes Del Silencio and Robert Wyatt, and has periodically crossed into production and live instrumentation.

Name variations on credits: C. Rees, C.Rees, Charles, Charles Reese, Charlie Rees, Charly Rees, Rees.

Career Overview

Gallery Studios period (1992–1999)

Rees cut his teeth at Gallery Studios in London in the early 1990s, working as an assistant engineer on major international sessions that came through the room. His earliest significant credits are on Héroes Del Silencio’s platinum album El Espíritu Del Vino (EMI, 1993), where he and Ash Howes assisted chief engineer David Nicholas and producer Phil Manzanera. The album was a landmark for the Spanish rock band and gave Rees exposure to high-budget production early in his career.

During this period he also logged assistant credits on a cluster of Latin-market albums — Os Paralamas Do Sucesso’s Severino (EMI, 1994), Héroes Del Silencio’s La Araña EP (Mercury, 1994), and Enrique Bunbury’s solo debut Radical Sonora (Chrysalis, 1997). He served briefly as crew/assistant on Fito Páez’s sessions and contributed recording/engineering on the obscure Desde Las Entrañas (1994, BMG/Culebra), where he is additionally credited with acoustic guitar, electric guitar, and bass — an early sign that he was more than a desk operator.

His Gallery period culminated with the Paul Weller and Robert Wyatt connections. He is listed as assistant engineer on Weller’s Heavy Soul (Island, 1997) and as co-mixer on Robert Wyatt’s Shleep (Hannibal, 1997). On Shleep he mixed tracks 1–4, 6–9, and 11 alongside Wyatt and Jamie Johnson, and mixed tracks 5 and 10 alongside Weller and Johnson. That triangular credit on a critically admired Wyatt album — appearing alongside the session’s primary forces — was an unusual level of recognition for an assistant-era engineer.

Black Barn Studios and the Weller axis (1999–present)

When Weller opened Black Barn Studios in Surrey around 1999, Rees moved with him and became studio manager. From this point his career is inseparable from Weller’s output. He is credited — as engineer, mixer, recorded-by, or some combination — on virtually every Weller studio album and live release from 2000 onward:

  • Heliocentric (Island, 2000) — engineer (assisted by)
  • Days Of Speed (Independiente, 2001) — mixed by
  • Illumination (Independiente, 2002) — engineer, mixed by
  • Studio 150 (V2, 2004) — engineer (selected tracks alongside Joeri Saal)
  • Catch-Flame! (V2, 2006) — mixed by
  • 22 Dreams (Island, 2008) — mixed by, recorded by
  • Wake Up The Nation (Island, 2010) — engineer, mixed by (with Jamie Johnson)
  • Sonik Kicks (Island, 2012) — recorded by, engineer (also credited: drums, percussion, written-by)
  • Saturns Pattern (Parlophone, 2015) — mixed by (alongside Jan “Stan” Kybert)
  • A Kind Revolution (Parlophone, 2017) — recorded by
  • True Meanings (Parlophone, 2018) — engineer, recorded by
  • On Sunset (Polydor, 2020) — recorded by, engineer, additional producer, edited by
  • Fat Pop (Volume 1) (Polydor, 2021) — recorded by, edited by
  • 66 (Polydor, 2024) — recorded by, additional producer, mixed by

On Sonik Kicks (2012) Rees moved beyond the glass into the recording: he is credited not only as recording engineer but also as drummer/percussionist and co-writer — one of a handful of non-Weller creative contributions to that album. He received additional production credits on the Jawbone soundtrack (Parlophone, 2017), where he is listed as sole producer alongside Weller as primary artist, and on Soul Wandering (Polydor, 2024).

Non-Weller work

Beyond Weller, Rees has a consistent thread of work at Black Barn for artists who record or mix there:

Robert Wyatt — beyond Shleep, Rees is credited on the Robert Wyatt Box Set (Domino, 2009) as “engineer [remix]; mixed by [with]” and on Robert Wyatt’s Comicopera (Domino, n.d.) as engineer for vocals.

The Rifles — engineered/mixed None The Wiser (Cooking Vinyl, 2014) and Freedom Run (Right Hook/Cooking Vinyl, 2011), on which he is also credited as producer and guitarist.

The Moons — worked across several releases for Andy Crofts’s band.

Declan O’Rourke — credited on This Old Town (Regal, 2007; engineer, mixed by) and subsequent releases including Arrivals (EastWest, 2021), totalling 11 shared releases in the DB.

Amy MacDonald — studio and technical manager credit on A Curious Thing (Mercury/Melodramatic, 2010), and percussion on the single “Love Love.”

Stone Foundation — engineered releases on Acid Jazz (2010–11), including Let It Go (Acid Jazz, 2010) where he is credited as engineer and producer.

Graham Coxon — shared credits on Bankbusted Nuclear Detergent Blues (Gearbox, 2015) and a handful of other Coxon releases recorded at Black Barn.

Steve Pilgrim — multiple releases (7 shared); engineer on Morning Skies (2017) and The Magic Strings Session (2019).

Ghost Box / The Advisory Circle — engineer, recorded by, and mixed by on In Another Room (Ghost Box, 2020); additional production manager credit.

Barricada — 5 shared releases, all from the Gallery Studios era.

Skeletal Family — 3 shared releases.

FSOL / Future Sound of London — piano, acoustic guitar, recorded by on We Persuade Ourselves We Are Immortal Remixes (fsoldigital.com, 2020).

P.P. Arnold — 5 shared releases (context likely Black Barn sessions).

The Charlatans — 3 shared releases at Black Barn.

From The Jam — produced From The Jam - Live! (Absolute, 2017).
