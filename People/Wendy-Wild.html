Wendy Wild

Real name: Wendy Andreiev
Discogs artist ID: 398399
Also credited as: Wendy Andreiev, Miss Wendy, Miss Wendy Wild, Wendy, Wild
Role: Vocalist, multi-instrumentalist (glockenspiel, cowbell, Casio keyboards, chimes), art director, producer
Active: c. 1981–1990s, NYC downtown scene

Biography

Pulsallama (1981–1983)

Wendy Andreiev was a founding member of Pulsallama, the all-female bass-and-percussion ensemble from Manhattan’s Lower East Side. Within the group she played Vocals, Glockenspiel, and Cowbell — confirmed by her credit on the 2020 Modern Harmonic retrospective compilation. Pulsallama released two 12” singles on Y Records in 1982 and 1983, produced by Disc O’Dell. The band dissolved around 1983 without ever issuing an album. Other members with whom she shared the Pulsallama stage include April Palmieri, Jean Caffeine (2), Stace Elkin, Kimberly Davis (4), Min Sanchez, Judy Streng, and (in the early lineup) Ann Magnuson and Lori Montana.

Mad Violets (1982–1984)

Immediately overlapping with her Pulsallama years, Wendy Andreiev co-founded the Mad Violets — a neo-psychedelic garage band from NYC — alongside Dino Sorbello in 1982. The band broke up in late 1984. Members at various points included Danny B. Harvey (guitar), Keith Streng (drums, bass, guitar, co-producer), Chaz Leyland (bass), Dan Reich (drums), Marc Seligman, Bob Kazuri, and Walter Sczesny. The Streng connection placed Mad Violets directly in The Fleshtones orbit.

The Mad Violets released “World Of…” in 1986 on the French label Lolita (Discogs release 1605856) — a posthumous release of material recorded while the band was still active. Wendy is credited as Lead Vocals, Keyboards [Casio], Chimes, Art Direction, and co-Producer on the B-side tracks. The A-side was produced by Raphael Laderman and engineered by David Lichtenstein. Side B was self-produced by the Mad Violets (i.e., Wendy Wild) and engineered by Robert Miller. Photographer Nat Finkelstein (Andy Warhol associate) shot for the release.

Love Delegation (1986, 1989)

Wendy was the lead vocalist on both recorded outings by Peter Zaremba’s Love Delegation, Peter Zaremba’s “garage disco” side project:

  • Spread The Word (Moving Target (2), 1986; Discogs release 2497179): Co-produced by Zaremba and James A. Ball. The session brought in jazz saxophonists Hamiet Bluiett (baritone) and Tony Orbach (tenor), Barrence Whitfield as a featured vocalist, and additional handclap/vocal work from Bill Milhizer, Dave Faulkner, and Pat Dinizio.
  • Delegation-Time (Accord, France, 1989; Discogs release 1846949): Full album produced by Zaremba. Other vocalists include Zaremba himself and Michael Ullmann. Keith Streng plays guitar/vocals; Robert Burke Warren on bass.

Mid-1980s Downtown Orbit

Wendy’s credit on the Full Time Men album Fast Is My Name (Shigaku Presents, Europe, 1985; Discogs release 2399986) is for Back Cover art and Art Direction, alongside a session that features Peter Buck (R.E.M.) producing and Keith Streng performing. This documents her active art direction practice alongside her performance work.

She provided Voice [Dialogue] on the Bronski Beat compilation Hundreds & Thousands (London/Metronome, 1985; Discogs release 485259), credited as “Miss Wendy Wild” — a cameo bridging her downtown NYC world with UK dance-pop.

In 1987 she contributed Backing Vocals to Frieda’s single “Disco Lover / Plastic Rap” (Varla Records, cat. V122; Discogs release 564227). Frieda was a NYC performance-art act centered on an enormous singing/dancing doll controlled by Barbara Lipp; the single was produced by Dany Johnson (also a Pulsallama alumna), featured John Flansburgh of They Might Be Giants on guitar, and was released on Varla Records, the small label run by Lori Montana and Dany Johnson. Other backing vocalists: Tom Koken and Bambi McDermott.

Also in 1987 she contributed Percussion and Vocals [Yelps] on track B7 of Link Protrudi And The Jaymen’s Drive It Home! (Music Maniac Records, Germany, 1987; Discogs release 1596382), alongside Deb O’Nair. Rudi Protrudi (Glen Dalpis) is better known as leader of The Fuzztones.

The 1988 Downtown (10) single “Downtown” on Virgin (Discogs release 1141856) is the highest-profile credit of this stretch. Wendy is one of four lead vocalists on an all-star downtown NYC benefit/anthem, alongside Dean Johnson (2), Jan Marek Pakulski, and Tiffani Time. The cast list is a who’s-who of the downtown nightlife scene: Sara Lee (bass), Jerry Harrison (guitar), Bernie Worrell (keyboards, also co-producer), James White (2) (saxophone), Larry Rivers (saxophone), Uptown Horns, plus chorus from Karen Finley, RuPaul, Michael Alig, Willy DeVille, Marcus Leatherdale, Dean Johnson, Chi Chi Valenti, Dianne Brill, Michael Musto, Richie Stotts, and others. Steve Jordan on drums. This is the late-1980s NYC club underground collected in one room.

The Fleshtones compilation Living Legends Series (I.R.S., 1989; Discogs release 2457774) carries a Backing Vocals credit for Wendy on track 11 — a further attestation of her integration into the Fleshtones/Zaremba circle.

In 1990 she provided Vocals [Moans By] on A Bitch Named Johanna’s “I’m A Bitch” (Project X Records; Discogs release 68924), produced by Kraze.

Scene Context

Wendy Wild was a persistent figure in the downtown Manhattan scene from 1981 through the early 1990s. Her credit trail runs through three overlapping worlds:

  1. No Wave / post-punk percussion — Pulsallama on Y Records, the all-female percussion ensemble circuit (Bush Tetras, ESG adjacency)
  2. NYC garage/psych underground — Mad Violets with Keith Streng and Peter Buck; the Fleshtones/Love Delegation circle; Link Protrudi/Fuzztones adjacency
  3. Downtown club nightlife — Varla Records (Frieda, Dany Johnson), the Virgin “Downtown” all-star session placing her alongside RuPaul, Karen Finley, Michael Alig, James White, and Bernie Worrell

Her art direction credits (the Full Time Men back cover, the Mad Violets release) indicate a visual/design practice running alongside her performance work.
