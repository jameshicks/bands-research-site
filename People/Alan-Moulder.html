Alan Moulder

Alan Moulder

Oct 27, 20263 min read

Alan Moulder

Discogs ID: 2768
Role: Mixer, producer

British mixer and recording engineer. Mixed Interpol’s Interpol (2010), El Pintor (2014), and The Other Side Of Make-Believe (2022); also contributed additional production on TOSMB. 48 shared releases with Interpol (counting pressings). Best known for mixing work with Smashing Pumpkins, Nine Inch Nails, Ride, My Bloody Valentine, and Foals — a central figure in the post-shoegaze rock mixing world.

Early credit: co-engineered and mixed These Immortal Souls’ debut Get Lost (Don’t Lie) (1987, Mute) alongside Flood and Tony Cohen.

Also produced the A-side of Adorable’s debut single “Homeboy” (Creation Records, 1992, CRE 140T) and the album version of “Homeboy” on Against Perfection (1993).

Co-produced and engineered Swervedriver’s Mezcal Head (Creation, 1993) and Ejector Seat Reservation (Creation, 1995), and co-produced 99th Dream (DGC, 1997) — their primary production collaborator from 1992 through the DGC era. Also mixed the “Never Lose That Feeling” single (1992). 73 shared releases with Swervedriver on Discogs.

Working relationship with Interpol

Notes (web)

Interpol (2010) was the first album Moulder mixed for the band, recorded at Electric Lady Studios, New York, and mixed at Assault & Battery Studios, London. Catherine Marks served as mix engineer and John Catlin as mix assistant. (Wikipedia — Interpol album, as of 2026-07-31)

The band self-produced the record and brought Moulder in purely as mixer — not as a producer with his own vision. In a later interview about El Pintor (2014), guitarist Daniel Kessler explained why they returned to Moulder: “The reason we wanted to go back to him after he mixed our last record was that although we always intended to produce this record ourselves, he still brings a lot of good ideas with him. We really enjoyed working with him last time. He’s also not just an established name, but someone that actually works hard on your music… Some days he’ll work forty-eight hours straight through if he’s trying to lay down a particular track. He actually listens to our music and what we have to say. He’s not there to try and change everything. He’s there to enhance it.” (Drowned in Sound — DiS meets Daniel Kessler from Interpol)

Kessler also described the relationship as a personal one: “I really think of him as a good friend.” (ibid.)

Sonny DiPerri, who later became a prominent engineer/producer in his own right, has recalled observing Moulder mixing the Interpol album during a period he was apprenticing in London — he ended up recording Paul Banks’ vocals for a few songs during those sessions. (Tape Op — Sonny DiPerri interview, issue 129)

What the web could not confirm: No source documents who initiated the contact — whether Interpol approached Moulder directly, were introduced through a manager or label (Matador Records), or whether the connection came via a mutual collaborator.
