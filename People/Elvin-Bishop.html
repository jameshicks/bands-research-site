Elvin Bishop

American blues guitarist, vocalist, and songwriter. Born 21 October 1942, Glendale, California (sources vary — often cited as Tulsa, Oklahoma). Real name: Elvin Richard Bishop. Nickname: “Pigboy Crabshaw.” Discogs artist ID: 310690.

Bishop came up through the Chicago blues world as rhythm guitarist in The Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1964–1968), then led his own groups through a long solo career spanning four-plus decades. Best known outside the blues circuit for the 1975 pop-crossover hit “Fooled Around and Fell in Love” (Capricorn Records, vocal by Mickey Thomas). Long association with Alligator Records from 1988 through 2020.

Band Memberships

  • The Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1964–1968) — rhythm guitar, occasional vocals. Alongside Paul Butterfield, Mike Bloomfield, Mark Naftalin, Jerome Arnold, Billy Davenport, and others. Bishop left after In My Own Dream (1968) to form his own group.
  • The Elvin Bishop Group (Discogs ID: 447972) — his primary vehicle through the Capricorn era.
  • The Elvin Bishop Band (Discogs ID: 2548647) — later iteration.
  • Elvin Bishop’s Big Fun Trio (Discogs ID: 6597336) — stripped-down trio format; produced his 2017 Alligator album.

Capricorn core band: The recurring unit across 1974–1978 was — Johnny Vernazza (lead/rhythm guitar), Michael “Fly” Brooks (bass), Donny Baldwin (drums), Mickey Thomas (vocals), Philip Aaberg (piano/keyboards), Bill Slais (multi-reeds/keyboards). Allan Blazek engineered and produced most of the studio albums; Bill Szymczyk was involved as co-producer on Struttin’ My Stuff and executive producer on Hometown Boy Makes Good! Johnny Sandlin produced Let It Flow and Juke Joint Jump (both recorded at Capricorn’s Macon, Georgia setup).
