LoneLady (Julie Campbell)

Born: Ashton-under-Lyne, Greater Manchester (raised in Audenshaw, East Manchester) Based: Manchester / London Labels: Filthy Home Recordings (self, 2005–07), Warp Records (2009–present) Genre: Post-punk / Electronic / Synth-funk

Background

Self-taught as a musician, producer, and engineer. Studied Fine Art (institution not documented), and began making music simultaneously — recording on a 4-track cassette recorder in her Manchester tower block flat. That DIY constraint became her aesthetic foundation: “Working with basic tools, mainly an 8-track cassette recorder, a Telecaster and a drum machine forced me to be economical and inventive.”

Named herself LoneLady as an artist name — performing solo or with a rotating band depending on the project.

Sound & Approach

Plays Fender Telecaster — sharp, trebly, rhythmically driven, post-punk in DNA. Explicitly influenced by Martin Hannett’s drum production on Joy Division and A Certain Ratio records: the cavernous, space-conscious Factory Records aesthetic is a formative reference rather than a direct professional connection (Factory collapsed in 1992; Campbell was a child of that geography and sound).

Arc across three albums:

  • Nerve Up (2010): wiry post-punk guitar, drum machine, taut and urgent
  • Hinterland (2015): guitar + electronics, psychogeographic conceptualism, funk entering
  • Former Things (2021): full electro / synth-driven, R&B and dance-floor oriented — guitar largely gone

Hinterland was mixed on a desk previously owned by Sly Stone.

Three distinct solo artists converging: post-punk/electronics underground (Campbell), Sheffield industrial lineage (Mallinder / Cabaret Voltaire), and analogue synthesis culture (Benge).

Manchester / Factory Records Connections

Campbell grew up in East Manchester — the same geography as Factory Records, Joy Division, and A Certain Ratio. Her aesthetic relationship to that scene is explicit and acknowledged (Hannett’s drum sound, the industrial psychogeography of Manchester’s built environment), though Factory had dissolved years before her career began.

The The Other Two remix (2022) — Stephen Morris and Gillian Gilbert, i.e. the rhythm section of New Order — represents a meaningful formal crossing of that lineage. See Factory Records.

Visual Art Practice

Campbell maintains a parallel conceptual/installation practice rooted in her Fine Art background:

Scrub Transmissions — Ongoing series: an mp3 player loaded with unique, unreleased music is embedded inside an urban or suburban structure. The music plays on loop until the battery dies. The work engages psychogeography and the hidden sonic life of built environments. The recording is unavailable elsewhere — the installation is the only instance.

Retreat or Danger? — July 2015 residency at the Barbican Art Gallery, London, examining “the mental states induced by concrete” and brutalist architecture’s effect on psychology. Included live performance, AV projection, a modular synth tutorial (with Ben Edwards), and a limited 12” vinyl pressing.

Shutters Down! — Experimental club night using derelict and unconventional venues, celebrating urban wilderness and the repurposing of abandoned space.

Themes across all visual work: concrete, post-industrial ruin, urban wastelands, and the psychic weight of built environments.

Foel Studio Connection

What Foel Studio is

Foel Studio is a residential recording studio in Llanfair Caereinion, Powys, mid-Wales. Founded 1973; billed as the second oldest residential recording studio in the world after Rockfield. Owned and operated by bassist Dave Anderson (3) (ex-Hawkwind, ex-Amon Düül II), who functions as both studio owner and in-house engineer. (foelstudio.co.uk; recordproduction.com)

Notable Foel Studio clients include: Hawkwind, Amon Düül II, Gong, The Fall, The Stranglers, Young Marble Giants, The Pop Group, Au Pairs, Van Der Graaf Generator, Peter Hammill, Godflesh, Electric Wizard, Napalm Death, Porcupine Tree, Scritti Politti, James, Sparks, John Foxx, My Bloody Valentine, and many more. (foelstudio.co.uk/clients/)

My Bloody Valentine at Foel — and the Fixsen question

The brief assumed Guy Fixsen engineered Isn’t Anything at Foel. This is incorrect. The facts, confirmed:

  • My Bloody Valentine did record at Foel Studio — Isn’t Anything (1988) was recorded across three studios: Foel Studio (Wales), Time Square Studios, and The Greenhouse (London). (Discogs; search result confirming engineers)
  • Engineers on Isn’t Anything at Foel were: Dave Anderson, Alex Russell, and Steve Nunn. Dave Anderson engineered in his own studio. Guy Fixsen is not credited on Isn’t Anything.
  • Guy Fixsen’s MBV connection is to Loveless (1991), not Isn’t Anything. He was one of ~16 engineers on Loveless, working at Protocol Studios in North London (where he began his career) and Britannia Row Studios. No evidence places Fixsen at Foel Studio. (Fast Company; Guy Fixsen biography via IMDb/search results)

The actual LoneLady / Foel lineage

LoneLady’s connection to Foel is through Guy Fixsen co-producing Nerve Up (2010). Fixsen’s MBV work (Loveless) gave him the shoegaze/experimental production pedigree that makes him a credible collaborator for her post-punk aesthetic. The Foel Studio thread and the Fixsen thread are parallel routes, not the same route:

No confirmed direct connection between Dave Anderson and Guy Fixsen — they both touched MBV’s catalog but at different albums, different studios, different years. Foel Studio’s lineage (psych, prog, post-punk, metal) and Fixsen’s lineage (shoegaze, leftfield rock, Stereolab, Pixies) are distinct threads that happen to share the MBV discography as a common reference point.

Foel Studio’s broader scene significance

The client list spans late-1960s psychedelia through 2000s metal and indie: Young Marble Giants, The Pop Group, Au Pairs, and Godflesh all recorded there, making it a document of British alternative music’s range across four decades. The studio’s Welsh residential character (remote, live-in) likely contributed to the dense, room-sound character of recordings made there.
