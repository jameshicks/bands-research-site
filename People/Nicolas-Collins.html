Nicolas Collins

Composer, performer, electronic musician. Born New York City, March 26, 1954. Discogs artist ID: 55394.

Name variations: Collins, N. Collins, Nic Collins, Nicholas Collins, Nick Collins.

Overview

Nicolas Collins is a composer and sound artist rooted in the experimental tradition of Alvin Lucier, David Behrman, and David Tudor — all of whom he worked with closely. Trained at Wesleyan University (B.A., M.A.) where he studied composition with Lucier, he was also a Watson Fellow. He later earned a PhD from the University of East Anglia. In the 1980s Collins immersed himself in the New York improvised-music scene, building home-made electronics — circuits woven around trombones, slide guitars, and microcomputers — and performing alongside Tom Cora, Shelley Hirsch, Christian Marclay, Zeena Parkins, and John Zorn. His central technique deploys a trombone’s slide mechanism to scan and trigger live radio signals and computer processes, an approach heard most directly on Devil’s Music (1985) and 100 Of The World’s Most Beautiful Melodies (1989).

Collins produced early recordings for Robert Poss and the Poss-led acts Western Eyes (2) and Tot Rocket And The Twins (1981, 1984), and the two collaborated directly on the Inverse Guitar cassette LP (1988). Together they define much of the creative output of Trace Elements Records, the label Poss operates and on which five of Collins’s releases appear.

Beyond performance, Collins has been a curator and institutional figure: Curator of Music Performances and Sound Installations at PS1/The Clocktower (from 1985); Visiting Artistic Director of STEIM Amsterdam (1992–1995); DAAD composer-in-residence in Berlin (1996–97); Editor-in-Chief of the Leonardo Music Journal (MIT Press, 1997–2017); Professor of Sound at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago. He lives and works between Berlin, Chicago, and rural Massachusetts. Wikipedia source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Collins.

His book Handmade Electronic Music: The Art of Hardware Hacking (Routledge) went through three editions (2006, 2009, 2020) and became a standard reference for circuit-bending and hardware-hacking communities worldwide.
