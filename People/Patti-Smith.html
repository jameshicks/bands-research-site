Patti Smith

Patti Smith

Discogs ID: 193816
Born: December 30, 1946, Chicago, IL
Groups: Patti Smith Group (ID 97733), Patti Smith And Her Band
Alias: R.e.f.m.
Rock and Roll Hall of Fame: 2007

Poet, singer, artist. Central figure of the proto-punk and punk movement rooted in the New York CBGB scene, 1973 onward. Merged beat poetry and raw rock and roll. Led the Patti Smith Group through four albums (1975–1979), went on hiatus after marrying Fred Smith (MC5, Sonic’s Rendezvous Band), returned to recording in 1996 following his death.

Sister: Todd Smith (ID 2299436). Children: Jackson Smith (ID 379078, guitarist), Jesse Paris Smith (ID 2483495, musician/activist).

Early Work and Debut (1974–1975)

First release: Hey Joe / Piss Factory (1974, Mer Records) — a 7” produced with Lenny Kaye, who had been her consistent guitar collaborator since her earliest poetry readings. The single established the template: declamatory vocals over minimal rock backing.

Horses (1975, Arista Records) — debut LP; produced by John Cale. Core band:

Horses remains her most canonical record. Verlaine’s guest appearance links her world directly to Television; Lanier’s to Blue Öyster Cult.

Patti Smith Group: Core Albums (1976–1979)

Radio Ethiopia (1976, Arista — SPARTY 1001)

Produced by Jack Douglas; engineered by Jay Messina. Same core band. Cover photo: Judy Linn. The album insert was photographed by Lizzy Mercier Descloux, who was then rooming with Smith in New York.

Easter (1978, Arista — SPART 1043)

Produced by Jimmy Iovine; mixed by Iovine and Shelly Yakus. Bruce Brody replaced Richard Sohl on keyboards. “Because The Night” co-written with Bruce Springsteen — became her biggest commercial single. Photography by Robert Mapplethorpe and Lynn Goldsmith.

Wave (1979, Arista — SPART 1086)

Produced and engineered by Todd Rundgren. Art direction by Robert Mapplethorpe. Final PSG studio album before the hiatus. Smith married Fred Smith in 1980 and moved to Detroit.

Hiatus and Dream Of Life (1980–1988)

After marrying Fred Smith (guitarist of MC5 and Sonic’s Rendezvous Band) in 1980, Smith withdrew from the music industry and lived in Detroit. The couple co-wrote and co-produced Dream Of Life (1988, Arista — cat 209 172) with Jimmy Iovine.

Fred Smith died November 4, 1994, in Detroit from heart failure. Richard Sohl also died in 1990. The losses ended the hiatus.

Return: Gone Again and Later Work (1996–)

Gone Again (1996, Arista — 07822-18747-2): return album; produced by Lenny Kaye and Malcolm Burn. Notable guests:

Track contributorRole
Tom Verlaineguitar (multiple tracks)
Jeff Buckleyvoice (“Beneath The Southern Cross,” “Fireflies”)
John Caleorgan

Tony Shanahan joined as bassist from this period onward (183 shared releases on Discogs — her most active later-era collaborator alongside Kaye and Daugherty).

Subsequent studio albums continued with the reconstituted band (Kaye, Daugherty, Shanahan, Ray).
