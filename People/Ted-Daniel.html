Ted Daniel

Trumpet, flugelhorn, French horn, bugle. Born June 4, 1943 in Ossining, New York. American jazz trumpeter active from 1969 through at least 2025, primarily in free jazz and avant-garde contexts. Discogs artist ID: 297036. Also credited as: T. Daniel, T. Daniels, Ted Daniels, Teddy Daniel, Teddy Daniel Jr., Daniel.

Overview

Ted Daniel is a New York-based trumpeter whose career spans the full arc of post-Coltrane free jazz. He appeared on Sonny Sharrock’s 1969 debut Black Woman while barely into his career, then moved through the orbit of Impulse! Records sideman work (Archie Shepp, Dewey Redman) before establishing himself as a bandleader with Ted Daniel Sextet (1970, Ujamaa Records) — one of the earliest self-released records in the loft jazz era. Through the 1970s he was a core member of Andrew Cyrille’s group Maono and of The Jazz Composer’s Orchestra. In 1980 he appeared on Defunkt’s debut, bridging free jazz and the No Wave / funk-punk scene. He later joined Henry Threadgill Sextett for Rag, Bush And All (1989). His own ensembles — the Ted Daniel Quintet, Ted Daniel Trio, and Ted Daniel’s Energy Module — continued recording into the 2010s, with the 2014 NoBusiness Records LP Innerconnection as a late-career statement.

Daniel plays a notably wide range of brass instruments: in addition to trumpet and flugelhorn he has recorded on French hunting horn, Moroccan bugle, and has credited effects such as “electric multi-divider.” He produced his own 1970 Ujamaa debut and has written liner notes for his own records.

Early Career (1969–1972)

Daniel’s first documented recording is Sonny Sharrock’s Black Woman (1969, Vortex Records (2)), credited as “Teddy Daniel.” He played trumpet on side B (tracks B1–B3), alongside Milford Graves on drums, Norris Jones on bass, Dave Burrell on piano, and Linda Sharrock on voice. The session was produced by Herbie Mann.

The following year Daniel recorded his own debut, Ted Daniel Sextet (1970, Ujamaa Records), which he produced himself. The personnel included Otis Harris (alto saxophone), Hakim Jami and Richard Pierce (bass), Warren Benbow and Kenneth Hughes (percussion). Daniel played trumpet, flugelhorn, French hunting horn, and Moroccan bugle — the polyinstrumentalism that would define his recorded work. Ujamaa Records appears to have been a self-run outlet.

Also in 1970 he appeared on Brute Force (Embryo Records), a record by the group Brute Force (Discogs artist ID: 1059319).

In 1971 Daniel was part of the large ensemble on Archie Shepp’s Things Have Got To Change (Impulse!), one of Shepp’s most politically charged records. The session, recorded at Van Gelder Studio and engineered by Rudy Van Gelder, also included Dave Burrell, James Spaulding, Beaver Harris, Grachan Moncur III, Howard Johnson, and Joe Lee Wilson.

Impulse! Years and Loft Jazz Context (1973–1976)

Daniel appeared on three significant Impulse! releases during this period:

  • Dewey Redman — The Ear Of The Behearer (1973, ABC/Impulse!). Alongside Sirone (bass), Eddie Moore (drums), Jane Robertson (cello). Produced by Ed Michel.
  • Archie Shepp — Coincide (1975, ABC/Impulse!).
  • Archie Shepp — Crystals (1974, ABC Impulse!).

He also joined The Jazz Composer’s Orchestra for Clifford Thornton’s The Gardens Of Harlem (1975, JCOA Records), a major JCOA production featuring Carla Bley (piano), Dewey Redman, Wadada Leo Smith, Hannibal Marvin Peterson, Jerry Gonzalez, Bob Stewart, and Carlos Ward, among others.

In 1975 Daniel appeared on Celebration (IPS (2)), and in 1976 he co-led Junction on IPS (2) with Andrew Cyrille and Maono — the group also featuring David S. Ware (tenor saxophone) and Lisle Atkinson (bass). Junction was produced partly by Alan Douglas and partly by Andrew Cyrille and Michael Cuscuna. Daniel played trumpet, flugelhorn, and electric multi-divider effects.

Andrew Cyrille & Maono (1976–1983)

Daniel was a core member of Andrew Cyrille’s working group Maono (Andrew Cyrille & Maono, Discogs artist ID: 1472125), alongside David S. Ware and bassist Nick DiGeronimo. The group recorded three albums:

Metamusicians’ Stomp was recorded in Italy and released on the Black Saint label. Special People (1981, Soul Note) was produced by Giovanni Bonandrini.

The collaboration culminated in The Navigator (1983, Soul Note), credited to Andrew Cyrille featuring Ted Daniel, Sonelius Smith (piano), and Nick DiGeronimo (bass). With liner notes by jazz critic and photographer Valerie Wilmer.

Japan, Europe, and Broader Sideman Work (1976–1978)

A 1976 Japanese session produced Song Of Pat (Nadja label), on which Daniel played trumpet, French horn, and flugelhorn. In 1977 he appeared on Tapestry (Sun Records (2), France), playing flugelhorn. In 1978 a Japanese compilation Jazz Fellows (I.J.E.) captured him on trumpet and flugelhorn; this release has 21 known pressings, suggesting significant circulation in Japan.

Defunkt and the No Wave Crossover (1980)

Daniel played trumpet on Defunkt’s self-titled debut LP (1980, Island Records), one of the defining records of the New York funk-punk scene. He appears on tracks A1, A3, A4, B1, and B2. The band was led by trombonist and vocalist Joseph Bowie, with Melvin Gibbs (bass), Ronnie Burrage (drums), and Kelvyn Bell (guitar). The album was produced by Byron Bowie.

Henry Threadgill Sextett (1989)

Daniel (credited as “Ted Daniels”) played trumpet and flugelhorn on Henry Threadgill Sextett’s Rag, Bush And All (1989, Novus). The ensemble also included Fred Hopkins (bass), Diedre Murray (cello), Bill Lowe (bass trombone), Newman Baker and Reggie Nicholson (drums/percussion).

Later Work and Energy Module (1993–2025)

Daniel remained active through the 1990s and 2000s with sporadic appearances:

  • Every Silver Lining Has A Cloud (1993, Not On Label) — flugelhorn
  • 25 Years (1994, Polystar, Japan) — flugelhorn
  • Vietnam: The Aftermath (2001, Justin Time) and Vietnam: Reflections (2005, Justin Time) — trumpet
  • How Many Stories Do You Read On My Face (2006, Intoxicate Records, Japan) — brass

Ted Daniel’s Energy Module (Discogs artist ID: 8082305) recorded Innerconnection (2014, NoBusiness Records), a 2-LP set released in Lithuania. The group featured Oliver Lake (alto saxophone, soprano saxophone, piccolo flute, cowbell), Richard Pierce (bass), Tatsuya Nakamura (drums), and Daniel Carter (tenor saxophone). The repertoire included compositions by Albert Ayler, Dewey Redman, Ornette Coleman, and Sunny Murray, alongside three Daniel originals. Daniel wrote both the liner notes and several compositions.

A 2023 Japanese reissue of Junction appeared on the Octave Lab / Whynot labels, attesting to continued interest in the 1976 Maono material.
