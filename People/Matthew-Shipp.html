Matthew Shipp

Discogs ID: 222595 Born: 7 December 1960, Wilmington, Delaware, USA

American jazz pianist and composer. Central figure in post-free jazz and the downtown New York improvised music scene since the late 1980s. Studied at New England Conservatory of Music. Moved to New York in 1984. Long associated with William Parker, Whit Dickey, and the David S. Ware Quartet; served as Artistic Director of Thirsty Ear’s Blue Series in the early 2000s.

Biography

Early Career and First Recordings (1984–1992)

Shipp moved to New York City in 1984 to begin his professional career. His first recorded work as a leader was Sonic Explorations (1988, Cadence Jazz Records), a duo with alto saxophonist Rob Brown (4); both artists also produced the album alongside label head Bob Rusch. The record was a debut for both players in the leader role.

He appeared on several early recordings for Swedish label Silkheart (1990’s Great Bliss Volumes 1 and 2) and Japanese label DIW, primarily in contexts led by others. His first solo piano album, Symbol Systems, came in 1995 on the small US imprint No More Records, produced by Alan Schneider (2) and engineered by Chris Flam. The record demonstrated his interest in systematic, almost structural free improvisation — 14 solo pieces composed and performed entirely by Shipp.

David S. Ware Quartet and Early Trio Work (1992–1997)

Throughout the 1990s Shipp was a core member of the David S. Ware Quartet, one of the definitive New York free jazz groups of the era. This association brought him visibility on labels including DIW, Black Saint, and Columbia (via Ware’s Go See The World, 1998 and Surrendered, 2000).

In 1997 he recorded By The Law Of Music for hat ART (cat. hat ART CD 6200), credited to the Matthew Shipp “String” Trio. The session was produced by Art Lange and Werner X. Uehlinger (as Pia And Werner X. Uehlinger) and engineered by Carl Seltzer.

The Multiplication Table and hatOLOGY (1998)

The follow-up trio session, The Multiplication Table, appeared in 1998 on hatOLOGY (cat. hatOLOGY 516), again produced by Art Lange and Werner X. Uehlinger. Susie Ibarra replaced Mat Maneri on drums.

AUM Fidelity and the Quartet (1997–2001)

Shipp began recording for AUM Fidelity in 1997 (Wisdom of Uncertainty), laying the groundwork for a sustained parallel catalog there. By 2000 the Matthew Shipp Quartet configuration was in place for Pastoral Composure (Thirsty Ear, 2000):

Thirsty Ear Blue Series and Electronica Crossover (2001–2006)

Shipp became Artistic Director of the Blue Series at Thirsty Ear, curating a run of releases that brought free jazz into dialogue with hip-hop, electronic music, and sound art. His own Blue Series album Optometry (2002) was co-led with DJ Spooky (Paul D. Miller):

The collaboration with Spring Heel Jack (Masses, Thirsty Ear, 2001) brought an even wider ensemble — Evan Parker, Tim Berne, Mat Maneri, Roy Campbell, Daniel Carter, Guillermo E. Brown — under a through-composed framework credited jointly to Spring Heel Jack and The Blue Series Continuum.

Later Thirsty Ear Solo Work (2005–2011)

After the Blue Series concluded, Shipp continued recording for Thirsty Ear with shifting personnel. Art Of The Improviser (2011, 2xCD) paired a trio live set with solo piano material.
