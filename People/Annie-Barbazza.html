Biography

Early Years and Conservatory Training (to 2013)

Anna “Annie” Barbazza was born in Milan on 15 June 1993. She began her formal training as a drummer while simultaneously studying piano at the Conservatorio Nicolini in Piacenza. The dual grounding — classical piano discipline alongside rhythm-section instincts — shapes the multi-instrumentalist character of her later solo work.

Her first Discogs-documented appearance is a backing vocal credit on Forty Years Of Voice Impossible – Vox 40 Live (2013, Ma.Ra.Cash Records), a compilation celebrating the career of Italian progressive vocalists.

The Greg Lake Connection and Manticore Debut (2015–2018)

The pivotal early break came when Greg Lake — of ELP and King Crimson — discovered her vocal ability and invited her to perform alongside him at a concert in Piacenza, Italy. That performance became the basis for Live in Piacenza (2017, Manticore, label ID 35662), on which Barbazza is credited for backing vocals and post-production alongside Lake. The association with Lake led directly to her first principal release: Moonchild (2018, Manticore), a duo album co-credited with pianist Max Repetti. The project had originally been conceived by Lake for himself; falling ill, he redirected it to Barbazza, and produced it himself alongside Massimo Marchini. Lake died later that year.

During this same period, Barbazza contributed to John Greaves’ Life Size (2018, Manticore) — playing acoustic guitar (track A3), piano (track B5), vocals (tracks A3, A6, B1, B5), and lyric credits (track A3). This marked the start of one of her central creative partnerships.

Annie’s Playlist Series and Early Solo Work (2015–2017)

In parallel with the Lake/Greaves connections, Barbazza launched her own Annie’s Playlist performance concept — a live set (later recorded) of cover songs spanning her formative influences, from The Beatles to Slapp Happy, The Residents, and Zappa. The concept was conceived for her by Lake.

Folly Bololey and International Breakthrough (2019)

The major breakthrough in terms of critical profile was Folly Bololey (Songs From Robert Wyatt’s Rock Bottom) (2019, Dark Companion, label ID 926330) — a complete re-staging of Robert Wyatt’s 1974 masterpiece Rock Bottom, co-credited to North Sea Radio Orchestra, John Greaves, and Annie Barbazza. Barbazza takes lead vocal across the album and also plays Farfisa organ; Greaves plays bass and contributes spoken word and vocal parts; the North Sea Radio Orchestra provides the chamber ensemble. The album’s creative director is Craig Fortnam (NSRO arranger/director), with William D. Drake (piano), Cheb Nettles (drums), Harry Escott (cello), Laurent Valéro (violin, viola), Nicola Baigent (clarinets), Tommaso Franguelli (vibraphone), and Luke Crookes (bassoon).

The record drew major critical attention in European avant-prog circles. Barbazza and Greaves performed the project at the Rock In Opposition Festival in Lyon (May 2019) and at Café Oto, London — giving her a foothold in the UK experimental music scene.

Liner notes on Folly Bololey were written by Robert Wyatt himself and by novelist Jonathan Coe.

Vive — Solo Debut (2020)

Vive (2020, Dark Companion) is Barbazza’s debut solo album proper, and the fullest statement of her range as composer-performer. She is sole primary artist and plays grand piano, autoharp, guitar, bass, bass drum, synthesizer, harmonium, field recordings, and e-bow across the record, in addition to lead vocals.

Vive received 5-star international reviews and was named album of the month by Italian audio magazine Audio Review. Blow Up magazine listed it in their Best Albums of 2020. Track 13 was among the last sessions Lake produced before his death.

Tarkus Revisited and Continued Collaboration (2021)

Tarkus Revisited (2021, Ma.Ra.Cash Records, MRC103) is a percussion-and-voice re-staging of the ELP Tarkus suite, co-credited to Tempus Fugit Percussion Ensemble, Annie Barbazza, and Lino Capra Vaccina. Barbazza provides lead vocals and backing vocals on tracks 1–8. Vaccina plays vibraphone, gong, chimes, percussion, bells, and finger cymbals on track 9 — “Descent Into The Void,” a co-composition by Vaccina, Francesco Brianzi, Massimo Marchini, and Tommaso Franguelli. Jordi Tagliaferri plays drums and percussion on tracks 1–7. Francesco Brianzi served as co-producer for those tracks; Marchini produced overall. The album extends the ELP tribute thread running through her Manticore-era work.

Earthly Powers — Greaves Duo Album (2023)

Earthly Powers (2023, Dark Companion) is the most fully formed statement of the Barbazza/Greaves duo format — co-credited equally to both artists. The album was recorded across multiple sessions (Alberto Callegari engineering tracks 1–7 and 18–19; Simone Sfameli tracks 8–14; Daniele Menegatti tracks 15–17; Luca Frigo additional engineering). Barbazza contributes vocals, acoustic guitar, piano (track 18), and Indian harmonium (track 19); Greaves provides vocals and piano throughout.

Other Projects and Ensemble Work

Beyond the Greaves partnership and solo recordings, Barbazza has been an active ensemble collaborator:

  • Moonchild (Discogs ID: 84692) — listed as a member in Discogs; the Moonchild project was the 2018 duo recording with Max Repetti.
  • North Sea Radio Orchestra (Discogs ID: 616338) — full member per Discogs; participated in the Folly Bololey project and the 2019 live performances.
  • Michael Mantler’s New Songs Ensemble (Discogs ID: 15034601) — member; recorded and performed with Michael Mantler’s ensemble in 2023.
  • Boxhead Ensemble (Discogs ID: 147341) — listed as a group member on Discogs.
  • Markus Stockhausen quintet — recorded and performed as a member (2024 onwards per profile).
  • Giorgio Fico Piazza (PFM founder) — invited to sing lead vocals on “Impressioni di Settembre” on his Autumn Shades album, widely noted for the interpretation.
  • John Greaves and Friends touring band — featuring Mel Collins and Jakko M. Jakszyk (both ex-King Crimson), Annie Whitehead, and others; Italian touring circuit.
  • Robyn Hitchcock, Eugenio Finardi, Aldo Tagliapietra, Osanna — live appearances alongside.

Scene Context

Barbazza emerged from Piacenza — the same city where the Greg Lake concert took place — and her Italian context is closely tied to the progressive rock intelligentsia of northern Italy. Her first documentary trace is in the Italian prog-anniversary compilation orbit (Ma.Ra.Cash Records); her major development ran through the Lake/Manticore circle and the Dark Companion label network around Massimo Marchini.

The John Greaves partnership places her directly within the Henry Cow / Rock In Opposition lineage. Greaves was a founding member of Henry Cow (1969–1976), the central British ensemble in the European avant-prog and RIO movements, and has maintained those connections across fifty years. The Folly Bololey project, staging Robert Wyatt’s Rock Bottom with the North Sea Radio Orchestra, is the most explicit RIO-lineage work in her catalogue — a record that connects the Canterbury scene (Wyatt) with British chamber-art-rock (NSRO) through Italian execution.

The Vive cast broadens the map considerably: Daniel Lanois (Ambient/Americana production, Brian Eno collaborator), Fred Frith (Henry Cow, avant-guitar, ReR Megacorp), and Paolo Tofani (Area, Italian progressive rock) give the album unusual range. Tofani (Discogs ID: 490217), who later adopted the devotional name Krishna Prema Dasa, was a founding member of Area — the Milan-based band that was Italy’s closest equivalent to Henry Cow in the 1970s.

Barbazza’s ensemble memberships in Michael Mantler’s New Songs Ensemble and (per profile) the Markus Stockhausen quintet place her in the contemporary European chamber/jazz-adjacent world; the Markus Stockhausen connection (son of Karlheinz Stockhausen) extends the experimental classical thread.
