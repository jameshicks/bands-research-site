Steve Kilbey

Discogs ID: 242433 Real name: Steven John Kilbey Role: Vocalist, Bassist, Guitarist, Keyboardist, Songwriter, Producer, Painter, Poet Born: 13 September 1954, Welwyn Garden City, England (UK-born, raised Australia) Active: 1980–present Associated Acts: The Church, Jack Frost (5), Isidore, The Refo:mation, Hex (7), Gilt Trip, Mimesis, Baby Grande, Speed Of The Stars, Curious (Yellow)

Biography

Early Career and The Church (1980–1987)

Steve Kilbey was born in Welwyn Garden City, England, and grew up in Australia. He formed The Church (Discogs ID: 258153) in Sydney around 1980, serving as primary vocalist and bassist throughout the band’s entire run. The Church debuted with the single “She Never Said” (1980, Parlophone) and released their first album Of Skins And Heart in 1981 on Parlophone, with Kilbey credited for vocals, bass, and keyboards. A second album The Blurred Crusade (1982) followed on Parlophone, establishing the band’s jangle-pop/psychedelic rock sound on the Australian and European market (European releases on Carrere). Early compilation Sing-Songs (1982) collected early material.

Seance (1983, Parlophone) and Remote Luxury (1984, Parlophone/Warner Bros.) developed the band’s atmospheric guitar rock further; the latter was licensed to Warner Bros. for North America. Heyday (1985, Parlophone) continued their Australian success.

In parallel, Kilbey launched his solo career with the single “This Asphalt Eden” (1985, Red Eye Records, RED 13) — his first release on what would become his primary independent home. His debut solo LP Unearthed (1986, Polydor/Red Eye Records, RED LP 1) was recorded and mixed by Kilbey himself, with contributions from brother Russell Kilbey (fretless bass on “Famine”) and Karin Jansson (guitar, keyboards on “Swampdrone”; vocals on “Othertime”). He wrote all songs and designed the sleeve. Earthed (1987) and The Slow Crack (1987) followed on Red Eye, with Kilbey as producer, engineer, mixer, and performer across all three — an unusually hands-on approach that persisted into most of his solo work.

Breakthrough and Arista Years (1988–1992)

Starfish (1988, Arista, AL-8521) was The Church’s commercial peak and international breakthrough, produced by the band alongside Greg Ladanyi and Waddy Wachtel. “Under The Milky Way” — co-written by Kilbey and Karin Jansson — became the band’s signature track and remains their best-known song. The album reached the US charts and established the band’s profile in North America. Kilbey played bass guitar and lead vocals; other Church members on the record were Marty Willson-Piper (guitar), Peter Koppes (guitar), and Richard Ploog (drums).

Gold Afternoon Fix (1990, Arista/BMG) and Priest = Aura (1992, Arista) continued the band’s run on the label. During this period Kilbey also produced Curious (Yellow)‘s Charms & Blues (1990, Polydor/Red Eye Records, RED CD 13) — the band included Mark Dawson (2), Karin Jansson (vocals, acoustic guitar), and Pryce Surplice; Kilbey contributed drum programming, instruments, and co-wrote several tracks. He also contributed to Vast Halos (1990, Rykodisc) as performer and sole songwriter, and produced Bohemia (1992, Epic) for another project, playing bass, guitars, keyboards, and vocals across its tracks.

Jack Frost and Continued Solo Output (1990–1996)

Jack Frost (Discogs ID: 636488) was a collaboration with Grant McLennan of The Go-Betweens. Their self-titled debut album Jack Frost (1990, PolyGram/Polydor/Red Eye Records, RED CD 18) was co-produced by both principals alongside Pryce Surplice. Kilbey played vocals, guitar, bass, keyboards, and drums; McLennan played vocals, bass, and guitar; the record also featured chamber ensemble (cello, oboe, violin) and Karin Jansson on backing vocals. A second Jack Frost album Snow Job was released in 1996 on Beggars Banquet/Karmic Hit, with Kilbey again as producer and performer.

Solo albums in this period: Remindlessness (1990, Polydor/Red Eye Records) — Kilbey as engineer, producer, mixer, and performer — and Narcosis + (1997, Vicious Sloth Collectables), Gilt Trip (1997, Vicious Sloth Collectables), the latter a duo with brother Russell Kilbey.

The Church in Transition and Cooking Vinyl Era (1996–2006)

The Church moved to Deep Karma / White (6) for Magician Among The Spirits (1996), then to Cooking Vinyl for Hologram Of Baal (1998), A Box Of Birds (1999, Thirsty Ear in North America), After Everything Now This (2001), Parallel Universe (2002), Forget Yourself (2003, spinART in US), Song In Space (2003), and Uninvited, Like The Clouds (2006). During this period Tim Powles replaced Richard Ploog on drums. Members Peter Koppes, Marty Willson-Piper, Powles, and Kilbey formed a three-way side project The Refo:mation (Discogs ID: 637624).

Isidore (Discogs ID: 636474) was a project with US guitarist Jeffrey Cain and Kilbey’s brother John Kilbey; their self-titled album (2004, Karmic Hit, KH019) was a double CD blending alternative rock, ambient, and psychedelic influences, recorded and mixed by Cain in Los Angeles, with Steve Kilbey contributing vocals and co-writing.

Kilbey’s own Karmic Hit label (Discogs ID: 17577, 55 releases) served as home base for much of his solo work across this period: Dabble (2001), Freaky Conclusions (2003), Painkiller (2008).

Prolific Solo Period and Kilbey/Kennedy (2008–present)

From 2009 onwards Kilbey became extremely prolific as a self-releasing artist, issuing multiple albums per year through his own imprints — Inevitable Recordings, and later Foghorn Records (2) — alongside continued Church output. His ambient/instrumental work appeared on Unseen Music Unheard Words (2009, Inevitable Recordings) and the related Present series; Hear In Noiseville (2009, 24 Hour Service Station) was a lyric-focused release. Asleep In The Downlights (2011, Hammock Music) was a collaborative ambient record with the US duo Hammock. White Magic (2011, Second Motion Records) and Glow And Fade (2017, Golden Robot Records) are notable solo album releases from this run.

His deepest ongoing collaboration was with Melbourne ambient/electronic musician Martin Kennedy (alias All India Radio, Discogs ID: 433990), with whom he amassed over 77 shared release credits — releasing the Kilbey Kennedy series and related material including Unseen Music Unheard Words, You Are Everything (2013, Inevitable Recordings), The Rare Earth (2014), Inside We Are The Same (2015), and the EP Beyond The Blue Horizon (2018, self-released). Kennedy writes the music; Kilbey supplies lyrics and vocals.

The Church moved to Easy Action for recent output: The Hypnogogue (2023), As Seen On TV (2025). Kilbey personally released The Road To Tibooburra (2025, Foghorn) and Beauty in Forlorn Places (2025, self-released).

He has also been active as a visual artist: his paintings appear on Church covers (El Momento Descuidado, Uninvited, Like The Clouds) and his own solo sleeves. His website operates as The Time Being (thetimebeing.com).

Open Questions

  • Hex (1989, First Warning Records) credits Kilbey as producer, writer, and “all other instruments” — the Hex (7) group with Donnette Thayer; further detail on Thayer’s role would require web research.
