Biography

The Coral years (2000–2008)

Bill Ryder-Jones was lead guitarist and songwriter for The Coral (Discogs ID: 138082) from their formation in West Kirby. He played on the band’s first five albums on Deltasonic Records:

  • The Coral (2002) — debut
  • Magic And Medicine (2003) — credited as “Band [The Coral Are], Guitar; Written-By”
  • Nightfreak And The Sons Of Becker (2003)
  • The Invisible Invasion (2005)
  • Roots & Echoes (2007)

The Coral released through Deltasonic Records, later also on Columbia.

Departure timeline (confirmed via web sources): Ryder-Jones had two distinct breaks from the band. The first was in June 2005, when he temporarily stopped touring due to a stress-related illness; he was replaced for The Invisible Invasion tour by David McDonnell, with a statement at the time suggesting he might still help with future recordings. He returned as a full member in early 2006 and the band wrote new material that became Roots & Echoes (2007). His permanent departure was announced on 9 January 2008 — The Coral issued a statement confirming he had left and that the remaining five members would continue. Wikipedia on The Coral cites the reasons as “experiencing panic attacks before playing live, and finding that his desire to be part of a commercially successful band had disappeared.” [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Coral, as of 2026-07-14; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Ryder-Jones, as of 2026-07-14; NME news item published 9 January 2008: https://www.nme.com/news/music/the-coral-35-1341340]

No direct primary-source quote from Ryder-Jones himself giving the exact date has been located in accessible interviews — the 9 January 2008 date rests on the Wikipedia articles (which cite the NME report) and the NME URL metadata. Subsequent interviews (NARC Magazine, Big Issue 2024, WordPress blog 2010) confirm he left in 2008 but do not supply a more precise date or reproduce a band/management press release verbatim.

He left before the band’s Butterfly House (2010). The Coral continued after his departure and released Distance Inbetween (2016) and subsequent records without him.

Solo debut and film work (2011–2013)

Ryder-Jones launched his solo career on Double Six (2), a Domino imprint. His debut:

  • A Leave Taking Soundtrack (2011, Double Six DS056T) — score for Leave Taking, a short film (2009) directed by Laurence Easeman (a friend of Ryder-Jones); the four compositions were released first as a free download in July 2011, then on 12” and digital on 26 September 2011; Ryder-Jones credited as Performer, Piano, Violin, Written-By; accompanied by a small chamber ensemble (Abby Best – cello, Dean Ravera – double bass, Megan Swinchin – viola, Laura Smith (6) – violin) [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Ryder-Jones, as of 2026-06-28]
  • If… (2011, Double Six DS037LP/CD) — first proper solo album; also released on Domino (DS037CDP)
  • If… Remixes (2012, Double Six DS061)

In parallel, he contributed guitar to two tracks (A2, B1) on Alex Turner’s Submarine soundtrack (2011, Domino) — produced by James Ford.

In 2013 he added additional guitar to track 8 (“No. 1 Party Anthem”) of Arctic Monkeys’ AM (Domino WIGCD317 / WIGLP317), one of the album’s 32 pressings. The AM credits also feature James Ford (producer, keyboards) and Josh Homme (backing vocals, tracks 3 & 11).

A Bad Wind Blows In My Heart and wider production work (2013–2015)

  • A Bad Wind Blows In My Heart (2013, Domino WIG308D / WIGLP308 / WIGCD308) — second solo album; principal collaborators were Liam Power, Andrew Royden, and James Ford (additional production). Darren Jones (2) and James Ford contributed.
  • He Took You In His Arms (2013, Domino RUG523CDP) — single
  • Wild Swans (2013, Domino RUG543CDP) — single
  • West Kirby County Primary (2015, Domino WIGLP346 / WIGCD346) — third solo album; Ryder-Jones played all instruments except drums, which were split between Andrew Royden and Jack Prince. Guro Gikling provided vocals on several tracks. Emma Leatherbarrow co-wrote one track (B2). Additional engineering by Chris Taylor (30); additional production by James Ford. Mastered by Guy Davie.

During 2014–2015 he produced several other artists:

  • Burn Out The Bruise (2014, Heavenly) — unidentified act
  • Wire Frame Mattress (2014, Heavenly)
  • Jasper / Standing Knees (2015, Heavenly)
  • In The Seams (2014, Surface Area) — arranged and produced
  • Various other Domino-adjacent acts

He also played guitar on The Curse Of Love (2014, Skeleton Key Records) by The Coral — acoustic and electric guitar.

Yawn era and Bird Dog (2016–2019)

  • The Bird Dog Tapes Volume 1 (2016, Bird Dog Recordings BDR0005) — collaborative release; features Nick Power (2) and Jack Prince
  • Wild Roses (2016, Domino RUG729CDP) and You Can’t Hide A Light With The Dark (2016, Domino RUG746CDP) — singles
  • Two To Birkenhead (2015, Domino RUG702CDP) — single
  • Yawn (2018, Domino WIGLP383) — fourth solo album; Ryder-Jones handled all writing, instruments, production and engineering. Mixed by Craig Silvey (assisted by Max Prior). Mastered by Guy Davie. Engineers: Liam Power and Rich Hurst (4). Also released as double LP (WIGLP383X / WIGLP383XM).
  • And Then There’s You (2018, Domino Recording Company RUG985D1/CDP) — single
  • Don’t Be Scared, I Love You (2018, Domino RUG990CDP) — single
  • Yawny Yawn (2019, Domino WIGLP457 / WIGCD457-2) — expanded/remix edition of Yawn

He also produced other artists in this period: Stranger Today (2018, Cannibal Hymns), I Really Like It (2018, Cannibal Hymns), Like An Island (2018, Seahorse Music (2)) among others.

Iechyd Da and recent work (2022–2024)

  • Q&A Session (2023, Shipwrecked Records) — collaborative live/studio release
  • Iechyd Da (2024, Domino WIGLP485) — fifth solo album; title is Welsh for “good health.” Ryder-Jones wrote, arranged, produced, played all instruments, and co-mixed with James Ford. Engineers: Felipe Chapman-Fromm and Nathanial Cummings. Mastered by Guy Davie. Features Michael Head, Amy Chalmers, Andy Frizell, Martin Smith (2), Ian Stephens (2), Poppy Hankin, Jacob Downes, Phil Murphy (5), Carl Roberts (3), and Bidston Avenue Primary School Choir.
  • Peachy (2024, Moshi Moshi Records) — produced

He also contributed writing to The Coral’s Sea Of Mirrors (2023, Modern Sky UK / Run on Records).
