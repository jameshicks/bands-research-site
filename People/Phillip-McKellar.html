Phillip McKellar

Australian sound engineer and record producer, active from the early 1980s Melbourne post-punk scene through to the present. Also a performer and bandmember in Informatics and Toy Division (2). Best known in the broader context for his work as producer and engineer on the Dirty Three’s founding recordings. Discogs artist ID: 530593

Biography

Early Melbourne Underground (1982–1990)

McKellar’s earliest documented activity is as a performer and mixer in Toy Division (2), the Melbourne new wave / electro duo he formed with Dick Sallows. Their sole release, the cassette Cute (1983, Temporary Art Tapes), credits McKellar under the name “Phil” for backing vocals, additional sounds, and mixing — placing him squarely inside the early-80s Melbourne cassette underground.

Concurrently or shortly afterward he joined Informatics, a post-punk / industrial five-piece active from 1982 (debut LP Dezinformatsiya, Au Go Go). That band remained a long-running concern: Informatics released material on Germany’s Last Chance Records in 1985, resurfaced on compilations in the 2000s-2010s, and still recorded as recently as 2023–24 on Minimalkombinat (Germany). McKellar is a performer and co-producer across their catalog, most recently on 30 Second Jetpack (Minimalkombinat, 2023).

His freelance engineering work began in earnest by the late 1980s, with a credit on the 1989 Jas H. Duke retrospective on NMA Publications and production/recording work on the Pot Plant Records single Full Circles / Travel Agent (1990).

Breakout: Early 1990s Australian Rock

Through the early 1990s McKellar established himself as a go-to engineer and producer for the Australian indie-rock scene centred on rooArt, Mushroom, and Triple J. Key engagements in this period:

  • Ratcat — Recorded the live album Alive (1991, rooArt/Phonogram)
  • Jas H. Duke — Engineer, The Best Of Jas H. Duke (1989, NMA Publications)
  • Triple J sessions — Produced and recorded multiple tracks on Totally Wireless (The Triple J Acoustic Sessions) (1993, ABC Music/Triple J Music), an ABC Records compilation
  • Kev Carmody — Stompem Ground (1993, ABC Music) — Engineer, Producer

By 1993 he was recording at the intersection of the Melbourne underground and the national radio ecology, bringing acts from both worlds through the same hands.

Dirty Three: The Definitive Early Credit (1994)

McKellar’s most significant production credit is the Dirty Three’s self-titled debut LP (1994, Torn & Frayed), which he produced and engineered — billed on the sleeve as “Phil Mackellar.” This is the release that established Dirty Three’s sound: Warren Ellis’s violin-driven instrumental intensity over Mick Turner’s guitar and Jim White’s drums. McKellar’s 17 shared Discogs releases with Dirty Three are almost entirely pressings and reissues of this debut, plus two outliers: Ulterior Motives (2012, mixed one track) and Dirty Three Live At Sydney Festival (2016, mixed by McKellar for ABC Music). His direct creative role — Producer, Engineer — was concentrated in the band’s founding moment.

Mid-to-Late 1990s: Major-Label and International Work

After the Dirty Three debut, McKellar moved into a heavier rotation of major-label and internationally distributed work:

  • Silverchair — Recorded and mixed tracks 2–4 on the Israel’s Son single (1995, Murmur), alongside Kevin Shirley’s separate production of the lead track
  • Hole (2) — Mixed tracks on the Australian leg of Live Through This (Geffen, 1995 Australian pressing), alongside J Mascis, Pat McCarthy, Scott Litt, and Hal Willner — a significant international credit
  • Ash / various — Recorded and mixed the 1977 (Special Edition) (1997, Infectious Records/Facedown)
  • Grinspoon — Produced and recorded/mixed Guide To Better Living (1997, Grudge) — the band’s debut album, one of his highest-profile production credits
  • Grinspoon — Repeat EP (1997), Grand Slam (1999), and New Detention (2002, all Grudge/Universal), establishing a multi-album production relationship
  • Jebediah — Ivy And The Big Apples (1996, Polydor), Hang Around (1995), The Unfinished Spanish Galleon of Finley Lake (1995)
  • You Am I — Mixed #4 Record (1998, BMG/Ra Records)
  • The Juliana Hatfield Three — Produced and engineered one track on Spin The Bottle (1993, Atlantic/Mammoth) — his only clearly North American major-label credit

This period shows McKellar as a studio-based producer/engineer working across guitar-rock’s spectrum: post-grunge, alt-rock, hard rock. His name appears on Geffen, Atlantic, Polydor, Phonogram, and Mushroom releases from 1993–2000.

2000s–2010s: Continued Studio Work, Harder Rock

McKellar continued production and engineering through the 2000s with a range of Australian acts:

  • Bodyjar — Shut Your Mouth (2000, Epic), Between Birth And Death (2003, Epic)
  • Reel Big Fish / ska-punk — Everything Everyway (2003, Epic)
  • Various Australian rock acts on Below Par Records, Eleven: A Music Company, and Rapido
  • The Jezabels — mixed early self-released single The Man Is Dead (2009)
  • Ash — mixing credits on Dust (2017, HNE Recordings)

The arc from 2000 onward suggests a continued base in Sydney/Melbourne studios rather than a transition to live sound. The Recovery credit (2016, ABC Music/Universal) is listed as “Producer [Live Music]” — possibly a live TV or broadcast context.

Ongoing: Informatics and Beyond (2010s–Present)

McKellar has remained active as a performer within Informatics through the 2010s and 2020s. The Dance To A Dangerous Beat compilation (Dark Entries, 2013) compiled their early 80s material, and new Informatics recordings appeared on Germany’s Minimalkombinat in 2023 (30 Second Jetpack) and 2024 (Lawn And Order). The Flying Nun Records release The Peel & JJJ Sessions (2025, New Zealand) carries a Producer, Engineer credit — confirming continued active studio work.

Open Questions

  • What studio(s) did McKellar work out of in the 1990s? Discogs records mention “Mangrove Studios, Sydney” for at least one 2001 credit — his base city is unclear (Melbourne roots, possibly Sydney by mid-90s).
  • The “Producer [Live Music]” credit on Recovery (2016 ABC Music) may indicate live broadcast / concert production work rather than strictly studio. Nature of this role is unconfirmed.
  • Exact relationship to the Torn & Frayed label (operators, ownership) is not in Discogs. Did he have any ongoing label involvement beyond the Dirty Three record?
  • The 1993 Scuzz Productions pressing of Dirty Three has no additional credits on Discogs — it’s unclear whether McKellar was involved in that earlier iteration or only the 1994 Torn & Frayed version.
  • Flying Nun The Peel & JJJ Sessions (2025) — which artist(s) is this for? The primary artist is not fully clear from Discogs metadata alone.
