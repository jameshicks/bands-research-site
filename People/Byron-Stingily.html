Jul 26, 202610 min read

Byron Stingily

Discogs artist ID: 11004 Real name: Byron A. Stingily Also credited as: “Lord” Byron Stingily, B. Stingily, alias B. Pearl

Chicago-born vocalist, songwriter, and house music figure. Lead singer of Ten City (1987–1996, reformed 2021). Pioneer of the soulful house and gospel-house aesthetic; ran Deep Soul (via Nervous Records) with Mike Dunn and Mike Weiss, 1998–2000. Also ran Stingily Music (via Home Recordings (2)) in 2006–2007. Father of artist Diamond Stingily.

Biography

Origins and Ragtyme (mid-1980s)

Stingily grew up in Chicago and came up singing in the city’s vibrant house music scene. He first appeared on Bright Star Records in 1987 — a label tie credited “I Can’t Stay Away” to him as a featuring vocal. The same year he was writing and producing for Dezz 7. He met producer Marshall Jefferson at Trax Records after Jefferson heard his vocals; the two recorded approximately five songs together (unreleased) before producing “Devotion.” Stingily sang with local group Ragtyme alongside guitarist Herb Lawson and keyboardist Byron Burke.

Atlantic originally wanted to sign Stingily to a solo deal, but he preferred performing with a group; he recruited Lawson and Burke, and Ragtyme became Ten City.

Ten City on Atlantic (1987–1992)

Ten City signed to Atlantic in the late 1980s. Their debut album Foundation (Atlantic, 1989) was produced principally by Marshall Jefferson with the band, featuring dense horn and string arrangements and live drums from Earl Young. The record contained “That’s the Way Love Is,” “Suspicious,” and “Where Do We Go?” — a mix of Marshall Jefferson-produced and Stingily/Burke-produced tracks, with additional mixing contributions from Steve “Silk” Hurley and Timmy Regisford.

The second album State of Mind (Atlantic, 1990) continued with Jefferson producing and added a wide backing choir including Shawn Christopher, Yvonne Gage, and Kenny Bobien. Executive producers were Merlin Bobb and Sylvia Rhone. Singles “Superficial People” and “Whatever Makes You Happy” continued strong R&B and dance circulation.

Atlantic dropped Ten City in 1992, shortly after releasing the No House Big Enough / My Peace Of Heaven / Only Time Will Tell run on EastWest Records America.

Columbia Period and Breakup (1993–1996)

Ten City signed to Columbia. The 1993 single “Fantasy” (co-produced with Ten City, with Masters At Work adding additional production on the remix versions) did well on the club scene. Their fourth album That Was Then, This Is Now (Columbia, 1994) brought in a range of collaborators: Masters At Work co-produced “Love In A Day,” Larry Heard co-produced “Under You,” Kerri Chandler co-produced “Say Something,” Tommie Jenkins co-produced “Joy & Pain,” and Ross Anderson & Simon Law co-produced “When I’m Gone, I’m Gone.” The album bridged house and contemporary R&B. After its release the group dissolved. (Note: Rex Rideout is mentioned in some sources as contributing to this album, but no corresponding Discogs credit has been confirmed.)

Around 1996 Ten City released All Loved Out and Nothing’s Changed on Spiritual Life Music, but the group had effectively ceased.

Solo Debut and Nervous Records (1995–2000)

Stingily launched his solo career while Ten City was still nominally active. His first solo 12” was “Don’t Fall In Love” (Nervous Records, 1995), produced by David Morales with co-writing from Terry Burrus; it became a Garage house classic. Mike Dunn produced the flip “I’m Wit’cha Baby,” with backing from Ron Carroll.

He became a mainstay on Nervous Records through the late 1990s. His debut solo album The Purist (Manifesto / Nervous, 1997) was a showcase of producers from across the soulful house world:

Notable singles from this era include “Get Up” / “Get Up (Everybody)” (Nervous, 1996) — produced by Paul Simpson and Zack Toms, remixed by Derrick Carter — and “Sing A Song” (Manifesto, 1997) produced by Mousse T., which achieved wide European distribution and remixes.

His second solo album Club Stories (Nervous, 1999) was a three-disc vinyl set pulling together material across his solo singles catalog.

Stingily co-ran Deep Soul (a Nervous Records sublabel) with Mike Dunn and Mike Weiss 1998–2000, which put out various Chicago house projects including Party Lites, The Disco Kidz, and Singing After Dark.

Gospel-House Period: It’s All Jesus (2004)

In 2004 Stingily made his most overtly spiritual release: It’s All Jesus (Spiritually Sound Records, cat# SSND12-01), credited “DJ Spen Presents Byron Stingily.” Produced and arranged by DJ Spen (Sean Spencer), Gary Hudgins, and Irvin Madden — the team operating under the The MuthaFunkaz and DJ Spen & The MuthaFunkaz umbrella. Writers additionally include Gary Deane (credited as G. Ahrens). Stingily served as Executive-Producer. Three mixes: Extended Club Vocal, To The Church Dubb, Mix Show Edit. The release intersects house music’s church-gospel lineage with Baltimore’s soulful house sound.

The same year Stingily appeared on the Keep Hope Alive compilation (King Street Sounds / BPM / West End / LIFEbeat, 2004), a benefit record with wide distribution including a Japan edition via Victor.

Mid-Period Solo (2005–2009)

He continued releasing on King Street/BPM (e.g., “Spread Love,” 2005, with Underground Dance Artists United For Life), Odyssey Records (“I’ll Give You,” 2005), and his own Stingily Music (“Just Believe,” 2007). He collaborated with Terry Hunter on “Piece of Meat” (BPM King Street Sounds, 2007), recorded with Quentin Harris, and worked with UK vocalist Van Christie on “Hate Won’t Change Me” (2006). In Japan, his recordings with Yasushi Ide’s Lonesome Echo Production (2002) received separate release.

He appeared on Omni Music Solutions tracks through 2009–2013, including “Pray” (2009) and remixes of “Father” (2013).

Ten City Revival and Redimension Period (2021–present)

Ten City reformed in 2021, with Stingily and Marshall Jefferson working remotely (Jefferson in Manchester, Stingily in Chicago). Byron Burke and Herb Lawson were not involved. The reunion album Judgement (Ultra Records, 2021) included a DJ Spen & Thommy Davis remix of “Devotion” and a Marshall Jefferson remix of the same track. A sixth album Love Is Love (Helix Records (7), 2023) followed.

As a credited writer-composer, Stingily’s catalogue continues to resurface: Bob Sinclar and Dimitri From Paris both used his work (“Love Is The Answer,” 2015; “Be Free” 2022), and Masters At Work remixed “Love Changed Me” (Redimension, 2021).
