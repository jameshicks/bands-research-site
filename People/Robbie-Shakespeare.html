Robbie Shakespeare

Robbie Shakespeare

Oct 12, 20264 min read

Robbie Shakespeare

Discogs ID: 30420
From: Kingston, Jamaica
Role: Bassist, producer
Partner: Sly Dunbar (as Sly & Robbie)

Jamaican bassist and producer. One half of Sly & Robbie, the most recorded rhythm section in reggae and dub history. Produced “Stare Me Out” and “Stare Me Out (Crash Mix)” on Chakk’s 10 Days In An Elevator (MCA, 1986) alongside Sly Dunbar — an unusual cross-genre production credit.

Sly & Robbie / Chakk Collaboration (1986)

How the connection came about

Chakk’s interest in Sly & Robbie grew out of their admiration for the Compass Point All Stars sound. The FON Studios team was described as “really into that Compass Point sound” featuring Sly & Robbie and Gwen Guthrie. Specifically, Chakk were inspired by the Gwen Guthrie EP Padlock (1983), remixed by Larry Levan, which featured Sly Dunbar and Robbie Shakespeare. Wax Poetics; initial search synthesis from multiple contemporaneous sources.

After signing to MCA Records in 1985 and using their advance (reportedly £100,000) to build FON Studios in Sheffield, Chakk requested Sly & Robbie as producers for their debut album. According to accounts of the sessions, the band brought Sly & Robbie up the M1 motorway from London to Sheffield to work at FON. Andrew Darlington blog, sourcing 1984–86 era Chakk coverage

No broker or intermediary has been documented by name in available sources. The request appears to have come directly from Chakk to MCA, who facilitated the booking.

What was produced and what happened

Sly & Robbie recorded sessions for the full album that were ultimately not used — described in fan accounts as “dumped for being too weird.” The material on the released album (10 Days In An Elevator, MCA, 1986) was re-recorded and/or remixed without them. The sole surviving production credit on the released LP is “Stare Me Out” (and its “Crash Mix”), which lists Sly Dunbar and Robbie Shakespeare as producers. funderglass.blogspot.com, comment thread

Mark Brydon (Chakk bassist, co-founder of FON) described the MCA album experience as “kind of sanitised” and “an incredible learning experience about the nature of major labels — playing that hard game of keeping them happy and yourselves happy, which are not necessarily compatible.” He confirmed Chakk “asked for Sly and Robbie and got them, but… didn’t have them playing on the album” in the wider sense — only the one track survived. Record Collector “Under the Radar” feature, as cited in search sources

Source quality note

No first-person interview with Sly Dunbar or Robbie Shakespeare about this session has been located. The above reconstruction draws on: the Wax Poetics piece (named byline, reputable music press), the Record Collector feature (named byline, reputable music press), and the Andrew Darlington blog (journalist, contemporaneous interviews republished 2013 — some content explicitly flagged as rumour/unconfirmed in the original text). The Darlington “bussed 180 miles up the M1” phrasing appears in a rumour-list section of that piece and should be treated as likely but unverified until a primary source surfaces.
