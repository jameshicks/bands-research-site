Markus Detmer

Markus Detmer

Discogs ID: 503864
ANV: Detmer, M. Detmer
Active: 1990–present
Based: Cologne (early 1990s–2003) → Berlin (2003–2011) → Perpignan, France (2011–present)

German musician, journalist, and independent label founder. Best known as the founder and operator of Staubgold (1998–present) and as a core member of the Cologne/Berlin electroacoustic duo Klangwart (with Timo Reuber, 1996–present). Also a member of early 1990s indie rock group Amrol and the undocumented duo Detmerkugel (with Christof Kugel).

Early Years: Cologne Cassette Scene (1990–1997)

The earliest documented Discogs credit for Detmer is a cover-art contribution to The Incredible World Of Irre-Tapes (1990, STAUBGOLD!), a Various Artists cassette compilation compiled by Matthias Lang — a notable hint that the “Staubgold” name predates the formal label by nearly a decade, initially as an informal cassette-scene identifier.

In 1993, Detmer played keyboards in Amrol, a Cologne indie rock/new wave group. Amrol’s sole release was Demoral (Progressive Entertainment PE 09, cassette), recorded and mixed by Ingo Vieten. The lineup included Gregor Lehnick (bass), Oliver Schrade (guitar), Markus Neuer (drums), and Rainer Hungenberg (vocals). Lehnick was simultaneously releasing ambient/experimental music as Lufties In Europa on the same label (Progressive Entertainment PE 01).

In 1996, Detmer co-founded Klangwart in Cologne with Timo Reuber. The duo’s first releases appeared in 1997 on Klangstelle, their own cassette/CD-R imprint (later formalized as a Staubgold sublabel). The debut was Inkiek (Klangstelle 1, 1997), a mini-CD with electronics and Minimoog contributions from guest Niklas Georg, mastered by Marcus Schmickler. The follow-up Immerland (Klangstelle 2, 1997, cassette) notably featured co-writing credit for Christof Kugel on Side A (“Immer Weiter”), suggesting the Detmerkugel partnership was active around this time even if unreleased as such. The third Klangstelle release was Zwei (Klangstelle 5, 1999, LP), two side-long drone pieces with no additional credits.

Staubgold: Founding and Cologne Period (1998–2003)

Detmer founded Staubgold formally in Cologne in 1998. The label’s first official catalog entry was Sphere of Magnetick Virtue (staubgold 01, 1998) by Erixma, for which Detmer provided cover concept and realization. The early catalog concentrated on the nascent German microsound/turntablist scene, with Institut Für Feinmotorik’s Wenig Information: Kein Titel (staubgold 5, 1998) — turntables and found objects — as the defining early statement.

The Cologne-period roster expanded rapidly to include Mapstation, Reuber, Rafael Toral, Oren Ambarchi, and Ekkehard Ehlers, establishing Staubgold as the premier label for German electroacoustic and microsound work at the turn of the millennium.

Staubgold: Berlin Period and Growth (2003–2011)

The label moved to Berlin in 2003, broadening both the roster and the aesthetic palette. Key arrivals included Kammerflimmer Kollektief (20 releases, 2003–2015, the label’s most prolific relationship), Dean Roberts, Franz Hautzinger, Joseph Suchy, and the US hip-hop act Dälek (whose collaboration Derbe Respect, Alder with Faust became one of the label’s landmark releases).

Detmer served as Compilation Producer and Executive Producer on Faust’s retrospective Patchwork 1971–2002 (staubgold 37, 2002), co-produced with Hans Joachim Irmler, which surveyed the band’s history from the Polydor era through their 1990s reactivation.

In 2005, Detmer co-produced Childish Music (staubgold 60), a Various Artists compilation compiled by Ekkehard Ehlers, alongside co-producer Tim Tetzner. Cover art was by Michael Krebber; mastered by Stefan Betke (Pole).

In 2009, Alec Empire (Atari Teenage Riot) curated Rauschgold: Alec Empire Plays Staubgold (staubgold 92), compiling material exclusively from the Staubgold back-catalog; Detmer served as Compilation Producer.

Staubgold: Perpignan Period (2011–present)

The label relocated to Perpignan, in the south of France, in 2011 — driven by Detmer’s own relocation rather than any organizational change. The Perpignan-era catalog shows a shift toward French, Latin American, and Catalan artists: Telebossa, Meridian Brothers, Los Pirañas, Pascal Comelade, and collaborations with Colombian and Venezuelan musicians. Staubgold’s address registered on Discogs is 11 rue des Lices, 66000 Perpignan.

The label has continued to issue reissues alongside new work: We Know Nonsense (staubgold 123, 2013) and Monkey Banana Kitchen (staubgold 130, 2014) for The 49 Americans and Family Fodder respectively, both with Detmer as Reissue/Executive Producer.
