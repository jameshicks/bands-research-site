Vic Chesnutt

American singer-songwriter. Born James Victor Chesnutt, November 12, 1964, Jacksonville, Florida. Died December 25, 2009, Athens, Georgia. Discogs artist ID: 272599

One of the most distinctive voices of American indie-folk, Chesnutt was a prolific, sardonic, and deeply personal songwriter. A car accident at age 18 left him partly paralyzed from the waist down; he performed for the rest of his life from a wheelchair. He recorded roughly a dozen albums between 1990 and 2009, working with a remarkably varied cast of collaborators — from the Athens, Georgia R.E.M. orbit to the Montreal post-rock collective around Godspeed You Black Emperor! and A Silver Mt. Zion.

Early Life and the Athens Scene

Chesnutt grew up in Zebulon, Georgia and eventually settled in Athens, where he became a regular at the 40 Watt Club. The Athens scene of the early 1980s — centered around R.E.M. and a loose network of art-rock bands — was his primary community. In 1983, a drunk-driving accident left him paralyzed from the waist down, but he continued writing and performing.

His connection to Michael Stipe was central to his early career. Stipe spotted Chesnutt at the 40 Watt and produced his first two albums on Texas Hotel Records.

Discography

Texas Hotel Records era (1990–1995)

Little (1990, Texas Hotel) — debut album. Produced entirely by Michael Stipe. Spare home-recording aesthetic; Chesnutt alone with guitar and vocals. Discogs release ID: 1212470

West of Rome (1992, Texas Hotel) — second album. Again produced by Michael Stipe, who also plays keyboards. Band includes Tina Chesnutt (bass), Kelly Kniepp and Ray Neal (guitars), Jeffrey Richards (drums), Mandi Durrett (cello), and Liz Durrett (violin). Discogs release ID: 6653071

Drunk (1993, Texas Hotel / Caroline) — third album. Self-produced with co-producer Caz Mack; engineered by Don Zientara among others. Chesnutt drew the cover artwork himself. Discogs release ID: 397326

Is The Actor Happy? (1995, Texas Hotel / Caroline) — produced by John Keane. Band includes Tina Chesnutt (bass), Jimmy Davidson (drums), Alex McManus (multi-instrumentalist), and David Henry (cello). Discogs release ID: 1020325

The Salesman And Bernadette (1998, Rough Trade)

A key mid-career record, produced by Kurt Wagner of Lambchop. Full Lambchop-adjacent ensemble: Alex McManus (guitar, accordion, euphonium), Marc Trovillion and Tina Chesnutt (bass), Allen Lowrey (drums), Paul Burch (piano, vibraphone), Deanna Varagona (saxophone), Jonathan Marx and Dennis Cronin (brass), Paul Niehaus (pedal steel and guitar), John Delworth (organ), Scott Chase (percussion). Guest vocal by Emmylou Harris on track 10. Discogs release ID: 1212613

About To Choke (1996, Rough Trade Germany / Capitol)

Partly self-produced, partly produced with John Devries and Mark LaFalce. Mixed by Bob Mould. Discogs release ID: 938075

Silver Lake (2003, New West Records)

First album on New West Records. Mastered by Joe Gastwirt. Discogs release ID: 693355

Ghetto Bells (2005, New West Records)

A major late-career statement. Produced by John Chelew. Key collaborators: Bill Frisell (guitar), Van Dyke Parks (piano, accordion, organ), Tina Chesnutt (electric bass), Dominic Genova (acoustic bass), Don Heffington (drums), Liz Durrett (vocals). Discogs release ID: 1485876

North Star Deserter (2007, Constellation)

First album on Constellation Records, recorded in Montreal. Produced by Jem Cohen with co-producers Efrim Menuck, Guy Picciotto, and Thierry Amar. Full backing band: Thee Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra + Tra-La-La Band (i.e., members of A Silver Mt. Zion) — Bruce Cawdron, Chad Jones, Eric Craven, Geneviève Heistek, Nadia Moss, T. Griffin — alongside Guy Picciotto and Howard Bilerman (recorded by). Mastered by Harris Newman. Discogs release ID: 1013491

Dark Developments (2008, Orange Twin Records)

Collaborative album with Elf Power and The Amorphous Strums. Vic co-engineered with Derek Almstead; full ensemble includes Andrew Rieger (12-string guitar), Sam Mixon, Curtiss Pernice, Laura Carter (keyboards, clarinet, accordion), Josh Lott (drums). All songs written by Chesnutt. Discogs release ID: 1487736

Skitter On Take-Off (2009, Vapor Records)

Surprising late-career collaboration. Produced by Jonathan Richman and drummer Tom Larkins, who also play on the record. A lo-fi duo session. Discogs release ID: 1992610

At The Cut (2009, Constellation)

Final studio album, again on Constellation Records with the Montreal circle. Band: Efrim Menuck (guitar, keyboards, vocals), Guy Picciotto (guitar), Thierry Amar (contrabass), Chad Jones (guitar), David Payant (drums, keyboards, vocals), Nadia Moss (organ, piano, vocals), Jessica Moss (violin, vocals). Recorded by Howard Bilerman; mastered by Harris Newman. Released a few weeks before Chesnutt’s death. Discogs release ID: 1939515

Brute (2) — Collaboration with Widespread Panic

At some point in the mid-1990s, Chesnutt formed Brute (2) with members of Widespread Panic — David Schools, Domingo S. Ortiz, John Bell, John Hermann, Michael Houser, and Todd Nance. Discogs artist ID for Brute (2): 700755. Their record Nine High A Pallet (1995, Capricorn Records) is documented under that Discogs entry. Discogs release ID: 862686

Sweet Relief II — The Tribute Album

In 1996, Sweet Relief II: Gravity Of The Situation (The Songs Of Vic Chesnutt) was released on Columbia Records (Discogs master 120021), a benefit record for the Sweet Relief Musicians Fund. Contributors included R.E.M. (covering “Sponge”), Garbage (“Kick My Ass”), the Smashing Pumpkins and Red Red Meat (“Sad Peter Pan”), Madonna and Joe Henry (“Guilty By Association”), Kristin Hersh (“Panic Pure”), Sparklehorse (“West of Rome”), Indigo Girls (“Free of Hope”), Nanci Griffith and the Blue Moon Orchestra (“Gravity of the Situation”), and Cracker (“Withering”). Mary Margaret O’Hara covered “Florida.” The record raised Chesnutt’s profile considerably outside his underground following.

Death

On December 25, 2009, Chesnutt died in an Athens, Georgia hospital from an overdose of muscle relaxants that had left him in a coma. He had attempted suicide on Christmas Eve. He was 45. At The Cut had been released on Constellation weeks earlier.
