Jimmy Garrison

Full name: James Emory Garrison Born: March 3, 1933 — Miami, Florida, USA Died: April 7, 1976 — New York City, New York, USA (aged 43) Instrument: Double bass Discogs ID: 254065

American jazz bassist, best known for his decade-long tenure as the anchor of John Coltrane’s classic quartet (1961–1967). Garrison’s arco and pizzicato work — especially his extended solo bass interludes, which Coltrane used as feature moments within sets — became a defining sound of post-bop jazz. After Coltrane’s death he played with Alice Coltrane, Archie Shepp, Elvin Jones, and others, and taught at Bennington and Wesleyan colleges.

Biography

Early Life and Pre-Coltrane Work (1933–1961)

Born in Miami, Garrison grew up in Philadelphia, the same city that produced John Coltrane, McCoy Tyner, and Benny Golson. His Discogs trail begins in the late 1950s with a cluster of session credits for major jazz labels.

By 1958 he was recording for Riverside Records, appearing on bassist Philly Joe Jones’s Blues For Dracula (1958). Work followed with Curtis Fuller (Blues-ette, 1959, Savoy Records), Kenny Dorham, and Stan Getz. The 1959–1961 period documents him as an in-demand Philadelphia-area sideman moving through Savoy, Riverside, Blue Note, and smaller labels: he appears on Jackie McLean’s Swing, Swang, Swingin’ (1960, Blue Note), and on sessions for Jaro International, Time Records, Old Town, and others.

A notable 1962 credit outside the Coltrane world is Ornette Coleman’s Ornette On Tenor (Atlantic), with Don Cherry and Ed Blackwell — a document of Garrison moving in the same avant-garde orbit before his full commitment to the Coltrane group.

The John Coltrane Quartet (1961–1967)

Garrison joined Coltrane’s working group in 1961, completing the classic quartet alongside McCoy Tyner (piano) and Elvin Jones (drums). His first major recording with the group was Africa/Brass (1961, Impulse!), credited to The John Coltrane Quartet. He remained through the entire Impulse! period.

A Love Supreme (1964) remains the most documented: the quartet performs as Garrison (bass), Tyner (piano), Elvin Jones (drums), and Coltrane (tenor saxophone/composer). Garrison’s bass solo in “Resolution” — an unaccompanied spot Coltrane built into the arrangement — is among the most celebrated bass feature moments in jazz.

An additional 1962 session with Garrison in a non-quartet context: Coleman Hawkins’s Further Definitions (Impulse!), a septet session with Benny Carter.

Bill Dixon’s Intents And Purposes (1967)

Alongside the final Coltrane recordings, Garrison appeared on Bill Dixon’s Intents And Purposes (1967, RCA Victor, LSP-3844), credited to The Bill Dixon Orchestra. Garrison and Reggie Workman share the bass parts (Garrison on tracks A1 and B1), alongside Byard Lancaster (bass clarinet), Catherine Norris (cello), and Robert Pozar (drums), with Dixon on trumpet and flugelhorn. This was Dixon’s only major-label album as a leader, and Garrison’s appearance there connects the two figures of the avant-garde scene who overlapped at Coltrane’s periphery.

Post-Coltrane Work (1967–1976)

After Coltrane’s death in July 1967, Garrison continued recording across several contexts:

  • Alice Coltrane — He appeared on A Monastic Trio (1968, Impulse!), Cosmic Music (1968, Coltrane Recording Corporation — also credited with violin), and Universal Consciousness (1971, ABC/Impulse!). The Universal Consciousness credit specifies him on double bass on four of the record’s five tracks (alongside Jack DeJohnette, Leroy Jenkins, and string players).
  • Archie Shepp — Garrison is listed in Discogs groups as a member of the Archie Shepp Quintet and The Archie Shepp Group, documented in Shepp’s Attica Blues (1972, ABC/Impulse!) and live European dates.
  • Elvin Jones — He formed the Elvin Jones-Jimmy Garrison Sextet (Discogs artist ID: 2317449), a documented working band. Recordings appeared on Impulse! (Illumination!, 1964, already dates from the quartet era; the post-Coltrane Elvin Jones context appears in Live In Paris releases and European concert recordings).
  • European tours — Multiple live documents from BYG Records (Paris) and other European labels, including Live In Paris Part 1 & 2 (BYG, 1972) from the Elvin Jones/Garrison Sextet concerts.
  • Hampton Hawes — referenced in Garrison’s Discogs profile as a collaborator in the post-1967 period.
  • Rolf & Joachim Kühn Quartet — Garrison is primary artist on Impressions Of New York (1968, Impulse!), co-credited alongside the Kühn brothers and drummer Aldo Romano; produced by Bob Thiele.
  • Ornette Coleman — The Atlantic compilation The Art Of The Improvisers (1970) includes Garrison’s 1962 Ornette On Tenor tracks.

As a primary artist on his own dates, Garrison’s Discogs record shows two releases on Muse Records: Golden Moments (1982) and I’ll Remember (1984). These postdate his death (April 1976) and are presumably archival material from earlier sessions.

He taught music at Bennington College and Wesleyan University in the early 1970s, creating a connection to the academic wing of avant-garde music — notably the same institutional milieu where Bill Dixon held his long teaching post at Bennington.

Open Questions

  • The exact dating and label details of Garrison’s solo sessions for Muse Records (Golden Moments, I’ll Remember) are unclear — both were released posthumously. The original recording dates are not in Discogs.
  • Discogs does not document Garrison’s teaching activity at Bennington and Wesleyan; this cannot be researched further without web access.
  • The Hampton Hawes connection mentioned in his Discogs bio is not fully documented in the releases visible in his discography — no Hampton Hawes album under Garrison’s credit appears in the trawl.
  • The Elvin Jones/Jimmy Garrison Sextet’s full catalog (BYG, Historic Performances Records, live documents from Sweden and Japan) deserves a dedicated trawl — multiple pressings and live documents across 1967–1972 suggest a substantial post-Coltrane working period.
