Sam Mills

Born: 1963, London Role: Guitarist, producer In 23 Skidoo: 1979 – June 1982 (founding member; dismissed)

23 Skidoo (1979–1982)

Founding guitarist. Credited on “Tearing Up The Plans” (Fetish FP 20, 1982) — the last recording with the original lineup, made while the Turnbull brothers were in Indonesia. Dismissed alongside Tom Heslop in June 1982 when the Turnbulls returned with a new musical direction rooted in gamelan and world music percussion.

After 23 Skidoo: Anthropology and India

Mills studied anthropology at the LSE, also spending time in Japan and Bangladesh. His PhD (completed 1992) was specifically on Sufism in Bengal — a natural academic frame for the Baul tradition he was already encountering through travel. He travelled to India in 1986 and met Paban Das Baul in Calcutta in 1988. That encounter became the central thread of his post-23 Skidoo life.

Baul music is a devotional folk tradition of West Bengal and Bangladesh, practiced by wandering minstrels whose syncretic blend of Sufi, Hindu Tantric, and Vaishnava elements produces a music of intense rhythmic and spiritual directness.

Gum (1994)

Reunited with Tom Heslop (and a third collaborator, Jerry Heslop) to release Gum (Bold Records (2), GBR 1001, 1994) under the group name Gum (19). 12” EP (Limited Edition, Numbered); genres: Rock; styles: Experimental, Indie Rock. Mixed by Rob Keyloch. All four tracks written jointly by Sam Mills, Tom Heslop, and Jerry Heslop. Side A (“Home Side”): “Barbecue (Raw Mix)” / “One On One (Real Mix)”; Side B (“Away Side”): “One On One (Unreal Mix)” / “Barbecue (Full Grill)“.

Paban Das Baul — Real World Records

Long-term collaborator with Paban Das Baul, one of the most internationally recognized contemporary Baul singers. Mills served as guitarist, programmer, producer, and composer across multiple Real World Records releases:

Fritz Catlin co-produced Real Sugar and contributed to some of this run — the only post-dismissal collaboration between two of the three departed members.

Real Sugar was also released on Caroline Records (US) and received wide distribution. The series ran almost a decade, with Virgin distributing the later titles internationally.

  • Member of Tama (11): a world music trio comprising Malian singer Tom Diakite, Mills on guitar, and Djanuno Dabo on percussion

Susheela Raman

Mills is the long-term partner and primary guitarist/producer of Susheela Raman — a Tamil-British singer whose debut album Salt Rain was shortlisted for the Mercury Prize in 2001. Their collaboration spans at least seven albums:

Fritz Catlin contributed to Vel (recorded by, edited by). The Susheela Raman project effectively became Mills’s primary creative identity from the late 1990s onward — a sustained, critically received body of work connecting Indian classical/folk aesthetics to Western production.
