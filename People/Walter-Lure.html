Walter Lure

New York guitarist and singer. Born April 22, 1949; died August 21, 2020. Real name: Walter C. Luhr Jr. Discogs artist ID: 340327. Brother of Richie Lure.

Biography

Early Career and The Heartbreakers (1975–1984)

Walter Lure joined The Heartbreakers (2) in July 1975 as second guitarist, debuting at CBGB on 4 July 1975. He was not a replacement — he joined while Richard Hell was still in the band. When Hell departed in May 1976 over a dispute about stage and vocal dominance, Lure sided with Johnny Thunders and drummer Jerry Nolan. Billy Rath came in on bass to complete the classic four-piece lineup.

The band recorded L.A.M.F. (Track Record, 1977, Discogs ID: 877185) with Lure credited as Lead Guitar, Vocals alongside Thunders. Producers were Daniel Secunda and Speedy Keen. The album is infamous for its muddy, disputed mix; Jungle Records issued L.A.M.F. Revisited in 1984 with a remixed version (Discogs ID: 1014469), and L.A.M.F. (The Lost ‘77 Mixes) in 1994 (Discogs ID: 467492) with the original board mixes. Lure received Written-By credits on several tracks across these releases.

Lure also appeared on So Alone (1978), Johnny Thunders’ first solo album, playing guitar on one track (“Can’t Keep My Eyes On You”) alongside a stellar cast of guests including Phil Lynott, Steve Jones, Paul Cook, Peter Perrett, Chrissie Hynde, Patti Palladin, and Steve Marriott.

The Heartbreakers gigged extensively through the late 1970s and into the 1980s. Key live documents include:

  • Live At Max’s Kansas City (Beggars Banquet, 1979; Discogs ID: 1630664)
  • D.T.K. (Live At The Speakeasy) (Jungle Records, 1982; Discogs ID: 1751173)
  • Vintage 77 (Jungle Records, 1983; Discogs ID: 1448510)
  • Live At The Lyceum Ballroom 1984 (ABC (9), 1984; Discogs ID: 1694081)

Lure also appeared on The New Too Much Junkie Business (ROIR, 1983; Discogs ID: 593218), playing guitar on specific tracks alongside Thunders with producers Jimmy Miller and Thunders himself. He played guitar on the Thunders solo album Diary Of A Lover (PVC Records, 1983; Discogs ID: 5425322) on the “A” sides.

The compilation D.T.K. – L.A.M.F. (Jungle Records, 1986; Discogs ID: 1204049) combined the Speakeasy live set with a version of the album, giving Lure Written-By credits.

The Blessed (3) and Side Work (1979)

While still associated with the Heartbreakers orbit, Lure played in The Blessed (3), a short-lived New York band featuring vocalist Billy Stark, drummer Brad “BJ” Barnett, and bassist Howie Pyro (later of D Generation). The Blessed released one single, “Deep Frenzy / American Bandstand” (Daven Records, 1979; Discogs ID: 2572512), produced by David Eng, with Lure on guitar, piano, and vocals. It is their only Discogs credit.

The Heroes (3) (1978)

The Heroes (3) was a loose band that included Lure, fellow Heartbreaker Billy Rath on bass, Henri Paul (who had played guitar on So Alone), and Steve Nicol. Their sole Discogs entry is Too Much Junkie Bizness (1978, not on label; Discogs ID: 31340824), an early live or demo recording. The release has no additional credits listed.

Johnny Thunders continued collaboration (1983–1991)

Lure remained part of Thunders’ touring band through the 1980s. He played guitar and vocals on Stations Of The Cross (ROIR, 1987; Discogs ID: 1534871), recorded September 30, 1982 at the Mudd Club, NYC. The band for that session was Thunders, Lure, Jerry Nolan, and Steven Tallarico. Mixed by Lech Kowalski, who produced it for the soundtrack to his film Story of a Junkie.

He appeared on What Goes Around… (BOMP!, 1991; Discogs ID: 409316), a posthumous Thunders anthology, as performer.

A 1991 single, “Crazy Little Baby” (Baylor Records; Discogs ID: 2182193), captures a late-period collaboration between Lure, Tony Coiro (bass/vocals), and Joey Pinter (guitar/vocals) — the three would soon form the nucleus of The Waldos.

The Waldos (1994–2020)

After Thunders’ death in April 1991, Lure formed The Waldos with ex-Knots (2) members Joey Pinter (guitar) and Tony Coiro (bass/vocals), plus drummer Jeff West. The band’s debut album Rent Party (Sympathy For The Record Industry, 1994; Discogs ID: 2770805) had a raw pub-rock and punk feel. Personnel: Lure (guitar/vocals), Coiro (bass/vocals), Pinter (guitar), Jeff West (drums).

Earlier that same year the Live In NYC (Dionysus Records, 1994; Discogs ID: 3571819) live album appeared, credited to “Rick Blaze And Walter Lure.”

The Waldos also co-produced the Heartbreakers archival release L.A.M.F. Live At The Village Gate 1977 (Cleopatra, 2015; Discogs ID: 7644973) — Lure and Andy Shernoff served as co-producers alongside engineer/producer Brooke Delarco. That recording captured the classic lineup (Thunders, Lure, Rath, Nolan) at a 1977 Village Gate show.

Wacka Lacka Loom Bop A Loom Bam Boo (Cleopatra, 2018; Discogs ID: 12469953) was the final Waldos studio album and Lure’s last major release before his death. Personnel: Lure (guitar/vocals), Takanori Ichiuji (bass/vocals), Joe Rizzo (drums/vocals), Takto Nakai (guitar/vocals), with guest contributions from Andy Shernoff (backing vocals, overdubs, co-producer), Tricia Scotti (backing vocals), Arno Hecht (saxophone), Randy Pratt (harmonica), and Brian Hurd (harmonica). Mixed by Joe Blaney; mastered by Paul Gold.

The Last Ditches (2016)

The Last Ditches was a project featuring Lure alongside Binky Philips (guitar/vocals), Bobby Rondinelli (drums), and Randy Pratt (bass/vocals). They released one album, Spilt Milk (Hyperspace Records / Rocker Records, 2016; Discogs ID: 12894873).

Live In Berlin (2008)

Live In Berlin (Tornado Ride Records, 2008; Discogs ID: 2630907) was credited to Walter Lure as a solo primary artist. The touring band for this Italian release included Annette Gucci (guitar/backing vocals), Dee Jaywalker (guitar/backing vocals, lead vocals on several tracks), Rina Regina (bass/backing vocals), and Paolo Serlino (drums). Lure produced and mixed alongside Joe Stab.

L.A.M.F. Legacy Performances (2015–2020)

In his final years Lure led multiple L.A.M.F. tribute performances:

  • L.A.M.F. Live At The Village Gate 1977 (Cleopatra, 2015) — archival release of the 1977 original show (see Waldos section above)
  • Yonkers Demo 1976 (Cleopatra, 2019; Discogs ID: 14022508) — the pre-album Heartbreakers demo with Richard Hell still on bass, featuring Thunders, Lure, Hell, and Nolan
  • L.A.M.F. Live At The Bowery Electric (Jungle Records, 2017; Discogs ID: 11191817) — a 2017 performance credited to Clem Burke, Tommy Stinson, Walter Lure, and Wayne Kramer, with additional guitarists Cheetah Chrome and Jesse Malin and Liza Colby on vocals; produced by Margaret Saadl Kramer and James Walsh
  • L.A.M.F. Live In Tokyo (Secret Records (8), 2020; Discogs ID: 16273283) — released posthumously, credited to Walter Lure featuring Mick Rossi; Lure produced; Dan Graziano on bass, Mark Reback on drums, Mick Rossi on guitar/vocals; mixed and mastered by Craig Parker Adams

Walter Lure died on August 21, 2020, of liver cancer.
