Chris Stamey

American singer, songwriter, guitarist, and record producer. Born December 6, 1954, Chapel Hill, North Carolina; grew up in Winston-Salem, NC (full name: Christopher Charles Stamey). Discogs artist ID: 253466. Owner of Modern Recording studio, Chapel Hill. Active 1972–present. Author of memoir A Spy in the House of Loud: New York Songs and Stories (University of Texas Press, 2018).

Biography

Early Years: Winston-Salem / North Carolina (1972–1976)

Stamey grew up in Winston-Salem, NC, where he attended R.J. Reynolds High School (class of 1973) alongside future dB’s bandmates Peter Holsapple, Will Rigby, and Gene Holder, as well as Mitch Easter. The school had a vibrant music culture — students including Stamey congregated at a spot dubbed “Combo Corner” — and Stamey’s high school band teacher Bob Smith gave him a grounding in music theory that shaped his compositional approach. He began recording at age nine using a mono Sony reel-to-reel. In 2023 he and Holsapple were inducted into the R.J. Reynolds High School Arts Hall of Fame.

Stamey’s first recorded appearance is as bassist on the 1972 self-titled LP by Rittenhouse Square (label: R 2 Records), a Winston-Salem-area group that also included Holsapple and Easter — making it a pre-Sneakers proto-lineup of the same core circle. Following Rittenhouse Square, Holsapple and Rigby played in a proto-punk band called Little Diesel, which at various times also included Stamey and Easter; Little Diesel recorded an album, No Lie, in 1974 (unreleased until 2006).

By 1975 Stamey had enrolled at UNC-Chapel Hill to study music composition and theory, and that year teamed with drummer Will Rigby to form Sneakers (12). The lineup expanded to include Mitch Easter, Rob Slater (guitar), and Robert Keely (bass); in 1976 Sneakers released a self-titled 6-song EP (Carnivorous Records) engineered by Don Dixon, featuring all-Stamey originals (one co-written with Rigby). Easter later replaced Slater for the second release. In 1978 they released the EP In The Red on Car Records. Sneakers placed Easter, Rigby, and Stamey in the same room years before New York, and is a direct precursor to the dB’s.

NYC: Erasers, Alex Chilton, Car Records (1976–1978)

In December 1976, Stamey relocated to New York City, where he played with The Erasers — a CBGB-orbit band fronted by Susan Springfield — and then was hired as bassist for Alex Chilton’s touring band, performing as “Alex Chilton & The Kossacks” for the duration of 1977. Playing with Chilton immersed Stamey in the Ork Records / CBGB new wave scene alongside Television, Richard Hell, the Feelies, and others.

In 1977 Stamey made his solo debut with the single The Summer Sun / Where The Fun Is (Ork Records, cat. 81982). Alex Chilton produced the session (recording it “in the Beach Boys manner,” as Stamey described), played drums, and sang backing vocals — one of the earliest documents of the Big Star axis that would shape Stamey’s career. That year Stamey also founded Car Records, an independent NYC label that released singles by himself, Holsapple, and — notably — Chris Bell’s I Am the Cosmos / You and Your Sister (1978), one of the first releases of that posthumous Big Star-adjacent classic.

He also contributed bass, backing vocals, and production to a pair of 1978 Car Records singles by other acts (Big Black Truck, In The Red), establishing his dual role as performer and producer early on.

The dB’s: First Period (1978–1982)

In spring 1978, Stamey recruited Gene Holder (then living in Philadelphia) and Will Rigby (still in NC) to join him in New York. The trio began performing as Chris Stamey and the dB’s and released the debut Car Records single “(I Thought) You Wanted To Know / If And When.” In autumn 1978 Peter Holsapple — who had moved to NYC and was working at a record store called Musical Maze at 294 Third Avenue — joined as organ player and backing vocalist, quickly becoming a full co-writer and lead singer. The classic lineup was now set: Chris Stamey (guitar, vocals), Peter Holsapple (guitar, vocals), Gene Holder (bass), Will Rigby (drums). Their debut performance as a full four-piece was at Irving Plaza, October 1978.

Key releases:

  • (I Thought) You Wanted To Know / If And When (Car Records, 1978) — first dB’s single
  • Stands For Decibels (Albion Records, 1981) — debut album, released January 15, 1981; produced by the band with Alan Betrock as “éminence grise” (Stamey’s phrase); recorded at Blue Rock Studios, SoHo; mixed by Scott Litt (Power Station, NYC), Don Dixon (Drive-In, NC), and Martin Rushent (Genetic, UK); demos recorded on Stamey’s home four-track with Mitch Easter; cover art by Victoria DeVeraux. Albion Records (UK) was the only label interested at the time; the album received a warm UK press reception and some US college radio play, landing at #26 in the Village Voice Pazz & Jop poll for 1981. Later cited by Pitchfork among its 100 top albums of the 1980s.
  • Repercussion (Albion Records, 1982) — recorded in London over seven weeks in summer 1981; produced by Scott Litt (his first full production credit); Stamey and Holsapple split writing evenly (six songs each); featured The Rumour Brass on “Living a Lie”; John Entwistle loaned Gene Holder his eight-string bass for “Neverland”; Andy Clark (Be-Bop Deluxe) played keyboard on one track.

Stands For Decibels and Repercussion established the band in the British music press. Stamey departed the dB’s in early April 1982 to pursue his solo career; Peter Holsapple continued the band with new members for Like This (Bearsville, 1984) and The Sound Of Music (IRS, 1987).

Solo Work: First Phase (1982–1993)

After leaving the dB’s in April 1982, Stamey released It’s A Wonderful Life (DB Recs, 1982), a solo EP he directed. Scott Litt produced this EP as well, continuing their working relationship from Repercussion. He also produced Fetching Bones’ Chomp (DB Recs, 1983) and began his run of outside production work.

His debut full-length solo album came with It’s Alright (A&M Records / Coyote Records, 1987), a dense, orchestrated pop record tracked at Water Music Recorders (NJ) and featuring a who’s-who of New York session players: Anton Fier and Ted Lyons (drums), Richard Lloyd and Mitch Easter and Keith Mack (guitars), Graham Maby and James MacMillan and Mary Mac (bass), Bernie Worrell and Joel Diamond and Cathy Harrington (keys), Jane Scarpantoni (cello), Dave Schramm (lap steel). Harmony vocalists included Alex Chilton, Marshall Crenshaw, Faye Hunter, Lisa Herman, and Fran Kowalski. Engineered and mixed by Scott Litt, Bill Scheniman, Garry Rindfuss, Steve Fjelstad, and Steve Wiese. Mastered by Bob Ludwig.

Mavericks (New Rose Records, 1991) — a co-credited album with Peter Holsapple, produced by both; recorded by James MacMillan, with additional engineering from Bill Scheniman, John Siket, and Jon Rosenberg; mixed by George Cowan.

Fireworks (RNA / R.N.A. Rhino New Artists, 1991) — solo album; Stamey produced and played all key instruments; Mitch Easter contributed additional guitar and recording; mixed by James MacMillan and George Cowan; engineered by Jon Rosenberg. Notable for a guest writing credit from William Bell on the final track.

Additional solo releases in this period: Angels (New Rose Records, 1991, France) and Alive (Singles Only Label, 1993).

The Chris Stamey Group and Interlude (1985–1986)

The Chris Stamey Group released Christmas Time (Coyote Records, TTC 8564, 1985/1986) — a holiday-themed 12” mini-album featuring special guest appearances by The dB’s. Side A: Christmas Time, Something Came Over Me, The Only Law That Santa Claus Understood, Sha La La; Side B: Snow Is Falling, You’re What I Want (For Christmas), It’s A Wonderful Life. Recorded at Water Music and Bearsville. The release date is disputed between sources: Coyote’s catalog numbering places it in 1985, AllMusic lists 1986. The original EP was expanded to a 17-song CD in 1993 and a 21-song CD in 2006, with contributions from Mitch Easter, Ryan Adams, Marshall Crenshaw, and Don Dixon added on later editions.

Production Peak: Chapel Hill Scene (1993–2002)

Stamey moved back to Chapel Hill in the early 1990s (some sources specify 1993 as his return). In 1996, at Scott Litt’s suggestion and with his assistance, Stamey opened Modern Recording studio — situated in a century-old house on the edges of Chapel Hill, roughly a mile from where Stamey was born. The first project recorded there was Whiskeytown. Stamey operated Modern Recording (Discogs label ID: 309048) as owner, chief engineer, and mixer, using vintage analog equipment (Neve preamps) alongside Pro Tools, and made records there with Whiskeytown, Chatham County Line, Caitlin Cary, Le Tigre, Flat Duo Jets, Alejandro Escovedo, Tres Chicas, Thad Cockrell, Roman Candle, and Tift Merritt, among others. Stamey’s production work through the 1990s is the central chapter of his contribution to the Chapel Hill / Americana / alt-country scene.

Solo Work: Later Period (2004–2025)

Stamey’s solo output continued alongside production work:

  • V.O.T.E. (Yep Roc, 2004) — recorded with Yo La Tengo (Ira Kaplan, Georgia Hubley, James McNew); Stamey on vocals, guitar, produced; Mitch Easter mixed
  • A Question Of Temperature (Yep Roc, 2005) — solo; Stamey as performer, producer, additional recording
  • Lovesick Blues (Yep Roc, 2013) — orchestral concept album; Stamey as performer, writer, orchestrator; Jeff Crawford co-produced; Andy Partridge (XTC) credited with suggestions; large orchestra assembled from Chapel Hill musicians; harmony vocals from Caitlin Cary, Django Haskins, Skylar Gudasz, and others; Mitch Easter performed
  • Euphoria (Yep Roc, 2015) — solo; Stamey wrote, performed, arranged, recorded, mixed; Mitch Easter contributed additional guitar; trumpet by Michael Stipe (2) (a different Michael Stipe from R.E.M.); vocals from Norman Blake (Teenage Fanclub), Pat Sansone, and others
  • Here And Now (Bar/None, 2009) — with Peter Holsapple; produced and recorded jointly; Jon Wurster (drums), Branford Marsalis (saxophone), Caitlin Cary (co-writer), Will Rigby (drums on one track)
  • Falling Off The Sky (Bar/None, 2012) — dB’s reunion album; Stamey on guitar, string/wind arrangements, recorded and mixed; string/wind writing is prominent
  • Our Back Pages (Omnivore Recordings, 2020) — Stamey as primary artist
  • Anything Is Possible (Label 51 Recordings, 2025) — Stamey as writer, arranger, producer, recorded/mixed

Big Star’s Third Live (2010–2017)

Stamey was the driving force behind Big Star’s Third — the ensemble performing Alex Chilton’s Third/Sister Lovers live with its full original string and wind arrangements, which had never been performed publicly. Stamey had played bass in Chilton’s touring band in 1977, cementing his personal connection to the material. Years before the first performance, he tracked down Carl Marsh (who had orchestrated Third/Sister Lovers) and obtained access to the original Ardent Studios multitracks from John Fry, allowing the lost scores to be reconstructed.

The inaugural performance was set for South by Southwest in March 2010, but Alex Chilton died of a heart attack on March 17, 2010 — the night before rehearsals were to begin. That December, the project went ahead as a tribute: on December 10, 2010, the ensemble performed at Cat’s Cradle, Chapel Hill, NC, with Jody Stephens on drums, Mike Mills on bass, Mitch Easter and Stamey on guitar, nine guest singers, a seven-piece string section, and five brass and woodwind players. Stamey served as Musical Director.

The project then toured the US, UK, Spain, and Australia between 2010 and 2016 — including a March 2011 NYC show, a May 2012 Barbican concert in London, a January 2014 Sydney show, and multiple US runs — with guest vocalists including Ken Stringfellow, The Bangles, Peter Buck, Cat Power, Kurt Vile, Matthew Sweet, Michael Stipe (R.E.M.), Aimee Mann, Ray Davies, and Van Dyke Parks.

The live album and DVD Thank You, Friends: Big Star’s Third Live…And More (Concord Bicycle Music, 2017) was recorded at the Alex Theatre, Glendale, CA, in 2016. It features Stamey on guitar and vocals alongside Jody Stephens, Mike Mills, Ken Stringfellow, Mitch Easter, Benmont Tench, Ira Kaplan, Pat Sansone, Django Haskins, and Skylar Gudasz. This project is the fullest expression of Stamey’s lifelong Alex Chilton/Big Star connection.
