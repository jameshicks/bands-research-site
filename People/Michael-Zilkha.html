Michael Zilkha

British-born co-founder of ZE Records, of Iraqi Jewish descent. Oxford graduate, New York music entrepreneur 1978–1986, subsequently oil/gas and renewable energy executive in Houston, philanthropist, and publisher.

Biography

Family Background

Born 1954 in the UK. His grandfather, Khedouri Zilkha, was a Baghdad-based banker who founded K.A. Zilkha in 1899 (later Zilkha Bank), expanding to Beirut, Damascus, Cairo, Alexandria, Geneva, New York, and Paris. The Zilkha banking empire was compared in a 1955 New York Times article to the Rothschilds: “One room Baghdad bank became a world-wide family financing empire…unmatched since Amschel Rothschild and his five sons built their organization.” [source: sothebys.com] The family’s assets were systematically expropriated by nationalist governments: Iraq in 1951–52, Syria in 1954, Egypt in 1956; the Lebanon operation was sold in 1957. Khedouri had transferred assets to New York as early as 1938 and emigrated to the US in 1941 (the year of the Farhud pogrom in Baghdad, in which approximately 200 Iraqi Jews were murdered). He died in New York in 1956. After settling in the United States in 1941, Khedouri ran the international finance and foreign exchange firm Zilkha & Sons with his four sons: Maurice, Abdulla, Ezra, and Selim.

His father, Selim Zilkha, worked at Zilkha & Sons from 1947 to 1960 before leaving to found Mothercare, the UK retail chain for mothers and children. Michael’s parents divorced by 1962; his mother, Diane Bashi, subsequently married British politician Harold Lever (later Baron Lever of Manchester).

Growing up in London, Michael attended Westminster School, where he later described experiencing bullying and “an undercurrent of antisemitism.” He rejected following his father into the family business: “Mothercare was already built. I wanted something that was mine.” The Guardian (2023) noted he left Britain partly because he felt alienated by its class structure and antisemitism. [source: theguardian.com/music/2023/mar/16/contort-yourself-the-mutant-disco-mayhem-of-new-yorks-ze-records]

Education

Studied French literature at Lincoln College, Oxford, graduating with an MA in 1975.

New York, 1975–1978

After Oxford, Zilkha moved to New York in 1975. He later described the city as a draw because “I wanted to start again in an immigrant city.” [source: theguardian.com/music/2023/mar/16/contort-yourself-the-mutant-disco-mayhem-of-new-yorks-ze-records] He recalled finding CBGBs “within three days” of arriving and seeing the emerging punk scene unfold. [ibid.] He worked in the New York publishing industry for three years and contributed to the cultural/theatre pages of the Village Voice as a theatre critic — one of the city’s youngest at the time. It was through this role that he first met Cristina Monet-Palaci, who was also writing theatre criticism for the Voice; reportedly he called her after hearing she had claimed to be the city’s youngest critic. On their second date, they reviewed a play together in East Orange, NJ. [source: byronesque.com/cristina-monet-interview/]

In 1977, Zilkha interviewed John Cale for Interview Magazine. Cale subsequently introduced him to Michel Esteban, who was then working with Cale on SPY Records. [source: lydianspin.libsyn.com/episode-151-michael-zilkha-and]

Meeting Michel Esteban and Founding ZE Records

Michel Esteban was a French art-school graduate who had opened “Harry Cover,” a Paris concept store specialising in UK and US record imports, whose basement became a rehearsal space for Parisian new wave bands. In 1977 Esteban began producing, signing French new wave band Marie et les Garçons; he engaged John Cale to produce their single. Cale had been introduced to Esteban by Patti Smith. When Cale and manager Jane Friedman started SPY Records in 1977, Esteban contributed logo design and both he and Zilkha were involved. Esteban and Zilkha left SPY in 1978 to found their own label.

An earlier account of the Cristina “Disco Clone” single sheds light on the Cale falling-out: Cale produced “Disco Clone” but creative disagreements (Cristina wanted something Brechtian; Cale allegedly wanted a “Monster Mash”) caused a rupture, and the single ended up as ZE’s first release rather than a SPY release. [source: post-punk.com/ze-records-revived]

ZE Records was founded in New York in 1978. The name is taken from the initials of their surnames — Zilkha and Esteban.

Role at ZE vs. Esteban’s Role

Zilkha provided the financial backing, social connections, and conceptual direction. He personally signed most of the US acts, including Kid Creole and the Coconuts, Lydia Lunch, and Suicide (though Suicide had been active since the early 1970s, ZE released key material). He described the operation as feeling “more like a repertory company than a record label” and noted he “could only afford to sign people nobody else wanted.” Esteban brought in the European roster, most notably Lizzy Mercier Descloux.

Zilkha characterised ZE’s sound and approach: the first album he put out was James White and the Blacks — the Contortions “slowed down to a disco beat” — because he wanted a dance band after the group declined to sign. ZE also pioneered early sampling techniques, notably incorporating a Ronald Reagan State of the Union address into politically charged material — Zilkha described Tell Me That I’m Dreaming (with Reagan’s first State of the Union address chopped up) as “the first overtly political song I made.” [source: post-punk.com/ze-records-revived]

ZE’s critical reputation peaked around 1980–1982. John Peel described it in Melody Maker (1980) as “the best independent record label in the world”; Paul Tickell in The Face (1982) called it “the world’s most fashionable label.” [source: c86show.org/e/ze-records-with-michael-zilkha/; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZE_Records] Many ZE releases debuted at the Paradise Garage in New York.

On Was (Not Was): Zilkha was called by “the jazz critic for the LA Herald Examiner” who told him about an extraordinary record. “I had no money at the time, but they let me have the record anyway…partly because no one else wanted it, and also because it really belonged on my label.” [source: post-punk.com/ze-records-revived]

Decline of ZE and Departure

ZE operated actively from 1978 to approximately 1984–1986. Commercial tensions mounted as successful artists departed for larger labels. Esteban left New York in 1982 and relocated to Paris; the commercial failure of John Cale’s rapturously reviewed 1982 album Music for a New Society — a ZE release — left Zilkha “gutted.” [source: en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/3903241 citing ZE Records article] Zilkha and Esteban parted over financial disagreements. The label closed in 1984; Zilkha then worked as an editor at Atlantic Monthly Press (c.1984–1986) before relocating to Texas. [source: lydianspin.libsyn.com/episode-151-michael-zilkha-and]

Zilkha later said: “I felt like I said what I had to say” and felt later records lacked vitality. [source: post-punk.com/ze-records-revived] He cited “money had really destroyed NYC” as the reason for his 1986 departure. [ibid.] Esteban continued operating a version of ZE from Paris.

Zilkha retained ownership of the ZE master recordings and remained their custodian through subsequent decades of reissues.

Personal Life

Zilkha and Cristina Monet-Palaci met while both were writing theatre criticism for the Village Voice in the late 1970s. He married Cristina (Cristina Monet-Palaci) in 1983; they have a daughter, Lulu, born on Christmas Day. They divorced in 1990. [source: byronesque.com/cristina-monet-interview/] Cristina died on 1 April 2020 of COVID-19 complications in New York, aged 61. [source: stereogum.com, nme.com/news/music/cristina-ze-records-and-things-fall-apart-singer-dies-at-61-2639500] Her death was announced on Facebook by Michel Esteban. No public statement from Michael Zilkha about her passing has been located in web sources.

By 2023 Zilkha had at least one grandchild; he told the Guardian that “some of my happiest hours now are driving my granddaughter to school” — with a playlist featuring Aural Exciters and The Waitresses. [source: theguardian.com/music/2023/mar/16/contort-yourself-the-mutant-disco-mayhem-of-new-yorks-ze-records]

He is currently married to Cornelia “Nina” O’Leary, a descendant of Houston philanthropist Hugh Roy Cullen; they have one son.

Energy Career, Houston, 1986–2018

From 1986, Zilkha worked with his father Selim on oil and gas exploration through Zilkha Energy, serving as co-owner and executive vice president. The company focused on Gulf of Mexico shelf exploration, deploying 3-D seismic data across the shelf from South Texas to the Florida panhandle. At the time of sale the company had approximately 65 employees on a single floor of a downtown Houston building. [source: hpcwire.com/1998/03/27/hpc-geophysical-exploration-zilkha-strikes-rich/] In late 1997 they agreed to sell Zilkha Energy to Sonat for approximately $1.04 billion in stock, closing in 1998; Selim and Michael became Sonat’s largest shareholders at 21%, each with a board seat. Sonat was subsequently acquired by El Paso Corporation. [source: renewableenergyworld.com/energy-business/energy-finance/zilkha-acquired-by-goldman-sachs-24014/]

He then co-founded Zilkha Renewable Energy with Selim, becoming president and co-owner (1998–2005), developing a wind energy portfolio of nearly 4,000 MW across approximately a dozen states including New York, Oklahoma, and Washington. Goldman Sachs acquired the company in July 2005 for an undisclosed sum, renaming it Horizon Wind Energy; Selim and Michael retained a financial interest and board seats. Goldman Sachs Chairman Henry M. Paulson, Jr. led the acquisition. Michael Zilkha stated at the time: “Goldman Sachs’ acquisition of Zilkha is a clear sign of wind’s emergence as a competitive, efficient, and mainstream energy resource.” [source: renewableenergyworld.com] Horizon Wind Energy was subsequently sold to Energias de Portugal (EDP) in 2007, becoming EDP Renewables North America.

Following the wind energy sale, Zilkha spent roughly a decade developing biomass energy projects through Zilkha Biomass Energy; the venture was discontinued after an Alabama facility in Selma failed to operate continuously. [source: turtleconservancy.org/people/michael-zilkha] He retired from energy in 2018.

ZE Books and Later Life

In 2017, a visit from dying friend Glenn O’Brien inspired Zilkha to return to publishing; he wanted to release a definitive collection of O’Brien’s writings. In 2019 he launched ZE Books, a Houston-based literary imprint with the stated ambition of being “a hip version of the Library of the Americas.” [source: publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/80612-michael-zilkha-launches-ze-books.html] He describes its mission as publishing “literary mix-tapes from visionary writers, artists, and musicians.” [ibid.] The imprint publishes 2–4 titles annually with print runs of 7,500–10,000.

Key personnel: editor Lucas Wittmann (co-founder of House of Speakeasy, Time magazine editor-at-large); consulting publishing director Nathan Rostron; book cover design by Jimine Ha (With Projects); publishing partner Unnamed Press; North American distribution via Publishers Group West; UK distribution via Inpress Books. [source: publishersweekly.com ibid.; zebooks.com/about]

Published titles include: Intelligence for Dummies: Essays and Other Collected Writings by Glenn O’Brien (October 2019, the inaugural ZE Book); Stay by Nick Flynn; The Devil’s Treasure by Mary Gaitskill; The Ocean is Closed by Jon Bradshaw; The Skinny by Jonathan Wells; works by Adele Bertei and Bud Lee; and titles by Nan Goldin, Claudia Rankine, Bun B, Lynn Hershman Leeson, Roni Horn/Tacita Dean, and Fred Baldwin/Wendy Watriss announced as forthcoming. [source: publishersweekly.com ibid.; zebooks.com]

Philanthropy

Based in Houston, Zilkha is active on several cultural and conservation boards:

  • The Menil Foundation (Houston) — board member
  • Museum of Fine Arts Houston — co-chairs the film committee
  • Inprint (Houston literary organisation) — board member
  • Cinema Arts Festival Houston — board member
  • Kate McGarrigle Foundation — board member
  • Turtle Conservancy — board member (conservation focus)
  • Holocaust Museum Houston — the Nina and Michael Zilkha Endowment Fund bears his and his wife’s name

Overview / Key Facts

  • Born 1954; grandson of Khedouri Zilkha (Baghdad banker, Zilkha & Sons), son of Selim Zilkha (Mothercare founder)
  • Attended Westminster School, London; MA French Literature, Lincoln College, Oxford, 1975
  • Moved to New York 1975; worked in publishing and wrote for the Village Voice
  • Co-founded ZE Records with Michel Esteban in New York, 1978; Zilkha provided finance and signed US acts, Esteban handled European artists
  • ZE active 1978–c.1984; Zilkha departed 1986 after financial disagreements with Esteban
  • Retained the ZE master recordings throughout subsequent decades
  • After ZE: co-owned Zilkha Energy (sold to Sonat, 1998, ~$1 billion); founded Zilkha Renewable Energy (sold to Goldman Sachs, 2005, renamed Horizon Wind Energy, sold to EDP 2007)
  • Retired from energy 2018; founded ZE Books publishing imprint 2019
  • Based in Houston, Texas; significant cultural philanthropist

Key Credits / Releases

  • Co-produced and oversaw the ZE Records catalog, including releases by Suicide, Was (Not Was), Kid Creole And The Coconuts, Cristina, Material, James White and the Blacks, Lydia Lunch, Lizzy Mercier Descloux, and many others
  • Co-produced Falling In Love Again (ZE, 1984) with Davitt Sigerson — Sigerson released his own material on ZE and was part of the label’s extended circle
  • ZE catalog encompasses over 300 releases across its active years and reissue history
