Donna Dresch

Discogs ID: 455771. American guitarist and bassist. Alias: Donna Biddle. Name variations include “Donna Shake The Guitar Dresch,” “Donna Take A Walk Dresch,” “D Dresch,” “Doctor Donna Dresch,” “Donna!,” and “Dresch.”

Central figure in the 1990s queercore movement. Founder of Team Dresch and founder/operator of Chainsaw Records (Portland, Oregon). Active as performer, producer, and recording engineer across multiple Pacific Northwest scenes from the late 1980s onward.

Biography

Late 1980s — Early years and Dangermouse

Donna Dresch’s earliest Discogs-traceable credit is as bassist on Have Sole Will Travel (1989, Horton/Reflex), the debut cassette by Olympia, Washington band Dangermouse (2). That band also featured John Goodmanson on guitar and Tammy Rae Carland in a photography role — collaborators who would recur across Dresch’s network throughout the decade. The recording was handled by Steve Fisk and Sam Albright.

Early 1990s — Scene work and freelance credits

Through the early 1990s Dresch appeared as a sideperson and session bassist across the emerging Pacific Northwest indie scene:

  • 1991: Cover art credit on Holy Rollers / Cringer split 7” (Shred Of Dignity)
  • 1993: Bass on Lois Maffeo’s Strumpet LP (K Records), alongside Lois Maffeo, Molly Neuman on drums, and Stephen Immerwahr — produced by Calvin Johnson
  • 1993: Guitar on Mary Lou Lord’s Some Jingle Jangle Morning 7”, with Kathi Wilcox and Tobi Vail (recorded by Goodmanson and Fisk)
  • 1993: Mixing credit on the compilation Babies And Bunnies

1993–1998 — Team Dresch and Chainsaw Records

Dresch founded Team Dresch in Olympia/Portland circa 1993–94, one of the defining queercore bands. The band released three studio records:

  • Hand Grenade 7” (1994, Kill Rock Stars) — earliest Team Dresch Discogs release
  • Personal Best LP (1995, Chainsaw / Candy-Ass Records) — co-produced with John Goodmanson; Dresch plays bass on tracks 3, 4, 6, 9, 10 and guitar on 1, 2, 5, 7, 8; lineup included Kaia Wilson, Jody Bleyle (credited as Jody Coyote), and Marci Martinez
  • Captain My Captain LP (1996, Chainsaw / Candy-Ass Records) — produced and recorded by John Goodmanson; Dresch plays guitar on tracks 1, 2, 4, 6–11 and bass on 3, 5; Melissa York on drums

Concurrently, Dresch founded Chainsaw Records (Discogs label ID: 14714) in Portland, Oregon, dedicated to queercore and adjacent independent music. The label’s catalog included Sleater-Kinney’s first two LPs (Sleater-Kinney, 1995; Call The Doctor, 1996), Kaia Wilson’s solo record, Excuse 17, Longstocking, and Tracy + The Plastics, among others.

Dresch also appeared on Fifth Column’s 36c (1994, K Records) — guitar on tracks 2 and 9. She co-wrote the song “Donna” (track 6) with Caroline Azar (lyrics credit; that track is credited separately in liner notes).

Other 1994–96 production and recording credits:

  • Bass on Phranc’s Goofy Foot (1994, Kill Rock Stars) — tracks 1, 2, 4; with Tobi Vail and Patty Schemel on drums
  • Producer on Hazel (4)‘s Are You Going To Eat That? (1995, Sub Pop Records)
  • Various recording/engineer credits (Safety First, Blank Florida, Direction…, Tommy Slich) — 1994–95

Team Dresch released a final 7” It’s A Conversation / Never Nowhere (1998, Sub Pop) and the New Team Dresch V 6.0 Beta cassette (1998). The band disbanded in 1998.

Late 1990s–2000s — Production work and collaborations

After Team Dresch’s first run, Dresch continued working as producer and recording engineer:

  • 1998: Recording credit on Frumpie One-Piece LP (Kill Rock Stars)
  • 2000: Bass, guitar, producer, and engineer on The Transfused (Kill Rock Stars) by The Need & Nomy Lamm, credited as “Doctor Donna Dresch”
  • 1999: Producer on Back To Go release
  • 2002: Uncredited producer on 17 Nussbaum Road, 1st Experience (Small Clone, Japan)
  • 2002–03: Recording credits on Forever Sucks EP and Lights Are Changing (guitar also on the latter, with Rastro!)
  • 2004: Guitar on vs. Love EP by Davies Vs. Dresch — duo with Kristina Davies

2019 — Team Dresch reunion

Team Dresch reunited and issued two releases on Jealous Butcher Records:

  • Your Hands My Pockets LP (2019) — mixed by John Goodmanson, produced and recorded by Lawrence Crane; lineup included Dresch, Jody Bleyle, Kaia Wilson, and Marcéo Martinez
  • Choices, Chances, Changes: Singles & Comptracks 1994–2000 compilation (2019) — retrospective collecting singles and compilation tracks

Open Questions

  • The original stub listed credits with Screaming Trees and Dinosaur Jr. — these do not appear anywhere in Discogs. Likely sourced from web (Wikipedia/AllMusic). Worth verifying with web search if desired; may be touring or informal credits not in the database.
  • Rastro! released only June (1993, Simple Machines) per Discogs — but Dresch’s credit on Lights Are Changing (2003) is attributed to this group. Possible the 2003 entry is a different version/era of the band, or a mislabeled credit.
  • Several mid-period recording/engineering credits (Safety First, Blank Florida, Direction…) have no artist disambiguation in this summary — artist IDs not checked.
  • Chainsaw Records ceased active releases after 2002 (CHSW 25); the 2022 Team Dresch repress appears under catalog JB 182, suggesting a Jealous Butcher co-release rather than a new Chainsaw release.
