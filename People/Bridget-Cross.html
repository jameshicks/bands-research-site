Bridget Cross

Vocalist, guitarist, bassist, multi-instrumentalist. Also credited as Bridget Cross Kuhar. Discogs artist ID: 543877. Name variations: B., B. Cross, B.Cross, Bridget, Cross.

DC-area indie rock musician active from 1990 onward. Core member of Unrest (2) (1990–1994) and Air Miami (1994–1998) — both centered on Mark Robinson and the Teen Beat Records label. Also a member of Panax, Pigeons, Velocity Girl (early period), and later Maybe It’s Reno and The Cloud People with partner George Kuhar. Guest backing vocalist on Fugazi’s The Argument (2001, Dischord Records).

Velocity Girl (1990)

Cross’s earliest Discogs credit is as a member of Velocity Girl, the DC-area dream pop/indie rock band, on their 1990 single I Don’t Care If You Go (Slumberland Records). She is credited for “Voice [Girl Voice], Bass” — alongside Archie Moore (boy voice, bass), Jim Spellman (drums), Berny Grindel and Kelly Riles (guitars), recorded by Barrett Jones. This appears to be a transitional early credit; Cross did not remain a long-term member of that lineup.

Unrest (1991–1994)

Cross was a full member of Unrest (2) — the DC indie rock project led by Mark Robinson — from roughly 1991 through the band’s end in 1994. The core trio during her tenure was Robinson, Cross, and drummer Phil Krauth.

Unrest had been active since 1985 on Robinson’s own Teen Beat label, but Cross joined the lineup that produced the band’s best-known records.

The 4AD deal brought Unrest significant UK exposure; Perfect Teeth (1993) and the Isabel Bishop E.P. were the label’s flagship releases for the band. Cross co-wrote material on the Isabel Bishop E.P. (credited “Cross” on track 4). The Cath Carroll single is notably credited as produced by Simon Le Bon of Duran Duran — an unusual pairing reflecting Robinson’s pop-crossover sensibility.

Unrest disbanded in 1994; the retrospective B.P.M. (1991-1994) compiled their work.

Air Miami (1994–1998)

Following Unrest’s breakup, Cross and Robinson formed Air Miami in 1994. The group expanded to include Gabriel Stout (drums), Fontaine Toups, Ben Currier, Lauren Feldsher, and Mike Fellows at various points (Discogs member list: artist IDs 1330064, 577978, 624466, 782765, 228502). Air Miami released on both Teen Beat and 4AD, continuing the transatlantic indie relationship Unrest had established.

On Me. Me. Me. (1995), Cross’s involvement was unusually deep: she played vocals, guitar, and bass guitar; co-wrote tracks; co-mixed with Robinson and engineer Guy Fixsen; and co-designed the sleeve with Teenbeat Graphica. Executive producer was Ivo Watts-Russell (4AD founder). Cover illustration by Hideaki Kodama.

I Hate Milk Milk (1995, 4AD) is an EP credited to Air Miami; Cross plays vocals, guitar, and bass. Gabriel Stout plays drums. The release includes a remix by Steve Murphy and a self-remix by Air Miami.

Panax

Cross is listed as a member of Panax (Discogs artist ID: 1887551), a group also comprising Kathi Wilcox and Doug Bailey. The Teen Beat website listed them at the URL slug bridgetkathidoug. The trio recorded a 7” single, Love Will Rule Our Party (1999, Teenbeat TEENBEAT 280), recorded by Guy Picciotto; Discogs also notes they recorded three songs total (the third is unaccounted for in the catalog).

Pigeons

Cross is listed as a member of Pigeons (Discogs artist ID: 1411345), a group also comprising Kathi Wilcox and Guy Picciotto (Fugazi, Rites of Spring). No releases appear in the Discogs database for this project either.

Fugazi — The Argument (2001)

Cross sang backing vocals on two tracks of Fugazi’s final studio album The Argument (2001, Dischord Records), recorded at Inner Ear Studios with Don Zientara.

Kathi Wilcox also appears on the album, credited for “Second Backing Vocals” on track 3. Other additional personnel included Amy Domingues (cello, tracks 1, 2, 7), Jerry Busher (second drums, tracks 4, 8, 9; percussion on multiple tracks), Chad Clark (piano, tracks 6–7), and Nick Pellicciotto (mix assistance, track 5). (Note: the Discogs pressing cataloged as release ID 28253899 is dated 2016, likely a reissue; the original release was 2001.)

KingXmas (2009)

Cross contributed piano and trumpet to KingXmas (2009, Teen Beat), a release by Mark Robinson. Robinson plays vocals and guitar. The two-person collaboration marks a return to the Robinson/Cross pairing more than a decade after Air Miami.

Maybe It’s Reno (2008)

Cross released the album Maybe It’s Reno (Teenbeat, April 22, 2008; TEENBEAT 434) under the band name Maybe It’s Reno (Discogs artist ID: 1964872). The project is described as “Bridget Cross and George Kuhar sometimes with Bridget’s Unrest bandmates Phil Krauth and Mark Robinson.” All ten songs on the album are written by Cross, and she performs all vocals.

Personnel on the album splits into two groups: Mark Robinson (producer, guitar, electric piano, mellotron) and Phil Krauth (drums, mellotron) appear on tracks 1–7; George Kuhar (guitar, keys, also recording engineer) and Jordan Strudel (drums) appear on tracks 8–10/8–9. Cross plays bass guitar, vocals, and electric piano/Rhodes throughout.

Wikipedia’s entry on Unrest (2) describes the album as “almost equating this project to another reunion of Unrest.”

George Kuhar (Discogs ID: 1964888) is Cross’s partner — her name variation “Bridget Cross Kuhar” confirms this. Kuhar’s Discogs presence is limited to Maybe It’s Reno and The Cloud People.

The Cloud People (2015)

Cross is also a member of The Cloud People (Discogs artist ID: 4734784), alongside George Kuhar and Nick Gallas (Discogs ID: 4734783). The project continues the Cross/Kuhar creative partnership established in Maybe It’s Reno, adding Gallas on bass clarinet and clarinet.

The Cloud People have one documented release: Time To Land (2015, Shadow Island Records, Discogs ID: 7772181), a six-track CD album. Cross is credited as “Bridget Cross Kuhar” — lead vocals, electric piano, and artwork. George Kuhar produced, played guitar and percussion, and handled layout. The album was recorded by Guy Picciotto (Fugazi / Rites of Spring), who also shared membership with Cross in Pigeons. The label Shadow Island Records has only this one release documented in Discogs, suggesting a self-released or vanity-label release.
