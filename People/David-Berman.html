Died: August 7, 2019, Park Slope, Brooklyn, New York, USA

American musician, singer-songwriter, poet, and cartoonist. Founder and sole constant member of Silver Jews, and later the project Purple Mountains. Regarded as a significant indie rock cult figure, particularly for his literary lyrical voice and his single published poetry collection, Actual Air (1999). Died by suicide at 52, one month after the release of his final album.

Early Life

David Craig Berman was born January 4, 1967, in Williamsburg, Virginia, into a secular Jewish family. His father, Richard Berman, worked as an attorney in labor law and later became a prominent corporate lobbyist representing firearms, alcohol, and foodservice industries — a fact that would generate estrangement and political friction. His parents divorced when he was seven; he later described his childhood as “grindingly painful.” Raised mostly in Texas, he attended Greenhill School in Addison, Texas. He suffered from depression throughout his life and by his own account began taking drugs as a teenager.

Dallas’s emerging new wave scene was an early musical influence — he cited Art of Noise, Prefab Sprout, X, the Replacements, the Cure, New Order, and Echo and the Bunnymen. He began experimenting with poetry by writing to high school girlfriends, hoping to imitate the lyrics of Jello Biafra and Exene Cervenka.

He attended the University of Virginia, where he met Stephen Malkmus and Bob Nastanovich, then later pursued a master’s degree in poetry at the University of Massachusetts Amherst. His UMass thesis, “Ruined Entrances,” later formed part of Actual Air. (Source: Wikipedia, “David Berman (musician)“)

Origin of Silver Jews: 1989–1994

Upon graduating, Berman, Malkmus, and Nastanovich moved to Hoboken, New Jersey, where they shared an apartment and formed Silver Jews in 1989, recording discordant tapes in their living room. Berman and Malkmus worked as security guards at the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York; museum visits — particularly the work of Bruce Nauman, Jean-Michel Basquiat, and Louise Lawler — influenced Berman’s lyrical sensibility.

Dan Koretzky, founder of the Chicago-based indie label Drag City, met Berman at a Pavement show; hearing of the Silver Jews’ tapes, Koretzky offered to release them. The band’s early EPs — Dime Map of the Reef (1992) and The Arizona Record (1993) — gained them attention. Kim Gordon was an admirer; Will Oldham credited Dime Map of the Reef with inspiring him to send recordings to Drag City.

Berman worked to protect Silver Jews’ individuality from their close association with Pavement, at times listing Malkmus and Nastanovich under aliases. Though the Pavement connection frustrated him, it secured them a national audience large enough that Berman did not have to tour.

During this period Berman studied for his master’s in poetry at UMass — dubbing this time an “academic exile.” He attempted to publish in the American Poetry Review but was rejected, which increased his investment in music “despite scarcely knowing how to sing or play guitar.” (Source: Wikipedia)

Critical Acclaim and Substance Abuse: 1996–2001

Recording The Natural Bridge (1996) left Berman distraught — he was hospitalized with sleep deprivation, the sessions “searing.” Producer Mark Nevers held the album together. The album received strong reviews, establishing Berman as “a world-class rock lyricist,” but he refused to tour.

American Water (1998) — produced with Nicolas Vernhes and featuring Stephen Malkmus, Mike Fellows, Chris Stroffolino, and Tim Barnes — was a turning point. Critics saw it as Silver Jews “stepping out of Pavement’s shadow”: clearly Berman’s vision. His drug use continued through the recording sessions; a planned 1998 tour ended after a fistfight ruptured his eardrum.

Actual Air, his debut poetry collection, was published in 1999 by Open City Books — a press founded partly to publish the book. It sold over 20,000 copies, unusual for a poetry collection, marketed partly through Drag City and record stores. Critic Carl Wilson called it “even better than his albums.” The book gathered new poems alongside excerpts from his UMass thesis. It remained his only published poetry collection; later work appeared in The Baffler, Open City, and The Believer, and scraps were compiled by fans into the unofficial anthology “The Colonial Manuscript.”

Bright Flight (2001) introduced Berman’s wife Cassie Berman as a performer; the album featured Paul Niehaus, Tony Crow, William Tyler, and Mike Fellows, recorded and produced by Mark Nevers.

Attempted Suicide, Rehabilitation, and First Touring: 2003–2008

Berman’s substance abuse — primarily heroin and cocaine — culminated in a suicide attempt in 2003. He underwent rehabilitation, converted seriously to Judaism (which he described as lasting from roughly 2004 to 2010), and toured for the first time, playing his first live shows in Silver Jews’ history. This era produced Tanglewood Numbers (2005), self-produced with Mark Nevers recording; the band for those sessions included Bob Nastanovich, Cassie Berman, Stephen Malkmus, Mike Fellows, Azita Youssefi, Paz Lenchantin, Tony Crow, William Tyler, Brian Kotzur, and guests including Will Oldham and Duane Denison. Cover photos by William Eggleston.

Lookout Mountain, Lookout Sea (2008) — again produced by Berman, with Mark Nevers recording — featured a tighter band: Cassie Berman, Brian Kotzur, Peyton Pinkerton, William Tyler, and Tony Crow. One track co-produced and co-written with Maher Shalal Hash Baz.

In 2008 Drag City also issued the documentary film Silver Jew.

Dissolution and Hiatus: 2009–2017

In 2009, Berman disbanded Silver Jews, stating he wanted to move into “screenwriting or muckraking.” He largely withdrew from public life. During this period he encountered Yonatan Gat while touring Israel — shows he later called “pretty much the most amazing experience of my life” — and in 2018 co-produced Gat’s album Universalists (Joyful Noise Recordings). He maintained a blog and continued writing, though a second poetry collection never materialized.

Purple Mountains and Death: 2019

After separating from Cassie in 2018, Berman moved to a room above Drag City’s Chicago office, living off royalties. Financial pressures — approximately $100,000 in debt from his drug use years — and encouragement from Dan Koretzky prompted a return to music under the new name Purple Mountains.

The project was backed by members of Woods: Jarvis Taveniere (producer, engineer, bass, guitar, mixed), Jeremy Earl (drums, guitar, vocals, mellotron, percussion), and Anna St. Louis (vocals). Additional players included Kyle Forester (organ, mellotron, piano, melodica, harmonica), Justin Brown (pedal steel), Cole Kamen-Green (trumpet), John Andrews (organ, vocals), Haley Fohr (vocals on one track), Aaron Neveu (bass, drums, guitar), Chris Stroffolino (Hammond organ), and Peyton Pinkerton (auxiliary recording). Dan Koretzky served as executive producer.

The Purple Mountains album was released on Drag City in July 2019 to immediate critical acclaim — “an instantly mythologized album.” A planned tour with Bill Callahan and Will Oldham was discussed but never came together; Berman had notified the band that his depression might interfere, but was also excited for his “solitude to end.”

On August 7, 2019 — one month after the album’s release — Berman hanged himself in his Park Slope apartment. He was 52. A private funeral followed on August 16; filmmaker Lance Bangs organized a memorial at the Met Breuer (former Whitney location) in New York.
