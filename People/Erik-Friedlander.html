Erik Friedlander

Cellist, composer, arranger. Born 1 July 1960, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 146031.

Overview

Erik Friedlander is one of the most widely credited cellists to emerge from the New York downtown avant-garde scene. Son of photographer Lee Friedlander (Discogs ID: 460760), he built a career spanning jazz improvisation, contemporary classical, film scoring, and pop session work. From the early 1990s onward he was a fixture in John Zorn’s extended orbit — appearing on core Masada-related projects — while simultaneously logging session credits across Broadway, film scores, R&B, and rock records. His own discography as leader runs from the 1991 debut Framework through at least the 2018 solo album Artemisia, with the Skipstone Records releases in the 2010s forming the core of his mature solo output.

Name variations in Discogs credits: E. Friedlander, Eric Friedlander, Erik Freidlander, Erik Friedland, Erik V. Friedlander, Friedlander.

Early Career and Framework

Friedlander’s first appearance as a primary artist is the 1991 Newport Classic CD Framework, credited to the group Framework (2) (Discogs ID: 1366920), a trio with drummer Kevin Norton and violinist Laura Seaton. The group recorded avant-jazz with cello and voice at the center; Friedlander contributed cello and vocals alongside composition and production from the group collective.

The same year he appeared as sideman on Chesky Records sessions: the Orquesta Nova self-titled (1991) and various other Chesky releases through the early-to-mid 1990s. His earliest credits outside his own projects cluster around the New York jazz and Latin jazz world — Orquesta Nova (1991), David Chesky’s Maracanã (1993), The Raven (1994), and several Chesky Records all-star sessions through 1996.

The Zorn / Masada World

The central creative relationship of Friedlander’s career is with John Zorn (Discogs ID: 99731). He is a member of two Zorn ensembles documented in Discogs:

Masada String Trio (Discogs ID: 320769) — cello trio with violinist Mark Feldman and bassist Greg Cohen. The Masada String Trio was one of several Zorn chamber units built around the Masada songbook.

Bar Kokhba Sextet (Discogs ID: 611664) — the larger Masada chamber ensemble: Friedlander (cello), Mark Feldman (violin), Greg Cohen (bass), Marc Ribot (guitar), Joey Baron (drums), Cyro Baptista (percussion). The 1996 double-LP Bar Kokhba (Tzadik) is the key document.

He also co-led a Tzadik release directly with Zorn: Volac (Book Of Angels Volume 8) (2007, Tzadik), part of Zorn’s Book of Angels series. Friedlander arranged and performed cello on Zorn’s compositions for that volume.

Tzadik is the label where Friedlander’s own recordings are most consistently placed. His leader albums Grains Of Paradise (2001) and Volac appeared there; he also appears on Zorn’s Filmworks series (volumes III, VI, XIII), the Masada chamber recordings, and other Tzadik projects including the string quartet set The Circle Maker (1998) and The String Quartets (1999).

Skipstone Records became Friedlander’s primary home for solo work from 2010 onward. No Compass is unusual: it is credited jointly to “Solter Resets Friedlander,” with Scott Solter as remix co-lead alongside Friedlander’s original compositions.

Haunted (2013) is a three-way co-lead with Ikue Mori and Sylvie Courvoisier; Solter engineered and mixed.

Artemisia (2018) features the chamber group Throw A Glass (Discogs ID: 6494452), with Uri Caine on piano, Mark Helias on bass, and Ches Smith on drums. The cover artwork is by Japanese artist Akino Kondoh (Discogs ID: 2502733).

Session Work — Broader Credits

Friedlander’s sideman credits through the 1990s and 2000s span film scores, R&B, jazz, and rock:

Film scores: Spike Lee’s Malcolm X score (1992, Columbia); Dead Man Walking score (1996, Columbia); The Corruptor score (1999, Varèse Sarabande); Changing Lanes score (2002, Varèse Sarabande); S.W.A.T. score (2003); John Zorn Filmworks III, VI, XIII on Tzadik.

R&B / pop: Maxwell’s Urban Hang Suite (1996, Columbia) — cello; Ricky Martin self-titled (1999, Columbia) — cello; Alanis Morissette MTV Unplugged / You Learn (1999, Maverick) — cello; N’Dea Davenport self-titled (1998, V2); Joss Stone Introducing Joss Stone (2007, Virgin).

Jazz: Marcus Roberts Standard Time Vol. 5 (1998, Columbia); Wynton Marsalis It’s Time (1997, Atlantic); Bob Belden various; Strings For Holiday (1996, Enja Records); Passion Flower — Billy Strayhorn tribute (1996, Nonesuch).

Contemporary classical: Sofia Gubaidulina The Tower Of Mirrors (1995, Sub Rosa); Osvaldo Golijov Ayre (2005, Deutsche Grammophon) — one of his most visible classical credits; string quartet work on Tzadik.

String arrangements / arranger credits: John Vanderslice Pixel Revolt (2005, Barsuk Records) — string arrangements; Get Lonely by The Mountain Goats (2006, 4AD) — cello and string arrangements; Beat The Champ by The Mountain Goats (2015, Merge Records) — strings and string arrangements on tracks C8 and C10.

The Mountain Goats

Friedlander appears on three Mountain Goats albums, all connected through his network with Scott Solter (who engineered/produced all three):

On The Sunset Tree (produced by John Vanderslice) the credit is simply “Performer [Played By],” placing him in the ensemble alongside Solter and others. On Get Lonely he receives both a performance and an arrangement credit — his most substantive Mountain Goats contribution in terms of credited authorship. On Beat The Champ he returns for two specific tracks as string arranger, a wrestling-themed concept album produced by Brandon Eggleston.

Open Questions

  • Framework (1991) on Newport Classic appears in Discogs without a master ID and with no companion releases in the catalog — Newport Classic context for this album is thin.
