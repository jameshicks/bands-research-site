Caspar Brötzmann

Oct 14, 20269 min read

Caspar Brötzmann

Born: 13 December 1962, Wuppertal, Germany Instruments: guitar, bass, vocals Discogs artist ID: 123581 Name variations: Brotzmann, Brötzmann, C. Brötzmann, C.B., C.Brötzmann, Caspar, Caspar Brotzmann, Casper Brötzmann

German avant-metal guitarist, composer, and visual artist. Son of free jazz saxophonist Peter Brötzmann. He plays guitar and bass, sings, composes, and paints, working in what he describes as a “modern-primitive” style. Has toured extensively across the US, Europe, Japan, and Africa. Best known as the sole creative force behind Caspar Brötzmann Massaker, a band he has led since the mid-1980s, and for the 1996 guitar duo album Zulutime with Page Hamilton on Blast First / Sub Rosa.

Early Career: Die Alliierten (1983)

Brötzmann’s earliest Discogs credit is as guitarist on Ruhm Und Ehre (Rock-O-Rama Records, 1983; release ID: 838413) by Die Alliierten, a West German post-punk/new wave group. The lineup: Bodo Schwarz (bass), Christoph Hundt (2) (drums), Caspar Brötzmann (guitar), Thomas Tschoeltsch (vocals). Produced by Herbert Egoldt; engineered by Hans-Jürgen Fickel. This was Brötzmann as a young guitarist — likely still a teenager — before he developed the sonic identity associated with Massaker.

Caspar Brötzmann Massaker

Massaker is Brötzmann’s primary project, formed in mid-1980s West Germany. It is not a straightforward guitar/bass/drums rock band: Brötzmann’s approach uses the guitar as a sonic object — atmospheric noise, tonal dynamics, tribal rhythm — over structured compositions with melodic singing and vocal cries. He plays a left-handed Stratocaster upside down, strung right-handed, to extend the sonic potential of the string lengths from bridge, neck, and headstock tuners. Lyrics are mostly in English, some in German, and address existential themes: love, suffering, war, dreams.

The label Our Choice (Discogs 7682; parent: Zomba Records GmbH) was a key home for Massaker from 1992 to 1993. Big Cat co-released Koksofen for the UK market. Home (1994) moved the band to Blast First, co-produced by F.M. Einheit alongside Bruno Gebhard — a significant link between Brötzmann’s avant-metal world and the Berlin industrial underground.

Marat Records (Discogs 38312; sublabel of Zensor) held the early Massaker catalog (The Tribe, Black Axis); notably, Jost Gebers of FMP handled recording on The Tribe — a direct link to Peter Brötzmann’s world.

Solo and Collaboration Work

Last Home (1990) — with Peter Brötzmann

Last Home (Pathological Records, UK, 1990; release ID: 551510) is a duo with Caspar’s father Peter Brötzmann. Peter plays E-flat clarinet, bass saxophone, tenor saxophone, and tárogató; Caspar plays guitar and co-composed. Produced and engineered by Robert Musso; Kevin Martin (Discogs 142639, also known as The Bug (2)) served as additional producer. Photography by Larry Stanley.

This is the most direct documented collaboration between the two generations of the Brötzmann family.

Merry Christmas (1994) — with F.M. Einheit

Merry Christmas (Blast First, 1994; release ID: 238624) is a duo album with F.M. Einheit (Frank Martin Strauß), the percussionist formerly of Einstürzende Neubauten. Both credited as Music By; Blast First catalog number BFFP 97. This was also Caspar’s appearance on F.M. Einheit’s Radio Inferno (1993, Our Choice / Reihe EGO) — a radio-drama adaptation of Dante’s Inferno with voices including Blixa Bargeld and John Peel.

Zulutime (1996) — with Page Hamilton

Zulutime (Blast First / Sub Rosa / Our Choice, 1996; release ID: 103680) is a guitar duo album with Page Hamilton (of Helmet (2)). Both players are credited as Guitar, Producer, and Written-By. Wharton Tiers co-produced alongside Hamilton, Brötzmann, and Bruno Gebhard. Brötzmann supplied the cover artwork (drawing); Bernd Lohaus contributed sculpture artwork; Inspiral Icon handled design.

Blast First catalog number: BFFP 129. Sub Rosa catalog: SR98. This is the only release on which Hamilton appeared as a lead artist outside of Helmet. The meeting bridged the NYC noise-rock world (Hamilton / Tiers / Blast First) with the German avant-metal orbit.

Mute Massaker (1999)

Mute Massaker (Our Choice, 1999; release ID: 655264) is a solo-credited Caspar Brötzmann release — not under the Massaker band name. Lineup: Caspar (guitar, music, cover art), Ottmar Seum (bass), Robert Dämmig (drums). Recorded and mixed by Bruno Gebhard.

Nohome (2013)

Nohome (Discogs 3300341) is a trio: Caspar Brötzmann (electric guitar), Marino Pliakas (electric bass), Michael Wertmüller (drums). The self-titled album was released on Trost Records (Vienna, 2013; release ID: 4580896). F.M. Einheit guests on steel (tracks 3 and 4). Mastered by Martin Siewert; pre-mastering by Ingo Krauss. Photography by Jost Gebers and Peter Gannushkin.

Pliakas and Wertmüller are also the rhythm section in Peter Brötzmann’s Full Blast (2) — making Nohome a generational relay between the two Brötzmanns’ worlds.

Live At Candy Bomber Studios Vol. 1 (2017)

A live trio recording with Massimo Pupillo (bass; also of Zu) and Alexandre Babel (drums). Released on Karlrecords (Discogs 78432; release ID: 10300896). Mastered by Ingo Krauss. This configuration is separate from Massaker.

Caspar Brötzmann Bass Totem (2024)

Caspar Brötzmann Bass Totem (Discogs 15199058) is a trio with Eduardo Delgado-Lopez and Saskia Von Klitzing — the current Massaker rhythm section — released The Lovers And Destroyers (Exile On Mainstream Records, 2024). The Bass Totem name suggests a lower-register, heavier variant of the Massaker approach.

Caspar Brötzmann & The Bonkers

A self-released project (Discogs 6976468), members listed as: Caspar Brötzmann, Bodo Schwarz (bass — also his old Die Alliierten bandmate), Christoph Hundt (2) (drums), Thomas Tschoeltsch (vocals). One self-released recording in the DB; year unknown.

The Peter Brötzmann Tentet — März Combo (1993)

Caspar Brötzmann played guitar on The März Combo Live In Wuppertal (FMP, 1993; release ID: 15874745), credited to The Peter Brötzmann Tentet (Discogs 8181628). The ensemble included William Parker (bass), Anton Fier (drums), Nicky Skopelitis (guitar), Larry Stabbins, Peter Brötzmann, Werner Lüdi (saxophones), Johannes Bauer, Paul Rutherford (2) (trombones), and Toshinori Kondo (trumpet). Produced by Jost Gebers, Peter Brötzmann, and Uli Armbruster. A major cross-generational document: Caspar in a large free-jazz ensemble led by his father, alongside leading figures in the European/American free improv community.
