Gene Dinwiddie

American saxophonist and flautist. Born Charles Eugene Dinwiddie, September 19, 1936; died January 11, 2002. Discogs artist ID: 290033.

Best known as the tenor saxophonist and flautist for The Paul Butterfield Blues Band from 1967 through the band’s dissolution in 1971 — appearing on every album from The Resurrection of Pigboy Crabshaw onward. After Butterfield, Dinwiddie remained active as a session musician and ensemble player through the 1970s and into the 2000s, appearing on sessions with Todd Rundgren, the Guess Who, Todd Rundgren, Etta James, the Gregg Allman Band, and contributing music for Hustlers Convention (1973). His alias “Brother Gene Dinwiddie” appears on the Full Moon (5) self-titled album (2000) and the Hustlers Convention credits.

Biography

Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1967–1971)

Dinwiddie joined the expanded horn-section lineup of The Paul Butterfield Blues Band for The Resurrection of Pigboy Crabshaw (Elektra, 1967), alongside David Sanborn on alto saxophone and Keith Johnson on trumpet. The move toward a larger ensemble was a deliberate stylistic pivot from the earlier blues-rock sound — Phillip Wilson had also just come aboard on drums, and bassist Bugsy Maugh replaced Jerome Arnold.

Dinwiddie’s multi-instrumental range expanded across subsequent albums: by In My Own Dream (1968) he was playing mandolin and contributing to the “The Icebag Four” vocal group alongside Butterfield, Wilson, and Maugh. On Keep On Moving (1969, produced by Jerry Ragovoy) he sang on tracks alongside Buzzy Feiten and other band members. On the live album Live (1970, produced by Todd Rundgren) he took on soprano saxophone as well as tenor. The final studio album, Sometimes I Just Feel Like Smilin’ (1971, produced by Paul A. Rothchild), featured him alongside backing vocalists Merry Clayton, Clydie King, and Venetta Fields.

After the Blues Band dissolved in 1971, he appeared on the posthumous best-of Golden Butter (Elektra, 1972), which compiled material from his tenure.

Session Work (1971–1977)

Following the Butterfield band’s end, Dinwiddie appeared on a cluster of significant 1970s sessions:

  • The James Cotton Blues Band — Taking Care of Business (Capitol, 1971): horns section alongside David Sanborn, Steve Madaio, and Trevor Lawrence, produced by Todd Rundgren and Mark Klingman.
  • Todd Rundgren — Something / Anything? (Bearsville, 1972): tenor saxophone on the side-D ensemble tracks (track D6), where he appeared alongside other ex-Butterfield musicians in Rundgren’s assembled band session.
  • Guess Who — #10 (ABC/Probe, 1972): saxophone.
  • Lightnin’ Rod — Hustlers Convention (United Artists, 1973): composer credit for music on five tracks (A2, A3, A4, A5, B2), the soundtrack to the landmark proto-rap spoken-word album produced by Alan Douglas. Credited as “Brother Gene Dinwiddie.”
  • Etta James — Etta Is Betta Than Evvah! (Chess, 1976): saxophone.
  • Melissa Manchester — Melissa (Arista, 1976): tenor saxophone.
  • Harvey Mason — Crackin’ (Warner Bros., 1977): tenor saxophone, soloist.
  • Warner Bros. sessions — also on Makings of a Dream (Warner Bros., 1977): horns.

The Gregg Allman Band

Dinwiddie is listed as a member of The Gregg Allman Band (Discogs artist ID: 1337588) alongside Neil Larsen (keyboards), Willie Weeks (bass), Dan Toler (guitar), and others. The Gregg Allman Band recorded Playin’ Up A Storm (Polydor, 1977) — Dinwiddie has a saxophone credit on this release.

Full Moon (5)

Dinwiddie was a member of Full Moon (5) (alias of the Larsen-Feiten Band; Discogs ID: 981378), the ensemble led by Neil Larsen and Buzzy Feiten. The self-titled Full Moon album (Dreamsville Records, 2000 — a Japan-only release) credits him as “Brother Gene Dinwiddie” across multiple roles: tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, flute, mandolin, vocals, and backing vocals. The session also featured Phillip Wilson on drums, Willie Weeks on bass, Airto Moreira and Ray Barretto on percussion, and Randy Brecker on trumpet. Producer: Alan Douglas.

Juma Sultan’s Aboriginal Music Society

Dinwiddie is listed as a member of Juma Sultan’s Aboriginal Music Society (Discogs ID: 3051874), the collective led by percussionist Juma Sultan. The album Father of Origin (Eremite Records, 2011) — a compilation of archival recordings — credits him on tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, and flute on the MTE-54 and MTE-56 sessions, alongside Julius Hemphill (alto sax), Frank Lowe (tenor sax), Abdul Wadud (cello), Phillip Wilson (drums), and others.

Later Credits

A 1995 recording, Strawberry Jam (Winner Records), featuring unreleased Butterfield Blues Band material from 1967–68, credits Dinwiddie on tenor saxophone — produced by Mark Naftalin. He died January 11, 2002.
