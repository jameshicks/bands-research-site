Paul Northfield

Record producer and recording/mixing engineer. Discogs artist ID: 252389

Career spans from tape operator at Advision Studios (London, UK) in the early 1970s through to the 2010s–2020s, with a defining chapter anchored at Le Studio (Morin Heights, Québec), the residential facility where he became the in-house engineer for Rush’s mid-period run. Name variations on records: Northfield, P Northfield, P. Northfield, Paul “Slowhand” Northfield, Paul North Field.

Biography

Early Years — London (1973–1979)

Northfield started in the industry at age 17 as a tape operator at Advision Studios (London), a studio whose parent company was Advision Ltd. His first recorded credits (1973) are as an assistant engineer on sessions for Flash (Out Of Our Hands) and the solo album Peter Banks (Peter Banks), both on Sovereign Records. Through the mid-1970s he worked as assistant engineer on significant progressive rock sessions:

  • Steve Hillage — Fish Rising (1975, Virgin) — mix assistant
  • Gentle Giant — Free Hand (1975, Chrysalis) and Playing The Fool (live, 1977) and The Missing Piece (1977)
  • Judas Priest — Stained Class (1978, CBS) — engineer
  • Manfred Mann’s Earth Band — The Roaring Silence (1977) — mixing assistance

He moved up from tape op to full engineer credit during 1975–1977 on albums including Beggar’s Opera (Beginnings, 1975, Atlantic) and multiple Gentle Giant records for Chrysalis.

After five years at Advision he joined the engineering staff at Le Studio (Morin Heights, Québec), a residential recording facility in the Laurentian Mountains owned by André Perry. Le Studio had become the primary recording base for Rush, and Northfield’s arrival there marks the start of his long association with the band.

The Rush Years at Le Studio (1980–1989)

Northfield is credited on every Rush studio and major live record from Permanent Waves (1980) through A Show Of Hands (1988), working alongside producer Terry Brown (“Broon”).

Grace Under Pressure was a transitional album — producer Terry Brown was replaced by Peter Henderson, and Northfield’s role shifted to synthesizer programming support rather than lead engineer. This is the one Rush album in the run where he is not the primary studio engineer.

The Signals (1982) era also saw Northfield credited on a Rush single: “The Weapon / Digital Man” (Anthem, 1983) as engineer and mixer.

Le Studio and the Canadian Rock Scene (1979–1989)

Based at Le Studio, Northfield became a central figure in the Canadian rock recording world. Key non-Rush projects from this base:

  • April Wine — You Want It, You Got It (1981, A&M) — technician/engineer; Animal Grace (1984, Aquarius/Capitol) — recording engineer
  • Queensrÿche — Operation: Mindcrime (1988, EMI-Manhattan) — Engineer at Le Studio (James “Jimbo” Barton engineered at Kajem/Victory; Peter Collins produced)
  • Queensrÿche — Empire (1990, EMI USA) — Recorded By (one track, B3); Barton recorded the main sessions
  • Asia (2) — Alpha (1983, Geffen) — Engineer, Mixed By, Recorded By alongside Mike Stone (who also produced)
  • Asia (2) — Don’t Cry single (1983, Geffen) — Engineer
  • Bryan Adams — Cuts Like A Knife (1983, A&M) — Engineer [Assistant] (main engineer was Bob Clearmountain)
  • Men Without Hats — Pop Goes The World (1987, Mercury) — Mixed By
  • Kim Mitchell — Shakin’ Like A Human Being (1986, Alert) and Rocket Science material — Recorded By / Mixed By

Also during this period, Le Studio sessions touched the Québécois francophone market — Paul Piché (Paul Piché, 1982, Kébec-Disc) — where Northfield served as producer (Réalisation) and recorded/mixed the album; unusual for an anglophone engineer of this era to cross into the French-Canadian market.

Return to Rush — Different Stages and Vapor Trails (1998–2002)

Northfield returned to a prominent Rush credit as co-producer and engineer on two late-period releases:

Different Stages · Live (1998, Anthem/Atlantic)

  • Produced by Geddy Lee and Paul Northfield
  • Engineered and Mixed By Paul Northfield
  • Mix assistants: Denis Tougas, John Biondich
  • Included a 1978 Hammersmith concert (engineered by Terry Brown)

Vapor Trails (2002, Anthem/Atlantic)

  • Produced by Rush and Paul Northfield
  • Recorded by Alex Lifeson, Geddy Lee, and Paul Northfield
  • Mixed by David Leonard (assisted by Joel Kazmi)
  • Chris Stringer assisted recording
  • This was Rush’s first studio album in five years, following Neil Peart’s personal tragedies; Northfield’s steady presence was a notable continuity factor

Single releases from the Vapor Trails campaign:

  • “One Little Victory / Earthshine” (2002, Anthem/Atlantic) — Producer
  • “Secret Touch” (2002, Anthem/Atlantic) — Producer

Pure Prog / UK Prog (2005–2006)

A parallel thread in the mid-2000s placed Northfield as producer/engineer on UK progressive rock releases:

  • Pure Reason Revolution — The Bright Ambassadors Of Morning (2005) and The Intention Craft (2005, Holograph/Sony BMG) — Engineer, Mixed By, Producer
  • Pure Reason Revolution — The Dark Third (2006, Columbia) — Producer

Studio Affiliations

Three studios define the arc of Northfield’s career:

  1. Advision Studios (London) — tape op / early engineer, early-to-mid 1970s. Parent: Advision Ltd. Had a mobile unit (Advision Mobile).
  2. Le Studio (Morin Heights, Québec) — resident engineer, ~1979–1989+. Parent: Studios André Perry. This is the facility inextricably linked to Rush’s classic period.
  3. Avatar Studios (New York) — later-career sessions. Avatar has sublabels including Avatar Studio A and Avatar Studio C. Northfield has also engineered there.
