Caiphus Semenya

Discogs ID: 298574 Real name: Caiphus Katse Semenya Active: 1959–2023 (Discogs span) Also credited as: Caution (9) (Discogs ID: 1278745)

South African composer, multi-instrumentalist (alto saxophone, piano, vocals), arranger, and producer. Husband and primary collaborator of singer Letta Mbulu. One of the key figures of the South African exile music community in Los Angeles from the late 1960s through the late 1980s, working at the intersection of African mbaqanga, jazz, soul, and orchestral pop. Returned to South Africa with Mbulu in the early 1990s after approximately 26 years of self-imposed exile driven by apartheid.

Biography

Early Career and South Africa (1950s–late 1960s)

Semenya’s earliest Discogs credit is a 1959 recording: King Kong — All African Jazz Opera (Gallotone, South Africa), where he sang with the Katzenjammer Kids ensemble. This was a landmark South African jazz musical that toured internationally. By the mid-1960s he was writing and recording under the name Katse Semenya and was contributing songs to township and urban jazz projects.

Los Angeles Exile Period (late 1960s–1991)

In the late 1960s Semenya relocated to Los Angeles, driven out of South Africa by apartheid. He quickly embedded himself in the city’s jazz and soul scene, connecting with Harry Belafonte and Quincy Jones, and networking closely with fellow South African exiles Hugh Masekela, Miriam Makeba, and Jonas Gwangwa.

1968–1971: Early LA Credits

He contributed piano to UNI Records’ Elements Of Now! (1968) and wrote compositions and liner notes for Capitol Records’ Free Soul (Letta Mbulu, 1968). He appeared as vocalist and alto saxophonist on Hugh Masekela & The Union Of South Africa (Chisa, 1971), alongside Masekela and Gwangwa — one of the sharpest documents of the exile community’s sound.

1972–1974: Producer and Arranger

Semenya co-produced (with Stewart Levine) Hugh Masekela’s Home Is Where The Music Is (Blue Thumb Records, 1972). He then produced and arranged Letta Mbulu’s Naturally (Fantasy, 1973), which featured Cannonball Adderley and Nat Adderley as guest artists. In 1974 he co-produced and arranged Miriam Makeba’s A Promise (RCA Victor / AMIGA), handling both the overall arrangement and co-production alongside Makeba herself, with string arrangements by David Axelrod and a rhythm section including Joe Sample (piano), David T. Walker (guitar), and Stix Hooper (drums).

1976–1978: A&M Records Period

Semenya became a central figure at A&M Records in the second half of the 1970s, producing and co-producing several albums for Letta Mbulu and collaborating extensively with Herb Alpert:

  • There’s Music In The Air (Letta Mbulu, A&M, 1976) — co-produced, arranged, orchestrated, and sang on multiple tracks; the sleeve notes were written by Quincy Jones
  • African Summer (Herb Alpert, A&M, 1977) — composed, arranged, and co-produced the entire album alongside Alpert
  • Herb Alpert / Hugh Masekela (A&M, 1977) — arranged horns, played piano, sang backing vocals, co-produced
  • Roots: The Saga of an American Family (Quincy Jones, A&M, 1977) — contributed arrangements, percussion, and vocals (alongside Letta Mbulu as a featured soloist) for the landmark TV miniseries soundtrack
  • Letta (Letta Mbulu, A&M, 1978) — produced (with Alpert and Stewart Levine), arranged rhythm and background vocals, sang backing vocals

1977: “Roots” and the “Many Rains Ago (Oluwa)” credit

Semenya co-wrote “Many Rains Ago (Oluwa)” / “Roots Medley” for the Roots soundtrack — one of the most-heard pieces of music of 1977. The writing credit and his vocal/percussion performance on the LP cemented his profile in Hollywood.

1980–1984: Return to African Contexts

Sound Of Rainbow (Letta Mbulu, Disques Espérance, 1980) — backing vocals and vocal arrangements. Kilimanjaro (Letta Mbulu, MJS Records, 1981) — produced and co-wrote. In 1982 Semenya released his first full solo LP under his own name: Angelina (Munjale, South Africa, 1982), produced by himself. Listen To The Wind (CBS, Kenya, 1982) followed as another primary-credited solo release. In The Music The Village Never Ends (Munjale, 1983) was a further Letta Mbulu production. His most substantial solo statement in this period was Streams Today, Rivers Tomorrow (Priority Records (6), Kenya, 1984), featuring South African musicians including Sipho Gumede, Mara Louw, and Condry Ziqubu.

Late 1980s–1991

Semenya contributed “The Places You Find Love” (written-by credit) to Quincy Jones’s Back On The Block (Qwest/Warner Bros., 1989/1991). He also wrote on the 1989 Quincy Jones album The Places You Find Love (Qwest). He and Letta Mbulu returned to South Africa in the early 1990s.

Post-Return South Africa (1991–present)

Back in South Africa, Semenya continued producing Letta Mbulu (Not Yet Uhuru, Munjale Productions, 1992) and issued new solo material: Woman Got A Right To Be (Artone, 1994). Multiple compilation releases gathered their joint catalog through the 1990s and 2000s. A 2017 Essential Caiphus Semenya (Sony Music, South Africa) marked continued catalogue recognition. His vocal solo credit appears as recently as 2013 on the Mandela: Long Walk To Freedom film score (Decca/Universal, 2013).
