Mark Linkous

Real name: Frederick Mark Linkous. US singer, songwriter, and multi-instrumentalist. Discogs artist ID: 251879. Principal figure behind Sparklehorse. Born September 9, 1962 in Arlington, Virginia. Died March 6, 2010 in Knoxville, Tennessee.

Biography

Early Career — Dancing Hoods and Punk Scene

Linkous began his musical career in punk bands, co-founding Dancing Hoods (Discogs ID: 1685609) with members Bob Bortnick, Don Short, and Eric Williams. The band released two albums on Confidential Discs and Relativity Records:

  • Dancing Hoods (1984, Confidential Discs) — Linkous: vocals, guitar
  • 12 Jealous Roses (1985, Relativity)
  • Hallelujah Anyway (1988, Relativity) — electric guitar, acoustic guitar, vocals; co-written

He was also briefly credited as a member of A Loose Confederation Of Saturday City-States (Discogs ID: 1548773).

Sparklehorse, 1995–2010

Sparklehorse was Linkous’s primary vehicle from the mid-1990s until his death. The project was fundamentally solo — Linkous played nearly every instrument on the studio records — but carried a full band name. On the debut he played vocals, guitar, bass, electric piano, piano, organ, sampler, vibraphone, harmonium, concertina, percussion, and drum machine. Other credited members over the years include David Bush, Kendall Jane Meade, Paul Dillon, and Scott Minor.

Sparklehorse debuted on Capitol Records in the US, with UK releases through Parlophone/EMI. The final two albums moved to Astralwerks (US) and later Anti-.

Dark Night Of The Soul (2009 / 2010)

A collaboration between Sparklehorse (Linkous), Danger Mouse (Brian Burton), and filmmaker David Lynch, who contributed visual art / photography to an accompanying photo book. Primary credit: Danger Mouse And Sparklehorse Visuals By David Lynch.

Linkous’s roles: engineer, co-written-by, co-producer, mixed by (with Danger Mouse). The project was originally self-released in 2009 by Danger Mouse on Not On Label when EMI blocked the commercial release due to a dispute with Danger Mouse. The album circulated as a blank CD-R with a photo book. A commercial version was eventually released in 2010 on Lex Records / Parlophone / EMI after the legal dispute resolved — the 2010 pressing credits Linkous as Engineer; Written-By, Producer, Mixed By.

Guest vocalists on the album include Black Francis, Gruff Rhys, Iggy Pop, James Mercer, Jason Lytle, Julian Casablancas, Nina Persson, Suzanne Vega, Vic Chesnutt, and The Flaming Lips.

Linkous had already worked with Danger Mouse when DM co-produced four tracks on Dreamt For Light Years In The Belly Of A Mountain (2006).

In The Fishtank 15 (2009)

Released as Sparklehorse + Fennesz on the Konkurrent label (Netherlands), part of the long-running In The Fishtank series of artist collaboration recordings. Both Linkous and Christian Fennesz are credited with: Acoustic Guitar, Electric Guitar, Keyboards, Computer, Electronics. All songs written by both (co-credited songwriter). Produced, mixed, and mastered by Zlaya Hadzich.

A Camp was the project of Nina Persson (of The Cardigans), Nathan Larson, and Niclas Frisk. Linkous produced the entire debut self-titled album. It was an extensive session: he contributed sampler, guitar, vocals, and Chamberlin synthesizer, and the album featured a large ensemble including Jonathan Donahue (Saw), Joan Wasser (violin), Jane Scarpantoni (cello), and Julia Kent (cello).

The Daniel Johnston work is particularly significant: Linkous produced Fear Yourself (2003), Johnston’s first major-label album, and subsequently served as executive producer on the tribute compilation The Late Great Daniel Johnston: Discovered Covered (2004, Gammon Records).
