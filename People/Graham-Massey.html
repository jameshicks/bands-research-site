Graham Massey

British musician, producer, multi-instrumentalist, remixer. Born 4 August 1960, Manchester, UK. Discogs artist ID: 4724. Real name: Graham Vernon Massey.

Core member and primary instrumentalist of 808 State, the Manchester electronic group whose run from 1988 to the mid-1990s on ZTT Records placed them at the centre of the UK rave and post-acid-house moment. Before 808 State, Massey spent most of the 1980s in post-punk/industrial group Biting Tongues, recording for Factory Records. After 808 State’s commercial peak he continued as a producer and session musician across a wide range of contexts — co-writing “Army Of Me” with Björk, producing tracks on her Post album, playing with large ensemble Homelife on Ninja Tune, and recording solo as Massonix (Discogs ID: 295188) and under the alias Prof Vernon World (Discogs ID: 1188723). Active through the 2020s.

Name variations in Discogs: G. Massey, G. V. Massey, GM, Graham Massex, Graham Massey (Massonix), Graham Massey Of 808 State, Graham V. Massey, Graham Vernon Massey, Massey, Massey Graham.

Biography

Pre-Biting Tongues: The Post Natals

Before Biting Tongues, Massey and Colin Seddon played together in a Manchester group called the Post Natals. When they disbanded, both joined Biting Tongues — which had been started by Howard Walmsley, Ken Hollings and Eddie Sherwood in 1979 at the Factory Club in Hulme, initially as a live soundtrack ensemble for a Walmsley film. Massey thus came to Biting Tongues already working with Seddon.

Biting Tongues and the Factory Records Years (1979–1987)

Massey joined Biting Tongues in 1979, a few weeks after the group’s formation, becoming the group’s central instrumentalist and eventually its producer. The collective also included Howard Walmsley, Ken Hollings, Colin Seddon, Eddie Sherwood, Paddy Steer, and others. The band’s debut LP Don’t Heal appeared on Situation Two in 1981 (release 236220), with Massey credited as “Graham V. Massey” on guitar. Their album Libreville followed on Paragon in 1983 (release 231909).

Biting Tongues moved to Factory Records for a run of releases from 1985. The Factory relationship placed Massey in the same milieu as Joy Division, New Order and the Manchester scene that would generate acid house. His detailed instrumental credits on Compressor (1987, release 93222) — “vocals, synthesizer [ST20, DX7, VSS100]” — document his move toward electronics and already indicate the hybrid approach he would carry through his career.

During the mid-1980s, Massey trained as a sound engineer at Manchester’s Spirit Studios (while also working as a live sound engineer at the Boardwalk Club). This technical grounding directly enabled his move into production for 808 State. He also acted as a thief character in Howard Walmsley’s Feverhouse film (1985), for which Biting Tongues provided the soundtrack.

808 State Formation and the Creed Records Period (1988–1989)

808 State formed in Manchester in 1988, initially a collective drawn from the Hacienda/acid-house scene. Massey co-founded it alongside Martin Price and Gerald Simpson while still nominally a member of Biting Tongues. In November 1988, he simultaneously began recording two albums: Recharge for Biting Tongues, and Quadrastate for 808 State — both in overlapping sessions. The story goes that if Howard Walmsley had not left his soprano saxophone in the studio overnight, the saxophone line on “Pacific State” might never have existed. Recharge circulated only on white label when the Cut Deep label collapsed in 1989; 808 State became the primary focus from that point.

The founding lineup of 808 State included Massey, Martin Price, and Gerald Simpson (who recorded separately as A Guy Called Gerald). Their first full-length, Newbuild (Creed Records, 1988; release 3551), was produced by the group with Massey as engineer; A Guy Called Gerald appeared as featured guest. Songwriting credits on Newbuild were split between Massey, Simpson, and Price.

Gerald Simpson departed to pursue his own career before the next release. The group now comprised Massey, Martin Price, Andy Barker, and Darren Partington. The Quadrastate mini-LP (Creed Records, 1989; release 3554) contained the original six-minute version of “Pacific State” — credited to Massey/Simpson/Price — alongside tracks credited to Massey with Barker and Partington. Massey is credited as “Recorded By, Programmed By, Edited By” on the full release. Quadrastate was the first document of the new four-piece configuration.

808 State on ZTT: Ninety / 90 and ex:el (1989–1991)

The group signed to Trevor Horn’s ZTT Records and issued Ninety in 1989 (release 3497; also known as 90). 808 State are credited collectively as composer and producer; Massey is individually credited as engineer. The album’s styles span acid house and techno, with “Pacific 202” and “Magical Dream” (vocals: Vanessa) among the standout tracks.

ex:el (1991; release 52327, ZTT/Tommy Boy) became the group’s defining album: a set of collaborations with high-profile guests that demonstrated the range of Massey and the group’s musical reach. Björk contributes vocals and lyrics to “Qmart” and “Ooops”; Bernard Sumner (New Order) sings “Spanish Heart.” Massey is individually credited as engineer on tracks 9, 10 and 13; the group collectively as producer and composer.

Around this period Massey remixed Primal Scream’s “Don’t Fight It, Feel It” (Creation Records, 1991; release 167580), taking producer, programmer and remix credits.

Gorgeous (1993) and the Peak ZTT Years

Gorgeous (1993; release 46999) documented a more eclectic phase: credits include vocals from Ian McCulloch, Paul Weller, UB40, and Caroline Seaman, with Massey credited in the unusual role of “CGI Artist [Images]” alongside Howard Walmsley. The album also incorporates a New Order instrumental interpolation (“Contrique,” written by Sumner/Curtis/Hook/Morris).

Other key ZTT singles from this era include “Cübik / Olympic” (WEA/ZTT, 1990), “In Yer Face” (1990), “Tunes Splits The Atom” (1990, with MC Tunes), “Lift / Open Your Mind” (1991), and “Ooops” (1991). 808 State contributed remixes of Frankie Goes To Hollywood’s “Two Tribes” (ZTT, 1993) and David Bowie’s “Sound + Vision.”

Björk: Post Productions (1994–1996)

Massey’s relationship with Björk began during the ex:el sessions. He co-wrote and co-produced “Army Of Me” — the lead single from Post (One Little Indian, 1995; release 102481) — sharing writing and production credit with Björk and Nellee Hooper. He also co-wrote and co-produced “The Modern Things” and plays keyboards on both tracks plus on the release overall (tracks 1 and 3).

808 State: Don Solaris and Later Work (1996–present)

By Don Solaris (ZTT, 1996; release 14817) the 808 State lineup had contracted to three: Massey, Andy Barker, and Darren Partington (Martin Price having departed). The album features guest vocals from Mike Doughty (Soul Coughing), Louise Rhodes, James Dean Bradfield (Manic Street Preachers), and Ragga.

The compilation 808:88:98 (ZTT, 1998) surveyed the group’s decade. 808 State issued Outpost Transmission (Circus Records/Simply Vinyl, 2002) and a series of live sets and self-released material through the 2000s–2010s, including the archival Blueprint (Salvo/ZTT, 2011). In 2021, Massey released Moments In Love (The Massey Mixes) on ZTT, reworking the Art of Noise track.

Massonix Solo Work

Under the Massonix alias (Discogs ID: 295188), Massey issued a 12” single — “Just A Little Bit More” (Noise Records 2, 1990; release 427809) — featuring Denise Johnson on vocals and Massey producing. The full-length SubTracks (Skam, 2006; release 856742, cat no: SKALD080) is a 13-track solo CD exploring oceanic imagery through abstract electronics, IDM and electro, released on Skam Records (the Manchester label also home to Autechre and Boards of Canada). Massey is sole composer and producer.

A third Massonix release, State Ritual Live @ Glastonbury 2008, documents a live performance (self-released, 2008).

Sisters of Transistors and Prof Vernon World (2009)

Massey served as composer, producer, drummer and additional synthesizer player for Sisters Of Transistors’ At The Ferranti Institute (This Is Music, 2009; release 2272542), appearing throughout under the alias “Prof Vernon World.” The album is a concept piece framed around north-west English electric organ culture; Massey wrote music for most tracks and contributed synth and vocals under the alias. The group (Discogs ID: 1188727) is a collective of women performers. Prof Vernon World is a separate Discogs entry (ID: 1188723).

Homelife (1998–2009)

Homelife (Discogs ID: 10507) is a large Manchester-based ensemble — roughly 19 credited members — including Massey alongside Paddy Steer, James Ford, Seaming To, and others. The group released on Ninja Tune: Flying Wonders (2002; release 43660), the Flying Wonders / Wobbly Jack EP (2003; release 246338, with Massey on Moog synthesizer, vocoder and bass clarinet), and Guru Man Hubcap Lady (2004; release 309202, Massey on baritone sax, bass clarinet, piano, prepared piano, recorders and synthesizer). Also: Exotic Interlude (Humble Soul, 2009).

Film Scores and Other Work (2000s)

Massey composed the original score for the film It’s All Gone Pete Tong (Positiva, 2005; release 1029224). He curated the Rephlex compilation Prebuild (2004; release 333194), drawing on his knowledge of the pre-808 State Creed Records recordings, and contributed liner notes to LTM’s Compressed (The Factory Recordings 1984-87) (2003) and appeared as interviewee on LTM’s Shadowplayers (Factory Records post-punk compilation, 2006).

Other credits in this era: vibraphone on Lamb’s debut album Lamb (Fontana, 1996; release 11651) and on Gold’s Gold (Fontana, 1996; release 32696).

Turkish Jazz Collaborations and Live Work (2016–present)

Massey has collaborated extensively with the Istanbul-based free jazz ensemble Konstrukt (Discogs ID: 638280) and associated musicians, including Umut Çağlar, Korhan Futacı, Berke Can Özcan and Barlas Tan Özemek. The shared releases include Live At Islington Mill (Astral Spirits/Monofonus Press, 2016; release 8690664) on which Massey plays electric guitar and bass clarinet. Multiple releases in this constellation document an ongoing engagement with improvised jazz and free music.

In 2023 Massey contributed additional writing to the track “Venom” (Republic Records/Slowplay, 2023; release 28714675). He also remixed Electronic’s “Disappointed” (Rhino, 2023) and provided liner notes for the 808 State archive release 808 Sunset 90 (DDS, 2023).
