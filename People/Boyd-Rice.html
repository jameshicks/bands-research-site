Boyd Rice

Discogs ID: 49845
Real name: Boyd Blake Rice
Born: December 16, 1956, Lemon Grove, California, USA
Based: Denver, Colorado
Primary alias: NON (Discogs ID: 12710)

Boyd Blake Rice is an American experimental musician, noise/industrial pioneer, performance artist, writer, and photographer. Operating primarily as NON since the mid-1970s, he is one of the founding figures of industrial music — preceded by very few practitioners worldwide when he began manipulating tape loops and building noise structures in 1975. Over five decades he has released across Mute, Soleilmoon, New European Recordings, World Serpent, and smaller imprints, and collaborated with a broad network of post-industrial, neofolk, and occult-experimental artists including Frank Tovey (Fad Gadget), Douglas Pearce (Death in June), John Balance and Peter Christopherson (Coil), David Tibet (Current 93), Rose McDowall (Strawberry Switchblade), and Michael Moynihan (Blood Axis).

Rice’s music spans pure noise, looped-groove experiments, drone, neo-martial folk, and spoken-word. He has cultivated deliberately provocative iconography drawing on fascist aesthetics, Social Darwinism, and occult / Satanic imagery; he was a priest and later magister in Anton LaVey’s Church of Satan and co-founded the Abraxas Foundation (1987). His views have generated sustained controversy and contributed to formal separations with collaborators including Peter Christopherson, Frank Tovey, and Jello Biafra. (Source: Wikipedia / Boyd Rice)

Research Queue

  • Does the 2012 Back To Mono LP contain previously unreleased material or is it exclusively archival? The Discogs entry credits both NON and Boyd Rice as primary — is this a proper new recording or compilation?
  • Confirm whether the Abraxas Foundation (1987–1992) released any Discogs-documented recordings distinct from Rice’s solo and NON output, or whether it existed only as a print/lecture organisation.
  • The Scorpion Wind LP (Nerus, 2007, Discogs ID 1213132) is a reissue/remaster of the 1996 Heaven Sent double LP (11 tracks), with 2 bonus tracks added (“Funeral For 3” 13:12; “A Toast” 0:14). The Discogs primary credit “Death In June & Boyd Rice” appears to be a label cataloguing choice rather than a distinct recording. Note: Heaven Sent was a double LP, not a 7”.
