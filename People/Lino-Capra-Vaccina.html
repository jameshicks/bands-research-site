Biography

Formation: Aktuala and the Italian Avant-Garde (early 1970s)

Lino Capra Vaccina emerged from the fertile Italian experimental music scene of the early 1970s as a percussionist with an unusually broad timbral palette — African percussion, South Asian tabla, orchestral gong, and vibraphone all appear in his documented credits from the very beginning. His first major affiliation was as a founding member of Aktuala (Discogs ID: 1775431), a Milan-based world-music and free-improvisation ensemble led by Walter Maioli that released two albums on the Bla Bla label in 1973 and 1974.

The debut Aktuala (1973, Bla Bla, cat. BBL 11054) is a representative document of what the group did: a long-form improvised sonic world drawing on Moroccan bongos, African drums, tabla, gong, xylophone, Arabic oboe, bamboo flute, and extended saxophone — music that prefigured what would later be called world fusion or ECM-style free improvisation. The Aktuala lineup included Trilok Gurtu (later an internationally prominent tabla virtuoso) and saxophonist Daniele Cavallanti. The second Aktuala album, La Finestra Dentro (1974, Bla Bla), continued in the same direction with Vaccina contributing percussion throughout.

Telaio Magnetico: Franco Battiato’s Experimental Circle (1973–1978)

The other defining early association was Telaio Magnetico (Discogs ID: 1972713), the avant-garde ensemble whose members included Franco Battiato, Juri Camisasca (Roberto Camisasca), Roberto Mazza, Giacomo Di Martino, and Terra Di Benedetto. Battiato in this period was producing his most uncompromising electronic and conceptual work — the “noise” LPs on Bla Bla (Fetus, Pollution, Sulle Corde Di Aries) and the 1973 collaboration Vietato Ai Minori Di 18 Anni? (Philips), on which Vaccina is credited with tabla and percussion.

The Telaio Magnetico circle is where Vaccina met Juri Camisasca, and the two formed a long-term musical partnership. Camisasca’s devotional, liturgical sensibility would become one of the key tonalities in Vaccina’s mature solo work.

Also from this period: Energie (1981, CGD), the album by Battiato’s pop-crossover phase, on which Vaccina plays timpani — and the Philarmonic credit under Claudio Abbado at La Scala. Vaccina played with the La Scala Philharmonic under Abbado, establishing his classical orchestral credentials alongside his avant-garde work.

Antico Adagio — Solo Debut (1978)

Vaccina’s debut as a sole primary artist came with Antico Adagio (1978, Nō Records, cat. LP 001) — a six-track LP that stands as one of the more unusual Italian records of its decade. Credited to Vaccina alone, with no additional performer credits listed on the release, Antico Adagio leans into minimalism and contemplative space. Track titles — “Elegia,” “Canti Delle Sfere,” “Movimenti E Silenzi Per Spazi Bianchi,” “Antico Adagio” — signal the meditative mode that would define his solo aesthetic. The label Nō (cat. LP 001) appears to be a private or micro-edition release. The album has since accumulated a cult reputation and been reissued multiple times (7 pressings tracked on Discogs).

Studio Silence (1978–1992)

After the solo debut, Vaccina’s primary-artist credits pause for fourteen years. He continued as a session and ensemble player: timpani on Franco Battiato’s Energie (1981), marimba and timpani on Alla Periferia Dell’Impero (1984, Polydor), and tabla on the Battiato masterwork Mondi Lontanissimi (1985, La Voce Del Padrone) — one of Battiato’s most commercially successful records and a landmark of Italian art-pop. In the late 1970s and early 1980s he also contributed timpani to Capo Nord (1980, EMI), which appears to be a Battiato-adjacent project.

L’Attesa — Return as Solo Artist (1992)

The second solo album, L’Attesa (1992, Lynx Records, cat. Z 00841), is a more fully elaborated production — a limited LP with a seven-track programme. For this record Vaccina assembled a small ensemble: Juri Camisasca on voice, Roberto Colombo (computer, piano, co-producer), Martin La Serra (piano), Aldo Orsini (guitar), Samuele Vaccina (presumably a family member; credited for acoustic effects and eolotubolari — aeolian tubes), and Esau Remor (engineer/mix). Vaccina himself plays gong, tabla, vibraphone, tambura, piano, eolotubolari, and a range of acoustic effects, and co-produces. The album spans ambient, experimental, and new-age territories, with “Echi Del Telaio Persico” explicitly referencing the Telaio Magnetico heritage.

Dormancy and the Live Archive (1992–2015)

Between the 1992 LP and the 2015 comeback, Vaccina’s Discogs footprint is sparse. The 1995 live album Live ‘75 (Musicando, cat. M3 04) — documenting an Aktuala-adjacent performance, with Vaccina on vibraphone, tabla, gong, and cymbals — is the only release from this twenty-year span.

Dark Companion Era — The Late Flourishing (2015–2022)

Vaccina’s third creative period began with the launch of Dark Companion (Discogs ID: 926330), the Italian experimental label operated by producer Massimo Marchini. From 2015 to 2022, Vaccina released a sustained sequence of solo and collaborative albums on Dark Companion, establishing himself as a central figure in the label’s identity.

Arcaico Armonico (2015, Dark Companion DC 004): Five tracks, limited CD. The core ensemble was Vaccina (gong, cymbal, voice, tabla, bells, bass drum, African monostring, piano, vibraphone, cowbell, sabar drum, aeolustubulars) with Paolo Tofani contributing mandhura (Indian fretted lute) on track 2, and Camillo Mozzoni on oboe (tracks 4–5). Massimo Marchini (credited as Max Marchini) produced and contributed additional gong and percussion; Alberto Callegari (Discogs ID: 533691) recorded and mixed. The album’s language is post-minimalist, with long sustaining timbres and a global-instrument approach that traces a line directly back to the Aktuala period. Track time totals exceed 50 minutes across five pieces.

Metafisiche Del Suono (2017, Dark Companion DC 005): Six tracks, limited CD. Vaccina composes all music and plays vibraphone, grand piano, metallophone, gongs, bells, tabla, strings, wind chimes, cymbal, timpani. Guest contributors: Michael Tanner (Discogs ID: 950300, also known as Michael Tanner / Plinth) on psaltery, autoharp, dulcimer, electric guitar, and treatments (tracks 5–6); Alison Cotton on viola (track 6); Mozzoni on oboe and English horn (track 2). Marchini produced and recorded; Callegari mixed and mastered. The album consolidates the drone-and-resonance approach with a more chamber-like refinement.

A Mid Autumn’s Night Dream (2019, Dark Companion DCLP006): LP. A collaborative recording co-credited to Keith Tippett and Julie Tippetts and Paolo Tofani. The four principals jointly composed and performed the two side-long improvisations: Keith Tippett on piano, musical box, woodblocks, pebbles, and maracas; Julie Tippetts on voice, small bells, thumb piano, swivel drum, and miniature xylophone; Paolo Tofani on veena (madhura), santoor, electronics, and various devices; Vaccina on vibraphone, cymbals, percussion, gongs, tam-tam, timpani, chimes, and bells. Vaccina and Callegari co-mixed; Marchini produced. The album sits at the intersection of free improvisation, South Asian classical influence, and the English avant-jazz tradition that the Tippetts represent.

Perpetual Possibility (2019, Dark Companion DCLP011): LP. A duo with Untitled Noise — the electronics/sound-art project of Michele Lombardelli and Luca Scarabelli. Vaccina plays vibraphone, piano, glockenspiel, gongs, chimes, percussion, bowed instruments, and cymbals against their analog electronics, oscillators, and struck metals. Marchini produced; Callegari mastered. The album extends Vaccina’s sound-art credentials into dark ambient and abstract territory.

Sincretico Modale (2022, Dark Companion DC014): CD. The most recent solo release. Vaccina plays grand piano, vibraphone, mallets, gong, chimes, percussion, harmonium, cymbal, bells, tabla, and voice, and composes all music. Camillo Mozzoni returns on oboe (track 2); Marchini produces; Callegari records, mixes, and masters. Five tracks; the title — “syncretic modal” — accurately describes the music’s synthesis of Indian modal concepts with contemporary chamber minimalism.

Collaborative Highlights: Barbazza, Greaves, Tarkus Revisited (2018–2021)

Beyond the Dark Companion solo series, three collaborative credits of particular weight:

John Greaves — Life Size (2018, Manticore, MANLP003): Vaccina composed the music and plays piano and percussion on track B6 (“Lie Still, Sleep Becalmed”) — a setting of a Dylan Thomas poem. This is the only track on the album not composed by Greaves himself (or Phil Miller, who contributes one other). The same recording features Annie Barbazza in multiple roles, Jakko M. Jakszyk (electric guitar, track A4), Zeena Parkins (harp, track B4), Sophia Domancich (piano, track B2), Olivier Mellano (guitar, four tracks), and Vincent Courtois (cello). Callegari engineered, mixed, and mastered.

Annie Barbazza — Vive (2020, Dark Companion): Vaccina plays percussion, gong, bells, and vibraphone, and contributes vocals, on tracks 4, 6, 10, and 12. This is the record that most fully situated Vaccina within the Dark Companion circle’s international context.

Tarkus Revisited (2021, Ma.Ra.Cash Records, MRC103): Co-primary credit alongside Annie Barbazza and Tempus Fugit Percussion Ensemble. The album re-stages the ELP Tarkus suite in a percussion-and-voice format, with Barbazza on lead vocals (tracks 1–8), Vaccina on vibraphone, gong, chimes, percussion, bells, and finger cymbals (track 9), and a larger percussion ensemble. The one original track, “Descent Into The Void” (track 9), was jointly composed by Vaccina, Francesco Brianzi, Massimo Marchini, and Tommaso Franguelli. Co-produced by Brianzi; produced overall by Marchini.

Lino Capra Vaccina Ensemble

Discogs also lists a project called Lino Capra Vaccina Ensemble (Discogs ID: 4499210), with members Vaccina and Juri Camisasca; this appears to be the formal vehicle for some of his ensemble performances, though specific releases under this name are not in the database.

Scene Context

Vaccina’s career traces a path through three of the most important Italian experimental-music formations. Aktuala was one of the first Italian groups to seriously engage with non-Western percussion traditions — a global-instrument approach that predates the “world music” category by a decade. Telaio Magnetico placed him in the orbit of Franco Battiato’s most radical experimental phase (1971–1976), a period when Battiato was producing musique-concrète-adjacent noise records that were entirely at odds with his later pop-star career. And the Battiato session work of the early 1980s — Mondi Lontanissimi especially — gave Vaccina a presence on some of the most iconic Italian art-pop records.

The forty-year hiatus between the Battiato period and the Dark Companion era is the most significant gap in the Discogs record. What Vaccina was doing between 1985 and 2015 — the La Scala period under Abbado, any teaching, any unreleased recordings — is not documented in the database.

The Dark Companion period (2015–2022) represents a complete creative renewal at age sixty-plus. The core ensemble of this period — Marchini producing, Callegari engineering, Mozzoni on oboe, with rotating guest contributors — is a small, coherent working unit. The invitations extended to Keith Tippett, Julie Tippetts, Paolo Tofani, Michael Tanner, and Alison Cotton position these records within the European free-improvisation and chamber-drone world rather than the Italian progressive rock scene. The Tippetts connection in particular (A Mid Autumn’s Night Dream, 2019) is the most direct link between Vaccina’s work and the British avant-jazz lineage.
