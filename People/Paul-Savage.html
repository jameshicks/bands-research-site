Paul Savage

Scottish producer, engineer, and drummer. Discogs artist ID: 293556

Drummer and co-founder of The Delgados (1994–2005; reformed 2022), and co-founder of Chemikal Underground records. After the Delgados split in 2005, became the primary in-house producer and engineer for the Chemikal Underground roster. Recurring producer in the FatCat/Glasgow indie scene. 16 producer credits and 23 engineer credits across FatCat Records’s catalog. Associated with The Twilight Sad, Frightened Rabbit, and other Scottish acts. Based at CaVa Studios, Glasgow (per Discogs credit strings referencing “CaVa, Glasgow”).

Connections

  • The Delgados — drummer, co-founder; also production role on Peloton (1998)
  • Chemikal Underground — co-founded with Delgados; primary in-house producer post-2005
  • FatCat Records — recurring producer/engineer
  • The Twilight Sad — producer credits
  • Frightened Rabbit — engineer/production
  • Tony Doogan — fellow Glasgow producer; both worked across the Chemikal Underground axis
  • Dave Paterson — fellow Glasgow-based engineer at CaVa (Discogs ID: 409074; 18 FatCat credits)
  • Niall McMenamin — worked alongside Savage at Castle of Doom on Mogwai sessions; Savage is senior/primary credit, McMenamin as assistant; on Les Revenants (2013) both are credited as co-producers with Savage absent from the recording credit
  • RM Hubbert — recurring Savage production client; recorded and mixed Thirteen Lost & Found (2012); sole producer/recorder/mixer/masterer on Telling The Trees (2016); recorded Here Lies The Body (Aidan Moffat & RM Hubbert, 2018)
  • Jamie Savage (Discogs ID: 463245) — additional recording engineer on Hubbert’s Thirteen Lost & Found and Telling The Trees; surname relationship to Paul Savage unverified

CaVa Studios

Professional recording studio, West End of Glasgow. Current address: 30 Bentinck Street (former Kelvingrove Church of Scotland building). Founded 1974 by Brian Young, who named it after his former band “Ca Va.” Young remains owner (LinkedIn profile, as of 2026: https://uk.linkedin.com/in/brian-young-0132956).

Address history (from cavasound.com/History, via web archive):

  • 1974 – 30 May 1977: Ca Va in the home of Brian Young
  • June 1977 – June 1981: Ca Va Studio, 201 St. Vincent Street
  • June 1981 – 1985: Ca Va Sound Workshops, 30 Bentinck Street (provisional)
  • July 1985 onwards: purpose-built studio at 30 Bentinck Street; Studio 1 (July 1985), Studio 2 (1991), digital Studio 3 (1994)
  • 1992 – Christmas 1997: Ca Va East branch in Edinburgh (then closed)

Facilities (as described on cavasound.com, year unknown): Neve VR Legend mixing console in main room; smaller broadcast ISDN studio; mobile studio with SSL4000E 48-track and Pro Tools. Described as “only studio in Scotland to have PrismSound audio converters.”

Other producers and engineers based or working there:

  • Tony Doogan — started at Ca Va c. 1990 as runner, progressed to intern engineer, then producer. Per Tape Op #72 interview, he made five Belle and Sebastian albums and four Mogwai albums at Ca Va, plus Teenage Fanclub, The Delgados, Young Knives, and Mojave 3’s Out of Tune. Ca Va’s large room eventually closed after the building’s church portion was sold to property developers; Doogan subsequently co-founded Castle of Doom with Mogwai. (Tape Op #72, Tony Doogan interview)
  • Dave Paterson — Discogs ID 409074; 18 FatCat credits; noted on this page as a Glasgow-based engineer at CaVa.

Notable artists recorded at CaVa (Wikipedia/cavasound.com, not exhaustive): John Martyn, Belle and Sebastian (Tigermilk recorded there), Teenage Fanclub (Thirteen, October 1992–April 1993, per Wikipedia), Mogwai, The Fall, Travis, Snow Patrol, Paolo Nutini, Eddi Reader, David Byrne, Proclaimers.

Sources: Wikipedia — CaVa Studios (as of 2026-08-09); cavasound.com/History (direct URL, retrieved via search; page unreachable at time of research but timeline text confirmed by multiple secondary sources); Tape Op #72, Tony Doogan interview; Brian Young LinkedIn.

Conflict note — Paul Savage’s studio affiliation

The current overview line states Savage was “Based at CaVa Studios, Glasgow (per Discogs credit strings referencing ‘CaVa, Glasgow’).” However, the Tape Op #72 Paul Savage interview describes his early studio as a small facility with “a 16-track Fostex 1/2” machine, Allen & Heath 16-channel desk and no mics of any note” — clearly not CaVa, which is a large professional room with a Neve console. He and The Delgados later purchased that small studio (originally called MCM) through Chemikal Underground in 1997. The Discogs credit string “CaVa, Glasgow” may refer to specific overdub or mixing sessions booked at CaVa, rather than Savage being a resident/house engineer there. Tony Doogan is the producer confirmed by primary source to have been based at CaVa. The current overview line should be treated with caution pending liner-note confirmation. (Tape Op #72, Paul Savage interview)
