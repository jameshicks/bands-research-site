Katharine Gifford

Keyboardist, vocalist, and songwriter. Member of Stereolab (1993–1994), Moonshake (1994), leader of Snowpony (1996–2001), and member of The Wargs (2017–). Also known as Katharine Pugwash; credited in various Discogs entries as K. L. Gifford, K.L. Gifford, KL Gifford, Kath Gifford, and Katherine or Kathrine Gifford.

Discogs artist ID: 388667

Overview

Katharine Gifford came to wider attention as a keyboard player in the 1993–1994 Stereolab lineup, where she played Vox organ, Farfisa, and Moog on the band’s US breakthrough double album Transient Random-Noise Bursts With Announcements (1993) and its follow-up Mars Audiac Quintet (1994). She had been part of the London post-rock and experimental scene through her involvement with Moonshake, appearing as vocalist on their 1994 album The Sound Your Eyes Can Follow — alongside PJ Harvey among others. After leaving Stereolab she founded Snowpony, an experimental pop project active from 1996 to at least 2001, releasing two albums and several singles on Radioactive and See No Evil. A later project, The Wargs (with Kevin Bass, who also played in both Moonshake and Snowpony), released a 7” in 2017. In 2023 she contributed lyrics and vocals to a track on Hive, the album by UK experimental group Teeth of the Sea.

Career by Era

Moonshake (1994)

Moonshake were a London experimental rock/post-rock group formed around vocalist and writer David Callahan and guitarist/multi-instrumentalist Margaret Fiedler. Their discography on Too Pure ran from the debut LP First (Creation Records, 1991) through several Too Pure releases. Katharine Gifford joined for their third full-length, The Sound Your Eyes Can Follow (Too Pure, 1994), contributing vocals on tracks 2, 5, and 10. The session was notable for also featuring PJ Harvey (vocals on tracks 2, 3, 6, 8, and 10) — giving the album an unusually dense vocal texture. Recording was handled by Paul Tipler, who was simultaneously working with Stereolab. She does not appear on earlier Moonshake records (Eva Luna, 1992; Big Good Angel, 1993).

Stereolab (1993–1994)

Gifford joined Stereolab in 1993 as part of a keyboard-heavy expanded lineup that also included Sean O’Hagan (also of The High Llamas). Her first credited appearance was the Sub Pop 7” “Lo Boob Oscillator / Tempter” (1993), where she played Farfisa organ, Vox organ, Mellotron, and sang backing vocals alongside the core band of Tim Gane, Lætitia Sadier, Mary Hansen, Andy Ramsay, and bassist Duncan Brown.

She appeared on the Duophonic singles “Ping Pong” (1994) and “Wow and Flutter” (1994), both drawn from or concurrent with the recording sessions for Mars Audiac Quintet. On “Ping Pong” she played Farfisa, Vox, and Moog; on “Wow and Flutter” she is credited as Performer (alongside the full band). The ABC Music: Stereolab Radio 1 Sessions (Strange Fruit, 2002) retrospective collection credits her on keyboards for tracks 1-9 through 2-2, confirming BBC session appearances in 1993–1994. The singles box set Oscillons From The Anti-Sun (Too Pure, 2005) and anthology Serene Velocity / A Stereolab Anthology (Elektra/Rhino, 2006) also carry her credits as Performer/keyboards for the relevant 1993–1994 material.

Snowpony (1996–2001)

Gifford launched Snowpony as her main creative vehicle after leaving Stereolab. The project’s lineup shifted across releases but drew heavily from her existing network:

  • The debut single “Easy Way Down” (See No Evil, 1996) was credited solely to Snowpony with Gifford as songwriter.
  • “The Little Girls Understand” (Rough Trade, 1996) featured liner notes by writer Patrick Califia.
  • “Chocolate In The Sun” (See No Evil, 1997) followed.
  • The EP/single “John Brown” (Radioactive, 1998) was recorded and mixed by John McEntire of Tortoise, establishing a Chicago connection.
  • The LP “They Were Doing The Temptation Walk” (Radioactive, 1998) — actually released as a compilation/album — documents the Snowpony lineup as Gifford, Deb Googe (ex-My Bloody Valentine, later Primal Scream), and Max Corradi; produced by John McEntire.
  • The Slow-Motion World Of Snowpony (Radioactive, 1998) is the debut full-length, with all songs written by Gifford and recorded/mixed by John McEntire; additional mixing on tracks 1, 7, 8, 9 by Faynee, Jamie Maher, and Martin Eden.
  • Sea Shanties For Spaceships (Dead Pan Alley, 2001) is the second full-length, with Gifford joined by Deb Googe, Debbie Smith (ex-Echobelly, ex-Curve), and Kevin Bass.

The Wargs (2017)

The Wargs is a duo of Katharine Gifford and Kevin Bass (also a Snowpony and Moonshake member). Their sole Discogs entry is the 7” “The Trickster Mistress / Electric Eve” (Angry Love Productions, 2017, US), on which Gifford played vocals, guitar, Jew’s harp, and sampler. Additional credits: Jo Gate-Eastley (bass), Anna-Marie Henderson (loops/sampler); Andy Ramsay (Stereolab’s drummer) recorded the session; Deb Googe (also ex-Snowpony) produced.

Later work — Teeth of the Sea (2023)

In 2023 Gifford (credited as “Kath Gifford”) contributed lyrics and vocals to the track at position A03 on Hive (Rocket Recordings), the album by London experimental group Teeth of the Sea (members: Jimmy Martin (4), Mike Bourne (2), Sam Barton (2); associated member Mat Colegate). This represents her most recent Discogs credit.
