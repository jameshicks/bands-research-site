Ryuichi Sakamoto

Discogs ID: 5087
Born: 17 January 1952, Tokyo, Japan
Died: 28 March 2023, Tokyo, Japan
Real name: 坂本龍一 (Sakamoto Ryuuichi)
Role: Composer, pianist, synthesizer player, producer, electronic musician; film composer; co-founder of Yellow Magic Orchestra

Japanese composer, pianist, and electronic music producer; one of the most internationally recognized Japanese musicians of the 20th and 21st centuries. Active from the mid-1970s through his death in 2023, Sakamoto worked across pop, electronic, classical, experimental, ambient, and film music. He won the Academy Award for Best Original Score for The Last Emperor (1987), alongside co-winners David Byrne and Cong Su. Founder of the Japanese label Commmons (sublabel of Avex Trax, ID: 81681). Married to Akiko Yano (pianist/singer); father of filmmaker Neo S. Sora and singer Miu Sakamoto (alias Sister M).

Biography

Training and Early Career (mid-1970s)

Sakamoto studied at the Tokyo National University of Fine Arts and Music, graduating with a BA in composition and a Master’s degree with emphasis on electronic and ethnic music. He began his career in the mid-1970s as a composer, arranger, and producer working with Japanese rock, jazz, and classical artists.

His debut recording under his own name is Disappointment-Hateruma (1976, ALM Records; Discogs release ID: 4204356), an ethnographic-influenced work predating his mainstream breakthrough.

First Solo Album and Better Days (1978)

His first proper solo album, Thousand Knives Of = 千のナイフ (1978, Discogs release ID: 1065394), was released on Better Days (2) (Discogs label ID: 73854; parent: Nippon Columbia), a short-lived avant-garde imprint. Credits include Haruomi Hosono (finger cymbals, liner notes), Yukihiro Takahashi (fashion coordination), Tatsuro Yamashita (castanets on B1), Kazumi Watanabe (guitar), Hideki Matsutake (computer operation/programming), Motoya Hamaguchi (syn-drum), and Yuji Takahashi (acoustic piano). Sakamoto served as composer, producer, orchestrator, and performer. The same year he contributed to a project released on Victor.

Yellow Magic Orchestra (1978–1983, reunions later)

Sakamoto co-founded Yellow Magic Orchestra in 1978 with Haruomi Hosono (bass, keyboards, conceptual lead) and Yukihiro Takahashi (drums, vocals). YMO’s fusion of synthesizers, electronic pop, and a postmodern wit around technology and Japan’s relationship with Western culture became massively influential globally. Key albums: Yellow Magic Orchestra (1978/1979), Solid State Survivor (1979), BGM (1981), Technodelic (1981). Sakamoto handled keyboards, synthesizer, and arrangements. He also had 62 shared releases with Hosono and 68 with Takahashi documented in Discogs.

The group’s technological programmer, Hideki Matsutake (Logic System), was a key technical collaborator throughout this period (67 shared releases with Sakamoto).

YMO disbanded in 1983, reunited for Technodon (1993), and had further reunion activity as Human Audio Sponge / HASYMO from 2007, with releases on Commmons.

Sakamoto also recorded with the Pre YMO formation (Discogs ID: 67584) and with The Kakutougi Session (ID: 974750) and By-By Session Band (ID: 4743254), documenting his mid-1970s studio work.

Film Scoring: International Breakthrough (1983–1993)

Merry Christmas, Mr. Lawrence (1983)

Scored Nagisa Oshima’s WWII prisoner drama, in which Sakamoto also appeared as an actor opposite David Bowie. The soundtrack was released on Virgin Records and became one of his best-known compositions. The main theme has been recorded and reissued extensively since.

The Last Emperor (1987) — Academy Award

The score for Bernardo Bertolucci’s epic (Discogs release ID: 1214751), co-composed with David Byrne and Cong Su, won the 1988 Academy Award for Best Original Score. Released on Virgin Records. Sakamoto produced and scored Side A; Byrne produced Side B. Engineers: Haydn Bendall, Shinichi Tanaka, Mike Jarratt, Shigeru Takise, Clive Martin. Fairlight programming by Hans Zimmer and Hiroaki Sugawara. Orchestral conductor: Gavyn Wright. Mixed by Steve Nye. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Hans Zimmer served as musical associate and compiled the album. This cemented Sakamoto’s international profile as a major film composer.

Neo Geo and the New York Period (1987–1990s)

Neo Geo (1987, CBS/Terrapin; Discogs release ID: 54697) was a landmark album produced by Sakamoto and Bill Laswell. Credits include Iggy Pop (vocals), Bootsy Collins (bass), Tony Williams (drums), Sly Dunbar (drums), David Van Tieghem (percussion), Lucia Hwong (pipa), Emmett Chapman (Chapman Stick), Eddie Martinez (12-string guitar), Yukio Tsuji (shakuhachi/gayageum), and Haruo Kubota (guitar). Fairlight CMI programming by Hans Zimmer, Hiroaki Sugawara, and Masa Sekijima. Engineers: Jason Corsaro, Robert Musso, and others. The album embodied Sakamoto’s “world music” period fusing electronics with diverse global inputs.

Beauty (1989, Virgin/Virgin Japan; Discogs release ID: 812712) continued this direction. Self-produced; engineered and mixed with Jason Corsaro and Naoto Shibuya; mastered by Greg Calbi. Featured Adrian Belew (guitar), David Sylvian (vocals/lyrics), Iggy Pop, and others. Synthesizer/drum programming by Jeff Bova and Bill Seely.

The Ryuichi Sakamoto & David Sylvian duo project (Discogs ID: 22799) was a sustained relationship — Sylvian appeared on Beauty and collaborated across 26 documented shared releases. Sylvian also contributed to Heartbeat (1991). Andy Partridge (XTC) had 18 shared releases with Sakamoto.

Akiko Yano Collaboration

Sakamoto collaborated extensively with his then-wife Akiko Yano across 25 shared releases. He appeared as arranger and musician on several of her albums. UTAU (2010, Commmons) — “Singing Together” — was a duo concert album released after their separation.

Orchestral / Classical Work (1988–2000s)

Playing The Orchestra (1988, Virgin) was an early orchestral compilation/performance project. The 1990s saw Sakamoto move toward more stripped-down and acoustic-leaning work:

  • BTTB (Back To The Basics) (1998, Sony Classical/WEA Japan) — solo piano album, among his most intimate recordings; issued internationally by Sony Classical
  • ウラ BTTB (1999, WEA Japan) — companion album

1996 (1996, For Life Records/Güt) was a solo piano tour document of concerts performed following his battle with throat cancer. Discord (1997, For Life Records/Güt; 1998 Sony Classical) explored orchestral-electronic synthesis.

The Güt Years (1993–1999)

His Japanese label Güt (under For Life Records) was the primary imprint for his mid-1990s solo work: Sweet Revenge (1994), Smoochy (1995/1996), Snooty (1996), 1996 (1996), Discord (1997), The Other Side Of Love (1997). The Güt catalog was compiled in The Very Best Of Güt Years 1994–1997 (1998) and Complete Index Of Güt (1999).

Morelenbaum² / Sakamoto (2001–2002)

The group Morelenbaum2 - Sakamoto (Discogs ID: 336424) — with Brazilian cellist Jaques Morelenbaum (27 shared releases) and vocalist Paula Morelenbaum (8 shared releases) — released Casa (2001, Sony Classical) and Ao Vivo (2002). The project drew on Bossa Nova and tropical music, partly as a tribute to Antonio Carlos Jobim. Casa was recorded in Jobim’s former house in Rio de Janeiro.

Commmons Label and Later Solo Work (2007–2023)

Sakamoto founded Commmons (ID: 81681) as a sublabel of Avex Trax around 2006–2007. It became the primary Japanese release home for his later solo work, HASYMO reunion material, and the Fennesz + Sakamoto collaborations.

Key solo albums on Commmons and other labels:

TitleYearLabelNotes
Out Of Noise2009Commmons / DeccaAmbient/electronic; released internationally on Decca

122023Commmons / MilanFinal album; twelve pieces composed as a diary during his illness

Async (2017) was conceived for an unrealized Andrei Tarkovsky film installation. Async - Remodels was released through multiple pressings in 2017–2018 and gathered remixers from across the electronic spectrum.

12 (2023) was released on 17 January 2023 — his 71st birthday — and was his final studio album. He died 28 March 2023 from rectal cancer.

Other notable late-career film scores:

  • Silk (2007, dir. François Girard, Commmons) — co-composed with Jaques Morelenbaum
  • Tony Takitani (2005/2007, Commmons) — scored the Jun Ichikawa adaptation of Haruki Murakami’s short story
  • The Revenant (2015/2016, Milan) — with Alva Noto and Bryce Dessner
  • Black Mirror: Smithereens (2019, Milan) — episode of Charlie Brooker’s anthology
  • Minamata (2021, Milan) — dir. Andrew Levitas; documentary-drama about W. Eugene Smith in Japan
  • Monster (Kaibutsu) (2023, Commmons) — dir. Hirokazu Kore-eda; scored in his final months

Other Collaborative Projects

Christopher Willits (Discogs artist 56536; 11 shared releases): Ocean Fire (2007, 12k/Commmons) — ambient guitar-electronics duo album.

Taylor Deupree (18 shared releases; 12k label head): Disappearance (2013, 12k/Commmons) — glacial ambient collaboration.

David Van Tieghem (48 shared releases): Appears across the Neo Geo/Beauty era as percussionist.

Seigen Ono (158 shared releases): Long-running technical/engineering collaborator.

Steve Nye (72 shared releases): Engineer and mixer throughout the YMO and early solo period; also worked with David Sylvian / Japan.

Arto Lindsay (25 shared releases): Multiple collaborations in the Brazil-NYC crossover circuit. Sakamoto contributed to O Corpo Sutil (1995, For Life Records/Güt) and other Lindsay projects.

Sketch Show / HASYMO: Sketch Show was formed by Hosono and Takahashi in 2002. Sakamoto joined them as Human Audio Sponge (Discogs ID: 581920) and later HASYMO (Discogs ID: 1149955) for reunion activities 2007–2014, releasing material on Commmons including Rescue / Rydeen 79/07, HC (2008), and concert albums.

Simon Jeffes (8 shared releases): Penguin Café Orchestra connections.

Nicky Skopelitis (6 shared releases): Bill Laswell circle, appearing in the Neo Geo sessions.

DJ Spooky (10 shared releases): Overlapping in the New York experimental/electronic scene.

Open Questions

  • Beauty (1989) credits include a large international cast; the specific role of Brian Wilson and exact session circumstances are not determinable from Discogs alone.
  • The nature of the Güt label (For Life Records sublabel) and Sakamoto’s ownership/creative involvement: Discogs marks it as a sublabel but does not clarify the founding story.
  • Love Is The Devil (1998, Asphodel) — scored John Maybury’s Francis Bacon biopic; Discogs has minimal credits on this release.
  • The Async (2017) project was conceived in response to Tarkovsky’s work; the specific installation context is referenced in press but not in Discogs.
