Caroline Loeb

Born: 5 October 1955, Neuilly-sur-Seine, France Discogs ID: 88517 Name variations: C. Loeb, Catherine Loeb, Leob Active: 1973–present (acting); 1983–present (music) Labels: ZE Records / Island (1983), Barclay (1986–87), Flarenasch (1995) Role: Singer, actress, writer, director

Background

Born into a family with deep art world roots: her grandfather Pierre Loeb (24 September 1897 – 4 May 1964) was a prominent Parisian art dealer who founded the Galerie Pierre in 1924 and exhibited Picasso, Miró, Max Ernst, Paul Klee, Giacometti, and others — including staging the first collective Surrealist exhibition in 1925; her father Albert Loeb also ran an art gallery; her mother Cécile Odartchenko was a writer and editor. “Loeb” is her real family surname, not a stage name. [fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Loeb, as of 2026-05-24; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Loeb] Loeb spent ages 4–10 in New York City, living in the Eldorado Towers on Central Park West — an early NYC immersion that later informed her recording relationship with ZE Records.

She studied theatre at the Cours Florent under Francis Huster, alongside classmates including Sabine Haudepin and Jean-Pierre Darroussin.

Overview

Singer, actress, writer, and director. Her best-known record is “C’est La Ouate” (1986), which charted #5 in France, #1 in Italy, #3 in Spain, #10 in Germany, and #30 in Austria. Her early recording career (1983) ran through ZE Records in New York — the downtown NYC label home to Lizzy Mercier Descloux and the No Wave–adjacent French-American scene around Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban. The Barclay years (1986–87) saw her work almost exclusively with producer Philippe Chany; the “C’est La Ouate” chorus assembled a circle of Paris collaborators including singer Amina Annabi and comedian-actor Dominique Farrugia.

Era 1 — ZE Records / New York (1983)

First single: “A Malibu” (1983, Island/ZE Records, catno 6863 231) — a 12” promo, produced by Ron Rogers; co-written by Loeb and Jean-Marie Salaun. The B-side is an instrumental version.

First album: Piranana (1983, Island/RCA/ZE Records, catno 208231) — a full LP recorded in New York. Ron Rogers produced and played nearly everything (Prophet 5 synth, Linn Drum, synth bass). Michael Zilkha executive-produced. Loeb wrote all lyrics; Ron Rogers most of the music.

The ZE Records stable at this point included Lizzy Mercier Descloux, Aural Exciters, and the broader No Wave/dance-punk scene orbiting downtown Manhattan. The presence of Ronnie Bird (French singer, b. Ronald Méhu) and Mina on backing vocals places the sessions in an international pop context rather than strictly post-punk.

Era 2 — “C’est La Ouate” / Barclay (1986)

“C’est La Ouate” (12”, Barclay 885 341-1, 1986) — the career-defining single. Written by Loeb and Pierre Grillet. Produced, arranged, all instruments, and mixed by Philippe Chany. Guitar: Philippe Spiessert. Engineer: Bernard Moulinier.

The track’s distinctive chorus was assembled from Paris friends/associates: Loeb, Amina Annabi (credited as Amina Annabi), Philippe Chany, and Dominique Farrugia.

The 12” runs 6:02 (Version Longue) / 3:48 (Version 45 T) / 3:48 “Paresseuse Dub”.

The single was widely licensed internationally: Italian release on PolyGram (1986), Spanish release on Prismatic Records (1987), Italian cover on Limited Edition Records, covered by multiple artists (see below).

Era 3 — Loeb C.D. album (1987)

Loeb C.D. (LP, Barclay 833 865-1, 1987) — her major album release. Produced, arranged, and all instruments by Philippe Chany; mixed by both Chany and Phil Delire. Tracks recorded by Phil Delire and Stéphane Cauvin respectively. Released in Germany (and other territories) on Barclay via PolyGram.

Genres: Electronic, Pop; Styles: Disco, Downtempo, Synth-pop.

The same Barclay period produced four singles: “A Quoi Tu Penses?” (Barclay 885 836-1, 1987), “Amants Z Aimants” (1987), “Le Telefon” (1987), “Mots Croisés” (1987) — all produced by Philippe Chany with the same core team.

A further Italy release: “Heartache / C’est La Ouate” (PolyGram, 1986) — pairing her single with Pepsi & Shirlie’s “Heartache” on the B-side.

Era 4 — Later work (1989–present)

“C’est La Ouate” continued generating writing credits throughout the late 1980s and 1990s:

  • “Merge” (1989, A&M Records, US) — Loeb listed as written-by
  • “C’Est L’Kawa’T” (1990, Bird Production, France) — written-by credit

“J’ Te Hais Dans La Peau” (12” maxi, Flarenasch 470256, 1995) — a dance-oriented comeback single. Written by Loeb and Micky Milan; arranged by Philippe Guez; mixed by DJ Schmites, DJ Micky (Micky Milan), DJ Yann Philips. The shared writing credit with Lizzy Mercier Descloux on this Discogs master is a discrepancy — the release credits only show Loeb + Milan.

Guest vocals on John Greaves’ Songs (CD, Resurgence RES112CD, 1995) — tracks “Eccentric Waters” (6) and “L’Aise Aux Ex-Sans-Trique” (9). The album also features Robert Wyatt (vocals, tracks 2/5/11), Elton Dean (saxello), Sophia Domancich (piano), Mireille Bauer (vibraphone), David Cunningham (electric guitar).

“Paris Sens Interdits” (2015, Parlophone) — written-by credit on a release; indicates continued songwriting activity.

Best Of compilation (2004, Choice Of Music); reissue on Junk compilation (2024, Wewantsounds).

Connections

  • ZE Records — first two releases; label home of Lizzy Mercier Descloux, Aural Exciters; co-founded by Michael Zilkha
  • Philippe Chany — her primary producer and instrumentalist for all Barclay releases; b. 1959, Gap; also member of Count 3, Love International
  • Amina Annabi — recurring vocalist; later represented France at Eurovision 1991
  • Dominique Farrugia — recurring backing vocalist; French comedian and director; member of Les Nuls
  • Lizzy Mercier Descloux — French singer on ZE Records; shared Discogs master; b. 16 Dec 1956, d. 20 Apr 2004
  • Ron Rogers — NYC producer of the Piranana LP
  • Michael Zilkha — ZE Records co-founder; exec prod on Piranana

Acting and Theatre

Acting career predates her music: first screen credit in Jean Eustache’s La Maman et la Putain (1973). Film appearances include Lady Oscar (1979), Quartet (1981, James Ivory), The Favour, the Watch and the Very Big Fish (1991, Ben Lewin), and works by Jacques Demy, Adolfo Arrieta, René Féret, and others.

Theatre direction: directed and adapted Shirley (a portrait of painter Shirley Goldfarb) — actress Judith Magre won a Molière Award for the production. In 2016 created and starred in Françoise par Sagan (on writer Françoise Sagan), directed by Alex Lutz, premiering at the Festival d’Avignon. Co-wrote and starred in George Sand et moi! with Tom Dingler, also directed by Alex Lutz, at the Théâtre du Gymnase and Avignon.

Writing

Published books 1992–2015 with Le Seuil and Flammarion, spanning literary fiction, humor, and biography. Novel Has Been (Flammarion, 2006).
