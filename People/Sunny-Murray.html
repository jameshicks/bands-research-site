Sunny Murray

Sunny Murray

Aug 24, 202611 min read

Sunny Murray

Discogs ID: 253093 Real name: James Marcellus Arthur Murray Jr. Born: September 21, 1936, Idabel, Oklahoma, USA Died: December 8, 2017, Paris, France

American free jazz drummer. One of the first drummers to decisively abandon fixed-pulse timekeeping, dissolving the beat into continuous textural sound and opening the rhythmic language of the music into something approaching pure atmosphere. His work with Cecil Taylor and Albert Ayler in the early 1960s was foundational to the entire free jazz movement; his BYG/Actuel sessions of 1969–71 in Paris represent the fullest expression of his own voice as a leader.

Biography

Early Life and New York Arrival (1936–1959)

Born in Idabel, Oklahoma, Murray moved to New York City in the mid-1950s. His early recorded name variations — “Jimmy Murray,” “Arthur Murray,” “Arthur ‘Sonny’ Murray” — reflect some instability in how he was billed before establishing himself. He worked around the New York scene as a sideman before connecting with the circle around Cecil Taylor, which changed his approach entirely.

The Cecil Taylor Years (1960–1963)

Murray was working with Cecil Taylor by the early 1960s. His earliest documented recording appearance is on Into The Hot (1962, Impulse!), a Gil Evans-fronted record on which Taylor led one half — Murray plays drums on Taylor’s tracks alongside alto saxophonist Jimmy Lyons (2) and bass player Henry Grimes, billed as “Jimmy Murray.” This is the unit that would define the next several years of his career.

In 1963 the trio traveled to Europe, recording Live At The Cafe Montmartre for Debut Records in Copenhagen — a foundational document of the Taylor trio in performance, with Murray credited as “Arthur Murray.” The Candid sessions from 1960–61, collected on the 1989 Mosaic box The Complete Candid Recordings Of Cecil Taylor And Buell Neidlinger, include Murray on a small number of tracks (tracks A2 and B1) alongside the more frequently used Denis Charles. But it was in live performance with the Taylor trio that Murray found the approach he would carry through the rest of his life: treating the drum kit as a continuous tonal and textural environment rather than a pulse-keeping mechanism, weaving around the piano’s attack and creating density and space rather than meter.

Albert Ayler and the New York Free Scene (1963–1966)

Murray’s relationship with Albert Ayler produced some of the most celebrated recordings in free jazz. He appears on the Albert Ayler releases for Debut Records beginning in 1964: Spirits (1964) — with Ayler on tenor, Henry Grimes and Earle Henderson on basses, and Norman Howard on trumpet — and the same year’s material that would eventually circulate as Nefertiti, The Beautiful One Has Come (Fontana, 1966).

The essential collaboration with Ayler is Spiritual Unity (1965, ESP-Disk’). Recorded with Gary Peacock on bass, it is one of the most concentrated free jazz recordings ever made: Ayler’s keening, hymn-derived tenor lines against Peacock’s oscillating bass and Murray’s wholly liberated percussion. Murray plays here with an almost orchestral sense of weight and motion — cymbal washes, tom strikes, rim-work — that has nothing to do with ride-cymbal timekeeping. It remains the benchmark of his early career.

Also in 1964 he appears on the Savoy LP Bill Dixon 7-Tette / Archie Shepp And The New York Contemporary 5 — playing the Shepp/NYCF side with Don Cherry, John Tchicai, Ronnie Boykins, and Ted Curson. The New York Contemporary Five was a key formation of the moment; it is one of the bands where Murray’s name appears in the Discogs group credits.

New York Eye And Ear Control (1966, ESP-Disk’) is co-led by Ayler, Cherry, Tchicai, Roswell Rudd, Gary Peacock, and Murray — the soundtrack to Michael Snow’s film, another major document.

Murray also cut his first album as sole leader in this period: Sunny Murray (1966, ESP-Disk’), featuring Byard Lancaster and Jack Graham on alto saxes, Alan Silva on bass, and Jacques Coursil on trumpet. It is a stark, uncompromising record and a clear statement of intent as a bandleader.

Also recorded in 1965 but released on the Jihad label (founded by Amiri Baraka / LeRoi Jones): Sonny’s Time Now — with Albert Ayler and Don Cherry among others. The title is a political and aesthetic declaration.

Paris and BYG/Actuel (1969–1971)

Murray was living in Paris by 1969, part of the large wave of American free musicians who relocated to Europe. He was a central figure in the BYG/Actuel series — the historic batch of free jazz sessions recorded in Paris in the summer of 1969, produced by Jean Georgakarakos and Jean-Luc Young, and supervised by Claude Delcloo.

His own BYG leaders:

  • Big Chief (1969, Pathé) — recorded just before or alongside the BYG sessions; with François Tusques on piano, Kenneth Terroade on tenor, Bernard Vitet on trumpet, Beb Guerin on bass, Alan Silva on violin, Hart Leroy Bibbs on voice/poetry
  • An Even Break (Never Give A Sucker) (1970, BYG) — his most politically charged record; he reads poetry himself; with Byard Lancaster on reeds, Malachi Favors on bass, Kenneth Terroade on tenor
  • Hommage To Africa (1970, BYG) — a large-ensemble session drawing together the widest cast of the Paris free scene: Roscoe Mitchell, Alan Silva, Clifford Thornton, Archie Shepp, Lester Bowie, Jeanne Lee, Malachi Favors, Dave Burrell, Grachan Moncur III, Arthur Jones
  • Sunshine (1971, BYG) — lean trio session, Malachi Favors/bass, Kenneth Terroade/tenor

He also plays on Dave Burrell’s BYG album Echo (1969), with Archie Shepp, Alan Silva, Clifford Thornton, Arthur Jones, and Grachan Moncur III.

Shandar (1971)

Murray’s Shandar self-titled LP (1971) is the other major document from this Paris period. The band draws on the French avant-garde scene: François Tusques on piano, Michel Portal on bass clarinet and tárogató, Beb Guerin on bass, Kenneth Terroade on tenor, Ambrose Jackson on trumpet, Bernard Vitet on trumpet, and Hart Leroy Bibbs reading a poem on the B-side. He also plays on François Tusques’s Intercommunal Music (1971, Shandar) — a collective improvisation session with Alan Silva, Alan Shorter, and Steve Potts.

Later Career (1972–2017)

Murray continued working across Europe and the US with consistent activity through the 1970s and 80s:

  • Swing Low Sweet Spiritual (1971, Osmosis Records) — with a Dutch/international free ensemble
  • Journey To The Song Within (1974, Birth Records, Germany) — quartet session
  • Apple Cores (1978, Philly Jazz) — with liner notes by Murray
  • Live At Moers Festival (1979, Moers Music)
  • The Hilversum Session (1980, Osmosis) — further documentation of the European free circuit
  • It Is In The Brewing Luminous (1981, Hat Hut) — with Hat Hut, the Swiss label that became a key home for creative improvisation
  • Smoke (1990, FMP) — the German Free Music Production label
  • A Sanctuary Within (1992, Black Saint, Italy) — Italian free jazz circuit
  • 13# Steps On Glass (1995, Enja) and Clapping Music (1995, Enja) — both on the German Enja label
  • Albert Smiles With Sunny (1996, InRespect) — his explicit tribute to Ayler
  • We Are Not At The Opera (1998, Eremite Records)
  • Ode To Albert Ayler (2009, Ayler Records, France)
  • Intercommunal Dialogue 1&2 (2019, Ni-Vu-Ni-Connu, Luxembourg) — co-led with François Tusques, recorded before his death in 2017 and released posthumously

He died in Paris on December 8, 2017.
