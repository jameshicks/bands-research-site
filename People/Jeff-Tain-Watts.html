Jeff “Tain” Watts

Jazz drummer. Born Jeffrey Watts, 20 January 1960, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, USA. Discogs artist ID: 253394. Also credited as Jeff Watts, Jeff ‘Tain’ Watts, Tain, Jeffrey Watts, J. Watts.

Website: chambersoftain.com

Biography

Pittsburgh and Berklee

Jeff “Tain” Watts came up in Pittsburgh — a city with a notably deep jazz tradition (Art Blakey, Ahmad Jamal, Roy Eldridge, Billy Strayhorn all have Pittsburgh roots). He attended Berklee College of Music in Boston, where he developed the hard-bop vocabulary and technical firepower that would make him one of the most coveted drummers of the 1980s neoclassical jazz revival.

Early Career: Wynton Marsalis (1982–1985)

Watts’s recording debut came on Wynton Marsalis’s self-titled Columbia debut in 1982, sharing the drum chair with Tony Williams across sessions. He was part of a core unit that also included Branford Marsalis (saxophone) and Kenny Kirkland (piano) — a rhythm section that would reconstitute itself under different bandleaders for the better part of a decade.

He remained in the Wynton orbit through 1985, appearing on Think Of One (1983), Hot House Flowers (1984), Black Codes (From The Underground) (1985), J Mood (1986), and Marsalis Standard Time Vol. 1 (1987). His work on Black Codes in particular is widely cited as among the most intense drumming on a mainstream jazz album of that decade.

Branford Marsalis Quartet / Trio (1986–2000s)

When Branford Marsalis launched his own Columbia career, Watts followed — forming the core rhythm section of the Branford Marsalis Quartet and Branford Marsalis Trio alongside Kenny Kirkland (piano) and Robert Hurst or Reginald Veal (bass). This association proved to be the defining relationship of Watts’s career.

The Quartet toured relentlessly and recorded a substantial Columbia catalog through the 1990s: Trio Jeepy (1989), Crazy People Music (1990), The Beautyful Ones Are Not Yet Born (1991), Bloomington (1993), The Dark Keys (1996), Requiem (1999). Watts also appeared on the Do The Right Thing soundtrack (1989) and the Mo’ Better Blues soundtrack (1990) — Spike Lee soundtracks that inserted the Marsalis aesthetic into mainstream film culture.

His work with Branford continued into the 2000s through Marsalis Music: Footsteps Of Our Fathers (2002), Romare Bearden Revealed (2003), Eternal (2004), Performs Coltrane’s Love Supreme Live In Amsterdam (2004), Braggtown (2006), Metamorphosen (2009).

As Leader

Watts made his debut as a primary recording artist with Megawatts (1991, Sunnyside), a trio date with Kenny Kirkland on piano and Charles Fambrough on bass — a stripped-down format that showcased his compositional voice and polyrhythmic concept.

His second leader date, Double Gemini (1997, Challenge Records), was a trio co-led with guitarist Paul Bollenback and organist Joey DeFrancesco.

Citizen Tain (1999, Columbia) is his landmark recording as a leader — a quintet date featuring Branford Marsalis (tenor saxophone), Kenny Garrett (alto saxophone), Reginald Veal (bass), Kenny Kirkland (piano), and Wynton Marsalis (trumpet). Notably, it brought both Marsalis brothers together on one session under Watts’s name. Kenny Kirkland died in November 1998; Citizen Tain stands as one of the last documents of his playing.

Bar Talk (2001, Columbia) was a hip-hop/jazz fusion record that Watts produced as well as played on, featuring Kenny Kirkland compositions among its material.

Watts (2009, Dark Key Music) gathered a formidable quintet: Christian McBride (bass), Branford Marsalis (saxophone), Terence Blanchard (trumpet), and Lawrence Fields (piano). Self-released on his own Dark Key Music imprint.

Family (2011, Dark Key Music) continued his leader arc with David Kikoski (piano), James Genus (bass), and Steve Wilson (2) (alto saxophone).

My Witch’s Blue (2012, Verve) was a co-leader date with Japanese pianist Makoto Ozone and Christian McBride — a trio recording dedicated to Ellington/Strayhorn material plus Ozone originals.

WAW! (2024, Hobby Horse Records) is a Danish trio co-lead with Carl Winther (piano) and Richard Andersson (6) (bass).

Other Key Sideman Work

Beyond Marsalis, Watts’s Discogs credits span a wide range of post-bop leaders:

  • Geri Allen — The Nurturer (1990, Somethin’ Else/Blue Note), with Kenny Garrett and Robert Hurst
  • Kenny Kirkland — Kenny Kirkland (1991, GRP), Kirkland’s only leader recording
  • Ellis Marsalis Trio — Ellis Marsalis Trio (1991, Somethin’ Else); member of the Ellis Marsalis Trio unit
  • Terence Blanchard — Terence Blanchard (1991, Columbia); Pontius Pilate’s Decision (1992)
  • Joey Calderazzo — Joey Calderazzo (2000, Columbia)
  • Jim Snidero — Mixed Bag (1988, Criss Cross); member of the Jim Snidero Quintet
  • Sadao Watanabe — Parker’s Mood (1985)
  • David Kikoski — recurring trio context; member of David Kikoski Quintet and David Kikoski Trio
  • When Harry Met Sally… soundtrack (1989, Columbia) — featured in Harry Connick Jr. big-band numbers

Wynton Marsalis Quintet

Watts was a member of The Wynton Marsalis Quintet (Discogs ID: 1963836). In addition to the early Columbia work, he returned to Wynton’s fold at various points — notably appearing on Citizen Tain as a guest alongside the full Branford ensemble.

Tain & The Ebonix

Watts led a funk/soul-inflected unit called Tain & The Ebonix (Discogs ID: 4248194) — representing a harder groove-oriented side of his playing outside the standard jazz trio format.

Mingus Big Band / Mingus Orchestra

Watts is a listed member of both the Mingus Big Band (ID: 1415849) and Mingus Orchestra (ID: 1565216) — the ongoing institutional ensembles that maintain Charles Mingus’s legacy.
