Billy Davenport

American blues drummer. Born April 23, 1931, Chicago, Illinois; died December 24, 1999, Chicago, Illinois. Discogs artist ID: 774576. Name variations: B. Davenport, Bill Davenport.

Overview

Billy Davenport was a Chicago-based drummer who worked across the city’s blues circuit for several decades, appearing on sessions with Muddy Waters, James Cotton, Junior Wells, Howlin’ Wolf, Jimmy Dawkins, Willie Dixon, and others. His most documented role is as drummer for The Paul Butterfield Blues Band, which he joined in 1965 replacing Sam Lay. He played on East-West (Elektra, 1966) — the band’s second album and their most celebrated record — and remained in the lineup through approximately 1967. Beyond the Butterfield band, Discogs credits him on sessions with blues artists Fenton Robinson (1966), Buster Benton (1978), and Mack Simmons (late 1970s/80s), confirming an ongoing Chicago blues session presence across multiple decades. He is also credited with Photography on the 1996 archival release East-West Live.

The East-West Session (1966)

The album’s centerpiece is the 13-minute title track, a free-ranging improvisation drawing on Indian raga structures. Davenport’s drumming underpins both the tighter blues tracks and the extended modal exploration of the title cut.

Chicago Blues Circuit

Beyond the Butterfield band, Davenport’s session credits document a sustained presence in the Chicago blues world across multiple decades:

  • Fenton Robinson (1966): The 1966 USA Records single is among Robinson’s earliest releases. Robinson was a sophisticated blues guitarist known for jazz-influenced phrasing who later became a cornerstone of the Alligator Records roster.
  • Buster Benton (1978): Benton’s Spider In My Stew (P-Vine, Japan) was a Japan-market release; Davenport plays on Willie Dixon–produced tracks, with Dixon also on bass. The session also features Lafayette Leake (piano) and Carey Bell (harmonica).
  • Mack Simmons (1971–1982): The PM/Simmons Collection documents Little Mack Simmons’s self-produced Chicago sessions over a decade; Davenport appears alongside Lonnie Brooks and Detroit Junior.
  • James Cotton, Junior Wells, Muddy Waters, Howlin’ Wolf, Jimmy Dawkins, Willie Dixon: Credited in Davenport’s Discogs profile as bandleaders he worked with; specific sessions for these artists are not yet individually catalogued in Discogs under his ID.
