Nina Canal

Guitarist, drummer, vocalist. NYC No Wave scene from 1977. Co-founder of Ut. Discogs artist ID: 531898

Name variations on record: Canal, N, N. Canal, N. Young, Nina.

Overview

Canal was one of the central figures in the first wave of the downtown Manhattan experimental scene. She came up through Rhys Chatham’s compositional ensembles (1977–78) and simultaneously moved through the No Wave orbit, performing in Dark Day before co-founding Ut in December 1978 — the band she would remain with through its 1991 hiatus and 2010 return. Her trajectory bridges the minimalist-composer strand (Chatham, Glenn Branca) and the No Wave band strand more directly than most of her contemporaries.

Biography

Rhys Chatham’s Ensembles (1977–1978)

Canal entered the scene in 1977 as a performer in Rhys Chatham’s Tone Death ensemble. The piece — for tenor saxophone, two electric guitars, and drums, approximately 35 minutes — was first performed at Franklin Furnace (1977). Canal played guitar alongside Chatham; Peter Gordon played saxophone and Robert Appleton played drums. The ensemble performed again at the Artists Space five-night festival in May 1978, the same series that documented what became No New York. Chatham’s groups were not included on that Eno-produced compilation.

She also performed in Chatham’s Guitar Trio ensemble. Guitar Trio (1977) — for three electric guitars, electric bass, and drums — applied La Monte Young drone logic to rock instrumentation. Its first performance was at Max’s Kansas City, with Glenn Branca, Chatham, David Rosenblum, and Wharton Tiers rounding out the group. Canal was among the guitarists in this ensemble.

Dark Day (1978–)

Immediately after the Artists Space festival, Canal became a member of Dark Day, Robin Crutchfield’s project formed upon leaving DNA. The lineup on the first Dark Day record — Hands In The Dark / Invisible Man (7”, Strike It From The Records, 1979) — was:

InstrumentPerformer
Piano, VoiceRobin Crutchfield
GuitarNina Canal
DrumsNancy Arlen

Produced by Charles Ball. The broader Dark Day ensemble at various points also included David Rosenbloom (bass), Phil Kline (guitar, keyboards), and Barry Friar (drums). Canal’s presence in Dark Day put her in direct orbit with Arlen, who had previously drummed in Mars — one of the clearest links between the Mars strand of No Wave and the DNA/Dark Day strand.

Ut (December 1978–1992, 2010–)

Canal co-founded Ut in December 1978 with Jacqui Ham and Sally Young. The band’s founding premise was explicitly anti-hierarchical: all three members rotated instruments (guitar, bass, drums) and the role of vocalist and director across the set, song by song. Songs were built through collective improvisation. This made the conventional notion of a “lead” member inoperative — a deliberate structural position that also made Ut internationally distinctive.

An early member, Karen Achenbach (Discogs: 1176021), played before the stable Canal/Ham/Young trio was established; she appears credited as “Former Member” on the Early Live Life retrospective.

The band went on hiatus in 1991, returning for live dates from 2010 onward.

Rhys Chatham Large-Ensemble Work (2006)

Canal returned to Chatham’s orbit for the recording released as Die Donnergötter (Radium (3), 2006, TOE-CD-801). She plays electric guitar on tracks 3 and 4 — a recording of “Drastic Classicism” and a 2006 rendering of “Guitar Trio” — alongside Chatham, Peter Gordon (mixing), Wharton Tiers (mixing), David Linton (drums), Ned Sublette (guitar), and others. The album also includes “Die Donnergötter” (the large-scale title piece, conducted by Chatham with a brass/guitar ensemble) and “Massacre On Macdougal Street.”
