Eric Random

Real name: Eric Ramsden Born: 1961, Manchester, England Roles: Guitar, bass, synthesizer, percussion, tabla, electronics, voice, drum programming Discogs artist ID: 194976 Discogs band entity (The Bedlamites): 36945

Post-punk, industrial, and electronic musician from Manchester; one of the principal figures in the orbit of New Hormones (Richard Boon’s Buzzcocks-adjacent imprint) in the early 1980s. Close working relationship with Cabaret Voltaire — particularly Stephen Mallinder and Richard H. Kirk — whose production and musicianship runs through his core catalog. Played tabla on Cabaret Voltaire’s Micro-Phonies (Virgin, 1984), contributed guitar to the 2X45 live document (Rough Trade, 1982), and appeared on the Sensoria and James Brown singles. Also contributed to Nico (3)‘s Behind The Iron Curtain (Dojo, 1986).

Aliases in Discogs: Eric Paul (7), Eric Ramsden. Name variations: E. Random, Eric Random Diagram, Random.

Pre-Tiller Boys — The Panik (1977–1978)

Before The Tiller Boys, Ramsden played guitar in The Panik, a punk band formed in Prestwich, Manchester in June 1977. The lineup was Ian Nance (guitar/vocals), Eric Random (guitar), Paul Hilton (bass), and Steve Brotherdale (drums). The Panik played approximately fifteen shows — sharing stages with the Damned, the Vibrators, and Warsaw/Joy Division — and released one single, It Won’t Sell (Rainy City Records), with tracks “Modern Politics”, “Urban Damnation”, and “Murder”. The band folded in May 1978. (Sources: LTM biography; Punk 77)

After The Panik, Random joined Buzzcocks’ road crew, which brought him into the New Hormones orbit and led to the Tiller Boys co-founding.

Early Career — The Tiller Boys (1978–1980)

Before releasing under his own name, Ramsden was a founding member of The Tiller Boys — a Manchester trio that also included Peter McNeish (Pete Shelley of Buzzcocks) and Francis Cookson (ID 1735867). The group made their live debut on 9 June 1978 supporting Joy Division at Manchester’s Factory Club — the gig documented on Peter Saville’s iconic Fac 1 poster. They had a Krautrock/Fripp and Eno orientation and were a regular presence on Mancunian post-punk bills in 1978–79. Random also ran the Free Agents alongside Cookson and a rotating pool of musicians during this period.

The Tiller Boys recorded a single at Arrow Studio in January 1979 — three tracks described as “pounding percussion and Neu!-like guitar clangour” — intended for Factory Records but ultimately not issued. The group’s final performance was 27 October 1979 at the YMCA, London (with Cabaret Voltaire and The Passage), with Barry Adamson of Magazine guesting on bass. When the group disbanded their recordings were eventually released by New Hormones as Big Noise From The Jungle (ORG 3, 1980). Random is credited on guitar (uncredited at the time); sleeve by Malcolm Garrett. (Sources: LTM biography; Electricity Club interview, May 2021)

Also in 1980: uncredited drum machine on The Durutti Column’s Lips That Would Kiss (Factory Benelux FAC BN2-005), produced by Martin Hannett with Vini Reilly.

Jell — Some Bizzare Album (1981)

Alongside solo activity, Random contributed to the Some Bizzare Album compilation (1981) as a member of Jell, performing “I Dare Say It Will Hurt A Little”. The track was recorded at Cargo Studios, Rochdale, and featured Lyn Walton (as Lynne Seed) on vocals. (Source: Electricity Club interview, May 2021)

Solo Debut — That’s What I Like About Me (New Hormones, 1980)

Eric Random’s first solo LP, That’s What I Like About Me (ORG 6, New Hormones, 1980), is a largely self-contained minimal electronic record — all core parts (guitar, bass, voice, syn-drums, drum machine) by Random himself. Co-produced with Stephen Mallinder (Cabaret Voltaire), who was already embedded in the New Hormones extended network. The connection to Cabaret Voltaire was there from the very first release.

Eric Random And The Bedlamites (1981–1986)

Between 1981 and 1986, Random released three albums under the collective name Eric Random And The Bedlamites (Discogs ID: 36945). The Bedlamites were not a fixed band but a shifting ensemble; the stable nucleus across releases was Random, Graham Dowdall (Dids), Stuart James, and Wayne Sedgeman / Wayne Worm, with substantial contributions from Cabaret Voltaire members, Diagram Brothers, and others.

Name variations used on releases: “Eric Random Meets The Bedlamites”, “Eric Random & The Bedlamites”, “Eric Random And His Bedlamites”, “The Bedlamites”.

Earthbound Ghost Need (New Hormones ORG 18, 1982)

The debut Bedlamites LP is the most densely connected record in the New Hormones catalog — a rich intersection of Manchester post-punk, Sheffield industrial electronics, and world music elements including shakuhachi and violin.

Ishmael (Fon Records BED 7, 1986)

The third and final Bedlamites LP, on Sheffield’s Fon Records (label ID 4664). Produced by “The Bedlamites” and Robert Gordon (Warp Records co-founder, FON Studio). Six tracks with a pronounced world-music orientation — tabla, berber lute, Collin Walcott cover — alongside electronics and dub production. The Designers Republic handled sleeve design.

2020s Output — Emergency Hearts

eMERGENCY heARTS is an Austin, TX-based independent label founded in 2019 by anarchist author and musician scott crow (Lesson Seven). The label focuses on post-punk, industrial, darkwave, electronic, and experimental music, operating three divisions: eH VINTAGE (unreleased vintage material 1980–2000), NuHorizons (contemporary international electronic), and eHMEDIA (radical publishing and arts). (Sources: Emergency Hearts About; scottcrow.org announcement)

From 2022 onward, Random has been active on the label, with Stephen Mallinder as the primary credited collaborator — a resumption of the Cabaret Voltaire production relationship from the 1980s. Mark Pistel and Meat Beat Manifesto appear as remixers. A broader “VS” album project featuring Mallinder, Mark Stewart, Adrian Sherwood, Patrick Codenys (Front 242), Mika Vainio (Pan Sonic), Mike Watt, and others was under development as of the May 2021 Electricity Club interview.

Open Questions

  • Adi Newton connection — web research found no evidence of any direct collaboration between Eric Random and Adi Newton (Clock DVA / The Anti Group). Multiple searches returned nothing. The original brief’s mention of this connection is likely in error; Adi Newton is not a Cabaret Voltaire member. Confirmed non-connection.
  • Tiller Boys Factory single — The Arrow Studio recording from January 1979 has not been commercially issued. Whether recordings circulate informally is unknown.
  • Jell personnel beyond Lyn Walton — Some Bizzare Album track “I Dare Say It Will Hurt A Little” was recorded at Cargo Studios, Rochdale, with Lyn Walton on vocals. Other Jell members not documented in web sources found.
  • Nico / The Faction — Random was associated with Nico’s backing band The Faction between approximately 1982 and 1988 but did NOT appear on Camera Obscura (1985, prod. John Cale) — he was travelling at the time. His Nico credit is Behind the Iron Curtain (live, 1986) as confirmed by Discogs. The Crepuscule biography incorrectly implies he played on Camera Obscura.
  • “VS” album (Emergency Hearts) — mentioned as forthcoming in the May 2021 Electricity Club interview, featuring Mallinder, Mark Stewart, and numerous others. Whether this was ever released has not been confirmed.
