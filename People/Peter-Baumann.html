Peter Baumann

German electronic musician, label founder, and producer. Born Hans-Peter Baumann, January 29, 1953, Berlin. Member of Tangerine Dream 1971–1977. Discogs artist ID: 54855.

Biography

Early Life and Tangerine Dream (1971–1977)

Baumann joined Tangerine Dream in 1971 at age 18, applying for a vacant keyboard position despite having no prior synthesizer experience. He was accepted by Edgar Froese, the group’s founder and leader. Baumann is credited with pushing the band toward synthesizer-dominant sounds during his tenure — the shift that defined their Virgin Records period and influenced the broader krautrock/electronic scene.

He recorded and toured with the band for six years, appearing on a string of landmark albums:

He also co-wrote “Stratosfear” (the single) and contributed compositional credits to Rubycon and the Sorcerer score. During this period he owned Paragon Studio in Berlin, which served as a recording base.

Baumann left Tangerine Dream in 1977 due to creative differences. He briefly rejoined in June 2015 — working on sketches for Quantum Key and Quantum Gate with Edgar Froese before Froese’s death — but departed again in December 2015.

Solo Career (1976–1983)

While still in Tangerine Dream, Baumann released his debut solo album Romance 76 (Virgin, 1976), produced and written by himself with mixing by Ottmar Bergler. Two further solo records followed after his departure:

  • Trans Harmonic Nights (Virgin, 1979) — with Will Roper (engineer, recorders), Wolfgang Thierfeldt (drums), Bernhard Jobski (horn); produced and composed by Baumann
  • Repeat Repeat (Virgin, 1981) — pop-leaning electronic album; enlisted Robert Palmer as vocalist, plus Jan Hammer, John Tropea, Carsten Bohn, and others
  • Strangers In The Night (Portrait, 1983) — co-produced with Robert Clifford; a synthesizer-pop cover of the Kaempfert/Singleton/Snyder standard

A single Biking Up The Strand (Virgin, 1979) was also released. In 1982, Realtimes appeared on Virgin (Netherlands pressing), alongside a See Those Eyes / Daytime Logic single (Portrait/Trouser Press, 1982).

The Yanni relationship was particularly significant — Baumann produced or executive-produced multiple Yanni albums through the late 1980s and into the 1990s, including the 1997 compilation Devotion: The Best Of Yanni and the 2000 collection The Very Best Of Yanni.

He was also involved with Berlin Express as a band member (Discogs group ID: 105279), for whom he wrote and produced The Russians Are Coming (1982).

Private Music Label (1984–1996)

Baumann founded Private Music (Discogs label ID: 10447) in 1984, running it until 1996. Based in the US, the label specialized in instrumental and new-age music. It was eventually absorbed into BMG. Key signings included Yanni, Patrick O’Hearn, Eddie Jobson, Ravi Shankar (as the Ravi Shankar Project), Suzanne Ciani, and Sanford Ponder. The label also issued compilations (Etosha, The World Of Private Music, The World Of Private Music Vol. II).

Post-Music Career

Since 2009, Baumann has headed the Baumann Institute, described as “dedicated to exploring the nature of awareness and its relationship to human health and well-being.”

Return to Recording (2016–present)

Baumann returned to recording and releasing music under his own name in 2016 via Bureau B (Discogs label ID: 111528, parent: Tapete Records):

ReleaseYearLabel
Machines Of Desire2016Bureau B
Paracon (The Paragon Session Outtakes 1978–1979)2021Bureau B
Nightfall2025Bureau B

Paracon compiled archival material recorded at Paragon Studio Berlin in 1978–1979. Nightfall (2025) was produced, performed, and written by Baumann with additional production/mixing by Sebastian Lee Philipp.

Bureau B has also issued archival and reissue material. The label is a sublabel of Tapete Records.
