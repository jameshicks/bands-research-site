Shingai Shoniwa

Discogs artist ID: 52225 Real name: Shingai Elizabeth Maria Shoniwa Also known as / solo alias: Shingai (Discogs ID: 2737883) Name variations: S. Shoniwa · Shoniwa Role: Vocalist, bassist, songwriter Active: 2001–present Associated acts: Noisettes, Chrome Hoof, The Matthew Herbert Big Band

Biography

Shingai Elizabeth Maria Shoniwa is a British vocalist, bassist, and songwriter of Zimbabwean heritage, best known as the frontwoman and primary visual identity of the London indie-rock trio Noisettes.

Early career and The Noisettes formation

Shoniwa’s recorded presence dates to 2001, when she contributed vocals to Matthew Herbert’s album The Audience (released on !K7 Records under the name “Herbert”), alongside vocalist Dani Siciliano. This places her in the orbit of Herbert’s expansive big-band and electronic jazz world early on — she is listed as a member of The Matthew Herbert Big Band on Discogs.

The Noisettes formed in London in 2003, when guitarist Dan Smith (6) and Shoniwa — previously both in a band called Sonarfly — began writing together more intensively. They recruited drummer Jamie Morrison in December 2003. The three-piece played London gigs through the winter of 2003–04 and recorded their debut EP Three Moods of The Noisettes, released in April 2005 on London indie label Side Salad Records. US distribution followed on Universal’s Low Altitude imprint.

The Noisettes: debut era (2005–2007)

The band signed internationally with Universal Music Group. Early singles “IWE” (2006, Transgressive Records) and “Scratch Your Name” (2006, Mercury/Side Salad) established their raw, punk-inflected indie sound, with Shoniwa credited as songwriter across all of them. They supported Muse on a UK/European arena tour in late 2006, alongside touring stints with TV on the Radio, Bloc Party, Babyshambles, and others.

Their debut album What’s the Time Mr. Wolf? was released in February 2007 on Vertigo Records. All subsequent Noisettes releases came through Vertigo.

Also in 2006, Shoniwa contributed “Voice Clicks” and “Ghostly Mumbles” to Chrome Hoof’s From The Cliffs (Shock, Australia) and their earlier I Saw Such Things In My Sleep EP (2005, Fantastic Plastic) — connecting her to the London experimental rock collective Chrome Hoof well before the Noisettes reached wider prominence.

Wild Young Hearts era (2008–2010)

The second Noisettes album Wild Young Hearts (2009, Vertigo) was the band’s commercial breakthrough, driven by the UK top-ten single “Don’t Upset the Rhythm (Go Baby Go).” The album was produced primarily by Jim Abbiss and Ewan Davies, with additional production contributions. It reached a broad mainstream pop audience while retaining Shoniwa’s energetic stage presence as the band’s defining visual element.

The band appeared on an Annie Lennox compilation (Songs of Mass Destruction, 2007) and a Craig David single (Sing, 2008), and Shoniwa contributed uncredited vocals to DJ Dennis Ferrer’s track “Hey Hey” (2009, Objektivity).

A double A-side with Fall Out Boy and Razorlight (“America’s Suitehearts / Don’t Upset The Rhythm”) extended the band’s reach into North American rock markets. A split with U2 and Pixie Lott also appeared in 2009.

Contact and later Noisettes work (2012–2014)

The third and final Noisettes album Contact was released in 2012 on Absolute/Mono-Ra-Rama. Singles “That Girl” (2012) and “Rifle Song (USA)” (2014, Transgressive) followed. Morrison had by this point moved on to work with the Stereophonics. Discogs places the band’s active span as 2005–2014.

Chrome Hoof

In parallel with the Noisettes’ peak years, Shoniwa was a member of Chrome Hoof, the London multi-piece experimental ensemble led by brothers Leo and Milo Smee. She contributed vocals to their 2013 album Chrome Black Gold (Cuneiform Records), appearing on tracks 2, 3, 5, and 10. Chrome Hoof’s sound — described as “death disco” and “prog doom” — is strikingly different from the Noisettes’ indie-rock.

Solo work as “Shingai”

Shoniwa released a solo album under the name Shingai, Too Bold (2020, Zimtron), a 14-track LP in an R&B/Hip-Hop vein. The album features guest appearances from Happy Cat Jay, Okzharp, Karen Nyame KG, Charlie Kay, Simon Le Saint, Roseau, Chipo, and Kwaye (Kwayedza Kureya, also a Noisettes collaborator). The track “Tsi Tsi” features Kwaye alongside Chipo.

A separate one-off collaboration, “Lov Madiba” (2013, Buffalo Gang/Sony Music/The Cherry Party), credited King Charles, Katie Melua, and Shingai Shoniwa jointly, was a folk/vocal single released as an iTunes digital single.

The Matthew Herbert Big Band

Shoniwa is listed as a member of The Matthew Herbert Big Band, the sprawling ensemble through which producer/composer Matthew Herbert has recorded orchestral jazz works. Her vocal contribution to the 2001 The Audience album (credited to “Herbert”) is the earliest documented recording in her Discogs credit history. The Big Band’s key albums include Goodbye Swingtime (2003) and There’s Me And There’s You (2008), both on Accidental.
