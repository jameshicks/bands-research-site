Rick Rubin

Producer, label co-founder, and one of the most commercially successful and stylistically wide-ranging record producers in American popular music. Born Frederick Jay Rubin. Discogs artist ID: 68211. Also performs as guitarist in Hose (noise/punk) and The Pricks (3).

Overview

Rick Rubin co-founded Def Jam Recordings with Russell Simmons (Discogs ID: 94380) while still a student at NYU in 1983, operating initially out of his dormitory room. His early work defined the sonic template for hip-hop and crossover rap-rock in the mid-1980s — minimal drum machine arrangements, hard-rock guitar samples, and an aggressive low-end mix approach he characterized as “reduced by” rather than fully produced.

After departing Def Jam around 1988, Rubin founded Def American Recordings (later renamed American Recordings in 1993, Discogs ID: 2569) and moved operations to Los Angeles. At American, he pursued a deliberately eclectic roster — simultaneously building one of the most important heavy metal catalogs (Slayer, Danzig, Trouble) while pioneering stripped-down country-folk reinventions (Johnny Cash’s American Recordings series from 1994 onward). Later American Recordings signings included System of a Down, The Black Crowes, and Audioslave.

In 2007 Rubin was appointed co-president of Columbia Records, a post he held until 2012, which further expanded the scale of his production work. Throughout the 2000s and 2010s he produced across virtually every major genre: hip-hop (Jay-Z, Eminem, Kanye West), stadium rock (Linkin Park, Metallica, Red Hot Chili Peppers, AC/DC), pop (Adele’s 21, Shakira), and country/Americana (The Avett Brothers, Damien Rice, Yusuf Islam/Cat Stevens).

Career by Era

Phase 1 — Def Jam (1983–1988)

Rubin’s first productions appeared on the Def Jam label he started from his NYU dorm. The credit he used — “Reduced By” rather than “Produced By” — was a deliberate aesthetic statement: he stripped hip-hop to drum machine patterns, hard breakbeats, and minimal ornamentation.

Phase 2 — Def American / American Recordings (1988–2000)

After leaving Def Jam, Rubin founded Def American Recordings (the “Def” was dropped after an internal dispute in 1993, becoming American Recordings). His production shifted away from hip-hop toward hard rock, metal, and eventually a very spare acoustic-guitar-and-voice aesthetic.

Phase 3 — Columbia Co-Presidency and Broader Production (2000–2012)

Rubin’s American Recordings continued under various distribution arrangements (Columbia, Universal). In 2007 he was appointed co-president of Columbia Records. His work expanded dramatically in both scale and genre range.
