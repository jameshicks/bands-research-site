Aug 13, 20267 min read

Denise Johnson

Born: 31 July 1963, Hulme, Manchester
Died: 27 July 2020, Manchester (three days before her 57th birthday)
Role: Vocalist (session/guest, also member of A Certain Ratio)
Discogs artist ID: 2944
Name variations on releases: D. Johnson, Denise J. Johnson, Denise Johnston, Johnston

Biography

Manchester roots and early session work (1987–1990)

Denise Johnson grew up in Hulme, one of the inner-city districts closest to the Haçienda scene that reshaped Manchester in the 1980s. Her professional career on record began in 1987 with backing-vocal work on two European pop releases — Come Closer (Slik Records) and Love Is No Crime (Coconut). By 1988 she was picking up further session work (Bodybeat, FFRR) and some uncredited contributions. The period 1990–91 saw a surge of collaborations, including A&M Records singles for acts such as A Certain Ratio (on Won’t Stop Loving You and Four For The Floor from the acr:mcr campaign), establishing her as a go-to Manchester session voice.

Primal Scream and Screamadelica (1991–1996)

The most prominent single credit of Johnson’s career came in 1991 when Primal Scream issued Don’t Fight It, Feel It on Creation Records, with Johnson co-billed as a primary artist (the only time she held primary-artist status on a Creation release). Produced by Andrew Weatherall with co-production assist from Hugo Nicolson, the track was the acid-house centrepiece of the Screamadelica album, where Johnson sings lead throughout. The album itself (1991/Creation) lists her in vocals; it went on to be reissued in anniversary editions (2011/Sony). Her relationship with Primal Scream extended across several more records: Movin’ On Up (1991, featuring), Give Out But Don’t Give Up (1994, Memphis sessions produced by Tom Dowd), Intelligentsia (1994), The Big Man And The Scream Team Meet The Barmy Army Uptown (1996, with Irvine Welsh-connected Scream Team alias), and uncredited backing on People Move On (1997, the Bobby Gillespie solo album).

The Joy / Robs Records cluster (1991–1993)

In parallel, Johnson became a fixture on the Robs Records roster alongside M-People’s Andy Connell and others. Credits here include lead vocals on Twenty Seven Forever, Loosen Up Your Mind / The Planet, Up In Downsville, Mello (M-People Mixes), and the written-by/primary credit Turn Me On (1993, Robs Records) — one of her rare songwriting credits during this period.

Solo work under her own name: Hologram Records (1994–1997)

Johnson founded her own label, Hologram Records (2) (label Discogs ID implied by credits), and released her only conventional solo single from this era: Rays Of The Rising Sun (1994, Magnet). She wrote and produced the record alongside collaborator Dave Tolan. A second self-released single, I Believe (1996, Hologram Records), has her listed as producer, programmer, engineer, and songwriter. A third, Inner Peace (The Remixes) (1997), again credits her as producer, engineer, and programmer. A version of Rays Of The Rising Sun was also licensed to EastWest/Perfecto in 1996. Johnny Marr and Owen Morris appear in the production credits of the Magnet pressing.

Electronic and dance-circuit session work (1995–2000)

Through the mid-to-late 1990s Johnson appeared on a wide range of dance and electronic records:

  • Electronic (Bernard Sumner / Johnny Marr project): backing vocals on Forbidden City (1996, Parlophone), Second Nature (1997), Until The End Of Time (1997)
  • Mike Scott (Still Burning, 1997, Chrysalis): backing vocals
  • Michael Hutchence (posthumous V2 album, 1999): backing vocals
  • Numerous Italian dance productions (Matter Of Time, D-Inspiration; Nuova Prospettiva; Wanna Move Up)
  • All Funked Up 96 (Avex UK/Six6): co-writer and performer

A Certain Ratio: integral live member (1990–2020)

Discogs formally lists Johnson as a member of A Certain Ratio (artist ID 11203), alongside Andy Connell, Donald Johnson, Jez Kerr, Martin Moscrop, Tony Quigley, Matt Steele, Simon Topping, Peter Terrell, and others. She joined the live lineup from 1990 and performed with the band for over 200 shows across 30 years per Discogs biography.

Her ACR studio appearances in the Mute Records era:

  • ShadowParty (2018, Mute) — additional vocals; this is the Tom Chapman/Josh Hager project with ACR’s Phil Cunningham also involved
  • Dirty Boy & Shack Up Remixed (2019, Mute) — additional vocals
  • ACR Loco (2020, Mute) — lead vocals on tracks A2, A3, B1, B5
  • Berlin (2020, Mute) — featured vocals (released same year she died)
  • ACR:EPA (2021, Mute) — listed as Performer (posthumous release drawing from Loco sessions)
  • Loco Live At Hope Mill Studios (2022, Mute) — vocals credited posthumously from ACR Loco sessions

Note: the 2018 acr:set live album (Mute) credits are minimal in the DB (all songs credited to ACR collectively); Johnson’s role in the live set is documented in the Discogs artist profile rather than the release credits.

New Order credit

Johnson sang backing vocals on two tracks (A3 and C2) of New Order’s Music Complete (2015, Mute, Discogs release ID 7513259), alongside Dawn Zee and La Roux. This is her only confirmed studio New Order credit in the DB.

Final years and posthumous release (2018–2020)

In 2020 Johnson was working toward her debut solo acoustic album, Where Does It Go (Adassa label, Discogs release ID 15965338), which she produced and performed. The album features Thomas Twemlow (acoustic guitar, engineer), Martin Hederos (Hammond organ), Ian Person (slide guitar), and Matt Colton (mastering). She died on 27 July 2020, and the album was released posthumously on 25 September 2020 as planned.
