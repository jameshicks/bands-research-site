Jim Beattie

Discogs ID: 532197 Real name: James Robertson Beattie Alias: Jim Navajo (used on Sonic Flower Groove, 1987; Discogs alias ID: 769923) Role: Guitarist, songwriter, producer Origin: Glasgow, Scotland Active: 1985–2006 (Discogs credits span) Associated acts: Primal Scream, Spirea X, Adventures In Stereo

Biography

Primal Scream (1982–1988)

Jim Beattie was a co-founder of Primal Scream alongside vocalist Bobby Gillespie. The pair began as “The Primes (2)” around 1982 in Glasgow before consolidating into Primal Scream by 1984. Beattie was the principal guitarist through the band’s first recording period: he played guitar on the debut single All Fall Down (1985, Creation Records CRE 017) and lead guitar on the 12” EP Crystal Crescent (1986, Creation Records CRE 026T), both co-written with Gillespie. The B-side of Crystal Crescent — “Spirea - X” — was written by Beattie alone, and it gave his next band its name.

On the debut album Sonic Flower Groove (1987, Elevation/WEA), Beattie was credited not under his own name but as Jim Navajo, playing 12-string lead guitar. The album’s songs were all written by Gillespie and Beattie jointly. Production was split between Mayo Thompson (most tracks) and Clive Langer / Colin Fairley (the “Imperial” track). The lineup also included Andrew Innes (rhythm guitar), Robert Young (2) (bass), Dave Morgan (drums), Martin Duffy (piano), and Frank Sweeney (viola). Earlier single Gentle Tuesday (1987, Elevation ELV 2) and its B-side Imperial were also Gillespie/Beattie co-writes.

Beattie departed Primal Scream in 1988, after Sonic Flower Groove but before the band’s pivot toward rave and house music. The Spanish pressing of Gentle Tuesday / Imperial (1988, WEA) credited him for twelve-string guitar and co-writing — the last official release in his Primal Scream credit trail until the 2023 retrospective.

In 2023, Beattie contributed liner notes and 12-string guitar credits to Reverberations (Travelling In Time) BBC Radio Sessions & Creation Singles 1985–86 (Young Tiki, YT001CD), compiled by Gillespie. The collection covered the 1985–86 Creation singles period and BBC radio sessions from the early Primal Scream lineup. Beattie is credited as written-by on tracks 1–4 and 16 (“Spirea X”).

Spirea X (1990–1992)

After leaving Primal Scream, Beattie formed Spirea X in Glasgow in 1990 — the name taken directly from his Crystal Crescent B-side. The band signed to 4AD and released three records: the Chlorine Dream single (1991, 4AD BAD 1004 CD), the full album Fireblade Skies (1991, 4AD CAD 1017 CD), and a US-only 7” pressing of Fireblade Skies / Cobalt Blue (1992, 4AD). Spirea X lineup: Beattie (guitar, vocals, producer), Judith Boyle (vocals), Thomas McGurk (rhythm guitar), James O’Donnell (bass), Andy Kerr (drums). Fireblade Skies was engineered by Brian McNeil, Dave Pine, and Paul Doyle; Beattie co-produced with Dave Pine (credited as “Dine” on the LP). The cover art was designed by Vaughan Oliver — 4AD’s house designer. The sound was shoegaze-adjacent alternative rock. The album includes a cover of Arthur Lee’s “Signed D.C.” (Love).

The Speed Reaction single (1991, 4AD) came out alongside Fireblade Skies, making four releases total across the Spirea X catalog.

Adventures In Stereo (1996–2000)

After Spirea X disbanded, Beattie formed Adventures In Stereo in the mid-1990s. The group’s origins were a collaboration with Simon Dine, who created much of the early music. The initial lineup also featured Judith Boyle (continuing from Spirea X), Gayle Brogan, David McCluskey, and Docherty. The band’s history became complicated when Dine departed but both factions continued releasing records under the Adventures In Stereo name — Beattie’s through Creeping Bent and Dine’s through Underground Sounds (a separate imprint).

The self-titled LP (Adventures In Stereo, 1997) collected early recordings and unreleased material; Alternative Stereo Sounds (1998) was the proper second album.

Bobsled Records period (2000):

The band moved to the US label Bobsled Records for their final releases. Monomania (2000, BOB-11) was the third and final album — a 14-track record (plus hidden track) produced, arranged, and written by Beattie, with performers including Judith Boyle, David McCluskey, Morag Brown, Brad Cunningham, Pamela Cobb, and Johnny Cameron (3) — the last of whom also recorded the album. Mastered by John Golden. A companion single, International (2000, BOB-13), was written and produced by Beattie alone. A US release of Alternative Stereo Sounds (1998) also appeared on Bobsled (cat. Bob-10 / Bob-10CD).

Later work (2006)

In 2006, Beattie recorded the B-side of a 7” by Glasgow band Popup — “In Her Day (Chuck’s-a-legend Mix)” — credited as “Recorded By” alongside Andy Fernan and Sandy Allison (2). The single was released on Art-Goes-Pop (agp4). This is the last Discogs credit under Beattie’s name (aside from the 2023 Primal Scream retrospective).
