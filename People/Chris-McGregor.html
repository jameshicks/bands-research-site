Chris McGregor

South African pianist and bandleader. Discogs artist ID: 285389. Born 1936 in South Africa; died 1990.

Leader of the Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath and a central figure in the South African jazz exile community in Europe. Left South Africa in 1964 with the Blue Notes when apartheid law made their racially mixed band’s public performance illegal. He settled in London and assembled the Brotherhood of Breath from both exile community members and British free improvisers. Connected to the founding of Ogun through his close collaboration with Harry Miller, Hazel Miller, and Keith Beal.

Ogun solo/leader albums: In His Good Time (1979, OG 521) — solo piano — and (with Brotherhood) Live At Willisau (1974, OG 100) and Procession (1978, OG 524). His compositions anchor the The Dedication Orchestra albums Spirits Rejoice (1992) and Ixesha (Time) (1994).

Also recorded Grandmothers Teaching (1988, ITM 979.028) for ITM Records (2) alongside Harry Beckett, Johnny Dyani, Marilyn Mazur, and Peter Kowald. Posthumous Brotherhood release on ITM: The Memorial Concert (1994, ITMP 970086).

European Label Documentation

Research into FMP, ICP, and peer labels conducted via web sources (Wikipedia, label sites, discography archives) — see “Web sources” below.

Ogun (UK)

The primary European documentation label for McGregor’s work. Founded 1973 by Harry Miller, Hazel Miller, and Keith Beal. McGregor appears on Ogun releases across three projects:

As leader / solo:

  • In His Good Time (1979, OG 521 / OGCD 014) — solo piano, recorded Paris 1977. source

Brotherhood of Breath:

  • Live at Willisau (1974, OG 100 / OGCD 001) — recorded Willisau, Switzerland, 27 Jan 1973. source
  • Procession (Live at Toulouse) (1978, OG 524 / OGCD 040) — recorded Halle aux Grain, Toulouse, 10 May 1977. source

The Blue Notes:

  • Legacy: Live in South Afrika 1964 (1995, OGCD 007) — recorded 1964, released posthumously. source
  • Blue Notes for Mongezi (1976, OG 210; reissue 2022 OGCD 028) — recorded London, 23 Dec 1975, tribute to Mongezi Feza. source
  • Blue Notes in Concert Volume 1 (1978, OG 220) — recorded 100 Club, London, 16 Apr 1977. source
  • Before the Wind Changes (2012, OGCD 015) — recorded 1 Jul 1979, released posthumously. source
  • Blue Notes for Johnny (1987, OG 532; reissue 2022 OGCD 028) — recorded Redan Studios, London, 18 Aug 1987. Trio: McGregor, Dudu Pukwana, Louis Moholo. source
  • The Ogun Collection (2008, OGCD 031) — five-CD box set collecting the four Blue Notes albums above plus additional material. source

With Harry Miller:

  • Different Times, Different Places (2013, OGCD 041) — archival recordings 1973–1976, featuring McGregor alongside Mike Osborne, Mark Charig, Nick Evans, Malcolm Griffiths, Keith Tippett, Louis Moholo. source

Musica (France)

French label. McGregor recorded two solo piano albums after relocating to the south of France. Both have been collectors’ items and are among his rarest releases.

  • Piano Song Vol. 1 (1977, MUS 3019) — solo piano, recorded 28 Jun 1977. source
  • Piano Song Vol. 2 (1977) — solo piano. source

In and Out (France)

French free jazz label. McGregor’s Brotherhood of Breath (the “BoB Mark II” formation with many French musicians) recorded for In and Out in 1981.

  • Yes Please (1981, IaO 1001) — recorded Théâtre Municipal, Angoulème, 1–2 Jun 1981. Personnel included Harry Beckett, Mark Charig, Nick Evans, Radu Malfatti, Louis Sclavis, François Jeanneau, Brian Abrahams, Didier Levallet. source

Venture / Virgin (UK)

  • Country Cooking (1988, Venture) — final studio recording by the Brotherhood; released on Virgin’s Venture imprint. source

52e rue est (France)

French label. The last Brotherhood of Breath live recording with McGregor:

  • En Concert à Banlieues Bleues (1989, RECD 017) — with Archie Shepp; recorded Espace John Lennon, La Courneuve, France, 18 Mar 1989. source

RCA / Neon (UK)

Early Brotherhood of Breath releases on a major-label subsidiary:

  • Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood of Breath (1971, Neon NE 2) — debut album, produced by Joe Boyd. source
  • Brotherhood (1972, RCA) — second studio album. source

FMP (Free Music Production, Germany)

No confirmed Chris McGregor releases on FMP as leader or sideman. The FMP artist roster includes Harry Miller and Louis Moholo (both recorded for FMP, the latter with Peter Brötzmann), but no documented sessions include McGregor. The vault item flagging FMP as a “likely connection” is not confirmed by web sources. (Wikipedia’s Category:FMP/Free Music Production artists lists neither McGregor, Brotherhood of Breath, nor Blue Notes — as of 2026-07-03.) source

ICP (Instant Composers Pool, Netherlands)

No confirmed Chris McGregor releases on ICP. ICP was founded by Willem Breuker, Misha Mengelberg, and Han Bennink; its roster overlaps with European free improvisation circles McGregor moved in, but no documented ICP session featuring him has been identified. source

Incus (UK)

No confirmed Chris McGregor releases on Incus identified by web research.
