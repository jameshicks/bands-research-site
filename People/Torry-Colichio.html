Overview

Torry Colichio is an American drummer and graphic designer, twin sister of Tammi Colichio, whose recorded work is concentrated in the 1990s across indie rock, lo-fi, and punk. She was a member of Unrest, Fifth Column, Flying Saucer, Kickstand, and Cobalt, accumulating 21 Discogs credits from 1991 onward with releases primarily on Teenbeat and a cluster of associated indie labels. Her densest collaborations — nine shared releases each — are with Unrest members Bridget Cross, Mark Robinson, and Phil Krauth, as well as producer Wharton Tiers, pointing to a core involvement in the DC Teenbeat orbit of the early-to-mid 1990s.

Notes (web) — Fifth Column connection

Summary: No source documents the specific personal or professional pathway through which Colichio came to play with Fifth Column. The available evidence establishes the what and when but not the how.

What is established:

  • A 1992 poster for a “Fifth Column Homocore Party #1” at Czar Bar, Chicago lists Colichio alongside Fifth Column’s core members (Caroline Azar, G.B. Jones, Beverly Breckenridge) and Donna Dresch, placing her in that queercore social orbit before the studio work. arquives.ca
  • Wikipedia lists her as Fifth Column drummer 1993–1994. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Column_(band) (as of 2026-10-24)
  • She drummed on four songs of Fifth Column’s 1994 K Records LP 36C, credited as “Torry Colichio of Kickstand.” The Trouserpress review describes the album as “recorded as a Briede-less trio, with guest guitarist Michelle Breslin and Torry Colichio of Kickstand drumming on four songs.” trouserpress.com/reviews/fifth-column/
  • Donna Dresch (Team Dresch) played guitar on the same LP and is a named “longtime compadre” of Fifth Column — Dresch sits at the intersection of the Pacific Northwest queercore scene and broader North American homocore networks, and both she and Colichio were guests on 36C at the same time.

Likely mechanism (inferred, not sourced): Fifth Column had well-documented connections across North American queercore/homocore networks by the early 1990s, recruiting guests from outside Toronto for 36C — Donna Dresch (Portland/Olympia) and Colichio (DC/Hoboken) both contributed. 36C was released on K (Calvin Johnson’s Olympia label), which also had ties to both the Toronto queercore scene and the DC indie world. Kickstand’s own 1994 LP appeared on Queenie, a label whose release Plastic Fruit (1991, Homestead Records) also has Colichio drumming credits. The shared queercore touring and festival circuit — documented in the 1992 Chicago show poster — is the most plausible vector, but no interview or press piece examined names the specific connection or who made the introduction.
