Joanna Newsom

Joanna Newsom

Jun 21, 20268 min read

Joanna Newsom

Discogs ID: 268383
Born: 18 January 1982, Grass Valley, California (raised in Nevada City, California)
Instruments: Harp, piano, harpsichord, Wurlitzer electric piano, clavichord, vocals
Labels: Drag City (2004–present)

American harpist, pianist, harpsichordist, singer, and songwriter. One of the most singular voices in American indie-folk; known for an idiosyncratic vocal delivery, densely allusive lyrics, and the use of pedal harp as the central instrument in a pop-adjacent context. All four of her solo studio albums were released on Drag City.

Early Years and Sacramento Scene

Newsom’s earliest documented recording credit is a guest appearance on vocals and piano on Golden Shoulders’ Let My Burden Be (2002, Doppler Records) — a connection to the Sacramento area indie scene. Golden Shoulders’ roster at the time included several people who would remain part of Newsom’s orbit: Neal Morgan (drums; later her primary drummer), Richard Good (guitar; later a co-member of The Pleased), Dana Gumbiner (bass), and Josh Klinghoffer.

Around the same period she was a member of The Pleased (Discogs ID: 491384), a short-lived Bay Area band also including Noah Georgeson, Richard Good, and Luckey Remington. The Pleased released one known record, One piece from the middle (Not On Label, 2002). This is also where the long-running Newsom/Georgeson collaboration began — Georgeson went on to produce her first two Drag City releases and has been a recurring collaborator through Divers (2015).

She also played classical harp on one track on Nervous Cop’s self-titled album (2003, 5 Rue Christine), alongside Deerhoof’s Greg Saunier and John Dieterich, and Hella’s Zach Hill.

Walnut Whales (Self-Released EP, 2002)

Before signing to Drag City, Newsom self-released the CDr EP Walnut Whales (Not On Label, 2002). The eight-track tape is the earliest document of her solo voice and contains early versions of songs that would reappear on The Milk-Eyed Mender: “Erin,” “Cassiopeia,” “Peach, Plum, Pear,” “Clam Crab Cockle Cowrie,” “The Book Of Right-On,” and others. It circulated in limited copies and became something of a bootleg artifact before Drag City picked her up. No additional credits on Discogs.

The Milk-Eyed Mender (Drag City, 2004)

Discogs release ID: 458611. Produced by Noah Georgeson. Newsom played harp (Lyon & Healy Style 15), Wurlitzer electric piano, harpsichord, and piano; she wrote all the songs. The album’s cover and back were designed and embroidered by Emily Prince (Discogs ID: 1831715) — a distinctive hand-embroidered visual that set the template for Newsom’s packaging aesthetic. Photography by Alissa Anderson. Released the same year as the Sprout And The Bean single (Drag City, 2004), which featured the same production team.

Ys (Drag City, 2006)

Discogs release ID: 827807. The major-statement record. Five long songs, full orchestral arrangements by Van Dyke Parks, harp and vocals by Newsom, co-produced by Newsom and Van Dyke Parks, recorded in two distinct sessions: harp and vocals recorded by Steve Albini; orchestra recorded by Tim Boyle. Mixed by Jim O’Rourke. Mastered by Nick Webb.

The album draws on Parks’ California orchestral vocabulary — the same world as Song Cycle and Discover America — applied to Newsom’s extended narrative songs. The collaboration is one of the more unlikely and successful pairings in Drag City’s history. Parks conducted the orchestra himself and played accordion.

Have One On Me (Drag City, 2010)

Discogs release ID: 2148029. Triple album — 18 songs across three LPs. Arranged, produced, and written entirely by Newsom; she arranged harp, piano, and vocals. Ryan Francesconi served as arranger and conductor, contributing additional piano and vocal arrangements and playing Bulgarian tambura, acoustic guitar, electric guitar, banjo, mandolin, electric bass, kaval, soprano recorder, and kora. Neal Morgan contributed drum parts and percussion. Mixed jointly by Jim O’Rourke (selected tracks) and Noah Georgeson (remaining tracks). Cover painting by Becca Mann.

Divers (Drag City, 2015)

Discogs release ID: 7619971. Her fourth studio album, a return to orchestral ambition after Have One On Me’s more intimate arrangements. Orchestral arrangements contributed by Nico Muhly (track 1) and David Longstreth of Dirty Projectors (track 11, credited as Dave Longstreth). Newsom self-arranged harp, piano, celesta, clavichord, harpsichord, mellotron, marxophone, field organ, keyboards, synthesizers, and vocals; she co-produced and mixed with Noah Georgeson. Recorded partly by Steve Albini. Mastered by John Golden. Back cover photography by Paul Thomas Anderson (Discogs ID: 1938831).

Film Soundtrack

Newsom appeared as a musician on Jonny Greenwood’s Inherent Vice soundtrack (Nonesuch, 2014) — Paul Thomas Anderson’s film adaptation of the Thomas Pynchon novel. She is credited as “Musician” on tracks 5 and 13 alongside Can, Supergrass, and others. The Greenwood/Anderson/Newsom connection accounts for Anderson’s photography on Divers’ back cover.

Other Guest Appearances

  • Nervous Cop — Nervous Cop (2003, 5 Rue Christine) — harp, classical. Session with Greg Saunier, Zach Hill, John Dieterich.
  • Golden Shoulders — Let My Burden Be (2002, Doppler Records) — vocals, piano. Early Sacramento-area credit.
  • Could This Be the End — Golden Shoulders, (2019) — piano. A return to the Golden Shoulders orbit.
  • Rock Bottom Riser (Smog/Bill Callahan, Domino, 2006) — credited as Performer. Drag City family connection.
  • How I Got Over (The Roots, 2010, Def Jam) — Written-By credit (one song).
