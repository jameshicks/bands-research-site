Jim Glennie

Jim Glennie

Oct 06, 20268 min read

Jim Glennie

Discogs ID: 274927
Real name: James Patrick Glennie
Born: 10 October 1963, Moss Side, Manchester, England
Instruments: Bass guitar, backing vocals
Name variations: Glennie · J. Glennie · James Glennie · James Patrick Glennie · Jim · Jimmie Glennie

Bassist and co-founder of James, the Manchester indie/alternative rock band. One of four original members alongside Tim Booth, Larry Gott, and Gavan Whelan. Present in every era of the band across more than four decades: Factory Records beginnings (1982–85), the Sire/Blanco Y Negro transition (1986–89), the Fontana/Mercury commercial peak (1990–98), the first break-up and Mercury final albums (1999–2001), and the 2007-to-present reformation (Fontana → Infectious Music → Virgin Music). Also co-writer on nearly all James material and co-produced the landmark Gold Mother (1990). No Discogs-documented solo projects or side bands outside James.

Biography

Formation and Factory Years (1982–1985)

James formed in Manchester in 1982. Glennie is credited on the debut release — the Jimone EP (Factory, FAC 78, 1983) — with an uncredited artwork contribution alongside the bass performance. The three-track 7” (Folk Lore / What’s The World / Fire So Close) was engineered by Chris Nagle and established the band’s early folk-inflected indie sound. The early lineup — Glennie, Booth, Gott, Whelan — plus keyboardist Mick Armistead was documented on the live album One Man Clapping (One Man Records, 1989, recorded 1983), which captures the pre-Sire band performing for a student audience; Glennie plays bass and sings. The Village Fire EP (Factory, 1985) collected five early recordings and was pressed in Japan as a compilation (Discogs ID 705037).

Sire / Blanco Y Negro (1986–1989)

The band was picked up by Sire Records, and Glennie played bass on the debut album Stutter (Sire, 1986), produced by Lenny Kaye (of the Patti Smith Group), with Gil Norton engineering. The follow-up Strip-mine (Blanco Y Negro/Sire, 1988) was produced and mixed by Hugh Jones with Steve Power mixing certain tracks; the lineup had contracted to a trio of Glennie, Gott, and Whelan for much of it, with Glennie taking bass and vocals.

The Come Home single appeared first on Rough Trade in 1989 (with Glennie as co-producer and co-writer) before being re-released on Fontana in 1990.

Fontana / Mercury Commercial Peak (1990–1998)

The band expanded its lineup with Andy Diagram on trumpet, Saul Davies on violin/guitar, and Mark Hunter on keyboards — the seven-piece that became the classic configuration. Glennie’s role expanded to include co-production on Gold Mother (Fontana, 1990), the album that broke the band nationally. The production credit reads Booth / Glennie / Gott / Garside (Nick Garside, engineer). Brian Eno was not involved in Gold Mother; his production partnership came later.

Seven (Fontana, 1992) is the commercial peak: it reached #2 in the UK albums chart. The album was co-produced by James alongside Youth (Martin Glover, Killing Joke bassist; tracks 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 11) and Steve “Barney” Chase (tracks 2, 5, 7, 9, 10), mixed by Tim Palmer. Glennie plays bass throughout.

Laid (Fontana, 1993) introduced the Brian Eno production partnership. Eno co-produced and also played bass (additional) and keyboards on several tracks; Glennie’s bass anchors the album. The simultaneously recorded ambient/experimental sister album Wah Wah (Fontana, 1994) is credited to “James are” with Glennie listed as band member.

Whiplash (Fontana, 1997) was produced by Stephen Hague with Eno providing “Frequent Interference And Occasional Co-production.” The lineup for Whiplash added Adrian Oxaal on guitar. Eno contributed keyboards and backing vocals; Andy Duncan played additional percussion.

The Best Of (Fontana/Mercury, 1998) is the definitive summary of this era, listing Glennie with a bass credit.

Late Mercury and First Split (1999–2001)

Three final albums appeared on Mercury: Millionaires (1999), Pleased To Meet You (2001, co-produced with Brian Eno), and the singles/rarities collection B-Sides Ultra (2001). Glennie holds Written-By credits throughout. The live album Getting Away With It…Live appeared on Sanctuary (2002) after the split; Glennie is credited with bass.

The band officially split in 2001.

Return and Post-2007 (2007–present)

James reformed in 2007. Hey Ma (Fontana, 2008) was produced by Lee Muddy Baker and James; Glennie plays bass guitars and holds a Written-By credit alongside Booth and Gott.

Subsequent studio albums:

  • The Night Before / The Morning After (Mercury, 2010) — live/studio material
  • La Petite Mort (BMG / Cooking Vinyl, 2014) — produced by Max Dingel; Glennie plays bass guitar and backing vocals, and co-writes
  • Girl At The End Of The World (BMG, 2016) — performer credit
  • Living In Extraordinary Times (Infectious Music, 2018) — produced by Charlie Andrew and Beni Giles; Glennie plays bass guitar and percussion, and co-composes all tracks; he also serves as executive producer on the related live album Live In Extraordinary Times (Nothing But Love Music, 2020)
  • All The Colours Of You (Virgin Music, 2021) — produced by Jacknife Lee; Glennie credited as Music By / Performer / Producer (Pre-production); also conducted pre-production alongside Mark Hunter and Peter Glennie (Discogs ID 5122487)
  • Be Opened By The Wonderful (40 Years Orchestrated) (Virgin Music, 2023) — orchestral retrospective
  • Yummy (Virgin Music, 2024) — Glennie credited with bass, liner notes, and Written-By
  • Nothing But Love // The Definitive Best Of (EMI/Mercury/Universal, 2025) — backing vocals, bass, liner notes, producer

In 2021, a Future Islands remix of “Beautiful Beaches” appeared on Virgin Music.

James Lineup Notes

The founding four were Glennie, Booth, Gott, and Whelan, with Mick Armistead on keys in early gigs. By 1990 the lineup had grown substantially — Whelan had departed and David Baynton-Power joined on drums; Diagram, Davies, and Hunter filled out a seven-piece. The “classic” lineup for the commercial peak was:

Booth (vocals) · Glennie (bass) · Gott (guitar) · Diagram (trumpet) · Davies (violin/guitar) · Hunter (keyboards) · Baynton-Power (drums)

By Whiplash (1997), Adrian Oxaal had joined on guitar. The 2007 reformation brought back the core of Booth, Glennie, Gott, Diagram, Davies, Hunter, and Baynton-Power, with Oxaal continuing. Later lineups added Chloe Alper (bass/backing vocals), Debbie Knox-Hewson (drums), Ron Yeadon, Michael Kulas, and Jenny Belinda Glennie (Jim’s wife, Discogs ID 1840029, listed as a James member).
