Bunny Brunel

US-French electric bassist and composer. Born Bernard Brunel, March 2, 1950, Nice, France. Discogs artist ID: 668802. Real name: Bernard Brunel.

Active primarily in jazz-fusion across a five-decade career spanning French session work, American fusion supergroup recordings, solo albums on major labels, and the long-running CAB project. A technically advanced fretless and piccolo-bass specialist, Brunel bridges French jazz-rock of the mid-1970s with the Los Angeles fusion scene and 2000s progressive instrumental rock.

Biography by Era

1974–1978 — French Jazz-Rock Scene

Brunel emerged in the mid-1970s French jazz-rock milieu, credited on a string of albums recorded in France and released on Polydor, RCA Victor, and related majors. Key early credits include Jan Akkerman’s Jan Akkerman 3 (1979, Atlantic) and work alongside Didier Lockwood and Patrick Gauthier on the 1976 French release Jazz-Rock (Music-Zag) — recorded and co-presented by Brunel alongside the group Lockwood. During this period he also appeared in French pop and variety contexts, honing an adaptable session style.

1979 — Tony Williams Lifetime and the Corea Orbit

By 1979 Brunel was embedded in two premier American fusion contexts simultaneously. He was a member of The Tony Williams Lifetime in its later reconstituted lineup — the Berkeley 1979 recording (released 2023 on Equinox) documents this band: Tony Williams (drums), Bunny Brunel (bass), Tod Carver (guitar), Bruce Harris (keyboards), Tom Grant (keyboards). This is the post–Jack Bruce / post–John McLaughlin version of the group.

Concurrently he was working closely with Chick Corea. The Summer Jam 1979 (Show Company, Japan, 1994) and Live In Tokyo ‘79 (Hi Hat / Inter Art Committees, 2021) document a quartet of Corea, Al Di Meola, Tony Williams, and Brunel — a genuine fusion all-star configuration. The Live Under The Sky… ‘80 release (Equinox, 2021) captures the same general orbit, again with Brunel alongside Corea, Don Alias, and Tom Brechtlein.

1979 — Touch (Warner Bros.)

Brunel’s first billed solo record, Touch (Warner Bros. Records, 1979), was a co-production with keyboardist Daniel Goyone (credited as “Bunny Brunel featuring Daniel Goyone”). Recorded partly in France and partly in Los Angeles, the album employed a large cast of engineers (Jean-Pierre Massiera, Bernie Kirsh, Joe Bellamy, John Rosenthal) and was accompanied by sleeve notes written by Brunel and Goyone themselves.

1982 — Ivanhoe (Baybridge Records, Japan)

The Ivanhoe album (Baybridge Records, Japan, 1982; 8 pressings documented) is Brunel’s most high-profile leader date of the early period — a fusion showcase deploying an extraordinary personnel roster:

Brunel produced the album and plays bass throughout. Executive producer: Ron Moss.

1989 — Momentum and the Kilowatt Session

Momentum (America Records, France, 1989) is a fully self-produced solo album on which Brunel plays bass, keyboards, and handles all arrangements and composition. Key collaborators: Frank Gambale (guitar, tracks 3–6, 8–10), Kei Akagi (piano and keyboards), Patrick Moraz (keyboards, track 1), John Wackerman (drums/percussion).

Also in 1989, Brunel co-produced Kazumi Watanabe’s Kilowatt (Gramavision) — playing bass, synth bass, and keyboards while Watanabe handled guitar and co-production. Drummer again was John Wackerman.

1992 — Dedication (Musidisc)

A collaborative leader date credited to Brunel, Mike Stern, Billy Childs, and Vinnie Colaiuta. Brunel plays acoustic upright bass throughout — a departure from his usual electric work. Stern on guitar, Childs on piano, Colaiuta on drums.

1994 — For You To Play (Média 7)

Solo album featuring Mike Stern and Terri Lyne Carrington among others.

1998 — Bunny Brunel’s L.A. Zoo (Tone Center)

The Bunny Brunel’s L.A. Zoo album (Tone Center, 1998) introduced the L.A. Zoo alias in fully released form. Mixed by Phil Ramone and Phil Brown.

2000–2003 — CAB

CAB (Creative Art Band) is the defining ensemble of Brunel’s post-2000 output — a jazz-fusion/progressive supergroup with a fixed core lineup:

  • Bunny Brunel — bass, keyboards, producer
  • Tony MacAlpine — guitar, keyboards
  • Dennis Chambers — drums
  • Brian Auger — organ (core member from CAB 2 onward)

2011–Present — Later Work

Live On Sunset (Nikaia Records, 2011) is a US live album. The 2021 archival series — Live In Tokyo ‘79 and Live Under The Sky… ‘80 — documents the late-1970s Corea group. Virgil Donati appears on several later live and studio dates.
