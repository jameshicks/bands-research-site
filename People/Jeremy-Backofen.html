Overview

Jeremy Backofen is an American guitarist, bassist, drummer, engineer, and producer. He operates under multiple name variations — most notably “The Searcher” — and has built a career straddling two distinct roles: recording engineer and session/collaborative musician. His Discogs credits span from low-key self-released recordings in 2001 through major independent label productions in the 2010s.

He is best known in the context of this vault for his work with Adam Pierce and Mice Parade: he engineered What It Means To Be Left-Handed (FatCat Records, 2010) and served as Pierce’s assistant mixer on the bulk of that album; two years later he appeared as a performer on Candela (P-Vine Records, 2012). Alongside the Mice Parade work, Backofen spent several years as the primary producer and engineer for The Felice Brothers, producing or co-producing their self-titled 2008 album, Yonder Is The Clock (2009), and Celebration, Florida (2011), as well as assorted solo recordings by Felice Brothers members. He also contributed engineering and mixing to Frightened Rabbit’s The Midnight Organ Fight (FatCat Records, 2008).

Early Credits (2001)

The earliest Discogs entry for Backofen is a 2001 cassette/CDR-era release, Non-Friction Society: The Hero’s Journey, on Not On Label, where he is credited with Band, Written By, and Recorded By. This is his only primary-artist-adjacent credit and likely represents a very early self-released or DIY project.

Work with The Felice Brothers (2007–2011)

Backofen’s most sustained body of production work is with The Felice Brothers, the folk-rock group from the Catskills, NY. He produced or co-produced four of their early records:

On the self-titled 2008 album he shares production with the band; on Yonder Is The Clock (2009) he is sole producer and engineer. Celebration, Florida (2011) brought the band to Fat Possum Records with Backofen again producing and engineering, with additional engineering by Jaden Harris and mastering by Chris Athens.

He also produced the 2008 release Tonight At The Arizona (New York Pro) and co-produced Simone Felice’s 2011 solo debut (Team Love Records), sharing production with Simone Felice, Ben Lovett, James Felice, and Jesse Glick.

Work with Mice Parade / Adam Pierce (2007–2022)

Backofen’s connection to Adam Pierce and Mice Parade runs across nearly fifteen years:

On What It Means To Be Left-Handed (2010), Backofen engineered the full album and assisted Adam Pierce (credited as “Pierce”) on mixing duties — Pierce handled the mix lead on most tracks. Alan Douches mastered.

For Candela (2012), the relationship shifts: Backofen appears as a performer alongside a full ensemble including Dan Lippel, Joshua Larue, Jeremy Thal, Peter Truffa, Larry Moses, Gisellse Saad Assi, and Caroline Lufkin (voice). This is the most band-like Mice Parade recording in the catalog, and Backofen’s “guitarist/bassist” background makes his performer role consistent with a live-instrument ensemble session.

By Lapapọ (2022), Backofen is back in the engineer/mixer seat, recording and mixing the album — the most recent documented collaboration with Pierce.

Frightened Rabbit — The Midnight Organ Fight (2008)

Backofen is one of four engineers credited on Frightened Rabbit’s breakthrough second album The Midnight Organ Fight (FatCat Records, 2008), alongside Greg Giorgio, Kevin McMahon, and Peter Katis. Katis produced and mixed the record; Backofen’s engineering credit is not track-scoped in the Discogs entry, placing him in the broader session crew.

Gill Landry — Piety & Desire (2010)

On Gill Landry’s solo debut Piety & Desire (2010, self-released), Backofen appears as producer, engineer, and performer (drums and bass), credited as “The Searcher” and “Jeremy Backofen The Searcher.” The album is a notable meeting point of the Felice Brothers world: Ian Felice plays electric guitar and organ, James Felice plays piano/accordion/organ, and The Felice Brothers are co-producers. Additional vocalists include Jolie Holland, Brandi Carlile, Samantha Parton, and Phil/Tim Hanseroth. Backofen’s drums and bass here are his most prominent performance credits outside the Mice Parade context.

Gregory and the Hawk — Moenie And Kitchi (2008)

On Gregory And The Hawk’s Moenie And Kitchi (FatCat Records, 2008), Backofen is the engineer and mixer, with Adam Pierce producing and co-mixing. This is an early meeting point of the Backofen–Pierce collaboration prior to their work on What It Means To Be Left-Handed.

Ólöf Arnalds — Ólöf Sings (2012)

Backofen engineered and mixed Ólöf Sings (One Little Indian, 2012) for Icelandic folk singer Ólöf Arnalds, produced by Skúli Sverrisson. Mastering by Emily Lazar and Joe LaPorta.
