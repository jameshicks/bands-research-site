Joe Chiccarelli

Discogs ID: 147534
ANV / name variations: Joe Chicarelli (most common), Joseph Chiccarelli, Joey Chiccarelli, ‘St.’ Joe Chiccarelli, Joe “The Tweek” Chiccarelli, and various spelling permutations across decades of credits
Based: Los Angeles, CA (since late 1970s); works primarily out of EastWest Studios
Role: Record producer, mixing engineer, recording engineer

Joe Chiccarelli is an American record producer and engineer from Boston, Massachusetts, whose career spans the late 1970s through the 2020s. He started as a tape editor and assistant engineer at Fleetwood Recording Studio (owned by a cousin) and Music Designers in Boston. In the late 1970s he relocated to Los Angeles, where he broke into major-label sessions as a recording engineer on Frank Zappa projects, then built a production career through the 1980s and 1990s before becoming one of the most sought-after indie-rock producers of the 2000s–2010s.

Early Career — Boston to Los Angeles (1971–1978)

Chiccarelli’s first credits are as a tape editor and associate producer on Fleetwood Records (2) sports/spoken-word releases in the early 1970s, including Pittsburgh Pirates and NHL anniversary records. By 1974 he had moved to general engineering work, and by 1976–1978 he was recording and mixing sessions on both coasts, working on releases for Arista, Warner Bros., ABC, and Mercury.

His big break came through the Zappa orbit: he is credited as recording engineer on Frank Zappa’s Sheik Yerbouti (1979), Joe’s Garage Act I (1979), and Joe’s Garage Acts II & III (1979) — major projects that established his reputation in Hollywood studios. Those sessions placed him inside what was then the most technically demanding independent rock operation in the US. He also did engineering on Tinsel Town Rebellion (1981) and later You Can’t Do That On Stage Anymore Vol. 4 (1991) and Vol. 6 (1992), as well as a stereo/multi-channel remix credit on the 1978 Halloween concert DTS release (2003).

His work with Oingo Boingo (producing Nothing To Fear in 1982 and the best-of Skeletons In The Closet in 1989) is among the earliest major production credits. He co-produced multiple Pat Benatar albums with Peter Coleman through the mid-1980s, and produced Stan Ridgway’s The Big Heat on I.R.S. Records. He also mixed the debut album of the Bangles (All Over The Place, Columbia, 1984) — a connection to the mid-80s Los Angeles jangle-pop scene.

The early Tori Amos record Y Kant Tori Read (Atlantic, 1988) lists Chiccarelli as producer and recording engineer — a project that has since become notorious as the pre-breakthrough album Tori Amos largely disavows.

By the late 1980s he was also placing records with Latin and Australian artists, and working across gospel, Christian rock, and soundtrack releases — the breadth of a Los Angeles session professional who moved fluidly through genres.

1990s — San Francisco Indie Scene and Transition

The early 1990s brought Chiccarelli into the San Francisco indie orbit most significantly through American Music Club. He mixed the majority of their album Everclear (Alias, 1991) — specifically tracks 1–5, 7–9, and 11, with Bruce Kaphan handling tracks 6 and 10. AMC co-produced with Kaphan and Norman Kerner. Chiccarelli then co-produced and served as primary producer on San Francisco (Reprise, 1994) alongside the band itself, with Jim Scott handling mixing on most tracks and Tracy Chisholm on recording. Discogs records 38 total shared releases between Chiccarelli and AMC across their history.

The Odelay deluxe reissue (2008) confirms he recorded and mixed additional bonus tracks — specifically tracks 2–15 and 2–16 — for Beck’s classic 1996 Geffen album. His involvement with Beck is in the expanded-edition capacity rather than the original recording, though his name appeared on those reissue sessions. The Dust Brothers, Rob Schnapf, Tom Rothrock, and Mario Caldato Jr. produced the core album.

2000s — Indie Rock Centrality

The 2000s represent Chiccarelli’s highest-profile run as a producer, during which he worked with a string of critically central indie-rock artists:

The Shins — the most sustained collaboration of this era. He co-produced the single “Phantom Limb” (Sub Pop, 2006) with James Mercer, then produced and mixed Wincing The Night Away (Sub Pop, 2007) — the Shins’ third album and their commercial breakthrough — again with Mercer co-producing. He also produced the “Australia” single (2007). Phil Ek contributed additional production to three tracks on Wincing.

The White Stripes — Chiccarelli recorded and co-mixed Icky Thump (XL/Third Man, 2007) alongside Jack White (2), who produced and co-mixed. He also appears on the White Stripes’ 2020 greatest-hits compilation as a studio professional credit.

Manchester Orchestra — produced and mixed Mean Everything To Nothing (Canvasback/Favorite Gentlemen, 2009), the Atlanta band’s second album.

My Morning Jacket — co-produced Evil Urges (ATO Records, 2008) with Jim James, and co-produced the single “I’m Amazed” and “Touch Me I’m Going To Scream Pt. 2.”

The Strokes — recorded and co-produced Angles (RCA/Sony, 2011) with the band.

Alanis Morissette — primary producer on Havoc And Bright Lights (2012), where he also played keyboards and drum programming on multiple tracks, demonstrating his hands-on studio involvement beyond pure engineering.

His work with Morrissey across three studio albums (World Peace Is None Of Your Business, 2014; Low In High School, 2017; I Am Not A Dog On A Chain, 2020) and a covers album (California Son, 2019) represents a significant sustained relationship. The Harvest/BMG-era Morrissey band on those records included Boz Boorer, Jesse Tobias, Gustavo Manzur, Solomon Walker, and Matt Walker.

Spoon (They Want My Soul, Loma Vista, 2014) and Broken Social Scene (Hug Of Thunder, Arts & Crafts, 2017) are among the other notable credits, as is Torres (Apocalipstick, Secretly Canadian, 2017) — one of the more unexpected pairings, given that artist’s post-punk intensity.
