Jazzie B

Producer, DJ, sound system operator. London, UK. Discogs ID: 52584. Real name: Beresford Romeo.

Founder of Soul II Soul, the London sound system / collective that became one of the most successful UK music acts of the late 1980s. Co-produced both Soul II Soul albums with Nellee Hooper — Club Classics Vol. One (1989) and Vol. II (1990 – A New Decade) (1990). “Keep On Movin’” (UK #5, US #11) and “Back To Life (However Do You Want Me)” (UK #1) were both from Club Classics Vol. One.

Post-Vol. II Trajectory and Hooper Split

Hooper’s departure

Nellee Hooper departed Soul II Soul before the recording of Volume III: Just Right (1992). Vol. III was produced solely by Jazzie B; Hooper receives no production credit on that album or on any subsequent Soul II Soul release. Time for Change (1997) credits likewise show no Hooper involvement — its production was handled by Jazzie B, with engineering by Lee Hamblin and Eugene ‘Engenius’ Ellis. (Discogs — Time for Change)

Wikipedia internal conflict resolved (likely error): The Soul II Soul Wikipedia article contains two contradictory entries for Hooper with no citations for either: the Former members section lists him as “producer (1988–1992)”, while the Touring and session musicians section lists him as “producer, programming (1988–1997)“. Neither entry carries a footnote or source. Given the absence of any Hooper credit on Vol. III, Vol. V, or Time for Change, the 1997 date in the touring/session table appears to be a Wikipedia data error — most likely copied or entered without a source — rather than evidence of continued involvement. The 1992 date in the Former members section aligns with all other available evidence. (Wikipedia — Soul II Soul, as of 2026-07-06)

No primary source interview — from either Hooper or Jazzie B — has been located that directly explains the circumstances or gives a stated reason for the split.

No documented post-1990 Jazzie B / Hooper collaboration

No released recording, production credit, or interview reference to a post-Vol. II collaboration between Jazzie B and Nellee Hooper has been located. After leaving Soul II Soul’s core production team, Hooper built a separate career: Massive Attack (Blue Lines, 1991), Björk (Debut, 1993), Sinéad O’Connor, Madonna, U2, and the Romeo + Juliet film soundtrack (1996, co-produced with Craig Armstrong and Marius de Vries). None of those projects involved Jazzie B. (Wikipedia — Nellee Hooper, as of 2026-07-02)

Soul II Soul without Hooper: Vol. III–V

  • Volume III: Just Right (1992) — produced by Jazzie B without Hooper; introduced male vocalists (Rick Clark, Kofi, Richie Stephens). Critically received as weaker than the first two albums, with Hooper’s absence widely cited as a factor. (Wikipedia — Soul II Soul, as of 2026-07-02)
  • Volume IV: The Classic Singles 88–93 (1993) — compilation.
  • Volume V: Believe (July 1995, Virgin) — Caron Wheeler returned for “Take Me Higher”; UK Top 40 singles “Love Enuff” and “I Care” (vocalists Penny Ford and Charlotte Kelly). (musicianguide.com)
  • Time for Change (September 1997, Island) — final 1990s studio album; label shifted from 10/Virgin to Island.

Jazzie B’s outside production work (early–mid 1990s)

In March 1991 Jazzie B launched the Funki Dreds label, signing Lady Levi and Kofi. Outside Soul II Soul he produced and co-wrote for James Brown (including “Just Do It”, “Watch Me”, “Show Me Your Friends”), Teena Marie, Cheryl Lynn, The Jones Girls, Maxi Priest, The Chimes, Kym Mazelle, and later Nas, Destiny’s Child, Ziggy Marley, and his wife Efua Baker. He also hosted a show on Kiss FM during this period. Exact release years for individual external credits are not confirmed from available sources. (Wikipedia — Jazzie B, as of 2026-07-02)
