Ned Oldham

American musician and singer-songwriter based in Louisville, Kentucky. Discogs artist ID: 254227. Brother of Will Oldham and Paul Oldham; also brother-in-law of Amber Oldham; son of Joanne Oldham and Joe Oldham. Husband of Jennie Oldham. His primary alias is Captain Anomoanon (Discogs artist ID: 475153). Online presence at nedoldham.blogspot.com and Bandcamp.

Overview

Ned Oldham occupies a distinctive position in the extended Palace Records / Bonnie “Prince” Billy constellation as the sibling most continuously active as a bandleader in his own right. While Will Oldham built an internationally recognized career and Paul Oldham became the family’s primary studio engineer and producer, Ned led The Anomoanon — a Louisville folk-rock group — across a full arc of releases from 1997 to 2004. His work ranges from nursery rhymes and traditional folk material to original Americana songwriting, with a catalog that touches Palace Records, Temporary Residence Limited, and Box Tree Records.

Beyond the Anomoanon, Ned has credits spanning the early Louisville underground (Maurice (51), a pre-Slint / pre-Squirrel Bait metal band in the mid-1980s) through Will’s Palace-era records and into the 2010s and beyond, most recently contributing bass to the 2026 Bonnie “Prince” Billy album We Are Together Again.

Early Louisville — Maurice (51)

Before the Palace Records era, Ned was a founding member of Maurice (51) (Discogs ID: 4252570), a heavy/aggressive metal band started by Britt Walford, Brian McMahan, and Ned Oldham, with Sean Garrison on lead vocals. Ned was replaced by Mike Bucayu in the band’s early evolution; David Pajo also cycled through the lineup (replacing McMahan). The band toured with Black Flag in 1985 and eventually split into Squirrel Bait and Slint. Maurice (51) released one known Discogs entry: The First Shall Be Last (2011, Blazebirth Records, digital), which preserves early recordings with the Pajo/Bucayu lineup. The band connects Ned directly to the formative Louisville underground that produced Slint, Squirrel Bait, and the early Palace circle.

The Viva Last Blues session (recorded with Steve Albini) is the deepest studio engagement: Ned plays bass, slide guitar, and additional vocals alongside Bryan Rich (lead guitar), Jason Loewenstein (drums), and Liam Hayes (piano/organ). Arise Therefore (also Albini, with David Grubbs on piano/organ) credits Ned for bass, effected guitar, and additional singing. On Ease Down The Road (2001), the Bonnie “Prince” Billy record that marks the transition into the mature BPB sound, Ned shares credits with David Pajo, Matt Sweeney, Catherine Irwin, Todd Brashear, and others.

He also photographed the Little Joya EP (1997, Drag City) and contributed “Players/writers/recorders” to the compilation Lost Blues And Other Songs.

The Anomoanon (1997–2004)

The core of Ned’s recorded output. See The Anomoanon for the full discography. Ned functions as lead vocalist, primary songwriter, guitarist, harmonica player, and occasional bassist, with Paul Oldham producing and engineering throughout. The band’s lineup stabilizes around Ned, Aram Stith (lead guitar), Willy MacLean (bass), and Jack Carneal (drums), with Jason Stith on early keyboards and David Heumann joining from 2002.

On the debut 7” (“Hello My,” Palace Records cat. PR14, 1997), Ned is credited as songwriter and performer alongside Aram Stith, Jack Carneal, Jason Stith, Willy MacLean, and Paul Oldham. By The Derby Ram (2004), Ned has expanded roles: vocals, acoustic and electric guitar, bass, harmonica, piano, toms, recording engineer, mixer, photographer (colored dot arrangement). He also takes sole “Music By” credit on eleven of twelve tracks on The Derby Ram — a traditional folk record — while Aram writes the music for “Little Birdy.”

The Envoi Villon EP (Galaxia, 2002) is the most unusual item: four tracks setting poems by François Villon to music by Ned Oldham, credited to “Anomoanon” and billed as single-sided etched vinyl, with Paul co-producing and Jason Stith (synths/piano) and Mike Portera (guitar) as guests.

Captain Anomoanon

Ned’s alias “Captain Anomoanon” appears on the Bonnie “Prince” Billy EP Sings Agnes, Queen Of Sorrow (2004, Drag City / Palace Records, cat. PR30 / DC278CD). He is credited as “Performer” on tracks 1 (“Agnes”) and 3 (“Blokbuster”) alongside a large Nashville session cast that includes Jack Carneal, William Tyler, Tony Crow, Stuart Duncan, and others, with Mark Nevers recording. The alias is used here rather than his given name, distinguishing his role as an Anomoanon-identified participant in a Nashville-style BPB recording.

On Discogs, Captain Anomoanon (artist ID: 475153) is listed as an alias of Ned Oldham (254227).

Old Calf

Old Calf (Discogs ID: 2924877) is a folk group with members Brian Caputo, Matty Metcalfe, Michael Clem, and Ned Oldham. Their sole release in the DB is Borrow A Horse (2011, No Quarter, cat. NOQ 027-2; CD). The record is rooted in English and American folk tradition: most tracks are Traditional (with Ned writing music on tracks 1, 3–11), with one track drawing on a Shirley and Dolly Collins arrangement.

Ned plays guitar, harmonica, and vocals; Michael Clem covers bass, vocals, mandola, and trombone; Matty Metcalfe plays keyboards, accordion, guitar, vocals, bouzouki, and whistle; Brian Caputo handles percussion, vocals, and glockenspiel. David Heumann guests on guitar on track 6 (“Far From Home”), and Sarah White contributes harmony vocals on two tracks. Paul Oldham masters. Engineered by Rob Evans (2); produced by Old Calf and Rob Evans.

Oldham Brothers

Oldham Brothers (Discogs ID: 774349) lists Ned, Will Oldham, and Paul Oldham as members. No releases appear in the Discogs database under this artist ID — it may reference informal or archival recordings not formally cataloged, or the entity exists for credit disambiguation.

Later Credits (2011–2026)

After the Anomoanon went dormant, Ned’s Discogs credits are sparse but continuous:

The Spiderland reissue (Touch and Go, 2014) credit is minor but symbolic — Ned contributed a photograph to the booklet of the landmark Slint album, linking him back to the Louisville underground of his youth (Brian McMahan and Britt Walford of Slint were also in Maurice (51) with him). The 2026 Bonnie “Prince” Billy LP We Are Together Again (No Quarter) is Ned’s most recent traced credit, playing bass alongside a large ensemble including Catherine Irwin, Eamon O’Leary, Nuala Kennedy, and others, with Will producing alongside Jim Marlowe.

He is also credited as a featured performer on:

  • Either She Or Me (1999, Domino) — featuring credit
  • Villa Claustrophobia (2002, Alien8 Recordings, Tanakh) — featuring credit alongside David Lowery, Jessica Billey, and Mick Turner
  • Long Live The Well-Doer (2004, Box Tree Records) — “Also Played / Sung” credit
