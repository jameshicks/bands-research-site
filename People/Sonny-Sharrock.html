Aug 15, 20267 min read

Sonny Sharrock

Discogs ID: 174748 Real name: Warren Harding Sharrock Also known as: Sunny Sharrock (name variation on Discogs), Warren “Sonny” Sharrock Born: August 27, 1940 — Ossining, NY Died: May 25, 1994 — Ossining, NY

Avant-garde guitarist; one of the first Americans to apply free jazz principles to the electric guitar. Career spans late-1960s sideman work through a mid-career hiatus to a resurgent final decade that yielded his best-regarded recordings. His sound — sheets of feedback, slashing dissonance, occasional melody buried in noise — influenced noise rock and no wave as much as jazz. Discogs lists him as “Sonny Sharrock”; “Sunny Sharrock” is a name variation used on some early credits.

1960s — Free Jazz Sideman

Sharrock came up in the New York free jazz scene. His earliest documented Discogs credit is Tauhid (1967, Impulse!) with Pharoah Sanders — produced by Bob Thiele, with Dave Burrell on piano, Henry Grimes on bass, Roger Blank on drums, and Rudy Van Gelder engineering at Van Gelder Studio. This was one of the first jazz records to feature electric guitar in an unambiguously free context.

He spent extended time in Herbie Mann’s fusion-oriented band, appearing on multiple Atlantic releases. The Mann connection produced the widely circulated Memphis Underground (1969, Atlantic), with Roy Ayers on vibraphone, Larry Coryell on guitar, Reggie Young on guitar, and the Memphis session rhythm section (Gene Chrisman/drums, Tommy Cogbill and Mike Leech/bass, Bobby Wood/keys, Bobby Emmons/organ). Produced by Tom Dowd.

His debut as leader, Black Woman (1969, Vortex Records (2)), is a defining document of free jazz guitar. Produced by Herbie Mann:

1970 — French Sessions and Wayne Shorter

During 1970 Sharrock recorded in France, yielding Monkey-Pockie-Boo (BYG Records). Sharrock played guitar, slide whistle, and vocals. Linda Sharrock again on vocals and sleeve notes, with Beb Guerin (bass) and Jacques Thollot (drums). Produced by Jean Georgakarakos and Jean-Luc Young; executive produced by Claude Delcloo.

Also in 1970: Super Nova (Wayne Shorter, Blue Note) — an important early Sharrock sideman credit alongside John McLaughlin, Chick Corea, Jack DeJohnette, Airto Moreira, and Miroslav Vitous. Produced by Duke Pearson.

He also appeared on Wayne Shorter’s The Inspiration I Feel (Atlantic, 1968). These Blue Note/Atlantic appearances with Shorter are among the few major-label jazz records of the era to feature a free improvising guitarist.

Mid-1970s — Paradise and Hiatus

Paradise (1975, ATCO Records) was Sharrock’s last solo record before a six-year silence. A co-credit with Linda Sharrock (credited as “Sonny & Linda Sharrock”). Produced by Ilhan Mimaroglu. Engineered by Phil Ramone. Notable for its commercial-leaning arrangements (keys, timpani, session players) relative to the raw earlier work.

Sharrock then largely disappeared from recorded music for approximately six years. The couple divorced in 1978.

1981 — Return via Material and Celluloid

Bill Laswell brought Sharrock back into circulation through Material’s Memory Serves (1981, Celluloid Records). Sharrock plays guitar alongside a sprawling cast.

This began a partnership with Laswell that defined the rest of his career.

1986 — Solo Comeback and Last Exit

Guitar (1986, Enemy Records, catalog EMY 102) — Sharrock’s solo comeback album. Entirely solo guitar, no overdubs. Produced by Bill Laswell and Sharrock; recorded and mixed by Mike Krowiak. The stripped-to-the-bone format — just Sharrock’s electric guitar — made his method unmistakable: long melodic lines dissolving into abstract noise, blues vocabulary erupting through sheets of feedback.

The same year he co-founded Last Exit (Discogs ID 265435) — described as an “American free jazz supergroup, active 1986–1994.” Members:

The Last Exit self-titled LP (1986) was mixed by Robert Musso, who became a recurring presence in Sharrock’s production credits. Last Exit released studio and live material through 1993 (Headfirst Into The Flames – Live In Europe), with Sharrock dying in 1994 shortly after the group’s final documented period. See Last Exit for full discography.

1987–1990 — Sonny Sharrock Band

Under the Sonny Sharrock Band name (Discogs ID 282987), Sharrock released Seize The Rainbow (1987, Enemy Records) — his most ensemble-oriented record of the late 1980s.

Sharrock also appeared in this period on Machine Gun (MU Records, 1988) — a reference to the Peter Brötzmann classic — as a guest guitarist. He continued appearing on recordings through 1990 (Last Exit’s Köln, Highlife; miscellaneous improv sessions). Also notable: Improvised Music New York 1981 (MuWorks Records, released 1992) — live free improvisation set.

1991 — Ask The Ages (Axiom)

Widely considered Sharrock’s masterpiece. Ask The Ages (1991, Axiom, catalog 422-848 957-2) reunites him with Pharoah Sanders (tenor and soprano saxophones) alongside Elvin Jones (drums) and Charnett Moffett (bass). Produced by Bill Laswell and Sonny Sharrock.

Also in 1991: Faith Moves (CMP Records) — a solo guitar recording in a quieter register, suggesting the range Sharrock continued to explore in his final years.

1973 Note — Pharoah Sanders Sessions (Out of Sequence)

Pharoah Sanders continued to use Sharrock through the early 1970s: Izipho Zam (My Gifts) (Strata-East, 1973) featured Sharrock (credited as “Sonny Sherrock” — a misspelling), with Lonnie Liston Smith on piano, Leon Thomas on vocals, Cecil McBee on bass, Sonny Fortune on alto sax. This placed him in the Strata-East orbit alongside key figures of the Black spiritual jazz movement.

1994 — Space Ghost: Coast To Coast and Death

Sharrock composed and performed the theme for Cartoon Network’s Space Ghost: Coast To Coast (1994), giving his guitar playing an unexpected mass-cultural coda. He died May 25, 1994, in Ossining, NY — the same city where he was born — at age 53, before the show’s full run aired.
