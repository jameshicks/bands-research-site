Mark Naftalin

American keyboardist. Born Mark H. Naftalin, 2 August 1944. Discogs artist ID: 476175.

Organist and pianist best known as a core member of The Paul Butterfield Blues Band across their entire Elektra recording run (1965–1971). After the Blues Band dissolved, he became a prolific Chicago-circuit session player, worked in the Bay Area blues scene, produced archival blues compilations, and operated a radio program called the Blues Power Hour.

Early Career and Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1965–1971)

Naftalin appears on all seven studio and live albums the Blues Band released on Elektra Records. His credited instruments shift across the run — organ and piano on the early records, expanding to full keyboards by the brass-augmented later period.

Name Variations and Pseudonyms

Naftalin used several aliases across his career, all documented in Discogs name variations:

  • Naffy Markham — credited on all pressings of In My Own Dream (1968, Elektra) and one pressing of Golden Butter; the most persistent stage name
  • Nafty Markham — credited on Moogie Woogie (1970, Chess) sleeve notes and some Golden Butter pressings
  • Mark “Boogie” Naftalin — credited on Mother Earth (4)-related releases (Full Time Woman, Fantasy, 1970)
  • Mark Doe — used on Casting Pearls (1973, Verve) by the Mill Valley Bunch, where the entire roster used “Doe” surnames for anonymity
  • M. Naftalin, Marc Naftalin, Naftalin, Mark H Naftalin — variant spellings on various releases

Groups and Ensembles

In addition to the Butterfield band, Naftalin was a member of four groups in Discogs:

Mother Earth (4)

Austin/San Francisco band led by vocalist Tracy Nelson. Naftalin co-produced their self-titled 1968 Mercury album alongside Dan Healy and the band itself, with Barry Goldberg as executive producer. He also played on Full Time Woman (Fantasy, 1970).

The Zeet Band

Experimental collective featuring Mike Bloomfield, Paul Beaver (Moog pioneer), and Norman Dayron. Released Moogie Woogie (Chess, 1970) — electronic boogie and blues composed on Moog synthesizers. Naftalin is credited as performer, assistant producer, and sleeve notes writer (as “Nafty Markham”).

Mill Valley Bunch

Large Bay Area collective centered around Mike Bloomfield and Nick Gravenites. Released Casting Pearls (Verve, 1973), on which all performers used “Doe” pseudonyms. Naftalin credited as “Mark Doe” for piano and organ. The 2003 Comet Records reissue Casting Pearls… restored full real-name credits and added primary billing to Naftalin alongside Bloomfield, Gravenites, Barry Goldberg, Michael Shrieve, Spencer Dryden, and others.

The Zeet Band / Record Plant session

A 2015 archival release The Record Plant ‘73 (Shady Grove) credits Naftalin alongside Mike Bloomfield as co-primary artist — piano and vocals — from a 1973 session.

Solo Releases

Naftalin had limited primary-credited solo output on Discogs:

  • “Honest And True (Theme From The Blues Power Hour)” / “Treehouse Blues” (7”, 1988, Sounds Like A Winner Productions, cat. GL 2814) — the only confirmed solo single. The A-side is the theme for his radio program.

No solo albums appear in Discogs under his name.

  • Producer — Just Like A Devil by Ron Thompson (Winner, 1990)

  • Written-By — “Got A Mind To Give Up Living” credited to Naftalin on The Third Mind (Yep Roc, 2020)

Blues Power Hour

Naftalin’s 1988 solo single “Honest And True” is subtitled “Theme From The Blues Power Hour,” indicating he hosted or was closely associated with a radio program of that name. No further Discogs documentation of the program exists; the single was self-released on his own Sounds Like A Winner Productions label.
