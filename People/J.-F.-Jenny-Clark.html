J.-F. Jenny-Clark

Aug 12, 20267 min read

J.-F. Jenny-Clark

Full name: Jean-François Jenny-Clark
Born: 12 July 1944, Toulouse, France
Died: 6 October 1998, Paris, France
Instrument: Double bass / contrabass
Discogs ID: 256667

French double bassist, widely regarded as one of the most important bass players in European jazz. Active from the mid-1960s until his death, he ranged across free jazz, mainstream modern jazz, and contemporary classical music, appearing on well over 80 releases.

Biography

Early Career and International Connections (1960s)

Jenny-Clark came of age during the ferment of mid-1960s European free jazz. One of his earliest significant credits is Don Cherry’s Symphony For Improvisers (Blue Note, 1967), where he shared double bass duties with Henry Grimes alongside Pharoah Sanders and Gato Barbieri — a document of Cherry’s free-ranging European quintet. He also provided rhythm-section duties alongside drummer Aldo Romano for Cherry’s European sessions of the period.

He recorded with Steve Lacy and appeared on François Tusques’s Le Nouveau Jazz (1967) in the context of the French avant-garde. His work spanned film scores as well — he appears uncredited on Karlheinz Stockhausen’s Aus Den Sieben Tagen (Harmonia Mundi, 1969) and on Luciano Berio’s Laborintus 2 (Harmonia Mundi, 1969).

Contemporary Classical Work — Ensemble Musique Vivante

As a member of Diego Masson’s Ensemble Musique Vivante, Jenny-Clark was central to the interpretation of contemporary music by composers including John Cage, Luciano Berio, Mauricio Kagel, Karlheinz Stockhausen, Pierre Boulez, and Vinko Globokar. This placed him at the intersection of jazz improvisation and the European new-music avant-garde.

ECM and International Sessions (Late 1970s–Early 1980s)

Jenny-Clark made his most internationally visible appearances during the ECM era. Key credits:

  • Enrico Rava Quartet (ECM, 1978) — with Enrico Rava (trumpet), Roswell Rudd (trombone), Aldo Romano (drums); produced by Manfred Eicher.
  • Le Voyage (ECM, 1979) — another ECM appearance in this period.
  • Around 6 (ECM, 1980) — further ECM work.
  • Jasper van’t Hof’s Pork Pie — he was a member of this group (with Charlie Mariano, ca. 1975), documenting his involvement in the pan-European post-bop circuit.

He performed concerts with Keith Jarrett around 1970.

The Albert Mangelsdorff Collaboration (1981–1987)

A defining strand of his career was the duo and ensemble work with German trombonist Albert Mangelsdorff. The trio with Mangelsdorff and drummer Ronald Shannon Jackson recorded Albert Live In Montreux! (MPS Records, 1981). Together with Mangelsdorff he co-led a German-French jazz ensemble from approximately 1984 to 1987, documented on Session In Paris, Vol. 2 “Colour Of Dream” (Paddle Wheel, 1980) and other recordings.

The Kühn–Humair Trio (1985–1998)

From 1985 until his death, Jenny-Clark worked primarily in a celebrated trio with German pianist Joachim Kühn and Swiss drummer Daniel Humair — the group that most defines his mature period. Discogs credits them together on at least six releases, including:

  • Unison (CMP Records, 1987) — with Christof Lauer (saxophone); Jenny-Clark as primary artist; produced by Walter Quintus / Kurt Renker.
  • 9 - 11 pm Town Hall (Label Bleu, 1988) — a larger-ensemble live recording featuring also Michel Portal, Martial Solal, and Marc Ducret.

The trio was active under the Discogs group name J.-F. Jenny-Clark (ID: 2671809).

Later Projects and the Italian Connection (Late 1980s–1990s)

Jenny-Clark was a regular collaborator on the Italian Soul Note label — appearing notably on Claudio Fasoli and Kenny Wheeler records:

  • Welcome (Soul Note, 1987) — Claudio Fasoli / Kenny Wheeler / Jenny-Clark / Daniel Humair quartet; compositions all by Fasoli.
  • Land (Soul Note / Innowo, 1989) — same core group.

He also led or co-led several dates:

  • En Direct D’Antibes (Carlyne Music, 1980) — as primary artist in the Quintet René Urtreger with Jean-Louis Chautemps, Aldo Romano, Eric Le Lann.
  • A Winter’s Tale (Nato, 1993) — co-led trio with pianist Tony Hymas and drummer Jacques Thollot; Jenny-Clark co-wrote track 7.
  • The Unique Concert (JMS, 2020 release, rec. earlier) — with Didier Lockwood, Gordon Beck, Allan Holdsworth, Aldo Romano.
  • Le Vivre (Minuit Regards, 1994) — Jenny-Clark as primary artist.
  • Autumn in Paris (Ninety-One, 1992) — with Masahiko Satoh and Masahiko Togashi.

He also contributed bass (uncredited) to Guesch Patti’s Men’s Land (Label Bleu, 1987) and appeared on Michel Petrucciani’s debut Michel Petrucciani (Owl Records, 1981).
