Paul Shaffer

Canadian musician, bandleader, arranger, and television personality. Born Paul Allen Wood Shaffer, 28 November 1949, Thunder Bay, Ontario. Discogs artist ID: 328195.

Keyboardist, arranger, and co-writer who moved from SNL staff to become the defining television bandleader of the late-twentieth century as frontman of The World’s Most Dangerous Band on Late Night and Late Show with David Letterman (1982–2015). Before that, a prolific session player and musical director in New York — most significantly as co-originator and musical director of The Blues Brothers Band during its SNL years and on its first Atlantic releases.

Early Career (1968–1977)

Shaffer’s recorded debut is as producer on a 1968 Canadian single, It’s Link Up Time With The Missing Links (Not On Label). His first major credit outside Canada came in 1973 as keyboardist on the Godspell motion picture soundtrack (Bell Records) — one of Broadway’s marquee pop-gospel events of that era. The same year he played on Consider The Heart (1973, Smile Records) in Canada.

By 1974 he was back on Broadway-adjacent territory: The Magic Show soundtrack (Bell Records, 1974). His arranging career began in earnest in 1975–76, with credits on A&M, RCA Victor, Midland International, and Kirshner releases. He co-produced (with Jay Siegel, exec-produced by Don Kirshner) the Good-Bye Pop 1952–1976 album (Epic, 1975).

A Year At The Top (1977)

Shaffer’s first headline credit as a primary artist was the soundtrack to the CBS television series A Year At The Top (Casablanca, 1977), co-credited to him and actor Greg Evigan. He co-produced and appeared on selected tracks alongside Jay Siegel (Jeff Barry produced others). The show was a Don Kirshner production; Shaffer’s presence in Kirshner’s orbit during 1975–77 appears to be a recurring connection.

He also co-produced and released She’s A Rebel (Casablanca, 1977) as a primary artist that same year.

Saturday Night Live Years (1975–1980)

Shaffer was staff keyboardist and special material writer for Saturday Night Live from its debut season. He is credited as Written-By [Special Musical Material] on the 1976 Arista SNL album, which also credits Howard Shore as Music Director. Shore held the MD title; Shaffer was the show’s arranger and in-house musical utility — the person who got the comedy pieces to work musically from behind the keys. Dan Aykroyd, Chevy Chase, Lorne Michaels, and other cast members all appear as co-writers on the same LP.

A key SNL output was Shaffer writing and arranging “Chevy’s Girls” (1976, Arista).

The Blues Brothers (1978–1981)

The most significant pre-Letterman chapter. When Dan Aykroyd and John Belushi developed the Blues Brothers as an SNL musical act, Shaffer became their Musical Director — the person who built the band and shaped the sound. He is credited on the Blues Brothers’ first Atlantic Records releases:

On Briefcase Full Of Blues he plays under the alias “Paul ‘The Shiv’ Shaffer”, alongside Donald “Duck” Dunn (bass), Steve Jordan (drums), Steve Cropper and Matt “Guitar” Murphy (guitars), Tom Malone, Lou Marini, Tom Scott, and Alan Rubin in the horns section — producer Bob Tischler. On Made In America he adds a Producer credit alongside Tischler. The Very Best Of The Blues Brothers (1995) retrospectively credits him as Arranged By [Background Vocals], Co-producer, Keyboards, and Musical Director.

Shaffer departed the Blues Brothers when he moved to the Letterman show in 1982; Murphy Dunne (already present on Made In America) continued as pianist.

Soul Man (1978)

Also from the Aykroyd/Belushi orbit: the Soul Man film soundtrack (Atlantic, 1978), where Shaffer is credited as Directed By [Musical].

“It’s Raining Men” deserves particular note: Shaffer co-wrote (with Paul Jabara) what became a disco/dance standard for The Weather Girls (1982, CBS Netherlands). The song has generated dozens of pressings and re-issues through to 2024; it is one of the most commercially durable things he ever wrote.

He also arranged Joan Armatrading’s Take Me Home (Casablanca, 1979) and One Man Ain’t Enough (A&M, 1975), arranged and conducted John Denver’s Ride A Wild Horse (Midland International, 1976), and appeared on Cher’s Take Me Home (conductor credit), and Joan Armatrading’s Me Myself I.

Gilda Radner — Live From New York (1979)

Shaffer produced and arranged the comedy album Live From New York for SNL castmate Gilda Radner (Warner Bros., 1979), alongside Jerry Wexler (executive producer) and Lorne Michaels (director/exec). He is credited as Piano, Drums, Arranged By [Music And Vocals], Producer. Howard Shore also appears as saxophone player and co-music director.

The Legendary Paul Butterfield Rides Again (1986)

Shaffer played keyboards on Paul Butterfield’s final studio album (Amherst Records, 1986). The session also brought in Anton Fig on drums, Blondie Chaplin on guitar and vocals, and Mark Sidgwick on bass, with Thom Panunzio producing. This is one of the clearest cases of the Letterman band inner circle playing as a unit on an outside project.

Late Night / Late Show with David Letterman (1982–2015)

Shaffer joined Letterman as bandleader when Late Night with David Letterman launched on NBC in 1982, and remained through Late Show on CBS until Letterman’s retirement in May 2015 — 33 years. The band was known first as The World’s Most Dangerous Band, then as The CBS Orchestra on CBS.

Late Night (Capitol, 1989) was co-produced by Shaffer, Anton Fig, Will Lee, and Sid McGinnis — the core Letterman band rhythm section. Coast To Coast is a compilation (exec-produced by Shaffer and Tim Carr).

The World’s Most Dangerous Party (1993)

Shaffer released this album (SBK Records) as Paul Shaffer & The Party Boys Of Rock ‘N’ Roll (Discogs artist ID: 3948238). Produced, engineered, and mixed by Todd Rundgren. The regular Letterman band played: Anton Fig (drums/percussion), Will Lee (bass), Sid McGinnis (guitar), Shaffer (keyboards). Featured Party Guests on vocals span a remarkable range — see full list below. Mastered by Greg Calbi.

Featured Party Guests (vocal credits): Aaron Neville, Bill Murray, Bootsy Collins, Dana Carvey, David Letterman, David Sanborn, Derek Smalls (Harry Shearer), Eartha Kitt, George Clinton, Harry Shearer, James Coburn, Jennifer Finch, Joan Jett, John Popper, Jon Lovitz, Judith Owen, Leonard Cohen, LL Cool J (uncredited), Lou Reed, Martin Short, Mike Myers, Phil Spector, Phoebe Snow, Richard Belzer, Ringo Starr, Suzi Gardner, Tony Bennett, Charles Koppelman.

The brief also notes Dana Carvey, Jon Lovitz, and Mike Myers as specific “Featured Party Guests” — all SNL alumni, all appearing here in Shaffer’s orbit.
