Ethan Buckler

American bassist and guitarist. Louisville, Kentucky. Discogs ID: 377812.

Founding member of both Slint and King Kong (3). Played bass on Tweez (1989), Slint’s debut, then departed before Spiderland was recorded. Went on to lead King Kong (3) — a long-running Louisville project with shifting membership — across eight releases on Drag City and smaller labels, spanning 1989 to 2007.

Biography

Louisville Origins and Slint (1986–1989)

Buckler came up in the same Louisville, Kentucky teenage underground that produced Slint and Squirrel Bait. He was a founding member of Slint alongside Brian McMahan (guitar, vocals), David Pajo (guitar), and Britt Walford (drums) — all four were teenagers when the band formed around 1986.

His only studio appearance with Slint is Tweez (1989, Jennifer Hartman Records), recorded in 1988 and engineered by Steve Albini under the pseudonym “Some Fuckin’ Derd Niffer.” Buckler is credited with bass; the album is one of the key early documents of the Louisville post-hardcore scene. Shortly after Tweez he departed Slint, and was replaced by Todd Brashear for the Spiderland sessions.

The circumstances of his departure from Slint are not documented in Discogs.

King Kong (3) — Formation and Early Lineup (1988–1990)

Running in parallel with his time in Slint, Buckler founded King Kong (3) in Louisville in 1988. The debut 7” Movie Star (1989, self-released) was recorded with the Slint social orbit: McMahan (guitar, vocals), Pajo (drums, guitar), and Walford (drums, vocals) — giving King Kong (3) its origins as essentially a side-project of the entire early Slint lineup. Buckler plays bass, guitar, and sings.

The 1990 EP Bring It On (Trash Flow Records) reflects a personnel shift to a trio of Buckler, Darren Rappa (bass), and Richard Schuler (drums), with engineer Howie Gano.

Transition: Guitar and Songwriting (1991–1993)

By Old Man On The Bridge (1991, Homestead Records) Buckler had moved to guitar and vocals, with Rappa on bass and Schuler on drums. Notably, McMahan and Todd Brashear (Buckler’s own Slint replacement) both appear as engineers, and Pajo contributes congas. Buckler is credited as songwriter for all eight tracks. This release shows the band in transition — the Slint alumnus connection is maintained through personnel, though the core of the live band was now Buckler-Rappa-Schuler.

Funny Farm (1993, Drag City) is the most densely staffed King Kong (3) release: Buckler plays bass, harmonica, guitar, keyboards, and sings, with Walford returning on keyboards, Brad Wood on keyboards/saxophone/recorded by, and guest guitar from David Grubbs. Dan Koretzky (Drag City co-founder) appears in a choir credit. Amy George (later Amy Partin Ritchie) joins on vocals — she will remain a fixture in the band for decades.

Drag City Continuity (1995–2002)

Me Hungry (1995, Drag City) marks another lineup consolidation: Willy MacLean on bass, Pajo returning on drums, Todd Hildreth on organ, and Amy Greenwood (same person as Amy George / Amy Partin Ritchie) on vocals. Buckler plays guitar and vocals. Recorded by Doug Easley and Davis McCain.

Kingdom Of Kong (1997, Drag City) stabilizes around MacLean (bass), Ray Rizzo (drums), Hildreth (organ), and Amy Greenwood (vocals) — the ensemble that would define the band’s later period. Buckler plays guitar and vocals.

The Big Bang (2002, Drag City) is produced by Neil Hagerty (of Royal Trux), with Mikael Jorgensen (later of Wilco) credited on Rhodes piano and engineering.

Later Period (2007)

Buncha Beans (2007, Drag City) — the most recent Discogs release — is recorded by Paul Oldham (Will Oldham’s brother, frequent Louisville-scene recordist). The lineup is Buckler, MacLean, Peter Townsend (drums), and Amy Partin Ritchie. This is the latest documented King Kong (3) release in Discogs.

Scene Context

Buckler sits at the founding layer of the Louisville post-hardcore / experimental underground. His dual role — as Slint’s original bassist and as King Kong (3)‘s primary creative engine — makes him one of the connective nodes between the Slint moment (1986–1989) and the longer tail of Louisville-affiliated releases on Drag City through the 2000s.

The first King Kong (3) 7” is in effect a Slint-lineup recording under a different name, a reminder of how permeable band identity was in this scene. The movement of McMahan, Pajo, Walford, and Brashear through King Kong (3) credits across different years shows the social network remained active long after Slint formally dissolved.
