Michael Anthony Keane

Musician, composer, producer, electronic artist. Liverpool (not Manchester — he formed the band in Toxteth, Liverpool). Principal and sole continuous member of Royal Family And The Poor from its formation in 1978 through all subsequent phases. Discogs artist ID: 533706.

Aliases / name variations: Mike Keane, Michael Keane, Michael A. Keane, M. Keane, The Project, Mike Windsor.

Overview

Keane co-founded Royal Family And The Poor in 1978 with Arthur McDonald, moving into a shared house in Toxteth, Liverpool. McDonald, an art graduate with deep interest in Situationism, introduced Keane to Raoul Vaneigem’s The Revolution of Everyday Life, which shaped the band’s early aesthetic. The original sound was built from a dismantled radio receiver wired to an analogue synthesizer and stereo gram, manipulated by magnets.

After the original duo dissolved (1981/82), he remained the sole continuous creative force through five distinct lineup configurations and label changes spanning Factory Records, his own Gaia Communications World Network imprint (destroyed by Red Rhino’s collapse at end of 1988, leading to personal bankruptcy), and a late-period solo phase on Boutique (2).

Keane formed the Legend Agency as a solo touring vehicle post-1981, touring with China Crisis (approx. 20 UK dates, May–June 1982). He briefly came under Stevo’s management (Some Bizzare, 1982–83) and recorded at Trident Studio, London, in January 1983 with Matt Johnson (The The) providing backing vocals on tracks including “I Love You,” “Moonfish Is Here,” and “Motherland.” He rejected Stevo’s major-label strategy, and these recordings were not released.

Following a severe heroin addiction and the Some Bizzare collapse, Keane undertook a walking pilgrimage from Winchester to Canterbury (late June – late August 1983), living rough. This experience preceded his return to Factory Records at Rob Gretton’s instigation in late 1983. Recording of Temple Of The 13th Tribe began at Strawberry Studios, Stockport, in February 1984 with Peter Hook producing.

After Red Rhino’s collapse (end of 1988) and his subsequent bankruptcy, Keane remained largely silent until 1995 when he resumed via a PC and extensive Gaia Communications website. Songs For The Children Of Baphomet (1999/2001 limited edition) received five stars from Melody Maker; praised by Uncut and The Wire. After North-West Soul (2004) he again fell silent.

Multi-instrumentalist throughout: vocals, guitar, keyboards, bass, drum programming, electronics, percussion. Also responsible for composition, lyrics, artwork, layout, and production on most RFATP releases from 1986 onwards.
