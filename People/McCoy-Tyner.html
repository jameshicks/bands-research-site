McCoy Tyner

American jazz pianist, composer, and bandleader. Born Alfred McCoy Tyner, December 11, 1938, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania; died March 6, 2020. Discogs artist ID: 10620.

Best known as pianist for The John Coltrane Quartet (1960–1965), one of the most influential rhythm sections in jazz history. After leaving Coltrane, recorded prolifically as a leader — first for Impulse! (1963–1965), then Blue Note (1967–1972), then Milestone from 1972 onward. By the 1990s and 2000s recorded for Impulse!, Blue Note again, and Telarc Jazz, sustaining an active concert and recording career until shortly before his death.

Biography

Philadelphia Origins and Early Career (1938–1960)

Alfred McCoy Tyner grew up in Philadelphia in a musical household. He adopted the name McCoy professionally. Early professional work included a stint with The Jazztet (the Art Farmer / Benny Golson group), documented on Meet The Jazztet (Argo, 1960), and appearances on sessions for Freddie Hubbard (Open Sesame, Blue Note, 1960) and Art Blakey (Goin’ Up, Blue Note, 1960).

The John Coltrane Quartet (1960–1965)

Tyner joined John Coltrane’s working group around 1960, forming the classic quartet with Jimmy Garrison (bass) and Elvin Jones (drums). This lineup is among the most documented and celebrated in jazz history, producing the majority of Coltrane’s Impulse! output.

Tyner also appeared on Coltrane’s earlier Atlantic recordings (My Favorite Things, Coltrane Jazz, Olé Coltrane, Coltrane Plays The Blues, Coltrane’s Sound, all 1960–1962), engineered at Atlantic by Tom Dowd and produced by Nesuhi Ertegun.

Tyner left the Coltrane group in late 1965 — by the Meditations sessions (1965, released 1966) the group was moving into freer territory with Pharoah Sanders and Rashied Ali, a direction Tyner did not pursue. He played on Ascension (June 1965) but not on the later free-period Coltrane records.

As a leader during the Coltrane period, Tyner recorded for Impulse! under his own name: Inception (1962), Nights Of Ballads & Blues (1963), Illumination! (1964, with Jimmy Garrison and Elvin Jones), Today And Tomorrow (1964), Live At Newport (1964), and McCoy Tyner Plays Ellington (1965).

After leaving Coltrane, Tyner signed to Blue Note and recorded a run of albums that are considered his definitive leader statements:

Rudy Van Gelder engineered the recording sessions for most of these albums. Duke Pearson produced the three later Blue Note albums (Time For Tyner, Expansions, Extensions); The Real McCoy was Alfred Lion’s production and Tender Moments was produced by Francis Wolff. Extensions was recorded in 1970 but not released until 1972.

Also during the Blue Note period: Asante (1974, Blue Note) — recorded after the Milestone contract had begun.

Milestone Jazzstars In Concert (1978) documented a touring package with Sonny Rollins, Ron Carter, and Elvin Jones.

CBS / Elektra / Denon (1981–1987)

A mid-career detour into major-label territory: La Leyenda De La Hora (CBS, 1981) and Looking Out (CBS, 1982), both produced by Tyner himself. Dimensions (Elektra Musician, 1984) also self-produced. Double Trios (Denon, 1986) for a Japanese label.

Return to Blue Note and Later Career (1985–2009)

Tyner returned to Blue Note for: It’s About Time (1985), Revelations (1989), Things Ain’t What They Used To Be (1990), Soliloquy (1992, solo piano, Japan), Manhattan Moods (1994).

Moved to Telarc Jazz in the late 1990s–2000s: McCoy Tyner And The Latin All-Stars (1999), Jazz Roots (2000), McCoy Tyner With Stanley Clarke And Al Foster (2000), Land Of Giants (2003), Illuminations (2004), Afro Blue (2007).

His own imprint McCoy Tyner Music (distributed via Half Note) produced Guitars (2008) and McCoy Tyner Solo - Live From San Francisco (2009).

Posthumous releases: Forces Of Nature: Live At Slugs’ (Blue Note, 2024), recorded 1970 with early Milestone-era personnel.

Groups

Tyner was a member of or credited under: The John Coltrane Quartet, The Jazztet, Art Blakey Quartet, Eric Dolphy Quartet, Eric Dolphy Quintet, McCoy Tyner Trio, McCoy Tyner Quartet, McCoy Tyner Quintet, McCoy Tyner Big Band, and various touring configurations.
