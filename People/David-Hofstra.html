David Hofstra

American bassist and tuba player, born May 21, 1953 in Leavenworth, Kansas. Discogs artist ID: 282724. Active in New York City from the late 1970s onward across punk, no wave, jazz, and avant-garde experimental contexts.

Overview

David Hofstra is a multi-instrumentalist specializing in electric bass, double bass, and tuba who became a mainstay of the downtown New York improvised and experimental music scene from the late 1970s through the 2010s. His earliest major credits place him at the edges of the no wave / punk convergence — bass on Lester Bangs’s sole single “Let It Blurt” / “Live” (1979, SPY Records) alongside Robert Quine, Jody Harris, and Jay Dee Daugherty, and on The Contortions’ debut Buy (ZE Records, 1979). He also appeared on The Waitresses’ debut LP Wasn’t Tomorrow Wonderful? (Polydor, 1982).

His primary orbit through the 1980s–2000s was the jazz and experimental wing of the downtown scene, where he was a working member of multiple bands simultaneously: Phillip Johnston’s Big Trouble, The New York Composers Orchestra, Microscopic Septet, and Metropolitan Klezmer, among others. He appeared on key records by Bobby Previte, Robin Holcomb, Wayne Horvitz, Bill Frisell, and John Zorn (including Zorn’s landmark Filmworks 1986–1990 and the Spillane-related The Bribe, 1998). His tuba work is credited on many of these same sessions — an unusually broad instrumental footprint for a sideman of his era.

He also maintained cross-genre work in klezmer, Irish folk (Luka Bloom’s Riverside, 1990), blues (Universal Blues, 1991), and avant-classical settings (Anthony Braxton’s Composition No. 19 (For 100 Tubas), 2011).

Name variations credited on record: Dave Hofstra, David C. Hofstra, David Hofsta, David Hostra, Hofstra.
