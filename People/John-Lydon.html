Singer and lyricist (born 31 January 1956 in London, UK).

Best known for his work as lead singer initially of the Sex Pistols (using the name Johnny Rotten), then secondly under his real name in Public image Limited (or “PIL” for short). His work with the Sex Pistols is recognised as being the lead UK band for the emerging late-1970’s punk scene, especially in the UK.

Continually throughout his career, has been very outspoken about his dislike for so-called authority figures or institutions; whether monarchy, political, or religious. Most of his tracks have featured his objections vehemently to these and other issues. When appearing with bandmates on British TV in the late 1970’s to early 1980’s, usually had little time for the presenters; often walking out halfway through any interview, due to being paraded as some kind of hate figure.

In the early 1980’s he was forced to leave the UK after repeated raids by the Police on anywhere he and band PIL tried to play. This unfair and unlawful intrusion by another state institution that at the time could get away with such malicious actions, meant moving to the US for the following 25 years, where he could live and work normally.

He continues to live in Los Angeles, USA. As of early 2010, currently re-touring with a reformed PIL.

Married to Nora Forster (6 November 1942 – 6 April 2023), stepfather to singer Ariane Forster (aka Ari-Up), and brother of Jimmy Lydon.

— Discogs artist profile, ID 40586

Overview

John Lydon (born 31 January 1956, London) rose to prominence as Johnny Rotten, the provocative frontman of the Sex Pistols, whose confrontational approach made them the defining band of Britain’s late-1970s punk explosion. After the Pistols dissolved he co-founded Public Image Ltd (PiL) under his real name, channelling post-punk, experimental, and new wave influences across a catalogue spanning five decades and over a thousand Discogs credits. His core collaborators throughout PiL’s run include Martin Atkins, Keith Levene, Bill Laswell, and Jah Wobble, with Virgin the dominant label across his career (32 releases, 1979–2023). Early-1980s police harassment of PiL performances led Lydon to relocate to the United States, where he lived for roughly 25 years; he remains based in Los Angeles.

Police Harassment and Departure from the UK (1980–1981)

The Discogs profile states Lydon was “forced to leave the UK after repeated raids by the Police on anywhere he and band PIL tried to play.” Web sources refine this picture: the documented incidents were primarily raids on his home at 45 Gunter Grove, Chelsea (on the Fulham border), where PiL also rehearsed and worked, rather than raids on concert venues per se. Two legal proceedings are confirmed.

Confirmed legal incidents

1. Firearms Act charge, February 1980 — London Police broke down the front door of Lydon’s Gunter Grove flat in the early morning and charged him under the Firearms Act for possession of a small pen-sized teargas canister. Lydon noted the device was legal in the United States. Outcome: six months’ conditional discharge. Lydon told The Face (November 1980): “They seemed very disappointed they didn’t find anything more. I really got the impression they thought they were going to discover an arms cache.” He also noted that officers had been conducting surveillance on the property for months before the raid. [Source: The Face interview, fodderstompf.com, November 1980 — https://www.fodderstompf.com/ARCHIVES/INTERVIEWS/face80.html]

2. Dublin arrest, 6 October 1980 — Horse & Tram pub, Dublin Lydon was arrested by the Garda after a confrontation at the Horse & Tram pub in Dublin (the barman refused to serve him). He was held overnight in Mountjoy Prison, where he claimed warders hosed him down in the yard as a deliberate humiliation. The case came to trial roughly three months later; it was dismissed when the two prosecution witnesses failed to appear, but Lydon was required to donate £100 to a poor box. He was on bail at the time of the November 1980 The Face interview. The incident inspired the song “Francis Massacre” on The Flowers of Romance (1981) and kept Lydon off Irish stages for over 20 years. [Sources: ultimateclassicrock.com — https://ultimateclassicrock.com/john-lydon-arrested/; The Face interview, fodderstompf.com, November 1980 — https://www.fodderstompf.com/ARCHIVES/INTERVIEWS/face80.html]

Home raids (undated within the period)

In a 1992 interview (Volume 3 CD), Lydon described at least three police raids on his Gunter Grove home within a four-month period that directly precipitated his departure: “I was raided by the police three times in a four month period, and that was just too much. I left the country after that.” One raid, at 6 a.m., involved plainclothes officers “running through your house, smashing things, and all armed with axes.” Another involved the bomb squad, called because an Irish flag had been raised through a window: “We have reason to believe there are bombs on the premises.” In Uncut (2007) he stated more broadly: “I couldn’t stay in England. I was being nicked twice a week. It was ridiculous. My life became excruciating.” [Sources: johnlydon.com/press/vol3.html — https://www.johnlydon.com/press/vol3.html; johnlydon.com/press/uncut07.html — https://www.johnlydon.com/press/uncut07.html; The Face 1983 interview, fodderstompf.com — https://www.fodderstompf.com/ARCHIVES/INTERVIEWS/JL_face83.html]

The official PiL timeline notes simply that in 1981 “PiL decide to relocate to New York from London due to logistics and Police harassment.” [Source: pilofficial.com/timeline/ — https://pilofficial.com/timeline/]

Note on Discogs profile framing

The Discogs profile says raids targeted “anywhere he and band PIL tried to play.” No web sources confirm raids on concert venues specifically; the documented raids were on his Gunter Grove residence, which also served as a rehearsal and creative base. PiL had very few UK concert dates in 1980–81 (none are listed in the fodderstompf.com gig archive for those years), so venue-targeted raids may be conflated with the home raids in popular retellings, or the Discogs text may be a loose paraphrase of Lydon’s own accounts. No primary source reviewed here names a specific concert venue as a raid target.
