Michael Hampton (3)

Oct 15, 20269 min read

Michael Hampton (3)

Discogs ID: 543818 Origin: Washington, DC, USA Name variations (Discogs): Hampton, M Hampton, M. Hampton, Michael, Mike, Mike Hampton

American guitarist central to the first and second waves of Washington DC hardcore and post-hardcore. Hampton played guitar in State Of Alert (S.O.A., 1981) alongside Henry Rollins, then co-founded Faith (3) (1982–83) and Embrace (2) (1985–86), co-produced the Rites Of Spring debut LP (1985), and joined One Last Wish (1986). Post-DC, he led Manifesto (3) in London (1991–92) and returned to active recording with Fake Names from 2019 onward. All major DC work released on Dischord Records.

Biography

S.O.A. (1981) — Entry Into DC Hardcore

Hampton’s first significant credit is as guitarist for State Of Alert (S.O.A., Discogs ID: 271698), the band fronted by Henry Garfield (later Henry Rollins). The lineup was Henry Garfield (vocals), Hampton (guitar), Wendel Blow (bass), Simon Jacobsen (drums), and Ivor Hanson (drums — Hampton and Hanson appear to have overlapped). The band recorded the No Policy EP (1981, Dischord No. 6), produced by Skip Groff and engineered by Don Zientara. Hampton’s guitar credit is the earliest entry in his Discogs record.

He also appears on Flex Your Head (1982, Dischord No. 7), the essential DC hardcore compilation, in guitar credits connected to this early-scene period.

Faith (3) (1982–1983)

By 1982 Hampton was a founding member of Faith (3) (Discogs ID: 309576, also known as The Faith), with Alec MacKaye (vocals), Eddie Janney (guitar), Chris Bald (bass), and Ivor Hanson (drums). The band released two records:

After Faith dissolved, Hampton, Bald, and Hanson moved directly into Embrace with Ian MacKaye.

Rites Of Spring LP — Production Credit (1985)

Before Embrace began gigging, Hampton co-produced the debut LP of Rites Of Spring (1985, Dischord No. 16), alongside Ian MacKaye. This is Hampton’s first Discogs production credit.

Hampton was not a member of Rites Of Spring; this is a production role only. His presence in the studio alongside MacKaye for the Rites Of Spring sessions prefigures the Embrace lineup, and connects him to the Picciotto/Janney/Canty/Fellows cluster that would later form One Last Wish with him.

Embrace (2) (1985–1986)

Hampton joined Embrace (2) (Discogs ID: 265295) as guitarist — the band formed by Ian MacKaye after Minor Threat dissolved, with the same rhythm section from Faith: Chris Bald (bass) and Ivor Hanson (drums). Embrace played 11 shows and dissolved before completing a planned Boston tour leg while MacKaye was in England. Their self-titled LP was recorded but released posthumously in 1987.

He appears as a member-performer rather than a production figure on this particular release.

One Last Wish (1986)

Immediately after Embrace dissolved, Hampton joined One Last Wish (Discogs ID: 309588) — formed from three members of Rites Of Spring (Guy Picciotto, Eddie Janney, Brendan Canty) plus Hampton on guitar. Janney shifted to bass to accommodate Hampton. The band played six shows and recorded an LP in 1986, but the recording sat unreleased for 13 years before Dischord and Peterbilt Records finally issued it in 1999.

Brief Weeds (late 1980s / early 1990s)

Following One Last Wish, Hampton participated in Brief Weeds (Discogs ID: 756877) — a home-recording project with the same lineup as OLW minus Janney, regrouping Picciotto, Canty, and Hampton. Hampton is credited (uncredited on the sleeve) as bassist:

Psychedelic pop home recordings. Released on K rather than Dischord. Hampton plays bass rather than guitar — a recurring pattern when the OLW lineup reconvened without its full complement.

Manifesto (3) (1991–1992, London)

Hampton relocated to the UK and formed Manifesto (3) (Discogs ID: 292673) with Ivor Hanson (drums) and Bert Queiroz (bass) — two veterans of the Faith/Embrace/S.O.A. network. Released on Fire Records (London).

Manifesto LP (1992, Fire Records)

CreditPerson
Voice, Guitar, Drum Programming, Songwriter [All Songs]Michael Hampton (3)
Drums [Drum Set, Etc.]Ivor Hanson
Bass GuitarBert Queiroz
Keyboards [Additional], Programmed By, ProducerJohn A. Rivers

Hampton is credited as sole songwriter and handles drum programming alongside Hanson’s live drumming. The sound moved significantly away from DC hardcore — Fire Records was primarily an indie/alternative imprint. Earlier singles on Fire included Walking Backwards (1991, credited as “Manifesto Is”) and Sugar (1992, Voice/Guitar/Drum Programming).

The band’s 1990 Burn single (Y&T Records) and History (1990, Fire Records) are both credited to Manifesto (3) on Discogs — the name was already in use from at least 1990, making these pre-LP singles under the same project name rather than a predecessor or unnamed period. The 1991 Walking Backwards single uses the slight variant “Manifesto Is” as the performer credit, but the primary release artist is still listed as Manifesto (3).

Post-Manifesto Activity (1993–2010s)

Hampton contributed guitar or loops to various releases through the 1990s and 2000s:

  • Pattern 26 (1993, EastWest Records America) — Written-By credit
  • Dormer (1994, spinART Records) — Guitar, Organ, Producer
  • Long Distance (2001, Nettwerk America) — Loops [Additional Loops], Sounds
  • Wonderfully Nothing (2003, Unfiltered) — Electric Guitar
  • This Is Where We Live (2004, Unfiltered) — Producer, Recorded By
  • Heaven’s Alright (2010, Cycle (2)) — Lead Guitar

These credits span multiple different projects and labels; Hampton functioned as a hired contributor or producer-for-hire during this stretch rather than as a primary bandleader.

Fake Names (2019–present)

Hampton re-emerged as a founding member of Fake Names (Discogs ID: 7840255), a DC hardcore supergroup formed in 2019. The lineup: Hampton (guitar, vocals), Brian Baker (guitar, vocals; Minor Threat, Dag Nasty, Bad Religion), Dennis Lyxzén (vocals; Refused, The International Noise Conspiracy), Brendan Canty (drums; Rites Of Spring, Fugazi), and Johnny Temple (bass).

Hampton also mixed the debut LP. Releases on Epitaph:

The Foreign Correspondents (2) (2024)

Hampton’s most recent Discogs credit is Lovin’ You Ain’t Easy (2024, Outer Battery Records), credited as The Foreign Correspondents (2) (Discogs ID: 14343242). This appears to be a new project; details are sparse in Discogs.
