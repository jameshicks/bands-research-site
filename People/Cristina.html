Cristina

Real name: Cristina Monet-Palaci (later Cristina Monet Zilkha) Discogs ID: 55646 Aliases / name variations: Cristina Palaci, New York (5), C. Monet, Christina, Christina Monet, Cristina Monet, Cristina Monet-Zilkha, Cristina Zilkha Active: 1978–1984 (recording); died 1 April 2020 (COVID-19), aged 61 Role: vocalist, lyricist, producer

Singer, writer, and one of the signature artists of ZE Records. Noted for a sardonic, deadpan delivery over disco-inflected, theatrical productions. Wrote theatre criticism for the Village Voice before pivoting to recording; her marriage to ZE co-founder Michael Zilkha placed her at the centre of the label’s social world. Three producers across her recording career — John Cale on the debut single, August Darnell on the first LP, and Don Was on the second LP — giving each release a distinct character.

Biography

Early life and Village Voice period

Born Cristina Monet-Palaci; birth date disputed in sources — Wikipedia’s infobox gives 17 January 1959, while the same article’s text gives 1956; Legacy.com and most obituaries give 1959. Parents: Dorothy Monet (writer-illustrator) and Jacques Palaci (French psychoanalyst). She attended Harvard University, studying playwriting under William Alfred (the Irish-American dramatist best known for Hogan’s Goat) and won the History and Literature Prize in her sophomore year. During a leave of absence from Harvard she was involved in a motorcycle accident in which a friend died; she decided not to re-enroll and did not complete her degree. [Source: Wikipedia Cristina (singer), rev. 2024, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_(singer); prabook.com/web/cristina.monet-palaci/2521650]

Before recording, she worked as a theatre critic for the Village Voice in New York — reportedly the city’s youngest at the time. Michael Zilkha (who would co-found ZE Records) was also writing theatre criticism for the Voice and called her after hearing she had made the same “youngest critic” claim. They later married (1983) and have a daughter, Lulu, born on Christmas Day. They divorced in 1990. [Source: byronesque.com/cristina-monet-interview/; People/Michael Zilkha.md]

“Disco Clone” and the founding of ZE Records (1978)

The first release Zilkha and Michel Esteban put out on the newly formed ZE Records was Cristina’s debut single, “Disco Clone” (cat. no. 12 ZE 001, 1978). John Cale was attached as producer — he and Zilkha had been introduced earlier, and Cale’s involvement with Esteban on SPY Records created the Zilkha-Esteban connection.

Two-version history (CONFLICT / revised): The Cale-produced recording was in fact released — the original 12” was pressed in a limited edition of approximately 1,500 copies and constitutes the actual inaugural ZE release, with Anthony Haden-Guest on male vocals (English and French versions). A subsequent re-recording was made with male vocals by Kevin Kline (then a little-known actor) and remixed by Chris Blackwell; it is this second version (co-produced by Zilkha, with vocal direction by Cristina and engineering by Bob Blank) that became the more widely distributed pressing. The current vault text stating that “Cale is not credited on the finished release” applies only to the re-recorded version; Cale did complete and release a production. [Source: Wikipedia, “Disco Clone”, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Clone; ZE Records site, https://www.zerecords.com/release/135/disco-clone.html]

The creative friction: Cristina described wanting to perform the song (which she initially called “the worst song I have ever heard”) as a “Brechtian pastiche” rather than a straight disco track. Zilkha later characterised the tension as: “Cristina wanted something Brechtian and John wanted Monster Mash.” [Source: post-punk.com interview summary, https://post-punk.com/ze-records-revived-with-the-future-is-mine-interview-with-legendary-producers-michael-zilkha-and-john-robie/] Esteban’s account frames the broader SPY/ZE split differently: SPY “was John’s baby and he wanted to produce every artist himself,” so he and Zilkha struck out independently. No source confirms whether Cale and Zilkha had a direct falling-out over this specific session, or whether the split was simply structural (SPY = Cale’s project; ZE = Zilkha/Esteban’s). Tom Moulton declined to mix the track because he opposed its mocking tone toward disco. [Source: Wikipedia, “Disco Clone”.]

The single appeared on ZE rather than SPY, marking the effective launch of the label. The re-recorded version credits Zilkha as producer, Cristina as vocal director, and Bob Blank as musical director/engineer; Cale is not credited on that pressing.

The track was arranged by Bhen Lanzaroni (strings/general) and Carlos Franzetti (strings and horns), engineered by Bob Blank, with lyrics by Cristina (additional) and music written by Ronald Melrose. Kevin Kline (the actor) provided uncredited vocals. Format: 12” 45 RPM, Limited Edition.

Cristina LP — produced by August Darnell (1980)

Cristina’s debut album, Cristina (Island Records / ZE Records, ILPS 7004, 1980), was produced by August Darnell (who also wrote all the songs except “La Poupée Qui Fait Non”). Darnell also provided the arrangements and orchestrations alongside his cousin Coati Mundi (Andy Hernandez), who conducted and arranged. The album was recorded at Blank Tape Studios (engineered by Bob Blank, Butch Jones, Joe Arlotta) with Michael Zilkha credited as Director.

”Things Fall Apart” single (1981)

A standalone single on Island Records, 1981 (master ID 193739). No additional credits survive in Discogs for this pressing. A compilation It’s A Holiday / Things Fall Apart also documents a collaboration with Material and vocalist Nona Hendryx around 1981.

Sleep It Off — produced by Don Was (1984)

The second and final studio album, Sleep It Off (Mercury, 814 980-1, 1984), was produced by Don Was, with Michael Zilkha as executive producer. Cristina wrote all the lyrics; songwriting credits are collaborative with Was, Barry Reynolds, Doug Fieger (of The Knack), and others.

Cover art by photographer and director Jean-Paul Goude.

Genres: Rock / New Wave / Leftfield. The album includes covers of Brecht/Weill (“Ballad of Immoral Earnings,” from The Threepenny Opera) and Van Morrison (“Blue Money”), alongside originals — consistent with Cristina’s theatrically inflected approach. James Chance and both Was brothers appear on the same track (B4).

Guest appearance: Was (Not Was) (1984)

Cristina provided female vocals on Was (Not Was)‘s “(Return To The Valley Of) Out Come The Freaks – Remixed Version” (Geffen Records / ZE Records, 1984) — the only documented external-artist feature in the Discogs data.

Doll In The Box reissue (ZE Records, 2004)

A retrospective release combining material from both albums, coordinated by Michel Esteban and executive-produced by Michael Zilkha. The 2004 digipack was designed by Esteban and Yves Castelain, with booklet liner notes by Kurt B. Reighley and photography by Wayne Maser and Erica Lennard. The original album band is listed with guest appearances by Kid Creole (August Darnell) and Gichy Dan’s Beachwood #9. A Japanese pressing followed in 2010 (P-Vine Records).

Later life and death (1985–2020)

After Sleep It Off failed commercially, Cristina retired from music and moved with Zilkha to Texas in 1985. She and Zilkha divorced in 1990 after seven years of marriage. Following the divorce she returned to New York. In the years that followed she contributed essays and reviews to publications including the Times Literary Supplement (London) — her LinkedIn profile listed this as her primary professional affiliation in later life. She battled autoimmune disorders, including relapsing polychondritis, for close to two decades. Discogs shows no further recording activity under her name after 1984, with the sole exception of a 2000 logo art credit on an Italian release.

One later musical appearance is documented outside Discogs: around 2006 she recorded a track for Ursula 1000 (per Post-Punk Monk obituary, 2 April 2020, https://postpunkmonk.com/2020/04/02/cristina-monet-zilkha-1959-2020/). No other post-1984 creative output has been confirmed in web sources.

She died on 1 April 2020 in New York from complications of COVID-19, aged 61 (or 64 — see birth year conflict above). Her death was announced on Facebook by Michel Esteban. [Sources: Stereogum, 1 April 2020, https://www.stereogum.com/2078996/new-wave-singer-cristina-dead-from-coronavirus/news/; NME obituary, https://www.nme.com/news/music/cristina-ze-records-and-things-fall-apart-singer-dies-at-61-2639500; Wikipedia as of 2024-06-05; LinkedIn profile https://www.linkedin.com/in/cristina-monet-zilkha-6586b7192/]
