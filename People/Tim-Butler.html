Tim Butler

Tim Butler

Jul 17, 20266 min read

Tim Butler

Discogs ID: 428960
Born: 7 December 1958, Teddington, Middlesex, England
Real name: Timothy George Butler
Role: Bassist, songwriter, co-founder

English bassist and co-founder of The Psychedelic Furs alongside his brother Richard Butler. One of only two members to have been present across every era of the band — from the 1977 formation through the 1991 hiatus, the reformed touring band from 2000 onward, and the 2020 studio comeback. Also co-founded Love Spit Love with Richard Butler during the Furs’ hiatus years (1992–1997).

Biography

Formation and Early Furs (1977–1979)

Tim Butler and Richard Butler formed the core of what became The Psychedelic Furs in London in 1977. The full original lineup — Tim (bass), Richard (vocals), John Ashton (guitar), Roger Morris (guitar), Duncan Kilburn (saxophone/keyboards), and Vince Ely (drums) — coalesced around the Butler brothers.

Their first release was the single “We Love You” (1979, Epic), produced by Howard Thompson and Ian Taylor alongside the band themselves. This predates the CBS signing that would follow. The lineup is the full six-piece: Tim, Richard, John Ashton, Roger Morris, Duncan Kilburn, and Vince Ely.

The Psychedelic Furs — CBS/Columbia Era (1980–1991)

Tim Butler played bass across all seven studio albums released during this period. He is credited as a co-writer on key tracks including “Love My Way” (his most prominent co-writing credit, which reached the US Top 50 in 1982), and contributed to the band’s collective songwriting credited as a whole.

Songwriting credits in Discogs confirm Tim as co-writer on: “Love My Way” (1982), “Heaven,” “The Ghost In You,” “Here Come Cowboys” (all 1984), “Heartbreak Beat,” “Pretty In Pink” (re-recording, 1986), “Angels Don’t Cry,” and the 12” single Shock (US, 1987, a Shep Pettibone remix).

Notably on World Outside (1991), the lineup had changed substantially — the credited performers are Richard Butler, Tim Butler, John Ashton, Knox Chandler, Joe McGinty, and Don Yallech, with Stephen Street producing and engineering.

Love Spit Love (1992–1997)

After the Furs went on hiatus in 1991, the Butler brothers formed Love Spit Love in New York City (some sources say founded 1992). Tim appears on the debut album:

Love Spit Love (1994, Imago): Tim Butler on bass; Richard Fortus on guitar, cello, mandolin; Frank Ferrer on drums. Produced by Dave Jerden; mixed by Dave Jerden. Guest appearance by Jon Brion (Chamberlin, keyboards). Discogs members list: Richard Butler, Richard Fortus, Frank Ferrer, Tim Butler.

Trysome Eatone (1997, Maverick): Tim Butler is not credited in Discogs as a performer on this album. The credited band members are Richard Butler, Richard Fortus, Frank Ferrer, and Chris Wilson (8). Tim’s absence from the 1997 album may indicate he had departed the project before that recording.

Love Spit Love is notably remembered for recording the theme to the TV series Charmed (“How Soon Is Now?,” a cover of The Smiths).

Tim is also credited as co-writer on a track on the Counting Crows August And Everything After (“Round Here” — Words By, Music By credit on a promo/comp pressing) and contributes a co-writing credit to “Pretty In Pink” as covered by a re-recording in the Pretty In Pink film (1986). His co-writing also extended to a track recorded by the band Live (Richard Butler, John Ashton, Tim Butler, and Vince Ely credited as writers of track 4 on Freaks, 1997 — “Lakini’s Juice” based on the Discogs data).

Compilation Production (late 1990s–2000s)

Tim Butler is credited as Compilation Producer on two Furs retrospective releases:

  • Should God Forget: A Retrospective (1997, Columbia/Legacy) — also credited as Bass
  • Greatest Hits (2000, Columbia/Legacy)

This production role is notable — he took an active hand in curating the band’s legacy during the hiatus years.

Reformed Psychedelic Furs (2000–present)

The Furs reformed for touring in 2000. Tim returned on bass and appears on both live documents from the early 2000s:

  • Beautiful Chaos: Greatest Hits Live (2001, Columbia/Legacy)
  • Live From House Of Blues (2001, Eagle Vision)

Made Of Rain (2020, Cooking Vinyl) — the first studio album in 29 years. Tim Butler on bass; lineup: Richard Butler (vocals), Paul Garisto (drums/percussion), Richard Good (guitar), Amanda Kramer (keyboards), Mars Williams (saxophone). Produced by Richard Fortus (also playing guitar) with additional production from David Maurice on tracks 4, 6, 8, 12; mixed by Tim Palmer.

Tim also appears as co-writer on The New Abnormal by The Strokes — specifically credited as “Music By” on that 2020 release (release ID 15049564, Cult Records/RCA). This is a curious credit that may reflect a sample clearance.
