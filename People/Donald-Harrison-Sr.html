Donald Harrison Sr.

Big Chief of the Guardians of the Flame Mardi Gras Indian tribe, New Orleans. Father of jazz saxophonist Donald Harrison. Discogs: “Donald Harrison (2)”, ID 132961. Also credited as “Donald Harrison Sr.”

Biography

Donald Harrison Sr. was the Big Chief of the Guardians of the Flame, one of New Orleans’ Mardi Gras Indian tribes. The Mardi Gras Indian tradition is a distinct Black New Orleans cultural practice in which neighborhood groups (called “tribes” or “gangs”) create elaborate hand-sewn beaded and feathered suits and perform on Mardi Gras, Super Sunday, and St. Joseph’s Night. The Big Chief is the leader of a tribe and its principal masker and singer.

His son Donald Harrison Jr. grew up immersed in the tribe’s ceremonial life — chants, call-and-response vocal traditions, and the specific repertoire of Mardi Gras Indian songs (“Indian Red,” “Shallow Water,” “Hu-Ta-Nay,” “Two-Way-Pockey-Way”) that form the core of the tradition. The elder Harrison’s influence shaped his son’s musical identity to the extent that Donald Harrison Jr. eventually became Big Chief of the Guardians of the Flame himself, succeeding his father, and began billing himself as “Big Chief Donald Harrison Jr.” on later releases.

The elder Harrison’s sole known recording appearances are as a vocalist, most prominently on his son’s Indian Blues (Candid, 1992) — the definitive recorded synthesis of jazz and Mardi Gras Indian tradition — and on a handful of related New Orleans recordings.
