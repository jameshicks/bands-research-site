Marie Scroggins

Percussionist and vocalist. Member of ESG. Discogs ID: 488125. Sister of Renee Scroggins, Valerie Scroggins, and Deborah Scroggins.

Overview

Marie Scroggins is one of the four Scroggins sisters at the core of ESG, the South Bronx art-funk/dance-punk group active from the late 1970s onward. She plays congas and contributes vocals across the band’s entire recorded output, from the 1981 debut EP on 99 Records through to the 2006 album on Soul Jazz Records. In the 99 Records era she shares the percussion floor with Tito Libran (also on congas) while Valerie Scroggins holds down drums — the three-way interlocking rhythm section that defines ESG’s sound. By the later albums her role expands to include keyboards and hand percussion as the lineup evolves.

The Scroggins family grew up in the South Bronx. Their mother arranged music lessons and community centre performances; Renee took guitar, Valerie drums, Marie and Deborah following in the rhythm section. They were playing local events before Ed Bahlman of 99 Records saw them and offered to record them in 1980.

Role in ESG Across Eras

99 Records Era (1981–1983)

The formative period. Marie appears on all three 99 Records releases as Congas and Vocals, credited as “Marie” on the sleeve. The live side of the 1981 EP shows the full original five-piece rhythm unit in action.

Post-99 Records / Own Label (1987)

After 99 Records closed, ESG continued on their own Emerald Sapphire & Gold Records imprint. On Bam-Bam Jam (1987, released as ESG II) Marie’s credit shifts to Percussion — Deborah is absent, and the lineup includes Leroy Glover on bass and David Miles on guitar.

1991 Reunion (ESG, Pow Wow Records)

By the 1991 self-titled release on Pow Wow Records, Marie’s role has broadened: credited for Keyboards, Congas, Percussion (Hand Percussion), and Backing Vocals. This is the most expansive instrumental credit she carries across any ESG release.

Soul Jazz Era (2000s)

On the Soul Jazz-era releases Marie returns to congas and backing vocals. A South Bronx Story (2000) re-assembles the original 99 Records lineup (including Deborah and Tito Libran) for new recordings alongside the compiled originals. Step Off (2002) introduces the next-generation lineup — nieces Chistelle (guitar) and Nicole (bass) — while Marie remains on congas. On Keep On Moving (2006) her credit is Percussion (Hand) and Backing Vocals; Valerie now handles the full percussion kit including timbales.

2014 Compilation

Dance To The Best Of ESG (Fire Records, 2014) credits Marie, Renee, Valerie, and Deborah all as Performer — the original four sisters acknowledged together on a retrospective release.

Open Questions

Web sources

Marie Scroggins and Closure (2012) / What More Can You Take? (2017)

Marie carries no Discogs credit under her ID (488125) on either of these releases. Web research finds no source that explicitly names her as a studio participant on either album. The picture that emerges from interviews is one of occasional live involvement rather than consistent studio presence during this period.

What the sources say:

  • A WNYU Static Magazine interview with Renee Scroggins (undated, post-2006) states: “my sister Marie comes in and plays percussion sometimes” — framing her participation as occasional rather than structural. No album-specific recording credits are given. (staticmag.org)

  • A 2018 Secondhand Setlists interview, conducted after the release of What More Can You Take? (2017), identifies the touring/recording lineup for that period as Renee’s daughter Nicole, son Nicholas Nicholas (who also engineered What More Can You Take?), and Anthony Alieso. Renee says of Marie: “Marie was supposed to come along this time, but she got ill.” No recording credit for Marie is mentioned. (secondhandsetlists.com, as of 2026-07-12)

  • The SF Station Q&A (Feb 2012) with Renee Scroggins, timed to the Closure release and final west coast tour, does not name Marie in the performing lineup. (sfstation.com, as of 2026-07-12)

  • Wikipedia’s ESG article infobox lists Marie Scroggins as a current member alongside Renee, Nicole Nicholas, and Nicholas D. Nicholas, but provides no album-specific credit for Closure or What More Can You Take?. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESG_(band), as of 2026-07-12)

Assessment: The balance of evidence suggests Marie was not a primary studio participant on either Closure (2012) or What More Can You Take? (2017). By the early 2010s the active recording/touring unit had shifted to Renee plus second-generation members (Nicole and Nicholas Nicholas, Anthony Alieso). Marie retained membership status and joined live performances when available, but her participation became intermittent due to health issues that were specifically cited around the 2017–2018 period. No source names an alternative credit under a different name for her on these releases; the most probable conclusion is that she did not appear on either record, and the absence of her Discogs credit reflects actual non-participation rather than a mislabelling.

No conflict with Discogs: Discogs omits her credit on both releases, consistent with what interviews indicate about the lineup.

Marie’s intermittent live participation — what interviews document

Web research (2026-07-20) found no source that gives a specific date or period when Marie’s live participation became intermittent. The picture assembled from multiple interviews is consistent but undated.

Primary sources:

  • WNYU Static Magazine interview with Renee Scroggins (conducted by Lizzy Jack; date not stated in article, but the publication context is post-2006): Renee says “my sister Marie comes in and plays percussion sometimes.” This is the earliest available published framing of her participation as occasional rather than structural. No explanation is given for why it is occasional. (staticmag.org/lizzy-jack/esg-interview/)

  • Secondhand Setlists interview with Renee Scroggins (October 24, 2018, conducted ahead of the Regent Theater LA show on October 25, 2018): Renee says “Marie was supposed to come along this time, but she got ill. I’m not sure my other sister will or not…” This is the only interview found that gives a specific reason — illness — for a specific absence. The 2017–2018 touring period around What More Can You Take? is confirmed as a period where Marie was present in intention but absent in practice due to health. (secondhandsetlists.com/2018/10/24/travel-world-on-a-song-an-interview-with-esg/, as of 2026-07-20)

  • SF Station Q&A with Renee Scroggins (February 21, 2012, timed to the Closure west coast tour): Marie is not named in the performing lineup. No explanation is offered. (sfstation.com/2012/02/21/esg-stops-at-mezzanine-for-last-tour/, as of 2026-07-20)

  • DJ Mag feature (May 2, 2024): Notes that “today, ESG remains a family affair but with Scroggins’ children now playing the band rather than her sisters,” implying the founding sisters (including Marie) are no longer the regular live unit, but gives no Marie-specific detail. (djmag.com/features/esgs-infinite-influence-how-south-bronx-sisters-shaped-hip-hop-and-house, as of 2026-07-20)

What the sources collectively establish:

The intermittency predates the October 2018 interview (which treats her absence as a familiar situation, not a new development), and the WNYU Static framing (“comes in sometimes”) suggests the pattern was already settled before that. The 2012 west coast tour already featured her absence without comment. No source documents the transition from regular to occasional participation — no interview, no press release, no review from a specific show marks it as a change. The onset is undocumented in currently accessible web sources.
