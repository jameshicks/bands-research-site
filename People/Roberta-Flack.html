Roberta Flack

Discogs ID: 63771 Born: February 10, 1937, Black Mountain, North Carolina, USA Died: February 24, 2025, Manhattan, New York City, USA Active: 1962–2024 Genres: Soul, R&B, Soft Rock, Jazz Labels: Atlantic Records, Capitol Records, MCA Records, 429 Records Real name: Roberta Cleopatra Flack

Soul singer and pianist whose career at Atlantic Records produced three decades of landmark recordings. Known above all for “The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face” (1972) and “Killing Me Softly With His Song” (1973), both Grammy winners for Record of the Year. Her sustained duet partnerships with Donny Hathaway and Peabo Bryson expanded her reach well beyond solo work.

Biography

Early Life and Howard University

Roberta Cleopatra Flack was born in Black Mountain, North Carolina, on February 10, 1937. A classically trained pianist, she won a full scholarship to Howard University at fourteen — where she studied with the pianist and pedagogue Hildegard Hoffmann — and graduated at age eighteen. She taught music in public schools in Washington, D.C., and worked as a singer and pianist at various D.C. clubs, including the restaurant Mr. Henry’s on Capitol Hill, where she honed a repertoire that ranged from opera and classical art song to jazz standards and protest folk.

Atlantic Records Debut — First Take (1969)

Les McCann, himself an Atlantic artist, heard Flack at Mr. Henry’s and recommended her to the label. Atlantic signed her, and she cut her debut album First Take in 1969 with producer Joel Dorn. Recorded essentially live in the studio with a core band anchored by Ron Carter on bass and Bucky Pizzarelli on guitar, the album covered material ranging from Leonard Cohen (“Hey, That’s No Way To Say Goodbye”) to Ewan MacColl (“The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face”) to songs written by Donny Hathaway and Leroy Hutson. The record sold modestly on release; Flack played piano and arranged throughout and was already writing her own sleeve notes. Arranger William S. Fischer scored horns and strings for several tracks.

Chapter Two (1970), again with Joel Dorn and co-produced by King Curtis, continued the orchestrated soul and jazz-pop direction. Quiet Fire (1971) was her most overtly arranged album to that point, with Flack credited for piano, vocals, and arrangement; Arif Mardin contributed string and flute charts, and the band included Chuck Rainey (bass), Bernard Purdie (drums), Richard Tee (organ), Hugh McCracken (guitar), and Ralph MacDonald (percussion).

Breakthrough — The Clint Eastwood Film and Grammy Wins

“The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face” had been recorded for First Take in 1969 but lay largely unheard until director Clint Eastwood licensed it for his 1971 film Play Misty for Me. Atlantic released it as a single in 1972; it spent six weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and won the Grammy Award for Record of the Year. The parent album First Take belatedly became a massive seller, eventually reaching over 1 million copies.

Killing Me Softly (1973, Atlantic SD 7271) — produced by Joel Dorn — contained the title track written by Charles Fox and Norman Gimbel, reportedly inspired by a Don McLean performance. The single reached number one and won a second consecutive Grammy for Record of the Year, an unprecedented achievement. The album also included notable covers of Janis Ian’s “Jesse” (with Eumir Deodato arranging strings) and Leonard Cohen’s “Suzanne.”

“Feel Like Makin’ Love” (Atlantic 45-3025, 1974, written by Eugene McDaniels) reached number one and became her third major chart hit.

Donny Hathaway Duets

Flack and Donny Hathaway had known each other since their time at Howard University. Their first duet album, Roberta Flack & Donny Hathaway (1972, Atlantic), was produced by Arif Mardin and Joel Dorn, with both Flack and Hathaway credited for arrangement. The record included their cover of “You’ve Lost That Loving Feeling,” “Where Is The Love,” and “Be Real Black For Me.” The follow-up recordings — issued as Roberta Flack Featuring Donny Hathaway (1979) after Hathaway’s death in January 1979 — were produced by Flack herself with contributions from Eric Mercury and Arif Mardin. “Back Together Again” and “You Are My Heaven” (the latter featuring Hathaway and written by Stevie Wonder) gave the posthumous collection its commercial life.

Mid-Atlantic Period — Blue Lights In The Basement and Roberta Flack (1977–1978)

Blue Lights In The Basement (1977, Atlantic SD 19149) marked a shift toward a more studio-polished, multi-producer approach. The record drew on sessions featuring Steve Gadd and Idris Muhammad on drums, Anthony Jackson on bass, Hugh McCracken and Reggie Lucas on guitars, Don Grolnick, Leon Pendarvis, and Rob Mounsey on keyboards, and backing vocals from Deniece Williams, Gwen Guthrie, James Mtume, and Luther Vandross. Producers included Eugene McDaniels and Joe Ferla, with executive production from Ahmet Ertegun. “The Closer I Get To You,” a duet with Donny Hathaway, became a Top 5 R&B single from the album.

Capitol Records and Peabo Bryson (1981–1984)

After leaving Atlantic briefly for MCA Records (the Bustin’ Loose soundtrack, 1981), Flack signed to Capitol Records and recorded two albums with Peabo Bryson. Born To Love (1983, Capitol EST 7122841) drew on diverse producers including Michael Masser, Bob Crewe and Bob Gaudio, and Carole Bayer Sager, and produced the hit “Tonight, I Celebrate My Love.” Their earlier pairing also yielded the 1983 single “Tonight, I Celebrate My Love” (backed across multiple producers on individual tracks).

Return to Atlantic and Later Career

Flack returned to Atlantic in 1988 with Oasis (Atlantic 7 81916-4), a synth-influenced record produced by Marcus Miller, Jerry Hey, Andy Goldmark, and Barry Miles. It included the R&B chart entry “Uh-Uh Ooh-Ooh Look Out (Here It Comes).” The single “Set The Night To Music,” a 1991 duet with reggae singer Maxi Priest, reached number 6 on the Hot 100.

Roberta (1994, Atlantic) was her final major Atlantic studio album of the period. Her godson was jazz-funk keyboardist Bernard Wright, who appears on the 2012 Beatles covers album Let It Be Roberta (429 Records FTN17852), produced primarily by Sherrod Barnes and Barry Miles.

In later years Flack suffered from ALS, which she publicly disclosed in 2022. She died on February 24, 2025, in Manhattan.
