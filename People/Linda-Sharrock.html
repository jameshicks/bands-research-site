Linda Sharrock

Born: 2 April 1947, Philadelphia, PA, USA Real name: Linda Sharrock, née Chambers Role: Vocalist, composer, visual artist Discogs ID: 370805

American avant-garde and jazz vocalist with a career spanning over five decades. Known for her extended-technique vocal work on Sonny Sharrock’s early records, her solo work on ATCO Records and ECM Records, and an extensive later career based in Vienna as part of the Austrian jazz and experimental music scenes.

Biography

Early Career: The Sonny Sharrock Years (1969–1975)

Linda Chambers married guitarist Sonny Sharrock in 1966, and her voice became central to his most radical early recordings. On his debut as leader, Black Woman (1969, Vortex Records (2)), she contributed wordless, extended vocal improvisations alongside Milford Graves (drums), Dave Burrell (piano), Norris Jones (bass), Ted Daniel (trumpet), and producer Herbie Mann. The album is a key document of late-1960s free jazz.

The following year she traveled to France for sessions that produced Monkey-Pockie-Boo (1970, BYG Records), again under Sonny’s name, with Beb Guérin (bass) and Jacques Thollot (drums). She wrote the sleeve notes.

The marriage and musical partnership produced Paradise (1975, ATCO Records), their first co-credited album, recorded at A&R Studios in New York with engineer Phil Ramone, keyboardist Kenny Armstrong, drummer Buddy Williams, bassist Dave Artis, and producer Ilhan Mimaroglu. The Sharrock marriage ended in 1978.

Solo and Transitional Work (1975–1985)

After Paradise, Linda continued as a vocalist for hire and occasional bandleader. She appeared on Angel Eyes (1976, Muse Records) and began a lengthy residency in the Austrian/German jazz circuit. By the early 1980s she was based in Vienna. Credits from this period include:

  • Und So Weiter (1983, Polydor) — choir credit with a German pop ensemble
  • All My Life / Easy (1983, Green Elephant Music) — 7” single, Austria
  • Lobster / The Word (1984, Green Elephant Music) — 7” single, Austria; part of her involvement with The Word (10)
  • Pat Brothers No. 1 (1986, Moers Music) — with The Pat Brothers; she contributed voice and lyrics

Vienna Scene and ECM (1986–1995)

Linda became a member of the Vienna avant-jazz scene in the second half of the 1980s, with two significant collectives:

Every 2nd — a trio with Pyrolator (Kurt Dahlke, of Deutsch Amerikanische Freundschaft fame) and Frank Samba. Their self-titled album appeared in 1988 on Ata Tak / Wintrup Musikverlag.

AM 4 / Red Sun — her most sustained creative partnership was with alto saxophonist Wolfgang Puschnig. Together they formed the core of multiple projects:

  • … And She Answered: (1989, ECM Records) — credited to AM 4 (Wolfgang Puschnig, Linda Sharrock, Uli Scherer). Produced by Manfred Eicher, engineered by Jan Erik Kongshaug at Rainbow Studio, Oslo. Linda also did the cover art.
  • On Holiday (1990, Polydor) — leader credit; included Jamaaladeen Tacuma (bass guitar), Kae Williams Jr., and others. Austrian Polydor release.
  • Then Comes The White Tiger (1994, ECM Records) — credited to Red Sun / SamulNori, with Puschnig, Jamaaladeen Tacuma (bass guitar), and the Korean percussion ensemble Samulnori (Kim Duk-Soo, Lee Kwang Soo, et al.). Engineered again by Kongshaug. Linda contributed voice and cover design.

This period also included work with the large ensemble The Reform Art Unit (later the Reform Art Quartet), an Austrian free-jazz collective with over a dozen members including Franz Koglmann, Fritz Novotny, Giselher Smekal, and others. She appeared on their Millenium CD (2001, Quinton) and is listed as a group member on Discogs.

French Sessions and Later Work (1994–2006)

Listen To The Night (1994, Owl Records (4)) was a Paris-recorded duo record with pianist Eric Watson, produced by Jean-Jacques Pussiau and François Lemaire — a quieter, more introspective context than her ECM work.

She continued collaborating with Puschnig’s projects and appeared on:

  • Alpine Aspects (1991, Amadeo) — with Puschnig’s group; she contributed vocals and cover collage artwork
  • Mixed Metaphors (1995, Amadeo) — similar format
  • Nanjang – A New Horizon (1995, King Record, South Korea) — with Red Sun; Korean release
  • 風花 (Punghwa) (1997, Samsung Music) — Korean release, further Red Sun material
  • Miroirs (2006, Discograph / Minium) — vocals

The Linda Sharrock Network and Current Work (2016–present)

In 2016 she released Gods (Golden Lab Records, UK) — a primary leader credit. She also formed The Linda Sharrock Network (Discogs ID: 4919745) and Linda Sharrock & The Chants Band, releasing material on Improvising Beings (a French free-improvisation label). A live document Live, Vol.1: Bab-Ilo, 2016.08.25 appeared on Improvising Beings. She appears on They Begin To Speak with collaborators Julien Palomo, Makoto Sato, Itaru Oki, and Yoram Rosilio.

Collected Works (2019, Edition Hawara, Austria) — compiled vocal, compositional, and arrangement credits.
