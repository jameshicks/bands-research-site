Mike Kelley

American visual artist, performer, and musician. Born 27 October 1954, Detroit, Michigan; died 31 January 2012, South Pasadena, California (apparent suicide). Discogs artist ID: 75335. Name variations: Kelzo, M. Kelley, MK, Mike (The Pirate) Kelley.

One of the most significant American artists of the late 20th century, best known for work in installation art, performance, and video. In the music world he is primarily associated with his long involvement with the Detroit art-noise collective Destroy All Monsters, his artwork for Sonic Youth’s Dirty (1992), and a cluster of Los Angeles–based art-music collaborations during the 1980s–2000s.

Biography

Detroit Formation (1954–1977)

Kelley grew up in suburban Detroit. The Detroit art scene of the early 1970s — shaped by the aftershocks of the MC5, the Stooges, and an aggressively DIY sensibility — formed his foundational aesthetic. While still in the area he connected with Cary Loren and Jim Shaw, both students at the University of Michigan; together with Niagara they formed Destroy All Monsters around 1973–1974. The band was less a conventional rock group than an art-noise collective generating performances, zines, Super-8 films, and audio assemblages. Kelley played vocals and percussion in this earliest incarnation (1973–1976).

He subsequently relocated to Los Angeles to attend CalArts, graduating in 1978. His mentor there was conceptual artist John Baldessari. From that point his primary career developed in the LA gallery and museum world, though he maintained a strong engagement with music and sound art throughout.

Los Angeles Art-Music Scene (1978–1999)

In LA Kelley became part of a circle of artist-musicians who moved between visual art practice and performance/recording. Key formations included:

Extended Organ — Kelley was a founding member alongside Paul McCarthy (fellow CalArts-connected artist), Tom Recchion, Joe Potts, and Fredrik Nilsen. Extended Organ operated as an improvising ensemble focused on drone and noise performance. They released two albums: XOXO (Birdman Records, 1999) and Vibe (Important Records, 2019).

Gobbler — A looser formation with Paul McCarthy and Cameron Jamie. No full-length discography surfaces in Discogs under this entry.

The Peristaltic Airwaves (1987) — Kelley released a cassette on High Performance Records (cat. HP012) credited under his own name. Side A, “The Peristaltic Airwaves, 1986,” featured collaborators including Jack Skelley and Nic Greene. Side B, “Plato’s Cave, Rothko’s Chapel, Lincoln’s Profile,” was a Sonic Youth performance recorded by Carol Parkinson — featuring Kim Gordon, Lee Ranaldo, Thurston Moore, and Steve Shelley. This is the earliest documented Kelley/Sonic Youth connection in Discogs.

Tellus - The Audio Cassette Magazine -18- - Experimental Theater (1987) — Kelley contributed cover art to issue #18 of the influential Tellus Audio Cassette Magazine, the New York-based audio art series. Curated by Carol Parkinson and Claudia Gould.

Ann Magnuson’s The Luv Show (Geffen Records, 1995) — Kelley played drums and percussion on this album by Ann Magnuson, the downtown New York performance artist. Also on the session: Don Fleming (guitar, theremin, producer), Dave Rick (guitar), and Art Byington (guitar; also a DAM-adjacent figure).

Dirty Artwork (1992)

Kelley created the artwork for Sonic Youth’s Dirty (DGC Records, 1992), the band’s major-label breakout produced by Butch Vig. The cover image — Kelley’s assemblage of thrift-store stuffed animals arranged in a heap — became one of the most recognizable sleeve images of the 1990s alternative era and one of the best-known instances of fine-art crossover into rock packaging. Art direction on the release was by Kevin Reagan. Photography by Richard Kern.

The connection to Sonic Youth preceded this commission: Kelley’s 1987 cassette had featured them on Side B, and his DIY/abject aesthetic aligned closely with the band’s interests.

Later Period (1996–2012)

Destroy All Monsters — Silver Wedding Anniversary (Sympathy For The Record Industry, 1996) — Kelley returned to active participation in DAM for this retrospective/reunion release. He is credited as lead vocals (on track 8), performer, producer, and artwork. Fellow participants included Cary Loren, Jim Shaw, Niagara, and various later-period members.

DK720VB12 (Not On Label, 2006, UK) — A release by The Vitamin B12 & Deepkiss 720 on which Kelley provided “Featuring [Recordings Provided By]” — recordings from his archive used as source material, alongside similar contributions from artist Albert Oehlen and others.

Prince Rama Of Ayodhya — Shadow Temple (Paw Tracks, 2010) — Kelley is credited as “Recorded By” under his nickname “Kelzo.” Also recorded by Dave Portner (Avey Tare) and Josh Dibb (Deakin) of Animal Collective. The Paw Tracks label was Animal Collective’s imprint.

Destroy All Monsters 2xLP (Munster Records, 2014, Spain) — A posthumous retrospective release compiling early DAM material. Kelley is credited for vocals and percussion on tracks 1-2 through 1-11 (the original 1973–1976 period recordings).
