Epic Soundtracks

Oct 19, 20268 min read

Epic Soundtracks

Discogs ID: 264772 Real name: Kevin Paul Godfrey Born: 23 March 1959, UK Died: 5 November 1997, London, England Aliases: Amphibious (2), Amphibious Landing Craft, Kevin Godfrey (Discogs ID: 806185 as member credit) Name variations: E. Soundtracks, E.S., ES, Epic, Epic Soundtrack, Epic The Artist, Soundtracks

Drummer, pianist, vocalist, visual artist, and multi-instrumentalist. Co-founder of Swell Maps with his brother Nikki Sudden (real name Adrian Godfrey). Responsible for much of the band’s cover artwork and sleeve design alongside his drumming. His artistic name is a tribute to the Epic label and an album by Kevin Ayers.

Biography

Swell Maps era (1977–1980)

Epic Soundtracks co-founded Swell Maps in Birmingham with Nikki Sudden in the mid-1970s, the band eventually releasing on their own Rather Records and then Rough Trade. On the early singles — 5A-Sided 45 (1978), Read About Seymour (1978), A Trip To Marineville (1979), Dresden Style (1979) — Epic contributed drums/percussion, cover artwork, sleeve design, and co-writing. His visual practice was integral to the band’s identity from the start.

The debut LP A Trip To Marineville (1979, Rough Trade) had Epic on drums and credited with cover and co-writing. The second LP …In “Jane From Occupied Europe” (1980) followed. After the Maps compilation Micro-Chips & Fish (1979), the band dissolved around 1980.

Early solo work (1981–1982)

Epic moved immediately into solo work on Rough Trade. His debut solo release, the 7” Popular Classical (1981), was entirely self-produced and self-performed — vocals, piano, bass, drums, melodica, all credited to him alone. Engineer was Vic Sweeney.

In 1982 he collaborated with fellow Swell Maps member Jowe Head on the EP Rain, Rain, Rain (Rough Trade), jointly produced and co-written. Epic played drums, piano, and drum machine; Jowe played guitar, bass, and electronics. The session brought in cellist Chopper (3), violinist Ben Coleman, and vocalist Carmel McCourt. Recorded by John A. Rivers, Jon Dee, and Martin Daley.

He also appeared on the Rough Trade compilation Collision Time (1982), which gathered various Swell Maps-adjacent artists.

Session work: Crime & The City Solution (1985–1987)

Epic joined Crime & The City Solution — the Australian post-punk group led by vocalist Simon Bonney — as drummer for the key mid-period recordings on Mute. The lineup at this point included Rowland S. Howard and Mick Harvey on guitars (both ex-The Birthday Party), Harry Howard on bass, and Engineer Flood.

He also appeared on the Wings Of Desire compilation (1989, Elektra Nonesuch) — the Wim Wenders soundtrack — playing drums on the Crime & The City Solution track alongside Simon Bonney, Rowland S. Howard, and Mick Harvey.

The Collision Time Revisited compilation (1989, Mute/Restless) collected the Crime & The City Solution material and credits him on drums, piano, organ, and backing vocals.

Session work: These Immortal Souls (1987–1989)

Epic drummed on These Immortal Souls’ debut LP Get Lost (Don’t Lie) (1987, Mute). The band was led by Rowland S. Howard post-Birthday Party/Crime; members included Harry Howard (bass), Genevieve McGuckin (piano, organ), and various session players. Engineers were Flood, Tony Cohen, and Alan Moulder. Epic played drums and school bells across all tracks.

He is also listed as a member of These Immortal Souls on Discogs (under the name Kevin Godfrey, ID 806185).

Nikki Sudden collaborations and wider network (1984–1993)

Epic continued working with his brother and the wider post-Swell Maps network throughout the 1980s–early 1990s:

  • Shame For The Angels EP (1984, Pawn Hearts Records) — produced by Epic
  • Robespierre’s Velvet Basement (1985, Glass Records) — Nikki Sudden & Dave Kusworth & The Jacobites; Epic on drums, percussion, piano, and co-producer alongside Nikki Sudden, Dave Kusworth, and Bob Lamb. Also featured Andy Wickett, Mark Lemon, and Tyla on slide guitar.
  • Lost In A Sea Of Scarves (1985, What’s So Funny About..) — drums, organ, backing vocals
  • Strawberry Deutsche Mark (1986, Constrictor) — drums
  • Texas (1986, Creation Records) — performer and producer
  • When The Rain Comes / Country Girl (1986, Glass Records) — performer
  • I Knew Buffalo Bill (1987, Flicknife Records) — primary credit alongside Jeremy Gluck, Nikki Sudden, Rowland S. Howard, and Jeffrey Lee Pierce; Epic co-wrote B3 and produced. Tony Cohen engineered and mixed.
  • Marry Me (Lie Lie) (1987, Mute) — band member credit
  • Train Out Of It (1987, Antar) — Swell Maps retrospective compilation; Epic co-compiled, performed, and co-wrote
  • The Ragged School (1986, Twin/Tone) — These Immortal Souls US release; drums
  • Turn Of The Screw (1990, ReR Megacorp) — lyrics and music
  • Jacobites (1993, Regency Sound) — performer alongside Nikki Sudden, Dave Kusworth, Andy Wickett, Mark Lemon, Tyla

Solo peak (1992–1996)

Epic’s most substantial solo output came in the 1990s:

Rise Above (1992, Rough Trade) — self-produced LP (co-produced with Victor Van Vugt). All compositions and arrangements by Epic. Strings arranged by Henry Olsen; engineering split between Wharton Tiers (NYC) and Victor Van Vugt. Photography by Bleddyn Butcher.

Sleeping Star (1994, Normal Records, Germany) — produced by Epic, with Henry Olsen again on string arrangements. Six-pressing run across multiple formats.

Change My Life (1996, Bar/None Records, US) — final completed studio LP released in his lifetime. Produced by Henry Olsen and co-produced by Epic. Engineered and mixed by Nick Terry and Henry Olsen. Epic credited as songwriter on all tracks. Support from Kevin Junior (guitar, backing vocals).

He also appeared on:

  • The Lemonheads — Car Button Cloth (1996, Atlantic/Tag Recordings) — Written-By credit
  • The Lemonheads — The Outdoor Type (1997, Atlantic) — drums, organ, backing vocals

Death and posthumous releases

Epic Soundtracks died on 5 November 1997 in London. Cause of death was initially reported as unknown. He was 38.

Posthumous releases include:

  • Everything Is Temporary (1999, Innerstate Records) — compilation of previously unreleased/rare material
  • Good Things (2005, DBK Works) — recorded with Kevin Junior (guitar/vocals), mixed by Henry Olsen and Nikki Sudden, with John A. Rivers. Remastered by Matt Snow.
  • Film Soundtracks (2017, Troubadour) — composed piano pieces
  • Wild Smile (An Anthology) (2012, Troubadour) — anthology compilation

His Swell Maps material has been reissued extensively via Mute: Train Out Of It (multiple pressings), International Rescue (1999, Alive Records), Extra (2024, Mute), and The John Peel Sessions (2025, Mute/The Grey Area).
