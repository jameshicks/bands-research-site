Lana Del Rey

American singer, songwriter, producer, and poet. Born Elizabeth Woolridge Grant, June 21, 1985, New York City, NY. Also performed as Lizzy Grant, May Jailer, and Sparkle Jump Rope Queen. Discogs artist ID: 2445772.

Biography

Early Life and Pre-Fame Projects (1985–2009)

Grant grew up in Lake Placid, a small village in upstate New York. At fifteen she was sent to Kent School in Connecticut, where she began developing her musical interests. After graduating in 2003 she spent time on Long Island, learning guitar from her uncle and performing at local nightclubs under names including “Sparkle Jump Rope Queen” and “Lizzy Grant and the Phenomena.”

In fall 2004 she enrolled at Fordham University in New York to study metaphysics. Between 2005 and 2009 she worked under the names May Jailer and Lizzy Grant, accumulating a body of demo material — later estimated at over 200 songs — much of which leaked online. A 2006 recording session produced a collection known in bootleg circles as Sirens: The Lost 2006 Album (circulated unofficially; eventually surfaced on Discogs as Danni Discos issue 31063922).

Debut Album and the Lana Del Ray Persona (2010)

Her first formal album, Lana Del Ray A.K.A. Lizzy Grant, was released digitally in 2010 on 5 Points Records (a subsidiary of Nudgie Music LLC; Discogs label ID: 25716). Produced by David Kahne, it contained 13 tracks of folk-inflected indie pop — “Kill Kill,” “Mermaid Motel,” “Pawn Shop Blues,” “Yayo” — credited to the name Lana Del Ray (with an ‘a’). Photography was by her sister Caroline “Chuck” H. Grant (uncredited). The album was released only as an MP3 download and gained little commercial traction at the time; it has since been heavily bootlegged and issued on vinyl by third parties.

Breakthrough: Video Games and Born To Die (2011–2012)

The self-released single “Video Games” / “Blue Jeans” (2011) on Stranger Records (Discogs label ID: 253010) became a viral sensation, its homemade YouTube video circulating widely before any label deal was in place. A follow-up digital single, “Blue Jeans (Penguin Prison Remix)” (2011), appeared on RCRD LBL.

Her major-label debut Born To Die was released on Interscope Records / Polydor in early 2012 (Discogs release ID: 3354383). Mastering by John Davis (4) at Metropolis. Management throughout this period: Ben Mawson and Ed Millett. The album generated a string of singles — “Video Games,” “Blue Jeans,” “National Anthem,” “Summertime Sadness,” “Ride” — and spawned a companion EP, Paradise (2012, Interscope/Polydor), expanding the album’s cinematic Americana aesthetic. The title track also appeared in a drum & bass rework by Marcus Intalex on Soul:R.

“Summertime Sadness” gained an unexpected second life via a 2013 trance remix by Cedric Gervais (released on Vertigo/Universal).

Ultraviolence (2014)

Ultraviolence (Interscope/Polydor, 2014) marked a shift toward heavier, guitar-driven production. Discogs credits for the main pressings are sparse (no producer listed at the release level on the primary Discogs entry), but the album is widely attributed to producer Dan Auerbach of The Black Keys — a connection not captured in the Discogs DB for the editions queried. The tracklist includes: “Cruel World,” “Ultraviolence,” “Shades Of Cool,” “Brooklyn Baby,” “West Coast,” “Sad Girl,” “Pretty When You Cry,” “Money Power Glory,” “Fucked My Way Up To The Top,” “Old Money,” “The Other Woman.”

Honeymoon (2015)

Honeymoon (Interscope/Polydor, 2015; Discogs release ID: 7461030) was co-produced by Del Rey, Kieron Menzies, and Rick Nowels. Orchestral arrangements by Patrick Warren. Photography by her sister Chuck Grant and Neil Krug. Mastered by Adam Ayan. Del Rey herself is listed as producer — the first album where her production credit is explicit in the DB. Additional recording by Chris Garcia (5) and Trevor Yasuda.

Norman Fucking Rockwell! (2019)

Norman Fucking Rockwell! (Interscope/Polydor, 2019; Discogs release ID: 14052058) is her most thoroughly documented album in the DB. Produced predominantly by Jack Antonoff (co-producing with Del Rey on most tracks), with Rick Nowels and Kieron Menzies / Dean Reid on select cuts. Engineer / mixer throughout: Laura Sisk and Jack Antonoff. Mastered by Chris Gehringer.

Chemtrails Over The Country Club (2021)

Chemtrails Over The Country Club (Interscope/Polydor, 2021; Discogs release ID: 16883925) continued the Antonoff partnership, with Rick Nowels producing “Yosemite.” Notable guest appearances: Weyes Blood and Zella Day on “For Free” (a Joni Mitchell cover); Nikki Lane on “Breaking Up Slowly.” Mastered by Chris Gehringer.

Did You Know That There’s A Tunnel Under Ocean Blvd (2023)

  • “Season of the Witch” (2019, Polydor) — Del Rey credited as producer and vocalist
  • Violet Bent Backwards Over The Grass (2020, Interscope/Polydor) — spoken word poetry album; Del Rey credited as composer, author, reader
  • “Watercolor Eyes” (2022, Interscope/Polydor) — composed and performed for HBO’s Euphoria
  • “Buddy’s Rendezvous” (2022, Bella Union) — Del Rey covers a Fritz Garber song
  • “Say Yes to Heaven” (2023, Interscope/Polydor)
  • “The Grants” (2023, Polydor)
  • “Bluebird” (2025, Interscope/Polydor)
  • “Henry, Come On” (2025, Interscope/Polydor) — Del Rey as composer, producer, vocalist

Guest appearances include: backing vocals on The Weeknd’s “Starboy” (2016, XO); vocals and co-composer credit on Bleachers’ Take the Sadness Out of Saturday Night (2021, RCA).

Family Credits

Del Rey’s immediate family appears in the credits of several releases:

  • Caroline “Chuck” H. Grant (sister) — photography on debut (uncredited), Honeymoon, Lust For Life; co-writer on “Sweet Carolina” (Blue Banisters)
  • Robert Grant Jr. (brother) — co-writer and pianist on “Sweet Carolina” (Blue Banisters)
  • Wynton Grant — violin on Blue Banisters (“Arcadia,” “Violets For Roses”)

Connections

  • Stevie Nicks — guested on “Beautiful People Beautiful Problems” (Lust For Life)
  • Sean Lennon — guested on “Tomorrow Never Came” (Lust For Life)
  • The Weeknd — title track guest on Lust For Life; Del Rey provided backing vocals on his “Starboy”
  • ASAP Rocky — featured on “Summer Bummer” and “Groupie Love” (Lust For Life)
  • Weyes Blood — guest on “For Free” (Chemtrails)
  • Miles Kane — co-wrote and sang on “Dealer” (Blue Banisters)
  • Zachary Dawes — bassist/producer, also of Mini Mansions

  • Brad Nowell — Sublime, credited as co-writer on “Doin’ Time” (NFR!)
  • Sublime (2) — Eric Wilson and Bud Gaugh performed on “Doin’ Time”

Open Questions

  • Discogs credits for Ultraviolence are notably thin — no producer listed in the DB entries queried. Dan Auerbach’s production role is widely reported but not captured in Discogs for the pressings checked.
  • Born To Die track-level producers (Rick Nowels, Justin Parker, and others reported in press) are not listed in the Discogs entry for the main UK/Europe pressing (3354383).
  • The “Sirens” 2006 material (Danni Discos release) appears to be an unofficial bootleg issue; official status unclear.
  • Pre-Fordham demos and the May Jailer recordings exist only as leaks; no Discogs entries for those projects.
