Jonathan Heine

Bassist and multi-instrumentalist, active primarily in the German post-metal / progressive metal scene. Best known as the long-running bassist of The Ocean (2) (Berlin/Hamburg), appearing on their core catalog from the band’s earliest releases through the mid-2000s. Also a member of American Music Club during their 2008 reunion, and of experimental Berlin outfit Ruin (8). Also credited as “Joni Heine.” Discogs artist ID: 625426. Active from at least 1993 through 2025 per Discogs credits.

Name variations on record: Heine, J. Heine, Jonatan Heine, Jonathan ‘Joni’ Heine, Joni Heine.

Overview

Heine’s earliest documented credit is an executive-producer role on the 1993 Austrian self-release Lifetime by Hot Pants Road Club — likely a pre-Berlin connection or youthful production venture.

His core identity, however, is as a founding-era bassist of The Ocean (2), the Berlin-based progressive/post-metal collective led by guitarist and composer Robin Staps. Heine played on the band’s first three studio releases: the self-released 2002 demo, the debut proper Fogdiver (2003, Make My Day Records), and the follow-up Fluxion (2004, Make My Day Records / Throne Records). He continued through Aeolian (2005, Metal Blade Records), The Ocean’s major-label breakthrough, and the ambitious double album Precambrian (2007, Metal Blade Records), on which his bass credit is limited to specific tracks (disc 2, track 6), suggesting a partial lineup overlap with other bassists including Mike Pilat and Hannes Hüfken. The 2009 Fluxion re-issue on Metal Blade also credits Heine on bass guitar, reflecting a revised/expanded version of that early material.

Alongside The Ocean work, Heine appears as a member of Ruin (8) (also stylized RUIN), a Berlin experimental project. Their split release ½ Skull (2011, Viva Hate Records) — a collaboration with Solistenensemble Kaleidoskop, a chamber music ensemble — credits Heine on bass and as co-writer. Other Ruin (8) members include Jochen Arbeit (Einstürzende Neubauten guitarist), Martin Eder, Roderick Miller, and Thomas Fietz. The band bridges metal and contemporary classical composition.

Separately, Heine contributed a single track’s music (“Music By,” track 3) to Suspension (2012, Cheap Satanism Records), an album by Bee And Flower — the project of Dana Schechter. This is notable because Schechter also played with American Music Club during the same reunion period. Heine appears under the Joni Heine alias in Bee And Flower’s Discogs member list (artist ID 1577274), confirming the identity.

The AMC reunion credit is from Food, Sex And Shelter (Live Cambridge 2008) (released 2021 as a self-release), a 2008 Cambridge performance featuring Mark Eitzel, Vudi, Schechter, Steve Didelot, and Heine as performers. His instrument in that lineup is not specified in the Discogs credits — context from other vault research (AMC pages) may clarify.

A 2019 guitar credit on the single Swahili by Swan Williams & Martin Gallop (Kontor Records) suggests continued session activity in Germany’s commercial/pop-adjacent scene.
