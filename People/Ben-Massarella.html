Ben Massarella

Drummer, percussionist, and occasional producer. Also known as Benny Massarella, Benedetto Massarella. Discogs ID: 213689. Chicago-based. Core member of Red Red Meat and early Califone; longstanding session collaborator for Modest Mouse, Iron And Wine, and others.

Name variations on record: B Massarella, B. Massarella, Ben Massaralla, Ben Masserella, Benedetto Massarella, Benny, Benny Massarella, Massarella.

Overview

Massarella came up in the Chicago indie underground of the early 1990s as part of the Red Red Meat nucleus around Tim Rutili and Brian Deck. He appears on Bunny Gets Paid (1995, Sub Pop) — the band’s most celebrated record — alongside Rutili, Deck, Tim Hurley, and Neil Rosario, with Brad Wood producing. He also co-wrote (Written-By credit) the earlier self-titled Red Red Meat LP (1992, Perishable Records).

When Red Red Meat dissolved, Massarella stayed in the orbit, playing on the transitional 1998 Loftus LP (Perishable Records) with Rutili, Deck, Hurley, Bundy K. Brown, Phil Spirito, and Doug Scharin. That same year he co-produced the debut LPs for two other Perishable Records acts: the self-titled Orso EP/LP (with Spirito and Deck) and the Drumhead (2) LP (with Deck, Tony Maimone, and Sheila Bosco).

He became a core member of Califone from the 1998 debut (Flydaddy Records) through Roots & Crowns (2006, Thrill Jockey), credited with drums, hand drums, slit gong, pistons, gourd percussion, glass percussion, oak chimes, ribbon crasher, zuni rattle, and various exotica — a palette that grew more elaborate with each record. He also held co-writing credits across Quicksand/Cradlesnakes (2003), Heron King Blues (2004), and Roots & Crowns (2006).

After 2006 his Califone credits shift: he appears on All My Friends Are Funeral Singers (2009, Dead Oceans) playing percussion, handclaps, drums, steel drums, vibraphone, and fiddle (drumming on fiddle), and again on Echo Mine (2020, Jealous Butcher Records) with drums and percussion — suggesting his involvement was periodic rather than fully ended. He remained in the broader Rutili/Deck/Orso network throughout.

Concurrently from 2000 onward, Massarella built a parallel career as a session percussionist. He appeared on Modest Mouse’s The Moon & Antarctica (2000, Epic) — produced by Brian Deck — and on the Matador pressing of Everywhere And His Nasty Parlour Tricks (2001, Epic). He contributed to Fruit Bats’s Echolocation (2001, Perishable Records), to Iron And Wine’s Kiss Each Other Clean (2011, 4AD, produced by Deck), and to the Iron & Wine Archive Series Volume No. 6 (2024, Black Cricket Recording Co.).

The Modest Mouse connection deepened significantly: he played percussion and sonic textures on Strangers To Ourselves (2015, Epic) and appeared extensively across The Golden Casket (2021, Epic), credited with bongos, snare, claves, cowbell, handbell, xylophone, electronic drums, cymbal, percussion, shaker, tambourine, tom tom, and vibraslap across multiple tracks.

He also collaborated with Tim Rutili outside Califone on the duo project 10 Seconds To Collapse (2018, Jealous Butcher Records), credited with drums and percussion alongside Rutili and Craig Ross (5).

A separate Orso thread runs through his discography: the Ask Your Neighbor LP (2008, Contraphonic) credits him on percussion, and an undated Orso self-released recording (First Egg: Part One Transmission, Transference, Transformation) also lists him. He appears on the Orso Christmas release Is Xmas Tomorrow? (2006, self-released).

Additional session credits include: Califone-adjacent artist September 000’s September 000 (2002, Ace Fu Records, percussion/multi-instrumentalist); Fire the Show’s Saint The Fire Show (2002, Perishable Records, drums); and Like A Smoking Gun In Front Of Me’s 2005 EP (File 13 Records, percussion).

Discogs groups listing also notes membership in Friends Of Betty (Discogs ID: 1048665) and Lake Wandawega Resort Band (Discogs ID: 1300182), though no releases for those groups appear in the DB.
