Overview

Al Bloch is a bassist, vocalist, and songwriter from Seattle who relocated to Los Angeles with Bombardiers in 1985 and later to Roanoke, Virginia in 2001. The younger brother of Fastbacks guitarist Kurt Bloch, he accumulated credits across five bands — “S” Box, Bad Scene, Bombardiers, Cheaters, and My Favorite Martian — working in punk, power pop, indie rock, and alternative rock from the early 1980s onward. His deepest collaborations were with Peter Stahl and Franz Stahl of Scream and Wool, with 20 and 19 shared releases respectively, suggesting the Bombardiers’ LA move brought him into sustained contact with that DC-rooted circle; producers Rob Schnapf and Tom Rothrock appear alongside those records with 15 credits each. Early label ties run to Bong Load Records, External Records, and London Records in the early 1990s, with a single Grand Royal appearance in 1997.

Notes (web)

How Al Bloch connected with Pete and Franz Stahl

Short answer: The connection almost certainly formed through the Los Angeles scene, not through an earlier DC/Seattle circuit. No source documents a prior relationship between Bloch and the Stahls before both parties were simultaneously in LA c.1990.

Timeline of convergence:

  • Bloch relocated from Seattle to Los Angeles with Bombardiers in 1985 and remained LA-based. (Discogs profile, ID 504122)
  • Bloch joined Concrete Blonde as full-time bassist for the album Free (IRS Records, 1989), allowing Johnette Napolitano to focus on vocals. He departed before Bloodletting (1990), leaving him without a band. (Wikipedia: Concrete Blonde, as of 2026-11-11; Encyclopedia.com: Concrete Blonde)
  • Scream collapsed mid-US-tour in 1990 — bassist Skeeter Thompson had already left, then Dave Grohl departed to join Nirvana. Pete Stahl has described the band ending up in LA “completely by accident” due to tour problems. After Grohl’s departure, Pete and Franz Stahl chose to stay in North Hollywood rather than return to DC. (fearandloathingfanzine.com/scream.html, Pete Stahl interview)
  • With both Bloch (LA-based, recently departed Concrete Blonde) and the Stahls (stranded in North Hollywood, Scream over) simultaneously in the LA area in 1990, Wool formed. Multiple sources describe Bloch being “introduced to” the Stahl brothers and former Peter Moffett (ex-Government Issue) at the dawning of the 1990s. (Wikipedia: Wool (band), as of 2026-11-11; Dine Alone Records artist page)

What no source says: None of the sources checked — Wikipedia (Wool, Concrete Blonde, Scream, Franz Stahl articles), the Fear and Loathing fanzine Pete Stahl interview, the Dine Alone Records bio, Encyclopedia.com, Green Monkey Records, Last.fm, or Spirit of Metal — gives an account of a pre-LA connection between Bloch and the Stahls. No source names a DC show, a touring circuit, or a mutual DC contact as the link. The word “introduced” that appears in several bios is used generically and implies no prior relationship.

Conclusion: The Bloch–Stahl connection is an LA-scene introduction, most likely c.1990, after Bloch left Concrete Blonde and the Stahl brothers were newly stranded in North Hollywood. The LA base is what all three parties shared; there is no evidence of an earlier DC/Seattle circuit connecting them.
