Amos Garrett

American-Canadian guitarist, born 26 November 1941 in Detroit, Michigan. Discogs artist ID: 370606.

Garrett is best known for his guitar solo on Maria Muldaur’s “Midnight at the Oasis” (1973), one of the most recognizable guitar lines of the decade. A deeply versatile session player and bandleader, he has recorded with more than 150 artists, including Emmylou Harris, Bonnie Raitt, Todd Rundgren, and Stevie Wonder. Jimmy Page cited him as one of his favorite American guitarists in a 1975 Rolling Stone interview.

Though born in Detroit, Garrett became embedded in the Canadian folk and country-rock scenes of the late 1960s before migrating into the Bearsville-connected American roots music world of the early 1970s — a world built around Paul Butterfield, Geoff and Maria Muldaur, and Albert Grossman’s Woodstock orbit. He remained active as a primary artist and touring musician through the 1990s and 2000s.

Early Career and Canadian Roots (1968–1971)

Garrett’s first Discogs-documented credits place him in Canada and the UK folk circuit of 1968. He played guitar on:

  • More Hits From Tin Can Alley (1968, Vanguard, UK) — early folk session
  • Pottery Pie (1968, Reprise, US) — Noel Harrison’s LP
  • The Travellers Applaud Canada (1968, Arc Records, Canada)
  • This Way Is My Way (1969, Capitol, Canada) — Anne Murray
  • Honey Wheat & Laughter (1970, Capitol, Canada)

His most significant early credit is the Great Speckled Bird self-titled LP (1969, Ampex Records), the country-rock band fronted by Ian Tyson and Sylvia Tyson. Garrett is credited as Lead Guitar and, notably, as Written-By on track B4 — one of his few documented songwriting credits. The album was produced by Todd Rundgren. Great Speckled Bird’s lineup also included Jeff Gutcheon (piano), N.D. Smart II (drums), Jim Colegrove (bass), and pedal steel player Buddy Cage.

He also appeared on:

  • In My Own Time (1971, Paramount) — Karen Dalton’s LP (guitar)
  • …Beautiful Lies You Could Live In. (1971, Reprise) — Eric Andersen LP (electric guitar)
  • Straight, Clean And Simple (1971, Capitol, Canada)

Bearsville and the Butterfield–Muldaur Axis (1972–1974)

By 1972 Garrett was firmly embedded in the Bearsville Records / Woodstock scene around Albert Grossman. This period produced his densest cluster of significant credits.

Hungry Chuck (1972, Bearsville)

Before Better Days, Garrett was a full member of Hungry Chuck, a loose ensemble on Bearsville whose self-titled 1972 LP (release ID: 2605557) listed him as “Hungry Chuck Is: Lead Guitar, Trombone, Vocals.” Fellow members included Peter Ecklund (cornet/trumpet), Ben Keith (pedal steel/dobro), Jeff Gutcheon (piano/organ), Jim Colegrove (bass), and N.D. Smart II (drums).

Eric Von Schmidt — 2nd Right 3rd Row (1972, Poppy)

One of Garrett’s most eclectic credits. Eric Von Schmidt’s album (release ID: 1709221) is an anything-goes folk-blues affair, and Garrett’s credited roles give a sense of his range: Vocals, Guitar, Slide Guitar [Bottleneck], Bass, Trombone, Body Percussion [Feet], Vocalese [Duck Call]. The record also featured Geoff Muldaur (vocals, guitar, slide guitar [knife], clarinet), Maria Muldaur (backing vocals, bass drum, percussion), and Paul Butterfield (harmonica). Producer: Michael Cuscuna. Engineer: Nick Jameson. This session captures the Cambridge-to-Woodstock network at its most intimate.

Geoff & Maria Muldaur — Sweet Potatoes (1972, Reprise)

The final studio LP under the joint Geoff & Maria Muldaur billing (release ID: 3508685). Garrett plays Guitar and Vocals throughout. Other credits: Bill Keith (pedal steel), Joe Boyd (producer on B5), Eric Von Schmidt (cover art). Engineers: Mark Harman and John Haeny.

Bobby Charles (1972, Bearsville)

Garrett is credited as “Musician” on the self-titled Bobby Charles LP (release ID: 2563226).

Paul Butterfield’s Better Days (1973–1974, Bearsville)

Garrett was a core member of Paul Butterfield’s Better Days, the band Paul Butterfield formed after dissolving the Butterfield Blues Band. The lineup across both albums:

Better Days (1973, Bearsville, release ID: 5711039): Garrett plays guitar across most of the album (A1–B2, B4, B5) and bass on B3, where he also sings lead. Maria Muldaur contributes backing vocals on B1.

It All Comes Back (1973, Bearsville, release ID: 2518775): The band is primary artist; Garrett credited as “Musician.” Guests include Bobbye Hall (congas) and Bobby Charles (vocals). Nick Jameson served as both engineer and producer. Bob Ludwig mastered. Design by Milton Glaser.

Learn To Love It (1974, Bearsville, release ID: 1690046): Garrett plays guitar on this Jesse Winchester LP, a distinct project from the Better Days recordings but part of the same Bearsville period.

Maria Muldaur — Maria Muldaur (1973, Reprise)

This is the album that made Garrett famous. He plays Electric Guitar on track A2 only — which is “Midnight at the Oasis,” written by David Nichtern. The rest of the album features entirely different guitarists per track: Ry Cooder (A1), Mac Rebennack / Dr. John (B1, B5), etc. Producers: Joe Boyd and Lenny Waronker.

The Canadian pressing (release ID: 15820424, master 122044) documents Garrett’s credit at Electric Guitar on A2. His solo — built on a slithering, jazz-inflected phrase over a bossa nova groove — became an instantly recognizable signature of 1970s AM radio.

Mid-1970s Session Work (1974–1977)

Maria Muldaur — Waitress In A Donut Shop (1974, Reprise)

Garrett returns as a key sideman across multiple tracks (release ID: 2222250): A4, B2, B3, B5, B6, credited Guitar and Backing Vocals. This is a much denser session than the first album, featuring an enormous cast:

Producers: Joe Boyd and Lenny Waronker (B2). Engineers: Donn Landee, Lee Herschberg.

Emmylou Harris — Elite Hotel (1975, Reprise)

Garrett plays Electric Guitar on B1 — one track on this landmark country-rock LP (release ID: 3718262). The album is dominated by James Burton on electric guitar and Brian Ahern as producer. Garrett’s presence here, alongside Burton and Bill Payne, shows his reach into the country-rock establishment.

Geoff Muldaur — Is Having A Wonderful Time (1975, Reprise)

A lavish, genre-hopping production recorded in New York and London (release ID: 1693677), produced by Joe Boyd. Garrett plays guitar (A3, A4B), lead guitar (A2, B4), and harmony vocals (A1), and is credited Arranged By [Vocals] on A1 — a rare arranger credit. The album draws in an extraordinary cast: Richard Thompson (lead guitar, B5), John Cale (viola, B1), Ron Carter (bass), Bernard Purdie (drums), James Booker (organ/piano), Merl Saunders (electric piano/organ), Bill Keith (pedal steel), Stephen Bruton (guitar), Cornell Dupree (guitar), Fritz Richmond (jug).

Maria Muldaur — Sweet Harmony (1976, Reprise)

Garrett is credited for Harmony Vocals and Bass Vocals on this LP (release ID: 1580097, master 239014) — a vocal contribution rather than guitar.

Jesse Winchester — Learn To Love It (1974, Bearsville, release ID: 1690046)

Guitar credit on this Canadian singer-songwriter’s Bearsville LP.

Geoff Muldaur & Amos Garrett (1978, Flying Fish)

The duo LP Geoff Muldaur & Amos Garrett (1978, Flying Fish, release ID: 2825179) is the only album where Garrett shares top billing with a collaborator. Recorded with a small ensemble:

Key Later Session Credits

  • Emmylou Harris — Evangeline (1981, Warner Bros., release ID: 874150): Lead Guitar on B5, alongside James Burton, Albert Lee, Ricky Skaggs, and Mac Rebennack.
  • Jerry Garcia — Garcia (1990, Grateful Dead Records, release ID: 4267921): Trombone — an unusual credit in the Garcia discography.
  • Maria Muldaur — Classic Live! (2003, DIG MUSIC, release ID: 5042692): Guitar, appearing alongside David Nichtern and Mike Finnigan in a band built around the 1970s Muldaur repertoire.
  • Emmylou Harris — Pieces Of The Sky (2004 reissue, Reprise/Rhino): Electric Guitar on the original 1975 recording.
  • Waylon Jennings — Ain’t Living Long Like This (1978, Warner Bros.): Electric Guitar.
  • Rodney Crowell — But What Will The Neighbors Think (1980, Warner Bros.): Guitar [2nd].

Open Questions

  1. Garrett’s Discogs profile says “American-Canadian” but the brief describes him as Canadian — which nationality did he hold when working? Did he become a Canadian citizen, or was his Canadian association purely based on where he worked?
  2. On the Maria Muldaur album, is Garrett credited on track A2 specifically as “Midnight at the Oasis,” or does Discogs map the track position to a different song? (The Canadian pressing credits him on A2 — confirm this is indeed “Midnight at the Oasis.“)
  3. Is Garrett credited as a songwriter on any track on the Better Days albums beyond the single songwriting credit on the Great Speckled Bird LP (B4)?
  4. What tracks specifically does Garrett play on in Waitress In A Donut Shop? Discogs credits him on A4, B2, B3, B5, B6 — are all five tracks confirmed, and is one of those a reprise or bonus version of “Midnight at the Oasis”?
  5. What is the Amos Garrett, Doug Sahm, Gene Taylor Band (Discogs ID: 2903468)? Was this a touring group or a recording project, and when was it active?
  6. What is The Dirty Shames (2) (Discogs ID: 2463693)? This appears to be an early Canadian group predating the Bearsville period — what years was it active and who else was in it?
  7. The Amosbehavin’ LP (1982, Cuppamore Records) has only one additional credit: producer Bill Garrett — is Bill Garrett a relative, and what was Cuppamore Records?
  8. Garrett played Trombone on the Jerry Garcia Garcia LP (1990) — this is an unusual credit in both his discography and Garcia’s. On which specific track does he appear, and in what context was this session arranged?
  9. The 2025 Bearsville Studio Sessions (Bearsville/Rhino, release ID: 33673089) credits Garrett as “Musician” — is this an archival compilation of 1970s-era Bearsville recordings, and does it include previously unreleased Better Days material?
  10. Garrett’s Go Cat Go (1980, Flying Fish) is credited as a solo LP with Vocals, Guitar, Ukulele — who else appears on this record, and is it related stylistically to the Geoff Muldaur & Amos Garrett LP?
