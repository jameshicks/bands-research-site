Nona Hendryx

Nona Hendryx

Oct 08, 20269 min read

Nona Hendryx

Vocalist, songwriter, and producer; born October 9, 1944, Trenton, NJ. Career spans 1962–present. Core member of Patti LaBelle And The Bluebells (1962–71) and LaBelle (1971–76); extensive solo career from 1977. Discogs artist ID: 30092.

Biography

Patti LaBelle & The Bluebells era (1962–1971)

Nona Bernis Hendryx began performing in the early 1960s in Trenton, NJ, where she connected with Patricia Holte (later Patti LaBelle), Sandra Owens (later Sarah Dash), and Cindy Birdsong. The quartet formed the vocal group that became known as Patti LaBelle And The Bluebells (Discogs: 403181). They recorded for Newtown Records (a subsidiary of Cameo-Parkway) before signing to Atlantic.

When Cindy Birdsong departed in 1967 to join The Supremes, the remaining trio continued as a three-piece. A significant early collaboration was Gonna Take A Miracle (Columbia, 1971) — a co-billed album with Laura Nyro, produced by Gamble & Huff, on which all three (Hendryx, LaBelle, Dash) sang uncredited.

LaBelle era (1971–1976)

The trio renamed themselves LaBelle (Discogs: 21267) in 1971, pivoting from doo-wop toward funk, glam, and eventually proto-disco. Hendryx was the group’s primary songwriter, credited with arrangements on the Nona Hendryx material throughout the discography.

Key albums:

  • Labelle (Warner Bros., 1971)
  • Pressure Cookin’ (RCA, 1973)
  • Nightbirds (Epic, 1974) — produced by Allen Toussaint, backed by The Meters; contained “Lady Marmalade” (written by Bob Crewe and Kenny Nolan) and several Hendryx originals (“Nightbird,” “Space Children,” “Are You Lonely?“)
  • Phoenix (Epic, 1975)
  • Chameleon (Epic, 1976)

The group disbanded in 1976. Vicki Wickham served as manager/executive producer throughout the Epic years.

Solo debut and transition (1977–1980)

Hendryx launched a solo career on Epic with Nona Hendryx (1977), produced by Michael Sherman. A second Epic album, Winning (1977), followed. She moved to Arista for Love It (1979) and the single You’re The Only One That I Ever Needed, produced by Christopher Neil.

Simultaneously, she began working in the broader NYC rock/funk orbit. In 1980 she contributed vocal overdubs (“Voice”) to Talking Heads’ Remain In Light (Sire, 1980) — produced by Brian Eno — one of the period’s landmark art-rock/Afrobeat crossovers. She also co-wrote “Magic Hymie” and arranged/produced vocals on Jerry Harrison’s The Red And The Black (Sire, 1981).

Material and the downtown NYC scene (1981–1982)

The pivotal downtown connection came through Material — the collective around Bill Laswell, Michael Beinhorn, and Fred Maher. Hendryx was the lead voice on “Bustin’ Out,” released as a 12” on Celluloid (France, CEL 6592, 1981) and later issued in various formats. Personnel: Laswell (bass), Maher (drums), Beinhorn (synthesizer), Ronald “Head” Drayton (guitar), Martin Bisi (producer). The track is credited to Material with Nona Hendryx as co-primary artist, and both parties co-wrote material on the release. This placed her squarely in the ZE/Celluloid post-punk funk scene.

She also appeared on ZE Records-adjacent material (see labels below) and contributed to the Do What Ya Wanna Do single (Metropolis, 1982), which featured Rusty Egan and Steve Barnacle.

RCA period — the funk-electro albums (1983–1986)

A major new phase began with the RCA deal. Hendryx co-produced all four RCA-era albums, retaining creative control unusual for a Black woman artist at a major label in this period.

  • Nona (RCA, AFL1-4565, 1983) — co-produced with Material (Laswell/Beinhorn). Track-by-track personnel reads like a downtown NYC/funk-fusion directory: Nile Rodgers (guitar, “Keep It Confidential”), Tina Weymouth + Kim Clarke (dual bass, “Design For Living”), Gina Schock (drums, same track), Laurie Anderson (violin, same), Jamaaladeen Tacuma (bass), Sly Dunbar (drums), Carmine Rojas (bass), Kashif (keyboards), Olu Dara (horns). Effectively a collective record under her name.
  • The Art Of Defense (RCA, AFL1-4999, 1984) — again co-produced with Material (Laswell, Beinhorn, also Trevor Gale, Eddie Martinez, Bernie Worrell, Daniel Ponce, Aiyb Dieng).
  • The Heat (RCA, PL 85465, 1985) — shift toward a more commercial electro-funk sound. Guest appearances by Keith Richards (“Rock This House”), Sarah Dash (backing vocals, “The Heat Part II”), Bernard Edwards (bass), Doug Wimbish (bass); produced by Arthur Baker on several tracks alongside Jason Corsaro and Hendryx herself.
  • Moving Violations (RCA, 1985) — further electro-funk direction.

Singles from the RCA period include B-Boys (RCA Victor, 1983), I Sweat (Going Through The Motions) (RCA Victor, 1984), I Need Love (RCA, 1985), and If Looks Could Kill (D.O.A.) (RCA, 1985).

She was also part of Artists United Against Apartheid for “Sun City” (Manhattan Records, 1985) alongside Bruce Springsteen, Bob Dylan, Miles Davis, Run-DMC, Peter Gabriel, Lou Reed, and dozens of others; produced by Arthur Baker and Little Steven.

EMI America period (1987–1988)

Moved to EMI America for Female Trouble (EMI America, ST-17248, 1987) — an ambitious album co-produced and executive-produced by Hendryx and Dan Hartman. Notable collaborators: Prince (wrote “Baby Go-Go,” credited as Joey Coco), George Clinton (additional vocals on “Baby Go-Go”), Mavis Staples (same), Peter Gabriel (keyboards and additional vocals on “Winds Of Change”), Bernie Worrell (piano), Jimmy Jam & Terry Lewis (produced “Rhythm Of Change” and “Why Should I Cry?”). The album demonstrated her reach across pop, funk, and the nascent Minneapolis sound.

Private Music and later solo work (1989–present)

Skin Diver (Private Music, 1989) with producer Peter Baumann marked a turn toward synth-pop. Women Who Fly (Private Music, 1989, written/produced by Hendryx) was a companion single.

From the 1990s onward she has appeared in diverse contexts: backing vocals on Talking Heads’ live retrospective The Name Of This Band Is Talking Heads (Sire, 1982) and Sand In The Vaseline compilation (1992); David Byrne’s Uh-Oh (Luaka Bop/Sire, 1992); Yoko Ono’s Starpeace (Polydor, 1985); John Cougar Mellencamp’s Scarecrow (“Hell In Paradise,” Polydor, 1985). She worked as producer on soundtrack albums for the Showtime series Sleeper Cell (Rhythmbank Entertainment, 2005, 2007).

Later releases include Urgently In Love (V2, 1998, with Billy Crawford), Tear Down The Walls (2009), Shine (This Is It) (Classic, 2017), Keep Funkin EP (Soul Clap Records, 2018). She contributed liner notes and production to Rhythm And Paranoia: The Best Of Bush Tetras (Wharf Cat, 2021) — evidence of her ongoing connection to the NYC post-punk/no-wave world.

She contributed vocals to Bill Laswell’s Time Out (2021) and appears on Last Two Inches Of Sky (Punkt Editions, 2023).

LaBelle reunion: The group reformed in 2008 for Back To Now (Verve Records).
