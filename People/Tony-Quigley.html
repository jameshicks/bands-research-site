Tony Quigley

Real name: Anthony Quigley
Alias: Rotary Head (Discogs ID: 426768)
Discogs ID: 83336
Based: Manchester, UK
Active: 1981–present
Instruments: Soprano saxophone, tenor saxophone, bass, percussion, metalaphone, keyboards, vibraphone

Saxophonist and multi-instrumentalist at the centre of Manchester’s Factory Records jazz-funk and post-punk scenes. Core member of Swamp Children, Kalima, and A Certain Ratio across more than four decades. Also appeared in Domino Park and Free Agents, and contributed production and recording work outside his main groups. Brother of Ann Quigley (Swamp Children, Kalima vocalist).

Career

Swamp Children (1980–1983)

Tony Quigley co-founded Swamp Children in Manchester around 1980 alongside his sister Ann Quigley (vocals), John Kirkham (guitar), Ceri Evans (keyboards/bass), Cliff Saffer (saxophone/clarinet), and Martin Moscrop (drums). Swamp Children released three records on Factory Records and Factory Benelux before transitioning directly into Kalima.

On the debut 12” Little Voices (FAC 49, 1981) Quigley is listed as a songwriter; production was split between Simon Topping (A Certain Ratio) on the A-side and Stephen Mallinder (Cabaret Voltaire) on the B-sides. The Taste What’s Rhythm 12” (FBN 16, 1982) followed on Factory Benelux — the B-side “Softly Saying Goodbye” features an uncredited appearance by A Certain Ratio.

The group’s only LP, So Hot (FBN 21, 1982), is Quigley’s most comprehensive statement from this period: he is credited with bass, metalaphone, and percussion, and together with the full band shared co-production, arrangement, and composition credit. The album’s nine tracks — including “Samba Zippy,” “El Figaro,” and “Spark the Flame” — move between post-punk funk and Afro-Latin rhythms, with flute contributions from Elvio Ghigliordini.

A Certain Ratio (1985–present)

Quigley’s first documented ACR credit is the Force LP (FACT 166, 1986), where he is listed as a full performer on soprano and tenor saxophone alongside Jez Kerr, Martin Moscrop, Donald Johnson, and Andy Connell. This places his formal entry into the lineup in the mid-1980s, though his connections to ACR’s orbit predate this via the shared membership with Moscrop in Swamp Children.

He remained with ACR through their A&M Records period (Good Together, 1989; Backs to the Wall, 1989; Feeling Fine Kalima crossover credits), their independent 1990s phase on Robs Records and Creation Records (Looking For A Certain Ratio…, 1994), and their 2000s and 2010s revivals. His saxophone credit appears on the Mute-era albums ACR Loco (STUMM454, 2020) and 1982 (STUMM480, 2023), and he is credited as performer on acr:set (2018) and ACR:EPA and ACR:EPR (both 2021). As of 2023, his Discogs credit trail with ACR spans nearly four decades.

ACR’s Greetings Four (Materiali Sonori, Italy, 1986) and the Spanish compilation Música De Contrabando (Producciones Twins, 1986) also document Quigley’s saxophone work during this period.

Kalima (1983–1991; reformed 2001)

All six Swamp Children core members carried over into Kalima when the group reconstituted in the early 1980s. Quigley played soprano saxophone throughout the band’s Factory catalog and also took on bass on the earlier releases. On Four Songs (Fac 127, 1985) — an EP featuring guest turns by Andy Diagram (trumpet) and Jez Kerr (vibes) — he is credited on bass and soprano saxophone. This overlapping ACR-Kalima personnel arrangement was not accidental: Kalima drew substantially on ACR’s extended membership.

On the Night Time Shadows LP (Fact 155, 1986) he contributes bass and soprano saxophone across the full album, alongside Moscrop on drums and Kirkham on guitar. Kalima! (FACT 206, 1988) — produced by Michael Johnson (Discogs ID: 29010) — shows Quigley exclusively on soprano saxophone; on the track “Julan” (B4) he is additionally credited with keyboards. Feeling Fine (Facd 249, 1990) expanded his role further: soprano saxophone, keyboards, and vibraphone.

After the group’s dissolution, Kalima briefly reformed in 2001. The reunion produced one release: In Spirit (Kin Records, 2002), on which Quigley is credited as songwriter, arranger, engineer, and producer — his most complete production credit under the Kalima name.

Guest and session work (1986–1999)

Beyond his main groups, Quigley appeared as a guest musician on several Manchester-adjacent recordings:

  • Eric Random And The Bedlamites, Ishmael (Fon Records, BED 7, 1986): credited as guest musician on soprano saxophone and percussion. Other guests include Andy Diagram (trumpet) and Graham Dowdall.
  • Spaceheads, Angel Station (Pandemonium Records, PAN 032, 1999): credited as recording engineer on tracks 4, 6, and 7 — operating in a technical rather than performance role, under the name “T. Quigly.”
  • The Sermon (Dun For Money Records, DFM 007, 1990): saxophone.
  • Cyclic (Syntactic, AGENT 47, 1998): saxophone; Austrian label release.

The Baby Namboos / Ancoats 2 Zambia (1999)

Quigley co-produced and co-wrote the Baby Namboos album Ancoats 2 Zambia (Palm Pictures, 1999) alongside Mark Porter (Discogs ID: 83335) and Leo Coleing, with Tricky credited as executive producer and co-writer. The album’s title combines Ancoats — an inner-Manchester district — with a reference to Zambia, reflecting the Baby Namboos’ Afro-influenced sound. Quigley shares full producer and writer credit.

Domino Park

Domino Park (Discogs ID: 23272) features Quigley alongside Andy Connell (his ACR and Kalima bandmate) and Corinne Drewery (Swing Out Sister vocalist). Domino Park released Danse Macabre (1998) and contributed to the Vivo compilation (Vivo Recordings, 1999) — both on the Vivo Recordings label. The Connell-Drewery combination suggests this was a Swing Out Sister-adjacent side project.

Free Agents (2020)

Free Agents (Discogs ID: 324334) is described on Discogs as a side project of The Tiller Boys. Members include Eric Random (real name Eric Ramsden), Alan Deaves, Andy Boothman, Francis Cookson, and Seema Gupta. Quigley contributed saxophone to Eric Random & Free Agents (Klanggalerie, gg318, 2020), which was produced by Random and Simon Crompton.

Rotary Head alias

Quigley operated under the alias Rotary Head (Discogs ID: 426768) for at least one production credit: a contribution to the Vivo compilation (Vivo Recordings, 1999). The alias has no separate discography in Discogs beyond this connection.
