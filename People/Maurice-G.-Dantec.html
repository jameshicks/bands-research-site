Maurice G. Dantec

Maurice G. Dantec

Born: June 13, 1959, La Tronche, Grenoble, France Died: June 25, 2016, Montreal, Quebec, Canada Real name: Maurice Georges Dantec Discogs ID: 326538 Name variations: Dantec, M. Dantec, M. G. Dantec, M.Dantec, Maurice Dantec, Maurice G Dantec, Maurice G.Dantec

French-born Canadian cyberpunk science fiction writer and musician. Active in the French post-punk scene in the late 1970s–early 1980s, then pivoted almost entirely to a literary career. Later re-emerged as an occasional musical collaborator, principally with Richard Pinhas.

Biography

Paris Years and the Music Scene (1977–1982)

Living in Paris in the late 1970s, Dantec co-founded the band État d’Urgence, which became Spions Inc. (active 1977–1982), playing keyboards/synthesizer. The band released a retrospective compilation, The Party (1979–1984) (Dorian), which documents this period. He also played in Artefact (2), a French post-punk/new-wave group that issued Agit’Pop (1981) on Celluloid, where Dantec contributed music composition and synthesizer.

Literary Career (1983–1998)

After the dissolution of those early groups, Dantec shifted focus to writing. He published La Sirène Rouge (1993) and Babylon Babies (1999), the latter a dense cyberpunk novel later adapted into a film. He emigrated to Montreal, Canada in 1998. His later self-defined style was “christian futurist”; some critics observed a drift toward mysticism beginning with Babylon Babies.

Schizotrope: The Deleuze Tribute Project (1997–2001)

While still based in France, Dantec collaborated with Richard Pinhas on Schizotrope (Discogs: 493530) — a duo project conceived as a tribute to philosopher Gilles Deleuze (whose texts were used as lyrics). The project ran from approximately 1997 to 2001 and released three albums:

  1. Le Plan (1999, Sub Rosa) — Schizotrope as sole primary credit
  2. The Life And Death Of Marie Zorn – North American Tour 1999 (2000, Cuneiform Records) — live recording; Dantec credited as Voice [Texte]; texts by Gilles Deleuze; music by Pinhas
  3. Schizotrope III – Le/Pli (2001, France) — co-credited as “Richard Pinhas + Maurice G. Dantec = Schizotrope”; Dantec as producer, Voice, co-composer

Heldon Revival (2001)

Dantec is listed as a member of Heldon on Discogs, and appeared on the band’s 2001 revival album Only Chaos Is Real (25pm), contributing synthesizer and lyrics. He shared the credit with Richard Pinhas (guitar, composed), Benoît Widemann (synthesizer), Olivier Manchion (bass), Antoine Paganotti (drums), writer Norman Spinrad (lyrics, vocals), and vocalist David Korn. This remains his most prominent musical credit.

Later Collaborations (2006–2016)

  • Richard Pinhas – Metatron (2006, Cuneiform Records): Dantec contributed composed music (track 2-4) and voice (additional). The release also features a William S. Burroughs voice credit.
  • Dead Sexy Inc. – Kamikaze (2008, Enragé Production): Dantec arranged track 4.
  • No One Is Innocent – Utopia (1997, Island Records): Dantec contributed lyrics for tracks 4, 5, 9, 11, and 14 — this predates the Schizotrope period and is his earliest non-Spions/Artefact credit.
  • No One Is Innocent – Suerte (2005, AZ/Island/Universal): Written-By credit for track 12.
  • Richard Pinhas – De L’Un Et Du Multiple (2016, Bam Balam): Dantec contributes voice on track D4 — his final recorded credit, in the year of his death (June 2016).
