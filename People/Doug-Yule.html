Doug Yule

Born Douglas Alan Yule. Bassist, multi-instrumentalist. Discogs artist ID: 383465.

Joined The Velvet Underground in 1968 as replacement for John Cale; effectively led the band’s final years 1970–1973.

Overview

Yule came to the band’s attention through his Boston apartment, which he rented from Hans Onsager (road manager for VU and son of Nobel Prize-winning chemist Lars Onsager). The Velvets stayed there when playing Boston. Sterling Morrison heard Yule practising guitar in the apartment and mentioned to Lou Reed that Yule was getting better — that comment, combined with manager Steve Sesnick’s calculation that Yule would be easier to manage than John Cale had been, drove the recruitment. Yule himself has described the offer as “a complete surprise” and “just a gig.” The exact mechanics are disputed: Sesnick later claimed Morrison brought Yule in; Sesnick was likely the one who made the formal offer. No primary source connects Jonathan Richman to Yule’s recruitment — see Notes (web) below.

Yule played bass, guitar, keyboards, drums, piano, and acoustic guitar — and sang lead on several tracks on The Velvet Underground (1969) and Loaded (1970). After Lou Reed’s departure (1970), Yule effectively ran the band, adding Walter Powers and Willie Alexander, and eventually recorded Squeeze (1972, Polydor) — widely considered not a legitimate VU release as no other original members appeared.

Post-VU he played in American Flyer and Red Dog (4), and became a violin maker (see dougyuleviolinmaker.com).

Notes (web)

Jonathan Richman / recruitment — conflict resolution

The vault’s original Overview stated Yule “was recommended to the band by Jonathan Richman from the Boston music scene.” No primary source has been found to support this claim.

Multiple sources give a consistent, Richman-free account:

  • Yule’s own interview at Perfect Sound Forever (furious.com/perfect/yule.html): Sterling Morrison heard Yule practising at the Boston apartment, told Reed “I was getting better,” and that comment — plus Sesnick wanting someone manageable — drove the hire. Yule also mentions an astrological factor (needing a Pisces to replace Cale). Richman not mentioned in connection with joining.
  • International Times interview (internationaltimes.it/doug-yule-tales-of-the-velvet-underground/): same Boston apartment / Morrison / Sesnick account; Richman absent.
  • Please Kill Me interview (pleasekillme.com/doug-yule/): Sesnick + Morrison credits confirmed; Richman appears only later, when Yule watched him perform at a Springfield, MA show (April 1970) and was “amazed” — a fan encounter, not a prior connection.
  • Multiple secondary summaries (freudslips.wordpress.com, Wikipedia Doug Yule article) corroborate the Onsager apartment / Morrison observation route; none mention Richman.

Richman was a devoted VU follower (reportedly saw them 80+ times as a teenager; Sterling Morrison called him the band’s “protégé”), and he was in the same Boston orbit — which may be the origin of the confusion. But orbiting the scene is not the same as brokering Yule’s hire, and Yule’s own accounts do not mention it.

Conclusion: The Richman-as-recommender claim is unverified and likely erroneous. The Overview has been corrected. The Connections entry for Richman is retained but scoped accurately.
