Mark Gouldthorpe

Oct 30, 20266 min read

Mark Gouldthorpe

Discogs artist IDs: 395486 (Mark Gouldthorpe), 7205656 (Mark Hanson (7) — alias). Sheffield vocalist, guitarist, lyricist, and co-producer. Co-founder and driving creative force behind Artery (2) (1978–1985, 2007–c.2011). Also recorded as part of The Flight Commander duo with Simon Hinkler. Name variations: Gouldthorpe, M.Gouldthorpe.

Career

Origins and early Artery, 1978–1980

Mark Gouldthorpe co-founded Artery in Sheffield in 1978, growing the band out of an earlier punk outfit called ‘The’. In the original lineup he played guitar, with Toyce Ashley handling lead vocals. A self-financed debut single, Mother Moon (Limited Edition Records, 1979), documented this configuration.

In 1980 the band released Unbalanced / The Slide on Sheffield’s Aardvark label, co-produced with Steve Hopkins. The Discogs session credits for that single already list Gouldthorpe on vocals, harmonica, and guitar alongside Michael Fidler (saxophone, guitar, vocals) and Simon Hinkler (keyboards, backing vocals) — indicating Hinkler joined earlier than commonly stated.

Taking the lead: 1981 Peel sessions and Red Flame, 1981–1983

When Toyce Ashley departed around 1981, Gouldthorpe moved permanently to lead vocals — a role he would hold for the remainder of Artery’s existence. The band recorded two BBC Radio 1 sessions for John Peel, the first in July 1981, a second in early 1982. “Into The Garden” reached number nine in the Festive Fifty at year-end 1982.

The band signed to Red Flame in 1982. On Oceans (mini-LP, Red Flame RFM 4, 1982) Gouldthorpe is credited as co-producer alongside Hinkler (on the majority of tracks), lyricist, and vocalist. The album was also produced across its various tracks by Dale Griffin, Tony Wilson (producer, Discogs ID 43308), and Tony Cook (2). Oceans remains the most-cited Artery release; “Into The Garden” (reissued here as A2) is the band’s signature song.

In 1983 Gouldthorpe again played guitar and sang on One Afternoon In A Hot Air Balloon (Red Flame RF 18), now a reduced trio with Christopher Hendrick (who handled piano, keyboards, bass, and more) and Garry Wilson. The lyric credits remain Gouldthorpe’s throughout. The same year’s The Alabama Song (Red Flame / 10 Records) — a Brecht/Weill cover — is Gouldthorpe’s sole vocal credit on that single, with Simon Hinkler producing and arranging and David Hinkler adding trombone and organ.

Golden Dawn era and split, 1984–1985

Moving to Golden Dawn, Gouldthorpe sang on three releases in 1984: A Big Machine (12”), Diamonds In The Mine (12”, a Leonard Cohen cover), and The Second Coming (mini-LP, produced by Tim Parry), the latter co-credited as composer. Murray Fenton joined on guitar for this phase; Neil McKenzie returned on bass. The band documented their final months with a live album, Number Four (Live In Amsterdam) (Golden Dawn GDLP 02, 1985), and a demo/live cassette. The band split in 1985.

The Flight Commander, 1985–1993

Gouldthorpe and Simon Hinkler continued as a duo. Flight Commander Solitude & the Snake (Golden Dawn, 1985 per Discogs — commonly cited as 1986) was credited to “Mark Gouldthorpe & Simon Hinkler”. A second album, A Room Full of This (Cosmic Dice Recordings, 1993), was credited to The Flight Commander (Discogs artist ID: 1624358); Gouldthorpe is listed as performer and co-writer under his alias Mark Hanson (7). The same label issued In The Arc Of The Bow (1993), a further Flight Commander single. Vocalist Susie Mew and backing vocalist Steph appear on A Room Full of This.

After music

Gouldthorpe stepped back from music after the Flight Commander recordings. In a 2009 interview he described it as: “I lost it with music for a bit and went into dark corners.” He ran his own hair salon during this period.

Reunion, 2007–c.2011

Artery reformed in 2007 after Jarvis Cocker invited them to perform at the Meltdown Festival, and recorded a session for Marc Riley’s BBC 6 Music show. The Standing Still EP (Phantom Power Records, 2009) was the first new recording. Gouldthorpe sang on the reunion album Civilisation (Twinspeed Records, 2011), with Wilson on drums, Fenton on guitar, John Clayton (3) on bass, and James Bacon (2) on keyboards. The band folded again around 2011.
