Merzbow

Discogs artist ID: 12551 (Merzbow); see also Masami Akita (ID: 58248) for contributor credits

Real name: 秋田 昌美 (Akita Masami). Japanese noise project based in Tokyo, operating since 1979. Recognised as one of the founding forces of Japanese noise (Japanoise). Highly prolific — hundreds of releases across a span of more than four decades.

Name derived from Kurt Schwitters’s “Merzbau”; pronounced identically.

Biography by Era

Formation and early duo (1979–1989)

Merzbow was formed in Tokyo in 1979 as a duo by Masami Akita and Kiyoshi Mizutani (ID: 73463). The project’s earliest recordings — the “Collection” cassette series on Akita’s own label Lowest Music & Arts — date from 1980–1982, and document an approach drawing on musique concrète, industrial noise, and performance art. Equipment credits from the period reference electric guitar, bass, tape, and electronics.

In 1983 the project expanded its output through ZSF Produkt (Akita’s second label), a prolific source for cassette and limited-run releases throughout the 1980s. International collaborations and releases on European labels (ADN in Italy; Produktion in Australia; V2 Uitgave in the Netherlands) began appearing from 1983–1984.

Kiyoshi Mizutani departed in 1989. From that point Merzbow operated as Akita’s solo vehicle.

Solo and trio phases (1989–1999)

From 1991 to approximately 1999 Merzbow operated as a trio, adding Reiko Azuma (ID: 231450, electronics and voice) and Tetsuo Sakaibara / Bara (ID: 231449, voice and dance). Both appear on the 1994 album Venereology (Reiko Azuma’s noises on track 3; Tetsuo Sakaibara/Bara on vocals).

The mid-1990s brought Merzbow’s first wide international distribution. Releases on Release Entertainment (a sublabel of Relapse Records) — including Venereology (1994) and Pulse Demon (1996) — put the project in front of a broader metal and experimental audience in North America. Pulse Demon remains among the most widely distributed Merzbow releases (15 pressing variants in the DB). The Canadian label Alien8 Recordings published several key late-1990s albums including Door Open At 8 AM (1999, 13 pressings).

The 50-disc box set Merzbox (2000, Extreme label, Australia) is a landmark — a career retrospective that documented the project’s scope while placing Akita firmly in the global noise canon.

Digital/laptop period and Boris collaborations (2000–2015)

In the 2000s Akita shifted toward laptop and software-based noise alongside continuing hardware work. Important Records (2) (ID: 12114) became a key US outlet, publishing Merzbeat (2002) and a series of other albums. Hydra Head Records released the major collaborations with Boris (3) — Sun Baked Snow Cave (2005, 8 pressings) and Walrus / Groon (2007, 6 pressings) — and Daymare Recordings in Japan put out Rock Dream (2007, 7 pressings).

The Boris relationship is the most sustained collaborative thread in the DB: Boris members Atsuo (ID: 435812/FangsAnalSatan), Takeshi (ID: 360797), and Wata (ID: 431874) each appear on 38–41 shared releases with Merzbow in the collaborator graph.

In 2014 Full Of Hell · Merzbow was released on Profound Lore Records (10 pressings), crossing noise into grindcore/hardcore territory and reaching a new audience.

Relapse era and continued output (2016–present)

From 2016 Relapse Records became a primary Western label: Gensho (2016, with Boris again, 7 pressings), 2R0I2P0 (2020, 9 pressings), and Retribution By All Other Creatures (2022, 7 pressings). In parallel, Akita co-founded スローダウンRECORDS (Slowdown Records, ID: 1116791) in Japan — the label has issued dozens of compilations and albums of Merzbow material from 2017 onward, including archival cassette reissues. The Italian label Urashima (ID: 140339) has issued vinyl reissues of early material from 2016 onward, including the nine-volume Nine Studies Of Ephemeral Resonance series (2024–2025).

Merzbow remains active through 2025–2026 with releases on Pan (3) (Germany), iDEAL Recordings (Sweden), Les Disques Victo (Canada), and others.

Open Questions

  • Exact formation date (1979 or 1980?): the profile says 1979 but the earliest DB releases are dated 1980–1981.
  • Details on the ZSF Produkt label — is it a legal entity or purely a self-release vehicle?
  • What caused Kiyoshi Mizutani’s departure in 1989? No Discogs data.
  • Precise timeline of the Reiko Azuma/Tetsuo Sakaibara involvement — DB credits are sparse.
  • The Merztronics (1981) entry shows 360 pressing variants — almost certainly a digital distribution artifact; the actual physical count is likely much lower.
  • Akita’s animal rights / vegan activism is documented elsewhere but not in Discogs; the “Go Vegan” (2020) and “Animal Liberation” (2022) compilation titles suggest this is a prominent theme in his work.
  • Full discography counts: Discogs DB shows ~370+ unique master releases for Merzbow. Any complete catalog would require a dedicated Discogs export.
