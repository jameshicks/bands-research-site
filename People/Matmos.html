Matmos

Experimental electronic duo. Formed San Francisco, mid-1990s; later based in Baltimore, Maryland. Discogs artist ID: 1926. Members: Drew Daniel (Drew Daniel, b. Andrew N. Daniel) and Martin Schmidt (Martin C. Schmidt, a.k.a. M.C. Schmidt).

Overview

Matmos are an American experimental electronic duo whose work sits at the intersection of musique concrète, conceptual art, and postmodern electronica. They are known above all for their concept-album approach: each record is built around a single idea or constraint, and the sound sources used to realise it are listed exhaustively in the liner notes. These sources have included amplified crayfish nerve tissue, a cow uterus, snails and contact microphones on human hair, life-support machines, inflatable blankets, solid gold coins, and a Whirlpool washing machine. The band takes turns proposing the concept for each new record.

The group is named after a “seething lake of evil slime” from the 1968 cult science-fiction film Barbarella.

Drew Daniel is a professor of English at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore and a scholar of melancholy, Robert Burton, and queer theory. Alongside Matmos he records as The Soft Pink Truth, a project focused on queer politics and dance music, and has been a member of several other projects (Disc, IAO Core, Cerebellum (4), EsRM).

Martin Schmidt (M.C. Schmidt) performs, programs, and builds electronics. He has released solo material and, like Daniel, has been involved in numerous side collaborations. Both are members of Disc with Miguel Depedro and played in occult-avant-garde band IAO Core.

Formation and Early Period (c.1993–1998)

Schmidt and Daniel met in San Francisco in the early 1990s. They began making music together around 1993, self-releasing an 8-bit mono cassette In Lo-Fidelity (c.1994, Discogs ID: 4314385) through their own imprint Vague Terrain. A second self-released recording, Bathysphere (1996, ID: 35592241), followed. Their first proper album, the self-titled Matmos (1997), appeared on the Vague Terrain label (ID: 63794).

In 1997–98 they participated in the glitch project Disc with Miguel Depedro, and appeared on the 1998 IAO Core album (release ID: 975281). During this period they also contributed to the UK split Music For Rolex / The Soldering Social (1998, Lissy’s Records, ID: 170308) and released Quasi-Objects (1998, Vague Terrain, ID: 183461).

A Chance To Cut Is A Chance To Cure (2001, Discogs ID: 1381) is widely considered the definitive Matmos statement — a single-concept album built entirely from sounds recorded in operating theatres and medical contexts. Credits: mastered by Rick Wilson (8); design by Rex Ray.

The Rose Has Teeth In The Mouth Of A Beast (2006, Discogs ID: 688338) is a tribute record, with each track dedicated to a queer historical figure. Produced by Daniel and Schmidt; mastered by Thomas Dimuzio; cover design by Rex Ray, cover illustration by Robert Syrett.

The Marriage of True Minds (2013, Discogs ID: 4283536): the concept required participants to undergo Ganzfeld sensory deprivation and attempt to transmit the album telepathically to Matmos before the music was recorded. Mixed by Jon Leidecker (“Wobbly”) and Brett Allen; mastered by Thomas Dimuzio.

Ultimate Care II (2016, Discogs ID: 8105610): a single Whirlpool washing machine is the sole sound source. Dan Deacon provided MIDI programming; Max Eilbacher built a virtual washing-machine MIDI model; Jon Leidecker (as Wobbly) handled processing; Jason Willett and Sam Haberman contributed additional washing-machine recordings. Mastered by Rashad Becker.

Plastic Anniversary (2019, Discogs ID: 13281488): all sounds derived from plastics. Features Whitefish High School Drumline as ensemble, with plastic trombones and trumpets played by Montana high-school students.

Regards/Ukłony Dla Bogusław Schaeffer (2022, Discogs ID: 23226137): tribute to Schaeffer, with electronics, synthesizer, bass guitar, sampler. Artwork by Robert Beatty; mastered by Rashad Becker. Matmos’s 12th studio album.

Other Collaborative Work and Projects

Film and performance. Matmos contributed music to Daria Martin’s film Minotaur and to works by Berlin-based choreographer Ayman Harper. From 2011 to 2013 they played in the stage ensemble for Robert Wilson’s production The Life and Death of Marina Abramovic, alongside J.G. Thirlwell (guitar, clarinet), Mikhail Rudy (piano), and Tav Falco (guitar, bass).

Gay porn and other film. Drew Daniel and Martin Schmidt made music for hardcore gay fetish pornography; selected tracks were released on the 2002 CD-R (Discogs ID: 60953). They recorded a song with David Grubbs for Les Invisibles (2006, Universal Music France, ID: 624804), a documentary about elderly French gay and lesbian people.

Vague Terrain. The duo runs the private imprint Vague Terrain and have published over a dozen collaborative albums and EPs on international labels including Tigerbeat6, FatCat Records, Important Records, Cantaloupe Music, and Die Schachtel.
