Stephen Malkmus

Singer-songwriter, guitarist, vocalist. Born May 30, 1966, Santa Monica, California; raised in Stockton, California. Discogs ID: 81763. Real name: Stephen Joseph Malkmus. Also credited as: Steve Malkmus, Steven Malkmus, S.M., S. Malkmus, Ess. Malk, Clarence Skiboots, Hazel Figurine.

Primary songwriter, lead singer, and guitarist of Pavement (1989–1999, reunions 2010, 2022–present). Leader of Stephen Malkmus & The Jicks from 2001. Member of Silver Jews (early), The Hard Quartet (from 2024). One of the defining figures of American indie rock in the 1990s.

Biography

Early Life and Education

Malkmus was born in Santa Monica, California, and grew up in Stockton, California, attending Tokay High School in Lodi. He learned guitar by playing along to Jimi Hendrix’s “Purple Haze” and played in Stockton punk outfits — Bag O’ Bones, The Straw Dogs, Crisis Alert — from around age 16. He attended the University of Virginia, where he majored in history and DJed at college radio station WTJU. At WTJU he met David Berman (later of Silver Jews) and James McNew (later of Yo La Tengo), and formed the lo-fi group Ectoslavia. In the late 1980s Malkmus worked as a security guard at the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York City alongside Berman and Bob Nastanovich. (Source: Wikipedia)

Pavement (1989–1999)

Malkmus co-founded Pavement with Scott Kannberg (Spiral Stairs) in Stockton in the late 1980s. The band began as a duo, evolving into a quintet: Gary Young (drums, original, fired 1993), Mark Ibold (bass), Steve West (drums, from 1993), and Bob Nastanovich (percussion/keyboards). Their first releases — the Slay Tracks EP (Treble Kicker, 1989) and early Drag City material — built a lo-fi reputation. Pavement signed to Matador for Slanted And Enchanted (1992), the record most associated with launching indie rock’s lo-fi era. Four more albums followed: Crooked Rain, Crooked Rain (1994), Wowee Zowee (1995), Brighten The Corners (1997, recorded by Bryce Goggin and Mitch Easter), and Terror Twilight (1999, produced by Nigel Godrich). Malkmus is credited as sole songwriter across the catalog. Pavement disbanded in 1999 and reunited for world tours in 2010 and 2022–2023.

Silver Jews (early 1990s)

Malkmus contributed guitar on early Silver Jews albums alongside David Berman and Bob Nastanovich, all three having met at UVA. He remained involved in the project’s early years before his commitments to Pavement took over.

Solo Career and The Jicks (2001–present)

Following Pavement’s breakup Malkmus relocated to Portland, Oregon and assembled Stephen Malkmus & The Jicks. The debut Stephen Malkmus (2001, Matador) featured the core trio of Malkmus, Joanna Bolme (bass, vocals), and John Moen (drums). Pig Lib (2003, Domino) expanded to a quartet adding Mike Clark (4) on keyboards and guitar; it was produced by Ryan Hadlock and the Jicks, with some tracks recorded by Lawrence Crane. Face The Truth (2005) was produced by Phil Ek. Real Emotional Trash (2008) brought in Janet Weiss on drums; it was mixed by Nicolas Vernhes. Mirror Traffic (2011) was produced by Beck — Malkmus’s first Jicks record not self-produced; Darrell Thorp engineered and mixed. Wig Out At Jagbags (2014) returned to a more direct approach. Sparkle Hard (2018, produced by Chris Funk of The Decemberists) introduced drummer Jake Morris, with recording and mixing by Adam Lee.

Groove Denied (2019, Matador) was an electronic solo album Malkmus had worked on for over a decade; Matador initially declined it, eventually releasing it as a standalone statement. Malkmus performed all instruments and production; Tucker Martine handled mixing. Traditional Techniques (2020, Matador) was produced by Chris Funk and featured guitarist Matt Sweeney and Afghan rubab player Qais Essar.

Film and Other Projects

In 2007 Malkmus contributed to Todd Haynes’s Bob Dylan film I’m Not There, performing “Ballad of a Thin Man,” “Can’t Leave Her Behind,” and “Maggie’s Farm.” In 2016 he scored the Netflix series Flaked. In December 2012 he performed Can’s Ege Bamyasi in its entirety at the WEEK-END Festival in Cologne; a live recording was released on Record Store Day 2013 (Domino).

The Hard Quartet (2024–present)

In 2024 Malkmus formed The Hard Quartet with Matt Sweeney (of Chavez), Jim White (2) (of Dirty Three), and Emmett Kelly (of The Cairo Gang / Ty Segall’s circle). The project has been described as an indie/alt-rock supergroup.

Personal Life

Malkmus met his wife, artist Jessica Jackson Hutchins, in Portland. They have two daughters: Lottie (born 2004) and Sunday (born 2007). The family lived in Berlin from 2011 through roughly 2014, returning to Portland. Malkmus moved to Chicago in July 2024. He is a supporter of Hull City AFC and plays tennis and golf. He has described himself as autistic. (Source: Wikipedia)

Notes

Mirror Traffic (2011) — Beck as producer

Beck’s involvement came through the industry grapevine: Malkmus heard word that Beck “was a producer now” and reached out on that basis. The two had a cordial but not close prior relationship dating to crossing paths on tours during the early-to-mid 1990s (“the grunge years”) — Malkmus recalled they “got along, always,” with interactions along the lines of “Hey, dude, what’s up?” Beck was not a heavy socialiser on the road, so their contact was friendly but limited.

Malkmus chose Beck specifically for his production sensibility: Beck “doesn’t overtalk things too much,” brings a positive attitude to the studio, and approaches the work from an artist’s perspective rather than a technical producer’s. Malkmus described him as “a passive life coach” who respected an established artist’s direction rather than pushing for wholesale reinvention. Beck heard the demos and concluded the band needed “a good performance and quick, but not overcooked.” Malkmus contrasted the experience favourably with working with Nigel Godrich (Pavement’s final album Terror Twilight), saying Beck was “more sympathetic to what I might want to do.” Mirror Traffic was the first Jicks album Malkmus had not self-produced.
