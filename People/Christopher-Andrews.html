Christopher Andrews

Pianist, producer, and programmer. Discogs artist ID: 481324. Name variations: Andrews, C. Andrews, Chris Andrews. Canadian background (grew up in Canada; mother from Cuba). Active in the Discogs record c. 1995–1999.

Member of Mojave 3 (debut album only, 1995) and Cuba (duo with Ashley Bates, 1997–1999, on 4AD). The Rachel Goswell page notes him as her husband, which aligns with the Mojave 3 Discogs profile text — not independently confirmed in Discogs structured data.

Overview

Christopher Andrews is best known as the pianist on Mojave 3’s debut album Ask Me Tomorrow (1995), the first record by the band formed from the ashes of Slowdive. Andrews appeared on that debut but not on any subsequent Mojave 3 albums — from Out Of Tune (1998) onwards the keyboard chair passed to Alan Forrester.

Shortly after Ask Me Tomorrow, Andrews co-founded Cuba with Ashley Bates, former drummer of Chapterhouse. Based in South London, Cuba released a handful of singles on 4AD between 1997 and 1999, trading in beats and samples while describing themselves as a rock band. In the United States the group operated as “Air Cuba.” Andrews co-wrote, produced, and programmed across all Cuba releases alongside Bates, and the duo also produced a 1999 single for Cuba crediting vocalist Shara Nelson.

Cuba also contributed a remix to Hurricane #1’s “Rising Sign” (1998, Creation Records) — a small point of contact with the broader Britpop/indie ecosystem outside 4AD.

Andrews appears in the credits on Hefner’s The Fidelity Wars (1999, Too Pure) providing scratches — a one-off session that places him briefly in the South London indie scene beyond his 4AD work.

The Mojave 3 Discogs profile explicitly identifies Andrews as Rachel Goswell’s husband and notes he was “playing in the group Cuba” at the time of writing.

Cuba — Band Notes

Cuba (Discogs ID: 56825) was a duo: Christopher Andrews and Ashley Bates (ex-Chapterhouse, later Tunng). Profile from Discogs:

“Based in South London, they cited influences ranging from The Supremes to Tom Waits, revelled in beats and samples but proclaimed themselves a rock band. Andrews grew up in Canada, but his mother came from Cuba — hence the name.”

All Cuba releases appeared on 4AD, 1997–1999. The Black Island single (1999) featured guest vocals from Shara Nelson (of Massive Attack fame). Cuba also remixed Hurricane #1 for Creation Records in 1998. No Cuba full-length album is documented in Discogs.

Scene Connections

Andrews sits at the intersection of two overlapping scenes:

  • Slowdive / Mojave 3 orbit — directly from the shoegaze-to-Americana pivot that produced Mojave 3; shares the 4AD institutional home with Neil Halstead, Rachel Goswell, Ian McCutcheon, Simon Rowe
  • 4AD mid-to-late-1990s — Cuba fits the label’s post-shoegaze experimental pop period alongside acts like Tindersticks and Lush
  • South London indie (late 1990s) — the Hefner connection (Too Pure) places him briefly in that overlapping scene; Hefner’s Darren Hayman and Cuba were contemporaries in the same geographic and label ecosystem
