Thomas Fehlmann

Swiss-born German electronic musician, producer, and DJ. Born 1957 in Switzerland. Discogs artist ID: 2718. Active from the Hamburg avant-garde scene (1979) through Berlin techno (1980s–90s) to contemporary ambient electronic. Core member of The Orb. Founder of Teutonic Beats. Long-term resident at Tresor Berlin.

Aliases: Ready Made (2) (ID: 95378), Flowing (2) (ID: 2995670), Rotogravures (ID: 2263270)

Groups: Palais Schaumburg, 3MB, FFWD, The Orb, Ocean Club, Ingator, 2MB (2), Schizophrenia, Le Petite Orb, Sally Davis Junior

Biography

Early Years: Palais Schaumburg (1979–1985)

Fehlmann moved to Hamburg in 1979 and co-founded the avant-garde/neue deutsche Welle band Palais Schaumburg together with Holger Hiller. The group also included Moritz von Oswald among its members, prefiguring the Berlin techno milieu that all three would later inhabit. Palais Schaumburg released two studio albums (Palais Schaumburg, 1981; Lupa, 1982) on the Ata Tak and Palais Schaumburg/Phonogram labels, plus a run of singles through 1984, before disbanding in 1985.

Fehlmann played synthesizer and trumpet in the band and contributed writing credits on several singles. His visual work also emerges in this period — the Rotogravures alias covers graphics/art direction credits on releases for CBS artists including Rio I. (1986) and several Rainbirds sleeves.

Teutonic Beats & Ready Made (1986–1991)

After Palais Schaumburg dissolved, Fehlmann relocated to Berlin and immersed himself in home studio and sampler work. Under the alias Ready Made (2), he released the LP Ready Made (Mercury, 1986) and the single Uro Breaks (WAU! Mr. Modo Recordings, 1990), bridging new wave and the emerging electronic dance sensibility.

In 1988 he founded the Teutonic Beats label (a Mercury sublabel), which functioned as a platform for early German techno and electronic crossover. The Teutonic Beats compilation (Mercury, 1989) carried contributions from Jörg Burger, Wolfgang Voigt, Moritz von Oswald, Sun Electric, and Westbam — a snapshot of the Berlin-Hamburg scene before it crystallized into the Kompakt/Tresor axis. It was through this label that Fehlmann befriended Alex Paterson of The Orb.

He also worked extensively as a producer-for-hire in this period: production and programming credits for Rainbirds (Swimming With Sharks, Atlantic, 1987), Fischerman’s Friend (Fontana, 1988–89), and Love Park (Money $, EG, 1989) among others.

3MB and Berlin-Detroit (1992–1994)

Fehlmann was a Tresor Berlin resident through the early 1990s, a central hub of the Berlin-Detroit techno exchange. He formed 3MB with Moritz von Oswald and collaborated with Juan Atkins (credited as “Magic Juan Atkins”) to produce the seminal 3MB Featuring Magic Juan Atkins EP (Tresor, 1992), co-produced, written, and mixed by all three. A follow-up EP appeared on NovaMute in 1993. The group is also credited with the alias Schizophrenia and a related project 2MB (2).

He appears on the Berlin 1992 - Tresor Kompilation - Der Klang Der Familie (NovaMute, 1992) in a special effects role, situating him firmly in the foundational Tresor roster alongside Atkins, von Oswald, and others.

The Orb (1991–present)

Fehlmann’s connection to The Orb began through the Teutonic Beats label friendship with Alex Paterson. He contributed uncredited writing to “Perpetual Dawn” (Big Life, 1991) and appears as a player on U.F.Orb (Big Life/WAU! Mr. Modo, 1992). From Orbus Terrarum (Island, 1995) onward he is a full co-producer and co-writer: he co-produced the entire album with The Orb, mixed multiple tracks, and wrote several pieces. He appears on the Orb members roster and is credited on all subsequent major Orb releases through the 2010s.

Solo Career: R&S and Kompakt (1994–2010)

The Flow EP (R&S Records, 1994) marked Fehlmann’s first fully credited solo electronic release, six tracks of ambient/downtempo techno self-produced. Around the same time, he released under the Flowing (2) alias on Apollo (R&S’s ambient sublabel), producing Good Fridge. Flowing: Ninezeronineight. (Apollo, 1998), a richly textured ambient album featuring collaboration with Alex Paterson on two tracks (“6ix Days,” “Teufel”), Sun Electric on one (“Snake Salvador”), and Moritz von Oswald on the closing “Schizophrenia.” Stefan Betke (Pole) mastered; The Designers Republic handled design.

Further Flowing-alias material appeared through 1999–2000: One To Three. Overflow; Ninenine/nd (Apollo, 1999) and U-God Embryo Battle. Overflow Minus-One (Apollo, 2000).

From 2002 onward Fehlmann’s solo work moved to Kompakt, the Cologne label where he remained for the next two decades:

Bureau B and Conrad Schnitzler (2012–2015)

Fehlmann maintained a parallel relationship with Bureau B (a Hamburg-based label under Tapete Records specializing in German electronic and krautrock reissues). In 2012 Bureau B released a recompilation of the Palais Schaumburg catalog. In 2015 Fehlmann curated Kollektion 05 - Compiled And Assembled By Thomas Fehlmann (Bureau B BB 188) — a 16-track LP drawn entirely from the archive of Conrad Schnitzler (Discogs ID: 17135), the Cologne-born electronic pioneer and ex-Tangerine Dream / Cluster member. The sequence includes Schnitzler tracks titled “Contempora” (multiple versions), “Tanze Im Regen,” “Das Tier,” “Fata Morgana,” and “Conrad & Sohn 01 / 02.” Fehlmann appears only as compiler; all music is credited to Schnitzler.

The Bureau B Kollektion series paired curators with archival subject artists; Fehlmann curated Kollektion 05 (BB188, 2015), compiling Conrad Schnitzler material. Note: Kollektion 03 (BB186, 2015) was compiled by Holger Hiller as a Populäre Mechanik compilation — not a Fehlmann subject compilation.

Collaborations with Terrence Dixon and Move D (2018–2019)

We Take It From Here (Tresor, 2018) paired Fehlmann with Detroit techno producer Terrence Dixon, co-written and co-produced by both. The release marks a return to Tresor, the label where Fehlmann was a resident in the early 1990s.

Fehlmann has been a long-time collaborator with Move D (David Moufang) under the duo name Reagenz (Discogs ID: 36348). Reagenz released a debut LP on Source Records (Germany) and Reflective Records (US) in 1994, a classic of minimal techno. The duo reconvened for Playtime (Workshop, 2009), The Periodic Table (The Bunker New York, 2014), and Roll Split / 460 Melrose Ave (Away Music, 2017). The Building Bridges (Aus Music, 2019) album is credited primarily to Move D, not to Reagenz as a duo — Discogs lists it under Move D’s name.

Late Period: Kompakt Solo and Böser Herbst (2018–2021)

1929 - Das Jahr Babylon (Kompakt, 2018) features artwork by German painter Albert Oehlen and was co-designed by Fehlmann himself. Los Lagos (Kompakt, 2018) features Max Loderbauer as co-contributor. Böser Herbst (Kompakt, 2021) is the most recent solo LP in the Discogs database. Design on recent Kompakt releases is by John Harten; mastering by Michelle Grinser (also credited as Mike Grinser).
