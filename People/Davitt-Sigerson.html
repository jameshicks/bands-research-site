Davitt Sigerson

American record producer, songwriter, journalist, and music industry executive. Born New York City, 1957. Discogs artist ID: 252225.

Biography

Early Career — ZE Records and Journalism (1976–1984)

Sigerson emerged in the late 1970s at the intersection of music criticism and downtown New York. His earliest Discogs credit is a 1976 UK single on Movers (“For The Love Of Money”), where he is credited as arranger and producer — an outlier that predates his main career arc.

His core early association is with ZE Records, the downtown NYC label run by Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban that bridged No Wave, disco, and post-punk. Sigerson released his own debut material on ZE: a self-titled album on Ariola (1980, licensed out of ZE), and the ZE singles “I Never Fall In Love / Cry For Love” (1980) and “Twist” (1980) — on the latter he is credited as producer and writer. A second ZE release, Falling In Love Again (1984), was co-produced with Michael Zilkha.

He was also active as a music journalist during this period, contributing to major publications (documented in his Wikipedia entry, not in Discogs credits). The journalistic work ran in parallel with his production career.

His performing credits in this era come through two groups: the Disco Dub Band (with Mike Dorane) and The Royal Macadamians (with Bob Thiele Jr., John Philip Shenale, and John Beverly Jones) — the latter appearing on the 1990 Island Records compilation Experiments In Terror.

Production Peak — A&M, Columbia, and the Bangles (1985–1991)

Sigerson’s most commercially significant stretch ran from the mid-1980s into the early 1990s. Key productions:

David + David (Boomtown, A&M, 1986) — debut album for David Baerwald and David Ricketts. Recorded by John Beverly Jones, who became one of Sigerson’s most consistent engineering collaborators.

The Bangles (Everything, Columbia/CBS, 1988) — the band’s third album and commercial peak. Produced all tracks. Featured “Eternal Flame” (US #1) and “In Your Room.” Session team included John Philip Shenale (keyboards/programming), John Beverly Jones (engineer), Frank Filipetti (mixing), Paulinho Da Costa (percussion), David Lindley (sax/guitar), and Doug Sax (mastering).

Tori Amos (Little Earthquakes, Atlantic, 1991) — produced six of the album’s twelve tracks (tracks 1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9); the remaining tracks self-produced by Amos with Eric Rosse. Sigerson also produced the standalone singles “Silent All These Years” (Atlantic, 1991), “Winter” (Atlantic, 1991), “Me And A Gun” (EastWest, 1991; also plays church bells), and “China” (EastWest, 1992), along with “Crucify” (Atlantic, 1992). His portion of the Little Earthquakes sessions was again recorded by John Beverly Jones.

Olivia Newton-John (The Rumour, MCA, 1988) — produced most of the album (seven tracks). Also later executive-produced her compilation Quick…Don’t Ask Any Questions (Capitol/EMI, 1990).

James Reyne (James Reyne, Capitol, 1987) — Australian artist; session used Bob Thiele Jr., John Philip Shenale, David Lindley, Paulinho Da Costa, and John Beverly Jones. Olivia Newton-John appears as a guest vocalist.

Cock Robin (In Vitro, Manhattan Records, 1987; Best of Cock Robin, Columbia, 1991) — produced the duo’s second album and later compilation.

John Doe (Meet John Doe, DGC, 1990) — solo debut for the X bassist/vocalist. Session included John Philip Shenale, Richard Lloyd (Television), Vicki Peterson (The Bangles), and John Beverly Jones (engineer).

Zappa’s Universe (Verve, 1993) — credited as “Album Sort Of Producer” on this Frank Zappa tribute concert album.

Other notable productions: Soul Man soundtrack (A&M, 1986, with Lou Reed-adjacent cast); Cock Robin’s Sweet Revenge (Passport, 1984); King Of Babylon soundtrack (Passport, 1985); various singles for The Motels, Loverboy, Philip Bailey, and Toni Childs.

Songwriting Credits

Sigerson wrote or co-wrote across the decade:

  • “Beat Street” (performed by Grandmaster Melle Mel, Capitol, 1983) — featured on the Beat Street film soundtrack
  • “Lovin’ Every Minute Of It” — recorded by Loverboy (Columbia, 1985, 18 pressings)
  • “Fall Of Rome” — James Reyne (Capitol, 1987), credited producer and writer
  • Songs for Philip Bailey, Gene Simmons (KISS), The Motels, and others (per his Discogs profile)

Label Executive Phase (1991–1999)

The credits thin considerably after Little Earthquakes as Sigerson moved into executive roles:

  • President, Polydor Records, 1991 (A&R credit on Kalifornia soundtrack, 1993)
  • President, EMI and Chrysalis Records, 1994
  • Chairman, Island Records, 1998–1999

EMI-era production credits include Albert King’s …Message For Albert… (EMI, 1997) and Bella’s Birthday Cake (EMI, 1997). The Island connection loops back to ZE — Island had distributed ZE, and Sigerson contributed liner notes to the ZE retrospective Zetrospective: Dancing In The Face Of Adversity (Island, 1989).

Post-executive credits are scattered: A Piano: The Collection (Rhino, 2006) for Tori Amos, and On Earth As It Is: The Complete Works (Republic/Mercury, 2016) where he receives both “Producer” and “Pre-production” credits on a posthumous reissue.

Later Career

Sigerson subsequently turned to fiction writing. His Discogs profile identifies him as “an American novelist whose first career was in the music business.”
