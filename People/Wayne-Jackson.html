Wayne Jackson

Jul 05, 20267 min read

Wayne Jackson

Born: November 24, 1941, West Memphis, Arkansas, USA
Died: June 21, 2016, Memphis, Tennessee, USA
Discogs artist ID: 255405
Real name: Wayne Lamar Jackson

Trumpet and trombone player; one half of The Memphis Horns (with Andrew Love). One of the most recorded session musicians in American music, Jackson appeared as a brass player on hundreds of soul, R&B, and rock recordings from 1961 through the 2000s. He came up as a member of The Mar-Keys, the house band at Stax Records that also spawned Booker T & The MG’s, and from that position became embedded in the Stax session economy from its earliest days. With Love he went on to constitute the horn section backbone of Willie Mitchell’s productions at Hi Records throughout the 1970s — appearing on virtually every Al Green, Ann Peebles, Syl Johnson, and O.V. Wright session. The Memphis Horns continued working extensively through the 1980s and 1990s, appearing on recordings for Peter Gabriel, Robert Cray, Steve Winwood, Joe Cocker, the Raconteurs, and many others.

Early Career: The Mar-Keys and Stax (1958–1969)

Jackson joined The Mar-Keys (originally The Royal Spades) when the group formed in 1958 in Memphis. The band became the primary backing unit at the newly founded Satellite / Stax Records and cut their own debut single “Last Night” (1961), which charted nationally. Their albums Last Night! (Atlantic, 1961) and The Great Memphis Sound (Stax, 1966) document this early era. By 1962 the rhythm section had evolved into Booker T & The MG’s, while the horn players — Jackson prominent among them — continued as the Stax house brass section.

Jackson appears on the earliest key Stax and Volt sessions: Otis Blue / Otis Redding Sings Soul (Volt, 1965), The Soul Of A Bell — William Bell (Stax, 1967), Back To Back — Booker T & The MG’s (Stax, 1967). He was present at the foundational sessions that defined the Stax sound. King Curtis credited him on The Best Of King Curtis (Atco, 1968).

By the late 1960s he had formal co-billing under the Memphis Horns name, and he and Love formed Memphis Horns, Inc. as a business entity around 1969.

Hi Records and Willie Mitchell Productions (1969–1982)

The most concentrated period of Jackson’s studio work ran through Willie Mitchell’s productions at Hi Records in Memphis. He and Love were the fixed horn section on essentially the entire Hi Records soul catalog of the 1970s:

The So (Peter Gabriel, 1986) appearance — arranging horns on one of the decade’s biggest-selling albums — is among the most widely-heard single credits in his career. The Roll With It (Steve Winwood) LP was also a #1 US album.
