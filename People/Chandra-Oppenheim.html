Chandra Oppenheim

Vocalist, lyricist, performance and visual artist. New York City. Born c. 1968.

Discogs recording name: Chandra (4) — ID: 1050512

Biography

Origins: The Downtown Loft World (c. 1978–1979)

Chandra grew up embedded in the early downtown Manhattan arts scene. Her father, conceptual artist Dennis Oppenheim, was an art professor whose students included Eugenie Diserio, frontwoman of Model Citizens (3) and then The Dance. Parties at the Oppenheim family’s downtown loft regularly featured these bands, and the 10–12-year-old Chandra was part of the scene’s fabric from childhood.

When Model Citizens (3) split around 1979, it fractured into two projects: The Dance (Diserio, Steve Alexander, et al.) and Polyrock (Billy Robertson-led). Chandra was close enough to The Dance that Diserio and Alexander — who wanted to record a project fronted by a child — approached her as vocalist.

The Transportation EP (1980)

Chandra recorded her debut EP in 1980, aged approximately 12. Backed, arranged, and produced entirely by The Dance — specifically Eugenie Diserio and Steve Alexander (6) — with Fred Maher on drums for three of the four tracks. Chandra wrote all lyrics and sang; Diserio and Alexander wrote all music.

The record was issued on GO GO Records (the same small label that released The Dance’s debut Dance For Your Dinner EP the same year), co-released with ON (3). Both releases were closely tied to the downtown NYC no wave scene around Diserio and Alexander.

Chandra and The Dance toured the northeast US extensively on double bills, playing the Mudd Club and Danceteria while she was still a pre-teenager. She also began designing her own clothes at this time, influenced by Diserio’s fashion sensibility.

The Chandra Dimension and the Shelved Second EP (c. 1980–2008)

Following Transportation, Chandra recorded a second EP with an all-kid band billed as “The Chandra Dimension.” This EP was shelved and remained unreleased for nearly three decades. Its lineup and full tracklist are undocumented in Discogs.

Cantor Records Reissue (2008)

In 2008, Cantor Records issued both EPs together as a single vinyl LP (Transportation, Cantor Records C-01) in a limited pressing of 500 copies — the first official release of the Chandra Dimension material. The 2008 LP runs eight tracks across two sides, including four titles not on the original 1980 EP.

An additional unofficial cassette reissue appeared in 2011 on Holy Rodent (release ID 5177906), carrying the same eight-track program.

Transportation EP’s (Telephone Explosion Records, 2018)

Canadian label Telephone Explosion Records issued a double-LP remaster of the complete EP material in 2018, adding two further tracks to the sequence. Side D adds “Day Without Success” and “They’re All Alike” — titles not on any previous release.

Blue Stage Sessions (Third Man Records, 2019)

In 2019 Third Man Records (Jack White’s Detroit label) released a new 7” single under their Blue Stage Sessions imprint. Recorded in Detroit at Third Man’s studio with a Toronto-based band lineup, this marked Chandra’s full return to active recording nearly four decades after the original EP.

Victoria Cheong also records as New Chance / DJ New Chance and is a member of Hvywtr. Jesse Locke is a member of Tough Age, Century Palm, and Simply Saucer. Warren Defever is founder/leader of His Name Is Alive.

Part Time Punks Session (We Are Time, 2022)

A lathe-cut 10” EP released on We Are Time, recorded for the Part Time Punks radio show / session series. Revisits four songs from the original Transportation EP. Issa Oppenheim-Pressman contributes vocals on “Concentration” — likely Chandra’s child.
