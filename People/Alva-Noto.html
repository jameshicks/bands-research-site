Oct 22, 20268 min read

Alva Noto

Discogs ID: 3823
Real name: Carsten Nicolai
Born: 18 September 1965, Karl-Marx-Stadt (now Chemnitz), East Germany
Role: Electronic musician, sound artist, visual artist; founder of Noton, co-founder of Raster-Noton

Carsten Nicolai is one of the central figures of the glitch/microsound movement and the Raster-Noton aesthetic. He works simultaneously as a musician (Alva Noto, Noto) and as a visual artist (Carsten Nicolai), the two practices informing each other across installations, concert performance, publication, and film. He founded Noton in 1994 as an archiv für ton und nichtton (“archive for sound and non-sound”) in Chemnitz, merged it with Frank Bretschneider’s Rastermusic in 1999 to create Raster-Noton, and revived Noton as a fully independent label following the Raster-Noton split in 2017–18.

Biography

Early Work: Karl-Marx-Stadt / Chemnitz (1991–1994)

Nicolai’s earliest documented releases are from 1991–92 on the local Chemnitz label Nora (as Carsten Nicolai), predating the Noton imprint. His 1993 release Morbus (as Carsten Nicolai, Eigen + Art) is classified as an art-object combining drawings, woodcuts, collages, and sounds — indicating that the convergence of visual art and sound was present from the very beginning.

In 1994 he founded Noton (full name: noton.archiv für ton und nichtton) in Chemnitz as both a label and a conceptual platform for the intersection of art, music, and science. Catalog number N-001 was the 1995 cassette/CD Spin + Käfig.

Noto Era: noton.archiv (1995–1999)

Under the Noto alias, Nicolai released a sequence of rigorously minimal electronic works through noton.archiv für ton und nichtton (co-released with Rastermusic, Frank Bretschneider’s Berlin label):

  • Spin (1996, N-002) — sine tones, white noise, radical reduction
  • Mikro Makro (1997, N-003) — with Mika Vainio (as Ø); cross-label collaboration
  • ∞ / Endless Loop Edition (1997, N-004) — infinite loop concept; reissued 2017 by The Vinyl Factory
  • Polyfoto (1998, N-008) — with art-publishing imprint Verlag Für Moderne Kunst
  • Time..Dot (1999, N-009)
  • Empty Garden (Inside.Out) (1999, A1, co-credit with Carsten Nicolai) — among the first Raster-Noton cat#s
  • 13 (1999) — with Japanese author/poet Yoko Tawada (voice)

During this period Nicolai was also active as Carsten Nicolai on audio-visual and art-context releases, including the 1999 Gaia 125 · Eine Welt Reise (not-on-label) and contributions to the Microscopic Sound compilation (Caipirinha Productions, 1999).

Raster-Noton Founding and Transition (1999–2000)

In 1999 Noton and Rastermusic formally merged as Raster-Noton. The joint label launched with early catalog numbers shared between both imprints. Under the new name, Nicolai released Telefunken (2000, cdr032) as Noto and made his debut as Alva Noto with Prototypes (2000, Mille Plateaux) — a two-album transition period.

The Alva Noto name appears to have been adopted around 1999–2000, with Prototypes and Transform both appearing on Mille Plateaux (the Frankfurt glitch/digital label run by Achim Szepanski), alongside the ongoing Noto catalog on Raster-Noton. Transform (2001, Mille Plateaux) was the last Alva Noto release on Mille Plateaux; thereafter all solo Alva Noto albums came through Raster-Noton.

Scene Context

Alva Noto / Noto is a core figure of the glitch and microsound movement of the late 1990s–2000s. Raster-Noton developed a distinct aesthetic: systematic reduction of electronic sound to its minimal components (sine tones, clicks, static, digital noise), rigorous visual identity (black/white geometric design, grid-based typography), and a consistent roster including Byetone (Olaf Bender), Frank Bretschneider, Robert Lippok (To Rococo Rot), Ryoji Ikeda, Mika Vainio, and many others.

The Mille Plateaux connection (2000–01) placed Alva Noto in the Frankfurt-based glitch-techno scene around Achim Szepanski alongside Kim Cascone, Oval, and related artists.

The Xerrox series (Vols. 1–5, 2007–2024) is his major ambient/drone statement, building dense, slowly-evolving textures from unauthorized samples of other artists’ recordings (reportedly including Sakamoto’s piano).

HYbr:ID (Vols. I–III, 2021–2024) represents a recent direction combining electronics with classical ensemble.

Visual Art (Out-of-Discogs Scope)

Nicolai has a parallel international career as a visual artist under his real name. This includes large-scale installations, audio-visual artworks, and gallery representation (Eigen + Art, where Morbus 1993 was released).
