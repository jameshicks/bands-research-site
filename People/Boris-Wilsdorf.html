Overview

Boris Wilsdorf is a German record producer and audio engineer born in Berlin in 1966, best known as the founder of studio andereBaustelle in Berlin, where he works alongside engineer Marco Paschke. His credits span more than three decades from 1992, covering experimental, industrial, and avantgarde territory, with the bulk of his catalogue on Potomak and Mute. He is most closely associated with the Einstürzende Neubauten extended network — Blixa Bargeld, Alexander Hacke, Jochen Arbeit, N. U. Unruh, and F.M. Einheit all figure among his heaviest collaborators — suggesting andereBaustelle has functioned as a key facility for that circle across multiple eras.

Notes (web)

Working relationship with Einstürzende Neubauten — origin and timeline

Wilsdorf’s entry into the Neubauten circle came through his work as a staff assistant engineer at Hansa Tonstudios in Berlin. His first documented credit with the band is on the Malediction EP (1992), where Discogs lists him as “Engineer [Assistant At Hansa]” under producer Jon Caffery. By Tabula Rasa (1993) he is credited as co-engineer alongside Caffery — a step up from assistant that marks the transition to a full working relationship. The Quietus describes this trajectory as Wilsdorf having “learned his chops working at the city’s Hansa Studios before developing an ongoing creative relationship with Einstürzende Neubauten.” (The Quietus, Eros interview, 2024)

The relationship deepened over the following two decades. A 2015 Sound on Sound profile notes he had been the band’s “long-serving front-of-house and recording engineer” for “over two decades” at that point, consistent with a c.1992 start. (Sound on Sound, 2015) His own SoundBetter profile describes it as formative: “basically my work education is working 20 years with Einstürzende Neubauten and learning how to do it different.” (SoundBetter, andereBaustelle profile)

A further marker of institutional entrenchment: around 2009 Wilsdorf purchased the band’s own Bunker rehearsal and recording space in Berlin, renaming it andereBaustelle Tonstudio, which subsequently became his base for work with other acts (Swans, Tiger Lillies, Mick Harvey, Princess Superstar) while continuing to serve as the band’s primary recording facility. (Sound on Sound, 2015)

Mechanism of entry (summary): He was a Hansa in-house assistant on a Neubauten session in 1992, working under Jon Caffery. From that assistant credit he progressed to co-engineer, then lead engineer/co-producer, and eventually studio owner — the relationship was continuous from the start rather than a later hire.

Note on sourcing: The specific “assistant at Hansa” role appears in Discogs release credits (via search-result snippets citing the Malediction release page); no independent interview source has been found that describes in Wilsdorf’s own words how the first session came about. The progression from assistant to engineer is inferred from the credit sequence across releases.
