Richard Bona

Richard Bona

Aug 23, 20268 min read

Richard Bona

Discogs ID: 299647 Real name: Bona Pinder Yayumayalolo Born: October 28, 1967, Minta, Cameroon Based: New York, USA Roles: Bassist, vocalist, guitarist, keyboardist, percussionist, composer, producer

Cameroonian-born multi-instrumentalist and singer who became one of the most in-demand bassists in jazz-fusion and world music from the late 1990s onward. Known for an exceptionally fluid fretless bass technique, layered polyrhythmic percussion, and a high, clarion vocal style informed by Cameroonian folk music and Duala tradition. A solo recording artist since 1999, first on Columbia then on the Universal/EmArcy group. Member of The Zawinul Syndicate, the Pat Metheny Group, and the African vocal trio Toto Bona Lokua. Led the Cuban-influenced ensemble Mandekan Cubano from the mid-2010s.

Overview

Early career and European session work (1986–1996)

Bona grew up in Minta, learning traditional music from his grandfather and absorbing Cameroonian popular styles before relocating to Europe in the mid-1980s. His earliest Discogs-traceable credit is a 1986 French compilation (Tamis D’Or Vol. 1, Tamwo Records), and by the early 1990s he was in demand as a session bassist in the Paris African/world music scene — credits include recordings with Mino Cinelu, Diblo Dibala sideman dates, and various Cameroonian artists releasing on French labels (Musidisc, Sonodisc, Energy Productions). His French-language bass/vocals credits on Time Ni Time (1991) and Vol. 3 (1991) show him already serving dual roles as instrumentalist and backing vocalist.

He surfaced in the US jazz orbit through a web of connections centred on Pat Metheny and Joe Zawinul. His high-profile pre-solo sideman years culminated in work with Michael Brecker and a spot in The Zawinul Syndicate, Joe Zawinul’s globe-spanning world-jazz collective. The 1997 Spaces Revisited (Shanachie) — a live recording featuring Bona alongside Biréli Lagrène and Larry Coryell — provided an early document of his already-mature solo voice within a small-group format.

Solo debut on Columbia (1999–2003)

Scenes From My Life (1999, Columbia CK 69768 2) was a fully self-produced debut: Bona played bass, guitar, keyboards, organ, mbira, and percussion, and sang throughout. He also served as co-mixer. The ensemble showcased a cast of New York jazz regulars — Omar Hakim, Ari Hoenig, Jean-Michel Pilc, Edsel Gomez and Michael Brecker (tenor sax on one track) — alongside Cameroonian percussionist Mokhtar Samba and keyboardist Etienne Stadwijk, who became a long-running collaborator.

Reverence (2000, Columbia COL 504497 2) broadened the palette with a string section arranged and conducted by Gil Goldstein, guest appearances by Pat Metheny (acoustic guitar on “Reverence”), Michael Brecker (tenor on “Esoka”), drummer Vinnie Colaiuta, and pianist George Whitty. The Columbia era concluded with Munia - The Tale (2003, Universal Music Jazz France), a concept album framed as an African folk tale.

During this period Bona was simultaneously active as a top-flight sideman. Key credits include: Pat Metheny Group — Speaking Of Now (2002, Warner Bros.), touring and playing percussion and vocals; Chick Corea’s Motherland (2002, Verve) where he sang and played electric bass; Sadao Watanabe sessions in Tokyo; and multiple appearances on recordings associated with the Joe Zawinul orbit.

EmArcy / Universal era and Tiki (2004–2009)

Toto Bona Lokua (2004, No Format, NØF. 3) was a side-project/supergroup with Congolese singer Lokua Kanza and Martinican singer-guitarist Gerald Toto. Bona contributed bass, guitar, keyboards, bouzouki, and vocal percussion alongside the other two singer-writers. Produced by Laurent Bizot. A second album, Bondeko (2017, No Format NØF 39 LP), followed.

Tiki (2005, EmArcy 9833457) marked the move to the Universal stable and is one of Bona’s most densely populated sessions. A 12-track album, it assembled a large international cast: Indian-British vocalist Susheela Raman (lead on “Soul Makossa”), Brazilian guitarist Toninho Horta, percussionist Marcos Suzano, Brazilian vocalist/rapper Davi Vieira, drummer Vinnie Colaiuta, guitarist Mike Stern, pianist Osmany Paredes Gonzalez, Venezuelan tres player Aquiles Baez, Etienne Stadwijk on keyboards, and Gil Goldstein for string arrangements. Brazilian singer Djavan guested on “Ponta De Areia.”

Bona Makes You Sweat – Live (2008, Decca 0600753054628) and The Ten Shades Of Blues (2009, EmArcy 00600753202258) completed the decade’s output. Ten Shades was a blues-focused departure, produced and recorded by Bona himself, with additional vocals from Franck Mc Comb and Morley, and Indian musicians in the ensemble.

Pat Metheny Group — The Way Up (2005)

Bona appears on Pat Metheny Group’s The Way Up (2005, Nonesuch) in the “Toy Instrument Ensemble” sequence and on peripheral vocal/percussion roles — the main bass chair was held by Steve Rodby. This documents the sustained Metheny relationship alongside Bona’s broader profile as a call-in contributor for ambitious ensemble projects.

2010s: Bonafied, Heritage, Mandekan Cubano

Bonafied (2013, Universal Music France 0602537363773) returned to stripped-down self-production. Core band: Etienne Stadwijk (piano), Osmany Paredes (piano), Obed Calvaire (drums), Dave Eggar (cello), Luisito Quintero (percussion). Notable guests: French accordionist Vincent Peirani and French pop vocalist Camille (on one track, “written-by” credit also hers). Bona played balafon prominently alongside his usual bass/guitar/percussion.

Heritage (2016, Qwest Records / Membran 234245) was credited jointly to Richard Bona & Mandekan Cubano — an Afro-Cuban outfit whose members were Osmany Paredes Gonzalez (piano), Luisito Quintero and Roberto Quintero (percussion), Ludwig Afonso (drums), Dennis Hernández (trumpet), and Rey David Alejandre (trombone). The record draws on Afro-Caribbean and Cameroonian traditions simultaneously. Qwest Records is Quincy Jones’s label.

Bondeko (2017, No Format) was the second Toto Bona Lokua record, again on the Paris-based No Format imprint.
