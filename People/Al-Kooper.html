Aug 16, 202612 min read

Al Kooper

Born 05 February 1944, Brooklyn, New York. Real name: Alan Peter Kuperschmidt. Multi-instrumentalist (keyboards, organ, guitar, synthesizer, vocals), songwriter, and producer. Discogs artist ID: 255185. Inducted into the Musicians Hall of Fame (Nashville) in 2008; Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Musical Excellence Award 2023.

Overview

Al Kooper is one of the key connective figures of 1960s–70s American rock, touching Bob Dylan, blues, jazz-rock fusion, Southern rock, and session R&B over a career of more than six decades. He is best known for three things: crashing the Highway 61 Revisited session and playing the organ part on “Like a Rolling Stone” (1965); co-founding and leading the first lineup of Blood Sweat & Tears, whose debut album Child Is Father To The Man (Columbia, 1968) effectively invented jazz-rock; and producing Lynyrd Skynyrd’s first two albums (1973–74), which launched Southern rock as a commercial genre.

Beyond those signature moments Kooper was a tireless session player and producer throughout the 1960s–80s, appearing on Bob Dylan’s Blonde On Blonde (1966), New Morning (1970), and several later albums; the Rolling Stones’ Let It Bleed (1969); Jimi Hendrix’s Electric Ladyland (1968); Ringo Starr’s Stop And Smell The Roses (1981); and George Harrison’s Somewhere In England (1981), among dozens of others. He also produced The Tubes’ debut (A&M, 1975) and multiple Nils Lofgren and Lynyrd Skynyrd releases. In the 1990s he led the blues-soul ensemble The Rekooperators, recording Rekooperation (Music Masters Rock, 1994) and Soul Of A Man: Live (1995) with Anton Fig, Harvey Brooks, Jimmy Vivino, and Randy Brecker.

Father of Sam Kooper.

Career by Era

Early Work — The Royal Teens, Songwriting, First Sessions (1963–1965)

Kooper’s first Discogs credits are as songwriter in 1963, credited on releases for Leopard Records and United Artists, and as producer for Kapp Records. He was a member of The Royal Teens (Discogs ID: 864317), the New Jersey pop group best known for “Short Shorts.” His 1965 debut as lead artist appeared on Aurora Records (“New York’s My Home”).

The career-defining moment came in July 1965 when he talked his way into the Highway 61 Revisited session as a guitarist and ended up playing the Hammond organ — uncredited, over the objections of producer Tom Wilson — on “Like a Rolling Stone.” Kooper also appears on Dylan’s Positively 4th Street single (1965, organ) and Blonde On Blonde (1966, general musician credit).

The Blues Project (1966–1967)

Kooper co-founded and played organ and vocals with The Blues Project, the New York electric blues group. Key releases include Live At The Cafe Au Go Go (Verve Folkways, 1966) and Projections (Verve Folkways, 1966). He left in 1967 to form Blood, Sweat & Tears. The Blues Project also included Danny Kalb (guitar), Steve Katz (guitar), Andy Kulberg (bass, flute), and Roy Blumenfeld (drums) — most of whom returned in 1995 for the Soul Of A Man: Live album.

Blood, Sweat & Tears — Child Is Father To The Man (1968)

Kooper co-founded Blood Sweat & Tears and is the primary architect of Child Is Father To The Man (Columbia, 1968). Producer: John Simon. Personnel: Kooper (organ, piano, Ondioline synthesizer, vocals), Jim Fielder (bass), Bobby Colomby (drums), Steve Katz (guitar), Fred Lipsius (alto sax), Dick Halligan (trombone), Randy Brecker and Jerry Weiss (trumpets). The album was a critical success but Kooper departed before the band’s commercial breakthrough (the self-titled 1969 LP with David Clayton-Thomas).

Super Session / Live Adventures (1968)

The summit collaboration with Mike Bloomfield and Stephen Stills. Super Session (Columbia, 1968) has Bloomfield on side A, Stills on side B; Kooper produced and played piano, organ, Ondioline, vocals, and guitars throughout. Personnel also included Harvey Brooks (bass) and Eddie Hoh (drums). Engineers: Fred Catero and Roy Halee. Followed by The Live Adventures Of Mike Bloomfield And Al Kooper (CBS, 1968), also produced by Kooper.

Solo Career and Production — Columbia Years (1968–1975)

Kooper recorded a series of Columbia solo LPs while continuing as a first-call session player and in-house producer:

  • I Stand Alone (Columbia, 1968) — debut solo LP; Kooper as artist and producer
  • You Never Know Who Your Friends Are (Columbia, 1969) — arranged and produced by Kooper, with Charlie Calello as additional arranger/conductor; session players including Chuck Rainey, Bernard Purdie, Eric Gale, Joe Farrell
  • Kooper Session (Columbia, 1969) — produced and co-billed with Shuggie Otis (guitar), Stu Woods (bass), and Wells Kelly (drums)
  • Easy Does It (Columbia, 1970) — Kooper playing piano, organ, guitar, vocals, Ondioline, sitar, vibraphone
  • New York City (You’re A Woman) (CBS, 1971) — single
  • A Possible Projection Of The Future / Childhood’s End (Columbia, 1972) — Kooper handling most instruments including ARP and Moog synthesizers, Mellotron
  • Naked Songs (CBS, 1972) — Kooper as artist, producer, engineer, and remixer
  • The Landlord original movie soundtrack (United Artists, 1970) — composed, arranged, and conducted

Concurrent production work included the Autumn LP (Columbia, 1968) — production and liner notes — and work for the Don Ellis band (The New Don Ellis Band Goes Underground, CBS, 1969).

Lynyrd Skynyrd Productions (1973–1975)

Kooper’s most commercially significant production work. He signed Lynyrd Skynyrd to MCA’s Sounds Of The South imprint (run by Alan Walden and others) and produced:

  • (Pronounced ‘Lĕh-‘nérd ‘Skin-‘nérd) (MCA/Sounds Of The South, 1973) — produced and engineered; band: Ronnie Van Zant (vocals), Gary Rossington, Allen Collins, Ed King (guitars), Leon Wilkeson (bass), Bob Burns (drums), Billy Powell (keyboards). Engineers: Rodney Mills, Bob Langford, Danny Turbeville.
  • Second Helping (MCA/Sounds Of The South, 1974) — produced and engineered; same core lineup.
  • Nuthin’ Fancy (MCA, 1975) — produced and engineered; also co-wrote.
  • Additional Skynyrd single and compilation credits through 1979.

Kooper also produced Mose Allison’s Mose Knows! (MCA, 1974) and the Reunion In Central Park concert (MCA, 1973) — a reunion of the Blues Project which he produced, designed, and played.

The Tubes and Mid-1970s Production (1975–1977)

  • The Tubes (A&M, 1975) — produced, arranged, and mixed by Kooper; a key New Wave–adjacent debut.
  • Cry Tough (A&M, 1976) — Nils Lofgren; produced by Kooper.
  • Act Like Nothing’s Wrong (United Artists, 1976) — Kooper as primary artist; arranged horns, designed cover, produced and remixed.
  • Al’s Big Deal / Unclaimed Freight — An Al Kooper Anthology (Columbia, 1975) — solo compilation.

1977–1985: Session and Production Work

Kooper remained active as a session player (Alice Cooper’s Lace And Whiskey, 1977; Ravi Shankar’s Tana Mana, 1987; various Dylan returns including Empire Burlesque rhythm guitar, 1985 and Knocked Out Loaded keyboards, 1986) and as a producer (Christopher Morris Band, MCA 1977; Latimore; Roberta Flack).

  • Ringo Starr — Stop And Smell The Roses (RCA, 1981) — Kooper: piano, electric guitar, synthesizer. Session also featured George Harrison, Paul McCartney.
  • George Harrison — Somewhere In England (Dark Horse, 1981) — Kooper: keyboards and synthesizer; produced by Harrison and Ray Cooper. Also on the session: Ringo Starr, Jim Keltner, Dave Mattacks, Herbie Flowers, Willie Weeks, Gary Brooker, Ray Cooper, Tom Scott.
  • Championship Wrestling (Columbia, 1982) — Kooper as primary artist; horn arrangements.
  • Was (Not Was) — What Up, Dog? (Chrysalis, 1988) — Kooper on keyboards.
  • Four On The Floor (Casablanca, 1979) — Kooper as arranger and synthesizer.

2000s Onward

  • Black Coffee (Favored Nations Entertainment, 2005) — solo LP; produced and arranged by Kooper; liner notes by Andrew Loog Oldham.
  • White Chocolate (Sony Japan, 2008) — Japan-market solo LP.
  • The Best Of Al Kooper (Camden/Sony Music, 2009) — compilation.
  • Kooper appeared on the Beastie Boys’ Ill Communication (Grand Royal, 1994) and Root Down EP as a writer credit (“I Can’t Quit Her” interpolation).
  • Contributed organ and guitar to sessions into the 2010s (Blue Rose Records, 2015; various reissues).

Research Queue

  • Super Session: Discogs credits Bloomfield on “A1 to A5” and Stills on “B1 to B4” — was the album recorded in two separate sessions on two different days, or in one continuous session? The liner notes should specify.
  • Child Is Father To The Man: John Simon is the listed producer, but Kooper is widely cited as having produced the album himself. Confirm the exact division of production duties between Simon and Kooper from the original liner notes.
  • Kooper Session (1969): Shuggie Otis was reportedly 15 or 16 years old during the session. Confirm his exact age from a primary source (e.g., session paperwork or liner notes).
  • Lynyrd Skynyrd signing: Kooper is credited as finding/signing the band to Sounds Of The South, but the imprint was run by Alan Walden. Confirm specifically which party (Kooper, Walden, MCA) held the production deal from the original contract documentation or authoritative biography.
  • Nuthin’ Fancy (1975): Discogs credits Kooper as “Producer, Engineer; Remix; Written-By.” Confirm which specific tracks he co-wrote from the original sleeve, since the album was otherwise dominated by Ronnie Van Zant as sole credited writer.
  • Somewhere In England (1981): Confirm which specific tracks Kooper played keyboards/synthesizer on — the Discogs credit is album-wide but the sleeve may specify track-by-track.
  • Rekooperation (1994): Liner notes written by Stephen King — confirm whether King wrote them as a personal tribute or at Kooper’s invitation, as this is an unusual credit; the liner notes themselves would clarify.
  • Soul Of A Man: Live (1995): Was this recorded at a specific venue or as a multi-venue compilation? The release title implies live but Discogs does not specify a recording location.
  • Black Coffee (2005): Andrew Loog Oldham is credited for liner notes. Confirm which tracks and personnel are listed in the physical liner notes (Discogs tracklist is sparse).
  • The Royal Teens: Confirm whether Kooper was a full member or only a session contributor — the Discogs group credit lists him as a member, but biographical sources vary.
