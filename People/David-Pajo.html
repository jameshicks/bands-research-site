David Pajo

American guitarist and multi-instrumentalist, born June 25, 1968, Louisville, Kentucky. Full name: David Christian Pajo. Discogs artist ID: 254025. Central figure of the Louisville underground: founding member of Slint, member of Tortoise, and a prolific solo artist under the aliases Aerial M, Papa M, and M Is The Thirteenth Letter.

Biography

Louisville Origins — Slint / King Kong (1987–1993)

Pajo came up in the Louisville hardcore and post-punk scene as a teenager. He played drums and guitar in Solution Unknown (an early hardcore band, 1984) and early in the scene around Squirrel Bait. He was a founding member of King Kong (3) (Discogs: 318171) alongside Ethan Buckler in 1988, also featuring Britt Walford and Brian McMahan. The first King Kong 7” appeared in 1989; Pajo played drums and guitar in the band’s early configuration, and returned again circa 1994–1995.

Simultaneously, Pajo was guitarist in Slint — the quartet with McMahan, Walford, and Buckler (later Todd Brashear on bass) that would define a generation of post-rock and math rock. Slint’s debut Tweez (Jennifer Hartman Records, 1989) was recorded with Pajo on guitar alongside McMahan, Walford, and Buckler. Their second and final album, Spiderland (Touch And Go, 1991), was recorded with Brashear replacing Buckler; the credits are famously uncredited — Pajo’s guitar role is listed as “Guitar [Uncredited].” The cover photograph was taken by Will Oldham. Slint disbanded shortly after Spiderland’s release; the album’s influence grew enormously through the 1990s and beyond.

During the early 1990s Pajo also contributed congas to a King Kong release (Old Man On The Bridge, Homestead, 1991) and was involved in King Kong’s active 1994–95 period.

Chicago and Tortoise (1994–1999)

After Slint, Pajo joined Tortoise (Discogs: 4727) in Chicago, a post-rock collective including John McEntire, Douglas McCombs, John Herndon, Dan Bitney, and (earlier) Bundy K. Brown. Pajo appears on two Tortoise albums:

  • Millions Now Living Will Never Die (City Slang/Thrill Jockey, 1996) — credited as “Tortoise Is”
  • TNT (Thrill Jockey, 1998) — credited as “Band [Tortoise Is]”, with the full lineup now including Jeff Parker

He also contributed written-by credits to Turnstyles & Junkpiles (Thrill Jockey, 1998). The Tortoise compilation A Lazarus Taxon (2006) notes his membership as 1995–1997. Pajo was NOT on Standards (2001) — he had departed by then.

Aerial M Solo Work (1997–1999)

While still in Tortoise, Pajo launched his solo alias Aerial M (Discogs: 68469) described as “mellow-fi ambient pop.” Key releases:

  • Aerial M (Domino, 1997, WIGCD37) — self-titled debut, 7 tracks of atmospheric, post-rock guitar work
  • M Is… (Drag City, 1997) — Pajo on guitar and organ
  • October EP (Domino, 1998) — further Aerial M material
  • Vivea (Domino, 1998) — single/EP
  • Post Global Music (Domino, 1999, WIGCD63) — remix album featuring reworks by Flacco, Tied & Tickled Trio, DJ Your Food, and Bundy K. Brown

He also appeared on Up North Kids No. 2 (Acetone Records, 1999) as Aerial M.

The For Carnation (1995–2000)

Pajo was part of the orbit of The For Carnation (Discogs: 135335), Brian McMahan’s post-Slint project. The For Carnation issued three records on Matador and Runt before their self-titled full-length (Domino, 2000). Pajo wrote and performed on the EPs Fight Songs (Matador, 1995) and Promised Works (Runt, 1997). The self-titled album (Domino, 2000; WIGLP77) features a wide-ranging cast including McMahan, John McEntire, Kim Deal, Britt Walford, Alison Chesley, and Rachel Haden — though Pajo is not explicitly credited there, his association with the project ran through the EPs.

Papa M Solo Era (1999–2006)

After Tortoise, Pajo moved to the Papa M alias (Discogs: 30391) for a more organic, fingerpicked guitar direction. The first Papa M album established the template of spacious, folk-inflected instrumental guitar with occasional vocals:

  • Live From A Shark Cage (Drag City, 1999, DC170CD) — debut album; Pajo credited as “All Sounds By, Written-By”; recorded in part by Steve Albini and Tim Gane; 11 tracks including “Drunken Spree” (9:05) and “I Am Not Lonely With Cricket” (14:55)
  • Travels In Constants (Vol. 5) (Temporary Residence Limited, 1999) — Vol. 5 of the Travels In Constants series; single untitled 13:43 track; Will Oldham credited for lyrics
  • Papa M Sings (Galaxia, 2001) — vocal EP/album; Pajo uncredited
  • Whatever Mortal (Domino, 2001, WIGLP103) — double LP; collaborators include Will Oldham (bass, piano, electric guitar, backing vocals) and Tara Jane O’Neil (banjo, acoustic guitar); Britt Walford plays drums on “Beloved Woman”; Pajo plays effectively everything else and mastered the record
  • Hole Of Burning Alms (Drag City, 2004) — compilation collecting earlier Aerial M / Papa M material
  • Pajo (Domino, 2005, WIGCD159) — released under his surname alone; an all-acoustic covers album including a Misfits cover (“Hybrid Moments”) and folk/traditional material
  • 1968 (Drag City, 2006, DC315CD) — rock album; Pajo on all instruments, self-engineered and mixed; lo-fi indie rock aesthetic

He also contributed mixing to Will Oldham’s I See A Darkness (Palace Records, 1998) and bass to Oldham’s Ease Down The Road (Palace, 2001).

Zwan (2001–2003)

Pajo joined Zwan (Discogs: 277868), the alternative rock supergroup led by Billy Corgan and Jimmy Chamberlin of Smashing Pumpkins. Other members were Paz Lenchantin and Matt Sweeney. The band released one album, Mary Star Of The Sea (Reprise, 2003), on which Pajo is credited for guitar and one co-written track (“Danger Boy”). The band broke up acrimoniously during the album’s world tour.

Dead Child and Scream With Me (2006–2009)

Pajo formed Dead Child (Discogs: 1236313), a hard rock band also featuring Michael McMahan, Todd Cook, Tony Bailey, and vocalist Dahm. Their album Attack was released on Quarterstick Records (2008). He also released Scream With Me (Black Tent Press/Wildlife Vinyl, 2009) as Pajo — a Misfits covers LP (all Glenn Danzig compositions), issued as a limited LP+CD set.

Gang Of Four Reunion (2009)

Pajo participated in the 2009 reunion of post-punk group Gang Of Four (Discogs: 33929) as guitarist.

Later Papa M and Aerial M (2016–2024)

After a decade-long gap in solo releases, Pajo returned:

  • “Highway Songs” (Drag City, 2016, DC671) — Papa M; produced by Pajo; Brian McMahan handled sequencing; Pajo’s children provide voice whispers on one track
  • A Broke Moon Rises (Drag City, 2018, DC715) — Papa M; five tracks including a 13-minute cover of Arvo Pärt’s “Spiegel Im Spiegel”
  • The Piano Sessions (Blazebirth Records, 2018) — Papa M solo piano recordings
  • Nazarite (Southern Lord, 2022) — collaborative digital single with The Lord (2) / Greg Anderson of Sunn O))), with Brad Wood engineering; Pajo on strings and vocals
  • Ballads Of Harry Houdini (Drag City, 2024, DC845) — Papa M; six tracks; Pajo recorded and mastered
  • The Peel Sessions (Drag City, 2024, DC912) — Aerial M; archival BBC Peel Sessions recordings; band includes Tony Bailey (drums), Cassie Marrett (bass), Tim Furnish (guitar); produced by Mike Robinson
