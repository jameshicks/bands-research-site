Overview

Brian Yeager is a guitarist and vocalist active in Seattle’s punk and power pop scene, best known as a member of The Cute Lepers alongside a tight-knit core group that includes Duffy McGuire, Josh Blisters, Prisila Ray, and Steven Emanual Nix. His recorded output concentrates in 2009–2011 across four releases, split between the 2000s and 2010s. He also appears in Deep Creep and M. Krebs, working with the same circle of collaborators throughout. His releases landed on Damaged Goods (UK), 1-2-3-4 Go! Records, and No Front Teeth Records, with producer Johnny Sangster present on nearly all of them.

Notes (web)

How The Cute Lepers came to sign with Damaged Goods

The most direct account comes from an Ox Fanzine interview with Steve E. Nix (issue #77, 2008, around the release of Can’t Stand Modern Music): Nix sent the band’s recordings to multiple labels that passed; Ian Ballard of Damaged Goods responded with immediate enthusiasm (“Ian von Damaged Goods war auch sofort begeistert”). The interview does not describe a specific UK tour, personal introduction, or prior relationship that brought the band to the label’s attention — it reads as a cold or semi-cold demo submission that landed well. https://www.ox-fanzine.de/interview/cute-lepers-3002

For the European debut, Damaged Goods handled the UK/Europe release while Blackheart Records (Joan Jett’s US label) covered North America. A snippet on the Damaged Goods site notes the label released three Cute Lepers albums and a string of 7”s, suggesting the relationship deepened over time rather than starting from a tour or face-to-face connection. https://www.damagedgoods.greedbag.com/buy/cant-stand-modern-music-0/

A possible background factor: The Briefs (Steve Nix’s main band) later released Platinum Rats (2019) on Damaged Goods, suggesting Nix or someone in the camp had an ongoing relationship with Ian Ballard that predated or ran alongside the Cute Lepers deal. The Fear & Loathing fanzine Briefs interview confirms: “we’re all happy that Damaged Goods is releasing it in the UK.” https://www.fearandloathingfanzine.com/the-briefs.html Whether the Briefs connection came first or the Cute Lepers connection opened the door to the later Briefs deal is not established by any source found.

What was checked and not found: The Cute Lepers official Q&A page, the band’s news page, the Fear & Loathing Damaged Goods label interview (which does not mention The Cute Lepers at all), the band’s news archive, and the EverybodyWiki article all contain no account of the specific signing route. No interview with Ian Ballard discussing how he found The Cute Lepers has been located.

Wayback Machine archive of the DG news page (retrieved 2026-11-13): The Damaged Goods “sign to DG” news page (damagedgoods.co.uk/news/steve-e-nix-the-cute-lepers-sign-to-dg/, archived 16 Jan 2022 — the only Wayback capture of this URL) has now been retrieved via agent-browser. It is a short announcement that the debut album would come out on Damaged Goods in April (2008), preceded by the Out Of Order 7”. The page quotes the band’s MySpace bio, which includes the phrase: “We’ve got one album ‘in the can’, and we’re currently looking for a home for it.” This confirms the band was actively seeking a label at the time Damaged Goods picked them up, but the news page contains no account of how Ian Ballard came to know about the band — no mention of a referral, a demo, a tour encounter, or any prior connection. The archived URL is: https://web.archive.org/web/20220116000000*/damagedgoods.co.uk/news/steve-e-nix-the-cute-lepers-sign-to-dg/

Summary of what is established: The band had a finished album and was seeking a label (per their MySpace, quoted on the DG announcement page). The Ox Fanzine #77 interview (2008) indicates Ian Ballard responded with immediate enthusiasm to the band’s recordings. The most likely route — demo/recording submission to multiple labels — is supported by the Ox Fanzine account, but no source names a specific person who passed on the demo, a UK tour that brought them to Ballard’s attention, or a third-party referral. The specific mechanism remains unconfirmed.
