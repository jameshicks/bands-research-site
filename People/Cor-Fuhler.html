Cor Fuhler

Born 1964 in Barger-Oosterveld, Drenthe, Netherlands. Died 19 July 2020 in Turramurra, Australia. Dutch/Australian sound artist, composer, and instrument builder. Discogs artist ID: 161030

Overview

Cor Fuhler was a Dutch-Australian experimental musician and instrument inventor whose work centred on extended piano technique, analogue synthesis, and turntables. He is perhaps best known for inventing the keyolin — a hybrid keyboard/violin instrument — and for deploying electromagnetic string stimulators (Ebows, motorized actuators) to produce continuous tones from piano strings without striking them. He also played EMS Synthi AKS, crackle box, organ, harmonium, guitaret, and a range of gamelan percussion instruments.

Fuhler was a founding member of MIMEO (Music In Movement Electronic Orchestra), the large-ensemble European electroacoustic group whose Discogs records identify him playing piano, electronics, and organ. He ran his own label, Conundrom, which issued virtually all of his solo work and many of his collaborative recordings from 1998 onward.

He relocated to Australia in his later years (settled in Turramurra, New South Wales), where he continued performing and recording until shortly before his death in 2020.

In 2017 Fuhler published the book Disperse and Display, covering modular composing strategies and extended piano techniques.

He also recorded under the alias DJ Cor Blimey And His Pigeon.

Name variations used in credits: C. Fuhler, C.Fuhler, Fuhler.

Alias: DJ Cor Blimey And His Pigeon

Used on the 1999 Conundrom release 10 Songs In 62 Minutes And 23 Seconds (ConundromCd 1). The Discogs entry for the alias (artist ID: 1718643) credits Cor Fuhler for the full production role. The recording showcases his widest instrumental range: turntables, tape, Hammond and Dakota organs, sampler, melodica, trumpet, celeste, prepared piano, clavichord, Indian harmonium, and an array of extended techniques including bowed turntable and keyolin.
