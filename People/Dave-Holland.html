Dave Holland

British jazz double bassist, composer, and bandleader. Born David Holland, October 1, 1946, Wolverhampton, England. Discogs artist ID: 84214.

One of the central figures in post-1960s jazz: a sideman on two of Miles Davis’s landmark fusion albums, a founding participant in the free improvisation collective Circle, and a prolific leader across ECM, Dare2 Records, and Edition Records from the 1970s through the 2020s. His output spans solo bass, duos, small groups, and a full big band, with long-running ties to ECM Records and producer Manfred Eicher.

Biography

Early Career and Miles Davis (1968–1971)

Holland came to international attention when Miles Davis recruited him from London at age 21 to replace Ron Carter in his electric band. He appears on two of the watershed Davis albums: Filles de Kilimanjaro (1968, CBS) — one of the last acoustic-leaning Davis sessions — and both In A Silent Way (1969, CBS) and Bitches Brew (1970, Columbia), the twin foundations of jazz-rock fusion. The In A Silent Way personnel also included Herbie Hancock, Chick Corea, John McLaughlin, and Wayne Shorter. Bitches Brew added Jack DeJohnette and Chick Corea alongside Holland.

He also appeared on the live document Miles Davis At Fillmore (1970) and Live-Evil (1971), recording the full arc of Davis’s early electric period.

Circle and Free Improvisation (1970–1971)

Shortly after leaving Davis, Holland co-founded Circle with Chick Corea, Anthony Braxton, and Barry Altschul. The group recorded prolifically in 1971, including live CBS/Sony Japanese releases (Circle-1 Live In German Concert, Circle 2: Gathering) and the ECM studio album A.R.C. (1971) — a Corea/Holland/Altschul trio date produced by Manfred Eicher. Circle dissolved in 1972 when Corea departed to form Return to Forever.

ECM Leader Debut and Conference of the Birds (1971–1976)

Holland launched his ECM solo discography in 1971 with three releases: Improvisations For Cello And Guitar (solo), Music From Two Basses (duo with Barre Phillips), and the free-jazz collaborative Where Fortune Smiles (with Kenny Wheeler, John McLaughlin, and others on Dawn Records). He produced Where Fortune Smiles himself.

Conference of the Birds (ECM, 1973) is widely regarded as his defining early statement as a leader. Credited to the David Holland Quartet, it brought together Sam Rivers (reeds, flute), Anthony Braxton (reeds, flute), and Barry Altschul (percussion, marimba), with Holland on bass and cover design, produced by Manfred Eicher. The album became a touchstone of ECM’s acoustic avant-garde.

Gateway (ECM, 1975) formed a new trio with guitarist John Abercrombie and drummer Jack DeJohnette — a group that continued recording as Gateway through Gateway 2 (ECM, 1978).

Solo Bass and Quartet Work (1976–1987)

Emerald Tears (ECM, 1978) is a solo bass record. Life Cycle (ECM, 1983) is a solo cello album, demonstrating his facility across both instruments.

The Quintet-era began with Transit (Black Saint, 1987), which introduced a working small group format he would refine through the 1990s and 2000s. The quintet lineups across this period included Steve Coleman, Robin Eubanks, Marty Ehrlich, and Eric Harland among others.

Quintet and Big Band (1990s–2000s)

Through the 1990s Holland built his most sustained group. Question And Answer (Geffen Records, 1990) is a trio with Pat Metheny and Roy Haynes. Like Minds (Concord Jazz, 1998) paired him with Gary Burton, Chick Corea, Pat Metheny, and Roy Haynes — produced by Gary Burton, engineered by James Farber.

Also in 1998, Thimar (ECM) brought Holland together with Tunisian oud player Anouar Brahem and British saxophonist John Surman, produced by Manfred Eicher at Oslo’s Rainbow Studio (engineer Jan Erik Kongshaug).

Angel Song (ECM, 1997) is a co-led quartet date with Kenny Wheeler, Lee Konitz, and Bill Frisell, all compositions by Wheeler, again produced by Eicher at Rainbow Studio.

Holland also appeared on the trio The Life Of A Song (Telarc, 2004) with Geri Allen and Jack DeJohnette, produced by Geri Allen and Elaine Martone.

His Dave Holland Big Band — Discogs ID: 323257 — began recording in this period and continued as a parallel vehicle alongside the quintet.

Crosscurrents Trio and Edition Records (2010s–2020s)

Holland formed the Crosscurrents Trio with saxophonist Chris Potter (2) and tabla/percussionist Zakir Hussain. Their debut Good Hope (Edition Records, 2019) was co-produced by all three members. The group also released Uncharted Territories (Dare2 Records, 2018) as a precursor recording.

The Art Of Conversation (Impulse!, 2014) is a duo with pianist Kenny Barron, both co-producing, engineered by James Farber, mastered by Greg Calbi.

Prism (Dare2/Okeh, 2013) introduced a new quartet with Craig Taborn (piano, Fender Rhodes), Kevin Eubanks (guitar), and Eric Harland (drums) — Holland producing, engineered by James Farber.

Another Land (Edition Records, 2021) continued the trio format with Kevin Eubanks and drummer Obed Calvaire.

In his ECM return, After The Last Sky (2025) and Memories Of Home (2025) — both produced by Manfred Eicher — brought him back to his long-standing label relationship after the Dare2/Edition years.

Sideman Highlights

Beyond his leader work, Holland appeared as a sideman across an enormous range of sessions:

  • Herbie Hancock — River: The Joni Letters (Verve, 2007): bass
  • Anthony Braxton — Creative Orchestra Music 1976 (Arista): bass alongside George Lewis, Barry Altschul, and others
  • Chick Corea — multiple mid-1970s Blue Note sessions including Circling In (1975) and Chick Corea (1975)
  • Sam Rivers — duo recordings including Dave Holland / Sam Rivers (Improvising Artists Inc., 1976)
  • Paul Bley & Scorpio (Milestone, 1973): double bass, fuzz pedal effects
