Annik Honoré

Belgian music journalist, venue programmer, and label co-founder. Born 12 October 1957, Mons, Belgium; died 3 July 2014 (cancer), aged 56. No Discogs entry found.

Overview

Honoré came to prominence in the post-punk scene through two overlapping roles: programming the Brussels cultural venue Plan K and co-founding Les Disques du Crépuscule with journalist Michel Duval. She also co-founded Factory Benelux alongside the same partners. She coined the label name “Crépuscule” (twilight), which defined the label’s aesthetic. After leaving the music industry c. 1985 she worked as a civil servant in the European Commission’s Research and Innovation department in Brussels. She was portrayed by Alexandra Maria Lara in Anton Corbijn’s 2007 film Control.

Journalism

Honoré worked as a bilingual secretary at the Belgian Embassy in London in summer 1979 while freelancing for the Belgian cultural magazine En Attendant, contributing pieces on the UK music scene. Her first major interview was with Ian Curtis of Joy Division, arranged through manager Rob Gretton after she approached him following a Joy Division concert at the Nashville Rooms in London in August 1979. This journalistic contact was the origin of her relationship with Curtis and, through him, with Plan K and Crépuscule. Her journalism output beyond En Attendant appears limited; she gave very few interviews and reportedly preferred to remain out of the spotlight. (disordertc.wordpress.com, as of 2026-06-25)

Plan K

In late 1979 Honoré and Michel Duval began programming music events at the Raffinerie du Plan K, a converted sugar refinery in Brussels. Joy Division and Cabaret Voltaire performed on the venue’s opening night, 16 October 1979 — Joy Division’s first performance outside the UK. She continued programming Plan K until 1984. She expressed frustration in her 2010 interview that public interest focused on her relationship with Ian Curtis rather than her substantive work at Plan K and Crépuscule. (Wikipedia: Annik Honoré, as of 2026-06-25; mancreview.com — 2010 interview)

Les Disques du Crépuscule / Factory Benelux

Co-founded both labels with Michel Duval in 1980. She chose the name “Crépuscule.” Factory Benelux operated as a continental offshoot of Factory Records. She left the music industry around 1985. (Wikipedia: Annik Honoré, as of 2026-06-25; slicingupeyeballs.com)

Relationship with Ian Curtis

Honoré met Curtis in August 1979 and the two maintained a close relationship through correspondence and her visits to Manchester until his suicide on 18 May 1980. She was present at the Plan K concerts in October 1979 and January 1980 and assisted him through epileptic episodes. She described the relationship in her 2010 interview as “a completely pure and platonic relationship, very childish, very chaste,” attributing the lack of physical intimacy to his medication. Ian Curtis’s widow Deborah Curtis characterised it as an affair beginning in August 1979 in her memoir Touching from a Distance (1995). Curtis’s letters to Honoré were made available to authors Mick Middles and Lindsay Reade for their 2006 biography Torn Apart: The Life of Ian Curtis (Omnibus Press) — this is the closest thing to Honoré’s own account entering the written record; she cooperated with but did not co-author that book. (Wikipedia: Annik Honoré, as of 2026-06-25; justbackdated.blogspot.com on Torn Apart)

Published Writings and Materials

No solo-authored books or standalone publications by Honoré are documented. Her written output consists of:

  1. Journalism for En Attendant (Belgian cultural magazine, 1979) — pieces on the UK music scene including the early Joy Division interview. Copies/archives not digitised as of this writing.
  2. Interview in Au Plan K 40 – The Addendum (Philippe Carly, photographer; supplementary volume to the Au Plan K photobook) — an extended interview conducted by journalist Philippe Cornet for Le Vif magazine a few years before her death, reproduced in this book. (newwavephotos.com)
  3. Cooperation with Torn Apart: The Life of Ian Curtis (Mick Middles & Lindsay Reade, Omnibus Press, 2006) — she contributed correspondence and personal recollections; extracts from Curtis’s letters to her are included but she is a source, not an author.

Her archives (tickets, notes, contracts from Plan K) were made available to Philippe Carly by her daughter Sasha after her death. (newwavephotos.com)

Bottom line: No solo book, no memoir, no exhibition of her own. Her primary documentary legacy is curatorial — the Plan K programming, the Crépuscule catalog, and the Curtis correspondence which surfaced via third-party books.
