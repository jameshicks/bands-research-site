Phillip Wilson

Born: September 8, 1941, St. Louis, Missouri
Died: April 1, 1992, New York City
Real name: Phillip Sanford Wilson
Instruments: Drums, percussion (balafon, kalimba, bells, congas), vocals
Discogs artist ID: 33736
Also credited as: Phil Wilson, Philip Wilson, Phillip S. Wilson, P. Wilson

Drummer and percussionist. Career spanned Chicago AACM avant-garde jazz in the 1960s–70s, downtown NYC experimental scenes in the 1980s, and late-career leadership recordings up to his death in 1992. Core member of Deadline alongside Bill Laswell.

Biography

St. Louis and Chicago: Paul Butterfield and the AACM (1960s)

Wilson came up in St. Louis before moving to Chicago, where he became embedded in two largely separate worlds simultaneously. On the R&B/blues side, he joined Paul Butterfield’s band, playing drums on the Paul Butterfield Blues Band’s The Resurrection of Pigboy Crabshaw (1967, Elektra) and In My Own Dream (1968, Elektra) — records that took the band toward psychedelia and jazz-tinged arrangements, with David Sanborn on alto sax and Elvin Bishop on guitar. Wilson was listed as “Phil Wilson” on these sessions and also sang with the in-joke “Icebag Four” vocal group (alongside bandmates).

Simultaneously, Wilson was drawn into Chicago’s AACM (Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians) orbit. He became a member of the Roscoe Mitchell Art Ensemble — the forerunner of the The Art Ensemble Of Chicago — and joined the full Art Ensemble when it cohered around Roscoe Mitchell, Lester Bowie, Malachi Favors, and others. His Art Ensemble tenure was in the mid-to-late 1960s; he departed before the group relocated to Europe in 1969 (and before Joseph Jarman joined full-time).

He also continued working with Paul Butterfield through Keep On Moving (1969, Elektra).

AACM Aftermath and Free Jazz (1970s)

After leaving the Art Ensemble, Wilson remained active in the free jazz and avant-garde space across the 1970s. Key credits:

  • Julius Hemphill — Dogon A.D. (1972, Mbari): Hemphill’s debut recording, with Wilson on percussion alongside Abdul Wadud. A founding document of the Black Artists Group (BAG) St. Louis scene.
  • Anthony Braxton — Creative Orchestra Music 1976 (Arista): Wilson participated in this large-ensemble project, playing marching cymbals, drums, and percussion alongside Roscoe Mitchell, Dave Holland, Muhal Richard Abrams, Barry Altschul, George Lewis, Wadada Leo Smith, and others.
  • David Murray — Flowers For Albert (1976, India Navigation): Drums, alongside Fred Hopkins on bass and Olu Dara on trumpet. One of Murray’s first recordings as a leader.
  • David Murray — Endangered Species (1976, India Navigation).
  • Lester Bowie / Lester Bowie Ensemble — The 5th Power (1978, Black Saint): With Arthur Blythe (alto sax), Malachi Favors (bass), Amina Claudine Myers (piano, vocals).
  • Lester Bowie / Phillip Wilson — Duet (1978, Improvising Artists Inc.): Co-billed album, Wilson credited as primary artist alongside Bowie. Produced by Paul Bley. Wilson composed and played percussion.
  • Phillip Wilson — Esoteric (1979, Hat Hut Records): Wilson’s own leader date, with Olu Dara and produced by Clifford Jordan.
  • Frank Foster (John White) — John White (1971, Mainstream Records)
  • Clifford Jordan — Esoteric producer connection confirmed above
  • John Carter Quintet — Member (Discogs group listing)

He also appeared on the Roscoe Mitchell Art Ensemble Town Hall 1972 (Trio Records, Japan) and various other sessions.

Downtown NYC and Material/Celluloid (1982–1985)

By 1982 Wilson had joined the downtown New York world centered on Bill Laswell and his collective Material. His entry point was the hip-hop/funk crossover scene forming around Celluloid Records:

  • Fab 5 Freddy / B-Side — Change The Beat (1982, Celluloid): Wilson plays bells on this hip-hop landmark (produced by Material; the B-side contains the scratch vocal “Ahh, this stuff is really fresh” later sampled into countless records). Laswell plays bass; Michael Beinhorn plays synthesizer and DMX drum programming.
  • Bill Laswell — Baselines (1983, Elektra Musician): One track (A2) featuring Wilson on drums alongside Ronald Shannon Jackson, Martin Bisi, and others in Laswell’s rotating cast.
  • Last Poets — Hustler’s Convention (1984, Celluloid): Wilson on drums.
  • Nots — Nots (1982, Glass Records UK): Drums.

Deadline (1985)

The apex of Wilson’s NYC period was Deadline, his co-led project with Laswell. Two 12” singles on Celluloid Records in 1985:

Down By Law (Celluloid, 1985) — co-produced by Wilson and Laswell. Engineers: Rob Stevens, Robert Musso (also mixing), Hahn Rowe (assistant). Artwork: Futura 2000.

Makossa Rock (Celluloid, 1985) — co-produced by Wilson and Laswell. Personnel expanded to include Jaco Pastorius, Bernie Worrell, Manu Dibango, Olu Dara, Aiyb Dieng, Steve Turre, and Hahn Rowe.

Also in 1985: percussion on Lester Bowie’s Oh My People (Celluloid) and on Cairo Nights (Celluloid).

An archival live recording from the CBGB venue (Deadline, CBGB, released 2021 on Laswell’s self-released imprint) documents a related live lineup: Wilson (drums), Laswell (bass), Robert Quine (guitar), Fred Maher (guitar), Michael Beinhorn (synth).

ECM and European Sessions (1981–1987)

Running parallel to the Celluloid work, Wilson maintained a high profile on ECM Records through membership in The Carla Bley Band:

  • The Great Pretender (1981, ECM)
  • I Only Have Eyes For You (1985, ECM)
  • Avant Pop (1986, ECM)
  • Twilight Dreams (1987, Venture/Virgin)

He also appeared on All The Magic! (1983, ECM) and Lester Bowie’s Nots (1982, UK).

Later Work (1988–1992)

  • Lester Bowie’s Brass Fantasy / Lester Bowie’s New York Organ Ensemble — member (Discogs group listing)
  • George Lewis — Live At The Lower Manhattan Ocean Club Volumes 1 & 2 (1989, India Navigation): Drums.
  • Lester Bowie Ensemble — Jazzbühne Berlin ‘82 (1990, Repertoire Records)
  • Frank Lowe Orchestra — member (Discogs group listing)
  • DIW sessions (1991, Japan): Funky T. Cool T. and The Organizer

The Phillip Wilson Project (1994, posthumous)

The Phillip Wilson Project (Jazz Door, 1994) — released posthumously. Wilson’s most extensive leader date, bringing together downtown and avant-jazz worlds: Lester Bowie (trumpet), William Parker (bass), Jonas Hellborg (bass), Frank Lowe (tenor sax), Carlos Ward (alto sax, flute), James Carter (baritone sax), Michael Marcus (alto sax), Rob Stevens (piano), Bill Laswell (DMX drum programming). Engineered by Robert Musso.

Wilson died April 1, 1992 in New York City, before this record was released. He was 50.

Connections

  • Deadline — co-led with Bill Laswell; two Celluloid 12”s, 1985
  • The Art Ensemble Of Chicago / The Roscoe Mitchell Art Ensemble — AACM founding generation; Wilson was an early member
  • The Paul Butterfield Blues Band — blues-rock crossover work, 1967–69; link between Chicago R&B and jazz worlds
  • Celluloid Records — home for all Deadline material and other 1982–85 NYC work; connects to Material, Last Poets, hip-hop crossover scene
