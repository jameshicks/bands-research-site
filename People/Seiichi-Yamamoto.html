Seiichi Yamamoto

Oct 16, 20262 min read

Seiichi Yamamoto

Discogs ID: 285764
Also credited as: Yamamotor, No,1 Y

Guitarist. Core member of Boredoms across the band’s key recording period (from Soul Discharge 1989 through at least Vision Creation Newsun 1999). Also a member of Ground Zero (3) (Otomo Yoshihide’s ensemble).

Departure from Boredoms

Yamamoto and bassist Hira both left Boredoms around 2000, after the release of Vision Creation Newsun (1999). The 2004 album Seadrum/House of Sun incorporated samples of Yamamoto’s previously recorded guitar work rather than new contributions, confirming he was no longer an active member by that point. (Wikipedia — Seiichi Yamamoto, as of 2026-10-16)

Reason for departure — not documented in any traceable press or interview source. A web trawl (October 2026) checked: the Relix interview with Yamantaka Eye, the BOMB Magazine 2008 interview with Eye, the Exclaim! Boredoms retrospective, a Timeout Tokyo profile of Yamamoto, a 2026 Mitsuru Tabata interview (japanesealternative.net), the Red Bull Music Academy Boredoms guide, and Scaruffi’s band history. None gives an explicit reason.

The closest any source comes is the Exclaim! retrospective, which notes the departure without citing a source: “Yamamoto and Hira leave, Yamamoto perhaps to focus more on his other nine groups, which include the folkie Rashinban (formed in ‘91) and electro-acoustic minimalists Rovo (formed in ‘98).” (Exclaim! — Boredoms: Art of Noise) This reads as editorial speculation, not a statement from Yamamoto or the band.

Eye has referenced the lineup change only obliquely: in a Relix interview he said “after our guitarist and our bassist left, it was a natural process to become like the way we are now,” framing the shift to a percussion-centred lineup as organic rather than explaining the circumstances of the departures. (Relix — Interview with Yamantaka Eye of the Boredoms)

Notes (web)

  • Yamamoto’s active side projects at the time of departure included Rovo (formed 1998), Rashinban (formed 1991), Para, and others. The Timeout Tokyo profile (https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/music/seiichi-yamamoto-you-ishihara) describes him as “formerly of Boredoms, currently of Rovo, Para and various other acts” without further context on the split.
