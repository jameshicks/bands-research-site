Johnny Waller

UK music journalist, fanzine founder, label operator, and co-founder of Fire Records. Born c.1956; died October 1996 (aged 40), London, in a bicycle accident. Discogs artist ID: 555838.

Overview

Waller was a central figure in the Scottish post-punk scene before relocating to London and becoming a professional music journalist and label operator. He founded the Kingdom Come fanzine in Dunfermline in 1977, then the Deep Cuts Records label in Edinburgh in 1979 (two 7” singles released). After moving to London he wrote for Sounds, NME, Record Mirror, and Zig Zag, and co-ran a photo agency. In 1984–85 he co-founded Fire Records with Clive Solomon, which became one of the longest-running UK independent labels. He held creative and production roles on early Fire releases — co-producing the debut Sign Language EP and mixing the Lives Of Angels LP — and contributed liner notes to posthumous reissues through the 1990s–2000s. He died in a bicycle accident in London in October 1996, aged 40.

Background and Pre-Fire Activity

Kingdom Come fanzine (1977–1979)

Waller founded Kingdom Come in Dunfermline on 29 September 1977 — described by kinemagigz.com as “Fife’s first music fanzine.” He published new issues every few weeks; the run reached at least 17 issues before he wound it down in late 1979. The highest confirmed issue number is 14 (dated January 1979, held by the British Library). Price started at £0.15, rising to £0.20 by the final issues. Contributors included Sandy Fenwick and Stuart Adamson (of The Skids). The fanzine covered the early Scottish punk and post-punk scenes, with features on The Scars, Simple Minds, The Skids, The Rezillos, Josef K, and the Edinburgh label Fast Product.

Sources: kinemagigz.com; punk77.co.uk/band/scars/

Deep Cuts Records (1979)

Waller founded Deep Cuts Records in Edinburgh in 1979, operating from Flat 2, 13 Union Street, Edinburgh. Two 7” singles were released, both in 1979, both engineered by John Brierley at Cargo Studios, Rochdale:

Waller also managed Visitors during this period. The label did not continue after he relocated to London.

Music journalism (c.1980–1984)

After relocating to London, Waller wrote for Sounds (confirmed bylines include a Phil Collins interview, Sounds 25 December 1982, and a review of The Sound’s All Fall Down LP), NME, Record Mirror, and Zig Zag. He also chaired a punk debate published in Sounds (25 December 1982). He contributed to the Canadian publication Music Express and co-ran a London-based photo agency alongside his label work (name unconfirmed).

He authored two books: The Phil Collins Story (1985) and a biography of Eurythmics (1985).

Sources: kinemagigz.com; thegenesisarchive.co.uk; punkrocker.org.uk

Fire Records (1984–c.1996)

Waller co-founded Fire Records with Clive Solomon in 1984–85. The label rapidly built a roster of UK post-punk and indie-pop bands, with key early signings including Pulp, The Blue Aeroplanes, Spacemen 3, Close Lobsters, Sign Language, Lives Of Angels, The Rose Of Avalanche, and 1000 Mexicans.

Waller held hands-on production and A&R roles in the early years. He took a co-producer credit on Sign Language’s debut EP alongside John Robb, and is credited as mixer on the Lives Of Angels debut LP. After his death in October 1996, Clive Solomon continued running Fire until approximately 2001–2002, when James Nicholls took over and relaunched the label.
