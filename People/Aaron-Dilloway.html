Aaron Dilloway

Aaron Dilloway

Oct 24, 20269 min read

Aaron Dilloway

Discogs ID: 298561 Born: 1976, USA Role: Noise musician, tape-loop artist, label operator, improviser

American experimental musician and central figure in the US noise underground. Founding member of Wolf Eyes. Owner and primary artist of Hanson Records (Ypsilanti, Michigan). Works across harsh noise, tape manipulation, psychedelic drone, and power electronics. Prolific under his own name and a sprawling network of aliases and group projects.

Biography

Early Years and Wolf Eyes (mid-1990s–2006)

Dilloway began releasing material in the mid-1990s through the Midwestern cassette underground. His earliest credited work appears on Bulb Records with Piss / Allein (1994, drums) and The Heroin Bench (1995, guitar/vocals) alongside early Hanson Records releases.

He was a founding member of Wolf Eyes, the industrial noise group from Ann Arbor/Ypsilanti, Michigan (Discogs artist ID: 164195), alongside Nate Young and John Olson. Wolf Eyes became the flagship act of the US noise scene of the early 2000s. The group’s most commercially significant release, Burned Mind (Sub Pop, 2004), credits Dilloway on “Tape, Guitar, Voice, Electronics” alongside Young (Vocals, Electronics) and Olson (Electronics, Tape, Horns). During this period Wolf Eyes toured extensively and collaborated with artists including Black Dice (the split Wolf Eyes & Black Dice, 2003, on Fusetron) and Kevin Drumm.

Dilloway left Wolf Eyes around 2006, after which Mike Connelly joined as the third member. Dilloway’s later member credits show Wolf Eyes with: Aaron Dilloway, James Baljo, John Olson, Mike Connelly, Nate Young.

Hanson Records (1996–present)

Dilloway runs Hanson Records (ID: 21058), founded in the mid-1990s and based in Ypsilanti, Michigan. As of the Discogs catalog the label has issued over 349 releases. Dilloway is by far its most-released artist (109 primary credits on the label roster), with Wolf Eyes second (25). The label functions as the central hub for the Midwestern noise/experimental underground, with a roster including Robert Turman, Emeralds (2), Nate Young, John Olson, James Twig Harper, Nautical Almanac, C. Spencer Yeh, Kevin Drumm, John Wiese, and many others. Hanson has three sublabels: Herbst, Plaster Caster, and Underground Series (3).

Solo Career (2000–present)

Dilloway’s solo discography under his own name is substantial, running from Seizure (Hanson Records, 2000) through to recent work. Key landmarks include:

  • I Drink Your Skin (American Tapes, 2002) — early American Tapes collaboration
  • Corpse On Horseback (Hanson Records, 2003) — key early solo statement
  • Bad Dreams (Hanson Records, 2004)
  • Beggar Master (Hanson Records, 2005)
  • Hiss Dementia (Hanson Records, 2006)
  • The Squid (Hanson Records, 2007) — uses tape loops, effects, tape delay, voice
  • Infinite Lucifer (Hanson Records, 2007) — works from original sound sources composed by Bobby Beausoleil
  • Concealed (No Fun Productions, 2007)
  • Under Pressure (Hanson Records, 2008) — collaboration with Emeralds (2)
  • Blizzard (Hanson Records, 2009) — collaboration with Robert Turman (Dilloway: synthesizer; Turman: tape)
  • Psychic Driving Tapes (Medusa, 2010)
  • Modern Jester (Hanson Records, 2012/reissue) — reissued from 2008 original
  • Grapes & Snakes (Hanson Records, 2012) — synthesizer, organ, tapes
  • Songs About Jason (Lost & Found Records, 2013)
  • Siena (Hanson Records, 2013)
  • The Beauty Bath (Rockatansky Records, Japan, 2013)
  • Medicine Stunts (Lal Lal Lal, Finland, 2014)
  • The Gag File (Dais Records, 2017) — recorded with Nate Young, Robert Nuuja, Thomas Buhl-Wiggers; mastered by Josh Bonati
  • Sniper Counter Sniper (Hanson Records, 2019) — collaboration with John Wiese
  • The Blob (Hanson Records, 2021)
  • Blue Studies (For Tom Smith) (self-released, 2022) — dedicated to Tom Smith (2)
  • Bhoot Ghar: Sounds Of The Kathmandu Horror House (Hanson Records, 2023) — field recording-based
  • Freak-Out Your Friends! (Hanson Records, 2024)

He has also appeared on compilations including the Wierd Compilation Vol. II (Wierd Records, 2008) under the alias Spine Scavenger, and contributed to Smithsonian Folkways’ Return To Archive (2024).

Nepal Field Recordings Project (2005–2006, 2013, 2023)

A distinctive strand of Dilloway’s work involves field recordings from Nepal. He released Rotting Nepal and Sounds Of Nepal (both Hanson Records, 2005), the three-volume Radio Nepal series (compiled and edited by Dilloway, 2006), Sounds Of The Indian Snake Charmer (Hanson Records, 2005) and its sequel (2013), and most recently Bhoot Ghar: Sounds Of The Kathmandu Horror House (Hanson Records, 2023). He is also credited as the engineer and transfer agent on related archival releases through Dais Records (The Sound Of Porridge Bubbling, 2009; Sugarmorphoses, 2011; Home Aged And The 18 Month Hope, 2013).

Continued Collaborations (2010s–2020s)

Dilloway has maintained a wide network of collaborative projects. Notable pairings include:

  • Robert Turman — 9 shared releases, including Blizzard (2009)
  • Nate Young — 10 shared releases, including The Gag File (2017)
  • John Wiese — 9 shared releases, including Sniper Counter Sniper (2019)
  • Jason Lescalleet — 8 shared releases
  • C. Spencer Yeh — 5 shared releases
  • Kevin Drumm — 4 shared releases
  • James Twig Harper — 4 shared releases
  • Emeralds (2) — 3 shared releases, including Under Pressure (2008)
  • Hive Mind — collaborated on Forgotten Thrist (Chondritic Sound, 2005)
  • Bill Nace — 3 shared releases
  • Jeph Jerman — collaborated on Casual Collisions (2013, Not on Label)
  • Bobby Beausoleil — source material for Infinite Lucifer (2007)
  • Thurston Moore — 1 shared release
  • Warren Defever — 2 shared releases
  • Quintron — shared Hanson Records release (2024)
  • Body/Dilloway/Head — collaborative project (Three Lobed Recordings, 2021), with Dilloway contributing sounds and editing/manipulation

He contributed sleeve notes to two releases on Third Man Records / Touch And Go (2018): Merry Go Round and You Can’t Pray A Lie.
