Overview

Joan Wasser (born July 26, 1970, Norwalk, Connecticut) is a classically trained violinist and vocalist best known as the driving force behind Joan As Police Woman, a project that debuted with an EP in 2004 — the name reportedly inspired by her resemblance to Angie Dickinson’s role in the TV series Police Woman. After studying at Boston University and playing with its symphony orchestra, she moved into indie and alternative rock, becoming associated with artists including Antony And The Johnsons and The Dambuilders before launching her solo vehicle. Her 517 Discogs credits span violin, viola, vocals, production, and songwriting across more than three decades, with Reveal Records the primary outlet (2004–2024) and a tight core of recurring collaborators — particularly arranger Maxim Moston and cellist Jane Scarpantoni — anchoring her studio work.

Notes (web)

Why did The Dambuilders dissolve in late 1997?

Result: no direct answer found across multiple sources.

The web trawl (September 2026) checked: Noise11.com interview with Wasser (Dec 2024), Metroactive Metro profile (Aug 1997), The Arts Desk interview, Line of Best Fit interview, Chip Midnight (2020), Doubtful Sounds (2011), Trouser Press review, and 2026 reunion coverage (God Is In The TV, Ghettoblaster, Real Gone).

What the sources do establish:

  • The band had been on a major label (EastWest/Elektra) for approximately four years as of August 1997, per the Metroactive profile. No source mentions Elektra dropping them or label pressure as a cause of disbandment. (Metroactive, 1997-08-28)
  • Wikipedia dates the official disbandment as 1998, not October 1997. The October 1997 date in the brief appears to come from the Joan As Police Woman Wikipedia article and may refer to the release of the final single (“Break Up With Your Boyfriend”, 27 October 1997) rather than the formal end of the band.
  • Jeff Buckley drowned in Memphis on 29 May 1997 — five months before the band’s dissolution. No source directly links his death as a cause of the Dambuilders breaking up. It is consistently framed as the catalyst for Wasser forming Black Beetle after the Dambuilders ended, not as what ended them. (The Arts Desk)
  • In a December 2024 Noise11 interview, Wasser said: “I love everybody in that band and I am very grateful to have been part of that band. I am just too busy to do much of anything.” She gave no account of the original split. (Noise11, 2024-12-19)
  • A documentary titled I Used to Be Different is in production (Dave Derby + director Bruno Corbo), tracing the band “from our origins in Honolulu to Boston, Berlin, and back.” This may be the first source to address the breakup directly. (God Is In The TV, 2026-04-21)

Speculative context (not source-confirmed): Against the Stars was self-produced (by Masunaga) and recorded in drummer Kevin March’s basement — both suggesting reduced label involvement or budget by 1997. The band’s shift toward more accessible pop on Encendedor and Ruby Red had already alienated some indie fans without breaking through commercially (per Metroactive). These circumstances are consistent with a label parting, but no source states this explicitly.
