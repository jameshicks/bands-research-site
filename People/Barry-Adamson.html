Barry Adamson

Bassist, multi-instrumentalist, composer, producer. Born 11 June 1958, Moss Side, Manchester. Discogs artist ID: 2769. Alias: The Pimp Floyd.

Active 1978–present. Top labels: Mute Records, Central Control International (own label from c.2006), Virgin.

Biography

Manchester Youth and Magazine (1978–1982)

Raised in Moss Side, Adamson joined post-punk band Magazine in 1978 as bassist. The band formed around vocalist Howard Devoto after Devoto’s departure from Buzzcocks. Magazine’s debut Real Life (1978, Virgin) was recorded with guitarist/saxophonist John McGeoch, keyboardist Dave Formula, drummer Martin Jackson, and produced by John Leckie. Adamson co-wrote “Parade” (with Formula) on that album.

By the second album Secondhand Daylight (1979), drummer John Doyle had joined. Adamson contributed to several co-writes including “Rhythm Of Cruelty” (with McGeoch) and “Believe That I Understand” (with Devoto). Producer was Colin Thurston.

Play (1980) replaced McGeoch temporarily with Robin Simon on guitar; Adamson co-wrote five of the nine tracks. Magic, Murder And The Weather (1981) was produced by Martin Hannett (the Factory Records house producer) — notable as one of Hannett’s later-career credits. Adamson co-wrote six tracks on that record including “The Honeymoon Killers,” “Vigilance,” and “The Great Man’s Secrets.” Guitarist Ben Mandelson joined for that final studio album.

Magazine officially dissolved in 1982 after After The Fact (compilation, Virgin).

Also in this period: session bass on the Visage self-titled debut (1980, Polydor).

Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds (1984–c.1992)

Adamson became a founding member of Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds in 1984 when Cave relocated from Melbourne to London/Berlin. The debut From Her To Eternity (Mute, 1984) featured Adamson alongside Blixa Bargeld, Mick Harvey, Hugo Race, and Anita Lane, with Flood as engineer. Adamson co-wrote the title track with Bargeld, Race, Harvey, Cave, and Lane.

On The Firstborn Is Dead (Mute, 1985) Adamson played bass, guitar, organ, and drums — a multi-instrumental role. He remained on Kicking Against The Pricks (1986), the covers album. His final confirmed Bad Seeds studio appearance in this period appears to have been around 1986–1988 (the discography shows no role on Your Funeral… My Trial 1984, though credited on its reissue). He is listed in the Crime & Punishment Japanese release (1989) on bass/guitar/organ/drums/vocals, suggesting some overlap with his solo launch.

He returned briefly to the Bad Seeds for Push The Sky Away (Bad Seed Ltd., 2013), credited on bass.

Solo Career — Mute Years (1988–2005)

Adamson launched his solo career with The Man With The Golden Arm (Mute, 1988), an EP of film-music covers. The debut album proper was Moss Side Story (Mute, 1989, STUMM 53) — conceived as a fake film soundtrack, a concept that defined his aesthetic for decades. Personnel on Moss Side Story included Diamanda Galás (voice on “On The Wrong Side Of Relaxation”), Rowland S. Howard (guitar on “Autodestruction”), and a “Freedom Choir” of Anita Lane, Kid Congo Powers, Mick Harvey, Jessamy Calkin, and Katy Beale. Strings were arranged by Adamson with Bill McGee; keyboardist Seamus Beaghen contributed extensively (organ, piano, rhythm guitar). Engineers: Paul Kendall and John Fryer; mixed by both. Mastered by John Dent.

This cinematic noir aesthetic — crime-film orchestration, jazz inflections, spoken-word elements, lounge kitsch — ran through the entire Mute catalog:

  • Soul Murder (Mute, 1992) — continued the orchestral approach; featured Atticus Ross (beats/sounds), Kevin Armstrong (guitar), brass ensemble, narrator tracks, and the James Bond theme “007” arranged by Adamson.
  • The Negro Inside Me (Mute, 1993, EP) — included a cover of Gainsbourg/Birkin’s “Je T’Aime… Moi Non Plus” with vocalist Louise Ness; featured Seamus Beaghen (Hammond, piano), Atticus Ross (beats/sounds).
  • A Prayer Mat Of Flesh (Mute, 1995) — sampler/promo LP of material leading to Oedipus Schmoedipus.
  • Oedipus Schmoedipus (Mute, 1996) — his most collaborative solo record; featured Jarvis Cocker (vocals on “Set The Controls For The Heart Of The Pelvis”), Billy MacKenzie (vocals on “Achieved In The Valley Of Dolls”), a Massive Attack co-write (“Something Wicked This Way Comes”), and a Miles Davis cover (“Miles”). Atticus Ross again on additional programming.
  • As Above So Below (Mute, 1998) — bass, programming, vocals by Adamson.
  • The Murky World Of Barry Adamson (Mute, 1999) — compilation/remix survey.
  • Black Amour (Mute, 2002) — Adamson played bass guitar, guitar, electric piano, piano, harpsichord, and programmed/engineered the entire record himself.
  • The King Of Nothing Hill (Mute, 2002) — major statement; added banjo (Dave Holmes), Nick Plytas (piano/organ), Pete Whyman (saxes/clarinet), James Johnston (guitar), trumpet/trombone ensemble; one track credited “Written-By” to jazz legend Archie Shepp (“Le Matin Des Noire”).

Central Control International (2006–2023)

Moving to his own imprint, Adamson continued releasing: Stranger On The Sofa (2006), The Long Way Back Again (2006), Back To The Cat (2008), Rag And Bone (2010), I Will Set You Free (2012), Know Where To Run (2016), Love Sick Dick (2017).

ACR Collaboration and Return to Mute (2018–present)

In 2018 Adamson collaborated with A Certain Ratio on the single “Dirty Boy” — released on Mute as a co-primary credit (A Certain Ratio feat. Barry Adamson). The 2019 release Dirty Boy & Shack Up Remixed (Mute) extended this collaboration. The ACR connection closes a circle: both acts were rooted in Manchester’s post-punk/Factory-adjacent scene, and ACR bassist Jeremy Kerr had long overlapped with Adamson’s world.

Memento Mori (Mute, 2018) returned him fully to the label. Steal Away (Mute, 2021) followed. Cut To Black (Barry Adamson Incorporated, 2024) appeared on his own imprint. Scala!!! (Original Soundtrack) (Mute, 2026) is his most recent credit — a return to the film-soundtrack format that defined his career.
