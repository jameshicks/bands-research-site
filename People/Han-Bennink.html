Han Bennink

Real name: Hendrikus Johannes Bennink Born: April 17, 1942, Zaandam, Netherlands Instruments: Drums, percussion, clarinet, soprano saxophone, viola, banjo, trumpet, found objects, voice Active: 1960s–present

Dutch drummer and multi-instrumentalist; one of the defining percussionists of European free jazz and free improvisation. Known for an intensely physical, often theatrical performance style incorporating humor, extended technique, found sounds, and visual performance art. A founding member of the Instant Composers Pool (ICP) alongside Misha Mengelberg and Willem Breuker in 1967, he has been a central figure of the Amsterdam improvised music scene for over six decades. Equally important to the British free improvisation axis through his long partnerships with Derek Bailey and Evan Parker, and to the German free jazz axis through his duo with Peter Brötzmann and ties to FMP.

Bennink’s multi-instrumentalism is unusually literal: on records he is documented playing piano, viola, banjo, bass clarinet, soprano saxophone, trumpet, saw, and unspecified “found objects” and homemade instruments, often alongside his primary drum work. He also contributed cover artwork to several releases (notably his own and ICP releases). The name variations on Discogs run to over a dozen spellings — “Hans Bennick,” “H.J. Bennink,” “Han Benninck,” and several Japanese transcriptions among them.

Early Career (1960s)

Bennink came to prominence in the mid-1960s as a working jazz drummer in the Netherlands, playing in post-bop and swing contexts. His first significant documented appearance is Last Date (1965, Fontana), a quartet session with Eric Dolphy in what was Dolphy’s final recording before his death. The personnel: Dolphy on flute, bass clarinet, and alto saxophone; Misha Mengelberg on piano; Jacques Schols on bass; Bennink on drums.

The following year he appears on Lover, Come Swing With Me (1966, Philips) and quickly moves with Mengelberg toward the avant-garde.

In 1967 he and Willem Breuker recorded New Acoustic Swing Duo (Instant Composers Pool), an early duet documenting Bennink’s extravagant percussion setup: bass drum, multiple tom-toms, street parade drum, snare drum, tabla, banya, vibrapan, homemade rattles, cow and temple bells, Chinese cymbals, scraper, Chinese temple blocks, hi-hat, and iron and wooden sticks — as well as voice and cover artwork, all credited to him.

Also in 1967, ICP itself was founded. The first ICP label release, Instant Composers Pool (1968), was credited to a trio of Bennink, Mengelberg, and John Tchicai, with compositions by both Mengelberg and Tchicai.

The ICP and Amsterdam Scene (1967–present)

The Instant Composers Pool (ICP, Discogs label ID: 99922) is the organizational backbone of Bennink’s career. He is a co-founder of both the collective (1967) and its self-titled record label. Over the following decades the ICP expanded from a small group into the ICP Orchestra (Discogs 1669446), a larger ensemble based around Mengelberg’s arrangements and a rotating cast of Amsterdam improvisers.

The Incus Axis and British Free Improvisation (1970–1988)

Bennink’s connection to the British free improvisation scene runs through Derek Bailey and Incus Records. The very first Incus release — The Topography Of The Lungs (1970, Incus 1) — is a trio of Evan Parker (soprano and tenor saxophone), Bailey (guitar), and Bennink (percussion), recorded by Andy Bereza and Bob Woolford. It remains one of the landmark documents of European free improvisation.

The formal duo project Derek Bailey / Han Bennink (Discogs 1467663) generated three releases:

Brötzmann Duo and FMP (1970s–2019)

The duo with Peter Brötzmann (Brötzmann / Bennink, Discogs 1753702) is one of the longest-running partnerships in European free jazz:

The trio with Brötzmann and Misha Mengelberg also recorded prolifically: 3 Points And A Mountain (1979, FMP) and the later Fifty Years After… Live At Lila Eule Bremen 26.05.2018 (2019, Trost Records) — the last with Alexander von Schlippenbach replacing Mengelberg on piano.

Bennink was connected to FMP through both his Brötzmann partnership and through recordings at FMP sessions. Engineer Jost Gebers (also FMP producer) recorded many of the key sessions, including the 1974 Mengelberg/Bennink duo Einepartietischtennis and the FMP Brötzmann/Bennink records.
