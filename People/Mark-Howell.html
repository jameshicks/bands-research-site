Mark Howell

Mark Howell

Oct 28, 20265 min read

Mark Howell

Discogs ID: 514043
Name variations: Howell; M. Howell
Instruments: Guitar, trumpet, vocals

American guitarist, trumpeter, vocalist, and songwriter active in the experimental/post-punk/downtown New York orbit from the mid-1980s through the early 1990s. Best known for playing guitar and co-writing material on Curlew’s North America (1985) and Live In Berlin (1988). Also a member of Better Than Death, Zero Pop, Love Camp 7, Timber, and Camping Trip, and a producer for Judy Dunaway.

Career Summary

Howell’s earliest documented recording credit is on the 1985 Better Than Death cassette Swimman, where he plays guitar and sings alongside clarinetist Michael Lytle and a rhythm section of Jeff Myers and Jerry Myers, with engineer Martin Bisi recording. A second, slightly different edition of Swimman appeared in 1988 on Lost Records (9) where Howell also plays trumpet mouthpiece.

In 1985, Howell appeared on Curlew’s North America (Moers Music) — an LP produced by Fred Frith and recorded by Martin Bisi — as the sole guitarist, sharing the record with Frith (bass), Tom Cora (cello), Pippin Barnett and Rick Brown (drums), and George Cartwright (sax). Howell co-wrote one track: “She Done Already Left Me” (B5), credited jointly to Howell, Brown, and Cartwright.

By 1988 Howell had moved on to other projects and was replaced in Curlew by Davey Williams. He is, however, credited as Written-By on track B3 of Live In Berlin (Cuneiform Records, 1988), co-written with Rick Brown and Tom Cora — suggesting the composition predated the lineup change.

Also in 1988, Howell appeared on Zero Pop’s All The Big Mystics (RecRec Music, Switzerland) playing vocals, guitar, and arranging, alongside vocalist/drummer Jim Meneses and vocalist/keyboardist Bruno Meillier, with guest drum track from Ikue Mori. This was a Swiss-released record on the Zurich post-punk label.

Through the early-to-mid 1990s Howell’s center of gravity shifted to Timber, a collaborative group with Rick Brown and Jenny Wade, with whom he co-wrote songs and sang lead on most tracks. Parts And Labor (1992, Rift) credits him with guitars, trumpet, and “most vocals.” He also guested on guitar and trumpet on Love Camp 7’s Where The Green Ends (1993, Bowlmor Records), a band he was a full member of alongside Bruce Hathaway, Dann Baker, and Dave Campbell (8).

Outside performing, Howell produced and engineered the 1990 self-titled Judy Dunaway LP on Lost Records (9), engineering selected tracks himself while working with co-engineer Doug Henderson and Wharton Tiers on the rest.

Later he was a member of Camping Trip alongside Eric Keil (2), Jarey Myers, and Katie O’Looney, though no Camping Trip release has been located in the Discogs database as of this research.
