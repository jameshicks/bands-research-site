Discogs ID: 73253 Real name: Christopher Butler Aliases: Charas (Discogs ID: 62236) Origin: Akron, Ohio, USA Labels: Clone Records, Polydor, ZE Records, Antilles, Future Fossil Records, Smog Veil Records, Cowboy Records (UK), Bar/None Records, Bearsville

Overview

Chris Butler (born Christopher Butler) is an American songwriter, guitarist, and producer from Akron, Ohio, best known as the conceptual founder and primary songwriter of The Waitresses (1978–1984). He wrote or co-wrote the band’s entire catalog, including “I Know What Boys Like” (1980) and “Christmas Wrapping” (1981/1982), as well as the theme song for the CBS sitcom Square Pegs (1982). Before The Waitresses, Butler was a member of Tin Huey, one of the central art-punk bands of the Akron scene. After The Waitresses disbanded in 1984, he continued as a producer and occasional performer, with solo output under his own name on Future Fossil Records (1995) and Smog Veil Records (2018), and an electronic music project under the alias Charas (Cowboy Records, UK, 1992). His Discogs credits span 1978 to 2024 and range across production, songwriting, guitar, and arrangement work for artists outside the Akron orbit.

Tin Huey (1977–1979)

Butler was a guitarist and member of Tin Huey (Discogs ID: 312146), an Akron art-punk/new wave band that also included Ralph Carney (saxophone), Harvey Gold (piano/synth), Mark Price (4) (bass), Michael Aylward (guitar), and Stuart Austin (drums). Tin Huey released the single “Puppet Wipes” (1977, Clone Records) and the LP Contents Dislodged During Shipment (1979, Warner Bros. Records), produced by Paul Wexler. Butler played guitar, percussion, and birdcalls on the LP alongside the full band lineup.

The Waitresses (1978–1984)

Butler founded The Waitresses and served as guitarist, primary songwriter, and co-producer across the band’s entire output. The core live/recording lineup included Patty Donahue (vocals), Billy Ficca (drums, ex-Television), Mars Williams (saxophone), Tracy Wormworth (bass), Dan Klayman (keyboards), David Hofstra (bass), Ralph Carney (reeds), and Ariel Warner (backing vocals).

Charas — the Electronic Alias (1992)

Under the alias Charas (Discogs artist ID: 62236), Butler wrote, produced, arranged, and mixed “Lunar Musique” (1992, Cowboy Records UK, cat# RODEO 3), a progressive house / tribal 12” with two mixes: “Full Moon Mix” and “Indoors After Mix.” Keyboards and programming were by Gary Burns (Discogs ID: 37783) and Jagz Kooner (Discogs ID: 9660). The Discogs alias entry for “Charas” (ID 62236) is a separate artist record from Butler’s main entry (ID 73253); the connection is documented in Butler’s alias list on his artist page.

Solo Output

The Wilderness Years Volume 1 (1995, Future Fossil Records)

A 7” single (cat# DIG1 FF/A) released on Butler’s own Future Fossil Records label (Discogs label ID: 470473). Two tracks: “A Hole In The Sky” (A-side) and “Davey’s Sister’s Home From College” (B-side). Engineered by Peter Dilg (Discogs ID: 723103). The label has only this one release in the Discogs database.

Songs For Unsung Holidays (2018, Smog Veil Records)

A CD album (cat# SV135CD) co-credited to Butler and Ralph Carney, released on Smog Veil Records (Discogs ID: 35627). 18 tracks, each titled after an obscure national holiday. Butler wrote tracks 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11–16; Carney wrote tracks 1, 3, 6, 8, 10. Both credited for “all noises.” Engineered and mastered by Gary Hobish (Discogs ID: 330016; alias Armin Hammer). Christine Repella (Discogs ID: 7214349) plays iPad synthesizer on “Lobster Day.” Hippie crowd vocals on track 13 by Becky Sue Armstrong, Scott Shepard, Tim Daugherty, Tracey Thomas Shepard, and Verne Davis. Genres: Jazz / Rock.

Scruffy the Cat — Tiny Days (1987, Relativity) was the Boston band’s debut LP. Butler produced; members included Charlie Chesterman (vocals/guitar/harmonica), Mac Paul Stanfield (bass), Stephen Fredette (guitar), Randall Lee Gibson IV (drums), Stona Fitch (banjo/organ/accordion).

Freedy Johnston — The Trouble Tree (1990, Bar/None Records) was the Kansas-born singer-songwriter’s debut album. Butler produced all 12 tracks and played guitar on tracks 5 and 9. Jody Harris also appeared on track 5.

The dB’s — Like This (1984, Bearsville) was the Chapel Hill/New York band’s third album, their first without Chris Stamey. Butler produced.
