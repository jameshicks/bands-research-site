Overview

Andy LeMaster is a multi-instrumentalist, songwriter, engineer, and producer based in Athens, Georgia, who co-founded the recording studio Chase Park Transduction with David Barbe and Andy Baker in 1997. Despite his Georgia base, his production and engineering work became deeply embedded in the Omaha indie scene: his closest collaborators include Mike Mogis, Conor Oberst, and Bright Eyes, with 31 credits on Saddle Creek spanning 1998–2023. Beyond the studio, he has performed and recorded as a member of Now It’s Overhead, Drip, Poulain, and The Remedy, working across indie rock, folk rock, and acoustic modes. His total credit count approaches 700 releases.

Notes (web)

Athens–Omaha connection: how it formed

The connection between LeMaster and the Saddle Creek/Omaha world ran through Chase Park Transduction before Now It’s Overhead existed as a signed act.

Chase Park Transduction as the entry point (1997–1998). Bright Eyes’ second album Letting Off the Happiness (Saddle Creek, 1998) was recorded across two locations: Conor Oberst’s basement in Omaha and Chase Park Transduction in Athens. LeMaster is credited on that record as recording/engineering engineer for tracks 4, 5, 6, and 9, with additional performance credits (vocals track 4 & 8, percussion track 4, bass track 5, lead guitar track 8). Chase Park Transduction opened in 1997 (per Discogs); the Letting Off the Happiness sessions ran March 1997 – March 1998, making this the earliest documented professional contact between LeMaster and the Oberst/Saddle Creek orbit. Source: Wikipedia — Letting Off the Happiness (as of 2026-11-03).

Saddle Creek label head Robb Nansel on the connection. In an interview with Lazy-I / Tim McMahan, Nansel described meeting LeMaster on the road: “We met Andy LeMaster (from Now It’s Overhead) on the road. He’s a good friend of ours.” He also noted that Orenda Fink and Maria Taylor of Azure Ray were “really good friends with Andy LeMaster and also play in Now It’s Overhead,” indicating the Athens cluster connected through LeMaster. Source: Lazy-I interview with Robb Nansel.

The Athens–Omaha connection documented (2017). Flagpole (Athens alt-weekly) traced the connection in a 2017 feature ahead of Conor Oberst’s 40 Watt show. It places Chase Park Transduction centrally: Letting Off the Happiness tracks were recorded there, and that studio relationship brought LeMaster into the Saddle Creek engineering and performing network. Now It’s Overhead and Azure Ray then became “two of the first non-Nebraska bands” signed to Saddle Creek. Source: Flagpole — “Tracing the Athens–Omaha Connection” (2017-10-11).

Conor Oberst on LeMaster (2024 Bandcamp reissue note). When the debut Now It’s Overhead album was reissued, Oberst described LeMaster as “one of the longest and most trusted musical collaborators of my life,” language consistent with a relationship that predates the 2001 Now It’s Overhead Saddle Creek debut. Source: Now It’s Overhead Bandcamp.

Summary of connection sequence:

  1. Chase Park Transduction opens 1997; Bright Eyes travel to Athens to record portions of Letting Off the Happiness there — LeMaster engineers and performs.
  2. LeMaster and Saddle Creek/Nansel meet “on the road” (date not pinned to a specific year in sources).
  3. Now It’s Overhead (with Athens friends Fink and Taylor) signed to Saddle Creek; debut released October 2001 — first non-Nebraska Saddle Creek signing.
  4. LeMaster becomes a fixture of Bright Eyes touring and recording through the 2000s.

What was not confirmed: No source names the precise year or tour on which Nansel and LeMaster first met on the road. The Flagpole article does not give a direct LeMaster quote about his own recollection of how it started. The Chase Park Transduction angle is documented via album credits and press, not via an LeMaster interview explaining his side.
