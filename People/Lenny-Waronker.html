Lenny Waronker

Lenny Waronker

Oct 13, 20269 min read

Lenny Waronker

Real name: Leonard Waronker
Born: October 3, 1941, Los Angeles, California, USA
Discogs ID: 249406
Name variations on sleeves: L. Waronker, Lennie Waronker, Lenny Woronker, Leonard Waronker, Waronker

American record producer and music executive. Staff producer at Warner Bros./Reprise from the mid-1960s through the early 1980s; president of Warner Bros. Records 1982–1992; co-founder (with Mo Ostin) of DreamWorks Records (1994–c.2003). Primary production partner of Russ Titelman across dozens of releases. Core producers’ stable included Randy Newman, Gordon Lightfoot, Ry Cooder, James Taylor (2), Rickie Lee Jones, and Maria Muldaur.

Bio

Family and early years

Waronker is the son of Si Waronker (Simon Waronker, Discogs ID: 352703), founder of Liberty Records. He grew up around the music business in Los Angeles. He started in the promotional division of Liberty Records in the early 1960s. He later moved to Reprise/Warner Bros., brought in on the recommendation of Dick Glasser (Discogs ID: 269663) and Jimmy Bowen, who had ties to the Warner roster.

Lenny’s first wife was Donna Loren (married 1968, divorced 1986); their children include Anna Waronker (Discogs ID: 280195, musician/singer-songwriter, that dog.) and Joey Waronker (Discogs ID: 293887, drummer — Beck, Atoms for Peace, R.E.M.).

1960s — Warner/Reprise staff producer

By the mid-1960s Waronker was producing singles and albums on the Warner Bros. and Reprise imprints. Early credits include the Beau Brummels on Warner Bros. (notably the debut Introducing the Beau Brummels, and later their 1975 reunion LP), the Harpers Bizarre series (their lush orchestral pop LPs Feelin’ Groovy, Anything Goes, The Secret Life of Harpers Bizarre, and Harpers Bizarre 4, 1967–1969), and Arlo Guthrie on Reprise (including the 1969 Reprise LP and later releases through the 1970s).

The pivotal early credit is Van Dyke Parks’s debut Song Cycle (Warner Bros., 1967, release ID: 3103416) — an elaborately orchestrated, critically celebrated art-pop album. Waronker is the sole producer. The session convened an extraordinary roster of LA studio musicians: Tommy Tedesco, Hal Blaine, Earl Palmer, Jim Gordon, and a full string and woodwind section.

He began collaborating with Parks again on the debut LP of Ry Cooder (Reprise, 1970, release ID: 2959324), where Van Dyke Parks co-produced and also played piano alongside Waronker.

Late 1960s–early 1970s — Randy Newman

Waronker produced Randy Newman’s entire early catalog on Reprise. The self-titled debut Randy Newman (1968, release ID: 4669713) was co-produced with Van Dyke Parks, with Lee Herschberg engineering. 12 Songs (1970, release ID: 2077568) followed, with Ry Cooder on slide guitar and a stripped-down band. Sail Away (1972, release ID: 897090) was co-produced with Russ Titelman — this became the standard production partnership for Newman throughout the 1970s and beyond. Good Old Boys (1974, release ID: 1050329) brought in Glenn Frey and Don Henley on backing vocals, with Ry Cooder on electric guitar. Little Criminals (1977, release ID: 495916), containing “Short People,” was again Waronker/Titelman.

The partnership continued into the 2000s: Waronker co-produced Harps and Angels (Nonesuch, 2008, release ID: 1556914) with Mitchell Froom, and the Randy Newman Songbook volumes (2008, 2011, 2016) on Nonesuch.

1970s — Gordon Lightfoot and the Reprise folk roster

Waronker was the sole producer of Gordon Lightfoot’s sustained commercial peak on Reprise throughout the early-to-mid 1970s. Key LPs:

  • If You Could Read My Mind (Reprise, 1970) — breakthrough pop crossover
  • Summer Side of Life (1971), Don Quixote (1972), Old Dan’s Records (1972)
  • Sundown (1974) — his biggest charting album, US #1
  • Cold On The Shoulder (1975), Summertime Dream (1976), Endless Wire (1978)

He also produced the compilations Gord’s Gold (1975) and later Gord’s Gold, Volume II (1988).

1970s — Ry Cooder, James Taylor, and others

Waronker produced Ry Cooder’s debut (1970) and several later albums including Into the Purple Valley (1972), Boomer’s Story (1972), and Paradise And Lunch (1974, Reprise). Co-producer with Russ Titelman on James Taylor’s Gorilla (Warner Bros., 1975, release ID: 700527) and subsequent Taylor LPs including In The Pocket (1976, co-produced). He also produced James Taylor’s Greatest Hits (Warner Bros., 1976, one of the best-selling compilation LPs on the label, 141+ pressings documented on Discogs).

The Doobie Brothers’ self-titled debut (Warner Bros., 1971) carries a Waronker producer credit, beginning an association with the band. Michael McDonald’s solo period on Warner Bros. in the 1980s also carried Waronker production credits.

1973–74 — Maria Muldaur with Joe Boyd

Waronker co-produced (with Joe Boyd) Maria Muldaur’s self-titled debut (Reprise, 1973, release ID: 1476142) and Waitress In A Donut Shop (Reprise, 1974, release ID: 2222250). Engineers on both: Donn Landee and Lee Herschberg. The debut contained “Midnight at the Oasis.” Waitress featured an extraordinary cast of session players: Doc Watson, Lowell George, Amos Garrett, Paul Butterfield, David Grisman, Mac Rebennack (Dr. John), Benny Carter, Ray Brown, Anna and Kate McGarrigle (backing vocals), Linda Ronstadt (backing vocals), and others — a showcase of Boyd’s and Waronker’s combined networks.

1979 — Rickie Lee Jones debut

Waronker and Russ Titelman co-produced Rickie Lee Jones’s debut album (Warner Bros., 1979, release ID: 719414). Engineered by Loyd Clifft and Roger Nichols. Session players included Michael McDonald (backing vocals), Steve Gadd, Jeff Porcaro, Mac Rebennack, Willie Weeks, and orchestral arrangements by Nick DeCaro and Johnny Mandel. The album went platinum and established Jones as a major artist.

1981 — Rickie Lee Jones Pirates

Waronker and Titelman produced Pirates (Warner Bros., 1981, release ID: 944344), Jones’s critically acclaimed second LP.

1980s — Paul Simon and others

Waronker is credited as co-producer on three tracks of Paul Simon’s Hearts And Bones (Warner Bros., 1983, release ID: 1177784), alongside Simon, Russ Titelman, and Roy Halee. He produced Randy Newman’s Trouble In Paradise (1983) and The Natural film score (1984). He also produced Eric Clapton’s Behind The Sun (Duck/Warner Bros., 1985) and early Arlo Guthrie LPs.

1982–1992 — President of Warner Bros. Records

In 1982 Waronker became president of Warner Bros. Records, succeeding Mo Ostin (who remained chairman). During this period his personal production credits shifted to executive producer roles on label-wide releases while he oversaw the broader Warner Bros. roster. He maintained close creative involvement with Randy Newman, and also executive-produced releases including the Ragtime film score by Newman (Elektra, 1981), the Three Amigos! soundtrack (1987), the Parenthood soundtrack (1989), and Orange Crate Art (Brian Wilson/Van Dyke Parks, Warner Bros., 1995).

1994–2003 — DreamWorks Records

After leaving Warner Bros. in 1992, Waronker co-founded DreamWorks Records with Mo Ostin. The label signed Rufus Wainwright (debut 1998, executive-produced by Waronker), released the Prince of Egypt soundtrack (1998), Shrek soundtrack (2001), and gave A&R direction to Elliott Smith (XO, 1998; Figure 8, 2000) and Rufus Wainwright (Poses, 2001; Want One, 2003). The label also issued soundtracks for several DreamWorks Animation films.

2000s–present — Later credits

Waronker returned to studio production with Randy Newman’s Harps and Angels (Nonesuch, 2008) and the Songbook series. He continued as A&R at Warner Bros. Records in later years (Gary Clark Jr., Kacey Musgraves, Jenny Lewis, Gary Allan, JPEG RAW). His most recent listed Discogs credit is 2024.
