Mike Banfield

Mike Banfield

Oct 28, 20266 min read

Mike Banfield

Guitarist, Pittsburgh PA. Discogs artist ID: 256515

Biography

Before Don Caballero: Slag (1989–1990)

Before founding Don Caballero, Banfield played guitar in Slag (11), a State College, Pennsylvania band. Slag released two records: a cassette or demo I Want Some Life (1989, Not On Label) and the LP Everything Is Nothing (1990, Broken Giraffe, ID: 210313). On both, he shared guitar duties with Len Jarabeck, with George Draguns on bass and Darren Zentek on drums (I Want Some Life credits Neal Witmer on drums instead of Zentek). These are the earliest documented Banfield recordings.

Founding Don Caballero (1991–1999)

Banfield co-founded Don Caballero in Pittsburgh in 1991 with drummer Damon Che (real name Damon Che Fitzgerald) and bassist Pat Morris. The band is widely cited as one of the originating acts in math rock, though the members reportedly disliked that label. Banfield and the other founding members brought in Ian Williams as a second guitarist in 1992, completing the classic four-piece lineup.

The band’s sound was built around two interlocking guitar parts — Williams’s finger-tapping, delay-heavy approach and Banfield’s complementary lines — held together by Che’s compositionally driven polyrhythmic drumming. There were no guitar solos in Don Caballero’s music.

The early recording history is documented on Singles Breaking Up (Vol. I) (1999, Touch And Go): early 7”s recorded by Lee Hollihan, with the Third Gear Records 7” And And And And And And And And And And / First Hits (1993) produced by Steve Albini (uncredited). Banfield’s first major label credit appears on the debut LP:

For Respect (1993, Touch And Go TG120) — debut LP with the founding four-piece (Morris on bass). Mastered by John Golden (credited as “JG”). Pat Morris left the band after this record.

Our Caballero / My Ten Year Old Lady Is Giving It Away (7”, 1993, Touch And Go) — four-piece single with Morris on bass, bridging the first two LPs.

Don Caballero 2 (1995, Touch And Go TG143) — widely regarded as the band’s landmark record. Matt Jencik replaced Morris on bass. Produced by Al Sutton, who became the band’s primary producer through the rest of the original run.

Waltor / Shuman Center 91 (7”, 1996, Chunklet Magazine) — credited to Damon Che, Mike Banfield, and Pat Morris (no Ian Williams), suggesting a trio configuration for this session.

What Burns Never Returns (1998, Touch And Go tg185cd) — Pat Morris returned on bass for this record, reuniting the full four-piece. Produced by Al Sutton, mixed by Bob Ebeling. Banfield’s guitar credit is listed with characteristic Don Caballero humor: “Mike Plays Guitar In The Bandfield.” After touring behind this record, both Morris and Banfield left the band. Banfield was replaced by Jon Fine (of Bitch Magnet); Morris was replaced by Eric Emm (of Ian Williams’s side project Storm And Stress).

When Damon Che revived Don Caballero in 2003 — drawing a new lineup from Pittsburgh’s Creta Bourzia scene — he invited both Banfield and Morris back. Both declined.

Banfield is credited on the 2014 archival compilation Five Pairs Of Crazy Pants. Wear ‘Em: Early Caballero (Chunklet Industries), which collects early-era Don Caballero material and documents the founding lineup’s recordings.

Knot Feeder (active c. 2009)

After Don Caballero, Banfield formed Knot Feeder with Andy Curl (bass), Rob Spagiare (drums, vocals), and Andrew Grossmann (guitar). Knot Feeder are described as a Pittsburgh math rock band featuring ex-members of Don Caballero, Tabula Rasa, and Southpaw.

Their only documented release is Light Flares (2009, Strings & Hammers Recordings, ID: 579936) — recorded by J. Robbins at his Magpie Cage studio; mastered by Kim Rosen. The release also features cellist Gordon Withers.
