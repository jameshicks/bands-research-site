Alec MacKaye

Alec MacKaye

Oct 24, 202610 min read

Alec MacKaye

Discogs ID: 543817 Origin: Washington, DC, USA Name variations (Discogs): A. Mac Kaye, A. MacKaye, A. Mackaye, Alec, Alec Mac Kaye, Alexander MacKaye, MacKaye, Mckay

Washington DC vocalist and younger brother of Ian MacKaye. A fixture of the Dischord Records world from its earliest days, MacKaye sang for four bands across four decades — Faith (3) (1982–1983), Untouchables (pre-Faith), Ignition (5) (1986–1989), The Warmers (1994–1997), and Hammered Hulls (2019–present) — all on or adjacent to Dischord Records. He also had a stint in Bells Of, an experimental DC outfit. His career arc runs from DC hardcore’s founding moment through Revolution Summer post-hardcore and into 21st-century work, always within a tight network of collaborators centered on Inner Ear Studios and the MacKaye family orbit.

Biography

Untouchables (1979–1981) — Pre-Faith

MacKaye’s earliest documented band is the Untouchables (Discogs ID: 71433), one of the founding DC hardcore groups, which he shared with Eddie Janney (guitar), Bert Queiroz (bass), and Rich Moore (drums). The Untouchables are credited on Flex Your Head (1982, Dischord No. 7), the essential DC hardcore compilation, where MacKaye’s vocals appear on tracks including “Nic Fit” — a song later covered by Sonic Youth. The band was active October 1979 to January 1981.

Henry Rollins (then with State Of Alert) reportedly tried to recruit MacKaye as his replacement when he left DC in 1981 to join Black Flag, but MacKaye declined. Instead, MacKaye, Michael Hampton (3) and Ivor Hanson (both formerly of State Of Alert) linked up with Chris Bald to form Faith.

Faith (3) / The Faith (1982–1983)

Faith (3) (also credited as The Faith; Discogs ID: 309576) was MacKaye’s primary band for roughly eighteen months and his most historically significant. The original lineup — MacKaye (vocals), Hampton (guitar), Bald (bass), Hanson (drums) — recorded one side of the landmark split LP with Void (12). Guitarist Eddie Janney, MacKaye’s former Untouchables bandmate, joined before the Subject To Change EP.

The Faith / Void split LP (1982, Dischord No. 8 — Discogs ID: 1128409)

The Faith occupy Side B (B1–B12); Void takes Side A. This is a co-release in the sense that the two bands share the record, but they are recorded and produced independently.

Faith split in 1983. Hampton, Bald, and Hanson moved to Embrace (2) with Ian MacKaye. Janney became a founder of Rites Of Spring. MacKaye’s next project took a few years to materialize.

Both Faith records were later compiled with the Void side on the Faith • Void • Faith CD (1993, Dischord; reissued 2002). Subject To Change was reissued with a first demo as Subject To Change Plus First Demo (2011, Dischord; reissued 2023).

Bells Of (mid-1980s)

Discogs lists MacKaye as a member of Bells Of (Discogs ID: 447089), an experimental project formed in 1985 by guitarist Lawrence McDonald. Bells Of started as a Revolution Summer-adjacent punk outfit with members from DC hardcore bands Faith and Capital Punishment, evolving into something closer to song-based free jazz indie-pop. MacKaye’s specific releases with Bells Of are not separately identified in his Discogs credits, but his membership overlaps with the mid-1980s gap between Faith and Ignition.

Ignition (5) (1986–1989)

Ignition (5) (Discogs ID: 261148) was MacKaye’s most prolific band in terms of releases, active from 1986 to 1989. The lineup was MacKaye (vocals), Chris Bald (guitar — the former Faith bassist, now switched instruments), Chris Thomson (bass), and Dante Ferrando (drums). All four major releases were produced by Ian MacKaye or in collaboration with him, and engineered by Don Zientara at Inner Ear.

MacKaye’s credit on this release is unusual: “Written-By [Conceived], Performer [Created]” — suggesting a more compositional or conceptual role. All four Ignition members share the same “Conceived / Created” credit. Geoff Turner directed/produced.

The Warmers (1994–1997)

The Warmers (Discogs ID: 309587) was a post-hardcore / indie rock band MacKaye formed in July 1994, breaking up in December 1997. The lineup was MacKaye (guitar, vocals), Juan Luis Carrera (bass, vocals), and Amy Farina (drums). Notable that Farina would later partner with Ian MacKaye in The Evens.

Side Credits and Collaborations (1989–2004)

MacKaye appears on several releases in non-primary capacities during the years between his main band projects:

  • Lily’s Stroke (1994, Dischord / Liberation Communications — Discogs ID: 1540559): Backing Vocals; Producer
  • Thin Air (1995) — The Warmers 7” (above)
  • Motor Cycle Wars Can Go Fuck Themselves E.P. (2001, Dead Teenagers self-released — Discogs ID: 12191164): Additional Vocals
  • Wanted: More (2004, Dischord — Discogs ID: 1630378): Performer
  • The Odds (2012, Dischord — Discogs ID: 4038334): Vocals [Guest]

The Orafying Mysticle Of… credit also appears in 2010 on a Dischord reissue (ID: 2403839) where MacKaye’s role is listed as “Created By.”

A 1989 credit on The Orafying Mysticle Of… and a 1995 credit on Thin Air include a writing credit — Thin Air (Discogs ID: 1140937) notes MacKaye as “Written-By.”

There is also a curious credit: Dirty (2015, DGC / Universal — Discogs ID: 7800891) lists MacKaye as “Written-By.” This appears to be the Sonic Youth album, which contains “Nic Fit” — the Untouchables song MacKaye co-wrote that Sonic Youth covered. His songwriting credit on the DGC release reflects that composition origin.

Hammered Hulls (2019–present)

Hammered Hulls (Discogs ID: 7308472) is MacKaye’s current active band, formed when guitarist Mark Cisneros brought together MacKaye (vocals), Mary Timony (bass), and Chris Wilson (drums). Timony and MacKaye grew up in the same Washington neighborhood. Cisneros, a DC-based guitarist influenced by both MacKaye’s and Timony’s earlier work plus free jazz, is described as the band’s initiating force.

All studio output produced by Ian MacKaye and recorded by Don Zientara — continuing a collaboration that runs back to Faith’s 1982 recordings.
