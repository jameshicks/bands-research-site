René Tinner

Swiss engineer, mixer, and producer. Born 18 February 1953, St. Gallen, Switzerland. Discogs artist ID: 99808.

Biography

René Tinner is a Swiss recording engineer and producer whose career spans from the mid-1970s to the present. He entered the Can orbit early: his first credited work is as a recording assistant on Can’s Landed (1975), where he is also listed with road management duties — a combination that suggests he worked inside the band’s touring and studio operation simultaneously. He continued with Can through Flow Motion (1976, Recorded By; Tour Manager) and Saw Delight (1977, Recorded By; Producer “with help from”), by which point he had moved from assistant to full recording engineer.

Through the late 1970s and into the 1980s, Tinner operated out of the Can Studio (later Inner Space Studio) in Weilerswist, Germany — the private facility built and maintained by the Can members. His credit as “Engineer [Toning. Inner Space Studio]” on Filmmusik (1980) and “Engineer [Recording, Can Studio, Weilerswist]” on a 1992 repress of Musk At Dusk confirms him as a house engineer at that facility. This studio association made him the natural engineer for any project passing through Can-connected infrastructure during the 1980s.

His production work expanded considerably through the decade. He produced or co-produced releases for major German acts including Marius Müller-Westernhagen (multiple albums and singles, 1987–1990), as well as a range of German-market rock, pop, and dance acts on labels including Warner Bros., WEA, Polydor, Mercury, Intercord, and Ariola. By the mid-1990s he was active in German dance and electronic music, with credits on Ladomat 2000 releases (From: Disco To: Disco, The Cold Song, both 1996) and ElektroMotor. His work for Spoon Records on Can archival reissues — CD pre-mastering and restoration — runs from roughly 1989 through the 2020s.

From the late 1980s onward his most consistent creative relationship is with Irmin Schmidt: Tinner is credited as engineer, mixer, and/or producer on every major Schmidt solo release. He continued with Schmidt through 5 Klavierstücke (2018) and Nocturne (2020), and handled the mixing for the archival Villa Wunderbar / A Selection (2013). From 2021 onward he is credited as compiler and editor on the ongoing series of Can live archive releases (Stuttgart 1975, Brighton 1975, Cuxhaven 1976, Paris 1973, Aston 1977, Keele 1977), which places him in the role of primary archivist for the Can live catalog in his later career.

Marius Müller-Westernhagen

Tinner’s most commercially significant production relationship outside Can was with German rock singer Marius Müller-Westernhagen, for whom he served as producer and recording/mixing engineer across several major Warner Bros. releases in the late 1980s to early 1990s. Many of these releases featured Jaki Liebezeit on drums.

Can Studio

Tinner’s credit as engineer at the Can Studio / Inner Space Studio in Weilerswist is a recurrent marker on Can and Can-adjacent recordings from 1975 through the early 1990s. The studio was the private recording facility of the Can members, and Tinner’s presence there from the beginning of his career — as assistant, then full engineer, then producer — makes him one of the longest-serving engineers associated with that space. His name appears as “Inner Space Studio” engineer on Irmin Schmidt’s Filmmusik series (1980–1989), on releases by Jaki Liebezeit (drums on multiple Westernhagen albums and Julian Dawson’s Luckiest Man), and on various German projects that used the facility.

  • Marius Müller-Westernhagen — producer and engineer on multiple major German rock releases, 1986–1992

  • Michael Karoli — co-engineer on Musk At Dusk; both active at Can Studio
  • David Sylvian — technical assistance on Flux + Mutability (1989); remixed on Plight & Premonition / Flux & Mutability (2018, Grönland Records)
  • Lou Reed — assistant engineer on Take No Prisoners (1978), engineer/mixer on The Bells (1979)
  • Jono Podmore — co-credited on Schmidt’s Villa Wunderbar and Cyclopean

  • Kompakt — mixed Was Ist Musik (2002) and Doppelleben (2005)
