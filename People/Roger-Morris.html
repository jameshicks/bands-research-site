Roger Morris

Full name: Roger Nicholas Morris
Discogs ID: 305879
Origin: UK
Role: Guitarist

Founding guitarist of The Psychedelic Furs, active with the band from their formation in 1977 through their second album Talk Talk Talk (1981). Departed alongside saxophonist Duncan Kilburn after that record; neither returned for the band’s subsequent studio work. Discogs carries no solo releases or post-Furs band credits beyond songwriter royalty attributions.

Biography

Origins and The Psychedelic Furs (1977–1981)

Roger Morris was one of the founding members when The Psychedelic Furs coalesced in London in 1977 around brothers Richard Butler (vocals) and Tim Butler (bass). The initial lineup also included Duncan Kilburn on saxophone, Vince Ely on drums, and John Ashton on guitar — Morris and Ashton sharing the dual-guitar role that gave the early Furs their dense, abrasive texture.

Their debut single, “We Love You” b/w “Pulse” (Epic, 1979), was produced by Howard Thompson and Ian Taylor alongside the band. It credited Morris as “R. Morris” on guitar, establishing the lineup that would carry through both albums.

The self-titled debut LP The Psychedelic Furs (CBS, 1980) was produced primarily by Steve Lillywhite (six tracks) with Thompson and Taylor producing three others. Morris is credited as “R Morris” on guitar throughout. The album positioned the Furs at the harder, noisier end of post-punk — tracks like “India,” “Sister Europe,” and “Imitation of Christ” are among the most abrasive in the catalog.

Talk Talk Talk (Columbia/CBS, 1981) was again produced by Lillywhite and again featured Morris on guitar alongside Ashton. It is the last studio album on which Morris played. The tracklist includes “Pretty In Pink,” “Dumb Waiters,” and “Into You Like A Train” — songs to which Morris holds a co-writing credit (attributed collectively to The Psychedelic Furs) and which continue to generate royalties on compilations into the 2010s.

Departure (1981)

Following Talk Talk Talk, both Roger Morris and Duncan Kilburn left the band. The Furs restructured around the Butler brothers, Ashton, and Ely for Forever Now (1982, produced by Todd Rundgren), which moved toward a more polished sound. Morris’s departure is well-documented but his reasons have not been detailed in Discogs sources.

Post-Furs Activity

Discogs records no solo releases, side projects, or credited session appearances by Morris after 1981. His credit trail in the database is entirely Psychedelic Furs material, either as guitarist on original recordings or as songwriter on reissues and compilations.

Kilburn, by contrast, surfaced on a 1982 single by Georgia II (“The Flag,” PRT) as a session saxophonist — no parallel session work appears under Morris’s name.

Legacy Credits

Morris appears as co-writer (credited via the band collective) on every major Psychedelic Furs retrospective:

  • All Of This And Nothing (Columbia, 1988) — compilation; Morris credited Written-By
  • Pretty In Pink soundtrack single (CBS/A&M, 1986) — the re-recorded hit, using the Furs’ original co-writing credit
  • Should God Forget: A Retrospective (Columbia/Legacy, 1997) — 2xCD; Morris credited Guitar on the tracks sourced from the first two albums (disc 1, tracks 1–12)
  • Alive (For Once In My Lifetime) (Columbia/Legacy, 2001) — Written-By
  • Various best-of compilations through Sony BMG (2007–2011)

In 2021, the UK synth-pop act Rocket Report released “Highs Like This” (Rocket Report LAUNCH002), a digital single whose B-side is a cover of “Sister Europe.” Morris is credited Written-By on that track alongside the original Furs co-writers.

Radio Sessions

The Radio One Sessions (Strange Fruit, 1997) collects BBC recordings from across the band’s career. Morris is credited on guitar for tracks 1–12 (the early sessions), while Knox Chandler covers the later ones. Duncan Kilburn plays saxophone on the same tracks 1–12. This is the most detailed documentation in Discogs of Morris performing across different BBC sessions circa 1979–1981.
