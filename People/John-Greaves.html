Biography

Early Years and Henry Cow (1969–1976)

Greaves played double bass and bass guitar in school, and was with the Ray Irving Showband from 1964 to 1968. He encountered little musical stimulation at Cambridge until he met Henry Cow in the autumn of 1969, and joined the band as bassist. Henry Cow — whose other core members included Fred Frith (guitar), Tim Hodgkinson (organ, reeds), Chris Cutler (drums), Geoff Leigh (woodwinds), and later Lindsay Cooper (bassoon) and Dagmar Krause (vocals) — was the most compositionally ambitious British rock ensemble of the 1970s, blending free improvisation, contemporary classical influence, and collective composition.

Greaves contributed bass, piano, and vocals across all the band’s studio albums (Virgin, 1973–1976). His composition credits appear on Unrest (1974), and his multi-instrumentalism — bass, piano, whistle, celesta — is documented across all releases. He left Henry Cow in March 1976.

Kew. Rhone. and Post-Cow Collaborations (1977–1982)

After leaving Henry Cow, Greaves immediately co-produced and performed on Kew. Rhone. (1977, Virgin), a landmark album credited to John Greaves, Peter Blegvad, and Lisa Herman. Greaves composed the music and arranged the album; Blegvad wrote the lyrics. The album was recorded in New York with an impressive cast including Carla Bley (saxophone, vocals), Michael Mantler (trumpet, trombone, engineer), Andrew Cyrille (drums), and Michael Levine (violin/viola). Greaves played piano, organ, bass, and vocals; Blegvad played guitar and vocals. Kew. Rhone. is considered one of the key documents of the art-song/chamber-rock wing of the Canterbury/RIO scene.

In parallel, Greaves joined two Canterbury-adjacent groups:

  • National Health (Discogs ID: 299212): the progressive jazz-rock ensemble featuring Dave Stewart, Phil Miller, Pip Pyle, and others. He appeared on their debut self-titled album (1978, Affinity) and Of Queues and Cures (1978, Charly Records).
  • Soft Heap (Discogs ID: 299215): the Canterbury improv group with Alan Gowen, Elton Dean, Hugh Hopper, and Pip Pyle. Their eponymous LP appeared in 1979 on Charly Records.

He also appeared on Peter Blegvad’s The Sound of the Sand (1981, Rough Trade) as bassist/pianist.

Solo Work and Paris Period (1982–1995)

From the early 1980s, Greaves pursued a solo singer-songwriter/composer path, largely based in France. His debut solo album Accident (1982, Europa Records, France) featured Pip Pyle on drums, Geoffrey Richardson (viola), Jean-Luc Fauvel (guitar), and Yochk’o Seffer, with Blegvad contributing lyrics and cover art. The follow-up Parrot Fashions (1984, Europa Records) brought in Mireille Bauer (drums/vibraphone), François Ovide (guitar/trombone), Denis Van Hecke (cello), and Kristoffer Blegvad (backing vocals), with Joe Blaney as engineer/co-producer.

A sustained partnership with David Cunningham produced Greaves, Cunningham (1991, Eva (7), Japan), a co-billed album on which Cunningham composed most tracks and produced, while Greaves provided voice, bass, keyboards, and lyrics. The album also featured Jakko M. Jakszyk (vocals) and Kristoffer Blegvad (acoustic guitar).

Songs (1995, Resurgence RES112CD; also issued in Japan on Toy’s Factory/Idyllic Records) is the centrepiece of this middle period — a richly collaborative song-cycle featuring:

Also during this period, Greaves contributed to The Yellow Box (1996, Piano), the duo album by Peter Gordon and David Cunningham, on which he plays bass guitar and is credited as co-composer on multiple tracks.

Around this period he also appeared on Michael Mantler’s Live (1987, WATT) and The School of Understanding (1997, ECM), and Peter Blegvad’s Just Woke Up (1995, ReR Megacorp) — a trio album with Blegvad and Chris Cutler.

Continued Collaboration with Blegvad and Cutler (1995–2000)

The Blegvad/Greaves/Cutler trio recorded two albums:

  • Just Woke Up (1995, ReR Megacorp): produced by Bob Drake; guests include Tim Hodgkinson, Jakko M. Jakszyk, BJ Cole, Geraint Watkins
  • Hangman’s Hill (1998, ReR Megacorp): produced by Bob Drake; guests include BJ Cole, Chris Stamey, Adam Phillips, Geraint Watkins

Greaves also issued solo albums Unearthed (1994, Sub Rosa / The Catalogue) and The Caretaker (2000, Blueprint (4)), and collaborated on La Petite Bouteille de Linge (1991, La Lichère) and the Rose C’est La Vie and Passage du Nord Ouest projects with French musicians.

France-Based and Later Group Work (2000–present)

Greaves was a member of Le Dogme Des VI Jours (Discogs ID: 716806), a French project led by Marcel Kanche, resulting in their 2004 album on No Format / Universal Music Jazz France. He was also part of Maman (Discogs ID: 740158), a French ensemble also featuring Fabrice Sansonetti, Jef Morin, Nicolas Mizrachi, and Thierry Negro.

He released The Pig Part (2001, Voiceprint), The Trouble With Happiness (2003, Le Chant Du Monde), and Chansons… (2004, Le Chant Du Monde), and an extended sequence of albums on ReR Megacorp.

A major later project was an extended Verlaine song-cycle across multiple volumes (Verlaine, Verlaine 2: Divine Ignorante, Verlaine Gisant), drawing on texts by Paul Verlaine. Other late releases include Life Size (2018, Manticore) and Folly Bololey: Songs From Robert Wyatt’s Rock Bottom (2019, Dark Companion) — a tribute to Wyatt’s 1974 masterpiece featuring members of the North Sea Radio Orchestra and others.

Greaves formed a duo with Italian singer/multi-instrumentalist Annie Barbazza, and toured Italy with a band featuring King Crimson members Mel Collins and Jakko M. Jakszyk, along with Régis Boulard and Annie Whitehead.

The most recent Discogs credits include Earthly Powers (2023, Dark Companion) and Then Again (2025, Dark Companion).
