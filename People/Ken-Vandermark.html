Based: Chicago, Illinois, USA (from 1989)
Instruments: Tenor and baritone saxophone, clarinet, bass clarinet

Overview

Ken Vandermark is an American reedist and composer who has been a central organising force in the Chicago avant-garde jazz and free improvisation scene since relocating from Boston in 1989. His career spans solo recordings, dozens of ensembles of his own design (most prominently the Vandermark 5 and the six-chapter Territory Band series), and an extensive network of international collaborations — most densely with the Scandinavian and Central European free improv world. He is a MacArthur Fellow (1999). Beyond performance he has been a key presenter: he and writer John Corbett founded the Empty Bottle Wednesday Night Jazz Series in 1996, and from 2006 he co-directed the Immediate Sound Series at the Hideout with Mitch Cocanig. He has also been a member of Umbrella Music, a musician-run Chicago presenter collective, since 2005.

His father is Stu Vandermark (Discogs artist ID 2017901), also a musician.

Early Career (pre-1997)

Vandermark’s earliest dated Discogs credit is a 1984 session (tenor saxophone on a Crystal Reflections release by 4th Stream, artist ID 13297377). After moving to Chicago in 1989 his first documented leader work was The Vandermark Quartet (1993–94, Platypus Records (2)): Big Head Eddie (1993) and the 7” Here Comes Mr. Weasel / Leadfoot (1994), with Kent Kessler (bass), Michael Zerang (drums), and Daniel Scanlan (violin/guitar/cornet). Kessler carried directly into the Vandermark 5.

He joined the NRG Ensemble for Constructive Destruction (Quinnah Records, 1994) — the post-Hal Russell studio album — playing tenor, clarinet, and bass clarinet. He is also listed as a member of The Flying Luttenbachers, whose name references Russell’s birth surname. First recordings under his own name as primary artist: A Meeting In Chicago (Eighth Day Music, 1997) and Like Rays (Knitting Factory Works, 1998).

Notable guest appearances include clarinet on Gastr del Sol’s Camoufleur (Domino, 1998), saxophone on Alan Licht and Loren Mazzacane Connors’s Hoffman Estates (Drag City, 1998), and saxophone on a Superchunk tribute compilation Come Pick Me Up (Merge Records, 1999).
