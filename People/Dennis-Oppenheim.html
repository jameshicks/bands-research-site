Dennis Oppenheim

Conceptual artist, performance artist, earth artist, sculptor, photographer. Born September 6, 1938, Electric City, Washington, USA. Died January 21, 2011, New York City. Discogs ID: 519503.

Father of Chandra Oppenheim and Kristin Oppenheim. Connected to the no wave scene through his daughter Chandra: Eugenie Diserio (of Model Citizens (3) / The Dance) knew Chandra because Chandra performed in Dennis’s performance art pieces from an early age — Diserio described them as “really haunting and demonic.” Diserio was a fan of Oppenheim’s visual art work independently of this personal connection. Their overlapping downtown NYC worlds led to Diserio and Steve Alexander collaborating with Chandra on the Transportation EP (1980). [source: Talkhouse interview with Eugenie Diserio, https://www.talkhouse.com/bile-sister-and-eugenie-diserio-discuss-the-apocalypse/] [corroborated: Post-Punk Monk, https://postpunkmonk.com/2023/04/05/model-citizens-nyc-1978-1979-a-vibrant-blast-of-no-wave-skirting-the-edges-of-pop/]

Conflict note: An earlier vault entry described Diserio as Oppenheim’s “student” and Oppenheim as an “art professor.” Neither the Talkhouse interview nor the Post-Punk Monk article supports a teacher-student relationship. Wikipedia’s Dennis Oppenheim article lists no teaching position at Columbia or any university where Diserio studied (she attended Tyler School of Art / Temple University for BFA and Columbia University for MFA — no confirmed overlap with Oppenheim’s faculty positions). The “student” framing appears to be an error; the actual relationship was scene-based, mediated through Chandra.

Web sources — downtown NYC music connections

Theme for a Major Hit (1974) — musical collaboration

Oppenheim’s 1974 installation Theme for a Major Hit is the clearest documented intersection with the music world. The piece featured a motorized marionette (a self-portrait cast in bronze) “clop-dancing” to a two-hour rock recording of a song Oppenheim wrote: “It Ain’t What You Make It’s What Makes You Do It.” Recorded at Angel Sound, New York, 1974. The performers were:

  • Diego Cortez — electric organ
  • Roger Welch — drums
  • Bill Beckley — guitar and vocals
  • Connie Beckley — vocals
  • Jim Ballard — vocals
  • Christa Maiwald — vocals
  • John Shole — vocals

All participants were SoHo conceptual-art-world peers, not musicians from the No Wave circuit per se — but Diego Cortez is the significant overlap figure: he would go on to curate the landmark “New York/New Wave” exhibition at P.S.1 in 1981, and is a central connective tissue between the visual art and No Wave music worlds. Roger Welch was a lifelong friend of Oppenheim’s from a 1969 studio visit in Brooklyn; Bill Beckley was another SoHo conceptualist. None of the band members has a documented No Wave music career.

The recording was reissued in 2016 on Slowscan as a double LP. It is on Discogs (release ID 7975260, master 943208).

[source: Hyperallergic, https://hyperallergic.com/homage-to-absurdity-the-restless-legacy-of-dennis-oppenheim/] [source: Soundohm product page, https://www.soundohm.com/product/theme-for-a-major-hit] [source: web search aggregate — Discogs, MoMA, Wikipedia entries confirm the same credits list] [Roger Welch biography: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Welch, as of 2026-07-28]

The Kitchen — March 1979

Oppenheim presented recent work at The Kitchen (NYC) in a show running 7–31 March 1979. This establishes a direct institutional link to the venue central to the downtown experimental music and performance scene of that period. Documentation held at the Getty Research Collections.

[source: Getty Research Collections finding aid, https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/component/10ZRBA]

Chandra at The Kitchen and Franklin Furnace — indirect connection

Chandra Oppenheim performed her own theatrical works at The Kitchen and Franklin Furnace as a child — venues Dennis was embedded in through his art-world connections — and once opened for Laurie Anderson at one of these spaces. These appearances were a product of the Oppenheim household’s immersion in the downtown performance art world, not a direct Dennis-to-music-scene collaboration, but they document the pipeline by which Chandra entered those spaces and eventually the No Wave scene.

[source: Slamjam/ADDPMP — Chandra Oppenheim profile, https://addpmp.slamjam.com/posts/chandra-oppenheim]

Summary assessment

Beyond the Diserio/Chandra overlap, Oppenheim’s documented connection to the downtown NYC music scene is primarily through:

  1. Theme for a Major Hit (1974) — a rock recording made with art-world peers, including Diego Cortez who was a No Wave scene hub figure.
  2. A 1979 show at The Kitchen, the institutional heart of downtown experimental music/performance.
  3. The broader SoHo/TriBeCa art-world milieu (shared with Cortez, Welch, Beckley) that overlapped heavily with the No Wave scene’s venue circuit and social world.

No evidence found of Oppenheim collaborating directly with No Wave bands, being documented at CBGB or Mudd Club himself, or having commissioned or performed alongside No Wave musicians in any work.
