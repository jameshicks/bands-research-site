David Leonard

David Leonard

Aug 06, 202610 min read

David Leonard

Recording engineer, mixer, and producer. Discogs ID: 130653

Career spans from 1979 to the present, encompassing four distinct phases: Los Angeles studio work in the late 1970s–early 1980s (Sunset Sound and associated rooms); a commercial mixing peak through the late 1980s and 1990s; a significant production run with John Mellencamp and the Indigo Girls in the 1990s; and an enduring relationship with Metalworks Studios and Rush-orbit Canadian rock from 2000 onward. He is one of the most commercially active mixing engineers of the mainstream rock and pop era, with credits across multi-platinum albums by Prince, the Bangles, Fishbone, Soul Asylum, the Barenaked Ladies, and k.d. lang — alongside his signature role as the primary mixer of Rush’s Vapor Trails (2002) and producer/engineer of Geddy Lee’s solo album My Favorite Headache (2000).

Biography

1979–1983 — Los Angeles Assistant Engineer

Leonard’s Discogs-documented career begins in 1979 as an assistant and house engineer at Sunset Sound Studios in Los Angeles, one of the city’s premier tracking rooms. Early credits in this period are secondary — “Engineer [Assistant]” or “Engineer [House]” designations — and span a remarkably wide range of commercial sessions:

  • Stanley Clarke — I Wanna Play For You (Epic / Nemperor, 1979): house engineer and mixer
  • Hall & Oates — X-Static (RCA, 1979): assistant engineer at Sunset Sound
  • John Mellencamp — Nothin’ Matters And What If It Did (Riva/Mercury, 1980): assistant / additional engineer — an early point of contact with an artist he would return to repeatedly
  • UB40 — Shine (RCA, 1980): assistant engineer at Sunset Sound Studios
  • Elton John — 21 At 33 (MCA, 1980): second engineer
  • Toto — Toto IV (CBS, 1982): recording engineer (additional) — one of the era’s biggest-selling albums; chief engineers Al Schmitt and Greg Ladanyi; Leonard among a large team in the room
  • The Go-Go’s — Vacation (I.R.S., 1982): “Engineer [Assisted By]”

This phase establishes him as a sought-after floor engineer in the major-label West Coast circuit. By the mid-1980s he had transitioned from assistant work to lead roles.

1984–1986 — Sunset Sound Lead Engineer: Prince, Bangles, Fishbone

Leonard’s step up to lead engineering coincides with his most recognizable early credit: Prince and the Revolution — Purple Rain (Warner Bros., 1984). He is credited across multiple locations and functions on this landmark album:

  • “Recorded By [At The Warehouse In The Summer Of ‘83]” (tracks A1, A4), alongside Susan Rogers
  • “Recorded By [Live At 1st Avenue In The Summer Of ‘83 By]” (tracks B2–B4), alongside David Rivkin
  • “Recorded By [Sunset Sound]” (tracks A2, A3, B1), alongside Peggy McCreary
  • “Overdubbed By [All Overdubs]” throughout, alongside Peggy McCreary

He continued in Prince’s studio orbit: Prince and the Revolution — Around The World In A Day (Paisley Park, 1985) credits him as one of the core engineers alongside McCreary, Susan Rogers, and David Tickle. He would return once more for Prince — Parade (Paisley Park, 1986) as an additional recorder. These credits reflect Leonard’s deep integration into the Sunset Sound / Prince production workflow of the mid-1980s.

Outside the Prince camp, key 1984–1986 engineering credits include:

  • Ice Cream Castle — The Time (Warner Bros., 1984): engineer
  • Fishbone — Fishbone (Columbia, 1985) EP: engineer
  • Bangles — Different Light (CBS, 1985): mixer — his first prominent mixing credit on a mainstream hit album (produced by David Kahne; “Walk Like An Egyptian” is among the tracks)
  • Sheena Easton — remix engineer for “Strut” (1984) and “Sugar Walls” (1984) on EMI America
  • Fishbone — In Your Face (Columbia, 1986): mixer — his second full Fishbone album credit
  • First sole producer credits appear: Hand To Mouth (I.R.S., 1986) and related I.R.S. singles, and George Strait productions

1987–1991 — Mixing and Production: Bangles, Alarm, Cougar, Michelle Shocked

Through the late 1980s Leonard becomes a go-to mixing engineer on the major-label circuit, with a growing stack of credits:

  • Bangles — “Walking Down Your Street” (CBS, 1987): mixer
  • John Mellencamp — The Lonesome Jubilee (Mercury, 1987): engineer and mixer — a major Mellencamp album with Lisa Germano on violin; Bob Ludwig mastered
  • Michelle Shocked — Short Sharp Shocked (Cooking Vinyl / London, 1988): mixer — one of the era’s most critically noted roots-rock debuts
  • Fishbone — Truth And Soul (Columbia, 1988): mixer
  • A Vision Shared: A Tribute To Woody Guthrie And Leadbelly (CBS, 1988): engineer and mixer
  • Pretenders — From Langley Park To Memphis (CBS / Kitchenware, 1988): engineer and mixer
  • 101 — Depeche Mode (MCA, 1989): mixer (live album)
  • Runaway Horses — Belinda Carlisle (Virgin, 1989): engineer

Production credits begin accumulating: Voices Of Babylon — The Outfield (CBS, 1989); Blackout In The Red Room — Love/Hate (CBS/Columbia, 1990); Chance — Philip Bailey (Epic, 1990, producer/engineer/mixer).

He also picks up work as an independent producer:

  • Nothing But A Burning Light — Bruce Cockburn (Columbia, 1991): mixer
  • Rumor And Sigh — Richard Thompson (Capitol, 1991): recording engineer

1992–1999 — Sustained Commercial Run: Soul Asylum, Indigo Girls, Mellencamp, Barenaked Ladies

The 1990s represent Leonard’s period of highest commercial throughput. Mixing credits across the decade include:

  • Grave Dancers Union — Soul Asylum (Columbia, 1992): mixer — “Runaway Train” is among the tracks; one of the best-selling alternative rock albums of the early 1990s
  • Rites Of Passage — Indigo Girls (Epic, 1992): mixer — first contact with the Indigo Girls
  • Tongues And Tails — Sophie B. Hawkins (Columbia, 1992): mixer
  • Family Groove — Maynard Ferguson (A&M, 1992): mixer and producer
  • Swamp Ophelia — Indigo Girls (Epic, 1994): engineer and mixer — second Indigo Girls album
  • Human Wheels — John Mellencamp (Mercury, 1993): producer, engineer, and mixer — the deepest engagement yet on a Mellencamp record; also credited: Malcolm Burn, Mike Wanchic, Lisa Germano (violin); mastered by Bob Ludwig
  • Harbor Lights — Bruce Hornsby (BMG/RCA, 1993): mixer
  • Shaming Of The Sun — Indigo Girls (Epic, 1997): producer, engineer, and mixer — full production role on a major Indigo Girls album; also engineer Caram Costanzo; mastered Bob Ludwig
  • Stunt — Barenaked Ladies (Reprise, 1998): producer, mixer, and Phase One studio recording engineer — the biggest commercial project of his career by sales (the album contained “One Week”); co-produced with Susan Rogers; mastered by Stephen Marcussen
  • Stunt also generates successful singles that Leonard mixed: “Call And Answer” and “One Week” remix (Reprise, 1998)
  • k.d. lang — Hymns Of The 49th Parallel (Nonesuch, 2004): recorded and mixed — Canadian collaborators included Ben Mink (guitar/producer) and arranger Eumir Deodato

Notable mixing credits in the period also include: Disc One: All Their Greatest Hits 1991–2001 — Barenaked Ladies (2001) and Musical Chairs — Hootie & The Blowfish (Atlantic, 1998).

2000–2004 — Metalworks and Rush

By 2000 Leonard has shifted toward Toronto and Metalworks Studios as his primary base. Two recordings from this period define his Canadian legacy:

Geddy Lee — My Favourite Headache (Anthem, 2000 Canada; Atlantic, 2000 US):

  • Credit: Producer, Engineer, Mixed By
  • Co-producers: Geddy Lee and Ben Mink
  • Joel Kazmi and Ian Bodzasi assisted at Metalworks
  • Matt Cameron (Soundgarden/Pearl Jam) on drums for most tracks; Jeremy Taggart (Our Lady Peace) on track 8; Chris Stringer additional percussion
  • Howie Weinberg mastered
  • A dense hard rock / progressive record; Lee’s only solo album

Rush — Vapor Trails (Anthem / Atlantic, 2002):

  • Credit: Mixed By
  • Producers: Rush (self-produced) and Paul Northfield
  • Recorded by Geddy Lee, Alex Lifeson, and Paul Northfield; Chris Stringer assisted recording
  • Joel Kazmi (credited as “Joel Kamzi” — a Discogs name variant) assisted Leonard on mixing at Metalworks
  • Mastered by Howie Weinberg and Roger Lian
  • Rush’s first album after a nearly six-year hiatus following Neil Peart’s personal tragedies

Rush — Feedback (Atlantic, 2004):

  • Credit: Producer, Recorded By, Engineer, Mixed By — a full-service production role
  • A covers EP of 1960s garage/psychedelic influences
  • Phase One Pro Tools recorded by Michael Jack; Jeff Muir assisted; mastered by Stephen Marcussen
  • Art direction by Hugh Syme

These three records represent the apex of his relationship with the Rush / Anthem / Metalworks ecosystem.

2005–Present — Ongoing Work

Leonard has remained active as a mixer and engineer through the 2010s and 2020s, working primarily in Americana, country, and legacy-rock contexts:

  • k.d. lang follow-up work: Watershed (Nonesuch, 2008), Recollection (Nonesuch, 2010), Jupiters Darling / Red Velvet Car (Eagle Records, 2014)
  • Dwight Yoakam — multiple entries: Dwight Live (1995), Dwight Sings Buck (New West, 2007), 3 Pears (Warner Bros., 2012), Fanatic (Legacy, 2012)
  • John Mellencamp — Plain Spoken: From The Chicago Theatre (Eagle Rock, 2018), Sad Clowns & Hillbillies (Republic, 2017), Other People’s Stuff (Republic, 2018), Strictly A One-Eyed Jack (Republic, 2022), Orpheus Descending (Republic, 2023)
  • Emmylou Harris — Red Velvet Car (Legacy, 2010)
  • When Doves Cry (Atmos Mix) — Prince (NPG Records, 2019): Atmos mix
  • Scarecrow — John Mellencamp (UMe, 2022): new Dolby Atmos and stereo remixes
  • D — Dwight Yoakam (Virgin Music, 2024): Atmos mixing engineer

He also received an unusual “Musician” credit on a 2010 Live Horses EP (Atlantic) — one of only a handful of non-engineering credits in his entire discography.

Open Questions

  • Leonard’s exact relationship with Metalworks Studios (staff engineer vs. long-term freelancer) is not documented in Discogs.
  • His early 1980s assistant credits cluster at Sunset Sound specifically — whether he was on staff or a regular freelancer there is unclear.
  • The gap between ~1999 and 2000 (transition from LA to Toronto-based work) has no documentary record in Discogs.
  • Whether he has toured with any artists he has worked with (the 2010 “Musician” credit on Live Horses EP is unexplained).
  • His Atmos remixing work (Prince, Mellencamp) suggests active engagement with spatial audio, but the full scope of that work may not be captured in Discogs.
