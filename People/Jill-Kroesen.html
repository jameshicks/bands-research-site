Jill Kroesen

Composer, musician, performance/video/visual artist. Discogs ID: 294085.

Active in New York in the 1970s and 1980s as a No Wave musician, composer, and multimedia artist. Studied experimental music at Mills College (Oakland, California) before moving to New York — the same Mills lineage that produced Peter Gordon and connects to Robert Ashley and Terry Riley. One of the founding members of Love of Life Orchestra (1977 founding lineup alongside Arthur Russell, Peter Zummo, Blue Gene Tyranny, David Van Tieghem).

Her work sits at the intersection of the Ashley/text-sound tradition, No Wave performance, and LOLO’s pluralist downtown scene. Appeared on Arthur Russell’s 24→24 Music (Sleeping Bag, 1981, as Dinosaur L); on Ashley’s Perfect Lives performances. Primary labels: Lovely Music, Infidelity (2) — both core to the Downtown Music Scene.

Scene Affiliation (resolved)

Primary affiliation: New York downtown / No Wave circle.

The ONCE Group was Robert Ashley’s 1961–1969 Ann Arbor collective (with Roger Reynolds, Gordon Mumma, et al.) — it predates Kroesen’s professional life by roughly a decade. Kroesen’s link to Ashley is as a student (Mills College MFA, where Ashley directed the Center for Contemporary Music) and later as a performer on Perfect Lives (1983 TV opera, The Kitchen commission). She carried Ashley’s text-sound and art-music-meets-pop-vernacular aesthetic to New York, but she is not and has never been described as a member of or successor to the ONCE Group itself.

Her career is wholly rooted in Downtown Manhattan:

  • Arrived New York 1974; first NYC performance Dear Ashley at The Kitchen (1975), with Rhys Chatham on guitar and Arthur Russell on cello. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-16)
  • Major works performed at The Kitchen, Mudd Club, CBGB, the Performing Garage, and similar downtown venues. (Whitney Museum)
  • Whitney Museum identifies her as “an essential figure in the 1970s downtown New York performance milieu.” (whitney.org)
  • Long-term collaborators — Peter Gordon (since Mills, 1973), Joe Hannan, Carol Clements, Sharon Mattlin — are all downtown New York figures, not Ann Arbor/ONCE circle members. (Hyperallergic)
  • 1980 EP on Lust/Unlust Music; 1982 LP Stop Vicious Cycles on Lovely Music — both No Wave / downtown experimental imprints. (Wikipedia)
  • Received NYFA video fellowship 1985; subsequently worked as a video engineer in NYC.

The Ashley influence is real and significant — her own quote: “We didn’t think of ourselves as composers doing music, but as artists doing art, with music one of our components” reflects his teaching — but the scene she built and performed in was unambiguously the New York downtown / No Wave world, not the Michigan experimental circuit.
