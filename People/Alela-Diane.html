Alela Diane

Real name: Alela Diane Menig
Discogs ID: 1139858
Born: April 20, 1983, Nevada City, California
Base: Portland, Oregon (as of profile)
Name variations: Alela; Alela Diane & Wild Devine
Groups: Headless Heroes

American folk singer-songwriter and multi-instrumentalist. Emerged from the Northern California freak-folk scene in the mid-2000s, self-releasing early material before signing to the UK independent Names (a sublabel of 679 Recordings). Known for dark pastoral songwriting, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, and a close-knit circle of collaborators centered on family and the Portland/Bay Area folk underground.

Biography

Early Years & Self-Releases (2003–2005)

Alela Diane Menig grew up in Nevada City, California. Her earliest documented recordings — Forest Parade (2003, self-released, credited as “Alela”) and The Pirate’s Gospel (2004, self-released) — were made at home with family and friends. The Pirate’s Gospel featured her father Tom Menig on guitars, recording, and mixing; Mariee Sioux (born Mariee Sioux Sobonya) on backing vocals across multiple tracks; Miranda Menig (sister) on vocals; and a small ensemble of Nevada City locals — Matt Gottschalk (banjo, vocals), Jeff Thorsby (vocals), Kaleb Wilbourne (vocals), Arthur Herald Echternacht IV (bass), Ryan Schwartz (mandolin, vocals), and Lindsay Clark (piano). Tom Menig recorded and mixed the whole album; Alela Diane is credited as performer, songwriter, vocals, and guitar. The self-released cassette circulated regionally and found its way to the UK label Names.

UK Breakthrough: Songs Whistled Through White Teeth & the Names Deal (2006)

Names (UK, sublabel of 679) licensed and released Songs Whistled Through White Teeth in 2006 — a stripped-down document of her early style with Matt Bauer on banjo, Mariee Sioux on vocals, and Tom Menig performing and recording. Alela Diane handled design herself. The record introduced her to a European audience and launched a sustained UK/European touring and press profile.

Headless Heroes & The Silence Of Love (2008)

In 2008 Alela Diane appeared as vocalist on The Silence Of Love by Headless Heroes, an LA-based orchestral art-pop collective led (uncredited) by producer Hugo Nicolson and featuring a cast including Leo Abrahams (strings, arrangements, BVs), Gus Seyffert (bass, guitar), Joey Waronker (drums), Josh Klinghoffer (guitar, bass, piano, organ, strings), and Woody Jackson (guitar, lap steel). The album appeared on Fargo (5) in Europe. Alela Diane is credited as one of “The Cast” — vocalists assembled by Nicolson around a collection of songs. The group also recorded The North Wind Blew South (2008, Names) and a self-titled EP (2009).

To Be Still (2009)

Her second proper studio album, released on Names. Co-produced by Alela Diane, Tom Menig, and Dan Elkan. Additional musicians included Thom Monahan, Adam Selzer, Neal Morgan, Nina Gerber, Michael Hurley, Mariee Sioux, Matt Bauer, and Alina Hardin. Mastered by JJ Golden. A deeper, fuller production than the self-released debut, retaining acoustic folk instrumentation while expanding into light electric and string textures. Also that year: the collaborative EP Alela & Alina (with Alina Hardin, on Names/Family (3)), mixed by Tom Menig and Jason Powers; and the Tatted Lace single (Names).

Alela Diane & Wild Divine (2011, Rough Trade)

Her third studio album, the first on Rough Trade Records. Produced by Scott Litt, who also engineered. Additional engineers: Dana Nielsen, Jesse Lauter, Michael Logue. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Art direction and design by Diane Chonette; portrait photography by Chloe Aftel; landscape photography by Alela Diane herself. The record represented a fuller band sound and her highest-profile label placement to that point. Also documented: Home Recordings & B-Sides From The Wild Divine Sessions, featuring contributions from Eric Earley.

About Farewell (2013)

Released on Believe Recordings and her own Rusted Blue Records, with the promo pressing documented from France. Personnel not detailed in Discogs credits for the primary pressing (the entry is a CDr promo). Ten-track album in a folk-rock vein. Co-credited to Believe Recordings and Rusted Blue in the label field; Josh Powell (2), John Askew (2), and Kelly Christ appear as engineers/contributors across the Discogs master. Ben Landsverk and Galen Cohen also credited. Holcombe Waller appears as a collaborator. Mastered by Roger Seibel.

Cold Moon (2015, with Ryan Francesconi)

A collaborative album credited jointly to Alela Diane & Ryan Francesconi (guitarist known for his work with Joanna Newsom). Alela Diane on vocals and songwriting; Francesconi on acoustic guitar, bass, and co-writing. The record is the first in a long partnership — Francesconi reappears on Live At The Map Room and Looking Glass. Released on Believe Recordings. Mirabai Peart appears as a string player here and continues through subsequent records.

Cusp (2018, AllPoints)

Fifth studio album. Produced by Peter M. Murray. Engineered by John Askew (2), with additional engineering from Jake Aron, Josh Powell (2), and Peter M. Murray. Mixed by Noah Georgeson. Mastered by JJ Golden. Design by Diane Chonette (recurring art collaborator). Released on AllPoints.

Live At The Map Room (2021, AllPoints)

Live album credited to Alela Diane with Heather Woods Broderick and Mirabai Peart. Recorded by Josh Powell (2); mixed and mastered by Daniel J. Goodwin. String arrangements by Ryan Francesconi. Heather Woods Broderick contributes backing vocals, piano, cello, acoustic guitar, flute, and percussion. Includes a cover of an Ola Belle Reed song. Released on AllPoints.

Looking Glass (2022, Believe / Naïve)

Sixth studio album, produced by Tucker Martine (who also engineered, mixed, played drums and percussion). Mastered by JJ Golden. Key contributors: Heather Woods Broderick (arrangements, vocals, multiple instruments), Ryan Francesconi (arrangements, guitar), Mirabai Peart (viola, violin), Peter Broderick (violin), Carl Broemel (harmonica, guitar, pedal steel), Scott Avett (vocals on one track), Anna Fritz (cello), JT Bates (drums). Released on Believe / Naïve.

It’s Always Christmas Somewhere (2023, Rusted Blue Records)

Holiday record on her own Rusted Blue Records imprint. Performed and produced by Alela Diane. Features The Hackles and Nevada Sowle.
