Cynthia Connolly

Born 1964. American photographer, curator, graphic designer, and visual artist based in Washington DC. Discogs ID: 1228553. Alias on Discogs: Mortica. Name variations on record: Cynthia, Cynthia Connelly, Cynthia Conolly.

The central visual chronicler of the DC hardcore scene from 1981 onward. Her third-ranked position among all Dischord design/photography credits (142 releases) puts her just behind Glen E. Friedman (165) and Jason Farrell (166) — but her work predates both in the hardcore era and is unique in spanning raw live documentation, formal cover art, book-making, and archival preservation. Co-created Banned in DC: Photos and Flyers from the DC Punk Underground 1979–85 with Leslie Clague and Sharon Cheslow, the foundational photographic record of the first wave of DC punk.

Biography

Early work (1981–1983)

Connolly’s first Discogs credit is the insert lettering on the 1981 Minor Threat self-titled EP (Dischord). The following year she contributed photography to Flex Your Head (1982), the pivotal Dischord compilation that documented the DC and East Coast hardcore underground — her photos covered the Red C insert page. These early credits place her inside Dischord’s production circle from the label’s founding generation, alongside Jeff Nelson (graphics) and Don Zientara (engineering).

Her most prominent early work is the cover art for Minor Threat’s Out Of Step (Dischord, 1983, release ID 370582) — the sheep-and-flock image that became one of hardcore’s most recognisable sleeves. The cover is credited “Artwork [Cover Art]” to Connolly; Jeff Nelson handled graphics; Don Zientara engineered. The image (sheep breaking from the flock) directly illustrates the album’s title and became an enduring symbol of straight-edge non-conformity.

Mid-period: post-hardcore and Dischord’s second generation (1985–1993)

Through the mid-to-late 1980s Connolly remained embedded in the Dischord world as it expanded beyond hardcore into post-hardcore:

  • Rites Of Spring (1985, release ID 437153): inner sleeve photos alongside Bert Queiroz and Cristina Martinez; Eddie Janney’s woodcut artwork; Don Zientara engineering; Ian MacKaye and Mike Hampton producing.
  • Salad Days EP (Minor Threat, 1985, release ID 408684): insert photography alongside Glen E. Friedman (front cover) and Jim Saah.
  • Embrace (1987, release ID 471006): “Artwork [Screens By]” — a silkscreen/visual production role distinct from straight photography, showing the breadth of her design involvement.
  • Wig Out at Denkos (Beefeater, 1987, release ID 470990): graphics credit.
  • Less Deep Inside Keeps (1987, release ID 1126451): design credit.
  • Fire Party (1988, release ID 1285793): back cover photography and “Technical Art Shit” — the band’s self-deprecating credit for mechanical artwork/paste-up production; Don Zientara and Ian MacKaye co-produced.
  • Minor Threat Complete Discography (1989, release ID 3302772): artwork, alongside Jeff Nelson and Glen E. Friedman.
  • Dark Days Coming (1989, release ID 1024743): front cover photograph.
  • Fugazi In On the Kill Taker (1993, release ID 373060): “Photography By [Carry Out Photo]” — documentary photo of a takeaway/street scene used in the booklet; Jem Cohen handled graphic concept and assemblage.

Later Dischord work and beyond (1997–2002)

  • Branch Manager Anything Tribal (Dischord, 1997, release ID 1629119): front and back cover, and additional photos. Layout/artwork by Kristin Thomson and Dave Allen.
  • Lungfish Artificial Horizon (Dischord, 1998, release ID 1065843): band photography alongside Pat Graham.
  • 20 Years of Dischord (1980–2000) retrospective box set (2002, release ID 446604): photography and fact-checking/proofreading — a dual role that signals her function as an institutional archivist as much as a photographer.
  • The Capitol City Dusters Rock Creek (Dischord, 2002, release ID 1689274): Photography — one of three photographers credited (with Pat Graham and Artur Estrada).

Outside Dischord

Her credits extend beyond Dischord:

  • Double-O (1983, Dischord / R&B Records (2)): graphics.
  • High Priest of Harmful Matter (Jello Biafra, Alternative Tentacles, 1989): insert contributions.
  • Yoyo A Go Go compilation (Yoyo Recordings, 1999): photography.
  • A Future Lived in Past Tense (DeSoto Records, 2001): photography.
  • R.E.M. Up (Warner Bros., Europe pressing, 1998): photography credit.
  • A Little Give and Take (Civilian Art Projects, 2009): banjo — a rare performance credit rather than visual.
  • Blue Period (Ernest Jenning Record Co., 2023): photography — active into her sixties.

Banned in DC

Co-created with Leslie Clague (Discogs ID 2465296) and Sharon Cheslow (Discogs ID 533431). Full title: Banned in DC: Photos and Flyers from the DC Punk Underground 1979–85. The book collects photographs and xeroxed flyers from the first wave of DC punk and hardcore. It functions as the primary visual primary source for the scene and has been cited extensively in later DC punk histories.

Leslie Clague’s Discogs credits include: photography on the Minor Threat Subject To Change insert (1983), Out of Step (1986 pressing), Take It Back (Dag Nasty, 1986), Embrace (1987), End On End (Rites of Spring reissue, 1991), and 20 Years of Dischord (2002) — a parallel Dischord trajectory to Connolly’s.

Sharon Cheslow (Discogs ID 533431) was guitarist/vocalist with Chalk Circle and has Discogs credits under her name from 1984–2024 (labels include Decomposition, Collective Jyrk, Curor Recordings). Her credits include guitar/vocals on the 1984 EPU/WGNS compilation Roadmap to Revolution and photography on Fabuley (Dischord, 1991).
