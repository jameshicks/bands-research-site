Linda Reid

Born: 1971, Scotland. Singer, songwriter, producer, visual artist. Younger sister of Jim Reid and William Reid of The Jesus And Mary Chain.

Discogs IDs:

  • Linda Reid: 769909
  • Sister Vanilla: 105125

Overview

Linda Reid (b. 1971) is the younger sister of Jim Reid and William Reid, the founders of The Jesus And Mary Chain. She operates across three Discogs identities: Linda Reid (her birth name, used for JAMC-adjacent artwork and vocal credits), Sister Vanilla (her primary solo recording alias), and Linda Fox (4) (her married name, used for guest appearances).

Her connection to JAMC runs deep but starts in a supporting role. In 1987, aged around 16, she contributed graphic concept artwork to two early JAMC singles on Blanco Y Negro: “April Skies” and the “Happy When It Rains” EP, both with layout credited to Helen Backhouse. This places her in the JAMC visual world during the band’s peak post-Psychocandy period.

Her solo recording career as Sister Vanilla emerged in 2005. The debut single “Song For A Secret / Can’t Stop The Rock” was a joint release with Jim Reid on Transistor (4) (cat. TR6 45004), with “Can’t Stop The Rock” (the Sister Vanilla B-side) co-written by Linda and William Reid. The debut album Little Pop Rock followed the same year, released in Japan on P-Vine Records (PCD-23573). The album was co-produced by all three Reids together with Ben Lurie, JAMC’s longtime guitarist, and all songwriting was shared among the same four. This made Little Pop Rock effectively a family-plus-Lurie project in everything but name.

A second single, “Can’t Stop The Rock / What Goes Around” (2007), appeared on Chemikal Underground (CHEM091) — Glasgow’s flagship post-rock indie, home of Arab Strap, Mogwai, and others. The release’s A-side (co-written by Linda and William) was produced by William Reid and Linda; the AA-side (co-written by Linda and Jim) was produced by Jim Reid and Ben Lurie. No second Sister Vanilla album followed on Discogs.

A UK promo CDr, Three Track Soup Promo (2005), appeared on Soup Records (7) with three tracks from the album era: “Totp”, “Pastel Blue”, and “Down”.

Back in the JAMC fold: a 2008 US compilation The Power Of Negative Thinking: B-Sides & Rarities on Blanco Y Negro / Rhino Records credits Linda Reid on vocals for tracks 4-15 to 4-19 — late-period JAMC material alongside Hope Sandoval and Shane MacGowan.

On JAMC’s reunion album Damage and Joy (2017, Artificial Plastic Records), Linda appears under the name Linda Fox (4), providing guest vocals on tracks 7 and 14. She is one of several female guest vocalists on the record, alongside Isobel Campbell (tracks 5–6), Sky Ferreira (track 13), and Bernadette Denning (track 4). The album was primarily produced by Youth.

Scene Connections

  • Scottish indie / JAMC family: All Sister Vanilla recordings are a direct extension of the JAMC creative circle — same writers, same producer (Ben Lurie shares all credits), same family.
  • Chemikal Underground: The 2007 single places Sister Vanilla within the Glasgow post-indie axis alongside Arab Strap and Mogwai.
  • Blanco Y Negro / JAMC era: Her earliest credits (1987 artwork) connect her to the mid-80s UK noise-pop moment centered on Creation Records-adjacent labels.
  • Bobby Gillespie — early JAMC drummer, later Primal Scream frontman — appears in the shared personnel on The Power Of Negative Thinking but not in direct collaboration with Linda’s credited work.

Open Questions

  • No second Sister Vanilla album is on Discogs — was Little Pop Rock the only full album, or were there later releases not yet catalogued?
  • Tracks 7 and 14 on Damage and Joy are “Los Feliz (Blues And Greens)” and “Can’t Stop The Rock” respectively — notably the latter is a reprise of the Sister Vanilla single from 2005, now appearing on a JAMC album.
  • The Little Pop Rock P-Vine release was Japan-only; was a UK/European pressing ever released or planned?
