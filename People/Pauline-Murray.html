Pauline Murray

Vocalist, songwriter. Born 8 March 1958, Durham, County Durham, UK. Lead singer and co-founder of Penetration, the punk/post-punk band from Ferryhill, County Durham (formed 1976, active 1977–1979, reformed 2001). Following Penetration’s split she launched a solo career in 1980 under the name Pauline Murray and the Invisible Girls, working with producer Martin Hannett and The Invisible Girls. Returned to Penetration for the 2000s reformation. Released solo LP Elemental (2020) on her own Polestar label. Discogs artist ID: 277654.

Overview

Penetration (1976–1979)

Murray formed Penetration in Ferryhill with guitarist Neale Floyd and bassist Robert Blamire. The band signed to Virgin Records and released two studio albums — Moving Targets (1978) and Coming Up For Air (1979) — as well as a run of singles beginning with “Don’t Dictate” (1977). Moving Targets was produced by Mike Howlett and Mick Glossop. Coming Up For Air was produced by Steve Lillywhite, by which point Fred Purser had joined on lead guitar. Penetration disbanded in 1979, having recorded a live album (Race Against Time, 1979, on the Clifdayn label — a roadside bootleg-style release).

Pauline Murray and the Invisible Girls (1980–1982)

After the split Murray relocated to Manchester and began work with Martin Hannett and The Invisible Girls — the studio collective Hannett co-ran with keyboardist/programmer Steve Hopkins. The debut album Pauline Murray and the Invisible Girls (Illusive, 1980) was produced and arranged by The Invisible Girls (Hannett and Hopkins), with Robert Blamire, John Maher (drums) and Vini Reilly guesting. Cover design by Peter Saville (2) and Trevor Key. The follow-up single “Searching for Heaven” / “Dream Sequences” (Illusive, 1981) continued the collaboration. “Searching for Heaven” featured Wayne Hussey on guitar — one of his earliest significant session credits before The Mission / Sisters of Mercy.

Murray also sang backing vocals on The Only Ones’ “Baby’s Got a Gun” (CBS, 1980) and contributed to the CBS single “Fools” (1980).

1980s and Polestar

In the mid-1980s Murray worked under the Pauline Murray and the Storm and Pauline Murray and the Saint monikers (both on Polestar Records), releasing “New Age” (1986) among others. She also performed on a track for the film The Boy’s Heart (London Records, 1992).

Penetration Reformed (2001–present)

Murray reformed Penetration around 2001. The reconstituted lineup released singles on Damaged Goods (“Our World”, 2008; “The Feeling”, 2010) and then two albums on Polestar: Resolution (2015) and two further singles In the Future / Shake Some Action (both 2017).

Elemental (2020)

Murray’s second proper solo album, Elemental (Polestar, 2020), was co-produced and mixed by Robert Blamire and his son Alex Blamire, with mastering by Tim Turan. Artwork included Murray’s own paintings.
