Oct 26, 202610 min read

Rashied Ali

Discogs ID: 254546
Born: Robert Patterson Jr., July 1, 1935, Philadelphia, PA
Died: August 12, 2009, New York, NY
Name variations: Ali, R. Ali, R/A, Rashed Ali, Rasheed Ali, Rashid Ali

American free jazz drummer. One of the key figures in post-Coltrane avant-garde music: Ali co-created the two-drummer polyrhythmic approach with John Coltrane during Coltrane’s final period (1965–67), appeared on some of Coltrane’s most radical recordings, and spent the subsequent decades pursuing his own vision on the label he founded, Survival Records, and through collaborations across the downtown NYC free music scene. He ran the venue Ali’s Alley in SoHo through the 1970s and revived his public profile in the 1990s as leader of Prima Materia, a Knitting Factory ensemble focused on Coltrane and Ayler tributes.

Biography

Early Career and the Coltrane Years (1965–1967)

Ali arrived in New York in the early 1960s after studying in Philadelphia. His reputation grew through the mid-1960s avant-garde circuit; he appears as early as the 1966 Marion Brown Quartet session for ESP-Disk’ (Marion Brown Quartet, with Bennie Maupin, Grachan Moncur III, and Beaver Harris).

His most consequential entry was into John Coltrane’s working band in 1965. Coltrane added Ali to the group alongside existing drummer Elvin Jones, creating the famous double-drum experiments heard on Meditations (1966, Impulse!). The lineup for that session was Coltrane, McCoy Tyner (piano), Jimmy Garrison (bass), Jones and Ali (drums), and Pharoah Sanders (tenor sax). The double-drum configuration dissolved when Jones and Tyner departed; Ali became the sole drummer for Coltrane’s final working band.

Interstellar Space is the apex of this period: a pure duo recording of Coltrane and Ali, with no bassist or pianist, where Ali’s polyrhythmic approach is fully exposed. Stellar Regions (posthumously released 1995) documents the same studio session at which Ali also recorded. Both were engineered by Rudy Van Gelder.

After Coltrane’s death in July 1967, Ali continued working in free music. He appears on the Live At Slugs 1967 session (issued as First Time Out: Live At Slugs 1967 on Survival Records in 2020), one of the only live documents of the final Coltrane quintet.

Post-Coltrane Collaborations (1968–1972)

In the years immediately after Coltrane’s death Ali worked across the New York avant-garde, including sessions with Alice Coltrane (Journey In Satchidananda, 1971, with Pharoah Sanders and Cecil McBee; Universal Consciousness, 1971; Huntington Ashram Monastery, 1969; Infinity, 1972) and with the Burton Greene Quintet, the Frank Wright Quartet, and the Gary Bartz NTU Troop.

He also formed the Rashied Ali Quartet and Rashied Ali Quintet during this period, laying the groundwork for his own label activity.

Survival Records and Ali’s Alley (1972–1979)

Ali founded Survival Records (Discogs: Survival Records (2), label ID 45889) in 1972, operating out of 77 Greene Street, New York, NY 10012 — the same address as his venue Ali’s Alley (also called Studio 77, NYC). The label was entirely self-operated; Ali controlled recording, mixing, design, and production. He ran it until his death in 2009; the label was revived by his family in 2019 for reissues.

Duo Exchange (1973) is the earliest and most celebrated of the label’s original releases: a fully improvised tenor-drums duet with Frank Lowe, engineered by Marzette Watts, with photography by Valerie Wilmer. It established the sonic template Ali would return to across his career.

Swift Are The Winds Of Life (1976) pairs Ali with violinist Leroy Jenkins in another duo format; liner notes by Stanley Crouch. Ali self-engineered and mixed all the 1976 sessions.

Notable early Quintet sideman: James Blood Ulmer appears on Rashied Ali Quintet (1973) — one of Ulmer’s early Discogs credits.

FMP and European Connections (1993–1994)

In the early 1990s Ali recorded two important albums for the German label FMP (Free Music Production), produced by Jost Gebers:

  • Touchin’ On Trane (1993) — trio with Charles Gayle (tenor sax) and William Parker (bass). Compositions credited to all three. Recorded and produced by Jost Gebers; engineering by Holger Scheuermann.
  • Songlines (1994) — trio with Peter Brötzmann (alto, tenor, tárogató) and Fred Hopkins (bass). Produced by Jost Gebers.

Both are primary-credit releases for Ali and document his standing in the European free improvisation world.

Phalanx (4) and the Downtown Scene (1987–1991)

Ali was a member of Phalanx (4), a quartet with James Blood Ulmer (guitar), George Adams (tenor sax, flute), and Sirone (bass). Their key releases were on DIW (Japan):

  • Original Phalanx (1987, DIW) — recorded in Japan, produced by Kazunori Sugiyama
  • In Touch (1988, DIW)

He also appears on Rudolph Grey’s Mask Of Light (1991, Ecstatic Peace! / New Alliance Records), alongside Thurston Moore and Don Fleming as producers, Alan Licht (guitar), and Jim Sauter (sax). Separately, he worked with the Purple Trap ensemble.

The Blackbird album (1991, Alfa Jazz / Timeless Records) is an unusual credit: a duo recording with Jaco Pastorius, attributed as primary co-credit to both.

Prima Materia and the Knitting Factory (1993–2001)

In the early 1990s Ali assembled Prima Materia, a large rotating ensemble centered on his drumming and saxophonist Louis Belogenis, who produced the group’s key recordings. The group operated through the Knitting Factory ecosystem and focused on extended collective improvisation in tribute to Coltrane and Albert Ayler.

The duo with Louis Belogenis (Rings of Saturn) is his most sustained post-1990s statement as a co-equal leader. It was followed by the trio Live at Tonic with bassist Wilber Morris.

Later Work (2001–2009)

Ali continued leading Quintet sessions for Survival Records through the 2000s (Judgment Day Vol. 1 and 2, 2006). A late trio session, Duo + One (2007/2008), features Ali with saxophonist Hayes Greenfield and bassist Don Pate.

He appears in the Bill Laswell-related The Stone (2023, posthumous), and is credited posthumously on various Coltrane reissue compilations. His family revived Survival Records in 2019 specifically to issue archive material.
