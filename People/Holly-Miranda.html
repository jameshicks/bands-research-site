Holly Miranda

American singer-songwriter and multi-instrumentalist, born Detroit, Michigan, 1982. Discogs artist ID: 1054293. Brooklyn-based; known for guitar, keys, trombone, and vocals.

Name variations on record: Holly Miranda Grice; Miranda.

Biography

The Jealous Girlfriends (2004–2008)

Holly Miranda came up in Brooklyn as a founding member of The Jealous Girlfriends (Discogs ID: 930000), an indie collective also including Josh Abbott (guitar, vocals, drums), Alex Lipsen (keys, synth, bass modules), and Mike Fadem (drums). Miranda played guitar, keys, trombone, and vocals in the band.

The group released two records:

  • Comfortably, Uncomfortable (2004, Sweet Spot) — self-released CD; 8 tracks
  • The Jealous Girlfriends (2008, Good Fences) — LP; released in Canada; cat. no. Q200811

The 2008 LP appeared on Good Fences, a Canadian independent label, alongside licensing to Last Gang Records and These Are Not Records. The band was active 2004–2008 per Discogs, disbanding before Miranda moved into solo work.

Solo debut and XL Recordings (2009–2010)

Miranda signed to XL Recordings for her solo debut, issuing the 7” single “Forest Green Oh Forest Green” / “Nobody Sees Me Like You Do” in 2009 (XLS467) as a preview of the full record.

Her debut album, The Magician’s Private Library (2010, XL Recordings, XLLP472), was produced by Dave Sitek (co-production by Sugar Rose), with all songs written by Miranda except “Forest Green Oh Forest Green” (co-written with Brendan Coon). The double LP was released simultaneously in the UK and US and is catalogued as Alternative Rock / Rock. Tracklist includes “Joints,” “Waves,” “Slow Burn Treason,” “Canvas,” and “Sleep On Fire.”

Also in 2010, Miranda appeared as a guest vocalist on Dave Sitek’s solo project Maximum Balloon, singing and co-writing track 8, “The Lesson”, on the LP Maximum Balloon (DGC, 2010). The album placed a different vocalist on each track; other contributors included Tunde Adebimpe, Karen O, David Byrne, Kyp Malone, and Little Dragon.

That same year, a separate single “Waves” was issued (XL Recordings, US, 2010).

Transition period and Federal Prism (2012–2014)

Following the XL debut, Miranda released a collaboration with electronic duo Creep (7) — the digital single “Animals” (2012, Creep INTL) — straying into synth-pop territory. A digital two-track single, “Everlasting” / “Desert Call” (2013, Federal Prism), served as a preview of her second album.

Self-titled album and Dangerbird Records (2015)

Miranda’s second album, Holly Miranda (2015, Dangerbird Records, DGB117-1), marked a shift from the first record’s single-producer model. Sitek produced one track (“Come On,” side A5); the remaining ten tracks were co-produced by Miranda and Florent Barbier, who also engineered and played bass, drums, and guitar throughout. Mixed by Miranda and Matty Green (2).

Beyond The Wizards Sleeve collaboration (2016)

Miranda contributed vocals and co-wrote material for UK psychedelic/electronic duo Beyond The Wizards Sleeve (Erol Alkan and Richard Norris), appearing on the 12” single “Black Crow” / “White Crow” (2016, Phantasy Sound, PH57). The A-side “White Crow” was remixed by Erol Alkan; co-writers on the record include Angelo Badalamenti alongside Miranda, Alkan, Norris, and William Cameron.

Mutual Horse and Dangerbird (2018)

Miranda’s third album, Mutual Horse (2018, Dangerbird Records, DGB149), was released on CD. The Discogs entry carries no additional personnel credits beyond Miranda as primary artist; the record is categorized as Indie Pop / Indie Rock. 14 tracks including “Golden Spiral,” “On The Radio,” “All Of The Way,” “Exquisite,” and “Mt. Hood.”

Later work (2022–present)

Miranda appears in the credits of two Amanda Palmer self-releases: “Another Christmas” (2022) and “Live To Tell” (2023, a Madonna cover), both released on Palmer’s own imprint. Miranda’s credits on these include guitar, synth, backing vocals, engineer, producer, and mixed by — suggesting a producing/engineering role in the sessions.
