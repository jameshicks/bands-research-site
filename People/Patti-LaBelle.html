Patti LaBelle

Patti LaBelle

Oct 08, 20265 min read

Patti LaBelle

Real name: Patricia Louise Holte
Discogs ID: 3474
Born: May 24, 1944, Philadelphia, PA
Groups: Patti LaBelle And The Bluebells, LaBelle
Labels: Newtown, Parkway, Atlantic → Epic (solo) → Philadelphia International → MCA Records

One of the defining voices of American R&B, soul, and gospel-inflected pop. Active continuously since 1962. Career spans four distinct phases: the Bluebelles vocal group (1962–70), the art-rock reinvention as LaBelle (1971–76), a Philadelphia-International solo peak (1977–84), and a mainstream crossover breakthrough (1984–86).

Phase 1 — Patti LaBelle And The Bluebelles (1962–1970)

Formed in Philadelphia c.1961. Members at various points: Patti LaBelle, Nona Hendryx, Sarah Dash, Cindy Birdsong (who left c.1967 to join The Supremes). Discogs group ID: 403181.

Label timeline:

  • Newtown Records (1962–63): debut singles including “I Sold My Heart To The Junkman” and “Down The Aisle (Wedding Song)”; LPs Sleigh Bells, Jingle Bells and Bluebelles and Sweethearts Of The Apollo (both 1963)
  • Parkway Records (1963–64): “You’ll Never Walk Alone,” “Danny Boy,” live LP Patti Labelle And Her Bluebells On Stage (1964)
  • Atlantic Records (1965–70): singles including “All Or Nothing,” “Over The Rainbow,” “Take Me For A Little While,” “He’s My Man” (1968)

Phase 2 — LaBelle (1971–1976)

Renamed and reinvented under the management of Vicki Wickham. Moved into glam/funk/rock territory. Discogs group ID: 21267.

Laura Nyro And LaBelle — Gonna Take A Miracle (1971, Columbia KC 30987):
Produced by Gamble & Huff (Kenneth Gamble, Leon Huff). Featuring: Ron Baker (bass), Norman Harris (guitar), Roland Chambers (guitar), Vincent Montana Jr. (percussion); string/horn arrangements by Bobby Martin, Lenny Pakula, Thom Bell. Recorded at Sigma Sound, Philadelphia. Vocals (Patti LaBelle, Nona Hendryx, Sarah Dash, Laura Nyro) uncredited on the sleeve. Co-artist: Laura Nyro.

LaBelle — Nightbirds (1974, Epic EPC 33075; master 154212):
Executive producer: Vicki Wickham. Producer/arranger: Allen Toussaint. Recorded in New Orleans with The Meters (Art Neville — organ, George Porter Jr. — bass, Leo Nocentelli — guitar). Full brass/reed section: Earl Turbinton (soprano/alto sax), Alvin Thomas and Lon Price (tenor sax/flute), Clarence Ford (alto sax), Carl Blouin (baritone sax), Lester Caliste (trombone), Clyde Kerr Jr. and Steve Howard (trumpets). Nona Hendryx wrote five of ten tracks. “Lady Marmalade” (written by Bob Crewe & Kenny Nolan) reached #1.

Nightbirds tracklist:

  • A1 “Lady Marmalade” — Crewe/Nolan | A2 “Somebody Somewhere” — Hendryx | A3 “Are You Lonely?” — Hendryx
  • A4 “It Took A Long Time” — Crewe/Brown/Bloodworth | A5 “Don’t Bring Me Down” — Toussaint
  • B1 “What Can I Do For You?” — Batts/Ellison | B2 “Nightbird” — Hendryx | B3 “Space Children” — Hendryx
  • B4 “All Girl Band” — Toussaint | B5 “You Turn Me On” — Hendryx

LaBelle disbanded 1976.

Phase 3 — Epic / Philadelphia International Solo (1977–1984)

Patti LaBelle (1977, Epic PE 34847): solo debut, produced by David Rubinson. Includes “You Are My Friend,” “Since I Don’t Have You,” and Bob Dylan’s “Most Likely You Go Your Way.”

I’m In Love Again (1983, Philadelphia International FZ 38539). Recorded at Sigma Sound. Producers include Bunny Sigler & Kenneth Gamble, Dexter Wansel & Kenneth Gamble, Leon Huff, Cecil Womack. Key track: “If Only You Knew” (Wansel/Gamble/Biggs) reached #1 R&B. Engineers: Joe Tarsia, Arthur Stoppe, Jim Gallagher, Dirk Devlin.

Guest: Nona Hendryx — Nona (1983, RCA PL 14565). Produced by Bill Laswell / Material. Patti LaBelle — backing vocals on A4 “Design For Living” only (alongside Tina Weymouth, Gina Schock, Nancy Wilson/Heart, Valerie Simpson, Laurie Anderson). Other players on the LP: Nile Rodgers, Bernie Worrell, Sly Dunbar, Jamaaladeen Tacuma.

Phase 4 — Crossover Breakthrough (1984–1986)

“New Attitude” (1984, Beverly Hills Cop soundtrack, MCA) — launched mainstream pop breakthrough.

Winner In You (1986, MCA MCA-5737). Patti LaBelle as executive producer; production supervised by James Budd Ellison:
