David McDonnell

Discogs ID: 543107 Aliases: The Diminisher (ID: 1121116), Da Minister, Thousand Rabbits Running (ID: 902380) Name variations: D. McDonnell, D. Mcdonnell, Dave “The Diminisher” McDonnell, Dave McDonnell

American multi-instrumentalist and composer, based in the US. His primary instruments span wind (soprano, alto, tenor, baritone, C-melody saxophones; clarinet, bass clarinet, contra-alto clarinet; oboe; trombone), keyboards (Fender Rhodes, organ, synthesizer, electric piano), and percussion, with additional credited roles in electronics and voice. He is a co-founder of the avant-fusion trio Bablicon (1999–2001) alongside Griffin Rodriguez and Jeremy Barnes, and a core member of the Chicago experimental ensemble Icy Demons (2004–present). On Icy Demons records he is credited under the aliases “The Diminisher” and “Thousand Rabbits Running.” He has also appeared as a session player and guest on releases spanning jazz, indie rock, and avant-garde contexts, and co-founded the group Michael Columbia with Dylan Ryan and Chris Kalis. Wikipedia’s article on Icy Demons and Dylan Ryan’s entry confirm McDonnell’s multi-project role in the Chicago experimental underground.

Notes on selected guest appearances:

  • Gravity Won’t Get You High (The Grates, 2006, Dew Process): McDonnell arranged the horns and played saxophone; produced by Brian Deck. He also played alongside Nick Broste (trombone) and Patrick Newbery (trumpet) — a horn section configuration that recurs in the Chicago experimental scene.
  • The Simp (Baby Teeth, 2007, Lujo Records): credited by his full alias “Dave ‘The Diminisher’ McDonnell,” playing baritone, tenor, and alto sax alongside Tomeka Reid (cello), Nick Broste (trombone), and Patrick Newbery (trumpet). Blue Hawaii (Griffin Rodriguez) also appears on drum programming.
  • Uchū (Herculaneum, 2012): composed tracks 1 and 6; played alto saxophone. Herculaneum was led by Dylan Ryan and included Nick Broste, Nate Lepine, Patrick Newbery — an overlapping Chicago improvised music circle.
  • Moh Lhean (Why?, 2017, Joyful Noise Recordings): contra-alto clarinet and saxophone on this album by Yoni Wolf’s project.
