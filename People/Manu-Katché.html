Manu Katché

Manu Katché

Aug 21, 20268 min read

Manu Katché

Discogs ID: 253252
Real name: Emmanuel Katché
Born: 27 October 1958, Saint-Maur-des-Fossés, France (Ivorian origin)
Role: Drummer, songwriter, composer, record producer

French drummer of Ivorian origin; one of the most in-demand session and touring drummers in European pop and jazz from the early 1980s onward. First prominent in the Parisian session scene, then elevated to global visibility through a decade-long association with Peter Gabriel and parallel work with Sting. From 2005 onward recording leader on ECM Records, primarily with the Marcin Wasilewski Trio and collaborators from the Oslo/ECM orbit.

Biography

Early Career and Paris Session Scene (1977–1985)

Katché’s first documented appearances are on late-1970s French prog-adjacent records: Yochk’o Seffer’s Ima (1977, Moshé-Naïm) and Seffer/ZAO (3)‘s Typhareth (1977, RCA Victor), placing him immediately in the European jazz-rock scene. He appears on Jean-Philippe Goude’s Drones (1980, Polydor/Ramses) — an ambitious large-ensemble concept record from the Ramses studio orbit — drumming on track B1 “Trépidanse.”

Through the early 1980s he built a dense session résumé in French pop: albums for Sarbacane (Francis Cabrel), Vanessa Paradis-adjacent sessions, Julien Clerc, Michel Jonasz (he was a member of the Michel Jonasz Quartet), and dozens of French chart artists. He also appeared on the 1980: No Sex project (1979, Polydor) and was a member of avant-prog groups ZAO (3) and Preface, alongside occasional work with Yochk’o Seffer Neffesh Music.

The Peter Gabriel and Daniel Lanois Years (1986–1994)

The pivotal breakthrough came with Peter Gabriel’s So (1986, Geffen), one of the decade’s definitive pop records. Katché drummed on tracks A2–B1 and added percussion on A3–B1. The session was co-produced by Daniel Lanois, who also drew on Katché for Robbie Robertson’s self-titled 1987 album on Geffen (drums on A1, A2, B2–B4, produced by Lanois). Gabriel’s Us (1992) and Up (2002) followed.

He toured extensively with Gabriel through the So and Secret World cycles, with live documents including Secret World Live (1994, Geffen/Real World) and the video PoV (1990). He appears on Gabriel’s Passion (1989) as a performer, and on the Shaking The Tree compilation (1990). The Gabriel connection brought him to Joni Mitchell’s Chalk Mark In A Rain Storm (1988, Geffen) — where he plays drums, percussion, snare, and talking drum — and to a one-off credit on Simple Minds’ Street Fighting Years (1989, Virgin).

The Sting Sessions (1987–2003)

Katché appears on three major Sting studio albums:

  • …Nothing Like The Sun (1987, A&M) — drums alongside Andy Newmark; produced by Neil Dorfsman
  • The Soul Cages (1991, A&M) — drums; produced by Hugh Padgham, with Kenny Kirkland, Dominic Miller, Branford Marsalis, David Sancious
  • Sacred Love (2003, A&M) — drums

He also appeared on the Dire Straits Sultans Of Swing compilation (1998) in a “Musician” credit, and on Sting’s Brand New Day (1999, A&M) and live recordings …All This Time (2001). Dominic Miller — Sting’s guitarist — is a recurring co-credit across this period.

The Jan Garbarek/ECM Sessions (1990–1993)

Katché was drawn into the ECM orbit through Jan Garbarek’s group. He drums on:

  • I Took Up The Runes (1990, ECM) — with Eberhard Weber (bass), Rainer Brüninghaus (piano), Naná Vasconcelos (percussion), Bugge Wesseltoft (synthesizer); produced by Manfred Eicher at Rainbow Studio with Jan Erik Kongshaug
  • Twelve Moons (1993, ECM) — Jan Garbarek Group; with Eberhard Weber, Rainer Brüninghaus, Marilyn Mazur, Agnes Buen Garnås, Mari Boine

He was a member of the Jan Garbarek Group during this period.

Solo Recordings and It’s About Time (1991)

Katché’s first solo album, It’s About Time (1991, Ariola/BMG), has him as primary artist, drummer, and co-producer. Collaborators include Peter Gabriel, Sting, Branford Marsalis, Pino Palladino, David Sancious, Daniel Lanois, John Paul Jones, Richard Galliano, and others — a who’s-who of his session world assembled under his own name for the first time.

The core rhythm partnership with Marcin Wasilewski and Sławomir Kurkiewicz (the Wasilewski Trio) runs through Neighbourhood and Playground. Touchstone For Manu (2014, ECM) is a limited-edition compilation of this period.

Later Work (2014–present)

  • Live In Concert (2014, ACT) — live album; quartet format with Tore Brunborg, Jim Watson, and bassist Reno Di Matteo / Luca Aquino
  • Unstatic (2016, Anteprima) — solo album with vocals; written and produced by Katché; features Nils Landgren, Ellen Andrea Wang
  • The Scope (2019, Anteprima/Bendo music) — further solo work
  • Continued Peter Gabriel touring: Back To Front tour (2012–2014); appears on I/O (2023, Real World)
  • Absinthe (2019, ECM) — Jan Garbarek session

He also appeared on Joe Satriani’s self-titled 1995 album, Pat Metheny Group’s Calling (1996), Tori Amos’s Boys For Pele (1996, Atlantic), Richard Wright’s Broken China (1996), Loreena McKennitt’s The Book of Secrets (1997) and An Ancient Muse (2006), and the film score for Un Indien Dans La Ville (1994, Virgin) where he is listed as composer, performer, and producer.
