Tom Brechtlein

American jazz-fusion drummer. Born 10 May 1957. Discogs artist ID: 360622.

Active years (Discogs): 1978–2021.

Overview

Tom Brechtlein is a Los Angeles-based session and touring drummer who built his career inside the jazz-fusion and smooth-jazz ecosystem radiating from Chick Corea’s orbit. His earliest documented Discogs credit is on Chick Corea’s Secret Agent (Polydor, 1978), placing him in that band from the late 1970s. Through the 1980s he was a fixture on Corea sessions and related Stretch Records/GRP releases, and he logged a long concurrent stint with Al Di Meola (credited under Al Di Meola Project from 1987). From the early 1990s through the 2000s his most prominent association shifted to Robben Ford & The Blue Line, appearing on the band’s key GRP/Blue Thumb run (Robben Ford & The Blue Line, 1992; Mystic Mile, 1993; Handful Of Blues, 1995) and serving as co-producer on Mystic Mile. He also appears on the 2020 archival release Meets Dizzy Gillespie In Hollywood (recorded in Corea’s circle with Dizzy Gillespie, Bunny Brunel, Gayle Moran, and Allen Vizzutti) and on the Chick Corea Band live set Live Under The Sky… ‘80 (rec. 1980, rel. 2021). He has Yamaha artist endorsement pages and a personal site (tombrechtlein.com) and is listed on Drummerworld.

Discogs name variations on file: Tim Brechtlein, Tom Brechlein, Tom Brechtlien, Tom Breichtlein, Tom Bretchclein, Tommy Brechtlein, Tommy Brechtlien.

Group memberships (Discogs): Al Di Meola Project, Max Bennett & Freeway, Robben Ford & The Blue Line, Third Rail (8), Wayne Shorter Quartet.

Key Release Personnel Detail

Secret Agent — Chick Corea (Polydor, 1978)

Corea’s first Polydor release. Brechtlein on drums alongside Bunny Brunel (bass), Joe Farrell (reeds/flute), Airto Moreira (percussion), Gayle Moran (vocals/backing), Allen Vizzutti (trumpet), and Al Jarreau (vocals on B3). Establishes Brechtlein in the core Chick Corea band of the late 1970s.

Ivanhoe — Bunny Brunel (Baybridge Records, 1982)

Brechtlein plays A2, A3, A4, B2. Other drummers on the album: Tony Williams (A1, B4) and Jean-Paul Ceccarelli (B1, B3). Fellow sidemen on Brechtlein’s tracks include Chick Corea (keyboards, A2/A4), Herbie Hancock (piano, A4/B2), Kei Akagi (piano, A3), Allen Vizzutti (trumpet), Steve Kujala (saxophone), Don Alias (congas), and Laudir de Oliveira (percussion). Heavy-fusion date on a Japanese label; multiple international pressings.

Robben Ford & The Blue Line (GRP/Stretch, 1992)

The band’s debut album. Core trio: Brechtlein (drums), Roscoe Beck (bass), Robben Ford (guitar/vocals). Executive-produced by Chick Corea and Ron Moss.

Mystic Mile — Robben Ford & The Blue Line (GRP/Stretch, 1993)

Brechtlein co-produced alongside Ford and Beck — the only release in his credits where he takes a producer role. Same core trio.

Handful Of Blues — Robben Ford & The Blue Line (Blue Thumb/Stretch, 1995)

Produced by Danny Kortchmar. Brechtlein adds background vocals alongside drums. Evelyn Brechtlein credited as Stretch Productions coordinator — likely a family connection.

Meets Dizzy Gillespie In Hollywood (archival, rel. 2020)

Archival live recording from the Chick Corea circle. Primary artists: Chick Corea, Dizzy Gillespie, Allen Vizzutti, Steve Kujala, Bunny Brunel, Brechtlein, Laudir de Oliveira, Don Alias, Gayle Moran. Personnel lineup is identical to the late-1970s/early-1980s Corea touring band.

Live Under The Sky… ‘80 — Chick Corea Band feat. Sadao Watanabe (Equinox, 2021; rec. 1980)

Live Japanese festival recording. Brechtlein and Don Alias share drums across tracks; Bunny Brunel on bass, Sadao Watanabe on saxophone.

The Ultimate Adventure — Chick Corea (Concord/Stretch, 2006)

Multi-drummer lineup: Brechtlein alongside Vinnie Colaiuta and Steve Gadd. Frank Gambale on guitar.

Scene Connections

Brechtlein is a career member of the Los Angeles jazz-fusion session world that coalesced around Chick Corea’s Stretch Records roster through the late 1970s–1990s. Key labels in his discography: Polydor, GRP, Stretch Records, Blue Thumb Records, Concord Records. Repertoire Records re-issued two of his live performances in 2021, indicating archival interest in the Blue Line-era material.

His Discogs group memberships connect him to the broader post-fusion LA scene: Wayne Shorter Quartet and Al Di Meola Project place him in the top tier of that world. The Third Rail (8) and Max Bennett & Freeway connections are less-documented; the former has an Ignition - Live Across Europe release shared with George Whitty and Janek Gwizdala.

The I’m Your Man credit (Leonard Cohen, Columbia, 1988) is notable as an outlier — pop-rock session work outside his usual fusion lane.

The The Royal Tenenbaums soundtrack appearance (Hollywood Records, 2002) suggests session work overlapping with film scoring.
