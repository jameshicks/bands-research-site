Eric Clapton

British guitarist, singer, and songwriter. Discogs artist ID: 17827. Born 30 March 1945, Ripley, Surrey, England. Widely regarded as one of the most influential electric guitarists in rock history. Active from 1963 to the present day.

Overview

Clapton’s career divides into several distinct eras. The first (1963–1965) was a rapid progression through British R&B bands — the Roosters, Casey Jones & the Engineers, The Yardbirds — culminating in the Bluesbreakers year (1965–1966) with John Mayall, which established his reputation as Britain’s foremost blues guitarist. The second era (1966–1970) was dominated by Cream (2), the supergroup with Jack Bruce and Ginger Baker that pioneered extended electric blues and hard rock, followed by the brief supergroup Blind Faith (2) (1969) with Steve Winwood, Ginger Baker, and Rick Grech, and a period backing Delaney & Bonnie.

The third era (1970–1974) saw Clapton form Derek & The Dominos — the quartet with Bobby Whitlock, Carl Radle, and Jim Gordon — recording Layla and Other Assorted Love Songs (1970), his acknowledged masterpiece. Duane Allman guested on the sessions. A period of personal difficulty followed before his 1974 comeback with 461 Ocean Boulevard, produced by Tom Dowd, which marked the beginning of his mellow, song-oriented solo career.

The Tulsa band era (1974–1981) kept the same core group — Radle on bass, Jamie Oldaker on drums, Dick Sims on keyboards, George Terry on guitar — through several albums produced by Dowd (There’s One In Every Crowd, 461 Ocean Boulevard) and later Glyn Johns (Slowhand, Backless). No Reason To Cry (1976), produced by Rob Fraboni with Clapton and Radle, was recorded at Shangri-La in Malibu and featured an extraordinary guest cast drawn from The Band and beyond.

From 1982 onward (the Duck Records / Warner Bros. period), Clapton worked with a succession of producers: Tom Dowd again (Money And Cigarettes, 1983; Another Ticket, 1981), Phil Collins and Ted Templeman (Behind The Sun, 1985), then Collins alone (August, 1986) which brought in Nathan East, Greg Phillinganes, and Collins’s own rhythm section from Genesis-adjacent projects. Russ Titelman produced the more polished Journeyman (1989), From The Cradle (1994), and the MTV Unplugged set (1992) that became Clapton’s bestselling album. From the late 1990s, Simon Climie co-produced almost all Clapton’s studio work (Pilgrim, Riding With The King, Reptile, Me And Mr. Johnson, Back Home, Road To Escondido, Old Sock), with Doyle Bramhall II becoming Clapton’s primary guitarist and collaborator through the 2000s–2010s. I Still Do (2016) returned to Glyn Johns as producer.
