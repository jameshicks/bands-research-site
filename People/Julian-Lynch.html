Julian Lynch

Full name: Julian Anthony Lynch. Discogs ID: 1499009. US lo-fi / folk-electronic / bedroom pop musician, multi-instrumentalist (guitar, clarinet, bass clarinet, saxophone, synth, drums), and academic ethnomusicologist. Based in Madison, WI (as of profile). Active from at least 2008.

Bandcamp: http://julianlynch.bandcamp.com/

Career Overview

Background

Julian Lynch grew up in Ridgewood, NJ, where he learned clarinet and guitar as a youth. In high school he performed in bands alongside future members of Real Estate (2), Titus Andronicus, and Family Portrait — a tight NJ scene that would later generate several notable indie acts. During a year in Scotland he collaborated with Gary Caruth of Sad City. While working for Smithsonian Folkways Recordings in Washington, D.C., he played drums with the band Hairizona.

Madison / OESB Period (2008–2010)

Lynch moved to Madison, WI in Fall 2008 to begin a Master’s program in Ethnomusicology. In Madison he self-released three CDRs under his given name — Born 2 Run (2008, later reissued on Wild Animal Kingdom, 2009), Birthday (2008, self-released), and Garden Is Adventure (2008, self-released).

In 2009 and 2010, Olde English Spelling Bee released his two breakthrough LPs: Orange You Glad (2009, OESB-41) and Mare (2010, OESB-51). Both were mastered by Carl Saff. These records established Lynch’s signature: woozy bedroom recordings with cassette-warped folk guitar, clarinet, and electronic textures, sitting comfortably among the OESB roster (Ducktails, James Ferraro, etc.).

Also in 2009 he appeared on the Ducktails split 7” (Julian Lynch / Ducktails) with cover art by Martin Courtney and the Julian Lynch / Zodiac Toys split (UK release). He played guitar on the Alex Bleeker debut (Alex Bleeker And The Freaks, Underwater Peoples Records, 2009).

Underwater Peoples / Expansion (2010–2013)

Lynch continued releasing on Underwater Peoples Records through the early 2010s: Droplet On A Hot Stone (2010), Terra (2011, his most fully realized solo statement on Discogs), Music For How Mata Hari Lost Her Head And Found Her Body (2011, a soundtrack/ambient record), Buffalo Songs (2011), and Lines (2013). He also contributed bass clarinet to M. Pyres & The Season Creeps’ Mountain Pacific (2010) and produced and played on Family Portrait (2)‘s self-titled record (2011).

Terra (Underwater Peoples, 2011) featured artwork by Ken Cro-Ken and design by Mike Mimoun.

Real Estate Connection

Lynch is a credited member of Real Estate (2) (Discogs ID: 1415073) alongside Martin Courtney, Matthew Mondanile (Ducktails), and Alex Bleeker. He appears as a multi-instrumentalist (“Clarinet, Synth, Saxophone”) on Cate Le Bon’s What For? (Carpark Records, 2015, one of the most notable sessions credits), and on Real Estate’s In Mind (Domino, 2017) as a full band member. He also played clarinet on Double Double Whammy’s The Cover Of Hunter (2014) and guitar/bass on Real Estate’s Daniel (Domino, 2024).

Rat’s Spit (2019)

Rat’s Spit (Underwater Peoples Records, 2019) marks his most recent primary solo release on Discogs, with a Japan pressing also documented.

Academic Work

Lynch’s Discogs profile notes he was enrolled in a joint PhD program in Ethnomusicology and Anthropology at UW-Madison (as of the profile writing date). His time at Smithsonian Folkways Recordings reflects a deep institutional connection to ethnomusicological archiving. This dual identity — serious academic and lo-fi bedroom pop auteur — is central to his cultural position.

Scene Connections

  • Olde English Spelling Bee (OESB) roster — Lynch’s first major platform, situating him alongside Ducktails, James Ferraro, and other early 2010s lo-fi / hypnagogic artists
  • Underwater Peoples Records — the NJ/Brooklyn indie scene label that also released Alex Bleeker; tightly linked to the Real Estate orbit
  • Real Estate (2) — Lynch’s most prominent ongoing band; his membership connects him to Domino Records from 2017 onward
  • Smithsonian Folkways / Ethnomusicology — institutional counterpart to his bedroom recording practice; his academic program at UW-Madison was a known influence on the textural, exploratory character of his solo work
  • Ridgewood, NJ scene — formative high-school milieu shared with Titus Andronicus, Real Estate, and Family Portrait
