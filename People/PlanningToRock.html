Overview

Jam Rostron (formerly Janine Rostron), performing as PlanningToRock, is an English electronic musician, producer, and performance artist based in Tallinn, Estonia. Working across electro, synth-pop, leftfield electronica, and art-pop, Rostron self-produces their material, handling songwriting, performance, production, mixing, and visual direction. They co-created and co-produced the Darwin-inspired opera Tomorrow, In A Year (2010) with The Knife and Mount Sims, one of the decade’s most ambitious collaborative works in electronic music. Their DFA-era albums W (2011) and All Love’s Legal (2014) established them as a distinctive voice in queer and feminist electronic music. The 2021 single “Gay Dreams Do Come True” and 2018 album Powerhouse continue the thread on their own Human Level imprint.

Biography

Early Work (2004–2006)

PlanningToRock’s recorded debut was Topics On A Foreign Mind (2004), a seven-track digital release on Manchester’s Twisted Nerve (catalog TN I 004). Rostron self-wrote, performed, and produced the record, and also handled artwork; the release credits list “Janine Rostron” as producer and writer. The record foreshadows the project’s theatrical orchestral-electronic aesthetic. Oliver Benn contributed live drums to “Waldt.”

The second album, Have It All (2006), came out on Chicks On Speed Records (a Munich-based feminist art-music imprint distributed via Disko B). Mixed by Cornelius Rapp and Rostron, with mastering by Loop-o (Andreas Lubich), and cover photography by Melanie Bonajo. A companion release, Have It All Stringed Up, included orchestral string arrangements with players including Micha Kaplan, Therese Heth, and Zoe Cartier, and Ben Cottrell.

Tomorrow, In A Year and the Rabid Records Connection (2010)

In 2010, Rostron co-created Tomorrow, In A Year with The Knife (Karin Dreijer and Olof Dreijer) and Mount Sims (Matthew Sims). The work was commissioned by Copenhagen’s Hotel Pro Forma theatre company, directed by Kirsten Dehlholm, and is based on Darwin’s On the Origin of Species. The three-way collaboration credited all parties as co-producers, co-mixers, and co-recorders. Live percussion was played by Hjörleifur Jónsson and recorded by Kyle Gudmundson. Mastering was by Rashad Becker. The release came out on The Knife’s own Rabid Records.

Also in 2010, Seeds — a separate EP associated with the opera project — appeared on Rabid Records, featuring contributions from Jonathan Johansson and Sara Hernández.

DFA Period — W and Living It Out (2011)

Rostron signed to DFA Records for the full-length W (2011; catalog DFA 2273). This is the most widely noted PlanningToRock album: twelve tracks of dense electro-pop with theatrical orchestration, lyrically focused on gender and identity. Production credits are deep: additional production came from Al Doyle (tracks 2, 3, 6, 12), Christoffer Berg (tracks 1, 2, 6–8), Felix Martin (tracks 2, 3, 6, 12), Olof Dreijer (track 9), and Tom Hopkins (tracks 2, 3, 6, 12). Live percussion throughout by Hjörleifur Jónsson. Mixed by Christoffer Berg and Rostron. Mastered by Simon Davey. Sleeve artwork by Bureau (2) and Rostron. Track 11, “Janine,” was a cover of a song written-by Arthur Russell. The Doorway EP preceded the album (DFA, 2011). A Living It Out single (mixed by Sebastian Müller, with drums by Patrick Mahoney) was also released on DFA.

All Love’s Legal and Human Level (2012–2014)

Rostron launched their own imprint, Human Level (ID 424507), starting with the Hl001 EP (2012), which also featured remixer RRoxymore. Misxgyny Drxp Dead (2013) was a politically charged single; Holly Herndon contributed a remix. The full-length All Love’s Legal (2014; Human Level HL004CD) is an explicitly queer political album — tracks include “Let’s Talk About Gender Baby,” “Misogyny Drop Dead,” and “Patriarchy Over & Out.” Mixed partly by Lexxx, with mastering by Guy Davie. Artwork by Martin Falck and Rostron.

Powerhouse and Later Work (2018–2021)

Powerhouse (2018; DFA / Human Level / [PIAS], catalog DFA2617CD) was produced entirely by Rostron, with mixing by Marta Salogni and mastering by Heba Kadry. The track “Much To Touch” featured additional co-production by Olof Dreijer. Planningtochanel (2020, Human Level) and Gay Dreams Do Come True (2021, Human Level HL103) continued solo output.

Rostron also contributed a remix to Robyn’s “Ever Again” (Konichiwa Records, 2019) and a remix to “Holes” (Konichiwa Records, 2020). In 2024, Rostron contributed an additional remix and additional production to a release on London Records (catalog 33859).

Guest and Session Credits (as Janine Rostron)

The Janine Rostron Discogs entry (ID 622632) captures older credits before the PlanningToRock name was established. Notably: backing vocals on LCD Soundsystem’s The Long Goodbye: LCD Soundsystem Live At Madison Square Garden (2014; DFA / Parlophone / Warner Bros.). Also: additional vocals on an unidentified 1999 Twisted Nerve / XL Recordings release (It Came From The Ground); writer/performer on the Twisted Nerve Jukebox 45 Singles Issue 3 (2003); backing vocals on Yesterday Was Lived And Lost (Lanark, 2012).

Open Questions

  • The Discogs profile says “lives in Tallinn, Estonia” but the brief describes them as British-Swedish; Discogs lists country as “UK” for debut. No clear Swedish connection documented in the DB beyond Rabid Records / The Knife affiliation.
  • Discogs does not clarify when the name change from Janine to Jam Rostron occurred.
  • !K7 Records connection mentioned in the brief but not evident in Discogs releases — possibly a distribution arrangement not captured in release labels. Needs web confirmation.
  • “Aquarian Jugs” alias appears in multiple Discogs entries but has no dedicated discography entry and no releases found under that name.
  • Topics On A Foreign Mind has master_id 0 — may be a standalone entry with no master grouping; other pressings may be missing.
  • The 2024 London Records release (ID 32009690, The Age Of Consent) is a compilation with Rostron listed as “Additional Remix, Additional Production” — primary artist not noted.
  • Cornelius Rapp (Discogs ID 580243) appears across 6 shared releases but is not well-documented in the vault.
  • LCD Soundsystem Long Goodbye live album backing-vocal credit (as Janine Rostron) — context of this appearance not clear from Discogs alone.
