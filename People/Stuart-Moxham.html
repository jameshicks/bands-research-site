Stuart Moxham

Stuart Moxham

Oct 19, 202613 min read

Stuart Moxham

Discogs ID: 287595 Born: — Active: 1979–present Origin: Shaftesbury, UK (Welsh post-punk scene; Cardiff-based with YMG) Roles: guitarist, organist, bassist, vocalist, songwriter, producer, engineer

Welsh multi-instrumentalist and songwriter. Co-founder of Young Marble Giants alongside his brother Philip Moxham and vocalist Alison Statton. After YMG dissolved in 1981 he formed The Gist with Philip, recorded several solo albums, produced records for Marine Girls and Lois Maffeo (via K Records), and has continued releasing material solo and in collaboration with Louis Philippe well into the 2020s.

Biography

Young Marble Giants (1978–1981)

Young Marble Giants formed in Cardiff around 1978. The band was a trio: Stuart Moxham on guitar and organ, Philip Moxham on bass, and Alison Statton on vocals. Their sound was radically stripped down — no drums, no percussion, minimal overdubs — built from Stuart’s guitar and organ riffs, Philip’s dry bass lines, and Statton’s understated, conversational vocals.

Their debut LP Colossal Youth appeared on Rough Trade in 1980 (ROUGH 8). Stuart wrote virtually the entire record (Alison Statton co-wrote “Eating Noddemix,” Philip co-wrote “Choci Loni”). Dave Anderson (3) produced and engineered. Stuart contributed guitar and organ; Philip bass; Statton voice. A single instance of slide guitar from Dave Dearnaley appeared on “Include Me Out.” The LP is credited as produced jointly by Young Marble Giants and Dave Anderson.

Two singles accompanied the LP cycle: Final Day (1980, Rough Trade RT 043) — four tracks including two alternate versions of the LP’s title track — and Testcard E.P. (1981, Rough Trade). A compilation, Nipped In The Bud (1983, Rough Trade), gathered non-album material. The band also recorded a John Peel session later released as The Peel Sessions (1991, Strange Fruit) and a live set eventually released as Live At The Hurrah! (1994, Visionary Communications).

YMG broke up shortly after the 1980 LP. Alison Statton went on to form Weekend and then later Saturday Night Band. Stuart and Philip Moxham moved on to The Gist.

The Gist (1980–1984 / revived 2017–2020)

The Gist was the Moxham brothers’ post-YMG project. Discogs lists both Philip and Stuart as members. The first release was the single This Is Love (1980, Rough Trade RT 058) — still on Rough Trade, engineered by Dave Anderson, with Stuart credited for vocals, instruments, and songwriting; Philip on bass for side A. Lewis Mottram played bass on side B (“Yanks”).

The debut LP Embrace The Herd (1982, Rough Trade ROUGH 25 / RTD 6, released in Germany) is Stuart’s most ambitious early-solo statement — he is credited for all instruments and vocals across most of the record, plus arrangements and songwriting, with Phil Legg producing and engineering. The LP brought in an array of guests on specific tracks: Philip Moxham (bass, “Love At First Sight,” “Clean Bridges,” “Carnival Headache”), Dave Dearnaley (guitar), Wendy Smith (vocals), Alison Statton and Vivien Goldman (vocals, “Clean Bridges”), Lewis Mottram and Debbie Pritchard (“Simian”), Epic Soundtracks (drums, “Dark Shots”), Charles Bullen and Jake Bowie (percussion, “The Long Run”), and Phil Legg (toms). Cover painting by Sasha Gadsby; cover design by Ian Denning.

The single Love At First Sight (1982, Rough Trade) and Fool For A Valentine (1983, Rough Trade) followed. A second LP, Lazy Ways — credited to The Gist and released on Cherry Red (BRED 44, 1983) — does not have Stuart in the primary credit on the Discogs canonical release; the Cherry Red compilation Lazy Ways / Beach Party (1984) bundles the LP alongside Beach Party (a Marine Girls compilation that Stuart produced — see below).

The Gist was revived much later. Holding Pattern (2017, Tiny Global Productions PICI 0009-CD) is a CD of archival and new Gist material, with Stuart performing and recording and a guest cast including Andrew Moxham, Dean Brodrick, Debbie Debris, and Spike Reptile on some tracks. Interior Windows (2020, Tiny Global Productions) continued this late revival.

A Japanese compilation The Gist Complete: Another Way Of Being (2021, Hayabusa Landings) gathered the full catalogue.

Production Work (1983–1993)

Stuart became a working producer in the early-to-mid 1980s and into the 1990s, taking on records for Rough Trade and Cherry Red artists.

Marine Girls — Lazy Ways (1983, Cherry Red BRED 44): Stuart produced the debut LP by the Marine Girls (Tracey Thorn, Alice Fox, Jane Fox). Phil Legg recorded. Tracey Thorn and Jane Fox are the primary credited songwriters.

Marine Girls — Don’t Come Back / You Must Be Mad (1983, Cherry Red): Stuart produced this single.

Beat Happening — You Turn Me On (1992, K / Sub Pop SP 45/207): Stuart co-produced with Steve Fisk. Stuart handled tracks A1, A5, B2, B3; Fisk handled A2, A3, A4, B1, B4. This is his K Records entry point — a split production credit on one of Beat Happening’s most prominent releases, on the K / Sub Pop co-label.

Lois Maffeo — Butterfly Kiss (1992, K Records KLP 15): Stuart’s most substantial production credit of this era. He produced the entire LP and also played bass throughout. Molly Neuman (Bratmobile) played drums; Stuart Hallerman engineered. This placed Stuart inside the Olympia, WA underground — an unusual transatlantic connection between the Welsh post-punk world and the Pacific Northwest indie scene. See Lois Maffeo for the full personnel breakdown.

Barbara Manning — Barbara Manning Sings With The Original Artists (1993, Feel Good All Over f7001): Stuart produced and performed on this LP, playing guitar, vocals, drums, percussion, and organ. His brother Andrew Moxham played drums, organ, guitar, and bass. Jon Langford (2) (The Mekons) also contributed guitar, drum programming, and synth. Stuart wrote six of the eleven tracks; the album title references him and the other original artists whose songs Manning was interpreting.

Peel (1994, Hi-Ball Records): Stuart is credited as producer.

Solo Work (1993–present)

Stuart Moxham And The Original Artists — Random Rules (1993, Peak Records PEAK 1): Stuart’s first release under his own name as primary artist. A CD of folk-inflected acoustic songs. The band (Stuart Moxham And The Original Artists, Discogs ID 1710587) featured Louis Philippe (organ, piano, harp, guitar, vocals, strings — across multiple tracks), Andrew Moxham (drums throughout), Chris Ridgway (harmonica), Spike (16) / Alun Mark Williams (bass, guitar, strings), and Cher Lewis (vocals). Stuart produced, wrote, mixed, sang, and played guitar and various instruments throughout. Louis Philippe’s multi-instrumental contributions here established the core of their long-running creative partnership.

Stuart Moxham and Louis Philippe — The Huddle House (2007, Wonder (4) WONDER DELTA): A joint LP, with both co-credited as primary artists. Ken Brake produced; Stuart played steel-string acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, keyboards, and percussion; Louis Philippe contributed nylon-string acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, and harmony vocals and co-arranged. Andrew Moxham contributed hi-hats on one track (“Desert Rain”). 13 tracks of crafted acoustic pop.

Stuart Moxham and Louis Philippe — The Devil Laughs (2020, Tiny Global Productions PICI-0019-CD): A second joint LP. Stuart played acoustic guitar, vocals, shaker, guitar, tambourine, cabasa, bongos, handclaps, and bass drum; Louis Philippe played electric guitar, vocals, acoustic guitar, electric piano, keyboards, 12-string acoustic guitar, and bass. Danny Manners played double bass on most tracks. Ken Brake contributed to three tracks (guitar/vocals, keyboards) and recorded and mastered the album.

Stuart Moxham — Fabstract (2024, Tiny Global Productions PICI-0062): A deluxe LP/CD release described as drawing from archival and new recordings. Stuart recorded, mixed, and played the full range of instruments himself (acoustic guitar, bass, drum machine, drums, electric guitar, handclaps, harmony vocals, hihat, melodica, organ, piano, tambourine, vocals). Notable guests: Philip Moxham (bass, tracks A3/A7/B6), Andrew Moxham (bass, A5; drums A3/B6), Spike Williams / Spike (16) (electric guitar, viola, A3/A5), Debbie Pritchard (vocals, A5), Melody Moxham (vocals, A6), Anthony Silvestros (bass, B3), Dean Brodrick (percussion, A5), Louis Philippe (piano, harpsichord, arranged vocals, mixed B, tracks A8/B1). Track B6 “The Long Run (Demo)” and the “YMG Unused Organ Riff” closing track index back to the earliest Gist/YMG era.

Stuart Moxham — The Devil Laughs (2025 and ongoing): Winter Sun (2025, Tiny Global Productions) appeared as his most recent primary credit as of this research.

Mastering Work (2017–2024)

From 2017 Discogs also credits Stuart as mastering engineer for a run of Tiny Global Productions releases: Brutalism Begins At Home (2017), Funambulist We Love You (2017), Skull Jam (2017), Neon Primitives (2019), Four Against Fate (2020), Dreams Take Flight (2021), My Beautiful England (2022), Fasted Beautiful Mistakes (2023). All are Tiny Global Productions releases, suggesting an ongoing relationship with that label as both artist and house engineer.

Song Licensing / Cover Versions

Stuart’s songs have been covered and licensed across several decades. Notable credits:

  • Hypocrite (1994, 4AD) — Stuart has a written-by credit on a 4AD release.
  • Live Through This (1994, Hole / Geffen) — a written-by credit on Hole’s landmark LP; “Credit In The Straight World” (from Colossal Youth) was covered by Hole with Courtney Love’s vocal interpretation.
  • Pop Satori (1986, Virgin, France) — written-by credit, likely a Étienne Daho cover of a YMG/Gist composition.
  • Cookie (1996, 4AD, Japan) — written-by.
  • La Varieté (2006, Cherry Red) — written-by.
