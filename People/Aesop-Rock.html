Aesop Rock

Discogs ID: 28104
Real name: Ian Matthias Bavitz
Aliases: Bazooka Tooth; Ian Bavitz
Born: June 5, 1976, Long Island, New York, USA
Instruments: Vocals (MC); Production

American MC, producer, and visual artist. One of the most distinctive voices in underground hip-hop, known for densely layered lyrics, oblique imagery, and expanding vocabulary. Moved from self-released cassettes in the late 1990s through the Definitive Jux era into a long tenure on Rhymesayers Entertainment where he remains active as of 2025. Collaborates frequently with producer Blockhead and rapper/producer Rob Sonic.

Early Work and Self-Releases (1996–2000)

Bavitz began releasing music under the Aesop Rock name while studying at Boston University. His earliest recordings circulated as self-released demo tapes and CD-Rs:

Float (2000, Mush) was his commercial debut and introduced the dense internal rhyme schemes and abstract subject matter that would define his work. Blockhead (James Anthony Simon) contributed production and became his primary beatmaker.

Definitive Jux / Def Jux Era (2001–2008)

Aesop Rock became a central figure on Definitive Jux (Def Jux), the New York underground hip-hop label founded and run by El-P:

Labor Days (2001) is widely considered his defining statement: a meditation on alienated labor with Blockhead producing the bulk of the beats (tracks 2–5, 7, 11–14) and Omega One producing “Coma.” Aesop produced “Labor,” “One Brick,” “Battery,” and “Boombox” himself. Mixed by Cryptic One.

Bazooka Tooth (2003, triple LP) was more maximalist — Aesop producing nearly everything himself, with El-P executive producing and appearing on “We’re Famous,” and Blockhead producing “Cook It Up,” “Babies With Guns,” and “11:35.” Features appearances from Murs, Cannibal Ox, Camp Lo, Mr. Lif, and Party Fun Action Committee.

None Shall Pass (2007) is generally regarded as his strongest Def Jux LP, tighter than Bazooka Tooth and more refined than Labor Days.

The Impossible Kid (2016) is a self-produced album; the Grimace Federation contributes live instruments (synths, guitar, organ, Rhodes) throughout. Notable features: Chuck D on “Lazy Eye,” Kimya Dawson on “Tuff.”

Malibu Ken (2019) is a full collaboration with Tobacco (3) (Tom Fec of Black Moth Super Rainbow). Tobacco produced the album; genres listed as electronic/hip-hop/experimental/synthwave.

Garbology (2021) is a co-credited LP with Blockhead, the most formal documentation of their partnership in album form. Blockhead produced all tracks; features Homeboy Sandman on “All Day Breakfast.”

Production Style and Key Collaborators

Aesop Rock self-produced the majority of Bazooka Tooth and from Skelethon onward; earlier work relied heavily on Blockhead for beats. His most consistent engineering/mixing collaborator is Joey Raia (credited on multiple Rhymesayers-era records). Mastering credits include Emily Lazar and Joe LaPorta.

Rob Sonic (Rob Smith) is his primary touring and creative partner — together they form Hail Mary Mallon and have appeared on each other’s records across multiple labels.
