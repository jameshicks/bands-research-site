Sharon Cheslow

Sharon Cheslow

Oct 22, 20269 min read

Sharon Cheslow

Musician, photographer, writer, and archivist based in Washington DC. Discogs ID: 533431. Active 1981–present (Discogs credits span 1984–2024). Co-creator of Banned in DC: Photos and Flyers from the DC Punk Underground 1979–85. Founder of her own label Decomposition. Guitarist/vocalist with Chalk Circle (2) (1981–1983) and Bloody Mannequin Orchestra (1984). Subsequent career ranges across indie rock, noise, free improvisation, and experimental electronics.

Website: http://www.sharoncheslow.com

Biography

Early DC Punk: Chalk Circle (1981–1983)

Cheslow co-founded Chalk Circle (2) in Washington DC in 1981 alongside drummer Anne Bonafede and guitarist/vocalist Mary Green, with alternating bassists (Jan Pumphrey, Tamera Lyndsay, and Chris Niblack). The band operated on the edges of the early DC hardcore scene — their raw, rhythmic, minimal sound had more in common with post-punk or art punk than with the D.C. hardcore community they initially helped pioneer. The band disbanded in 1983.

Chalk Circle’s recorded output was collected and released as a retrospective LP, Reflection (2011), on Mississippi Records and Post Present Medium (cat. MR-069 / PPM-040). Cheslow produced the record, compiled the audio, edited the sessions, designed the booklet, and contributed photography — a full package that she assembled nearly three decades after the original recordings. The tracklist documents the band’s rotating lineup across twelve tracks (guitar, vocals, and fretless guitar from Cheslow throughout). Liner notes by Don Fleming. Recorded by Don Zientara (at Inner Ear), Howard Wuelfing, and Malcolm Riviera at various sessions. Mixed by Geoff Turner (tracks A1–A2).

Bloody Mannequin Orchestra (1984)

Cheslow joined Bloody Mannequin Orchestra alongside Roger Marbury (bass, vocals, percussion), Colin Sears (drums, vocals, keyboards, cover art), Alex Mahoney (guitar, vocals), and Charles Bennington (vocals, saxophone). The band released two records in 1984:

  • Streetlights In The Dark — cassette, WGNS Recordings (cat. WGNS 7), 14 tracks. Art punk / No Wave / post-punk.
  • Roadmap To Revolution — LP, EPU Records / WGNS Recordings (cat. DM-84009), 10 tracks. Produced by Don Zientara and Bloody Mannequin Orchestra. Genres: Art Rock, Hardcore, No Wave, Punk.

Cheslow played guitar and sang on both records. Roadmap To Revolution also appeared on the 1991 compilation New World Order: War #1 (Maximumrocknroll) alongside Noam Chomsky and Bad Religion.

Photography and Archival Work

By 1984 Cheslow was already active as a documentary photographer in the DC scene. Her most significant credit in this mode is Banned in DC: Photos and Flyers from the DC Punk Underground 1979–85, co-created with Cynthia Connolly and Leslie Clague. This book stands as the primary photographic document of the first wave of DC punk and hardcore.

Her photography credits on Discogs:

  • Fabuley — Holy Rollers LP (Dischord, 1991) — back photo credit (release IDs 1996920, 1996929, 15171752, 32199645)
  • Get Up Resolution: Love — Hawnay Troof (The Company With The Golden Arm Records, Germany, 2004)
  • Reflection — Chalk Circle (2) retrospective (2011) — design, booklet design, and photography (also credited: Compiled By, Edited By)
  • For The Light I Wait — Sharon Cheslow / Corin Tucker / Julia Holter single (Decomposition, 2020)

She also contributed sleeve design to Suture! (Suture (3), Decomposition / Dischord, 1992).

Red Eye (14) (mid-1990s)

Cheslow formed Red Eye (14) as a duo with guitarist/multi-instrumentalist Tim Green (2). They released:

  • Special Delivery To My Heart / Reservoir — 7” single (Decomposition DE 02 / Dischord DIS 99.75, 1995). Cheslow: voice and guitar (Side A); Green: sonic space guitar and bass (Side B). Written by Red Eye (14). Artwork woodcut by Lydia Ely.
  • Static Storm (Original Soundtrack Recording) — cassette (Decomposition DE05, 1998). Cheslow: vocals, guitar, bass, electronics. Green: electronics, guitar, bass, drums, backing vocals.

The Electrolettes (1998–1999)

The Electrolettes was a duo of Cheslow and drummer Julianna Bright. Releases:

  • Plug Me In — cassette (Decomposition DE04, 1998). Cheslow: vocals, guitar, electronics, co-producer. Tim Green (2) co-produced and engineered. 8 tracks; genres: Electronic, Rock (Art Rock, Experimental, Garage Rock, Indie Rock, Psychedelic Rock).
  • Octane Lies b/w Anxiety — 7” single (Kill Rock Stars KRS 325, 1999). Cheslow and Tim Green co-produced. Bright: drums, backing vocals. Kill Rock Stars release confirms national distribution reach.

Solo / Coterie Exchange (2002–2004)

Under her own name and as Coterie Exchange (a rotating collaborative project), Cheslow released two Decomposition CDrs:

  • Lullabye From The Sky — Sharon Cheslow & Coterie Exchange (Decomposition DE06, 2002). 16 tracks. Cheslow: vocals, guitar, oscillators, samples, organ, digital audio, producer. Collaborators included Greg Saunier (Deerhoof) on drums/percussion/piano; John Dieterich (Deerhoof) on guitar, synthesizer, and electronics; Daan Vandewalle on piano; Randy Nordschow on trumpet; Jorge Boehringer on amplified viola; Julianna Bright on drums and percussion; Concentrick on Moog; Tim Green (2) as engineer on select tracks; and Alan Cheslow on Moog, computer, and voice.
  • Uncertainty Rides The Waves — Sharon Cheslow & Coterie Exchange (Decomposition DE07, 2004, limited edition CDr). 5 tracks. Cheslow: performer, vocals. Track 4 “Sonic Triptych” features a large ensemble including Blevin Blectum, Jenny Hoyston, Erin Weber, and others. Track 5 features KIT (2) with Pete Swanson, Gabriel Mindel Saloman, and others.

Collaborations (2005–2007)

  • Collaborations — Yellow Swans / Sharon Cheslow / Chuck Bettis (Collective Jyrk / Decomposition DE09, 2005). CDr compilation of live recordings from a Northeast US tour. Cheslow co-primary artist; she also edited, sequenced, and designed the artwork. Musicians: Pete Swanson and Gabriel Mindel Saloman (Yellow Swans), Chuck Bettis (electronics, voice), Carly Ptak (electronics, voice on select tracks), Jerry Lim (guitar), Kris Thompson (theremin, electronics), Inca Ore / Eva Inca Ore (voice). Yellow Swans later appeared on Not Not Fun Records.
  • Plants That Kill — Liz Allbee / Sharon Cheslow / Weasel Walter (Curor Recordings, 2007). CDr album, free improvisation. Allbee: trumpet, electronics, conch shell, voice. Cheslow: guitar, electronics, objects, voice. Walter: drums, percussion. UK release.

Suture (3) (early 1990s)

Cheslow was a member of Suture (3) alongside Kathleen Hanna and Dug Birdzell. Suture’s debut Suture! (Decomposition / Dischord, 1992, DE1) was a 7-inch; Cheslow contributed sleeve design (credited as “Sharon”). Recorded by Jesse Quitslund, Seth Lorinczi, and Suture (3).

Later Work: Julia Holter Connections (2020–2024)

Two significant late-career credits involving Julia Holter:

  • For The Light I Wait — Sharon Cheslow / Corin Tucker / Julia Holter (Decomposition DE-12/HolTuCh-1, 2020). Digital single. All three: written-by, arranged by, producer. Cheslow: lead guitar, rhythm guitar, bass, synth, electronics, backing vocals, mixed by, photography by, lyrics by. Tucker: lead vocals, rhythm guitar, lyrics by. Holter: electric piano, synth, cello, backing vocals. Genres: Art Rock, Experimental, Free Improvisation, Indie Pop, Indie Rock, Psychedelic Rock.
  • Never Rarely Sometimes Always (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack) — Julia Holter (Real Gone Music RGM 1742, 2024). LP (limited edition). Cheslow credited: Electronics. Other players: Chris Votek (cello), Christin Hablewitz (clarinet). Holter: all composition, arrangement, production, synth, piano, vocals.
