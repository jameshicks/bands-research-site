Overview

Zac Pennington is a vocalist and songwriter primarily known as a member of Parenthetical Girls, the Portland-based experimental indie pop group that was active from the mid-2000s through the early 2010s. Beyond Parenthetical Girls he carries credits with Xiu Xiu and The Microphones’ Singers, and his 44 Discogs credits lean heavily toward vocals and songwriting. His catalogue clusters on Slender Means Society — the Parenthetical Girls’ own imprint — across 2004–2013, with wider release through Bella Union, Arts & Crafts, and the K label. His most frequent collaborators are Jamie Stewart of Xiu Xiu and multi-instrumentalist Jherek Bischoff (21 shared credits each), alongside core Parenthetical Girls members Sam Mickens and Izaak Mills.

Parenthetical Girls wind-down (2013)

Pennington has described the band’s end as a slow dissolution rather than a single decision. In a 2021 interview with Spectrum Culture (spectrumculture.com) he said: “The band more or less ended three times between the first and final EP. From the beginning, Parenthetical Girls had only ever really existed as a product of my own hubris and stubborn will — over the years, it died a lot of deaths.” He identified Jherek Bischoff as the project’s only real constant collaborator — “the only real consistency between P. Girls recordings was the collaboration between Jherek Bischoff and I; by the time we reached Privilege, recordings had mostly whittled down to the just two of us” — but noted Bischoff “was never especially interested in taking the level of ownership or investment that I had,” and was by then getting busier with his own work. The Privilege EP series itself was not planned as a farewell but as a way to accelerate output: “Mostly impatience. It takes me too long to make things, and I had initially hoped that the incremental release ‘strategy’ (loose as it was) would help me to more efficiently continue whatever conversation Parenthetical Girls had been trying to engage with.” By around the fourth EP, Pennington realised the project had been the subject of its own grief: “it became pretty clear to me that the object I’d been grieving in a lot of the songs had been Parenthetical Girls all along.” He told The Line of Best Fit (thelineofbestfit.com): “By the time we had finished the record, I felt like I didn’t have anything else to say that needed to be under the banner of Parenthetical Girls.”

No label conflict or external pressure is cited in any interview found. The key factors were: (1) the project had always been Pennington’s effectively solo endeavour, with a rotating cast and Bischoff as the only stable collaborator; (2) Bischoff’s increasing outside commitments reduced shared availability; (3) Pennington experienced the final EPs as having already eulogised the band from within; and (4) he felt creative exhaustion with the name after finishing Privilege.

Post-PG: After moving to Los Angeles, Pennington formed a short-lived project called Comedienne with Bischoff and Greg Saunier of Deerhoof; it stalled when he could not distinguish the new work from his prior output. He later began collaborating with Prudence Rees-Lee, releasing a covers album (In Darkness) and eventually co-forming Popular Music (2), with whom he recorded Minor Works in Melbourne. (Source: Line of Best Fit interview, as above.)
