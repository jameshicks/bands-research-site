Ian Crause

Singer, guitarist, songwriter. UK. Discogs artist ID: 289992. Also credited as “Crause” (name variation on sleeve credits).

Primary songwriter, vocalist, and guitarist of Disco Inferno. Originator of the band’s defining technique: routing guitar through a Roland S-750 sampler to generate both melodic texture and percussive triggers in real time. After Disco Inferno dissolved in 1995–96, co-formed Floorshow with Robert Whatley; the resultant album was shelved by a label (unconfirmed; Crause described the sessions as “catastrophic”). Released the 7” single Elemental (2000, Tugboat Records, TUG S 025) and subsequently produced records for other artists, including Spanish bands. Returned to solo work on Bandcamp from 2012 onward, releasing a full double LP The Vertical Axis / The Song of Phaethon (2019, Sonido Costa Verde).

Biography

Formation of Disco Inferno and the sampler technique (1989–1992)

Crause co-founded Disco Inferno in Essex in January 1989, initially as a four-piece with Paul Willmott (bass), Robert Whatley (drums/programming), and Daniel Gish (keyboards). The quartet signed to the small independent ché Records in 1991, releasing the debut 7” Entertainment (CHÉ 1S) and the debut LP Open Doors • Closed Windows (CHÉ 2 LP), both produced by Charlie McIntosh.

The pivotal shift came around 1991–92, when Crause purchased a Roland S-750 sampler with his savings and spent approximately six months developing a new approach. He MIDI-wired his guitar so that individual strings or frets triggered different pre-loaded environmental samples — sounds the band had recorded themselves (running water, wind, birds, boots, car crashes, angry voices) rather than sampled recordings. Whatley mirrored the technique on drum pads; Willmott’s conventional bass grounded the texture. Crause has cited The Young Gods (for their sampling approach) and Public Enemy / The Bomb Squad (for production philosophy) as the key influences on this pivot: “We kind of got ours from Public Enemy, who were too black and The Young Gods, who sang in French, for fuck’s sake!” (2011 interview). After Gish departed around 1991–92 to join Bark Psychosis, the trio of Crause, Willmott, and Whatley became the definitive lineup.

Summer’s Last Sound (1992, Cheree Records, CHEREE 28 CD) was the first release to fully document the new approach, with Crause credited for sample programming, guitar, and vocals alongside Willmott and Whatley as co-authors. Charlie McIntosh returned as co-producer and studio sequencer.

Rough Trade years (1993–1995)

Disco Inferno moved to Rough Trade for what became their most celebrated and documented run. Crause continued as primary songwriter and sonic architect across all releases. Production arrangements shifted: McIntosh handled the first three Rough Trade releases (A Rock To Cling To / From The Devil To The Deep Blue Sky, 1993; The Last Dance, 1993 — split with Michael Johnson on the A-side tracks; D. I. Go Pop, 1994). For the final two releases, John Coxon and Mads Bjerke took over as recording and mixing engineers: Coxon produced Second Language (1994) and both co-recorded and co-mixed Technicolour (1995), the band’s second and final studio album.

Crause also co-designed the sleeve for Summer’s Last Sound, credited alongside Cactus Design.

The D. I. Go Pop LP (1994, Rough Trade R3072) is regarded as the band’s definitive statement. All songs are written by Disco Inferno (Crause, Willmott, Whatley jointly). Technicolour (1995) was produced by the band itself, with Crause retaining the written-by and performer credits.

Dissolution and Floorshow (1995–1999)

The band split in 1995 amid an irreparable creative and personal rift between Crause and Willmott. As Crause’s compositional approach moved further toward sampler-dominated abstraction, Willmott’s bass role became increasingly peripheral; Crause reportedly proposed an arrangement where he would record alone and the others would add parts afterwards. Willmott later stated that Crause had been unable to be in the same room with him.

Crause and Whatley — who were not the source of the acrimony — immediately formed Floorshow together. Sessions produced an album-length work tentatively titled The Vertical Axis, but this was shelved by a label (Crause has referred to “a major label” and called the sessions “catastrophic” in interviews; no label name has been publicly confirmed). Crause subsequently relocated to Bolivia and spent roughly nine years away from active music-making.

During this period, Crause appeared as performer on The Mixing It Session (1999, Tugboat Records), compiled from a BBC Radio 3 Mixing It session recorded at Maida Vale Studios on 19 December 1994, released alongside Whatley.

Solo work: Elemental single (2000)

Crause salvaged material from the Floorshow sessions for two early solo singles. Elemental (2000, Tugboat Records, TUG S 025) is a 7” single — not a full album — with two tracks. Crause handled vocals, sampler, guitar, bass, recording, and mixing himself. Personnel:

A second solo single, Head Over Heels (2001, Acuarela), also drew on Floorshow session material and again featured Richie Thomas on drums. (This release is not indexed under ID 289992 in the Discogs database and may be catalogued separately.)

Return and solo LP (2012–2019)

Crause returned to releasing music from around 2012, initially via Bandcamp. “More Earthly Concerns” appeared in mid-2012; the EP The Song of Phaethon (Bandcamp, November 2012) followed — a long-form piece inspired by Greek mythology and British involvement in the Second Gulf War. Additional solo tracks appeared on Bandcamp in early 2013. His full solo long-player, also using the The Vertical Axis title, was released on Bandcamp in December 2013.

A physical double LP The Vertical Axis / The Song of Phaethon was issued in 2019 on the Spanish label Sonido Costa Verde (cat. SCV-02). The 10-track double LP collects the Bandcamp material in physical form. Crause is credited as sole producer; mastered by Bernardo Calvo; cover by Iker Spozio; layout by Elisa Pérez. Genres/styles on Discogs: Electronic; abstract, experimental, rhythmic noise, sound collage. Tracklist: “And On And On It Goes” / “Black Light” / “Suns May Rise” / “The Vertical Axis” / “Foreign Land” / “More Earthly Concerns” / “A World Of Ghosts” / “Phaethon’s Call” / “Phaethon’s Zenith” / “Phaethon’s Fall.”

Production work (2014–2016)

Crause contributed lyrics to track 6 (“Monument II”) of the Epic45 Monument EP (2014, Wayside And Woodland Recordings, W&W022), where he also appeared as a featured vocalist. The EP otherwise uses lyrics by Epic45’s Ben Holton (and one cover with lyrics by Roland Orzabal).

In 2016, Crause produced Movimientos by the Spanish punk/post-punk band Juventud Juché (Sonido Muchacho, SM 030). Recorded by Oscar Moreno Gómez (a.k.a. Ojo). This is Crause’s only documented production credit for a non-UK artist in the Discogs database. The connection to Spain evidently carried over to the Sonido Costa Verde physical release of his own LP in 2019.

Liner notes (2017)

Crause contributed liner notes to the 2017 Rocket Girl reissue In Debt (Discogs ID: 10230664), alongside Neil Kulkarni.
