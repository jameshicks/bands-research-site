David Murray

American jazz tenor saxophonist and bass clarinetist. Born 1955, Berkeley, California. Discogs artist ID: 259154.

One of the central figures of the post-free / loft jazz movement. Murray arrived in New York from the West Coast in the mid-1970s and quickly became a ubiquitous presence in the loft circuit — playing, composing, and recording prolifically. Over five decades he has led dozens of configurations, from spare duo and trio dates to his landmark octet and big band, while remaining a co-founding member of the World Saxophone Quartet. His work touches free jazz, post-bop, R&B, blues, Cuban rhythms, and Guadeloupean gwoka percussion — one of the widest ranges of any improviser of his generation.

Early Career: Loft Jazz Era (1976–1979)

Murray arrived in New York around 1975 and became immediately active in the city’s loft jazz scene — the network of informal performance spaces (Studio Rivbea, Artists House, Tin Palace, and related venues) that incubated the generation between free jazz and what would become “downtown” improvised music.

His debut recordings came quickly. The earliest confirmed India Navigation date is Flowers For Albert (1976), a tribute to Albert Ayler with Phillip Wilson on drums, Fred Hopkins on bass, and Olu Dara on trumpet. Murray composed the bulk of the material and played tenor saxophone throughout.

The same year he recorded Low Class Conspiracy (Adelphi Records, 1976) under the David Murray & Low Class Conspiracy banner. European dates followed: 3D Family (Hat Hut Records, Switzerland, 1978), Interboogieology (Black Saint, Italy, 1978 — with Butch Morris on cornet, Johnny Dyani on bass), and Let The Music Take You (Marge, France, 1978).

By 1977, Murray was also a member of the newly formed World Saxophone Quartet (see below), and appearing as a sideman on sessions across the avant-garde circuit — including two Circle Records (2) volumes (Penthouse Jazz and Holy Siege On Intrigue, both 1977) with the No Wave / loft overlap crowd.

World Saxophone Quartet (1977–present)

Murray co-founded the World Saxophone Quartet in 1977 alongside Julius Hemphill (alto), Oliver Lake (alto/soprano), and Hamiet Bluiett (baritone) — all veterans of the St. Louis Black Artists Group (BAG). The group performed without a rhythm section, relying entirely on four-saxophone interplay. It became one of the defining ensembles of the post-free era.

WSQ’s first recording, Point Of No Return, appeared on Moers Music (Germany) in 1977. They moved to Black Saint for their canonical run, then to Nonesuch / Elektra Nonesuch in the mid-1980s, and later to Justin Time in the late 1990s–2000s.

David Murray Octet (1980–2000s)

The David Murray Octet (Discogs ID: 516648) was Murray’s main compositional vehicle during the 1980s, recording for Black Saint (the classic run) and later DIW and Justin Time. The core lineup rotated but consistently featured Butch Morris on cornet, Henry Threadgill on alto saxophone, George Lewis on trombone, Olu Dara on trumpet, Anthony Davis (2) or Curtis Clark on piano, Steve McCall or Craig Harris (3) on various roles, and Wilber Morris on bass.

David Murray Big Band (1985–1995)

The David Murray Big Band (Discogs ID: 348221) expanded the octet concept into a full large ensemble, conducted by Butch Morris using his conduction system. The band was documented live at Sweet Basil in New York.

Personnel on Live At “Sweet Basil” Vol. 1 (1985): Murray (tenor sax, bass clarinet, arranger, producer), Butch Morris (conductor), Olu Dara (cornet), Fred Hopkins (bass), Steve Coleman (alto sax, soprano sax), John Purcell (alto sax, clarinet), Craig Harris (3) (trombone), Baikida Carroll (trumpet), Bob Stewart (tuba), Vincent Chancey (French horn), Billy Higgins (percussion), Rod Williams (piano).

Columbia / Portrait Crossover (1989)

Ming’s Samba (Portrait, 1989) was Murray’s mainstream-crossover attempt on the Columbia subsidiary, reaching a broader audience while retaining his core language.

Blues For Coltrane (Impulse!, 1988)

A Tribute To John Coltrane / Blues For Coltrane (Impulse!, 1988) — a festival-documentary release anchored in Coltrane tribute material; one of the few times Murray appeared on a major American jazz imprint.
