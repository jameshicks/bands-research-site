Tony Cohen

Discogs artist ID: 115046 Real name: Anthony Lawrence Cohen Born: 4 June 1957 Died: 2 August 2017, Dandenong, Victoria, Australia Role: Recording engineer, mixer, producer Base: Melbourne, Australia Active: c. 1974–2016

Biography

Early Career (1974–1979)

Tony Cohen began his career in the mid-1970s as a tape operator and assistant engineer. His earliest credited work is on the 1974 debut album by Perth glam-rock group Supernaut (3), followed by engineering sessions for The Ferrets’ 1976 debut Don’t Fall In Love. Through the late 1970s he worked across the Melbourne scene, engineering sessions for Cold Chisel (early pressings), Models (2), and various Mushroom Records artists. By 1979 he had credits on sessions for The Laughing Clowns, Hunters & Collectors, and the independent Melbourne label Missing Link.

The Boys Next Door / Birthday Party (1978–1982)

Cohen’s most defining early relationship began in 1978 when he first worked with what was then known as The Boys Next Door — the band that would soon become The Birthday Party. He engineered the band’s debut LP Door, Door (1979, Mushroom) and co-produced their self-titled sophomore album The Birthday Party (1980, Missing Link) alongside Keith Glass and the band itself.

He remained their primary engineer through the Birthday Party era, working on the landmark records Prayers On Fire (1981, 4AD) and Junkyard (1982, 4AD — recording tracks A1–B1, B3, B5; Richard Mazda handled B2 and B4). He also mixed the live album It’s Still Living (1985, Missing Link), released after the band’s breakup.

Through the Birthday Party, Cohen became part of the inner circle around Nick Cave, Mick Harvey, Rowland S. Howard, Tracy Pew, and Phill Calvert.

Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds — Core Era (1984–2001)

Cohen’s relationship with Nick Cave extended seamlessly into Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds. He is credited on their debut From Her To Eternity (1984, Mute) — handling the bonus track on the 1990 reissue — and served as primary engineer and co-producer across most of the band’s classic catalogue through to 2001.

Key Bad Seeds albums and roles:

  • Kicking Against The Pricks (1986, Mute) — engineer (all backing tracks)
  • Your Funeral… My Trial (1986, Mute) — engineer, producer (with Flood)
  • The Singer (1986, Mute) — engineer
  • Tender Prey (1988, Mute) — engineer, mixed by (with Flood)
  • The Mercy Seat single (1988) — engineer, mixed by
  • Henry’s Dream (1992, Mute) — engineer, mixed by (tracks 1–3, 6–9; produced by David Briggs)
  • Let Love In (1994, Mute) — mixed by, producer
  • Where The Wild Roses Grow (1995, Mute) — producer (with Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds and Victor Van Vugt)
  • Murder Ballads (1996, Mute) — mixed by (tracks 1–4, 6–10), producer
  • The Best Of (1998, Mute) — mixed by, producer, recorded by
  • No More Shall We Part (2001, Mute) — mixed by, producer, recorded by
  • As I Sat Sadly By Her Side single (2001, Mute) — mixed by, producer, recorded by

His ARIA Award for Producer of the Year in 1995 (following an initial 1994 win for The Cruel Sea’s The Honeymoon Is Over) and Engineer of the Year in 1995 reflect the peak of this period.

Cohen co-produced several Serge Gainsbourg tribute records with Nick Cave in the mid-1990s: Intoxicated Man (1995, Mute) and Pink Elephants (1997, Mute), as well as the Gainsbourg-influenced singles Harley Davidson (1996) and Henry Lee (1996, with PJ Harvey).

The Melbourne Underground Network

Beyond the Cave axis, Cohen was deeply embedded in the broader Melbourne underground of the 1980s–1990s:

  • Crime & The City Solution — multiple credits including Just South Of Heaven (1985), Room Of Lights (1986, Mute — engineer/producer), Séries Américaines (1987 — engineer/producer), and the An Introduction To… anthology.
  • The Go-Betweens — engineered and co-produced their debut album Send Me A Lullaby (1982, Rough Trade) with Robert Forster and Grant McLennan; credited on the 2026 G Stands For Go-Betweens anthology.
  • The Beasts Of Bourbon — multiple credits through the late 1980s and early 1990s.
  • Dave Graney & The Coral Snakes — mixed several releases including Living… In Large Rooms And Lounges (1995, Mushroom).
  • The Johnnys — engineered their debut (1984) and subsequent releases.
  • TISM — recording credits.
  • Kim Salmon And The Surrealists — engineer/producer, Kim Salmon & The Surrealists (1995, Red Eye Records).
  • Hunters & Collectors — early sessions (1982, White Label Records).
  • The Moodists — through Dave Graney connection.

The Cruel Sea and ARIA Recognition (1993–1995)

Cohen produced The Cruel Sea’s The Honeymoon Is Over (1993, Red Eye Records/Polydor), which became one of the defining Australian rock albums of the decade. His production work earned him the ARIA Award for Producer of the Year in 1994. The following year he won both Producer of the Year and Engineer of the Year again.

Tex Perkins, vocalist of The Cruel Sea, also led The Beasts Of Bourbon, another Cohen client.

International Reach and Serge Gainsbourg Work

Cohen’s profile extended beyond Australia. He recorded Anita Lane’s album Dirty Pearl (1993, Mute) and worked on Crime & The City Solution’s European releases. The Gainsbourg projects — Intoxicated Man and Pink Elephants — placed him in the Paris recording circuit alongside Cave and Mick Harvey.

Blackeyed Susans

Cohen has 6 shared releases with Blackeyed Susans. He mixed Mouth To Mouth (1995, Hi-Gloss Records/MDS — a Kakulas production, recorded by Victor Van Vugt) and mixed a batch of tracks (CD1-14 to CD1-19) on the career retrospective Reveal Yourself (2009, Liberation Music).

Later Career (2000–2016)

Cohen continued active work into the 2000s and 2010s. Notable later credits include:

  • The Devastations — recorded/mixed (2004, Munster Records)
  • Powderfinger — producer on Fingerprints compilation (2004, Universal)
  • Immolation & Ameliorations [1995–2005] for Spencer P. Jones (2006, Spooky Records)
  • In-Stereo (2006, Dixiefrog) — engineer/producer/mixed by, with spoken word credit (“Voice”)
  • Various Spencer P. Jones compilations through to All The Way With Spencer P. Jones – Volume 1 (2021)
  • TISM’s final recordings

His last dated Discogs credits are 2016–2017, the year of his death. Posthumous compilations carrying his credits include the 2023 The Honeymoon Is Over Cruel Sea reissue and the 2026 Go-Betweens anthology G Stands For Go-Betweens.

Connections

  • Nick Cave — Primary creative partner across four decades; Cohen engineered/produced every major Bad Seeds album from Your Funeral… My Trial (1986) to No More Shall We Part (2001).
  • Mick Harvey — Recurring collaborator through Birthday Party, Bad Seeds, and Harvey’s solo career.
  • The Birthday Party / The Boys Next Door — The origin point; Cohen was their house engineer and co-produced their self-titled 1980 LP.
  • Rowland S. Howard — Credited on Junkyard (Birthday Party); Howard’s post-BP career intersects with Cohen.
  • Anita Lane — Produced Dirty Pearl (1993, Mute); Lane was a recurring Cave-circle presence.
  • Crime & The City Solution — Second most documented Melbourne-to-Berlin post-punk group in his catalogue.
  • The Go-Betweens — Co-produced debut album Send Me A Lullaby (1982); a rare example of Cohen outside the post-punk/Cave orbit.
  • The Cruel Sea — ARIA-winning partnership; The Honeymoon Is Over (1993) was a commercial and critical peak.
  • Tex Perkins — Vocalist of The Cruel Sea; also of The Beasts Of Bourbon, both Cohen clients.
  • Blackeyed Susans — 6 shared releases; mixed Mouth To Mouth (1995) and tracks on Reveal Yourself (2009).
  • Phil Kakulas / Blackeyed Susans — Kakulas produced Mouth To Mouth; Cohen the mixer.
  • Victor Van Vugt — Recurring co-engineer, particularly on late Bad Seeds and Blackeyed Susans sessions.
  • Flood — Co-engineered/produced Your Funeral… My Trial (1986) and Tender Prey (1988) with Cohen.
  • Spencer P. Jones — Australian underground; Cohen worked with Jones through Spooky Records into the 2010s.
  • Kim Salmon And The Surrealists — Engineer/producer on 1995 album; Kim Salmon was a central Melbourne figure.
  • Dave Graney & The Coral Snakes — Mixed multiple releases; Dave Graney from The Moodists, another Melbourne underground band.
  • Kylie Minogue — Credited on Where The Wild Roses Grow (1995) and throughout the Murder Ballads / Best Of era.
