Ben Coleman

Aug 09, 20267 min read

Ben Coleman

UK violinist. Discogs artist ID: 309718.

Overview

Ben Coleman is a UK electric violinist, best known as the original third member of No-Man, the art-pop group formed in the mid-to-late 1980s with Tim Bowness (vocals) and Steven Wilson (instruments/production). Coleman was a core presence on every No-Man release from the debut single (billed as “No Man Is An Island Except The Isle Of Man”) through the end of the One Little Indian period in 1993–94, providing the distinctive violin textures that defined the group’s early sound. He also contributed co-writing credits on several tracks.

His departure coincided with the band’s move toward the more expansive, band-backed sound of Flowermouth (1994), on which he does not appear.

Discogs credits him variously as “B. Coleman”, “Ben Colman”, and “Coleman”.

No-Man Tenure (1988–1993)

Formation and Early Singles

The earliest Discogs documentation of Coleman with No-Man is the 1989 single “The Girl From Missouri” (Plastic Head Records Limited), credited to “No Man Is An Island” — the group’s original name. The lineup on the credits: Wilson (guitar, keyboards, fretless bass), Coleman (violin), Bowness (vocals), plus Stuart Blagden on guitar.

A companion archival release, Speak: 1988–89 (Hidden Art Creations, 1993), documents the pre-OLI era material with the same three-piece core (Coleman violin, Wilson bass/guitar/keyboards, Bowness vocals/guitar) and confirms Coleman’s involvement from the group’s formation.

The Colours EP (Probe Plus, 1990) is the first release with full production credits: Wilson plays electric guitar, keyboards, and drum programming; Coleman contributes violin across the EP. Notably the cover includes a Donovan cover (“Colours”).

Loveblows & Lovecries is the high-water mark of Coleman’s involvement: he contributes violin throughout and co-wrote tracks 1 and 10 (the only two tracks on the album not co-written by Bowness and Wilson). This is his sole album-level writing credit in the No-Man catalog.

Final Credit and Departure

Coleman receives a Written-By credit on track 6 of Taking It Like A Man (550 Music / Epic / One Little Indian, 1994), a US-market compilation, confirming his compositions remained in the catalog even after his departure from the active lineup.

He does not appear on Flowermouth (One Little Indian, 1994), the group’s second full-length, which marks the transition to a more conventional band configuration. The violin role on later No-Man material was subsequently filled by Natalie Box (credited on Radio Sessions 1992–96, tracks 15–16).

Later Session Work

Coleman continued working as a session violinist after leaving No-Man’s core lineup:

PulseXPulse (1998)

Played violin on tracks 1 and 6 of PulseXPulse by Pulse (43) (Medium Productions Limited), a project produced and arranged by Steve Jansen (Japan) and Yukihiro Takahashi (Yellow Magic Orchestra). Other contributors included Theo Travis (flute, saxophone) and Zoe Niblett. A notable Japanese connection in the post-No-Man period.

No-Man Radio Sessions 1992–96 (Hidden Art, 1998)

Credited on tracks 1–10 of this BBC radio sessions compilation — the first ten tracks, covering the early-to-mid 1990s sessions. Tracks 15–16 (the 1996 sessions) feature Natalie Box on violin instead, which pinpoints approximately when the changeover occurred.

The Invisible Circus Soundtrack (2001)

Played electric violin on the original motion picture soundtrack composed by Nick Laird-Clowes (Chapter III Records, 2001). The orchestra was the London Metropolitan Orchestra; other soloists included Kate St. John.

Porcupine Tree — Fear of a Blank Planet (2007)

Contributed electric violin to one track (D3) on Porcupine Tree’s Fear of a Blank Planet (Tonefloat/Atlantic, 2007), produced by Steven Wilson. A reunion of sorts with his former No-Man collaborator; other guest players on that album included Robert Fripp and Alex Lifeson.

Porcupine Tree — Nil Recurring (2015 reissue)

Guest electric violin on track 4 of the expanded edition of Nil Recurring (Kscope, 2015), again via the Wilson connection.

Tim Bowness — Butterfly Mind (2022)

Contributed violin to tracks B3, B4, B5 (CD-9, 10, 11) of Bowness’s solo album Butterfly Mind (Inside Out Music / Sony, 2022), reconnecting with both Bowness and Wilson (who mixed and mastered the album). Other contributors included Ian Anderson (Jethro Tull), Peter Hammill, and Nick Beggs.

No-Man — Swagger (2024)

Credited as “Performer [No-Man Is]” on Swagger (Lost Not Lost Volume One: 1989/1990) (Burning Shed, 2024), an archival release of early No-Man material from 1989–90. The release explicitly documents the three-piece lineup of Coleman, Wilson, and Bowness as “No-Man Is” — distinguishing the original trio from the later configurations.

Pre-No-Man Session Work

Epic Soundtracks & Jowe Head — “Rain, Rain, Rain” (Rough Trade, 1982)

The earliest Discogs credit for Coleman: violin on side A of this single by Epic Soundtracks and Jowe Head (both ex-Swell Maps). Coleman is credited as “Ben” — a bare first name only. This places him in the post-punk/art-rock London milieu at least six years before the No-Man formation.

This Virgin Deep — “Who Do You Love?” (Purple Plum, 1985)

Electric violin on this single by This Virgin Deep, produced by John Milner. A mid-1980s session credit that bridges the Rough Trade period and the No-Man formation.
