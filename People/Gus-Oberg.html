Gus Oberg

American record producer and engineer, based in New York. Also credited as Erik Gus Oberg and Gus ‘Cobra’ Oberg. Discogs artist ID: 654582.

Overview

Gus Oberg built his career through New York’s Loho Studios in the mid-2000s, starting as an assistant engineer on sessions for Moby, Albert Hammond Jr., and the Nublu jazz scene before becoming the de facto in-house producer/engineer for The Strokes from Angles (2011) onward. He produced or co-produced every Strokes record from Angles through the Future Present Past EP (2016), and handled pre-production demo engineering for The New Abnormal (2020). In parallel he has maintained a long-running production partnership with Albert Hammond Jr. spanning five releases across 2006–2023. Outside those two anchors he has produced records for Scottish indie band The View, NYC garage-pop act Public Access T.V. (under the alias Unicorn Parade), and Australian pop artist Natalie Imbruglia, among others.

Career by Era

Early Career — Loho Studios Assistant (2004–2006)

Oberg’s first Discogs-visible credits place him at Loho Studios in New York, working as an assistant engineer. Key early sessions:

  • Moby — Hotel (Mute, 2005): Assistant engineer at Loho Studios alongside head engineer Joe Hogan. His introduction to a major-label studio environment.
  • Albert Hammond Jr. — 101 (Rough Trade, 2006): Engineer on Hammond’s debut EP. This begins what becomes a decade-plus collaboration.
  • Albert Hammond Jr. — Yours To Keep (Rough Trade, 2006): Engineer on Hammond’s first full-length, which also features Sean Lennon on bass/piano.
  • Nublu Orchestra Conducted By Butch Morris (Nublu Records, 2006): Recording engineer on this large-ensemble jazz session, documenting Butch Morris’s conduction work with the Nublu Orchestra — a notable detour into the NYC avant-jazz scene.
  • Wonder Wheel — Lyrics By Woody Guthrie (Jewish Music Group, 2006): Engineer at Loho Studios.

Other 2004–2006 engineering credits include sessions for Howler Records artists (including a Tunnel Vision EP) and a recording credit on a Nettwerk America release (Funnel Cloud, 2006).

Albert Hammond Jr. Partnership and Growing Production Role (2006–2010)

Oberg’s relationship with Hammond deepened through his three solo albums on Rough Trade. By the second album he was co-producing:

  • Albert Hammond Jr. — GfC (Rough Trade, 2008): Co-producer alongside Matt Romano.
  • Albert Hammond Jr. — ¿Cómo Te Llama? (Rough Trade, 2008): Co-producer, engineer, and mixer, again alongside Matt Romano. Session also featured Sean Lennon on piano.

A notable 2010 credit: Julian Casablancas — “11th Dimension” (Live From Electric Lady Studios For WRXP) (Cult Records/RCA, 2010) — recorded by Oberg, signaling his growing involvement in the Strokes/Casablancas orbit ahead of Angles.

The Strokes: Primary Producer (2011–2016)

Oberg became the lead producer on three Strokes studio projects:

Singles / EPs (2011–2013)

  • “Under Cover Of Darkness” (RCA, 2011): Producer, engineer, mixer
  • “Taken For A Fool” (RCA, 2011): Producer, engineer, recorded by, mixer
  • “All The Time” (RCA, 2013): Producer, engineer, mixer

Future Present Past EP (Cult Records, 2016)

Producer. The band’s last release before a four-year gap. Dave Kutch mastered.

The New Abnormal (Cult Records/RCA, 2020)

Oberg’s role shifted here — credited for pre-production demo recordings and engineering only. Rick Rubin produced the final album; Jason Lader engineered and mixed.

Other Notable Productions (2013–2021)

  • Walking Shapes — “Taka Come On” (NoShame, 2014): Producer and mixer, co-produced with Stephen Mowat.
  • Public Access T.V. — Never Enough (Cinematic Music Group, 2016): Produced and recorded two tracks at Electric Lady Studios, credited as executive producer under the alias Unicorn Parade. Sessions mixed by Alan Moulder (for some tracks).
  • Memory Den — Say Goodbye to Memory Den (Hardenough, 2017): Producer.
  • Annelies Monseré — With You Tonight (Kissability / Dtf Records, 2016–17): Mixer.
  • Natalie Imbruglia — Firebird (BMG, 2021): Producer on track A5, credited as “Erik Gus Oberg.” Albert Hammond Jr. co-produced the same track.
