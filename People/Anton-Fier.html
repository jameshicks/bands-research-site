Anton Fier

Drummer, composer, producer, bandleader. Discogs ID: 58286. Real name: Anton John Fier III. Born June 20, 1956, Cleveland, Ohio; died September 14, 2022, Zürich, Switzerland (aged 66).

One of the central rhythmic figures of the 1980s NYC downtown scene. Founded and led The Golden Palominos (1981–1996), a rotating-cast experimental rock project that functioned as a collision point between no wave, jazz, funk, and post-punk. Before that: The Feelies, The Lounge Lizards, and the downtown avant-garde circuit surrounding Bill Laswell, John Zorn, and Arto Lindsay. As a session drummer he played on records by Mick Jagger, Swans, Bob Mould, and dozens of others. From the late 1980s onward he increasingly moved into production.

Cleveland Years (c. 1973–1978)

Fier grew up in Cleveland, Ohio, where he emerged in the mid-1970s proto-punk and avant-rock underground. Early bands included:

  • Electric Eels (Discogs group ID: 252896) — Cleveland proto-punk, one of the most volatile acts of the era, credited as a forerunner of no wave
  • Mr. Stress Blues Band (Discogs group ID: 5064764) — Cleveland blues/rock institution led by Bill “Mr. Stress” Miller; held bi-weekly residencies at the Brick Cottage (1972–79) and Euclid Tavern (1979–late 1990s). The 2016 archival LP Live At The Brick Cottage 1972-1973 (Smog Veil SV121) documents the 1972–73 lineup; Fier’s own tenure is not captured on the released recordings but is documented in the band’s Discogs member list alongside Peter Laughner and Chrissie Hynde
  • The Styrenes (Discogs group ID: 529193) — Cleveland new wave; their 1977 single “I Saw You” (Mustard (2)) is his earliest Discogs credit

The Cleveland connection also included Peter Laughner and the broader scene around Rocket From the Tombs / Pere Ubu; Fier would later formally join Pere Ubu (Discogs group ID: 83071), appearing on their Song Of The Bailing Man (Rough Trade, 1981) and documented in the 2023 archive box Architecture Of Language 1979–1982 (Fire Records).

New York: Arrival and First Sessions (1978–1982)

Fier relocated to New York City and got a job at the SoHo Music Gallery record store — a key hub for the downtown scene. His first NYC move was joining The Feelies in 1978 after answering a Village Voice ad. He appears on their debut LP Crazy Rhythms (Stiff America, 1980) credited under the pseudonym Andy Fisher (drums and percussion); Glenn Mercer and Bill Million produced.

He then passed through The Pedestrians — a short-lived group in the orbit of Steve Piccolo and the Lounge Lizards circle — before joining The Lounge Lizards, playing on their self-titled debut (Editions EG, 1981). The Lounge Lizards were led by John Lurie and Evan Lurie, with Arto Lindsay on guitar and Steve Piccolo on bass; Fier co-wrote “Conquest of Rar” with John and Evan Lurie on that record.

He also appeared on Material’s Memory Serves (Elektra Musician, 1982) — the flagship free-funk record produced by Bill Laswell and Michael Beinhorn — alongside Fred Frith, Sonny Sharrock, Henry Threadgill, and Fred Maher. Fier drums on the final track, “For A Few Dollars More.”

The Golden Palominos (1983–1996)

The Golden Palominos were Fier’s primary vehicle for over a decade: he founded, produced, and led every iteration from the Celluloid debut through the Restless Records years. The lineup rotated with the project’s stylistic shifts.

The Golden Palominos (1983, Celluloid CELC 5002)

The debut. Essentially a no-wave supergroup with a funk rhythm section. Produced by Fier, Bill Laswell, and Arto Lindsay; engineered and mixed by Martin Bisi.

x_x and the Cleveland Singles (1979–80)

Fier appears as drummer (credited “Tony Fier”) on both original x_x singles on Drome Records: A / You’re Full Of Shit (1979, Drome DR-2) and No Nonsense / Approaching The Minimal With Spray Guns (1980, Drome DR8). Both were produced by John Morton (the 1979 single also with John Thompson); other performers: Andrew Klimek and James Ellis. He is also credited on the 2014 archival LP X Sticky Fingers X (Full Contact Records / Ektro). The 1980 single was reissued in 2020 via Soul Jazz Records as part of the Punk 45: Approaching The Minimal With Spray Guns box. See x x for full discography.

Swans: White Light From The Mouth Of Infinity (1991, Young God Records YGCD 3)

A significant guest credit on Michael Gira’s most orchestral Swans record. Fier appears alongside Jarboe, Nicky Skopelitis, Hahn Rowe, Kristof Hahn, Norman Westberg, and Vinnie Signorelli; J.G. Thirlwell co-produced and added drum programming on one track.

Cosmetic — So Tranquilizin’ (1985, Gramavision)

Fier provided DMX drum programming on this Jamaaladeen Tacuma–led session — a strange intersection of the downtown network — which also featured Adele Bertei and Maripol on backing vocals, Ornette Coleman on saxophone, and John Robie on Emulator.
