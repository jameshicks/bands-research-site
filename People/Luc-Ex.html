Luc Ex

Discogs ID: 254021 (as Luc Ex) Also credited as: Luc Klaasen (Discogs ID: 5644901) Real name: Luc Klaasen Born: 1958, De Bilt, Netherlands Website: http://www.lucex.nl

Dutch bassist and multi-instrumentalist. Best known as the bassist of The Ex from approximately 1985 to 2005 — one of the longest-serving members during the band’s most internationally prominent period. Since leaving The Ex, he has continued to lead and participate in a wide range of experimental, jazz, and improvised music projects, operating from Amsterdam.

Career Overview

Early work and pre-Ex activity

Luc Ex’s earliest documented Discogs credit is a 1983 organ appearance on Miljoenen Magen by Verz Ed — a Amsterdam-scene record produced by Dolf Planteijdt and featuring several musicians who would become longtime associates: Terrie Hessels (later Terrie Ex), Ferry Heijne, and Sabien Witteman. This predates his joining The Ex and establishes his roots in the loose Amsterdam collective around the Gramschap / MMM Records network.

The Ex: 1985–c.2005

Luc Ex joined The Ex around 1985, appearing on the Pokkeherrie LP (bass, guitar). He remained a primary member for approximately two decades, spanning the band’s most documented and internationally released period. He is credited as bassist (and frequently on additional instruments) across all major releases from 1936, The Spanish Revolution (1986) through Dizzy Spells (2001) and Beautiful Frenzy (2004), giving him 82 shared primary credits with the band on Discogs. His last consistently credited primary-release appearances are around 2004–05; the Singles Period compilation (2005, Touch And Go) was his final Ex-credited item.

On The Ex records, his role frequently exceeds bass alone: Scrabbling At The Lock (1991) credits him with “guitar, drums, bass, accordion, voice” — typical of the band’s collective, interchangeable approach to instrumentation. He also played steel drums on And The Weathermen Shrug Their Shoulders (1993, with Tom Cora).

Post-Ex projects: 2001 onwards

Even while still with The Ex, Luc Ex developed a network of parallel projects. After departing, these became his main activity:

4Walls (Discogs ID: 1419741) — a quartet with Phil Minton (voice), Michael Vatcher (drums), and Veryan Weston (piano). Released And The World Ain’t Square (2001, Red Note) and Which Side Are You On (2004, Orkhêstra). Luc Ex produced both albums and co-mixed And The World Ain’t Square with Zlaya Hadzich. The group also released a DVD A Film By Jerome de Missolz (2006, La Huit).

Roof (Discogs ID: 366105) — an earlier quartet with Tom Cora (cello), Michael Vatcher (drums), and Phil Minton (vocals). Released The Untraceable Cigar (1996, Red Note / Manifatture Criminali) and Trace (1999, Red Note). Produced by Dolf Planteijdt.

Regenorchester XII (Discogs ID: 1733327) — an international chamber/improv ensemble. Luc Ex is bassist, co-composer, and co-producer. Released Town Down (2008, Red Note), featuring Franz Hautzinger (quartertone trumpet), Fennesz (guitar/computer), Otomo Yoshihide (turntables/guitar), and Tony Buck (drums). The follow-up Relics (2020, Trost Records) reunites the core lineup. On Town Down Luc Ex is credited as co-producer (with Hautzinger), mixer, and bassist.

Luc Ex’ Assemblée (Discogs ID: 13066389) — a quartet with Ab Baars (tenor saxophone, shakuhachi), Ingrid Laubrock (tenor and soprano saxophone), and Hamid Drake (drums). Released Assemblée (2014, Red Note). Luc Ex plays acoustic bass and is credited as composer/writer.

Summit Quartet (Discogs ID: 5987468) — with Ken Vandermark (tenor/baritone saxophone), Hamid Drake (drums), and Mats Gustafsson (baritone saxophone). Released Live in Sant’Anna Arresi – 2016 (2017, self-released/Ken Vandermark).

Naked Wolf (Discogs ID: 4170850) — a Brussels-based quintet with Felicity Provan (trumpet), Yedo Gibson (reeds), Mikael Szafirowski (guitar), and Gerri Jäger (drums). Released Naked Wolf (2014, El Negocito Records).

Madame Luckerniddle (Discogs ID: 3311085) — a quartet with Tom Cora, Zeena Parkins (harp/electronics), and Michael Vatcher. Released Madame Luckerniddle (2015, Fédération Hiéro Colmar). A posthumous release: Tom Cora died in 1998, making this a document of archival recordings.

Sol6 (Discogs ID: 2229095) — released Sol6 (2010, Red Note).

Speeq (Discogs ID: 1828777) — released Or (2008, Red Note).

Bouge (Discogs ID: 754244) — released Bouge (2013, Vand’Oeuvre).

Brique (2) (Discogs ID: 14891848) — quartet with Eve Risser, Bianca Iannuzzi, Francesco Pastacaldi. Released La Danse Du Béton (2024, Umlaut Records).

Blik (5) (Discogs ID: 10858762) — trio with Annelie Koning and Tristan Renfrow. Released Prepare Move Finish (2022, self-released).

De Ridders Van Den Apocalyps (Discogs ID: 2869197) — released De Man Met De Grasparkiet (2004, Pure Note).

RKeT (Discogs ID: 4487980) — released RKeT (2014, The Korn).

Solo work

Under his own name, Luc Ex released Music Of Inevitable Sounds (2020, Trost Records) — a chamber ensemble record featuring acoustic bass alongside cellists Nina Hitz and Saartje van Camp, pianist Veryan Weston, pianist Nora Mulder, percussionist Pepe Garcia, reeds player Yedo Gibson, trombonists Joost Buis and Vladislav Psaruk, and Renato Ferreira (tenor saxophone/bass). Mixed and recorded by Mika Szafrowski; mastered by Martin Siewert.

Ex Orkest

Luc Ex was also a member of Ex Orkest (Discogs ID: 432162), the large Amsterdam ensemble spun out from The Ex’s extended circle. The Orkest released Een Rondje Holland (2001, Ex Records). Members include Andy Moor (2), Han Buhrs, Jaap Blonk, Ab Baars, Joost Buis, Wolter Wierbos, Michael Moore (4), Roy Paci, Felicity Provan, Katherina Bornefeld, and others.
