Dennis Ferrer

May 31, 20269 min read

Dennis Ferrer

Real name: Dennis Joseph Ferrer Born/based: Union City, New Jersey (raised in New York City) Discogs artist ID: 3694 Active: 1993–present Aliases: Aurasfere (ID 86741), Jax (ID 42290), Son Of Raw (ID 463014), The Lost Tribes Of Ibadan (ID 120016), U.C. Heights (ID 213141)

House music producer and DJ rooted in the New York/New Jersey underground. Co-founded label Sfere with Kerri Chandler in 1998. Runs Objektivity (originally a Sfere sub-label, later tied to Nervous Records). Mentored The Martinez Brothers. One of the defining voices of New York deep house through the 2000s, with international releases on Defected, BPM King Street Sounds, and Strictly Rhythm.

Biography

Early Career — Engineering and Production (1993–1997)

Ferrer’s production career began in New York in 1993 as an engineer and co-producer on releases for C & S Records, including “Accelerator (Can You Feel It)” and the “Instant Enlightenment” series — early deep-trance house records that placed him at the city’s underground house margins. He also co-produced “Stormwatch” (1994) for Synewave and New Electronica, and produced “The Greenhouse Effect” (1994) for EXperimental. Through 1994–1996 he remained primarily a behind-the-scenes figure, contributing engineering and production rather than fronting primary releases.

His first primary-credited release as a solo artist was “I Need Your Love” (1998, Hot ‘N’ Spycy).

Sfere and the NYC Deep House Network (1998–2001)

In 1998 Ferrer co-founded Sfere with Kerri Chandler, one of the central labels in the New Jersey/New York deep house scene. The label’s debut album First Steps (1999, Sfere SF-005) — a joint release credited to Kerri Chandler and Dennis Ferrer — stands as one of the formative documents of the Sfere aesthetic: warm, percussion-forward deep house with roots in Chicago and New Jersey garage. Tracks include “Your Love,” “Waterfall,” “Bandaleh!,” and “Walkin’ Into The Sunshine.”

Around this time Ferrer was also working within the orbit of Ibadan (the label run by Jerome Sydenham), contributing edits and arrangements. His collaboration with Sydenham led to the formal duo Jerome Sydenham & Dennis Ferrer (Discogs ID 3806), which has released extensively under numerous name variations (Ferrer & Sydenham, Sydenham & Ferrer Inc., etc.).

Ferrer’s alias The Lost Tribes Of Ibadan straddles both camps — the name references Ibadan Records and appears as a primary credit on Sfere releases including The Lost Tribes Of Ibadan 3 (2001, Sfere).

Under the Dennis Ferrer name he began releasing the Soul Collectives series on Large Records — Vol. 1 (2000), Vol. 2 (…Going Back To My Rootz) (2001), Vol. 3 (2002) — which featured live saxophone from Talib Kibwe (T.K. Blue), trumpet from Fabio Morgera, and vocals from Christopher McCray. This series cemented his reputation for jazz-inflected deep house with a live-instrument sensibility.

Peak Output and International Breakthrough (2002–2008)

From 2002 onward Ferrer expanded significantly. Key releases in this period:

  • “Reach 4 Freedom” (2002, Nite Grooves) — keyboards, drum programming
  • “Funu” (2002, Basic Recordings, France) — co-written with Congolese bassist Dominic Kanza
  • “Hit It Off / Jero” (2004, Defected) — Defected debut, 5 pressings
  • Remix of Blaze’s “Most Precious Love” (2004, Defected)
  • “Son Of Raw” (2005, Ibadan, irc070) — mixed by Jerome Sydenham; the alias name is derived from this record’s title
  • “Grateful” (2005, Sfere) — co-written, produced, keyboards

The 2006 year was his breakthrough:

  • “Church Lady” (2006, Defected, DFTD143DJ) — 12 pressings; his most widely distributed single to that point; features gospel choir 3rd Ministry Of Faith and vocal credits to Danil Wright
  • “The Cube” (2006, Objektivity, OB001) — co-produced and co-written with Karizma (credited as Karizma Ltd. / C. Clayton); debut release of the Objektivity label
  • The World As I See It (Vol. 01) (2006, Defected) — 13 pressings; DJ mix compilation
  • Soul Heaven (2006, Soul Heaven Records) — two-LP compilation set co-compiled with Kerri Chandler

“Touched The Sky” (2007, Defected/Objektivity/BPM King Street/Sfere, DFTD156R) — feat. vocalist Mia Tuttavilla, with Yass and Sébastian Grand remixes — achieved 11 pressings across four labels and marked the full integration of his Objektivity catalog into the Defected distribution network. The B-side features percussion from The Martinez Brothers (Chris and Steve Jr. Martinez).

In The House (2007, ITH Records) was a UK-pressed DJ mix/compilation that broadened his profile on the European club circuit. He also contributed to the Telepopmusik “Love Can Damage Your Health” remix package (Objektivity OBJ003).

Objektivity: Running the Label (2006–present)

Objektivity (ID 73775) launched with “The Cube” (OB001) and quickly became a platform not just for Ferrer’s own productions but for curated new artists. Early releases show the full range of his A&R instincts:

  • OBJ002 — My Rendition (2007) by The Martinez Brothers — the duo’s debut release; Ferrer mentored Chris and Steve Martinez from their teenage years in the Bronx
  • OBJ003 — Telepopmusik remix package
  • OBJ004 — Ibadan Revisited 1 (2007, Dennis Ferrer) / Son Of Raw (Loco Dice Remix); remixed by Loco Dice
  • OBJ005 — I Can’t Go Under (2007) feat. Malena Pérez
  • OBJ006 — Noiz (2007) by Rasu / United People Of Zion
  • OBJ007 — A Black Man In Space (2007) by Son Of Raw alias
  • OBJ008 — Astral (2008) by The Regisford-Harris Project
  • OBJ009 — Headphone Silence (2008) by Ane Brun

The label’s address: 304 Hudson St., #503, New York NY 10013. It is a sub-label of Sfere Recordings Inc. (and in turn has ties to Nervous Records, parent ID 212 in Discogs).

”Hey Hey” and Continued Activity (2009–2016)

“Hey Hey” (2009, Objektivity OBJ 014) was Ferrer’s biggest commercial moment. The record features uncredited vocals by Shingai Shoniwa (of The Noisettes), co-written with Vivien Goldman (music journalist/musician) and Dominique Keegan. It generated at least 30 pressings across multiple formats and territories, was remixed by Maya Jane Coles and Hardrive (Louie Vega), and remains his most internationally recognized track.

Other notable late-period releases:

  • Sinfonia Della Notte (2009, Strictly Rhythm) — minimal / deep house digital single
  • The Red Room (2010, Objektivity OBJ016) — remixed by Kyle Hall and The Martinez Brothers with Jerome Sydenham
  • No Difference (2011, Objektivity OBJ 019) — co-credited with André Hommen
  • Mind Ur Step / Mind Ur Dub (2013, Objektivity OBJ 024) feat. vocalist Janelle Kroll
  • Maniac 3000 (2016, Objektivity OBJ045D) — AIFF single; later-period deep house

He also issued retrospective compilations: My World As They Remixed It (2008, BPM King Street Sounds) and House Legends (2015, BPM King Street Sounds).
