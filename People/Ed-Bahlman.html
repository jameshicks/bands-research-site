Ed Bahlman

Label founder, record store operator, producer. Founder of 99 Records. Discogs ID: 54478.

Overview

Ed Bahlman founded 99 Records and the underground record store of the same name at 99 MacDougal Street, Greenwich Village, New York. He co-ran both with Gina Franklyn, whose fashion boutique occupied the same space — Bahlman’s corner selling rare British indie imports (via Rough Trade connections) eventually became the store. The label was launched at Glenn Branca’s urging around 1980–81.

99 Records became the home of ESG and Liquid Liquid — the two central acts — and also issued early releases by Bush Tetras, the first Glenn Branca LP, and a handful of other downtown New York artists. Bahlman produced every Liquid Liquid record and the core ESG catalog, and handled or co-handled mix duties. He also co-produced work by Glenn Branca Ensemble and Y Pants, and worked with Swiss artist Stephan Eicher on two Off Course Records releases (1983, 1988).

The label collapsed around 1985–86 after the Sugar Hill Records lawsuit over Grandmaster Flash’s “White Lines (Don’t Don’t Do It)” (which sampled Liquid Liquid’s “Cavern”) drained the store’s finances. After the collapse, Bahlman withdrew entirely from the music industry. He has refused all interviews and has withheld the Liquid Liquid and ESG master tapes, making fully authorised reissues impossible.

Background before 99 Records: Not documented in Discogs. No credits appear before 1980.

Notes (web) — Off Course Records / Stephan Eicher connection

Off Course Records context: Off Course Records was a Swiss independent label run by Urs Steiger. It was active in the Swiss post-punk and new wave scene from around 1980 onward, and in 1980 merged with Another Swiss Label to issue the Swiss Wave — The Album compilation (which included Grauzone, the band Eicher co-founded with his brother). That compilation is considered the commercial launch of Eicher’s career in France. Off Course’s first single was Eicher’s Noise Boys project, pressed in December 1980 after Steiger attended one of Eicher’s happenings at the F+F art school in Zurich. (bornbadrecords.net; highdive.de snippet)

Recording location: Les Chansons Bleues was recorded at Studio Sunrise (Kirchberg, Switzerland) and Powerplay Studios (Maur, near Zurich) — both Swiss facilities. Powerplay opened 1980–83. (powerplaystudios.ch) This means the sessions happened in Switzerland, not in New York: Bahlman traveled to Switzerland, or the project was organized around the Swiss studio infrastructure.

How the connection came about — not documented: No published source (English or French Wikipedia, major press features on 99 Records, Born Bad Records liner notes, or the Vinyl Factory/RBMA long-reads) explains the mechanism by which Bahlman was brought in as producer. The Vinyl Factory article notes only that Bahlman “dropped out of sight” after 99 Records closed in early 1986 and refused all interviews. (thevinylfactory.com) Notably the Les Chansons Bleues sessions are from 1983, while 99 Records was still active — so this was parallel work, not post-collapse activity.

Plausible pathway (no source): 99 Records had supply-chain ties to Rough Trade (Bahlman stocked rare British indie imports via Rough Trade before launching the label). Rough Trade operated a European distribution network that connected Swiss new wave labels to British and American scenes. Off Course Records was distributed in part through this network. A connection brokered through Rough Trade or the wider European post-punk distribution circuit is consistent with what is known, but no source states it. The 1988 compilation credit shows Bahlman remained involved with the Eicher catalog five years after 99 Records folded.

Other Swiss credits: No other Swiss artist or label credits appear in Discogs or in any source checked. The Eicher / Off Course work appears to be the sole Swiss thread.

Answer to Research Questions

RQ1 — Background before 99 Records: Discogs has no credits predating 1980. The store/label origin is documented (British indie imports corner in Gina Franklyn’s boutique, Rough Trade connections, Glenn Branca’s encouragement), but his biography before that is absent from Discogs entirely. Would require web/archival research.

RQ2 — What happened after 1985/86?: Discogs confirms he produced Stephan Eicher material through 1988 on Off Course Records (Switzerland), which is later than commonly cited. After 1988 there are no new production credits — only archival reissue appearances. The 2021 Bush Tetras comp is the most recent Discogs entry, but that is purely a historical producer credit. Consistent with the “withdrew from the music industry” narrative; the Off Course work may have overlapped with or slightly outlasted the 99 Records collapse.

RQ3 — Master tape situation: Not documented in Discogs. The tape-withholding is attested in vault articles on Liquid Liquid, ESG, and 99 Records (sourced from interviews and press), but Discogs credits carry no notation about it.
