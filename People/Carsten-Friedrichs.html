Overview

Hamburg-based German singer, songwriter, and guitarist born in 1971, Carsten Friedrichs has been active in the city’s indie and punk scene since the late 1980s. He co-founded Superpunk in 1996, remaining with the band until its 2012 split, before co-founding Die Liga Der Gewöhnlichen Gentlemen with fellow Superpunk member Tim Jürgens the same year. His catalogue spans Indie Rock, Punk, Mod, and New Wave across labels including Tapete Records (27 works, 2008–2025) and Bureau B (12 works, 2012–2021); since 2007 he has also been employed at Tapete, the label run by close collaborator Gunther Buskies. Beyond his band work, Friedrichs is a prolific songwriter who writes for other artists, notably Andreas Dorau.

Notes (web)

Superpunk’s 2012 split

The dissolution was announced by Tapete Records on 10 February 2012, alongside a final seven-date club tour; the band’s last festival show was 25 August 2012 (Melt! / BootBooHook) and the final live performance was 10 September 2012 in Vienna. (de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpunk, as of 2026-10-16)

Friedrichs consistently described the split as a gradual loss of momentum rather than any dramatic external event or internal conflict. Key quotes (originally in German):

  • “Die Luft ist raus” (“the air has gone out”) — his most-repeated formulation. (taz.de, February 2012; soundsandbooks.com)
  • “die Abstände zwischen den Proben wurden länger, so dass wir irgendwann beschlossen haben, es zu lassen” (“the gaps between rehearsals grew longer, so at some point we decided to let it be”). (taz.de)
  • “Wir haben uns wie in einer Beziehung ganz stinklangweilig auseinandergelebt. Es war kein Zug mehr drin, wir haben kaum noch geprobt” (“We drifted apart in a relationship-like way, totally unspectacularly. There was no momentum left, we barely rehearsed anymore”). (jungle.world, May 2012)
  • “wenn man merkt, dass der Schwung schwindet, ist es besser, die Notbremse zu ziehen” (“when you notice the momentum slowing, it’s better to pull the emergency brake”). (ox-fanzine.de)

On the band’s modest commercial fortunes, Friedrichs offered only wry deflection: “Der Konsument hat eben versagt” (“the consumer simply failed”) — he said he could just as easily complain about the rain. (taz.de)

Notably, he acknowledged mixed feelings about the Superpunk period: “Da verbinde ich nicht nur Positives” (“I don’t associate only positives with it”), contrasting it with Die Liga, of which he said “mit der Liga verbinde ich bis auf unseren anhaltenden Misserfolg nur Positives” (“with Die Liga, aside from our ongoing commercial failure, I associate only positives”). (soundsandbooks.com)

Die Liga der gewöhnlichen Gentlemen — continuation or new direction?

Die Liga Der Gewöhnlichen Gentlemen was founded in 2012 immediately after the split, carrying over Friedrichs and Tim Jürgens (bass) from Superpunk. Initial members also included Gunther Buskies (keyboards), Philip Morton Andernach (guitar/saxophone), and Zwanie Jonson (drums, later replaced by Heiko Franz); sources also mention former Blumfeld member Andre Rattay as an early participant. (freitag.de; de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Liga_der_gewöhnlichen_Gentlemen, as of 2026-10-16; ox-fanzine.de)

The Jungle World piece (May 2012) describes the new band as forming while Superpunk’s farewell was still underway; Friedrichs cautioned against premature enthusiasm, comparing celebrating the new project to “flirting at a funeral.” (jungle.world)

Friedrichs himself characterised the relationship between the two bands as continuity of songwriting identity within a genuinely new ensemble. When asked directly whether Die Liga was “the old Superpunk thing,” he confirmed: “Ja, klar, die Texte sind von mir, also schreibe ich die so. Aber der Schlagzeuger ist völlig anders, unser Gitarrist spielt auch Saxofon — das ist schon was ganz anderes. Aber klar — letztendlich ist der Unterschied nicht so groß” (“Yes, sure, the lyrics are from me, so I write them that way. But the drummer is completely different, our guitarist also plays saxophone — that’s something quite different. But clearly — ultimately the difference isn’t that big”). (entertaimnet.wordpress.com)

Stylistically, Die Liga self-describes as blending “Northern Soul, C86-Indiepop und Mod-Powerpop”, with Friedrichs calling Northern Soul “die beste Musik der Welt” and valuing C86 as “der wahre Punkrock” because “jeder und jede konnte mitmachen”. The new band expanded arrangements by using real string and brass players rather than synthesizers. (ox-fanzine.de) The Freitag essay characterises Die Liga as Superpunk’s “official follow-up,” noting its Northern Soul orientation was already central to Superpunk’s identity. (freitag.de)

In sum: the split was a quiet, undramatic wind-down after 16 years of diminishing rehearsal energy; Die Liga was neither a sharp break nor a simple continuation but a reconstituted vehicle for the same songwriter with a refreshed and expanded lineup and a more explicitly Northern Soul / Mod-Powerpop framing.
