Jesse Rae

Scottish singer-songwriter (born William Philip Jesse Rae, 1951, St Boswells, Scottish Borders). Discogs artist ID: 47736. Realname: William Philip Jesse Rae.

Overview

Jesse Rae is one of the more singular figures in 1980s British funk — a Scotsman who planted a deep-rooted tartan identity (kilts, claymores, Scots-language lyrics, nationalist politics) directly into an American P.Funk lineage. His work sits at the intersection of George Clinton’s extended family, Roger Troutman and Zapp, and the British post-punk dancefloor, and he operated largely through his own label infrastructure (Scotland Video Records, then Luzuli Video Records) rather than major-label machinery.

His most commercially visible moment came as a songwriter: he wrote “Inside Out,” the 1982 UK hit for Odyssey (2), produced by Jimmy Douglass, which became one of the defining soul-funk crossover singles of that year. His own single “Over The Sea” (1985, UK chart position #65) is the performance most associated with him — a shape-disc picture 7” featuring bagpipes, New York session heavyweights, and a video shot in both the Scottish Highlands and New York City that won a Vira award. “Rusha,” an earlier Scotland Video Records 12”, was included on the 2021 Cherry Red anthology Shake The Foundations (Militant Funk & The Post-Punk Dancefloor 1978-1984).

Rae also had a parallel career in Scottish politics, standing as an independent candidate for the Scottish Parliament (Roxburgh and Berwickshire) in 2007 and 2011.

Name variations used in credits: J Rae, J. Rae, J.Rae, Jesse Rae And The Thistles, Jessie Rae, Rae, William Rae.
