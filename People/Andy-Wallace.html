Andy Wallace

Record producer, mixing engineer, and recording engineer from the USA. Real name: Andrew C. Wallace. Discogs ID: 59472. Based in New York; longtime resident mixer at Soundtrack Studios (NYC).

One of the most consequential mixing engineers in rock history, responsible for the final sonic shape of some of the most commercially and culturally significant albums of the 1990s — including Nirvana’s Nevermind (1991), Rage Against The Machine’s self-titled debut (1992), and Sepultura’s Arise (1991) and Chaos A.D. (1993). Won a Grammy in 1999 for Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical for Sheryl Crow’s The Globe Sessions.

Biography

Early Career (1970s)

Wallace began in the 1970s by opening recording studios, taking engineer credits on obscure releases through the decade. His earliest confirmed Discogs credits are from 1977 (engineering on Nature’s Secret, a Hare Krishna devotional record on Golden Lotus Records) and 1970 (as a performer on a self-titled release by the group First Friday on Webster’s Last Word, playing electric piano, organ, acoustic guitar, bass, and vocals). The First Friday connection suggests studio activity in New York from the very start of his career.

New York Hip-Hop and R&B (1980s)

Wallace relocated to New York and became embedded in the early hip-hop and R&B recording scene. By the mid-1980s he was working at Atlantic, Tommy Boy, and Epic, engineering and mixing for artists across the early hip-hop wave:

  • Run-DMC — Raising Hell (1986, Profile Records) — engineering credit; one of the landmark rap-rock crossover albums
  • Afrika Bambaataa & Soulsonic Force / Shango — Frantic Situation (1984, Tommy Boy) — mixed and engineered
  • Planet Patrol — self-titled (1983, Tommy Boy) — recorded and engineered under his “E.A.R.S.” credit
  • Arthur Baker — Breaker’s Revenge (1984, Atlantic) — mix engineer
  • Force MD’s — Forgive Me Girl (1984, Tommy Boy) — mix
  • Nu Shooz — Poolside (1986, Atlantic) — mixdown engineer
  • Oran ‘Juice’ Jones — Juice (1986, CBS/Def Jam/Columbia) — engineer
  • Big Audio Dynamite — The Bottom Line remix (1985, Columbia) — remix/engineer
  • Freeez — I.O.U. and Gonna Get You (1983, Streetwise/Beggars Banquet) — mix engineer
  • Pet Shop Boys — Opportunities (Let’s Make Lots of Money) (1986, Parlophone) — mix engineer
  • Madonna — You Can Dance (1987, Sire) — remix engineer; also remixed Into the Groove

He also worked in this period with Level 42, Roy Ayers, Brass Construction, Thelma Houston, and Marlon Jackson.

Transition to Rock: The Cult and British Acts (1987–1989)

A pivotal shift came in 1987 with his work on The Cult’s Electric (Beggars Banquet/Sire), produced by Rick Rubin — engineered and mixed in full. Wallace would remain deeply linked to Beggars Banquet–adjacent hard rock through this era, working with:

  • The Cult — Electric (1987), Wild Flower, Lil’ Devil, Love Removal Machine singles, The Electric Mixes (1989)
  • New Model Army — Thunder and Consolation (1989, EMI/Capitol); Vagabonds, Poison Street remix
  • The Godfathers — More Songs About Love & Hate (1989, Epic); several singles
  • Flesh For Lulu — Plastic Fantastic (1989, Beggars Banquet/Capitol)
  • Iggy Pop — Cold Metal / Tuff Baby remix (1988, A&M)
  • Hugh Cornwell — Wolf and Dreaming Again (1988, Virgin)
  • Jeff Beck — Flash (engineering, Epic, 1988–1991)
  • Slayer — Reign in Blood (engineering, Def Jam, 1986; became a recurring Slayer collaborator)

He also produced and mixed the Australian act Machinations (White Label Records, multiple releases 1987–1990) and served as full producer/engineer for The Front (3) on their 1989 CBS debut.

The Landmark Run: 1991–1996

The early 1990s constituted Wallace’s most concentrated period of career-defining work. Operating out of Scream Studios in Studio City, CA and later Soundtrack Studios NYC, he mixed an extraordinary string of major alternative, hard rock, and metal albums that collectively defined the decade’s sonic palette.

1991

  • Nirvana — Nevermind (DGC/Sub Pop) — Mixed By. Perhaps the single most widely heard piece of his work; Butch Vig produced, Wallace mixed. His decision to add shimmer and depth to Vig’s rough mixes gave the album its arena-ready sheen while preserving its rawness.
  • Sepultura — Arise (Roadracer Records) — Mixed By
  • Sonic Youth — (mixed singles)
  • White Zombie — La Sexorcisto: Devil Music Vol. 1 (Geffen, 1992 release) — produced, engineered, and mixed; full involvement
  • Rollins Band — The End of Silence (Imago, 1992 release) — produced, engineered, and mixed

1992

  • Rage Against The Machine — self-titled debut (Epic/Epic Associated) — Mixed By. Rick Rubin produced; Wallace mixed.
  • Helmet (2) — Meantime (Interscope/Atlantic) — Mixed By
  • Sonic Youth — Dirty (DGC) — Mixed By
  • Screaming Trees — Sweet Oblivion (Epic) — Mixed By
  • Soul Asylum (2) — Grave Dancers Union (Columbia) — Mixed By; includes “Runaway Train”
  • Social Distortion — Somewhere Between Heaven and Hell (Epic) — mixed and engineered
  • Ned’s Atomic Dustbin — Are You Normal? (Sony Soho Square) — produced, engineered, mixed

1993

  • Sepultura — Chaos A.D. (Roadrunner/Epic) — produced, mixed, recorded; heavily involved
  • Fishbone — Give a Monkey a Brain… (Columbia) — Mixed By
  • Front 242 — 06:21:03:11 Up Evil (Epic) — Mixed By
  • Galactic Cowboys — Space in Your Face (DGC) — Mixed By
  • Slayer — Seasons in the Abyss (Def American) — co-produced, engineered, mixed

1994

  • Jeff Buckley — Grace (Columbia) — produced, engineered, and mixed in full; the album is widely considered one of the most beautifully recorded debuts of the era
  • Rage Against The Machine — continued pressing credits
  • Bad Religion — Stranger Than Fiction (Atlantic) — produced and mixed
  • Toadies — Rubberneck (Interscope) — Mixed By
  • Helmet (2) — Betty (Interscope/Atlantic) — Mixed By
  • Alice Cooper (2) — The Last Temptation (Epic) — produced and mixed (segues); also mixed several tracks
  • Shudder To Think — Pony Express Record (Epic) — Mixed By
  • Green Jellÿ — 333 — Mixed By

1995

  • Rancid — …And Out Come the Wolves (Epitaph) — Mixed By; one of the defining punk-comeback albums of the decade
  • Faith No More — King for a Day / Fool for a Lifetime (Slash/Reprise) — produced, mixed, recorded
  • Blind Melon — Soup (Capitol) — produced, engineered, mixed (the band’s second and final studio album)
  • Skunk Anansie — Paranoid & Sunburnt (One Little Indian/Epic) — Mixed By
  • Rocket From The Crypt — Scream, Dracula, Scream! (Interscope) — Mixed By
  • Seaweed — Spanaway (Hollywood Records) — Mixed By

1996

  • Rage Against The Machine — Evil Empire (Epic) — Mixed By
  • Screaming Trees — Dust (Epic) — Mixed By
  • Rush — Test for Echo (Atlantic/Anthem) — Mixed By
  • Silverchair — Freak Show (Murmur/Columbia) — Mixed By
  • Sepultura — Roots (Roadrunner) — Mixed By
  • Descendents — Everything Sucks (Epitaph) — Mixed By
  • Cracker — The Golden Age (Virgin) — Mixed By
  • The Wallflowers — Bringing Down the Horse (Interscope) — Mixed By

Grammy Win and the Nu-Metal Era (1997–2003)

Wallace continued as one of the most in-demand rock mixers in the industry throughout the late 1990s and early 2000s:

  • Ben Folds Five — Whatever and Ever Amen (550 Music/Epic, 1997) and The Unauthorized Biography of Reinhold Messner (1999) — Mixed By
  • Alabama 3 — Exile on Coldharbour Lane (1997, Geffen/Virgin) — Mixed By; the album containing the Sopranos theme
  • Everclear — So Much for the Afterglow (Capitol, 1997) — Mixed By
  • Limp Bizkit — Three Dollar Bill, Yall$ (Flip/Interscope, 1997) and Chocolate Starfish and the Hot Dog Flavored Water (2000) — Mixed By
  • Jane’s Addiction — Kettle Whistle (Warner, 1997) — Mixed By
  • Misfits — American Psycho (Geffen, 1997) — Mixed By
  • Sheryl Crow — The Globe Sessions (A&M, 1998) — Mixed By; won Wallace the 1999 Grammy for Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical
  • Phish — The Story of the Ghost (Elektra, 1998) — produced and mixed
  • Foo Fighters — There Is Nothing Left to Lose (Roswell/RCA, 1999) — Mixed By
  • Heather Nova — Siren (V2/[pias], 1998) — Mixed By
  • Atari Teenage Riot — 60 Second Wipe Out (DHR/Elektra, 1999) — Mixed By
  • At The Drive-In — Relationship of Command (Grand Royal/Virgin, 2000) — Mixed By
  • Disturbed — The Sickness (Giant/Reprise, 2000) and Believe (2002) — Mixed By
  • Linkin Park — Hybrid Theory (Warner Bros., 2001) and Meteora (2003) — Mixed By; among the best-selling albums of the 2000s
  • Korn — Untouchables (Epic/Immortal, 2002) — Mixed By
  • Guns N’ Roses — Live Era ‘87–‘93 (Geffen, 1999) — Mixed By

Post-2005 Activity

Wallace maintained consistent activity through the 2000s and into the 2010s, including:

  • Soulfly — Soulfly (Roadrunner, 1998) — Mixed By (Max Cavalera’s post-Sepultura project)
  • VAST — Visual Audio Sensory Theater (Elektra, 1998) — Mixed By
  • Bernard Butler — Friends and Lovers (Creation/Epic, 1999/2000) — Mixed By
  • Cracker — Garage D’Or (Virgin, 2000) — Mixed By

Connections

  • Butch Vig — Vig produced Nevermind, Wallace mixed it; the pairing shaped the album’s commercial sound
  • Rick Rubin — Recurring collaboration across genres (Slayer, The Cult, RATM, White Zombie)
  • Jack Endino — Both worked on Seattle grunge-adjacent acts though usually separately
  • Steve Albini — Albini engineered Nirvana’s In Utero (1993); Wallace had mixed Nevermind — often discussed in terms of contrasting sonic philosophies
  • Jerry Finn — Another major 1990s rock mixer operating in similar territory (Bad Religion, Green Day)
  • Page Hamilton — Helmet frontman; worked closely on Meantime and Betty
  • Max Cavalera — Sepultura and Soulfly; long-running relationship
  • Jeff Buckley — Produced and mixed Grace entirely; the record is inseparable from Wallace’s involvement
