John Vanderslice

Singer-songwriter, record producer, engineer, and studio founder. Born May 22, 1967, Gainesville, Florida. Based in San Francisco. Discogs artist ID: 280268. Alias: Orangepurplebeach. Name variations: JV, John Vaderslice, Mr. John Vanderslice.

Overview

John Vanderslice is one of the defining figures of the San Francisco indie rock scene of the 2000s — simultaneously a recording artist with a distinctive melodic and conceptual sensibility, and the owner-operator of Tiny Telephone recording studio, which became a hub for West Coast independent recording. He released seven solo studio albums between 2000 and 2013 and a collaborative EP/live album with The Mountain Goats in 2009. His most sustained outside production role is his ongoing work with The Mountain Goats, where he produced The Sunset Tree (2005) and Heretic Pride (2008) — two of the band’s most critically prominent releases. His long studio partnership with engineer/producer Scott Solter was the creative engine behind five of his own records and shaped his sound through the Barsuk Records era.

Vanderslice consistently worked at the intersection of indie rock craft and studio experimentation. On White Wilderness (2011) he collaborated with the Magik-Magik Orchestra, a San Francisco chamber ensemble led by arranger Minna Choi, for a full orchestral album. His most recent solo record, The Cedars (2019), was released on his own Native Cat Recordings imprint.

Early Career — MK Ultra (5)

Before his solo work, Vanderslice was a member of MK Ultra (5) (Discogs ID: 746068), a Gainesville/San Francisco-linked band. MK Ultra (5) released a self-titled record in 1993 on Artichoke Records (2) and a final album, The Dream Is Over, in 1999 on the same label — by which point Vanderslice was already producing the record. The 1995 release Original Motion Picture Soundtrack (Artichoke Records (2)) documents the band’s transitional period, with Vanderslice credited for lyrics, vocals, guitar, and sampler alongside members Dan Carr (bass), Matt Torrey (drums/keyboards), and Jordan Newhouse (guitar). Another Discogs-linked group, Google Earth (Discogs ID: 14967431), lists Vanderslice as a member, though this project has minimal release documentation.

MK Ultra (5) members overlapping into Vanderslice’s early solo records include John Tyner (guitar, design on Mass Suicide Occult Figurines) and Matt Torrey (drums on the debut).

Solo Discography

Barsuk Records Era (2000–2007)

Vanderslice signed to Seattle-based Barsuk Records (label ID: 37418) and released five studio albums over seven years:

Pixel Revolt2005JV: lead vocals, music by, songwriter; Solter: engineer, mixed by, co-music by; John Darnielle: lyrics editing credit; string arrangements by Erik Friedlander
Emerald City2007JV: performer, arranger, recorded by, songwriter; Solter: producer, mixed by; chamber ensemble arranged with David Broecker, David Douglas, Ian Bjornstad

Scott Solter entered on Time Travel Is Lonely (engineer) and became co-producer from Cellar Door onward. By Emerald City he was the principal producer and arranger alongside Vanderslice. Their partnership on these records was one of the tightest artist-producer pairings in the Barsuk/indie-SF sphere of that period.

John Croslin (recorded by, mixed by) co-engineered the first two albums. Erik Friedlander arranged strings on Pixel Revolt, marking his first overlap with Vanderslice — he then appeared on The Sunset Tree in the same year. John Darnielle’s lyric editing credit on Pixel Revolt (“edited, expanded and otherwise improved upon by”) reflects the deepening Vanderslice/Mountain Goats relationship ahead of The Sunset Tree.

MGM Endings: Cellar Door Remixes (2004, Artichoke) is a companion remix EP, with Vanderslice credited for additional tracking.

Dead Oceans Era (2009–2013)

After Emerald City, Vanderslice moved to Dead Oceans (label ID: 94740), an Indiana-based independent (under Secretly Group):

ReleaseYearKey Credits / Notes
Romanian Names2009JV: vocals, guitar, keyboards, recorded by, producer, photography; Solter: recorded by, mixed by, producer; large ensemble including Carla Kihlstedt (violin/nyckelharpa), Ben Goldberg (clarinet), Matthias Bossi (drums), Daniel Hart (violin), Matt Cunitz (Orchestron/Mellotron/organ), Sylvain Carton (sax/flute)
White Wilderness2011With the Magik*Magik Orchestra; arranged and conducted by Minna Choi; produced by John Congleton; JV: vocals, acoustic guitar, songwriter; full orchestra; mastered by Bernie Grundman
Dagger Beach2013Self-released; JV: recorded by, producer, written-by; Ian Pellicci: recorded by, mixed by, producer; mastered by Bob Weston

Romanian Names is Vanderslice’s most densely arranged solo album, featuring an extensive ensemble of Bay Area players. Notably, Carla Kihlstedt (violin, viola, nyckelharpa), Ben Goldberg (clarinet), and Matthias Bossi (drums) all appear — a cluster that also turns up in Bay Area new-music and jazz-adjacent circles. Daniel Hart (violin, vocals, piano) appears here early in his career before his work scoring David Lowery’s films. The production credit on Romanian Names is shared between Vanderslice and Solter, continuing their collaboration.

White Wilderness is structurally distinct: John Congleton (Dallas-based producer) replaces Solter in the producer chair, and the Magik*Magik Orchestra provides all orchestration under Minna Choi’s direction.

Dagger Beach (2013, self-released) marked a return to a more stripped approach, with Ian Pellicci as co-engineer and producer.

The Mountain Goats Connection

Vanderslice’s relationship with The Mountain Goats is the most-documented outside connection in his career.

ReleaseYearRoleNotes

John Darnielle is credited on Vanderslice’s own Pixel Revolt (2005) for lyric editing, making the exchange bilateral — each contributing to the other’s output in the same year. The Moon Colony Bloodbath EP (2009, Cadmean Dawn) is a direct co-release: Vanderslice is a primary artist alongside The Mountain Goats, contributing guitar, vocals, mandolin, keyboards, and production, with Chris Stamey handling bass, cello, and production duties alongside them.

The Bombadil (3) credit is notable: all of Vanderslice’s contributions to Fences (2017, Ramseur Records) — including producer, engineer, and Minimoog synthesizer — are listed as uncredited in Discogs, suggesting he may have worked on the album without seeking formal billing.

Bernie Grundman mastered several Vanderslice records (Cellar Door, Pixel Revolt, Romanian Names, White Wilderness, Fences), making him a consistent post-production presence.

Tiny Telephone Studio

Tiny Telephone (Discogs label ID: 269206) is listed on Discogs with two sublabels: Tiny Telephone, Oakland (ID: 1591168) and Tiny Telephone B, San Francisco (ID: 1591169) — indicating at least two physical studio spaces. The studio appears in session credits throughout Vanderslice’s own records and the Mountain Goats work through the Tiny Telephone label entry, though most releases credit the studio informally through engineer credits rather than as a label imprint.
