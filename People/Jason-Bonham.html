Jun 14, 20267 min read

Jason Bonham

Discogs ID: 361213 Born: 1966, Dudley, England Name variations on releases: Bonham, J. Bonham, J.Bonham, Jason, Sonham

British rock drummer. Son of John Bonham, drummer of Led Zeppelin. Active professionally from 1984. Career spans AOR/hard rock session work in the 1980s, his own band Bonham (1989–1993), several supergroup formations with Glenn Hughes, touring stints with Foreigner and Sammy Hagar, a prominent role at the 2007 Led Zeppelin O2 reunion, and the ongoing tribute project Jason Bonham’s Led Zeppelin Evening.

Early Career — Airrace and Virginia Wolf (1984–1988)

Jason Bonham’s first documented studio appearance is on Shaft Of Light (1984), the debut album by Airrace, a British AOR act on Atco Records. The Airrace lineup included vocalist Keith Murrell, guitarist Laurie Mansworth, bassist Jim Reid, and keyboardist Toby Sadler; the album was produced by Beau Hill. The band released a second album (Airrace, 1986) and Bonham remained a member.

From 1986 he drummed for Virginia Wolf, a British band signed to Atlantic. Their 1986 self-titled debut was produced by Roger Taylor and David Richards (Queen/Queen-adjacent production team), with vocalist Chris Ousey, bassist Jo Burt, guitarist Nick Bold, and keyboardist Spike Edney. A second album, Push (1987), followed on Atlantic. Neither broke through commercially, but the Atlantic relationship placed Bonham in professional rock circles at an early age.

In 1988 he appeared on Jimmy Page’s solo album Outrider (Geffen Records), playing drums on six of nine tracks alongside various bassists. This was his highest-profile credit to date and a clear acknowledgement of his lineage.

Bonham — The Disregard Of Timekeeping and Mad Hatter (1989–1993)

Bonham formed his own band — simply called Bonham — in the late 1980s. The lineup was Jason Bonham (drums), Daniel MacMaster (vocals), Ian Hatton (guitar), and John Smithson (keyboards, bass, violin). Their debut album The Disregard Of Timekeeping (1989, WTG Records) was produced by Bob Ezrin. Bonham co-wrote the material with the full band and Ezrin.

A second album, Mad Hatter (1992, Epic/WTG Records), was co-produced by the band alongside Tony Platt and Ron St. Germain, with mastering by Bob Ludwig. The band wrote and arranged the material themselves. The album’s cover art was by Mark Ryden.

Discogs also lists a single Wait For You (1989) and a single Guilty (1989), both co-written by Bonham. The band dissolved in the early 1990s.

Session and Guest Work (1988–2005)

Throughout the 1990s Bonham maintained a busy session career:

  • Jimmy Page — Outrider (1988, Geffen): Six tracks, alongside Chris Farlowe (vocals on several cuts).
  • Paul Rodgers & Friends — Live At Montreux 1994: Drums on the live recording (released 2011 on Eagle Records).
  • Slash — Muddy Water Blues (A Tribute To Muddy Waters) (1993, Victory): Drums and arrangement credits.
  • In The Name Of My Father — The Zepset — Live From Electric Ladyland (1996, BMG Direct): A Zeppelin tribute live set.
  • Foreigner (ongoing from mid-2000s): Touring drummer; credited on Extended Versions (2006, CMG) as “Performer [Foreigner], Drums” and Can’t Slow Down (2009, Atlantic/Rhino) and Playlist: The Very Best Of Foreigner (2016, Sony).
  • Rock Star (Original Soundtrack) (2001, Virgin/Priority): Drums; the film featured a fictional metal band.
  • Man Of Steel (Original Soundtrack) (2013, WaterTower Music): “Drums [Drum Orchestra].”
  • No Time To Die (Original Soundtrack) (2021, Decca): “Soloist, Drums.”

Led Zeppelin Reunion — O2 Arena 2007 / Celebration Day (2012)

On 10 December 2007, Jason Bonham played drums for the Led Zeppelin reunion concert at the O2 Arena in London — filling the seat held by his father. The surviving members were Jimmy Page, Robert Plant, and John Paul Jones. The concert was filmed and released in 2012 as Celebration Day (Atlantic/Swan Song/Warner Music), with Bonham credited for Drums, Percussion, and Backing Vocals. Mixed by Alan Moulder, mastered by John Davis.

This performance is widely considered the definitive moment of his career as symbolic and musical heir to his father.

Black Country Communion (2009–2013, 2017–present)

Black Country Communion (BCC) was formed in 2009 in Los Angeles. Lineup: Glenn Hughes (vocals, bass), Joe Bonamassa (guitar, vocals), Jason Bonham (drums), Derek Sherinian (keyboards). All four studio albums were produced by Kevin Shirley.

The band split in 2013 (reportedly due to tensions between Hughes and Bonamassa), then reunited in 2017. BCCIV and V confirm the partnership has continued into the 2020s. Kevin Shirley produced all five albums; Bob Ludwig mastered the first four.

California Breed (2013–2015)

After the initial BCC breakup, Hughes and Bonham formed California Breed with guitarist/vocalist Andrew Watt (3) and bassist/drummer Joey Castillo. Their self-titled debut (2014, Frontiers Records) was produced by Dave Cobb and featured additional keyboards from Mike Webb (2) and backing vocals from Kristen Rogers. The band dissolved by 2015 with no follow-up album released.

Sammy Hagar & The Circle (2014–present)

Bonham joined Sammy Hagar’s touring and recording band The Circle, alongside Michael Anthony (bass/vocals) and Vic Johnson (guitar). Primary recording output on Discogs:

  • Live: At Your Service (2015, Mailboat Records) — Bonham credited as primary artist (“Band [The Circle], Drums”)
  • Space Between (2019, BMG) — Drums, Percussion, Backing Vocals
  • Lockdown 2020 (2021, Mailboat Records) — Drums, Percussion, Backing Vocals
  • Crazy Times (2022, UMe) — Drums, Percussion, Backing Vocals; co-Written-By

Jason Bonham’s Led Zeppelin Evening

An ongoing touring tribute project (Discogs artist ID: 17397205). Bonham leads a rotating band performing the Led Zeppelin catalog. No studio releases documented in the Discogs DB; the project exists primarily as a live touring entity.
