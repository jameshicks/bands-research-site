Ari Brown

Real name: Richard Brown
Born: February 1, 1944
Discogs ID: 322783
Instruments: Tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, alto saxophone, flute, piano
Based: Chicago, Illinois, USA

American free jazz saxophonist, composer, and educator. Prominent member of the AACM, which he joined in 1971. Active across more than five decades, with a primary association with Delmark Records and later Eremite Records and Aguirre Records. Name variations in the DB include “Richard ‘Ari’ Brown,” “Richard (Ari) Brown,” and plain “R. Brown.”

Overview

Brown occupies a mid-generation position in the Chicago AACM axis — too young to be a founding figure (the AACM was formed in 1965), but a consistent presence from the early 1970s onward. His Discogs credit trail begins in 1972 on Black Jazz Records sessions; his first leadership records arrive in the 1980s. His playing covers the full saxophone family and extends to piano and flute, and his compositions (credited across his Delmark albums) are rooted in the post-Coltrane free tradition.

He has been a member of several extended Chicago-based ensembles including Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio (as a regular guest/co-leader), the Lester Bowie Ensemble, the Elvin Jones Jazz Machine, the Juba Collective, and the Joshua Abrams-led Cloud Script and Natural Information Society projects. As of 2023 he remained active, co-leading the large ensemble record Since Time Is Gravity with Joshua Abrams’ Natural Information Society.

Early Credits (1972–1984)

Brown’s earliest appearances on Discogs are as a sideman on three Black Jazz Records sessions — Gene Russell’s Hear, Sense And Feel (1972), Mirage (1973, Black Jazz), and Plenty Good Eaton (1975, Black Jazz) — placing him early in the Chicago-LA crossover of spiritual jazz. He also appears on Delmark sessions in the mid-1970s: Things To Come From Those Now Gone (1975) and the Wilbur Campbell-led Reality (1978).

Other 1970s–early 1980s credits include Flowers (CBS, 1976, UK), Gonna Grow Some Gold (Kelli-Arts Records, 1979), and All The Magic! (ECM Records, 1983) — a significant placement suggesting his reach into the broader international free jazz market. He also appears on Jazzbühne Berlin ‘82 (Repertoire Records, 1990, Germany) from that festival recording.

Ultimate Frontier (1996, Delmark)

Brown’s solo Delmark debut. Personnel:

  • Ari Brown — tenor sax, alto sax, soprano sax, flute, piano; all compositions
  • Yosef Ben Israel — bass
  • Avreeayl Ra — drums
  • Kirk Brown — piano
  • Dr. Cuz / Enoch (10) — percussion (track 2)
  • Steve Wagner / Robert G. Koester — producers

Jam For Your Life (1985, AECO Records)

Co-led with Famoudou Don Moye (AEC drummer) and vocalist Luba Raashiek. A live or studio session on the AACM’s own label (AECO Records). Personnel includes Bill Brimfield (trumpet), Rollo Radford and Thomas Palmer (bass, alternating), Ken Prince (piano), Sonny Covington (trumpet), Joel Brandon (whistling).

Live At The Progressive Arts Center (1982, L’Orsa Maggiore)

Co-led with Famoudou Don Moye. Recorded in Italy. Personnel: Milton Suggs (bass), Moye (drums/percussion), Brown (tenor/alto/soprano saxes). An early document of the Brown–Moye partnership.

Alika Rising At Leverkusener Jazztage (1990, Sound Aspects Records)

Live at Leverkusener Jazztage, Germany. A trio-plus: Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio (Kahil El’Zabar on kalimba, earth drums, bells, voice; Malachi Favors on bass) with Brown on tenor sax, soprano sax, and piano.

Since Time Is Gravity (2023, Aguirre Records)

Large ensemble record co-led with Joshua Abrams’ Natural Information Society (credited as “Natural Information Society Community Ensemble”). Personnel:

  • Ari Brown — tenor saxophone (tracks A1, A2, D3)

  • Hamid Drake — congas, tabla, tar drum
  • Mikel Avery — drums
  • Nick Mazzarella — alto saxophone
  • Mai Sugimoto — alto sax, flute
  • Jason Stein (2) — bass clarinet
  • Ben Lamar Gay — cornet
  • Josh Berman — cornet
  • Kara Bershad — harp
  • Lisa Alvarado — harmonium, painting
  • Cooper Crain / Greg Norman — recorded/engineer
  • Helge Sten (Deathprod) — mastered

Africa N’da Blues (Delmark, date unclear in DB)

Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio featuring Pharoah Sanders, with Brown on piano, tenor sax, and soprano sax; Malachi Favors on bass; Pharoah Sanders on tenor sax.
