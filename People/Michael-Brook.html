Michael Brook

Discogs ID: 16388
Origin: Canada (Toronto)

Canadian guitarist and producer, known for developing the “infinite guitar” (sustain through live feedback). Appeared on Jon Hassell’s Fourth World Vol. 1 — Possible Musics (1980, Editions EG). Co-credited on Brian Eno / Daniel Lanois / Michael Brook Hybrid (1985, Editions EG, EGED 41). Later produced records for artists including Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan and worked extensively in film music.

The Infinite Guitar

Background and inspiration

Brook’s interest in sustained tones grew out of lessons he took with La Monte Young — on Jon Hassell’s suggestion — which focused on holding and gliding between notes so that Brook could “sustain a note, and make a drone like a sitar.” (insheepsclothinghifi.com interview)

The direct catalyst came in 1983 when Brook attended a Bill Nelson performance in Toronto and saw Nelson open with an EBow. Brook feared audiences would assume he was copying Nelson’s approach. He ordered his own EBow, but it never arrived. Drawing on his electronics background (he holds an electronics degree, per Sound on Sound July 1995), he set out to build an alternative himself. (Yahoo/Guardian article)

There is also an earlier prior art precedent: Wikipedia notes Brook was inspired by the “Holland Infinite Sustain Guitar,” a modified SG built by Randy Roos and Steve Holland in 1978. (Wikipedia, Infinite Guitar, as of 2025-10-22)

Development — when and where

Brook developed the instrument in the early 1980s while working as an engineer at Ontario’s Grant Avenue Studios. He built his first prototype on an inexpensive Tokai Stratocaster copy. (Yahoo/Guardian article)

By 1985 it was sufficiently developed for use on his debut album Hybrid, recorded with Brian Eno and Daniel Lanois. The 1990 Sound on Sound / Muzines “Beyond the Infinite” interview (June 1990) is the earliest published interview describing it in technical detail, by which point Brook was living in West London. (muzines.co.uk — Beyond the Infinite, Jun 1990)

Technical design

Brook described it as “an ordinary guitar that’s been modified with added electronic components which I built for it.” The circuit takes the output signal from a standard magnetic pickup, amplifies it, and feeds it back into a second pickup coil, causing the strings to oscillate continuously — a controlled feedback loop entirely within the instrument. Because the note does not decay, pitch changes are made simply by moving a finger along the string. The result simulates “a loud amplifier — it’s just like having a 200 watt Marshall stack,” in Brook’s words. (muzines.co.uk — Beyond the Infinite, Jun 1990)

Key physical modifications to the host guitar:

  • Scalloped fretboard — allows sitar-like finger bending and microtonal pitch gliding. (insheepsclothinghifi.com; muzines.co.uk — Beyond the Infinite, Jun 1990)
  • On-board feedback circuit — feeds signal between pickups in an endless loop.
  • Filter pedal in the feedback loop — allows switching between normal guitar mode and infinite sustain mode, and enables harmonic overtone effects. (muzines.co.uk — Infinite World, Oct 1992)
  • Two large volume (echo) pedals used in live performance for dynamic control. (muzines.co.uk — Beyond the Infinite, Jun 1990)

Brook noted the system is “not totally controllable. In fact, it’s quite organic,” acknowledging a natural, less-predictable response quality. (muzines.co.uk — Infinite World, Oct 1992)

Other guitars built — The Edge and Daniel Lanois

By 1990, only three Infinite Guitars existed: Brook’s own instrument, one owned by The Edge (U2), and one owned by Daniel Lanois. (muzines.co.uk — Beyond the Infinite, Jun 1990)

The Edge’s guitar came about in the mid-1980s when Lanois (who was co-producing U2 at the time) suggested the device might suit The Edge. Edge visited Brook and requested a modified instrument — specifically a 1975 Stratocaster retrofitted with Brook’s circuit. The Edge used it on “With or Without You” on The Joshua Tree (1987). (Yahoo/Guardian article)

Brook’s 1986 Captive soundtrack is identified as the first recorded use of the Infinite Guitar by The Edge — predating The Joshua Tree by a year. (muzines.co.uk — Beyond the Infinite, Jun 1990)

Commercial production — never happened

Manufacturers including Fender and IVL expressed interest in producing the guitar commercially, and as of 1990 Brook said he was “talking to a manufacturer at the moment about the possibility of marketing the guitar.” (muzines.co.uk — Beyond the Infinite, Jun 1990) By the 1992 Muzines interview he remained secretive about specific technical details, citing potential future production rights. (muzines.co.uk — Infinite World, Oct 1992) The Yahoo/Guardian article reports he ultimately chose not to commercialize it, preferring to keep it as a personal creative tool. (Yahoo/Guardian article)

Similar technology later became available through the Sustainiac, Fernandes Sustainer, and Moog Guitar. (Wikipedia, Infinite Guitar, as of 2025-10-22)
