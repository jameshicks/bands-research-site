Overview

One of the most recorded drummers in jazz history, Billy Higgins (1936–2001) bridged the hard bop mainstream and the free jazz avant-garde across a career of extraordinary breadth. Los Angeles-born, he was central to Ornette Coleman’s early Atlantic recordings alongside Don Cherry and Charlie Haden, while simultaneously sustaining a decades-long partnership with Cedar Walton and deep ties to the Blue Note stable — Lee Morgan, Dexter Gordon, and Herbie Hancock among them. His catalogue peaks in the 1980s but spans from the late 1950s through the late 1990s, running through Blue Note, Atlantic, Prestige, and SteepleChase, with Rudy Van Gelder engineering the lion’s share of his studio work.
