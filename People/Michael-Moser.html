Michael Moser

Austrian cellist, composer, and sound artist. Born 1959 in Graz, Austria. Based in Vienna. Discogs artist ID: 317755.

Overview

Michael Moser is a central figure in the Viennese reductive/electroacoustic improvisation scene that coalesced in the 1990s. He has been a constant member of Polwechsel from the group’s founding, where his role extends well beyond performance — he has served as producer, mixing engineer, photographer, and cover artist across the group’s catalog. Outside Polwechsel, he has worked with Zeitkratzer and Ton-Art, appeared on David Sylvian’s landmark Manafon album, and released a solo work on Edition RZ in 2016. His practice spans acoustic cello, electronics, glass plate performance, and sound installation.

Name variations on releases: “M. Moser”, “Moser”.

Polwechsel — Full Discography

Polwechsel was founded in Vienna in the early 1990s. The original core was Moser (cello), Werner Dafeldecker (double bass, guitar), Burkhard Stangl (guitar), and Radu Malfatti (trombone). John Butcher joined from Polwechsel 2 (1999) and left in 2009. Burkhard Beins and Martin Brandlmayr (both percussion) joined for the post-2003 incarnation. Radu Malfatti left after the 1998 hat[now]ART reissue; Burkhard Stangl left around 2008.

Moser and Dafeldecker jointly produced, mixed, and edited most Polwechsel albums through the 2000s.

Other Recordings and Collaborations

Ton-Art — Mal Vu. Mal Dit. (1991, hat ART)

Moser’s earliest Discogs-traceable recording. Ton-Art was a large Viennese ensemble including Moser (cello), Dafeldecker (bass), Stangl (guitar), Bernhard Spahn (alto sax), Hans Steiner (bass clarinet), Paul Skrepek (drums), Tom Varner (French horn), Klaus Peham (trumpet), Susanna Heilmayr (viola, baroque oboe). Produced by Ton-Art and Franz Koglmann. Engineer: Ulrich Goebel. Released on hat ART (Switzerland).

Zeitkratzer — SonX and Xtensions (2000, Timescraper Records)

Two releases with Zeitkratzer, the Berlin-based new music ensemble directed by Reinhold Friedl. Moser appears as cellist alongside a large chamber ensemble including Axel Dörner (trumpet), Alexander Frangenheim (double bass), Ulrich Krieger (saxophone), Luca Venitucci (accordion), Ray Kaczynski (percussion), Burkhard Schlothauer (violin), Melvyn Poore (tuba). Moser is listed as a formal member of Zeitkratzer on Discogs (member list).

David Sylvian — Manafon (2009, Samadhisound)

Moser plays cello on tracks 1, 3, 6, and 9 of David Sylvian’s Manafon, one of the most significant records to draw from the Viennese/European electroacoustic scene. Fellow contributors from the same orbit: Dafeldecker (acoustic bass), Stangl (guitar), Fennesz (laptop/guitar), Franz Hautzinger (trumpet), John Tilbury (piano), Evan Parker (saxophone), Keith Rowe (guitar), Toshimaru Nakamura (no-input mixer), Otomo Yoshihide (turntables). Produced by Sylvian.

Antiphon Stein (2016, Edition RZ) — Solo

Moser’s primary solo release on Discogs. He is credited as composer, performer (glass plates), electronics, sound installation artist, recordist, mixer, and mastering engineer — this is substantially an autonomous work. Pre-recorded source materials were contributed by Berndt Thurner (drums, percussion) and Klaus Lang (organ). Mixed and mastered (software) by Wolfgang Musil. Text contributions by Hannah Schwegler and Nik Hummer. Released on Edition RZ (Germany).

Sing Mit Mir Ein Halleluja (Die Neuen Religiösen Lieder 2) (1985, Amadeo)

Earliest Discogs credit: cello on an Austrian compilation, 1985.

Disco Prova (2007, absinthRecords)

Photography credit on this German release. Non-performance role.

Embrace 2: Chains And Grain (2023, Ni-Vu-Ni-Connu)

A second Polwechsel release from 2023 (separate from Chains And Grain), Ni-Vu-Ni-Connu. Part of the “Embrace” series of 2023 releases.

Production and Technical Role

Moser’s contribution to Polwechsel is not limited to performance. Across the discography:

  • Producer: Polwechsel (1995, 1998), Polwechsel 2 (1999), Polwechsel 3 (2001) — always jointly with Dafeldecker
  • Mixed By: Polwechsel 2, Polwechsel 3, Archives Of The North, Field, Unseen, Embrace (Jupiter Storm) — typically jointly with Dafeldecker
  • Photography / Cover Art: Polwechsel (1995, 1998), Archives Of The North, Field, Traces Of Wood, Embrace (Jupiter Storm), Chains And Grain
