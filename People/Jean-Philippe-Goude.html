Jean Philippe Goude

Jean-Philippe Goude

Discogs ID: 97971 Born: 1952, Paris, France Role: Composer, keyboardist (piano, Minimoog, Moog, synthesizer, harpsichord, harmonium, celesta, mellotron)

French composer and keyboardist whose career spans experimental rock, progressive electronics, early French chanson production, and contemporary classical composition. He emerged from the early-1970s Paris experimental scene, passed through the Magma orbit via Weidorje, and later built an independent compositional voice under his own name and ensemble. Alongside Richard Pinhas he is one of the more sustained synthesizer figures to come out of the French prog underground.

Biography

Origins and Jeunes Années (1976)

Goude’s earliest documented recording is Jeunes Années (1976, Saravah), a debut co-credited with drummer Olivier Cole. He composed most of the material and played acoustic piano, Moog and Elka synthesizers, Fender electric piano, acoustic guitar, and voice. The album was produced by Pierre Barouh (Saravah’s founder) and realized by Michel Carras. It sits in an experimental jazz-fusion/prog space, with styles logged as Avant-garde, Experimental, Fusion, and Prog Rock.

Weidorje (1978)

Goude joined Weidorje, the post-Magma (6) project assembled largely from the Magma extended family. The group’s sole self-titled album (1978, Cobra, COB 37014) is the canonical Zeuhl-adjacent record of the era. Goude shared keyboard duties with Patrick Gauthier; the writing credits went entirely to Gauthier and bassist Bernard Paganotti.

Tracks: “Elohims Voyage” (A, written by Paganotti), “Vilna” and “Booldemug” (B, both written by Gauthier).

Drones and the Ramses Circle (1979–1982)

Goude appeared on Richard Pinhas’s Iceland (1979, Polydor/Ramses) playing Mini Moog on the closing track “Greenland.” This was the start of a recurring association with the Pinhas/Heldon orbit and with Studio Ramsès.

Drones (1980, Polydor/Ramses) is Goude’s first fully self-authored LP. He was concept author, composer, lyricist, and producer, and played piano and synthesizer across nearly every track. The ensemble is large and varied — a suite-like series of pieces drawing in Zeuhl musicians (Paganotti, Klaus Blasquiz, Kirt Rust, Gauthier), jazz musicians (Sylvin Marc, Manu Katché), Pinhas on synthesizer (track A3 “Machine”), string quartet (Quatuor Margand), and others. Engineer and executive producer for the Ramses label side was Jean-Louis Rizet.

Around the same time he contributed synthesizer, piano, and harpsichord to Renaud’s breakthrough Marche À L’Ombre (1980, Polydor) — a high-profile commercial assignment that opened a parallel strand of mainstream French pop work. He also contributed Minimoog arrangements to Pinhas’s L’Ethique (1982) at this period.

L’Ethique (Richard Pinhas, 1982)

One of Goude’s most noted sideman appearances. On L’Ethique (Pulse, PULSE 006) he shares Minimoog and percussion credits with Gauthier across the three-part title suite and “L’Ethique Part 3,” and co-arranged those tracks with Jean-Louis Rizet. Philosopher Gilles Deleuze provides spoken-word vocals on the title tracks.

Mistral Gagnant (1985) is among the best-known French pop albums of the decade. Goude produced and arranged alongside a large Los Angeles/Paris session team.

He was also a member of the satirical/new wave group Odeurs — a collective anchored by Ramon Pipin (alias of Alain Ranval) and including Klaus Blasquiz, Dominique Esnault, and others from the Zeuhl/chanson margins.

DWW (Richard Pinhas, 1992)

Goude’s last major sideman credit in the Pinhas orbit. On DWW (Cuneiform Records, Rune 40) he is extensively involved: Minimoog drums and synth drums (tracks 2 and 4), synth programming (tracks 2, 3, 5, 7, 9), orchestration of the string quartet passages (tracks 3, 5, 7, 9), and a co-writing credit covering the entire album. The string ensemble credited on those tracks is The Paris Opera String Quartet. Gauthier also appears playing Polymoog, Minimoog solo, and piano/drums/synthesizer on track 6.

Ensemble Jean-Philippe Goude and Hopi Mesa Years (1992–2001)

From the early 1990s Goude developed his own ensemble and label Hopi Mesa. These works blend early music aesthetics (harpsichord, viola da gamba, countertenor) with electronics and contemporary compositional sensibility.

De Anima (1992, Hopi Mesa HMK 006) is the most expansive: 15 tracks covering pavane, allemande, élégie, a Libera Me, and a Salve Regina, scored for a mixture of period (viol consort featuring Jérôme Hantaï, Anne-Marie Lasla, Sylvia Abramowicz) and modern chamber players. Aux Solitudes and Later Work (2008–2023)

Aux Solitudes (2008, Ici D’Ailleurs, IDA047) marks a return to public visibility. Goude composed and wrote lyrics; the ensemble includes ondes Martenot (Christine Ott), countertenor (Paulin Bündgen), clarinet (Paul Meyer, Catherine Delaunay), strings, and piano. Pierre-Yves Roupin mixed.

Le Salon Noir (2023, Ici D’Ailleurs) is his most recent credited primary release.

The live document Live At Toulon 1978 (2023, Noizu! Records, Mexico) captures a Weidorje concert.
