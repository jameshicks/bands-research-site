Mars Williams

Mars Williams

Aug 26, 20267 min read

Mars Williams

Discogs ID: 211626
Real name: Marc Charles Williams
Born: May 29, 1955, Elmhurst, Illinois, USA
Died: November 20, 2023, Chicago, Illinois, USA
Instruments: Tenor, alto, soprano, baritone, sopranino saxophone; clarinet; flute; didgeridoo; toy instruments; vocals

Multi-reed player whose career spanned new wave pop and international free jazz without fully belonging to either world. Best known to rock audiences as a member of The Psychedelic Furs from 1984 through their 2020 comeback; best known to jazz audiences as a founding member of the NRG Ensemble under Hal Russell, a member of the Vandermark 5, and a regular presence in the Peter Brötzmann Chicago Tentet. He spent nearly forty years straddling both scenes while also teaching woodwinds and improvisation at Bard College and elsewhere.

Early Chicago Work and The Waitresses (1981–1984)

Williams was born in Elmhurst, Illinois, a suburb of Chicago, and developed his playing in the Chicago area. His earliest Discogs credits place him in the NRG Ensemble from 1981 onward (see below), but the same years saw him working in new wave contexts far from Chicago.

The Waitresses were an Akron, Ohio-based post-punk/new wave band built around songwriter and guitarist Chris Butler and vocalist Patty Donahue. Williams joined as their saxophonist and appeared on both their studio albums:

YearTitleLabelNotes
1982Wasn’t Tomorrow Wonderful?PolydorDebut LP; “I Know What Boys Like”
1983BruiseologyPolydorSecond LP

The band also released the EP I Could Rule The World If I Could Only Get The Parts (1982, Polydor, PX-1-507) featuring “Christmas Wrapping,” which became a perennial holiday classic. Williams plays saxophone throughout; bassist Tracy Wormworth and drummer Billy Ficca (ex-Television) anchored the rhythm section.

The Psychedelic Furs (1984–2020)

Williams joined The Psychedelic Furs for Mirror Moves (1984, Columbia), produced by Keith Forsey. He played saxophone across the album and remained a member through the band’s commercial peak and beyond:

Made Of Rain was the band’s first studio album in nearly thirty years. Williams’s saxophone is a consistent texture across the Furs’ discography — notably on Midnight To Midnight, where he’s credited with both “Horns, Saxophone.” Key collaborators through the Furs years included singer Richard Butler, guitarist John Ashton, and bassist Tim Butler.

NRG Ensemble and Chicago Free Jazz (1981–1993)

In parallel with his new wave work, Williams was a core member of the NRG Ensemble (also credited as “Hal Russell NRG Ensemble”), the Chicago free jazz collective led by multi-instrumentalist and drummer Hal Russell. The ensemble’s membership at various points included:

  • Ken Vandermark — reeds
  • Kent Kessler — bass
  • Steve Hunt — drums/percussion
  • Brian Sandstrom — guitar/bass
  • Chuck Burdelik — reeds
  • Curt Bley — bass

Hal Russell died in 1992; Williams continued performing and recording the ensemble’s history posthumously, appearing on The Hal Russell Story (ECM, 1993) which documented the ensemble’s trajectory. Williams’s role in the ensemble — playing multiple saxophones alongside didgeridoo and toy instruments — was central to its aesthetic of aggressive, often chaotic improvisation.

Session Work: New Wave and Rock (1983–1995)

Williams was a high-demand session saxophone player through the 1980s and early 1990s, appearing on some of the era’s biggest commercial rock records:

The Ministry connection is notable: his saxophone appears on “Cannibal Song” on The Mind Is A Terrible Thing To Taste, one of the defining industrial metal albums. He also co-wrote material on Engine (1994) alongside Al Jourgensen. The Power Station session placed him alongside Robert Palmer, Andy Taylor, and John Taylor of Duran Duran.

He also played on records by Wild Child (1985, Stiff Records), Everplastic, and various others through the 1990s.

Liquid Soul (1995–2002)

Liquid Soul was a Chicago-based acid jazz and jazz-funk ensemble that Williams co-founded and led. Core members included Ricky Showalter (bass), Ron Haynes (trumpet), and vocalist Lisa Simone (daughter of Nina Simone). The band appeared on the Soul What Records label — Williams’s own imprint — and later signed to Shanachie.

Here’s The Deal (Shanachie, 2000) earned a Grammy nomination for Best Contemporary Jazz Record in 2001 — the high-water mark of that phase of his career. The Discogs DB shows no album titled “The Chicago Project”; the fourth album is One-Two Punch (Telarc, 2006) with a substantially changed lineup featuring DJ Logic, Hugh Ragin, and producer Van Christie.

Vandermark 5 (1997–2009)

Williams was a member of the Vandermark 5, Ken Vandermark’s working quintet built around composed and improvised structures. The ensemble also included Fredrick Lonberg-Holm (cello/electronics), Jeb Bishop (trombone), Kent Kessler (bass), and Dave Rempis or Tim Daisy on drums at various points.

Around the same period, Williams appeared in two other Vandermark-adjacent groups: Witches & Devils (with Vandermark, Lonberg-Holm, Jim Baker, Kessler, and Steve Hunt) and the Peter Brötzmann Chicago Tentet (the large ensemble convened by Peter Brötzmann with Hamid Drake, Joe McPhee, Mats Gustafsson, Michael Zerang, and others).

Boneshaker and Later Career (2007–2023)

Boneshaker (3) was Williams’s later working trio with Kent Kessler (bass) and Paal Nilssen-Love (drums, from Norway). The group released Thinking Out Loud (2017, Trost Records, TR158) — a free jazz LP with four side-length tracks.

Williams also ran an annual Albert Ayler tribute concert series, releasing recordings under the title Mars Williams Presents: An Ayler Xmas. Volume 4 — subtitled Chicago vs N.Y.C. — appeared in 2020 on Astral Spirits/Soul What Records.

Other late-career appearances include:

  • Crumbling Brain (2010, Okka Disk) — free jazz
  • Fake Music (2019, Soul What Records)
  • Blues From The Inside Out (2019, Delmark Records) — tenor saxophone
  • Phosphorus (2023, Not On Label/Custard Flux self-released) — among his last recordings
  • Dancing With Angels (2024, 33.3 Music Collective) — posthumous release, UK & US

Williams was also a member of several other Chicago creative music ensembles: Chicago Edge Ensemble, Chicago Reed Quartet, Harrison Bankhead Quartet, Harrison Bankhead Sextet, Keefe Jackson’s Likely So, Paul Giallorenzo’s Gitgo, and Michael Zerang & The Blue Lights.

He died on November 20, 2023, in a hospice facility in Chicago after a struggle with ampullary cancer. He had continued performing into his final months.

Teaching

Williams taught woodwinds and jazz improvisation for over thirty years. He held a position at Bard College and conducted master classes and clinics at multiple institutions.
