Oliver Lake

American saxophonist (alto, soprano), flutist, composer, poet, and visual artist. Born September 14, 1942, Marianna, Arkansas. Discogs artist ID: 49759. Long-time resident of Montclair, New Jersey. Artistic director of Passin’ Thru Records.

Overview

Oliver Lake is one of the central figures of post-1960s American avant-garde jazz. He came up through the jazz program at Sumner High School in St. Louis (alongside Lester Bowie and Floyd LeFlore) and studied at Lincoln University alongside Julius Hemphill. In 1968 he co-founded the Black Artists Group (BAG), the St. Louis multidisciplinary arts collective that gathered jazz musicians, poets, dancers, and visual artists under the same umbrella. BAG was a direct parallel to Chicago’s AACM, though unique in its cross-disciplinary scope.

After BAG dissolved in 1972, Lake relocated to Paris and then New York — part of the general post-BAG diaspora that seeded the loft-jazz scene of the 1970s. In 1977 he co-founded the World Saxophone Quartet with Julius Hemphill, Hamiet Bluiett, and David Murray; it became one of the defining groups of the era, recording prolifically for Black Saint and later Elektra/Nonesuch and Justin Time. From the early 1980s Lake also led the funk-inflected ensemble Jump Up and his own big band. He received a Guggenheim Fellowship in 1993, a Mellon Jazz Living Legacy Award in 2006, and a Doris Duke Performing Artist Award in 2014. His collection (1974–2009) is held at the Library of Congress. (Source: Wikipedia / Oliver Lake)

He is the father of drummer Gene Lake.

Career Periods

St. Louis / BAG Era (1968–1972)

Lake was a core member of Black Artists Group, active 1968–1972. BAG received major funding from the Danforth and Rockefeller Foundations and operated out of a building at 2665 Washington Blvd in St. Louis. BAG musicians (including Lake, Hemphill, Bluiett, Joseph Bowie, Baikida Carroll, and Charles Bobo Shaw) recorded under several umbrella names — The Human Arts Ensemble, New Dalta Ahkri, and Solidarity Unit, Inc. — as well as BAG itself. Lake appeared on Whisper of Dharma (1972, Human Arts Ensemble), Red, Black & Green (1972, Solidarity Unit), and the Paris recording In Paris, Aries 1973 (self-released; reissued Aguirre 2018). (Source: Wikipedia / Black Artists Group)

Paris / New York Emergence (1973–1979)

After BAG, Lake spent time in Paris before settling in New York. He played on Baikida Carroll’s Orange Fish Tears (Palm, 1974) and on Anthony Braxton’s landmark New York, Fall 1974 (Arista, 1975) — the recording that also brought together Hemphill, Bluiett, and Murray for the first time, laying the groundwork for the World Saxophone Quartet.

Lake’s own early solo output appeared on Arista / Freedom: Passing Thru (Africa Publishing, 1974), Heavy Spirits (1975), Under The Sun (1975), and NTU: Point From Which Creation Begins (1976 — recorded 1971). Holding Together (1976) was his first release for Black Saint. A duo album with Joseph Bowie (Joseph Bowie • Oliver Lake, Sackville, 1976) appeared in Canada. He also recorded Buster Bee for Sackville Recordings (1978) and Life Dance of Is (Arista Novus, 1978).

World Saxophone Quartet (1977–2016)

The WSQ was founded in 1977; its debut Point of No Return appeared on Moers Music (Germany). The group recorded thirteen studio albums and numerous live sets for Black Saint (1979–1994), then for Elektra Nonesuch (1986–1994), and finally for Justin Time (1996–2006). Julius Hemphill left due to illness in 1991 (he died April 2, 1995); Arthur Blythe and others subsequently filled his chair. The WSQ officially split in 2016. Hamiet Bluiett died October 4, 2018. (Source: Wikipedia / World Saxophone Quartet)

Gramavision Years (1982–1991)

Lake’s most commercially visible period as a solo leader. He signed to Gramavision and released six studio albums: Jump Up (1982), Plug It (1983), Trickle Down Theory (1983), Gallery (1986), Impala (1987), Otherside (1988), plus Again and Again (1991). The band Jump Up — a funk/jazz hybrid — anchored the early Gramavision releases. Key personnel across this period: drummer Pheeroan Aklaff, bassist Fred Hopkins, pianist Geri Allen, guitarist Jerome Harris, producer Jonathan F. P. Rose, engineer Jim Anderson, and mastering engineer Bob Ludwig.

Later Period (1990s–2020s)

From the mid-1990s Lake ran his own imprint, Passin’ Thru Records, releasing a steady stream of his own recordings and those of associated artists. He continued working with Trio 3 (with Reggie Workman and Andrew Cyrille) and the OGJB Quartet (with Graham Haynes, Joe Fonda, Barry Altschul). Recordings for Intakt Records (Switzerland) — For A Little Dancin’ (2010), All Decks (2013), To Roy (2015), Visiting Texture (2017) — mark his most recent sustained label relationship. He appeared on Björk’s Debut (1993), Surrounded (2006), and MTV Unplugged reissues, credited for brass arrangements. Spirit (SFÄR, 2023) is his most recent primary release.

Research Queue

  • Wikipedia notes that Lake, Hemphill, and Bluiett formed a group called “African Continuum” and organized the 1971 multimedia concert “Images: Sons/Ancestors” at Powell Symphony Hall in St. Louis — is there more documentation of African Continuum as a distinct ensemble, separate from BAG and NTU?
  • The Discogs entry for NTU: Point From Which Creation Begins (1976) says recorded 1971 — Wikipedia confirms this. The album title echoes the BAG-era collective name “New Dalta Ahkri” / NTU. Was this literally the same group and session as New Dalta Ahkri, or a distinct project that adopted the NTU name?
  • Discogs shows Apple Cores (1979, Baystate/Japan) under Oliver Lake’s primary credits, but Wikipedia lists it as a Sunny Murray album (Apple Cores, Philly Jazz, 1978). Which is the primary release and what was Lake’s role?
  • Clevont Fitzhubert (A Good Friend of Mine) (Black Saint, 1981) lists Lake credited for “Painting [Cover]” — did Lake exhibit visual artwork beyond album covers, and is there documentation of his visual art practice alongside his music career?
  • Lake’s name variation “Oliver Lake/NTU” appears on Discogs — does this refer to a specific touring or recording configuration of his group distinct from other Lake ensembles?
  • The Wikipedia article describes Lake as having appeared on Björk’s Celebrating Wood and Metal (MTV, 1997) — this title does not appear in the Discogs credits listing. Was this a separate release or an alternate title for the MTV Unplugged broadcast?
