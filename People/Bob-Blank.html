Producer, engineer, guitarist, and studio owner. Born / based in New York City. Opened Blank Tape Studios on the Lower East Side in 1976, which became a foundational recording space for the downtown NYC underground through the early 1980s. Later relocated to Connecticut and opened a second facility. Currently (as of Discogs profile) a competitive ballroom dancer.

Biography

Origins and the Latin / Disco Circuit (1972–1978)

Bob Blank’s earliest Discogs-traceable credits run from 1972. Through the mid-1970s he was principally active as a recording and mixing engineer on New York’s Latin music circuit — salsa, charanga, and boogaloo sessions for imprints including TR Records, Salsa Records, Coco Records, Cotique, and Vaya Records. Clients included Orquesta Cimarron, Joe Bataan (later), and numerous barrio-scene acts. By 1975–1976 he was also doing remix and mixing work on disco sessions for De-Lite Records and Disko-Mania.

This Latin-to-disco experience made Blank one of New York’s most versatile utility engineers going into 1977–1978, when the dance floor and the downtown art scene began to converge. His work on early Prelude Records disco sessions (Keep On Jumpin’, 1978; Musique II, 1979) and Salsoul Records sessions (Instant Funk, Dancin’ & Prancin’, Witch Doctor, Loleatta Holloway’s Love Sensation mixing credit, 1979–80) placed him at the center of the club-music production economy.

Blank Tape Studios and the ZE Records Circle (1978–1983)

The studio Blank opened at some point around 1976–1978 became a key node in what would be called the ZE Records downtown scene. His earliest ZE credit is the 1978 single Disco Clone (engineer/producer, ZE Records). From there, the studio became a through-line for the label’s early catalog:

  • Lizzy Mercier Descloux — Press Color (1979, ZE/Philips): Blank is credited “Recorded By.” The 15-day Blank Tape Studios session in February 1979 yielded one of the pivotal No Wave–adjacent LPs, co-produced by Descloux, D.J. Banes, and Erik Elliasson, engineered by Blank and Joe Arlotta.
  • James White & The Blacks — Off White (1979, ZE/Buddah): Engineer. Blank recorded James Chance and his band with personnel including George Scott (2) (bass), Jody Harris (guitar), Don Christensen (drums), and Pat Place (slide guitar).
  • Aural Exciters — Spooks In Space (1979, ZE): Engineer, Producer, Guitar. Blank is credited as performer (guitar) as well as producer. The Aural Exciters project was a Blank Productions vehicle; personnel included James Chance (horns), Andy Hernandez (vibraphone), Walter Steding (violin), Taana Gardner (vocals), and Allen Wentz.
  • James White’s Contort Yourself / (Tropical) Heatwave (1979, ZE): Engineer.
  • Lydia Lunch — Queen Of Siam (1980, ZE): Producer, Recorded By, Mixed By. One of his most prominent full-producer credits on the ZE catalog; personnel included Pat Irwin, Robert Quine (guitar), Dougie Bowne (drums), with executive producer Michael Zilkha.
  • Kid Creole And The Coconuts — Off The Coast Of Me (1980, ZE/Island): Engineer, Mixed By (credited as “Bobby Blank” and “Robert Blank”). Produced by August Darnell.
  • Cristina — Cristina (1980, ZE/Island): Engineer.
  • Kid Creole And The Coconuts — Tropical Gangsters (1982, ZE): Engineer [Chief], alongside Michael Frondelli. August Darnell produced.
  • James White’s Flaming Demonics (1983, ZE): Mixed By, Engineer. His wife Lola Blank (credited as backing vocalist on the same record) appears here too.
  • 8 Eyed Spy (1981, Fetish): Producer, Engineer (studio tracks). The 8 Eyed Spy LP combined Blank-produced studio sides featuring Lydia Lunch, Pat Irwin, and Jim Sclavunos with live recordings.

Beyond ZE, the studio attracted other downtown and post-punk clients. Walter Steding (1980, Red Star Records) and the Rosa Yemen EP (1979, Celluloid) both list Blank as engineer.

Salsoul / Disco Production Parallel Track (1979–1983)

While working the ZE circuit, Blank simultaneously ran a busy parallel track of Salsoul and Gold Mind Records sessions:

  • Loleatta Holloway — Love Sensation (1980, Gold Mind): Recording and Mixing Engineer. This track became a foundational house music sample.
  • Salsoul Orchestra sessions: mixing/recording on Dancin’ & Prancin’ (1979), The Funk Is On (1980), Funtaztik (1981), The Jammers (1982), and Instant Funk’s Witch Doctor (1979).
  • Larry Levan’s Greatest Mixes Volume Two (1980, Salsoul): Mixing Engineer — an early indication of his club-world currency.

Mid-1980s Diversification (1983–1989)

After the core ZE period, Blank moved across a wider range of R&B, reggae, and dance-pop production:

  • Weekend (1983, Sleeping Bag Records): Engineer, Producer. Class Action featuring Christine Wiltshire; co-produced with Lola Blank.
  • Over Like A Fat Rat (1982, Vanguard): Producer.
  • Chinese Rap (1982, Top Flight): Mixed By, Producer.
  • Event Horizon (1982, Sire): Producer.
  • Go Deh Yaka (1983, Easy Street): Mixing engineer — a Monyaka reggae-funk track.
  • Tropical Gangsters (1982): Chief engineer as above.
  • Willie Colon (via A&M): Recording/mixing engineer on Set Fire To Me and She Don’t Know I’m Alive (1986).
  • Jump Street Records productions: Once You Got Me Going, In The Life, Wax The Van (1986–87) — producer credits.
  • The Sleeping Bag Sessions (Sleeping Bag Records, 2017 archival release): Producer.

Blank also appears on the 1989 ZE/Island retrospective Zetrospective: Dancing In The Face Of Adversity as tape editor, and contributed liner note testimonials to the 2011 Arthur Russell anthology 24 → 24 Music: The Definitive Arthur Russell (Get On Down).

The Blank Generation Compilation and Legacy (2010–)

In 2010, Blank partnered with DJhistory.com and Strut Records to release The Blank Generation (Blank Tapes NYC 1975–1987), a double-CD / double-LP compilation curated by Blank with compilation producers Bill Brewster, Frank Broughton, and Quinton Scott. It served as the definitive archival document of the Blank Tape Studios era and gathered tracks across the No Wave, disco-punk, Latin, and downtown-dance spectrum.

He subsequently contributed photography to the August Darnell career retrospective Going Places — The August Darnell Years 1974–1983 (Strut, 2008).

Blank’s Lola group (Discogs ID 61264; active 1987–2020) released material on Jump Street Records, Syncopate, and Vinylmania, often with Lola Blank as co-producer or performer.
