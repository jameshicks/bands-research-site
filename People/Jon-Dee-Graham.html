Jon Dee Graham

American guitarist, singer, and songwriter. Born February 28, 1959, Quemado, Texas. Died March 27, 2026. Discogs artist ID: 447985.

Central figure in the Austin music scene across five decades, spanning punk (The Skunks), roots-rock (The True Believers), a long solo career, and steady session work for Americana and alternative artists.

Biography

Early Years — The Skunks (1977–1980)

Graham moved to Austin to attend the University of Texas and co-founded The Skunks in 1977 — one of the city’s earliest punk groups. The Skunks lineup included Jesse Sublett (bass/vocals), Billy Blackmon (drums), Doug Murray, Greg Murray, Eddie Muñoz (later of The Plimsouls), and Bill Mansell. Graham produced several of the band’s early releases on their own Skunks Records label:

Graham left the band in 1980.

The True Believers (mid-1980s)

Graham joined The True Believers alongside Alejandro Escovedo and Javier Escovedo. The full lineup: Alejandro Escovedo (lead vocals, guitar), Javier Escovedo (vocals, guitar), Jon Dee Graham (vocals, guitar), Denny DeGorio (bass), Kevin Foley (drums), with JD Foster and Rey Washam also connected. Their 1986 self-titled album on EMI America / Rounder Records was produced by Jim Dickinson and engineered by Stuart Sullivan.

Los Angeles Period (1987–early 1990s)

Graham relocated to Los Angeles around 1987 and joined John Doe (2)‘s touring band. He appears as a band member (guitar) on John Doe’s 1990 DGC album Meet John Doe (prod. Davitt Sigerson), alongside Richard Lloyd, Tony Marsico, and Jeff Donavan. He also appeared on the DGC compilation A Matter Of Degrees, A Shot & Three Beers (1990).

During this period he worked with Exene Cervenka and contributed session guitar (lap steel, dobro, bottleneck) to Simon Bonney’s Forever (1992, Mute), produced by Gareth Jones. He also played pedal steel on Exene Cervenka / the Wrecks release Running Sacred (1990, Rhino) and contributed to the Elektra sampler There Can Only Be One (1992).

In 2000, Graham was named Songwriter of the Year at the Austin Music Awards.

Also recorded a live concert document, “Musikwerkstatt” Neustadt/Weinstrasse (Germany) 24.5.2002 (Blue Rose Records, 2002), and Full, released in Germany 2006 (Blue Rose) and the US on Freedom Records (9).

The Resentments

Graham was a founding member of The Resentments, an Austin Sunday-night residency collective that recorded a self-titled album in 2003 (Freedom Records 9). The group — a rotating cast of Austin stalwarts — included Jud Newcomb, Stephen Bruton, Bruce Hughes, and John Chipman. Engineered and mixed by Stuart Sullivan.
