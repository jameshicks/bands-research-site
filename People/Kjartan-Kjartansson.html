Kjartan Kjartansson

Origin: Iceland Role: Recording engineer, mixer, mastering engineer, live sound engineer Discogs artist ID: 761890 Active: 1982–2023 (per Discogs credits) Name variations on credits: Kjartan Kjartan, Kjartan-Kjartan, Kjarntan Kjartansson, Kjarntand Kjartansson

Overview

Kjartan Kjartansson is an Icelandic recording, mixing, and live sound engineer based in Reykjavík. His credit history on Discogs spans four decades (1982–2023), though the total is modest — twelve releases — suggesting he is either selective, works substantially in live sound and sessions not captured in release data, or both.

He was active at the heart of Iceland’s post-punk underground in the early-to-mid 1980s, recording the Kukl Söngull 7” (1983) and the Vonbrigði debut EP (1982), both on Gramm. These two releases mark the nucleus of the early Reykjavík punk/experimental scene that produced Björk and Einar Örn Benediktsson, and later The Sugarcubes. His continued engineering credit on Life’s Too Good (1988, One Little Indian) — the Sugarcubes’ debut album — bridges his earliest 1982 work to the band’s international breakthrough.

The Crowleymass credit (1987, Maldoror) places him in the orbit of Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson and Current 93 / David Tibet, reflecting the cross-channel Icelandic–UK experimental connections of that period.

Post-1988 credits are sporadic and varied: a technician credit on a 1993 Frostbite (2) EP on One Little Indian; a 2005 engineer credit on a Norwegian rock album; sound design for an Icelandic music documentary; and then a return to Vonbrigði for a 2021 career-retrospective LP — the same band he recorded 39 years earlier.

Notable Sessions

Kukl — Söngull (1983)

Kjartan’s most historically significant engineering credit. Söngull is a two-track 7” — the only Kukl release issued in Iceland on Gramm, and the earliest physical documentation of a lineup that included Björk (voice), Einar Örn Benediktsson (voice, trumpet), Guðlaugur Kristinn Óttarsson (guitar), Siggi Baldursson (drums), Birgir Mogensen (bass), and Einar Melax (keyboards, guitar). Kjartan is credited on the sleeve as “Kjartan-Kjartan,” the nickname variant that appears across his early Gramm-era work. He handled both recording and mixing.

Vonbrigði — Vonbrigði (1982)

The earliest Discogs credit. Vonbrigði — a separate Reykjavík punk band, not connected to the Kukl circle — recorded their debut EP at what was presumably a Gramm-affiliated facility with Kjartan as engineer (credited “Kjartan Kjartan”). He returned to record the band’s career-spanning retrospective Hanagal in 2021, nearly four decades later.

The Sugarcubes — Life’s Too Good (1988)

One of five engineers credited on the debut LP. The Sugarcubes formed directly from Kukl’s dissolution in 1986, so Kjartan’s Life’s Too Good credit is a continuity from his Söngull work: the same core personalities (Björk, Einar Örn Benediktsson, Siggi Baldursson, Þór Eldon, Bragi Ólafsson), now at a larger London-anchored production (producers: Derek Birkett and Ray Shulman). Credited here as “Kjarntan Kjartansson” — one of four name-spelling variants in Discogs.

Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson / Current 93 — Crowleymass (1987)

Mixed by Kjartan. The album was recorded by Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson at Studio Syrland in Iceland, with compositions by Hilmar and David Tibet (Current 93). Rose McDowall contributes backing vocals. Kjartan’s mixing role places him in the flow of occult/neofolk material that Hilmar was producing in Iceland for UK experimental labels during this period.

Screaming Masterpiece (2005)

Icelandic music documentary for which Kjartan served as sound designer. The film is a broad survey of Icelandic pop/rock from the 1980s through 2000s, with performances and interviews from Björk, Sigur Rós, The Sugarcubes, mum, and many others — functioning as a kind of document of the very scene in which Kjartan had been active since 1982.
