Susie Ibarra

Discogs ID: 308171
Born: November 15, 1970, Anaheim, California
Origin: USA (raised in Houston, TX; based in New York since 1989)

American percussionist, composer, and documentary filmmaker. One of the most significant drummers to emerge from the downtown New York experimental and free-jazz scene of the 1990s, Ibarra is known for her extraordinary dynamic range, integration of Philippine kulintang gong traditions with avant-garde jazz and contemporary classical music, and a career that spans free improvisation, opera, and ecological field recording. She received the 2025 Pulitzer Prize for Music for Sky Islands (premiered at Asia Society, NYC, 2024). (Source: Wikipedia)

Named “Best Percussionist” in the 2010 DownBeat International Readers Poll and “Best Percussionist, Rising Star” in the 2009 and 2011 DownBeat Critics Poll.

Background

The youngest of five children of Filipino immigrant physicians, Ibarra began piano at age four, sang in church and school choirs, and played in a punk rock band in high school. She attended Sarah Lawrence College in the late 1980s, where a Sun Ra performance sparked her interest in jazz. She also studied at Mannes School of Music and Goddard College (B.A. in music). Based in New York since 1989.

She studied with jazz drummers Vernel Fournier, Earl “Buster” Smith, and Milford Graves, and studied Philippine Kulintang music under NEA Heritage Artist Danongan “Danny” Kalanduyan in both the US and Cotabato, Mindanao. (Source: Wikipedia)

Indigenous Music and Ecology Work

From 2004 onward, Ibarra began field recording kulintang gong music in the Philippines. In 2007 she received an Asian Cultural Council Fellowship to research indigenous and folkloric music in the Philippines. From 2008–2009 she and Roberto Juan Rodriguez filmed seven endangered indigenous tribes there, also documenting conservation efforts for the near-extinct Philippine eagle. In 2009 they founded Song of the Bird King (a preservation organization). The Electric Kulintang ensemble (Discogs ID: 1054486) reflects these researches.

In 2018 the Asian Cultural Council supported her Himalayan Glacier Soundscapes project, recording the sounds of glacial recession along the Ganges River. (Source: Wikipedia)
