Bert Jansch

Born: Herbert Jansch, 3 November 1943, Glasgow, Scotland
Died: 5 October 2011, Hampstead, London
Discogs ID: 363204
Groups: Pentangle, Bert Jansch Conundrum

Overview

Bert Jansch was a Scottish folk guitarist, singer-songwriter, and founding member of Pentangle. Born in Glasgow and raised in the West Pilton area of Edinburgh, he came to prominence in London in the early 1960s through the folk-club circuit — particularly Bunjies and Les Cousins — developing a singular fingerpicking style that fused folk, blues, and early music. His 1965 debut LP on Transatlantic Records sold 150,000 copies and was immediately recognised as a landmark; Jimmy Page called it “so far ahead of what everyone else was doing.” Neil Young cited Jansch as his favourite acoustic guitarist, comparing him to Jimi Hendrix on electric. Paul Simon’s recording of “Anji” (on Sounds of Silence) was directly inspired by Jansch’s version. Nick Drake, Donovan, Johnny Marr, and Mike Oldfield all named him a primary influence.

In London, Jansch shared a flat with John Renbourn, and the two developed a duo interplay described as “folk baroque” — resulting in the 1966 collaborative LP Bert and John. With the addition of singer Jacqui McShee, bassist Danny Thompson, and drummer Terry Cox, they formed Pentangle in 1967, one of the most ambitious British folk-jazz ensembles of the era. After Pentangle’s break-up in 1973, Jansch continued as a solo artist and under the Conundrum project, working with changing lineups until his death from lung cancer in 2011. He received BBC Radio 2 Lifetime Achievement Awards both for his solo work (2001) and as a member of Pentangle (2007). Rolling Stone ranked him No. 94 in its 2003 list of the 100 Greatest Guitarists.

Key influences cited by Jansch: Davy Graham, Anne Briggs, Big Bill Broonzy, Brownie McGhee, Martin Carthy. His song “Blackwaterside” was adapted (without credit) by Jimmy Page as Led Zeppelin’s “Black Mountain Side.”
