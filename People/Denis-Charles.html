Denis Charles

Denis Charles

Aug 11, 20268 min read

Denis Charles

Born: December 4, 1933, Saint Croix, US Virgin Islands
Died: March 25, 1998, New York City
Real name: Denis Alphonso Charles
Discogs artist ID: 319789
Also credited as: Dennis Charles, D. Charles, Denis “Jazz” Charles, Dennis “King” Charles

Free jazz drummer. One of the first-generation avant-garde drummers in New York, central to Cecil Taylor’s earliest recordings and a long-running presence in the downtown free scene through the 1980s and 1990s. His brother is artist ID 2213905 on Discogs.

Early Career and Cecil Taylor (1957–1963)

Charles arrived in New York from the Virgin Islands and quickly became part of the circle around Cecil Taylor, a pianist then working out of the East Village and still largely unknown outside a small avant-garde contingent. He appeared on the first Taylor recordings to see commercial release.

Jazz Advance (1957, Transition) — the Cecil Taylor Quartet’s debut, one of the landmark documents of early free jazz. Personnel: Taylor (piano), Steve Lacy (soprano saxophone, here credited as Steve Lacey), Buell Neidlinger (bass), Charles (drums). Produced by Tom Wilson; recorded by Stephen Fassett.

The World of Cecil Taylor (Candid, 1961) marked Charles’s last major studio appearance with Taylor for some years. Shepp’s presence on that date connects Charles to the broader New York free scene that was coalescing around that period.

School Days (Emanem, 1975) — a 1963 live session released much later. Co-billed with Steve Lacy, Roswell Rudd, and Henry Grimes; all four are primary credited artists. Recorded by Paul Haines and Vashkar Nandi; produced by Martin Davidson of Emanem. This documents Charles working in a collective free setting outside the Taylor context, the quartet performing Monk material freely.

The 1970s: Sonny Rollins and Smaller Engagements

Charles’s Discogs credits thin out through the mid-1960s and 1970s, suggesting he was working in contexts that were less documented. He appears on:

  • The Magic of Ju-Ju (Archie Shepp, 1967, Impulse!) — percussion credit
  • New York City R&B (Sonny Rollins, 1972, Barnaby Records) — listed under Sonny Rollins’s ensemble; Charles on drums
  • Simple Facts (Wayne Horvitz, 1980, Theatre For Your Mother) — an early New York downtown session with Wayne Horvitz (piano/electronics), William Parker (bass), John Zorn (alto, game calls on selected tracks), Roy Campbell (trumpet), Dave Sewelson (saxophones)

The Soul Note / hat Hut Years: Billy Bang and the 1980s Scene

The most productive documented period of Charles’s later career is concentrated in the early 1980s, largely around the Soul Note label and the milieu around Billy Bang.

Rainbow Gladiator (1981, Soul Note) — Billy Bang Quintet. Personnel: Bang (violin), Charles Tyler (alto and baritone saxophone), Michele Rosewoman (piano), Wilber Morris (bass), Charles (drums). Produced by Giovanni Bonandrini.

Invitation (1982, Soul Note) — Billy Bang Quintet. Personnel: Bang (violin), Tyler (alto and baritone saxophone), Curtis Clark (piano), Morris (bass), Charles (drums). Liner notes and photography by Valerie Wilmer.

Bangception (1983, hat MUSICS) — duo album, co-billed Bang & Charles (Charles credited as Dennis Charles). Bang (violin), Charles (drums). Produced by Pia & Werner X. Uehlinger. One of the most direct documents of the Bang–Charles working relationship, stripped to two voices.

The Flame (1982, Soul Note) — trio co-billed Steve Lacy Feat. Bobby Few And Dennis Charles. Personnel: Steve Lacy (soprano saxophone), Bobby Few (piano), Charles (drums). Produced by Giovanni Bonandrini; liner notes by Nat Hentoff.

Capers (1981, Hat Hut Records) — Steve Lacy. Personnel: Lacy (soprano saxophone), Ronnie Boykins (bass), Charles (drums). Recorded at a concert produced by Verna Gillis; released on Werner X. Uehlinger’s Hat Hut imprint.

The Kill (1982, Soul Note) — Peter Kuhn Quartet. Personnel: Peter Kuhn (soprano clarinet, bass clarinet), Wayne Horvitz (piano, synthesizer), William Parker (bass), Charles (drums, voice). Produced by Kuhn; executive producer Bonandrini.

Konstanze’s Delight (1983, Soul Note) — Jemeel Moondoc Sextet. Personnel: Jemeel Moondoc (alto saxophone), Roy Campbell (trumpet), Khan Jamal (vibraphone), William Parker (bass), Charles (drums), Ellen Christi (voice). Produced by Moondoc.

Nostalgia In Times Square (1986, Soul Note) — Jemeel Moondoc Quintet. Personnel: Moondoc (alto and soprano saxophone), Bern Nix (guitar), Rahn Burton (piano), William Parker (bass), Charles (drums). Produced by Bonandrini.

Wilber Force (1984, DIW) — co-billed Wilber Morris, Dennis Charles, David Murray. Personnel: Morris (bass), Charles (drums), Murray (tenor saxophone, bass clarinet). Produced by Morris. Released on the Japanese DIW label.

Intensive Care (1984, Cadillac Records) — The Jazz Doctors. Personnel: Billy Bang (violin), Frank Lowe (tenor saxophone), Rafael Garrett (bass), Charles (drums). Liner notes and photography by Valerie Wilmer.

Later Work (1989–1998)

Charles continued recording into the 1990s, often in trio settings with younger players who had grown up on the free jazz tradition he had helped establish.

Breath Rhyme (1989, Silkheart) — Rob Brown Trio. Personnel: Rob Brown (alto saxophone), William Parker (bass), Charles (drums). Brown’s leader debut on Silkheart.

Air (1990, Candid) — Cecil Taylor. A Candid reissue project drawing on the original 1960–61 sessions; Charles appears on tracks 2–6, with Sunny Murray on track 1. The full personnel mirrors the early Candid recordings: Taylor (piano), Neidlinger (bass), Archie Shepp (tenor saxophone on selected tracks).

Collective Improvisations (1994, Bleu Regard) — Wilber Morris Trio. Personnel: Morris (bass), Charles (drums, here credited as Denis “Jazz” Charles), Charles Tyler (alto and baritone saxophone). Recorded in France.

The Discogs record shows Charles active through 1998 (the year of his death), with his last credited appearance being Love for Sale (Blue Note, 1998) — though this is likely an earlier recording released posthumously or a compilation.

Open Questions

  • Specific dates of early New York arrival; biographical details on his path from Saint Croix to the Taylor circle are not in the Discogs record
  • Work between approximately 1963 and 1967 is largely undocumented in the DB
  • Love for Sale (1998, Blue Note) credit — unclear if this was recorded before his March 1998 death or is a posthumous release of archival material
  • Elliott Levin Quartet, Claude Lawrence Trio, Bob Ackerman Trio, Luther Thomas Quartet/Quintet, Patricia Nicholson Ensemble, John Blum Astrogeny Quartet, New York 3 — all groups he belonged to per Discogs; releases from these contexts have not been individually checked
  • Discogs lists a Queen Mary (Silkheart) with Charles as composer, drummer, and percussionist — no year given; likely from the late 1980s Silkheart relationship but details unknown
