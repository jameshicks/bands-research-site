Leon West

Discogs ID: 799219 Also known as: Secondson (performing/production alias; separate Discogs entry: ID 184546) Real name: Leon West Origin: Wales (born, lives, and as his Discogs profile notes, “will eventually die in Wales”)

Welsh musician, producer, engineer, and designer. Active since at least 2000 in Welsh hip-hop, underground rap, and post-punk adjacent circles. His roles span performance, production, audio engineering, mastering, layout, photography, A&R, and project coordination, often across many of these simultaneously on a single release. His alias “Secondson” (Discogs ID 184546) is used as the performing/production persona and has its own separate Discogs artist page.

Biography

Early 2000s — Newport hip-hop and SFDB

Leon West’s earliest Discogs-documented work dates to 2000, when he receives a cluster of credits — A&R, artwork, photography, and remastering — on Pussy Whipped Breakz (Peace Attack Records, 2000), a Welsh hip-hop compilation that logs 76 pressing variants on Discogs, suggesting considerable underground circulation.

Through 2001–2006 he was the hub of the SFDB label (Discogs ID 22670, 39 releases), a Newport-based imprint whose roster ran to Welsh hip-hop acts. His two main groups from this era were:

Beefeaters (Discogs ID 184547) — Welsh hip-hop collective. Members: Leon West, DJ Big Mac (ID 184548), Lil’ Angry Man (ID 185038), Vee Kay (ID 426102). One documented release: Can Of Worms (SFDB, 2002). West handled recording, mixing, mastering, photography, layout, coordination, and writing; Secondson produced; DJ Big Mac handled scratches.

Fleapit (Discogs ID 184670) — Members: Leon West, Matt Herbert (ID 66765), Richard Bradley (11) (ID 16428220). Two releases on SFDB: Mangle To This (2001) and Music From The Ditch (2002). Secondson produced both; Matt Herbert receives a Written-By credit on Music From The Ditch — a notable connection given Herbert’s separate profile as a UK electronic producer/composer. West recorded, mixed, mastered, and photographed both records.

As the performing alias Secondson, he released:

  • Valley Of The Crows (SFDB, 2003) — 7” single, 19 pressings logged
  • High Stakes (SFDB, 2004)
  • Secondson & His Orchestra (SFDB, 2004) — double LP featuring Goldie Lookin Chain, Jehst, Sway, Task Force (2), Lewis Parker, and others

The Secondson & His Orchestra LP is the key document of his production world: 12 tracks, each featuring a different rapper or MC, with West credited for coordination, mixing, mastering, writing, design, layout, photography, and A&R — covering virtually every non-performance role on the record. Goldie Lookin Chain appear on the closing track “Famous As Fuck.”

Also as Secondson he produced releases by Humurak D Gritty (Rags To Rags, SFDB, 2005 — West handling A&R, coordination, design, layout, mastering, mixing, recording) and co-ran Plague (below).

Goldie Lookin Chain

Goldie Lookin Chain (Discogs ID 184984) — Newport, Wales novelty/comedy hip-hop group. Discogs lists three members: Adam Hussain (ID 1331629), Leon West, and Rhys Hutchings (ID 7045509). Their career arc:

  • Self-released debut Don’t Blame The Chain (2001, 31 pressing variants)
  • The Manifesto (Gold Dust Gramophonics, 2003)
  • Greatest Hits, Guns Don’t Kill People Rappers Do, Your Mother’s Got A Penis, You Knows I Loves You Baby (all Atlantic, 2004) — major-label peak period
  • Safe As Fuck, Your Missus Is A Nutter, Love Kraft (Atlantic/Epic, 2005)
  • Subsequent indie releases through 1983 Records (2009–2019)

West takes photography credits on most of the Atlantic/Epic releases (2004–2005). His role on Greatest Hits (Atlantic, 2004), the breakthrough major-label release, is Photography. GLC also appear on the Secondson LP, suggesting an overlapping Newport scene.

Plague

Plague (Discogs ID 202239) — Members: Leon West and Xeno (ID 235073). Group also connected to Pay As You Go Cartel (ID 24971) via Xeno. Releases:

  • Anywhere (Pay As U Go Recordings, 2001)
  • The Avenger (Solid City Records, 2001)
  • Plague (SFDB, 2014) — a later revival; West credited for layout, mixing, mastering, photography, writing; Secondson credited for bass and production

Coppertree Records / 3 Syllables Records period (2007–2011)

From 2007, West appears regularly as coordinator and layout person for Coppertree Records releases, including work by unidentified acts (Aw Come Aw Wry, Little Amber Bottles, Time Being [uncredited], Lost And Found, Fear Yourself). From 2010 he performs a similar function for 3 Syllables Records on releases including Dinero Severo, Guards EP, Now More Than Ever, Oh Vanille/Ova Nil, and Heavy Meadow (2011).

One Little Indian — Consultant (2007–2009)

The most unexpected chapter of West’s credit trail: between 2007 and 2009 he received a “Consultant” role on approximately 30–35 vinyl releases as part of One Little Indian’s large-scale DMM (Direct Metal Mastering) vinyl reissue campaign. The releases cover the bulk of OLI’s catalogue — Björk’s entire solo discography (Debut, Post, Homogenic, Vespertine, Medúlla, Drawing Restraint 9, Volta, Volta singles, Selmasongs, Gling-Gló, Telegram, Jóga), The Sugarcubes (Life’s Too Good, Here Today Tomorrow Next Week, Stick Around For Joy, It’s-It), plus Skunk Anansie (Stoosh/*Banana), Rocket From The Crypt, Sneaker Pimps, Throwing Muses, Dan Sartain, and others.

The two Kukl albums appear within this campaign:

Kukl — The Eye (DMM LP, One Little Indian, 2008 — release ID 1970692, cat. TPLP325DMM) Credits (additional): Dada Nana (artwork), Einar Melax (pipes, bells, harmonium, strings), Birgir Mogensen (bass), Leon West (Consultant), Trix/11 (drums), Tony Cook (2) (engineer), Einar Örn (percussion, voice), Godkrist (2) (strings), Björk (woodwind, voice).

Kukl — Holidays In Europe (The Naughty Nought) (DMM LP, One Little Indian, 2008 — release ID 1970687, cat. TPLP326DMM) Credits (additional): Birgir Mogensen (bass), Leon West (Consultant), Siggi Baldursson (drums), Mel Jefferson (engineer), Penny Rimbaud (producer), Godkrist (2) (strings), Einar Melax (DX7, piano), Björk (vocals), Einar Örn (vocals, trumpet).

The “Consultant” credit also appears on one 2007 OLI release — Scissors In My Pocket (release ID 3744895) — suggesting the role began just before the main 2008 DMM campaign. Two additional 2008 consultant credits appear on Clean Up Records releases (Becoming X by Sneaker Pimps; Splinter by Sneaker Pimps), which shared some OLI infrastructure. A further three 2009 OLI Múm releases (Citrus, Kurr, Love In The Time Of Science) also carry the consultant credit.

The specific nature of the consultancy is not described in Discogs credits — it could relate to vinyl quality assessment, DMM process oversight, coordination with the mastering facility, or A&R advisory. Given West’s established roles as mastering engineer and project coordinator on his own SFDB releases, a technical/logistics role in the vinyl production chain is plausible, but unconfirmed.

Later engineering work (2014–2025)

From 2014 onward West returns to engineering/mastering credits in a more straightforward capacity:

  • B-Side (Dental Records Digital, 2014) — Artwork
  • IV (Spun Out Of Control, 2017) — Mixed By, Mastered By
  • V (Burning Witches Records, 2017) — Engineer, Mastered By, Mixed By
  • Caes Kalpurnia (Bruton Sound, 2022) — Mixed By, Mastered By
  • Hiraeth & Loathing (Specialist Subject Records, 2022) — Mastered By
  • Oso Jugoso (Specialist Subject Records, 2022) — Mastered By
  • Pando (Strangetown Records, 2025) — Photography
  • Sonic Dissidence (Imprint Music Ltd., 2025) — Mixed By
