Danny Blume is a New York-based guitarist, recording engineer, and producer whose career spans the early 1980s to the present. He has worked across a wide stylistic range — from downtown no-wave-adjacent rock with The Fertile Crescent in the early 1990s, through electronic and avant-jazz production with his duo GoodandEvil (with Chris Castagno), to big-band jazz orchestration with Ghost Train Orchestra and John Zorn-sphere projects. As a session guitarist and engineer he has appeared on records by Kid Creole And The Coconuts, Uri Caine, Medeski Martin & Wood, Vernon Reid, and many others.

Early Career

Danny Blume (then credited as “Dan Blumenfeld”) appears on the 1986 debut by Tom Keegan And The Language (Thwakk Records), playing lead guitar, rhythm guitar, and vocals. By 1990 he was contributing guitar to Kid Creole And The Coconuts’ CBS album Private Waters In The Great Divide.

The Fertile Crescent (1991–1992)

The Fertile Crescent — Blume with bassist/vocalist Erik Sanko and drummer Ben Perowsky — released two versions of their self-titled album: a cassette-only “Not On Label” issue in 1991, and a 1992 CD on Knitting Factory Works (executive produced by Michael Dorf, produced and engineered by Roger Moutenot). Blume played guitar and backing vocals.

Blume also contributed guitar to the Get Shorty original soundtrack (1995, Antilles/MGM), alongside John Medeski, Erik Sanko, John Lurie (score), and Steven Bernstein.

Liminal (2) (1995)

With Gregor Asch, Blume formed the electronic/experimental duo Liminal (2), whose sole documented release is Nosferatu (1995, Knitting Factory Works). The record drew in downtown figures DJ Olive (turntables/effects) and Lloop (sampler). Blume produced and played guitar, tape, and bass (one-string duct tape bass).

GoodandEvil (2000–2007)

GoodandEvil — Danny Blume and Chris Castagno — was an active production and performance duo throughout the 2000s. They engineered, mixed, and produced Elysian Fields’ Queen Of The Meadow (2000, Jetset Records), produced Felix Da Housecat’s Rocket Ride LP (2004, Emperor Norton, with co-producer Tommie Sunshine), and worked extensively with the Yohimbe Brothers (Vernon Reid + DJ Logic) on The Tao Of Yo (2004, Thirsty Ear). Other notable credits include Devin Dazzle & The Neon Fever (2004) and the Klezmer-folk crossover Wonder Wheel Lyrics By Woody Guthrie (2006, Jewish Music Group).

The GoodandEvil Sessions LP (2003, Thirsty Ear, on the Blue Series Continuum imprint) documented the duo’s own work with jazz collaborators including Matthew Shipp, William Parker, Joshua Roseman, and Roy Campbell. Executive producer was Peter Gordon (3).

Sum (1998)

Sum was Blume’s duo with Chris Kelly (2). Their sole Discogs-documented release is Nag Champa (1998, Caipirinha Productions).

Ghost Train Orchestra (2011–present)

Blume is listed among the large ensemble of Brian Carpenter’s Ghost Train Orchestra, an early jazz revivalist big band. He is credited as producer on their debut Hothouse Stomp (2011, Accurate), alongside conductor/leader Brian Carpenter. The band later released Songs And Symphoniques – The Music Of Moondog (2023, Cantaloupe Music) and Who Is The Sky? (2025, Matador).
