Jaleel Bunton

Discogs ID: 189108
Origin: Brooklyn, New York
Name variations: Bunton, J. Bunton, Jaleel, Jaleel From TV On The Radio

American multi-instrumentalist, drummer, and songwriter. Primary drummer of TV On The Radio from the debut album onward; also a credited guitarist, bassist, keyboardist, organist, Rhodes player, synthesizer programmer, and backing vocalist across the TVOTR catalog. One of the most instrumentally versatile members of the band, his role expanded significantly from album to album. Also active in the side project The Pleasure Unit.

TV On The Radio

Bunton joined TV On The Radio in time for their debut album Desperate Youth, Blood Thirsty Babes (2004, Touch And Go), appearing on drums for select tracks alongside the core of Tunde Adebimpe and Dave Sitek. By Return To Cookie Mountain (2006) he was a full-band member with a locked-in role.

His instrumental range expanded with each album. Dear Science (2008) was the peak of this — across that record he played drums, guitar, Rhodes, organ, bass, and synthesizer, co-arranged four string sections, and wrote the music for the track “Crying.”

Arrangements and composition

On Dear Science, Bunton co-arranged string parts for four tracks alongside cellists and string players:

  • “Stork & Owl” — string arrangement with Claudia Chopek and Janis Shen
  • “Golden Age” — string arrangement with Chopek and Shen
  • “Family Tree” — string arrangement with Chopek and Shen
  • “Love Dog” — string arrangement with Chopek and Shen

He is also listed as writing the music for “Crying” (track 2, Dear Science).

The Pleasure Unit

Bunton’s side project, operating under the name The Pleasure Unit (Discogs ID 6134818). Two releases documented:

  • Mercy (2013) — single, Federal Prism
  • Departures (2014) — Superball Music

Other Credits

Bunton appears on a small number of releases outside TVOTR. Guest credits include work on Mutual Horse (2018, Dangerbird Records) and Run Deep (2018, Motéma Music), and on a 2003 !K7 single (backing vocals, guitar, keys). The precise nature of these credits (guest vs. featured player) is not fully resolved from Discogs alone.
