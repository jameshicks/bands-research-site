Robert Quine

Discogs ID: 50098 Born: 30 December 1942, Akron, Ohio Died: 31 May 2004, Manhattan (heroin, ruled suicide)

American guitarist. Central to the late-1970s NYC punk scene and early-1980s post-punk. Co-founder of Richard Hell & The Voidoids; guitarist on Lou Reed’s The Blue Mask and Legendary Hearts; member of supergroup Dim Stars. Also appeared on SPY Records SPY 003 — Lester Bangs “Let It Blurt” (1979), on the same single as Jody Harris.

Before moving to New York he bootlegged Velvet Underground concerts in 1969; those recordings were eventually released as a 3-CD set.

Richard Hell & The Voidoids (1976–1982)

Co-founded the band with Richard Hell (bass, vocals), Ivan Julian (guitar), and Marc Bell (drums). Bell left after Blank Generation to join the Ramones as Marky Ramone; Fred Maher took the drum seat for Destiny Street.

Production: Teenage Jesus And The Jerks (1978–1979)

Migraine was Lydia Lunch’s own label (not a third-party imprint); per the fromthearchives.com Lydia Lunch discography, “Migraine was Lydia’s label.” [source: https://fromthearchives.com/ll/discography1.html]

Lunch has described how the production connection came about: she saw the first Richard Hell & The Voidoids show when she was seventeen and immediately sought out Quine: “I went right up to Bob Quine, who was baldheaded, had sunglasses and a screwdriver in his pocket, and said, ‘You are God.‘” She credits that encounter as the seed of the working relationship — “I think because I was his first fan and a baby-faced killer, he kind of took a shine to me.” [source: https://www.moredarkthanshark.org/eno_int_an-may15.html — mirror of ArtNews interview, as of 2026-09-28] She later called him “the best guitar player the world has had, in my opinion” and said of the production: “Much better was being produced by Robert Quine.” (That last comment was contrasting Quine’s work with Brian Eno’s production on the No New York tracks.)

Quine’s own account in the Perfect Sound Forever interview is brief: “Yeah, it was a nice scene for a minute. I produced Teenage Jesus (with Lydia Lunch) and DNA (with Arto Lindsay and Ikue Mori).” He adds: “My personality isn’t really suited for being a producer. Groups need hand-holding and counseling and I can’t do that.” [source: https://www.furious.com/perfect/quine.html]

So the connection was personal: Lunch approached Quine at an early Voidoids show, and their mutual admiration in the overlapping CBGB/no-wave scene led to him producing her band’s Migraine releases. No formal account of who initiated the specific recording sessions has surfaced in any interview found.

Quine handled most of the LP’s tracks; co-producer Woody Payne produced only two tracks (“Burning Rubber,” “Red Alert”). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Jesus_and_the_Jerks_(album)]
