Jost Gebers

Jost Gebers

Aug 02, 20266 min read

Jost Gebers

Discogs ID: 293826 Born: October 26, 1940, Berlin Died: September 15, 2023, Borken Also credited as: Wolfgang Walter (alias — see Research Queue) Name variations on Discogs: Gebers, J, J Gebers, J. Gebers, J.Gebers

German producer, recording engineer, label co-founder, and visual documenter. Co-founder and longtime director of FMP (Free Music Production), Berlin, which he ran alongside his partner Dagmar Gebers. The primary production force behind FMP’s documentation of European free improvisation from the label’s founding through the 2000s — his credits span producer, recording engineer, mixer, layout designer, and photographer across well over 200 releases, nearly all on FMP.

Overview

Gebers was the operational and creative backbone of FMP. The label emerged from the Berlin “Workshop Freie Musik” concert series (which ran from 1969), and Gebers wore every hat needed to get recordings onto vinyl: booking sessions, operating the recording equipment, handling layout and cover design, and frequently supplying the cover photographs. His credit lists across the catalog often read “Recorded By, Producer, Design, Photography By” on a single release — a one-man production infrastructure for a movement that had none.

The breadth of the FMP roster he documented is extraordinary: Peter Brötzmann, Alexander von Schlippenbach, Evan Parker, Cecil Taylor, Han Bennink, Fred Van Hove, Paul Lovens, Peter Kowald, Paul Lytton, Rüdiger Carl, Irene Schweizer, Albert Mangelsdorff, Manfred Schoof, Kenny Wheeler, William Parker, Andrew Cyrille, Paul Rutherford (2), Hans Reichel, Ulrich Gumpert, Joëlle Léandre, Phil Minton, Barry Guy, Günter Sommer, Ernst-Ludwig Petrowsky, Johannes Bauer, Gerd Dudek, Günter Christmann, Sven-Åke Johansson, Wolfgang Fuchs, and many more. His Discogs credit history covers 1969–2022.

Outside FMP, Gebers occasionally appeared on recordings for other labels — John Stevens (2)’ 1975 Away on Vertigo, Derek Bailey’s Saxophone Solos on Incus (1976), the Soul Note catalog (several sessions including work with Hamid Drake), Mats Gustafsson’s Nohome on Trost Records, and a handful of other European free improvisation imprints.

Non-FMP Credits

While almost exclusively an FMP figure, Gebers’ name appears on a handful of releases for other labels:

  • Incus Records — Saxophone Solos (Derek Bailey, 1976): recorded by
  • Vertigo — John Stevens’ Away (1975): recorded by
  • BV Haast Records — Live In Berlin (Han Bennink / Pierre Favre, 1976): recorded by, cover design (co-released with FMP)
  • Black Saint — Live In Berlin (1984): recording engineer
  • Soul Note — For Olim (1987), Olu Iwa (1994): recording engineer
  • ECM Records — Berlin Contemporary Jazz Orchestra (1990): layout only
  • Uhlklang (FMP sublabel) — several productions including Frogman’s View (1984), Music Is Music Is… (1985)
  • Jazzwerkstatt — The Complete FMP Recordings (Peter Brötzmann, 2007): mastered by, mixed by, producer, recorded by
  • Trost Records — Nohome (Peter Brötzmann / Mats Gustafsson, 2013): photography; First Recordings (2014): producer
  • Corbett vs. Dempsey — Bonobo Beach: Some More Guitar Solos (Hans Reichel, 2022): producer
  • Atavistic — Peter Brötzmann reissues Balls (2002) and The Living Music (2002): digital transfer

Dagmar Gebers

Dagmar Gebers (Discogs ID: 1212213) was FMP’s staff photographer and appears on 434 shared releases with Jost Gebers — the highest collaboration count in his Discogs record by a wide margin. She is credited consistently as “Photography By” across the FMP catalog. Her relationship to Jost Gebers is not specified in Discogs but the scope of co-credits across the entire FMP lifespan strongly suggests she was his partner in running the label.
