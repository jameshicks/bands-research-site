Donald Harrison

Alto saxophonist, composer, bandleader, and Mardi Gras Indian Big Chief. Born June 23, 1960, New Orleans, Louisiana. Nicknamed “The King of Nouveau Swing.” Discogs artist ID: 65744. Also credited as Donald Harrison Jr., Big Chief Donald Harrison.

Biography

New Orleans Roots

Harrison grew up in New Orleans, the son of Donald Harrison Sr. (Discogs: Donald Harrison (2)), a prominent Mardi Gras Indian Big Chief of the Guardians of the Flame tribe. This immersion in the Mardi Gras Indian tradition — chant, percussion, collective improvisation — would eventually become a defining strand of his music. He studied at the New Orleans Center for Creative Arts and later at Berklee College of Music and the New England Conservatory.

Art Blakey’s Jazz Messengers (early 1980s)

Harrison joined Art Blakey’s Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers in the early 1980s alongside fellow New Orleanian Terence Blanchard on trumpet. The Messengers recording that best documents this period is New York Scene (Concord Jazz, 1984), with the quintet of Blanchard, Harrison, Mulgrew Miller (piano), Lonnie Plaxico (bass), and Blakey. Liner notes by Hugh Wyatt; produced by Frank Dorritie. Harrison and Blanchard absorbed Blakey’s hard-bop schooling while also bringing post-bop curiosity to the band.

Harrison/Blanchard Quintet (1984–1988)

After both men left the Messengers, Harrison and Blanchard co-led a quintet that became one of the key acoustic jazz units of the mid-1980s. Discogs lists the group entity as Blanchard (ID: 2635497), with the canonical lineup of Harrison, Blanchard, Mulgrew Miller (piano), Cyrus Chestnut (piano on later dates), Reginald Veal (bass), and Carl Allen (drums).

Solo Career — New Acoustic Phase (1989–1991)

After the quintet wound down, Harrison moved into solo leadership. Full Circle (1990, Sweet Basil) — credited as “Donald ‘Duck’ Harrison” — featured Carl Allen (drums), Dwayne Burno (bass), Cyrus Chestnut (piano), and Mark Whitfield (guitar). Harrison produced; exec. prod. Shigeyuki Kawashima (Japanese production involvement reflected the Sweet Basil label’s Japan market orientation).

He also appeared as a sideman on Tony Williams’s Foreign Intrigue (Blue Note, 1985), alongside Mulgrew Miller (piano), Ron Carter (bass), and Wallace Roney (trumpet).

CTI Period and The Power of Cool (1991–1994)

The Power of Cool (CTI Records, 1991) was Harrison’s major-label debut as a leader on that imprint. Produced by Creed Taylor, it was a fusion-leaning session with an unusual cast: Chuck Loeb (guitar/keyboards), Will Lee and Mark Egan (bass), Zach Danziger and Joe Bonadio (drums), Larry Coryell (classical guitar on two tracks), Marlon Graves (guitar/vocals). Vocalists: Sharon Bryant, James “D-Train” Williams, Carmen Cuesta-Loeb, Vaneese Thomas, Lani Groves. The record tilts toward accessible funk-pop jazz, typical of the CTI aesthetic under Taylor.

Additional CTI-era releases as leader: Close The Door (1994, CTI).

Indian Blues and the Mardi Gras Synthesis (1992)

Indian Blues (Candid, 1992) is Harrison’s landmark fusion of jazz and New Orleans Mardi Gras Indian tradition. Primary credits: Harrison featuring the Guardians Of The Flame (2), The Mardi Gras Indians, and Dr. John.

Personnel: Donald Harrison Jr. (alto/tenor sax, tambourine, backing vocals); Phil Bowler (bass, backing vocals); Carl Allen (drums, backing vocals); Cyrus Chestnut (piano, backing vocals); “Big Chief” Smiley Ricks (congas, tambourine, backing vocals); Donald Harrison Sr. (lead vocals on tracks 1, 3, 6 — his father, Discogs: Donald Harrison (2)); Dr. John (lead vocals on tracks 1, 4, 10, 12; piano). Produced by Mark Morganelli; exec. prod. Alan Bates (Candid label chief); recorded by Malcolm Addey.

This album situates Harrison as the inheritor of the Guardians of the Flame tradition — his father’s tribe — and as a bridge between jazz improvisation and the Mardi Gras Indian chant/call-and-response form. Harrison later became Big Chief of the Guardians of the Flame himself.

Follow-up: Spirits of Congo Square (Candid) continued this direction.

For Miles (Candid, 1995) — tribute to Miles Davis.

Nouveau Swing and Impulse! (1997–1999)

Nouveau Swing (Impulse!, 1997): Harrison co-produced with Tommy LiPuma. Sidemen include Reuben Rogers, Christian McBride (bass), Dion Parson (drums), Anthony Wonsey (piano).

Free To Be (Impulse!, 1999): Broad-cast session with Christian McBride, Reuben Rogers, Vicente Archer (bass), Carl Allen, John Lamkin (drums), Eddie Palmieri, Mulgrew Miller, Andrew Adair (piano), Rodney Jones (guitar), Brian Lynch (trumpet), Teodross Avery (tenor sax). Heavily Latin-tinged through Palmieri’s involvement.

2000s–2020s: Continuing Work

Heroes (Nagel Heyer, 2004): Ron Carter (bass, tracks 1–8), Vicente Archer (bass, tracks 9–11), Billy Cobham (drums, tracks 1–8), John Lamkin (drums, tracks 9–11). Produced by Frank Nagel-Heyer; engineered by Jim Anderson.

Harrison remained active as a sideman across many contexts — The Headhunters, Brian Lynch/Eddie Palmieri Project, The Cookers, various New Orleans ensembles. Late records include The Chosen and Quantum Leap (with Victor Gould, piano; Joe Dyson, drums; Luques Curtis, bass).

Recent releases on Ropeadope: Indigo Blue Live at the Iridium (2019); The Stunt Man (2024, Ropeadope).
