Overview

Stephen John Pearce (born 26 December 1962), known as Stevo, came up as a DJ in early-1980s London, running “Electronic Parties” at venues like the Chelsea Drugstore and the Clarendon Hotel in Hammersmith where acts such as Fad Gadget and DAF performed. His work compiling an electronic music chart for Sounds magazine led him to field demos from unsigned bands, which he assembled into the Some Bizzare Album — the record that seeded both the Some Bizzare label and his management company. Through the decade he signed some of the defining alternative acts of the era: Soft Cell, Einstürzende Neubauten, The The, and Test Dept. His 87 Discogs credits span 1981 to 2025 and skew toward production and co-production; Marc Almond and Dave Ball are his most frequent collaborators, but a notable mid-1990s cluster of credits with Dubstar (2) and Stephen Hague on the Food and Polydor labels marks an unexpected turn toward Britpop-adjacent territory.

Notes (web)

Dubstar management

Stevo was appointed manager of Dubstar (2) in September 1996, confirmed by the band’s official biography. (dubstar.com/biography)

The arrangement came about by a combination of label pressure and exhaustion. Food Records was losing patience with the band, who were without management and interviewing multiple candidates. After an extended round of meetings, they settled on Stevo essentially because he was the last person they saw and the best of the lot — vocalist Sarah Blackwood has said the band were “physically and emotionally exhausted” by the process by that point. (side-line.com)

Guitarist Chris Wilkie was already familiar with Some Bizzare Records going in and had a particular appreciation for Stevo’s philosophical sleeve notes. Blackwood’s account suggests Stevo’s girlfriend Cleo Rocos was a significant draw: Rocos gave Blackwood practical personal support during the touring period and actively encouraged her writing, which Blackwood credited as a genuine factor in the band’s willingness to work with him. Stevo himself made an impression with his refusal to acknowledge limitations and his daily habit of learning new vocabulary; Blackwood found his opening pitch (“See that billboard? Your name will be on there one day, in lights!”) unimpressive on its own, but the overall package won them over. (side-line.com)

The first encounter between Stevo and Dubstar took place in Leeds. Sarah Blackwood has said: “Just like Marc Almond, our first encounter with Stevo was in Leeds. His first words were, ‘See that billboard? Your name will be on there one day, in lights!‘” (side-line.com) The reference to Marc Almond is apt: Stevo’s first meeting with Almond and Soft Cell was also in Leeds, in July 1980, when he hitch-hiked north and they met him there. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Cell, as of 2026-10-15)

Prior to Stevo, Dubstar’s manager was Graeme Robinson — a Darlington-based producer and talent manager who had seen them play in Newcastle in March 1994 and subsequently co-produced Disgraceful with Stephen Hague. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubstar, as of 2026-10-15; dubstar.com/faq) Robinson also arranged the name change from The Joans to Dubstar at Andy Ross’s request. By the end of 1996 the band had parted ways with Robinson.

Work on Dubstar’s second album began shortly after the appointment, recording in late 1996 at Stephen Hague’s studio in Woodstock, New York. (dubstar.com/biography) Stevo’s Discogs credits with the band and Hague run across the Food and Polydor labels from 1995 to 1998.
