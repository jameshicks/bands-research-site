Biography

Origins — North Carolina Central University (early 2000s)

Patrick Douthit began making beats in his dorm room at North Carolina Central University (NCCU) in Durham, NC, using FL Studio (then known as Fruityloops). He developed a soulful, sample-based production style drawing on Al Green, Curtis Mayfield, and soul/funk records, with bass lines modeled on the approaches of DJ Premier, Pete Rock, and J Dilla, and vocal sampling technique influenced by RZA. His earliest documented production work appears around 2002.

Little Brother and the ABB Records era (2002–2005)

9th Wonder was a founding member of Little Brother (3) alongside rappers Phonte (Phonte Lyshod Coleman) and Big Pooh (Thomas Louis Jones III). The group debuted on ABB Records with The Listening (2003), a critically acclaimed record entirely produced by 9th Wonder. The album established a template — warm, soul-drenched beats under sharp, introspective lyricism — that became the calling card of the Justus League / North Carolina underground hip-hop scene.

His first major crossover move came when he produced a remix album of Nas’s 2002 God’s Son LP, releasing it as God’s Stepson (2003) — an early example of what became a trend of full-album producer remixes in hip-hop. This project brought him to wider attention and began a relationship with Nas that recurred across several further remix volumes.

Also in 2003, filmmaker Tee Smith and engineer Young Guru (Gimel “Young Guru” Keaton) facilitated 9th Wonder’s production credit on Jay-Z’s The Black Album (2003, Roc-A-Fella). Although this credit does not appear in Discogs’s release-level credits for that album, 9th Wonder’s profile explicitly names it. His track reportedly appeared on the deluxe/bonus configuration.

Little Brother’s second album, The Minstrel Show (2005, ABB Records / Atlantic), was 9th Wonder’s most high-profile mainstream release to that point. He produced fourteen of the seventeen tracks; the album was mixed by Young Guru and Khrysis engineered recording. The record received widespread critical attention and cemented Little Brother as one of the era’s most respected underground rap groups.

Parallel to Little Brother, 9th Wonder became prolific with individual production credits across the mid-2000s underground scene: De La Soul (The Grind Date, 2004), Murs (Murs 3:16: The 9th Edition, 2004), and a slew of Duck Down and Definitive Jux releases.

Collaborative LPs and Solo Breakout (2005–2008)

9th Wonder’s first billed collaborative album was Chemistry (2005, Duck Down), a full-length with Buckshot (of Black Moon). He produced, mixed, and recorded the album himself (with Khrysis on one track). Duck Down executive producers Buckshot and Dru Ha oversaw the project. The album was followed by The Last Stand (Buckshot, 2006) and The Formula (Buckshot, 2008), cementing a long-term Duck Down partnership.

Murray’s Revenge (2006, Record Collection) — a joint album with Los Angeles rapper Murs — became one of his most praised collaborative efforts. 9th Wonder produced, recorded, and mixed the entire album; it was issued on Warner-distributed Record Collection. The two followed it up with Sweet Lord (2008, mixtape/download) and Murs For President (2008, Warner Bros. Records), on which 9th contributed three tracks alongside DJ Quik.

Murs 3:16: The 9th Edition (2004, Definitive Jux) preceded Murray’s Revenge and was an earlier full collaboration — 9th produced and recorded nine of ten tracks, co-writing all of them with Murs. It is one of the pivotal records in the cross-pollination of LA underground rap with North Carolina production.

He also co-produced Destiny’s Child’s “Girl” single (2005, Columbia), sharing producer credit with Beyoncé — a high-profile mainstream placement that expanded his industry visibility.

His first credited solo album under his own name was The Dream Merchant Vol. 2 (2007, 6 Hole Records). The Dream Merchant Vol. 1 appears as a limited/promotional predecessor. Both were beat-forward instrumental/showcase records. He also released The Graduate Mixtape (2007), a conceptual project framed around Kanye West’s Graduation cycle.

Jeanius (2008, Blacksmith Music) — a full collaborative LP with Jean Grae — collected a strong critical response; 9th produced most of the record (eleven of twelve tracks).

Around 2008, 9th Wonder and Christopher “Play” Martin (of Kid ‘N’ Play) were named Artists-in-Residence by North Carolina Central University, teaching a hip-hop history class in NCCU’s Music Department.

Jamla Records and Label Building (2009–2015)

9th Wonder founded the Jamla Records imprint (later operating as Jamla, associated with It’s A Wonderful World Music Group) as a home for North Carolina-area artists including Rapsody and others from his extended crew. The label became a vehicle for records with Skyzoo (Cloud 9: The 3 Day High, 2006; The Salvation, 2009), Buckshot, and others.

The Wonder Years (2011, It’s A Wonderful World Music Group / Jamla) was his most ambitious solo statement — a full-length LP with a deep guest roster, produced and mixed by 9th, co-mixed by Khrysis, with Phonte recording several vocal parts. Executive producers listed include his family members (Jada, Jordan, Xavier Douthit), a recurring practice on his solo material.

The Solution (2012, Duck Down) continued the Buckshot partnership; The Final Adventure (2012, Jamla) and Tutankhamen (2012, Jamla) were further solo/executive-produced projects from this period.

Indie 500 (2015, It’s A Wonderful World Music Group / Jamla / Javotti Media) was a joint album with Talib Kweli; both served as presenters and executive producers.

Later Work and the Roc Nation/Jamla Phase (2016–present)

From the mid-2010s onward 9th Wonder shifted more heavily toward executive production, with Jamla becoming a joint venture with Roc Nation. Key releases include Rapsody’s Laila’s Wisdom (2017, Jamla) — for which 9th served as executive producer — and Eve (2019, Jamla / Roc Nation).

Black American Gangster (2013, Not On Label) was a conceptual mixtape project with Jay-Z material; Brighter Daze (2016) was a collaborative LP. His 2021 partnership with Dutch rapper The Musalini produced The Don & Eye (2022, de Rap Winkel Records) and Don & Eye 2 (2023, Daupe!), expanding his catalog into the European underground.

The Supergoat (2024, Jamla Records) is his most recent credited primary release, featuring Hus Kingpin.

He joined P’Tones Records as a spokesperson around 2008 — a music-industry education program affiliated with NCCU giving students scholarship pathways.
