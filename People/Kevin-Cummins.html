Kevin Cummins

Manchester music photographer, closely associated with the post-punk and new wave scene. Shot the back cover of The Panik’s “It Won’t Sell!” (1977) — credited in the sleeve notes as “Expensive back photo.” Also photographed Joy Division and New Order, producing some of the most iconic images of that era (the Hulme footbridge Joy Division photos, etc.). Long association with NME.

Factory Records and Rob Gretton

Rob Gretton — longstanding personal friendship

Cummins and Rob Gretton were friends from early youth, both Manchester City supporters. They knew each other before either was involved in the music industry: one account describes them as “fresh-faced young mods” in 1969, hitching to Coventry to watch City play and posing together in a photobooth to send to girls they had met. Cummins later recalled going to City matches regularly with Gretton and Mike Pickering. (mancity.com, [search result summary])

This pre-existing friendship gave Cummins unusually close access to Joy Division during their brief career. The Dazed & Confused profile quotes Cummins recounting spending the Saturday night before Joy Division’s cancelled US tour (the night before Ian Curtis’s death, May 1980) with Gretton: “I was out on the Saturday night with Rob Gretton, the band’s manager, at a wedding. Then we went back to Rob’s and sat up all night talking about going to the States.” (dazeddigital.com)

Cummins posted a public tribute to Gretton after his death in May 1999: “Another Blue, sorely missed: Rob Gretton, who died the morning of the Wigan play off in 99. Today’s for you Rob MCFC” (twitter/X, @KCMANC).

Relationship to Factory Records — access via NME, not formal contract

No source found confirms that Cummins was ever formally contracted to or employed by Factory Records, or that a FAC catalogue number was assigned to him personally. The Cerysmatic Factory site lists him as a “Photographer” associated with Factory and connects him to FAC 151 (Festival of the Tenth Summer) and FAC 288, but provides no contract or commissioning details. (factoryrecords.org)

His access to Joy Division and New Order appears to have been arranged primarily through his NME role (he was commissioned by NME from May 1977) and his pre-existing personal friendship with Gretton, rather than through any formal Factory Records arrangement. He is described as moving “in circles made up by mavericks, including graphic designers Peter Saville and Ben Kelly, and Factory Records founder Tony Wilson” — indicating social proximity rather than contractual relationship. (i-D via search result summary)

Archive context

Both the Kevin Cummins Archive and the Rob Gretton Archive are held at the John Rylands Library, University of Manchester, as part of the British Pop Archive. They are distinct collections but sit alongside each other in the same institutional home, alongside the Ian Curtis Archive. (rylandscollections.com, 2022-06-09)
