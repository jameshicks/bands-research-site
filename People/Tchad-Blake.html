Tchad Blake

Tchad Blake

Oct 13, 20268 min read

Tchad Blake

Discogs ID: 129495

American record producer, engineer, and mixing specialist. Started as an assistant engineer in LA in the late 1970s; became a primary engineer/mixer through the 1980s and 1990s with a circle of artists orbiting Mitchell Froom (his long-standing collaborator) — Crowded House, Los Lobos, Richard Thompson, Sheryl Crow, Suzanne Vega, Soul Coughing, Elvis Costello. From the 2000s onward built a second identity as the go-to mixer for a wider alt-rock and Americana roster, most notably the sustained relationship with Peter Gabriel (the “dark-side” mixer on I/O, 2023). Also a performing member of Latin Playboys (the Los Lobos offshoot). Moved to England in 2001 and worked at Real World Studios; moved to Wales in 2008 and has since mixed from his home studio, Mongrel Studios.

Career Phases

Early Work: LA Assistant Engineer (1979–mid-1980s)

Started in the studio as an assistant engineer on mainstream pop and rock sessions in Los Angeles: Peter Frampton (Where I Should Be, 1979), John Denver (A Christmas Together, 1979), Peggy Lee (Close Enough For Love, 1979). Also assistant work on Hoodoo Gurus, Jackson Browne, and session dates through the early 1980s. During this period he was building studio craft in a conventional LA session-player environment, not yet defining a sound.

The Mitchell Froom Partnership (late 1980s–1990s)

The central relationship of Blake’s career. By the late 1980s he was operating as full engineer/recorder alongside Mitchell Froom on a consistent stream of albums. This pairing defined his creative period. Together they shaped the idiosyncratic sound — saturated bass, unusual mic placement, textural experimentation — that characterised the best of these records:

  • Crowded House: Woodface (recorded, 1991), Temple Of Low Men (recorded), Afterglow, Recurring Dream compilations, and multiple single sessions. Froom produced; Blake engineered/recorded.
  • Los Lobos: Colossal Head (produced/engineered, 1996), This Time (produced/recorded/mixed, 1999), The Town And The City (mixed, 2006). One of the deepest and most sustained relationships in his career.
  • Elvis Costello: Brutal Youth (recorded/mixed, 1994). Appeared in subsequent Costello compilations (Extreme Honey) as engineer.
  • Soul Coughing: Ruby Vroom (produced/recorded/mixed, 1994), Irresistible Bliss (mixed, 1996), El Oso (produced, 1998). Key figure in their studio identity.
  • Richard Thompson: Rumor And Sigh (mixed), Mirror Blue (engineered), You? Me? Us? (engineer/guitar, 1996 — he plays guitar).
  • Suzanne Vega: Nine Objects Of Desire (mixed, 1996), Beauty & Crime (mixed, 2007), 99.9F° (engineered/mixed).
  • Sheryl Crow: self-titled album (recorded/mixed, 1996), The Globe Sessions (mixed, 1998/1999). Regular Crow collaborator through the decade.
  • Tom Waits: Bone Machine (mixed, 1992), engineer credits on Franks Wild Years and the Orphans trilogy.
  • Luscious Jackson: Under Your Skin (mixed, 1997).
  • The Dandy Warhols: …The Dandy Warhols Come Down (mixed, 1997). Multiple pressings across subsequent years.
  • Bonnie Raitt: Fundamental (recorded/mixed, 1998), Silver Lining (produced/recorded/mixed, 2002), Souls Alike (co-produced/recorded/mixed, 2005).
  • Ron Sexsmith: Other Songs (produced, 1997), Whereabouts (produced/engineered/mixed, 1999).
  • Neil Finn: Try Whistling This (produced/mixed, 1998), One All (produced/engineered/mixed, 2002).
  • Lisa Germano: Slide (produced/mixed, 1998).
  • Cibo Matto: Viva! La Woman (produced, 1996).
  • Sam Phillips: The Turning (recorded/mixed, 1997).
  • Randy Newman: Bad Love (produced/recorded/mixed, 1999).

Also performed as a member of Latin Playboys (Los Lobos spin-off) and as a studio instrumentalist: plays guitar on Richard Thompson’s You? Me? Us?, keyboards and percussion on various sessions.

Jonathan Fire*Eater Connection (1998)

Blake remixed “No Love Like That” for Jonathan Fire*Eater’s 1998 DreamWorks promo (Discogs release 11085811). A single Remix credit — marginal connection to the NYC post-punk world, likely commissioned during the brief DreamWorks indie period.

Peter Gabriel and Real World (2001–present)

After relocating to England in 2001, Blake worked at Real World Studios and began a sustained relationship with Peter Gabriel. He mixed Up (2002), the Play: The Videos DVD (stereo and 5.1 mixes, 2004), Scratch My Back (2010), And I’ll Scratch Yours (2013), The Veil (2016 single). The relationship culminated in his billing as the “dark-side mix” engineer on I/O (2023), where each track was released in two versions — Blake’s darker, more textural mix alongside the “bright-side” version by Mark “Spike” Stent. He is also on the 2026 I/O single run (“Been Undone”, “What Lies Ahead”, “Put The Bucket Down”).

2010s–2020s: Wider Alt-Rock Roster

Working from Mongrel Studios in Wales, Blake built out an extensive mixing practice across a varied roster:

  • The Black Keys: Brothers (2010), El Camino (2011), Turn Blue (2014), Let’s Rock (2019), Delta Kream (2021), Dropout Boogie (2022). Sustained multi-album relationship.
  • Fiona Apple: Fetch The Bolt Cutters (mixed, 2020). High-profile credit on one of the decade’s most acclaimed albums.
  • Arctic Monkeys: AM (mixed, 2013). Appears on every subsequent pressing.
  • The Kills: Ash & Ice (mixed, 2016), No Wow – The Tchad Blake Mix 2022 (an entire album remixed). Extended relationship with Alison Mosshart and Jamie Hince.
  • U2: Songs Of Innocence (mixed, 2014).
  • Paul Simon: Surprise (mixed, 2006) — a Froom-produced album; Father And Daughter single.
  • Robert Plant: Lullaby And… The Ceaseless Roar (mixed, 2014).
  • Pearl Jam: Binaural (produced/mixed, 2000). Continued to appear on Pearl Jam reissues and compilation credits.
  • Phish: Undermind (produced/mixed, 2004).
  • Gomez: Split The Difference (produced/mixed, 2004), Catch Me Up.
  • Travis: 12 Memories (mixed, 2003), Love Will Come Through (produced, 2004).
  • The Last Shadow Puppets: Everything You’ve Come To Expect (mixed, 2016).
  • Gerard Way: Hesitant Alien (mixed, 2014).
  • St. Vincent: St. Vincent (mixed, 2014 reissue).
  • Andrew Bird: Are You Serious (mixed, 2016).
  • Green Day: Father Of All… (mixed, 2020).
  • Low: Trust (mixed, 2002, 2016 reissue), Familial (Phil Selway solo, but Low connection).
  • Phil Selway (Radiohead): Familial (mixed, 2010).
  • Frightened Rabbit: Pedestrian Verse (recorded, 2013).
  • Stina Nordenstam: The World Is Saved (mixed, 2004), Lori Glory (produced/recorded/mixed, 2002), multiple subsequent credits as producer.
  • Bad Plus: Suspicious Activity? (produced/engineered/mixed, 2005), Give (produced/recorded/mixed, 2004), For All I Care (mixed, 2009).
  • Chrissie Hynde / The Pretenders: Alone (mixed, 2016); Hynde’s Standing In The Doorway (produced/mixed/played, 2021).

As Performer / Instrumentalist

Blake is not only a studio technician. He plays lap steel guitar and appears as performer:

  • Latin Playboys — performing member. The Latin Playboys (Los Lobos offshoot with David Hidalgo and Louie Pérez) released Latin Playboys (1994) and Dose (1999); Blake is credited as Performer.
  • Bernard Fanning — Tea & Sympathy (produced/engineered/mixed/lap steel, 2005). Full creative role.
  • Chrissie Hynde — Standing In The Doorway (produced/played keyboards, Hammond B3, percussion, tambourine, bells, 2021).
  • Seth Lakeman — Hearts & Minds (produced/engineered/harmonica/piano, 2010).
  • Richard Thompson — You? Me? Us? (guitar, 1996).

Vault / Indie Connections

  • Jonathan Fire*Eater — remix credit on “No Love Like That” (1998 DreamWorks promo). Peripheral but present in the collaborator graph.
  • Soul Coughing — Mike Doughty’s downtown NYC band; Blake produced Ruby Vroom (1994), defining their textural sound.
  • Low — mixed Trust (2002); the Duluth slowcore band has a vault page.
  • The Kills — Ash & Ice (2016); remixed No Wow as a full alternate album (2022). Cross-link: Alison Mosshart.
  • The Last Shadow Puppets — mixed Everything You’ve Come To Expect (2016). Cross-link: Alex Turner, Miles Kane.
  • Arctic Monkeys — AM (2013). Cross-link: Alex Turner.

Labels / Studios

  • Real World Studios (Peter Gabriel’s Bath studio) — worked there 2001 onward after relocating to England.
  • Mongrel Studios — Blake’s home studio in Wales, where he has mixed from c.2008. Credit appears explicitly on some Crowded House and Foy Vance releases.
  • DGC / Geffen — distributed much of the 1990s Froom-era work (Soul Coughing, Cibo Matto).
  • Capitol — Sheryl Crow’s label for the self-titled album.
