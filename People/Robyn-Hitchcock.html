Robyn Hitchcock

Robyn Hitchcock

Oct 09, 20265 min read

Robyn Hitchcock

Discogs ID: 269402
Real name: Robyn Rowan Hitchcock
Born: March 3, 1953, Paddington, London
Origin: England, UK
Married to: Emma Swift
Aliases: Dr Sticky, Ray Clamp, Violet (27)

English singer-songwriter, guitarist, and visual artist. Central figure in British post-punk and psychedelic pop from the late 1970s onward. Led The Soft Boys (1976–1981, 2001–2003), then embarked on a prolific solo career and fronted Robyn Hitchcock & The Egyptians (1984–1994). Known for surrealist lyrics, self-painted cover art, and tireless solo acoustic touring. Active continuously since 1981; later-career work on Yep Roc and Tiny Ghost Records.

The Soft Boys (1976–1981)

Hitchcock was songwriter and vocalist for The Soft Boys, originally called Maureen & The Meatpackers, formed in Cambridge 1976. Stable lineup: Hitchcock (guitar/vocals), Kimberley Rew (guitar/bass/synth), Matthew Seligman (bass), Morris Windsor (drums; alias Otis Fagg). Andy Metcalfe also played bass at various points.

Key releases:

  • A Can Of Bees (1979, Two Crabs Universal)
  • Underwater Moonlight (1980, Armageddon Records) — produced mostly by Pat Collier; includes cover of Syd Barrett’s “Vegetable Man”; “I Wanna Destroy You” became the band’s signature track; Glenn Tilbrook (Squeeze) on “Flesh Number One (Beatle Dennis)”

Band broke up 1981. Reunited 2001 for the 20th anniversary of Underwater Moonlight; recorded Nextdoorland (2002, Matador) in the process; disbanded again 2003.

Early Solo (1981–1983)

  • Black Snake Diamond Röle (1981, Armageddon Records) — solo debut LP; Hitchcock plays piano, bass, lead guitar; wrote all songs and designed cover; engineered by Jo Julian, Andy Llewellyn, and Pat Collier; personnel: Matthew Seligman (bass), Vince Ely and Morris Windsor (drums), Kimberley Rew (guitar on some tracks), Gary Barnacle (saxophone)
  • Groovy Decay (1982, Albion Records, ALB 110) — produced by Steve Hillage; Sara Lee bass; Anthony Thistlethwaite saxophone; Rod Johnson drums; James A. Smith backing vocals on multiple tracks; mastered by BilBo
  • Invisible Hits (1983, Midnight Music) — compilation of unreleased tracks

Midnight Music Years and I Often Dream Of Trains (1984–1986)

  • I Often Dream Of Trains (1984, Midnight Music, CHIME 00.05 S) — solo acoustic LP; engineers Pat Collier, Pascal Gabriel, Iain O’Higgins; 14 tracks including “Cathedral,” “Uncorrected Personality Traits,” and the title track; lacquer cut by John Dent
  • Groovy Decoy (1985, Midnight Music) — alternate/remixed version of the Groovy Decay sessions
  • Invisible Hitchcock (1986, Glass Fish Records / Relativity) — compilation; issued simultaneously UK and US
  • Happy The Golden Prince (1986, Bucketfull Of Brains) — limited single

Robyn Hitchcock & The Egyptians (1984–1994)

Robyn Hitchcock & The Egyptians formed 1984 with ex-Soft Boys Andy Metcalfe (bass/keyboards/vocals) and Morris Windsor (drums/vocals), plus Roger Jackson (keyboards, departed 1986) and James Fletcher (saxophone, departed 1984).

Post-Egyptians and Warner Bros. (1995–2001)

  • Gravy Deco — The Complete Groovy Decay / Decoy Sessions (1995, Rhino Records) — definitive collection of the 1982 Albion sessions
  • Eye (1990, Twin/Tone Records) — solo
  • Moss Elixir (1996, Warner Bros.) — Calvin Johnson (Beat Happening/K Records) contributes; Geoff Sykes prominent in credits
  • Jewels For Sophia (1999, Warner Bros.)
  • Storefront Hitchcock (1998, Warner Bros.) — concert film directed by Jonathan Demme; Tim Keegan in live band; film released through Warner; soundtrack album

Soft Boys Reunion and Matador (2001–2003)

The Soft Boys (without Metcalfe) reformed 2001 for the 20th anniversary of Underwater Moonlight, recorded Nextdoorland (2002, Matador), disbanded again 2003.

Yep Roc Years (2004–2017)

Long creative run on Yep Roc Records:

  • Spooked (2004, Yep Roc, YEP 2086) — produced by David Rawlings; Gillian Welch plays guitar, bass, drums, percussion and sings harmonies throughout; covers Dylan’s “Tryin’ To Get To Heaven Before They Close The Door”; Joey Spampinato (NRBQ) bass on two tracks
  • Olé! Tarantula (2006, Yep Roc) — with Robyn Hitchcock & The Venus 3 (Peter Buck, Scott McCaughey, William Rieflin)
  • Goodnight Oslo (2009, Yep Roc) — with Venus 3
  • The Man Upstairs (2014, Yep Roc) — covers album produced by Joe Boyd; Anne Lise Frøkedal on vocals
  • Robyn Hitchcock (2017, Yep Roc, YEP-2483) — self-titled; produced by Brendan Benson (engineers and mixes); Emma Swift harmony vocals; Pat Sansone (Wilco) harmonies; Grant Lee Phillips harmonies; Gillian Welch on “Raymond and the Wires”; recorded in Nashville; Jon Estes bass, Jon Radford drums

Tiny Ghost and Recent Work (2019–present)

  • Planet England (2019, Ape House) — collaborative EP with Andy Partridge (XTC)
  • Sunday Never Comes (2019, Tiny Ghost Records)
  • The President (2020, Tiny Ghost)
  • Shufflemania! (2022, Tiny Ghost)
  • Life After Infinity (2023, Tiny Ghost)
  • 1967: Vacations In The Past (2024, Tiny Ghost) — covers album; features Kelley Stoltz, Davey Lane
