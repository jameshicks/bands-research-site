Graham Lewis

Real name: Edward Graham Lewis Also known as: He Said, Sven (22), EGL, E Graham Lewis, G. Lewis Discogs ID: 121140 Role: Bassist, Vocalist, Composer, Producer Origin: Grantham, Lincolnshire, UK (b. 22 February 1953) Active: 1976–present Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Lewis Bandcamp: https://edvardgrahamlewis.bandcamp.com/

Associated Acts: Wire, Dome, Cupol, Duet Emmo, He Said, P’o, Elegiac (2), UUUU (2), Lewis Spybey

Biography

Formation of Wire (1976–1979)

Lewis joined Wire at formation in October 1976, alongside Colin Newman (vocals, guitar), Bruce Gilbert (guitar), and Robert Gotobed (drums). He played bass and contributed co-lead vocals across the band’s foundational trilogy. On Pink Flag (Harvest, 1977) he shared bass, composition, and sleeve concept credits; on Chairs Missing (Harvest, 1978) and 154 (Warner Bros./Harvest, 1979) he deepened his role to arranger and sleeve designer as well. These three albums defined a stripped-down, concept-driven approach to post-punk that has had an enduring influence. The band dissolved at the end of 1979.

Parallel Projects: Dome, Cupol, and 4AD Work (1980–1982)

During the Wire hiatus Lewis and Gilbert immediately formed two overlapping projects. Dome (Discogs ID: 8753) released three albums in rapid succession — Dome (1980), Dome 2 (1980), and Dome 3 (1981) — all on their own Dome Records (2) imprint, with a fourth EP Jasz also in 1981. The work is built from voice, guitar, bass, percussion, tape, and synthesizer, with both members sharing performance and production duties. Dome also issued Will You Speak This Word (Uniton, 1982) for Norway.

Cupol (Discogs ID: 74418) was an alias for the same Gilbert–Lewis pairing, releasing the 4AD single Like This For Ages (1980). Dome and Cupol are treated as alternate names for the same entity on Discogs.

Lewis simultaneously developed a solo/collaborative strand through 4AD. He appeared as co-primary with Gilbert and Russell Mills on the MZUI LP (Cherry Red, 1982), credited as “bcGilbert, gLewis, russellMills.” He also helmed 4AD singles 3R4 (1980) and Ends With The Sea (1981) as primary artist, credited as “Guitar, Synthesizer, Tape, Percussion, Producer.”

He produced the debut LP of Michael O’Shea (Dome Records (2), 1982) and the Dome Records release Alone On Penguin Island (1981).

Duet Emmo (1982–1983)

Duet Emmo (Discogs ID: 139759) was a one-off collaboration between Lewis, Gilbert, and Daniel Miller (founder of Mute). They released the single Or So It Seems (Part 1 & 2) (Mute/Vogue, 1982) and the LP Or So It Seems (Mute, 1983). The album was engineered by Eric Radcliffe and John Fryer. This was Lewis’s introduction to the Mute Records orbit that would define the next phase of his solo work.

P’o (1983)

P’o (Discogs ID: 289995) produced one LP, Whilst Climbing Thieves Vie For Attention (Court Records (3), 1983), with Lewis playing electric guitar, voice, and keyboards and credited as sole songwriter. His only release under this project name.

Wire Reforms, He Said Solo Career (1985–1991)

Wire reformed in 1985, resuming the post-154 exploratory direction. Lewis continued as bassist and co-songwriter through a sustained sequence of Mute releases: The Ideal Copy (1987), A Bell Is A Cup Until It Is Struck (1988), It’s Beginning To And Back Again (1989), Manscape (1990), and The First Letter (1991). He also contributed to the Snakedrill EP (1986, Enigma) and the live In Vivo (1989).

Concurrently Lewis ran the He Said solo project (Discogs ID: 40245) on Mute, recording under his own creative direction with John Fryer as co-producer and engineer. Releases:

  • Only One I (single, Mute, 1985) — first He Said release
  • Hail (LP, Mute, 1986) — debut album; produced by Lewis and Fryer; cover art by Sven (22); more electronic than Wire’s contemporaneous work
  • Pump (single, Mute, 1986)
  • Pale Feet / Pulling 3gs (single, Mute, 1986)
  • Could You? (single, Mute, 1988) — last He Said single
  • Take Care (LP, Mute, 1988/1989) — second album; produced, engineered, and mixed by Lewis and Fryer; additional drums by Keith LeBlanc

He Said’s visual identity was handled by Sven (22) (cover/video images); the projects overlap since “Sven (22)” is a Lewis alias used for artwork credits.

The compilation This Way To The Shivering Man (Mute, 1987) gathered He Said singles. 8 Time (4AD, 1988) compiled the earlier 4AD solo/collaborative works.

Wire Activity, WMO Era, and Beyond (1991–2004)

Wire wound down with The First Letter (Elektra/Mute, 1991) and a formal pause. Lewis contributed to So And Slow It Grows (Mute, 1991), a Wire offshoot release. During the hiatus years he pursued production and collaborative work internationally, including work with Swedish artists (MNW Zone releases Immanent, 1994, and Eclipsed, 1995, as composer and producer) and the He Said-related comps Pacific / Specific (In A Different Place) (WMO, 1995) and Catch Supposes (World Domination Recordings, 1997).

Wire resumed as a trio (Wir, without Gilbert initially) and eventually as the full reformed unit. Lewis remained the consistent constant throughout, also appearing on the Blair Witch 2: Book Of Shadows soundtrack (Priority Records, 2000) as producer.

Wire’s Continued Output and Pinkflag Era (2002–present)

Wire reformed fully in the 2000s on their own Pinkflag label: Read & Burn 01/02 EPs (2002), Send (2003), Object 47 (2008), Red Barked Tree (2010), Change Becomes Us (2013), Wire (2015), Nocturnal Koreans (2016), Silver / Lead (2017), Mind Hive (2020), 10:20 (2020). Lewis continued as primary bassist, vocalist, co-songwriter, and occasional photographer (he contributed cover images to several albums). Matthew Simms joined the band in 2010; Bruce Gilbert departed in 2004 and was not part of this phase.

UUUU (2) and Editions Mego (2014–2018)

Lewis worked with Russell Haswell and others under the name UUUU (2) (Discogs ID: 6051680), releasing LPs UUUU (Editions Mego, 2017) and a follow-up in 2018, as well as solo/collaborative material: All Over and All Under (both Editions Mego, 2014), and the Duke Of York release (Editions Mego, 2015) with Dave Coppenhall.

Lewis Spybey Collaboration and Elegiac (2) (2021–present)

Lewis has collaborated extensively with Mark Spybey (aka Dead Voices On Air). The Lewis Spybey project (Discogs ID: 15287073) released Castle Neptune, Boot Island Twin, and the self-titled Lewis Spybey (Emergency Hearts, 2024).

Elegiac (2) (Discogs ID: 8921035) pairs Lewis with Ted Milton (Blurt) and Sam Britton. Their sole LP Elegiac (Upp Records (2), 2021) has Lewis credited as Edvard Graham Lewis, playing synth, sampler, beats, and bass; he and Britton co-arranged the album.

Lewis also released Alreet? (Upp Records (2), 2025) as a primary artist, and contributed to Lewis Spybey’s Lewispybey (Upp Records (2), 2024) as producer and artwork.
