Winston Grennan

Jamaican drummer and session musician (16 September 1940 – 27 October 2000). Discogs artist ID: 376630. Alias: Winston Richards.

Overview

Winston Grennan was one of the most widely recorded Jamaican session drummers of the ska, rocksteady, and early reggae eras, active in Kingston from around 1963 and continuing in New York City through the 1970s and 1980s. His studio footprint on Discogs spans from 1967 (a credited co-release on SEP Records) through posthumous archival compilations into the 2020s.

His Jamaican work centred on two main ensembles: The Carib Beats (led by guitarist Bobby Aitken) and Tommy McCook & The Supersonics, the house band at Treasure Isle and the pre-eminent ska ensemble on the island. Those sessions produced tracks that have since appeared on dozens of archival compilations issued by Pressure Sounds, Kingston Sounds, Heartbeat Records (2), and Greensleeves Records.

After relocating to New York City, Grennan built a parallel session career in funk and pop. His highest-profile crossover credit was drums on track B5 of Paul Simon’s Greatest Hits, Etc. (Columbia / CBS, 1977), a reggae-flavoured cut that also featured guitarist Hux Brown, organist Neville Hinds, and percussionist Denzil Laing from Jamaica’s studio pool. By 1980 he had joined Kid Creole And The Coconuts, playing drums on their first three studio albums for ZE Records / Island Records (1980–1982). In 1982 he also produced and arranged his own sole LP, Winston Grennan’s Big Apple Reggae, on D.J. Nap Production Inc., on which he played drums, sang lead, and co-produced alongside keyboardist and arranger Peter Schott.

Grennan died on 27 October 2000.

Name Variations on Discogs

Grennan appears under numerous variant spellings in session credits: Grennan, Winston, Winston Granan, Winston Granham, Winston Grenna, Winston Grennan (Aka, Richards), Winston Grennon, Winston Grenon, Winston Richards, Winston Trennon. The alias “Winston Richards” is listed explicitly. His name is credited as “Winston Grennon” on Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places.
