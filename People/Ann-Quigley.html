Ann Quigley

Discogs ID: 411259
Name variations: AQ, Ann, Quigley
Real name: Ann Quigley
Based: Manchester, UK
Active: 1980–1991 (Swamp Children / Kalima); documentary appearance 2006
Roles: Vocalist, lyricist, visual artist (painting, sleeve design, layout, photography)

Lead vocalist and visual artist at the centre of Manchester’s Factory Records jazz-funk scene. Co-founder of Swamp Children (1980–1982) and its direct successor Kalima (1983–1991), both on Factory Records. Sister of Tony Quigley (saxophone, bass, co-founder of both groups). Beyond her vocal work, she produced cover paintings for several A Certain Ratio releases and handled the full album design for Swamp Children’s So Hot (Factory Benelux, FBN 21, 1982). Interviewed and credited as still photographer on the Shadowplayers Factory Records documentary DVD (LTM, 2006).

Biography

Background and Formation of Swamp Children, 1980

Ann Quigley co-founded Swamp Children in Manchester in 1980, alongside her brother Tony Quigley (saxophone, bass), John Kirkham (guitar), Ceri Evans (keyboards, bass), Cliff Saffer (saxophone, clarinet), and Martin Moscrop (drums). The formation had direct ties to A Certain Ratio’s extended orbit — Moscrop and Tony Quigley were simultaneously active in ACR, and the groups shared rehearsal space and Factory infrastructure.

Before Swamp Children released their own records, Ann Quigley was already contributing visual work to the Factory ecosystem. She is credited with paintings on A Certain Ratio’s Flight 12” (Factory, FAC 11, 1980) — a release produced by Martin Hannett, designed by Peter Saville (2), with additional artwork by Richard Boon and Phil Diggles. Her credit appears as “Ann” (anv), painting a recurring Discogs pattern throughout her career where her first name alone identifies her.

Painting for A Certain Ratio, 1981

In 1981 Ann Quigley contributed paintings to two further ACR releases: the To Each… LP (Factory, 1981) — produced by Martin Hannett, with cover co-ordination by Peter Christopherson — and The Double 12” compilation (Factory Benelux, 1981), designed by Christopherson. Her visual work on three consecutive ACR releases (1980–1981) places her as a recurring presence in the ACR/Factory design circle well before Swamp Children’s debut, suggesting an artistic relationship independent of the purely musical one.

Swamp Children Recordings, 1981–1982

Swamp Children’s recording debut came with the Little Voices 12” (Factory, FAC 49, 1981). Ann Quigley is credited here as co-designer of the cover (alongside Graham (25), Discogs ID 2236379), under the anv “Ann.” Production was split: Simon Topping (ACR vocalist) produced the A-side; the B-sides were produced by Stephen Mallinder of Cabaret Voltaire. She sang lead vocals across the single’s three tracks. The genres tagged on Discogs are Free Funk and New Wave — the most post-punk-proximate of the group’s releases.

The Taste What’s Rhythm 12” (Factory Benelux, FBN 16, 1982) was engineered by Phil Bush. Its B-side “Softly Saying Goodbye” features an uncredited appearance by A Certain Ratio. Ann Quigley sang lead vocals.

The So Hot LP (Factory Benelux, FBN 21, 1982) was the group’s fullest statement. Quigley contributed on multiple levels: she sang lead vocals, co-wrote and co-arranged all material (credited as the band collectively), co-produced (ditto), and handled the album design and layout credited to her individually — listed twice in the Discogs credits, once as “Design [Album Design], Layout” (anv “Ann”) and once as “Vocals.” Recording was done by Stewart Pickering (anv “Stuart Pickering”). The LP’s nine tracks include “Samba Zippy,” “El Figaro,” “Tender Game,” “No Sunshine,” and “Spark The Flame,” with flute by Elvio Ghigliordini. The album received five pressings across territories (master ID 89531). This dual vocalist/designer role on a single record is the most complete expression of her combined artistic practice.

Kalima, 1983–1991

After So Hot, Swamp Children reconstituted as Kalima — the name taken from a track by jazz drummer Elvin Jones. All six core Swamp Children members carried over. Ann Quigley remained lead vocalist throughout Kalima’s entire Factory catalog, credited on vocals and lyrics on every release:

  • Four Songs EP (Factory, Fac 127, 1985): vocals and lyrics; produced by the band
  • Night Time Shadows LP (Factory, Fact 155, 1986): vocals, lyrics
  • Weird Feelings 12” (Factory, Fac 187, 1987): vocals, lyrics — lineup shift, Martin Moscrop replaced by David Higgins on drums
  • Kalima! LP (Factory, FACT 206, 1988): vocals, lyrics — produced by Michael Johnson; Japanese edition issued as Kalima! = トゥインクル・アイズ
  • Flyaway CD compilation (Factory, FACD 219, 1989): vocals on Kalima tracks; the first two tracks are Swamp Children material
  • Feeling Fine CD (Factory, Facd 249, 1990): vocals, lyrics — produced by Tim Oliver; final Factory album before the group’s 1991 dissolution

Her “Voice, Lyrics By” credit on Four Songs is the only Discogs release where her anv is absent — she’s credited under her full name. Kalima reformed briefly in 2001 but no subsequent Ann Quigley credit appears in Discogs for that phase (the 2002 Kin Records release In Spirit credits Tony Quigley as producer but does not individually credit Ann Quigley in the Discogs entry).

Shadowplayers Documentary, 2006

Ann Quigley appears as both interviewee and still photographer on Shadowplayers (Factory Records & Manchester Post-Punk 1978-81), a documentary DVD compiled and directed by James Nice (LTM, 2006). The film also features interviews with Anthony H. Wilson, Peter Hook, Vini Reilly, Howard Devoto, Peter Saville, Simon Topping, and Chris Watson, among others. Her still photography credit places her alongside Annik Honoré and Marc Portée in the visual documentation of the Factory scene. Section 25 provided the film’s soundtrack music.

Visual / Design Work

Ann Quigley’s non-vocal output spans two distinct roles: paintings used as sleeve art on early releases, and full album design/layout.

Paintings (sleeve art):

  • Flight (A Certain Ratio, FAC 11, 1980) — credited as “Ann,” alongside Peter Saville (design), Martyn Atkins (design), Richard Boon (engravings), Phil Diggles (drawing)
  • To Each… (A Certain Ratio, 1981) — painting; alongside Peter Christopherson (cover co-ordination), Martin Hannett (producer)
  • The Double 12” (A Certain Ratio, Factory Benelux, 1981) — painting; alongside Peter Christopherson (design)

Design and layout:

  • Little Voices (Swamp Children, FAC 49, 1981) — cover design (credited alongside Graham (25))
  • So Hot (Swamp Children, FBN 21, 1982) — full album design and layout (sole design credit)

Photography:

  • Shadowplayers (Various, LTM, 2006) — still photography

This makes her visual practice a continuous thread running from 1980 through 2006. The three ACR painting credits in particular show her working within the core Factory design circle — Peter Saville, Martyn Atkins, Peter Christopherson — at a formative period.
