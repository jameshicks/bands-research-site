Arif Mardin

Producer, arranger, conductor. Discogs artist ID: 15446. Born Mehmet Arif Mardin, March 15, 1932, Istanbul, Turkey; died June 25, 2006, New York City. Father of Joe Mardin.

Arrived in America after winning the inaugural Quincy Jones scholarship to Berklee College of Music. Joined Atlantic Records in the early 1960s as a staff arranger and recording supervisor, rising through the late 1960s to become one of the label’s key producers. His career spans jazz supervision (1963–66), soul arrangement (1967–72), R&B and rock production (1973–80), adult contemporary and pop (1981–96), and a final chapter culminating in Norah Jones’s Come Away With Me (2002), which won five Grammys.

Career Arc

Phase 1: Atlantic Staff Arranger / Supervisor (1962–1966)

Began as a “Recording Supervisor” — the house credit for Atlantic staff personnel overseeing sessions. Early supervised sessions include Art Farmer Quartet (Live at the Half-Note, 1964), Betty Carter (‘Round Midnight, 1963–64), Sonny Stitt (Stitt Plays Bird, 1964), Dorothy Ashby (The Fantastic Jazz Harp, 1965), Max Roach (Drums Unlimited, 1966), The Charles Lloyd Quartet (Dream Weaver, 1966), and Hank Crawford (Dig These Blues, 1965).

Also supervised early The Young Rascals sides (1965–66) — one of his first consistent pop credits — and worked alongside Jerry Wexler and Tom Dowd in Atlantic’s tight production hierarchy.

Phase 2: Arranger Ascendant (1967–1969)

Shifted from supervision to credited arranger and early producer. Key credits in this phase:

  • Aretha Franklin — Aretha Arrives (1967): engineer, arranger, conductor. Continued through I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You (1967, engineering credit) and arranged strings/horns across her Atlantic peak.
  • The Young Rascals / The Rascals — role evolved through five distinct stages: Recording Supervisor (scoped to specific tracks, 1966); “Supervised By” (full album, Collections 1967); “Arranged By [Orchestra]” (orchestral only, Groovin’ 1967 — tracks A1/A3/A5); “Arranged By, Conductor” (full orchestral authority, Once Upon a Dream 1968); absent from Freedom Suite (1969); “Co-producer” (See, 1969); and full joint “Producer” credit (Search and Nearness, 1971). He does not appear on the two Columbia albums (1971–72).
  • King Curtis — horn and string arrangements on King Size Soul (1967) and subsequent records; also recording engineer credits.
  • Herbie Mann — arranged and conducted The Herbie Mann String Album and Our Mann Flute (1966–67); continued producing Mann through the late 1960s.
  • Freddie Hubbard — produced Backlash (1967), his first fully credited production on a jazz leader’s record.
  • Dusty Springfield — early production credit on Dusty in Memphis-era material (1968–69), listed as producer (credited and uncredited) across multiple Springfield releases at Atlantic.
  • Brook Benton, Solomon Burke, Ben E. King, Wilson Pickett — arrangement and production work across the Atlantic soul roster.

The AWB album (1974) is a significant crossover credit — Mardin produced the album that broke the Scottish funk band in America with “Pick Up The Pieces.” The same year he produced the Bee Gees’ transitional Mr. Natural, then Main Course (1975), which began the R&B-inflected sound that would lead to Saturday Night Fever. His orchestrations were core to that transition.

The Amazing Grace (1972) double live gospel LP with Aretha Franklin remains one of the best-selling gospel records of all time.

Phase 4: Pop and AOR Expansions (1978–1991)

Moved into adult contemporary and pop production while retaining R&B roots:

  • Chaka Khan — extended relationship across Chaka (1978), What Cha’ Gonna Do For Me (1981), I Feel For You (1984, executive producer/producer), Destiny (1986). 39 releases in Discogs spanning 1978–2025.
  • Melissa Manchester — multiple albums 1978–82, including “You Should Hear How She Talks About You” (Grammy, 1982).
  • Daryl Hall & John Oates — extensive string/horn arrangements across 14 releases; produced Rock ‘N Soul Part 1 (1983).
  • George Benson — produced In Your Eyes (1983), Love All the Hurt Away (1981, with Aretha Franklin).
  • Bee Gees — continued: E·S·P (1987); arranged and produced across 28 releases 1974–2022.
  • David Bowie — strings and synthesizer arrangements on Tonight (1984) and subsequent mid-80s period; 8 releases.
  • Culture Club — produced From Luxury to Heartache (1986).
  • Scritti Politti — produced “Wood Beez (Pray Like Aretha Franklin)” (1984) and Cupid & Psyche 85-era material.
  • Phil Collins — string arrangements across 8 releases (1981 onward).
  • Queen — horn arrangements on 4 releases.
  • Ringo Starr — string/horn arrangements, 9 releases (1976 onward).
  • Barry Manilow — additional orchestrations, 5 releases.
  • Leo Sayer — produced multiple albums 1981–83.
  • Howard Jones — produced One to One (1986) and related singles.
  • Boy Meets Girl — produced 6 releases (1988 era).
  • Ofra Haza — produced 4 releases (1989–90).
  • Patti LaBelle — arranged and produced (1993–98).
  • Bebe & Cece Winans — mixed and produced (1994–95).

Phase 5: Late Career — Capitol and Final Chapter (1992–2006)

Moved to EMI / Capitol Records orbit. His final major credit was Norah Jones’s debut Come Away With Me (Capitol, 2002) — a record he produced with Craig Street for certain tracks — which sold over 27 million copies and won Album of the Year at the 2003 Grammys. He also continued arrangements for Judy Collins and returned to Aretha Franklin compilations.

Also produced the original Broadway cast recording of Rent (Jonathan Larson, 1996).
