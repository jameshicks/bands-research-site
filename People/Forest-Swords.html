Biography

Origins and Early EPs (2009–2010)

Forest Swords is the solo project of Matthew Barnes, from The Wirral — the peninsula between Liverpool and North Wales. Barnes cites the landscape itself as a core influence: river, coastland and woodland feeding into music that carries what he describes as the “haunting melodies of nearby Liverpool’s enviable pop history” filtered through jagged hip-hop and R&B beats, looping guitar lines, and manipulated samples.

The project debuted in 2009 with the Fjree Feather E.P. (self-released) and the cassette-only Miarches on Brooklyn label Leftist Nautical Antiques. The latter was released in limited numbers and quickly became a sought-after curio in the blog-era underground. In 2010, two more releases appeared: Rattling Cage (No Pain In Pop, UK) and the defining Dagger Paths E.P. (Olde English Spelling Bee, US), which introduced Barnes to a wider international audience. Both were mastered outside the project — Rattling Cage by Mikko Gordon and Dagger Paths by Carl Saff.

Tri Angle and Engravings (2013)

The debut album Engravings appeared on Tri Angle in 2013, cementing Forest Swords’ reputation as a leading voice in post-dubstep and hauntological electronics. The record was again mastered by Carl Saff, with all production and writing credited to Barnes under his own name. Engravings is dense with treated vocals, gothic atmosphere, and slow-motion rhythm — stylistically adjacent to other Tri Angle artists (Balam Acab, oOoOO, Holy Other) but distinct in its folk-tinged melodic instincts.

In 2014, Barnes issued a Lee Perry remix of “Thor’s Stone” as a free download / limited release, one of two recorded cross-references to Perry in his catalog.

Dance / Commission Work and Shrine (2016)

Barnes extended into commissioned work mid-decade. Shrine: Original Dance Score (2016, Dense Truth) was composed for a contemporary dance production — Barnes retained the “Composed By” and “Art Direction” credits, with Carl Saff again mastering. This side of the practice (composing for dance, film, and visual work) became increasingly central.

Compassion and Ninja Tune (2017)

The second studio album Compassion arrived on Ninja Tune in 2017, marking a shift to a major independent. Barnes produced, wrote, and handled art direction himself. Carl Saff mastered. The album is more explicitly political in texture — harder in places than Engravings, with layered vocal samples and a more aggressive rhythmic palette. The same year produced two further Ninja Tune releases: the single Congregate / Free and the EP The Highest Flood.

Anneka Warburton (credited as Anneka) appears on the Engravings 2025 edition, suggesting a vocal collaboration added to the reissue. Rashad Becker also contributed mastering to the same edition.

DJ-Kicks and Film Work (2018–2019)

Barnes curated and compiled the Forest Swords edition of the long-running !K7 Records DJ-Kicks series in 2018 — a mix including Kindholm alongside archived and unreleased material. In 2019 he scored The Machine Air as a film soundtrack (self-released), mastered by Denis Blackham and mixed by Barnes himself.

Bolted and Recent Work (2023–present)

The third studio album Bolted appeared on Ninja Tune in 2023 (cat. ZENDNL297). Styles: Dub, Experimental, Industrial, Trip Hop. Neneh Cherry appears as a collaborator across two releases from this period, and the single Butterfly Effect (also Ninja Tune, 2023) generated a remixes package the same year. Bolted (Deconstructed) appeared in 2025, extending the album with reworked versions. Additional 2024 releases: Crow (self-released) and Torch / Pearl Of Hail (Ninja Tune).

Co-wrote track 2 (“Total Loss”) on How To Dress Well’s 2012 album of the same name on Acéphale — an early and somewhat unexpected cross-scene connection.
