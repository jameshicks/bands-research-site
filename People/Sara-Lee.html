Sara Lee

English bassist and singer, born 1958 in a small town in the West Midlands. Discogs artist ID: 218952.

Daughter of two music teachers. Learned tympani and double bass in school before switching to electric bass. Worked at Polydor Records where Robert Fripp recruited her for The League Of Gentlemen (2) in 1980. Moved to the United States in the mid-1980s and built a long career as a session and touring bassist across post-punk, alternative rock, folk-rock, and art-pop. Released one solo album in 2000 on Righteous Babe Records.

Name variations on Discogs: “Lee”, “S Lee”, “Sara”, “Sarah Lee”.

Biography

Origins and League of Gentlemen (1980–1981)

Growing up with two music-teacher parents, Sara Lee took up tympani and double bass before the electric bass guitar claimed her. By the late 1970s she was working at Polydor Records in London when Robert Fripp heard her play and invited her to join his new ensemble, The League of Gentlemen. Fripp had assembled the group in early 1980 with Barry Andrews on organ, Sara Lee on bass, and initially Johnny Toobad on drums (later replaced by Kevin Wilkinson). The group played Frippertronics-influenced new-wave — polytonal, rhythmically angular — and toured Europe and the US.

The debut album The League Of Gentlemen (EG/Polydor, 1981) documents this lineup with Sara Lee on bass throughout. She also appears on the 1985 archival/compilation LP God Save The King (Editions EG), which collected live material from the 1980–81 period alongside earlier Fripp solo tracks, and on Thrang Thrang Gozinbulx (Discipline Global Mobile, 1996), a further archival release drawn from the same era. A single from 1981, Your Last Chance To Get Lucky (Raven Records), also featured the League.

In 1984 she appeared on Bewitched (A&M), the collaborative guitar-and-electronics album by Robert Fripp and Andy Summers (guitarist from The Police), credited alongside Chris Childs on bass.

Gang of Four (1981–1984)

Sara Lee joined Gang of Four in 1981, replacing founding bassist Dave Allen. She played on three studio recordings and their major live release:

  • Songs of the Free (EMI, 1982) — bass and backing vocals; produced by Mike Howlett with Andy Gill and Jon King. The album’s more direct, almost pop-funk sound partly reflects Lee’s more melodic approach compared to Allen’s reggae-dub influence.
  • Hard (Warner Bros., 1983) — bass on tracks A3–B1, B3, B5; the remaining tracks used Jon Astrop on bass. Produced by Howard and Ron Albert.
  • At The Palace (Mercury, 1984) — live double LP recorded at Crystal Palace. Sara Lee on bass and vocals, with Steve Goulding on drums.

The band dissolved in 1984. She appeared on the 1990 Gang of Four compilation A Brief History of the Twentieth Century (Warner Bros.) and on the 2003 reissue Hard / Solid Gold (Wounded Bird).

Session work: US move and B-52’s (1985–1990)

After relocating to the United States, Sara Lee established herself in New York’s session community. Her most commercially prominent studio credit in this period was the B-52’s Cosmic Thing (Reprise, 1989), the band’s comeback album and their commercial peak — it produced “Love Shack” and “Roam” and sold over four million copies in the US. Lee plays bass on seven of the ten tracks, with Nile Rodgers and Don Was producing alternating sessions. She also contributed keyboards and backing vocals to “Follow Your Bliss.”

Around the same time she appeared on the 1988 Downtown twelve-inch (Virgin) featuring Jerry Harrison, Bernie Worrell, and an extraordinary cast including RuPaul, Karen Finley, James White, and Wendy Wild.

Indigo Girls and Ani DiFranco (1990–1998)

Sara Lee became closely associated with the Indigo Girls across their most critically successful years:

  • Nomads · Indians · Saints (Epic, 1990) — bass on eight of eleven tracks, alongside Kenny Aronoff and Paulinho Da Costa.
  • Rites of Passage (Epic, 1992) — bass on nine of thirteen tracks; co-stars include Budgie, Jerry Marotta, Lisa Germano, and The Roches.
  • 1200 Curfews (Epic, 1995) — live double album; Sara Lee on bass.
  • Power of Two (Epic, 1995) — studio credit.
  • Shed Your Skin (Epic, 1998) — studio credit.

She also played bass on two tracks of Fiona Apple’s debut Tidal (Work/Columbia/Clean Slate, 1996), tracks 6 and 9 (“The First Taste” and “Pale September”).

From 1996 to 1997 she toured extensively with Ani DiFranco, appearing on:

  • Little Plastic Castle (Righteous Babe, 1997) — bass on tracks 3–5.
  • Living In Clip (Righteous Babe, 1997) — double live album; bass, bass pedals, and vocals throughout; she co-wrote “Make It Beautiful” (track 1 of the solo album, lyrics by DiFranco) from this period.

Other 1990s credits include the Indigo Girls’ side project Hinterland, a live recording with French artist Magnum et Matinées Dansantes (1994), and session work on Ryuichi Sakamoto’s The Long Voyage (Columbia, 1995).

Solo album and 2000s (2000–2010s)

Sara Lee released her debut solo album Make It Beautiful (Righteous Babe Records, 2000; RBR 021-D). Produced primarily by Peter Scherer (of Antena and Scritti Politti collaborations), the album features Gail Ann Dorsey and Eric Redd on backing vocals, Charley Drayton and Carla Azar on drums, Cyro Baptista on percussion, and guest turns from Ani DiFranco, Emily Saliers, Andy Gill (guitar on “Come Round”), and others. Lee wrote or co-wrote most of the music, with lyricists including Pal Shazar and Barbara Gogan.

She sang soprano on the Music from the Divine Divinity Universe soundtrack (Larian Studios, Belgium, 2004). Other credits in the decade include the Sarah Fimm album Given Never Offered (2018) and various sessions.

Later years and Restaurant For Dogs (2020s)

Sara Lee joined the post-punk “supergroup” Restaurant For Dogs — a project led by Barry Andrews (Shriekback, XTC) and Bruce McRae, also featuring Kevin Wilkinson on drums and Clare Hirst on saxophone. Their self-titled debut (self-released, 2023) reunites several musicians from the League of Gentlemen-era orbit. The group is listed as one of her official group memberships on Discogs alongside Gang of Four and the League of Gentlemen.

She also appears on the 2020 album Slower (Funzalo Records) and the Canadian release Dear Poste Haste (Wild Strawberries, 2021).
