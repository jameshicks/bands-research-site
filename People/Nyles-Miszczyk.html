Nyles Miszczyk

Toronto-based recording engineer, producer, and musician. Discogs artist ID: 2149055. Name variation: Miszczyk. Active 2010–2024.

Overview

Nyles Miszczyk is a Toronto recording engineer embedded in the city’s DIY indie and post-punk scene. He is primarily known as the recording engineer for Century Palm’s full run of releases (2014–2015), placing him at the centre of the Toronto underground scene that also encompassed Ketamines, Tough Age, and related bands. Beyond Century Palm, Miszczyk’s credits span Toronto hip-hop, garage rock, and experimental territories, and he has appeared as a performing musician (bass, synth) on a handful of releases.

His Bandcamp page is at https://miszczyk.bandcamp.com/.

Century Palm Connection

Miszczyk recorded all three of Century Palm’s principal physical releases — the 2014 self-titled EP on Planet Of The Tapes, and the 2015 singles “White Light” and “Valley Cyan” — making him one of the primary sonic architects of the band’s recorded output. The band’s lineup across these releases included Andrew Payne (vocals, guitar), Paul Lawton (bass, backing vocals), Jesse Locke (drums), Alexander Hamlyn (lead guitar), Sarah Ford (synth, backing vocals), and later Penny Clark (synth). The “Valley Cyan” 7” was subsequently mastered by Mikey Young.

Mark Sultan Connection

Miszczyk appears on two Mark Sultan releases in 2011. On Livin’ My Life (Sultan Records) he produced one track (B2); on Whatever I Want (In The Red Recordings) he is credited with recording “some bed tracks and things.” Mark Sultan (also known as BBQ) is a prolific Canadian garage/lo-fi artist with roots in Montreal. These credits indicate Miszczyk was active as a producer and engineer in the Canadian indie circuit before his central Century Palm work.

Toronto Hip-Hop / Thesis Sahib

Miszczyk’s earliest Discogs credits are on Thesis Sahib’s Before The End (2010, Grimm Image Records / Tin Men), a Toronto hip-hop album featuring multiple producers. He recorded and produced specific tracks alongside producers James Kirkpatrick, Middlesex Wrestling Team, Lincoln William Cushman, Funken (2), Twiz The Beat Pro, and Adrian “Tank” McRae. This places him in Toronto’s early-2010s hip-hop recording community before his subsequent association with post-punk and indie acts.
