Louis Philippe

Discogs ID: 31838 Real name: Philippe Auclair Born: 24 June 1959 Nationality: French (long resident in UK; styled “French-British” in his milieu) Active: 1983–present Also known as: Philippe Auclair, Great Chefs Of Europe Roles: singer-songwriter, multi-instrumentalist (guitar, piano, keyboards, bass, bouzouki, melodica, percussion), arranger, conductor, producer

Biography

Early Career and Crépuscule (1983–1985)

Before his Él Records period, Philippe Auclair released material under pre-LP guises on Les Disques Du Crépuscule (Brussels). As The Border Boys he put out the single Tribute (1983, Les Disques Du Crépuscule), and as The Arcadians he released With All Our Loving (1984, Les Disques Du Crépuscule) and It’s A Mad Mad World (1985, Crépuscule Au Japon). Around the same time, the duo project Caprice (17) — Louis Philippe with Martyn Bates — was active; Martyn Bates is also an Él-adjacent figure, later appearing on Louis Philippe’s Ivory Tower and on the best-of compilation Claret. The Crépuscule scene (orchestrated chamber pop, Continental sensibility, beautifully designed 12” sleeves) was a natural seedbed for the style he would develop throughout the Él years.

Él Records and Mike Alway (1986–1989)

Louis Philippe was one of the central figures at Él, the London independent label founded by Mike Alway (Discogs ID 98257) in 1984, first distributed through Les Disques Du Crépuscule, then as a Cherry Red Records sublabel from 1986. Alway closed the label in 1989 following a dispute with Cherry Red boss Iain McNay. Discogs identifies LP as an “in-house writer and producer” for Él, a description that fits the evidence: he wrote or co-wrote across multiple Él releases and produced many of them.

His Él-era releases as primary artist:

  • Appointment With Venus (1986, Él) — debut single
  • Like Nobody Do (1986, Él) — produced; with Clive Ives credited
  • Red Roses & Red Noses (1986, Él) — produced; with Sam Hall / Momus (Nick Currie) contributing — early connection to what would become a recurring songwriter-arranger network
  • Anthony Bay / What If A Day (1987, Él)
  • Passport To The Pogie Mountains (1987, Él / Cherry Red / Vap Japan) — his debut LP, also released in Japan via Vap
  • You Mary You (1987, Él) — with Cedilla Ampersand credited; Brian Wilson has a credit on this release
  • Guess I’m Dumb (1988, Él) — produced
  • Ivory Tower (1988, Él) — his second LP (see personnel table below)
  • Yuri Gagarin (1989, Él) — final Él LP; the most densely populated band credit of his career (see personnel table below)

The Él label’s aesthetic — orchestrated torch-pop drawing on pre-rock Continental style, irony, wit, meticulous production — was also home to artists including Momus (Nick Currie), Spike (16) / Dave Gregory (the later pseudonymous multi-instrumentalist Lord Cornelius Plum), and the singer-performer Martyn Bates. The milieu overlaps with but is distinct from the Young Marble Giants / post-punk Rough Trade world; its reference points are Brel, Dury, Gainsbourg, and 1930s British light music rather than punk’s year zero.

Japan and the Polystar / Trattoria Years (1991–1997)

After Él closed, Louis Philippe found a significant audience in Japan. The label Trattoria (a Polystar imprint associated with Cornelius / Keigo Oyamada) released several of his records, and Japan-only or Japan-priority releases became a recurring pattern.

Key releases in this period:

  • Rainfall (1991, Blue Records / Polystar) — co-primary with Dean Brodrick; first post-Él LP
  • Jean Renoir (1992, Blue Records / Polystar / Trattoria) — co-primary with Dean Brodrick; Japan-first release; named after the French filmmaker
  • Delta Kiss (1993, Humbug (2)) — first UK post-Él solo album; he credited artwork and design in addition to writing, performing, and producing
  • Sunshine (1994, Humbug (2))
  • Let’s Pretend (1995, Humbug (2))
  • Jackie Girl (1996, Polystar / Trattoria) — Japan release

He also contributed as guitarist (listed as “Guitares”) on two Bertrand Burgalat-produced records: the 1995 Bertrand Burgalat / A.S Dragon compilation 95C and the 1996 Bertrand Burgalat album Chante, both on Tricatel / Disques Vogue (Paris). Burgalat and his Tricatel label were a major nexus of French chamber pop in the 1990s, and Louis Philippe contributed liner notes to two Tricatel Japan compilation releases (Au Royaume De Tricatel, Au Soleil De Tricatel, both 1998). Cornelius (Keigo Oyamada, Discogs ID 3879) appears alongside him in a shared credit on I Still Believe In You (1996 or later, Japan).

He also worked as arranger and conductor on Spain’s Siesta label, a home for orchestrated indiepop based in Madrid: Initial (1997, Siesta) and Soidemersol (1997, Mercury/Siesta) as arranger/conductor; Algebra Spaghetti (1998, Reverie/Siesta) and Beethoven Chopin Kitchen Fraud (1999, If…/Siesta) and Simultaneous Ice Cream (1999, Reverie/Siesta) as arranger/producer. These Siesta commissions demonstrate his established role as an internationally sought-after orchestral-pop arranger.

Azure and Tricatel Connections (1998–2001)

Nusch (Musiques De Francis Poulenc) (1998, L’appareil-Photo / Tricatel) — a co-primary release with Danny Manners, setting the poetry of surrealist muse Nusch Éluard to music by Francis Poulenc. A sharp left turn into French art-song territory.

Azure (1999, XIII BIS Records, France) — a solo LP with an unusually grand sound: Danny Manners conducted the Prague Philharmonic Orchestra on the record.

9th & 13th (2001, Tricatel, France) — co-primary with novelist Jonathan Coe and Danny Manners (Tricatel). A literary collaboration: Coe (the Booker-shortlisted British novelist and jazz enthusiast) speaks texts; Louis Philippe sings and plays; Manners plays piano, bass, melodica. The record is named after the jazz chords.

He participated in the Voyage London·Paris·Stockholm (1998, Ki/oon Japan) compilation as a member of the Tricatel Record Band choir.

He also arranged and conducted strings on The Clientele’s early releases, including Strange Geometry (2005, Pointy), God Save The Clientele (2007, Merge Records), and the best-of Alone And Unreal (2015, Pointy) — a significant connection to the UK indie-pop and softly psychedelic scene of the 2000s–2010s.

Wonder (4) and Stuart Moxham (2007–2011)

From 2007, Louis Philippe released on the Wonder (4) imprint. Two of these records are joint albums with Stuart Moxham (Young Marble Giants, The Gist):

  • The Huddle House (2007, Wonder (4)) — Stuart Moxham With Louis Philippe; produced by Ken Brake; Stuart played steel-string acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, keyboards, and percussion; Louis Philippe played nylon-string acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, and harmony vocals; co-arranged.
  • An Unknown Spring (2007, Wonder (4)) — solo LP; The Clientele’s Alasdair MacLean guests on acoustic guitar and bouzouki; the Covent Garden String Quartet (strings); Mari Persen (violin and harmonies); produced jointly with Ken Brake.
  • Live (2007, Wonder (4)) — live album.
  • The Ocean Tango (2010, Wonder (4)) — as The Ocean Tango, a 2010 collaboration with Swedish collective Dungen/associated musicians (Douglas Holmquist, Katja Ekman, Petter Herbertsson, Petter Samuelsson, Rikard Heberling, Siri af Burén).

He also contributed bass on The Clientele’s Street Of The Love Of Days (2011, Merge Records).

Tapete Records and Recent Work (2020–2025)

From 2020 onward, the Hamburg-based Tapete Records became his primary home for solo work:

  • Thunderclouds (2020, Tapete Records) — co-primary with The Night Mail (Discogs ID 4555601)
  • Sean O’Hagan Presents: The Sunshine World Of Louis Philippe (2022, Tapete Records) — a Sean O’Hagan-curated compilation of his work; Andy Partridge (XTC), Mike Thorne, and Jim Pepper contributed. A retrospective celebration organised by Sean O’Hagan (The High Llamas).
  • The Road To The Sea (2025, Tapete Records, Europe) — co-primary with The Night Mail

The joint LP with Stuart Moxham, The Devil Laughs (2020, Tiny Global Productions), continued their partnership. Moxham wrote the lyrics; Louis Philippe played keyboards, guitar, piano, and bass; Danny Manners played double bass on most tracks; Ken Brake recorded and mastered.

He contributed piano, harpsichord, vocal arrangements, and mixing to Stuart Moxham’s solo album Fabstract (2024, Tiny Global Productions).

Scene Context

Louis Philippe is a product of the mid-1980s UK/European independent pop moment where the post-punk label networks (Cherry Red, Les Disques Du Crépuscule, Rough Trade) intersected with an interest in pre-rock Continental song: Brel, Gainsbourg, Satie, Poulenc, 1930s light music. At Él, the label’s founder Mike Alway (Discogs ID 98257) cultivated a boutique sensibility around these reference points; Louis Philippe was the label’s house arranger-producer as much as its house artist.

This places him adjacent to but separate from the Young Marble Giants / post-punk lineage that Stuart Moxham represents. The Moxham connection is a meeting of two distinct threads of British independent music: YMG’s stripped minimalism and Louis Philippe’s orchestrated chamber-pop. Their collaborations from 1993 onward — with Louis Philippe adding piano, strings, and orchestral colour to Moxham’s bare acoustic framework — are productive precisely because of this contrast.

He is also a significant node in the Japanese indiepop market of the 1990s, where the Trattoria label (Cornelius / Keigo Oyamada’s imprint) served as a gateway. This Japan-friendly sophisticated-pop orientation connects him to a broader network of artists who found cult followings there (Saint Etienne, Momus, The High Llamas, etc.).

As an arranger, his string arrangements for The Clientele on Strange Geometry (2005) and God Save The Clientele (2007) extend his influence into the UK indie-pop of the 2000s. Sean O’Hagan’s curatorship of The Sunshine World Of Louis Philippe (2022) positions him within the High Llamas / lush-orchestral-pop family.

His career as a French football journalist and news correspondent (Philippe Auclair the writer is a distinct public figure) runs in parallel to his music career, though Discogs does not document this.
