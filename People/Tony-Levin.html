Tony Levin

Born: June 6, 1946, Boston, Massachusetts. Discogs artist ID: 256225. Real name: Anthony Frederick Levin.

Bassist, Chapman Stick player, and occasional vocalist. One of the most recorded session bassists in the New York studio world from the early 1970s onward; later became a defining low-end voice in progressive and art rock through his decades-long partnerships with Peter Gabriel and King Crimson.

Biography

Early Career: Boston to New York (1968–1973)

Levin began his professional career in the late 1960s in Rochester, New York, recording with the Gary Burton Quartet and the Chuck Mangione Quartet — both documented on Discogs from 1970 onward. By 1971 he was active as a New York session bassist, appearing on records for Carly Simon’s self-titled debut (1971), Tim Hardin, Bert Sommer, the Artie Kornfeld Tree, and Brother Jack McDuff.

The 1972–73 period saw a run of significant sessions. He played bass on Don McLean’s self-titled second album (1972, the “American Pie” follow-up) and Lou Reed’s Berlin (1973, produced by Bob Ezrin). The Berlin session was notable — Levin shared bass duties with Jack Bruce, alongside Steve Hunter, Steve Winwood, and Michael and Randy Brecker.

NYC Session Peak (1973–1980)

From 1974 onward Levin was a fixture of upper-echelon New York sessions. Key relationships established in this period:

Arif Mardin (Discogs ID: 15446): 8 shared releases across Mardin-produced records — including sessions for Carly Simon (Boys in the Trees, 1978; Spy, 1979), Ringo Starr (Ringo the 4th, 1977), Margie Joseph (Margie, 1975), and the Charlie Mariano session Mirror (1972). Mardin was the house producer at Atlantic and Levin was a recurring bassist in his productions.

Paul Simon: Bass on Still Crazy After All These Years (1975), one of the landmark records of the decade.

Carly Simon: Recurring session bassist across her early albums; credited on her self-titled debut (1971) and through the late 1970s.

Laura Nyro: Bass on Nested (1978).

Chuck Mangione Quartet / Chuck Mangione: Multiple concerts and studio releases 1970–1976, including Friends & Love (1971), Together (1971), Alive! (1972), and Main Squeeze (1976).

In 1980, he played bass guitar on John Lennon and Yoko Ono’s “(Just Like) Starting Over” (the lead single from Double Fantasy) and appeared on several tracks of Double Fantasy itself. He also played on Yoko Ono’s Season of Glass (1981), recorded in the months after Lennon’s death.

Peter Gabriel (1977–present)

Levin’s entry into Peter Gabriel’s band came with the first solo album (1977, “Car”). He is credited there on “Bass, Tuba, Leader [Barbershop Quartet]” alongside Robert Fripp (electric and classical guitar), Larry Fast (synthesizer), Steve Hunter, and Allan Schwartzberg; the album was produced by Bob Ezrin.

From that point Levin became the anchor of Gabriel’s touring and recording band. Key credits across the Gabriel catalog:

The So album (1986) is the commercial peak. Levin is credited at track level for bass (and “Bass [Drumstick Bass]” on one track), across a majority of the record. The album reached #1 in the UK and spawned “Sledgehammer” and “In Your Eyes.”

Alongside Levin in the Gabriel band across these years: Jerry Marotta (drums, 27 shared releases), David Rhodes (guitar), Larry Fast (synthesizer/Synergy, 16 shared releases), Kevin Killen (engineer, 7 shared releases), and David Bottrill (engineer, 6 shared releases).

King Crimson (1981–1984, 1994–1996, 2014–2021)

Levin joined King Crimson for the “Discipline” era in 1981, forming a core lineup with Robert Fripp (guitar), Adrian Belew (guitar/vocals), and Bill Bruford (percussion). He played Chapman Stick and bass guitar — the Stick’s ability to cover both bass and chordal/melodic lines made him central to the band’s interlocking texture.

The 1981–84 lineup (“Discipline” era) is one of Crimson’s most praised: the Fripp/Belew guitar interlock, Levin’s Stick, and Bruford’s electric kit. Discipline (1981) was the band’s commercial and critical comeback after a seven-year hiatus. Beat (1982) and Three of a Perfect Pair (1984) followed.

Levin departed after the 1984 tour when the band went on hiatus. He returned for the “Double Trio” lineup (1994–96) alongside Pat Mastelotto and Trey Gunn, which released Thrak (1995). He rejoined again for the final 2014–2021 era following Trey Gunn’s departure, appearing on the Live in Vienna and other archival releases from that period.

The “81 to 99” alias in King Crimson’s Discogs entry reflects the band’s own retrospective framing of this period.

Chapman Stick and Instrument Identity

Levin was one of the earliest prominent adopters of the Chapman Stick, an instrument developed by Emmett Chapman in the early 1970s. The Stick is played by tapping strings against the fretboard with both hands and can cover bass and treble registers simultaneously. Levin’s use of it as a dual bass/melodic-texture instrument — particularly in King Crimson — helped establish the instrument’s identity in rock. He later co-founded Stick Men (2) (with Pat Mastelotto and Markus Reuter) as a dedicated Stick-centered trio.

Steps Ahead

Levin is also listed as a member of Steps Ahead (Discogs ID: 29019), the jazz fusion group led by vibraphonist Mike Mainieri that also featured Steve Gadd and others in the late 1970s–80s NYC jazz scene. He also appears on the Mike Mainieri & Friends project.

White Elephant

Earlier in his career Levin was a member of White Elephant (Discogs ID: 142442), an NYC jazz-rock ensemble that released a self-titled album in 1972. White Elephant functioned as a collective featuring players from the New York session world.
