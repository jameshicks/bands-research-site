Pete Carr

American guitarist, producer, engineer, arranger, and songwriter. Born Jesse Willard Carr Jr., April 22, 1950, Daytona Beach, Florida. Died June 27, 2020. Discogs artist ID: 301107.

Also credited as: Jesse Carr, Jesse “Pete” Carr, Jesse Willard Carr, P. Carr, Peter Carr, The Fabulous Pete Carr.

One of the most in-demand session guitarists working out of Muscle Shoals Sound Studios from the early 1970s onward, alongside the core Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section. His lead guitar appears on landmark records by Bob Seger, Paul Simon, Rod Stewart, Art Garfunkel, Willie Nelson, Cat Stevens, and many others. He also recorded two solo leader albums on Big Tree Records (1976, 1978) and produced and engineered extensively from the mid-1970s into the 1990s.

Overview

Pete Carr entered the Muscle Shoals orbit in the early 1970s, initially as a session guitarist supplementing the core Rhythm Section lineup (which had its own guitarist in Jimmy Johnson (4)). He appeared on Livingston Taylor’s 1970 ATCO debut — one of the earliest traceable credits — and within a couple of years was logging sessions across soul, rock, country-soul, and pop for a wide range of labels.

His most sustained commercial relationship was with Bob Seger: Carr played lead guitar on Beautiful Loser (1975), Night Moves (1976), Stranger In Town (1978), and Against The Wind (1980), the last three of which were multi-platinum Capitol Records albums co-produced by the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section. On the Night Moves LP he is credited for lead guitar, rhythm guitar, and acoustic guitar across the full album — a measure of the trust Seger and the MSRS placed in him.

He also played on Paul Simon’s There Goes Rhymin’ Simon (1973, Columbia) — Simon co-produced the Muscle Shoals tracks with the MSRS, and Carr’s guitar appears on two tracks. The Simon connection carried through to Negotiations and Love Songs (1988 compilation) and The Essential Paul Simon (2010), reflecting the continued licensing of those recordings.

Art Garfunkel’s Watermark (1977, CBS) brought Carr into a somewhat different context: the album blends Muscle Shoals rhythm section tracks with orchestral arrangements and The Chieftains, and Carr is listed among the guitarists alongside the full MSRS.

Rod Stewart’s Atlantic Crossing (1975, Warner Bros.) — produced by Tom Dowd — drew on Muscle Shoals players including Carr, David Hood, Roger Hawkins, Jimmy Johnson, and Barry Beckett, mixing them with other session players (Duck Dunn, Steve Cropper, Jesse Ed Davis) on one of Stewart’s best-received albums.

Outside of straight session work, Carr served as producer and engineer on a significant number of records through the late 1970s and beyond, often working with acts on Big Tree Records (a label distributed through Atlantic). He produced and arranged records for acts including Mary MacGregor (“Torn Between Two Lovers”), Dr. Hook, and various others through Big Tree.

By the late 1980s and 1990s, Carr’s engineering work shifted toward blues (Alligator Records releases, King Snake Records projects), while he continued to play guitar on Malaco Records sessions.

Solo / Leader Albums

Not A Word On It (1976) — Big Tree Records

Carr’s debut as a primary artist. He wrote, produced, arranged, and engineered the entire album, playing guitar throughout. Key musicians: Chuck Leavell (keyboards, organ), Tim Henson (keyboards, synthesizer), Clayton Ivey (keyboards, engineer), Harvey Thompson (saxophone), Tom Roady (percussion), Lenny LeBlanc (bass), Roger Clark (drums). Mastered by George Marino.

Multiple Flash (1978) — Big Tree Records

Second leader album. Recorded partly at Muscle Shoals Sound and partly at Wishbone Sound and Fifth Floor studios. Carr produced and engineered. Musicians split across sessions: Roger Hawkins (drums, A1–A2), Roger Clark (drums, A3–B3), Gary Baker/Bob Wray/Butch Leadford (bass), Steve Nathan/Tim Henson/Randy MacCormick (keyboards), Harvey Thompson (saxophone solo, A3), Lenny LeBlanc (rhythm guitar, backing vocals, B3). Carr sings lead on B3 and takes lead vocals on A3.

Blues / Later Career (Late 1980s–1990s)

From the late 1980s onward Carr’s session work shifted toward blues and Southern roots music. He engineered a series of Alligator Records releases and King Snake Records albums, and continued as a session guitarist on Malaco Records (Dorothy Moore, Latimore, others). A 1995 credit on The Devil and Other Blues Demons lists him as bass player — an unusual departure from his primary role as guitarist. He contributed to Back Room Blood (1996, Genes) as a featured artist.
