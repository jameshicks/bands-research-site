John Yates

Graphic designer, US punk and hardcore underground. Discogs artist ID: 293237. Also known as Stealworks (his design studio) and “Popular Mechanics” (occasional credit alias). Born in Leeds, England; based in San Francisco Bay Area.

Biography

John Yates arrived in the US from Leeds, England, and established himself in the San Francisco Bay Area as one of the most prolific graphic designers in the American punk and hardcore underground. His career spans 1987 to the present, with a body of work running to well over 200 credited releases across Discogs.

From 1990 to 1999 he ran Allied Recordings (3) (Discogs label ID: 44003), a now-defunct hardcore label based in San Francisco (PO Box 460683, CA 94146). The label also published Punchline, a graphic magazine with socio-political content. Allied released records by acts including Antischism, Spitboy, and various Bay Area punk acts.

For roughly ten years he worked at Alternative Tentacles (Discogs label ID: 10389), Jello Biafra’s San Francisco-based label, handling design and layout across dozens of releases — making AT the single label he has most densely worked with. Alongside AT work, he built his own design company, Stealworks (Discogs label ID: 284562; web: stealworks.com), which became his main operating name for later decades. He additionally does design work for AK Press (Discogs label ID: 54998), PM Press (Discogs label ID: 179795), and G7 Welcoming Committee Records (Discogs label ID: 44432).

Yates is also a member of the UK art-rock group Ella Guru (Discogs artist ID: 498650), though his output there has been minimal in terms of Discogs credits.

His design style is associated with a cut-and-paste, screen-printing, political-graphic aesthetic — confrontational imagery, dense typography, and a DIY construction ethos. His credit lines often describe his role as “built by,” “assembled and constructed by,” or “sleeve built by,” reflecting the hands-on, collage-based approach rather than digital layout. He frequently uses the term “Popular Mechanics” as a credit alias (reflecting a mechanical/industrial process metaphor) and has logged dozens of variations of his name across pressings.

Open Questions

  • What is the actual nature of Yates’s involvement with Ella Guru? Discogs lists him as a group member but his primary output is all design work.
  • The two earliest credits (1969 Australian Hair cast recording; 1974 UK Just Us) almost certainly belong to a different John Yates — the dates predate any plausible career start and neither has any connection to Bay Area punk. These are Discogs misattributions.
