Rob Gretton

Oct 01, 20265 min read

Rob Gretton

Discogs ID: 236150
Real name: Robert Leo Gretton
Born: 15 January 1953, Wythenshawe, Manchester
Died: 15 May 1999, Manchester (heart attack), age 46
ANVs: Gretton, RG, Robert Leo Gretton
Long-time partner: Lesley Gilbert

Manager of Joy Division and New Order throughout their careers. Co-founder of Factory Records and The Haçienda nightclub in Manchester. Proprietor of Robs Records. One of the defining figures of the Manchester post-punk scene.

Early Career — Pre-Factory (1977–1979)

Before Joy Division, Gretton was a DJ at Manchester’s Rafters club, where he first encountered Warsaw (the band that would become Joy Division). He was already producing local Manchester punk and new wave records at this point.

The Panik — “It Won’t Sell!” (1977, Rainy City Records, SHOT 1)
Gretton co-produced this debut single for The Panik, a Manchester punk band. The release is notable because drummer Steve Brotherdale also played in Warsaw (3) — the pre-Joy Division lineup — connecting Gretton’s earliest production work directly to Joy Division’s formation. Kevin Cummins photographed the sleeve.

Foreign Press — “Downpour” (1979, Streets Ahead Records, SA 1)
Co-produced this 7” for Manchester new wave band Foreign Press (Ralph Bowe, Ralph Bowe, Stuart Bowe, Dave Midwood, John Midwood).

Factory Records Co-Founder (1978–1992)

Gretton was a partner in Factory Records alongside Tony Wilson, Alan Erasmus, Martin Hannett, and Peter Saville. He held a directorial stake and is credited on key releases.

A Factory Quartet (1980, Factory, FACT 24)
Four-artist 2LP compilation — The Durutti Column, Kevin Hewick, Blurt, Royal Family and the Poor. Gretton is credited as “Engineer [Assists]” on the Royal Family and the Poor tracks (D side), alongside engineer Chris Nagle and Martin Hannett as producer.

Section 25 — “Girls Don’t Count” (1980, Factory, FAC 18)
Co-produced (uncredited) with Ian Curtis. Recorded, per Factory lore, at a workshop for the deaf. The three tracks (“Girls Don’t Count,” “Knew Noise,” “Up to You”) are also collected on the Section 25 retrospective Always Now (LTM, 1991), where Gretton’s production credit appears alongside Curtis’s and Hannett’s.

Manager: Joy Division (c.1977–1980)

Gretton began managing Warsaw/Joy Division around 1977–78, having first seen them at Rafters. He remained manager through Joy Division’s entire active life. After Ian Curtis’s death in May 1980, the remaining members formed New Order — Gretton continued as their manager.

His role in Joy Division’s Discogs credits is editorial and conceptual:

  • “Pumped Full Of Drugs” (1985, Columbia/Factory Japan Laserdisc) — New Order live; credited “Other [Prime Mover]” alongside director Anthony H. Wilson
  • “A Factory Record” (1991, Sub Pop/Teenbeat) — compilation; credited “Producer [Fact]”
  • Heart and Soul (1997, London Records) — comprehensive 4CD Joy Division box; Gretton credited “Design Concept [Title And Concept]” alongside Bernard Sumner, Peter Hook, Stephen Morris, and Peter Saville
  • Joy Division (Their Own Story In Their Own Words) (2007, Universal) — posthumous documentary; Gretton credited “Interviewee [Archives]“

Manager: New Order (1980–1999)

Gretton managed New Order from their formation in 1980 until his death in 1999. He co-founded The Haçienda (FAC 51) with Tony Wilson and others in 1982 — the club that became central to the Manchester acid house and rave scene of the late 1980s.

Dun For Money Records (1988)

Gretton backed Dun For Money Records, an early Manchester house music label run by Dave Rofe. The label released six titles between 1988–1990 (DFM 001–DFM 008), including Metre (1988) by E.D.-209 — the acid house project of A Certain Ratio’s Martin Moscrop and Donald Johnson. Other acts on DFM include Fifth Of Heaven, Rowetta, and Ashley & Jackson.

Robs Records (c.1990–1999)

Gretton founded Robs Records as a Manchester independent operating alongside Factory. The label’s catalog was primarily dance, house, and indie-adjacent:

YearArtistRelease
1990The Beat Club”Security”
1991A Certain Ratio”27 Forever,” “Loosen Up Your Mind,” others
1992–1994Sub Sub”Coast EP,” “Ain’t No Love (Ain’t No Use),” “Angel”
1993A Certain Ratio”Turn Me On” (feat. Denise Johnson)

Sub Sub is the most historically significant signing — the project of Jimi Goodwin, Jez Williams, and Andy Williams, who would later form Doves. Their Robs Records run includes “Ain’t No Love (Ain’t No Use)” (with Melanie Williams, 1993) and multiple EPs. Robs Records ceased activity after Gretton’s death in 1999.
