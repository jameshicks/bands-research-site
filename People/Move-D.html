Move D

Discogs IDs: 838 (as Move D), 152 (as David Moufang)
Real name: David Moufang
Origin: Heidelberg, Germany
Active: 1992 – present
Roles: Producer, DJ, electronic musician
Aliases: Dan Jordan, Housegrooves, Liquid Sweet Dreams, Solitaire (12)
Name variations: Move D., Move-D, Move/D, Move D-Phazz

German producer and DJ from Heidelberg. One of the central figures in the 1990s German electronic scene, operating between ambient techno, deep house, and IDM. Co-founder of Source Records (2) (with Jonas Grossmann) and a prolific collaborator across a range of projects spanning Ambient, Techno, deep house, and experimental electronics.

Biography by Era

Early 1990s — Heidelberg Scene and Source Records

Move D (David Moufang) launched his career in Heidelberg in the early 1990s, co-founding Source Records (2) in 1992 with Jonas Grossmann (also known as ENV (2) / Bton). The label became a hub for the Heidelberg scene and released his first records under both the Move D name and the Deep Space Network project.

His debut Move D 12” appeared in 1993. That same year he released Big Rooms (1993, Source Records) as Deep Space Network — a duo with Grossmann — an influential album in the emerging German ambient-techno/IDM movement. The Deep Space Network project ran from 1993 to the mid-2000s and also collaborated with Higher Intelligence Agency.

His first full-length solo LP Kunststoff (1995, Source Records (2), Source 940917) established his range: the double LP moves through ambient, downtempo, electro, house, IDM, and techno across 16 tracks organized into “Phase” interludes. Mastering by Günter Pauler at Pauler Acoustics (Direct Metal Mastering).

Mid-Late 1990s — Warp, City Slang, and Collaborations

In 1996 Move D placed Cymbelin on Warp Records (WAP 80) — a 12” crediting The Designers Republic for artwork — extending his reach into the UK electronic market while the label was at the height of its Artificial Intelligence-era influence.

Also in 1996, Move D co-produced and mixed To Rococo Rot’s debut 12” EP Lips (City Slang, cat. 04987-1). The credit is shared with the band (To Rococo Rot also credited as producer/mixer); recording was by Bernd Jestram; mastering by Bo Kondren. Move D also mixed the To Rococo Rot album Veiculo (1997, City Slang).

He began his long series of collaborative recordings with Pete Namlook (Peter Kuhlmann) under the name Move D / Namlook (also known as Koolfang), releasing on Namlook’s Fax +49-69-450464 label starting with Exploring The Psychedelic Landscape (1996). The series eventually reached nine volumes through the mid-2000s.

He recorded two collaboration EPs with Roman Flügel performing as Ro70: Ro70 Meets Move D (1997) and Ro70 Meets Move D II (1998), both on Source Records. The second features Pole (Stefan Betke).

Hurt Me (1997, Compost Records) was a solo EP on Compost Records.

The Conjoint project brought together Move D with Karl Berger (vibraphonist/jazz educator), Jamie Hodge, Gunter Kraus, and Jonas Grossmann. Their self-titled release appeared on the KM 20 sublabel (1997).

Reagenz — a duo with Thomas Fehlmann — also took shape in this period. (Note: an earlier stub listed Jonah Sharp as the Reagenz partner; Discogs credits only Move D and Fehlmann as members. Sharp may have been confused with this project.)

2000s — Plug Research, BineMusic, and Expanded Network

µst / Preset (2000, Plug Research) brought a collaboration with Sutekh (Seth Horvitz).

Pop For Dwoozle (2004, Fax) continued the Namlook association.

The collaboration with Thomas Meinecke (of FSK) ran through the 2000s under the name Move D/Meinecke, yielding releases on liebe*detail (2005–2007) and including the Bavarian Radio (BR) Hörspiel commission Work (2009) with Michaela Melián and Daniel Kluge.

Linienbusse (2005, City Centre Offices) — a release from the Studio Pankow project with Kai Kroker (Rawell) and Jamie Hodge.

10/11 - Live At Johanneskirche (2006, Autoplate) is a collaboration with Benjamin Brunn; the two subsequently issued work under the name Move D & Benjamin Brunn (Myspace-era project).

Interference (2009, BineMusic) is a collaboration EP with Bedouin Ascent (Kingsuk Biswas), mastered by Helmut Erler.

2010s — Global Profile and Fabric 74

Move D’s profile on the international deep house and techno circuit grew substantially in the 2010s with releases on Running Back, Uzuri, Electric Minds, Curle Recordings, and Aus Music.

Fabric 74 (2014) — his mix CD for Fabric (2) — is a considered statement of his deep house aesthetic, drawing on tracks by Roy Davis Jr., Juju & Jordash (Gal Aner, Jordan Czamanski), Christopher Rau, Smallpeople, and others. Mastered by Dave Turner.

Magic Mountain High — a trio with Gal Aner and Jordan Czamanski (Juju & Jordash) — appears on Building Bridges and in the Fabric 74 tracklist.

Building Bridges (2019, Aus Music, AUSLP010) is his most complete solo album statement of the era. A double LP crossing deep house and ambient, featuring cameos from Reagenz meets Thomas Fehlmann, Fred P. (Fred Peterkin), and D-Man. Photography by Moufang himself.

2020s — Pandemix Sessions and Ongoing Output

The COVID-19 period generated the Pandemix Live Jams series (Vols. 1–6, 2022, Source Records), eventually collected as Pandemix Live Jams Collection. All You Can Tweak (2022, Smallville Records, SMALLVILLE LP 15) is a collaborative LP with longtime associate D-Man (Dirk Mantei), mastered by Helmut Erler, artwork by Stefan Marx.

Solitaire (2023, Mule Musiq) draws on his Solitaire (12) alias. Circle To The Square (2023, Rawax) and Something ‘Bout The D (2024, Source Records) continue the solo output.

Connections

  • To Rococo Rot — produced and mixed debut EP Lips (1996, City Slang) and album Veiculo (1997); connecting the Heidelberg and Berlin post-electronic scenes
  • Deep Space Network — core project with Jonas Grossmann; ambient-techno landmark Big Rooms (1993)
  • Source Records (2) — label co-founded with Jonas Grossmann in 1992; ran until 2005; sublabels include KM 20, 800trak, Urban Flow, reSource Heidelberg
  • Warp Records — Cymbelin (1996) places him in the Artificial Intelligence/IDM orbit alongside Autechre, Boards of Canada-era Warp
  • Pete Namlook — Frankfurt ambient architect; Move D/Namlook collab on Fax label is one of the label’s key long-form series
  • Roman Flügel — Frankfurt producer active as Ro70; two joint EPs on Source Records
  • Thomas Meinecke — FSK member and German cultural theorist; Move D/Meinecke crosses electronic music and literary culture
  • Reagenz — minimal techno duo with Thomas Fehlmann; 1994 debut on Source Records/Reflective Records
  • Magic Mountain High — live-improv trio with Juju & Jordash (Gal Aner, Jordan Czamanski)
  • Helmut Erler (≠) — Hamburg-based mastering engineer; recurs across Move D releases from 2008 onward
  • Karl Berger — Heidelberg-born jazz vibraphonist (Creative Music Studio); Conjoint project bridges jazz and electronics
