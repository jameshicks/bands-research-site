Derf Reklaw

Jul 31, 20267 min read

Derf Reklaw

Real name: Fred Walker
Born: January 11, 1947, Chicago, IL
Died: February 24, 2022, Los Angeles, CA
Discogs artist ID: 18124

Percussionist, flutist, vocalist, composer, choreographer, and lecturer. Reklaw was a polymathic musician whose career spanned Chicago’s soul-funk underground in the late 1960s through Los Angeles’ avant-garde jazz and spiritual jazz scenes of the 2000s–2020s. He is one of the original members of The Pharaohs (2), moved to LA from Chicago in 1979, and became a fixture of the Leimert Park artistic community alongside Horace Tapscott, Billy Higgins, and Kamau Daaood. In LA he became an integral member of The Pan-Afrikan Peoples Arkestra (PAPA) and Build An Ark, and served as an artist in residence at UCLA, Santa Monica College, LA Conservatory, and View Park Academy. He performed and recorded with a wide array of artists including Ramsey Lewis, Eddie Harris, Herbie Hancock, Aretha Franklin, Minnie Riperton, Ahmad Jamal, Sting, and Lionel Hampton.

His name appeared in many credit variants: Derf Reklaw Raheem, D. Reklaw, Derf Raheem, Super Derf, and others.

Chicago Origins: The Pharaohs

Reklaw was a founding member of The Pharaohs (Discogs: The Pharaohs (2), ID: 403653), a Chicago-based Afrocentric ensemble whose membership overlapped substantially with Earth, Wind & Fire session players. Their 1971 debut The Awakening (Scarab Records) credited him as “Pharaoh - Derf Reklaw Raheem (Fred Walker)” and documented his range: African drums (tumba), congas, cowbell, flute, and vocals alongside fellow Pharaohs including Don Myrick, Louis Satterfield, and Willie Woods (2).

During the same Chicago period he appeared on sessions for the Ju-Par Universal Orchestra (with Satterfield, Phil Upchurch, and Quinton Joseph), and contributed congas and bongos to The Dells’ The Dells Sing Dionne Warwicke’s Greatest Hits (Cadet, 1972) and Marlena Shaw’s The Spice of Life (Mercury, 1972).

Notable among these: on Sălongo he was produced by Charles Stepney and Maurice White (of Earth, Wind & Fire). On Love Notes he received an arranger credit alongside Ramsey Lewis — an unusual elevation for a session percussionist. Oasis (under the Ship Of The Desert name, his own project) saw him act as producer, multi-instrumentalist, mixer, and visual designer simultaneously.

From the Nile (1998) — Solo Album

Reklaw released one album under his own name: From the Nile (Ubiquity Records, 1998). No additional personnel are listed in the Discogs database entry; this is his only primary-credit release outside Ship of the Desert.

Los Angeles: Build An Ark (2004–)

After settling in Leimert Park, Reklaw became a member of Build An Ark, the large-format spiritual jazz collective led by Carlos Niño and featuring Dwight Trible on vocals. He appeared on the debut album Peace With Every Step (Kindred Spirits, 2004), playing hand drums and percussion across 15 of the album’s 18 tracks — one of the most consistently credited performers in the ensemble. The Build An Ark collective also included Nate Morgan, Adam Rudolph, Trevor Ware, and Phil Ranelin, many of whom also played with PAPA.

The Pan-Afrikan Peoples Arkestra (PAPA)

Reklaw was a member of The Pan-Afrikan Peoples Arkestra, founded by Horace Tapscott. He is credited on Little Africa (The Village, 2020) — one of the last recordings to feature him — alongside Carlos Niño, Dwight Trible, Jamael Dean, Mekala Session, and many other members. According to archived sources, at a March 2022 memorial performance following his death, the Arkestra dedicated their set to him, his djembe left empty on stage.
