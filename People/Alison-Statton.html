Alison Statton

Discogs ID: 350683 Born: March 1958, Cardiff, Wales Roles: Vocalist, bassist, songwriter, producer

Welsh singer best known as the voice of Young Marble Giants on Colossal Youth (1980, Rough Trade). Her unaffected, conversational delivery — understated to the point of near-silence — became one of post-punk’s most imitated vocal signatures. After YMG dissolved in 1981 she co-founded Weekend, a jazz-inflected post-punk group on Rough Trade that released two LPs and several singles through 1983. In the late 1980s she worked under the name Devine & Statton (with Belgian-based musician Ian Devine), releasing two albums on Les Disques Du Crépuscule. From the 1990s onward she recorded and toured as Alison Statton & Spike (with guitarist/multi-instrumentalist Spike Williams, born Alun Mark Williams) on the Vinyl Japan and Tiny Global Productions labels.

Young Marble Giants (1978–1981)

Young Marble Giants formed in Cardiff around 1978, comprising Statton on vocals and two brothers: Stuart Moxham (guitar, organ) and Philip Moxham (bass). Their sole studio LP, Colossal Youth (Rough Trade ROUGH 8, 1980), was recorded with engineer and co-producer Dave Anderson. The record is drumless and deliberately minimal — Stuart Moxham’s interlocked guitar and organ phrases with Philip Moxham’s bass, over which Statton sang in a near-whispered, pitch-perfect style.

Statton co-wrote “Eating Noddemix” (credited to Statton and Philip Moxham) and contributed the lyric to “Salad Days.” The 4-track 7” EP Final Day followed in 1980 on Rough Trade. The band dissolved around 1981. Colossal Youth was reissued by Domino in 2007 (expanded) and again in 2020 for its 40th anniversary.

Weekend (1981–1983)

After YMG, Statton formed Weekend in 1981. The Discogs profile describes it as a “British IndiePop PostPunk band formed by Alison Statton following the split of Young Marble Giants.” Weekend was a larger ensemble than YMG, incorporating jazz-influenced arrangements, saxophone, and strings alongside the post-punk core.

Live At Ronnie Scott’s Personnel (Rough Trade, 1983)

Released as Weekend with Keith Tippett. Statton and Simon Booth co-produced alongside Grant Showbiz (who also recorded it). Keith Tippett is credited as a primary artist — an unusual jazz collaboration for the band.

Devine & Statton (1989–1990)

In the late 1980s, Statton relocated to Belgium and collaborated with Ian Devine (real name Ian Pincombe, Discogs ID: 407676), a Manchester post-punk guitarist formerly of Ludus. Together they released under the name Devine & Statton on Les Disques Du Crépuscule, Brussels. Devine handled songwriting, guitar, and production; Statton sang.

Alison Statton & Spike (1993–2018)

From the early 1990s Statton collaborated with guitarist and multi-instrumentalist Spike Williams (born Alun Mark Williams; Discogs IDs: 529025 as “Spike (16)”, 4350260 as “Alun Mark Williams”). Williams had been in Weekend as Spike. Together they released three records on Vinyl Japan and one on Tiny Global Productions.

Open Questions

  • The brief mentions “Saturday Night Band (ID: 54906)” but ID 54906 is Working Week, not Saturday Night Band. Working Week was a continuation of Weekend by Simon Emmerson and Larry Stabbins — Alison Statton was not a member. The correct relationship is: Weekend → (some members became) Working Week. Needs clarification whether Statton had any involvement in Working Week or if the brief’s ID was an error.
  • Bizarre Love Triangle (1989) is credited as a New Order cover — the Cardiffians credits list New Order for track A3 as “Composed By, Arranged By.” Worth verifying which specific New Order composition this is and whether Peter Hook’s presence on Cardiffians connects to that.
  • Alison Statton’s Discogs profile only mentions one Wikipedia URL. No solo-artist recording career beyond the listed groups is documented in Discogs.
  • The “Ian Pincombe” entry on Discogs links Ian Devine (ID: 407676) and Ian Pincombe (ID: 3369788) — the Devine & Statton artist profile member uses Ian Pincombe (ID: 3369788), but the Ian Devine artist page uses real name Ian Pincombe. These appear to be the same person listed under two IDs; needs checking.
