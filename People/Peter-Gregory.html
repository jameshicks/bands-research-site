Drummer from Leicester. Best known as the second drummer of The Deep Freeze Mice, replacing Graham Summers from around 1983–84 onward and playing on the band’s entire Cordelia Records run through their 1989 dissolution. Also a member of Evil Dick And The Banned Members.

Biography

Background

Almost nothing is documented about Peter Gregory’s pre-Mice history. Discogs lists his hometown as Leicester, UK, and he credits no formal training or prior bands in the database. His only known alias is Skip Undo, under which no separate releases appear in the Discogs DB.

The Deep Freeze Mice (c.1983–1989)

The Deep Freeze Mice (Discogs ID: 294782) were a Leicester new wave / art-rock band led by Alan Jenkins (guitar, vocals), with Sherree Lawrence (organ, piano, synthesizer, vocals) and Mick Bunnage (bass) as the core trio. Graham Summers had been the band’s drummer through their self-released Mole Embalming Records era (1979–1983). Gregory appears in the DB first on the 1984 double LP I Love You Little BoBo With Your Delicate Golden Lions — the band’s first release on Jenkins’ new label, Cordelia Records — and is credited on every subsequent record through the 1989 finale.

The transition between drummers is not cleanly documented. The two intermediate albums — The Gates Of Lunch (1982, MOLE 3) and Saw A Ranch House Burning Last Night (1983, MOLE 4) — carry no drummer credit in the Discogs records. Rain Is When The Earth Is Television (1986), a compilation of studio and live material, clarifies the overlap: Summers and Gregory are each credited on drums for specific track ranges, suggesting some archival tracks with Summers were included alongside new Gregory-era material.

Evil Dick And The Banned Members (c.2002)

After The Deep Freeze Mice, Gregory is documented as a member of Evil Dick And The Banned Members (Discogs ID: 2995986), a project led by Richard Hemmings (who records under the “Evil Dick And The Banned Members” name). The group also includes Gimp Wilkinson. Their only Discogs-listed release is Rock ‘n’ Random (2002, Polemic Music, UK). This project is the sole post-Mice activity documented in the DB.
