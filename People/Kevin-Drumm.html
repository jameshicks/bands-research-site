Kevin Drumm

Kevin Drumm

Oct 24, 20268 min read

Kevin Drumm

Discogs ID: 40857 Born: 1970, South Holland, IL Based: Chicago, IL

Avant-garde guitarist, noise musician, and electronics artist. One of the key figures in Chicago experimental music from the 1990s onward. Career arc runs from prepared/tabletop guitar in free improvisation circles through increasingly dense electronics and noise, culminating in a massive self-release-driven solo output from the 2010s onward. Maintains strong ties to European and Japanese improvised music scenes alongside American underground noise.

Biography

Early Years and Prepared Guitar (1991–1999)

Drumm grew up in South Holland, Illinois, playing in rock bands before relocating to Chicago in 1991 to work at the Board of Trade. He quickly embedded himself in the city’s growing improvisation community, developing a prepared/tabletop guitar practice — applying magnets, binder clips, chains, violin bows, and other objects to alter the instrument’s sound. Through this work he connected with Jim O’Rourke, serving alongside him in Brise-Glace (Brise-Glace Discogs ID: 308665; members also included Darin Gray, Dylan Posa, Thymme Jones). He also contributed to Gastr Del Sol’s Upgrade and Afterlife and met Ken Vandermark, beginning a long relationship with Chicago’s free jazz axis.

His solo debut came in 1997 with the self-titled LP on Perdition Plastics. The same year he participated in Dirty Old Man River (Radial Records). A second Perdition Plastics LP, Second, followed in 1999.

Free Improv Networks (2000–2004)

Drumm became a prolific presence in international improvisation circuits. Key duo records from this period:

  • Particles And Smears (2000, Erstwhile Records) with Martin Tétreault — guitar and turntables
  • Axel Dörner / Kevin Drumm (2001, Erstwhile Records) with Axel Dörner
  • A split on BOXmedia (2001) with Stephen O’Malley
  • Untitled (2001, Freedom From) with collaborators including John Wiese and Stefan Kushima

He also recorded duo material with Taku Sugimoto (Sonoris) and Ralf Wehowsky (Selektion/Charhizma). During this period he joined Ken Vandermark’s large ensemble project as a member of Territory Band-2 (Discogs ID: 1190325) and Territory Band-3 (Discogs ID: 860389), alongside Axel Dörner, Fredrick Lonberg-Holm, and others.

In 2001 he was also part of Triangles (Discogs ID: 109100) with Leif Elggren.

Mego and the Noise Turn (2002–2007)

Sheer Hellish Miasma (2002, Mego) marked a decisive shift. The record — credited using the initials “KD” — deployed guitar, tapes, pedals, analog synthesizer, and computer assistance in a dense, abrasive format far removed from his improvisation work. It established Drumm’s relationship with Peter Rehberg’s Mego / Editions Mego that would persist for over two decades.

Simultaneously, Drumm appeared on Hanson Records with Land Of Lurches (2003) and collaborated with Aaron Dilloway on I Drink Your Skin (2002, American Tapes / Hanson Records). He also put out Impish Tyrant (2004, Spite) and Horror Of Birth (2005, Chondritic Sound).

The split Gauntlet (2007, Editions Mego) with Daniel Menche documents the improvised noise direction: Drumm on guitar and noise, Menche on organ and noise.

Hospital Productions and Industrial Drone (2007–2012)

A major phase: Drumm released a dense cluster of records through Dominick Fernow’s Hospital Productions — 21 releases 2007–2013, the single largest label concentration in his catalog by release count. Key titles: All Are Guests In The House Of The Lord (2007, split with Prurient), Imperial Distortion (2008), Snow (2008), Imperial Horizon (2009), Malaise (2009), Tannenbaum (2013).

Necro Acoustic (2010, Pica Disk) is a multi-LP retrospective document spanning prepared guitar, electronics, oscillators, organ, and tape — the full range of his instrumental practice across multiple recording sessions. Jim O’Rourke recorded one track; Lasse Marhaug handled layout.

Wrestling (2011, Bocian Records) is a trio record with Jérôme Noetinger and Robert Piotrowicz (Bocian), with Piotrowicz editing and mixing.

Self-Release Deluge (2011–present)

From 2011 onward Drumm launched a sustained program of self-released digital and physical titles under “Not On Label (Kevin Drumm Self-released).” The output is enormous — dozens of releases per year in some periods, released under his own name and the alias Big Ears (6) (a self-released digital series, e.g. Binned, 2021). Titles are often spare: The Back Room, The Kitchen, The Whole House, Dying Air, More Answers, etc. The self-release practice runs concurrently with label releases throughout the decade.

Notable releases during this era:

  • The Icy Echoer (2010, Fragment Factory) — solo, composed
  • The Obstacles Of Romantic Exaggeration (2010, Karl Schmidt Verlag) — produced, composed, mixed by Drumm
  • Relief (2012, Editions Mego) — mastered by Chris Goudreau
  • Venexia (2012, Pan (3)) — electronics, with Axel Dörner, Lucio Capece, Mika Vainio, Bill Kouligas
  • Shut In (2013, self-released) — widely distributed through Editions Mego catalog numbering
  • Trouble (2014, Editions Mego) — mastered by Russell Haswell
  • Wrong Intersection (2014, Handmade Birds) — duo with Joe Beres
  • The Abyss (2014, Erstwhile Records) — duo with Jason Lescalleet; Drumm: guitar, piano, electronics, tape
  • Busman’s Holiday (2015, Erstwhile Records) — duo with Jason Lescalleet
  • Night And Day (2015, Glistening Examples) — with Jason Lescalleet
  • Inexplicable Hours (2018, Sonoris) — with Giuseppe Ielasi
  • The Last Minute Or Later (2019, UZNAM) — large ensemble; extensive Polish improvised music connections (Lasse Marhaug, Maciej Maciągowski, Adam Gołębiewski, Piotr Macha)

2020s

Continued heavy output. Double The Room (2020, Boomkat Editions), Bang (2020, SUPERPANG). VAKNAR and VAAGNER label releases 2022–2025 (with collaborator Ian Hawgood / O.R.). Sheer Hellish Miasma II (2025, Erstwhile Records) — a return to the Erstwhile fold, with Greg Kelley and Taku Unami, electronics/tape/microphone/computer — revisiting the 2002 Mego record’s approach two decades on.
