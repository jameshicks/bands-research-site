Tori Amos

American singer-songwriter, pianist, and composer. Born Myra Ellen Amos, August 22, 1963, Newton, North Carolina. Discogs artist ID: 9902. Also credited as Ellen Amos (Discogs ID: 647203, pre-1988 releases).

Overview

Tori Amos is one of the defining piano-centric voices in 1990s art rock. She trained at the Peabody Conservatory from age five (admitted as the youngest student) before being expelled at eleven — the conservatory’s curriculum conflicted with her ear-based approach. She performed as Ellen Amos on early MEA Records singles (late 1970s–1980) before relocating to Los Angeles and forming Y Kant Tori Read.

Her breakthrough came with Little Earthquakes (Atlantic, 1991), which she co-produced in part with Eric Rosse and which was otherwise produced by Davitt Sigerson. From Boys For Pele (1996) onward she was self-producing. The core of her Atlantic-period sound was built by a tight crew of engineers and session musicians who recur across every album from 1991 to at least 2011.

Career by Era

The Pre-Tori Years — Ellen Amos and Y Kant Tori Read (1978–1989)

Amos released early solo work under her given name Ellen Amos on The Music Bank and MEA Records (late 1970s–1980). These are logged under Discogs ID 647203.

Y Kant Tori Read was a Los Angeles new wave/glam-metal band formed around 1987, with members: Tori Amos (vocals, keyboards), Steve Caton (guitar), Matt Sorum (drums), and Brad Cobb (bass). The band released two singles on Atlantic in 1988 — “The Big Picture” and “Cool On Your Island / Heart Attack At 23” — and a self-titled debut album (Atlantic, 1988). Amos co-wrote the album material and contributed lead vocals, acoustic piano, additional keyboards, and backing vocals. The album was produced and recorded by Joe Chiccarelli, with co-production and keyboards/programming from Kim Bullard. Additional guitar came from Steve Caton and Gene Black, with Paulinho Da Costa on percussion, Ed Thacker on mixing, and executive production by David Kershenbaum. Notably, Robin Zander and Rick Nielsen of Cheap Trick appear on backing vocals. Matt Sorum went on to join Guns N’ Roses. The album failed commercially and Amos has largely disavowed it — she later described it as representing a period when she was “trying to be someone she wasn’t.” It has been cited as an example of 1980s major-label artist development pressure on a fundamentally piano-oriented songwriter.

Amos also sang backing vocals on Al Stewart’s Last Days Of The Century (1988, Enigma) and on Mosquitos by Trimm (Geffen, 1989), suggesting active session work during this period.

Little Earthquakes (1991) — Atlantic Breakthrough

Little Earthquakes (Atlantic, 1991) was a split production between two teams:

Davitt Sigerson tracks (1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9): Recorded by John Beverly Jones, with assistance from Leslie Ann Jones (tracks 1, 3, 5, 6, 8). Sigerson also played church bells on “Me And A Gun” and produced the 1991–92 singles cycle: “Silent All These Years,” “Winter,” “Me And A Gun,” “China,” and “Crucify.”

Tori Amos + Eric Rosse tracks (2, 4, 10, 12): Recorded by Eric Rosse, with bass by Will McGregor on tracks 4, 10, and 12. Mixing on tracks 1, 4, 10, and 12 was handled by Paul McKenna.

The US Atlantic pressing (release ID 6272793) carries no additional credits in the Discogs record; the more detailed US pressing (7997054) confirms the full split. Paul Corkett is credited as recording engineer on a UK pressing (EastWest, 1992).

Cindy Palmano handled art direction and photography — she became a recurring visual collaborator through at least Boys For Pele.

Under The Pink (1994) — Atlantic

Under The Pink (Atlantic, 1993/1994) was co-produced by Tori Amos and Eric Rosse, who also handled recording of guitars and miscellaneous instruments. John Beverly Jones returned to record piano and vocals on nearly all tracks (Eric Rosse recorded track A3). Bass was played by Dominic Genova. Mixing was split between Kevin Killen (tracks A1, A2, A4, A5, A7, B1, B4, B5) and Ross Cullum (A3, A6, B2, B3, B5). Bob Ludwig handled mastering. A full string section (violin, viola, cello) appears, along with Paul McKenna recording bass and drums. Cindy Palmano again on photography.

The transition from the Sigerson co-production of Little Earthquakes to full Amos/Rosse co-production on Under The Pink marks the beginning of Amos’s independent studio control.

Boys For Pele (1996) — Atlantic, first full self-production

Boys For Pele (Atlantic, 1996) was Amos’s first album produced entirely by herself. The record introduced what became her long-running engineering team: Mark Hawley and Marcel van Limbeek (recording and mixing), who handled the majority of the album. Bob Clearmountain mixed tracks 4, 7, 12, and 15. Bob Ludwig mastered.

Session musicians on Boys For Pele:

  • George Porter Jr. (bass) — tracks 3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 12, 15, 16
  • Manu Katché (drums) — tracks 4, 11, 12, 16
  • Alan Friedman (drum programming) — tracks 4, 7, 12

Rob van Tuin assisted Hawley/van Limbeek on mixing and recording. The album was Amos’s most radical departure — recorded on a harpsichord in a church in Ireland, among other locations — and the production credits reflect that isolation: few session players, minimal outside mixing, full self-production.

From The Choirgirl Hotel (Atlantic, 1998) is the first album to feature the core band lineup that would carry Amos’s Atlantic period:

Mark Hawley and Marcel van Limbeek continued as primary engineers and mixers. Jon Astley handled mastering (first appearance in her discography, becomes recurring). Self-produced throughout.

Steve Caton was a member of the original Y Kant Tori Read lineup and returned as a central live and studio guitarist from this album forward.

To Venus And Back (1999) — Atlantic

To Venus And Back (Atlantic, 1999) — a two-disc set (studio disc + live disc) — continued the same core personnel: Matt Chamberlain (drums/percussion), Jon Evans (2) (bass), Steve Caton (guitar), Andy Gray (drum programming, additional tracks 1-6, 1-8, 1-9). Mark Hawley and Marcel van Limbeek again as engineers/mixers. Jon Astley mastered.

Strange Little Girls (2001) — Atlantic; Final Atlantic Album

Strange Little Girls (Atlantic, 2001) was a covers album — a self-produced project that closed out her Atlantic period. Atlantic released the best-of Tales Of A Librarian in 2003.

Scarlet’s Walk (2002) — Epic

Label switch to Sony/Epic. Scarlet’s Walk (Epic, 2002) is a concept album (the first) maintaining her core production team:

  • Mark Hawley and Marcel van Limbeek: recorded and mixed
  • Jon Astley: mastered
  • Matt Chamberlain: drums/percussion
  • Jon Evans (2): bass
  • Robbie McIntosh: guitars (acoustic on tracks 2, 3, 14; Dobro on 17; electric on 1, 3, 6, 14)
  • Mac Aladdin: guitars (acoustic on 5; electric on 2, 3, 11, 12, 15, 16)
  • David Torn: guitar (tracks 6, 16)
  • John Philip Shenale: arranged strings; played Chamberlin flutes (track 13)
  • Sinfonia Of London: orchestra
  • Steven Scott Smalley: orchestrations

John Philip Shenale had previously appeared on Davitt Sigerson productions (including Everything by The Bangles and Boomtown by David + David). His string arrangement work on Scarlet’s Walk marks his entry into the Amos orbit — a connection running through Sigerson.

The Beekeeper (2005) and Later Epic Albums

The Beekeeper (Epic, 2005) is a double album. Core team continued: Hawley/van Limbeek (record/mix), Jon Astley (master). Amos herself played B3 Hammond organ, Hammond chord organ, Bösendorfer piano, and Handclaps.

American Doll Posse (Epic, 2007) followed.

Abnormally Attracted To Sin (2009) — Universal Republic

Label move to Universal Republic. Abnormally Attracted To Sin (2009): same core — Hawley/van Limbeek (record/mix), Jon Astley (master), Matt Chamberlain (drums/loops/percussion), Jon Evans (2) (bass), Mac Aladdin (guitar). Amos: Bösendorfer, synthesizer, organ, Rhodes.

Night Of Hunters (2011) — Deutsche Grammophon

Night Of Hunters (Deutsche Grammophon, 2011) is a classical/art-song album, framing Amos’s work in a chamber music context. Engineers: Hawley/van Limbeek (recorded/mixed). Mastered by Jon Astley. John Philip Shenale arranged strings and woodwinds; Apollon Musagète Quartet performed. Amos’s daughter Natashya Hawley (daughter of Mark Hawley) sang on tracks 2, 5, 7, and 11, credited as “Annabelle.” Executive-produced by Dr. Alexander Buhr for Deutsche Grammophon.

Gold Dust (Deutsche Grammophon, 2012) — orchestral re-recordings.

Unrepentant Geraldines (2014) — Mercury Classics

Label move to Mercury Classics (Universal). Continued same production team pattern.

Native Invader (2017) and Ocean To Ocean (2021) — Decca

Further Universal catalog moves. Later albums on Decca.

Mark Hawley is the husband of Tori Amos. Marcel van Limbeek is their consistent co-engineer. The Hawley/van Limbeek team effectively became her in-house studio operation from Boys For Pele (1996) through at least the Deutsche Grammophon period. Mark Hawley also appears as father of “Natashya Hawley” (vocalist on Night Of Hunters).

Notable Guest and Session Appearances

  • Al Stewart — Last Days Of The Century (Enigma, 1988): backing vocals
  • Trimm — Mosquitos (Geffen, 1989): backing vocals
  • BT — Blue Skies (Perfecto, 1996): featured vocalist (UK single; also released on ESCM)
  • Jawbox — self-titled (Atlantic, 1996): credited as Written-By (wrote “Cutoff,” performed by Jawbox)
  • Pet (Igloo / Tag Recordings, 1996): executive producer
  • The Very Best Of Roy Orbison (Virgin, 1996): liner notes

The Jawbox connection is worth noting: Amos wrote a track (“Cutoff”) for Jawbox’s final Atlantic album, an unexpected bridge between her art rock world and DC post-hardcore.

Connections

  • Joe Chiccarelli — produced Y Kant Tori Read (Atlantic, 1988)
  • Davitt Sigerson — produced six tracks of Little Earthquakes and the 1991–92 singles cycle; also produced A Piano: The Collection (Rhino, 2006)
  • Eric Rosse — co-produced four tracks of Little Earthquakes; sole co-producer on Under The Pink; romantic partner during this period
  • John Beverly Jones — engineer on Sigerson tracks of Little Earthquakes and on Under The Pink
  • Paul Corkett — recording engineer on a UK pressing of Little Earthquakes (EastWest, 1992)
  • Kevin Killen — mixed eight tracks of Under The Pink
  • Bob Ludwig — mastered Under The Pink and Boys For Pele
  • Matt Chamberlain — primary drummer from From The Choirgirl Hotel (1998) through at least Abnormally Attracted To Sin (2009)
  • Justin Meldal-Johnsen — bass on From The Choirgirl Hotel (tracks 1–6, 10)
  • George Porter Jr. — bass on portions of Boys For Pele and From The Choirgirl Hotel
  • John Philip Shenale — strings/Chamberlin on Scarlet’s Walk and Night Of Hunters; previously a Davitt Sigerson/The Bangles collaborator
  • Y Kant Tori Read — her pre-solo band; members included Steve Caton (who returned to her live/studio band) and Matt Sorum (later Guns N’ Roses)
  • Manu Katché — drums on Boys For Pele (tracks 4, 11, 12, 16)
  • David Torn — guitar on Scarlet’s Walk (tracks 6, 16)

Open Questions

  • On the original 1992 US Atlantic pressing of Little Earthquakes (7997054), the Discogs record shows only ten additional credits — does the physical sleeve credit engineers on a per-track or global basis, and is Eric Rosse’s absence from tracks 4 and 10 a Discogs data gap or accurate?
  • The Paul Corkett credit on the EastWest 1992 UK pressing of Little Earthquakes — is this a separate UK-specific recording session or an alternate mix, or a mislabeled credit in Discogs? Corkett’s own page says “Recorded By” but the album notes this pressing only.
  • Boys For Pele was reportedly recorded partly on harpsichord in a church in Ireland (Ballintubber Abbey, County Mayo) — does the Discogs record carry a studio/location note anywhere, or is this web-only information?
  • Bob Clearmountain mixed four tracks on Boys For Pele (4, 7, 12, 15) — was this a deliberate stylistic contrast or a scheduling-driven arrangement? The distinction from the Hawley/van Limbeek tracks is audible.
  • Steve Caton appears in Y Kant Tori Read (1988) and then vanishes from Discogs credits until From The Choirgirl Hotel (1998) and To Venus And Back (1999) — was he part of Amos’s touring band during the Little Earthquakes / Under The Pink era without appearing on studio records?
  • Mark Hawley’s precise entry into the Amos production orbit: was he brought in specifically for Boys For Pele (1996), or were there earlier uncredited sessions? The Boys For Pele record is the first Discogs credit.
  • The Jawbox credit (“Cutoff,” Atlantic, 1996) — how did the Tori Amos / Jawbox connection come about? Both on Atlantic, but the musical distance is significant. Not answerable from Discogs alone.
  • Jon Astley’s mastering appears from From The Choirgirl Hotel (1998) onward. Was he involved on Under The Pink (1994) or Boys For Pele (1996)? Bob Ludwig is credited on those — was the switch from Ludwig to Astley a deliberate choice or label/logistics driven?
  • The Sword And Stone label (Flicker, 2016) — this appears to be Amos’s own imprint. Is it a properly registered label or a one-off credit? Discogs has only one release under it.
  • Night Of Hunters on Deutsche Grammophon: was this a licensing deal or a full label signing? The later albums moved to Decca (also Universal), suggesting a Universal corporate relationship rather than a Deutsche Grammophon artist deal specifically.
