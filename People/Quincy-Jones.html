Quincy Jones

American record producer, conductor, arranger, composer, film score composer, and trumpeter. Born 14 March 1933, Chicago, Illinois; died 3 November 2024, Los Angeles, California. Discogs artist ID: 17546. Real name: Quincy Delight Jones, Jr.

One of the most prolific and decorated figures in twentieth-century American music. His career spans more than seven decades, moving from 1950s bebop and big band jazz through film scoring, funk, and pop production on a global scale. Best known outside jazz for producing Michael Jackson’s trilogy Off The Wall (1979), Thriller (1982), and Bad (1987).

Founded Qwest Records in 1980 as a joint venture with Warner Bros. Records. Founded Vibe magazine in 1993.

Father of actress Rashida Jones and Kidada Jones (with actress Peggy Lipton).

Early Life and Jazz Training (1933–1956)

Born and raised in Chicago, Jones moved to Seattle in his teens, where he began playing trumpet and arranging for local bands. His earliest recorded work came in 1951 as a trumpeter and arranger on MGM sides for Lionel Hampton.

In the early 1950s he studied at the Berklee College of Music in Boston — a pivotal institutional connection: Jones later established the scholarship there that Arif Mardin won, enabling Mardin’s move from Turkey to America and his eventual rise as one of Atlantic’s key producers.

Jones worked as a sideman and arranger in bands led by Dizzy Gillespie and Lionel Hampton before leading his own ensembles. His first album as leader — Quincy Jones Swedish-American All Stars (Prestige, 1953) — documented a 1953 Stockholm session.

Big Band and Mercury Years (1956–1964)

Signed to Mercury Records (artist ID for label cluster: Mercury), Jones established himself as both a recording artist and a producer/A&R figure within the label. His Mercury period produced a substantial run of big band LPs.

“Soul Bossa Nova” (from Big Band Bossa Nova, 1962) became one of his most-sampled compositions, later gaining widespread recognition as the Austin Powers theme.

Production work at Mercury

While at Mercury, Jones produced sessions for Dinah Washington, Nana Mouskouri, and Dizzy Gillespie (including Dizzy On The French Riviera, Philips, 1962). He co-produced The Birth Of A Band alongside his own playing in the trumpet section, with personnel including:

Film Scoring (1965–1978)

After leaving Mercury, Jones transitioned into film scoring, becoming one of the first Black composers to score major Hollywood productions. His soundtrack work ran from the mid-1960s through the late 1970s.

He also scored Roots (A&M, 1977) — the landmark miniseries — and supervised music for The Wiz (Motown, 1978).

A&M Records and Fusion/Funk Period (1969–1981)

Jones moved to A&M Records and entered a productive period blending jazz, funk, soul, and orchestral pop. His A&M albums mark a shift away from purely instrumental big band toward funk and crossover material.

Walking In Space (1969) featured a remarkable ensemble: Freddie Hubbard, Roland Kirk, Hubert Laws, J.J. Johnson, Toots Thielemans, Bob James, Ray Brown, Grady Tate, and engineer Rudy Van Gelder at Van Gelder Studio.

Smackwater Jack (1971) — co-produced with Phil Ramone and Ray Brown — drew on a large New York session pool: Bob James, Eric Gale, Jim Hall, Joe Sample, Grady Tate, Jimmy Smith, Jerome Richardson, Milt Jackson, and Toots Thielemans.

The Michael Jackson Trilogy (1979–1987)

The centerpiece of Jones’s production legacy. He was brought in to produce Michael Jackson’s first solo adult LP, Off The Wall, in 1979, forming a creative partnership that ran through three albums.

Qwest Records and Later Work (1980–1995)

Jones founded Qwest Records in 1980 as a joint venture with Warner Bros. Records, operating it through the 1980s and 1990s. The label brought New Order to the US market and served as home for Jones’s own late-career releases.

The Dude (A&M, 1981)

Jones’s most decorated album as a solo artist: five Grammy Awards (including Album of the Year). Producers: Quincy Jones. Engineer/mixer: Bruce Swedien. Percussionist: Paulinho Da Costa. Guitarist: Steve Lukather. Mastering: Bernie Grundman.

Featured vocalists: James Ingram (“Just Once”, “One Hundred Ways”), Patti Austin (“Baby, Come To Me”), Siedah Garrett.

Back On The Block (Qwest/Warner Bros., 1989)

Grammy Award winner (Album of the Year). An all-star project bridging generations of Black music, featuring Miles Davis, Dizzy Gillespie, Ella Fitzgerald, Sarah Vaughan, Ray Charles, Ice-T, Big Daddy Kane, Kool Moe Dee, Melle Mel, Al B. Sure!, James Ingram, Barry White, Luther Vandross. Produced by Quincy Jones. Associate producer: Rod Temperton. Engineer/mixer: Bruce Swedien.

Q’s Jook Joint (Qwest, 1995)

Similar all-star format. Final major studio album on Qwest.

Connections

  • Arif Mardin — won the Berklee scholarship Jones established; indirect but pivotal connection to the Atlantic Records production lineage
  • Lionel Hampton — first major employer; Jones appears on Hampton’s MGM 78s from May 1951
  • Creed Taylor — produced Jones’s Grammy-winning CTI/A&M recordings; Taylor’s CTI Records later became a blueprint for jazz-crossover
  • Phil Ramone — engineering/production collaborator at A&M
  • Rod Temperton — English songwriter recruited for Off The Wall; his three tracks on that album (“Rock With You”, “Off The Wall”, “Burn This Disco Out”) and “Thriller” on the follow-up defined both albums’ sonic identities
  • Bruce Swedien — long-running engineering partner; present on all three MJ albums and The Dude
  • Rashida Jones — daughter; actress
  • Brothers Johnson — funk duo (Louis Johnson + George Johnson) who appeared on Body Heat, Mellow Madness, I Heard That!! before Jones produced their own albums
  • James Ingram — discovered and championed by Jones; featured on The Dude
