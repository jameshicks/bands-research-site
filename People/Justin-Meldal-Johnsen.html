Justin Meldal Johnsen

Justin Meldal-Johnsen

Oct 16, 202610 min read

Justin Meldal-Johnsen

Discogs ID: 293885
Born: 26 March 1970, Eugene, Oregon, USA
Based: Los Angeles, California
Roles: Bassist, producer, multi-instrumentalist, songwriter

American bassist, producer, and multi-instrumentalist whose career spans three decades from session bass work in the 1990s alt-rock scene through to major-label production credits in the 2010s–2020s. Closely associated with Beck’s core band and the LA indie-rock studio ecosystem. One of the most in-demand bass players of his generation before pivoting to become a full-production figure for artists including Paramore, M83, Wolf Alice, Deafheaven, and Metric.

Biography

Origins and Early Career (late 1980s–early 1990s)

Meldal-Johnsen began his career in the LA alternative rock band Last Carousel, formed in the late 1980s alongside Tony Hoffer. The band later renamed itself This Great Religion (Discogs ID: 3377831). The Hoffer connection proved lasting — the two men went on to work together repeatedly over the next two decades.

His early credited work includes session bass on Willie Nelson’s Healing Hands of Time (Liberty/SBK, 1994) — a credit that illustrates how quickly he was plugged into mainstream session circuits before his alt-rock profile solidified.

Beck’s Core Band (1996–2008)

The central thread of Meldal-Johnsen’s session career is his relationship with Beck. He appears on nearly every major Beck album from the Odelay era onwards, functioning not merely as a bassist but as a full band member and occasional co-mixer.

The Sea Change (2002) credit is especially prominent: Meldal-Johnsen plays bass and upright bass across the majority of the album, contributes backing vocals alongside Roger Joseph Manning Jr., and adds percussion. The album was produced and mixed by Nigel Godrich, making this one of JMJ’s highest-profile collaborations outside his own production work.

On Mutations (1998) he also co-mixed track 13 alongside Godrich and Beck Hansen. The core Beck band at this time also included drummer Joey Waronker and guitarist Smokey Hormel — a tight working unit that recurred across multiple albums.

Ima Robot (2003)

Meldal-Johnsen was a full member of Ima Robot (Discogs ID: 281358), the LA art-pop/rock band fronted by Alex Ebert (later of Edward Sharpe and the Magnetic Zeros). The self-titled debut (Virgin, 2003) features Meldal-Johnsen on bass and backing vocals, alongside Joey Waronker on drums. The band also included Oliver Goldstein and Tim Anderson. He played on all singles including “Dynomite” and “Song #1.”

AIR and the French Connection (2001–2009)

Meldal-Johnsen appears on six AIR releases across the Source/Virgin period. The most significant is 10,000 Hz Legend (2001), where he plays bass on four tracks (A2, A3, A5, B4), worked alongside Brian Reitzell on drums and Roger Joseph Manning Jr. on keyboards, with Tony Hoffer as mixer — a convergence of the same LA session circle. He also appears on Ether Song (2002), Light & Magic (Ladytron, 2002 — same AIR/Source family), and later on Charlotte Gainsbourg’s IRM (2009) which was produced by Beck.

Nine Inch Nails (2008)

Meldal-Johnsen is listed as a live performer on NIN’s The Slip (2008, The Null Corporation), the free download release that captured the touring band. His Discogs group membership page for Nine Inch Nails (Discogs ID: 3857) confirms this live-member status.

Production Career

Pivot to Production (mid-2000s onward)

While never abandoning session work entirely, Meldal-Johnsen began producing records in the mid-2000s. His production work tends towards a clean, layered alt-pop/rock sound — heavy use of keyboards, synthesizers, and drum programming integrated with live performance. He frequently engineers and mixes his own productions and regularly co-produces with his frequent engineering partner Carlos De La Garza.

The Paramore Partnership

The Paramore self-titled record (2013) is among his most prominent production credits, credited as primary producer across the majority of the album alongside Hayley Williams, Taylor York, and Carlos De La Garza as engineer. He co-wrote four tracks (1, 7, 10, 12) with Hayley Williams. The collaboration continued on After Laughter (2017), which he co-produced with Taylor York.

M83 Collaboration

The most creatively expansive production relationship in his catalog is with M83 mastermind Anthony Gonzalez. Hurry Up, We’re Dreaming. (2011) credits him as co-composer of additional music, co-producer, and engineer, and the double album is among M83’s most acclaimed. He returned for Junk (2016, Mute) and DSVII (2019, Naïve).

The Black Queen (Executive Producer)

Meldal-Johnsen served as executive producer on four The Black Queen releases: The End Where We Start (2015 EP), Ice to Never (2015 EP), Secret Scream (2016 EP), and Fever Daydream (2016 LP). He is not credited on their second LP Infinite Games (2018). The Black Queen is the project of Greg Puciato (of The Dillinger Escape Plan) and Joshua Eustis.

Scene Context

Meldal-Johnsen is a product of the Los Angeles session and studio ecosystem that formed around the alt-rock boom of the mid-1990s. His early career ran parallel to producers like Tony Hoffer, Jon Brion, and Rob Schnapf — all of whom circulated through the same Beck / DGC / Bong Load network. His production career is rooted in the same indie-rock infrastructure but crossed into mainstream pop-rock (Paramore, Neon Trees, Robbie Williams) more readily than most of his contemporaries.

The recurring presence of Carlos De La Garza as his engineering partner across a decade of records reflects the standard LA model of a production-engineer pairing functioning as a stable unit — similar to the Butch Vig / Billy Bush or Nigel Godrich / Darrell Thorp relationships. By the mid-2010s Meldal-Johnsen and De La Garza were effectively a production team for the Fueled By Ramen / Atlantic alt-pop corridor.

His work with Deafheaven (Infinite Granite, 2021) — a blackgaze band known for extreme metal — marks a distinct genre stretch, and the ongoing Sargent House credits (2023–2025) suggest a continuing presence in heavier rock production.

Open Questions

  • What is the story of Last Carousel / This Great Religion? Three releases confirmed on Discogs under This Great Religion (Intermission Records, 1990–1991; self-released cassette 1993). No releases found under the Last Carousel name — whether this means the band recorded nothing before renaming, or records exist but aren’t in Discogs, is unresolved.
  • Hankshaw, The Digital Noise Academy, The Elastic Purejoy — all listed as group memberships on Discogs with minimal detail. Are any of these recording acts or studio projects?
  • Nanaki And The Spice (Discogs ID: 8030906) — a 1992 Japanese mini-album on Victor (cat VICL-2100) featuring JMJ on bass, Tony Hoffer on guitar, and Matt Gunnell on drums (the TGR core rhythm section), with Japanese vocalist Satoe Nanaki. Arranged and directed by David Campbell; engineered by Joe Seta. Context: a Japanese-market pop-rock project; worth further investigation.
  • His name variations include “JMJ” — any releases where he was credited under that alias distinctly vs. a transcription error?
  • Did his live NIN tenure extend beyond 2008, or is the Discogs credit limited to The Slip release period?
  • Web sources (Wikipedia, AllMusic, interviews) would likely fill in biographical narrative about how he transitioned from session bassist to record producer, and any formal training or early influences.
