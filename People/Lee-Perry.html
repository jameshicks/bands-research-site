Lee Perry

Jamaican record producer, singer, and songwriter. Born Rainford Hugh Perry, 20 or 28 March 1936, Kendal, Jamaica. Died 29 August 2021, Lucea, Jamaica. Discogs artist ID: 41272.

Known as Lee “Scratch” Perry, The Upsetter, Pipecock Jackson, and numerous other names. One of the foundational architects of reggae and dub, credited as an innovator of studio technique whose work at the Black Ark studio (1973–1979) defined the aesthetic of both genres. Remixed Forest Swords’ “Thor’s Stone” in 2014.

Career Overview by Era

Early Career / Ska Period (1959–1967)

Perry cut his first record around 1959 in Jamaica. He worked through the early 1960s as a singer and general music operative, releasing material on Jamaican imprints including Rolando & Powie, C And N Records, and Worldisc before gaining UK distribution through Port-O-Jam and Island Records (1966–1967). His work at Studio One under Coxsone Dodd gave him early production experience, though the relationship eventually soured.

He released a string of singles in Jamaica and the UK between 1963 and 1967 on labels including Doctor Bird, Ska Beat, and Island Records — instrumentals, rudeboy tracks, and early rocksteady material. His work rate was enormous even at this stage: singles under his own name, productions for other artists, and session appearances.

Upsetter Period (1968–1972)

In 1968 Perry broke decisively with Coxsone Dodd, launching his own imprint called Upsetter. The first release, “People Funny Boy” (1968, Doctor Bird), was a direct attack on Dodd and became a Jamaican hit. He formed The Upsetters as his house band — a tight backing unit that appeared on hundreds of sessions throughout the period.

He ran a heavy production operation from 1968 onward, working with artists including The Uniques, The Mellotones, The Silvertones, Junior Byles, and many others. Key instrumental releases under the Upsetters name — “Return of Django” (1969, Upsetter) — became a UK hit.

Wailers productions (1969–1971): Perry produced two seminal early Bob Marley & The Wailers albums: Soul Rebels (1970, Upsetter) and Soul Revolution Part II (1971, Upsetter), plus key singles including “Small Axe” (1970), “Duppy Conqueror” (1970), and “My Cup” (1970). These recordings are among the first to document Marley’s Rastafarian political convictions in a developed musical form, and Perry’s production — heavy rhythm, unconventional arrangements — shaped their sound significantly. Additional Wailers material appeared posthumously on African Herbsman (Trojan, 1973) and Rasta Revolution (Trojan, 1974).

Dub pioneer: Perry is credited as a key early adopter of dub mixing as a compositional practice. Upsetters 14 Dub Blackboard Jungle (1973, Upsetter) — also known as “Blackboard Jungle Dub” — is considered one of the first dub albums, stripping down reggae backing tracks and treating the recording desk as an instrument. It was originally pressed in a small run for sound system use.

Cloak And Dagger (1972, Upsetter) and Rhythm Shower (1973, Upsetter / Trojan) continued the Upsetters’ LP output.

Black Ark Period (1973–1979)

Perry built his own studio — the Black Ark — at his home in Washington Gardens, Kingston, in 1973. The studio used a 4-track board (later upgraded) and was characterised by his obsessive layering technique: extra-long reverb tails, heavy use of echo units, unconventional instrument placements, and Perry’s own interventions (striking the studio walls, placing objects on tape heads, and other semi-ritualistic methods). The results were sonically distinctive and hugely influential on international reggae, post-punk, and later electronic music.

Personnel on key Black Ark sessions: Sly Dunbar (drums, Heart of the Congos and others), Boris Gardiner (bass, Super Ape), Earl “Chinna” Smith (guitar, Super Ape), Ernest Ranglin (lead guitar, Heart of the Congos), Keith Sterling (piano), Winston Wright (organ), Bobby Ellis / Herman Marquis / Vin Gordon (horns), Noel “Scully” Simms (percussion). The Meditations provided backing vocals on Heart of the Congos.

Black Ark also produced material for non-Jamaican artists, including Martha Velez (Escape From Babylon, 1976, Sire) — among the first non-Jamaican artists to record there.

“Punky Reggae Party” (1977, Tuff Gong): Perry produced this Bob Marley response to punk’s embrace of reggae. Junior Murvin’s “Police & Thieves” (Perry-produced, Black Ark) was covered by The Clash on their 1977 debut, one of the most consequential cross-genre transmissions of the period.

“Disco Devil” (1977, Tuff Gong / Upsetter): A pioneering 12-inch discomix; five volumes of Black Ark discomixes were issued on Studio 16 (2018–2020).

Collapse (1979): Perry burned down the Black Ark studio. Circumstances remain disputed — accounts attribute it to psychic crisis, disputes with Rastafarian associates, commercial pressure, or deliberate destruction. The studio was never rebuilt. Perry relocated to England, then settled in Switzerland.

Post-Black Ark / Exile Period (1980–1988)

After leaving Jamaica, Perry worked erratically across several continents. Releases in this period included:

  • The Return of Pipecock Jackxon (1980, Black Star Liner, Netherlands) — alias release
  • Heart of the Ark (1982, Seven Leaves Records, UK) — Black Ark-era material
  • Mystic Miracle Star (1982, Heartbeat Records, US)
  • Megaton Dub (1983, Seven Leaves Records) and Megaton Dub 2 (1984)
  • History Mystery Prophesy (1984, Mango) — Island-affiliated return
  • The Upsetter Box Set (1985, Trojan Records) — retrospective compilation

He collaborated with the Mad Professor (Neil Fraser) for two dub albums: Lee Perry Meets The Mad Professor In Dub (Chapter One) (1987, Angella Records) and Chapter Two (1987), presaging the long Ariwa relationship.

On-U Sound Period (1987–2021)

In 1987 Perry recorded Time Boom X De Devil Dead (On-U Sound) with Dub Syndicate, co-produced with Adrian Sherwood. Session personnel: Errol “Flabba” Holt (bass), Style Scott (drums), Bingy Bunny and Dwight Pinkney (guitars), Doctor Pablo (keyboards), Bonjo Iyabinghi Noah (percussion), Steely (piano), Deadly Headley Bennett (saxophone). Sherwood engineered and co-produced.

Ariwa Period (1989–2016)

Parallel to On-U Sound, Perry had an extended relationship with Ariwa (the Mad Professor’s UK label):

  • Mystic Warrior (1989) and Mystic Warrior In Dub (1989)
  • Black Ark Experryments (1995)
  • Experryments At The Grass Roots Of Dub (1995)
  • Super Ape Inna Jungle (1995)
  • Dub Take The Voodoo Out Of Reggae (1996)
  • Who Put The Voodoo ‘Pon Reggae (1996)
  • Dub Fire (1998)
  • Techno Dub / Techno Party (both 2000)
  • Black Ark Classics in Dub (2015) / Black Ark Classic Songs (2016)

Later Career (2002–2021)

Perry’s late period was unusually prolific and genre-diverse:

  • Jamaican E.T. (2002, Sanctuary) — widely reviewed as a strong late statement
  • The Orbserver In The Star House (2012, The End Records) — collaboration with The Orb
  • More Tales From The Orbservatory (2013, Cooking Vinyl) — follow-up Orb collaboration
  • Ball of Fire (2012) / Golden Clouds (2012) / Soulman (2012), all Cooking Vinyl
  • Super Ape Returns To Conquer (2017, Subatomic Sound) — with Subatomic Sound System
  • Rainford (2019, On-U Sound) — regarded as one of his strongest late albums
  • Life of the Plants (2019, Stones Throw) — collaboration with Stones Throw Records
  • Lee “Scratch” Perry’s Guide To The Universe (2021, We Are Busy Bodies) — released the year of his death

He continued touring and recording into his mid-80s. A documentary, The Upsetter: The Life & Music of Lee Scratch Perry (2008), documented his career. He died 29 August 2021 in Lucea, Jamaica.

Posthumous releases include King Scratch (Musical Masterpieces from the Upsetter Ark-ive) (2022, Trojan / BMG) — a substantial archival retrospective.
