Rick Danko

Discogs ID: 303351 Born: December 29, 1943, Simcoe, Ontario, Canada (note: the Discogs profile says December 29; biographical sources give December 9 — see Open Questions) Died: December 10, 1999 Nationality: Canadian Role: Bassist, vocalist, songwriter Associated Acts: The Band, Levon And The Hawks, Canadian Squires, Danko Fjeld Andersen, Paul Butterfield, Ringo Starr And His All-Starr Band, Rick Danko Band

Biography

Early Life and the Road to Ronnie Hawkins (pre-1964)

Richard Clare Danko was born in Simcoe, Ontario, a small town in southern Ontario’s farming country. He took up bass in his teens and was drawn to the American rock and roll and country sounds crossing the border. In the early 1960s he connected with the circuit of Canadian musicians orbiting Ronnie Hawkins, the Arkansas rockabilly singer who had relocated to Canada and was running one of the tightest road bands in North America.

Danko played in the loose precursor configuration known as the Canadian Squires before settling in as bassist with Hawkins’s backing band, The Hawks. That band — also known as Levon And The Hawks at various points — became the core ensemble that would eventually split from Hawkins and evolve into The Band.

The Hawks and Bob Dylan (1964–1967)

When Hawkins’s Hawks fragmented, the remaining core unit — Danko, Levon Helm, Richard Manuel, Garth Hudson, and Robbie Robertson — began working as an independent backing band. In 1965 they were hired by Bob Dylan to serve as his electric touring band, playing the controversial electric shows that scandalized folk audiences through 1965–1966. Danko handled bass and backing vocals, part of a three-vocalist front line with Helm and Manuel.

The Royal Albert Hall concert recordings (1966, the “Manchester Free Trade Hall” recordings) document this configuration, with Danko on bass and backing vocals. These eventually surfaced officially across multiple Columbia/Legacy Bootleg Series releases.

The Band (1968–1976)

Retreating with Dylan to Woodstock, New York in 1967, the group recorded the informal demos that became Columbia’s The Basement Tapes (released 1975). Working in the pink house that gave the debut album its name, they developed the sound that broke as The Band.

The Band’s Capitol Records debut Music From Big Pink (1968), produced by John Simon, introduced them as a fully formed entity. Danko contributed bass, vocals, and occasional violin. The self-titled second album The Band (1969, Capitol) — largely recorded at a rented house in Sammy Davis Jr.’s pool house in Los Angeles — deepened the country-Americana approach, with Danko playing bass, violin, and trombone and sharing vocal duties alongside Helm and Manuel.

Stage Fright (1970, Capitol), Cahoots (1971, Capitol), and the New Year’s Eve live document Rock Of Ages (1972, Capitol) followed, as did the covers album Moondog Matinee (1973). The Band reunited with Dylan for the 1974 tour (documented on Before The Flood, Asylum, 1974) and the studio album Planet Waves (Asylum, 1974), both engineered by Rob Fraboni.

The Band’s last great original-material run was Northern Lights-Southern Cross (1975, Capitol), engineered and mixed by Fraboni, followed by Islands (1977, Capitol). Danko received a mixing engineer credit on Northern Lights-Southern Cross in addition to his performer role.

All songs on Northern Lights-Southern Cross were written by Robbie Robertson, but across the Band catalog Danko co-wrote several key tracks — including “This Wheel’s On Fire” (with Dylan), “Caledonia Mission,” “Bessie Smith” (with Manuel), and “Stage Fright.”

The Last Waltz (1976/1978)

The Band’s formal farewell concert took place Thanksgiving Day 1976 at Winterland Ballroom, San Francisco. Filmed by Martin Scorsese and released as The Last Waltz (Warner Bros., 1978), co-produced by Robertson and Fraboni with John Simon, the concert featured an extraordinary roster of guests: Bob Dylan, Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Van Morrison, Eric Clapton, Neil Diamond, Paul Butterfield, Muddy Waters, Dr. John, and others. Danko played bass, violin, and sang lead and harmony vocals throughout.

Solo Work and Late Career (1977–1999)

Danko’s only official solo studio album, Rick Danko (Arista, 1977, cat. AB 4141), was co-produced with Fraboni. The record featured an all-star cast: Eric Clapton (lead guitar on “New Mexicoe”), Robbie Robertson (lead guitar on “Java Blues”), Garth Hudson (accordion on “New Mexicoe”), Richard Manuel (Fender Rhodes on “Shake It”), Levon Helm (harmony vocal on “Once Upon A Time”), Doug Sahm (guitar), Blondie Chaplin (background vocals), Gerry Beckley (guitar, background vocals), Bobby Charles (co-writer on multiple tracks), and Denny Seiwell (drums). Fraboni co-produced, played tambourine and vibraslap, and sang background vocals. His brother Terry Danko also played drums. A single, “What A Town” (Arista, 1977), and later “Java Blues” (Arista, 1978), were extracted from the album.

In 1978–1979 Danko toured extensively with Paul Butterfield, forming one of the most documented informal duo partnerships of the era. Live recordings from this period were later released commercially: Danko & Butterfield Live At The Golden Bear Huntington Beach 21.11.78 (Retroworld/Floating World, 2018) and Live From The Blue Note, Boulder Co., 1979 (Retroworld/Floating World, 2016). A Live At The Lone Star (Bear Records/Retroworld/Floating World, undated) also documents this duo.

In 1989 Danko joined Ringo Starr And His All-Starr Band for the inaugural All-Starr tour, appearing on the resulting album Ringo Starr And His All-Starr Band (1990) and the Live From The Pacific Amphitheatre video (1989).

In 1991 he joined Norwegian guitarist Jonas Fjeld and American singer-songwriter Eric Andersen (2) to record under the name Danko Fjeld Andersen, releasing Rick Danko Jonas Fjeld Eric Andersen (Mercury Norway, 1991) and follow-up Ridin’ On The Blinds (Grappa, 1994), with Danko serving as producer, vocalist, and guitarist. Aaron Hurwitz (Professor Louie) served as a key collaborator on The Band’s reunion releases through the 1990s.

The Band reunited (without Robertson) for Jericho (Essential!/Pyramid, 1993), High On The Hog (Transatlantic/Pyramid, 1996), and Jubilation (River North/Platinum, 1998). Danko remained a member through the final years. He issued the live albums In Concert (Breeze Hill, 1997) and “Times Like These” (Breeze Hill/Big River, 2000) under his own name with the Rick Danko Band.

Cryin’ Heart Blues (Other People’s Music, 2005) was released posthumously.

Rick Danko died on December 10, 1999, of heart failure at his home in Marbletown, New York, the day after his 56th birthday (or 56th anniversary of his birth date — see Open Questions on the date discrepancy).

Open Questions

  • Birth date discrepancy: The brief and Wikipedia give December 9, 1943; the Discogs profile text gives December 29, 1943. The true date is December 9 per most biographical sources — this is almost certainly a Discogs data entry error, but cannot be confirmed without web sources.
  • The Hawks / Ronnie Hawkins period (1960–1965): Discogs has very limited documentation of Danko’s earliest credits with Hawkins. The full chronology of his time as a Hawk is not captured in Discogs structured data.
  • The Basement Tapes sessions (1967): The informal recordings made with Dylan at Big Pink are heavily documented in bootleg form but the official release (1975 Columbia; 2014 complete bootleg series) doesn’t fully capture the session chronology. Personnel details for individual tracks are incomplete in Discogs.
  • Danko’s instructional video Rick Danko’s Electric Bass Techniques (Homespun Video, 1987): Discogs has a credit entry (with Happy Traum listed) but no full release details were pulled.
  • Full personnel on Danko/Butterfield live recordings: The live duo releases on Retroworld/Floating World have limited credits in Discogs. Alan Robinson (3) appears with 2 shared Danko releases in the collaborator data and is likely the drummer on these recordings.
  • The Band’s 1983 reunion lineup: The The Band Is Back (Pioneer Artists, 1983) video documents the first post-Last Waltz reunion; And Then There Were Four (1983, Chicago) documents this period as well. Personnel details beyond the core four (Danko, Helm, Manuel, Hudson) have not been fully investigated.
  • Richard Manuel’s death (1986): The band was on a reunion tour when Manuel died by suicide. The period 1983–1986 and the transition from five-piece to four-piece lineup deserves more documentation than Discogs provides.
  • “This Wheel’s On Fire”: Credited to Danko and Dylan across multiple releases. The writing relationship is documented in Discogs but the backstory is web-only.
