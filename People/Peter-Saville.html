Peter Saville

Graphic designer. Born 1955, Manchester. Discogs ID: 1069010 (listed as “Peter Saville (2)” on Discogs — see Peter Saville (2) for the full credits article and disambiguation note).

Created the visual identity of Factory Records from the label’s first release (FAC 2, A Factory Sample, 1979) and designed sleeves for nearly every significant Factory release through the label’s collapse in 1992. His approach — Constructivist/modernist typography, art historical reference, deliberate avoidance of conventional band photography — became as definitive to the Factory aesthetic as Martin Hannett’s production or Tony Wilson’s philosophy.

Key design credits:

  • FAC 2 A Factory Sample (1979)
  • FACT 10 Unknown Pleasures (1979, Joy Division) — black-on-black pulsar data graph
  • FACT 25 Closer (1980, Joy Division) — Bernard Pierre Wolff funerary photography; with Martyn Atkins
  • FACT 50 Movement (1981, New Order) — with Grafica Industria
  • FAC 73 “Blue Monday” (1983, New Order) — die-cut floppy disk shape
  • FACT 75 Power, Corruption & Lies (1983, New Order) — Henri Fantin-Latour painting

Work outside Factory is extensive: parallel design relationship with Dindisc (Virgin’s new wave subsidiary) covering Orchestral Manoeuvres In The Dark, Martha And The Muffins, and others (1980–1983); sleeve credits for EG (Roxy Music, King Crimson, Lounge Lizards), Chrysalis (Ultravox, Duran Duran), Virgin (Peter Gabriel’s So), and many others.

Studio entities over the years: D/R Institute (c. 1981–1982, with Brett Wickens — appears on Discogs credits as “15% D/R Institut (Saville/Wickens)”; no dedicated web documentation found, known only from sleeve credits); Peter Saville Associates (founded 1983 with Brett Wickens as partner, closed 1990 when Saville joined Pentagram); Grafica Industria; The Peter Saville Studio.

Later work: design director at Pentagram; Creative Director of Manchester.

Creative Director of Manchester — appointment details

Appointed consultant Creative Director to Manchester City Council in 2004. The role was structured as a consultancy rather than a staff position. Fee reported as £120k per annum. (confidentials.com, 2013 article)

Manchester City Council announced it would no longer fund the role on 25 February 2011, with funding ending 31 March 2011. Council leader Sir Richard Leese stated: “The severity of the budget cuts we face means the Council can no longer fund the role of creative director.” Leese also noted Saville had made “an important contribution as creative director over recent years,” citing work on “international exhibitions and festivals, the city’s cultural strategy and the new Metrolink design.” Marketing Manchester and partner organisations were asked to seek alternative funding to maintain the post. (The Drum, 2011-02-25)

Post-2011 status is ambiguous: a Bloomberg article dated April 2012 refers to him as “the Creative Director of Manchester” in its headline and appears to be a live interview, suggesting he continued to use or hold the title beyond the Council’s funding cut — possibly under Marketing Manchester. The original appointment did not carry a fixed term; funding cuts were the mechanism of termination, not expiry of a defined contract period.
