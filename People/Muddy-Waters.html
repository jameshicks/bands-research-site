Muddy Waters

Born McKinley Morganfield, 4 April 1913, Issaquena County, Mississippi; died 30 April 1983, Westmont, Illinois. Discogs artist ID: 59246. Also credited as McKinley Morganfield (Discogs ID: 695388 — a separate alias entry with its own name-variation list, primarily used for songwriter credits).

Overview

Muddy Waters is the central architect of postwar Chicago electric blues. Raised on Stovall’s Plantation in Mississippi, he was recorded in the field by Alan Lomax for the Library of Congress in 1941–42 — those sessions, eventually issued as The Complete Plantation Recordings (Chess, 1993), document the raw Delta style he carried north when he moved to Chicago in 1943. By 1948 he was recording for Aristocrat Records (the Chess precursor), and his first sides — “I Can’t Be Satisfied” / “I Feel Like Going Home” — were immediate local hits. Through the 1950s he built the canonical Chicago electric blues ensemble: electric slide guitar over amplified rhythm section, with Little Walter on harmonica, Jimmy Rogers on guitar, Otis Spann on piano, and a rotating cast of drummers including Fred Below and Francis Clay.

Waters was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987 (posthumously, as Performer). His direct influence on British R&B — the Rolling Stones took their name from his 1950 Chess single “Rollin’ Stone” — and on American rock is pervasive and well-documented. He was also a direct mentor to the South Side blues revival generation: Paul Butterfield worked the same clubs before forming the Paul Butterfield Blues Band, and the two collaborated formally on Fathers and Sons (Chess, 1969) and The Muddy Waters Woodstock Album (Chess, 1975).

Key Compilations / Archival Issues

  • The Complete Plantation Recordings (Chess, 1993) — 1941–42 Library of Congress field recordings.
  • The Chess Box (Chess/MCA, 1989) — definitive 3-disc retrospective.
  • Trouble No More: Singles 1955–1959 (Chess/MCA, 1989) — focused singles compilation.
  • His Best 1947–1955 and His Best 1956–1964 (Chess/MCA, 1997) — two-volume overview.
  • Checkerboard Lounge: Live Chicago 1981 (Eagle Vision, 2012) — late-career live document.

Notes on the Discogs Alias Entry

McKinley Morganfield (ID: 695388) is a distinct Discogs entry — not just an alias field on the main artist page. It carries its own extensive list of spelling variants (over 80 name variations) and songwriter credits. It also shows group memberships not on the main entry: Son Simms Four, Muddy Waters Blues Band (ID: 2842158), and Muddy Waters & Friends (ID: 6829429). These appear to be legacy Discogs data artefacts rather than substantively different entities.

Research Queue

  • Folk Singer (Chess, 1964) carries almost no personnel credits in Discogs beyond a lacquer cut. Who played bass and drums on that session? Sleeve credits or a reissue booklet would confirm — the musicians are reportedly Willie Dixon (bass) and Clifton James (drums).
  • Electric Mud (1968) Discogs credits only list three producers; the full session band is not credited. Who were the session musicians on that album?
  • Checker and Cadet Concept are listed as Chess sublabels in the vault — confirm whether After The Rain (1969) was on Cadet Concept specifically or on a different Chess imprint.
