Malcolm Mooney

African-American vocalist, poet, and visual artist. Original lead singer of Can, 1968–1970. Discogs artist ID: 110309.

Biography

Background and arrival in Cologne

Malcolm Mooney is an American sculptor and poet who was living and working in Europe in the late 1960s when he encountered the nascent Can. The core band — Holger Czukay, Jaki Liebezeit, Irmin Schmidt, Michael Karoli, and flautist David Johnson — had been operating under the name Inner Space. Mooney joined the group in 1968, and the ensemble briefly billed itself “The Can” before settling simply on Can.

Mooney’s vocal approach was unlike anything operating in rock at the time: stream-of-consciousness, incantatory, often cycling single phrases into repetitive rhythmic mantras. His delivery was rawer and more confrontational than the smooth psychedelic styles of the era, and it suited Can’s improvisational, tape-editing methodology.

Monster Movie era (1968–1970)

Mooney is the vocalist on Can’s debut album, Monster Movie (1969), credited as “Linguistic Space Communicator” on the sleeve (which listed the band as “The Can”). The album was recorded at Schloss Nörvenich and released on the short-lived Music Factory label (catalog SRS 001). It contains four tracks: “Father Cannot Yell,” “Mary, Mary, So Contrary,” “Outside My Door,” and the side-long “Yoo Doo Right” (20:14), the last being a reduction by Czukay of a six-hour improvisation.

Contemporaneous sessions produced what would later be compiled on Delay 1968 (released Spoon Records, 1981): Mooney is credited for vocals and writing across those tracks, which represent an even rawer early version of the band.

Mooney also appears on two tracks of Soundtracks (1970) — “Soul Desert” and “She Brings The Rain” — the only Can studio album to feature both Mooney and his successor Damo Suzuki, making it a transitional document.

Departure

Mooney left Can in 1970 reportedly due to mental health pressures. He returned to the United States. Damo Suzuki, a Japanese busker encountered by Liebezeit and Schmidt in Munich, replaced him and the band entered its most celebrated commercial and critical period (Tago Mago, Ege Bamyasi, Future Days).

Rite Time reunion (1986 recording / 1989 release)

Mooney reunited with Can for the album Rite Time, recorded in 1986 and released on Mercury in 1989. The full classic lineup — Czukay, Liebezeit, Schmidt, Karoli — assembled with Mooney back on vocals. The sessions were produced by Czukay, Karoli, and mixed with René Tinner. This is the only post-1970 studio album to feature Mooney as vocalist.

Malcolm Mooney And The Tenth Planet (1998)

Mooney led his own ensemble, Malcolm Mooney And The Tenth Planet, for a self-released CD (catalog 7012-2, 1998). The album features a shifting cast including guitarists Bruce Anderson and Myles Boisen, saxophonist/keyboardist David Slusser, tubist Tom Heasley, and percussionist Karen Stackpole, among others. Production credit is shared between Mooney, Paul Bang, and Myles Boisen (also engineer). The album includes a version of “Father Cannot Yell,” credited to Can. Discogs lists the group as also having members Dax Pierson, Jason Stamberger, Len Paterson, Peter Conheim, Stephen Clarke, Kenny Kearney, Kent Randolph, Marc Weinstein, Dean Santomieri, and Dave Barrett.

Post-millennium collaborations

  • Salted Tangerines (2002, Twisted Nerve / XL Recordings): A single-sided 7” with Andy Votel (catalog VOTEL2). Mooney contributes improvised vocals; mixed by Martin Desai.
  • Rip Van Winkle (2003, Twisted Nerve TN043): A full LP co-credited to Dave Tyack With Malcolm Mooney. Mooney reads Washington Irving’s original Rip Van Winkle text as spoken word; Tyack provides incidental music. Genres span IDM, Krautrock, leftfield, and modern classical.
  • The Lost Tapes (2012, Mute / Spoon Records): Can vault excavation curated by Irmin Schmidt, Jono Podmore, and Daniel Miller. Mooney has music/lyric credits on multiple tracks across the three-disc set (tracks spanning discs 1 and 2).
  • Tan Man’s Hat (2019, RareNoise Records): Album by Pavees Dance (Sean Noonan’s group). Mooney appears as vocalist alongside Jamaaladeen Tacuma (bass), Ava Mendoza (guitar), and Alex Marcelo (keyboards).

Mooney was also interviewed for the documentary Romantic Warriors IV: Krautrock Part 1 (2019, Zeitgeist Media).
