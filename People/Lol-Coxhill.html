Lol Coxhill

Real name: George Lowen Coxhill
Born: 19 September 1932, Southsea, Hampshire, England
Died: 10 July 2012, London, England
Discogs artist ID: 60742

British soprano saxophonist and free improviser. One of the most distinctive voices in post-1960s British jazz and improvised music, known for a highly personal approach that blended free improvisation with deadpan humour, vocal performance, street busking, and an eclectic range of sonic environments — from Canterbury prog and art-rock to solo concert tours and small-ensemble free jazz.

Name variations: “The Inimitable” (self-applied subtitle on several releases)
Aliases: Buck Funk · Loxhawn Rondeaux · Sonny Boy Saltz

Biography

Origins and early career (1950s–1960s)

George Lowen Coxhill spent years as a street busker and semi-professional musician before establishing himself in London’s experimental scene. He came up through the early British jazz circuit, eventually gravitating toward improvisation and the avant-garde. His association with the nascent Canterbury scene came via Delivery, where he played alongside Steve Miller (3), Pip Pyle, Phil Miller, Roy Babbington, Laurie Allan, and Jack Monck. Delivery were part of the same Canterbury/South-East milieu that produced Soft Machine and Caravan, though their music leaned harder into jazz.

Art-rock and prog adjacency (1970–1975)

By 1970 Coxhill had become a sought-after session voice. He appears on Kevin Ayers’s Shooting At The Moon (Harvest, 1970) and contributed to Matching Mole adjacents and broader Harvest / Brain label releases. The album Toverbal Sweet (Mushroom, 1972) — a trio with Dutch drummer Pierre Courbois and pianist Jasper Van’t Hof — is an early document of his collaborator-driven approach, blending composed material with freer passages.

The Caroline Records album Coxhill / Miller / Miller / Coxhill (1973), co-produced and co-sleeved with Steve Miller (3), signalled a departure from the session world toward a fully self-directed practice. Fleas In Custard (Caroline, 1975) is a pivotal solo statement: Coxhill played and processed soprano saxophone (acoustic, ring-modulated, synthesized multitracked), composed, produced, and photographed the record himself — a template for the radical self-sufficiency of his later solo work.

The split LP Welfare State / Lol Coxhill (Caroline, 1975) documents his involvement with Welfare State, the anarchic British theatre-arts company.

Solo and Nato years (1979–1990)

Through the 1980s Coxhill recorded extensively for the French label Nato, run by producer Jean Rochard — who became his most frequent collaborator (17 shared releases). Key Nato documents include:

  • Chantenay 80 (Nato, 1981) — live material from France
  • Instant Replay (Nato, 1983) — sopranino and soprano sax, vocals
  • The Dunois Solos (Nato, 1984) — solo saxophone, illustrated and sleeved by Coxhill himself
  • The Inimitable (Chabada/Nato, 1985) — the title says it all
  • Halim (Chabada/Nato, 1993)

He also recorded Slow Music (Pipe, 1980) with pianist/multi-instrumentalist Morgan Fisher, a duo document of quiet sustained improvisation. The Digswell Duets (Random Radar, 1979) project extended this practice.

The French Gigs LP (AAA, 1983) with Fred Frith — referenced in the original stub — documents live French concert appearances; Frith edited and collaged the Coxhill side.

Free improv scene networks (1980s–2000s)

Coxhill was a core participant in Derek Bailey’s Company (2) network, placing him in contact with virtually the entire European free improvisation world: Evan Parker, Fred Frith, Steve Lacy, Anthony Braxton, Han Bennink, George Lewis, and dozens more. Three Blokes (FMP, 1994) — soprano sax trio with Steve Lacy and Evan Parker — is the most direct document of this fraternity, recorded for Jost Gebers’s FMP label.

His collaboration with Scottish collective members Raymond MacDonald, George Burt, George Lyle, and others produced a run of releases on various labels through the 1990s–2000s.

Termite One (Bruce’s Fingers, 1990) documents a quintet with George Haslam, Paul Rutherford (2), Simon H. Fell, and Paul Hession — a key Northern English free jazz document.

Coxhill On Ogun (Ogun, 1998) collected earlier and new solo recordings for the Ogun label, with pianist Michael Garrick appearing on two tracks. He also appears on Ogun releases by the Dedication Orchestra and others as a key figure of the label’s network.

Final years and posthumous releases (2000s–present)

Coxhill continued performing and recording into the 2000s, including late sessions with Bill Wells and the Bill Wells Octet, releases on Ictus Records (the Darkly / Darkly Again / Moot & Lid trio of CDs), and continued appearances with Scottish musicians via the George Burt / Raymond MacDonald groups. He died in London on 10 July 2012.

Posthumous archival releases have appeared steadily: Collingbourne Kingston Village Hall (scatterArchive, 2022), Oceans Of Milk And Treacle (Room40, 2021), and the substantial retrospective All This Crazy Gift Of Time (The Recordings 1969–1973) (Esoteric Recordings, 2024) have extended his documented legacy.
