Chris Watson

Chris Watson

Oct 21, 20266 min read

Chris Watson

Born: Sheffield, England Full name: Christopher Richard Watson Role: Electronics, tapes — founding member of Cabaret Voltaire; field recording artist; BBC Natural History Unit sound recordist Discogs ID: 76866

Overview

Watson has had three distinct careers running sequentially: as the electronics and tape operator in Cabaret Voltaire (1973–1981); as co-founder of The Hafler Trio with Andrew McKenzie (early 1980s–1987); and as one of the most highly regarded field recording artists working today, with an extensive solo catalogue on Touch label from 1996 onward. His BBC Natural History Unit work — field recordings for David Attenborough documentaries and similar productions — runs parallel to the solo catalogue. He has also contributed location recordings to major artists including Sigur Rós and Björk, and to the Hildur Guðnadóttir score for the Chernobyl HBO miniseries.

Following Richard H. Kirk’s death (21 September 2021), Watson and Stephen Mallinder reactivated Cabaret Voltaire in October 2025 for the band’s 50th anniversary. A UK tour followed; a final world tour runs through October 2026.

Cabaret Voltaire (1973–1981)

Watson was building tape loops and oscillators independently before the group coalesced. He and Richard H. Kirk were the first to collaborate; Stephen Mallinder joined as vocalist and bassist. Watson’s role was electronics and tape manipulation — “Mr. Retro” as he was known within the band, managing the tape machine operations and live electronic processing. He also played organ (Vox Continental) and contributed to composition.

His final album with the full lineup was Red Mecca (Rough Trade ROUGH 27, 1981), on which he has Composed By credit and plays organ and tape recorder. His name appears in written-by credits on 2x45 (1982), which was in production as he left.

Watson left late 1981 for a job in television — reportedly a pragmatic decision, not an artistic rupture. He remained on cordial terms with Mallinder and Kirk.

The Hafler Trio (early 1980s–1987)

Watson co-founded The Hafler Trio with Andrew McKenzie (born 11 May 1963, Newcastle-upon-Tyne) shortly after leaving Cabaret Voltaire. The group’s name referred to a real duo (Watson and McKenzie) plus a fictional third member — Dr. Edward Moolenbeek — described as a scientist. The conceit was part of the group’s deliberately absurdist multimedia practice.

Early releases appeared on L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords and Touch (the newly founded label of Jon Wozencroft), with the work spanning audio, performance, and text. Watson’s era as Hafler Trio member produced: “BANG” - An Open Letter (Doublevision, 1984), Seven Hours Sleep (L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords, 1985), The Sea Org (Touch, 1986), Brain Song (Touch, 1986), and others.

Watson left in late 1987. McKenzie continued The Hafler Trio as a solo project with a floating roster of collaborators — including Neville Brody, Peter Greenaway, Annie Sprinkle, John Duncan, and Zbigniew Karkowski — through the 1990s and 2000s.

BBC and Film Work

Watson joined the BBC Natural History Unit, contributing field recordings to major documentary productions including David Attenborough series. This work runs parallel to but largely uncredited on the commercial discography.

Notable credited non-solo work:

  • Sigur Rós — Heima (EMI, 2007): field recordings alongside Jana Winderen for the documentary film of Sigur Rós performing in Iceland
  • Björk — Utopia (One Little Indian, 2017): field recordings
  • Hildur Guðnadóttir — Chernobyl (music from the HBO Miniseries) (Deutsche Grammophon, 2019): field recordings
  • Bill Wyman’s Blues Odyssey: film director credit (Scantrade Entertainment)

Solo Career (Touch, 1996–)

Watson’s solo career is built on field recordings — location sounds captured in specific environments and edited into album-length works. The recordings are non-manipulated in the electronic sense; the art is in selection, placement, and spatial fidelity. Touch is his primary label, though he has also released self-issued work and collaborations on Ash International, Moog Recordings Library, and others.

His closest creative partner across this career is Jon Wozencroft — Touch co-founder and designer — who contributes art direction and photography to virtually all Watson’s solo releases (30 shared credits on Discogs). Denis Blackham handles mastering across the catalogue.
