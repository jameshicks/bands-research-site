Henry Threadgill

Born: 15 February 1944, Chicago, Illinois
Discogs artist ID: 129531
Roles: Alto/tenor/baritone saxophone, flute, bass flute, composer, arranger, bandleader
Real name: Henry Luther Threadgill

American composer and multi-instrumentalist. AACM member since the mid-1960s. Leader of three successive ensembles that defined different phases of his career: Air (4) (free jazz trio, 1971–1986), Henry Threadgill Very Very Circus (early 1990s), and Henry Threadgill’s Zooid (2001–present). Winner of the Pulitzer Prize in Music (2016) for In For A Penny, In For A Pound. Appeared on Material’s Memory Serves (1981, Celluloid Records) playing alto saxophone.

Biography

Chicago Roots and AACM (1960s–1971)

Threadgill grew up on Chicago’s South Side and came of age in the orbit of the AACM. He was associated with the collective through the late 1960s, absorbing its principles of self-determination, original composition, and collective organization. His Discogs credits from this period include work with AACM peers — his 1979 solo project X-75 Volume 1 features AACM multi-instrumentalists Joseph Jarman and Douglas Ewart alongside four bassists: Fred Hopkins, Leonard Jones, Rufus Reid, and Brian Smith (9). Vocalist Amina Claudine Myers also appears. The unusual instrumentation — reeds, multiple basses, and voice, no drums — is characteristic of the AACM’s willingness to work outside jazz convention.

A distinctive personal invention from this period is the Hubkaphone, a homemade percussion instrument built from hubcaps, which Threadgill plays on multiple Air recordings.

Air (1971–1986)

Air formed initially as a trio to perform music by Scott Joplin and Jelly Roll Morton for a theatrical production (c.1971), but quickly became a vehicle for Threadgill’s original compositions. The core lineup was:

  • Henry Threadgill — reeds, flute, Hubkaphone
  • Fred Hopkins — bass
  • Steve McCall — drums/percussion

The trio recorded first in Japan on Trio/Whynot Records (Air Song, 1975; Air Raid, 1976; Morning Prayer, 1976), then moved through a succession of US and European labels. Their 1978 album Air Time (Nessa Records) was produced by Chuck Nessa and documents the trio at full development. Arista Novus became a key US home for several albums (Open Air Suit, 1978; Montreux Suisse Air, 1978; Air Lore, 1979; X-75 Volume 1, 1979), and Italian label Black Saint picked up international releases (Live Air, 1980; Air Mail, 1981). A notable concert document is Live At Montreux 1978 (Arista Novus).

Air Lore (1979, Arista Novus) is particularly notable for being tributes to Scott Joplin and Jelly Roll Morton, returning to the ensemble’s origins.

When Steve McCall quit, Pheeroan Aklaff took the drum chair and the group renamed itself New Air. Subsequent New Air releases appeared on Black Saint: 80° Below ‘82 (Antilles, 1982), continuing through the mid-1980s.

A comprehensive 2010 box set The Complete Novus & Columbia Recordings Of Henry Threadgill & Air (Mosaic Records) documents the Arista Novus and Columbia periods.

Henry Threadgill Sextett (1982–1993)

Overlapping with the New Air period, Threadgill formed the Sextett in the early 1980s — actually a septet, as two drummers were treated as a single percussion unit. The group was a larger, more compositionally elaborate vehicle. Key members across the Sextett’s life included:

  • Fred Hopkins — bass (continuity from Air)
  • Pheeroan Aklaff — drums (from New Air)
  • Diedre Murray — cello, co-producer
  • Frank Lacy — trombone, French horn, flugelhorn
  • Rasul Siddik — trumpet
  • Craig Harris (3) — trombone (early lineup)
  • Olu Dara — cornet (early lineup)
  • Ray Anderson — trombone
  • Reggie Nicholson — drums

The Novus albums (You Know The Number, Rag, Bush And All, Easily Slip Into Another World) were produced by Ed Michel and represent the Sextett at its most fully realized.

Material and the New York Scene (1981)

Threadgill appeared as a session player on Material’s landmark Memory Serves (1981, Celluloid Records), playing alto saxophone. The session brought together a remarkable group of New York and AACM musicians: Bill Laswell (bass), Fred Frith (guitar, violin, vibraphone), Sonny Sharrock (guitar), George Lewis (3) (trombone), Olu Dara (trumpet), Billy Bang (violin), Charles K. Noyes (drums/percussion), Fred Maher (drums), and Michael Beinhorn (synthesizer). Martin Bisi engineered and co-produced. Threadgill also played on Ornette Coleman’s Of Human Feelings around this period, and appears on a 1978 Delmark session (Reality) in a smaller group context.

Other notable session appearances from the early 1980s include:

  • David Murray — Ming (1980, Black Saint); Murray’s Steps (1983); Home (1982)
  • Shing02 — (see session credits)
  • Bill Frisell / Kip Hanrahan period sessions

Very Very Circus (1991–1997)

Threadgill dissolved the Sextett in the early 1990s and formed Very Very Circus, a band built around an unusual front-line instrumentation: two electric guitars (no piano), two tubas, and various winds. The name varied as “Henry Threadgill Very Very Circus” and “Very Very Circus Plus.” Key members:

  • Brandon Ross — guitar
  • Masujaa — guitar
  • Marcus Rojas — tuba
  • Edwin Rodriguez — tuba
  • Curtis Fowlkes — (trombone, per related sessions)
  • Mark Taylor (3) — French horn
  • Gene Lake — drums
  • Larry Bright — drums (two-drummer concept continuing from Sextett)
  • Pheeroan Aklaff — drums (some dates)

Too Much Sugar For A Dime (Axiom, 1993) is the most widely documented VVC album; co-produced by Bill Laswell, it features guests Leroy Jenkins and Jason Hwang (violin), Simon Shaheen (oud/violin), and Venezuelan percussion players. Oz Fritz engineered. The two-guitarist, two-tuba lineup gives the album a bottom-heavy, surging quality unlike any other jazz record of the period.

The Columbia albums (Carry The Day, Makin’ A Move, Where’s Your Cup?) represent Threadgill’s period on a major label. Makin’ A Move (1995) is credited to Threadgill solo and also documents VVC personnel.

Zooid (2001–present)

Around 2001 Threadgill founded Zooid, another unusually voiced ensemble. Zooid’s stable lineup through its mature period:

  • Henry Threadgill — alto saxophone, flute, bass flute
  • Liberty Ellman — acoustic guitar (also mixed and produced later releases)
  • Jose Davila — tuba, trombone
  • Christopher Hoffman — cello
  • Dafnis Prieto — drums (early/debut albums; later replaced)
  • Elliot Kavee (Elliot Humberto Kavee) — drums (later albums)
  • Tarik Benbrahim — oud (debut album)

Up Popped The Two Lips (2001) — the debut Zooid album — was mixed by Bill Laswell, connecting back to the Axiom-era relationship. By In For A Penny, In For A Pound (2015), Liberty Ellman was serving as guitarist, mixer, and co-producer. Ellman was also present on the subsequent Zooid releases Dirt…And More Dirt (2018, Pi Recordings, not a primary Zooid release — appears as a contributor) and Poof (2021).

Also on Pi Recordings: Everybody’s Mouth’s A Book (2001), credited to Threadgill with his “Make A Move” band rather than Zooid proper; personnel overlaps.

ECM and Chicago AACM Reunion (2015)

Made In Chicago (2015, ECM Records) is a live recording documenting a reunion of AACM figures convened by Jack DeJohnette: Threadgill (alto saxophone, bass flute), Muhal Richard Abrams (piano), Roscoe Mitchell (multiple reeds), Larry Gray (bass, cello), and DeJohnette (drums). Produced by DeJohnette and Dave Love; executive producer Manfred Eicher.

14 Or 15 Kestra: Agg and Later Work

Threadgill also leads the Henry Threadgill 14 Or 15 Kestra: Agg (Discogs ID: 6534536), a large ensemble. Listen Ship (2025, Pi Recordings) is his most recent release as of 2025, credited to Threadgill in the role of composer and conductor.

He also appears as arranger/conductor on Kip Hanrahan’s sessions (The Third Power, 1991, Axiom; Ritual Beating System, 1992, Axiom; Every Silver Lining Has A Cloud, 1993; Reality, 1993), and contributed to recordings by Shing02 (2001 Gigi sessions, Palm Pictures) and various other artists.
