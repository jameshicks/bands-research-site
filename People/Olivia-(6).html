Born December 9th, 1979 in Okinawa, Japan, Olivia is the oldest of three children (her sister Caroline, and brother Jeffery are also musicians). Her family moved around a lot, as her father was a U.S. Marine. In the mid 1990s, she went to the Okinawa Actors School, where in 1996 she was spotted by the boss of Rising Productions, who asked her to join a new pop-group, Dance & Dream. The female trio released their first single in August of that year.

Two years later, the group disbanded and and Olivia went on alone. She worked with Jean-Michel Jarre & Tetsuya Komuro on a single for the FIFA ‘98 World Cup, “Together Now”.

Overview

Olivia Lufkin is a Japanese-American singer-songwriter born in Okinawa on 9 December 1979, the eldest of three musical siblings in a family that moved frequently due to her father’s U.S. Marine service. Recruited through the Okinawa Actors School in 1996, she joined J-pop trio Dance & Dream (D&D), which debuted that year before disbanding in 1998; Olivia then moved into a solo career that opened with a collaboration alongside Jean-Michel Jarre and Tetsuya Komuro on the FIFA ‘98 World Cup single “Together Now.” Her output across the 2000s — rooted primarily on Cutting Edge and Avex Trax and spanning J-pop, pop rock, trip-hop, and emo — was built in close partnership with her brother Jeffrey Lufkin (14 shared credits) and Anna Tsuchiya (12 shared credits). After a decade-long gap, she returned to recording in 2023 via Microids Records, producing new material through 2025.
