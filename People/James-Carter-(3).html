James Carter (3)

Jazz saxophonist, clarinetist, and multi-reedist. Born James Marcellus Carter, 3 January 1969, Detroit, Michigan. Discogs artist ID: 337785. Name variations: Carter, J. Carter.

Overview

James Carter is one of the most technically commanding reed players to emerge from the post-bop generation. A product of Detroit’s rich jazz lineage, he plays tenor, soprano, alto, and baritone saxophone, bass clarinet, contrabass clarinet, flute, and the rare sarrusophone (a brass-keyed woodwind). His playing encompasses hard bop, post-bop, soul-jazz, free improvisation, and forays into Django Reinhardt’s gypsy jazz and orchestral settings. He came up in The Lincoln Center Jazz Orchestra and maintained a parallel career with avant-garde figures, including extended tenure in World Saxophone Quartet and as a member of Lester Bowie’s ensembles.

His Atlantic period (1994–2003) was his highest-profile commercial run; subsequent work has ranged from organ-trio dates at Kenny Cox’s Baker’s Keyboard Lounge in Detroit to free-saxophone quartet improvisation on the Mahakala Music label in 2022.

Biography

Early Career and Detroit (1969–1992)

Carter grew up in Detroit and came under the influence of the city’s jazz community. He first appears on Discogs records as a teenage participant in Bird/Trane/Sco Now! (ID: 1928690), a documented ensemble credit from 1986. His early professional work placed him in the DIW Records orbit in Japan, where many American post-bop players found a receptive market. In 1991 he appeared as a sideman on three DIW releases: the Don Pullen–George Adams–led Fat Man And The Hard Blues (Black Saint), Funky T. Cool T., and The Organizer. He also appeared on Alone Together (Antilles, 1991), an early solo credit under his own name.

The same year, 1991, he joined Julius Hemphill’s Sextet (ID: 1232279) — a significant avant-garde connection in the AACM-adjacent world.

DIW and Atlantic Debut (1993–1994)

JC On The Set (DIW, 1993, ID: 5627431) was his first full leader date. Recorded in Japan, it established his core working unit of that period: bassist Jaribu Shahid, drummer Tani Tabbal, and pianist Craig Taborn. The set mixed Carter originals with material by Don Byas, Duke Ellington, Sun Ra, and Ellington/Tizol’s “Caravan.” All three instruments — alto, tenor, baritone — were deployed across the session.

In 1994 he appeared on They Came To Swing (Columbia) and signed with Atlantic.

The Atlantic Years (1995–2003)

Carter’s Atlantic run produced six albums under his own name and established him as a known quantity in mainstream jazz coverage.

The Real Quietstorm (Atlantic Jazz, 1995, ID: 1315490) was his Atlantic debut proper — a solo-saxophone recital in which he switches instrument track by track, covering Monk, Sun Ra, Ellington, Don Byas, and Jackie McLean, with bass parts split between Dave Holland (tracks 2, 3, 7, 8) and Jaribu Shahid (tracks 4–6, 9), drums shared by Leon Parker and Tani Tabbal, and Craig Taborn on piano. Produced by Yves Beauvais, engineered by Joe Ferla, mastered by Ted Jensen.

Conversin’ With The Elders (Atlantic, 1996, ID: 1315512) was a tribute-band concept: Carter assembled a group around jazz elders — trumpet players Lester Bowie and Harry “Sweets” Edison, tenor player Buddy Tate, baritone player Hamiet Bluiett — and the core rhythm section of Shahid/Tabbal/Taborn. Material included Bowie’s “FreeReggaeHiBop,” Charlie Parker’s “Parker’s Mood,” Coltrane’s “Naima,” and Anthony Braxton’s “Composition 40Q.” The album document of Carter’s reverence for jazz history while working in an unencumbered post-bop style.

In Carterian Fashion (Atlantic, 1998, ID: 769867) was a three-organ-player showcase: Craig Taborn, Cyrus Chestnut, and New Orleans pianist Henry Butler each appear on separate tracks, alongside Shahid and three drummers (Tabbal, Alvester Garnett, Leonard King). Alto saxophonist Cassius Richmond appears on three tracks. Trumpet player Dwight Adams is present throughout. The tracklist included Carter originals and pieces by Spencer Barefield, Cassius Richmond, and Don Byas. Produced by Yves Beauvais, recorded by Danny Kopelson, mastered by Ted Jensen.

Chasin’ The Gypsy (Atlantic, 2000, ID: 472722) departed sharply — an entire album of Django Reinhardt repertoire, with Carter as both leader and co-producer alongside Yves Beauvais. The ensemble included guitarist Jay Berliner, guitarist Charlie Giordano, violinist Regina Carter, Brazilian percussionist Cyro Baptista, and bassist Romero Lubambo.

Layin’ In The Cut (Atlantic, 2000, ID: 500854) moved toward electric funk: the band was guitarist Marc Ribot, guitarist Jef Lee Johnson, bassist Jamaaladeen Tacuma, and drummer Calvin Weston — all Ornette Coleman/Material-adjacent players. Produced by Yves Beauvais.

Gardenias For Lady Day (Columbia, 2003, ID: 5154527) was a Billie Holiday tribute with full string arrangements. Arrangements shared between Cassius Richmond (tracks 1–4) and Greg Cohen (tracks 5–9), with John Hicks on piano, Peter Washington on bass, Victor Lewis on drums. A chamber ensemble featured Sandra Park (concertmaster/violin) and a full string section. Vocalist Miche Braden appeared on “Strange Fruit” and “More Than You Know.” Produced by Yves Beauvais, recorded by Danny Kopelson. Released on Columbia (SACD hybrid).

Kansas City, Blood on the Fields, Soundtrack Work (1996–1998)

Carter appeared on the Kansas City soundtrack (Verve, 1996, ID: 2872498) for Robert Altman’s film and the follow-up KC After Dark (Verve, 1997). He played clarinet, bass clarinet, and baritone on Wynton Marsalis’s Pulitzer-winning oratorio Blood On The Fields (Columbia, 1997, ID: 2772535). He also appeared on Herbie Hancock’s Gershwin’s World (Verve, 1998, ID: 5108073) as a featured soloist.

Live at Baker’s Keyboard Lounge (2004)

Live At Baker’s Keyboard Lounge (Warner Bros., 2004, ID: 834600) documented Carter at Detroit’s historic jazz club (a Carter family connection — Baker’s Keyboard Lounge is the world’s oldest jazz club, located in Detroit). Carter produced the date, with guests including David Murray, Johnny Griffin, and local Detroit players. Kenny Cox (ID: 327654) and Gerard Gibbs (ID: 722541) appeared as pianists/organists. This session also featured Leonard King on drums — the same personnel who would form the James Carter Organ Trio from roughly this period onward.

Gold Sounds (2005)

Gold Sounds (Brown Brothers Recordings, 2005, ID: 3877104) is the co-leader date with Cyrus Chestnut, Reginald Veal, and Ali Jackson noted in the stub. Remarkably, the tracklist is drawn entirely from Pavement songs — “Stereo,” “Cut Your Hair,” “Summer Babe,” “Trigger Cut,” etc. Carter plays contrabass sarrusophone on “Platform Blues,” soprano sax on tracks 2, 3, 6, and tenor sax on tracks 1, 4, 5, 7. Chestnut handles Fender Rhodes, Hammond B3, and acoustic piano across different tracks. Veal splits between acoustic and electric bass. Produced by Darryl Elkins and Jake Cohn. Catalog number BBR-CD1 on Brown Brothers Recordings.

Present Tense (2008) and EmArcy Period

Present Tense (EmArcy, 2008, ID: 1348942) brought a new rhythm section: D.D. Jackson on piano, James Genus on bass, Victor Lewis on drums, Rodney Jones on guitar (select tracks), and Dwight Adams on trumpet/flugelhorn. Carter wrote three tracks; repertoire also included a Django Reinhardt piece, Dave Burns’s “Rapid Shave,” and Gigi Gryce’s “Hymn Of The Orient.” Produced by Michael Cuscuna (of Mosaic Records), recorded and mixed by Jim Anderson.

Caribbean Rhapsody (EmArcy, 2011, ID: 5023984) extended this period into Caribbean-influenced jazz territory.

Later Work and Collaborations (2016–2022)

Carter appeared on Salutes The Chicago Blues Tradition (AECO Records, 2016), Live From Newport Jazz (Blue Note, 2019), and U2’s Live At The Apollo For One Night Only (Island Records, 2021).

(D)IVO (Mahakala Music, 2022, ID: 22668440) is a free-saxophone quartet improvisation: Carter on baritone, Ivo Perelman on tenor, Tony Malaby on soprano, and Tim Berne on alto. Seven untitled parts, no rhythm section. Recorded, mixed, and mastered by Jim Clouse. Perelman painted the cover watercolors.

Connections

  • Wynton Marsalis — played Blood On The Fields (1997); both in The Lincoln Center Jazz Orchestra
  • Julius Hemphill — member of The Julius Hemphill Sextet; Hemphill was a founder of World Saxophone Quartet which Carter later joined
  • Hamiet Bluiett — baritone sax peer; shared credits on Conversin’ With The Elders and World Saxophone Quartet
  • Lester Bowie — trumpet; guested on Conversin’ With The Elders; Carter in Lester Bowie’s New York Organ Ensemble
  • Herbie Hancock — featured soloist on Gershwin’s World (Verve, 1998)
  • Dave Holland — bass, The Real Quietstorm; Holland is a recurring Atlantic-era collaborator
  • Craig Taborn — long-running piano partner from early DIW dates through Atlantic era
  • Ginger Baker — drummer on Coward Of The County (Atlantic, 1999) — unusual cross-genre pairing
  • Marc Ribot — guitarist, Layin’ In The Cut (2000) — connects to the downtown NYC improv world
  • Jamaaladeen Tacuma — bassist, Layin’ In The Cut; also an Ornette Coleman alumnus
  • Dee Dee Bridgewater — Carter appeared on Bridgewater’s Eleanora Fagan (EmArcy, 2010)
  • Ivo Perelman — free improv collaboration, (D)IVO (2022)
  • Tim Berne — alto sax peer, (D)IVO (2022)
  • Michael Cuscuna — produced Present Tense; Cuscuna is the Mosaic Records/Blue Note archivist
