Discogs ID: 1465046
Name variations: “Beth”, “Anderson”
Active: 2003–2009 (Discogs-documented)

Multi-disciplinary contributor — vocalist, violinist, and visual artist — documented across two distinct musical networks in Scotland: the Fife-based Fence Records circle centred on King Creosote (Kenny Anderson), and the Aberdeen experimental-folk scene around Alan Davidson (4)‘s Kitchen Cynics. Her Discogs credits span performance, violin, and illustration, suggesting she moved comfortably between musical and visual roles.

Overview

Beth Anderson’s most substantial documented project is Kenny And Beth’s Musakal Boat Rides — an informal recording credited to herself and King Creosote (real name Kenny Anderson), released in 2003 on Fence Records and subsequently reissued by Domino Records (2003 and 2018). The shared surname Anderson and the intimate character of the project strongly suggest a close personal connection to Kenny Anderson, possibly as a partner or family member. The “Musakal Boat Rides” title is characteristically whimsical in the Fence tradition; the record features contributions from several Fence Collective regulars including James Yorkston, Pip Dylan, and Stu Bastiman, with all songs written by King Creosote except “Lavender Moon” (written by Pip Dylan).

She sang vocals on track 8 (“Floating”, co-credited with The Pictish Trail) of the Fence Reunited compilation (2004, Fence Records, FNC FC2) — a gathering of the core Fence Collective artists including King Creosote, James Yorkston, Lone Pigeon, HMS Ginafore, and others.

Her visual-art practice is documented in King Creosote’s 2009 Domino LP Flick The Vs, where she is credited with Illustrations — providing artwork for what was King Creosote’s highest-profile release to date, recorded with producers Paul Savage and Jon Hopkins and released on Domino.

Separately from the Fence Collective context, she appears as a featured violinist on the Kitchen Cynics album Disconnected (2009, Les Enfants Du Paradiddle, enf 65) — a CDr by Alan Davidson (4), credited as “The Kitchen Cynics.” This places her in Davidson’s Aberdeen experimental network alongside cellist Duncan Hart and slide guitarist Philip Johnston. Whether this was a one-off contribution or part of a longer working relationship with Davidson is unclear from Discogs data alone.

Release notes

Kenny And Beth’s Musakal Boat Rides (Fence Records, 2003 / Domino, 2003 & 2018)
13-track folk/acoustic album credited to King Creosote as primary artist; Beth appears as co-performer, effectively co-billed in the project title. All songs written by King Creosote (Kenny Anderson) except track 9 “Lavender Moon” (Pip Dylan). Other performers include James Yorkston, Pip Dylan, Stu Bastiman, Captain Birdseye, Captain Geeko The Dead Aviator, and Billy Pilgrim R.I.P. — a typically informal Fence Collective ensemble. Mastered by Guy Davie. Domino distributed/issued the album in the UK alongside the Fence original. Reissued again in 2018.

The Fence Collective — Fence Reunited (Fence Records, 2004, FNC FC2)
14-track Fence Collective compilation. Beth appears on track 8, “Floating” (written by Down The Tiny Steps), credited as vocalist alongside The Pictish Trail. Other artists on the compilation include King Creosote, James Yorkston, Lone Pigeon, HMS Ginafore, Unpoc, Gummi Bako, Down The Tiny Steps, and Jon Bews.

Disconnected (Les Enfants Du Paradiddle, 2009, enf 65, CDr)
16-track Kitchen Cynics album. Beth is a featured violinist, appearing alongside Duncan Hart (cello) and Philip Johnston (slide guitar). Tracks include “The Wind”, “Hoodie Craw”, “Oystercatchers”, “Black Is The Colour”, “Me & Arthur Farquhar”, “Now’s The Time / Clear Rinnin’ Burnie”, “John Is Gone”, “Burning Toast”. Genres: Folk, World, & Country.

Flick The Vs (Domino, 2009)
King Creosote’s LP for Domino — his most commercially mainstream release at that point. Beth credited for Illustrations (alongside album Design by Midget Squid). Produced by Paul Savage and Jon Hopkins; also produced/engineered by Steve Mason (2) and Christian Madden. The illustration credit extends Beth’s documented relationship with King Creosote into visual-art territory.
