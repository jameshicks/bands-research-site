Aug 11, 202610 min read

Lynne Tillman

Novelist, short story writer, and cultural critic. Born 1947, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 336488.

One of the downtown Manhattan literary scene’s most embedded figures from the late 1970s onward, with documented music-world connections spanning No Wave, avant-garde composition, and Broadway. Her Discogs credit history runs from 1980 to 2004 and covers four distinct releases across multiple genres — unusual range for a literary writer.

Overview

Tillman’s fiction and criticism were shaped by, and in turn helped articulate, the overlapping art, music, and literary worlds of lower Manhattan. She is associated with the same cultural constellation as Y Pants, Glenn Branca, 99 Records, and the Mudd Club — her connections to those scenes are documented in Discogs credits and not merely biographical lore.

Her novel Haunted Houses (1987) and collections such as The Madame Realism Complex (1992) are core downtown-literary artifacts of the period. She has been a contributing editor to Bomb magazine and has written extensively on visual art, making her a rare figure who operated simultaneously in literary, music, and art-critical registers.

The Discogs record confirms four credited appearances across 25 years: artwork on a British avant-pop LP (1980), words on a No Wave LP (1982), sleeve notes on a John Zorn album (1993), liner notes on a Y Pants compilation (1998), and liner notes on a Broadway cast recording (2004).

Tillman received an Artwork credit — alongside a co-credited “Dizi (4)” — on the UK pressing of Acnalbasac Noom, Slapp Happy’s back-catalogue LP on Richard Cutler’s Recommended Records. Acnalbasac Noom was originally recorded in 1972 for Polydor but rejected and shelved, then licensed to Recommended for reissue. The band (Anthony Moore, Peter Blegvad, Dagmar Krause, Jean-Hervé Peron, Werner Diermaier, Gunther Wüsthoff) was a British avant-pop group with European art-rock connections. Produced by Uwe Nettelbeck.

This is the earliest credit in the Discogs record and the most unexpected — it places Tillman in the British art-rock reissue world a full two years before her Y Pants connection. The nature of the artwork contribution (sleeve design, photography, illustration) is unspecified in the DB. Recommended Records was a central UK distribution/label node for experimental and art-rock material in this period, with ties to figures like Fred Frith and Chris Cutler.

2. Beat It Down — Y Pants (1982)

Tillman wrote the words for “Obvious,” the opening track of Y Pants’ only LP. Music for the track is credited to Y Pants collectively. The credit is track-specific — she contributed to one song only, not the album as a whole. Beat It Down was produced by Wharton Tiers, engineered by Naux (Juan Maciel), and mix-engineered by Tom Gartland (credited as T.P. Gartland).

“Obvious” runs 2:53. It opens the album’s A-side, a position that conventionally signals editorial intent about the track’s representativeness. The rest of the LP’s lyrics were written by the band members themselves (Ess, Piersol, Vachon), with two exceptions: Bertolt Brecht’s words on “Barbara’s Song” and Emily Dickinson’s adapted poem on “The Fly.”

Placing Tillman in the same frame as Brecht and Dickinson — however implicitly — reflects Y Pants’ literary-art world orientation. The collaboration is best understood as a product of shared downtown Manhattan social networks: Tillman, Ess, Piersol, and Vachon all operated in the same overlapping art/music/literary circles in Lower Manhattan in the late 1970s and early 1980s.

Tillman contributed sleeve notes to Zorn’s Kristallnacht, his 1992 composition commemorating the 50th anniversary of Kristallnacht (the Nazi pogrom of November 1938). The work is one of Zorn’s most confrontational pieces, combining chamber music, noise, and klezmer elements. The release appeared on 99 Records (the later incarnation, distinct from 99 Records the NYC indie).

Sleeve notes were also contributed by poet/philosopher Edmond Jabès and theater director Richard Foreman — high-profile literary and theater-world figures. Tillman’s inclusion in this company confirms her standing in the New York intellectual and artistic community well into the 1990s. Performers on the recording include Marc Ribot (guitar), Anthony Coleman (keyboards), Mark Dresser (bass), Mark Feldman (violin), and William Winant (percussion). Produced by Zorn; mastered by Bob Ludwig.

Tillman wrote liner notes for the 1998 CD compilation collecting Y Pants’ complete recordings. The compilation also carries liner notes from Barbara Barg, Dan Graham, Gail Vachon, and Kiki Smith — art-world figures who collectively testify to Y Pants’ reception in the visual art community. Production credits: Glenn Branca (producer, tracks 1–4, the EP-era material) and Wharton Tiers (producer, tracks 5–16, the LP-era material).

Note: the Discogs credit on this compilation is Liner Notes, not “Words By.” The “Words By” credit applies only to the Beat It Down LP (1982). Earlier documentation of this relationship sometimes conflated the two credits.

Scene Connections

Y Pants and the No Wave / Art World Nexus

Tillman’s most substantive music credit is her lyric contribution to Beat It Down. Y Pants occupied a specific node in downtown Manhattan: they performed at CBGB and gallery spaces simultaneously, their membership drawn from photography (Barbara Ess), film (Gail Vachon), and visual art (Verge Piersol). Glenn Branca produced their debut EP on 99 Records in 1980; Wharton Tiers produced the LP on Neutral Records in 1982. The invitation to Tillman to contribute lyrics connects the trio’s literary-art orientation to the broader downtown fiction/criticism scene she inhabited.

99 Records and Neutral Records

Y Pants bridged both labels — the EP on 99 Records, the LP on Neutral Records. Tillman’s Zorn credit also appeared on a release associated with 99 Records (the later German entity). These connections place her at the center of the post-punk indie geography she was embedded in socially.

Mudd Club

The Mudd Club (77 White Street, Tribeca, 1978–1983) was the primary social venue for the scene Tillman inhabited. While she has no documented Discogs credit tied directly to the Mudd Club, the overlap between its regular figures (Lydia Lunch, James Chance, Klaus Nomi, Jean-Michel Basquiat, Haring) and her literary/critical world is consistent across scene accounts. No Discogs evidence of a performance or spoken-word credit at the Mudd Club has been found.

Recommended Records (UK)

The 1980 Slapp Happy artwork credit is an outlier — it links Tillman to the British experimental/art-rock distribution network around Chris Cutler and Recommended Records, which had no obvious connection to the downtown NYC scene. One possibility: Tillman, based in New York, was known to European avant-garde circles through her fiction and criticism, and may have contributed sleeve artwork or design through a personal connection to Slapp Happy members or their producer Uwe Nettelbeck.

John Zorn

The Kristallnacht credit (1993) connects Tillman to Zorn’s downtown jazz and avant-garde network, a separate but geographically and socially overlapping scene. Zorn’s circle — Ribot, Coleman, Feldman — was rooted in the same lower Manhattan geography as Y Pants and No Wave, but operating in a jazz/improv tradition rather than the post-punk track. Tillman’s invitation to write sleeve notes alongside Edmond Jabès and Richard Foreman confirms her status as a literary figure with credibility across multiple overlapping avant-garde worlds.

What Discogs Confirms vs. What Requires Web Research

Confirmed in Discogs:

  • All five credited releases and their exact roles
  • Track-specific credit (“Words By”) on Beat It Down A1 “Obvious”
  • The distinction between her “Words By” (1982 LP) and “Liner Notes” (1998 compilation) credits
  • The unexpected Slapp Happy artwork credit (1980, Recommended Records UK)
  • The Zorn Kristallnacht sleeve notes credit and the company she kept (Jabès, Foreman)
  • The 2004 Broadway cast recording liner notes

Requires web research to establish:

  • The nature of the Slapp Happy artwork credit — what specifically she contributed (design, photography, text, illustration); her connection to the Slapp Happy members or Recommended Records
  • Her broader biography: date of birth (1947 is commonly cited but not in Discogs), publication history, teaching positions, magazine affiliations
  • Whether she has any spoken-word recordings, interviews on vinyl/cassette, or performance credits outside Discogs (e.g., Tellus Audio Cassette Magazine appearances, Artists Space events)
  • Her documented relationship with the Mudd Club specifically — any contemporaneous accounts of her attendance or involvement
  • The connection between her literary work and the Y Pants collaboration — was “Obvious” written for the LP or adapted from an existing piece?
  • Any relationship with Ed Bahlman, Gina Franklyn, or 99 Records beyond the shared social network
  • Her connection to Dan Graham and Kiki Smith (both co-credited on the 1998 compilation) — whether these are documentary friendships or professional relationships
  • Why she was chosen specifically to write Caroline, Or Change liner notes — Kushner connection? Theater-world relationship?
