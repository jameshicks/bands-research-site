Overview

Ricky Helton Wilson (born 19 March 1953, Athens, Georgia; died 12 October 1985, New York City) was a founding member and guitarist of The B-52’s, and the older brother of bandmate Cindy Wilson. His guitar work — along with bass, keyboards, and organ — anchored the band’s New Wave and Post-Punk sound from their 1978 debut onward, earning him nearly 290 Discogs credits almost entirely tied to the group. The catalogue was distributed primarily through Warner Bros. Records and Island Records. He died in October 1985 of AIDS-related illness, before The B-52’s reached their commercial peak with Cosmic Thing (1989).

Notes (web)

AIDS diagnosis — public disclosure timing

Question researched: Was the AIDS cause of death publicly disclosed at the time of Wilson’s October 1985 death, or only afterward?

Summary finding: AIDS was not publicly acknowledged at the time of death. The cause was initially given as lymphatic cancer. Public acknowledgment appears to have come relatively quickly afterward — by 1988 at the latest, Wilson’s AIDS death was being cited as established public knowledge in music press — but no contemporaneous 1985 press coverage confirming the AIDS cause was located.

Specific findings:

  • At the time of Wilson’s death on 12 October 1985, the official cause of death was reported as lymphatic cancer, not AIDS. This appears to have been a deliberate family decision: search result summaries referencing a biographical source (primary source not directly verified) state that an uncle in the family proposed not mentioning AIDS in order to protect Wilson’s father. The exact source for this family-uncle account could not be confirmed via a directly fetchable page during this research pass — it surfaced repeatedly in search-AI summaries aggregated across multiple music history sources, but the underlying article(s) could not be loaded.

  • Keith Strickland was the only band member Wilson had told directly about his HIV/AIDS diagnosis. Kate Pierson has stated that during the recording of Bouncing Off the Satellites she and others “suspected, but didn’t know” — and learned only when Strickland called to say “Ricky’s dying of AIDS.” (Source: search-result snippet from Billboard interview, https://www.billboard.com/culture/pride/b52s-lgbtq-pioneers-interview-billboard-pride-8460698/ — page behind paywall, not directly fetched.)

  • Pierson has also stated regarding the band’s post-death public discussion: “We just didn’t really talk about it publicly very much. That might have made people think we had some kind of shame about it or something, which was not the case. We just wanted to be respectful of Ricky’s family — it was 1985. It was so different then. Literally no one ever asked us about it at the time.” (Source: search-result snippet, same Billboard article; page not directly fetchable due to paywall redirect.)

  • By 1988, journalist Jim Fouratt wrote in Spin magazine: “Even though Klaus Nomi, The B-52s’ Ricky Wilson and Hibiscus are well known to have died of AIDS,” the rock industry was still doing nothing about the epidemic. This indicates the AIDS cause of death had become accepted public knowledge no later than 1988 — probably earlier, given the phrasing “well known.” (Source: search-result snippet referencing the 1988 Spin article; original article not directly fetched — Spin is on the blocked-domains list for this research.)

  • The website differentlistening.com (https://differentlistening.com/2026/05/02/give-me-back-my-man-remembering-ricky-wilson/) noted in a 2026 retrospective that the author “missed that he died of AIDS — it was not really reported anywhere,” suggesting the AIDS cause received very limited contemporaneous press coverage in October 1985, consistent with the official “lymphatic cancer” framing at the time.

  • Wilson is noted as “one of the first high-profile names in rock to succumb to AIDS.” Actor Rock Hudson died of AIDS just 10 days prior (2 October 1985), which received extensive coverage; Wilson’s death was far less publicized. (Vic Vela on X / Twitter)

What could not be confirmed: No directly fetchable contemporaneous 1985 newspaper obituary or press release was located to confirm exactly what language was used publicly at the time of death. The specific primary source for the family’s uncle-and-father account was not independently verified — it appeared only in search AI summaries. Spin magazine (blocked domain) would be the most direct contemporaneous press source to check for the 1988 Fouratt article.
