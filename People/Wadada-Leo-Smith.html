Wadada Leo Smith

Discogs ID: 347445 Real name: Ishmael Wadada Leo Smith Born: December 18, 1941, Leland, Mississippi Instrument: Trumpet, flugelhorn, multi-instrumentalist, composer

American free-jazz and experimental trumpeter, composer, and improviser. One of the most prolific composer-bandleaders in creative music from the late 1960s onward. AACM member and co-founder of the Creative Construction Company. In the mid-1980s he became Rastafarian and adopted the name “Wadada”; earlier recordings and performances appear under “Leo Smith.”

Name variations: Ishmael Wadada Leo Smith, Leo Smith, Leo Wadada Smith, Wadada Selassie (L. Smith), W.L. Smith, Léo Smith, WLS, among others.

Biography

Early Life and AACM Years (1941–1971)

Smith was born in Leland, Mississippi. His early musical life included drums, mellophone, and French horn in high school concert and marching bands, before settling on trumpet. At thirteen he became immersed in Delta Blues and improvisation traditions. Formal education included the U.S. Military band program (1963), Sherwood School of Music (1967–69), and Wesleyan University (1975–76) where he studied ethnomusicology.

He became an active member of the AACM in Chicago. His first recorded appearance is on Anthony Braxton’s 3 Compositions Of New Jazz (1968, Delmark) and on the BYG session Anthony Braxton (1969). He also appears on Fontella Bass and Marion Brown’s Comme À La Radio (1969, Saravah). With Braxton and Leroy Jenkins he co-founded the Creative Construction Company, documented on two Muse Records albums (1975, 1976).

Solo Emergence and New Dalta Ahkri (1972–1981)

Smith’s debut as sole leader, Creative Music - 1 (1972, Kabell Records), was six solo trumpet improvisations. He founded the New Dalta Ahkri, whose members included Anthony Davis (2), Oliver Lake, and Henry Threadgill. Key releases from this period:

  • Silence (1974, Freedom/Trio Records) — early leader date
  • Creative Improvisation Ensemble (1975, Freedom)
  • Duets (1975, Arista/Freedom) — with Marion Brown
  • Reflectativity (1975, Kabell Records)
  • Clarity (1977, Bija Records)
  • Divine Love (1979, ECM Records) — ECM debut produced by Manfred Eicher; personnel: Bobby Naughton (vibraphone/marimba), Dwight Andrews (reeds), with guest tracks featuring Kenny Wheeler, Lester Bowie, and Charlie Haden
  • Spirit Catcher (1979, Nessa Records) — personnel: Bobby Naughton (vibes), Pheeroan akLaff (drums), Dwight Andrews (reeds/winds), Wes Brown (bass); engineered by Rudy Van Gelder
  • Touch The Earth (1980, FMP) — trio with Peter Kowald (bass) and Günter Sommer (drums); produced by Jost Gebers

He also participated in Derek Bailey’s Company (1978) and appeared on Anthony Braxton’s large-ensemble records.

New Dalta Ahkri and Black Saint (1982–1993)

  • Go In Numbers (1982, Black Saint) — credited “Leo Smith & New Dalta Ahkri”; personnel: Bobby Naughton (vibes), Dwight Andrews (reeds), Wes Brown (bass)
  • Rastafari (1983, Sackville Recordings)
  • Human Rights (1986, Kabell Records)
  • Procession Of The Great Ancestry (1989, Chief Records)
  • Kulture Jazz (1993, ECM Records) — solo recording; Smith performs trumpet, flugelhorn, koto, mbira, harmonica, bamboo flute, percussion, and vocals; produced by Steve Lake

Teaching posts during this period: University of New Haven (1975–76), Creative Music Studio in Woodstock, NY (1975–78), Bard College (1987–93). Currently professor of music at California Institute of the Arts, directing the MFA program in African American Improvisation.

Golden Quartet Era and Tzadik (2000–2011)

Smith formed Wadada Leo Smith’s Golden Quartet (Discogs ID: 1034747). The original quartet featured Jack DeJohnette (drums), Anthony Davis (2) (piano), and Malachi Favors (bass). A later configuration featured Vijay Iyer (piano), John Lindberg (bass), and Ronald Shannon Jackson (drums).

  • Golden Quartet (2000, Tzadik) — debut recording of the ensemble
  • Reflectativity (2000, Tzadik) — reissue/new recording
  • 50⁸ (2004, Tzadik)
  • Sky Garden (2004, Cuneiform Records) — Yo Miles! project with Henry Kaiser and Larry Ochs among others
  • Upriver (2005, Cuneiform Records) — continuation of Yo Miles! project
  • America (2009, Tzadik)
  • Spiritual Dimensions (2009, Cuneiform Records) — Wadada Leo Smith’s Organic ensemble

Smith also appeared on Matthew Shipp’s New Orbit (2001, Thirsty Ear) and with Roscoe Mitchell’s Note Factory.

Major Works: Ten Freedom Summers and Beyond (2012–present)

  • Ten Freedom Summers (2012, Cuneiform Records) — 4-CD box set; 19-movement suite for civil rights history; personnel: Anthony Davis (2) (piano), John Lindberg (bass), Pheeroan akLaff and Susie Ibarra (drums), with Southwest Chamber Music ensemble. Named to many year-end and decade-end best lists; received the 2013 Pulitzer Prize nomination.
  • Heart’s Reflections (2011, Cuneiform Records) — Wadada Leo Smith’s Organic ensemble
  • Red Hill (2014, RareNoise Records) — quartet with Balázs Pándi among others
  • A Cosmic Rhythm / With Each Stroke (2016, ECM Records) — duo with Vijay Iyer; piano, keyboards, electronics / trumpet; produced by Manfred Eicher, recorded by James Farber
  • America’s National Parks (2016, Cuneiform Records) — double CD; personnel: Anthony Davis (2) (piano), John Lindberg (bass), Pheeroan akLaff (drums), Ashley Walters (cello)
  • Lebroba (2018, ECM Records) — trio with Bill Frisell and Andrew Cyrille
  • Burning Meditation (2018, NoBusiness Records)
  • Two Centuries (2022, Red Hook Records)
  • Defiant Life (2025, ECM Records)
