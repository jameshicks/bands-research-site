Lester Bangs

Rock critic and musician. Born Leslie Conway Bangs, 14 December 1948, Escondido, California. Died 30 April 1982, New York City (accidental overdose). Age 33. Discogs artist ID: 437490.

Overview

The most celebrated and most eccentric American rock critic of his generation. Bangs wrote in a first-person gonzo style shaped by the Beat poets and Hunter S. Thompson — extended arguments, hostile love letters to musicians, and lengthy digressions conducted at maximum emotional volume. His critical voice was simultaneously contemptuous and besotted: he hated what he loved and loved what he claimed to hate. He was also, intermittently, a practicing musician whose recordings place him inside the downtown NYC punk and No Wave scenes rather than merely adjacent to them.

He got his start by responding to a reader ad in Rolling Stone soliciting reviews; his first published piece was a negative notice of the MC5’s Kick Out the Jams. He was promptly hired and then fired. He moved to Detroit to write for Creem, which became his primary home through the 1970s. Later he wrote for Village Voice, Penthouse, Playboy, New Musical Express, and many others. He moved to New York City in the late 1970s.

He died on 30 April 1982 of an accidental overdose of Darvon and Valium while treating a cold, listening to the Human League’s Dare. His collected criticism was published posthumously as Psychotic Reactions and Carburetor Dung (ed. Greil Marcus, 1987, Knopf).

Death and Posthumous Legacy

Died 30 April 1982. The circumstances: he had a cold and was self-medicating with Darvon (a now-withdrawn opioid painkiller) and Valium. His body was found by Kristian Hoffman, who was visiting his apartment. He was 33.

Psychotic Reactions and Carburetor Dung (Knopf, 1987), edited by Greil Marcus, is the canonical collection of his work. It drew on material from Rolling Stone, Creem, Village Voice, and other outlets.

He has been portrayed fictionally — most notably by Philip Seymour Hoffman in Cameron Crowe’s Almost Famous (2000).
