Sandy Linzer

American songwriter, lyricist, and record producer. Born: December 8, 1941, Livingston, New Jersey. Discogs ID: 153544. Real name: Sanford Linzer. Active approximately 1964–2025.

Biography

1960s: Brill Building-Era Songwriting and the DynoVoice Years

Sandy Linzer emerged in the mid-1960s as part of the New York pop songwriting ecosystem orbiting the Four Seasons and their associated labels. His primary writing partner was Denny Randell, and together they produced a string of hits that positioned them as a reliable pop/soul team.

Their breakthrough was “A Lover’s Concerto” (1965), a song adapted from Bach’s Minuet in G Major (from the Anna Magdalena Notebook), recorded by The Toys on DynoVoice Records. The record reached #2 on the Billboard Hot 100 and #5 on the R&B chart, giving Linzer and Randell their first major hit. The follow-up for The Toys, “Attack” (1965), was also a Linzer/Randell production and composition (DynoVoice). Both sides of The Toys’ debut are Linzer/Randell productions — he co-produced both with Randell and received liner notes credit on the album The Toys Sing “A Lover’s Concerto” And “Attack” (Dynovoice Records, 1966).

Also in 1965, Linzer and Randell co-wrote “Let’s Hang On!” and “Working My Way Back to You” for The Four Seasons — two of the group’s signature Philips-era hits. These were not Linzer productions (Bob Crewe produced for Four Seasons), but the songwriting credits were an important commercial platform.

Throughout 1965–1968, Linzer worked extensively through DynoVoice Records (and its sister imprint Dynovoice Records), a label run in the orbit of Bob Crewe. He produced and/or wrote for multiple DynoVoice acts:

  • Mitch Ryder / Detroit Wheels-adjacent artists
  • Wee Willie & The Winners (“Silver Spoon / Can’t Get Enough of You Baby,” 1966)
  • The Toys (“Skiing In the Snow,” 1966)
  • “Hallelujah / Written On The Wall” (1965)
  • “May My Heart Be Cast Into Stone / On Backstreet” (1966)

A major songwriting success of 1968 was “Breakin’ Down the Walls of Heartache”, co-written and co-produced with Randell for Johnny Johnson and the Bandwagon (released on CBS/Direction in the UK). Arranger on that session was Charlie Calello. The song became a UK hit and has been covered extensively.

Late 1960s–Early 1970s: Epic Records Productions

From roughly 1967–1972, Linzer produced heavily for Epic Records, a CBS imprint. The roster included:

  • The Bandwagon (Johnny Johnson and the Bandwagon) — UK soul act
  • Baby Washington — “Across the Universe” (Epic, 1970) and related singles
  • Various Epic pop/soul acts including multiple singles across 1967–1972

Linzer’s Epic period was prolific but yielded fewer chart landmarks; the work was primarily single-based productions for mid-tier pop and soul acts. Charlie Calello continued as his primary arranger through much of this run.

In 1972 Linzer produced “You Can Do Magic” for Limmie & Family Cookin’ (Avco), which became a UK hit (#3). Co-producer on that session was Steve Metz; arranger was Calello.

1973–1975: Transitional Work

A sparser period. Linzer produced sessions for Bell Records, Metromedia, Polydor, and Big Tree Records. Key credits:

  • Dreamboat (Avco, 1973) — production
  • Till the Water Stops Runnin’ (Bell Records, 1973) — production
  • “Maybe I Should Marry Jamie” (Arista, 1975) — produced and wrote
  • “Mornin’ Beautiful” (Elektra, 1975) — songwriting credit (recorded by Tony Orlando and Dawn — a Linzer/Randell song from Dawn’s New Ragtime Follies, Bell Records, 1974)

1976–1980: Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band and Odyssey

The peak of Linzer’s production career in terms of cultural impact.

Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band (1976)

Linzer produced the self-titled debut album by Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band (RCA Victor, 1976). The album blended 1930s/1940s big-band nostalgia with 1970s disco and funk, yielding the hit “Cherchez La Femme” (which also appeared as a single on RCA Victor). The album charted in the pop Top 40, was certified gold, and received a Grammy nomination.

Full credits on the session:

  • Producer: Sandy Linzer
  • Musical Direction / Guitar, Piano, Music: Stony Browder Jr.
  • Arranged By / Orchestrated By: Charlie Calello and Stony Browder Jr.
  • Lead Vocals: Cory Daye
  • Vocals / Bass / Lyrics: August Darnell
  • Vocals: Stony Browder Jr.
  • Vibraphone: Andy Hernandez (“Sugar Coated” Andy Hernandez / Coati Mundi)
  • Drums: Mickey Sevilla
  • Percussion: Don Armando Bonilla
  • Engineer [Sound]: Jeffrey Kawalek
  • Engineer [Associate]: Jim Bonnefond

Linzer also produced the associated singles, including “Whispering / Cherchez La Femme / Se Si Bon” (RCA Victor, multiple territories) and “I’ll Play the Fool” / “Cherchez La Femme / I’ll Play the Fool” (RCA Victor Italy).

He did not produce the second Dr. Buzzard album (Meets King Penett, 1978), which was self-produced by Stony Browder Jr.

Odyssey (1977–1981)

After the Dr. Buzzard success, Linzer became the primary producer for Odyssey (2), a New York soul/disco trio. He co-produced both of their key albums alongside Charlie Calello:

  • “Native New Yorker” single (RCA Victor, 1977) — Produced, Written-By (with Randell). The song became one of the signature NYC disco anthems, reaching #21 pop in the US and charting across Europe.
  • Odyssey (RCA Victor, 1977) — Producer, Directed By, Arranged By [Vocals], Percussion, Mixed By; Written-By (with Randell and Ralph Kotkov on selected tracks). Session musicians included Gordon Edwards, Will Lee, Cornell Dupree, Hugh McCracken, John Tropea, Richard Tee, Michael Brecker, Randy Brecker, and Lew Soloff — a who’s-who of New York session elite.
  • Hollywood Party Tonight (RCA Victor, 1978) — Music Director, Producer
  • Hang Together (RCA Victor, 1980) — Producer, Written-By (also received a “Percussion [Additional]” credit)
  • “Use It Up and Wear It Out” (RCA, 1980) — Produced by Linzer; UK #1 hit
  • Bon Bon Vie (Gimme the Good Life) (Mirage, 1980–1981) — Producer

Linzer also produced the Weekend Lover single (RCA, 1977), and several later Odyssey releases through Mirage Records in 1980–1982 including House of Music, Too Much Too Soon, More of the Good Life, and Dr. Ruth.

1977–1981: New York International Records

Linzer launched or was closely associated with New York International Records, on which he produced:

  • Sour and Sweet / Lemon in the Honey (RCA, 1976)
  • Cory and Me (New York International Records, 1979)
  • Green Light (New York International Records, 1979) — produced and wrote

1981–1990: Later Productions and Songwriting Revivals

Activity diversified. Notable credits:

  • Rockapella — Rockapella (Windham Hill, 1989), produced by Linzer
  • Saraya — self-titled album (Polydor, 1989), Linzer as Executive Producer — a New Jersey glam/hard rock act
  • The Rumour (Interfusion, 1988) — produced
  • Calling All Beatniks! (Passport Records, 1984) — produced
  • Connect the Dots (Columbia, 1989) — arranged and produced

Songs from Linzer and Randell’s 1960s catalog saw continual cover and interpolation life: “Working My Way Back To You” became a #2 UK hit for Frankie Valli / the Four Seasons (Polydor, 1975–1976 rerecording) and a #2 US hit in the Spinners’ medley version with “Forgive Me, Girl” (Atlantic, 1979). “Native New Yorker” was sampled and covered repeatedly through the 1990s–2000s.

By 1984, Linzer received a lyric collaboration credit on Kool & the Gang’s “Emergency” (De-Lite Records), and the following year “Fresh” (De-Lite Records, 1984–1985) — on which Linzer and Randell are credited for the underlying composition — became a hit for Kool & the Gang. The “Fresh” songwriting credit generated further licensed appearances through the 1990s–2000s including Smash Mouth’s Astro Lounge (Interscope, 1999).

In 1981, Linzer received a Songwriters Hall of Fame nomination (with Randell) — the nomination was made in 2012 per the Discogs profile note.

Post-1990s: Catalog and Licensing

Linzer’s catalog continued to generate income through compilations and licensing. Credits appear on:

  • Barry Manilow (If I Should Love Again, Arista, 1981 — “Music By, Lyrics By” for one track)
  • Four Seasons compilations (Rhino, Curb, various)
  • 3 Feet High and Rising (De La Soul, 2023 reissue — “Written-By” on a sampled track, AOI/Chrysalis)
  • Rocketman soundtrack (2019, Interscope — “Songwriter” credit)
  • Whitney Houston’s I Believe in You and Me (Arista, 1996) — Written-By
