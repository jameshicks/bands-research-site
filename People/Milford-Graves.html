Milford Graves

Born: August 20, 1941, Jamaica, Queens, New York Died: February 12, 2021 Role: Drummer, percussionist, composer, professor, martial artist, herbalist, visual artist Discogs artist ID: 341514

Milford Graves was a foundational free jazz drummer from Jamaica, Queens, who fundamentally reoriented the role of drums in improvised music. Coming up through Latin jazz before gravitating to the avant-garde, he became one of the central percussionists of the 1960s free jazz explosion — active on landmark ESP-Disk’ sessions with the New York Art Quartet, Paul Bley Quintet, Giuseppi Logan, Lowell Davidson, and Albert Ayler, as well as Sonny Sharrock’s debut Vortex Records (2) LP Black Woman (1969). John Zorn called him “basically a 20th-century shaman.” Graves taught at Bennington College for decades and pursued parallel careers as a sculptor, herbalist, and researcher into the relationship between heartbeat rhythm and improvised drumming. He remained active into his final years, with posthumous releases issued by Black Editions Archive through 2024.

Biography

Early Career and Latin Jazz Roots (1964–1965)

Graves’s first Discogs-documented recordings come from 1964–65 in a Latin jazz context: he played drums and timbales on Montego Joe’s ¡Arriba! Con Montego Joe (Prestige, 1964) and Wild And Warm (Prestige, 1965). These early credits reflect the Queens Latin music scene where he developed his foundational technique before pivoting sharply toward the free jazz avant-garde.

The ESP-Disk’ Moment (1965–1966)

In 1965 Graves appeared on a remarkable concentration of ESP-Disk’ recordings that placed him at the center of New York’s free jazz world:

  • Paul Bley Quintet – Barrage (ESP-Disk’, 1965): with Paul Bley, Marshall Allen, Eddie Gomez, and Dewey Johnson; Carla Bley compositions throughout.
  • Lowell Davidson Trio – Lowell Davidson Trio (ESP-Disk’, 1965): with pianist Lowell Davidson and Gary Peacock on bass — a rare, austere ESP session.
  • New York Art Quartet – New York Art Quartet (ESP-Disk’, 1965): the core NYAQ session with John Tchicai (sax), Roswell Rudd (trombone), and Lewis Worrell (bass). Graves was a founding member of this group.
  • Giuseppi Logan Quartet – The Giuseppi Logan Quartet (ESP-Disk’, 1965): with Don Pullen (piano), Eddie Gomez (bass), and Giuseppi Logan (reeds).
  • Milford Graves / Sunny Morgan – Percussion Ensemble (ESP-Disk’, 1965): a duo percussion record, co-led with Sunny Morgan — one of the earliest free percussion ensemble recordings on the label. Graves composed the material.

In 1966, Graves and Don Pullen recorded In Concert At Yale University (SRP Records (3), 1966), the first document of their long duo partnership. The Yale concert was later reissued in expanded form as The Complete Yale Concert, 1966 on Corbett vs. Dempsey (2020).

Albert Ayler and the Impulse! Years (1967–1968)

Graves played drums on Albert Ayler’s Love Cry (Impulse!, 1968), one of Ayler’s major Impulse! statements — with Alan Silva (bass), Call Cobbs (harpsichord), and Donald Ayler (trumpet), produced by Bob Thiele. He was a member of the Albert Ayler Quintet, which at various points also included Gary Peacock, Henry Grimes, and Ronald Shannon Jackson.

Black Woman and Vortex Records (1969)

Graves is credited with drums on Sonny Sharrock’s debut LP Black Woman (Vortex Records (2), 1969). The session also featured Norris Jones and Richard Pierce (bass), Dave Burrell (piano), Ted Daniel (trumpet on B-side), Linda Sharrock (voice), and was produced by Herbie Mann.

Self-Released Work and the SRP / IPS Years (1966–1977)

From the mid-1960s through the 1970s, Graves increasingly operated outside conventional label structures:

  • In Concert At Yale University (SRP Records (3), 1966) — duo with Don Pullen
  • Nommo (SRP Records (3), 1967) — duo with Don Pullen; cover/photography by Roy DeCarava
  • Dialogue Of The Drums (IPS (2), 1974) — Graves as producer
  • Bäbi (IPS (2), 1977) — percussion, writing, editing; released on his own IPS imprint
  • Meditation Among Us (Kitty Records, Japan, 1977) — drums, percussion, piano, voice; Japanese release

Pieces Of Time and the Drummer Summit (1984)

Pieces Of Time (Soul Note, 1984) is a landmark multi-drummer summit: Graves, Andrew Cyrille, Kenny Clarke, and Famoudou Don Moye — four of the most distinctive free and post-bop drummers of the era, recorded in Italy. Liner notes by Max Roach.

Tzadik Period (1998–2004)

Three solo albums on John Zorn’s Tzadik label document Graves’s mature solo voice:

  • Grand Unification (Tzadik, 1998) — produced by Graves, executive producer John Zorn; recorded by Jim Anderson
  • Stories (Tzadik, 2000) — produced by Graves, executive producer John Zorn
  • 50² (Tzadik, 2004) — music and percussion by Graves

Late Period and Posthumous Releases (2020–2024)

  • The Complete Yale Concert, 1966 (Corbett vs. Dempsey, 2020) — expanded reissue of the duo with Don Pullen
  • Historic Music Past Tense Future (Black Editions Archive, 2022) — trio with Peter Brötzmann (reeds) and William Parker (double bass, doussn’gouni); recorded live
  • Redemption (Not On Label / Bill Laswell self-released, 2022) — drums and voice with Bill Laswell
  • Children Of The Forest (Black Editions Archive, 2023) — with Arthur Doyle and Hugh Glover; Valerie Wilmer jacket photographs
  • Webo (Black Editions Archive, 2024) — drums, vocals
