Vlatko Špindler

Slovenian bassist. Discogs artist ID: 1088501. Also credited as Vlado Špindler and Špindler. Original bassist in Begnagrad, active with the band during its first period (1975–1978), and returned as a credited performer on both of the band’s later studio recordings.

Begnagrad

Špindler was a founding or early member of Begnagrad, the legendary Ljubljana avant-garde ensemble that fused Balkan folk, jazz, and progressive rock. He played bass during the group’s first active period (1975–1978), alongside Bratko Bibič (accordion, vocals), Bogo Pečnikar (clarinet), and Igor Muševič (drums). Begnagrad went on hiatus after 1978 and reconvened for a second period (1981–1983) with a partly different lineup — Nino De Gleria replaced Špindler on bass, and Aleš Rendla and Boris Romih joined.

Despite not being part of the 1981–1983 lineup, Špindler returned to record with Begnagrad on both subsequent releases, contributing bass parts and, notably, co-writing credits on Tastare.

Jodlovska Urška (1990, Nikad Robom 010) — a cassette-only release in Yugoslavia; the core quartet of Bibič, Špindler, Pečnikar, and Muševič reunited. Recorded by Boris Romih.

Tastare (Theoldwones) (1993, Bess – PRO MARKET BPM 0001) — the band’s first CD release, issued in independent Slovenia. A 14-track, 65-minute document. Špindler is credited as composer on eight of the fourteen tracks (tracks 2–5, 7–10), sharing writing credits with Bibič, Pečnikar, and Muševič. The album was produced partly by Anđelko Maletić (tracks 1–11) and by Begnagrad themselves. Engineering split between Katalin Farkaš (tracks 1–11) and Boris Romih and Tine Žitko (tracks 12–13).
