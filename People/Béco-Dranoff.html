Béco Dranoff

Discogs ID: 280624
Real name: Roberto Dranoff
Name variations: B. Dranoff, Beco Dranoff, Becó, Béco, Dranoff, Roberto (Beco) Dranoff
Active: 1993–2023
Website: becodranoff.com

Brazilian songwriter and producer. Best known as the A&R director and executive producer behind Bebel Gilberto’s international breakthrough, particularly Tanto Tempo (2000) on Ziriguiboom / Crammed Discs, one of the best-selling Brazilian crossover albums of its era. A central figure in bringing late-1990s and 2000s Brazilian music to European and North American audiences, Dranoff’s career straddled New York, São Paulo, and Brussels, operating as a connector between the Red Hot Organization’s HIV/AIDS charity compilations, the Ziriguiboom label, and the broader bossa nova revival circuit.

Overview

Dranoff’s earliest documented Discogs credit is a finger-snaps performance on Maracanã (1993, Chesky Records), a Brazilian-flavored session recorded in New York, suggesting early involvement in the city’s Brazilian music community before he moved into production and A&R roles.

His breakthrough into label and compilation work came via the Red Hot Organization, the New York-based nonprofit that funded AIDS charities through themed music compilations. Dranoff co-produced Red Hot + Rio (1996, Antilles/Verve) alongside John Carlin and Paul Heck — a landmark Brazilian-music compilation that paired Brazilian artists with international names and became one of the most successful Red Hot projects. The same year he compiled and produced Nova Bossa: Red Hot On Verve, an associated compilation of bossa nova interludes, collaborating with Vinicius Cantuária and Andres Levin among others. He would return to the Red Hot series twice more: Red Hot + Rhapsody (1998, Antilles), Onda Sonora: Red Hot + Lisbon (1998, Movieplay), and Red Hot + Rio 2 (2011, EQ Music).

From the late 1990s, Dranoff became the key A&R and production figure at Ziriguiboom, the Belgian label founded by Marc Hollander as a Brazilian/world music sublabel of Crammed Discs. His role there combined A&R curation with executive production, and he shaped the label’s catalog substantially from 1998 onward. Ziriguiboom releases he oversaw or contributed to include: Bebel Gilberto’s Tanto Tempo (2000), the first of her Ziriguiboom albums; her second, the self-titled Bebel Gilberto (2004); and the third, Momento (2007). He is co-credited as producer and A&R on all three, consistently alongside Hollander.

Tanto Tempo was the pivotal record of Dranoff’s career. Co-produced with Antoine Midani and Suba (Mitar Subotic), the album fused Bebel’s vocal style with electronic textures and became a worldwide hit. Suba, who died in a fire in 1999 shortly after completing his contributions, had been the primary sonic architect; Dranoff and Midani handled the broader production framework. The Tanto Tempo Remixes album (2001) continued in the same vein, with Amon Tobin among the remixers.

Beyond Bebel Gilberto, Dranoff compiled or A&R’d a steady stream of Ziriguiboom compilations throughout the early 2000s: Beleza Tropical 2 (1998, Luaka Bop), Everything Is Possible! – The Best Of Os Mutantes (1999, Luaka Bop) for which he is credited as compiler, The Now Sound Of Brazil (2002), Brasilidade (2001), Samba Rock (2001), Samba Soul 70! (2001), and The Other Side Of Outro Lado (2001). He also wrote liner notes and contributed songs (e.g. writer credit on São Paulo Confessions, 1999, Ziriguiboom).

He co-produced Natural (2003, Ziriguiboom/Crammed) — attributed on Discogs to Bebel Gilberto as primary — and executive-produced Momento (2007), Bebel’s third Ziriguiboom studio album.

Later work includes producing Habitat (2010, Brazil) and an advisory credit on David Bowie’s Brilliant Adventure [1992–2001] box set (2021, ISO/Parlophone) in a producer capacity, likely linked to Bowie’s connections to the Brazilian underground.
