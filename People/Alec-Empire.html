Alec Empire

Real name: Alexander Wilke-Steinhof Born: 2 May 1972, Charlottenburg, West Berlin, West Germany Discogs ID: 4982

German electronic musician, producer, DJ, and label founder. Co-founder of Atari Teenage Riot (ATR) and the principal architect of the digital hardcore genre. Founded Digital Hardcore Recordings (DHR) (1994) and Eat Your Heart Out (2007). Also recorded for Force Inc. Music Works and Mille Plateaux throughout the 1990s under several aliases. Curated and mixed Rauschgold: Alec Empire Plays Staubgold (staubgold 92, 2009) for Staubgold, a DJ compilation drawing entirely from the Staubgold catalog, mastered by Kassian Troyer.

Biography

Early career (1990–1993)

Wilke-Steinhof grew up in West Berlin near the Wall, immersed in a politically charged environment shaped by Cold War tensions. After early phases of hip-hop and punk (he formed his first band, Die Kinder, at age 12), he gravitated toward the electronic rave scene following German reunification, frequenting underground parties in East Berlin. Known early on as LX Empire, he released his first 12-inch records on Force Inc. Music Works in 1991, brought to the label’s attention after DJing on a beach in France with friend Hanin Elias, where he was noticed by Ian Pooley.

ATR signed a short-lived deal with Phonogram/Vertigo in 1993, releasing two singles (ATR, Kids Are United!) before parting ways with the major label. The cash advance from that deal seeded DHR in 1994. (Source: Wikipedia / Alec Empire)

Atari Teenage Riot and DHR (1992–2000)

With Hanin Elias and Carl Crack, Empire formed Atari Teenage Riot in 1992. The group pioneered “digital hardcore” — a collision of hardcore techno, breakbeat, hardcore punk noise, and radical anti-fascist/anti-capitalist politics. DHR simultaneously served as the band’s label and a home for the broader scene (EC8OR, Sonic Subjunkies, Christoph de Babalon). A deal with Grand Royal (Beastie Boys’ label, co-signed with Mike D) brought ATR releases to the US market in 1996–1997.

Nic Endo joined ATR as a fourth member during the late 1990s touring period. The band played with Nine Inch Nails at Brixton Academy (1999), John Peel-sponsored shows at the Queen Elizabeth Hall (1999), and the CBGB festival in NYC (1998). ATR went on hiatus in 1999; Carl Crack died in 2001. Hanin Elias departed to found Fatal Recordings.

Alongside ATR, Empire maintained a prolific solo output on Mille Plateaux (Generation Star Wars, Low On Ice, Hypermodern Jazz 2000.5, Les Étoiles des Filles Mortes) and as a remixer — credits include Björk (Bachelorette, Jóga), Einstürzende Neubauten, Mogwai, Thurston Moore, and Techno Animal.

Solo post-ATR (2001–2009)

Empire rebounded with Intelligence and Sacrifice (2001), a double-disc work produced with Nic Endo. Its live premiere at Fuji Rock (Japan) featured Charlie Clouser (ex-Nine Inch Nails), Masami Akita (Merzbow), and Gabe Serbian (The Locust) on drums. Further solo LPs The Ride (2002), Futurist (2005), and The Golden Foretaste Of Heaven (2007, first release on Eat Your Heart Out) followed. In 2009 he curated Rauschgold: Alec Empire Plays Staubgold (staubgold 92) for Staubgold, compiled and mixed entirely from the Staubgold back-catalog; Markus Detmer served as compilation producer; Kassian Troyer mastered.

Later work (2009–present)

Shivers (2009, Eat Your Heart Out) was his last studio solo LP before a long pause. In 2017 he composed an original soundtrack for German sci-fi thriller Volt (released on Dependent (2)). He contributed production and co-writing to ATR’s late-career releases, including Adrenaline (2020, self-released). In 2023 he compiled The Jaguar (93–96) archiving his Jaguar alias material from the Force Inc era.
