Hermeto Pascoal

Brazilian multi-instrumentalist, composer, and bandleader. Born June 22, 1936, Lagoa da Canoa, Alagoas, Brazil. Died September 14, 2025, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Discogs artist ID: 256166. Known professionally and on many releases as “O Bruxo” (The Sorcerer), Pascoal composed over 2,000 instrumental pieces across a seven-decade career and could play virtually any acoustic or electronic instrument. His music synthesized northeastern Brazilian folk idioms, post-bop jazz, free improvisation, and experimental sound, including the incorporation of everyday objects, animal noises, and audience participation. He collaborated with Miles Davis (appearing on Live-Evil, 1971), was a founding member of Quarteto Novo, and worked in close partnership with Airto Moreira and Flora Purim throughout the early part of his career. His long-running group Hermeto Pascoal E Grupo (Discogs artist ID: 2115419) remained active from the mid-1960s through his death.

Overview

Early Career and Quarteto Novo (1958–1969)

Pascoal grew up in Brazil’s impoverished northeast, the region whose folk music — baião, xote, forró — would permeate his work throughout his life. He worked as a studio accordion player in the late 1950s (first documented Discogs credit: Batucando No Morro, 1958, for Regional Do Pernambuco Do Pandeiro). He moved to Rio de Janeiro in the early 1960s and began working as a multi-instrumentalist — flute, piano, organ — for hire, appearing on sessions for Sambrasa Trio (1965), Walter Santos (1965), and others.

In 1966–1967 he co-founded Quarteto Novo with Airto Moreira, Heraldo Do Monte, and Theo De Barros. The group recorded a single landmark album, Quarteto Nôvo (1967, Odeon), widely regarded as an early fusion of northeastern folk and jazz. The group also recorded two singles on RCA Victor and Som Maior. Quarteto Novo was short-lived but highly influential in Brazilian music.

US Debut and Miles Davis (1970–1972)

Airto Moreira brought Pascoal to New York in 1969–1970, where he was signed to Cobblestone (a Buddah sublabel) for his US debut album Hermeto (1970, Cobblestone CST 9000), produced by Airto and Flora Purim, featuring Ron Carter on bass and a large New York jazz orchestra including Hubert Laws, Thad Jones, and Joe Farrell. Pascoal played keyboards, flute, and served as arranger and conductor.

Miles Davis engaged Pascoal for a studio session that produced two tracks (“Nem Um Talvez” / “Little Church”) that appeared on the double album Live-Evil (Columbia, 1971). Pascoal played electric piano and whistling; his contribution was brief but introduced him to an international audience. He also appeared on Donald Byrd’s Electric Byrd (Blue Note, 1970) and on Antonio Carlos Jobim’s Tide (CTI, 1970).

Also in 1970, he appeared on Airto Moreira’s Natural Feelings (Blue Note) playing piano, organ, harpsichord, and flute, and on Edu Lobo’s Sergio Mendes Presents Lobo (A&M) and Cantiga De Longe.

Brazil: RCA and Sinter Period (1973–1976)

Returning to Brazil, Pascoal recorded A Música Livre De Hermeto Paschoal (1973, Sinter 1.906), produced by Rubens Barsotti — a freer, more experimental work than his US debut. In 1975 he released Porco Na Festa on RCA Victor (101.0288), featuring percussionists Nenê and Zé Eduardo Nazario, alongside acoustic ensemble players from his circle.

During this period he continued extensive session work: arrangements and conducting for Eduardo Gudin (1973), flute and piano for Edu Lobo, flute on multiple Taiguara albums (1975–1976), organ and flute for Sérgio Mendes & Brasil ‘77 (Homecooking, 1976), and compositions contributed to Flora Purim’s Open Your Eyes You Can Fly (1976). He also appeared on the OPA album Goldenwings (1976).

The Vice-Versa period of 1976 — sessions with a group that included Toninho Horta (guitar), Lelo Nazario (electric piano), Zé Eduardo Nazario (drums), Nivaldo Ornelas, Mauro Senise, Raul Mascarenhas (saxophones/flute), and Zeca Assumpção (bass) — remained unissued until Far Out Recordings released them in 2017 as Viajando Com O Som (The Lost ‘76 Vice-Versa Studio Session).

Warner Bros. and Atlantic Period (1977–1982)

Slaves Mass (1977, Warner Bros. BS 2980), produced by Airto Moreira and Flora Purim, is considered one of Pascoal’s finest recordings. The personnel split across two tracks: “Slaves Mass” features Chester Thompson (drums), Alphonso Johnson (electric bass), and David Amaro (guitars); the remaining tracks feature Airto (drums), Ron Carter (acoustic bass), Raul De Souza (trombone), and David Amaro. Pascoal played flute, acoustic guitar, soprano saxophone, piano, Rhodes, recorder, and clavinet throughout.

Zabumbê-bum-á (1978, Warner Bros. Brazil 436.104) introduced a more stable band configuration: Itiberê Zwarg (bass), Vitor Farias (guitar), Jovino Santos Neto (keyboards), Nenê (drums/percussion), Cacau (Claudio De Queiroz, percussion), and Pernambuco (4) (percussion). This core group — with some variation — remained central to Pascoal’s work through the early 1980s.

Ao Vivo Montreux Jazz (1979, Atlantic Brazil Br 20.052/053) captured the group live at Montreux. Elis Regina appeared alongside Pascoal on the 13th Montreux Jazz Festival release (1982, Elektra BR 22.032).

Cérebro Magnético (1980, Atlantic BR30.127) continued with the same basic group. After Atlantic the international major-label phase ended; Pascoal subsequently released primarily through Brazilian independents and European reissue labels.

Brazilian Independents and Som da Gente (1983–2006)

Brasil Universo (1988, Rio Records RICD 9.00523 O, Germany) and Hermeto Solo - Por Diferentes Caminhos (1988, Som Da Gente SDG-039/88) represent the label changes of the late 1980s. Som Da Gente (label ID: 64396) was a key Brazilian independent that housed his catalog in this period. He contributed to projects for Heraldo Do Monte (Cordas Vivas, 1983 and 1988) and produced/arranged Aleuda’s Oferenda (1984) in a full bandleader-for-hire role.

Eu E Eles (1999, Rádio Mec JABOUR99RMEC) was a late career recording; The Legendary Improviser (2001, West Wind Latina WW2242) collected material for the European market. Chimarrão Com Rapadura (2006, Not On Label HPAM02) was a self-released set.

Far Out Recordings and Late Period (2017–2025)

Far Out Recordings (label ID: 806) issued the archival Viajando Com O Som (2017, FARO 200 LP) and the new studio recording Palmares Fantasy (2018, FARO 203CD), the latter featuring Sean Khan (saxophone) and Daniel Maunick. Erika Ribeiro (2021, Rocinante), a collaboration with vocalist Erika Ribeiro, was one of his final recordings. The Hermeto Pascoal & Big Band formation (Discogs ID: 6410079) and Hermeto Pascoal & Grupo Vice Versa (ID: 5992704) were active into the 2020s. Pascoal died in Rio de Janeiro in September 2025.
