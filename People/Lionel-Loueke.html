Lionel Loueke

Discogs ID: 745365 Real name: Gilles Lionel Loueke Born: Benin, West Africa Instruments: Guitar, vocals, percussion Name variations: L. Loueke · Lionel Louéké · Lionel Lueke · Loueke

Beninese jazz guitarist, vocalist, and composer. One of the most distinctive voices in contemporary jazz guitar, known for weaving West African vocal and percussion traditions into an improvised jazz framework. Long-time associate of Herbie Hancock; a first-call sideman for Blue Note-era jazz royalty and a prolific bandleader in his own right.

Biography

Early Career and Paris (1990s)

Loueke studied in Benin and later in Paris, where he recorded his debut, Clarte’ Obscure (1997, self-released / Not On Label), on which he handled guitar, bass, programming, and production himself. He also recorded In A Trance (2005, Space Time Records) during his European years, credited as a solo leader with baglama/saz in addition to guitar.

Berklee and Thelonious Monk Institute (2000s)

Loueke came to the United States to study at the Berklee College of Music, then won a scholarship to the Thelonious Monk Institute of Jazz in Los Angeles. There he came to the attention of Herbie Hancock, who became a mentor and champion. This connection opened doors across the Blue Note and Verve worlds: Loueke appeared on Terence Blanchard’s Flow (2005, Blue Note), Herbie Hancock’s Possibilities (2005, Hear Music), and as a soloist on Angelique Kidjo’s Djin Djin (2007).

Virgin Forest and the Gilfema Trio (2005–2008)

The core of Loueke’s working group crystallized in the mid-2000s as Gilfema, a piano-less trio with bassist Massimo Biolcati and drummer Ferenc Nemeth. The name is a portmanteau of their first names. They released a self-titled debut on ObliqSound (2005) and Gilfema + 2 (ObliqSound, 2008).

Virgin Forest (ObliqSound, 2006) — Loueke’s first full American solo statement — used the Gilfema trio as its rhythm section and added Herbie Hancock on piano, Gretchen Parlato on vocals, and Cyro Baptista on percussion. It established his signature sound: kora-inflected guitar lines, throat-singing, and African language vocals layered over jazz harmony.

River: The Joni Letters (2007)

Loueke played guitar on Herbie Hancock’s Grammy Album of the Year–winning River: The Joni Letters (Verve Records, 2007). The album was produced by Hancock and Larry Klein, engineered and mixed by Helik Hadar, and featured Wayne Shorter on saxophone, Dave Holland on bass, and Vinnie Colaiuta on drums.

Karibu — Blue Note Debut (2008)

Karibu (Blue Note, 2008) was Loueke’s Blue Note debut, produced by Eli Wolf and recorded/mixed by Joe Ferla. It marked his arrival at the label where he would record most of his 2010s catalog.

Mwaliko (2010)

Mwaliko (Blue Note, 2010) broadened the ensemble with an all-star cast of African diaspora musicians.

Time’s Tales and the Jeff Ballard Trio (2013)

Time’s Tales (Okeh, 2013) was recorded as a trio with Jeff Ballard (drums) and Miguel Zenon (saxophone), accompanied by Dseny and Nina Paradiso. Loueke is also a member of the Jeff Ballard Trio.

Gaïa (2015)

Gaïa (Blue Note, 2015) returned to the Gilfema trio format (Biolcati, Nemeth) and was produced by Don Was. Notably, the closing track featured a Bee Gees composition (written by Barry, Robin, and Maurice Gibb).

Aziza and the Dave Holland Quartet (2016)

Aziza (12) is a quartet led jointly by Dave Holland (bass), Chris Potter (2) (saxophone), Eric Harland (drums), and Loueke (guitar). Their self-titled debut appeared on Dare2 Records in 2016.

Hope with Kevin Hays (2017)

Hope (Newvelle Records, 2017) is a co-billed duo album with pianist Kevin Hays, produced by Elan Mehler and Jean-Christophe Morisseau, engineered and mixed by Marc Urselli. Issued as a vinyl-subscription release on Newvelle.

HH — Tribute to Herbie Hancock (2020)

HH (Edition Records, 2020) is an album-length solo/trio tribute to Herbie Hancock’s songbook. Loueke produced it himself (with Massimo Biolcati as co-producer), playing guitar and vocals over Hancock compositions. Executive produced by Dave Stapleton of Edition Records.

Lean In with Gretchen Parlato (2023)

Lean In (Edition Records, 2023) is a co-billed duo album with vocalist Gretchen Parlato, with Mark Guiliana on drums/percussion for most tracks. Guest vocal appearances by Lisa Loueke (Lionel’s daughter) and Marley Guiliana (Mark Guiliana’s daughter) on selected tracks.

HH Reimagined with Gilles Peterson (2023)

HH Reimagined (Edition Records, 2023) is a reimagining of Hancock repertoire co-billed with DJ/broadcaster Gilles Peterson.
