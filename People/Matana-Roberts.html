Matana Roberts

Discogs ID: 324229

Chicago-born alto saxophonist, clarinetist, composer, and mixed-media practitioner based in Brooklyn, NY. Works across jazz, experimental, avant-garde, visual art, dance, poetry, and theatre. Best known for the ongoing Coin Coin series (Constellation Records), a planned twelve-chapter project exploring African American history and memory.

Credited on TV On The Radio’s Dear Science (2008), playing alto saxophone and clarinet on “Lover’s Day.” Their broader work operates at the intersection of improvised music, Black American history, and sound as social practice.

Biography

Born 1975 in Chicago, Illinois; raised partly on the city’s South Side. Father was a political analyst and fan of free jazz (Sun Ra, Albert Ayler), who introduced Roberts to the music around age five. Studied classical clarinet in youth; received a saxophone from teacher Reginald Willis in high school.

Education: B.A., DePaul University (1997); M.A., New England Conservatory of Music (2001). Primary formation also came through free arts programs in the Chicago public school system — Roberts has described these as their core training.

AACM: Member of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians, where Roberts studied and later taught. Connections there included saxophonist David Boykins and flutist Nicole Mitchell. Also member of the Black Rock Coalition. [wikipedia.org/wiki/Matana_Roberts, as of 2026-05-17]

Chicago years: Formed the trio Sticks and Stones with bassist Josh Abrams and drummer Chad Taylor; performed regularly at the Velvet Lounge.

Move to New York (2002): Initially busked in subway stations and published a zine, Fat Ragged, documenting those experiences.

Key early release: The Chicago Project (2008), produced by Vijay Iyer. [foundationforcontemporaryarts.org/recipients/matana-roberts]

Other activities: Guest curator at The Stone music venue (2010); Whitney Museum residency (2015), creating sound works titled I Call America; solo exhibition I Call America II at Fridman Gallery (2016); Copeland Fellow at Amherst College (2013).

Also appeared as a guest on Godspeed You Black Emperor!‘s Yanqui U.X.O. (2002) — credited for clarinet on “Rockets Fall On Rocket Falls.”

Coin Coin Series (Constellation Records)

Roberts conceived Coin Coin as “a multichapter musical work-in-progress exploring themes of history, memory and ancestry” — describing the compositional method as “panoramic sound quilting.” In Roberts’ words: “The entire project is a precarious love-hate-love letter to my birth country: a celebration of the human experience, American style.” [downbeat.com/news/detail/matana-roberts-coin-coin-sonic-patchwork-american-history]

The name “Coin Coin” was also the nickname of Roberts’ grandfather, as well as the name of freed slave and entrepreneur Marie Thérèse Metoyer (b. 1742), whose story anchors Chapter One.

Critical Reception

Chapter One: Gens de couleur libres (2011)

Released May 10, 2011. Live in-studio recording with a 17-piece ensemble. Metacritic: 84/100 (“universal acclaim,” 6 reviews). [metacritic.com]

Key reviews:

  • The Wire: Called Roberts “a major talent.” [cstrecords.com/pages/artist/matana-roberts]
  • BBC Music: “This is an ambitious work, and all of its aims have surely been fulfilled.” [wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_Coin_Chapter_One:_Gens_de_Couleur_Libres, as of 2026-05-17]
  • Tiny Mix Tapes: “one of the most conceptually ambitious, sonically captivating, emotionally raw musical odysseys.” [cstrecords.com/pages/artist/matana-roberts]
  • SPIN: “Deeply spiritual, sadistically dissonant, evocative as any novel.” [cstrecords.com/pages/artist/matana-roberts]
  • No Ripcord (Stephen Wragg): 9/10 — “Gens de Couleur Libres will make you like jazz. She does things with jazz I didn’t think possible.” [no-ripcord.github.io/archive/reviews/music/matana-roberts/coin-coin-chapter-one-gens-de-couleur-libres]
  • The Free Jazz Collective (Guy Peters): ★★★★★ — Invokes Ayler, Shepp, and Sanders; praises the vocal performances as alternating between soothing and “incredibly harsh and haunting.” [freejazzblog.org/2011/12/matana-roberts-coin-coin-chapter-one.html]
  • Los Angeles Times (Chris Barton): Named among the top ten jazz releases of 2011. [wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_Coin_Chapter_One:_Gens_de_Couleur_Libres, as of 2026-05-17]

Year-end/lists: Multiple JazzTimes 2011 Critics’ Lists; LA Times top ten jazz of 2011. [albumoftheyear.org/album/3226]

What reviewers identified as significant: The blending of free jazz, spirituals, blues, folk, musique concrète, and spoken word into a unified narrative structure; Roberts’ vocal performance ranging from primal to anguished; the conceptual ambition of the twelve-chapter cycle; and the album’s engagement with African American history through the figure of Marie Thérèse Metoyer.

Chapter Two: Mississippi Moonchile (2013)

Pitchfork (Grayson Haver Currin): 8.5/10 — compared the narrative approach to “James Baldwin’s first novel, Go Tell It on the Mountain.” SPIN (Seth Colter Walls): 9/10 — “The way Roberts mixes experimentalism with song-form” achieves rare balance. AllMusic (Thom Jurek): 4/5 — “a wildly creative, contrasting, and wide-ranging musical theater performance.” Chicago Reader (Peter Margasak): “stunning” — “carving out her own aesthetic space.” The Free Jazz Collective (Martin Schray): ★★★★★ — “might be the most socially relevant music (not just jazz) being made at the moment.” [wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_Coin_Chapter_Two:_Mississippi_Moonchile, as of 2026-05-17]

Pitchfork described Roberts at this point as “one of the most exciting new spirits in contemporary music.” [cstrecords.com/pages/artist/matana-roberts]

Chapter Three: River Run Thee (2015)

Pitchfork: Roberts represents “one of the most provocative ongoing bodies of work by any American musician.” Chapter Three appeared on Pitchfork’s Best Experimental Albums of 2015 and Rolling Stone’s Best Avant Albums of 2015. [cstrecords.com/pages/artist/matana-roberts; wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_Coin_Chapter_Three:_River_Run_Thee, as of 2026-05-17]

Chapter Four: Memphis (2019)

Metacritic: 91/100 (“universal acclaim,” 4 reviews). Pitchfork (Mina Tavakoli): 8.3/10 — “Matana Roberts saw how the tempo of history moved irregularly through both her memory and the national one.” AllMusic (Thom Jurek): 4/5. Spectrum Culture (Evan Welsh): 4.5/5. JazzTrail: A+ — “thoroughly narrated, revolutionary and liberating.” NPR described Roberts as a “multidisciplinary visionary.” The Quietus predicted the completed cycle will be “a singular masterpiece of twenty-first century sonic and narrative art.” [wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_Coin_Chapter_Four:_Memphis, as of 2026-05-17; cstrecords.com/products/matana-roberts-coin-coin-chapter-four-memphis]

Chapter Four appeared on: Pitchfork’s Best Experimental Albums of 2019; Bandcamp’s Best Jazz Albums of 2019; top ten of the NPR Music Jazz Critics Poll 2019. Anthony Fantano called it “one of the decade’s most compelling jazz projects.” [wikipedia.org/wiki/Matana_Roberts, as of 2026-05-17]
