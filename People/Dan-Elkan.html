Dan Elkan

Discogs ID: 669392
Name variations: D. Elkan, Dan
Origin: Sacramento, California
Instagram: @danskateland

Guitarist, vocalist, and producer. Member of Pocket For Corduroy, Golden Shoulders, Holy Smokes, and Hella (as live/associate member). Also production work for Alela Diane.

Biography

Elkan is a Sacramento-area multi-instrumentalist who surfaced in the late 1990s as a member of Pocket For Corduroy, a local emo/indie rock act. That band self-released a cassette EP in 1998 and later an EP on the small Lab Results imprint in 2001 (As Soon As Impossible), with Elkan on vocals and guitar alongside Andrew Hodgson, Peter Newsom, and Thaddeus Stoenner. A collected-works compilation appeared in 2009.

The mid-2000s brought Elkan into the orbit of the Sacramento/Bay Area experimental scene centered on Hella drummer Zach Hill. Zach Hill assembled Holy Smokes in 2003 as a vehicle for the “soundtrack” to his book Destroying Yourself Is Too Accessible. The resulting record — Masculine Drugs (2004, Suicide Squeeze / ChapelleTNI) — listed Elkan as a core band member (vocals, guitars, piano, keyboard) alongside Rob Crow, Carson McWhirter, Jonathan Hischke, and others, with Hill producing. A second Holy Smokes album, Talk To Your Kids About Gangs, was announced for Skin Graft Records but does not appear in Discogs.

Elkan also contributed guest vocals to Hella’s Bitches Ain’t Shit But Good People EP (2003, Suicide Squeeze) — he sang on track 4, “Elkan Sings Republic Of R+R,” credited as “D. Elkan.”

His other major Sacramento affiliation was Golden Shoulders, the project of Adam Kline. Elkan is listed as a member on Discogs; his documented instrumental credit is vibraphone on the full-length Friendship Is Deep (2004, Doppler Records). The Golden Shoulders roster was unusually deep — Joanna Newsom, Josh Klinghoffer, Neal Morgan, and Todd Roper all appear across various releases.

By the late 2000s Elkan had moved into production. He co-produced Alela Diane’s second album To Be Still (2009, Names/UK) alongside Alela Diane herself and Tom Menig.

Them Hills is his solo/small-band recording project. Two limited vinyl LPs are documented: Process (2009, Not On Label) and Rad Disguise (2019, Dowd Records). Both are entirely self-produced with no additional personnel credited.
