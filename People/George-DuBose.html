Oct 08, 20266 min read

George DuBose

American art director, designer, and photographer. Discogs artist ID: 1839444. Website: george-dubose.com.

Active from 1979 through at least the mid-2000s, DuBose was one of the most prolific cover photographers working across the New York downtown scene and into hip-hop and rock. He shot the debut album cover for The B-52’s in 1979, worked extensively with ZE Records artists, and became the house visual presence for Cold Chillin’ during hip-hop’s golden era. Total Discogs credits: over 1,400 release appearances (across pressings), representing roughly 250+ distinct masters spanning 1979–2024.

Career Arc

1979 — Downtown NYC Entry

DuBose’s first major credit lands immediately at the top: the self-titled debut LP by The B-52’s (1979, Island Records). The cover image became iconic, cementing the band’s campy retro aesthetic. In the same year he shot for Squeeze’s 6 Squeeze Songs Crammed Into One Ten-Inch Record (A&M) and The Reds (A&M), establishing early footholds at both Island and A&M.

1980–1982 — ZE Records and the Downtown Circle

Through ZE Records DuBose connected with the downtown No Wave / mutant disco scene. Key credits:

  • Cristina — Off The Coast Of Me (1980, Island / ZE)
  • Lydia Lunch — Queen Of Siam (1980, ZE)
  • August Darnell / Kid Creole And The Coconuts — Tropical Gangsters (1982, ZE); Stool Pigeon single (1982, ZE)
  • Joe Jackson — Night And Day (1982, A&M) — one of Jackson’s defining records
  • Walter Steding — debut LP (1980, Red Star Records)

The ZE cluster ran through to the 2003 and 2005 Mutant Disco compilations, where DuBose received photo credits for August Darnell portraits.

1981–1986 — Go-Go’s, Ramones, and Rock Clients

DuBose became a regular photographer for Sire and affiliated rock acts:

  • Go-Go’s — Beauty And The Beat (1981, I.R.S.) and Our Lips Are Sealed single (1981); Hexbreaker! (1983, I.R.S.)
  • Ramones — Subterranean Jungle (1983, Sire); Too Tough To Die (1984, Sire); Animal Boy (1986, Beggars Banquet, cover design + photography); Halfway To Sanity (1987, Sire); Brain Drain (1989, Sire); Mondo Bizarro (1992, Chrysalis); and the Ramones Mania compilation (1988, Sire)
  • Fred Schneider (B-52s) solo — Fred Schneider & The Shake Society (1984, Warner Bros., design + photography)
  • John Cale — John Cale Comes Alive (1984, Island / ZE)

1982–1990 — Island Records and 4th & Broadway

A major sustained relationship with Island Records and its subsidiary 4th & Broadway covered reggae, pop, and R&B:

  • Sly & Robbie — Sly & Robbie Meet The Paragons (1981, Island)
  • Melissa Etheridge — debut LP (1988, Island)
  • Toots Hibbert — Toots In Memphis (1988, Mango)
  • Various Mango / Island reggae and soca releases 1988–1991

1986–1994 — Cold Chillin’ and Hip-Hop’s Golden Era

DuBose became the primary visual identity for Cold Chillin’, the Queens-based hip-hop label that launched Biz Markie, Big Daddy Kane, Roxanne Shanté, Kool G Rap, and Marley Marl. He shot and/or designed the majority of the label’s catalog through its peak years:

  • Big Daddy Kane — Long Live The Kane (1988); It’s A Big Daddy Thing (1989); Taste Of Chocolate (1990); Daddy’s Home (1994, MCA); plus numerous singles
  • Biz Markie — Goin’ Off (1988); The Biz Never Sleeps (1989); I Need A Haircut (1991); All Samples Cleared! (1993)
  • Roxanne Shanté — Bad Sister (1989); Greatest Hits (1995)
  • Kool G Rap & DJ Polo — Road To The Riches (1989); Wanted: Dead Or Alive series
  • MC Shan — Down By Law (1987, Cold Chillin’)
  • Juice Crew compilations and retrospectives

DuBose’s design aesthetic — vivid color, strong portraiture, bold typography — defined the visual language of Cold Chillin’ for its entire run.

1987–1993 — Atlantic Starr, Polydor, and Broader R&B/Rock

Running parallel to hip-hop work:

  • Atlantic Starr — All In The Name Of Love (1987, Warner Bros.); Always (1987, WB); best-of compilations
  • Masters Of Reality — debut LP (1988, Def American)
  • Melissa Etheridge — debut (1988, Island, 84 pressings tracked in DB)
  • Was (Not Was) — Wasn’t Tomorrow Wonderful? (1982, Polydor)

1990s–2000s — Compilations and Legacy Credits

DuBose’s archival work continued into reissues:

  • Ramones — Ramones Mania (1988), Anthology: Hey Ho Let’s Go! (1999), Loud, Fast Ramones (2002), Greatest Hits (2024 — insert photo credit)
  • Big Daddy Kane, Biz Markie, and Cold Chillin’ anthology compilations throughout the 1990s–2000s
  • Pulsallama compilation (2020, Modern Harmonic) — cover photo credit
