Wilf Smarties

Scottish record producer, engineer, and musician. Discogs artist ID: 149792. Active from at least 1980 through the 2020s, based primarily in Scotland. Also credited under the name variations: Diabolical Dr. Smarties, Mr. Smarties, Wili Smarties, W. Smarties, Wolf Smarties, Wilf Smarty, Smarties. Member of early-1990s British house duo Within A Dream with vocalist Jayn McNamara (aka Jayn Hanna). Realname not documented on Discogs.

Overview

Wilf Smarties emerged in 1980 as part of the Scottish post-punk scene, engineering the debut single by Fire Engines on Codex Communications and producing tracks on the Edinburgh compilation Mint Sauce For The Masses (Playlist Records). Through the early 1980s he established himself as the go-to producer for the Aberdeen post-punk/funk band APB (2), working with them from their first Oily Records single Palace Filled With Love (1982) through the album Cure For The Blues (1986) and into the final Red River and Link Records releases of 1987–89. The APB relationship is the longest and most central of his career.

In parallel he produced early singles for Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie (whose lineup then included a young Shirley Manson) and The Shamen (their debut album Drop on Moksha Recordings, 1987). He assisted on Wet Wet Wet’s debut album Popped In Souled Out (Mercury/The Precious Organisation, 1987) — one of the year’s biggest-selling UK records — crediting specific tracks. He also produced The Jasmine Minks for Creation Records in 1988 and worked with The Wake as late as 2013.

The early 1990s saw a pivot into house music. As one half of Within A Dream with Jayn Hanna, he co-wrote “Where Is The Feeling” (Event (6)/Island, 1992), a track that was later covered by Kylie Minogue for her 1995 Deconstruction-era single — giving Smarties a writing credit on a top-15 UK hit. He also co-wrote “Lovelight (Ride On A Love Train)” (VC Recordings, 1996), which charted in the UK after Blue Amazon remixes. He worked on club-oriented productions through Stress Records into 1993.

Later credits include engineering for various reissue compilations through Cherry Red and Domino (2005–2024), mixed-by credits on Goodbye Mr. Mackenzie’s US compilation (Radioactive, 1991), and producing two tracks on The Wake’s Harmony LP (Factory Benelux, 2013).
