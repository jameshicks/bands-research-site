Stevie Jackson

Real name: Stephen Thomas Jackson. Born 16 January 1969, Glasgow, Scotland. Discogs artist ID: 264198. Guitarist, vocalist, songwriter, producer. Years active: 1994–2022 (Discogs-documented).

Name variations on record: S. Jackson, Stephen Jackson, Steve Jackson, Stevie, Stevie Reverb, Stevie Thomas Jackson.

Biography

Early Glasgow work (1994–1995)

Jackson’s earliest documented Discogs credit is a songwriting co-write on the Telstar Ponies EP Maps And Starcharts (Fire Records, 1994) — credited “S.Jackson” on track 3, alongside David Keenan. In 1995 he fronted or was central to Moondials (Discogs ID: 3286062), a Glasgow indie-rock act whose four-track CD EP Can You See? was released on Electric Honey (EHRCD03, the same label that would issue Belle & Sebastian’s Tigermilk the following year). The Moondials EP was produced by Alan Rankine (The Associates) and engineered by Keith Mitchell — the only Moondials release on record.

Belle & Sebastian: founding member (1996–present)

Jackson joined Belle & Sebastian at formation, appearing on the debut LP Tigermilk (Electric Honey, 1996) as one of six original members. He is a continuous presence across the band’s full catalog through A Bit Of Previous (Matador, 2022). His primary roles within the band are rhythm and lead guitar, harmonica, backing and occasional lead vocals, and songwriting. He has also contributed liner notes on several B&S releases (Storytelling, The Third Eye Centre).

Notable B&S credits in the Discogs record include: Tigermilk (1996), If You’re Feeling Sinister reissue (2010), Write About Love (2010), The BBC Sessions (2008), Girls In Peacetime Want To Dance (2015, composer credit), What To Look For In Summer live LP (2020), and A Bit Of Previous (2022, liner notes for “I Sing My Song Of Love”). Jackson appeared as a front-cover model on the B&S single Legal Man (Jeepster, 2000).

The band appeared on the Produced By Trevor Horn concert LP A Concert For The Prince’s Trust – Live At Wembley Arena 2004 (Clear Channel / ZTT, 2005) as musicians.

Bill Wells Trio (2000–2006)

Alongside Bill Wells (piano) and Robert Henderson (trumpet), Jackson formed the Bill Wells Trio (Discogs ID: 276199), issuing two albums on Geographic: Incorrect Practice (2000, GEOG 5CD) and Also In White (2002, GEOG15CD). Both are credited to his playing guitar and harmonica. A live album, Live At Banchory (P-Vine Records, Japan, 2006), documents the trio in performance. The trio represents a more minimal, jazz-inflected chamber-pop setting distinct from B&S. Also In White features guest contributions from Stephen Pastel and Katrina Mitchell on its closing track.

The Bill Wells connection predates the trio: in 1998 Jackson played guitar and harmonica on the split LP The Bill Wells Octet Vs. Future Pilot A.K.A. (The Bill Wells Octet / Future Pilot A.K.A., Domino, 1998). Other contributors on that record include Francis Macdonald (loop donation) and Sushil K. Dade (recording, editing).

God Help The Girl (2009/2014)

Jackson served as Associate Producer on Stuart Murdoch’s film-musical project God Help The Girl — both the studio LP (Matador, 2009, songwriting and associate producer credits) and the Original Motion Picture Soundtrack (Milan, 2014). He also co-wrote songs for the project.

Solo: (I Can’t Get No) Stevie Jackson (2011)

In 2011 Jackson released his first solo LP, (I Can’t Get No) Stevie Jackson, on his own imprint Banchory (the name also references his home studio). Arranged and produced by Jackson himself, with engineering distributed across a large roster of Glasgow associates: Chris Geddes (B&S keyboards), Tony Doogan (B&S/Mogwai engineer/mixer), David Carswell, Gregor Reid, John Clark, and John Collins (3). Mick Cooke contributed arrangements. The LP was mastered by Frank Arkwright and mixed by Tony Doogan. This is Jackson’s only release as a primary solo artist in the Discogs database.

Outside productions and session work (2010–2020)

Beyond B&S, Jackson’s most substantive external credit is as producer and multi-instrumentalist on Trembling Bells’ LP Abandoned Love (Honest Jon’s Records, 2010). He co-produced and mixed alongside Alex Neilson and John Cavanagh, and contributed harmonica, piano, lap steel guitar, e-bow, and backing vocals. Trembling Bells were a Glasgow-adjacent folk-psych group led by Neilson and Lavinia Blackwall.

Other session credits include: guitar on The Sexual Objects’ Sex With An X (Sub Pop, 2010); electric guitar on Delinquency Delinquency (Domino, 2000, electric sitar); guitar, harmonica and vocals on Fuerteventura (Octubre/Sony Music, 2011); electric guitar on Andromeda (Tin Angel Records, 2020); electric guitar on New Gods (Slumberland Records, 2014); harmonica on Bill Wells’ Standards Vol. II (Karaoke Kalk, 2013) and Standards Vol. IV (2018); harmonica on Leapling (Chemikal Underground, 2020); and twelve-string guitar (uncredited on sleeve) on the Syd Barrett / Wire ambient reissue project Avocet Revisited (Earth (13), 2017).

Jackson also provided additional recording facilities at his Banchory studio for Bill Wells’ Standards Vol. II (2013).

Side groups

Tsunami Allstars (ID: 1996296) — a supergroup-style collaboration with Norman Blake (Teenage Fanclub), Eugene Kelly (The Vaselines), and Eddi Reader (Fairground Attraction). No releases documented in the Discogs database under this act.

The Company Presents Roy Moller (ID: 1654753) — with Roy Moller and Gary Thom. Released Searching For The Now 1 (Slumberland Records, 2008) as a split 7” with The Hermit Crabs (2).

The Store Keys (ID: 3576870) — with Roy Moller, Marco Rea, Lorne Cowieson, and Joe Kane (5). Released a split 7” Daddy Was A Mod In The RAF / Red Light with The Wellgreen on The Barne Society (2011).

Scene Connections

Jackson is a core node of the late-1990s/2000s Glasgow indie and post-pop scene. His overlapping membership in Belle & Sebastian, the Bill Wells Trio, and multiple Roy Moller collaborations keeps him tethered to a tight cluster of Glasgow musicians. The Geographic label (sublabel of Geographic Music, distributed via Domino) served as the primary release home for his work outside B&S. His connection to Future Pilot A.K.A. (Sushil K. Dade) via the 1998 Bill Wells Octet split extends toward the broader experimental fringe of the Glasgow scene documented on Creeping Bent and Geographic. Tsunami Allstars bridges into the wider Scottish indie world (Teenage Fanclub via Norman Blake, The Vaselines via Eugene Kelly, Fairground Attraction via Eddi Reader). His production work on Trembling Bells’ Abandoned Love (Honest Jon’s, 2010) links him to the London-based folk-psych and avant-folk circuit around Alex Neilson.
