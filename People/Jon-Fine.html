Jon Fine

American guitarist, author, and journalist. Discogs artist ID: 411506. Name variations: Jonathan Fine, Jonathan “Dinky” Fine.

Biography

Background and Early Credits (1983)

Jon Fine’s earliest Discogs credit is from 1983, playing acoustic and electric guitar on a 7” single by Hayfever (2) (That Rainy Sunday / I Don’t Believe I’ll Fall In Love Today, Hayfever Productions). The context — a folk/country-inflected session with mandolin, pedal steel, and strings — is strikingly different from the noise rock he would become associated with, and likely represents a pre-college or early-college session.

Bitch Magnet (1986–1991, reunion 2011)

Fine was a founding guitarist of Bitch Magnet, the post-hardcore band formed at Oberlin College in 1986. The band’s lineup also included Sooyoung Park (bass, vocals), Orestes Delatorre (drums), and at various points David Galt (guitar) and David Grubbs (briefly). Early recordings were co-produced with Mike McMackin; debut 7” Star Booty (1988, Roman Candle Records) was also co-produced by Steve Albini.

Key releases with Bitch Magnet:

  • Star Booty (7”, 1988, Roman Candle Records) — debut; produced by Bitch Magnet and Steve Albini; Fine credited as “Jonathan Fine”
  • Umber (LP, 1989, What Goes On Records / EFA) — first full-length; Fine on guitar alongside Galt; co-produced with McMackin
  • Umber And Star Booty (CD compilation, 1989, Communion Label) — collects the first two records
  • Ben Hur (LP, 1990, Glitterhouse Records / Communion Label) — all songs credited to the band; three recording engineers across sessions (Arden Geist, Howie Gano, McMackin)
  • Mesentery / Motor / Big Pining (7”, 1990, Waterfront Records) — Australian release; standard quartet lineup

In 2011, Fine co-coordinated the Bitch Magnet retrospective box set reissue (Bitch Magnet, Temporary Residence Limited) alongside Jeremy deVine. The reissue collected the band’s full recorded output across multiple LPs; some tracks were mixed by John Congleton. This also marked a reunion performance run.

Vineland (1993–1995)

Parallel to the end of Bitch Magnet, Fine played guitar and sang in Vineland, a NYC-based noise rock band. The lineup shifted between their two releases:

  • Archetype b/w Unfriendly (7”, 1993, Land Speed / The Matt Label) — with Gerald Menke (bass), Lyle Hysen (drums), Bob Bannister (guitar), Fine (guitar, vocals); recorded by Mike McMackin and Fred Brockman
  • Obsidian (7”, 1995, Zero Sum Records) — lineup changed: Eamon Martin (bass), Doug Scharin (drums), Fred Weaver (guitar), Fine (guitar, vocals); recorded by Weaver

A self-titled cassette/CDr (Vineland, Not On Label) also exists with no year listed.

Alger Hiss (1996–1998)

Fine played bass (and occasionally guitar) in Alger Hiss, the NYC noise-rock trio led by Jordan N. Mamone, from 1996 to 1998. Alger Hiss issued two full-lengths:

  • Settings For Nudes (LP, 1995, Ba Da Bing!) — predates Fine’s tenure; lineup was Mamone, Chris O’Rourke (bass/guitar/vocals), Hajji Majer (drums)
  • Graft Vs. Host (LP, 1997, Feldspar Records / Tangential Records) — Fine’s tenure overlaps this period; lineup in Discogs credits Mamone, O’Rourke, Majer (no individual track credits for Fine on this release, but Discogs places his membership 1996–1998)

Don Caballero (1999–2001)

Following Mike Banfield’s departure from Don Caballero after touring for What Burns Never Returns (1998), Fine joined the band on guitar. By 2000 the lineup was a trio: Damon Che (drums), Ian Williams (guitar), Eric Emm (bass), and Fine on second guitar — though Fine is not credited on American Don’s personnel list. The Discogs credit for American Don (2000, Touch And Go tg218) lists only Che, Williams, and Emm as performers; Fine’s membership during this period is confirmed by the band’s own Discogs member listing and the Don Caballero profile text, which names him as Banfield’s replacement. He is not credited as guitarist on the American Don release itself.

The band dissolved in 2001 following a van accident on tour, compounded by internal tensions between Che and Williams.

Coptic Light (2000–2006)

Fine was a founding member of Coptic Light, a Brooklyn-based instrumental post-rock trio active 2000–2006. Members: Fine (guitar), Jeff Winterberg (bass), Kevin Shea (drums). They released one record:

  • Yentl (LP, 2003, Volumeone) — the sole Coptic Light release; standard trio lineup, no additional production credits beyond band members

Later Credits

Post-Coptic Light, Fine’s Discogs credits thin to non-performance roles:

  • san (2014, Damnably) by an unspecified artist — backing vocals
  • Daisychain Reaction (2016, Lotuspool) — liner notes essay
  • Possibilities (2024, Rhino Records / Hancock Music / Warner Music International) — photography

Fine has also worked as a journalist and author; his Discogs profile describes him as “American musician, author and magazine writer and editor.” Social media links on his Discogs entry reference an authorial identity (Facebook page “jonfineauthor”).

Open Questions

  • Jon Fine is listed as a Don Caballero member and as Banfield’s replacement in the band’s profile, but does not appear in the American Don (2000) performer credits (only Che, Williams, and Emm are listed). Whether he played on the record or only toured remains unclear from Discogs alone.
  • The 1983 Hayfever (2) credit — the context of that session (school band? local NC/OH scene?) is not documented in Discogs.
  • Fine’s Alger Hiss membership is dated 1996–1998 by the band’s profile, but his name does not appear in the Graft Vs. Host (1997) Discogs credits. Whether he appears on any Alger Hiss recording vs. only played live is unclear.
  • Post-2006 activities beyond journalism — any other musical projects not documented in Discogs?
  • The “san” (2014, Damnably) backing vocal credit — what release/artist is this and how did Fine come to be involved? The label Damnably is UK-based, which is an unexpected connection.
