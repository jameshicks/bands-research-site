Rusty McFarland

Discogs ID: 1101154 ANVs: Rusty MacFarland · Rusty “Mopar” McFarland · Rusty “Moper” McFarland · Rusty Mcfarland

Record producer, recording engineer, mixing engineer, and occasional session musician based in Tennessee. Active primarily from the late 1980s through the early 2000s, McFarland built a substantial body of work in blues, blues-rock, and roots music, with the vast majority of his credits centered on Bullseye Blues, Rounder, and related labels. His most notable credit outside that world is Curlew’s Paradise (1996, Cuneiform Records), a downtown New York experimental jazz record that sits in sharp contrast to the rest of his catalog — suggesting either a personal connection with the band or a period working in Memphis studios that crossed paths with the record’s production.

McFarland’s studio base appears to have been Memphis, Tennessee. Multiple credits reference 315 Beale Studios and Crosstown Recorders (on Them Update Blues, 1995) and Hum Depot (on Five Dollar Bill, 2002). He is listed as a musician on several records — mandolin (Back To The Well, 1993), additional bass, tambourine, shaker, cowbell, acoustic guitar, piano, and backing vocals — indicating a working session musician background alongside his engineering role. On It Aint Easy (1994, The Radio Kings) he co-produced and played multiple instruments. He also co-produced the Tony Spinner album My. ‘64 (1994, Blues Bureau International) and produced Crosstown Sessions (1996) for the same artist.

An early 1990 credit on a DC Talk album (Nu Thang, ForeFront Records) suggests he was working in Nashville / Tennessee Christian music circles before pivoting fully to blues. His first confirmed blues-world credit is Chain Smokin’ Texas Style (1992, Bullseye Blues). The Horny Holidays (1992, Triple X Records) producer/engineer credit is an outlier in genre terms.

His latest documented credit in the database is a 2017 compilation mix credit on A Legend Never Dies (Ruf Records), suggesting he remained active as a mixer into the late 2010s.

Key Studio Relationships

Curlew / Paradise (1996): McFarland recorded and mixed this album for the New York experimental group led by George Cartwright. The lineup on the session included Davey Williams (guitar), Ann Rupel (bass, vocals), Samm Bennett (drums), Chris Cochrane (guitar, vocals), and guest Jim Spake (baritone and soprano sax). Executive-produced by Steven Feigenbaum of Cuneiform Records. McFarland is credited as “Rusty MacFarland” on the sleeve. This is the only experimental/avant-garde credit in his catalog.

Tony Spinner: McFarland’s most sustained producing relationship. He produced and recorded My. ‘64 (1994, Blues Bureau International / Roadrunner) and Crosstown Sessions (1996, same labels), playing percussion and additional bass on the latter. Engineer Buddy Nemenz assisted on both.

Bullseye Blues: McFarland engineered a significant portion of the Bullseye Blues catalog in the 1993–1996 window, functioning essentially as a house engineer for the imprint across dozens of sessions.

Don Nix / Back To The Well (1993, Appaloosa): Recording engineer and remix; also played mandolin. Session also featured Jim Spake (alto and baritone saxophone), Greg Morrow (drums), Rick Steff (organ, accordion), and members of the Memphis Horns (Andrew Love, Wayne Jackson).

Luther Allison / Reckless (1997, Alligator): Mixed (alongside Jim Gaines). Recording done at Ardent Studios (Memphis) by Jeff Powell. This is one of the higher-profile titles in his filmography by label prestige.

‘68 Comeback / Mr. Downchild (1994, Sympathy For The Record Industry): Engineered with assistant Buddy Nemenz. Band includes Jeffrey Evans (of the Oblivians) and Ross Johnson.
