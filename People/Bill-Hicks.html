Bill Hicks

American stand-up comedian, social critic, and satirist. Discogs artist ID: 365190. Born December 16, 1961, Valdosta, Georgia; died February 26, 1994, Little Rock, Arkansas (pancreatic cancer, age 32).

Best known for politically charged, philosophically dense stand-up that addressed American culture, consumerism, religion, and media with dark wit. Achieved greater fame in the UK during his lifetime than in the US. Rykodisc became the primary custodian of his recorded catalog after his death, issuing and reissuing multiple albums.

Denis Leary controversy

Background

Hicks and Denis Leary were friends for years prior to the controversy. After Leary’s album No Cure for Cancer (recorded live at Irving Plaza, New York, October 10, 1992; broadcast Channel 4 UK February 3, 1993; Showtime US February 20, 1993), Hicks severed the friendship, which was unresolved when Hicks died 13 months later.

What Hicks said publicly

Two documented public statements from Hicks are in circulation, both originating from a 1993 Austin Comedy News interview. The citation trail for this interview runs through secondary sources (Wikipedia article, biographies) rather than to a digitized primary record; no audio or full transcript of the interview appears to have been preserved online.

Quote 1 (sarcastic, most widely cited):

“I have a scoop for you. I stole his act. I camouflaged it with punchlines, and to really throw people off, I did it before he did.”

Source trail: Quoted in Wikipedia “Bill Hicks” article (footnote citing Tim McIntire, “Bill Hicks: Frequently Asked Questions,” BillHicks.com, March 31, 1998); also cited by Plagiarism Today (2025-09-04) and Mental Floss without tracing to the original publication.

Quote 2 (on quitting smoking):

“I’m thinking of quitting smoking to see if he’ll copy that.”

Source trail: Widely circulated (e.g., @Bill_Hicks_RIP on X, 2023) but the originating publication is not confirmed in any source found. Often paraphrased as “I just wanted to see if Denis would, too.” Treat as unverified without an original citation.

Caution: The Austin Comedy News interview is the only named primary source for Hicks’s direct statements. Its text has not been found in any archive or digitized form; all citations downstream are secondhand. BillHicks.com FAQ (McIntire, 1998) is the closest to a primary relay found.

Primary-source evidence and evidentiary basis

Video/audio comparisons: YouTube compilations exist (e.g., “Denis Leary rips off Bill Hicks,” youtube.com/watch?v=ViPNcuknyd8 and youtube.com/watch?v=lf1ultenl3I) placing Hicks’s earlier sets alongside Leary’s No Cure material. These are fan-compiled and not independently verified against complete unedited sources, but they constitute the closest thing to a direct comparison record available online.

Books (secondary but substantive):

  • Cynthia True, American Scream: The Bill Hicks Story (2002) — primary biographical source. Describes Hicks as “furious” and states “Leary had, practically line for line, taken huge chunks of Bill’s act and recorded it.” Source of the Colleen McGarr account below. [Wikipedia footnote 36]
  • Kevin Booth and Michael Bertin, Bill Hicks: Agent of Evolution (2005) — written by Hicks’s best friend and collaborator; documents the conflict. [Cited in multiple secondary sources; no specific passage extracted]

Witness account (Colleen McGarr): Hicks’s manager and later fiancée. Per American Scream, McGarr was backstage at Montreal’s Club Soda when Leary performed at the “Nasty Show.” She overheard Leary performing material she recognized as Hicks’s. When Leary came offstage she confronted him: he “stared at her without saying a word, and briskly left the dressing room.” Source: True (2002) as relayed via Wikipedia and web summaries; not independently corroborated in a contemporaneous document found online. [Wikipedia “Bill Hicks,” footnote 36]

Comedians on record: Wikipedia’s “Bill Hicks” article states “at least three stand-up comedians” stated on record that Leary stole from Hicks. Named/cited third parties:

  • Joe Rogan — stated this view; cited in Rogan’s blog post (September 27, 2005, “Carlos Mencia is a weak minded joke thief”) and in Playboy “20Q: Joe Rogan” (October 2003, journalist Robert Crane). [Wikipedia footnotes 38, 39]
  • Louis C.K. — in a 2008 radio interview, claimed Leary’s “Asshole” bit was lifted from his material (separate from the Hicks allegation). Leary denied, crediting co-writer Chris Phillips. [Plagiarism Today (2025-09-04)]
  • Lenny Clarke — made a joke at the 2003 Comedy Central Roast of Denis Leary implying Hicks had sent Leary cigarettes; the joke was cut from the broadcast. [Wikipedia footnote 43, citing Boston Globe, August 10, 2003]

Leary’s response

In the August 2006 Playboy, when asked “People have accused you of appropriating [Hicks’s] persona and material,” Leary said:

“That’s a great story that people like to latch onto…Very quickly we got New York club owners saying, ‘You guys are too alike,’ while I was saying, ‘What are they fucking talking about?’ It’s the same approach to the subject maybe, but it’s not the same act…But as I’ve said many times, a fable is sometimes better than the truth.”

Leary did not directly deny specific joke-for-joke copying in this statement; he framed the controversy as embellishment. No other on-record Leary statements addressing the Hicks allegations specifically have been found.

Evidentiary summary

The case rests on: (1) side-by-side comparison of recordings (Hicks pre-1993 vs. Leary’s No Cure for Cancer); (2) Hicks’s own sarcastic public acknowledgment (one sourced interview, Austin Comedy News 1993, no digitized original found); (3) the Colleen McGarr witness account documented in American Scream (2002); (4) testimony from other comedians. No legal action was filed. There is no known audio or video of Hicks making a direct, unambiguous accusation on record — his public statements were framed as jokes. The strongest primary-source evidence is the recordings themselves, which predate No Cure for Cancer by several years.
