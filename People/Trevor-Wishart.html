Trevor Wishart

Biography

Trevor Wishart was born in Leeds in 1946. He completed his doctorate in musical composition at the University of York in 1973, embedding himself in the nascent electroacoustic scene forming there under figures such as Richard Orton (2). His work from the outset combined tape manipulation, large-scale collaborative performance, and a sustained focus on vocal sound as primary compositional material.

Early Electroacoustic Work and York (1973–1979)

Wishart’s doctoral year at York produced Journey Into Space (1973), a large-scale double LP involving over forty musicians from the University of York community. The work was released without label backing under a “Not On Label” imprint and stands as one of the more ambitious British electroacoustic efforts of its time in terms of performer count and scope. The cast drew heavily from the York music department, including colleagues Richard Orton (2), Jan Steele, Steve Beresford, Jonty Harrison, Dominic Muldowney, Martin Wesley-Smith, Bernard Rands, Roger Marsh, and Stanley Haynes, among others.

In 1978 he released Red Bird (A Political Prisoner’s Dream) on York Electronic Studios (YES 7), a solo LP in which he used himself and three other voice contributors as source material, constructing a tape work built from male and female vocal sound sources. The following year, Beach Singularity & Menagerie (YES 8, 1979) appeared under the credit Trevor Wishart & Friends / Trevor Wishart, with live performers from The Palm Beach Orchestra and collaborators including Martin Mayes, tuba player Melvyn Poore, and vocalist Poppy Holden.

Vox Cycle and Vocal Research (1980s–1990s)

From the mid-1970s onward Wishart pursued systematic research into vocal and speech sounds, producing an extended series of works exploring the boundaries of human vocal production and computer processing. This culminated in the Vox cycle, released on Virgin Classics in 1990. The album comprises six works and was co-produced by Wishart and vocal ensemble Electric Phoenix, with Wishart also serving as balance engineer. Featured vocalists included mezzo-soprano Meriel Dickinson, soprano Judith Rees, tenor Daryl Runswick, and bass vocalist Terry Edwards (2). The ensemble’s executive producer was Simon Foster (2).

During this period Wishart received international recognition, winning prizes at the International Festival of ElectroAcoustic Music (Bourges, 1978), Ars Electronica (Linz, 1988), the Euphonie d’Or (Bourges, 1992), and the Golden Nica Award (Ars Electronica, Linz, 1995). He was a jury member at Ars Electronica (1991–93), the International Computer Music Conference (1991–94), and the Stockholm International Electronic Music Festival (1995).

Sonic Art Network and Composers Desktop Project

From 1980 to 1999 Wishart was a member of the organizing committee of the Sonic Art Network (EMAS), serving as chairman from 1990 to 1993. He was also a founding figure in the Composers Desktop Project (CDP), aimed at giving composers access to high-quality computer music software; this project was also shaped by Richard Orton (2), who established CDP in 1986.

Wishart is the author of several theoretical books on musical composition and sonic art, including On Sonic Art (Gordon & Breach), Audible Design (Orpheus the Pantomime), and Whose Music? A Sociology of Musical Languages (Transaction Books). His work has appeared in Computer Music Journal, Leonardo, Contemporary Music Review, Interface, and Les Cahiers d’IRCAM.

Teaching and Commissions

Wishart taught at the University of Nottingham, the University of Leeds, and the University of York, and was also active as a visiting teacher internationally — at Sogetu Hall (Tokyo), Universidad Complutense (Madrid), Académie d’Été (Paris), Akademie der Kunst (Berlin), Kyoto University of Arts, Sibelius Akatemia (Helsinki), University of Sydney, the Royal Conservatory (The Hague), and Lund University. Commissioned works came from the BBC (London), the Paris Biennale, the Bourges Festival, Swedish Radio (Stockholm), and the French Ministry of Culture.

He designed the 54-channel sound installation for the Jorvik Viking Centre Multimedia Museum in York.

Later Output

Fanfare And Contrapunctus / Imago (2010) was released on the Berlin label Pan (3) (PAN 12) as a limited edition LP. The A side uses source material from Wishart and Martin Mayes; the B side, Imago, uses original sound sources from Jonty Harrison. Mastering was by Rashad Becker.

The 2014 double CD Red Bird / Anticredos & Voiceprints on ICR (ICR 77) compiled two discs: Voiceprints (CD 1, seven works including Tongues Of Fire and American Triptych) and Red Bird / Anticredos (CD 2), the latter including Anticredos directed by Gregory Rose and performed by vocal ensemble Singcircle.

Journey Into Space (1973)

Journey Into Space was released as a double LP in 1973 without label backing. Structured in four parts (Parts 1–4 across sides A–D), it drew on a large cohort of performers from the University of York music community. The 2002 Paradigm Discs reissue (PD 18) gave it its widest circulation, presenting the work as a CD in three titled movements: “Birth Dream” (13:03), “Journey” (47:29), and “Arrival” (18:18). The 24-bit A-to-D transfer for the reissue was carried out by Ambrose Field. Liner notes (2001) and photography by Wishart himself. Repackage design and additional mastering by Clive Graham.
