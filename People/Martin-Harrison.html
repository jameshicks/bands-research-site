Martin Harrison

UK bassist, guitarist, vocalist, and producer. Full name: Martin Frederick Harrison. Also credited as Martin Frederix (primary alias on On-U Sound and Crammed Discs releases). Member of Family Fodder, London Underground (band), Fellow Travellers, and People In Control. Active in the UK post-punk and experimental/dub scene from the early 1980s through at least 2021.

Discogs IDs: 298441 (Martin Harrison) · 122819 (Martin Frederix)

Biography

Early 1980s — Family Fodder and the Post-Punk Scene

Harrison first appears on record as a member of Family Fodder, the London art-punk collective led by Alig and Dominique Levillain. On the debut album Monkey Banana Kitchen (1980, Fresh Records (14)), credited simply as “Martin,” he played bass, guitar, vocals, and percussion. The album was produced collectively by People In Control — the loose collective that included Harrison alongside Charles Bullen, Ian Hill, Judy Carter, and Rick Wilson.

For Family Fodder’s follow-up Greatest Hits (1981, Crammed Discs), Harrison appeared under the name Martin Frederix (credited as “Martin Frederick”), playing bass and vocals alongside the band’s regular cast: Mick Hobbs, Charles Bullen, Felix Fiedorowicz, Rick Wilson, Mark Doffman, Graham Painting, and Ian Hill. He was also listed on the Crammed Discs 7” When It’s War (1981) and is credited at Berry Street Studio on Les Tueurs De La Lune De Miel (1981), the Honeymoon Killers album also on Crammed.

Also in 1981, under his Harrison name, he produced the single Crowds / Crossing The Border by A Popular History Of Signs on Melodia Records — an early credit as sole producer for a separate act.

1983–1985 — On-U Sound and London Underground

From 1983 onward, the Frederix alias anchors a substantial run of credits in Adrian Sherwood’s On-U Sound ecosystem. Harrison co-produced and played on At Home With The London Underground (1983, On-U Sound), the debut album by London Underground (band) — a group whose members included Sherwood, Doctor Pablo, Kishi Yamamoto, and others. He produced five of the album’s tracks (A2–A4, B2–B3); Sherwood handled the others.

Simultaneously, Harrison contributed bass and guitar to three key African Head Charge albums produced by Sherwood:

  • Drastic Season (1983) — bass and guitar alongside Bonjo Iyabinghi Noah, Style Scott, and Steve Beresford
  • Staggering Heights (1983) — guitar and penny whistle
  • North Of The River Thames (1984) — bass and guitar

He also played guitar on other On-U albums during this period: the Singers & Players compilation Leaps & Bounds (1984, with Cherry Red), Tunes From The Missing Channel (1985). For Corpus Christi Records he played guitar on Soul Possession (1984).

A one-off credit from 1983: Harrison played percussion, baritone saxophone, and produced Go Home Soldier on It’s War Boys Records, indicating his range extended well beyond conventional bass duties.

1987 — One Little Indian Work

In 1987 Harrison (as Frederix) contributed guitar to two releases on One Little Indian: the album Jackamo and the single As I Lie In Your Arms, both linked to the wider post-punk-into-pop transitional moment on that label.

Also in 1987 he played guitar on a release issued in Italy on Industrie Discografiche Lacerba (Night Train).

1989–1990 — Engineering, Production, and African Head Charge

Harrison worked as engineer on Against Nature (1989, Kitchenware Records) by The Fatima Mansions, the debut album by Cathal Coughlan’s group — placing him in a studio-professional role outside his usual post-punk circle.

On-U Sound’s Songs Of Praise (1990) by African Head Charge is his most substantial single credit in the Sherwood camp: as Frederix, he wrote tracks 10, 11, and 13; played bass on those tracks; co-mixed them with Sherwood; and handled programming — a full production/composition role, distinct from his earlier purely instrumental contributions.

For On-U’s Classic Selection Volume 1 (1989) and Great Vintage Volume 2 (1989) he contributed guitar and bass respectively.

1990–1995 — Fellow Travellers and OKra Records

Harrison formed Fellow Travellers with Jeb Loy Nichols and Lorraine Morley, releasing on the German label OKra Records. As the primary act:

  • No Easy Way (1990, OKra) — Harrison: bass, keyboards
  • Just A Visitor (1992, OKra) — Harrison: producer
  • Things And Time (1993, OKra) — Harrison: bass, keyboards, producer
  • Love Shines Brighter (1993, Return To Sender) — recorded by Harrison

In 1991 he co-produced a track on the On-U Sound compilation Pay It All Back Volume Three — sharing co-producer credits on a release that also listed Bim Sherman, Doug Wimbish, Lee Perry, and Mark Stewart.

In 1994 he mixed the Jeb Loy Nichols solo album Moving (Rough Trade, Japan pressing), and continued bass/programming work on the African Head Charge release All Mighty Dread (1994, Beat Records Japan) as Frederix.

For On-U’s Pay It All Back (1994, Restless Records) he was credited with guitar and producer.

In 1995 he mixed Light Years (Epic, Japan) — suggesting ongoing work in professional mixing/production contexts beyond the On-U world.

He also appears on On-U’s Strike The Balance (1998) playing rhythm guitar.

2000 — Water Shed

Harrison co-produced Water Shed (2000, Dark Beloved Cloud) — the Family Fodder reunion album — alongside Andy Le Vien. He played bass and sampler. The album featured longtime members Alig, Rick Wilson, and Dominique Levillain, plus guests including Inno Sorsy (backing vocals) and Ricky Edwards (bass clarinet/alto sax).

2001–2003 — Continued On-U Sound Presence

On-U Sound’s Time Boom X De Devil Dead (2001, Zonophone) — an African Head Charge release — lists Frederix on lead guitar and rhythm guitar. Further guitar credits appear on Murder Tone (2002) and Shrunken Head (2003), both On-U Sound African Head Charge albums.

2006–2008 — Live Engineering and Family Fodder Retrospective

Harrison provided live sound engineering for two Family Fodder archival releases on This Is Records: Live 80/81 (2006) and Out Of Cold Storage (2006). He also engineered Viva Dead Ponies (2007, Kitchenware/Sony BMG), another Fatima Mansions reissue.

In 2008 he appeared as a performer on More Great Hits! (Jungle Records), a Family Fodder retrospective/compilation gathering the full ensemble.

2009–2021 — Production, Mixing, and Later Activity

Harrison provided production assistance on Parish Bar (2009, Compass Records). He sang and played bass on Sunday Girls (Director’s Cut) (2015, Staubgold), a Family Fodder release. He contributed percussion to the On-U Sound Warzone release (2015) under the Frederix name.

In 2017 two more On-U Sound archival releases — Ambience In Dub 1982–1985 and Displaced Masters — carried Frederix credits (bass/guitar and guitar respectively), confirming his earlier work was still being surfaced.

He produced Funland (2017, Moone Records) and mixed Paragraphs And Principles (2021, Klanggalerie), indicating continued activity well into the 2020s.

Open Questions

  • What is the precise relationship between the “Martin Harrison” and “Martin Frederix” name choices? The Frederix name appears most often on Crammed Discs and On-U Sound releases; Harrison on Fresh Records and his own production work. No biographical source in Discogs clarifies the distinction.
  • Lorraine Morley (Fellow Travellers co-member) has no Discogs page under that name — her role and background are not documented.
  • The 2015 Warzone On-U Sound percussion credit is listed under the Frederix alias — unclear if this represents a new African Head Charge session or an archival pull.
  • No birth date, hometown, or early biography available from Discogs; real name confirmed as Martin Frederick Harrison via the Martin Frederix entry (ID 122819).
