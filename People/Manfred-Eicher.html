Manfred Eicher

German record producer, contrabassist, and founder of ECM Records. Born 9 July 1943 in Lindau. Discogs artist ID: 247567

Biography

Early Life and Formation

Eicher was born in Lindau on Lake Constance and trained as a musician at the Academy of Music in Berlin. He played double bass professionally — including with the Berliner Philharmoniker and the Bob Degen Trio — before turning his energies fully toward production and label management.

His formation as a player matters for understanding his production approach: Eicher brings a performer’s ear to the studio, with documented sensitivity to space, dynamics, and ensemble balance. Many ECM artists describe his role in the studio as active co-shaper of the final sonic result rather than a passive supervisor.

Founding ECM Records (1969–1974)

In 1969 Eicher co-founded Edition of Contemporary Music — ECM Records — in Munich with Manfred Scheffner (ID: 1264053) and Karl Egger. The label launched with a Mal Waldron session (Spanish Bitch, 1970) and a cooperative improvisation date from The Music Improvisation Company (1970), and quickly aligned with a loose network of European and American avant-garde players — Jan Garbarek, Terje Rypdal, Eberhard Weber, Arild Andersen, Jon Christensen, Paul Bley, and Chick Corea.

The earliest landmark production was Afric Pepperbird (Jan Garbarek Quartet, 1970), which established the label’s characteristic “silence” in the recording — wide dynamic range, audible room acoustics, dry but natural reverb. Facing You (Keith Jarrett solo piano, 1972) and Conference of the Birds (Dave Holland Quartet with Anthony Braxton, Sam Rivers, and Barry Altschul, 1973) followed as definitional early ECM statements.

The Köln Concert (Keith Jarrett, 1975) remains the best-selling solo piano record ever made and established Eicher as a producer willing to capture spontaneous live performance with full fidelity — no overdubs, no edits. Belonging (Jarrett / Garbarek / Jon Christensen / Palle Danielsson, 1974) defined what came to be called the “European Quartet” sound.

The Classic ECM Decade (1975–1984)

Throughout the late 1970s and into the 1980s Eicher built out ECM’s twin poles of jazz improvisation and composed contemporary music.

On the jazz/improvisation side he produced landmark records for:

  • Pat Metheny — Bright Size Life (1976), Watercolors (1977), Pat Metheny Group (1978), Offramp (1982), As Falls Wichita, So Falls Wichita Falls (with Lyle Mays, 1981)
  • Ralph Towner — sustained solo and ensemble output from 1973 onwards
  • Charlie Haden — including Ballad of the Fallen (1983)
  • Jack DeJohnette — Special Edition (1980) and its successors, including mixing credits alongside production
  • Meredith Monk — Dolmen Music (1981)
  • Paul Motian — extensive catalog from 1973
  • John Abercrombie — Abercrombie Quartet (1981) and many others
  • Egberto Gismonti — Sanfona (1981) and others
  • Gary Burton and Chick Corea — Crystal Silence (1973), In Concert, Zürich (1979)

On the contemporary classical side he began producing works by Arvo Pärt — starting with Tabula Rasa (1984), which introduced tintinnabuli composition to a wide audience, followed by Passio (1987) and Miserere (1991). This established ECM New Series as a serious imprint for sacred and minimalist contemporary music.

He also produced Jan Garbarek through multiple lineups and configurations across this entire period (Garbarek appears on 487 releases in the ECM Discogs catalog touching Eicher’s credits).

ECM New Series and World Music Expansion (1985–2000)

Officium (Garbarek with The Hilliard Ensemble, 1994) — in which Garbarek’s saxophone improvisations are layered over early polyphonic choral performances — became one of ECM’s best-sellers and demonstrated that the label’s aesthetic could reach mainstream classical audiences. Eicher produced it in a Norwegian stave church.

He expanded the label’s reach into world music and non-Western traditions through Shankar (violin/double violin, from 1981), Anouar Brahem (oud, from 1991), Kim Duk-Soo and Samulnori, and others.

The contemporary classical roster deepened through Arvo Pärt, Meredith Monk, and eventually Kim Kashkashian (from 1986), John Adams (Harmonium, mixed by Eicher, 1984), and András Schiff (from 1999). The Hilliard Ensemble accumulated 143 releases crediting Eicher in the database.

Charlie Haden, Oregon, and The Art Ensemble Of Chicago continued as recurring ECM voices through this period.

2000s–Present

Production output remained prolific into the 2000s and 2010s, encompassing:

  • Charles Lloyd — long association beginning in the 1990s, continuing through the 2010s
  • Jon Hassell — Last Night The Moon Came Dropping Its Clothes In The Street (ECM, 2009), produced by Eicher
  • The Tigran Hamasyan / Arve Henriksen / Eivind Aarset / Jan Bang quartet — Atmosphères (ECM, 2016), produced by Eicher
  • Larry Grenadier — The Gleaners (2019), mixed jointly by Eicher and Grenadier
  • Manu Katché — five solo albums as ECM leader (2005–2022)
  • Anouar Brahem — ongoing from 1991 through the 2010s and beyond
  • Wadada Leo Smith — Divine Love (1979) and several later releases

Discogs credits show Eicher with explicit producer credits on approximately 1,509 unique works across the full catalog.

Awards

  • “German Record Critics Award” (honorary), 1986
  • Grand Prix du Disque (France)
  • Edison Award (Holland)
  • Grammy Award (USA)
  • DownBeat Producer of the Year — ten times

Production Role

Eicher’s Discogs credit strings reveal an unusually wide scope: beyond “Produced By” (the most common), he appears as “Mixed By” on at least 264 releases, “Executive Producer” on 575, and with editing and mastering credits on smaller but meaningful subsets. He is hands-on through the post-production chain in a way that distinguishes him from executive-only label heads.

His name-variations in Discogs include “M. Eicher”, “M.E.”, “Manfred”, and (incorrectly) “Manfred Eichler”.
