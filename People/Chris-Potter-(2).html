Chris Potter (2)

Discogs ID: 256512 (listed as “Chris Potter (2)” on Discogs; real name Christopher Joseph Potter) Born: January 1, 1971, Chicago, IL; raised in South Carolina Instruments: Tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, alto saxophone, bass clarinet, flute, alto flute, bass flute, clarinet, piano, keyboards, guitar

American jazz saxophonist widely regarded as one of the most technically commanding and harmonically sophisticated players of his generation. Has led recordings since 1994 and maintained a continuous presence as one of jazz’s busiest and most in-demand sidemen — working alongside Dave Holland, Paul Motian, Pat Metheny, Herbie Hancock, and many others. Since the mid-2010s, has recorded primarily for Edition Records (London).

Overview

Potter grew up in Columbia, South Carolina, where he began playing saxophone as a child. He came to New York in the early 1990s and quickly established himself on the scene, appearing on sessions for the Mingus Big Band, Red Rodney, and the Steely Dan touring band. His Concord debut as leader, Concentric Circles (1994), announced a fully formed voice at age 23: hard bop fundamentals combined with a ferocious command of extended harmony, and a multi-instrumental range that included bass clarinet and alto flute.

His mid-1990s Concord period produced four leader albums (Concentric Circles, Pure, Moving In, Unspoken), all featuring regulars Scott Colley (bass) and Bill Stewart (drums). By the late 1990s he had moved to Verve, producing the ambitious Gratitude (2001) and Traveling Mercies (2002) — the latter featuring guest appearances by John Scofield and Adam Rogers (2), and marking a turn toward production-heavy, layered textures.

The most discussed phase of his leader work began with Underground (2006, EmArcy/Universal), which introduced the Chris Potter Underground quartet: Craig Taborn (electric piano), Wayne Krantz (guitar), and Nate Smith (3) (drums). The group pursued an aggressive electric-acoustic fusion territory, producing several more records — Ultrahuman (2008), Lifted (2009), Follow The Red Line — Live at the Village Vanguard (2007) — before evolving into the Underground Orchestra format. The Underground records elevated Potter’s profile internationally and are widely cited as among the most significant band-concept jazz recordings of the 2000s.

Parallel to his own projects, Potter became a cornerstone of Dave Holland’s groups throughout the 2000s-2010s — Holland’s Quintet, Octet, and Big Band — and joined Pat Metheny’s Unity Band in 2012 (Grammy-winning Unity Band, Nonesuch). With Paul Motian he contributed to the Trio 2000 + One and + Two configurations, appearing on Lost In A Dream (ECM, 2010), one of Motian’s final recordings.

His ECM period as leader produced three landmark albums: Lost In A Dream (2010, with Motian and Jason Moran), The Sirens (2013, with Taborn, Larry Grenadier, and Eric Harland), and The Dreamer Is The Dream (2017, with Taborn, David Virelles, Joe Martin (6), and Marcus Gilmore). All were produced by Manfred Eicher and engineered by James Farber. The Dreamer Is The Dream was Potter’s most expansive studio statement to that point — he played virtually all instruments including mbira and sampler.

From 2019 onward Potter signed with Edition Records (London), run by Dave Stapleton. His Edition catalog includes: Circuits (2019, solo recording plus trio with Eric Harland and Linley Marthe), Good Hope (2019, Crosscurrents Trio with Dave Holland and Zakir Hussain), There Is A Tide (2020, again playing all instruments), Got The Keys to the Kingdom — Live at the Village Vanguard (2023, with Craig Taborn, Marcus Gilmore, and Nate Wood), and Eagle’s Point (2024, with Brad Mehldau, John Patitucci, and Brian Blade).
