Paul McCartney

British singer, songwriter, bassist, composer. Born James Paul McCartney, June 18, 1942, Liverpool. Discogs artist ID: 35301.

Overview

McCartney is a founding member of The Beatles (1960–1970) and one of the most commercially successful songwriters in history. After the Beatles’ dissolution he launched a solo career and led the band Wings (2) through the 1970s. His solo catalog spans over twenty studio albums, several live albums, and a substantial body of classical and experimental work. The vault tracks him primarily through his working relationship with producer David Kahne (2001–2007) and through his experimental side project The Fireman with Youth (Martin Glover), which places him tangentially in post-punk and ambient production circles.

Pseudonyms/projects used: Apollo C. Vermouth, Percy Thrillington, Paul Ramon, Bernard Webb, The Fireman (with Youth).

Career Overview

The Beatles (1960–1970)

McCartney, alongside John Lennon, George Harrison, and Ringo Starr, made The Beatles the defining rock act of the 1960s. His songwriting partnership with Lennon (credited Lennon/McCartney) produced foundational pop, rock, and art-pop recordings. The Beatles’ later studio work, including Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967) and Abbey Road (1969), was engineered by Geoff Emerick at Abbey Road Studios. That engineer relationship carried forward into McCartney’s solo work for decades.

Wings and Early Solo Work (1970–1983)

After the Beatles split McCartney released McCartney (1970) and Ram (1971) as solo efforts, then formed Wings with Linda McCartney and Denny Laine. Wings produced several major commercial albums including Band on the Run (1973) and Wings at the Speed of Sound (1976). McCartney officially disbanded Wings in 1981.

His early 1980s solo work included Tug of War (1982) and Pipes of Peace (1983), both produced by George Martin. These albums featured notable guest appearances including Michael Jackson on several tracks.

Flowers in the Dirt — Elvis Costello Collaboration (1989)

Flowers in the Dirt (1989, Parlophone/MPL) is scene-relevant as the product of a writing partnership with Elvis Costello (Declan Patrick Aloysius MacManus, Discogs ID: 409185). McCartney and Costello co-wrote four tracks on the album (credited MacManus/McCartney): “My Brave Face,” “You Want Her Too,” “Don’t Be Careless Love,” and “That Day Is Done.” McCartney self-produced the album. The Costello collaboration positioned the record closer to the new wave / post-punk orbit that Costello inhabited via Stiff Records and beyond.

The Fireman — Ambient/Electronic Project (1993–2008)

McCartney’s most experimental work came through The Fireman, a project with Youth (Martin Glover, Discogs ID: 2656 / Martin Glover ID: 502761). Youth was bassist and co-founder of Killing Joke and became a significant ambient/dance producer (Butterfly Records). The Fireman released three albums:

Liverpool Sound Collage (2000, Hydra) was a McCartney solo experimental piece — a sound-collage work featuring Youth as engineer, alongside Paul Hicks and Lord Doufus. Super Furry Animals appeared on the companion EP pressing.

The David Kahne Period (2001–2007)

The vault’s primary reason for this page. David Kahne produced three McCartney releases between 2001 and 2007. This was McCartney’s most consistent producer relationship of the 2000s decade.

Open Questions
