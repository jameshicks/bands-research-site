Overview

Azita Youssefi is an Iranian-American experimental musician, artist, and music teacher born in 1971 in St. Louis, Missouri and based in Chicago, who performs and records under the name Azita. Her solo catalogue sits mainly on Drag City (2003–2021), with earlier releases on Atavistic; her work spans indie rock, art rock, and avant-garde styles. Within the Chicago scene she has been a member of Joan Of Arc and Miss High Heel, and her frequent collaborators include Bonnie “Prince” Billy, Rob Mazurek, and Joshua Abrams. A credit alongside Silver Jews marks an unexpected connection to that Nashville-adjacent project.

Notes (web)

Silver Jews connection — how Azita entered David Berman’s orbit

The most direct structural link is Drag City Records. Silver Jews were a Drag City act throughout their existence; Azita signed to Drag City in 2003 with Enantiodromia. By the time Silver Jews recorded Tanglewood Numbers (released October 2005), Azita had been a Drag City labelmate for roughly two years.

Her role on Tanglewood Numbers is listed as “vocals, etc.” in the album credits. She appears in the “additional performers” group alongside Will Oldham, Bobby Bare Jr., Duane Denison (The Jesus Lizard — another Chicago-rooted musician in the same Drag City/indie-underground orbit), Paz Lenchantin, and William Tyler. The core Tanglewood Numbers Band was Berman, Cassie Berman, Bob Nastanovich, Brian Kotzur, Mike Fellows, Steve Malkmus, and Tony Crow. Wikipedia: Tanglewood Numbers, as of 2026-11-10.

Berman’s own account of how he assembled the album: “All the musicians I know are on this record.” and “I don’t have a million friends who make music. The ten or so that do I got to come down here.” These quotes suggest personal acquaintance — not an open call — was the selection criterion. The Drag City roster is the most plausible meeting ground, though no interview explicitly states when or how Berman and Azita first met. Source: Coke Machine Glow feature on David Berman, cokemachineglow.com; Brooklyn Rail interview Oct 2005, brooklynrail.org.

Separately, in the Coke Machine Glow interview Berman named “The Azita Yousseffi Band” as his ideal tour opener for Silver Jews — indicating he held her work in genuine regard beyond a single session credit. cokemachineglow.com
