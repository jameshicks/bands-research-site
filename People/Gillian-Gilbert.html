Gillian Gilbert

Keyboardist, guitarist, vocalist. Born Gillian Lesley Gilbert. Manchester. Discogs ID: 156003. Also credited as Gillian Morris (3) (post-marriage alias).

Member of New Order (keyboards, guitar, 1980–2001; returned 2011–present) and co-founder of The Other Two with husband Stephen Morris. Her arrival in late 1980 gave New Order its defining keyboard voice and shifted the band’s sound toward synth-pop; she is a credited writer and producer across the full Factory and London Records catalogs.

Biography

1980 — Joining New Order

Gilbert joined New Order in late 1980 after the band had already recorded and released their debut album Movement (Factory, 1981) without a dedicated keyboard player. She had previously performed with Stephen Morris in a loose formation and was already in a relationship with him. Her first studio contributions appear from 1982 onward; she is credited on keyboards and guitar from Power, Corruption & Lies (Factory, FACT 75, 1983) forward.

Her addition transformed the band’s capability: New Order could now layer live keyboards and guitar against Morris’s drums and sequencers, enabling the dense electronic textures of the 1983–1989 Factory period.

Be Music: Gilbert is listed as a member of Be Music (Discogs ID: 156001) alongside Sumner, Morris, and Hook — the full New Order lineup operating as an in-house production alias. Be Music produced records for Factory labelmates including Section 25.

1991–1999 — The Other Two

With The Other Two, Gilbert and Morris operated as a duo working in a softer, pop-oriented direction. The project began on Factory with the “Tasty Fish” single (FAC 307, 1991), which features a guest backing vocal from Jez Kerr (A Certain Ratio). The debut album was recorded at Higher Plane Studios and Mayfair Studios with engineers Felix Kendall, Simon Gogerly, and Stuart James; Stephen Hague co-produced with the duo.

The Super Highways album features guest vocalist Melanie Williams on tracks 1, 2, 4, and 7; guitarist Mike Mooney; bassist Paddy Steer; and mixing by Ash Howes. Gilbert sings lead and co-writes on “Super Highways” itself, with co-write credit shared with Melanie Williams on several tracks.

1993 — Republic and London Records

After Factory’s collapse in 1992, New Order moved to London Records. Gilbert is credited as Written-By and producer across the Republic album (1993) and associated singles (“Regret,” “Ruined In A Day,” “World (The Price Of Love),” “Spooky”). She performs keyboards and guitar.

2001–2011 — Hiatus

Gilbert stepped back from New Order live duties in approximately 2001 to care for ill family members. The specific details are not captured in the Discogs record — her credit trail on New Order releases continues (writer/producer credits persist) but she is absent from live performance credits through this period. She returned to full membership around 2011, documented by the “Live At The London Troxy” release (2011) where she is credited as Performer.

2011–present — Return and Music Complete

From 2011 onward Gilbert resumes full Performer credits. New Order’s Music Complete (2015, Mute Records) credits her as “Performer [New Order].” She continues to co-write and co-produce on subsequent single releases and live recordings.

A solo side credit: in 2016 she appeared as a featured vocalist on “Lifetime” by Koishii & Hush (Grammaton Recordings, GTR 014), credited for Vocals and co-writing.
