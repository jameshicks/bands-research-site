Ken Hamann

Real name: Kenneth R. Hamann. Born 1 June 1928, Lakewood, Cuyahoga, Ohio. Died 10 January 2003, Cleveland, Cuyahoga, Ohio (age 74). Recording engineer, producer, and studio owner. Discogs artist ID: 264980.

Overview

Ken Hamann was a Cleveland recording engineer who spent more than four decades at the centre of that city’s recording history, first as a staff engineer at Cleveland Recording Company and later as the founder of Suma Recording Studio. Hired by CRC founder Frederick C. Wolf in the 1950s, Hamann gradually built the studio into a state-of-the-art facility. In 1970, he and production engineer John Hansen purchased the studio from Wolf and moved it to 1935 Euclid Avenue. Hansen and Hamann parted ways in 1977 when Cleveland State University acquired the property; Hamann founded Suma Recording in Painesville, Ohio. He was assisted from the start by his son Paul Hamann, who took over Suma after Ken’s death in 2003 and ran it until his own death in 2017 (Wikipedia: Cleveland Recording Company).

At Cleveland Recording, Hamann engineered some of the most commercially important rock records to come out of the Midwest: Grand Funk Railroad’s first five studio albums (1969–1971), the debut albums of James Gang and Wild Cherry (whose hit “Play That Funky Music” was recorded there in 1975), and chart singles for The Outsiders (4) (“Time Won’t Let Me,” 1966) and The Lemon Pipers (“Green Tambourine,” 1967). He also co-produced The Outsiders (4) debut LP Time Won’t Let Me (1966, Capitol).

At the transition point between Cleveland Recording and Suma, Hamann engineered and co-produced the foundational Pere Ubu records: the group’s first singles on the Hearthan label (1976), The Modern Dance (1978), Dub Housing (1978), and New Picnic Time (1979). These remain his most celebrated production credits. From the early 1980s onward, the Suma studio work passed largely to his son Paul, though Ken remained active through the 1990s on classical and jazz recordings for Telarc.

Cleveland Recording Company and Suma

Cleveland Recording Company was founded in 1938 by Frederick C. Wolf. Hamann joined as a staff engineer for both Wolf’s radio stations (WCCR, WDOK) and the recording studio in the 1950s. He built the facility from an early mono operation into a multi-track studio capable of handling major-label pop, rock, and later new wave sessions. In 1972 Michael Bishop joined as recording and disc mastering engineer. When the studio closed in 1977, Hamann founded Suma Recording in Painesville, Ohio, with Bishop and his son Paul. Bishop left for Telarc in 1988; Paul Hamann ran Suma through to 2017; the studio was sold to Michael Seifert and reopened after renovation in 2022 (Wikipedia: Cleveland Recording Company).

Connections

  • Pere Ubu — house producer and engineer, 1976–1979 (Hearthan singles and first three LPs); continued on archival releases
  • Paul Hamann — son and assistant engineer; took over Suma Recording after Ken’s death; credited on New Picnic Time (1979), The Modern Dance (1979 US pressing), and continuously from The Art Of Walking (1980) onward
  • Michael Bishop — staff engineer at Cleveland Recording from 1972; worked alongside Ken on The Modern Dance; later engineered for Telarc
  • Grand Funk Railroad — six studio/live albums engineered at Cleveland Recording, 1969–1972
  • James Gang — Yer’ Album (1969, assistant), Thirds (1971) engineered at Cleveland Recording
  • Wild Cherry — debut album engineered and mixed at Cleveland Recording, 1976 (includes “Play That Funky Music”)
  • Hearthan — Pere Ubu’s self-released label (sublabel of Hearpen Records); Hamann engineered all early Hearthan singles
  • Telarc — 1990s classical and jazz recording work (Mahler symphonies, Dave Brubeck, etc.)
  • David Thomas (2) — primary creative collaborator via Pere Ubu; ongoing relationship through reissues
  • Peter Laughner — credited on early Hearthan sessions (The Hearpen Singles)

  • Was Hamann the engineer on The Lemon Pipers’ “Green Tambourine” (1967)? Wikipedia attributes the session to him but Discogs does not currently credit him on that release.
