Aug 16, 20268 min read

Ronnie Barron

Real name: Ronald Raymond Barrosse Born: October 9, 1943, Algiers, New Orleans, Louisiana Died: March 20, 1997 Discogs artist ID: 304102 Roles: Pianist, organist, vocalist, songwriter, producer Genre: New Orleans R&B, blues rock, soul

Biography

New Orleans Origins (late 1950s – early 1960s)

Ronald Raymond Barrosse grew up in Algiers, across the Mississippi River from the French Quarter, and came up through the New Orleans bar and club circuit. His earliest professional work was as a vocalist and keyboardist with bands led by Leonard James and Earl Stanley, and he became closely associated with pianist-guitarist Mac Rebennack — the future Dr. John — during this period.

By 1958 Barron and Rebennack were recording under the pseudonym Morgus And The Ghouls (Discogs ID: 1431143) alongside Leonard James. The name was borrowed from a character — “Morgus The Magnificent,” a fictional mad scientist — from a popular Louisiana horror/sci-fi television program. Their single “Morgus The Magnificent / Lonely Boy” appeared on the Vin label in 1958. Barron also recorded under his own name as “Morgus The Magnificent” in this period.

A second early pseudonym, Drits & Dravy (Discogs ID: 2937600), designated Rebennack and Barron’s duo recordings for the AFO (All For One) label. Their single “Talk That Talk” appeared in 1963.

Barron also recorded under his own name on early singles: “It’s All In The Past” (1963, Soundex) and “The Grass Looks Greener Yonder / Did She Mention My Name” (1964, Michelle label).

Move to California and Session Work (1965 – 1970)

In 1965 Barron left New Orleans for Los Angeles to pursue session work. He signed with producer Don Costa to RCA, and also performed with a group called the Prime Ministers, who signed to Atlantic and backed Bobby Darin in 1966. During this period he worked steadily as a session keyboardist across the Los Angeles studio scene.

A songwriting credit appears in 1970: “1862 B.P.” (Et Cet’er-A Records, 1970), which he co-wrote. His composition “Louisiana Flood” (co-written with Mac Rebennack) also dates from this era and was recorded by Barron as his 1971 Decca single “Louisianna Flood / Mamma’s Kind Of Song.”

Solo on Decca and the Paul Butterfield Period (1971 – 1974)

Barron released two singles on Decca in 1971: “Louisianna Flood / Mamma’s Kind Of Song” and the double-sided “Reverend Ether” (which he recorded under the alias Right Reverend Ether — one of his known name variations). These were his last Decca releases before the Butterfield chapter.

In 1971 Barron joined Paul Butterfield’s newly-formed roots band Paul Butterfield’s Better Days (Discogs ID: 1995045). The band recorded two albums for Bearsville Records in the Woodstock, NY scene: Better Days (1973) and It All Comes Back (1973). Barron played piano, organ, and electric piano across both records, and contributed lead vocals on at least one track (“Please Send Me Someone to Love” on the debut). He also contributed the song “Broke My Baby’s Heart” to the debut album. The co-write “Louisiana Flood” (with Mac Rebennack) appeared on It All Comes Back.

The Better Days period overlapped with significant session work: in 1972 he contributed electric piano, organ, piano, and backing vocals to Dr. John’s Dr. John’s Gumbo (ATCO Records, 1972) — a landmark New Orleans tribute album produced by Harold Battiste and Jerry Wexler. He also played organ, electric piano, and percussion on Tony Joe White’s The Train I’m On (Warner Bros., 1972), recorded at Muscle Shoals with producers Tom Dowd and Jerry Wexler. In 1974 he sang backing vocals on Gene Clark’s cult album No Other (Asylum Records, produced by Thomas Jefferson Kaye).

Session Career Peak (1974 – 1983)

After Better Days dissolved in 1974, Barron became a highly active session keyboardist across a wide stylistic range:

  • Ry Cooder — Paradise And Lunch (Reprise, 1974): piano and organ. Produced by Lenny Waronker and Russ Titelman.
  • B.B. King — Hollywood Be Thy Name (United Artists, 1975): organ.
  • John Mayall — A Banquet In Blues (ABC, 1976): vocals.
  • John Lee Hooker / Canned Heat — Canned Heat & John Lee Hooker Recorded Live At The Fox Venice Theatre (Rhino, 1981): piano. Barron is listed as a member of Canned Heat (Discogs ID: 269094) for this period.
  • Tom Waits — Heartattack And Vine (Asylum, 1980): Hammond organ on “In Shades,” “Saving All My Love For You,” and “Downtown Train.” Swordfishtrombones (Asylum, 1983): organ on track B2.
  • Eric Burdon — Wicked Man (GNP Crescendo, 1988): keyboards.

Additional session credits: Redbone — Wovoka / Burst (Epic, 1972): percussion. Kim Carnes — City Lights (Horizon, 1978): backing vocals. Unnamed film — Stony Island soundtrack (Glades, 1978): keyboards and vocals. John Mayall’s King Size (ABC, 1977): organ.

Japan Period and Later Work (mid-1970s – 1990s)

Barron had a notable parallel release presence in Japan through the Better Days (2) label (Discogs ID not confirmed — see Better Days (2)). Several Japanese-market solo or associated releases appeared:

  • The Lucky Old Sun (Better Days (2), Japan, 1977): a Japanese-market record crediting Barron as arranger, piano, marimba, strings, and vocals.
  • The Smile Of Life (Better Days (2), Japan, 1978): co-produced by Barron; he is credited as a primary artist.

His American solo discography includes:

  • Blue Delicacies (1979, Sunshine Records): solo album, primary credit.

In the 1980s–90s Barron shifted partly toward production work: he produced Kim Carnes or associated artists (see Another Woman In Love, CBS, 1987, and Baby I’m Yours, BMG, 1992; Christmas With Maureen McGovern, CBS, 1990), and continued session keyboard work with John Mayall (Power Company, TELDEC, 1983; Dixie Fever, Showboat, 1977), Eric Burdon (Wicked Man, GNP Crescendo, 1988), and others.

In 1988 he made a cameo as a bartender in the Steven Seagal film Above The Law.

Barron died on March 20, 1997.

New Orleans Connections

Barron’s New Orleans ties run through the Mac Rebennack orbit from the very beginning. Key figures in the New Orleans scene he worked with:

  • Mac Rebennack (Dr. John) — childhood collaborator; Morgus And The Ghouls, Drits & Dravy; co-wrote “Louisiana Flood” with him; played on Dr. John’s Gumbo (1972) and Gris-Gris (original sessions, credited on 1987 reissue)
  • Harold Battiste — produced Dr. John’s Gumbo; also co-arranged; central figure in the New Orleans/Los Angeles crossover (AFO label)
  • Shirley Goodman — fellow vocalist on Dr. John’s Gumbo and Gris-Gris
  • Leonard James — early New Orleans band connection; member of Morgus And The Ghouls
  • Tami Lynn — New Orleans vocalist; appeared alongside Barron on Dr. John’s Gumbo

Barron does not appear in the Discogs credits for Professor Longhair or Allen Toussaint productions, though his early New Orleans club years would have overlapped with that scene.
