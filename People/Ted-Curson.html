Ted Curson

Ted Curson

Aug 25, 20269 min read

Ted Curson

Discogs ID: 256582
Real name: Theodore Curson
Born: June 3, 1935, Philadelphia, PA
Died: November 4, 2012, Montclair, NJ
Instruments: Trumpet, flugelhorn, piccolo trumpet, pocket trumpet

American jazz trumpeter working in post-bop, hard bop, and free jazz idioms. One of the key sidemen on Charles Mingus’s landmark Candid sessions (1960–61), co-leader with Bill Barron across multiple albums, and a significant figure in the early-1960s avant-garde through his guest appearance with The New York Contemporary Five. Widely travelled in Europe from the mid-1960s onward, recording in Poland, France, Norway, Japan, and Czechoslovakia.

Biography

Early Life and Philadelphia Roots (1935–1955)

Theodore Curson grew up in Philadelphia and studied at the Granoff School of Music there. He made his first professional appearances in 1953 playing with an unnamed group (Discogs references artist ID 262140, likely a local Philadelphia ensemble). His formal training gave him grounding in the bebop idiom before he relocated to New York.

New York Arrival and Cecil Taylor (1956–1959)

Curson settled in New York in 1956. His first recording appearance came in 1959 on Cecil Taylor’s Love for Sale (United Artists Records), a session that also featured Bill Barron — the beginning of a partnership that would define much of Curson’s career. The session placed him immediately in the avant-garde orbit of New York jazz.

The Mingus Candid Sessions (1960–1961)

The pivotal moment in Curson’s early career was joining Charles Mingus’s working group around 1960. He appears on three major Candid albums from this period:

  • Mingus (Candid, 1961) — the core Candid quintet with Eric Dolphy, Dannie Richmond, and rotating pianists including Paul Bley
  • Charles Mingus Presents Charles Mingus (Candid, 1961) — the famous quartet recording with Dolphy, Richmond, and Mingus; Curson is one of four primary voices
  • Folk Forms, No. 1 (Candid, 1961) — further documentation of the working band

This association brought Curson wide notice and established him as a front-line voice in the most adventurous small-group jazz of the era. The Mingus connection also links him to the broader post-bop avant-garde: Jimmy Garrison, Roy Haynes, Pete La Roca, Kenny Drew, and Paul Bley all appear across these sessions. A 1960 live recording at the Newport Rebels (later issued on Candid) and the Mingus at Antibes recording (Atlantic, 1960; released 1976) further document this band.

The composition “Tears for Dolphy,” written by Curson as a tribute after Eric Dolphy’s death in 1964, became one of his signature pieces, giving its name to his 1965 album on Fontana.

First Primary-Artist Albums (1961–1964)

Curson began leading his own dates concurrently with the Mingus work:

  • Plenty of Horn (Old Town Records, 1961) — debut as primary leader; band includes Bill Barron (tenor), Eric Dolphy (flute on selected tracks), Roy Haynes (drums), Dannie Richmond (drums on other tracks), Pete La Roca (drums), Jimmy Garrison (bass), Kenny Drew (piano). Liner notes by Nat Hentoff.
  • Fire Down Below / Plays Fire Down Below (Prestige, 1963) — recorded with Roy Haynes (drums), Gildo Mahones (piano), George Tucker (bass), Montego Joe (congas on selected tracks). Produced by Ozzie Cadena, engineered by Rudy Van Gelder at Van Gelder Studio.
  • Now, Hear This! (Audio Fidelity, 1964) — co-led with Bill Barron; band includes Kenny Barron (piano), Dick Berk (drums), Ronnie Boykins (bass)

The New York Contemporary Five (1964)

In 1964 Curson appeared as a guest trumpeter on the The New York Contemporary Five side of the Savoy split LP Bill Dixon 7-Tette / Archie Shepp and the New York Contemporary 5. The NYCF was led by Archie Shepp and included Don Cherry (regular trumpet), John Tchicai (alto saxophone), Ronnie Boykins (bass), and Sunny Murray (drums). Curson’s participation on that session’s B-side tracks places him at the intersection of free jazz and the avant-garde generation just below Ornette Coleman’s. The other side of the LP, led by Bill Dixon, shows how interlocked that New York circle was.

He also appears on Archie Shepp’s Fire Music (Impulse!, 1965) and Cecil Taylor’s Into the Hot (Impulse!, 1961/1987 reissue).

Atlantic Album and European Departure (1965–1966)

  • The New Thing & The Blue Thing (Atlantic, 1965) — the most widely distributed US album of Curson’s primary-artist run; produced under Arif Mardin, engineered by Tom Dowd; band includes Bill Barron (tenor), Georges Arvanitas (piano), Herb Bushler (bass), Dick Berk (drums). Liner notes by Nat Hentoff. The title signals the album’s two-sided commitment: free jazz (“the new thing”) alongside more mainstream blues-rooted material (“the blue thing”).
  • Tears for Dolphy (Fontana, 1965, recorded Netherlands) — the tribute to Eric Dolphy; produced by Alan Bates and Jef Gilson; Curson is credited on compositions, pocket trumpet, and standard trumpet alongside Bill Barron (tenor/clarinet), Herb Bushler (bass), Dick Berk (drums).

From the mid-1960s Curson increasingly worked in Europe. He settled for a period in Denmark and performed with Polish pianist Andrzej Trzaskowski for approximately two years.

European Years — Poland, France, Norway, Japan (1967–1979)

Curson’s European output is substantial:

  • Seant (Polskie Nagrania Muza, 1967, Poland) — recorded with the The Andrzej Trzaskowski Sextet; personnel includes Andrzej Trzaskowski (piano), Włodzimierz Nahorny, Janusz Muniak, and others from the Polish jazz scene
  • Teorema soundtrack (1968) — Curson reportedly contributed music to Pier Paolo Pasolini’s film but Ennio Morricone received sole credit
  • Break of Day in Molde (Sonet, 1969, Norway)
  • Ode to Booker Ervin (Columbia, 1970, Finland)
  • Pop Wine (Futura Records, 1971, France) — with Georges Arvanitas trio (Arvanitas on piano, Jacky Samson bass, Charles Saudrais drums); produced by Gérard Terronès
  • Cattin’ Curson (Marge, 1975, France) — again with Arvanitas/Samson/Saudrais
  • Blue Piccolo (Whynot, 1976, Japan) — with Jim McNeely (piano), Cecil McBee (bass), Steve McCall (drums); produced by Masahiko Yuh; notable for Curson’s extensive use of piccolo trumpet and flugelhorn

He performed annually at the Pori Jazz Festival in Finland from 1966 onward; at the request of Finnish President Tarja Halonen, he performed at Finland’s Independence Day Ball in 2007.

Arista/Freedom and the Flip Top Sessions (1977)

  • Flip Top (Arista/Freedom, 1977) — US release on Arista sub-label Freedom; two distinct sides: a jazz quartet set (Curson, Bill Barron, Herb Bushler bass, Dick Berk drums) and an orchestral side with the Zagreb Radio Orchestra (Simfonijski Orkestar HRT-a) conducted by Miljenko Prohaska, arranged by Zita Carno. Produced by Alan Bates; US coordination by Michael Cuscuna and Steve Backer.

Later Career — Interplay and Final Recordings (1978–2012)

Curson recorded several albums for Interplay Records in the late 1970s–80s:

  • Blowin’ Away (Interplay, 1978)
  • The Trio (Interplay, 1979)
  • I Heard Mingus (Interplay, 1980) — tribute to Mingus with Jim McNeely (piano), Ryo Kawasaki (guitar), Adam Nussbaum (drums), Mike Richmond (bass)
  • Contemporation and Jazz (Panton, 1984, Czechoslovakia)
  • Hoarded Dreams (Cuneiform Records, 2007)
  • Joy (Meantime Records, 2008, Norway)

Late-career live recordings include In Paris – Live at the Sunside and Live in Paris (both released posthumously on Sinner Lady Gloria, 2015).

Connections

  • Charles Mingus — employed Curson in his working band 1960–61; Candid sessions are the most documented period of their collaboration
  • Eric Dolphy — co-sideman on Mingus dates; Dolphy’s death in 1964 prompted Curson’s composition “Tears for Dolphy”
  • Bill Barron — the most sustained partnership in Curson’s career, from the Cecil Taylor 1959 session through the late 1970s Arista/Freedom albums
  • Archie Shepp / The New York Contemporary Five — guest appearance on 1964 Savoy LP links Curson to the Shepp/Tchicai/Cherry circle
  • Don Cherry — both appeared on the NYCF side of the 1964 Savoy LP; Cherry was the group’s regular trumpeter
  • Andrzej Trzaskowski — Polish pianist with whom Curson performed for approximately two years in the mid-1960s; Seant (1967) is the main document
  • Georges Arvanitas — French pianist; recurring collaborator on Paris recordings 1965–75
  • Ennio Morricone — Curson reportedly wrote music for the Teorema soundtrack (1968) but Morricone received sole credit; relationship unclear
  • Alan Bates — produced Tears for Dolphy (Fontana, 1965) and Flip Top (Arista/Freedom, 1977)
  • Nat Hentoff — wrote liner notes on multiple Curson albums and supervised the Candid Mingus sessions
