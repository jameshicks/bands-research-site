Overview

Greta Simone Kline is a New York City-born guitarist, bassist, and singer (b. 1994), best known as the central figure in Frankie Cosmos. She is the daughter of actors Kevin Kline and Phoebe Cates. Her recorded work stretches back to 2011 and spans lo-fi indie pop and acoustic indie rock, initially through Double Double Whammy, Bayonet Records, and a cluster of smaller DIY imprints before Frankie Cosmos moved to Sub Pop in 2018, where the band has remained through at least 2024. Beyond Frankie Cosmos she is also a member of Lexie (5) and Porches (3), and her closest studio collaborators across the catalogue include engineer Josh Bonati and Aaron Maine of Porches.

Notes (web)

Sub Pop signing (2017)

Frankie Cosmos announced the Sub Pop signing on 12 April 2017 alongside spring/summer tour dates. The Sub Pop announcement described it as “a universe-spanning type of record deal” and cited the band’s critical success on Next Thing (2016, Bayonet Records) as background, but gave no explanation for the label move. subpop.com announcement, 2017-04-12

Kline discussed the Sub Pop relationship in a November 2018 interview with the Seattle Weekly. Key quotes:

  • On why Sub Pop appealed: “Sub Pop has a really personable staff. It doesn’t feel like working with a big label.”
  • On the timing: recording on Vessel had already begun before the deal was formalised — “We’d been talking to them, but it wasn’t official when we were working on it. So we just made the album and sent it to them.”
  • On creative autonomy: “They had no changes to make artistically, they just let us do what we wanted to do.”

The picture is one of an organic relationship that grew during the making of Vessel (released March 2018) rather than a formal label switch negotiated upfront. Kline characterised the arrangement as allowing the band to maintain its DIY approach while gaining professional infrastructure. seattleweekly.com, 2018-11-07

A 2019 WLUW radio interview corroborates the resources framing: “Sub Pop is really great, and they have a lot of really great resources that help us.” Kline contrasted it with her earlier DIY approach of handling all merchandise and distribution herself. wluw.org, 2019-09-28

No source located (across the Sub Pop announcement, Paste, Tiny Mix Tapes, Bandcamp Daily, LPM, Northern Transmissions, Stereogum, Believer Magazine, The Thin Air, Brighton’s Finest, Under the Radar, Rolling Stone, and WLUW) explains what specifically prompted the departure from Bayonet Records — only the appeal of Sub Pop’s staff, hands-off approach, and professional resources is documented. The picture that emerges is of an organic progression: Next Thing (2016) built critical momentum, Kline began talking to Sub Pop informally during the making of Vessel, and the deal was formalised around April 2017 before the album was complete.
