Clive Gregson

Real name: Clive James Gregson Born: January 4, 1955 — Manchester, UK Discogs artist ID: 277276 Website: clivegregson.com

British singer-songwriter, guitarist, multi-instrumentalist, and producer. Co-founder and lead voice of Any Trouble (Stiff/EMI America, 1979–84). From the mid-1980s built a second career as a session musician and producer in the British folk/roots world, most visibly as half of the duo Clive Gregson And Christine Collister and as producer of Oysterband’s formative Cooking Vinyl recordings. Also a touring member of the Richard Thompson Band and later of Plainsong.

Biography

Manchester and early recordings (1975–1979)

Gregson formed Any Trouble in Macclesfield around 1978 with guitarist Chris Parks (2) and drummer Mel Harley. Before the major-label deal, the band self-released Yesterday’s Love (1979, Pennine Records) — Gregson already credited as vocalist, guitarist, and songwriter. He also produced and played on Rosemary Lane (Pennine Records, 1979), an early demonstration of the producer-performer dual role he would maintain throughout his career.

Any Trouble and Stiff Records (1979–1984)

Any Trouble signed to Stiff Records and released three LPs: Where Are All The Nice Girls? (1980), Wheels In Motion (1981), and Wrong End Of The Race (1984), the last two on EMI America. A fourth, Touch And Go (1982), was released as a US-only EMI America album. The band’s sound was a compact British power pop/new wave — melodic guitar rock with strong hooks and Gregson’s lead vocals and Telecaster at the centre.

Key personnel across the Any Trouble albums:

  • Chris Parks (2) — guitar, backing vocals
  • Martin Hughes — drums, percussion
  • Phil Barnes (3) — bass, saxophone
  • Steve Gurl — keyboards (later albums)

Producers varied: Where Are All The Nice Girls? (1980) was produced by John Wood with Paul Adshead engineering; Wheels In Motion (1981) was produced by Mike Howlett; Wrong End Of The Race (1984) by John Wood and Will Birch.

Gregson contributed backing vocals to Richard Thompson sessions during this period: Shoot Out The Lights (Hannibal, 1982, with Richard & Linda Thompson) and Hand Of Kindness (Hannibal, 1983).

The band reunited in 2007 for Life In Reverse (Blue Rose Records).

Strange Persuasions and the transition (1985)

With Any Trouble wound down, Gregson released his first solo album Strange Persuasions (Demon Records, 1985) — all instruments, vocals, and songwriting credited to himself, engineered by Paul Adshead (a collaborator throughout the next decade). Self-produced with minimal session help, it established the intimate singer-songwriter mode he would pursue alongside the duo work.

He also appeared on Richard Thompson’s Across A Crowded Room (Polydor, 1985) as backing vocalist — alongside Christine Collister and Phil Barnes — which appears to be where the Gregson/Collister partnership began crystallising.

Clive Gregson And Christine Collister (1986–1992)

The duo Clive Gregson And Christine Collister (Discogs group ID: 4397914) released four studio albums on Special Delivery (a sub-division of Topic Records):

Home And Away was co-recorded with Frank Kornelussen and Jamie Marshall (2); mixed by Mick Brophy. Mischief added second guitarist Howard Lees and was recorded by Dave Kenny. A Change In The Weather brought back Paul Adshead as engineer and Phil Barnes (3) on bass and saxophone.

The duo also appeared on the Richard Thompson live circuit; both Gregson and Collister were members of the Richard Thompson Band (Discogs group ID: 2791506, also including Danny Thompson, Pete Zorn, Gerry Conway, Rory McFarlane).

Oysterband productions (1986–1987)

Gregson’s most widely cited production work: the first three Oysterband releases on Cooking Vinyl, establishing the band’s recorded sound at the label’s launch.

The 1996 Nashville album I Love This Town brought in a full session cast: banjo player Alison Brown, bassist Garry West, pianist John R. Burr, and vocalist Dennis Locorriere among others; mastered by Glenn Meadows. The Compass-era records were also mastered by Meadows.

The 1993 release Wonderful Lie (Haven Records) lists co-billing with Boo Hewerdine and Eddi Reader, produced by Jon Kelly.

Plainsong and later years (2000s–2010s)

Gregson joined Plainsong (Discogs group ID: 306670), the UK folk-rock group previously associated with Iain Matthews, Andy Roberts (3), and Julian Dawson. Plainsong credits on Discogs include Gregson as a member alongside Iain Matthews, Andy Roberts, Dave Richards, Bob Ronga, Mark Griffiths, and Julian Dawson.

His later solo albums included Bittersweet (Fullfill, 2011), released on his own imprint Gregsongs. He contributed to compilation and tribute releases and appeared on a Strings Attached concert record (Gregsongs, 2011).

He contributed guitar and organ to Richard Thompson’s ongoing touring band, appearing on Rumor And Sigh (Capitol, 1991) as backing vocalist and on Clock Without Hands (Elektra, 2001) as electric guitarist.

He is also a member of 3 Boxes (Discogs group ID: 3660774), The Guilty Men (ID: 17366542), and Tickawinda (ID: 2176719).
