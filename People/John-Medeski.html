John Medeski

Born: June 28, 1965, Louisville, Kentucky
Real name: Anthony John Medeski
Role: Keyboards, piano, organ, composer
Discogs artist ID: 253996

American keyboardist and composer. Core member of Medeski Martin & Wood (with Billy Martin (2) and Chris Wood (3)). Based in New York City. A central figure in the NYC avant-jazz and jazz-funk scenes from the early 1990s onward. Known for a sprawling instrumental palette — Hammond B-3, Wurlitzer, clavinet, Moog, Rhodes, melodica, mellotron — deployed across jazz, funk, avant-garde, and sacred steel contexts. Extensive sideman history spanning David Byrne, John Scofield, Marc Ribot, and the jazz-meets-jam-band crossover of the late 1990s.

Biography

Early career (1985–1991)

Medeski’s earliest Discogs credit is on Treaty (1985, Jazzbeat Records), a piano credit. By 1990 he appears on The Half-Life Of Desire (Accurate Records) on piano, organ, and synthesizer. He was a member of Orchestra in the late 1980s–early 1990s, as well as early associations with Nova Express (7) / Nova Express Quintet and the Bill Lowe - Philippe Crettien Quintet.

Medeski Martin & Wood (1991–present)

Medeski, Billy Martin (2), and Chris Wood (3) formed Medeski Martin & Wood (MMW) in 1991. The trio emerged from Boston’s avant-jazz scene and quickly relocated to New York. Their early albums on Gramavision — Notes From The Underground (1992), It’s A Jungle In Here (1993), Friday Afternoon In The Universe (1995), Shack-man (1996) — established their blend of funk, jazz, and New Orleans rhythms. It’s A Jungle In Here already featured Marc Ribot on guitar and horns by Steven Bernstein and Josh Roseman.

The band signed to Blue Note for Combustication (1998), their commercial breakthrough, produced with David Baker and Scott Harding. The Blue Note run continued through The Dropper (2000, with Marc Ribot on guitar on three tracks), Uninvisible (2002), and End Of The World Party (Just In Case) (2004). In the late 2000s they moved to Indirecto Records for the Radiolarians trilogy (2008–2009).

MMW also released Farmer’s Reserve (1997) on Indirecto, Tonic (2000, Blue Note, live), Free Magic (2012, Indirecto), and Let’s Go Everywhere (2008, Little Monster Records, children’s album).

Surrender To The Air (1996)

Medeski was part of the large free-improvisation collective Surrender To The Air, which released one album on Elektra (1996). The ensemble included Marc Ribot, Trey Anastasio, Marshall Allen, Oteil Burbridge, Kofi Burbridge, Jonathan Fishman, and others — a rare merging of the downtown avant-garde and jam-band worlds.

Sideman work and collaborations (1994–2006)

Medeski’s session credits span a wide arc. Key appearances:

  • David Byrne — organ and Farfisa on Byrne’s self-titled 1994 album (Luaka Bop / Sire / Warner Bros.)
  • John Lurie / Marvin Pontiac — keyboards on The Legendary Marvin Pontiac: Greatest Hits (1999, Strange & Beautiful Music) alongside Marc Ribot, Billy Martin (2), G. Calvin Weston, Tony Scherr
  • Bar Kokhba — organ and piano on John Zorn’s Bar Kokhba (1996, Tzadik)
  • John Scofield — organ, clavinet, piano, Wurlitzer on Scofield’s A Go Go (1998, Verve Records) and Überjam (2002, Verve)
  • Get Shorty soundtrack — organ and piano (1995, Antilles), alongside John Lurie’s score
  • Cibo Matto — clavinet on Stereo Type A (1999, Warner Bros.)
  • Susana Baca — piano, organ, melodica on Eco De Sombras (2000, Luaka Bop) and Espíritu Vivo (2002, Luaka Bop)
  • Sex Mob — organ on Sex Mob Does Bond (2001, Ropeadope) and Stray Dog (2001, Ropeadope)
  • The Word (5) — keyboards and co-producer on the gospel-funk supergroup’s debut (Ropeadope, 2001), with Robert Randolph, Luther Dickinson, Cody Dickinson, and Chris Chew
  • Ray LaMontagne — organ, electric piano, mellotron, harmonium on Washington Square Serenade (2007, New West Records)
  • Ry Cooder — keyboards on Chavez Ravine (cited separately) and on I, Flathead (2008, Nonesuch)
  • Coheed and Cambria — piano, clavinet, synthesizer on The Afterman: Ascension and The Afterman: Descension (2012, Everything Evil Records)

Medeski Scofield Martin & Wood (2006–2014)

Medeski joined John Scofield alongside Billy Martin and Chris Wood as Medeski Scofield Martin & Wood (MSMW). Key releases: Out Louder (2006, Indirecto), Live: In Case The World Changes Its Mind (2011, Indirecto), Juice (2014, Okeh).

Solo work (2007–2019)

Medeski has released several solo records:

  • Altitude (2007, Thirsty Ear) — solo organ, electric piano, synthesizer, mellotron
  • Heaven On Earth (2009, Half Note Records) — Hammond B-3
  • Live In Willisau 2006 (2009, Thirsty Ear)
  • A Different Time (2013, Okeh) — trio format with piano
  • Mellotron Variations (2019, Spaceflight Records) — solo mellotron album

He also appeared on Marian McPartland’s Piano Jazz Radio Broadcast With John Medeski (2006, The Jazz Alliance).

Hudson (2017)

Medeski is a founding member of the supergroup Hudson (10) alongside Jack DeJohnette (drums/vocals), Larry Grenadier (bass/vocals), and John Scofield (guitar/vocals). Their self-titled debut Hudson (Motéma, 2017) engages American roots traditions; all four members sing and co-produced. Medeski is credited on piano, Rhodes, Hammond B-3, wooden flute, and vocals.

Other ensembles

  • Spectrum Road (2012) — tribute to Tony Williams Lifetime; one album on Palmetto Records
  • The Word (5) (2001) — gospel-funk supergroup
  • The Million Dollar Bashers — Dylan tribute ensemble for the I’m Not There film soundtrack (2007)
  • Chaos Magick — active 2010s
  • Simulacrum (3) (2015) — one album on Tzadik
  • John Medeski’s Mad Skillet (2018) — one album on Indirecto Records
