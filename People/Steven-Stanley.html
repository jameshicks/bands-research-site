Steven Stanley

Steven J. C. Stanley (b. July 11, 1958, Jamaica) is a Jamaican audio engineer, record producer, and keyboardist who has worked across reggae, dub, and rock since 1975. He is most closely associated with Tom Tom Club, Sly Dunbar and Robbie Shakespeare’s Taxi/Compass Point network, and Black Uhuru. His studio — Mixing Lab, Kingston — became a hub for Jamaican dancehall and reggae production through the late 1980s and 1990s. On Discogs he is credited under a wide range of name variations including Steve Stanley, Youthsound Productions, and Youth Sound; he is also listed as a member of Tom Tom Club.

Discogs artist ID: 15826 | Realname: Steven J. C. Stanley

Overview

Stanley began his engineering career in Jamaica in the mid-1970s, cutting his teeth on sessions for artists including Ernest Ranglin (Ranglin Roots, 1976), Third World (Journey To Addis, 1978), and Black Uhuru (Red, 1981). His reputation expanded internationally when he crossed into the Compass Point Studios circle in Nassau, Bahamas — Chris Blackwell’s facility that hosted Talking Heads, Grace Jones, Ian Dury, Serge Gainsbourg, and others. Stanley engineered and produced the self-titled Tom Tom Club debut (1981) — one of the best-selling records of that circle — alongside co-producers Chris Frantz and Tina Weymouth. He was credited as engineer, mixer, and co-producer on a string of Compass Point-era records.

Through the 1980s he moved fluidly between uptown international rock sessions and Kingston dancehall. He is the chief engineer behind most of the Harmony House and Penthouse Records catalog of the early 1990s, and his studio Mixing Lab appears as a credit on hundreds of Jamaican releases. He also performed keyboards and synthesizer on many of the records he engineered.

Research Queue

  • The “Zasu Pitts Memorial Orchestra” alias on Discogs has no linked artist ID — what was this project? Was it a band Stanley formed or a one-off recording name?
  • Stanley is listed as a member of Tom Tom Club on Discogs — was this a formal credit (he toured with them) or simply a studio-era relationship? Sleeve notes for Tom Tom Club and Close To The Bone may clarify.
  • …Yes Please! by Happy Mondays (Factory, 1992) lists Stanley as mixer — how did this connection come about? Was it brokered by Factory or a shared management link?
  • Remain In Light credits Stanley as “Additional Engineering” — which specific sessions or tracks did he contribute to? The primary engineer Dave Jerden is well-documented; Stanley’s portion is unspecified.
  • Lord Upminster (Ian Dury, 1981) credits Stanley as co-producer alongside Dury and Chas Jankel — was the Nassau session the full record or only select tracks? The Compass Point recording history of this LP is not well documented.
