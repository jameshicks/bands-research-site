Curtis Lundy

American jazz double bassist, composer, arranger, and producer. Born 1 October 1955 in Miami, Florida. Brother of singer Carmen Lundy. Discogs artist ID: 349804.

Name variations on record: C. Lundy, Curtis Lindi, Curtis Lundi, Lundy.

Career Overview

Curtis Lundy emerged from the New York jazz circuit in the late 1970s, quickly becoming one of the most reliably employed double bassists in hard-bop and post-bop settings. His earliest major credit is the 1978 recording Estimated Time Of Arrival (Roulette), but his career landmark came almost immediately afterward: he was the bassist on Betty Carter’s celebrated live double album The Audience With Betty Carter (1980, Bet-Car Records), recorded at The Great American Music Hall with pianist John Hicks and drummer Kenny Washington. That trio — Lundy, Hicks, and Washington — became one of the tightest working rhythm sections in contemporary jazz, a fact confirmed by his reappearance on Carter’s next studio album Whatever Happened To Love? (1982, Bet-Car Records).

By 1983 Lundy was co-leading his first recordings. Beatitudes (New Note, 1983), co-led with alto saxophonist Bobby Watson, was recorded at Rudy Van Gelder’s studio, with Mulgrew Miller and Kenny Washington rounding out the rhythm section. He co-produced the date alongside Watson — an early sign of his ambitions behind the board. That same year he appeared on a Belgian session, Steve Houben & Michel Herr Meet Curtis Lundy & Kenny Washington (Jazz Cats, 1983), cementing the Lundy/Washington pairing as an exportable unit.

Through the mid-1980s he ranged across European and American sessions: the Bobby Watson ensemble Gumbo (Amigo, 1985, Sweden), Good Morning Kiss (BlackHawk/Bellaphon, 1985, Germany), and a significant live document, Live · Jazzbühne Berlin ‘84 (AMIGA, 1984, GDR). He had become a first-call bassist for touring jazz groups passing through Europe as well as New York.

The signature achievement of this period was Africa (Timeless, 1987, Netherlands), a co-leader date with Pharoah Sanders, John Hicks, and drummer Idris Muhammad. Lundy is credited as a primary artist on this widely distributed recording, which appeared in nineteen pressings across multiple territories. The same year saw his first solo leader date, Just Be Yourself (New Note, 1987) — a quintet session produced by Lundy himself, with Bobby Watson (alto), Hank Jones (piano), Kenny Washington (drums), and Steve Nelson (vibes), plus his sister Carmen Lundy on vocals.

The late 1980s brought further sideman work with a range of major voices: Johnny Griffin (I’ll Give You Something To Remember Me By, Limetree, 1988), Bobby Watson (Blue Note, No Question About It, 1988), and two DIW sessions in Japan — Naima’s Love Song (1988) and East Side Blues (1988). Italian label Red Record also captured him on multiple sessions (Love Remains 1987; Live Session One and Live Session Two, 1990).

In 1997 Lundy appeared on Lewis, Watson, Bergonzi, Barron, Fink, Lundy (e.f.s.a. collection), a collective summit with drummer Victor Lewis, Bobby Watson, Jerry Bergonzi, Kenny Barron, and David Finck — the collaboration connecting him to Victor Lewis that features in several vault cross-references. That same year he played bass on Frank Morgan’s Bop! (Telarc, 1997) with the Rodney Kendrick Trio.

His second solo leader date, Against All Odds (Justin Time, 1999, Canada), assembled an ambitious cast: Bobby Watson and a young Roy Hargrove on trumpet, John Hicks and Anthony Wonsey splitting piano duties, Winard Harper on drums, and Carmen Lundy on vocals. Justin Time also issued two volumes of Vietnam: The Aftermath (2001) and Vietnam: Reflections (2005), projects in which Lundy appeared in a bass and choir-direction capacity.

From the early 2000s onward Lundy increasingly produced for his sister Carmen, co-producing and playing bass on This Is Carmen Lundy (Afrasia Productions, 2001) alongside Bobby Watson, Victor Lewis, and Ralph Peterson, and on Jazz And The New Songbook: Live At The Madrid (Afrasia Productions, 2005). He continued appearing as a sideman with Bobby Watson into the late 2000s — Watson’s From The Heart (Palmetto, 2007) features him alongside Harold O’Neal on piano and Leron Thomas on trumpet.

A late-career highlight is his appearance on the Lewis, Watson, Bergonzi, Barron, Fink, Lundy collective recording and the Mark Murphy tribute A Beautiful Friendship: Remembering Shirley Horn (Gearbox Records, 2013). In 2014 he appeared on The Colors Of Rhythm (Impulse!/Universal Music France). A live document of the Betty Carter trio from 1980 appeared belatedly as Live At Fabrik Hamburg 1980 (Jazzline, 2023), adding a further archival dimension to his discography.

Lundy is also a songwriter: he holds a “Written-By” credit on the 2006 Capitol Records hip-hop release This Is Why I’m Hot by MIMS, an unexpected crossover link.
