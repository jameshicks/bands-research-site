Jaki Liebezeit

Born Hans Heinrich Liebezeit, 26 May 1938, near Dresden, Germany; died 22 January 2017, Cologne, from pneumonia. Discogs artist ID: 48582

Drummer; one of the defining rhythmic voices in post-war German music. Best known as the founding drummer of Can, with whom he played from 1968 to 1978 and again at the 1986–1989 reunion. His locked, metronomic style — informed by jazz and non-Western percussion but executed with industrial precision — became a touchstone for post-punk, dance music, and experimental rock producers worldwide. After Can he sustained an unusually broad career as a session and collaborative drummer, working across krautrock, new wave, electronic, and world-music contexts until shortly before his death.

Biography

Early Career: Jazz and the German Avant-Garde (1960s)

Liebezeit trained as a jazz drummer and was active in West Germany’s free-jazz scene through the mid-1960s. His earliest credited recordings place him in serious company: he appears on the self-titled debut of the Manfred Schoof Sextett (Wergo, 1967), and on the landmark Globe Unity album (SABA, 1967) with the Globe Unity Orchestra — credited “Drums [Left Channel].” Both sessions connected him to the Cologne new-music world. He also drummed on a 1965 Schwann AMS Studio session and appeared on the CBS-Germany album Voices (1966).

On the 1967 Manfred Schoof Sextett Wergo LP he is credited with drums, flute, and a range of percussion instruments (schwirrholz, miniglocke, große kuhglocke, gestrichenes becken), illustrating the flexibility he brought beyond straightforward kit playing. He also appeared on Die Befristeten (Wergo, 1967), performing alongside composers in an opera/improvisation context.

Can: Formation and Core Years (1968–1978)

In 1968 Liebezeit became the drummer of the newly formed Inner Space, which soon became Can. The group formed in Cologne around keyboardist Irmin Schmidt, bassist/sound-sculptor Holger Czukay, guitarist Michael Karoli, and initially vocalist Malcolm Mooney (replaced from 1970 by Damo Suzuki). Liebezeit was central to the group’s sonic identity from the outset.

The Can catalog was originally issued on United Artists (1969–74), then Virgin (1975–76), then EMI Electrola/Harvest (1977–78). Can’s own archive label Spoon Records has managed reissues and archival releases since 1981, releasing compilations (Cannibalism, 1981; Delay 1968, 1981), rarities, and later the landmark The Lost Tapes (2012) and ongoing series of authorized live recordings.

Liebezeit developed what he called a “non-drumming” approach — eliminating fills and ego-display in favor of relentless, locomotive grooves that functioned as the structural spine of extended collective improvisations. His style was notably different from European free-jazz drumming: less reactive, more architectural.

Post-Can: Phantom Band and Dunkelziffer (1979–1985)

After Can’s initial dissolution Liebezeit remained active in Cologne’s music scene. He formed or joined several projects with Can-adjacent musicians.

Phantom Band (Discogs ID: 285471) formed around Liebezeit, guitarist Dominik Von Senger, keyboardist Helmut Zerlett, bassist/vocalist Rosko Gee (who had joined Can in 1977), and percussionist Olek Gelba. The band released two albums on Sky Records: Phantom Band (1980, mixed by Conny Plank and Holger Czukay) and Freedom Of Speech (1981). A third LP, Nowhere, appeared on Spoon Records in 1984. The debut is the most significant: Conny Plank’s mixing and Czukay’s occasional horn contributions tie it firmly to the Cologne network.

Dunkelziffer (Discogs ID: 372658) — a larger ensemble including Liebezeit, Damo Suzuki, Dominik Von Senger, Helmut Zerlett, Olek Gelba, and others — was based in Cologne and operated across the 1980s–2000s. Liebezeit appeared as a guest bongo player on their Colours And Soul (FünfUndVierzig, 1983) rather than as a full member. The band’s regular rhythm section included Stefan Krachten.

During this period he also appeared as session drummer on a wide range of recordings:

  • Eurythmics — In The Garden (RCA, 1981) and single Never Gonna Cry Again (1981), the band’s debut album, produced by Conny Plank
  • Phew (Japanese vocalist) — Phew (Pass Records, Japan, 1981), co-produced with Holger Czukay and Conny Plank; Liebezeit co-wrote the music and co-produced
  • Michael Rother — Flammende Herzen (Sky Records, 1977; reissued Bureau B, BB36, 2009) — credited uncredited on the Bureau B reissue

”Snake Charmer” and the Post-Punk Crossover (1983)

The mini-LP Snake Charmer (Island Records, 1983) by Jah Wobble – The Edge – Holger Czukay is a key international crossover moment. Liebezeit co-wrote track B1 alongside Czukay, Jah Wobble, Neville Murray, and Ollie Marland. Arthur Russell contributed lyrics to track A2, “Hold On To Your Dreams.” The release was produced by François Kevorkian. Jah Wobble’s touring band, Jah Wobble’s Invaders Of The Heart, performed on the record, and Liebezeit later joined that group as a member.

Brian Eno and the International Session Circuit (1978)

Liebezeit appeared on Brian Eno’s Before And After Science (Island Records, 1978) — drumming on track A2 alongside a cast including Phil Collins, Fred Frith, Cluster members Moebius and Roedelius, and Percy Jones. This connected him to Eno’s broad German network during the Berlin period.

Can Reunion: Rite Time (1986–1989)

In 1986 Can’s core members — Czukay, Schmidt, Karoli, Mooney, and Liebezeit — reformed. The reunion album Rite Time (Mercury, 1989) reunited the classic lineup with Malcolm Mooney on vocals. Liebezeit is credited Drums, Percussion.

Phew: Our Likeness (1992)

Liebezeit’s second collaboration with Japanese vocalist Phew, Our Likeness (Mute, 1992), was recorded with Alexander Hacke and Chrislo Haas (both Einstürzende Neubauten) and Liebezeit contributed drums/percussion on most tracks plus music co-composition credits.

Jah Wobble Collaborations (1990s)

As a member of Jah Wobble’s Invaders Of The Heart, Liebezeit appears on Take Me To God (Island Records, 1994) and Spinner (All Saints, 1995 — a Eno/Wobble collaboration). He is also listed as a member of the related ensemble Jah Wobble’s Solaris.

Holger Czukay Solo Projects

Liebezeit collaborated repeatedly with Czukay on solo work: On The Way To The Peak Of Normal (EMI, 1981); Der Osten Ist Rot (Virgin, 1984, where he played drums, harmonium, trumpet, piano, and organ); Rome Remains Rome (Virgin, 1987); and Radio Wave Surfer (Virgin, 1991), on which he co-composed the music with Czukay and Karoli.

Burnt Friedman Collaboration (2002–2020)

Liebezeit’s longest post-Can collaborative project was the duo Burnt Friedman & Jaki Liebezeit (Discogs ID: 31174) with electronic musician Bernd Friedmann (aka Burnt Friedman). The project ran across multiple albums on Friedmann’s Nonplace label:

Drums Off Chaos (2001–2018)

Drums Off Chaos (Discogs ID: 372672) was a Cologne-based percussion ensemble co-led by Liebezeit alongside Maf Retter, Manos Tsangaris, Olek Gelba, and Reiner Linke. Their discography includes Baum (Maria de Alvear World Edition, 2001), Drums Off Chaos & Jens-Uwe Beyer (Magazine, 2011), and releases on Nonplace (Compass 2017, Centre 2018, both recorded with Burnt Friedman). The group continued after Liebezeit’s death.

Late Career: Robert Coyne and Final Projects (2013–2017)

Late-career active collaborations included:

  • Robert Coyne & Jaki Liebezeit — The Obscure Department (Meyer Records, 2013), Golden Arc (Meyer Records, 2014), I Still Have This Dream (Meyer Records, 2016)
  • Akşak (Staubgold, 2015)
  • Flut (Klangbad, 2014)
  • B.I.L.L. (ID 2181717) and Cyclopean (ID 3051769) — later electronic projects

He appeared as interviewee on Romantic Warriors IV: Krautrock Part 1 (Zeitgeist Media, 2019) — a posthumous release from a documentary.

Death

Jaki Liebezeit died on 22 January 2017 in Cologne from pneumonia, aged 78.

Name Note

Discogs lists his realname as “Hans Heinrich Liebezeit”; the Can Discogs bio gives “Hans Liebezeit, born near Dresden.” Common biographical usage (and the original stub) gives “Jakob Liebezeit.” All three names circulate; “Jaki” was the stage name used throughout his career.
