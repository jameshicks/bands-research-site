Efrim Menuck

Full name: Efrim Manuel Menuck Discogs ID: 343914 Born: November 4, 1970, Montreal, Quebec, Canada Active: 1994–present Roles: guitarist, vocalist, composer, producer, engineer, recording artist

Co-founder and central voice of Godspeed You Black Emperor! and founding member of A Silver Mt. Zion (operating under numerous name variations). Also records solo under his own name and is co-owner of the Hotel2Tango recording studio in Montreal. Deeply embedded in the Constellation Records roster as both artist and de facto in-house engineer/recordist for a wide range of Constellation releases across the 2000s and 2010s.

Biography

Early Montreal scene / Godspeed (1994–2002)

Efrim Manuel Menuck was born in Montreal in 1970. He was a founding member of Godspeed You Black Emperor! and appears on every record the band issued in its first run.

All Lights Fucked On The Hairy Amp Drooling (1994 cassette)

The earliest documented GY!BE recording is the 1994 cassette All Lights Fucked On The Hairy Amp Drooling, a solo effort credited to Menuck under the name Godspeed You! Black Emperor. Menuck created the 70-minute, 27-track work while a college student studying film, recording late at night after drinking at a Montreal dive bar called the Miami, then refining ideas the following afternoon. Equipment: a TASCAM Portastudio, Hagstrom III guitar, cheap Yorkville amplifier, and a broken Roland Space Echo he repaired himself. (Bandcamp Daily, 2022)

The band on the cassette was not yet the large ensemble: at that point the group consisted only of Menuck, Mauro Pezzente (minor bass contributions), Dano LeBlanc (acoustic guitar), and a then-partner credited as “D.C.” (vocals on one track). (Wikipedia, as of 2025-05-17)

Copies: Released in two editions totalling 33 cassettes (December 1994). The first dozen were given away; 20 more were sold at a Moncton show. Menuck later said the limited run was “because that’s what I could afford” owing to the expense of handmade inserts. (Bandcamp Daily, 2022)

Sound: Described as a “grayscale kaleidoscope” mixing slow riffs, fast punk passages, instrumental pieces, and manipulated samples — including a warped recorded conversation with his mother. Menuck himself said it “bears little relation to the band’s later work.” The 2022 reissue condensed the 27 tracks into four sidelong pieces (total runtime ~69:34). Menuck conceived it as a “retirement letter” — his intended final musical statement — believing at the time that “playing music” was “a waste of time.”

Leak and official release: Segments surfaced online via Reddit in 2013 (subsequently deleted). The full recording appeared on 4chan on 2 February 2022 and on YouTube shortly after. On 14 February 2022 the band officially released it on Bandcamp without prior announcement, with all proceeds benefiting Canadians for Justice and Peace in the Middle East’s campaign supplying emergency oxygen to the Gaza Strip. (Exclaim!, 2022; Bandcamp Daily, 2022)

Menuck on his long reluctance: “This was 30 years ago, and I guess for the last 20 years, I’ve been answering questions about this fucking cassette… I don’t want to negotiate with that logic. I don’t want to have to do something because I ‘have’ to.” He also said: “I’ve worked hard at demystifying what the cassette was, and what the context of it was. People got hung up on what they thought was the more interesting story, the mystery of the thing. There’s nothing mysterious. It’s of its time, and it is what it is.” (Bandcamp Daily, 2022)

GY!BE’s debut proper, F♯ A♯ ∞ (1997, Constellation Records), credits Menuck on electric guitar and blueprint artwork, alongside a large ensemble that then included David Bryant, Mauro Pezzente, Thierry Amar, Aidan Girt, Bruce Cawdron, Norsola Johnson, Mike Moya, and several early members (Jessica Moss, Dano Leblanc, and others) listed as “Godspeed You Black Emperor! 1995–1997.” The record was recorded and mixed by Don Wilkie and Ian Ilavsky along with the band. The touring/recording lineup stabilized to a nine-piece for Lift Yr. Skinny Fists Like Antennas To Heaven! (2000, Kranky), widely regarded as the band’s masterpiece — a double LP recorded by Daryl Smith. The final record of the first run, Yanqui U.X.O. (2002), was recorded by Steve Albini at Electrical Audio.

A Silver Mt. Zion (2000–2014)

Menuck launched A Silver Mt. Zion as a looser, more song-oriented project while GY!BE was still active. He Has Left Us Alone, But Shafts Of Light Sometimes Grace The Corner Of Our Rooms… (2000, Constellation) was a stripped trio record: Menuck on piano, guitar, voice, organ, and electronics; Sophie Trudeau on violin and voice; Thierry Amar on contrabass and electric bass. Harris Newman mastered it.

The project expanded through a succession of name variations — “The Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra & Tra-la-la Band,” then progressively “Thee Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra” — taking in a rotating cast of Montreal players. The core ensemble that recorded Kollaps Tradixionales (2010) and Fuck Off Get Free We Pour Light On Everything (2014) included Jessica Moss, Thierry Amar, Sophie Trudeau, David Payant, and Scott Levine Gilmore; Howard Bilerman and Radwan Moumneh handled recording duties, Harris Newman mastered. A Silver Mt. Zion released its last primary album, Hang On To Each Other (2014), before going dormant; Menuck moved the ensemble’s energies back to Godspeed.

The project’s full name history on Discogs: “A Silver Mt. Zion” / “The Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra & Tra-la-la Band” / “Thee Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra” and many variants. All are registered under Discogs artist ID 43637.

Vic Chesnutt productions (2007, 2009)

Menuck co-produced two late-career Vic Chesnutt albums for Constellation alongside Guy Picciotto, Jem Cohen, and Thierry Amar:

  • North Star Deserter (2007) — Menuck credited as “Producer [With]” and “Recorded By [With]”; Thee Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra + Tra-La-La Band performed throughout. Howard Bilerman also recorded; Harris Newman mastered.
  • At The Cut (2009) — Menuck credited as “Engineer [Assistant],” Guitar, Keyboards, Vocals. The session band included Guy Picciotto, Thierry Amar, David Payant, Jessica Moss, Nadia Moss, and Howard Bilerman as primary engineer.

Both albums were recorded at Hotel2Tango. Chesnutt died in December 2009; the Constellation releases represent his final studio work.

Godspeed reunion and ongoing work (2012–present)

GY!BE returned with ‘Allelujah! Don’t Bend Ascend (2012) — Menuck credited on electric guitar and hurdy gurdy. The band continued with Asunder, Sweet And Other Distress (2015, guitar), Luciferian Towers (2017, electric guitar, organ, OP-1 synthesizer), G_d’s Pee At State’s End! (2021), and No Title As Of 13 February 2024 28,340 Dead (2024). Film artist Karl Lemieux became a recurring visual collaborator from the reunion onward.

Solo work (2011–present)

Menuck released High Gospel (2011, Constellation) as his first credited solo LP under the name “Efrim Manuel Menuck,” performing all instruments and vocals and handling his own engineering; Harris Newman mastered. The album artwork was a gatefold collage by Jessica Moss, with front cover painting by Corri-Lynn Tetz.

Pissing Stars (2018, Constellation) was a solo synthesizer and electric guitar record using modular equipment including Richter Wogglebug, Grendel DC2, Mangrove, and OP-1, among others. Menuck again self-recorded; Harris Newman mastered.

are SING SINCK, SING (2019, Constellation) is a duo album credited jointly to Efrim Menuck & Kevin Doria. Menuck’s son Ezra Steamtrain Moss Menuck contributes intro vocals on one track.

The 2024 release No More Apocalypse Father on Constellation credits Menuck as “Guitar, Voice, Leader [Matriarch]” — a new ensemble context.

Constellation Records and Hotel2Tango

Constellation Records was founded in 1997 by Ian Ilavsky and Don Wilkie — not by Menuck or GY!BE. The two met in 1994 through mutual friends; Ilavsky was a musician (formerly in the band Sofa) and Wilkie came from a corporate background. They started Musique Fragile, a monthly concert series run from an inner-city loft, and launched Constellation to release records by local Mile End bands. GY!BE was among the very first artists on the roster, debuting with F♯ A♯ ∞ in 1997. (Drowned in Sound label focus, 2009; Wikipedia, as of 2025-05-17)

Menuck’s role at Constellation is as a long-term artist and in-house engineer/recordist — not as a label co-founder or co-owner. No source confirms an ownership stake in the label itself. The Discogs profile’s reference to “co-owner of the [l278346] recording studio” refers to Hotel2Tango the studio, not to Constellation the label.

Hotel2Tango is a distinct entity. The space at what became the studio was first occupied by Mauro Pezzente and his partner Kiva Stimac in 1995 (initially as Gallery Quiva, a living space and performance venue). In 2000, Howard Bilerman approached Menuck and Thierry Amar about combining their equipment into a central recording facility; these three — Bilerman, Menuck, and Amar — became co-owners of the dedicated studio. A fourth partner-engineer, Radwan Ghazi Moumneh, later joined. In 2006, people associated with both Constellation and Hotel2Tango co-purchased an industrial building to permanently house the label and relocate the studio. (Wikipedia Hotel2Tango, as of 2025-05-17; Red Bull Music Academy Daily, 2016)

Studio/Engineering role at Constellation

Beyond his own projects, Menuck operated as an informal in-house recordist at Hotel2Tango for a significant portion of the Constellation catalog through the 2000s and early 2010s. Discogs documents him as “Recorded By” and/or “Mixed By” on at least the following non-GY!BE/ASMZ releases:

  • My Red Scare (2000, Sxip Shirey) — Glockenspiel; Recorded By, Mixed By
  • Sédatifs En Fréquences Et Sillons (2000) — Recorded By, Mixed By
  • Hangedup (2001) — Recorded By, Mixed By
  • Ceux Qui Inventent N’ont Jamais Vécu (?) (2002) — Recorded By, Mixed By
  • Kicker In Tow (2002, Hangedup) — Recorded By, Mixed By
  • Welcome Crummy Mystics (2003) — Recorded By
  • The City Swallows The Sparrow (2006) — Recorded By, Conductor
  • Evangelista (2006) — Recorded By
