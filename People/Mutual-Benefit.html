Overview

Mutual Benefit is the recording project of Jordan Lee, a singer-songwriter and the founder of the Kassette Klub cassette label. Lee’s music blends analog warmth with modern digital production tools, sitting across folk rock, indie rock, acoustic, and lo-fi territory. After self-releasing on Kassette Klub from 2010 to 2011, the project moved to Other Music Recording Co. and Transgressive Records, sustaining a steady output through the 2010s and into the 2020s. Recurring collaborators across the catalogue include Jake Falby (16 shared releases), Dillon Zahner (12), and Noah Klein (10).

Notes (web)

How Mutual Benefit came to Other Music Recording Co.

The clearest account is in a late-2013 Stereogum “Band to Watch” interview with Jordan Lee (stereogum.com). Lee explains that friends Cory and Marc — the duo who play in Lizard Kisses — started the small Brooklyn label Soft Eyes and agreed to release Love’s Crushing Diamond immediately on completion. The album was self-released via Soft Eyes on 7 October 2013 in a limited 250-LP run. It then received more attention than anyone had anticipated, including being named Pitchfork’s Best New Music. Lee’s words: “it got more attention than any of us were expecting. So basically it made more sense to do the repressing through a bigger label that had the infrastructure to support a lot of people trying to buy it.” The practical trigger was logistics — managing even 200 LPs without a fixed address was described as “super overwhelming” — which made Other Music Recording Co.’s distribution infrastructure the natural fit. The reissue appeared on 3 December 2013.

Lee expressed specific enthusiasm for Other Music’s eclectic international roster (he cites Swedish neo-classical chamber pop and Brazilian psych-rock), saying it meant he would not be “pigeonholed as some new-Americana kind of thing” and could “stay unclassifiable for a while.” (Stereogum interview, ibid.)

Who initiated contact is not stated in any source checked. No interview or press piece found specifies whether Other Music approached Jordan Lee, whether Lee or Soft Eyes approached Other Music, or whether the connection grew from the NYC store itself. The Stereogum account frames it as a reactive move driven by demand, but the first-contact detail is absent.
