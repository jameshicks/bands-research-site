Biography

Origins — Femme Fatale and the Lyon Scene

Marie Girard came up in the student rock scene in Lyon around 1975. She co-founded the group initially called Femme Fatale with her brother Philippe Girard (drums), Erik Fitoussi (guitar), Christian Faye (guitar), and Jean-Pierre Charriau (bass) — all students from Lycée Saint-Exupéry. The group formed for a school end-of-year concert and quickly became a going concern on the Lyon circuit. Marc Zermati (founder of Skydog Records) spotted them and suggested the name change that became Marie Et Les Garçons.

At this point Marie was the group’s lead singer, not its drummer. Her brother Philippe was the drummer.

1976–1978 — Marie Et Les Garçons, First Phase

In 1976 Philippe Girard left to co-found Electric Callas with Jangil Callas and Jean-Marc Vallod. This was the pivot that redefined Marie’s role: she moved from vocals to drums, Patrick Vidal became lead singer, and Vallod joined on bass before himself drifting toward Electric Callas.

The new lineup caught the attention of Michel Esteban, Parisian punk entrepreneur, shop owner (Harry Cover), and publisher of Rock News. He signed them first to Rebel Records (6) in 1977 (Rien À Dire 7”), then to his own SPY Records Ltd. in 1978 (Attitudes EP). The Attitudes session was recorded in New York in March 1978 and featured John Cale (Velvet Underground) on piano and marimba — an extraordinary collaborator for a French punk group barely three years old. Marie Girard co-wrote tracks on the EP, receiving a “Written-By” credit alongside Fitoussi, Charriau, and Vidal.

The band performed at CBGB in 1978 during the New York recording trip, and in July 1978 at the landmark “Le Rock d’Ici à l’Olympia” festival in Paris — a three-day event that also featured Electric Callas, Stinky Toys, and Starshooter, the three groups that led Libération to call Lyon “capitale du rock.”

1979 — Departure from Marie Et Les Garçons

During the recording of what became the Marie Et Les Garçons debut album (Celluloid, 1980), tensions arose over musical direction — Patrick Vidal was steering the project toward disco; Marie Girard was not. She left due to “musical incompatibility” and returned to France. The remaining members (now called simply Garçons) continued recording; the resulting album was produced by Michel Esteban and appeared on Celluloid with the full band name intact.

The same year, Marie appears as a session drummer on one track (A5) of the Pathé LP Mode (1979) by Starshooter, the other flagship Lyon group. The rest of the album was drummed by Phil Pressing; Marie’s track-level appearance suggests a specific one-off session or substitution.

1979–1981 — Electric Callas

After leaving Marie Et Les Garçons, Marie Girard joined Electric Callas, the group her brother Philippe had helped found — though Philippe himself had already departed by this point. The overlap was sequential, not simultaneous: she stepped in as Electric Callas entered its brief recorded phase. Jean-Marc Vallod was also a member.

She is credited on drums on the Winner 12” single (Celluloid, 1981 — cat. no. CEL 2-6563), the band’s final and most widely distributed release. It was produced by the band themselves. Logo design was by Belgian cartoonist Yves Chaland; photography by Gilles Riberolles (Roman Casino). The single appeared on both Celluloid (France) and K Records (9), placing it in the same distribution network as James Chance, Metal Urbain, and the downtown New York roster.

Electric Callas also recorded the So Chic 12” EP on Pathé in 1979, produced by Michel Zacha; Marie Girard is not credited on that release (she joined after it was recorded).

After Music

Following the dissolution of Electric Callas, Marie Girard left the music world and worked as a fashion designer. She died on 6 August 1996 from a cerebral aneurysm, aged 40.

Her name and the band she helped found continued to have an afterlife: Marie Et Les Garçons reunited periodically (1987, 1988, 1990, 2009, 2011, 2015), and the New York Sessions 79 archival release appeared on ZE Records in 2008, produced by Michel Esteban, documenting the 1979 New York recordings made before her departure.

The band X.Kicks also lists both Marie Girard and Jean-Marc Vallod as members (Discogs ID: 7189288). X.Kicks released a split 7” with Soma Holiday in 1997 (Killing Spiders / Leave Me Alone, Not On Label). X.Kicks’s profile has no biography on Discogs; the release credit for Jean-Marc Vallod (guitar) is confirmed on the Soma Holiday side of the split.

Scene Connections

Marie Girard sat at the intersection of two overlapping circuits:

Lyon post-punk — the cohort around Marie Et Les Garçons, Electric Callas, and Starshooter that Libération dubbed Lyon’s “capitale du rock” moment in 1978. The three bands shared venues, festivals, members, and label relationships (Pathé, Skydog, Celluloid).

Michel Esteban’s network — Esteban’s shop Harry Cover, his magazine Rock News, SPY Records Ltd., and then ZE Records formed a pipeline linking the French punk scene to the New York downtown world. Marie Et Les Garçons was his first and most central signing. This put Marie Girard in proximity to Lizzy Mercier Descloux, Michael Zilkha, and the entire ZE roster — even if she had departed the band before the New York Sessions were recorded.

Electric Callas / Celluloid — The Winner 12” placed Electric Callas on Celluloid Records, Jean Karakos’s Franco-American label whose roster also included James Chance & The Contortions, Material, Metal Boys, and Suicide.
