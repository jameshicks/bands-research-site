Aug 17, 202611 min read

Ivo Perelman

Discogs ID: 376339 | Born: 1961, São Paulo, Brazil | Based: New York, NY

Brazilian-American tenor saxophonist and visual artist. One of the most prolific figures in contemporary free improvisation, with a documented output exceeding 100 unique releases as leader or co-leader spanning 1989–2025. Also a practicing painter whose artwork appears on many of his album covers.

Biography

Origins: São Paulo (to c. 1985)

Born in São Paulo in 1961. Perelman came up in a Brazilian musical environment saturated with bossa nova, choro, and popular song before turning toward jazz and free improvisation. His earliest recorded work bears unmistakable marks of this heritage — traditional Brazilian songs, Afro-Brazilian percussion, and melodic materials drawn from the Candomblé tradition (Ibeji, the Yoruba deity of twins, names both his early label and recurs as a theme). Details of his formal training and the exact date of his move to New York are not documented in the Discogs record.

New York Arrival and Early Records (1989–1996)

Perelman’s debut album Ivo (1989, Estúdio Eldorado / K2B2 Records) established his voice as a tenor saxophonist capable of working across free jazz and Brazilian idioms. Children Of Ibeji (1992, Enja Records) was his first major international release, featuring a notably large cast: Don Pullen and Paul Bley on piano (different tracks), Andrew Cyrille on drums, Fred Hopkins on bass, Guilherme Franco and Cyro Baptista on percussion, Flora Purim on vocals, and Brandon Ross on guitar. The album draws on Brazilian traditional song and positions Perelman as an arranger-adaptor as much as an improviser.

Man Of The Forest (1994, GM Recordings) broadened the cast further — Joanne Brackeen on piano, Billy Hart on drums, Mark Helias on bass, Naná Vasconcelos, Cyro Baptista, Guilherme Franco, Duduka Da Fonseca — and was produced with Gunther Schuller, demonstrating early access to the mainstream jazz establishment.

Around this period Perelman ran his own imprint Ibeji Records (label ID: 1455169), which issued Soccer Land (1994) — a duo with Zé Eduardo Nazario (drums/voice) — and Tapeba Songs (1996), drawing on indigenous Tapeba music with a Brazilian ensemble including Paulo Bellinati, Rodolfo Stroeter, and the Nazario brothers. These releases are more ethnographic in character than his later free improvisation work.

Meeting Shipp / Homestead and Leo Lab (1996–2002)

The pivot that defines the bulk of Perelman’s career came with Cama De Terra (1996, Homestead Records), a trio session with Matthew Shipp (piano) and William Parker (bass) — the first documented Perelman-Shipp recording. This collaboration, still active as of 2025, would become the longest and most prolific partnership in Perelman’s discography: approximately 48 unique shared releases across Discogs masters.

Sad Life (1997, Leo Lab) — a trio with Rashied Ali and William Parker — marked the beginning of Perelman’s relationship with Leo Records and its founder/producer Leo Feigin, which would become by far his most important label relationship. Also in 1997: En Adir (traditional Jewish songs, Atração Fonográfica) and Bendito Of Santa Cruz (Cadence Jazz Records), showing breadth of cultural reference.

Brazilian Watercolour (1999, Leo Records, CD LR 266) is a significant quintet session — Shipp, Rashied Ali, Guilherme Franco, Cyro Baptista — blending the Brazilian percussion world with the New York free jazz axis.

The Ivo Perelman Quartet’s Sieiro (1999, Leo Records) and En Adir / Sound Hierarchy (1997, Music & Arts) show the rotating-lineup nature of this ensemble. The 1997 Music & Arts quartet featured Marilyn Crispell, William Parker, and Gerry Hemingway. The Alexander Suite (1998, Leo Records) added a string quartet (the C.T. String Quartet, with Dominic Duval on bass, Tomas Ulrich on cello, Ron Lawrence on viola, and Jason Hwang on violin) to Perelman’s saxophone.

The Hammer (2000, Leo Records), a duo with drummer Jay Rosen (2), shows Perelman’s comfort in minimal, stripped-down settings. The Complete Ibeji Sessions box (2008, Editio Princeps) compiled his early label work retroactively.

Leo Records Era: Prolific Output (2006–2019)

From roughly 2009 onward Perelman entered a phase of extraordinary productivity on Leo Records, releasing multiple albums per year in varying formats: duo, trio, and quartet. Key series include:

  • Art Of The Duet (2013): Duo recordings with Matthew Shipp
  • The Art Of The Improv Trio (2016): Six-volume series with Karl Berger (piano) and Gerald Cleaver (drums)
  • Saturn System (2017): Seven albums — Titan, Tarvos, Pandora, Hyperion, Rhea, Saturn, Dione — featuring Shipp (piano) and William Parker (bass)
  • Strings 1–4 (2018–2019): Four albums with Mat Maneri (viola), Mark Feldman (violin), and Jason Hwang (violin)
  • Efflorescence (2019): Continued string-ensemble explorations

The engineer throughout the bulk of the Leo Records period is Jim Clouse (2) (Discogs ID: 697084), credited on recorded by / mixed by / mastered by across dozens of releases. The designer for many sessions is Tom Beckham (Discogs ID: 2148769). Liner notes are frequently by Neil Tesser (Discogs ID: 1685044).

The Hour Of The Star (2011, Leo Records) presents the Ivo Perelman Quartet in a configuration of Shipp, Joe Morris (bass), and Gerald Cleaver (drums). The title nods to the Clarice Lispector novel — one of several Perelman releases that invoke Brazilian literary figures.

One (2013, RareNoise Records) marked a departure to a UK imprint and stands as one of the heavier, more electric-adjacent works in his catalog.

Callas (2015, Leo Records) is a two-CD set with Shipp, notable for its dedication to the soprano Maria Callas.

Ineffable Joy (2019, ESP-Disk’) features the piano trio of Shipp, William Parker, and Bobby Kapp (drums), on ESP-Disk’ — his first release on that historic label.

Post-Leo / Bandcamp-Distributed Era (2020–present)

Beginning around 2020 Perelman’s new releases shifted away from Leo Records toward a cluster of smaller labels and Bandcamp-distributed imprints:

  • Mahakala Music (label ID: 1738067, Bandcamp): Amalgam (2020, with Shipp), (D)IVO (2022, a saxophone quartet with Tim Berne, James Carter (3), and Tony Malaby), Shamanism (2020), Alien Skin (2022), Elliptic Time (2022), Reed Rapture In Brooklyn (2022), Embracing The Unknown (2024), Artificial Intelligence (2023)
  • Burning Ambulance Music: Polarity (2021), Polarity 2 (2023), Polarity 3 (2024), Polarity 4 (2025)
  • Fundacja Słuchaj! (Poland): Deep Resonance (2020), Molten Gold (2023), Seven Skies Orchestra (2023), Truth Seeker (2024), Ephemeral Shapes (2024), Parallel Aesthetics (2025), A Modicum Of The Blues (2025), 12 Stages Of Spiritual Alchemy (2025)
  • Ibeji Music (label ID: 3577141): Tuning Forks (2023), Interaction (2024), Duologues 1 - Turning Point (2024) — Perelman’s own revived imprint
  • Soul City Sounds (2): Magical Incantation (2024), Live In Carrboro (2023)
  • Tao Forms: Armageddon Flower (2025, with Matthew Shipp String Trio)

Armageddon Flower (2025, Tao Forms) pairs Perelman with the Matthew Shipp String Trio — Shipp (piano), Mat Maneri (viola), William Parker (bass) — and is produced jointly by Perelman and Shipp.

The Seven Skies Orchestra (group ID: 13751587) is a large ensemble project documented on Fundacja Słuchaj! (2023).

Visual Art Practice

Perelman contributes original paintings and watercolors to many of his own album covers, credited variously as “Paintings By”, “Cover Watercolors”, “Artwork”. This is a recurring element across Leo Records and Mahakala Music releases and is part of his self-presentation as an artist working across media.
