Eddie Janney

Eddie Janney

Discogs ID: 287901
Aliases: Eddie Machete
Name variations: Edward Janney, Eddie Janny, Edward
Role: Guitarist, vocalist, bassist, producer, photographer

Washington DC guitarist at the heart of the Revolution Summer post-hardcore scene. A founding member of Rites Of Spring (1984–1986), One Last Wish (1986), and Happy Go Licky (1987–1988) — all built around the same core: Janney, Guy Picciotto, Mike Fellows, and Brendan Canty. Pre-dating those bands, he played guitar in the earliest DC hardcore formation: Untouchables (1979–1981) and Faith (3) (1982–1983), alongside Michael Hampton (3), Alec MacKaye, and others inside the Dischord Records orbit. He is a relative of Eli Janney of Girls Against Boys (exact relation unspecified in Discogs).

Biography

Earliest DC Activity — Untouchables and Faith (3) (1979–1983)

Janney entered the Washington DC hardcore scene at its founding. He is listed as a member of the Untouchables (Discogs ID 71433) — the early DC hardcore band that included Alec MacKaye (vocals), Bert Queiroz (bass), and Rich Moore (drums). The Untouchables are documented as active from October 1979 to January 1981 and are credited on Flex Your Head (1982, Dischord Records No. 7), the essential DC hardcore compilation, where Janney is credited with guitar on the Untouchables’ three tracks (“Rat Patrol,” “Nic Fit,” “I Hate You”). Their “Nic Fit” was later covered by Sonic Youth.

By 1982–1983 Janney was a member of Faith (3) (Discogs ID 309576), alongside Michael Hampton (3) (guitar), Chris Bald (bass), Ivor Hanson (drums), and Alec MacKaye (vocals). Faith released Subject To Change (1983, Dischord No. 11), an eight-song 12” EP produced by Ian MacKaye and the band. Janney is credited with guitar on that release, alongside Hampton — the same Hampton who would later co-produce the Rites Of Spring LP and join One Last Wish.

During this same period — 1983 — Janney appears as producer and backing vocalist on Scream’s Still Screaming LP (Dischord 9). His producer credit here is noteworthy: it’s one of the earliest instances of Janney in a production capacity, alongside Ian MacKaye and Don Zientara, for a band he was not a member of.

Skewbald / Grand Union (1981–1982)

Separate from the Untouchables, Janney was part of Skewbald / Grand Union (Discogs ID 1372883), formed in 1981 by Jeff Nelson and Ian MacKaye after Minor Threat’s first dissolution. The lineup was MacKaye (vocals), Janney (guitar), John Falls (bass), and Nelson (drums) — housemates, in effect. The band recorded a three-song demo but never progressed beyond rehearsal stage. When Minor Threat reformed in spring 1982, the project was abandoned. The demo circulated in tape-trading circles (sometimes mistaken for Minor Threat outtakes) until 1991, when Dischord issued it as 2 Songs (DIS50V / Dischord No. 50), a single-sided 7” to commemorate the label’s 50th release. Recorded with Don Zientara.

Rites Of Spring (1984–1986)

In 1984, Janney joined the post-Insurrection lineup of what would become Rites Of Spring. The band — Janney (guitar), Guy Picciotto (guitar, vocals), Mike Fellows (bass), and Brendan Canty (drums) — became central to the DC “Revolution Summer” shift away from hardcore’s macho posturing toward emotionally direct songwriting. Their rare live appearances (approximately a dozen in two years) reportedly moved audience members to tears.

All Through A Life 7” EP (1987, Dischord 22)

Four songs: “All Through A Life” / “Hidden Wheel” / “In Silence / Words Away” / “Patience.” Released January 1987, just as the band dissolved. Co-produced by Ian MacKaye and the band (credited as R.O.S.). Janney is credited for photography with the “Piano” designation (“Photography By [Piano],” credited as “Edward”).

The band dissolved when Mike Fellows departed in January 1987. The EP was effectively a posthumous release, issued as the band was already finished. Rites Of Spring’s catalog was subsequently compiled as End On End CD (1991, Dischord) and the archival Six Song Demo 10” EP (2012, Dischord dis176V), on which Janney is credited as “Uncredited.”

One Last Wish (1986)

Following Rites Of Spring’s breakup, Picciotto, Janney, and Canty formed One Last Wish (Discogs ID 309588) with Michael Hampton (3) — Hampton had produced the Rites Of Spring LP and was now a full bandmate. To accommodate Hampton on guitar, Janney shifted from guitar to bass. The band played six shows and recorded an LP in 1986 before ceasing activity. Janney’s alleged unhappiness playing bass is cited as a contributing factor in the dissolution.

Dischord, which had expressed interest in releasing the LP, shelved it in the band’s aftermath (partly to shed its reputation for posthumous releases). The recording finally came out in 1999.

Happy Go Licky (1987–1988)

After One Last Wish dissolved, all four Rites Of Spring members — Picciotto, Janney, Fellows, and Canty — regrouped as Happy Go Licky (Discogs ID 309584). The name change was a deliberate identity break; Janney returned to guitar and shared vocal duties with Picciotto.

The Embrace LP Production (1987)

Alongside his work in HGL and One Last Wish, Janney is co-producer on three tracks (B5–B7: “Money,” “If I Never Thought About It,” “End Of A Year”) of the posthumous Embrace (2) LP (1987, Dischord No. 24). Embrace featured Ian MacKaye (vocals), Michael Hampton (3) (guitar), Chris Bald (bass), and Ivor Hanson (drums). The rest of the album was produced by Embrace themselves (tracks A1–B4) and those same final three tracks co-produced by Janney and MacKaye. His production credit here — for a band he wasn’t in — extends his behind-the-scenes role into production alongside his old scene peers.

Rozzlyn Rangers and Post-HGL Activity

After Happy Go Licky, Janney was a member of Rozzlyn Rangers (Discogs ID 309594) — a band also including Ian MacKaye, Jeff Nelson, Rich Moore, and Sab Grey. Discogs carries no release data for the Rozzlyn Rangers; the band appears not to have issued any recordings, or none have been catalogued.

He is also listed by Discogs as a member of the Untouchables (see above) and of various informal configurations that continued into the early 1990s.

Brief Weeds (early 1990s, K Records)

Brief Weeds (Discogs ID 756877) reunited Janney with Picciotto, Canty, and Michael Hampton — the One Last Wish lineup minus Fellows. Per the Discogs profile, the music was recorded in the late 1980s but released on K Records in the early 1990s. The sound moved away from hardcore into psychedelic-influenced home-recorded pop, informed by 1960s psych. One release is catalogued:
