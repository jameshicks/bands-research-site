Stu Goldberg

Discogs ID: 260958 Real name: Stuart Wayne Goldberg Born: July 10, 1954, Malden, Massachusetts, USA Instruments: Piano, organ, trombone, synthesizer (Moog, Prophet, Mini-Moog), electric piano (Fender Rhodes), clavinet Name variations: Stuart Goldberg, Stuart W. Goldberg, S. Goldberg, Stu

American jazz pianist, composer, and film/TV music producer. Goldberg came up in the fusion milieu of the mid-1970s and is best known as the keyboardist on Mahavishnu Orchestra’s final second-lineup album Inner Worlds (CBS, 1976) and as a member of John McLaughlin’s touring band The One Truth Band. He subsequently built a parallel career as a session player on German jazz labels (especially MPS Records) and, from the late 1980s onward, as a film and television keyboard session musician.

Biography / Career

Early Career and the Mahavishnu Connection (1975–1976)

Goldberg’s first documented Discogs credit is on Fly On (East Wind, 1975), a session for the Japanese-released band Air Pocket, where he played piano, electric piano, Mini-Moog, and clavinet alongside drummer Chester Thompson and the Fowler family of session musicians (Steve, Tom, Ed, Bruce, Walt Fowler). Producer was jazz arranger Oliver Nelson.

He then replaced Gayle Moran as keyboardist in the Mahavishnu Orchestra’s second lineup, appearing on Inner Worlds (CBS/Sony, 1976). The album was co-produced by John McLaughlin and Dennis Mackay. Goldberg is credited with organ, piano, backing vocals, clavinet, and synthesizer. Bandmates were McLaughlin (guitar, synthesizer), Ralphe Armstrong (bass), and Narada Michael Walden (drums, timpani, congas, marimba, vocals). This was the final Mahavishnu Orchestra album of the second era before McLaughlin pivoted to acoustic work with Shakti (2).

The One Truth Band (1978–1979)

Goldberg remained in McLaughlin’s orbit as a member of The One Truth Band, the electric ensemble McLaughlin assembled after Shakti. The band included Fernando Saunders (electric and acoustic bass), Tony Smith (2) (drums, vocals), Shankar (violin), and Alyrio Lima (percussion). Their sole studio album, Electric Dreams (CBS, 1979), was produced by McLaughlin and engineered by John Pace, with Greg Calbi mastering. Goldberg played electric piano, Moog with Steiner modifications, Prophet synthesizer, and Hammond organ. David Sanborn makes a guest appearance on alto saxophone on one track.

MPS Records Sessions and European Collaborations (1977–1982)

Goldberg became a recurring presence on the German MPS Records label in the late 1970s and early 1980s, appearing on several sessions:

  • Alphonse Mouzon — Virtue (MPS, 1977): Keyboards and Mini-Moog soloist; co-sessions with Gary Bartz (saxophone). Produced by Joachim Ernst Berendt.
  • Stu Goldberg with Larry Coryell and L. Subramaniam — Solos-Duos-Trios (MPS, 1978): A trio improvisation album featuring Goldberg on keyboards, Larry Coryell on guitar, and L. Subramaniam on violin, viola, harp, and tambura. Produced by Joachim Ernst Berendt. Goldberg is a primary credited artist.
  • Stu Goldberg / John Lee (3) / Gerry Brown — Fancy Glance (Sandra Music Productions, 1979): Goldberg as co-primary artist with bassist John Lee (3) and drummer Gerry Brown. Goldberg plays piano, electric piano, organ, and synthesizer. Produced by Wolfgang Lackerschmid.
  • Toto Blanke’s Electric Circus featuring Stu Goldberg — Friends (Bacillus Records, 1979): Co-featured artist alongside guitarist/arranger Toto Blanke; the band also included Norbert Dömling (bass), Heinrich Hock (drums), Mat Nadolny (saxophone), and Trilok Gurtu (tabla, percussion). Goldberg plays Mini-Moog and Fender Rhodes.
  • Charlie Mariano — Crystal Bells (CMP Records, 1980): Keyboards, with Gene Perla (bass) and Don Alias (drums, percussion). Produced by Kurt Renker.
  • Stu Goldberg — Eye Of The Beholder (MPS, 1982): Solo album on which Goldberg served as composer, arranger, producer, engineer, and pianist — as well as front-cover photographer. Personnel included Jim Lacefield (acoustic bass), David Crigger (drums), Lee Pastora (congas/bongos), Ken Goldberg (tenor saxophone, flute), and string players Doug Cameron, Clayton Haslop (violins), Jimbo Ross (viola), and Daniel Smith (cello).

Other European session credits:

  • Robby Krieger — Robbie Krieger & Friends (Blue Note, 1977): Organ and synthesizer on tracks 5–7 in a large LA session cast.

Solo Recordings (1979–1981)

In addition to Eye Of The Beholder, Goldberg released two other albums as a primary or co-primary artist:

  • Stu Goldberg — Friends (Bacillus Records, 1979): The Toto Blanke’s Electric Circus co-featured album (see above).
  • Stu Goldberg — Variations By Goldberg (Pausa Records, 1981): Solo piano album; Goldberg credited as pianist and soloist.

Film and Television Work (1987–2016)

From the late 1980s Goldberg transitioned substantially into screen music, appearing on keyboard/synthesizer on a range of film soundtrack recordings:

  • Over The Top (CBS, 1987) — synthesizer
  • Isn’t It Romantic (Elektra, 1988) — synthesizer
  • Pacifique (WEA, 1989) — piano
  • Yakko’s World (Kid Rhino / Warner Bros. Family Entertainment, 1994) — keyboards; animated TV music
  • Feel Your Dream (RVCD, 1995) — synthesizer
  • The Pick-Up Artist / Sherlock Holmes In New York (Intrada, 2006) — keyboards, small sessions
  • Testify: A Whole Lot More Original Music from the Television Series (Shout! Factory, 2007) — keyboards
  • Indiana Jones And The Last Crusade (Concord Records, 2008) — piano
  • Indiana Jones: The Soundtracks Collection (Concord Records, 2008) — piano
  • Innerspace (La-La Land Records, 2009) — keyboards
  • Joe Versus The Volcano — The Big Woo Edition (Masters Film Music, 2016) — synth

Open Questions

  • Discogs does not clarify the full circumstances of how Goldberg came to join the Mahavishnu Orchestra (who recommended him, timing relative to Gayle Moran’s departure for Return to Forever, etc.)
  • Whether Goldberg toured with the Mahavishnu Orchestra for Inner Worlds or only recorded is not resolvable from Discogs alone
  • The “Liner Notes” credit on Blues On The Other Side (Argo, 1962) is almost certainly a different Stuart Goldberg — a jazz writer rather than the musician, given the birth year of 1954 makes this implausible
  • Post-1995 activity is sparse in Discogs; the 2006–2016 film credits suggest ongoing work but the full scope of his TV/film career is not traceable via Discogs alone
  • The “Star Edition” (MPS, 1979) compilation credit is unverified for which session(s) it draws from
  • No Discogs data on his trombone playing — listed in his profile but no releases credit this instrument
