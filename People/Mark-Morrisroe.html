Mark Morrisroe

Photographer, filmmaker, performance artist. Born January 10, 1959, Malden, Massachusetts. Died July 24, 1989, Jersey City, New Jersey (AIDS complications, age 30). Discogs ID: 2295369.

Cover photography on Jeff And Jane Hudson’s Flesh LP (J & J Records, 1983) — a natural pairing: Morrisroe was a central figure in the same Boston queer-punk underground from which Jeff and Jane emerged, and both he and they were connected to the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston (where he studied and they later taught). Confirmed by Jeff and Jane Hudson in interview: “Mark Morrisroe was a student of ours… He was a photographer even then, and took the picture of Malcolm Travis, drummer for Human Sexual Response. That photo is the cover of Flesh.” (Dark Entries Records interview) — the cover subject is therefore Malcolm Travis of Boston New Wave band Human Sexual Response, not Jeff or Jane Hudson themselves.

One of the forerunners of the Boston School of photography alongside Nan Goldin, David Armstrong, and Jack Pierson. Signature technique: “sandwich” prints (two overlaid negatives enlarged together for a painterly effect). Polaroid Corporation sponsored him. Also directed three Super 8 films: Laziest Girl in Town (1981), Hello from Bertha (1983), Nymph-O-Maniac (1984).

Co-founded the Clam Twins drag duo with Stephen Tashjian (Tabboo!), debuting at the Pyramid Club in NYC’s East Village.

Solo shows at Pat Hearn Gallery (1985–1988). Group shows at Artists Space: Robert Mapplethorpe-curated Split Vision (1985) and Nan Goldin’s Witnesses: Against Our Vanishing (1989). Major retrospectives at ICA Boston (1995), MOCA Los Angeles (1997), and others. Over 2,000 artworks produced in his short career.

Music Scene Connections (Boston and NYC)

Boston punk/New Wave overlap (pre-NYC move)

Morrisroe’s music connections are primarily rooted in the Boston underground scene of the late 1970s–early 1980s, not the NYC No Wave scene. Key documented connections:

  • Malcolm Travis (drummer, Human Sexual Response): Morrisroe photographed Travis circa 1981; the resulting gelatin silver print “Malcolm Travis” (c. 1981, 20 × 16 in., sepia-toned) was used as the cover of Jeff and Jane Hudson’s Flesh LP. Confirmed by Jeff and Jane Hudson: “Mark Morrisroe was a student of ours… he took the picture of Malcolm Travis, drummer for Human Sexual Response.” (Dark Entries Records interview) The print was later offered for sale by ClampArt gallery. (ClampArt)

  • Steve Stain (drummer): Stain frequently collaborated with Morrisroe, Gail Thacker, and Pat Hearn in performances. He also rented a raw space at 38 Thayer Street (Boston South End) that hosted punk bands including Mission of Burma and The Girls — connecting Morrisroe’s immediate social circle to the post-punk/underground scene. (Big Red & Shiny)

  • Human Sexual Response and The Fucking Barbies performed at the 11th Hour venue, which was part of the same Boston underground world Morrisroe documented. (Big Red & Shiny)

NYC No Wave: no documented connections found

Extensive web search (2026-07-28) found no documentation of Morrisroe photographing, collaborating with, or documenting NYC No Wave musicians specifically (Lydia Lunch, James Chance, DNA, Teenage Jesus and the Jerks, Contortions, Sonic Youth, etc.). His NYC presence from circa 1982 onward centered on the East Village art world — Pat Hearn Gallery, Artists Space group shows, and the Pyramid Club drag-performance scene — rather than the CBGB/Mudd Club music axis where No Wave operated. Sources checked include: Dazed Digital, AnOther Magazine (Ryan McGinley essay), Musée Magazine, David Zwirner exhibition page, Aperture, JRP Editions monograph description, Fotomuseum Winterthur estate description, BOMB Magazine (Tabboo! catalogue), Big Red & Shiny (Boston exhibition). None name No Wave musicians.

The Pyramid Club, where Morrisroe and Tabboo! performed as the Clam Twins, overlapped socially with the No Wave world but press coverage of Morrisroe’s Pyramid appearances does not document encounters with No Wave bands specifically.
