Bob Last

Music manager, label founder, producer, music supervisor. Edinburgh, Scotland. Discogs ID: 325233. Discogs name variations: “B. Last”, “Fast Product.”

Founder of Fast Product (Edinburgh, 1977–1980) and Pop Aural (1981). One of the key figures in British post-punk as both label operator and manager. Managed The Human League through their commercial breakthrough (Dare, 1981) and Heaven 17 through their debut period. Later career in film music supervision (1990s–2000s) includes Chocolat, Backbeat, and Little Voice.

Fast Product Era (1977–1980)

Bob Last founded Fast Product in Edinburgh in the late 1970s, operating it as a conceptual art-meets-music enterprise rather than a straightforward record label. The label treated each release as a designed object — packaging, typography, and the physical format were considered integral to the work. It ran to approximately twelve catalog positions, including two zine publications (The Quality Of Life Nos. 1 and 2), alongside records by The Mekons, 2.3, The Human League, Gang of Four, and a UK edition of the Dead Kennedys’ “California Über Alles.”

Last is credited as producer on the Gang of Four debut single Damaged Goods / Love Like Anthrax / Armalite Rifle (FAST 5, 1978) — one of the most important post-punk records released by any independent label in the UK — co-producing alongside the band with engineer John Brierley. He also took design and construction credits on the Earcom compilation EP series (the label’s experimental sub-series).

By 1979 he had started managing The Human League, appearing in the management credit on their debut album Reproduction (Virgin, 1979) and as producer on the Human League’s early Virgin material.

Pop Aural and Scottish Post-Punk (1981)

After Fast Product wound down, Last launched Pop Aural, which released two significant Scottish post-punk/new wave compilations in 1981:

  • Big Gold Dream (Pop Aural, 1981) — produced by Last; featured Josef K, The Fire Engines, Aztec Camera, and others; became the definitive document of the early-1980s Edinburgh/Glasgow scene.
  • Lubricate Your Living Room (Pop Aural, 1981) — further material produced by Last.

Both were released as he was also managing The Human League at the height of their commercial success.

Management Career

Last managed The Human League from at least 1979 (Reproduction) through their Virgin period. The Human League’s Dare (1981) was one of the biggest-selling albums in the UK that year. He is credited on the reissue compilation + - Singles 1978-80 (Rhino, 2010) with an original cover credit.

He also worked with Heaven 17, who were formed from the Human League’s split lineup of Martyn Ware and Ian Craig Marsh. Last’s packaging credit appears on Heaven 17’s debut single “(We Don’t Need This) Fascist Groove Thang” (Virgin, 1981) and on their debut album Penthouse And Pavement (Virgin, 1981, executive manipulator credit), as well as on the Human League cassette-only releases Music For Stowaways and Music For Listening To (both Virgin, 1981).

He managed Scritti Politti through their mid-1980s commercial phase: management credit on “Wood Beez (Pray Like Aretha Franklin)” (Warner Bros US, 1984) and the Cupid & Psyche 85 album (Warner Bros US, 1985).

Additional management credit on the Human League US release Hysteria (A&M Records, 1984).

  • Hungry Beat (Acute Records, 2007) — definitive Fast Product/Scottish post-punk compilation; Last credited as liner notes author and producer
  • Chrome Dawns (Cherry Red, 2024) — produced by Last
