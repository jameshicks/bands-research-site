David M. Allen

English record producer, engineer, and remixer. Discogs artist ID: 93936. Active primarily from the late 1970s through the 2010s. One of the central production figures in British post-punk, new wave, and goth rock, defined above all by a decade-long partnership with The Cure and significant work with The Sisters Of Mercy, Depeche Mode, Wire, The Human League, and The Psychedelic Furs.

Sometimes credited as Dave Allen, Dave M. Allen, D.M. Allen, or a range of variants. Not to be confused with Dave Allen (5), the bassist for Gang of Four and Shriekback. He is the son of Reg Allen, a professional footballer (Q.P.R., Manchester United).

Has also performed and recorded as a member of The League Unlimited Orchestra (the dance alter-ego project connected to The Human League), as well as smaller groups Pinpoint, Terrible Lizard, Tarp Trio, and The Magic Sponge.

Overview

Allen’s career began in the late 1970s as an assistant/engineer, cutting his teeth on sessions at commercial studios in West Germany (Hansa International) and on projects around the UK new-wave scene. His assistant credit on The Human League’s Dare (1981, Virgin) — one of the landmark synth-pop records — placed him at the centre of the British electronic music world at a formative moment, even if he was still in a supporting role.

His trajectory shifted decisively when he became the go-to producer for The Cure, a relationship that spanned from The Top (1984) through Wish (1992) and the remix album Mixed Up (1990). He is credited as co-producer and engineer on all of The Cure’s major studio albums of the period: The Top, The Head On The Door (1985), Kiss Me Kiss Me Kiss Me (1987), Disintegration (1989), and Wish (1992), as well as the live album Concert (1984) and numerous singles.

In parallel, he produced The Sisters Of Mercy’s debut album First And Last And Always (1985, Merciful Release) — the defining document of the UK goth genre — and a string of associated singles. He also produced The Psychedelic Furs’s Book Of Days (1989, Columbia) and associated singles, Wire’s Manscape (1990, Mute), and The Chameleons’ Strange Times (1986, Geffen).

Beyond the major goth/post-punk axis, Allen worked extensively with Italian pop artists through the late 1980s and 1990s (via Metronome / Polydor Germany / Barclay), and continued producing British and European alternative acts well into the 2010s, including Buzzcocks and The Charlatans.

Italian Pop (Late 1980s-1990s)

A substantial secondary strand: Allen produced a large body of Italian-market pop through the Metronome / Polydor Germany / Barclay circuit. Key artists include Gianna Nannini (Scandalo, 1990; Giannissima, 1991; Dea, 1991; Sorridi, 1991) and various others on Ricordi and Barclay. This work sits largely outside the post-punk/goth narrative but represents a significant portion of his credit count.

Connections

  • The Cure — primary artistic partnership, 1984-1992; compilations and remixes through 2001
  • The Sisters Of Mercy — produced debut album and key singles (1984-1985)
  • Depeche Mode — engineering contributions to Black Celebration era sessions; assistant on The Singles 81-85
  • Wire — produced Manscape (1990)
  • The Chameleons — full production run from Statik through Geffen era
  • The Psychedelic Furs — produced Book Of Days (1989) and associated singles
  • The Human League — assistant engineer on Dare (1981); performer in The League Unlimited Orchestra
  • Buzzcocks — producer, engineer, mixer on The Way (2014)
  • The Charlatans — partial production on Some Friendly deluxe reissue (2010)
  • Robert Smith — closest creative partner; joint production credit across eight years of Cure output

Open Questions
