Yo-Yo Ma

French-born American cellist of Chinese descent. Born October 7, 1955, Paris, France. Real name 馬友友 (Mǎ Yǒuyǒu). Discogs artist ID: 276145.

One of the most celebrated cellists of the 20th and 21st centuries, active from 1976 to the present. Primary labels: Sony Classical, CBS Masterworks, Deutsche Grammophon. Member of The Silk Road Ensemble and Friends of Yo Yo Ma. Discography spans standard repertoire (Bach, Beethoven, Dvořák, Brahms) alongside cross-genre projects in Appalachian music, tango, Brazilian music, and film scores.

Biography by Era

Child Prodigy and Early Career (1955–1975)

Yo-Yo Ma was born in Paris to Chinese parents — his father Ma Xiaojun was a musicologist and composer. He began playing cello at age four. By age eight he appeared on American television with his sister in a concert conducted by Leonard Bernstein. The family moved to New York, and Ma graduated from Trinity School at fifteen. He studied at the Juilliard School, then entered Harvard University, where he appeared as a soloist with the Harvard-Radcliffe Orchestra. Prior to Harvard, he participated in the Marlboro Festival Orchestra under the nonagenarian cellist Pablo Casals. His primary cello, nicknamed “Petunia,” was built by Domenico Montagnana in 1733; he also plays the Davidov Stradivarius, which was previously owned by Jacqueline Du Pré.

CBS Masterworks Era (1976–1990)

Ma’s professional recording career began with a 1976 chamber music recital issued on the Marlboro Recording Society. From 1980 onward he recorded prolifically for CBS Masterworks (later CBS Records), establishing a comprehensive classical discography. Key landmarks include the complete Bach Unaccompanied Cello Suites (three-volume set, CBS Masterworks, 1983, prod. Richard Einhorn, eng. Tom Lazarus); Beethoven and Brahms sonata cycles with pianist Emanuel Ax; the Dvořák and Haydn cello concertos; the Beethoven Triple Concerto with Herbert von Karajan and the Berliner Philharmoniker (Deutsche Grammophon, 1980); and chamber music recordings with the Beaux Arts Trio.

The Bolling Suite For Cello & Jazz Piano Trio (CBS, 1984) with Claude Bolling was an early cross-genre venture, pairing Ma with a jazz pianist for a suite that bridged both worlds.

Sony Classical Era and Cross-Genre Breakthrough (1990–2002)

CBS became Sony Masterworks / Sony Classical in 1990. Ma’s output diversified markedly through the 1990s. The Hush album (Sony Masterworks, 1992) — a collaboration with vocalist Bobby McFerrin — became one of his most commercially visible recordings; it was produced jointly by McFerrin and Steve Epstein, Ma’s long-running Sony producer (341 shared releases on Discogs).

In 1996–99 Ma launched the “Inspired by Bach” series. Appalachia Waltz (Sony Classical, 1996) and Appalachian Journey (Sony Classical, 1999) — both co-credited with double bassist Edgar Meyer and violinist Mark O’Connor — placed his cello in an Americana / bluegrass context. Soul of the Tango: The Music of Astor Piazzolla (Sony Classical, 1997) explored Argentine tango with arranger Jorge Calandrelli, the Roma Sinfonietta, and pianist Gilda Buttá. The Cello Suites: Inspired by Bach (Sony Classical, 1997) paired six Bach suites with six collaborating filmmakers and choreographers including Atom Egoyan, Mark Morris, and Tamasaburo Bando.

He contributed cello solos to the Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon soundtrack (Sony Classical, 2000), composed by Tan Dun, with recording and mixing by Richard King.

Yo-Yo Ma Plays The Music Of John Williams (Sony Classical, 2002) — co-credited with John Williams (4) as conductor — featured arrangements of Williams’s film themes performed with the Los Angeles Recording Arts Orchestra. See release ID 6128453.

Yo-Yo Ma Plays Ennio Morricone (Sony Classical, 2004) — co-credited with Ennio Morricone as composer, orchestrator, conductor, and producer — was recorded with the Roma Sinfonietta in Rome, engineered by Fabio Venturi.

The Silk Road Project (1998–present)

In 1998 Ma founded the Silk Road Project, an initiative to explore cultural traditions along the historic Silk Road trade routes. The project resulted in The Silk Road Ensemble, a flexible group of musicians from Central Asia, the Middle East, East Asia, Europe, and North America. Recordings include:

  • Silk Road Journeys: When Strangers Meet (Sony Classical, 2001, prod. Steve Epstein, eng. Richard King)
  • Silk Road Journeys: Beyond the Horizon (Sony Classical, 2004)
  • Various live and themed recordings through Sony Classical and CSO Resound

The ensemble includes recurring members such as Wu Man (pipa), Kojiro Umezaki (shakuhachi), Kayhan Kalhor (kamancheh), and Sandeep Das (tabla).

Later Career and Deutsche Grammophon (2008–present)

Ma returned to Bach’s Cello Suites for Six Evolutions – Bach: Cello Suites (Sony Classical, 2018), produced by Steve Epstein and engineered by Richard King. The title frames each suite as a meditation on personal growth.

The Goat Rodeo Sessions (Sony Classical, 2011) — with Stuart Duncan (fiddle), Edgar Meyer (bass), and Chris Thile (mandolin) — extended his roots-music collaborations; a live sequel followed in 2012 and Not Our First Goat Rodeo in 2020.

Songs Of Joy & Peace (Sony Classical, 2008) was a holiday album featuring diverse collaborators. Sing Me Home (Masterworks, 2016) and A Playlist Without Borders (Masterworks, 2013) continued his cross-cultural programming.

He has appeared on several Deutsche Grammophon releases since 2015, including Beethoven Triple Concertos and the Shostakovich Symphonies · Concertos · Lady Macbeth set (2025). Songs Of Comfort & Hope (2020), a duo album with pianist Kathryn Stott, was recorded and released during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Ma was named UN Peace Ambassador in 2006 and has served on the US President’s Committee on the Arts and Humanities since 2009. He received the National Medal of Arts in 2001 and the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2011.
