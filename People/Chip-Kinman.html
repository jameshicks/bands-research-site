Chip Kinman

Chip Kinman

Aug 17, 202610 min read

Chip Kinman

Discogs ID: 408496
Also credited as: C. Kinman; H. Kinman; Kinman; Chip

American guitarist, vocalist, and songwriter. Co-founder of The Dils (San Diego/LA punk, 1977–1980) and Rank & File (cowpunk, 1981–1987). Brother and lifelong creative partner of Tony Kinman (died May 2018). Held virtually all songwriting credits alongside Tony across every phase of their shared career. After Tony’s death, Chip has continued releasing music under several names — most recently solo on In The Red Recordings (2022, 2025).

Biography

The Dils (1977–1980)

Chip Kinman and his brother Tony Kinman formed The Dils in Carlsbad, San Diego County, California, before relocating to San Francisco and then Los Angeles. The Dils cycled through several drummer lineups — including Endre Alqover, Rand McNally, Bill Chobotar (“Zippy Pinhead”), and John Silvers — but Chip (guitar, vocals) and Tony (bass, vocals) formed the consistent core in every configuration. All material was credited to “Kinman and Kinman.”

Their debut 7” “I Hate The Rich” / “You’re Not Blank” (What Records?, 1977) was recorded with drummer Endre Alqover and is one of the essential documents of first-wave American punk. The follow-up Dangerhouse 7”, 198 Seconds of The Dils (1977), captured Chip and Tony with drummer Rand McNally; it contains “Class War” and “Mr. Big,” which became their signature tracks. The band were often compared to The Clash for their overtly political stance.

The final Dils release was the three-sided 12” EP Made In Canada (Rogelletti Records, 1980), recorded in Vancouver with drummer Bill Chobotar and co-produced by Bob Rock and Gerry Barad (alongside the band themselves). The record moved toward melody and country-tinged rock — a direct preview of where the brothers were heading. The Dils dissolved in 1980.

Rank & File (1981–1987)

After The Dils dissolved, Chip and Tony Kinman relocated to Austin, Texas and co-founded Rank & File with Alejandro Escovedo (formerly of The Nuns) and drummer Slim Evans. Rank & File are widely credited as one of the founding acts of cowpunk — marrying punk directness with Bakersfield-style country and Everly Brothers harmonies.

Sundown (Slash, 1982), produced and engineered by David Kahne, was the debut LP. Chip played guitar, vocals, and harmonica; Tony played bass and vocals; Escovedo played guitar and vocals. The bulk of songwriting was credited “Kinman and Kinman.” Escovedo departed after this record, going on to form The True Believers in Austin.

The second LP, Long Gone Dead (Slash / Metronome, 1984), was produced by Jeff Eyrich. Chip added six-string bass to his instrumental duties. Session players expanded the palette considerably: Stan Lynch (then also drumming for Tom Petty & The Heartbreakers) played drums and percussion; Richard Greene contributed fiddle; Jeff Ross played guitar; Pete Grant (credited as “Peter Grant”) handled pedal steel, slide guitar, autoharp, and banjo; Billy Meyers added woodwind. Tony and Chip remained the sole songwriters.

The third LP, Rank And File (Zensor, Germany, 1987), found the band stripped back to the Kinmans plus Jeff Ross on guitar. A live album (Live!, Triple X Records, 1987) and an earlier UK single (“Rank And File,” Rough Trade/Slash, 1984) also appeared. The band dissolved after 1987.

A career retrospective, The Slash Years (Rhino Handmade, 2003), collected the Slash-era output. Chip is credited as “Reissue Producer” on that release.

Blackbird (1987–1993)

Following Rank & File’s dissolution, Chip and Tony Kinman formed Blackbird (295001), an electronic duo that represented a sharp stylistic break. The debut EP Blackbird (Iloki Records, 1987) was co-produced by the brothers and Ric Wallace. Further releases followed on Iloki (1989), Fundamental (1990, Europe), and Scotti Bros. Records (1992).

The Scotti Bros. era produced Class War (1992) — a direct callback to the Dils track — which received industrial remixes by KMFDM (Class War: The KMFDM Remixes, Scotti Bros., 1992). A single “Take Me” also appeared on Fully Radical / Scotti Bros. in 1992. A final Blackbird release — “Big Train / Waiting For The Man” (Iloki, 1993) — closes out the project.

All Blackbird material was written by Chip and Tony Kinman; the brothers co-produced and performed all parts.

El Vez’s Memphis Mariachis (1991–2000)

From 1991, Chip Kinman appeared as a member of The Memphis Mariachis, the large rotating backing ensemble for Mexican-American rock satirist El Vez (Robert López). The Memphis Mariachis also included Slim Evans (the Rank & File drummer), Jon Wahl, Kristian Hoffman, Garrett Ray, Paul Morris, Rami Jaffee, and many others — the group’s membership was fluid and varied by tour and record.

Chip is credited on guitar across multiple El Vez releases on Sympathy For The Record Industry and Poptones: “The Mexican Elvis” (1991), “Not Hispanic” (1992), “Fun In Español” (1994), Graciasland (1994), Merry MeX-mas (1994), El Vez Is Alive (1995), G.I. Ay, Ay! Blues (1996, also backing vocals), T C B (1996), NoElVezSi (2000), and Pure Aztec Gold (2000, Poptones UK).

Cowboy Nation (1996–2000)

By the mid-1990s the Kinmans had shifted again, this time toward stripped-back country and folk as Cowboy Nation (1557960). The self-titled LP (Cortex, Australia, 1996) was self-produced; Chip played guitar and harmonica and shared vocals with Tony (bass, dobro bass). Drummer ‘Taco’ John Norman completed the core lineup. Repertoire mixed originals with traditional arrangements. A second release, A Journey Out Of Time (Shanachie / Western Jubilee Recording Company, 2000), followed.

Post-Tony Solo Work (2008–present)

After Tony’s death in May 2018, Chip continued releasing music under several projects:

  • Chip Kinman and PCH (Discogs ID: 6611016) — released My First Punk Rock Record (Fundamental, 2008)
  • Ford Madox Ford (2) (Discogs ID: 6327606) — released “Expect It / Before The Fall” (Porterhouse Records, 2016)
  • The Tonys (2037663) — Some Things Never Change (Restless Empire, 2021), posthumous release drawing on compositions by both brothers
  • Solo as Chip Kinman — The Great Confrontation (In The Red Recordings, 2022; Discogs master 2729084) and a self-titled LP Chip Kinman (In The Red Records, 2025; Discogs master 4093600)

The 2022 and 2025 In The Red releases mark Chip’s most fully solo phase. No additional credits appear in Discogs for those records beyond Chip as primary artist.
