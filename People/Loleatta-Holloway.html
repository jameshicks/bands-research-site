Loleatta Holloway

Loleatta Holloway

Sep 27, 202610 min read

Loleatta Holloway

Discogs ID: 17413
Born: November 5, 1946, Chicago, Illinois. Died: March 21, 2011, Chicago, Illinois (heart failure, age 64).

Chicago-born soul, gospel, and disco vocalist. One of the most sampled female voices in popular music — The Independent identified her as the most sampled female singer in popular music. Major commercial presence in the late 1970s–1980s on Gold Mind Records (a Salsoul Records imprint). Her 1980 recording “Love Sensation” (Gold Mind Records), recorded and mixed at Blank Tape Studios by Bob Blank, became one of the most-sampled tracks in house music history. She is ranked the 95th most successful dance artist of all time by Billboard. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

Biography

Gospel Roots (1946–1970)

Holloway grew up singing gospel in Chicago. She began performing with her mother in the Holloway Community Singers. From approximately 1967 to 1971 she sang alongside Albertina Walker as a member of the Caravans, a prominent Chicago gospel ensemble. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

In 1971 she recorded “Rainbow ‘71,” a Curtis Mayfield composition. Producer Floyd Smith took an interest in her work; Smith would become her manager and later her husband.

Aware Records (1973–1975)

In the early 1970s Holloway signed with Aware Records, an Atlanta-based soul label owned by Michael Thevis. She recorded two albums there, both produced by Floyd Smith:

  • Loleatta (1973)
  • Cry to Me (1975)

Her single “Cry to Me” — a remake of a Solomon Burke hit — reached number 10 on the R&B chart and number 68 on the Hot 100. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

Gold Mind / Salsoul Years (1976–1982)

Philadelphia producer Norman Harris signed Holloway in 1976 to Gold Mind Records, a new subsidiary of Salsoul Records. Her debut Gold Mind single, “Worn Out Broken Heart” (from the 1977 LP Loleatta), reached number 25 on the R&B chart; its B-side “Dreaming” climbed to number 72 on the pop chart, establishing her as a disco performer. In 1978 the ballad “Only You,” produced by Bunny Sigler, reached number 11 on the R&B chart. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

Love Sensation (1980)

Holloway contributed vocals to Dan Hartman’s track “Relight My Fire.” Hartman then wrote and produced the title track of her 1980 album Love Sensation. According to Wikipedia’s “Love Sensation” article, Hartman originally envisioned either Bette Midler or Patti LaBelle for the song; after seeing Holloway perform at a nightclub he approached her instead. During recording, “Hartman had her perform the song 29 times over a two-day recording session.” Holloway lost her voice during the sessions and recovered by adding Vicks VapoRub to her coffee. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

The track was recorded and mixed at Blank Tape Studios by Bob Blank. “Love Sensation” spent a week at no. 1 on the US Hot Dance Club Play chart in September 1980.

The Black Box / “Ride on Time” Dispute (1989–c.1990)

The Unauthorized Sample

Italian house producers Black Box (Daniele Davoli, Mirko Limoni, Valerio Semplici) based their 1989 single “Ride on Time” on a sampled a cappella 12-inch of Holloway’s “Love Sensation” that Davoli had purchased in New York City. The sample was used without clearance or credit. At the time, Davoli mistakenly believed Holloway was deceased and that sampling under two seconds was legal without permission. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

“Ride on Time” topped the UK singles chart for six weeks and became the best-selling UK single of 1989. A model, Katrin Quinol, mimed Holloway’s vocals in all television appearances and promotional materials — Holloway received no credit. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25; TikTok/70s80s90sthrowback)

Legal Action and the Reissue

Copyright owners took legal action. The UK label Deconstruction Records grew concerned over clearing the sample from Salsoul Records, which owned the master recording. BMG, which owned Deconstruction, arranged for a re-recorded vocal version. The replacement vocals were recorded within approximately an hour by session singer Heather Small (later famous as the vocalist of M People), who was paid a flat fee and was not initially told what the recording was for or who originally sang the part. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

The reissued version (with Small’s vocals) was what officially charted, though radio stations continued playing the original Holloway-voiced version. Katrin Quinol continued to mime the vocals on television throughout the single’s run. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

Settlement and Financial Outcome

Dan Hartman, as songwriter of “Love Sensation,” initially requested one-third of the royalties. Though Black Box initially resisted, Davoli later acknowledged that Hartman “could have asked for 100%” and called him “a true gentleman.” An out-of-court settlement was eventually reached. (NME, 2019)

However, according to Davoli’s 2018 account, BMG’s legal team separately purchased the rights to the sample from Salsoul for $5,000 and 1% of royalties — and Holloway herself was not party to that negotiation and apparently received nothing personally from it. Davoli stated: “She never saw a penny” and expressed regret that he never had the chance to meet and apologize to Holloway before her death. (NME, 2019; WhoSampled)

Holloway herself was vocal about her frustration: “I’ve been around for years trying to get this one hit record. It annoyed me knowing that Black Box were number one and I was not getting any credit for it.” (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

Note: Wikipedia’s “Ride on Time” article states that Black Box “eventually purchased the sampling rights in 2018” — this appears to refer to a retroactive or supplemental rights deal, distinct from the original BMG/$5,000 Salsoul settlement. The precise timeline and terms of any 2018 agreement are not fully documented in available sources.

Holloway and Live Performance of “Ride on Time”

No documented evidence exists that Holloway ever performed “Ride on Time” live — on stage, on television, or in any other public setting. All TV appearances during the single’s run used Katrin Quinol miming to Holloway’s sampled vocals; Holloway was entirely absent from the promotional circuit. Multiple sources note that Holloway was eventually billed as “the voice of Black Box” after the settlement, but this was a credit designation rather than a live performance arrangement. Her own frustration makes the absence plain: she complained that Black Box were paid more for performances than she was, confirming she was not appearing alongside them. (Wikipedia — Ride on Time; Eats, Drinks & Leaves; NME, 2019)

The contrast with her 1991 Marky Mark collaboration is pointed: when she received a proper credited feature on “Good Vibrations,” she appeared in the music video and performed beside Wahlberg at live dates — the standard treatment she was denied by Black Box. (Wikipedia — Good Vibrations, as of 2025-06-25)

Web research conducted 2026-06-26; no evidence found in setlist.fm, Wikipedia, AllMusic, NME, or general search results of any live performance of “Ride on Time” by Holloway.

Industry Impact

The Black Box controversy was part of a broader reckoning with uncredited sampling and miming in pop music that culminated with the Milli Vanilli scandal (1990). It directly influenced how the industry subsequently handled sample credits and artist attribution.

Later Career

Marky Mark and Beyond (1991–2000s)

Holloway’s highest US pop chart position came when Marky Mark and the Funky Bunch featured her vocals — billed and credited — on “Good Vibrations” (1991, Interscope Records, US #1). (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

Throughout the 1990s and 2000s she continued recording dance tracks, including collaborations with Cappella, GTS, and Martin. “Love Sensation ‘06” — a re-recording/update of the original track — reached number 37 on the UK Singles Chart, demonstrating her continued relevance in dance music decades on. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)

Death

Holloway died on March 21, 2011, in Chicago from heart failure at age 64. She had four children with ex-husband Floyd Smith. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-06-25)
