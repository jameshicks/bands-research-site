Richard Heslop

Oct 14, 20264 min read

Richard Heslop

Born: 1961, London Role: Film director, music video director, visual artist Education: BA Honours Film/Fine Art, St. Martins School of Art and Design (graduated 1984)

Brother of Tom Heslop (23 Skidoo founding vocalist).

Connection to 23 Skidoo

Heslop directed all visuals for 23 Skidoo’s Seven Songs / Tranquiliser I & II (Doublevision, DV 06, 1984) — the band’s VHS/Beta video release, credited “Film Director [All Visuals].” This is the direct fraternal link between Tom Heslop (dismissed from 23 Skidoo June 1982) and the band’s continuing visual output.

Derek Jarman period

After graduating St. Martins in 1984, Heslop worked as camera operator on Derek Jarman’s experimental film Imagining October (1984) — shot in the Soviet Union, a response to Thatcher’s Britain. He worked as cameraman on several Jarman films in this period.

”The Queen is Dead” (1986)

“The Queen Is Dead: A Film by Derek Jarman” (1986) is a 13-minute Super 8 triptych commissioned mid-1986 for three Smiths tracks — “The Queen is Dead,” “There Is a Light That Never Goes Out,” and “Panic” — originally presented at the Edinburgh Festival. The footage was transferred to video and blown up to 35mm.

Credited directors (composite film): Derek Jarman, Richard Heslop, John Maybury — all three carry directorial credit on the triptych as a whole. [source: Letterboxd, TMDB, IMDb tt1171261]

Per-segment credits (where resolvable):

  • “There Is a Light That Never Goes Out” — directed by John Maybury [source: IMVDb]
  • “Panic” (triptych segment) — directed by Derek Jarman [source: IMVDb, Wikipedia – Panic]
  • “The Queen is Dead” (triptych segment) — no source found that assigns this to Heslop or any single director separately from the composite credit
  • Heslop’s specific segment within the film is not identified by any source found. His directorial credit is confirmed for the composite work but not segment-attributed.

Other confirmed credits:

  • Director of Photography / Cinematographer: Christopher Hughes (credited as “Phot.” on the composite film) [source: Senses of Cinema, TMDB, Letterboxd]
  • Editor: Sally Yeadon
  • Producers: Yvonne Little, James Mackay, Mayo Thompson, Peter Walmsley
  • Production company: Basilisk Communications

Note on “Chris Hughes” credit: Christopher Hughes’s role is cinematographer, not director. The four-name grouping “Jarman / Maybury / Heslop / Chris Hughes” therefore splits into three co-directors plus one DoP. [source: Letterboxd crew list as of 2025-06-30]

Note on overall credit: The official release title is “A Film by Derek Jarman,” and most critical sources treat it as a Jarman work. Senses of Cinema (2024) does not mention Heslop or Maybury by name, attributing the artistic vision entirely to Jarman. [source: Senses of Cinema, as of 2026-06-30]

Music video career

From “The Queen is Dead” onward, Heslop built a substantial commercial music video career while retaining the moody, sepia-toned film aesthetic developed in the Jarman orbit.
