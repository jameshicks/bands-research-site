Oct 20, 202610 min read

Tony Doogan

Producer and engineer from Glasgow, Scotland. Discogs artist ID: 252939. Active from 1990 onward, principally associated with the Glasgow indie scene — his two longest-running client relationships are with Belle & Sebastian and Mogwai, both of whom he has worked with across multiple decades. Also a member of the group Grey Dogs (Discogs artist ID: 8018447).

Name variations on credits: Mr. Tony Doogan, T. Doogan, Tony, Tony Doogans, Tony Dougan.

Background

Doogan began as an assistant engineer in the early 1990s, accumulating credits on records at Scottish and UK studios before stepping into production roles by the late 1990s. His first significant production work came via the Glasgow scene centred on Jeepster Recordings and Chemikal Underground. By 2000 he was co-producing or sole-producing Belle & Sebastian albums, and that association extended through the full run of their Jeepster and then Rough Trade output. In parallel he became the primary recording engineer and eventually co-producer for Mogwai, a relationship that has continued into the 2020s.

He has been managed under the “Breathing Protection” banner (with Peter Shershin listed as manager on several mid-2000s credits), which appears on Mogwai and Anathema releases from that period.

Early Engineering Work (1991–1997)

Doogan’s earliest Discogs credits are assistant engineering roles on releases well outside Glasgow: the Teenage Fanclub album Thirteen (1993, DGC), Dinosaur Jr’s Mobile Safari EP (1994, Domino), and a handful of Scottish and UK indie releases on Fire Records, Heavenly, and Chrysalis in the mid-1990s. The Thirteen credit in particular suggests he was working at a studio where major-label sessions were being tracked.

Between 1994 and 1996 he engineered or recorded the complete Fire Records output of Telstar Ponies — all five singles/EPs and both albums (Maps And Starcharts, Her Name, Not Even Starcrossed, In The Space Of A Few Minutes, Brewery Of Eggshells, Does Your Heart Have Wings?, and Voices From The New Music). This was his first sustained engineering relationship with a single act.

By 1997 he was credited as recording engineer on Belle & Sebastian’s If You’re Feeling Sinister (Japanese reissue pressing) and on the Jeepster singles “3.. 6.. 9 Seconds Of Light,” “Lazy Line Painter Jane,” and “This Is Just A Modern Rock Song” — placing him in the Cava Studios orbit at the point when B&S were at their early commercial peak.

Belle & Sebastian (1997–2006)

Doogan’s most sustained body of work in this period is with Belle & Sebastian. He progressed from recording engineer on their Jeepster singles to co-producer and then sole producer across their studio albums.

Fold Your Hands Child (2000) marks the first album where Doogan is credited as producer alongside the band. Storytelling (2002) was recorded partly in New York (at The Magic Shop and Water Music) with Doogan editing the result. Dear Catastrophe Waitress (2003) was primarily produced by Trevor Horn, with Doogan contributing additional mixing. Push Barman To Open Old Wounds (2005) is a compilation collecting the Jeepster-era singles — Doogan is credited as engineer and producer on most of the tracks he originally worked on.

Stevie Jackson and Mick Cooke are among the consistent B&S personnel across these sessions.

Mountain Goats — Tallahassee (2002)

Doogan co-produced The Mountain Goats’ Tallahassee (2002, 4AD) alongside John Darnielle, also handling recording and mixing. This was the album that moved the Mountain Goats from lo-fi cassette recordings into studio production. Peter Hughes (2) played bass, guitar, harmony vocals, drums, and keyboards. Michael Ivins (of the Flaming Lips) assisted on recording and mixing. Art direction was by Vaughan Oliver.

The pre-album single See America Right (2002, 4AD) carries the same co-producer / recorded by / mixed by credits for tracks 1 and 2, making it the first Mountain Goats release Doogan worked on.

Note: the subsequent Mountain Goats album We Shall All Be Healed (2004, 4AD) was not produced by Doogan — it was recorded by John Vanderslice and Scott Solter at Vanderslice’s Tiny Telephone studio in San Francisco.

Mogwai (1998–2024)

Doogan’s relationship with Mogwai is the other anchor of his career, running from 1998’s Peloton EP through to 2024.

Glasgow Eyes (2024) is notable because Doogan appears as a credited performer (piano, programmed electronics) alongside his production role — a departure from his usual behind-the-console function.

The Fountain (2006) placed him alongside composer Clint Mansell and the Kronos Quartet; he handled Mogwai’s contribution to the score while other engineers covered the orchestral and choir recording.

Doogan produced and engineered for The Delgados, another key Chemikal Underground act.

Universal Audio (2004) was the Delgados’ final album before the band dissolved and Emma Pollock and Paul Savage each moved into solo work.

Arab Strap (2001)

Credited on Arab Strap’s The Smell Of Outdoor Cooking EP and compilation Y’All Get Scared Now, Ya Hear! (2001, Bright Star) and When It’s All Over We Still Have To Clear Up (2001) in a recorded by / mixed by capacity. Also recorded their American Trilogy EP (2000, Chemikal Underground).

Other Chemikal Underground Work

Doogan worked across much of the Chemikal Underground roster in the late 1990s and early 2000s, handling engineering and mixing on releases by Aereogramme (EP, 1999; Y’All Get Scared Now, Ya Hear!, 2001) and assisting on recordings by Mogwai and the Delgados through the label’s peak years. The label’s house studio connection appears to run through Cava Studios in Glasgow.

Malcolm Middleton (2007)

After Arab Strap dissolved, Doogan produced Malcolm Middleton’s solo album A Brighter Beat (2007, Full Time Hobby) and associated singles “A Brighter Beat / Point Of Light,” “Beep Beep, I Love You,” and “We’re All Going To Die” — all on Full Time Hobby.

The Twilight Sad (2015–2019)

Doogan has production and engineering credits on The Twilight Sad releases in the mid-to-late 2010s, including work on their Rock Action output.

Belle & Sebastian Adjacent / Solo Work

  • Stevie Jackson solo: engineered and mixed “(I Can’t Get No) Stevie Jackson” (2011, Banchory)
  • Stuart Murdoch / God Help The Girl: adjacent to his B&S work
  • Bill Wells: Doogan’s name appears in the wider Glasgow scene documentation

Anathema (2017)

Produced, recorded, and mixed The Optimist (2017, Kscope) for Anathema, working at Castle Of Doom (Glasgow) and Attica Audio. The credit on that release also lists him under Breathing Protection management. This is one of his more prominent credits outside the Glasgow indie axis, working with a long-established UK progressive rock/metal band.

Open Questions

  • The Discogs profile notes Doogan “started in 1990” but the earliest credits in the DB date from 1991. Where he trained and which studio(s) he was based at in the earliest years is not confirmed from Discogs data alone.
  • We Shall All Be Healed (2004) is commonly attributed to Doogan in secondary sources, but Discogs credits John Vanderslice and Scott Solter as recorders with no Doogan credit — this discrepancy would benefit from web verification.
  • His group Grey Dogs (Discogs ID: 8018447) has no release credits in the DB; nature of that project is unclear.
  • Whether his piano/electronics appearance on Mogwai’s Glasgow Eyes (2024) reflects a broader performing role or was a one-off is not evident from Discogs alone.
