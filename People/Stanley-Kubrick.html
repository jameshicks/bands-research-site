Stanley Kubrick

American film director, writer, producer, and photographer. Born July 26, 1928, Manhattan, New York City. Died March 7, 1999, Harpenden, Hertfordshire, England. Discogs artist ID: 200975. Name variation: Kubrick.

In the Discogs film music context, Kubrick appears as director and/or producer on soundtrack releases spanning 1960–1976 — from Spartacus (scored by Alex North) through the pre-existing classical compilations he assembled for 2001: A Space Odyssey and Barry Lyndon, and the Walter Carlos electronic scores for A Clockwork Orange. His approach to music in film is one of his most documented characteristics: he consistently preferred sourcing and shaping existing recordings over commissioning original scores (with Spartacus as the principal exception), and his rejection of Alex North’s commissioned 2001 score is one of the defining episodes in Hollywood soundtrack history.

Discogs Credits

Kubrick’s seven credited releases on Discogs cover four films. His roles range from Film Director through Film Producer, Screenwriter, and (on 2001) Producer of the soundtrack album itself.

Spartacus (1960)

Release ID 3220879 — Spartacus (The Sound Track Album), Decca, 1960 (US). 7 pressings on Discogs master 241960.

The first and most conventional of his Discogs credits. Alex North composed and conducted the full orchestral score. The LP credits also list Kirk Douglas (2) as Executive Producer and Performer, Dalton Trumbo as Screenwriter, and Kubrick as Film Director. The score is North’s most celebrated work and the soundtrack has remained in print continuously.

2001: A Space Odyssey (1968)

Release ID 1893908 — 2001: A Space Odyssey (Music From The Motion Picture Sound Track), MGM Records, 1968 (US). 13 pressings on Discogs master 88539.

Unusually, Kubrick is credited here as Producer of the soundtrack album rather than Film Director — reflecting his direct control over the music selection and album preparation. The primary credit is “Various,” the music being assembled from pre-existing classical recordings (Richard Strauss, Johann Strauss II, Ligeti, Khachaturian) rather than an original score. Jesse Kaye and the MGM Studio Sound Department are credited as technicians who prepared the album.

The commissioned but rejected score by Alex North — recorded in late 1967, with Henry Brant as orchestrator — is documented on separate releases: Alex North’s 2001 (Varèse Sarabande, 1993, release ID 1836321, conducted by Jerry Goldsmith) and Music For 2001: A Space Odyssey (Intrada, 2007, release ID 3510239, from the original recordings). Neither of those releases credits Kubrick.

A Clockwork Orange (1971 film; 1972/1976 LP releases)

Two Discogs releases document the A Clockwork Orange soundtrack:

Release ID 3673343 — Arancia Meccanica, CBS, 1972 (Italy). Discogs master 398771. Primary artist: Walter Carlos (Wendy Carlos). Kubrick credited as Film Director.

Release ID 773329 — Stanley Kubrick’s A Clockwork Orange, Warner Bros. Records, 1976 (Italy; reissue). Discogs master 76290. Primary credit: Various. Kubrick credited as Film Director, Film Producer, and Screenwriter (Screenplay Based on Anthony Burgess’s novel). Rachel Elkind produced the Walter Carlos electronic tracks (A1, A3, A5, A6, B3, B7). Joe Boyd supervised the production.

The score is a mix of Walter Carlos’s Moog synthesizer arrangements and performances of classical works (Beethoven, Purcell, Rossini, Elgar), plus source music. The soundtrack’s eclectic classical-meets-electronic character — and Kubrick’s precise scene-to-music matching — made A Clockwork Orange a touchstone for the use of pre-existing music in film.

Barry Lyndon (1975)

Release ID 11587932 — Barry Lyndon (Music From The Academy Award Winning Soundtrack), Warner Bros. Records, 1975 (Belgium). Discogs master 74155. Primary credit: Various. Kubrick credited as Film Director, Writer (Written For The Screen By), and Producer.

Like 2001, the Barry Lyndon score is assembled entirely from pre-existing works — Handel’s Sarabande (performed by the National Philharmonic Orchestra, conducted by Leonard Rosenman), Irish traditional music performed by The Chieftains (including members Paddy Moloney and Seán Potts), Mozart, Schubert, Vivaldi, Bach. Jan Harlan (Kubrick’s brother-in-law and longtime executive producer) is credited as Executive Producer. William Makepeace Thackeray credited for the source novel.

The film won the Academy Award for Original Score — a fact the LP title announces — awarded to Rosenman for his adaptations of the Handel Sarabande rather than original composition.

Other Discogs Appearances

Two later credits fall outside the soundtrack context:

  • Release ID 1533476 — Love Song / Play With Fire (Crazy Mannequin Records, 1988, Italy) — credited for Artwork (Front Sleeve). Kubrick appears here as a visual artist, not a filmmaker. The primary artist on this release is not a Kubrick film connection; this is an anomalous credit.
  • Release ID 10949009 — Spent The Day In Bed by Morrissey (BMG/Étienne, 2017, Europe) — credited for Photography By. Kubrick’s photographic work (he was a professional photographer before becoming a director) appears to have been licensed for this single sleeve.

Music and Film: Kubrick’s Approach

Across the four film soundtracks documented on Discogs, a clear pattern emerges:

Pre-existing classical music as score. 2001, A Clockwork Orange, and Barry Lyndon all use assembled pre-existing recordings rather than commissioned scores. Kubrick edited his films to existing recordings during production, then retained that music in the final cut. This approach gave him total control over the music-image relationship and produced some of the most recognized uses of classical music in cinema.

Rejection of conventional scoring. The commissioning and rejection of Alex North’s 2001 score is the most dramatic instance of this pattern. North completed the sessions; Kubrick never informed him of the decision not to use the music. North did not learn the score had been rejected until he attended the premiere. The 1993 Varèse Sarabande recording of Alex North’s 2001 — produced by Jerry Goldsmith as a statement of the film music community’s regard for North — stands as the primary documentary record of what might have been.

Electronic and synthetic sound. A Clockwork Orange introduced Walter Carlos and the Moog synthesizer into a Kubrick film; the electronic arrangements of Beethoven and Purcell were as central as the classical originals. Carlos (later Wendy Carlos, following gender transition in the 1970s) produced the electronic tracks with Rachel Elkind.

Spartacus as exception. The 1960 film, produced by Kirk Douglas (2)‘s company, was Kubrick’s largest commercial assignment of that period. The North score was central to the film and represents Kubrick’s most straightforward use of an original commissioned score. Kubrick famously had a difficult relationship with Douglas during the production and later distanced himself from the film.

Connections

  • Alex North — commissioned for Spartacus (1960, North’s most celebrated score); commissioned and silently rejected for 2001 (1968)
  • Kirk Douglas (2) — star and executive producer of Spartacus; strained relationship during production
  • Dalton Trumbo — screenwriter of Spartacus (Trumbo was the first writer to receive an on-screen credit after the Hollywood blacklist, via this film)
  • Walter Carlos — composer/performer for A Clockwork Orange; Carlos later transitioned and is known as Wendy Carlos
  • Rachel Elkind — co-producer (with Carlos) of the A Clockwork Orange electronic tracks
  • Joe Boyd — supervised the A Clockwork Orange LP production
  • Anthony Burgess — author of the source novel for A Clockwork Orange
  • Leonard Rosenman — conductor and adapter of classical music for Barry Lyndon; Academy Award recipient for the score
  • Jan Harlan — executive producer on Barry Lyndon; Kubrick’s brother-in-law and longtime producer
  • The Chieftains — performed Irish traditional tracks on the Barry Lyndon soundtrack
  • National Philharmonic Orchestra — performed Handel’s Sarabande for Barry Lyndon
  • Jerry Goldsmith — conducted the 1993 premiere recording of Alex North’s rejected 2001 score (Varèse Sarabande)

Open Questions

  • The Discogs credit on Love Song / Play With Fire (1988, Crazy Mannequin Records) as Artwork (Front Sleeve) — the specific image and its context are not documented in Discogs beyond the credit line.
  • The Spent The Day In Bed Morrissey single (2017) photography credit — which specific Kubrick photograph was used, and whether it was from his pre-filmmaking career as a professional photographer, is not documented in Discogs.
  • Kubrick’s full filmography (including The Killing, Paths of Glory, Lolita, Dr. Strangelove, The Shining, Full Metal Jacket, Eyes Wide Shut) is outside the vault’s scope — these films have associated soundtracks but Kubrick is not credited on their Discogs releases in this database.
