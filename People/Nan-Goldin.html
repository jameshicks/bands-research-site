Nan Goldin

Photographer. Boston-born, active New York from c.1978. Discogs ID: 2254494. Real name: Nancy Goldin.

Documentary photographer best known for The Ballad of Sexual Dependency (slideshow/photobook), which recorded downtown New York’s queer, transgender, and drug subcultures from the late 1970s through the AIDS crisis. Met Adele Bertei at a Cleveland gay bar before either was in New York; Goldin was already establishing herself in the downtown Manhattan scene when Bertei arrived in 1977. Goldin is credited in Bertei’s memoir No New York: A Memoir of No Wave and the Women Who Shaped the Scene (Beacon Press / Faber & Faber, March 2026), which includes 55 rarely seen photographs from her personal archive and centers on Goldin, Lydia Lunch, Barbara Kruger, Kiki Smith, and others alongside Adele Bertei.

No Wave Connections (Web research)

Goldin arrived in New York in 1978 and was quickly embedded in the downtown scene spanning no wave cinema, post-punk clubs, and post-Stonewall queer culture. She is consistently named — alongside Beth B, Lydia Lunch, Pat Place, and James Chance — as a core figure in the no wave avant-garde collective. (Pirelli HangarBicocca event page, 2025)

Vivienne Dick

Goldin’s most documented no-wave-adjacent relationship is with filmmaker Vivienne Dick. Dick is described as having “introduced Goldin to the filmmakers of No Wave.” (Senses of Cinema, 2016) Dick was also a photographic subject for Goldin — her portrait ‘Vivienne in the green dress, NYC’ appeared in The Ballad of Sexual Dependency. (Last Bohemians / Substack) Goldin appeared in Dick’s 1980 film Liberty’s Booty, which documented sex work subcultures in late-1970s New York. (Pirelli HangarBicocca) The two artists held a joint screening-and-conversation event at Pirelli HangarBicocca in December 2025.

Dick’s no-wave-circle films (Super-8, late 1970s–early 1980s) also featured Lydia Lunch as a recurring actress. Goldin and Dick’s scenes overlapped: the 1980 film Empty Suitcases (dir. Bette Gordon, not Dick) features Goldin photographing Dick, and the film includes Pat Place, Cynthia Sley (Bush Tetras vocalist), musician Felice Rosser, and Lydia Lunch as subjects. (Senses of Cinema, 2016)

Goldin is described in the Senses of Cinema piece as “firmly enmeshed in the No Wave scene, appearing in films and shooting production stills for Dick and Gordon” and providing associated documentation for Bette Gordon’s feature Variety (1983) as exhibited in Ballad of Sexual Dependency. (Senses of Cinema, 2016)

Lydia Lunch

Goldin and Lydia Lunch had a documented personal and professional friendship. Key evidence:

  • Photograph, 1985: Goldin photographed Lunch at the Russian baths on 10th Street, East Village, for a Village Voice fashion insert called Vue (“Masculine/Feminine” editorial). The image was not published in the final layout and was first presented publicly as a limited-edition print at The Kitchen’s 2018 Spring Gala, which honored both women. (New Art Editions; The Kitchen OnScreen)
  • The Kitchen, 2018: The Kitchen Spring Gala honored Goldin and Lunch together, acknowledging their shared contributions to “post-punk New York art during the late 1980s and early 1990s.” (The Kitchen)

Album cover — resolved: The Wikipedia article for Drowning in Limbo (last edited 23 May 2025) lists only two personnel credits: J. G. Thirlwell (remastering, design) and Annie Sprinkle (photography). Nan Goldin is not credited anywhere on that page. The Wikipedia Personnel section was confirmed by direct browser render of the live article. (Wikipedia — Drowning in Limbo, as of 2025-05-23)

Secondary sources (artnet listing summary, New Art Editions page) that attribute the cover photograph to Goldin appear to be misattributions. The likely source of confusion is that Goldin did photograph Lunch (the 1985 Russian baths image), and both are associated with the same downtown New York scene, but the cover of Drowning in Limbo is credited to Annie Sprinkle — not Goldin — per the sleeve credits as reflected in Wikipedia’s Personnel section.

Note: physical sleeve access was not confirmed independently — Discogs was inaccessible (Cloudflare block) during this research run. Wikipedia’s Personnel section is the best available secondary source short of a physical sleeve scan.

Ballad of Sexual Dependency — slideshow screenings in no wave spaces

The slideshow was performed live at the Mudd Club and Rafik’s, with Goldin running 35mm slides while audiences included the downtown scene. One early performance accompanied Frank Zappa’s birthday party at the Mudd Club; another was accompanied live by the Del Byzanteens (featuring Jim Jarmusch). The slideshow soundtrack included Maria Callas, The Velvet Underground, and other choices shaped by friends in the room. (Tablet Magazine) Goldin also presented an early version of the slideshow (Dubbed in Glamour) at The Kitchen in 1980. (The Kitchen)

James Chance, DNA, other no wave bands

No source found documenting Goldin photographing or directly collaborating with James Chance, DNA, or other no wave bands such as Teenage Jesus and the Jerks or Mars. The primary no-wave-adjacent music figure in her documented circle is Lydia Lunch (through the Vivienne Dick connection and the 1985 photograph), plus Pat Place and Cynthia Sley (as subjects in the Empty Suitcases film, 1980). Julia Gorton is identified in multiple sources as the photographer who documented James Chance, DNA, and the Contortions specifically. (Forbes, 2023)

Adele Bertei / No New York memoir

Bertei’s memoir groups Goldin with Lunch, Kathy Acker, Barbara Kruger, Kiki Smith, Lizzie Borden, and Kathryn Bigelow as women who “challenged artistic conventions” in the no wave scene. Goldin’s photographs are among 55 archival images included in the book. (That Eric Alper, July 2026)
