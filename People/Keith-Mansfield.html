Keith Mansfield

British composer, arranger, conductor, producer, and saxophonist (born 1943, London). Spent the bulk of his career as a library music composer for KPM Music, while also serving as arranger and musical director for a wide range of commercial pop, jazz, and film recordings from the mid-1960s onward. Husband of jazz singer Salena Jones.

Biography

Early career and session work (1966–1969)

Mansfield emerged as an arranger and musical director in the mid-1960s, working almost exclusively out of London and primarily for CBS Records UK. His session credits from 1966–1969 cover an enormous breadth of British pop: he arranged and directed for artists including Dusty Springfield (whose I Close My Eyes And Count To Ten he directed accompaniment on, 1968), the Marmalade, the Tremeloes, and a succession of CBS pop acts. He also worked as musical director for Lulu, Dave Clark Five affiliates, and various Decca and Philips artists.

From 1966 he was simultaneously developing a library music catalogue for KPM Music. His first credited KPM releases — Happy Families (1966, with Johnny Hawksworth) and The First Christmas (1966) — established a relationship with the label that would last his entire career.

In 1968 Mansfield co-composed the KPM album Underscore alongside Johnny Pearson and David Lindup, a foundational library cue collection that remains heavily reissued. That same year he released the solo KPM album Soul Organ Showcase (1968), co-credited to his house band persona. 1969 saw Beat Incidental (KPM, co-credited with Alan Hawkshaw), The Big Beat (KPM, 1969), and Chorus And Orchestra (KPM, 1969), cementing him as one of KPM’s principal composers alongside Hawkshaw, Pearson, and Syd Dale.

Commercial production and film (1969–1973)

Running in parallel with the library work, Mansfield functioned as arranger/producer for jazz trumpeter Maynard Ferguson through the CBS years. He produced and arranged The Ballad Style Of Maynard Ferguson (CBS, 1969, with The Keith Mansfield Orchestra), The World Of Maynard Ferguson (CBS, 1970), Maynard Ferguson (Columbia, 1971), and M.F. Horn Two (Columbia, 1972) — a sustained relationship that gave him his most prominent work in the US market.

In 1970 Mansfield scored the film Loot (dir. Silvio Narizzano), composing music and lyrics and serving as conductor and producer on the CBS soundtrack LP. The cast included Richard Attenborough, Lee Remick, Hywel Bennett, and Roy Holder; co-credited writers were Richard Willing Denton and vocalist Steve Ellis.

He also became a key arranger and producer for his wife Salena Jones during this period — most notably on Everybody’s Talkin’ About Salena Jones (CBS, 1970) — and continued producing for her into the 2000s, including Live At Ronnie Scott’s (Columbia, 1999).

In 1971 he served as one of three co-arrangers and music directors on Clodagh Rodgers’s Rodgers & Heart LP (RCA Victor), alongside Johnny Arthey (who handled the bulk of tracks) and Artie Butler. Mansfield directed tracks A4, A5, B2, and B4; producer was Kenny Young.

BBC television themes and library peak (1973–1991)

From the mid-1970s Mansfield scaled back commercial session work and concentrated on library composition. He became one of KPM’s most prolific contributors: KPM releases across this period include Contempo (1976), Vivid Underscores (1977), Olympiad 2000 (1977), National Heritage / Rural Heritage (1978), Technology And Movement (1980), Lifeforce (1981), Future Perspective (1982), The Four Elements (1982), Contact (1983), The Video Connection (1984, co-composed with Richard Elen), Future Positive (1986), Innovations (1987), Stepping Stones (1987), Earth Stories (1988), Sun Fresh (1988), Communication Technology (1989), Power House (1989), Natural Science (1989), Second Circle (1990), The Decision Makers (1990), and Survival (1991).

It was the BBC sports theme commissions that became his best-known legacy. He composed “Grandstand” (the long-running BBC sports introduction theme), “Light And Tuneful” (Wimbledon tennis coverage), and “World Series” (BBC athletics). The “Grand Prix” theme referenced in popular accounts is often linked to his overall BBC sports output. For US audiences, his most prominent piece is “Funky Fanfare” (from Beat Incidental, KPM, 1969), used extensively in late-1960s National Screen Service cinema trailers and prominently sampled in Quentin Tarantino’s Kill Bill (2003) and Grindhouse (2007). A vocal version, “House Of Jack,” was recorded by James Royal in 1969.

Many KPM albums from 1979 onwards credit a co-composer: Richard Elen appears on 18 shared releases (including The Video Connection, Future Perspective, Contact), making him Mansfield’s most frequent identified collaborator within the KPM catalogue.

Later work and legacy

Mansfield continued producing for Salena Jones into the 2000s and contributed sleeve notes to library music retrospectives, including Blow Up Presents Exclusive Blend Volume 1 (1996) and The Other World Of The Soundcarriers (2013, The Great Pop Supplement). His “Funky Fanfare” was sampled on Gnarls Barkley’s St. Elsewhere (2006) and appears in fitness and broadcasting contexts globally. His last credited KPM primary release on Discogs is Swing Of The City (The Scoring House, 2011).

Scene Connections

Mansfield was a central figure in the British library music scene centred on KPM Music in the late 1960s through 1980s. His closest KPM peers were Alan Hawkshaw, Johnny Pearson, David Lindup, and Syd Dale — all of whom co-appeared on foundational KPM albums like Underscore (1968). The KPM roster also included Johnny Hawksworth, John Cameron (2), and Neil Richardson, among others.

The session pool he drew from for KPM recordings included notable players: drummer Terry Cox (Pentangle), guitarist Paul Keogh, bassist Brian Odgers, percussionist Frank Ricotti, saxophonist Alan Skidmore, and trumpeter Derek Watkins (all appear on Contempo, 1976). Engineer Adrian Kerridge at CTS Studios appears on the Ferguson sessions and Night Bird.

On the commercial side, Mansfield’s CBS work placed him in the same arranger pool as Johnny Arthey — both were house arrangers directing CBS pop acts through the late 1960s and early 1970s.

His library pieces have had extensive posthumous life in hip-hop sampling and soundtrack licensing. The Quentin Tarantino uses of “Funky Fanfare” (from Beat Incidental, KPM 1969) brought renewed attention to the KPM catalogue broadly, and crate-digger compilations such as Blow Up Presents Exclusive Blend (1996) included KPM-sourced Mansfield material.
