Griffin Rodriguez

Discogs ID: 351386 Also known as: Blue Hawaii (Discogs ID: 664987), Zandär (Discogs ID: 3545431) Active: 1998–2025 (Discogs range) Origin: Albuquerque, New Mexico / Chicago, Illinois Roles: Multi-instrumentalist (bass, guitar, veena, keyboards, strings, electronics), engineer, mixer, mastering, producer

Griffin Rodriguez is a multi-instrumentalist and recording engineer whose work spans the Elephant Six and post-rock adjacent scenes across Albuquerque, Chicago, and beyond. He is a co-founder of the avant-fusion trio Bablicon alongside Jeremy Barnes and David McDonnell, and has served as a recurring recording and mixing engineer for A Hawk And A Hacksaw. As a performer he has played bass, organ, and keyboards in HiM, Orso, and Icy Demons, and contributed engineering and production to a wide arc of artists from Beirut to Akron-Family.

Biography

Early Work and Bablicon (1996–2001)

Rodriguez emerged in the Albuquerque scene as a versatile string and keyboard player. Around 1996 he co-founded Bablicon with Jeremy Barnes (drummer, later of A Hawk And A Hacksaw) and David McDonnell (credited as “The Diminisher” on some releases). The band drew on Canterbury prog, RiO, no-jazz, and no-wave, and released four records: Chunks Of Syrup Amidst Plain Yoghurt (1999, Pickled Egg Records), In A Different City (1999, Pickled Egg), The Cat That Was A Dog (2000, Misra), and The Orange Tapered Moon (2000, Pickled Egg / Misra). Rodriguez played under his Blue Hawaii alias on Bablicon releases — credited on In A Different City playing electric bass, bass viol, fretless guitar, veena, cello, keyboards, organ, tape splices, and voice. He mastered The Cat That Was A Dog under the Blue Hawaii name. Bablicon is noted by Discogs as a split-off / related project to Need New Body.

Rodriguez is also credited as a performer and/or recording engineer on The Blackbook Sessions (1999, Galapagos4), an early Elephant Six/Albuquerque area compilation that also documented his engineering practice.

Chicago: HiM, Orso, and the Doug Scharin Network (2000–2006)

After Bablicon dissolved, Rodriguez moved in the orbit of Doug Scharin’s Chicago collective HiM, appearing on both major albums. On Many In High Places Are Not Well (2003, FatCat Records) he plays double bass, electric bass, bass, B3 organ, clavinet, voice, Wurlitzer, and EP30 keyboards across virtually every track. On Peoples (2005, Bubble Core Records) he plays bass, vocals, and synth throughout. The HiM lineup at this time also featured Adam Pierce (guitar, vibraphone), Carlo Cennamo (alto sax), Joshua Larue (guitar), Matt Schneider (guitar), Joe Goldring (guitar), Phil Spirito, and Julie Liu (violin/viola), among others.

In parallel he was a member of Orso, the “carny rock / chamber folk” ensemble led by Phil Spirito, with Cennamo, Liu, Brian Deck, Ben Massarella, and Gillian Lisee. He recorded and mixed portions of Orso’s My Dreams Are Back And They Are Better Than Ever (2004, Perishable Records), on which he also plays double bass, keyboards, and vocals.

He also played with Def Star, a Chicago-based project with Greg Davis, Peter Andreadis, and Tim Nordwind.

During this period Rodriguez built up his engineering practice through Galapagos4 releases — recording, mixing, or engineering records for Need New Body (UFO, Pals, Squints By Offwhyte), Shooters And Bowlers, and other Albuquerque-adjacent projects.

Rodriguez also contributed bass and electronics to Icy Demons’s In Chicago There Is Willy (2004, 482 Music). Icy Demons shared members with Bablicon: the band included David McDonnell and Christopher Powell.

A Hawk And A Hacksaw and the Albuquerque/European Folk Arc (2006–2013)

Rodriguez became an important engineer and occasional player for A Hawk And A Hacksaw, Jeremy Barnes’s Eastern European folk project with Heather Trost:

  • The Way The Wind Blows (2006, Leaf) — additional vocals
  • A Hawk And A Hacksaw And The Hun Hangár Ensemble EP (2007, Leaf) — mastered by
  • Délivrance (2009, Leaf) — mixed by
  • Cervantine (2011, LM Dupli-Cation) — guitar, recorded by
  • You Have Already Gone To The Other World (2013, LM Dupli-cation) — recorded by

The Délivrance session brought together a large Hungarian ensemble (cimbalom, baglama, clarinet, trumpet, upright bass) recorded partly in Budapest; Rodriguez handled the mix from Albuquerque.

A key credit in this era: Rodriguez recorded, mixed, and mastered Beirut’s The Flying Club Cup (2007, 4AD / Ba Da Bing!) for the Albuquerque tracking sessions, alongside Zach Condon, who co-mixed. Condon and Rodriguez are credited jointly for the Albuquerque recordings; Canadian portions were tracked by Mark Lawson.

Engineering Expansion and Akron/Family (2006–2015)

Rodriguez recorded Akron-Family’s Meek Warrior (2006, Young God Records), contributing bass, percussion, and backing vocals as well as engineering. The record also featured Hamid Drake on drums and Michael Gira as producer/co-designer.

He appears as mastering engineer on a range of records through this period: A Hawk And A Hacksaw’s EP (2007), Beirut’s town topic EP (2008), The Rip Tide by Beirut (2011, Pompeii Records — where he also produced and mixed), and numerous others across labels including Kranky, Dead Oceans, Delmark, Eremite, and Fire Records.

Rodriguez also engineered two Pillars And Tongues records (2007) and served as engineer for The Music Tapes’ Music Tapes For Clouds And Tornadoes (2008, Merge Records) — contributing electric bass.

He is credited as engineer on Modest Mouse’s Strangers To Ourselves (2015, Epic) and on Beirut’s No No No (2015, 4AD), both under his “Shape Shoppe” studio designation.

Icy Demons and Later Work (2008–present)

Rodriguez continued with Icy Demons into the 2010s. The band’s Smiles (2023, Cloud Recordings) is the most fully documented Icy Demons release in the Discogs database; here Rodriguez plays bass guitar, voice, upright bass, guitar, veena, percussion, synthesizer, keys, and piano, and serves as producer, engineer, and masterer — a complete cradle-to-grave role. Smiles also features guest appearances from Zach Condon (vocals), Silvia Bolognesi (bass), and Dylan Ryan (drums).

Rodriguez engineered and mixed Matthew Shipp Trio, Josh Berman, and other jazz-adjacent projects through Delmark, Eremite, and Cuneiform labels from 2010 onward.

His solo or primary-credited output under the name Blue Hawaii includes Fight Back! (2004, Cloud Recordings), a single listed primarily under that alias.

Under the Zandär alias there is one listed release: The Demo (no year, Sweden, Not On Label) — bass credit, minimal detail.

A 2025 Icy Demons release, A Study Of Losses (Pompeii Records), credits Rodriguez on upright bass.
