John Cooper Clarke

Discogs artist ID: 202048 Born: 25 January 1949, Salford, Greater Manchester, UK Role: Performance poet (“the punk poet”) Active: 1977 – present

John Cooper Clarke is a Salford-born performance poet who emerged from the late-1970s Manchester punk scene and became one of its most distinctive voices. Known for rapid-fire, darkly humorous verse delivered in a thick Mancunian accent over post-punk backings, he released a run of albums on CBS/Epic (1978–1982) produced by Martin Hannett and backed by The Invisible Girls. His poem “I Wanna Be Yours” was later set to music by Arctic Monkeys as the closing track of AM (2013, Domino), bringing him to a new generation. Also known for the poem “Evidently Chickentown”.

Overview

Clarke began performing on the Manchester circuit in the mid-1970s, initially backed by a loose group called The Curious Yellows. His earliest recordings appeared on Rabid Records (2) — the Manchester independent associated with Tosh Ryan — with the Innocents E.P. (1977), produced by Martin Hannett under his alias Martin Zero. The EP featured early Hannett production hallmarks: unconventional drum treatments and a cold, spatial sound.

CBS signed Clarke in 1978, and Hannett remained his producer throughout his CBS/Epic period. The debut album Disguise In Love (1978) assembled a stellar pool of Manchester musicians: Pete Shelley (Buzzcocks) on guitar across several tracks, Bill Nelson on guitar, The Invisible Girls as the core backing band, with keyboards and synthesizer programming from Steve Hopkins and Hannett himself. The mixing team of Laurence Diana and Dave Kent-Watson completed the sound.

Snap, Crackle & Bop (1980) continued the collaboration, now with Peter Saville (2) contributing design alongside Paul Welch. The Invisible Girls were credited for music, writing, production and arrangement. Karl Burns (The Fall), Dave Hassel, Lynn Oakey, Toby Toman, Trevor Spencer and Stephanie Formula all contributed as musical assistants, indicating the broad pool of Manchester post-punk personnel Hannett drew on.

Me And My Big Mouth (1981) brought in Vini Reilly (Durutti Column) as a performer alongside the Invisible Girls, and Chris Nagle engineered. The final CBS/Epic album, Zip Style Method (1982), was recorded across three studios (Hologram, Jacobs Farm, Ridge Farm) and included Richard Darbyshire as an additional musician.

After the CBS/Epic run ended, Clarke largely stepped back from recording through the 1980s and 1990s, though he contributed vocals to the Arka Sound label releases Seven (1987), Land Of A Thousand Churches (1991), and Alap - Joe - Jhala (1992). He appeared on the film All Tomorrow’s Parties (2009, Warp Films).

A 2016 album, This Time It’s Personal (Sony Music), was co-credited with Hugh Cornwell (formerly of The Stranglers), who produced and played guitar. Ian Anderson (Jethro Tull) contributed flute on “MacArthur Park”. The record is a set of covers — rock and roll and pop standards delivered in Clarke’s distinctive spoken/sung style.

The compilation Anthologia (Sony Music, 2015) and its highlights version gathered his CBS/Epic output, and BMG/Mute issued The Complete Singles in 2023. Clarke continues to perform and tour.
