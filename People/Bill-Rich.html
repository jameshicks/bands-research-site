Bill Rich

Bass player and guitarist. Also credited as “Billy Rich,” “B. Rich,” “E. W. Rich,” and “Eddie ‘Bill’ Rich.” Discogs artist ID: 323341. Brother of Herbie Rich (organist/saxophonist with The Electric Flag and Buddy Miles Express). Has a website at billrichmusic.com.

Overview

Bill Rich came up through the late-1960s rock and R&B scene in the orbit of Buddy Miles and Jimi Hendrix, then pivoted decisively toward American roots music in the early 1970s. His most significant association was as the core bassist for Paul Butterfield’s Better Days (1972–1974, Bearsville Records), and he remained a consistent presence in the Geoff Muldaur–Amos Garrett circle through the late 1970s. His Discogs profile also lists sustained work as bassist for Taj Mahal across multiple albums from 1971 to 1979, making him one of the more quietly versatile session bassists of the era.

Era 1: Buddy Miles and the Hendrix Circle (1968–1970)

Rich’s earliest documented credits are with the Buddy Miles Express, the funk-rock band led by drummer Buddy Miles. He plays bass on:

  • Expressway To Your Skull (1968, Mercury) — with Herbie Rich on organ/tenor sax, Jim McCarty on guitar; Jimi Hendrix wrote the liner notes
  • Electric Church (1969, Mercury) — Jimi Hendrix produced four tracks; Duane Hitchings on organ

In 1970 he appears on John McLaughlin’s solo debut Devotion (Douglas), credited as “Billy Rich,” alongside Buddy Miles on drums and Larry Young on organ and electric piano — produced by Alan Douglas. This was McLaughlin’s first US album, pre-Mahavishnu, and the band overlaps directly with the Miles-Rich Express connection.

Also in 1970 he plays bass (again as “Billy Rich”) on Jesse Ed Davis’s debut ¡Jesse Davis! (Atco), a star-studded Oklahoma-meets-LA session that also featured Eric Clapton on guitar, Leon Russell on keyboards, and Gram Parsons on backing vocals — produced by Davis himself.

Era 3: Paul Butterfield’s Better Days (1973–1974)

The core project of Rich’s career. He was the bassist for Paul Butterfield’s Better Days, the Bearsville band assembled around Paul Butterfield (vocals, harp), Geoff Muldaur (guitar, piano, vocals), Amos Garrett (guitar), Ronnie Barron (piano, organ, vocals), and Chris Parker (2) (drums). The band made two albums for Bearsville:

Better Days (1973, Bearsville / Warner Bros.)

Discogs release ID: 5711039 (Japan pressing; master 218813). Rich is credited as “Billy Rich” on bass for A1–B2 and B5; Amos Garrett covers B3 on bass. Produced by Paul Butterfield and Geoff Muldaur. Maria Muldaur guests on vocals (B1); Nick Jameson engineered.

Era 4: The Muldaur Circle (1975–1979)

After Better Days dissolved, Rich stayed in Geoff Muldaur’s orbit:

Is Having A Wonderful Time (1975, Reprise)

Geoff Muldaur’s first solo album. Discogs release ID: 1693677 (master 425332). Produced by Joe Boyd. Rich plays bass on three tracks (A3, A4B, B4), alongside Ron Carter (bass on A2, B2) and Jerry Jemmott (bass on B1). An exceptionally wide cast including Cornell Dupree, Bernard Purdie, James Booker, Richard Thompson, John Cale (viola), Amos Garrett, Chris Parker (2), Maria Muldaur, and Merl Saunders.

Motion (1976, Reprise)

Geoff Muldaur second solo album, Discogs release ID: 1386890. Rich is one of three bassists alongside James Jamerson and Klaus Voormann. Notable cast includes Dr. John, Jim Keltner, Bonnie Raitt (vocals), and Jesse Ed Davis (guitar). Produced by Trevor Lawrence.

Geoff Muldaur & Amos Garrett (1978, Flying Fish)

Duo LP, Discogs release ID: 2825179 (master 486867). Rich plays bass throughout; produced by Michael Melford. Fritz Richmond plays jug; Scott Matthews plays drums/percussion/tenor sax.

Blues Boy (1979, Flying Fish)

Geoff Muldaur solo, Discogs release ID: 2925253 (master 835109). Rich on bass; also features Amos Garrett and Stephen Bruton on guitar, Michael Utley on piano, produced by Michael Melford.

Name Variations on Record

Discogs documents him under several names across different releases:

  • “Billy Rich” — most common variant, used on Buddy Miles Express, Jesse Davis, Better Days, Roger Tillison
  • “Eddie ‘Bill’ Rich” — used on Evolution (The Most Recent) (Taj Mahal, 1978)
  • “B. Rich” and “Rich” — appear as name variations in the DB
  • “E. W. Rich” — DB name variation; full name may be Eddie W. Rich
