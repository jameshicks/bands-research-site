Leon Ndugu Chancler

Leon Ndugu Chancler

American drummer, percussionist, songwriter, and producer. Born July 1, 1952 in Shreveport, LA, USA. Died February 3, 2018 in Los Angeles, CA, USA. Discogs artist ID: 192112.

Instruments: Drums, timbales, rototoms, congas, vibraphone, synthesizer, vocals.

Most commonly credited as “Ndugu” or “Ndugu Chancler” on records. Over 100 credited name variations appear in the Discogs database, reflecting decades of session work across hundreds of releases.

Overview

Leon Ndugu Chancler was one of the most in-demand session drummers in Los Angeles from the late 1960s through the 1990s. He bridged late-1960s jazz modernism, fusion, funk, and mainstream pop with unusual range — appearing on Herbie Hancock’s landmark Mwandishi in 1971 and on Michael Jackson’s Thriller in 1982, two of the defining records of their respective decades. He was a member of Santana from 1974 to 1976, and later a fixture in the West Coast studio scene where he also produced, arranged, and led his own sessions. He remained active into the 2000s as a bandleader alongside Stanley Clarke and Patrice Rushen.

Career by Era

Early Sessions (1968–1973)

Chancler’s earliest Discogs-documented credit is on Everybody Loves A Winner (Revue, 1968), placed while still a teenager. By 1971 he was working at a high level in the jazz world: he appears alongside Billy Hart on Herbie Hancock’s Mwandishi (Warner Bros., 1971), the electric-fusion album that marked Hancock’s full departure from acoustic jazz. He is credited under both “Leon Chancler” and “Ndugu” on that release — the dual credit captures the transitional moment in his professional identity.

In 1972–73 he was active in the LA and New York scenes: sessions for the Strata-East label (Alkebu-Lan, with the Mtume Umoja Ensemble, 1972), Prestige (Universe, 1972; Blues For Walls, 1973; Before The Dawn, 1975), Atlantic, Black Jazz Records (Talk To My Lady, 1973; Proceed With Caution!, 1974; The Skipper At Home, 1974), and Milestone. This concentrated run on independent jazz imprints signals he was embedded in post-bop and spiritual jazz circuits before the crossover period accelerated.

Santana Period and Fusion Work (1974–1976)

Chancler joined Santana in 1974. His tenure produced Borboletta (CBS, 1974), Tale Spinnin’ (CBS, 1975), and the more commercially successful Amigos (CBS, 1976) — on which he served as co-producer and played drums, timbales, rototoms, congas, and contributed backing vocals. Amigos reached the top 10 on the Billboard 200 and is one of the band’s best-selling albums. He also received a co-producer credit on the Europa (Earth’s Cry Heaven’s Smile) single from this era.

Concurrently he was a go-to drummer for the MPS/BASF catalog out of Germany, playing on George Duke’s Feel (1974), I Love The Blues, She Heard My Cry (1975), The Aura Will Prevail (1975), and Liberated Fantasies (1976).

L.A. Studio Peak and Crossover Work (1977–1983)

After leaving Santana, Chancler settled into one of the most prolific stretches of any West Coast session drummer of the era. Key relationships:

George Duke — Chancler is effectively the house drummer for Duke’s entire Epic run: Reach For It (1977), From Me To You (1977), Don’t Let Go (1978), Follow The Rainbow (1979), Dream On (1982), Guardian Of The Light (1983), George Duke (1986).

Flora Purim — Nothing Will Be As It Was…Tomorrow (Warner Bros., 1977), on which he arranged the rhythm section and produced as well as drumming.

The Crusaders — recurring session drummer across the decade, becoming full co-producer by Ghetto Blaster (MCA, 1984).

Michael Jackson / Quincy Jones — Thriller (Epic, 1982). Chancler plays drums on “Billie Jean,” “Human Nature,” and “P.Y.T.” — his drumming on “Billie Jean” became perhaps the single most-heard drum performance of the 1980s. Jeff Porcaro handles the remaining tracks.

Dazz Band — co-wrote “Let It Whip” (Motown, 1982), their Grammy-winning R&B hit. The writing credit has generated royalties from compilations and re-releases continuously since.

Lionel Richie — the 1982 self-titled album (Motown).

Frank Sinatra — L.A. Is My Lady (Qwest, 1984).

Tina Turner — producer credit on Private Dancer (Capitol, 1984).

Patrice Rushen — mid-period collaborations; formed a working trio with Stanley Clarke that continued into the 2000s.

The 1980 album Do I Make You Feel Better? is a significant if underappreciated entry — a fully produced orchestral funk/soul LP with an extraordinary cast, showcasing Chancler’s production and multi-instrument abilities alongside a large West Coast studio ensemble.

Production Work (1983–1990)

Through the mid-1980s Chancler shifted substantially into production. He produced multiple MCA Records releases including Vesta Williams’ Oasis (1985) and Compass, Klique releases, and others. He also produced Flora Purim’s Nothing Will Be As It Was…Tomorrow and the Straight To The Bank (Prestige) project by Frankie Beverly. Work as arranger appears consistently alongside production credits in this period.

Later Period (1988–2018)

Session appearances continued into the 1990s and 2000s: Michael Jackson’s HIStory (1995), Dianne Reeves’ Blue Note debut (1987), various West Coast jazz and fusion releases, Santana retrospectives. He maintained the working trio with Stanley Clarke and Patrice Rushen through Standards (2006). A credit on the live Two Miles Live document (1990, Italy) attests to continued festival activity.

Open Questions

  • Track-level assignments on Santana’s Borboletta and Tale Spinnin’ are not broken out in Discogs.
  • The Dazz Band “Let It Whip” co-writing credit — who else co-wrote it and what was Chancler’s specific involvement? Discogs notes him as Written-By without additional detail.
  • Private Dancer producer credit appears only on certain pressings in Discogs — unclear if for a specific track or overall production role.
  • Born in Shreveport, LA — early training, teachers, and first LA arrival date not in Discogs.
  • Full Spread Of The Future (1979, Epic) personnel — Discogs credits are sparse for this release.
  • The Chocolate Jam Co. membership roster not fully mapped; the Discogs group entry (ID: 364156) requires separate research.
  • Web sources would likely clarify: teaching career (he was reportedly an educator), exact Santana departure circumstances, and the full scope of his MCA Records production work in the 1980s.
