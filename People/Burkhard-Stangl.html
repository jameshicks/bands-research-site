Burkhard Stangl

Born: 6 November 1960, Eggenburg, Austria
Discogs artist ID: 146257
Alias in Discogs: Paul Kling (listed, though Discogs also has a separate Paul Kling entry, ID 519591 — likely a conflation)
Name variations: B. Stangl, BS, Dr. Burkhard Stangl, Stangl
Website: stangl.klingt.org

Austrian jazz guitarist, composer, and electroacoustic improviser. A central figure in the Vienna experimental/improvised music scene from the late 1980s onward. Works across jazz, reductive improvisation, electroacoustic composition, and collaborative laptop/electronics contexts. Long associated with the Viennese post-free-improv milieu around Charhizma, Erstwhile Records, and Durian Records.

Overview

Stangl began in the Austrian jazz scene in the mid-1980s, appearing on the debut LP of Ton-Art (Ant · Ort, 1987, Extraplatte) alongside Werner Dafeldecker on bass. By the early 1990s he was orbiting the hat ART orbit, playing guitar on Franz Koglmann’s chamber jazz records. His central role in the experimental scene crystallized through two groups founded in the mid-1990s: Polwechsel, the landmark Vienna reductive-improv ensemble, and later Efzeg, a laptop/electronics collective.

His most intensive duo relationship is with Christof Kurzmann: the two recorded and performed as Schnee (the German word for “snow”), releasing Schnee (2000, Erstwhile Records) — Stangl on acoustic and electric guitar and percussion, Kurzmann on laptop (G3). The duo revisited this identity two decades later as Schnee with Kurzmann and Stangl (billed explicitly as such) on Снег (2021, Mikroton Recordings), with Stangl on guitar and tapes.

Stangl also maintained a solo practice: Récital (1997, Durian Records) is a solo guitar and piano record on which he composed, performed, and produced all material. Hommage À Moi (2011, Loewenhertz) is a later solo composer/producer work. He contributed to pop and singer-songwriter contexts as well — co-writing and co-producing Lynx (2019, Interstellar Records) with vocalist Joanna John, mastered by Martin Siewert.

Groups

Polwechsel (1995–c.2008)

Polwechsel is the group most associated with Stangl’s international reputation. The founding lineup on the debut Polwechsel (1995, Random Acoustics) was Stangl (guitar), Werner Dafeldecker (double bass, guitar), Michael Moser (cello), and Radu Malfatti (trombone). Co-produced by Moser and Dafeldecker. Polwechsel’s music operated at the outer edge of silence and restraint, influencing the reductive improvisation tendency across Europe. Stangl remained a member through at least Archives Of The North (2006, hatOLOGY); the Polwechsel page notes his departure around 2008.

Efzeg (2000–c.2004)

Efzeg was a Vienna laptop/electronics collective with a rotating but consistent core. Members: Billy Roisz, Boris Hauf, Burkhard Stangl, Dieb13 (Dieter Kovacic), Martin Siewert, and Steve Heather. Releases:

  • Grain (2000, Durian Records) — Stangl: guitar, electronics; Siewert and Hauf: production, mix, mastering
  • Würm (2004, Charhizma) — Stangl: “electronic and acoustic instruments”

The Year Of (2006)

The Year Of is a Vienna improv/post-rock ensemble. Members: B. Fleischmann (drums, electronics, piano), Christof Kurzmann (soprano/alto saxophone, voice, clarinet), Stangl, Martin Siewert (guitar, electronics, pedal steel, lap steel, organ), Werner Dafeldecker (bass). One release: Slow Days (2006, Morr Music), produced, mixed, and mastered by Christoph Amann. A separate player credited as Paul Kling (vibraphone) also appears; see note on the Discogs alias in the header.

Ton-Art (1985–late 1980s)

Early Vienna jazz/new-music ensemble. Discogs group ID: 1821295. Their debut LP Ant · Ort (1987, Extraplatte) also featured Werner Dafeldecker on bass.

Open Questions

  • The Discogs alias “Paul Kling” for Stangl appears to be a conflation or error: Discogs has a separate Paul Kling entity (ID 519591) credited as vibraphone on Slow Days and vocals on an unrelated release. Whether Stangl performed as Paul Kling or whether this is a database misattribution is unclear from Discogs alone — would need web confirmation.
  • Stangl’s departure date from Polwechsel is estimated as c.2008 based on the existing Polwechsel page; not explicitly confirmed in release credits pulled here.
  • Stangl’s activity after 2021 (Снег) — the Discogs record is likely incomplete for more recent releases.
  • The Récital (1997) solo record is the main documented solo statement; whether there are other solo releases not yet in Discogs is unknown.
  • Several group memberships (Chesterfield (6), EKG (17), Monoblue Quartet, Noiset, Qui.t, SQID (2), SSSD, etc.) are undocumented beyond the group-member listing.
