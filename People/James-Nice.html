Producer, record label executive, label owner, and author - mostly under his pseudonym James Hayward, though he published the book ‘Shadowplayers (The Rise And Fall Of Factory Records)’ (first published in 2010) under his real name. Nice founded the LTM (4) label in 1984. His earliest releases championed the work of Section 25, The Happy Family and Crispy Ambulance, among others. In 1987 he moved to Brussels and began work at Les Disques Du Crépuscule, handling their mail order operations. His role expanded swiftly into being, in his words, “a full-time job as a gofer for Les Disques Du Crépuscule boss Michel Duval”. His work was rewarded with his own sub-label, Interior Music. In 1988, he switched from Les Disques Du Crépuscule to a position at Pias, and in 1990 decided to reactivate LTM. This second phase of his label really hit its stride when Nice started licensing and reissuing classic, long-deleted Factory and Les Disques Du Crépuscule albums. In 2010, he established Factory Records Ltd., but after that didn’t kick off, he resurrected Factory Benelux by buying the FBN name and logos from former owner Michel Duval.

Overview

James Nice is a producer, label executive, and author who works primarily under the pseudonym James Hayward. He founded LTM in 1984, initially championing post-punk acts such as Section 25 and Crispy Ambulance, then relocated to Brussels in 1987 to handle mail order and production at Les Disques Du Crépuscule — a role that expanded into the sub-label Interior Music. After moving to Pias in 1988 he reactivated LTM in 1990, building its second phase on licensed reissues of long-deleted Factory and Crépuscule material; he also wrote Shadowplayers: The Rise and Fall of Factory Records (2010) under his real name, and later acquired the FBN name and logos from Michel Duval to resurrect Factory Benelux. His 330 Discogs credits span LTM’s 101-release catalogue, sustained work with Factory Benelux and Les Disques Du Crépuscule, and frequent collaborations across the Tuxedomoon, Durutti Column, and Section 25 orbits.

Notes (web)

Factory Records Ltd. and the pivot to Factory Benelux

Factory Records Ltd. (UK company number 07298302) was incorporated on 29 June 2010, founded by Nice together with Peter Saville, Alan Erasmus, and Oliver Wilson (son of Tony Wilson). The stated aim was to preserve and exploit the Factory name, brand, logos, and design elements. (search snippet, factoryrecords.org/faq.php area; company number cited in multiple snippets)

The venture produced only one release: a deluxe double-vinyl reissue of From the Hip by Section 25 (original FACT 90, 1984), with artwork restyled by Saville. (https://www.phonicarecords.com/product/section-25-from-the-hip-lp-factory-records/107282)

Why it stalled: according to multiple search snippets drawing on the company’s history, Erasmus and Oliver Wilson “were never really interested and managed to thwart most ideas and projects,” leaving the venture without the internal agreement needed to move forward. The company was dissolved on 5 March 2013, and immediately re-incorporated the same month (company number 08433603) by Saville alone, where it has remained dormant. (Source: search snippets citing UK Companies House records, retrieved via https://www.discogs.com/label/412707-Factory-Records-Ltd and related pages; Wikipedia also notes “limited output” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory_Records, as of 2026-10-21)

Conflict — incorporation year: Nice’s Discogs profile and UK Companies House records give the incorporation date as June 2010. The Wikipedia article on Factory Records states the company was formed in 2012. The 2010 date appears more precise (it comes from legal filing data); the Wikipedia figure may refer to when the first (and only) release appeared or to a later active phase.

With Factory Records Ltd. effectively stalled, Nice turned to a different legacy entity he had closer personal ties to: Factory Benelux, the Brussels-based sister label he had worked alongside in the late 1980s at Les Disques Du Crépuscule. He bought the FBN name and logos from former owner Michel Duval, and formally revived Factory Benelux in 2012 for reissues and new recordings by original FBN artists. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTM_Recordings, as of 2026-10-21)

The pivot thus reflects both the practical deadlock inside Factory Records Ltd. (no consensus among the original stakeholders) and Nice’s stronger operational footing with the Benelux entity — one he could acquire outright rather than share with reluctant co-directors.
