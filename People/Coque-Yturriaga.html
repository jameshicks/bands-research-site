Overview

Coque Yturriaga is a Spanish musician and record producer whose career spans the late 1990s to the present, primarily through his roles as keyboardist, guitarist, and effects player in Migala, Emak Bakia, and Num9. His catalogue sits in post-rock, folk rock, and lo-fi experimentation, with the 2000s as his most active decade. Acuarela has been his anchor label across nearly three decades of releases, though Migala also reached an international audience via Sub Pop in 2000–2001. His closest collaborators — Abel Hernández, Diego Yturriaga, Rubén Moreno, and Jordi Sancho — form a consistent working core, with Nacho Vegas also appearing across 13 shared credits.

Notes (web)

Migala and Sub Pop (2000–2001)

Migala’s entry onto Sub Pop was brokered by Stuart David of Looper (also Belle & Sebastian), who recommended the band to the label. No source gives detail on the mechanics of that introduction — whether David approached Sub Pop directly, passed on a demo, or made an informal introduction to label staff. (acuareladiscos.com; subpop.com/artists/migala)

The relationship produced two releases:

  1. “The North of Fire” / “Sad Corner Song” — Sub Pop Singles Club V2 #31, catalog SP524, released 17 October 2000. Limited to 1,300 copies on red vinyl. (pettediscographies.com/subpop/sp524; subpop.com/releases/migala/the_north_of_fire)

  2. Arde — released on Sub Pop as Migala’s “full-length US debut” in July 2001 (catalog not confirmed here), after the album had already appeared on Acuarela in Spain in December 2000. The Sub Pop page states the label was “exceedingly proud” to put out the first official US release. The release led to an eight-date West Coast US tour. (subpop.com/releases/migala/arde; acuareladiscos.com)

The arrangement appears to have been a US licensing deal rather than a full signing: Acuarela retained the Spanish release throughout, and an IndyRock profile described Sub Pop as licensing “their two latest works” — implying a discrete, limited scope. (indyrock.es/migala.htm)

Restos de un incendio (2002) and all subsequent Migala albums were released solely on Acuarela. No source found — in English or Spanish — explicitly states why Sub Pop did not license further albums or why the arrangement was not renewed. The question may be unanswerable from public sources; the most plausible framing from available evidence is that this was a one-off licensing arrangement that simply ran its course.
