Tony Bennett

Tony Bennett

Aug 25, 20268 min read

Tony Bennett

Born: August 3, 1926, Astoria, Queens, New York
Died: July 21, 2023
Real name: Anthony Dominick Benedetto
Discogs artist ID: 170754
Aliases: Antonio Benedetto; Joe Bari (early stage name)

Jazz and popular vocalist; one of the most commercially and critically durable American singers of the 20th century. His career spanned from early 1950s Columbia chart hits through late-career jazz duet albums with Bill Evans and a cross-generational partnership with Lady Gaga that produced two albums in the 2010s–2020s. He entered this vault as a “Featured Party Guest” vocalist on Paul Shaffer & The Party Boys Of Rock ‘N’ Roll’s The World’s Most Dangerous Party (1993).

Biography

Early Career and Columbia Years (1950–1971)

Bennett was drafted into the US Army in World War II and served in the infantry in France and Germany. After the war he studied singing at the American Theatre Wing in New York on the GI Bill. He was spotted performing in Greenwich Village by Bob Hope, who brought him to national television. His real name at that point was still “Joe Bari” as a stage name; Hope told him to use the professional name Tony Bennett.

He signed to Columbia Records in 1950. His first Columbia sides were immediate commercial successes: “Because of You” and “Cold, Cold Heart” (both 1951) reached #1 on the pop charts. “Rags to Riches” (1953) was another #1. He became one of Columbia’s most consistent hitmakers through the mid-1960s, recording hundreds of singles and LPs with the label.

The signature recording of his career, “I Left My Heart in San Francisco,” was recorded at a live performance at the Fairmont Hotel in 1961 and released as a single on Columbia Records in 1962. The song won Bennett two Grammy Awards (Record of the Year and Best Male Solo Vocal Performance). The album of the same name (Columbia, 1962) sold over 100 pressings worldwide across many international editions.

Through the 1960s Bennett recorded prolifically: orchestral pop, Broadway songbook material, jazz-inflected sets, and standards. Key albums of this era include I Wanna Be Around (Columbia, 1963), The Good Life (1963), The Many Moods of Tony (1964), If I Ruled the World (1965), and Tony Makes It Happen! (1967). He also collaborated with the Count Basie Orchestra, releasing Count Basie Swings / Tony Bennett Sings on Roulette Records in 1959 — one of the most prominent big-band vocal pairings of the era — and the follow-up Together Again (Improv, 1977).

Mid-Career and Label Transitions (1971–1985)

As pop tastes shifted in the early 1970s, Columbia let his contract lapse. Bennett moved through MGM/Verve and his own label Improv Records. These were commercially difficult years, but artistically significant: his Improv albums included The Tony Bennett/Bill Evans Album (Fantasy, 1975) and Together Again with Bill Evans (Improv, 1977) — two sparse, intimate piano-and-voice recordings that are now considered among the finest jazz vocal records of the decade.

He also recorded A Tribute to Duke for Concord Jazz in 1977 — one of the first major albums on what became an influential independent jazz label.

MTV Unplugged and Commercial Renaissance (1986–2000)

Bennett signed with Columbia again in 1986 and released The Art of Excellence, his first major label album in over a decade. The comeback gained real traction when his son Danny Bennett — who had become his manager — pursued an MTV Unplugged special. Tony Bennett MTV Unplugged (Columbia, 1994) was a crossover event, introducing Bennett to a new audience. The album won the Grammy for Album of the Year. It featured performances with k.d. lang (“Moonglow”) and Elvis Costello (“They Can’t Take That Away from Me”), produced by David Kahne.

The Unplugged moment repositioned Bennett as a hip legacy figure. Through the late 1990s he released Here’s to the Ladies (1995), On Holiday: A Tribute to Billie Holiday (1997), and Playin’ with My Friends: Bennett Sings the Blues (2001).

The Duets Era (2006–2021)

The next major phase came with the Duets albums on Columbia. Duets: An American Classic (Columbia, 2006) paired Bennett with a broad cast of contemporary names — Paul McCartney, Stevie Wonder, Bono, Elvis Costello, k.d. lang, Barbra Streisand, Billy Joel, Diana Krall, Elton John, among others — in newly recorded studio versions of his classic songs. Producer Phil Ramone oversaw the sessions; sound was engineered and recorded by Dae Bennett (Tony’s son).

Duets II (Columbia, 2011) continued the format with a partly different roster: Lady Gaga (on “The Lady Is a Tramp”), Amy Winehouse (“Body and Soul,” recorded shortly before her death), Aretha Franklin, k.d. lang, John Mayer, Michael Bublé, Norah Jones, Willie Nelson, Carrie Underwood, Mariah Carey, and others. Producer Phil Ramone and Dae Bennett again.

The partnership with Lady Gaga deepened into two full collaborative albums. Cheek to Cheek (Columbia/Interscope, 2014) was an all-jazz-standards program recorded with small combos and big band arrangements; it debuted at #1 on the Billboard 200 and won a Grammy for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album. Love for Sale (Columbia/Interscope/Streamline, 2021) — an all-Cole Porter program — was recorded in 2020–21 and released shortly before Bennett’s 95th birthday; it was widely understood to be a farewell album. It won the Grammy for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album and Best Album Package.

Final Years

Bennett was diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease in 2016; the diagnosis was made public in February 2021. Despite the diagnosis he continued to perform and record with Lady Gaga through 2021. He gave his final public performances at Radio City Music Hall in August 2021. He died July 21, 2023, at age 96.
