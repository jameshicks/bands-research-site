Fred Frith

Guitarist, composer, improviser. Born February 17, 1949, Heathfield, England. Real name: Jeremy Webster Frith. Discogs artist ID: 78003.

Biography

Early Years and Henry Cow (1968–1978)

Frith played in R&B groups at school and did solo folk club work from 1967–1969. In early 1968 he began exploratory musical activities with Tim Hodgkinson (Discogs ID: 292451), which led directly to the founding of Henry Cow (ID: 282968) — a British avant-rock group active 1968–1978 on Virgin Records (later Recommended Records). Henry Cow drew on free jazz, 20th-century composition, rock, and political agitation. Key members alongside Frith included Chris Cutler (drums, ID: 177209), Dagmar Krause (vocals, ID: 177200), Lindsay Cooper (wind, ID: 218806), John Greaves (bass, ID: 65788), Peter Blegvad (ID: 227438), Anthony Moore (ID: 100685), and Geoff Leigh (ID: 267016).

Henry Cow’s studio catalog ran from The Henry Cow Legend (1973) through Western Culture (1979), with the live double Concerts (1976, Compendium Records) as a career landmark. Frith played guitar, violin, viola, xylophone, and piano across these records.

After Henry Cow dissolved, Frith and Cutler formed Art Bears (ID: 203192) with Dagmar Krause — a trio releasing three albums on ReR Megacorp / Rē Records (1978–1981): Hopes And Fears, Winter Songs, and The World As It Is Today. Art Bears pushed further into expressionism and political text.

New York Period — Downtown Scene (late 1970s–mid-1990s)

Frith moved to New York in the late 1970s, plugging into the downtown experimental scene centred around Tribeca lofts, CBGB’s gallery, and the Knitting Factory. His key NYC groups:

Massacre (2) (ID: 99558) — formed with Bill Laswell (bass, ID: 661) and Fred Maher (drums, ID: 69826). A three-piece improvising on the edge of funk and noise. Debut album Killing Time (Celluloid, 1981), engineered in part by Martin Bisi (ID: 141327) at OAO Studio and partly in Paris at 24 Rue Dunois (engineered by Jean-Marc Foussat). Frith and Laswell reformed Massacre in 1998 with Charles Hayward replacing Maher, releasing four further albums on Tzadik: Funny Valentine (1998, TZ 7601), Meltdown (2001, TZ 7606), Lonely Heart (2007, TZ 7619), and Love Me Tender (2013, TZ 7642). All reunion albums are live recordings; Frith produced Lonely Heart and co-compiled Love Me Tender with Myles Boisen.

Skeleton Crew (2) (ID: 366111) — Frith and cellist Tom Cora (ID: 198937), with Zeena Parkins (harp/keyboards, ID: 210518). Released Learn To Talk (RecRec Music / Rift, 1984) and The Country Of Blinds (Rift, 1986). The group’s mix of composed song forms, improvisation, and home-made instruments was distinctive in the downtown ecosystem.

Keep The Dog (ID: 704156) — a sextet performing Frith compositions; more orchestrated than his improv groups.

He also played bass in John Zorn’s Naked City (ID: 205814) — the brutal free-jazz/hardcore hybrid featuring Wayne Horvitz, Marc Ribot (ID: 132535), and Yamatsuka Eye.

Composition, Dance, and Theatre (1980s onward)

From the mid-1980s Frith moved increasingly into writing for dance, film, and theatre. Scores for Rova Saxophone Quartet, Ensemble Modern, and Asko Ensemble. He composed for his own Fred Frith Guitar Quartet (ID: 510511). The Technology Of Tears (RecRec, 1988) documented this choreography-oriented work.

Major composition releases: Nous Autres (Les Disques Victo, 1987), The Top Of His Head (Crammed Discs, 1989 — film soundtrack), Dropera (RecRec, 1991), Allies (RecRec, 1996), Eye To Ear (Tzadik, 1997) and sequels.

Mills College and Later Career (1990s–present)

Frith became Professor of Composition at Mills College in Oakland, California, effectively relocating from New York. He continued releasing extensively: solo records on Tzadik (Clearing, 2001; Eye To Ear II, 2004), and on Intakt Records (The Compass, Log, And Lead, 2006; Backscatter Bright Blue, 2014; Edge Of The Light, 2014; It Rolls, 2015; You Are Here, 2017).

He remained prolific as a guest and collaborator well into the 2010s and beyond, with releases on Cuneiform (Friends & Enemies, 1999), Clean Feed, Sub Rosa, and others.

The documentary Step Across The Border (1990) by Nicolas Humbert and Werner Penzel won awards and remains the most widely seen document of his practice.

Etron Fou Leloublan

Frith produced Etron Fou Leloublan’s two studio albums recorded in Switzerland for Turbo Music S.A. — Les Poumons Gonflés (1982) and Les Sillons De La Terre (1984) — as well as the Ayaa reissue of Les Poumons Gonflés (1985) and the final EFL LP Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés (AYAA / RecRec Music, 1985). He also guest-played guitar and violin across these records. EFL were part of the Rock In Opposition movement; the collaboration extends to Frith’s Speechless LP (Ralph Records, 1981), on which Ferdinand Richard (bass, voice) and Guigou Chenevier (drums, tenor saxophone) appeared on the A-side alongside Frith and fellow NYC musicians. Speechless is one of the clearest documents of the RIO-meets-downtown-NYC axis.

Moers Music

Produced Curlew’s North America (1985, Moers Music momu 02042) and played bass on the record. Also co-produced and appeared on David Moss’s Full House (1984, Moers Music momu 02010) and Dense Band (1985). Appeared as performer on Rhys Chatham’s Factor X (1983, Moers Music) — not as producer but as ensemble member. Producer credit on 4 Moers Music releases total.
