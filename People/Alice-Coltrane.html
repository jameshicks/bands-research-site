Alice Coltrane

Harpist, pianist, organist, composer, swamini. Discogs artist ID: 42058. Real name Alice Coltrane née Alice McLeod; also known as Turiyasangitananda. Born August 27, 1937, Detroit, Michigan; died January 12, 2007, Los Angeles, California. Widow of John Coltrane, and one of the foremost figures in spiritual jazz.

Biography

Early Life and Formation (pre-1966)

Alice McLeod grew up in Detroit, where she learned harp at Cass Technical High School under teacher Velma Froude (who had also taught Terry Gibbs around the same period). She was already an accomplished pianist before taking up the instrument professionally. Her first marriage was to musician Kenny “Pancho” Hagood; she used the name Alice Hagood early in her career. She later played in the Terry Gibbs Quartet before meeting John Coltrane.

With John Coltrane (1966–1967)

Alice replaced McCoy Tyner on piano in John Coltrane’s final group in 1966, appearing in his live band through his death in July 1967. She is co-credited alongside John on Cosmic Music (1968, Coltrane Recording Corporation / Impulse!), which contains live and studio recordings from that period, with Pharoah Sanders on tenor saxophone/flute, Jimmy Garrison on bass, and Rashied Ali and Ben Riley on drums.

Impulse! Era — Spiritual Jazz (1968–1974)

Alice launched her solo career on Impulse! with A Monastic Trio (1968), where she produced the record herself and played piano (tracks A1–A3) and harp (B1–B3). The lineup drew directly from the Coltrane group: Pharoah Sanders on flute, bass clarinet, and tenor; Jimmy Garrison on bass; Rashied Ali on drums (A2–B3); Ben Riley on drums (A1). Executive producer Bob Thiele. Liner notes by Amiri Baraka (credited as Ameer Baraka / LeRoi Jones).

Huntington Ashram Monastery (1969, Impulse!) shifted the rhythm section to Rashied Ali on drums and Ron Carter on bass — the only recorded collaboration between Alice and Carter. Alice handled harp, piano, and all compositions.

Ptah, The El Daoud (1970, Impulse!) is a high-water mark: co-billed to Alice Coltrane, Pharoah Sanders, and Joe Henderson, with Ron Carter on bass and Ben Riley on drums. Alice plays piano and harp; she wrote all compositions. Produced by Ed Michel.

Journey In Satchidananda (1971, Impulse!) is her signature record. Co-billed with Pharoah Sanders (soprano sax, percussion), with Cecil McBee on bass, Rashied Ali on drums, and Tulsi on tambura. Alice produced alongside Ed Michel; she also wrote the liner notes. The devotional title track reflects her deepening commitment to Vedantic philosophy.

Universal Consciousness (1971, Impulse!) — solo-leader record; Alice plays harp and organ. Jimmy Garrison returns on bass (tracks A1, A3, B1, B2). Jack DeJohnette plays drums on three tracks; Clifford Jarvis on others. Strings: Leroy Jenkins and three other violinists. Co-produced with Ed Michel.

Lord Of Lords (1972, Impulse!) is the most orchestral Impulse! record: Alice serves as arranger and conductor of a large string ensemble. Charlie Haden on bass; Ben Riley on drums. Produced by Ed Michel. Alice plays harp, piano, organ, tympani, and percussion.

Reflection On Creation And Space (A Five Year View) (1973, Impulse!) compiles earlier material.

The Elements (1974, Milestone) — departure from Impulse!; a live/studio record.

Illuminations (1974, CBS) — collaborative album co-billed with Carlos Santana (credited as Devadip Carlos Santana / Turiya Alice Coltrane). Both produced the record; co-produced with Tom Coster. Large string ensemble, Jimmy Bond on bass.

Warner Bros. Devotional Period (1975–1978)

After founding the Vedantic Center in 1975, Alice moved to Warner Bros. Records for four albums that blend jazz orchestration with Hindu devotional practice. She signed a liner notes byline as “Turiyasangitananda” beginning here.

Eternity (1976, Warner Bros.) — produced by Ed Michel; Alice credited as “produced under the direction of.” All compositions by Alice except one by Igor Stravinsky (B3). Engineer: Baker Bigsby, who continues through the Warner run.

Radha-Krsna Nama Sankirtana (1976, Warner Bros.) — devotional chant record, a significant tonal departure.

Transcendence (1977, Warner Bros.) — Alice plays harp (A1–A3), organ (B1–B4), Fender Rhodes (B2), tambourine, tambura, and wind chimes. Arranged and adapted by Alice (B side). Produced by Ed Michel; mastered by Geoff Sykes.

Transfiguration (1978, Warner Bros.) — final Warner Bros. studio record.

Vedantic Center / Ashram Self-Releases (1982–1995)

After the Warner Bros. period, Alice largely withdrew from the commercial music industry. She founded the Shanti Anantam Ashram in California in 1983 (renamed Sai Anantam Ashram in 1994) and released recordings privately through the Avatar Book Institute imprint:

  • Turiya Sings (1982)
  • Divine Songs (1987)
  • Infinite Chants (1990)
  • Glorious Chants (1995)

These are primarily Hindu devotional recordings (bhajans, kirtans), issued in small quantities and rarely documented by mainstream music press.

In 1995 she appeared on Marian McPartland’s Piano Jazz With Guest Alice Coltrane (The Jazz Alliance), a radio program recording that documented her piano playing in an interview context.

Return to Jazz (2000s)

Translinear Light (2004, Impulse!) is her formal return to jazz after nearly three decades away from the major-label world. Produced by her son Ravi Coltrane, who also mixes the record and plays soprano sax (track 3), tenor sax (tracks 4, 7–9), and percussion. Alice plays piano, organ, and synthesizer across the album. Key personnel: Charlie Haden on bass (tracks 3, 5, 8, 10), James Genus on bass (2, 4, 7), Jack DeJohnette on drums (3, 5, 8, 9), Jeff “Tain” Watts on drums (2, 4, 7). Her son Oran Coltrane plays alto saxophone on track 6. The Sai Anantam Ashram Singers appear on the closing track (11). Two compositions are by John Coltrane (tracks 8–9). Mastered by Kevin Reeves.

The Impulse Story (2006, Impulse!) is a compilation. Alice died January 12, 2007.

Posthumous Releases

  • The Ecstatic Music Of Alice Coltrane Turiyasangitananda (2017, Luaka Bop) — compilation of the Vedantic Center ashram recordings, bringing the 1980s–90s devotional material to wide attention for the first time
  • Carnegie Hall ‘71 (2018, Hi Hat) — archival live recording
  • Kirtan: Turiya Sings (2021, Impulse!/UMe) — reissue of the 1982 ashram material
  • The Carnegie Hall Concert (2024, Impulse!/UMe) — full archival release
