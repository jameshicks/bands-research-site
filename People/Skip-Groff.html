Skip Groff

Full name: Frank Samuel Groff III
Born: November 20, 1948
Died: February 18, 2019
Discogs ID: 271697
Origin: Rockville, Maryland, USA
Name variations on Discogs: Skint Kröfts; Skip Grof

Overview

Skip Groff was a Rockville, Maryland record store owner, label operator, and producer who played a pivotal role in the emergence of Washington DC hardcore. He owned Yesterday & Today Records in Rockville from 1977 to 2002 (when it moved online-only) — a store that served as a hub and bulletin board for the nascent DC punk scene. He ran four labels: Limp Records (2) (ID: 102125), Y&T Records (ID: 68507), Boffo Socko (ID: 217914), and Gemini (7) (ID: 217917).

His production and mixing work on the earliest Dischord Records releases gives him a direct claim on the founding documents of DC hardcore. He produced the Teen Idles’ Minor Disturbance E.P. (Dischord #1, 1980), produced and mixed the S.O.A. No Policy E.P. (Dischord #2, 1981), and mixed the Minor Threat self-titled EP (Dischord #3, 1981) — the three releases that established Dischord’s initial identity. He followed those with production credits on Flex Your Head (1982), the compilation that defined the early DC hardcore scene. These sessions were all engineered by Don Zientara at Inner Ear Studios.

Before the hardcore scene, Groff had been active in the DC area new wave and power-pop ecosystem through Limp Records (2), documenting local acts and compiling the 1978 survey :30 Over D.C.~~Here Comes The New Wave!. After the DC hardcore moment, he operated Y&T Records in the 1990s, releasing Black Market Baby material and a Bad Brains split.

Limp Records (2) — selected roster

DC area artists documented on the label include: The Slickee Boys (multiple releases), Black Market Baby, The Afrika Korps, The Dark (6), Tina Peel, The Nurses, Razz (5), and Minor Threat (In My Eyes EP appeared on the label, catno No. 41).

Key Dischord Collaborations

Groff’s Dischord credits cluster in 1980–1982 and represent the label’s entire first year of existence:

  • Teen Idles, Minor Disturbance E.P. (Dischord #1, 1980) — Producer. Don Zientara engineered. The record that launched Dischord. Personnel: Ian MacKaye (bass), Jeff Nelson (drums/graphics), Geordie Grindle (guitar), Nathan Strejcek (vocals).
  • S.O.A., No Policy E.P. (Dischord #2, 1981) — Producer. Zientara engineered. Personnel: Michael Hampton (3) (guitar), Henry Garfield / Henry Rollins (vocals), Simon Jacobsen (drums), Wendel Blow (bass).
  • Minor Threat, Minor Threat (Dischord #3, 1981) — Mixed By. Minor Threat self-produced; Zientara engineered. Personnel: MacKaye (vocals), Brian Baker (bass), Lyle Preslar (guitar), Nelson (drums).
  • Various, Flex Your Head (Dischord #6, 1982) — Producer on tracks A7–A11 (S.O.A. and Minor Threat tracks). Zientara engineered A7–A16 and B5–B16; Steve Carr engineered A1–A6 and B1–B10.
  • Dischord 1981: The Year In Seven Inches (1993 compilation) — Producer (Teen Idles and S.O.A. tracks 1–18); Mixed By (Minor Threat tracks 19–26).

The Flex Your Head credit scope is notable: Groff produced specifically the S.O.A. and Minor Threat tracks (A7–A11), not the full compilation. The rest was produced by the individual bands.

Connections

  • Don Zientara — engineered all of Groff’s Dischord sessions; the two formed the core production team on the early Dischord catalog. Zientara’s Inner Ear Studios was the recording site.
  • Ian MacKaye — appeared on every major Groff-produced Dischord release (Teen Idles, Minor Threat, and Flex Your Head).
  • Jeff Nelson — drums and graphics on Teen Idles and Minor Threat; co-founder of Dischord alongside MacKaye.
  • Henry Rollins — vocals on S.O.A. No Policy E.P., one of Groff’s core Dischord productions (credited as Henry Garfield).
  • Michael Hampton (3) — guitar on S.O.A. No Policy E.P. and on Flex Your Head.
  • Brian Baker — bass on Minor Threat self-titled EP.
  • Lyle Preslar — guitar on Minor Threat self-titled EP and Flex Your Head Minor Threat tracks.
  • Dischord Records — five primary production/mixing credits across the label’s founding 1980–1982 period; credited on numerous reissues and compilations thereafter.
  • Limp Records (2) — his main label, pre-dating Dischord; documented the DC/MD new wave scene from 1977.
  • Y&T Records — 1990s imprint tied to the Yesterday & Today store; small catalog of DC-adjacent punk reissues.
  • Bad Brains — remixed a Bad Brains track on the Y&T Records split (1990). NOTE: First Daze Here (2002) and First Daze Here Too (2006) on Relapse Records are Pentagram releases, not Bad Brains — the vault previously attributed these incorrectly.
  • Pentagram — produced tracks 1–3, 6, 7, and 10 on First Daze Here (The Vintage Collection) (Relapse, 2002) and certain tracks on First Daze Here Too (Relapse, 2006). These credits reflect Groff’s role as original session producer in 1972–73 at Bias Recording Studios in Falls Church, VA — not archival compilation work. The compilations brought that 1970s-era production credit forward when the sessions were commercially released. Co-producers on the releases include Bobby Liebling, Chris Kozlowski, Gordon Fletcher, and Steve Lorber.
  • Black Market Baby — produced their 1981 Limp Records 7”; released their material on Y&T Records in 1990.
