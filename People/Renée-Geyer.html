Renée Geyer

Australian soul and R&B singer. Discogs artist ID: 49819. Born Renée Rebecca Geyer, 11 September 1953, Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. Died 17 January 2023, Geelong, aged 69 (inoperable lung cancer, complications from hip surgery). One of Australia’s most acclaimed vocalists, recognised for her powerful, husky contralto and a career spanning five decades across multiple continents.

Biography

Origins and early recordings (1972–1974)

Geyer first appeared on Discogs as lead vocalist on the group Sun (58)‘s 1972 single Sun 1972 (RCA Victor), a band listing that also appears in her Discogs group memberships (artist ID: 6412613). She released her first album under her own name, Renee Geyer, on RCA Victor (Australia) in 1973 — a debut with no additional credits listed in Discogs beyond her own primary credit.

Also in 1973 she contributed backing vocals to a recording in the early RCA Australia world — signalling from the outset that session work would run parallel to her solo career. Her second primary credit, the single If Only You Believe (RCA Victor, 1973), was among the most widely pressed of her early releases (45 different pressings documented in Discogs across various territories).

In 1974 she released It’s A Man’s Man’s World (RCA Australia / RCA Victor), on which she is credited not only as primary artist but with sleeve notes for the gatefold interior — an unusual curatorial credit for a debut-era release.

RCA Australia years (1975–1979)

Through the mid-1970s Geyer remained with RCA Australia, releasing a steady stream of albums and singles. Key primary releases in this period include:

  • Moving Along (1977, RCA Victor) — a 12-pressing release on which she also has a Written-By credit
  • Tender Hooks (1977, RCA Victor)
  • Stares And Whispers (1977, RCA Victor)
  • Winner (1978, RCA Victor) — credited as Mixed By, Vocals, Backing Vocals, and Performer with the Renee Geyer Band (Discogs ID: 183198)
  • The Thrill Is Gone (1978, RCA Victor)

She also signed with Polydor for two US-market releases in 1977: Heading In The Right Direction and Quicker Than The Eye, signalling an early attempt at the American market.

In 1977 she appeared on the Australian Radio 3XY tribute compilation 21 Years Of Rock ‘N’ Roll as a featured performer, and contributed to miscellaneous session work including backing vocals for other RCA artists.

In 1979 she produced and sang on Blues License (RCA Victor), co-produced with keyboardist Mal Logan, featuring guitarist Kevin Borich with the Kevin Borich Express, drummer John Watson, and backing vocalist Kerrie Biddell.

Mushroom Records and the Fraboni era (1980–1985)

Geyer’s most celebrated Australian recordings came after her move to Mushroom Records (Discogs label ID: 23489; parent: Warner Music Australia), the Melbourne-based independent that was the dominant force in Australian rock through the late 1970s and 1980s.

Her first Mushroom release, Hot Minutes (1980), was followed by three albums in 1981 that constitute the commercial and artistic peak of her Australian career:

I Can Feel The Fire (1981, Mushroom) — produced by Rob Fraboni. The single is co-written by Ron Wood (credited as “R. Wood”), placing Geyer in the orbit of the Stones/Faces world that Fraboni had been connected to since the mid-1970s.

Say I Love You (1981, Mushroom, release ID: 919395) — the Side A tracks produced by Rob Fraboni; Side B produced by Frank Wilson (3) and mixed by Christo Curtis, John Sayers, and Geyer herself. This was one of her signature releases.

So Lucky (1981, Mushroom, release ID: 1224397) — produced by Rob Fraboni; co-produced by Ricky Fataar; engineered by Tim Kramer. Fataar (Discogs ID: 219999), a South African-born drummer previously in The Beach Boys, became a key figure in Geyer’s recordings. Tim Kramer (Discogs ID: 174074) was Fraboni’s regular engineer, the same team that recorded Ian McLagan’s Bump In The Night the same year.

In 1982 Geyer released Love So Sweet (Mushroom), and also a self-titled album for Portrait Records (CBS) in the US (release ID: 3132723) — her most explicit attempt to break North America. That US release was produced by Fraboni and co-produced by Ricky Fataar, with The Bump Band serving as her backing group: Ray Ohara (bass), Ricky Fataar (drums/keyboards), Johnny Lee Schell (guitar/piano), and Ian McLagan (keyboards/backing vocals). Additional contributors included Blondie Chaplin (backing vocals), James Ingram (backing vocals), Venetta Fields (backing vocals), Bobby Keys (saxophone), and Bobby King (backing vocals) — an extraordinary studio gathering at the intersection of the Fraboni/Stones circle and Los Angeles session culture.

The 1983 compilation Faves (Mushroom, release ID: 7859573) drew on multiple producers who had worked with her by that point: Fraboni (tracks 1, 3), Ern Rose (tracks 2, 9, 11, 12, 14), Frank Wilson (tracks 4, 5, 8), Ricky Fataar (tracks 6, 7), Mark Moffatt (tracks 6, 7), Mal Logan and James “Jimbo” Barton (track 13), and Geyer herself (track 13). She also released the live set Renee Live (1983, Mushroom) and the studio album Trouble In Paradise (1983, Mushroom), produced by Ricky Fataar.

She then released Every Beat Of My Heart (1984, Festival Records) and Sing To Me (1985, WEA, release ID: 1294528) — the latter a complex session featuring Ricky Fataar as producer/drummer and Andrew Farriss of INXS on keyboards, alongside Tim Kramer as engineer and mixer. Also in 1985: All My Love (WEA, Germany).

Los Angeles session work: the major era (1985–1994)

After relocating to Los Angeles, Geyer entered the city’s upper tier of session vocalists. Major credits in this period:

Sting — …Nothing Like The Sun (A&M Records, 1987, release ID: 446305): backing vocals alongside Dolette McDonald, Janice Pendarvis, and Vesta Williams. Produced by Neil Dorfsman and Sting. One of the most widely pressed releases she appeared on (163 different pressings documented).

Easy Pieces (A&M Records, 1988, release ID: 3339563): Geyer was a primary member of this short-lived Los Angeles vocal/funk group alongside Hamish Stuart (ex-Average White Band). Produced by David “Hawk” Wolinski and Eric Thorngren; executive produced by Jimmy Iovine and John McClain. Other performers included Anthony Jackson (bass), Steve Ferrone (drums), Neil Larsen (keyboards), and Randy Brecker (additional musician). The Easy Pieces group is listed in her Discogs memberships (artist ID: 1416146).

Rod Stewart — Out Of Order (1988, Warner Bros.): backing vocals, continuing the Faces world connection through the Fraboni circle.

Neil Diamond — The Best Years Of Our Lives (CBS, 1988, Europe): backing vocals (17 pressings).

Sting — Nothing Like The Sun (additional entries) — she appears in further pressings of this LP.

Think Out Loud (A&M, Japan, 1988): backing vocals (10 pressings).

**Australian Olympians — You’re Not Alone ** (CBS, 1988): performer credit on the official 1988 Australian Olympic team song.

John Hiatt — Slow Turning (1988): John Hiatt collaboration.

Jackson Browne — Lives In The Balance (1986): organ (credited via McLagan who also played, but Geyer appears in backing vocal credits).

Trouble Over Here, Trouble Over There (Island Records, 1987): backing vocals.

Difficult Woman (Larrikin Records, 1994, release ID: 4531868): Australian comeback album produced by Paul Kelly (2). US-based session: Jimmy Haslip (bass), John Molo (drums), Johnny Lee Schell (guitar), Marty Grebb (keyboards), Lenny Castro (percussion), Dori Caymmi (guitar), Alain Johannes (guitar/sitar). Also featured Carmen Twillie, Julia Tillman Waters, and Maxine Waters (the Waters Sisters) on backing vocals for track 7, and Michael “Patches” Stewart (trumpet). Geyer also contributed solo whistle on track 5.

Return to Australia and later career (1996–2023)

After her LA session years, Geyer returned periodically to Australia for solo releases:

  • The Best Of Renée Geyer 1973–1998 (Mushroom, 1998, release ID: 720982) — a double-disc compilation produced across multiple producers: Rob Fraboni (tracks 1-10 to 1-14), Ern Rose, Frank Wilson, Gus McNeil, James Ingram (3), Joe Camilleri, Martin Armiger, Paul Kelly (2), and Tweed Harris
  • I Need Love (Groovilicious, 1998, US) — self-produced; written-by credit
  • Sweet Life (Mushroom, 1999) — primary artist; backing vocals producer
  • Two Wings (Mushroom, 1999) — co-production, arranged and produced backing vocals
  • The Great Renee Geyer (Rajon Music Group, 2001)
  • Tenderland (ABC Music / Universal Music Australia, 2003, release ID: 1260961) — self-produced; featuring Ross Hannaford (guitar), Mark Punch (guitar), Bruce Haymes (keyboards), Jex Saarelaht (piano), John Watson (drums/bongos), Steve Hadley (bass/drum programming), Kylie Auldist and Vika Bull and Linda Bull and others (backing vocals)
  • Tonight (ABC Music, 2005) — self-produced; credited for horns arrangement, strings arrangement, keyboards, and vocals
  • Seven Deadly Sins (ABC Music, 1993) — associated with the ABC TV series

She contributed to several Australian artist records through the 1980s and 1990s: backing vocals on Jimmy Barnes’s For The Working Class Man (Mushroom, 1985), the Jimmy Barnes self-titled US album (Geffen, 1985), and the Midnight Oil circle. She worked with Joe Camilleri (credited as producer on the 1998 compilation for track 1-18) and Paul Kelly (2), two of Melbourne’s most significant figures.

Geyer died on 17 January 2023 in Geelong following complications from hip surgery that had led to the discovery of inoperable lung cancer.

Group Memberships

Discogs documents Geyer as a member of several named groups:

  • Sun (58) (artist ID: 6412613) — early group; Sun 1972
  • Renee Geyer Band (artist ID: 183198) — her primary Australian touring group; credited on Winner (1978) and Faves (1983) as producer
  • Easy Pieces (artist ID: 1416146) — Los Angeles vocal/funk group (1988, A&M Records)
  • Australia Too (artist ID: 2110623)
  • Australian Olympians (artist ID: 867375) — 1988 Olympic song performers

Connections

  • Rob Fraboni — primary producer of her peak Mushroom releases; the Fraboni connection brought The Bump Band circle into her recordings
  • Ian McLagan — mutual appearance: she guested on Bump In The Night (B5); McLagan played on her 1982 US album
  • Ricky Fataar — co-producer from 1981 onwards; central to her transition from RCA to Mushroom and her LA session world
  • Paul Kelly (2) — produced Difficult Woman (1994); later compilation credit
  • Sting — session credit on …Nothing Like The Sun (1987)
  • Easy Pieces — LA group co-membership (1988)
  • Mushroom Records — primary label from 1980 onwards; retrospective compilation in 1998
