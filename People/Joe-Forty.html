Overview

Joe Forty is a bassist, songwriter, and occasional producer associated with the UK post-punk group The Lines, with a recording career documented from 1979 onward. His catalogue is concentrated in the early 1980s across Red Records, Illegal Records, and Linear Records, with scattered later releases through the 1990s and a reissue phase via Acute Records running through the 2000s and 2010s. His tightest ongoing collaboration is with Rico Conning (13 shared releases), followed by the broader Lines orbit including Nick Cash and Mick Linehan. His credited roles extend beyond bass to writing, production, and artwork, suggesting broad creative involvement rather than a purely instrumental role.

Notes (web)

The Lines — dormancy and Acute Records reissues

Why The Lines went dormant (1983). According to a 2016 Red Bull Music Academy interview with Rico Conning, “in ‘83 The Lines fell apart, mainly through financial reasons. We all had to work on other things to live.” (redbullmusicacademy.com, snippet) The band had recorded and performed from 1977 until approximately 1982–83, releasing two albums — Therapy (1981) and Ultramarine (1983) — plus a run of singles and an EP, all on Red Records (4), Illegal Records (2), and Linear Records (3). After Ultramarine the members drifted apart, with Conning going on to engineering and production work (Wire, Coil, Depeche Mode, Swans). No source identifies a specific split announcement or a single precipitating event beyond financial pressure and members pursuing other work.

There were also late-period sessions (April 1982–August 1983) recorded first as home experiments and then at William Orbit’s Guerilla Studios, but these were never finalised as the band dispersed. (carparkrecords.com)

The real name of “Jo Forty”. The JazzRockSoul and other sources consistently spell the name Jo Forty (not “Joe”), and describe Jo as a co-founder who met Rico Conning on an English-literature course around 1976. The Discogs page uses “Joe Forty” — possible variant or error. (jazzrocksoul.com)

Acute Records reissue campaign (2008–2016). The New York label Acute Records — run by Dan Selzer — reissued The Lines’ catalogue starting in late 2007/early 2008 because the original releases had become rare and expensive collector’s items and had never appeared on CD. (tinymixtapes.com snippet) Two compilations appeared in 2008:

  • Memory Span (ACU010, May 2008) — singles and EP tracks compilation; Jo Forty credited for artwork (original flyers).
  • Flood Bank (ACU011, November 2008) — the two albums in full (minus one track omitted for space).

In 2016 Acute released Hull Down (ACU016, May 2016) — described as a “lost third album” assembled by Selzer from the Guerilla Studios cassette tapes recorded circa 1982–83, blending and cross-fading different takes. Acute stated this was their final release. Jo Forty is credited on Hull Down as bass, sequencer, and inner gatefold art. (carparkrecords.com; thelineslondon.bandcamp.com/album/hull-down)

No source explicitly states why Selzer or Acute chose The Lines specifically; the implication across multiple reviews is that the records had cult status and genuine scarcity — they were circulating as expensive second-hand items while remaining unknown to a wider audience. The Fact Magazine 2016 end-of-year list cited Hull Down among the best reissues of the year. (factmag.com snippet)
