David Eyges

Discogs ID: 380102 | Real name: David Macaulay Eyges | Born: 6 November 1950, San Francisco, California, USA

Overview

David Eyges (pronounced “EE-jus”) is an American cellist, composer, and producer active from the early 1970s through at least the late 1990s. He occupies a distinct position in the free jazz and improvised music worlds as one of the few cellists to have sustained a career in those idioms: most of his recorded output treats the cello as a frontline improvising instrument rather than an orchestral ornament. His discography spans three broad phases: an early-1970s stint as a sideman in Gunter Hampel’s Galaxie Dream Band (Birth Records, Germany); a mid-to-late-1970s run as a leader on his own debut album and in small-group free improvisation settings around New York; and a 1990s return to recording through the Paul Bley Trio sessions on Venus Records (Japan) and his trio’s Crossroads on Quixotic Records. His closest and most sustained creative partnership was with multi-reed player Byard Lancaster, with whom he co-led the David Eyges Trio (also including drummer Sunny Murray) and recorded The Arrow as a duo (1981). He also participated in significant sideman dates for Cecil McBee and Arthur Blythe.

Name variations in credits: “D. Eyges”, “Eyges”.

David Eyges Trio

Discogs ID: 5269584

The trio consisted of:

  • David Eyges — cello
  • Byard Lancaster — alto saxophone, soprano saxophone, flute, piccolo flute
  • Sunny Murray — drums

The trio’s primary documented recording is Crossroads (2000, Quixotic Records (2)), which also credits Lancaster and Murray individually as co-headliners. The release was produced by Macaulay (3) and engineered by David Baker and John Kilgore.
