Mark Ibold

Mark Ibold

Oct 18, 20266 min read

Mark Ibold

Born: October 17, 1962
Real name: Mark Alan Ibold
Role: Bassist, multi-instrumentalist
Discogs ID: 403612
Name variations on records: IB, Ibold, Marcus Ibold, Mark, S I Bold, S Ibold, S. Ibold

American bassist best known for his decade with Pavement (1989–1999; rejoined for 2010 reunion) and his subsequent five years with Sonic Youth (2006–2011). Before either of those, he played in New York noise-rock outfit Dustdevils and remained active in the downtown experimental scene throughout his career, contributing to Free Kitten, Spectre Folk, Wall Drug, and various one-off sessions.

Career Timeline

Early Years — Dustdevils and the New York Underground (1989–1991)

Ibold’s first documented recordings are with Dustdevils, a New York noise-rock band. He appears as performer on the compilation …Is Big Leggy (1989, Teenbeat) and on the Dustdevils LP Struggling Electric And Chemical (1990, Matador / Teenbeat), which was recorded uncredited by Wharton Tiers. Personnel on that record: Jacqui Dulany, Ibold, Michael Duane, and Rick Nance.

In 1991 he appears on the four-band New York compilation Chinny Chin Chin: 4 N.Y. Bands (See Eye Records), credited alongside members of what would become Wall Drug — notably Lissmo Köb (later Martin Köb) and Tedd Prudhomme (credited as “Tedd Pnud’homme” on the comp). Ibold is listed as performer on tracks 22–27, i.e. the Wall Drug segment.

Pavement — Core Member (1992–1999)

Ibold joined Pavement in time to play on their full commercial breakthrough and carried through to the band’s dissolution. He appears on four studio albums:

He also appears on numerous associated singles: “Cut Your Hair” (1994), “Gold Soundz” (1994), “Range Life” (1995), “Rattled by La Rush” (1995), “Pacific Trim” (1996). The Brighten the Corners credit specifically names him as part of “Pavement, The Nicene Creeders,” the full touring and recording unit. Production on that album was handled by Bryce Goggin (recorded) and Mitch Easter (also recorded). Terror Twilight was produced and mixed by Nigel Godrich.

The Pavement lineup through this period: Stephen Malkmus (vocals, guitar), Spiral Stairs (guitar), Bob Nastanovich (percussion, vocals), Mark Ibold (bass), Steve West (drums).

Pavement disbanded in 1999.

Free Kitten — Intermittent Bass Role (1994–1997)

Ibold played bass on two Free Kitten releases, the band led by Kim Gordon and Julie Cafritz:

  • Nice Ass (1994, Wiiija Records) — bass, alongside Yoshimi (drums/trumpet/harmonica). Recorded/mixed by Fred Brockman.
  • Sentimental Education (1997, Wiiija Records) — performer. Produced by Kim Gordon and Wharton Tiers; mixed by Wharton Tiers. Drummer on this record was J Mascis. Laetitia Sadier and Marie-Pierre Bonniol contributed French translations to one track.

Also appears on the Free Kitten-adjacent Punks Suing Punks EP (1995, Kill Rock Stars) — bass — and the Unboxed collection (1995, Wiiija Records).

Session — Thurston Moore (1998)

Appears on “Piece For Yvonne Rainer” (1998, Sunship Records), a release credited to Thurston Moore, playing Jew’s Harp alongside Yoshimi (also Jew’s Harp) and Moore on guitar and amp. A brief but characteristic piece of downtown experimental scene cross-pollination.

After Pavement / Pre-Sonic Youth (1999–2006)

After Pavement’s dissolution in 1999, Ibold appears on the Stray Slack: About As Valid As A Record Contract compilation (1993, Pseudo Indie Label — likely documented/released later, catalogued under 1993) and is documented on the Chinny Chin Chin compilation from the early period. During the gap years he was involved with Spectre Folk (see below).

Sonic Youth — Full Member (2006–2011)

Ibold joined Sonic Youth as their bassist around 2006, filling a role the band had played with various configurations over the years. He is credited on:

The Eternal (2009) was the band’s final studio album, produced by Sonic Youth and John Agnello, recorded by Agnello and Aaron Mullan. The full performing lineup: Kim Gordon, Lee Ranaldo, Mark Ibold, Steve Shelley, Thurston Moore.

Sonic Youth dissolved in 2011 following Moore and Gordon’s separation.

Spectre Folk (2004–2017)

Ibold was a member of Spectre Folk, a loosely assembled folk-influenced ensemble also featuring Steve Shelley (drums), Pete Nolan (guitar), Samara Lubelski, Aaron Mullan, Brian Sullivan, and Peter Meehan. Key releases:

Pavement Reunion (2010; 2025)

Ibold participated in the 2010 Pavement reunion. On Discogs, a 2025 release Pavements (Matador) credits him as Performer [Pavement], suggesting further reunion activity.

Wall Drug (1990–1991)

Wall Drug is a New York trio of Ibold, Martin Köb, and Tedd Prudhomme. Their sole documented release on Discogs is Have You Dug (1990, Nur Sch. Rec! — Austria). The connection to the Chinny Chin Chin compilation (1991) places them in the same downtown New York orbit.
