Biography

Jozef Van Wissem is a Dutch minimalist composer and lutenist based in Brooklyn. His practice centres on the baroque and renaissance lute played through extended technique and drone-oriented composition — long, hypnotic lines built on tunings that run against the instrument’s historical function. NPR’s All Songs Considered described his work as “heavy and set in hypnotic darkness.” He plays a custom all-black, one-of-a-kind baroque lute built to his specification.

Van Wissem studied lute in New York with Patrick O’Brien. He founded his own imprint, Incunabulum, which handles much of his solo and smaller-run output; the label URL points directly to his own website, confirming it as a personal/artist label. His wider discography runs through Important Records (2), Sacred Bones, Crammed Discs, and the Belgian label Consouling Sounds.

He is a member of the group Brethren Of The Free Spirit alongside guitarist James Blackshaw, with whom he recorded The Wolf Also Shall Dwell With The Lamb (2008, Important Records, cat# IMPREC222), blending 13-course baroque lute with 12-string guitar in closely related tunings.

He is also listed as a member of Mort Subite (2) and Desert Corbusier.

Collaboration with Jim Jarmusch

Van Wissem’s most sustained and high-profile partnership is with filmmaker and musician Jim Jarmusch (member of SQÜRL). Their recorded collaborations begin at least as early as The Joy That Never Ends (2011, Important Records), where Jarmusch contributed electric guitar on one track, and deepen into a series of co-credited albums:

  • Concerning The Entrance Into Eternity (2012, Important Records) — Van Wissem on 13-course baroque lute, Jarmusch on electric guitar, acoustic guitar, and voice; texts drawn from St John of the Cross; produced by Van Wissem
  • Apokatastasis (2012, Incunabulum) — credited “Jozef Van Wissem Featuring Jim Jarmusch”; lute and 12-string electric guitar by Van Wissem, Jarmusch on guitars and tape loops
  • The Mystery Of Heaven (2012, Sacred Bones) — co-credited Van Wissem & Jim Jarmusch; also involved Tilda Swinton
  • Only Lovers Left Alive (2013/2014, ATP Recordings) — the soundtrack to Jarmusch’s vampire film; co-credited Van Wissem / SQÜRL; Van Wissem won the Cannes Soundtrack Award at the 2013 Cannes Film Festival for this score
  • It Is Time For You To Return (2014, Crammed Discs) — Van Wissem solo with Jarmusch on guitar for one track; also features vocalist Yasmine Hamdan
  • An Attempt To Draw Aside The Veil (2019, Sacred Bones) — Van Wissem & Jarmusch; Van Wissem on lute, electric guitar, electronics, and beats; Jarmusch on guitars and synthesizer; engineered by Paula Kiczek, mastered by Josh Bonati

The Discogs collaborator graph shows Jarmusch credited on 47 releases alongside Van Wissem — by far his most frequent collaborator.

Smegma / Experimental Collaborations

Van Wissem appeared on Mirage (2010, Important Records) by Smegma, playing lute on tracks 2 and 4. The session brought together multiple Smegma members: Ju Suk Reet Meate (bass, organ, trumpet, mix, recording), Dennis Duck (drums, recording), Ace Farren Ford (horns), Tom Recchion (effects, recording), Oblivia (3) (effects), and Donkey Flybye (bass, organ on the same tracks as Van Wissem’s lute).

Earlier, on Das Platinzeitalter (2007, Incunabulum), Van Wissem co-produced a session with Italian industrial/electronics composer Maurizio Bianchi (M.B.), playing renaissance lute alongside Bianchi’s archaic electronics. This was an early marker of his willingness to work across genre lines.

Film and Game Scoring

  • Only Lovers Left Alive (2013) — Cannes Soundtrack Award winner; the film starred Tilda Swinton and Tom Hiddleston
  • Partir To Live (2015, Sacred Bones) — soundtrack to the Chilean film; additional contributions from sound designers Domingo García-Huidobro and Felipe Zegers
  • New Lute Music For Film (2017, Incunabulum) — a compilation-style LP of lute cues composed for film contexts
  • Un Prince (2024, Incunabulum) — original soundtrack recording; Van Wissem on lute, electronics, 12-string electric guitar, and beats
  • The Sims Medieval (EA/Maxis video game) — Van Wissem composed the music score

He was also commissioned by the National Gallery, London to compose a sound piece responding to Hans Holbein’s painting The Ambassadors.

Scene Connections

Van Wissem sits at an unusual intersection: early music (baroque/renaissance lute practice), downtown New York experimental (Smegma adjacency, LAFMS circuit via Mirage), drone/minimal composition, and arthouse film scoring. His move to Brooklyn put him in contact with the film/music world around Jarmusch. The Brethren Of The Free Spirit project connects him to the UK post-folk / new weird America fringe via James Blackshaw. His Consouling Sounds releases link to the Belgian experimental/post-metal scene. David Tibet’s credit on A Priori gestures toward the broader Current 93 / spiritual-music underground.

The Cannes Soundtrack Award and The Sims Medieval commission are anomalies in an otherwise underground catalog — they represent institutional recognition without apparent compromise of the drone-lute aesthetic.
