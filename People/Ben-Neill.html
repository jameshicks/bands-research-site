Ben Neill

Composer, trumpeter, electronic musician, and inventor of the mutantrumpet. Born November 14, 1957, Winston-Salem, North Carolina. Based in New York City. Discogs artist ID: 26388.

Name variations: Ben; Neill Benjamin Stowe III.

Overview

Ben Neill is the inventor of the mutantrumpet — a modified acoustic trumpet with extra bells, valves, a trombone slide, and electronic modifications that allow him to control digital audio and video in performance. The first prototype (1981) was built with an analog processing system custom-made by Robert Moog; subsequent versions developed through residencies at STEIM Studios in Amsterdam (1985, 2008, 2014) added MIDI capabilities and expanded trigger interfaces. David Behrman also designed a pitch-sensing computer program tailored to Neill’s live performance setup.

Neill studied at the Dana School of Music at Youngstown State University (B.M., M.M.) and at Manhattan School of Music (D.M.A., 1983). His trumpet teachers included Esotto Pellegrini, Bernard Adelstein, and James Darling; he also studied privately with La Monte Young and was mentored by Jon Hassell. He trained alongside Wynton Marsalis at the North Carolina School of the Arts and Eastern Music Festival.

In 1980 he formed The B-Minors with drummer Don Yallech in Ohio, participating in the Northeast Ohio punk/new wave scene. By 1983 he had relocated to New York City, where he became embedded in the downtown experimental music world.

His recordings span avant-garde composition (Mainspring, 1988; ITSOFOMO, 1992), electronic club-music crossovers (Green Machine, 1995; Triptycal, 1996; Goldbug, 1998), and collaborative multimedia work. From 1992 to 1999 he served as Music Curator of The Kitchen, NYC’s key venue for new music, where he fostered the emerging electronic scene. He later taught at New Jersey Institute of Technology and Ramapo College (becoming Emeritus Professor in 2025). His book Diffusing Music: Trajectories of Sonic Democratization was published by Bloomsbury Academic Press in December 2024. (Source: Wikipedia)

Key Projects

Mainspring (1988, Ear-Rational Records)

Neill’s debut LP. Genres: Electronic, Jazz, Rock; styles: Avantgarde, Future Jazz, Minimal. Engineered primarily by Martin Bisi (sides A and B1) and Don Hünerberg (B2–B3). Cover painting by David Wojnarowicz. Track B1 “Dis-Solution II” uses a pitch-sensing computer program by David Behrman; track B2 “No More People” features Robert Poss on electric guitar alongside William Kannar (contrabass), Dora Ohrenstein (soprano vocals). The LP is co-released on the Dossier distribution infrastructure (matrix includes “DOSSIER ST 7552”).

ITSOFOMO (In The Shadow Of Forward Motion) (1992, New Tone Records)

Major collaborative multimedia work created with visual artist and AIDS activist David Wojnarowicz in 1989, released on CD in 1992 (Italy). Wojnarowicz supplies words, vocals, and cover artwork; Neill handles composition, computer, electronics, mutantrumpet, and production. Engineered by Martin Bisi and Wharton Tiers (the latter credited for “Hollerin’ Voices”). The piece has been exhibited at the New Museum, Tate Modern, and PPOW Gallery, and was featured in the PBS documentary Imagining America. (Source: Wikipedia)

Green Machine (1995, Astralwerks)

An installation/performance work presented at the Paula Cooper Gallery in 1994, before the CD release. Co-produced by Neill and Mikel Rouse. The album title track also appears on Triptycal (1996).

Triptycal (1996, Antilles / Verve Forecast)

Crossover album blending downtempo, drum and bass, and Future Jazz. Notable collaborators: DJ Spooky (as “DJ Spooky That Subliminal Kid,” turntables on three tracks) and DJ Olive (turntables on two tracks). Co-produced with Eric Calvi.

Goldbug (1998, Antilles)

Features Page Hamilton (Helmet) on guitar and vocals across three tracks, DJ Spooky on turntables, and Jane Scarpantoni on cello. Remix bonus tracks by Spring Heel Jack, X-Ecutioners, and Krust. Producer and engineer: Eric Calvi.

Automotive (2002, Six Degrees Records)

Composed entirely from extended versions of music originally written for Volkswagen TV and internet commercials — described by Timothy Taylor in The Sounds of Capitalism (University of Chicago Press, 2012) as an early example of the convergence of content and commerce. Robert Poss contributes electric guitar on tracks 2, 3, 7, and 8. (Source: Wikipedia)

Songs For Persephone (2011, Ramseur Records)

CD release of music from the 2010 music theater work Persephone, co-created with vocalist Mimi Goese, book by Warren Leight, directed by Ridge Theater, and featuring Julia Stiles. The production was presented at the Brooklyn Academy of Music’s Next Wave Festival in 2010. (Source: Wikipedia)

Die Donnergötter (2006, Radium (3))

Rhys Chatham’s ensemble piece. Neill plays electric guitar (track 1) and trumpet (track 2). Other guitarists on track 1 include Robert Poss, Karen Haglof, Bill Brovold, Mitch Salmen, Tim Schellenbaum. Mixed by Martin Bisi (track 1) and Wharton Tiers (track 2). Track 5 features George Lewis (trombone), Jim Staley (trombone), Olu Dara (trumpet), Anton Fier (drums).
