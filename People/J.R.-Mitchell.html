J.R. Mitchell

Discogs ID: 739240 Real Name: James Roland Mitchell Born: April 13, 1937, Philadelphia, PA Died: January 25, 2004, New York, NY Name variations: Dr. J.R. Mitchell, J. R. Mitchell, Mitchell

American jazz drummer, composer, educator, and label founder. Philadelphia-based free jazz and post-bop figure, active from the late 1960s through the early 2000s. Long-running collaborator of Byard Lancaster. Founded Doria Records as his self-run imprint. Also credited as “Dr. J.R. Mitchell” — suggesting an academic position, and he is identified in Discogs as an educator.

Groups

  • J.R. Mitchell Quintet (Discogs ID 11367929) — early ensemble, self-titled LP 1969
  • J.R. Mitchell Universal Ensemble (Discogs ID 14770112) — Mov’en cassette 1982
  • The Byard Lancaster Unit (Discogs ID 1478440) — with Byard Lancaster; Live At Macalester College reissue 2008
  • The J.R. Mitchell Experimental Unit (Discogs ID 1478439) — no documented releases in Discogs

Live At Macalester College 72 (1972, Dogtown Records)

  • Recorded at Macalester College, Minnesota
  • Personnel: Byard Lancaster — alto sax, trumpet, soprano sax, bass clarinet, flute, tenor sax; J.R. Mitchell — drums; Calvin Hill — acoustic bass (A1); Paul Morrison (4) — electric bass (A1); Jerome Hunter — electric bass (A2, B1, B2); Lester Lumley — congas, percussion (A1); Sid Simmons — piano (A2, B1, B2)

Live (1980, Doria Records, JRM 101)

  • LP; own-label release (catalog number JRM = his initials)
  • Personnel: J.R. Mitchell — percussion; John Neves — bass; Sonelius Smith — piano; Gary Hammond (2) — soprano and tenor saxophone
  • Tracks: “On A Windy Afternoon In Remini’s Of New York”, “Ballad For Sabina”, “The Need To Smile”, “Are You For Me?”, “A Sunday Afternoon With Her”, “Latin Fiesta In Mexico Marketplace With Irene & Michael”, “John’s Hymn”

Philadelphia Spirit In New York (2001, CIMP, CIMP 239)

  • CD; co-led quartet: Byard Lancaster, Odean Pope, Edward Crockett, J.R. Mitchell
  • Personnel: Lancaster — alto sax, bells, flute; Pope — tenor and soprano sax; Crockett — bass; Mitchell — drums
  • Produced by Bob Rusch; recorded and engineered by Bob Rusch
  • Mitchell composed: “People From Zimbabwe”, “Blues For A Beautiful Brother”, “Ballad For Sabina”; co-composed “McCoy T” and “Collage” (with Lancaster, Crockett, Pope)
  • Also features compositions by Odean Pope (6 tracks) and covers: “Miss Ann” (Eric Dolphy)
  • 12 tracks; includes liner notes by Byard Lancaster

The Outcry (undated, Lancaster Recording, 9003)

  • CD; co-led: Edward Crockett, J.R. Mitchell, Byard Lancaster
  • Personnel: Crockett — acoustic bass; Mitchell — drums; Lancaster — woodwinds; Kathy Kilpatrick — bassoon (guest)
