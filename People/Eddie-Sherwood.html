Eddie Sherwood

Manchester drummer and percussionist. Discogs artist ID: 952203. Name variations: E.Sherwood, Edward Sherwood, Sherwood.

Biography

Origins and Biting Tongues (1979–1983)

Eddie Sherwood was a founding member of Biting Tongues from their very first performance — a live soundtrack to a silent 16mm film at Tony Wilson’s Factory Club (the Russell Club, Hulme) in 1979. That one-off event brought together Sherwood, Howard Walmsley, and Ken Hollings; a few weeks later Colin Seddon and Graham Massey joined after disbanding their previous group the Post Natals, settling the classic five-piece lineup: Walmsley (sax), Seddon (bass), Sherwood (drums), Massey (guitar/violin/woodwinds), Hollings (text/vocals).

Sherwood drummed on every key release of this early period. The debut LP Don’t Heal (1981, Situation Two, SITU 1) — the first release on Situation Two, a Beggars Banquet subsidiary — was produced by Stuart James across two separate sessions (Drone Studios, Chorlton, and Pennine Studios, Oldham). The track listing included material played straight through with random tapes running throughout, their accidental intrusions kept in the mix.

Also in 1981, Biting Tongues issued Live It as the third entry in New Hormones’s cassette series (CAT 3; 500 copies). Sherwood is credited as composer on this release (as “Edward Sherwood”), reflecting the collective authorship the group always maintained for their material.

In 1982, Biting Tongues appeared on a split 12” with Belgian group Tank Of Danzig (Antler Records, Belgium, ANT 006). Sherwood plays drums, percussion, and backing vocals on both Biting Tongues tracks (“Evening State” and “Lock Up State”) and shares written-by credit alongside Hollings. The full Biting Tongues side featured Massey on guitar, trumpet and percussion; Seddon on bass and percussion; Walmsley on piano, saxophone and percussion. Belgian producer Roland Beelen produced the overall 12”.

Libreville (1983, Paragon (2), VIRTUE 1) was the group’s most sustained early statement, recorded at Revolution Studios, Cheadle Hulme, and Bootleg Studios. Sherwood is credited as “Musician [Biting Tongues]” alongside the other four members. Some sessions were self-produced by the band (with engineer Roger Salmon); others were produced by Roland Beelen (with engineer Stewart Pickering). The LP had originally been intended for New Hormones (catalogued as ORG 26) but was reassigned when that label ran short of funds.

Departure and Inner Sense Percussion Ensemble (1983–)

Sherwood and Seddon both departed Biting Tongues before the Factory period. They went on to co-found the Inner Sense Percussion Ensemble (Discogs group ID: 10613) in 1985, a long-running Manchester institution based initially in a Levenshulme bedsit and dedicated to samba batucada and world percussion. Inner Sense became a significant presence in Manchester’s world music scene.

Before Inner Sense, Sherwood had also briefly served as the first drummer in Simply Red — a post that led him toward the percussion ensemble path full time.

Nico Live Session (1982)

The ROIR cassette release Do Or Die! (1982) — a live document of Nico backed by various Manchester musicians — credits Sherwood on drums alongside Toby Toman (also drums), with a backing band drawn from Blue Orchids and Samarkand. Martin Bramah (guitar), Una Baines (keyboards), and Steve Garvey (bass) are also credited. This was a distinct project from Biting Tongues and places Sherwood within the broader Manchester underground scene of the early 1980s.

Retrospective and Reunion (2003–2020)

After The Click — Retrospective 1980–89 (2003, LTM (4), LTMCD 2371) compiled and remastered the early Biting Tongues catalog, with track selection and cover image by Graham Massey. Per-track credits on the CD document Sherwood’s specific contributions across the early recordings:

Tracks 12–15 (from the Factory and later periods) credit Phil Kirby on drums; track 16 credits Seddon on drums — confirming Sherwood was replaced at different points after his departure. The term “Dalinian Drums” on “Reflector” hints at the surrealist-percussive framework the group brought to even standard kit work.

The 2019 reissue of Live It on Cache Cache (credit as “Edward Sherwood”) confirmed him as composer alongside the other four members.

Sherwood returned for the Biting Tongues reunion LP Clear Your Screens (2020, The Wormhole), playing drums and sharing written-by credits with Seddon, Massey, Walmsley, and Hollings. Massey handled guitar, trumpet, woodwind, and electric violin; Walmsley contributed tenor saxophone, organ, and Moog; Hollings supplied text, voice, compiled, and liner notes. The album was mastered by Stephan Mathieu.
