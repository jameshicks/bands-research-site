Keith Beal

Keith Beal

Aug 19, 20268 min read

Keith Beal

Discogs artist ID: 272600

British recording engineer, producer, and multi-instrumentalist. Co-founder of Ogun in 1973 alongside Harry Miller and Hazel Miller. Served as the primary production force behind the Ogun catalog throughout the label’s foundational LP era (1974–1979), accumulating producer, engineer, editor, and liner notes credits across almost every release in that period. Later became a performing member of Trevor Watts’ Moiré Music, credited as a multi-reed saxophonist.

Background and Ogun Co-Founding

Beal co-founded Ogun Records in 1973 with bassist Harry Miller and Hazel Miller, forming what became one of the central British outlets for the London free jazz and South African exile jazz communities of the 1970s. His role was primarily that of studio overseer and production coordinator rather than performer — on the early releases he handled the complete production chain from recording through mixing and editing.

The Discogs artist profile cites two URLs connecting Beal to the label’s history: an article in the Morning Star and the official Ogun history page at ogunrecording.co.uk. No realname or biographical detail beyond “British recording engineer” is recorded in the Discogs database.

Ogun Era: 1974–1979

Beal is credited on the entire Ogun LP run from the label’s first releases in 1974 through 1979, typically in combined roles as Producer, Recorded By, Mixed By, and Edited By. His credits span almost the full roster of artists that defined the label in this period.

1974 — the first wave. Beal co-produced and engineered Harry Miller’s Children At Play (Ogun, 1974; release ID 403546), sharing producer credit with Miller himself and handling all recording, mixing and editing. He edited and remixed Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath’s Live At Willisau (Ogun, 1974; release ID 571482), a live document with original recording by Roland Janz. Also in 1974 he co-produced and mixed Border Crossing for the Mike Osborne Trio (Ogun; release ID 1660268), sharing production with Mike Osborne.

1975. On the S.O.S. album (Ogun, 1975; release ID 495242) — Mike Osborne, John Surman, and Alan Skidmore — Beal is credited as Producer and Recorded By (Electronics), alongside the band’s own production. A separate executive producer credit on that release goes to Wilf Whittingham.

1976. Beal co-produced and engineered Blue Notes For Mongezi (Blue Notes, Ogun, 1976; release ID 2173785), sharing Producer/Edited By/Mixed By credits with Chris McGregor, and contributing liner notes alongside Hazel Miller. He also co-produced All Night Long (The Willisau Concert) for the Mike Osborne Trio (Ogun, 1976; release ID 2485097) and handled production, recording and mixing on Ovary Lodge (Ovary Lodge, Ogun, 1976; release ID 797369), the duo record by Keith Tippett and Frank Perry, co-credited with the ensemble itself.

1977. Beal produced, recorded, mixed and edited Pipedream (Mark Charig / Keith Tippett / Ann Winter, Ogun, 1977; release ID 1791099), sharing production with Mark Charig. He co-produced and mixed They All Be On This Old Road for the Elton Dean Quartet (Ogun, 1977; release ID 586535), sharing credit with Elton Dean; recording on that release was by Ron Barron.

1978. Four Ogun releases in a single year show the breadth of his role. He was Executive Producer on Frames (Music For An Imaginary Film) by Keith Tippett’s Ark (Ogun, 1978; release ID 495229) — a large-ensemble record with Elton Dean, Trevor Watts, Hugh Hopper, Louis Moholo, Julie Tippetts, and Maggie Nicols, produced by Hugh Hopper and engineered by Gary Edwards. He co-produced and edited Procession (Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath, Ogun, 1978; release ID 584662), sharing credit with Chris McGregor, and contributed liner notes. He mixed and edited Blue Notes In Concert (Volume 1) (Blue Notes, Ogun, 1978; release ID 584690), alongside Ron Barron who held producer and recording credits. He also mixed and edited Rogue Element (Hugh Hopper / Elton Dean / Alan Gowen / Dave Sheen, Ogun, 1978; release ID 2383605), with production by Ron Barron and recording in France. Also in 1978 he produced and edited Cynosure by the Trevor Watts String Ensemble (Ogun; release ID 1628873).

1979. His Ogun-era work closes with In His Good Time (Chris McGregor, Ogun, 1979; release ID 614924), where Beal holds an Edited By credit; production and recording on that release were by McGregor and Ron Barron. In the same year Beal appears as Engineer and Editor on The London Concert (David Murray, Cadillac Records, 1979; release ID 1107083), his only documented credit outside Ogun during this period.

Post-Ogun Work

After the intensive Ogun run, Beal’s production credits become more scattered but continue into the 1980s and beyond.

In 1982 he engineered Sweet And S’ours (Maggie Nicols & Julie Tippetts, FMP, Germany; release ID 2420148), produced by Jost Gebers — the only FMP-label credit in his discography.

In 1985 Beal appears as a performer rather than engineer for the first time: on the self-titled debut LP of Trevor Watts’ Moiré Music (Arc, 1985; release ID 2259690) he plays Baritone Saxophone, Bass Saxophone, and Soprano Saxophone. The Discogs entry for the group lists him as a full member alongside Watts, Lol Coxhill, Veryan Weston, Simon Picard, Ernest Mothle, Liam Genockey, Nana Tsiboe, Peter Knight, and Steve Dunachie. This marks a notable shift — from behind-the-desk role to active ensemble participant.

In 1986 he returned to production on A Loose Kite In A Gentle Wind Floating With Only My Will For An Anchor (Keith Tippett Septet, Ogun, 1986; release ID 1792891), co-credited as Producer and Edited By alongside Keith Tippett and Eric Wetherell. The album features Elton Dean, Mark Charig, Larry Stabbins, Nick Evans, Paul Rogers, and Tony Levin.

In 2004 he co-produced the reissue compilation Border Crossing + Marcel’s Muse (Ogun; release ID 4548888), combining the 1974 Mike Osborne Trio album with additional material.

In 2009 he produced and recorded part of Happy Daze + Oh! For The Edge (Elton Dean’s Ninesense, Ogun; release ID 2737364) — a posthumous Elton Dean compilation where he recorded tracks 5–10, with Hazel Miller serving as Executive Producer.

In 2017 he received cover, liner notes, and production credits on Closer To You (Hi 4 HEAD Records; release ID 10157781), credited as “Original Production By” and “Recording By.”
