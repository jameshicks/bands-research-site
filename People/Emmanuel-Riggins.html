Emmanuel Riggins

Discogs ID: 356441 Role: Keyboardist, Organist, Pianist, Producer, Arranger Origin: Yazoo City, Mississippi (based in Detroit, Michigan) Born: September 17, 1942, Yazoo City, Mississippi Died: May 28, 2015, Youngstown, Ohio (age 72) Active: 1970–2015 Associated Acts: The Wooden Glass, The Nineteenth Whole, Grant Green, Marcus Belgrave

Biography

Emmanuel Riggins was an American keyboardist, organist, producer, and arranger born September 17, 1942 in Yazoo City, Mississippi. He passed away May 28, 2015 in Youngstown, Ohio at the age of 72. He is the father of drummer and hip-hop producer Karriem Riggins.

Early Career and the Grant Green Band (1970–1972)

Riggins came to prominence as a sideman on the Blue Note Records sessions of guitarist Grant Green at the turn of the 1970s. His first documented credit is on Green Is Beautiful (1970, Blue Note), where he played organ alongside a lineup that included drummer Idris Muhammad, bassist Jimmy Lewis, trumpeter Blue Mitchell, tenor saxophonist Claude Bartee, conguero Candido Camero, and percussionist Richard Lendrum. The session also featured Neal Creque on organ for one track. Producer was Francis Wolff; engineering credit went to Rudy Van Gelder.

The following year he appeared on Visions (1971, Blue Note) playing electric piano, with drummer Idris Muhammad, bassist Chuck Rainey, vibraphonist Billy Wooten, and Ray Armando on congas. Then came Shades Of Green (1972, Blue Note) — a large, lush arranged date produced by George Butler with arrangements by Wade Marcus, featuring the Crusaders rhythm section of drummer “Stix” Hooper and bassist Wilton Felder alongside Riggins on electric piano and clavinet. Van Gelder handled mastering.

His final Grant Green association yielded a posthumous archival release: Slick! — Live at Oil Can Harry’s (Resonance Records, 2018), drawn from a live recording of unknown vintage, on which Riggins is credited on electric piano alongside bassist Ronnie Ware and drummer Greg Williams. The album was produced by Zev Feldman and executive-produced by Michael Cuscuna and George Klabin.

The Wooden Glass and The Nineteenth Whole (1972)

In 1972 Riggins appeared as a member of two Detroit-area soul-jazz groups with overlapping personnel.

The Wooden Glass (Discogs ID: 136481) was the band led by vibraphonist Billy Wooten, which also included drummer Harold Cardwell and guitarist William Roach. Their live album The Wooden Glass Recorded Live (1972, Interim Records) captures the quartet with Riggins on organ. Wooten handled all arrangements; the project was produced by W.4 Productions (Wooten’s own imprint).

The Nineteenth Whole (Discogs ID: 1830894) was a larger ensemble — again centered on Wooten and Cardwell, but expanded with Cornell Dupree on guitar solos, Gordon Edwards on bass, Billy Johnson on guitar, and Buddy Caldwell on congas. Their album Smilin’ (1972, Eastbound Records) was produced by Bob Porter and recorded by Rudy Van Gelder. Riggins played organ and sang vocals on this date.

The overlapping membership of the two groups — Wooten, Riggins, Cardwell, with some shared session players — suggests a tight-knit Detroit-area circle working simultaneously across multiple projects in this period.

Soul and R&B Production: Betty Lou and the 1979 Session (1979)

A 1979 single, Betty Lou (YaYa, Inc.), credits Riggins on keyboards. The label (YaYa, Inc.) is a small independent imprint. No further credits from the 1970s beyond the 1972 cluster are documented in the database.

Detroit Production Work: Marcus Belgrave (1985)

In 1985 Riggins co-produced the single Maria / Two Ships Passing In The Night by Detroit jazz trumpeter Marcus Belgrave (Michigan Satellite Records). Riggins produced the A-side (“Maria”) while the B-side was produced by C. Manley and Sylvia Moy. Michigan Satellite Records (Discogs ID: 74944) was a Detroit-based imprint with a small catalog. This credit places Riggins in the Detroit jazz scene alongside Belgrave, who was a pillar of that community through his work with the Motown session pool and later the The Detroit Experiment.

Calvin Brooks Session (1989)

The 1989 album A Smooth Flight by guitarist Calvin Brooks (Brooks, Burgess-Brooks Productions) lists Riggins on keyboards alongside drummer Jerome Spearman, bassist Don Mayberry, and percussionist Lorenzo Brown. This appears to be an independent Detroit-area production.

Name Variations

Discogs records the following name variations: E. Riggins, Emanuel Riggins (the spelling used on several Blue Note sleeves), Emmanuel Riggins or Earl Neal Creque. The last variant is apparently a sleeve error on at least one pressing that conflated Riggins with co-organist Neal Creque.
