Juan Carrera

Discogs ID: 381792 Real name: Juan Luis Carrera Origin: Washington, DC, USA

Musician, recording engineer, label owner, and artist manager active from the early 1990s to the present. Carrera was bassist and vocalist of The Warmers (1994–1997, Dischord Records), then spent the late 1990s and early 2000s as a busy recording engineer in the DC/post-hardcore orbit — particularly for Slowdime Records and Touch And Go — before pivoting to artist management via his company Ravenhouse Ltd. (Discogs ID: 2042010). Under that banner he managed Modest Mouse through their mainstream breakthrough, Bright Eyes / Conor Oberst, and The Hold Steady, among others.

Name variations (Discogs): Carrera, J. Carrera, J. L. Carrera, J.Carrera, JL Carrera, Juan, Juan California, Juan Cerrera, Juan Cholera, Juan L. Carrera, Juan Luis Carrera

Career Overview

Early Work and The Warmers (1993–1997)

Carrera’s earliest Discogs credit is a 1993 production for “Swingset” on Redemption Records. By 1994 he was already in the DC studio world, co-engineering Hoover (3)‘s debut LP The Lurid Traversal Of Route 7 (Dischord, 1994) alongside Geoff Turner — the record that defined the post-Rites Of Spring DC sound that fed into emo and post-hardcore.

He simultaneously played bass and sang in The Warmers, a DC project featuring Alec MacKaye and Amy Farina, who released their self-titled LP on Dischord Records in 1996 (produced by Ian MacKaye). The same year Carrera appeared as a session bassist on Shy Town (K Records, 1995) and co-produced and mixed Kerosene 454’s Came By To Kill Me (Dischord/Slowdime, 1996) alongside J. Robbins and engineer Don Zientara — his first significant production collaboration with Robbins.

He also mixed the “Cyberporpoise” EP for Troubleman Unlimited in 1996, signaling that his engineering work extended beyond the Dischord house.

Peak Engineering Years (1997–2003)

After The Warmers wound down, Carrera became one of the most active engineers in the DC/Slowdime ecosystem. His credits from 1997–1999 are concentrated around Slowdime Records, the sublabel Dischord used for harder-edged material:

  • The Aesthetics Of No-Drag (Regulator Watts, Dischord/Slowdime, 1997) — Recorded By
  • Hoover (retrospective anthology, Slowdime, 1998) — Recorded By (archival)
  • Any Day Of The Night (Slowdime, 1998) — Mixed By, Recorded By
  • At Zero (Slowdime, 1998) — Engineer [Assisted On Everything]
  • The Mercury CD (Slowdime, 1998) — Recorded By
  • More There (Slowdime, 1998) — Technician [Assisted]
  • Movin’ Out (Slowdime, 1998) — Mixed By
  • Contemporary Music (Slowdime, 1999) — Mixed By, Recorded By
  • Canyon (Slowdime, 2001) — Recorded By

Outside Slowdime, 1998 was his most visible year: Carrera appears on the Modest Mouse EP Arches And Aisles (Sub Pop, 1998) as “Recorded By [Additional Recording, Washington],” capturing the band in DC while on tour. He also recorded Idea Of City for MoodFood Records and The Hawaiian Bronco for the Aesthetics imprint.

The alias “Juan California” appears on the Monorchid LP Who Put Out The Fire? (Touch And Go, 1998), where he recorded the bulk of the album (tracks 1–3, 5–7, 9, 11). The alias “Juan Cholera” appears on Skull Kontrol’s Deviate Beyond All Means Of Capture (Touch And Go, 1999) — both pseudonyms used on Touch And Go releases only, possibly a label convention or a personal choice for those sessions.

Additional 2000–2003 work includes:

  • The Convocation Of… (Gold Standard Laboratories, 2000) — Recorded By
  • Unsolved (Southern Records, 2000) — Recorded By
  • Post Mortem Anthem (Bluetip, Dischord, 2001) — Mixed By (select tracks), Recorded By (select tracks)
  • This Is Dance Floor Numerology (Rewika Records, 2001) — Engineer
  • Our Success (Christopher’s Records/Superbad, 2002) — Recorded By
  • All Around (Lookout! Records, 2003) — Recorded By, Mixed By
  • Bare Bones (V2, 2003) — Mixed By, Recorded By
  • Glass (Headz/Thrill Jockey, 2003, Japan) — Mixed By [Board Mix]
  • Live At Black Cat (Lovitt Records, 2005) — Recorded By (live archive)

The 2003 board-mix credit on the Thrill Jockey/Headz release (a Japanese market release) and the V2 Italian release indicate that by this point his work was reaching beyond purely DC-area clients.

He also played handclaps on Bobby Conn And The Glass Gypsies’ The Homeland (Thrill Jockey, 2004) — a one-off guest appearance suggesting a personal connection to the Chicago-based Conn.

The Modest Mouse work (2004–2005) covered the band’s commercial peak: Good News For People Who Love Bad News went platinum and produced “Float On.” His management of Conor Oberst ran across the Bright Eyes project (Cassadaga) and Oberst’s solo debut, and continued into the Vagrant era with Clear Heart Full Eyes (2012). The Ravenhouse Ltd. entity is explicitly credited alongside Carrera’s individual name on the Murder By Death and Holly Miranda releases.

His engineering work has continued in parallel on a reduced basis:

Open Questions

  • The “Juan California” and “Juan Cholera” aliases appear exclusively on Touch And Go sessions (Monorchid 1998, Skull Kontrol 1999). Whether this was a Touch And Go house convention, a Carrera preference for those specific projects, or connected to some other context is not documented in Discogs.
  • The 2001 This Is Dance Floor Numerology (Rewika Records, Europe) and 2002 Our Success (Christopher’s Records, Luxembourg) are outlying credits with no obvious DC-scene connection — the clients behind these releases are not further identified in the Discogs data.
