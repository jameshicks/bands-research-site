Overview

Kurt Gottschalk is a musician and producer working in experimental, free improvisation, and drone music, with a recorded output stretching from 2009 into the 2020s. He has been a member of ADD Orchestra, Ecstasy Mule, and rahrahree!, and has built an unusually sustained collaborative relationship with guitarist Loren Mazzacane Connors and vocalist Suzanne Langille — nine and seven shared releases respectively — suggesting either a long-running ensemble or a recurring studio alliance. His label base is anchored at Northern Spy, which released the bulk of his 2011–2013 output, with later credits spread across Cuneiform, Jazzwerkstatt, and Creative Works.

Notes (web)

Background

Gottschalk’s primary public identity is as a journalist, broadcaster, and record producer rather than a performing musician in the conventional sense, though he maintains an active practice in experimental music. He holds a master’s degree from Columbia School of Journalism (1997) and has contributed to All About Jazz, Signal to Noise, Time Out New York, The Village Voice, The Wire, Guitar Player, the NYC Jazz Record, and the Brooklyn Rail, plus outlets in Canada, France, Germany, Portugal, and Russia. [source: https://soundcloud.com/fun2know/f2k-ep-29-music-journalist-kurt-gottschalk] He produced and hosted the Miniature Minotaurs programme on WFMU and later hosted Afternoon New Music on WKCR. His musical interests are situated in what Anthony Braxton called the “post Ayler/Cage continuum.” [source: https://wavefarm.org/ta/archive/artists/k1hkd4]

How Gottschalk entered the Connors/Langille orbit

The connection originated at the Brecht Forum, the radical cultural centre then in Chelsea, Manhattan, where Gottschalk was curating a concert series. Connors got to know him through that curatorial work. [source: https://www.squidco.com/miva/merchant.mvc?Screen=PROD&Store_Code=S&Product_Code=17064&Category_Code=201211 — album notes for I Wish I Didn’t Dream] Northern Spy Records president Tom Abbs had also occasionally co-programmed weekend festivals at the same venue, which reinforced the Connors/Northern Spy/Gottschalk triangle. [source: same Squidco album notes]

Blue Ghost Blues (2011) — first production credit

Gottschalk’s first documented studio role with Connors and Langille was as producer of Blue Ghost Blues (Northern Spy NS012, 2011), the reunion album by Haunted House — the blues-rock quartet of Connors (guitar), Langille (vocals), Andrew Burnes (guitar), and Neel Murgai (Persian daf). The group had been active in the mid-to-late 1990s before a ten-year hiatus; Blue Ghost Blues was their return. Gottschalk also wrote the liner notes for the release. The album was recorded by Jason LaFarge at Seizure’s Palace. [source: https://www.squidco.com/miva/merchant.mvc?Screen=PROD&Store_Code=S&Product_Code=17064&Category_Code=201211]

I Wish I Didn’t Dream (2012) — conceived and produced

For the follow-up project, Gottschalk was the conceptual originator. He was selecting paintings and writing text for a forthcoming book on Brooklyn outsider artist MP Landis (M.P. Landis) when he and Connors began imagining a musical soundtrack for the book. Gottschalk recognised Connors — himself a painter — and Langille, an admirer of Landis’s work, as ideal collaborators. [source: https://www.squidco.com/miva/merchant.mvc?Screen=PROD&Store_Code=S&Product_Code=17064&Category_Code=201211] The session was booked at the same studio where Blue Ghost Blues was recorded (Seizure’s Palace, run by Jason LaFarge), and Connors and Langille improvised while slides of Landis’s WD series of paintings were projected on the wall. Langille brought prepared verses including her own poetry and work by Keats and Denis McCarthy. The result, I Wish I Didn’t Dream (Northern Spy, 2012), was their first duo album in fourteen years. [source: https://archive.org/details/I_Wish_I_Didnt_Dream-17868; https://www.tinymixtapes.com/news/loren-connors-suzanne-langille-interpret-the-paintings-of-m-p-landis-on-new-lp] Gottschalk’s Discogs credit on this release is “Producer, Concept By [Collaboration Conceived By]”, which aligns precisely with this account.

The Wire cover feature (July 2016)

Gottschalk wrote the Loren Connors cover story for The Wire issue 389 (July 2016), an in-depth feature on “the pioneering free blues guitarist” that also co-starred Langille. The article drew on interviews with Langille, Darin Gray, Alan Licht, Jim O’Rourke, and others. [source: https://www.thewire.co.uk/issues/389; https://family-vineyard.com/news/2016-06-14-loren-connors-in-the-wire] This journalism role ran alongside and presumably reinforced the production relationship — Gottschalk was simultaneously an insider collaborator and an external chronicler of the same scene.

Nature of the relationship

The picture that emerges is that Gottschalk entered the Connors/Langille world through venue curation (Brecht Forum) and then built a recurring alliance as producer, conceptual catalyst, and liner-notes writer rather than as a performing instrumentalist within their ensemble. His role on I Wish I Didn’t Dream — originating the entire concept from a visual art project — illustrates the pattern: he functions as a creative producer who brings contexts and artists together, then steps back from the foreground. The nine shared Discogs releases with Connors and seven with Langille most likely reflect a sustained combination of production credits, liner notes, and concert documentation across Haunted House albums and related projects, rather than Gottschalk appearing as an equal-voice performer.
