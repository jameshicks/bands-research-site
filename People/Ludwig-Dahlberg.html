Ludwig Dahlberg

Swedish drummer and percussionist. Born: October 17, 1978, Örgryte, Göteborg. Realname: Ludwig Erik Sebastian Dahlberg. Discogs artist ID: 883496.

Also credited as: Dahlberg, L. Dahlberg, Ludvig, Ludvig Dahlberg, Ludwig, Ludwig Dalhberg.

Biography

Umeå Scene and The International Noise Conspiracy (1998–2009)

Dahlberg came up in the Swedish punk and post-hardcore scene centred on Umeå, one of the most productive DIY rock scenes in Scandinavia during the 1990s and 2000s. He joined The International Noise Conspiracy (T(I)NC) at the band’s formation in 1998, the same year Dennis Lyxzén and Lars Strömberg launched the project out of the ashes of Refused.

T(I)NC was built around a Situationist-inflected politics and a sonic model pulled from 1960s garage rock, soul, and late-1970s punk — Phil Ochs’s idea of combining Elvis and Che Guevara as a rock act was a stated touchstone. Dahlberg was the band’s drummer throughout their entire run, contributing to all studio albums and major releases. The band moved from Swedish and North American DIY labels in 1999 (G7 Welcoming Committee Records, Black Mask Collective, Trans Solar) to Epitaph for Survival Sickness (2000) and then Burning Heart Records for the bulk of their output through to The Cross Of My Calling (2008). The band dissolved in 2009.

Session and Side Work (2007–2011)

While still in T(I)NC, Dahlberg contributed session percussion to the French garage rock band Thunder Express’s album Republic Disgrace (2007, Longfellow Deeds), alongside guitarist Mattias Bärjed who also produced the record. This was one of the first signals of a connection to the French rock world that would deepen later.

After TINC dissolved, Dahlberg joined Blessing, a Stockholm-based project described on Discogs as pulling from TINC alumni and related circles. Blessing’s sole known release is Fabric Of Wonder (2011, Kning Disk), recorded by Jonas Kullhammar — the saxophonist who had also appeared as a guest on TINC’s A New Morning, Changing Weather (2001) and Armed Love (2004). The full lineup on that record was Lars Johan Trogen (guitar, vocals), Karl Jacobson (keyboards, glockenspiel), Jesper Skarin (bass), and Dahlberg (drums). The band described its music as “organic, creative, groovy and progressive.”

Dahlberg also drummed on Swedish singer-songwriter Skaffa Ny Frisyr’s single (Razzia, 2009) and appeared on Ibland Kallar Jag Det Kärlek (Amigo, 2010).

Free Fall (5) (2012–2013)

Dahlberg formed or joined Free Fall (5), a Stockholm-based hard rock quartet on Nuclear Blast. Members: Kim Fransson (vocals), Mattias Bärjed (guitar), Jan Martens (bass), Ludwig Dahlberg (drums). The band released one album: Power & Volume (Nuclear Blast, 2013; earlier German pressing 2012). Twelve tracks of hard rock — the band Facebook page was titled “freefallpowerandvolume.” Nuclear Blast catalog numbers: NB 3033-1.

Indochine (2013–present)

From around 2013, Dahlberg became the drummer for Indochine, the long-running French new wave/rock act founded by Nicola Sirkis in Paris in 1981. This was a significant career shift — Indochine are a major act in France, with arena-level audiences.

Dahlberg has been credited on drums across multiple Indochine studio albums and associated releases:

He is also credited on Eleven Songs (Audiogram, 2017), Face A Face B (Sony Music, 2017), Pique-Nique (At(h)ome, 2017), and Kino Music (Kwaidan Records, 2019) — likely associated with or adjacent to the Indochine circle. Discogs also credits him on Yarol (Polydor, 2019) by Yarol Poupaud — credited for arranged by, drums, percussion, and backing vocals — and on an EP (2015, Close Harmonie / Le Chantier Des Francos). He is listed as a member of Indochine on Discogs. The core Indochine lineup alongside him includes Nicola Sirkis (vocals, multi-instrumentalist, producer), Oli de Sat / Olivier Gérard (guitar, keyboards, bass, synth), Boris Jardel (guitar, percussion), and Marc Eliard (bass).

He also drummed on Renan Luce’s D’une Tonne À Un Tout Petit Poids (Barclay, 2014) and Anja Bigrell (ET Go Home, 2013) as session work.
