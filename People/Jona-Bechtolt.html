Overview

Jona Bechtolt (born Jonathan Warren Bechtolt) is an American electronic musician and multimedia artist based in Portland, Oregon, active since 1996 across indie rock, experimental, electro, and dance-pop. He is best known as a member of Yacht, where the bulk of his 168 Discogs credits are concentrated, and through that project developed a sustained working relationship with Claire L. Evans (28 shared releases) and a long association with DFA Records (2008–2019). Earlier in his career he worked within the K Records orbit and appeared on releases by The Blow alongside Khaela Maricich; Adam Forkner and Rob Kieswetter are among his other recurring collaborators. Beyond Yacht, his credited work spans Wolf Colonel, The Badger King, and Allegro, with roles ranging from performer and producer to drummer and recording engineer.

Notes (web) — The Blow as precursor to the Bechtolt/Evans Yacht partnership

The Blow collaboration (2004–2007)

Bechtolt joined The Blow in 2004, when the project relocated from Olympia to Portland. He and Khaela Maricich produced two albums together: Poor Aim: Love Songs (2004, States Rights Records / Slender Means Society; reissued K Records 2007) and Paper Television (2006, K Records). Both albums are credited as written, recorded, performed, and produced by The Blow collectively, but the working dynamic was a clear division: Maricich wrote the songs and Bechtolt “had the main hand in the production” — she would write out how rhythms and bass parts should go, and he would “make it exist and make it awesome.” He also provided live beats and backup vocals on tour. [Source: Khaela Maricich interview, Autostraddle (https://www.autostraddle.com/khaela-maricich-of-the-blow-the-autostraddle-interview/)] [Source: Wikipedia — The Blow article, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blow, as of 2026-11-03] [Source: Wikipedia — Paper Television article, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_Television, as of 2026-11-03]

Departure from The Blow and pivot to Yacht (2007)

Bechtolt quit The Blow in 2007 to concentrate entirely on Yacht, which had been his solo project since 2002–2003. His absence from Blow live shows that year was already due to Yacht commitments; the formal split was announced alongside the Yacht album I Believe in You. Your Magic Is Real. (2007, Marriage Records). [Source: Three Imaginary Girls, https://threeimaginarygirls.com/no-more-blow-jona-its-all-about-yacht/] [Source: Wikipedia — Jona Bechtolt article, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jona_Bechtolt, as of 2026-11-03]

The Blow as a pop laboratory for Yacht

Secondary sources synthesizing multiple interviews attribute to Bechtolt the view that working on pop music with Maricich in The Blow crystallised what he wanted Yacht to be — specifically that the experience of writing and producing dance-pop material made him want to “reshape [Yacht’s] message in a pop framework so that people would actually listen to it, rather than eight-and-a-half minute records of mantras.” This framing (The Blow as the stylistic precursor that pushed Yacht toward accessible electropop) appears in aggregated sources but the originating interview has not been located. No single first-hand interview with Bechtolt spelling out this connection was found in sources checked (Wikipedia, Three Imaginary Girls, REDEFINE magazine 2010, QRO magazine, Cooking with Rockstars, Autostraddle, Out.com, Miami Rail). The claim circulates credibly but should be treated as unverified without the primary source. Bechtolt–Evans partnership formation (2007–2008)

Evans appeared on the Yacht track “The Summer Song” on I Believe in You. Your Magic Is Real. (2007) before joining as a full member. [Source: Three Imaginary Girls, https://threeimaginarygirls.com/no-more-blow-jona-its-all-about-yacht/]

Bechtolt and Evans met in Los Angeles at a show they were both on the bill for — Evans noticed Bechtolt performing with a laptop surrounded by objects he would interact with as though they had some bearing on the music. They began a relationship, and by 2008 were living together in Marfa, Texas, at which point Yacht formally became a duo. [Source: Apartamento Magazine interview with Claire L. Evans, https://www.apartamentomagazine.com/stories/claire-l-evans/] [Source: REDEFINE magazine, https://redefinemag.net/2010/yacht-band-interview-transformation-under-mystery-lights/]

Evans had previously played in noise bands in Los Angeles and had established herself as a science writer before joining Yacht. The collaboration with Bechtolt was both romantic and creative from the outset. The REDEFINE interview (2010) notes they described collaboration as requiring “some level of ego sacrifice” but does not discuss The Blow as a direct model. [Source: REDEFINE magazine, https://redefinemag.net/2010/yacht-band-interview-transformation-under-mystery-lights/]

Structural parallels between The Blow and Yacht

Both The Blow and Yacht (post-2008) operated as electropop duos in which Bechtolt handled production and one partner handled songwriting/lyrics — Maricich in The Blow’s case, Evans in Yacht’s. Both projects used laptop-driven beats, minimal live instrumentation, and were rooted in the K Records / Pacific Northwest indie ecosystem. The structural template Bechtolt developed producing The Blow records (dense digital production behind a vocalist-songwriter) maps directly onto how Yacht functioned in the See Mystery Lights era. This parallel is evident from the Discogs credits and Maricich’s description of their workflow, though no interview source makes this explicit comparison.
