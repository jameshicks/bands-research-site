Roscoe Mitchell

American saxophonist, multi-instrumentalist, and composer. Born 1940, Chicago. Discogs artist ID: 255026.

Overview

Roscoe Mitchell is one of the founding figures of the AACM (Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians) and a co-founder of The Art Ensemble Of Chicago. His debut LP Sound (1966) is considered a founding document of the AACM aesthetic — a dense, confrontational mix of extended technique, collective improvisation, little instruments, and graphic composition. Over six decades he has continued as both a leader and an active collaborator, issuing records through Delmark, Nessa, Black Saint, ECM, Wide Hive, and Bocian, among others. He plays sopranino, soprano, and alto saxophone as primary voices; his recordings also document him on tenor, bass saxophone, clarinet, flute, piccolo, recorder, oboe, and percussion.

Mitchell’s name variations across Discogs include “R. Mitchell”, “Roscoe E. Mitchell”, “Jazz Rosco” and others; “Roscoe Mitchell (2)” (ID 3392691) is a separate entry for his father, who receives a songwriting credit on Hey Donald (1995).

Ensembles

Mitchell has led or co-led a wide range of named groups, all documented on Discogs:

  • Roscoe Mitchell Sextet (ID 2359359) — the 1966 Sound band
  • The Roscoe Mitchell Art Ensemble (ID 958252) — early transitional name before the AEC was formalised
  • Roscoe Mitchell Quartet (ID 959790)
  • Roscoe Mitchell Quintet (ID 2015649)
  • Roscoe Mitchell Trio (ID 1049977)
  • Roscoe Mitchell And The Sound Ensemble (ID 959181)
  • Roscoe Mitchell And The Note Factory (ID 1049976) — large rotating ensemble; members include George Lewis, Craig Taborn, Vijay Iyer, Matthew Shipp, William Parker, Hugh Ragin, Tani Tabbal, Spencer Barefield, Corey Wilkes, Harrison Bankhead, Vincent Davis, Wadada Leo Smith, and others
  • Roscoe Mitchell Creative Orchestra (ID 959814)
  • Roscoe Mitchell Orchestra (ID 7174175)
  • Space (12) (ID 760346) — another grouping under Mitchell’s direction

He also appears as a member of the European Free Jazz Orchestra Des Art Ensembles Of Chicago (ID 3411917), Leo Smith Creative Orchestra (ID 709614), Transatlantic Art Ensemble (ID 1286749), Baden-Baden Free Jazz Orchestra (ID 755111), and Kirili Et Les Nymphéas (ID 1416552).

The Nessa recordings of this period establish Mitchell’s compositional language — graphic scores, extended solo passages, rigorously structured ensemble work. Chuck Nessa (Discogs ID 276926) appears across 18 shared credits with Mitchell, the highest count of any single collaborator in the Discogs data.

Art Ensemble ECM period begins 1979: Nice Guys (1979), Full Force (1980), Urban Bushmen (1982), The Third Decade (1985) — see The Art Ensemble Of Chicago.
