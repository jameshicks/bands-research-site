French/German bassist, vocalist, and performance artist. Co-founder and co-leader of Faust across all eras: the Polydor/Virgin classic period (1971–74), the 1990s reformation, and the post-2004 FaUSt faction (Faust (7), Discogs ID: 988814). Active in parallel as Art-errorist, a solo performance and multimedia project, and as co-organizer of the Art-Errorist Avant Garde Festival in Schiphorst.

Overview

Peron came up in Hamburg in the pre-Faust group Nukleus, alongside Rudolf Sosna and Gunther Wüsthoff. Producer Uwe Nettelbeck merged Nukleus with Campylognatus Citelli (the group of Werner Diermaier, Hans Joachim Irmler, and Arnulf Meifert) around 1970 to form Faust, which then relocated to a former schoolhouse at Wümme outside Hamburg. The Wümme period (1971–73) produced the band’s essential catalog: the self-titled debut on Polydor (1971), So Far (1972), The Faust Tapes (Virgin, 1973), Faust IV (Virgin, 1973), and the drone collaboration with Tony Conrad, Outside The Dream Syndicate (Caroline Records, 1973). Peron also played bass on Slapp Happy’s first two albums — Sort Of (1972) and Acnalbasac Noom (1973) — during the shared Wümme period.

Faust dissolved c.1975 after a chaotic clandestine recording session at Giorgio Moroder’s Musicland Studios in Munich ended with Peron, Sosna, and Irmler briefly arrested; their mothers settled the bills. Material from those sessions later appeared on Punkt. (Bureau B, 2022).

Peron, Diermaier, and Irmler reunited for performances in 1990 and 1992, then made a full US tour in 1994 documented on the Faust Concerts volumes (Table Of The Elements). In November 1996 Peron and Diermaier met French musicians Amaury Cambuzat and Olivier Manchion from Ulan Bator, performing together as “Collectif Met(z).” Peron departed Faust in June 1997, reportedly over tensions with Irmler about individual vs. collective decision-making within the group.

From 2004–05, Peron and Diermaier reconnected and formed the “FaUSt” faction (stylized capitalization, Discogs entity Faust (7)) with Cambuzat and Manchion. This lineup debuted at the Art-Errorist Avant Garde Festival in Schiphorst in 2005 and became prolific through the 2000s–2010s, releasing several albums on Bureau B alongside smaller self-released and label CDRs. Diermaier stepped back around 2021; the late-era Bureau B releases (Punkt., Momentaufnahme I–IV, 2022–24) are attributed to the main canonical Faust Discogs entity.

In parallel, Peron has pursued the Art-errorist persona as an autonomous multimedia/performance art project, releasing a handful of solo and collaborative records under that name.

Biography by Era

Pre-Faust: Nukleus (c.1968–70)

Peron played in the Hamburg group Nukleus alongside Rudolf Sosna (guitar/keyboards) and Gunther Wüsthoff (synthesizer/saxophone). Nukleus rarely performed live. When Nettelbeck brought the two Hamburg groups together for Polydor, Nukleus dissolved into Faust.

Polydor/Virgin Era: Faust (1971–74)

The core Wümme recording period. Peron played bass throughout and is credited on written compositions across So Far. The Wümme method was communal and experimental: the group lived together, treated the studio as an instrument, and generated large volumes of taped material. Peron, Diermaier, and Sosna are the three members who appear on the Tony Conrad collaboration Outside The Dream Syndicate — the most drone-oriented of the early Faust records.

Following Faust IV Virgin dropped the band. The 1975 Munich session ended badly (see Overview). Faust effectively dissolved.

Hiatus and Reformation (1975–96)

Little documented activity 1975–1990. Faust’s recordings were maintained in circulation by Chris Cutler’s Recommended Records and ReR Megacorp. Peron, Irmler, and Diermaier reunited for concerts in 1990 and 1992. The 1994 US tour — Peron and Diermaier with Steven Wray Lobdell, plus Sonic Youth as the opening act — was the first American Faust performance. Irmler did not participate in the US dates but took an increasingly central role in Faust’s studio output afterwards, producing records and founding Klangbad in 1996 with Cornelia Paul.

In November 1996 Peron and Diermaier performed as “Collectif Met(z)” with Amaury Cambuzat and Olivier Manchion of Ulan Bator — a quartet that would later form the core of the FaUSt faction. Shortly after, Faust played the Garage in London and Transmusicales de Rennes, featuring Chris Cutler.

You Know Faust (Klangbad, 1996) was the primary studio document of the reformation before the next split.

Departure from Faust (1997)

Peron left the band in June 1997. Irmler later characterized the tension as Peron making unilateral decisions that the collective could not sustain (“If a single person decides individually how everyone does things, a group can carry on like this for a short time but not all the time” — Freq interview, October 1998). Peron’s own framing is more philosophical: “We had to separate because our personalities clashed and our attitudes towards art diverged” (It’s Psychedelic Baby Magazine, 2011). From 1997 to 2004, Faust operated without him: Diermaier, Irmler, Lobdell, Paukstat, Stoll.

FaUSt Faction (2005–present)

In 2004, Diermaier fell out with Irmler and reached back to Peron. Both men reportedly called each other at almost the same time. They convened in early 2005 with Amaury Cambuzat and Olivier Manchion (both from Ulan Bator), debuting at the Art-Errorist Avant Garde Festival in Schiphorst. The FaUSt faction has been among the more active Faust-aligned incarnations, with a consistent touring presence across France, the UK, and beyond.

The stylized name “FaUSt” (or “faUSt”) was Peron’s deliberate marker: “It’s written as faUSt because it’s us — Zappi and me. There’s another Faust in the south of Germany, based on Joachim Irmler, but we don’t have contact and I want to make sure that people know which version of Faust they’re getting” (Louder Sound, 2017).

Diermaier’s later departure (c.2021) led to the band continuing with different configurations. The Momentaufnahme series (Bureau B, 2023–24) and Punkt. (Bureau B, 2022, built from the 1975 Munich tapes) were released under the main Faust Discogs entity, suggesting a degree of canonical reconciliation through the Bureau B relationship.

Art-errorist (2008–present)
