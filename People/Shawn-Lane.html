Shawn Lane

Born: March 21, 1963, Memphis, Tennessee
Died: September 26, 2003, Memphis, Tennessee
Discogs ID: 676694
Groups: Black Oak Arkansas

American guitarist and pianist from Memphis. Regarded as one of the most technically accomplished guitarists of his generation — known for exceptional speed, melodic invention, and a command of exotic scales and microtonal ideas that set him apart from mainstream fusion. His mature recorded output was small but widely revered: one studio solo album (Powers Of Ten, 1992), a solo Japan-only record (The Tri-Tone Fascination, 2000), and six records made in collaboration with Swedish bassist Jonas Hellborg. He died of complications from kidney disease at age 40.

Biography

Child Prodigy and Early Career

Shawn Lane’s abilities were apparent from childhood. By his early teens he was already regarded as a prodigious musician in Memphis. He joined Black Oak Arkansas as a teenager — the band’s Discogs personnel list places him in its membership, and archival recordings from this period circulated among collectors (the Leprechauns In Orlando and Savage Innocence recordings, both dated 1981, document this era). Instrumental Demos (1980, Not On Label, release 24676541) is the earliest Discogs-documented material under his involvement. This is a Black Oak Arkansas band recording (credited under the ANV “Savage Innocence”), not a Lane solo demo — the full lineup is Kinley “Barney” Wolfe (bass), Christopher Craig (drums), Keith Plunk (guitar), Shawn Lane (guitar), and Billy Batte (keyboards).

Black Oak Arkansas was a Southern hard rock band formed in the late 1960s in Black Oak, Arkansas, who had their commercial peak in the early-to-mid 1970s. Lane joined a later lineup. The Savage Innocence recording (1981, later released by Not On Label, master 2804642) captures his early work.

Solo and Session Work (1990–1994)

Lane worked as a session guitarist in the early 1990s, appearing on:

  • Highwayman 2 (1990, CBS) — with Johnny Cash, Willie Nelson, Waylon Jennings, Kris Kristofferson; guitar credit
  • Centrifugal Funk (1991, Heading West, France) — guitar
  • When Love Comes Down (1991, Scotti Bros. Records) — guitar
  • Toy Caldwell (1992, Cabin Fever Music) — lead guitar (“Banzai”)

His sole proper solo studio album, Powers Of Ten (1992, Warner Bros. Records), is a landmark of the period. Lane performed and produced the entire record himself — playing guitar, bass, keyboards, and drums, while also arranging, composing, engineering, and mixing. The only other creative credits were Bil VornDick (additional mixing on some tracks) and Bruce Dees / Michael Patterson (digital editing). The album demonstrated the full scope of his compositional and instrumental range.

The “Two Doors — In The Palace Of Dreams” compilation (1995, CMP Records, Germany) features Lane on guitar and voice, documenting further solo-context work from this period.

The Hellborg Trilogy (1995–1997)

The most celebrated and documented phase of Lane’s career: three albums recorded with Swedish bassist Jonas Hellborg and rotating drummers, released on Hellborg’s own labels.

Abstract Logic (1995, Day Eight Music, DEM 035)
Trio with Hellborg (bass, producer) and Kofi Baker (drums). Scud Noonan mixed; Stephane Jean recorded. Seven extended improvisations — compositions credited jointly except for individually attributed tracks. Lane plays guitar throughout. Two pressings confirm wide distribution for a small label release.

Later Work (1997–2003)

Lane’s health declined through the late 1990s and early 2000s due to kidney disease, which severely restricted his activity. Nevertheless he continued working.

Session appearances:

  • Calvin Russell (1997, Columbia / Last Call Records, France) — guitar
  • Fission (1997, Heptagon Records, Sweden) — guitar; 4 pressings
  • Polaris (2003, ATO Records / Tone-Cool Records) — guitar; posthumous release context

Good People In Times Of Evil (2000, Bardo Records)
The final Hellborg/Lane collaboration to appear in their lifetimes. Trio with V. Selvaganesh on kanjira and udu (replacing the drum-kit format with South Indian Carnatic percussion). Ustad Sultan Khan appears on sarangi (track 4). Produced by Hellborg. Danny Kadar and Scud Noonan record and mix.

The Tri-Tone Fascination (2000, Metal Frontier, Japan)
Lane’s second studio solo album, released only in Japan. He produced, engineered, composed, and performed. A small ensemble of Memphis-area musicians joins on individual tracks:

Note: Cody Dickinson and Luther Dickinson are sons of Jim Dickinson (Memphis producer), and members of the North Mississippi Allstars. Their participation connects Lane to the Memphis roots-music scene.

Powers Of Ten ; Live (2001, Eye Reckon Records)
Live album produced, mixed, and played by Lane. Sean Rickman on drums, Barry Bays on bass, Doug Scarborough on keyboards, Todd Bobo on saxophone. Lane self-produced; Les Birchfield served as executive producer. Bruce Dees and Michael Patterson again handle digital editing (as on the 1992 studio album).

Icon — A Transcontinental Gathering (2002, Bardo Records)
Hellborg/Lane release adding V. Selvaganesh, V. Umamahesh (vocals), and V. Umashankar (ghatam, konokol) — Hellborg’s South Indian classical direction fully established. Lane plays guitar. Tim Hunt mixes. Hellborg writes and produces.

Personae (2002, Bardo Records)
Final studio trio record with Hellborg and Jeff Sipe, composed jointly by Hellborg and Lane. Scud Noonan records and mixes.

Death: Lane died September 26, 2003 in Memphis from complications related to kidney disease. He was 40.

Posthumous:

  • RHP (2004, Liquid Note Records, UK) — guitar credit; archival release
