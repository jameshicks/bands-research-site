Steve Winwood

Born Stephen Lawrence Winwood, 12 May 1948, Great Barr, West Midlands, England. Vocalist, organist, keyboardist, guitarist. One of the most distinctive voices in British rock — broke through as a teenager with The Spencer Davis Group, went on to found Traffic, joined the supergroup Blind Faith (2), and from the late 1970s built a substantial solo career, primarily on Island Records. Younger brother of Muff Winwood. Discogs ID: 201571.

Biography

The Spencer Davis Group (1963–1967)

Winwood joined The Spencer Davis Group (Discogs ID: 86618) as a teenager, performing alongside his brother Muff Winwood (bass), Spencer Davis (guitar), and Pete York (drums). The group recorded for Fontana and then Island Records, producing a string of UK hits including “Keep On Running” (1965), “Somebody Help Me” (1966), “Gimme Some Lovin’” (1966), and “I’m A Man” (1967) — the latter two co-written by Winwood. His voice was astonishingly mature for his age and his organ/piano work gave the group its R&B drive. He departed in 1967 at age 18 to form Traffic.

Traffic (1967–1974)

Winwood co-founded Traffic (Discogs ID: 64748) with Jim Capaldi (drums/vocals), Chris Wood (2) (woodwinds/keyboards), and Dave Mason (guitar/vocals). The group signed to Island Records. Their debut Mr. Fantasy (1967) and the self-titled Traffic (1968) established a psychedelic folk-rock sound. After a brief dissolution, Winwood reformed the group (initially as a duo with Capaldi) and released John Barleycorn Must Die (1970) — one of the group’s strongest records, co-produced by Winwood and Chris Blackwell. Further albums followed: The Low Spark of High Heeled Boys (1971), Shoot Out at the Fantasy Factory (1973), On The Road (1973, live), and When The Eagle Flies (1974). Traffic finally disbanded in 1974. Winwood served as producer on much of their later catalog.

Blind Faith (1969)

In 1969 Winwood joined the short-lived supergroup Blind Faith (2) (Discogs ID: 269050) alongside Eric Clapton (guitar), Ginger Baker (drums), and Rick Grech (bass). The group recorded one self-titled album (Blind Faith, 1969, Polydor), produced by Jimmy Miller, and undertook one major tour before dissolving. Winwood sang lead and played organ/piano. The album reached #1 on both UK and US charts.

Between Groups: Session Work (1969–1977)

During and between his group commitments, Winwood appeared on several notable sessions:

  • Jimi Hendrix — organ on “Voodoo Chile” (Electric Ladyland, 1968; credited as “Stevie Winwood”)
  • Joe Cocker — organ on With A Little Help From My Friends (1969)
  • Ginger Baker’s Air Force — vocals and organ (1970)
  • Howlin’ Wolf / London Sessions — piano, organ on The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (Chess, 1971) and The London Muddy Waters Sessions (Chess, 1972)
  • Lou Reed — organ, harmonium on Berlin (RCA Victor, 1973)
  • Eric Clapton’s Rainbow Concert — keyboards, vocals (1973)
  • Sandy Denny — electric piano, organ (various)
  • Stomu Yamashta’s Go — part of the Go ensemble (Island, 1976)
  • George Harrison — harmonium, backing vocals, Polymoog, Mini Moog on George Harrison (Dark Horse, 1979)
  • Marianne Faithfull — on Broken English (Island, 1979)
  • David Gilmour — piano, organ on About Face (CBS, 1984)
  • Talk Talk — organ on The Colour of Spring (EMI, 1986) — three tracks (see below)

Solo Career — Island Records Era (1977–1987)

Winwood’s debut solo album Steve Winwood appeared on Island Records in 1977. Arc of a Diver (Island, 1980) was a major artistic statement: he played, produced, engineered, and wrote everything himself. The single “While You See A Chance” was a hit. Talking Back to the Night (Island, 1982) continued in the same vein.

Back In The High Life (Island, 1986) was a commercial peak, driven by the Grammy-winning single “Higher Love.” The album reached #3 in the US. This era solidified Winwood’s standing as a mainstream AOR act while retaining soul and R&B sensibilities.

Solo Career — Virgin Years (1988–1996)

Winwood moved to Virgin for Roll With It (1988), which topped the US Billboard chart on its opening week. Refugees of the Heart (1990) and Junction Seven (Virgin, 1996) followed, the latter marking his last Virgin album.

Later Solo Work (2003–present)

About Time (Sanctuary/Wincraft Music, 2003) saw a return to rootsier territory. Nine Lives (Columbia/Wincraft Music, 2008) — a collaboration with Eric Clapton (on live dates) — was followed by Live From Madison Square Garden (2009) documenting the Winwood/Clapton tour. His own label Wincraft Music appears on most releases from 2003 onward.

Talk Talk — The Colour of Spring (1986)

Winwood played organ on three tracks of Talk Talk’s The Colour of Spring (EMI, 1986, release ID 441237). The Discogs credit specifies tracks A1, A2, and B1 — corresponding to “Happiness Is Easy,” “I Don’t Believe in You,” and “Living in Another World.” He is listed on the sleeve alongside Tim Friese-Greene (organ on the other tracks) and Mark Hollis (piano, vocals). The stub claim that the contribution “was not publicised at the time” may be from press accounts; Discogs credits him on the release itself, so he is acknowledged on the record sleeve.
