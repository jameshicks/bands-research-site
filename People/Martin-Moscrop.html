Martin Moscrop

Discogs ID: 89650

Manchester drummer, percussionist, and trumpeter. Career spans 1979–present across four main projects, all rooted in the Factory Records ecosystem.

Overview

Martin Moscrop is one of the central connective figures in Manchester’s post-punk and jazz-funk scenes. His career threads through A Certain Ratio, Swamp Children, Kalima, and E.D.-209, with each project growing out of or alongside the others. The Factory Records world — and its subsidiaries — forms the spine of his output through the 1980s, with later work extending to A&M and Mute.

Swamp Children (1980–1982)

Swamp Children formed from members of A Certain Ratio and released on Factory Benelux (FBN series). The lineup brought together musicians who would later resurface in Kalima.

Members:

  • Martin Moscrop — drums, percussion, trumpet
  • Tony Quigley — saxophone
  • Simon Topping — vocals, trumpet
  • Jez Kerr — bass
  • Donald Johnson — drums, percussion
  • Peter Terrell — guitar

Key release: So Hot (1982, Factory Benelux, FBN 14)

A Certain Ratio (1979–present)

A Certain Ratio is Moscrop’s principal band. The group moved through Factory Records, then A&M, then Mute across four decades of releases.

Kalima (1984–1989)

Jazz-funk offshoot with Tony Quigley and Andy Connell. Started on Factory before moving to CBS.

E.D.-209 (1988)

Side project with Donald Johnson (ACR drummer) and Dave Rofe (who ran the label). Released one 12”: Metre (1988) on Dun For Money Records, catalog DFM 003. Both tracks written and produced by all three members. The label was backed by Rob Gretton (Joy Division/New Order manager). Discogs ID: 23537. See E.D.-209.
