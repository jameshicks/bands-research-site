Robert Poss

Guitarist, composer, producer. New York City. Discogs artist ID: 154042.

Name variations: Poss, Poss., R. Poss, R.M. Poss.

Overview

Robert Poss is the founder and constant member of Band Of Susans (1986–1995), the NYC wall-of-guitars group Rolling Stone described as “adamantly arty, brainy, visceral and bracing.” He owns and operates Trace Elements Records, a label for avant-guitar music enhanced by electronics, and the Trace Elements studio on East 4th Street in Manhattan. His musical circle overlaps substantially with the downtown New York experimental scene — Nicolas Collins, Phill Niblock, Ben Neill, Rhys Chatham — though Band Of Susans operated at a more rock-adjacent pitch than the strict minimalists.

Before Band Of Susans, Poss was a member of Western Eyes (2) (Discogs: 13722052) and the punk-adjacent Tot Rocket And The Twins (Discogs: 1724094), both recorded for what became the Trace Elements imprint. After Band Of Susans disbanded, Poss continued as a solo artist, releasing four solo albums on Trace Elements between 2002 and 2024. He has collaborated with Phill Niblock across multiple Touch label recordings, played with F.M. Einheit in 2021, and was one of 400 guitarists on Rhys Chatham’s A Crimson Grail (2010, Nonesuch). He also works with modern dance companies.

Early Work: Tot Rocket And The Twins / Western Eyes (1979–1984)

Poss first recorded as a member of Tot Rocket And The Twins — a punk/post-punk outfit that shared members with the early Band Of Susans orbit. Their 1980 single Reduced / Fun Fades Fast In The USA (Whiplash Records) listed Poss uncredited on guitar and vocals; the 1981 EP Security Risk (Trace Elements, cat. 110073X) credited him properly alongside Ron Spitzer (bass, lead vocals), Andrew Halbreich (harmonica, vocals), and Bulcken (drums) — produced by Nicolas Collins. The project also recorded under Western Eyes (2) for a 1984 LP, Western Eyes (Trace Elements TE1001), produced and remixed by Nicolas Collins. Both groups thus link Poss’s earliest recordings directly to Collins and to the Trace Elements infrastructure.

Members shared across both groups: Poss, Ron Spitzer, Andrew Halbreich.

Band Of Susans (1986–1995)

Discogs artist ID: 55969. Poss formed Band Of Susans in 1986; he produced all five full-length albums and the EPs. He co-wrote most material with Susan Stenger. The band’s lineup was fluid — an ensemble of multiple guitars over a rhythm section — and the roster shifted considerably across the decade.

Full Lineup Per Release

Sometimes (1986, Trace Elements TE 1014) — Robert Poss debut solo release credited to Poss alone; the Band Of Susans name was not yet in use. Personnel: Poss (guitar, vocals, bass, drums, recorded by, mixed by, words by, music by), Alva Rogers (backing vocals), Ron Spitzer (piano), Nicolas Collins (mastered by), George Bley (engineer).

Blessing And Curse EP (1987, Trace Elements TE 1015) — first Band Of Susans release. Personnel: Poss (producer, guitar, vocals, words by, music by), Susan Stenger (guitar, vocals), Susan Lyall (guitar, vocals), Susan Tallman (guitar), Alva Rogers (vocals), Ron Spitzer (drums). Engineers: Bill Hemy, Bob Coulter, Bob Power. The original all-Susan lineup.

Hope Against Hope (1988, Blast First BFFP 30 / Furthur) — lineup: Poss (guitar, vocals, producer, words by, music by), Susan Stenger (bass, vocals), Susan Tallman (guitar), Susan Lyall (guitar, vocals), Alva Rogers (vocals), Ron Spitzer (drums). Engineers: Jim Klein, John Herman. Mastered by Chris Gehringer.

Love Agenda (1989, Blast First BFFP 40 / Mute) — lineup shuffled. Poss (guitar, vocals, producer), Susan Stenger (bass, vocals), Karen Haglof (guitar, vocals), Page Hamilton (guitar, vocals), Ron Spitzer (drums). Engineers: Jim Klein (Platinum Island), Russ Landis (Planet). Mastered by Chris Gehringer.

The Peel Sessions (1992, Dutch East India Trading) — recorded across two different lineups. Tracks 1–4: Poss, Stenger, Spitzer, Page Hamilton (guitar). Tracks 5–6: Poss, Stenger, Spitzer, Mark Lonergan (guitar).

The Word And The Flesh (1991, Rough Trade / World Service) — lineup: Poss (guitar, vocals, producer), Susan Stenger (bass, vocals), Anne Husick (guitar), Mark Lonergan (guitar), Ron Spitzer (drums).

Now (1992, Restless Records) — same core as The Word And The Flesh: Poss (guitar, vocals, producer, written-by), Stenger (bass, vocals, written-by tracks 1–3, 5), Husick (guitar), Lonergan (guitar), Spitzer (drums). Engineer: Bryce Goggin.

Mood Swing EP (1993, Sing Fat) — same lineup: Poss, Stenger, Husick, Lonergan, Spitzer. Engineer: Bryce Goggin.

Veil (1993, Restless Records) — same lineup: Poss (producer, guitar, vocals, all songs by), Stenger (bass, vocals, all songs by), Husick (guitar), Lonergan (guitar), Spitzer (drums). Engineer: Bryce Goggin.

Here Comes Success (1994, Blast First / World Service) — same final-era lineup: Poss (guitar, vocals, producer, songs by), Stenger (bass, vocals, songs by), Husick (guitar), Lonergan (guitar), Spitzer (drums). Engineer: Bryce Goggin, assistant Royston Langdon.

Wired For Sound: 1986–1993 compilation (Blast First / Mute / World Service) — Poss: guitar, vocals.

Inverse Guitar (1988, TE-1016): a split LP co-credited to Nicolas Collins and Robert Poss. Side A (Collins): electronics, programmed by, instrument design; Poss: backwards bass, guitar; Susan Tallman: backwards guitar. Side B (Poss): drums, guitar, engineer; Collins absent.

The When People Were Shorter And Lived Near The Water release was produced by Poss; Ron Spitzer played drums under the pseudonym “The Ever Popular Klompenmeister.” Poss also produced their subsequent Shimmy Disc LP Bobby (1989), which featured Nicolas Collins (digital trombone) and an extensive Shimmy Disc cast including Kramer and Steve Shelley.
