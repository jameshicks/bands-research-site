Kazu Makino

Vocalist, guitarist, visual artist. Born Kyoto, Japan, 31 December 1969; based in New York City. Discogs artist ID: 274817.

Co-founder and co-vocalist of Blonde Redhead, alongside twin brothers Amedeo Pace (guitar, vocals) and Simone Pace (drums). Released her debut solo album Adult Baby in 2019 under her own Adult Baby Records imprint.

Background and Blonde Redhead Formation

Makino moved from Japan to New York, where she met the Pace brothers (Italian twins who had relocated from Rome). The three co-founded Blonde Redhead in 1993. From the outset Makino was the band’s primary vocalist, guitarist, and one of its principal songwriters — credits across the full catalog list her on guitar, voice, and written-by throughout.

The earliest formal Blonde Redhead release in the DB is the 7” Amescream / Big Song (1993, Oxo Records), crediting Makino on Guitar and Vocals. The self-titled debut album followed in 1995 on Smells Like Records.

Note on Masculin Féminin: This is a Numero Group compilation of early Blonde Redhead material (pre-Touch And Go era). The Discogs credit reflects Makino’s performance and songwriting across those recordings. Co-produced for this release by Steve Shelley (Sonic Youth) and Adam Luksetich.

Note on Barragán (2014): Self-released on the band’s own label, Blonde Redhead LLC. Produced by Drew Brown alongside the band; Makino took on an unusually wide keyboard/instrument spread (Chamberlin, Mellotron, harpsichord, piano, organ, synthesizer in addition to voice) and co-designed the artwork with Lucie Kim.

Solo Work

Adult Baby (2019)

Makino’s debut solo album, released on her own imprint Adult Baby Records (catalog: none). Credited under the name “Kazu” on the sleeve. Nine tracks: “Salty,” “Come Behind Me, So Good!,” “Meo,” “Adult Baby,” “Place Of Birth,” “Name And Age,” “Unsure In Waves,” “Undo,” “Coyote.” Genres tagged: Electronic, Pop, Rock / Indie Pop.

Guest Appearances and Collaborations

Battles — Gloss Drop (2011, Warp Records)

Makino contributed vocals, writing, and performance to track 8, “Sweetie & Shag,” on Battles’ second album. She is credited Written-By and Performer on that track. The track also appears in reworked form on Dross Glop (2012, Warp, WARPCD226) as “Sweetie & Shag (The Field Remix),” with her songwriter credit carried over.

Nosaj Thing — Eclipse/Blue (2012, Innovative Leisure Records)

Makino is a primary credit on this release, listed alongside LA producer Nosaj Thing (Jason Chung, Discogs ID: 246928). She appears as co-writer and vocalist (credited “Featuring, Vocals [Uncredited]” in the additional credits, despite being listed as a primary artist on the release itself).

Trentemøller — Lost (2013, In My Room)

Makino contributed vocals, performance, and lyrics to one track (position 9) on Danish producer Trentemøller’s third album. Other vocal guests on Lost include Alan Sparhawk (of Low), Mimi Parker (of Low), Jonny Pierce (of The Drums), and Sune Rose Wagner (of The Raveonettes).

Ryuichi Sakamoto — Ikari (Rage) OST (2016, Sony Records Int’l)

Makino contributed vocals to the soundtrack of the 2016 Japanese film Ikari (dir. Lee Sang-il), composed and produced by Ryuichi Sakamoto. The album also features Fennesz on guitar and 2Cellos.

Rone — Mirapolis (2017, Infiné)

Makino contributed vocals and co-writing to track 12 on French electronic artist Rone’s (Erwan Castex, Discogs ID: 1044335) fourth album. John Stanier (of Battles) appears as drummer on two tracks, and Bryce Dessner (of The National) contributes guitar and composition. The album artwork was designed by Michel Gondry and Paul Gondry.

Early Photography Credit

Two releases from 1987 list a “Photography By” credit that Discogs attributes to Kazu Makino (ID 274817): Bush Dance (1987, Antilles New Directions) and No Pain For Cakes (1987, Antilles New Directions). Given that Makino was born in 1969, this would make her 17–18 at the time — possible but unusual. These may be a Discogs data-matching artifact (a different Kazu Makino or an error in artist ID assignment). The 1987 credits should be treated with caution unless independently confirmed.

Other Credits

  • The Black Heart Procession — Three (2000, Touch And Go): Makino appears as “Long Distance Phone Voice From New York” on one track, credited in the Blonde Redhead page. The Touch And Go label connection is the likely bridge.
  • Arto Lindsay — Hyper Civilizado (Arto Lindsay Remixes) (1996, Gramavision): Art Direction credit. Makino is listed for Art Direction on this Arto Lindsay release — an early indication of her visual/design involvement alongside music.
  • Dreamspeed / Blind Light 1992–1994 (2003, Tzadik): Voice credit on this archival release documenting the Blind Light project. The Tzadik release collects early 1990s recordings; the Voice credit on the 2003 CD is Makino’s. Also: an original Dreamspeed release (1993, Avant) credits her for Written-By.
  • We Are The Works In Progress (2012, Asa Wa Kuru): Compiled By credit — Makino compiled this release on the label Asa Wa Kuru.
  • 3 O’Clock (2017, Asa Wa Kuru / Ponderosa Music & Art): Artwork credit.
  • Penny Sparkle (Blonde Redhead, 2010, 4AD): Album Design credit — reflecting her ongoing involvement in the band’s visual presentation.
