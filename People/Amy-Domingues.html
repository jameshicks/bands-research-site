Amy Domingues

Cellist, viola da gamba player, vocalist, pianist, and composer based in Washington, DC. Discogs artist ID: 368272. Also credited as “A. Domingues” and “Amy Dominguez.”

Active simultaneously in the classical chamber world and the DC indie-rock underground. She has played session cello on over 70 records — from Fugazi’s final album to Bob Mould, Jets To Brazil, Mary Timony, and Maritime — while leading her own project Garland Of Hours across three albums. In 2003 she co-composed the score to the Oscar-nominated documentary The Weather Underground with her Threnody Ensemble colleague Dave Cerf.

Overview

Domingues occupies an unusual position in the DC music ecosystem: formally trained enough to play baroque early-music ensembles (viola da gamba with the Washington Bach Consort) and experimental chamber groups (Threnody Ensemble), while also deeply embedded in the city’s indie rock scene through long-running collaborations with the Fugazi circle. Her cello appears on records produced at Inner Ear Studios and on Dischord, Jade Tree, Drag City, and Matador, touching the post-hardcore world without ever being confined to it.

Her own songwriting project, Garland Of Hours, synthesizes these threads — chamber textures, folk melody, and rock rhythm — across three albums released between 2003 and 2012. She has handled the engineering and production on Garland Of Hours records largely herself, with close collaborators Brendan Canty (Fugazi) and Chad Clark (Beauty Pill) as key co-contributors.

Groups and Projects

Garland Of Hours (2000–2012) — her primary songwriting vehicle. Led and engineered by Domingues; rotating cast including Canty, Jerry Busher, Mary Timony, Jonah Takagi, Brandon L. Butler, and Stefan Bauschmid. Three albums on Arrest Records (3) and Noble Task Records.

Tsunami (6) — Arlington, VA indie rock band co-founded by Jenny Toomey and Kristin Thomson. Domingues is listed as a member on Discogs alongside Andrew Webster, Franklin Bruno, John Pamer, and others. Active from 1990; released on Simple Machines through the 1990s.

Telegraph Melts — small DC duo of Domingues (cello) and Bob Massey (guitar). One self-released single: Heilgeschichte Und Weltgeschichte B/W Goodbye No. 20 (1997). Discogs artist ID: 263263.

Threnody Ensemble — experimental classical trio founded 1997 by Dave Cerf and Erik Hoversten, with Dominique Davison joining as cellist before Domingues. Discogs lists Domingues as a member. Their album Timbre Hollow (2000, New Albion) credits Domingues as a guest additional musician on cello. Discogs artist ID: 103199.

Washington Bach Consort — DC chorus and orchestra founded in 1977 to perform Bach’s complete vocal and instrumental works. Domingues is listed as a member. Discogs artist ID: 2209785.

Domingues & Kane — duo with Dennis Kane (electronics, keyboards, guitar). Released Gut + Voltage: Viola da Gamba & Electronics In Synthesis (2016, Verses Records, VCD-001) — eight tracks of viola da gamba against electronic textures. Domingues plays viola da gamba and co-writes most tracks. Discogs artist ID: 4952690.

Notes on key sessions:

The Argument — Fugazi’s final studio album (released 2001; the Discogs 2016 entry is a reissue pressing). Domingues plays cello on three tracks (1, 2, 7). Other additional performers: Jerry Busher (second drums/percussion on multiple tracks), Bridget Cross (backing vocals tracks 3, 5), and Kathi Wilcox (second backing vocals, track 3). The 2001 original is Discogs master ID 3652; the 2016 entry (ID 28253899) is the one on file with these credits.

The Golden Dove — Mary Timony solo album, produced by Mark Linkous (Sparklehorse) and Timony, with Alan Weatherhead engineering. Domingues on cello.

Four Cornered Night and Perfecting Loneliness — both Jets To Brazil albums on Jade Tree, recorded at Inner Ear Studios with J. Robbins engineering and mixing.

Body Of Song and District Line — two Bob Mould solo albums. On Body Of Song, the session also features Brendan Canty on drums and Don Zientara among the engineers, placing Domingues in familiar DC company.

Telescopic (Edith Frost, 1998) and Wonder Wonder (Edith Frost, 2001) — both on Drag City; the 2001 session was engineered by Steve Albini with Glenn Kotche also among the performers.

Co-Composition: The Weather Underground Score (2003)

The Discogs profile notes that in 2003 Domingues and Dave Cerf (Threnody Ensemble) co-composed the score for The Weather Underground, the Oscar-nominated documentary directed by Sam Green and Bill Siegel. This is not represented as a Discogs release entry but is noted in her artist profile.
