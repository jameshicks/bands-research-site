Howlin’ Wolf

American blues singer, guitarist, and harmonica player. Born Chester Arthur Burnett, 10 June 1910, West Point, Mississippi; died 10 January 1976, Hines, Illinois. Discogs artist ID: 340678.

One of the defining figures of postwar Chicago blues. Burnett learned guitar and showmanship from Charley Patton and harmonica from Sonny Boy Williamson (2) in the Mississippi Delta, worked the Southern club circuit through the 1930s, and served in the U.S. Army (April 1941 – November 1943). After the war he settled near West Memphis, Arkansas, and assembled a band that included guitarists Willie Johnson and Matt “Guitar” Murphy, harpist Little Junior Parker, and drummer Willie Steele. In 1951, Sam Phillips (2) recorded him at Memphis Recording Service for both Modern/RPM and Chess; after Leonard Chess secured exclusive rights, Burnett relocated to Chicago in 1952. His Chicago work — anchored by the songwriting of Willie Dixon, the guitar of Hubert Sumlin, and a series of classic singles on Chess Records — established him as one of the most powerful vocalists and bandleaders in the blues. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1991 (Early Influence category).

Core Band Members

Howlin’ Wolf maintained a notably stable working band by Chicago blues standards, partly because he paid musicians reliably — including unemployment insurance and Social Security — which was unusual among his peers.

Memphis-Era Players (Pre-Chicago)

The early Sun/RPM sides feature Willie Johnson (guitar), Willie Steele (drums), Little Junior Parker (harmonica), and Matt “Guitar” Murphy (guitar) before the move to Chicago.

Connections

  • Hubert Sumlin — primary guitarist 1954–1976; the defining voice of Wolf’s Chicago sound
  • Willie Dixon — chief songwriter and Chess house bassist; wrote “Spoonful,” “Wang-Dang-Doodle,” “Little Red Rooster,” “Back Door Man,” “Killing Floor,” “Tail Dragger” and many more; produced The Super Super Blues Band
  • Sam Lay — drummer who left band c.1965 to join The Paul Butterfield Blues Band
  • Jerome Arnold — bassist who left band c.1965 to join The Paul Butterfield Blues Band
  • Ralph Bass — principal Chess A&R man and producer for Wolf’s 1962–1973 period
  • Leonard Chess — secured Wolf’s contract from Sam Phillips; co-produced early sides
  • Marshall Chess — produced The Real Folk Blues (1966) and More Real Folk Blues (1967)
  • Ron Malo — Chess house engineer on most 1960s sessions
  • Norman Dayron — produced The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (1971) and London Revisited (1974)
  • Muddy Waters — Chicago peer on Chess; collaborated on Folk Festival Of The Blues (1963), The Super Super Blues Band (1967), and Muddy & The Wolf (1982)
  • Buddy Guy — appears on Folk Festival Of The Blues (1963) and as bassist on some Chess Box sessions
  • Otis Spann — pianist on mid-1950s Chess sessions and Muddy side of Muddy & The Wolf
  • Sonny Boy Williamson (2) — harmonica teacher in the Delta; co-appears on Folk Festival Of The Blues (1963)
  • Bo Diddley — co-appears on The Super Super Blues Band (1967)
  • Eric Clapton — The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (1971); also Muddy & The Wolf (1982)
  • Charlie Watts — The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (1971) and Muddy & The Wolf (1982)
  • Bill Wyman — The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (1971) and Muddy & The Wolf (1982)
  • Steve Winwood — piano and organ, The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (1971) and Muddy & The Wolf (1982)
  • Ian Stewart — piano, The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (1971) and Muddy & The Wolf (1982)
  • Mike Bloomfield — lead guitar on Muddy side of Muddy & The Wolf (1982)
  • Sam Phillips (2) — recorded Wolf at Memphis Recording Service, 1951; sold contract to Leonard Chess
  • Jimmy Rogers — Chicago blues guitarist; appears on Chess Box session annotations
  • Charles Stepney — produced The Howlin’ Wolf Album (1969) at Cadet Concept
  • Paul Butterfield — harp on Muddy side of Muddy & The Wolf; his Blues Band recruited Sam Lay and Jerome Arnold from Wolf’s band
  • Chess Records
  • Cadet — released The Howlin’ Wolf Album (1969) on Cadet Concept sublabel
