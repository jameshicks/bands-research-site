Pete Swanson

Discogs ID: 465548
Name variations: P. Swans, P. Swanson, Pete, Peter Swan, Peter Swanson
Role: Musician, noise/electronics performer, mastering engineer, label co-founder
Base: Portland, Oregon → Los Angeles, California
Active: 2002–present

Noise/electronics artist best known as one half of Portland duo Yellow Swans (with Gabriel Mindel Saloman). After Yellow Swans dissolved in 2008, Swanson pivoted to solo electronic music — initially abstract noise/drone, then increasingly techno-inflected work — releasing primarily on Type Records and self-releasing via Bandcamp. He also worked widely as a mastering engineer for the Portland/LA experimental underground, and co-founded the reissue label Freedom To Spend (a sublabel of RVNG Intl.) alongside Jed Bindeman.

Yellow Swans (2001–2008)

Swanson formed Yellow Swans with Gabriel Mindel Saloman in Portland, Oregon around 2001. The duo described their practice as “a constantly evolving mass of psychedelic noise that is both physically arresting and psychically liberating.” Swanson handled vocals, drum machine, and electronics; Saloman played guitar, feedback, and electronics. Their approach was improvisational — each live performance was unique.

They established and operated their own art collective imprint, Collective Jyrk, through which they self-released dozens of CDrs and cassettes. The Jyrk collective also included E*Rock and sometimes Pat Maherr (Sisprum Vish, Indignant Senility, DJ Yo-Yo Dieting). With heavy touring alongside artists such as Tussle, Xiu Xiu, and Japanther, the duo relocated briefly to Oakland, California before returning to Portland.

In April 2008, Swanson announced the breakup in an email to Pitchfork: “There are no specific reasons why we’ve come to this point, but both Gabriel and I have decided that it’s in our best interest to move on.” The final US show was in Chicago. In 2023, the duo reunited for a live performance at the Austin-based Oblivion Access Festival — their first in 15 years. (Source: Wikipedia, Yellow Swans)

Swanson on the breakup — interviews beyond the Pitchfork statement

Across several interviews spanning 2011–2025, Swanson has addressed the dissolution in varying levels of detail, though he consistently frames it as a practical and mutual decision rather than a falling-out.

Willamette Week “Exit Interview” (c. May 2011) — In a piece published around his final Portland performance before leaving for Columbia University (New York), Swanson mentioned looking at graduate programmes after the band broke up and stated: “I didn’t want to take the art more seriously. I didn’t think I could take it any further.” He also noted: “I’m glad I was able to tour a lot when I was younger, but I’m not interested in touring as hard as I did with YS.” (wweek.com)

A Closer Listen “Sound Propositions 05” (November 28, 2013) — Focused on his artistic direction after Yellow Swans, Swanson explained what drove his solo work: “We had already agreed to stop playing and I wanted to find a technological replacement for a bandmate that was as unpredictable and fit within the musical process that I had been developing in YS.” (acloserlisten.com)

Bandcamp Daily (July 22, 2020) — In a feature tied to the digitisation of the Collective Jyrk back catalogue, Swanson addressed the final album directly: “For what it’s worth, Going Places was very literal. It was in the design, like we knew that a lot had been put on hold because we had been prioritizing going on tour like six months out of the year.” (daily.bandcamp.com)

New Noise Magazine / Oblivion Access Festival preview (June 16, 2023) — Ahead of the 2023 reunion show, Swanson stated: “When we were doing the band, neither of us went to college. As soon as the band broke up, we both got degrees and started careers.” (newnoisemagazine.com)

Cyclic Defrost interview with Bob Baker Fish (June 4, 2025) — The most extended post-breakup reflection on record. Swanson discussed the practical impossibility of continuing: geographic separation (Saloman relocated to Canada, Swanson to New York City), career demands on both sides, and the collapse of the touring infrastructure they had relied on: “That kind of touring doesn’t really exist anymore. Nobody really does it. We would just get in a van with our own PA and be on the road for months on end.” He also noted that after Going Places there had been interest in further live activity, but: “we live like a continent apart, you know, like just impossible” and “We can’t because we both have really serious careers now.” (cyclicdefrost.com)

Pattern across sources: Swanson consistently emphasises practical factors — exhausting touring pace, deferred education and career goals, geographic separation after Saloman’s move to Canada — and avoids attributing the split to creative or interpersonal conflict. The A Closer Listen quote (2013) is the only instance where he gestures toward a creative impasse (“agreed to stop playing”), framing it as a precondition for his subsequent solo direction rather than a source of grievance. The Cyclic Defrost 2025 piece is the most detailed source but is a joint interview; Saloman provides more emotional detail on the touring burnout than Swanson does.

During the Yellow Swans period, Swanson also performed as part of Badgerlore, a collaborative project involving Ben Chasny (Six Organs of Admittance), Rob Fisk, and Tom Carter — documented on Stories For Owls (Free Porcupine Society, 2005). He appeared on a Six Organs of Admittance release (Smokescreen, Three Lobed Recordings, 2007, credited guitar) and contributed additional vocals to the Six Organs album The Sun Awakens (Spunk, 2006).

Solo Career (2010–present)

Early solo period — noise/drone (2010–2011)

Swanson’s first credited solo release was Feelings In America (Root Strata, 2010) — a limited vinyl LP of abstract noise/drone. He also collaborated with Rene Hell (Jeff Witscher) on the self-released cassette Waiting For The Ladies (2010).

Man With Potential (Type, 2011)

The breakthrough solo statement. Released on Type Records as a combined vinyl LP + CD package (TYPE101V/TYPE102). A double-format set of abstract electronics moving between noise, pulse, and early proto-techno structures. Mastered by Timothy Stollenwerk (CD) and Rashad Becker (vinyl cut). The Type relationship marked Swanson’s entry into a more international, more formally structured context for his music.

Freedom To Spend

Swanson co-founded Freedom To Spend with Jed Bindeman as a reissue/archival imprint under RVNG Intl., focused on obscure new-age, ambient, jazz, and avant-garde recordings from the 1970s–90s. The label has issued 73+ releases as of the mid-2020s.
