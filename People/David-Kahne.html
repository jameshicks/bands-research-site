David Kahne

David Kahne

Oct 02, 202610 min read

David Kahne

Full name: David Martin Kahne Role: Record producer, engineer, songwriter, multi-instrumentalist Discogs ID: 240971 Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kahne

American record producer and engineer based in Los Angeles / San Francisco. Active from the late 1970s through the 2020s. Career spans country-punk, new wave, alternative rock, ska-funk, pop, and mainstream rock. Among his most significant credits: Rank & File (1982), The Bangles (1984–1990), Fishbone (1985–1991), Sublime (2) (1996, mixing/producing select tracks), Sugar Ray (2) (1997–2001), Paul McCartney (2001–2007), The Strokes (2006), and Regina Spektor (2006).

Overview

David Kahne taught himself guitar, bass, and banjo in high school. He was offered a record deal by Capitol Records but chose to pursue production and engineering instead. He began his professional career at Hyde Street Recording in San Francisco. He moved into producing local SF-area bands in the early 1980s, quickly becoming the house producer for the 415 Records roster — the label most associated with the Bay Area post-punk / new wave scene (Translator, Romeo Void).

By 1984 he had moved to Columbia Records, where he produced the Bangles’ debut All Over The Place and then their blockbuster Different Light (1985/1986), which reached #2 on the Billboard 200 and produced “Walk Like An Egyptian” and “Manic Monday.” This success established Kahne as a go-to pop-rock producer. Throughout the late 1980s and early 1990s he continued working primarily for Columbia — producing Fishbone, Tony Bennett, and various other acts — while also holding A&R positions at Columbia and later Warner Bros.

His 1990s work expanded to include the self-titled Sublime album (1996, mixing and producing select tracks alongside Paul Leary), Sugar Ray’s Floored (1997) and 14:59 (1999), and Stevie Nicks’s Trouble In Shangri-La (2001, one track). In the 2000s he produced two major Paul McCartney studio albums — Driving Rain (2001) and Memory Almost Full (2007) — as well as The Strokes’ First Impressions Of Earth (2006) and Regina Spektor’s breakthrough Begin To Hope (2006). His later career includes credits on Lana Del Rey’s earliest recordings (2010), various indie rock acts, and continued session production work into the 2020s.

Kahne is also a musician (guitar, bass, keyboards, banjo) and occasional songwriter; he co-wrote tracks on Different Light (“Walking Down Your Street,” “Not Like You”), Floored (“R.P.M.,” “High Anxiety,” “Invisible,” “Right Direction”), and co-wrote “Bonin’ In The Boneyard” and “One Day” on Fishbone’s Truth And Soul.

He has been credited under numerous name variations including Dave Kahne, D. Kahne, D.K., David Kahn, David Hahne, and Kohne.

Career Timeline

Late 1970s — Apprenticeship

Engineered and produced at Hyde Street Recording, SF. First notable producing credits include Jorma Kaukonen’s Barbeque King (RCA Victor, 1980) and Pearl Harbor And The Explosions’ self-titled debut (Warner Bros., 1980).

1981–1983 — 415 Records / Columbia Era

Became the de facto house producer for 415 Records, the San Francisco post-punk/new wave label distributed by Columbia:

  • Translator — Heartbeats And Triggers (1982), No Time Like Now (1983), Everywhere That I’m Not (1982 single, 1986 compilation producer)
  • Romeo Void — It’s a Condition (1981, debut LP); Instincts (1984, third LP). The Never Say Never EP (1981) and Benefactor (1982) were produced by Ric Ocasek and Ian Taylor, not Kahne. Kahne also co-wrote several tracks on Instincts including “A Girl In Trouble (Is A Temporary Thing)”
  • Rank & File — Sundown (Slash/Warners, 1982): produced and engineered the debut LP by the Kinman brothers’ cowpunk band, featuring Alejandro Escovedo, Chip Kinman, and Tony Kinman

1984–1990 — The Bangles and Columbia Pop

Kahne produced all of the Bangles’ major Columbia releases:

  • All Over The Place (1984) — major-label debut; Kahne produced and engineered
  • Different Light (1985/1986) — breakthrough; #2 US, produced “Walk Like An Egyptian” (#1 US), “Manic Monday” (#2 US), “If She Knew What She Wants.” Kahne co-wrote “Walking Down Your Street” (with Susanna Hoffs and Louis Gutierrez) and “Not Like You” (with Debbi Peterson and Hoffs)
  • Everything (1988, represented by singles “In Your Room,” “Eternal Flame”) — Kahne produced
  • Greatest Hits (1990) — compilation

Simultaneously, Kahne began his long relationship with Fishbone (Columbia):

  • Fishbone EP (1985) — debut EP
  • In Your Face (1986)
  • Truth And Soul (1988) — widely considered Fishbone’s most cohesive record; Kahne co-wrote “Bonin’ In The Boneyard” and “One Day”
  • The Reality Of My Surroundings (1991) — associate producer; full band also produced

Also during this period: executive produced the Back To The Beach soundtrack (Columbia, 1987), produced the Outfield’s Voices Of Babylon (CBS, 1989), Deacon Blue’s When The World Knows Your Name (CBS, 1989), and the A Vision Shared tribute album (CBS, 1988).

1991–1996 — Columbia A&R and Diversification

Kahne held A&R positions at Columbia (and later Warner Bros.) while continuing to produce:

  • Fishbone — The Reality Of My Surroundings (1991) as associate producer; Give A Monkey A Brain… (1993) was produced by Terry Date, not Kahne
  • A&R coordination on Kriss Kross (Totally Krossed Out), Xscape, and other So So Def/Columbia acts
  • Sublime — selected tracks and mixing on the self-titled Sublime (MCA/Gasoline Alley, 1996): produced “What I Got,” “April 29, 1992 (Miami),” “Caress Me Down,” and “Doin’ Time”; played organ and piano on several tracks
  • Soul Coughing — Irresistible Bliss (Slash/London, 1996): producer and mixer on several tracks

1997–2003 — Sugar Ray

  • Sugar Ray — Floored (Atlantic/Lava, 1997): full album producer/engineer; co-wrote four tracks including “R.P.M.” and “Fly” backdrop elements; keyboardist
  • Sugar Ray — 14:59 (Atlantic/Lava, 1999): full producer/engineer/mixer/mastering; co-wrote “Falls Apart” and additional; produced “Every Morning” (#1 Mainstream Rock)
  • Sugar Ray — Sugar Ray (2001): not produced by Kahne — Discogs credits this record to Don Gilmore (producer, tracks 1–11); Kahne absent from this release
  • Sugar Ray — In The Pursuit Of Leisure (Atlantic, 2003): returned as full producer/engineer/mixer/programmer; co-wrote majority of tracks with the band
  • Stevie Nicks — Trouble In Shangri-La (Reprise, 2001): produced “Love Changes” (track 9)

2001–2007 — Paul McCartney and the Strokes

  • Paul McCartney — Driving Rain (Capitol/MPL, 2001): full album producer; recorded at Abbey Road and Henson Studios
  • Paul McCartney — Back In The U.S. (Capitol/MPL, 2002): live album producer
  • Paul McCartney — Memory Almost Full (Hear Music/MPL, 2007): producer, programmer, engineer, mixed by; McCartney’s first album on the Hear Music/Starbucks label
  • The Strokes — First Impressions Of Earth (RCA/Sony BMG, 2006): produced and engineered main album sessions; Gordon Raphael produced three tracks (4, 8, 9)
  • Regina Spektor — Begin To Hope (Sire, 2006): produced, engineered, mixed; co-produced with Regina Spektor; includes “Fidelity,” “Samson,” “Better,” “On The Radio”
  • Kelly Clarkson — My December (RCA/19 Recordings, 2007): producer/engineer on selected tracks; keyboardist

2008–2023 — Later Career

  • Lana Del Rey — Lana Del Ray A.K.A. Lizzy Grant (5 Points Records, 2010): produced the debut album (digital only); one of the earliest professional recordings under the Lana Del Rey name
  • Far — Far (Sire, 2009): producer
  • Various indie/alternative projects: The Rubens (2012), Lucius (2011–2013), Magic Trip documentary score (2011)
  • Tini Dori Nkam — Better In Blak (Warner, 2019)
  • No Solution (2023)

Open Questions

  • Everything (1988): Release credits confirm the album was produced by Davitt Sigerson, not Kahne. Kahne’s role on this release (if any) may have been limited to singles or pre-production work. This corrects an earlier assumption.
  • Tony! Toni! Toné! connection: the brief mentions this, but no direct production credit surfaced in the Discogs DB — may be a web-only claim or a role that’s not captured at the release level.
  • Exact scope of A&R vs. production work at Columbia (1992–1995): Discogs lists A&R roles but some productions from this period may be attributed inconsistently.
  • Kahne’s work at Hyde Street Recording SF — pre-1979 credits are sparse in Discogs.
  • Voudouris & Kahne (Discogs 4444800) — nature of this project is not documented in DB.
  • Roger Voudouris connection — “Street Player” (Capitol, 1976) lists Kahne as Written-By/Arranged By/musician; this is very early and relationship is unclear.
