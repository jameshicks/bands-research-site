Knox Chandler

Discogs ID: 174452
Origin: USA
Name variations: Chandler, K. Chandler, Knox
Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knox_Chandler

Guitarist and multi-instrumentalist active from the late 1980s to the 2020s. Best known for his extended runs with The Psychedelic Furs and Siouxsie and the Banshees / Siouxsie Sioux’s side project The Creatures, and for co-writing and performing across Dave Gahan’s debut solo album Paper Monsters (2003). His range is unusually wide — guitar and bass are home base, but he also plays cello, dulcimer, double bass, vibraphone, sampler, keyboards, and toy piano on record. Groups: Blind Light, Gawk, The Drones Of Praise.

Biography

Late 1980s — First Sessions

Chandler’s earliest Discogs credit is as guitarist and co-producer on Gary Windo’s Deep Water (1988, Antilles/Island), a late-career session for the British jazz-rock saxophonist. The following year he appeared as an additional musician on The Psychedelic Furs’ Book Of Days (1989, Columbia), produced by David M. Allen.

Early 1990s — The Psychedelic Furs, R.E.M., Love Spit Love

Chandler became a full performer-credit member of The Psychedelic Furs for their final studio album, World Outside (1991, EastWest), produced by Stephen Street, alongside core members Richard Butler, Tim Butler, John Ashton, and Joe McGinty. He also appeared on the EastWest single “Don’t Be A Girl” (1991) and the Columbia single “Until She Comes” (1991).

In 1992 he contributed cello on two tracks (“Nightswimming,” “Sweetness Follows”) to R.E.M.’s Automatic For The People (Warner Bros.), one of the landmark albums of the decade. String arrangements on that record were by John Paul Jones; Chandler appeared alongside a larger cello section.

When Richard Butler formed Love Spit Love with Tim Butler, Richard Fortus, and Frank Ferrer, Chandler co-wrote track 5 (“K. Chandler” credit) on the self-titled debut (1994, Imago). He also contributed electric guitar to Darden Smith’s Little Victories (1993, Columbia/Chaos).

Mid-1990s — Blind Light, Film Scores, Restless Records Era

Chandler was a member of Blind Light, the experimental project led by Anton Fier (drums) with Bill Laswell, Nicky Skopelitis, Aiyb Dieng, Lori Carson, and others. The Blind Light sessions (1992–1994) were eventually compiled on Dreamspeed / Blind Light 1992–1994 (2003, Tzadik); Chandler plays guitar on all Blind Light disc tracks.

In 1994 he played cello and double bass on the soundtrack to Hal Hartley’s film Amateur (Matador), arranged by Ned Rifle and Jeffrey Taylor. He also contributed guitar to Buckethead-adjacent and avant-rock contexts (cello on No More Mister Nice Girl by Diamanda Galás associate Bebe Buell, 1994, Imago; guitar on Pure by The Golden Palominos, 1994, Restless Records).

Through the mid-1990s he became a recurring presence on the Restless Records roster: guitar and/or bass on Where It Goes (1995), Dead Inside (1996), and Everything I Touch Runs Wild (1996) — all Restless artists in the alt-rock sphere. He also contributed guitar to the EastWest soundtrack The Photographer’s Wife (1996).

During this period, Chandler co-produced and performed everything on Maggie Estep’s Love Is A Dog From Hell (1997, Mercury/Mouth Almighty) — playing guitar, bass, cello, toy piano, sampler, programming, loops, and screamed vocals, plus writing most songs with Estep and co-producing with Steve Lyon. This is his most multi-role credit in the DB.

He also returned for The Psychedelic Furs retrospective Should God Forget: A Retrospective (1997, Columbia/Legacy) — guitar credit.

Late 1990s — The Creatures / Siouxsie

Chandler performed on The Creatures’ Anima Animus (1998, Sioux Records), credited as “Augmented with Guitar-o-scopes by Knox Chandler” — a distinctive timbral role in Siouxsie Sioux and Budgie (2)‘s duo project. He also appeared on The Creatures’ Sub Pop single “Take Mine / Sad Cunt” (2000), again under the “Guitar ‘O’ Scopes” credit.

2000s — Dave Gahan, Depeche Mode, Film Work

Chandler contributed string arrangement and solo cello to track 3 of Depeche Mode’s Exciter (2001, Mute) — produced by Mark Bell, with strings also by Cenovia Cummins, Joyce Hammann, Leo Grinhauz, Ralph Farris, and Todd Reynolds.

His highest-profile work of the decade was Dave Gahan’s debut solo album Paper Monsters (2003, Mute), on which he functioned essentially as co-writer and multi-instrumentalist-in-residence: guitar, dulcimer, cello, bass, sampler, vibraphone, additional programming, string arrangements, keyboards, and co-written credits on all ten tracks with Gahan. Producer Ken Thomas engineered and mixed; Jon Collyer handled primary programming. This relationship extended to live work — Chandler played guitar on Live Monsters (2004, Mute) and Dreamshow – Live At The Royal Festival Hall (2005, Demon Vision), Gahan’s concert releases.

He also appeared on Siouxsie’s solo work and on Siouxsie Sioux’s The Seven Year Itch Live (2003, Sanctuary/Wonderland) as guitarist, and on Dave Gahan’s 2008 Hourglass-era material (Same Ol’ Story, Epic, guitar uncredited).

On the jazz/downtown side, he contributed guitar and electronics to Bobby Previte’s work (their shared credit is in the DB as “Previte Chandler”), and guitar/electronics to Tsuker-zis (2009, Tzadik).

The Dave Gahan connection brought him into the Depeche Mode satellite world also via the Dave Gahan single “Bottle Living” / “Dirty Sticky Floors” / “I Need You” releases (2003, Mute), where Chandler is credited as additional programmer and co-writer.

2010s–2020s — The Drones Of Praise and Ongoing Sessions

Chandler is a member of The Drones Of Praise, the large ensemble led by Shaun Mulrooney. The group’s debut album Tau And The Drones Of Praise (2019, Drones Of Praise label, Germany) credited Chandler as guitar and electronics; other members include Earl Harvin, Robbie Moore, Idris Ackamoor, Kiyoshi Fujikawa, and vocalist Nina Hynes.

In 2018 he contributed acoustic guitar, bass, electric guitar, and strings to Love Is An Elevator (BMG/GYAT/Rupture). In 2020 he handled string arrangements for Marillion-adjacent progressive act’s Eupnea (Inside Out Music/Sony). In 2021 he appeared on More Talk About Tonight by Slang (20) (Terminus Records), alongside Lori Carson, Vic Chesnutt (voice sample), and DJ Logic.

He also has a member credit in Gawk (with Jim Fourniadis, Joe Trump, Marc Sloan, Q. R. Ghazala) — no standalone releases on Discogs under that name.

Instruments

Guitar (electric, acoustic, forward/backward), bass, cello, double bass, dulcimer, vibraphone, toy piano, sampler, keyboards, programming, loops, electronics. Credited with “Guitar-o-scopes” on Creatures releases — a processed/textural guitar role.
