Overview

Kevin Army is an American audio engineer and producer rooted in the Oakland/East Bay punk underground since the early 1980s, best known for operating Dangerous Rhythm studio in Oakland — a facility he purchased from his mentor Matt Wallace. Through that studio he became the primary recording architect of the mid-1980s East Bay scene, producing foundational acts including Operation Ivy, Jawbreaker, The Mr. T Experience, and Sweet Baby. His reach extended to major-label territory, including engineering Green Day’s Insomniac on Reprise. Across more than 750 credits, Lookout! Records accounts for the densest concentration of his catalogue (53 works, 1987–2005), with Billie Joe Armstrong, Rob Cavallo, and Jerry Finn among his most frequent collaborators; he also performed as a member of Rummage Sale and The Bomb Bassets.

Circumstantial context (not a primary source answer): Army’s Discogs profile shows 148 shared releases with Green Day and 153 with Rob Cavallo, indicating a dense and longstanding working relationship with both — consistent with either origin story. Army was deeply embedded in the East Bay punk scene (Operation Ivy, Jawbreaker, etc.) before Green Day’s major-label move, so a pre-existing local relationship is plausible. However, no interview or account has been found that directly states whether the Insomniac gig came through the band, through Cavallo, or through Reprise. The definitive answer likely sits inside the paywalled Tape Op interview.

Note on a false lead: A search result summary incorrectly claimed Army engineered both Dookie (1994) and Kerplunk! (1992) for Green Day. Wikipedia’s Dookie article does not name him as engineer; this was a hallucinated credit from a search aggregator and should not be relied upon. Army’s first confirmed Green Day credit per Discogs is Insomniac.
