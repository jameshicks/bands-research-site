Biography

Sputniks and Origins (1980–1981)

Dave Graney, Clare Moore, and Steve Miller were members of Adelaide punk group Sputniks (Discogs ID: 1837139) before relocating to Melbourne around 1980–1981. This move was the founding event of The Moodists.

The Moodists (1981–1987)

The Moodists formed in Melbourne as a post-punk group. Graney sang lead vocals throughout the band’s lifespan. The original lineup comprised Graney (vocals), Clare Moore (drums), Steve Miller (5) (guitar), and Chris Walsh (3) (bass); guitarist Mick Turner joined later.

After releasing two singles and a six-track EP on Melbourne’s Au Go Go label, the band relocated to London. Their UK output was produced in collaboration with Victor Van Vugt, who went on to work with Nick Cave, Beth Orton, and PJ Harvey. The Moodists toured extensively through Northern Europe and made a short US tour. In 1985 they recorded a single for Creation Records; in 1986 they released two EPs on the T.I.M. label.

Mick Turner left during the 1985–1986 period (he later formed Dirty Three). The final Moodists lineup included former Orange Juice members David McClymont (bass) and Malcolm Ross (guitar). Their last gig was in London.

The Moodists supported Public Image Limited on their 1984 Australian tour, alongside punk band Box of Fish.

Dave Graney With The White Buffaloes (1989–1990)

After the Moodists disbanded in 1987, Graney’s first post-Moodists vehicle was Dave Graney With The White Buffaloes, releasing on Fire Records (UK). The lineup included Clare Moore (drums), Chris Walsh (bass), Rod Hayward (guitar), Conway Savage (piano/organ), Martin Lubran (pedal steel), and Tony Wyzenbeek (harmonica) — a notably country-inflected sound. Producer was Phil Vinall.

Dave Graney & The Coral Snakes (1988–1998)

The Coral Snakes were Graney’s primary vehicle through the 1990s Australian indie boom, releasing on Id Records (then distributed via Mercury/Phonogram). The original lineup featured Malcolm Ross (guitar), Gordy Blair (bass), and Louis Vause (piano); their debut EP At His Stone Beach (1988, Fire Records) was produced by Barry Adamson. The band’s name was used interchangeably on Discogs as “Dave Graney With The Coral Snakes” and “Dave Graney & The Coral Snakes.”

The core Coral Snakes lineup stabilised as: Graney (vocals), Clare Moore (drums/percussion), Gordy Blair (bass/sax), Rod Hayward (guitar), and Robin Casinader (piano/keyboards/violin). Producer Tony Cohen worked on the 1994 album You Wanna Be There But You Don’t Wanna Travel and its precursors; Victor Van Vugt co-produced and engineered The Soft ‘N’ Sexy Sound (1995, Id/Mercury).

The Soft ‘N’ Sexy Sound (1995) reached the Australian ARIA charts. Night Of The Wolverine (1993) was co-produced with Tony Cohen and engineered by Cohen; I Was The Hunter… And I Was The Prey (1991, Fire Records) was recorded in London.

The Coral Snakes also appeared on the Murder Ballads (1996, Mute/Liberation) as The Moron Tabernacle Choir alongside Nick Cave.

The Dave Graney Show (1998–2003)

Named for Graney’s self-described persona, this project released on Festival Records (Australia) and then Cockaigne (his own label, which he has used continuously since). Members included Clare Moore, Bill Miller (3), Stuart Perera, and Adele Pickvance; the self-titled debut (1998) was co-produced by Graney and Moore. Kiss Tomorrow Goodbye (2000, Cockaigne) followed.

Dave Graney / Clare Moore as a Duo (2005–2009)

Hashish And Liquor (2005, Reverberation/Cockaigne) is credited to Clare Moore / Dave Graney as joint leads — primarily a duo recording with Mark Fitzgibbon (piano) on key tracks, self-produced and self-engineered. Knock Yourself Out (2009, Cockaigne) continues in this mode with Stuart Perera (guitar) and Stu Thomas (bass) participating.

Dave Graney And The Lurid Yellow Mist / MistLY (2008–present)

The Lurid Yellow Mist (also credited as Dave Graney And The MistLY, The Lurid Yellow Mist, and Dave Graney & The MistLY — Discogs ID: 5241940) is the band name Graney has used since the late 2000s. Lineup: Graney, Clare Moore (drums), Stuart Perera (guitar), Stu Thomas (bass), Mark Fitzgibbon (piano). We Wuz Curious (2008) was the first main release under this name.

The 2011 album Rock ‘n’ Roll Is Where I Hide (Liberation Music) is credited directly to Dave Graney (solo primary) with Victor Van Vugt mixing — a late return to that Moodists-era collaborator.

One Million Years DC (2019, Cockaigne) is credited to Dave Graney & Clare Moore, with Graney playing virtually all instruments (vocals, electric and acoustic guitar, bass, keyboards) alongside Moore (drums, vibraphone, marimba, percussion, keyboards, melodica, backing vocals).

In A MistLY (2022, Cockaigne) is another duo release credited jointly to Dave Graney / Clare Moore.

Scene Connections

  • Melbourne post-punk, 1980–1987 — The Moodists were one of the core Melbourne post-punk bands alongside The Birthday Party, The Go-Betweens, Laughing Clowns, and The Scientists. Their UK relocation in 1984 placed them alongside bands like Orange Juice (members of which — Malcolm Ross, David McClymont — joined the final Moodists lineup).
  • UK indie, 1984–1987 — The Moodists’ UK period coincided with their releases on Creation Records and T.I.M., situating them in the British indie underground at its formative moment. Victor Van Vugt’s production connects them to the wider Nick Cave / PJ Harvey axis.
  • Australian indie 1990s — The Coral Snakes were part of the Australian alternative music scene that produced ARIA chart success in the mid-1990s, alongside contemporaries like The Beasts Of Bourbon, Kim Salmon And The Surrealists, and The Cruel Sea.
  • Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds — Graney contributed liner notes to Moss Side Story (1989) and appeared on Murder Ballads (1996/1997 AU pressing) as The Moron Tabernacle Choir, maintaining a peripheral connection to the Cave circle through the Melbourne underground network and the shared producer Victor Van Vugt.
  • Dirty Three — Mick Turner left the Moodists to form Dirty Three, making the Moodists a genealogical precursor to that band.
