Terry Ork

Born: William Terry Ork (birth name: William Terry Collins; also used William Terry Drake, William Terry)
Born: c. 1947, Tulsa, Oklahoma
Died: October 20, 2004, San Diego, California (colorectal cancer)
Role: Band manager, label founder

Co-founder of Ork Records (with Charles Ball) and manager of Television. A central connective figure in the early CBGB scene — less a businessman than an enthusiast and impresario who functioned as “sponsorship and enthusiasm for the scene.”

Biography

Early Life and New York

Born in Tulsa, Oklahoma. Before moving to New York, he ran a bookstore called “The Tiny Ork” in San Diego — this was the direct origin of the “Ork” name he adopted, combined with his middle name Terry. kcur.org No dates for the bookstore’s operation have been confirmed; only that it predates his New York move in the late 1960s. Moved to New York City in the late 1960s. Worked briefly for Andy Warhol’s Interview magazine and was part of the Factory crowd. Subsequently managed Cinemabilia, a film bookstore in Manhattan, where he worked alongside Richard Hell.

Dedicated to in Bill Knott’s 1968 poetry collection The Naomi Poems (as “William Terry”), suggesting an early deep connection to the New York literary underground.

Meeting Television

At Cinemabilia, Ork encountered Tom Verlaine and Richard Hell, then performing as The Neon Boys. When Hell mentioned difficulty finding a second guitarist, Ork suggested Richard Lloyd — someone he had been actively championing. Lloyd completed the lineup that became Television. Ork became their manager, primarily acting as “a mouthpiece for his band” rather than a strategic planner.

Richard Hell’s description: “a joyous gay hedonist who loved French thought and art” — enthusiastic about New Wave cinema and the Situationists. He called everyone “Dog” and was known for a “continuous grin.”

Ork Records (1975–1979)

Co-founded the label in August 1975 with Charles Ball, who handled business operations while Ork handled A&R and artist relations. First release: Television’s “Little Johnny Jewel” (cat. 81975) — a limited pressing of approximately 1,000 copies recorded at CBS Studios. The label ran to 16 singles through 1979.

Ork signed Television, Richard Hell, Marbles (5), Prix, Alex Chilton, Chris Stamey, Cheetah Chrome, Mick Farren (Lost Johnny), Student Teachers, Revelons, The Idols, The Erasers, and The Feelies. Rock critics Lester Bangs and Mick Farren both recorded for the label despite their primary identities as writers.

Farren on Ork: “an idealist, as true to the punk ethic as you could be” — noting that commercial success left him behind. Numero Group later credited the label with birthing “punk, no-wave, power pop, and the next four decades of indie rock.”

Later Life

Allegedly forged Warhol silkscreens at some point. After Ork Records folded, he reportedly disappeared to Europe before returning to the US. He served approximately three years in jail between 1991 and 1994 kcur.org. Richard Hell described the offense as “some kind of accounting scam” pleasekillme.com. Secondary aggregator sources suggest the charge may have involved passport fraud or cheque fraud, but these accounts appear to be speculative; Hell’s “accounting scam” is the only first-person characterization on record. Relocated to San Diego after his release.

In the final email he sent Richard Hell — written from his hospital bed the day before his death — Ork asked if Hell would help distribute “a book of his poems he was planning.” pleasekillme.com No poetry collection by Ork has been identified as published, either before or after his death; it appears to have remained unfinished at his death. Note: Richard Hell’s own website (richardhell.com/hellnews2004.html) describes receiving “a longish email from him the day before he died” but characterizes it primarily as a response to Hell’s invitation to speak at the Robert Quine memorial, without explicitly mentioning the poetry collection request — a minor discrepancy between the two accounts. richardhell.com

Died October 20, 2004 of colorectal cancer.

Portrayed by Johnny Galecki in the 2013 film CBGB.

Warhol / Factory

How Ork came to the Factory

Ork was a Southern California cinephile (from Tulsa via San Diego) who encountered Andy Warhol’s production crew while they were shooting in California in 1968 — one source specifically links this to the San Diego Surf shoot (La Jolla, May 1968), though Ork is not in that film’s credited cast. issuemagazine.com He relocated to New York City in 1968 following this encounter.

Role at the Factory

Multiple sources describe Ork as having befriended poet and Factory stalwart Gerard Malanga shortly after arriving in New York, and becoming Malanga’s assistant at the Factory. He also worked as an assistant on Warhol’s films — Richard Lloyd, describing Ork’s later ambitions with Television, said Ork “wanted to sponsor a band, almost like Andy had done with the Velvets” and that he “worked as an assistant to Andy Warhol during nighttime hours.” damienlove.com / Friction piece

His connection to Interview magazine is confirmed separately: warholstars.org identifies him as “a contributing editor at Interview who worked at Cinemabilia on Bleeker Street,” and notes that Paul Morrissey knew him through the magazine, with early Interview film stills sourced from Cinemabilia. warholstars.org Interview launched in late 1969, so his contributing editor role dates from c. 1969–1972 at the latest (before he became Cinemabilia’s manager).

Departure from the Factory

At least one source states he “was removed from the Factory under suspicion of distributing unauthorized copies of Warhol’s screenprints.” alchetron.com This aligns with Richard Hell’s later account (in the Please Kill Me oral history piece) that Ork was “90% sure” to have worked “with an assistant to Warhol to forge some Warhol silkscreens” — the forgery allegations referenced in the Biography section above likely have their roots in this Factory-era exit.

Specific films

No specific Warhol film title with Ork in a credited capacity has been confirmed by any source checked. He is not in the cast or crew list of San Diego Surf — confirmed by both Wikipedia and the warholstars.org San Diego Surf article, which list the full cast (Joe Dallesandro, Viva, Taylor Mead, Louis Waldon, Ingrid Superstar, Eric Emerson, Tom Hompertz, Sepp Donahower, Michael Boosin) with no mention of Ork. warholstars.org His role appears to have been production assistant / general Factory assistant rather than as an on-screen performer or formal crew member. A dedicated search across Warhol film cast lists (Camp, Space, Kitchen, Chelsea Girls, Couch, Horse, Kiss, Soap Opera, San Diego Surf) returned no Terry Ork credit in any title. The San Diego Surf encounter story may describe an informal on-set presence rather than a formal credited role; alternatively it may be an origin myth. No primary source (Warhol diary, Morrissey interview, Factory memoir) confirming Ork’s presence on San Diego Surf or any other Warhol film has been located.

The Warhol model and Ork Records

The Factory period directly shaped Ork’s approach to managing Television and building Ork Records. Lloyd’s account makes this explicit: Ork modeled his patronage of Television on Warhol’s patronage of the Velvet Underground — providing housing (a room in his Chinatown loft), introductions (Richard Lloyd to Tom Verlaine), and infrastructure (the label itself), without commercial expectations. This aesthetic-patron rather than business-manager model is consistent across all accounts of Ork’s management style.

Source quality note

The Gerard Malanga / assistant claim appears in several secondary sources (formalcontentsonly.wordpress.com; search result aggregations) but was not confirmed in Wikipedia’s Terry Ork article or in first-person interview transcripts found during this search. The claim is plausible and appears in multiple independent rephrases, but no primary source (interview with Ork, Malanga, or Hell directly naming the Malanga assistant role) was located. Treat as probable but not primary-source confirmed.

Discogs Notes

Terry Ork does not have a personal artist page on Discogs — he is credited on the label profile for Ork Records (Discogs label ID: 38538) but not as a credited individual on any release. His co-founder Charles Ball appears as “Executive-Producer” on multiple Ork releases (Discogs artist ID: 265229). All release-level data is attributed through the label.

The Feelies released a 2014 single on Ork Records (cat. 81984: “Fa Ce’ La” / “Forces At Work”) — this was a Numero Group-era revival release and not part of the original 1975–1979 run.

The label’s “Play With Fire” (cat. 81980, 1977) — credited to Mick Farren — has “Lost Johnny” (co-written by Lemmy Kilmister / Ian Fraser Kilmister) as the B-side, with Marc Bell on uncredited drums and Jon Tiven on uncredited guitar. “Play With Fire” itself is a Rolling Stones cover (Nanker Phelge).

The Link Cromwell single (cat. ORK 81981, 1977) features Lenny Kaye on uncredited bass and vocals, with Ritchie Adams producing. Link Cromwell was a mid-1960s stage name used by Ritchie Adams himself — explaining Adams’ production credit on a single released under the name.
