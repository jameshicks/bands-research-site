Tom Wilson (2)

Born Thomas Blanchard Wilson Jr. Producer. Discogs artist ID: 253021.

Produced the first two Velvet Underground albums — The Velvet Underground & Nico (1967) and White Light/White Heat (1968) — on Verve Records.

Overview

Tom Wilson was a major 1960s rock producer, notable for producing Bob Dylan (Bringing It All Back Home, Highway 61 Revisited), Simon & Garfunkel, and Frank Zappa’s debut (Freak Out!, 1966). He also overdubbed electric instruments onto folk recordings (most famously adding electric backing to “The Sound of Silence” for Simon & Garfunkel). His work on the two VU albums on Verve brought technical direction to sessions that Andy Warhol nominally produced.

Wilson and Warhol on The Velvet Underground & Nico

The album’s production split across two distinct phases, with Wilson arriving for the second. The bulk of the tracks were recorded in four days in mid-April 1966 at Scepter Studios in Manhattan — sessions financed by Warhol and Columbia Records sales executive Norman Dolph, who co-engineered with John Licata. Wilson was not present at Scepter; the recordings were completed before he was involved. After the tapes were rejected by multiple labels, MGM/Verve accepted them on the condition that a “real producer” be brought in: Wilson, who had recently joined Verve as a staff producer after leaving Columbia. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground_%26_Nico, as of 2026-06-26]

Wilson’s contributions came in post-production and in additional sessions. Three songs — “I’m Waiting for the Man,” “Heroin,” and “Venus in Furs” — were re-recorded at TTG Studios in Hollywood in May 1966. In November 1966, Wilson brought the band to Mayfair Recording Studios in Manhattan to cut “Sunday Morning,” a track added late to finish out the album, on which he also supervised overdubs of viola and Nico’s vocals. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground_%26_Nico, as of 2026-06-26]

Warhol received the official producer credit but was largely absent from studio work. Lou Reed said Warhol “didn’t do anything” in production terms and identified Wilson as the album’s “real producer.” Reed also offered a more nuanced gloss on Warhol’s value: he “absorbed all the attacks… he just sat there and said ‘Oooh, that’s fantastic’” — functioning as a shield against label pressure rather than a technical hand. Sterling Morrison described Warhol’s role as producing “in the sense of producing a film”: finding the project, raising capital, and hiring people to execute it. John Cale similarly credited Wilson as the true producer and noted Warhol was mostly absent from the sessions. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground_%26_Nico, as of 2026-06-26; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Wilson_(record_producer), as of 2026-06-26]
