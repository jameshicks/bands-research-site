Cindy Sherman

Born: 19 January 1954, Glen Ridge, New Jersey. Discogs ID: 2244302

American conceptual artist and photographer based in New York City. Known primarily for photographic self-portrait series in which she assumes fictional characters and roles. Rose to prominence with Untitled Film Stills (1977–80), 69 black-and-white photographs evoking female archetypes from B-movies and European arthouse cinema. Studied photography at SUNY Buffalo (grad. 1976). (Wikipedia, as of rev. 1357435649; Britannica)

Relationship with Babes In Toyland

The collaboration began through Tim Carr, the Reprise Records A&R executive who signed Babes In Toyland to Reprise. Carr was embedded in New York’s art-world social scene and introduced Sherman to the band. Sherman attended several BIT shows in New York, including CBGB, and the two parties developed a rapport — drummer Lori Barbero has said Sherman “really liked us.”

Barbero visited Sherman’s SoHo loft to select images for Fontanelle; Sherman provided handwritten notes on photographs she gave to Barbero. Beyond the album cover, the relationship extended to:

  • Cover photograph on the Painkillers EP (1993)
  • Three large (~25×40 ft) hand-painted stage banners based on Sherman photographs, plus two stage scrims, used for BIT live performances
  • Appearance in the “Bruise Violet” promotional video (c. 1991, shot in Sherman’s SoHo loft): Sherman and Kat Bjelland wore matching white dresses and wigs; Barbero noted that assuming a character via costume was “like making toast” for Sherman

Sherman confirmed she approved the banner use but stated she never saw the final result.

(Primary source: Walker Art Center, “Completely Punk Rock: Cindy Sherman’s (Nearly) Forgotten History with Babes in Toyland”)

Notes

The Discogs entry (ID 2244302) shows 7 cover photograph credits tied to Southern Records, but a full query across all pressings (2025-07-15) reveals these are entirely accounted for by multiple pressings/editions of the two known titles: Fontanelle appears on 5 Southern-labelled pressings and Painkillers on 2. There are no additional distinct titles with Southern Records credits — the count reflects Discogs listing Southern as the label on several European/UK co-releases of both records.

Web sources — Fontanelle cover provenance

Question: Was the Fontanelle cover image (naked doll held up in front of a mirror) drawn from an existing Sherman series, or created specifically for the album?

Conclusion: selected from existing work, not commissioned. The Walker Art Center article makes this clear: Barbero visited Sherman’s SoHo loft and chose from photographs already in Sherman’s archive — “She opened a whole wall of drawers. I don’t know how many dozens we looked at.” The word “select” is used consistently; there is no indication of a new shoot for the band. The covers “focused on dolls instead” of the prosthetics work Barbero personally preferred, meaning the selection was a negotiation between what Barbero liked and what was available in Sherman’s drawers. (Walker Art Center, “Completely Punk Rock”; recordart.net)

Which series? The “Sex Pictures” series (1992, Untitled #250–#264+) is the wrong match: it uses medical mannequins and prosthetic body parts in pornographic tableaux, not toy dolls in front of mirrors. (The Broad, Untitled #264; picturingsex.com) The “Civil War” series (1991) similarly features close-up body parts, not dolls. (MutualArt, Untitled #240) Sherman’s actual Barbie/toy-doll dismemberment work (“Broken Dolls”) came in 1999. No named series from 1991–92 whose description matches “naked doll in front of a mirror” has been identified in published catalogs, MoMA collection notes, or online retrospectives.

Additional series ruled out (2026-07-04 research): Sherman’s Fitcher’s Bird (Rizzoli, 1992, with Untitled #272) is a photographic illustrated fairy tale featuring dismembered doll parts in gothic tableaux — distinctive subject matter, not a doll held in front of a mirror. The “Doll with Mask” and “Mask over Baby Doll” works (dated 1987–92 by dealers Printed Matter and Caviar20) are standalone chromogenic prints from Sherman’s transitional doll/mask period, not part of a formally catalogued series; they precede or overlap with the Fontanelle selection window and confirm Sherman was producing doll-related work outside named series at this time. (Printed Matter; Caviar20, “Doll with Mask”; Caviar20, “Mask Over Baby Doll”)

Open sub-question: The image may be from an unlabeled or transitional body of work (Sherman’s numbering scheme has gaps between the History Portraits c.200s and Sex Pictures 250s), or from standalone doll works she was producing across 1987–92. Identifying the exact Untitled # and series would require consulting the Rizzoli/MoMA 2012 monograph (Respini, ed.) or Sherman’s Metro Pictures archive directly. No web source resolves this definitively as of 2026-07-04.

Conflict — Wikipedia vs. Walker Art Center:

  • Wikipedia (Fontanelle album article) states the cover photo “was taken for the album by photographer Cindy Sherman.” Citation [3] in that article links to a page about Gilles de Rais / Blue Beard, which does not support the cover photo claim — the citation is misattributed or inapplicable. (Wikipedia, Fontanelle (album), accessed 2026-07-04)
  • Walker Art Center article (primary source with direct quotes from Barbero) consistently uses the word “select” and describes Barbero choosing from “a whole wall of drawers” of existing photographs. This is the stronger source.
  • Verdict: Wikipedia claim is unsourced and contradicted by eyewitness account. Treat Walker Art Center as authoritative.
