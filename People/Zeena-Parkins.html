Biography

Origins and arrival in New York (pre-1984)

Zeena Parkins was born in 1956 in Detroit, Michigan. She studied at Bard College, where she developed her foundational instrumental practice. In 1984 she relocated to New York City, landing in the thick of the downtown Manhattan experimental scene centred on lofts, clubs like the Knitting Factory, and P.S. 122.

Downtown NYC and Skeleton Crew (1984–1990)

Within months of arriving in New York, Parkins became a member of Skeleton Crew (Discogs ID: 366111), the avant-rock unit led by Fred Frith alongside cellist Tom Cora and percussionist Dave Newhouse. Skeleton Crew’s recorded output spans two studio albums: Learn To Talk (1984, RecRec / Rift) and The Country Of Blinds (1986, Rift), plus the posthumous compilation Learn To Talk / Country Of Blinds (1990, RecRec). Parkins is credited on multiple instruments — organ, electric harp, accordion, vocals, drums. The group was dissolved before the decade was out but left a foundational mark on European and American avant-rock.

Simultaneously Parkins began releasing recordings under her own name. Something Out There (1987, No Man’s Land, cat. NML 8712) is her first primary-credited solo LP. Recorded and produced by Tom Cora, it is a compilation of short pieces each credited to a different guest composer-performer — Ikue Mori, Christian Marclay, Tom Cora, Samm Bennett, Wayne Horvitz, Jim Meneses — with Parkins on harp, electric harp, and editing. The record documents the range of the downtown improv ecosystem she had already plugged into by 1987.

The trio LP 143 Ludlow St. NYC (1988, Dossier, cat. ST 7555) follows immediately: a free-improvisation session with percussionists Takashi Kazamaki and Samm Bennett. The B-side compositions are credited to Parkins and Kazamaki jointly. Tom Cora engineered and mixed.

During this period Parkins also appears on John Zorn’s game-piece recording Cobra (1987, hat ART), one of the most documented examples of the downtown improv scene’s organized spontaneity.

Table of the Elements and the Knitting Factory era (1990–1998)

The early 1990s brought Parkins her defining solo statement. Nightmare Alley (1993, Table Of The Elements, cat. H 1 / TOTE 1) is an all-solo harp record — acoustic and electric — entirely composed and performed by Parkins. Bryce Goggin and Doug Henderson share engineering duties; David Shea produced; Elliott Sharp handled pre-mastering. The album’s 15 tracks range from sub-two-minute studies to extended improvisations. It became a touchstone for the electric harp in noise and experimental contexts and introduced Parkins to the Table Of The Elements catalog, the label that defined a certain strain of American minimalism-meets-noise in the 1990s.

She continued to appear on Knitting Factory releases throughout the early 1990s: Live At The Knitting Factory (1993) as vocalist and sampler; John Zorn’s Cobra: Live At The Knitting Factory (1995). The Bribe — Variations And Extensions On Spillane (1998, Tzadik) extends the Zorn connection into the Tzadik orbit.

By this point Phantom Orchard (Discogs ID: 484271) was taking shape — a duo with Ikue Mori that would become one of Parkins’s most enduring projects. Their only studio album on Discogs, Orra (2008, Tzadik), was produced and mixed by Mori, but the duo performed and recorded together across the decade preceding that release. The Phantom Orchard Ensemble (ID: 3903427) and Phantom Orchard Orchestra (ID: 2097212) are expanded formations of the same core.

Björk and international visibility (2001–2006)

The most commercially prominent period of Parkins’s career came through her extended collaboration with Björk. She is credited as harpist and harp arranger on Vespertine (2001, Elektra, US pressing ID: 11408), one of Björk’s most chamber-oriented records. Parkins also plays electric harp, celesta, and accordion on the single It’s In Our Hands (2002, One Little Indian) and on the live document Vespertine Live At Royal Opera House (2002, One Little Indian). The credit on the live release reads: “Arranged By [Harp]; Musician, Harp, Electric Harp, Celesta [Celeste], Accordion [Accordian], Handclaps; Written-By” — indicating a compositional contribution to the harp arrangements.

The Björk association produced a cluster of releases across 2001–2014:

  • Vespertine (2001)
  • It’s In Our Hands single (2002)
  • Vespertine Live At Royal Opera House (2002)
  • Minuscule EP (2003, One Little Indian)
  • Livebox DVD (2003, Polydor)
  • Vespertine Live (2004, One Little Indian)
  • The Music From Drawing Restraint 9 (Matthew Barney, 2005, Polydor) — harp
  • Surrounded compilation (2006, Polydor/One Little Indian)
  • Biophilia (2011, Polydor) — harps
  • Biophilia Live (2014, One Little Indian) — contracted harpist

During the same period Parkins contributed harp to Matthew Barney’s Cremaster 3 music (2002) and collaborated with the Tin Hat Trio (Tin Hat Trio, ID: 103375), a San Francisco-based acoustic chamber-improv group.

Extended technique, electronics, and chamber work (2007–2020)

In the 2010s Parkins moved increasingly toward compositions for harp with live electronics and extended instrumental processing. Three Harps, Tuning Forks & Electronics (2016, Good Child Music, ID: 9335642) is a primary solo record; Captiva (2018, Good Child Music, ID: 12903291) features live electronics processing by programmer Matthew Ostrowski (frequent collaborator from 5 shared releases) and stands as a compact statement of her post-acoustic approach — five movements alternating acoustic and electric harp with real-time electronics. Lawson White produced and engineered.

Transmigration At The Solar Max (2018, Intakt Records, cat. Intakt CD 311) is a full-band session with Elliott Sharp (8-string guitarbass, soprano sax, electronics, compositions), Carbon (23) (Sharp’s working band, ID: 11040362), and drummer Bobby Previte. Sharp produced; Patrik Landolt and Jazzfestival Saalfelden are also credited as producers.

Scale (2020, Two Rooms, cat. TR-005, vinyl LP) pairs Parkins with radio-electronics artist Jeff Kolar. Parkins plays acoustic harp, electric harp, tuning forks, metal bolts, electronics, and field recordings; the credit reads like a catalog of her expanded palette. Mixed and mastered by Matt Mehlan.

Cosa Brava and ensemble work (parallel thread)

Cosa Brava (ID: 1748582) — a chamber improv group featuring Parkins alongside Fred Frith, Carla Kihlstedt, Matthias Bossi, and Trevor Dunn — produced at least one album, The Sad Machinery Of Spring (2007, Hannibal Records). The band also connected Parkins to the Tin Hat Trio circle. News From Babel (ID: 669683), the Chris Cutler and Lindsay Cooper-anchored ensemble, included Parkins as a member; Keep The Dog (ID: 704156) and OWT (ID: 714469) are further ensemble affiliations from the ReR Megacorp orbit.

Recent work (2021–2024)

Glass Triangle (2021, Relative Pitch Records, cat. RPR1118) is a digital free-improv trio with alto saxophonist Mette Rasmussen and drummer Ryan Sawyer. Recorded and engineered by John Thayer.

Dam Against The Spring Tide (2024, Relative Pitch Records, cat. rpr1167/RPR1211) is a composed double-suite. The first suite, “Past Turned Space In 5 Parts,” features pianist Brett Carson, percussionist William Winant, and vocalist Joan La Barbara alongside Parkins on e-bow and field recordings. The second suite, “Dam Against The Spring Tide,” features a larger ensemble: Laurent Bruttin (clarinet), Tony Buck (drums/objects), Sébastien Roux (electronics), Matthew Ostrowski (electronics), Magda Mayas (piano/clavinet/objects), and Christian Kesten (voice). Elliott Sharp and Quentin Chiappetta mixed; James Fei mastered.
