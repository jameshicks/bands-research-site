John Hicks

American jazz pianist. Born December 21, 1941, Atlanta, GA. Died May 10, 2006, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 294393

Real name: John Josephus Hicks, Jr. Name variations on Discogs: J. Hicks, John J. Hicks, John “Walk This Way” Hicks.

Biography

Early life and training

John Hicks grew up in Atlanta and received his first piano instruction from his mother. He went on to study at Lincoln University of Missouri, Berklee College of Music, and the Juilliard School — a path that gave him serious grounding in both formal theory and jazz practice.

1960s: First New York credits

By the early 1960s Hicks was picking up sideman work in a range of contexts. Discogs places him on piano for Grant Green sessions (1963), Oliver Nelson dates, and work with Johnny Griffin and Pharoah Sanders in the mid-decade period. In 1966 he appears on Sonny Simmons’s Staying On The Watch (ESP-Disk’), alongside Barbara Donald on trumpet — one of his earliest avant-garde credits. He joined Art Blakey and the Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers in 1964, a formative residency in the hard-bop tradition. He then moved into Betty Carter’s trio (1965–66), a role to which he would later return.

The mid-to-late 1960s also brought work with Woody Herman and His Orchestra (1968–69), and sideman appearances on Bobby Hutcherson and Grant Green sessions for Blue Note.

Early 1970s: Teaching, then back to Carter

In the early 1970s Hicks stepped back from performing to teach jazz history and improvisation at Southern Illinois University. He returned to New York to rejoin Betty Carter’s trio, and the association produced one of his most celebrated credits: The Audience With Betty Carter (1980, Bet-Car Records), recorded live with bassist Curtis Lundy and drummer Kenny Washington. That live album became a benchmark document of Carter’s mature style, and Hicks’s playing is central to it.

Late 1970s: Session peak and leader debut

By the late 1970s Hicks was in constant demand across the post-bop and loft-jazz scenes. Key session credits from this period:

  • Oliver Lake, NTU: Point From Which Creation Begins (1976, Arista/Freedom) — piano and producer credit, with Famoudou Don Moye, Baikida Carroll, Joseph Bowie
  • Chico Freeman, Spirit Sensitive (1979, India Navigation) — with Cecil McBee and Billy Hart
  • Pharoah Sanders, Journey To The One (1980, Theresa Records) — with Idris Muhammad

His own debut as leader came with After The Morning (1979, West 54), followed by Hells Bells — recorded earlier but released 1980 on Strata-East, with the John Hicks Trio (Clint Houston, bass; Clifford Barbaro, drums).

1980s: Theresa Records and the Japanese connection

Through the 1980s Hicks built up a substantial body of leader recordings. His primary domestic home was Theresa Records (San Francisco), where he released four albums between 1981 and 1988:

  • Some Other Time (1981) — with Walter Booker and Idris Muhammad
  • John Hicks (1984)
  • In Concert (1986)
  • Two Of A Kind (1988)

He also recorded in Japan for DIW Records (Tokyo), producing Inc.1 (1985) and Sketches Of Tokyo (1985), and appearing on numerous DIW sessions for other leaders through the decade.

Sideman highlights of the 1980s:

  • Arthur Blythe — Blythe Spirit (1981, CBS), Illusions (1980, CBS), Basic Blythe (1988, CBS). Hicks was a fixture in Arthur Blythe’s acoustic quartet through several albums.
  • David Murray Quartet, Morning Song (1984, Black Saint) — with Reggie Workman and Ed Blackwell
  • Bobby Watson — Horizon and various ensemble recordings
  • Archie Shepp Quartet
  • Mingus Dynasty / Mingus Big Band

1991–1998: Novus, Concord, Milestone

In the early 1990s Hicks moved into major-label and prestige-indie territory:

  • Power Trio (1991, Novus/RCA) — co-led with Cecil McBee and Elvin Jones; produced by Bob Thiele
  • Friends Old And New (1992, Novus/RCA) — with Ron Carter, Grady Tate, Joshua Redman, Clark Terry, Al Grey; produced by Bob Thiele
  • Live At Maybeck Recital Hall, Volume Seven (1991, Concord Jazz) — solo piano

Both Novus albums were produced by Bob Thiele, who had championed adventurous jazz since his Impulse! days. Friends Old And New brought together an unusual cross-generational lineup.

At Milestone he recorded:

  • Newklear Music — The Songs Of Sonny Rollins (1997, Milestone) — billed as Keystone Trio with George Mraz and Idris Muhammad; produced by Todd Barkan. Hicks contributed one original composition alongside eight Rollins pieces.
  • Solid! (1998, Milestone)

He also appeared on multiple Venus Records (Tokyo) releases through the later 1990s, a Japanese audiophile label that documented him extensively.

Billy Bang Vietnam Suites

Hicks’s most-noted late-career credits are his appearances on both of Billy Bang’s Vietnam-themed large-ensemble recordings on Justin Time:

Vietnam: The Aftermath (2001, Justin Time, release ID 613727) Piano alongside: Curtis Lundy (bass), Michael Carvin (drums), Butch Morris (conductor), Sonny Fortune (flute, tracks 7–8), Frank Lowe (tenor sax, tracks 4, 5, 8), Ted Daniel (trumpet). Produced by Billy Bang and Jean-Pierre Leduc.

Vietnam: Reflections (2005, Justin Time, release ID 1235706) Piano alongside: Curtis Lundy (bass), Michael Carvin (drums), Butch Morris (conductor), James Spaulding (alto sax/flute), Henry Threadgill (flute), Ted Daniel (trumpet), Co Boi Nguyen (vocals), Nhan Thanh Ngo (Vietnamese zither). Bang arranged and conducted both sessions from his violin chair.

The same core rhythm section — Hicks, Lundy, Carvin — persisted across both suites, providing continuity between the two releases.

Marriage to Elise Wood and final years

In 2001 Hicks married jazz flutist Elise Wood (Elise Wood-Hicks, Discogs ID: 2469408). They subsequently performed and recorded as the John Hicks / Elise Wood Duo, Trio, Quartet, and Quintet. His first wife had been Olympia Hicks, also a musician who recorded with him.

His final leader release in the Discogs catalog is I Remember You (2009, HighNote Records), issued posthumously; Hicks died May 10, 2006 in New York City.
