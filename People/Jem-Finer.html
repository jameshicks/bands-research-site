Jem Finer

Full name: Jeremy Max Finer
Born: 25 July 1955, Stoke-on-Trent, England, UK
Discogs ID: 207924
Alias: Country Jem (Discogs alias ID: listed under name variations)

English musician, artist, and composer. Best known as banjo player and co-songwriter for The Pogues, the Celtic punk band he co-founded in London in the early 1980s. Beyond the Pogues, Finer has pursued a parallel career as a conceptual artist and sound installation creator, with projects rooted in mathematics, deep time, and self-organising systems.

Overview

Finer has a background in mathematics and computer science — he worked with ICL 1900 mainframe computers in the early 1970s. This grounding in computation and systems thinking runs through much of his non-Pogues work.

His most noted installation is Longplayer (first sounded 1 January 2000), a thousand-year musical composition commissioned by Artangel and released on CD in 2003 (Artangel label). A permanent installation in the lighthouse at Trinity Buoy Wharf, London, it uses a self-organising algorithm to play without repetition for 1,000 years.

During a two-year artist residency in the astrophysics department at Oxford University, Finer coined the term “post-digital” to describe a return to direct engagement with materials and landscape — informed by but not dependent on computer mediation. Works from that period included sculptural radio-observatories Landscope (Lough Neagh, N. Ireland) and The Centre of the Universe (University Parks, Oxford).

Other installations include Score For a Hole In the Ground (2005 PRSF New Music Award winner), a permanent self-sustaining gravity-driven musical structure in a Kent forest; Kung Fu Pinball, a pinball machine modified to auto-compose music; Slowplayer, a 3 r.p.m. sound system; and Supercomputer, a computational sculpture indebted to Jean Tinguely and John Conway’s Game of Life.

The Pogues (1982–1996; reunion 2001–2014; 40th anniversary shows 2024–2025)

Finer was a founding member of The Pogues (originally Pogue Mahone), playing banjo, mandola, mandolin, saxophone, guitar, and percussion across the band’s full run. He is also a credited songwriter on numerous Pogues tracks.

Compilations, live albums, and box sets continue through to BBC Sessions 1984–85 (2020, BBC / Pogue Mahone Records), where he is credited as “Performer [The Pogues], Banjo, Vocals.”

Songwriter credits include “Fairytale Of New York” (co-written with Shane MacGowan) and “Misty Morning, Albert Bridge,” among others. “Fairytale Of New York” has been widely covered; the 2017 NOFX compilation More Betterness! (Fat Wreck Chords) and the Dutch compilation Een Gouden Kerst (2019) both carry Finer’s Written-By credit.

After Shane MacGowan’s departure from the Pogues in 1991, Finer stayed with the band through the Waiting For Herb (1993, WEA) and Pogue Mahone (1995, WEA) albums. The band reunited in 2001 and played their final show in August 2014. Spider Stacy, James Fearnley, and Finer reconvened under the Pogues name for 40th anniversary shows in 2024–2025.

Session and Guest Credits

The Psychedelic Furs — Book Of Days (1989)

Finer appears as Musician [Additional] on The Psychedelic Furs’ album Book Of Days (1989, Columbia). The Discogs credit does not specify which instrument he played on the record; the role is listed generically as “Musician [Additional].” Other additional musicians on the same session included Knox Chandler, Joe McGinty, Anthony Thistlethwaite, Emily Burridge, Gena Dry, and John Hymas. Produced by David M. Allen.

Steve Earle — Copperhead Road (1988)

Finer is credited with Banjo on Steve Earle’s Copperhead Road (1988, MCA Records). This is a session credit, not a full album appearance — the title track of that album is the likely vehicle.

Shane MacGowan And The Popes — The Snake (1994)

Guest credit: 5-String Banjo on Shane MacGowan’s post-Pogues debut album The Snake (1994, ZTT), alongside other guests including Spider Stacy (whistle), Johnny Depp (guitar), and Brian Robertson (lead guitar).

Solo and Collaborative Work

Longplayer (2003 / 2015)

Finer’s most prominent solo release on Discogs. Longplayer (2003, Artangel) is the CD documentation of the thousand-year installation. A vinyl edition, Longplayer Vinyl Version, 30/6/2015 #1 (2015, LP Editions), captures a specific moment in the ongoing composition.

DM Bob & Jem Finer — Piraña (2008)

A collaborative album with DM Bob (artist ID 318513), released on Hazelwood Vinyl Plastics. No additional session credits appear in the DB.

Marseille Figs — Jumbo / The Long Goodbye EP (2009)

Finer appears as Performer [With], Chorus [The Jumbo Chorale], and Producer on this EP by Marseille Figs (Figs of London label). Also performing: Andrew Ranken and Darryl Hunt (both ex-Pogues), among others.

Mole (30) & Marseille Figs — One In The Grave And One On The Beat (2018)

Finer is credited as Featuring and Producer on this release (Figs of London). A large ensemble of guest performers appears, including Andrew Ranken and Darryl Hunt.

Hrdy-Grdy (2022)

Solo album, released on Thanet Tape Centre (UK). Finer is credited as: Written By, Performer, Recorded By, Producer, Hurdy Gurdy, Electronics. Cover art by Benedict Drew. One of his most instrument-specific credits, showcasing his move into hurdy-gurdy as a primary instrument.

Underground (2024)

Solo release on his own label (Not On Label / Jem Finer self-released). No additional credits in the DB.
