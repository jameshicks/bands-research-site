Janet Weiss

American drummer and vocalist, born 24 September 1965 in Hollywood, California. Based in Portland, Oregon. Discogs artist ID: 320275. Core member of Sleater-Kinney, Quasi (2), and Wild Flag; also a long-tenured drummer for Stephen Malkmus & The Jicks. One of the most prominent drummers in indie rock’s Pacific Northwest axis, active continuously from the mid-1990s through the 2020s.

Aliases / name variations: J. Weiss, J.W., JW, Janet Lee Weiss. Also credited as “Slam (59)” in one context.

Biography

Early Years

Weiss played in a band called The Furies while a student at San Francisco State University. She also played in the short-lived projects Motorgoat (Discogs ID: 2973835) and Slang (22) (Discogs ID: 6221956) before her profile-raising work in the mid-1990s Portland/Olympia scene.

Quasi (1993–present)

Weiss co-founded Quasi (Discogs: Quasi (2)) with then-husband Sam Coomes in 1993. The band’s earliest cassette-only material predates her time in Sleater-Kinney. Quasi’s core lineup across its catalog is Weiss (drums, vocals) and Coomes (keyboards/Rocksichord, bass, vocals) — a two-piece built around Coomes’s heavily treated keyboard sound and their intertwined vocal harmonies. Bassist Joanna Bolme later joined as a third member.

Quasi signed to UK label Domino Records for the bulk of their 1990s–2000s output and released a string of critically regarded albums. Weiss co-produced and co-wrote on several records. On The Sword Of God (Touch and Go, US / Domino, UK, 2001) she is credited as co-producer alongside Coomes, with Larry Crane assisting. She co-wrote tracks 7 and 8 on that album. The band continued into the 2010s, releasing Mole City (Domino, 2013) — a sprawling double album on which Weiss is credited Performer, Producer, Recorded By, and Mixed By — and Breaking The Balls Of History (Sub Pop, 2022/2023), their most recent full-length.

Sleater-Kinney (1996–2019)

Weiss joined Sleater-Kinney in 1996, replacing Laura MacFarlane, for Dig Me Out (Kill Rock Stars, 1997). This was the band’s third album and the one that brought them widespread critical attention. Weiss’s drumming — powerful, propulsive, sometimes polyrhythmic — became central to the band’s identity from that point forward.

The move to Sub Pop for The Woods (2005) coincided with a noticeably heavier, more distorted sound, with Dave Fridmann producing and engineering. Weiss is credited on both The Woods and its single “Entertain” (2005, Sub Pop). The band went on indefinite hiatus in 2006, then reunited in 2014. Weiss departed Sleater-Kinney in 2019 ahead of The Center Won’t Hold (Mom + Pop, 2019), on which she retains a “Songwriter” credit but did not play.

Wild Flag (2010–2012)

Wild Flag (Discogs ID: 2171527) was a short-lived supergroup formed in Portland around 2010, comprising Weiss, Carrie Brownstein, Mary Timony (ex-Helium (3)), and Rebecca Cole. They released one self-titled LP on Merge Records in 2011, recorded by Chris Woodhouse. Mixing was split between Woodhouse (most tracks), Dave Fridmann (track A1), and Tucker Martine (tracks A4, B4). Mastered by John Golden.

Stephen Malkmus & The Jicks (c. 2006–2018)

Weiss served as drummer for Stephen Malkmus’s band Stephen Malkmus & The Jicks for over a decade, appearing on at least two studio albums:

The Go-Betweens (2000)

Weiss played drums on The Friends Of Rachel Worth (Clearspot/EFA, 2000) by Australian legends The Go-Betweens, recorded at Lawrence Crane’s Jackpot! Recording Studio in Portland. Sam Coomes played keyboards on the same record. Primary songwriters Grant McLennan and Robert Forster produced; Adele Pickvance played bass. This is a notable one-off credit connecting the Portland scene to the Australian indie lineage.

Drumgasm (2013)

Drumgasm (Jackpot Records, 2013) is a collaborative drum record credited to Weiss, Matt Cameron (Soundgarden/Pearl Jam), and Zach Hill (Hella/Death Grips). Recorded by Larry Crane at Jackpot! Recording Studio. Weiss is primary artist (credited as “Weiss”). This is one of the few releases where she is the lead credited artist.
