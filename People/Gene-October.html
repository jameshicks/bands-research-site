Gene October

Oct 17, 20268 min read

Gene October

Born John Peter O’Hara (also cited as Eugene James October), 1954, London, UK. Vocalist, songwriter, founding member and permanent frontman of Chelsea (2). Discogs artist ID: 342563.

Note: Discogs lists his real name as John Peter O’Hara; external sources cite Eugene James October. Both names appear in the record.

Biography

Origins and Chelsea’s Founding (1976)

Gene October co-founded Chelsea (2) in London in 1976, at the height of the nascent British punk scene. Chelsea’s original lineup is historically significant for including two musicians who would go on to major careers elsewhere: William Broad (later known as Billy Idol) on guitar, and Tony James on bass. Both departed in late 1976 — before Chelsea had released any records — to form Generation X, taking the pop-oriented songwriting direction with them. October retained the Chelsea name and rebuilt the band around a harder, more confrontational punk stance.

The early band also included guitarist James Stevenson, who would become the longest-serving musical collaborator in the Chelsea orbit (across multiple eras and lineups) and who later joined Generation X (4), Gene Loves Jezebel, The Alarm, and The Cult.

Step-Forward Records Era (1977–1982)

Chelsea signed to Step-Forward Records (label ID: 14962), the British punk imprint founded by Miles Copeland (2) and Mark Perry (of Sniffin’ Glue fame) and Harry T. Murlowski. Step-Forward was also home to The Fall, Sham 69, and The Cortinas — a defining independent punk roster.

“Right to Work” (1977, SF cat# 2) — Chelsea’s debut single. Produced by Mark Perry and Miles Copeland (2); backing vocals by Mark Perry. Lineup: James Stevenson (guitar), Henry Daze (bass), Carey Fortune (drums), Gene October (vocals). The single is one of the foundational documents of UK punk, and October was credited as co-writer (as “October”).

“High Rise Living” (1977, SF cat# 5) — Second single on Step-Forward. Minimal Discogs personnel data: no musician credits beyond sleeve design (Sandra-Lynn Tiffin).

“Urban Kids / No Flowers” (1978) — Single produced by Kit Lambert (of The Who fame), a notable left-field production choice. Lineup by this point: Dave Martin (guitar, vocals), James Stevenson (guitar, vocals), Geoff Myles (bass), Steve J. Jones (drums), Gene October (vocals).

Chelsea LP (1979, Step-Forward SFLP 2) — Debut album. Self-produced by Chelsea and Wally Brill. Lineup: Gene October (vocals), James Stevenson (lead guitar, vocals), Dave Martin (guitar, vocals), Geoff Myles (bass), Chris Bashford (drums, vocals). Multiple engineers including Nigel Gray (of Surrey Sound, where The Police recorded) and Pete Hammond.

“No-One’s Coming Outside” (1980, SF 14) — Single.

Alternative Hits / Evacuate (1981–1982) — The band released a compilation on Step-Forward (SFLP 5, 1981) and the album Evacuate (SFLP 7, 1982), produced by Harry T. Murlowski. A US pressing of Evacuate came out on I.R.S. Records (1982), as did a US version of No Escape (1980).

Mid-1980s: Independent Output and Lineup Churn (1984–1989)

After Step-Forward wound down, Chelsea moved through a series of independent labels:

  • Live And Well (1984, Picasso) — Live album, produced by Gene October.
  • Original Sinners (1985, Communiqué) — LP.
  • Valium Mother (1985, Communiqué) — Single; October co-wrote.
  • Give Me More (1986, self-released) — Single, produced by October.
  • Rocks Off (1986, Jungle Records / self-released) — LP.

Underwraps (1989, I.R.S. Records) — The most polished studio album of this era. Produced by Clive Mitten. Lineup: October (vocals), Steve Tannett (guitar), Paul Lincoln (bass), Jamie Abethell (drums), Willy Grip (additional guitar, keyboards). October and Chelsea are co-credited as writers and producers.

1990s: Archive Releases and Continued Activity

  • Unreleased Stuff (1989, Clay Records) — Archive material.
  • Live At The Music Machine 1978 (1992, Released Emotions Records)
  • The Alternative (1993, Alter Ego)
  • Traitors Gate (1994, Weser Label, Germany) — Gene October credited as “Lead Vocals.”
  • We Dare (1994, Weser Label)
  • Alternative Hits (1998, Captain Oi!) — Compilation reissue.
  • The Punk Singles Collection 1977–82 (1998, Captain Oi!) — Key archival singles comp.

2000s: Captain Oi! Reissues and the Stevenson/Barber Lineup

The 2000s saw both archival reissues and new studio recordings on Captain Oi! and TKO Records.

Faster Cheaper And Better Looking (2005, Captain Oi!) — New studio album, produced by James Stevenson and Chris Bashford at Ocean Studios (Battersea) and Pink Gun Studios (Richmond). Core lineup: Gene October (lead vocals), James Stevenson (guitar, vocals), Tony Barber (bass), Chris Bashford (drums, vocals). Mastered by Tim Turan. This represents the most tightly documented “classic” 2000s Chelsea lineup.

Sod The War (2007, TKO Records) — Same core lineup: October, Stevenson, Barber, Bashford.

Urban Kids — A Punk Rock Anthology (2004, Castle Music) — Comprehensive archival compilation.

2010s–Present: Westworld and Audio Platter

Saturday Night Sunday Morning (2015, Westworld Recordings) — Studio album, produced by Chelsea and Dick Crippen. Lineup: Gene October (lead vocals), James Stevenson (guitar, vocals), Nic Austin (guitar, vocals), Mat Sargent (bass, vocals), Lee Morrell (drums). October, Stevenson, Sargent, and Austin all share writing credits.

In Session (2017, Westworld Recordings) and Mission Impossible (2017, Let Them Eat Vinyl) — Further output; Let Them Eat Vinyl also compiled Right To Work — The Singles in 2015.

Meanwhile Gardens (2021, Audio Platter) — Lineup: October, Nic Austin (guitar, vocals), Mat Sargent (bass, vocals), Steve Grainger (drums). Tony Barber no longer in this lineup.

Evacuate Revisited (2022, Audio Platter) — Revisiting the 1982 album.

Radio Active Tapes (2022, Gutterwail Records, US) — Archival/compilation; October co-written.

The Step-Forward Years 1977–82 (2023, Captain Oi!) — Definitive archival collection of the label era.
