Adrian Borland

English singer, guitarist, and songwriter. Born Adrian Kelvin Borland, 1957, Wimbledon, South London. Died 26 April 1999, Wimbledon (suicide). Discogs artist ID: 79566.

Primary creative force behind The Sound (2); also worked as a solo artist and in several other projects across a career spanning 1976–1999.

Early Years and Pre-Sound Projects (1976–1979)

Borland was active in the South London music scene from the mid-1970s. His earliest documented band was The Outsiders (2) (Discogs ID: 131374), a UK punk outfit also featuring Graham Bailey, Adrian Janes, and Bob Lawrence. The Outsiders released three records on the South London imprint Raw Edge Records in 1977–1979: Calling On Youth (1977), One To Infinity (1977), and Close Up (1979). Borland sang, played guitar, and contributed songwriting across all three; the compilation Count For Something (Albums | Demos | Live | Unreleased 1976–1978) appeared on Cherry Red in 2021, drawing on this raw period. A further archival compilation, Vital Years (Three Lines Records, 1993), also documents the Outsiders.

In 1979, alongside Graham Bailey, Borland formed Second Layer (Discogs ID: 59911) — a more abrasive, experimental post-punk duo. Second Layer released the Flesh As Property E.P. (1979, Tortch Records / Fresh Records (14)) and State Of Emergency E.P. (1980, Tortch), followed by the album World Of Rubber (1981, Cherry Red). Second Layer operated concurrently with the early Sound — both acts used Tortch as a vehicle.

Also in 1979, Borland was part of The Witch Trials (Discogs ID: 293267), a one-off project featuring Jello Biafra (Eric Boucher) and East Bay Ray (Ray Pepperell) of Dead Kennedys, Morgan Fisher, and Christian Ingle (Christian Lunch). The session was recorded in London during the Dead Kennedys’ first European tour and produced a single single. The release appeared in 1991 on Alternative Tentacles.

The Sound (1979–1988)

See The Sound (2) for the full account of The Sound’s career. Summary for cross-reference:

The Sound formed in 1979 with Borland (vocals, guitar, primary songwriter), Bi Marshall (keyboards, departed December 1980), Graham Bailey / Graham Green (bass), and Michael Dudley (drums). Colvin Mayers joined on keyboards/guitar in 1981 and remained until the band’s dissolution.

Label arc:

  • Tortch Records (1979 — Physical World E.P.)
  • Korova / WEA (1980–1983 — Jeopardy, From The Lions Mouth, All Fall Down)
  • Statik Records (1984–1987 — Shock Of Daylight, Heads And Hearts, In The Hothouse)
  • Play It Again Sam Records (1987 — Thunder Up)

Borland’s mental health deteriorated during the Thunder Up tour. After a breakdown in Spain in 1987, the band’s final gig was in Zoetermeer, Netherlands. The Sound dissolved in 1988.

Throughout this period, Borland also produced for other artists and occasionally worked under the aliases Adrian Amsterdam and Joachim Pimento (Discogs alias ID: 268728).

Adrian Borland & The Citizens (1989–1992)

After The Sound dissolved, Borland moved to the Netherlands and formed Adrian Borland & The Citizens (Discogs ID: 272168) with a Dutch backing lineup: Jaap Vrenegoor (drums/percussion), Thijs Vermeulen (bass), Victor Heeremans (keyboards), and Wouter Planteijdt (guitar). The Netherlands had always been The Sound’s strongest territory, and the move was a logical extension of those ties.

The Citizens released three albums on Play It Again Sam Records:

Alexandria (1989, PIAS Benelux) — the debut. An expansive, orchestrated record co-produced by Nick Robbins, featuring string arrangements by John Metcalfe, saxophone and harmonica by Anthony Thistlethwaite (The Waterboys), double bass by Danny Thompson, and Colvin Mayers on keyboards. Borland wrote and arranged everything. A richly produced step beyond the rawer Sound sound.

Light The Sky (1989, PIAS Belgium) — single.

Beneath The Big Wheel (1989, PIAS Belgium) — single, produced and co-written by Borland.

Brittle Heaven (1992, PIAS Europe) — the second full Citizens album. Produced by Borland and Don Victor, co-engineered by Jim Barf, Sander Janssen, and Wilmer Schouten. The lineup was Vrenegoor, Vermeulen, Heeremans, and Planteijdt, with Anthony Thistlethwaite again on saxophone/mandolin/harmonica, and backing vocals from Juliette Van Dijk and Wilmer Schouten. An unreleased demo session, Adrian Borland; Demo Juli ‘90 (Not On Label), also circulates from this era.

The band also left behind The Amsterdam Tapes (2006, Pop One Records) — a posthumous archival release drawn from 1992 recordings captured by Victor Heeremans, produced for release by Bart Van Poppel, mixed by Joeri Saal, and mastered by Alan Ward.

White Rose Transmission (1995–1999)

White Rose Transmission (Discogs ID: 99199) was a collaboration between Borland and Carlo Van Putten, vocalist of The Convent and Dead Guitars, a Dutch act. The band also drew on guests including Marty Wilson-Piper (The Church), Mark Burgess (The Chameleons), and Amanda Palmer (later The Dresden Dolls).

Their self-titled debut, White Rose Transmission (1995, Red Sun Records / Strange Ways Records, Germany), appeared during the final phase of Borland’s life. A second album, 700 Miles Of Desert (1999, EastWest / Red Sun Records), was released posthumously — it appeared in the year of Borland’s death. The project continued after Borland’s death under Van Putten, with further albums in 2006 and 2008.

A compilation album, Presence (1999, Merlin), also appeared posthumously.

Solo Career (1994–1999)

Borland released three solo albums under his own name during his lifetime, all after relocating to the Netherlands. He handled production, multi-tracking, and virtually all instruments himself across these records.

Beautiful Ammunition (1994, Resolve Records) Co-produced with Mark Hunziker (drum programming, hand percussion) and Mark Kelser (engineer, keyboard programming). Borland played acoustic guitar, electric guitar, e-bow, bass, and sang. A stripped-down, personal record — the most solo of the three in conception.

Cinematic (1996, Resolve Records) Entirely self-produced and self-directed. Engineered by Mark Kelser; mastered by Denis Blackham. Borland played guitar and sang; the “directed by” credit implies an audiovisual dimension. Slightly more atmospheric than its predecessor.

5:00 AM (1997, Earth Records) The most collaborative of the three. Co-produced with Tim Smith (of Cardiacs), who also provided drums, keyboard and sample programming, backing vocals, and harmonium. Pat Rowles contributed bass and backing vocals; Don Alexis played drums; Michael O’Gorman played trumpet and flugelhorn; and Thorn (28) provided backing vocals. Photography by Andy Bell (3).

A fourth solo album, Harmony And Destruction (The Unfinished Journey) (2002, Red Sun Records), appeared posthumously. It was completed from Borland’s unfinished recordings with co-production by Wally Brill, additional recording and mixing by Pat Rowles, Eddie Dudzik, and Pete Barraclough, and drumming by John Miracle.

Death

Adrian Borland died by suicide on 26 April 1999 in Wimbledon, throwing himself in front of a train. He was 41. His death came four years after The Sound had already dissolved, at a point when several solo records and the White Rose Transmission project had shown him still creatively active, if increasingly isolated.

His mental health difficulties — which had ended The Sound — had never fully resolved. The deterioration that had led to his breakdown in Spain in 1987 and the band’s dissolution in 1988 continued through his years in the Netherlands.
