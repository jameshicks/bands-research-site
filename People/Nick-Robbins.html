Nick Robbins

UK record producer, engineer, and mastering engineer. Discogs artist ID: 261168. Also credited as “Elephantman Nick Robbins” and “Nick Robins.”

Based at Elephant Studios, London (often credited simply as “at Elephant” or “Elephant Studios” in release credits). Robbins’s career spans from around 1980 to the present, with a high-volume mastering practice running alongside more selective production and engineering work. Best known for his long association with The Sound (2) and Second Layer, and for a broad independent-label engineering practice in the 1980s and early 1990s that touched the UK post-punk, Irish indie (Setanta Records), folk-punk (The Pogues), world music (World Circuit / Globe Style), and alternative scenes.

Early Career and Elephant Studios (1980–1983)

Robbins emerges in the Discogs record in 1980, already working with the South London post-punk circle around Adrian Borland. His earliest credited sessions are on releases connected to Tortch Records, the self-run imprint Borland and Graham Bailey used for both The Sound (2) and Second Layer.

The Sound — Heyday single (1980, Korova KOW 10): Robbins produced this single alongside the band. It was The Sound’s breakthrough release on Korova, the WEA-distributed UK imprint also home to Echo & The Bunnymen and The Teardrop Explodes.

The Sound — Jeopardy (1980, Korova KODE 1 / WEA): Co-produced and recorded by Robbins and the band. The debut album — featuring Borland (vocals, guitar), Bi Marshall (keyboards), Graham Bailey (bass), and Michael Dudley (drums) — introduced The Sound’s signature post-punk idiom: taut, spare, emotionally direct. Robbins’s recording approach kept the sound close and live-feeling. Released through Korova in the UK and WEA for Benelux.

Second Layer — State Of Emergency E.P. (1980, Tortch TOR 006): Robbins co-produced and engineered this three-track 7” (tracks: “State Of Emergency,” “I Need Noise,” “The Cutting Motion”). The EP was credited as 2nd Layer on the label; Robbins shaped its stark drum-machine and synth textures. One of his earliest-documented engineering credits.

Second Layer — World Of Rubber (1981, Cherry Red B RED 14): Recorded by Robbins. The duo’s sole album, nine tracks of abrasive electronic post-punk / proto-industrial, released on Cherry Red. His role was recording engineer.

In 1981–82 Robbins was simultaneously working outside the Borland circle: engineering Family Fodder’s Film Music (1981, Fresh Records (14)), producing and engineering sessions for Airstrip-One (Longer To Live, Nothing Is Forever), and beginning a sustained run of work with the left-punk band Newtown Neurotics that would last through the late 1980s.

The Sound — All Fall Down (1982, WEA): Produced by Robbins and the band. The third album, recorded with broader use of synthesizers and drum programming alongside Michael Dudley’s live drums; Flood (Mark Ellis) and others assisted with engineering. The lineup was now Borland, Colvin Mayers (keyboards/guitar), Graham Bailey / Green (bass), and Dudley.

The Sound — Hot House single (1982, WEA): Co-produced by Robbins.

Also in 1982: produced the Cocktails EP for Attila The Stockbroker (Cherry Red); engineered and produced singles for Newtown Neurotics (Kick Out The Tories!, Licensing Hours / No Sanctuary).

Kevin Hewick / The Sound — This Cover Keeps Reality Unreal (1983, Cherry Red): Robbins engineered this collaborative 12” between Kevin Hewick and The Sound, and also contributed water sound effects.

Broadening Practice: The Pogues, Newtown Neurotics, and Independent London (1983–1987)

By 1983–84, Robbins was operating as a generalist independent engineer and producer, taking sessions from across the UK independent sector. Among the most durable working relationships:

The Pogues: Robbins engineered the band’s debut album Red Roses For Me (1984, Stiff Records) and their second, Rum Sodomy & The Lash (1985, Stiff Records), both produced by Elvis Costello. He continued engineering Pogues releases through their Stiff period and into the early 1990s (Poguetry In Motion EP, 1986; Once Upon A Time compilation, 1993; Waiting For Herb, 1993). The Pogues’ later compilation The Ultimate Collection (2005) credits Robbins as producer of selected tracks.

Newtown Neurotics: An extensive run spanning Kick Out The Tories! (1982, CNT/No Wonder) through Is Your Washroom Breeding Bolsheviks? (1988, Jungle Records), including the albums Beggars Can Be Choosers (1983, Razor), Repercussions (1985, Jungle), Kickstarting A Backfiring Nation (1986, Jungle), and Living With Unemployment (1986, Jungle). Robbins produced and engineered throughout. He later produced and mixed the 1996 Beggars Can Be Choosers reissue (Dojo) and subsequent repress series (Radiation Reissues, 2020).

Attila The Stockbroker: Cocktails EP (1982, Cherry Red), Ranting At The Nation (1983, Cherry Red) — producer and engineer.

Troy Tate: Love Is… (1983, Why Fi/Rough Trade; 1984, RCA) — engineer.

Virginia Astley: Melt The Snow (1985, Happy Valley/Rough Trade/Columbia) — engineer.

Siouxsie & The Banshees: Tinderbox (1986) — mix assistant.

The Men They Couldn’t Hang: Night Of A Thousand Candles (1985, Wired/Imp) — engineer, co-mixer.

Loudon Wainwright III: More Love Songs (1986, Demon/Rounder); Therapy (1989, RCA/Silvertone); T.S.D.H.A.V. (1989, Silvertone) — engineer.

Orchestre Somo Somo: Somo Somo (1985), Paris (1986/87) on Stern’s Africa/Globe Style — engineer. These were among his earliest world music credits; a significant strand of globe-straddling sessions followed through World Circuit and related labels in the early 1990s.

The Sound — Thunder Up (1987, Play It Again Sam Records / Polydor): Produced and recorded by Robbins and the band. The last Sound studio album, recorded for Play It Again Sam Records and released on Polydor in the UK and Nettwerk in North America. Also produced the Iron Years single (1987, Play It Again Sam) and Hand Of Love (1987, Rebel Rec.) from this period.

Nigel Kennedy — Let Loose (1987, EMI): Co-produced. An early major-label credit.

Adrian Borland & The Citizens, Setanta Scene, and Factory Classical (1989–1993)

By 1989 Robbins was working simultaneously on the Borland post-Sound projects, the nascent Irish indie Setanta label acts, and Factory’s classical subsidiary.

Adrian Borland & The Citizens: Robbins co-produced and engineered the band’s principal recordings:

  • Alexandria (1989, Play It Again Sam Records / Midnight Music) — co-producer, engineer, programmed by
  • Beneath The Big Wheel single (1989, Play It Again Sam) — co-producer
  • Light The Sky single (1989, Play It Again Sam) — co-producer
  • All The Words single (1992, Play It Again Sam) — producer

Adrian Borland & The Citizens was the main post-Sound vehicle for Adrian Borland, centred in the Netherlands where the Sound had long had their strongest following.

Kreisler String Orchestra — 226 (1989, Factory Classical): Produced and engineered by Robbins. Factory Classical was Factory Records’ short-lived classical/contemporary music sublabel; the Kreisler String Orchestra recordings were among its key releases.

The Frank And Walters: A sustained run for the Cork indie-pop act on Setanta: E.P. 1 (1991), E.P. 2 (1991), The Frank And Walters debut album (1991/92), Trains, Boats And Planes (1992/93), Fashion Crisis Hits New York single (1993). Robbins produced and engineered throughout, one of his longest outside-of-Sound label relationships.

A House: I Am The Greatest single (1991/92, Setanta/Radioactive) — engineer. Bingo single (1991, Setanta) — engineer.

My Bloody Valentine — Loveless (1991, Creation Records / Sire / Warner Bros.): Robbins is credited as engineer and assistant engineer on this landmark album. The specific sessions or tracks are not broken out in the Discogs credits, but the credit appears across multiple pressings.

The Godfathers — Unreal World (1991, Epic): Additional engineering at Elephant Studios.

Goat (5) — Everybody Wants To Be There (1991, Beggars Banquet): Engineer. The single was recorded at Elephant Studios; Goat (5) were a Brighton-based band on the Beggars Banquet roster.

Power Of Dreams — A Little Piece Of God (1989, Setanta): Engineer and mixer.

The Popguns: Crazy (1991, Midnight Music) — engineer/recorded by; Star (1994, 3rd Stone) — engineer; Love Junky (2019, 3rd Stone) — producer. A long if intermittent relationship.

The Divine Comedy — Europop (1992, Setanta): Engineer. Neil Hannon’s early Setanta album.

The Mighty Lemon Drops: Ricochet (1992, Sire/Reprise) — producer/engineer; Into The Sun (1992, Sire) — producer. Two late albums for the Wolverhampton post-punk act on Sire, their last.

World Circuit / Globe Style: From the mid-1980s through early 1990s, Robbins took ethnomusicology-adjacent sessions — Orchestre Somo Somo, Jean Toussaint (What Goes Around, World Circuit, 1992), Ali Farka Touré (The River, Mango/World Circuit, 1990 — mixer), Oumou Sangare (Ko Sira, World Circuit, 1993 — mixer), Abdel Gadir Salim All-Stars (The Merdoum Kings Play Songs Of Love, World Circuit, 1991 — assistant recorded and mixed). These suggest studio time at Elephant was well-regarded for world/jazz work.

Mastering Practice (1990s–present)

Robbins has an extremely high-volume mastering and remastering practice. Discogs records over 1,400 “Mastered By” credits and over 300 “Remastered By” credits, concentrated heavily in the 1990s and 2000s across the UK independent and heritage sector. This includes extensive Sound and Second Layer reissues:

  • Jeopardy (Renascent, 2001; Korova (3), 2010; 1972, 2012; Warner Music UK, 2024)
  • All Fall Down (Renascent, 2001; 1972, 2013; WEA/Warner Music UK, 2024)
  • World Of Rubber reissues (Cherry Red, 2009; Dark Entries, 2015; 1972, 2023; Desire, 2025)
  • Courts Or Wars compilation (1972, 2023) — credited as original engineer and remaster

The name variation “Elephantman Nick Robbins” appears on some mastering credits, signalling that Elephant Studios is the base of operations.
