Andy Sturmer

Marvin Andrew Sturmer (born March 11, 1965, Los Angeles County, California). American vocalist, drummer, songwriter, and record producer. Discogs artist ID: 303994. Known primarily as co-founder, lead vocalist, and drummer of Jellyfish (2) (1989–1994), and subsequently as a prolific producer/songwriter for Japanese pop acts and composer of animated television themes.

Overview

Sturmer grew up in Pleasanton, California, where he played drums in the high school jazz band and met keyboardist Roger Joseph Manning Jr.. The two joined Beatnik Beatch, an LA pop-rock band that released one album on Atlantic (1988) before dissolving. Sturmer and Manning then co-founded Jellyfish in San Francisco in 1989.

In Jellyfish, Sturmer was a rare figure: a drummer who played at the front of the stage (on a stand-up kit) and sang lead vocals, while also co-writing all material with Manning. Jellyfish released two critically acclaimed power pop albums — Bellybutton (1990) and Spilt Milk (1993) — on Charisma Records, toured extensively, but did not achieve commercial success commensurate with their critical reputation. Internal friction led to the band’s dissolution in early 1994.

Following Jellyfish, Sturmer declined a solo deal from Virgin Records and pivoted entirely to production and songwriting for other artists. He became closely associated with the Japanese pop duo Puffy (known outside Japan as Puffy AmiYumi), for whom he produced multiple albums between 1998 and 2004. He was credited as “godfather” of the group and gave them their name. He also collaborated with Japanese rock star Tamio Okuda on two albums (30, 1995; The Standard, 2001). Through his work with Puffy, Sturmer transitioned into composing themes and scores for animated television: Teen Titans (Cartoon Network, 2003), Hi Hi Puffy AmiYumi (Cartoon Network, 2004), Ben 10, Batman: The Brave and the Bold, The Batman (season 3), and Disney’s My Friends Tigger & Pooh. He reportedly had four or five concurrent Disney projects as of 2015. (Source: Wikipedia)

Sturmer maintains an extremely low public profile. He has refused most interview requests since the mid-1990s. He contributed to the 2002 Jellyfish box set Fan Club and was interviewed for Craig Dorfman’s 2016 book Brighter Day: A Jellyfish Story.
