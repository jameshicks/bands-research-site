Overview

Just-Ice (Joseph Williams Jr.) is a Brooklyn-born rapper credited as New York’s first gangsta emcee and one of the earliest hip-hop artists to openly incorporate the teachings of the Nation of Gods and Earths. Before rap, he worked as a bouncer at punk clubs around the city. His catalogue is concentrated in the late 1980s and early 1990s — primarily on Fresh Records — spanning hardcore hip-hop and electro, before a deliberate stylistic pivot on his third release toward ragamuffin-inflected beats and dancehall-reggae vocal cadences. His most sustained creative partnership was with KRS-One (51 shared credits), with producer Kurtis Mantronik also central to his early sound.
