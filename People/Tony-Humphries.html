Tony Humphries

Discogs ID: 22135 Real name: Tony Humphries Origin: New Jersey / New York City Active: 1980–present Alias: A Tony Humphries Project Group: Ollytone Inc. (with Ollie Dagois) Cousin: Merlin Bobb (Discogs ID: 88686)

American DJ, remixer, and producer. One of the defining voices of the New York/New Jersey house and dance underground, best known for a long residency at the Zanzibar Club in Newark, NJ, a weekly mix show on KISS FM 98.7 in New York, and a remix practice that spanned disco, post-disco, house, and R&B from 1980 through the 2000s. His “Sure Shot” mix credit — the signature he used on early 1980s remixes — became a recognizable brand in NYC dancefloor culture. He founded the labels Yellorange and Tony Records, and later TR Records (4), through which he served as executive producer on dozens of house vocal releases from the late 1990s onward.

Biography

Early Career and the Sure Shot Mixes (1980–1984)

Humphries’ Discogs credit trail begins in 1980 with a “Mixed By” on Eastbourne Records Ltd. releases — label home of early NYC disco. By 1981 he was doing “Special Mix” remix work, and by 1982 had established his “Sure Shot” remix signature.

His highest-profile early credits include:

  • Indeep — “Last Night A D.J. Saved My Life” (1982, Sound Of New York) — credited as “Sure Shot Mix By.” This became one of the most-pressed 12-inches in UK and European dance music; 63 pressings tracked on Discogs across its life.
  • West End Records — Master Mix Medley - 7th Anniversary Edition (1982) — Humphries’ only primary-credit solo release in this era: a DJ-mix LP celebrating West End Records’ seventh anniversary. Mastered by Herb Powers Jr. The record marks his formal introduction as a label-affiliated DJ mixer.
  • Visual — “The Music Got Me” (1983, Prelude Records) — “Sure Shot Mix,” produced by Boyd Jarvis, co-produced by Timmy Regisford. One of the early Prelude records to document the emerging house aesthetic.
  • Evelyn “Champagne” King — “Face To Face” (1983, RCA Victor) — remix work alongside Nick Martinelli on an album by a major-label R&B artist, showing early crossover reach.

During 1982–84 he also mixed records on Streetwise, Are ‘n Be, Aria, Reelin & Rockin Records, and TNT Unlimited. The “Sure Shot” tag appears consistently across these: it was his remix brand, not an alias artist name.

Zanzibar Residency and the “Zanz Mix” Era (1985–1989)

The Zanzibar Club (Newark, NJ) became the central institution of Humphries’ career through most of the 1980s. His 1987 remix of “Power” on Easy Street Records is credited “A ‘Zanz Mix’ By,” directly naming the club. Easy Street was a key label for the Zanzibar scene, appearing repeatedly in his credits across 1986–87 alongside other NJ-connected releases.

Significant credits from this period:

  • The Joubert Singers — “Stand On The Word” (1985, Next Plateau Records Inc.) — mixed by Humphries alongside producer George Rodriguez. “Stand On The Word” became a touchstone gospel-house record that built a cult audience during and after the Zanzibar years.
  • Cultural Vibe — “Ma Foom Bey” (1986, Easy Street Records) — Humphries received “Edited By” credit; the track was pressed over 7 editions and became a Zanzibar/house classic, later widely sampled.
  • Cultural Vibe — “Power” (1987, Easy Street Records) — “Zanz Mix” credit; same group as “Ma Foom Bey.”
  • Sweetheart (1986, Supertronics) — Mixed By; pressed 7 times.
  • Let’s Have Some Fun (1987, Atlantic) — Mixed By.

During this era he also began remixing for major labels — Columbia, RCA, Atlantic — showing the growing demand for NYC club DJs as remix specialists even before “house” had a full commercial identity.

His KISS FM 98.7 mix show ran alongside the Zanzibar residency. A 1983 Dutch compilation (98.7 Kiss FM Mastermixes Vol. II, Rams Horn Records) documents his radio presence; the show was a primary channel for breaking records across the New York market.

Strictly Rhythm and the House Era (1993–1996)

The early 1990s brought Humphries firmly into association with Strictly Rhythm, New York’s dominant house label. He compiled and mixed:

  • Strictly Rhythm Mix (1993, Natural Records) — his first named DJ-mix album for a house label; pressed 6 times in the Netherlands.
  • Ministry Of Sound: The Sessions Volume One (1993, Ministry Of Sound) — the inaugural installment of Ministry’s Sessions series, one of the most successful DJ-mix album franchises of the 1990s. This appearance helped establish Humphries’ international profile.
  • The Tony Humphries Strictly Rhythm Mix Vol. 2 (1994, Strictly Rhythm) — proper in-house Strictly release.
  • Club Nervous: First Five Years of House Classics (1996, Nervous Records) — curated retrospective of Nervous’ catalog, positioning him as a house scene authority.

He also wrote liner notes for Ten Years Of Strictly Rhythm (1999, Ministry of Sound/Strictly Rhythm), confirming his status as a scene historian as well as participant.

Yellorange and Label Years (1997–2008)

From the late 1990s onward Humphries shifted significantly toward executive producer and label operator roles via Yellorange (label ID: 721, parent: TR Records (4)). The Yellorange catalog between 1998 and 2003 is dense: he holds executive producer credits on at least 25+ releases including:

  • Circles (1998) — 6 pressings
  • Can’t Get Enough (1998) — 3 pressings
  • Freedom (1998)
  • Rescue Me (1999)
  • Can’t Give Up (2001) — 4 pressings
  • Ups And Downs (2002) — 4 pressings

These are predominantly house vocal tracks in the “soulful house” mode — gospel-influenced vocals over driving percussion, in the tradition he had helped build at Zanzibar. He also co-produced “Just Believe” (1993, Strictly Rhythm).

Alongside Yellorange work he continued DJ-mix projects:

  • Mix The Vibe (1996, BPM King Street Sounds/S3) — 5 pressings
  • Take Home The Club (1997, Bassline Records) — 3 pressings
  • Sessions Twelve (The Magic Sessions) (2001, Defected/Ministry Of Sound) — 2 pressings

In 2003 he compiled Azuli Presents Tony Humphries: Choice — A Collection Of Club Zanzibar Classics (Azuli Records, 3 pressings), with liner notes co-written by Bill Brewster, functioning as a definitive retrospective of the Zanzibar era. Humphries also wrote the liner note text.

The Fabric 04 (2002, Fabric) DJ mix for London’s Fabric club reached a wide international audience, his highest-profile single-DJ mix release in this period.

From 2007–2008 he launched Tony Records with releases including Sunday Morning and Trouble In Paradise, and continued with TR Records (4) into the 2010s.

Later Work (2013–present)

TR Records (4) releases continued through 2015 (Golden Lady, Sick, Ain’t Got Time, Closer), with Humphries credited as A&R and executive producer. He appeared on a Running Back mastermix release in 2017 (Germany), and remix credits on Master Mixes for Unidisc (2022, Canada) and “Last Night A DJ Saved My Life” reissues (2023) confirm ongoing presence.
