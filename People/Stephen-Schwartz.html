Stephen Schwartz

Discogs ID: 352050 Real name: Stephen Lawrence Schwartz Role: Composer, Lyricist Nationality: American Born: 6 March 1948, New York City, New York, USA Active: 1969 – present Associated acts / projects: The Pipe Dream, Alan Menken, John-Michael Tebelak, Leonard Bernstein Top labels: Walt Disney Records, Bell Records, Decca Broadway

Biography

Early Career and Off-Broadway (1969–1971)

Schwartz began in New York as a producer and arranger in the late 1960s, working for RCA on recordings including Butterflies Are Free (1969) and several single-artist projects. His first major creative work arrived with Godspell, a musical adaptation of the Gospel of Matthew devised by director John-Michael Tebelak, for which Schwartz wrote the music and new lyrics. The Off-Broadway cast recording (Bell Records, 1971, cat. SBLL 146) established him immediately; the show became a long-running phenomenon and spawned numerous international productions with cast recordings in German, French, Danish, Dutch, Italian, Spanish, and Australian editions through the 1970s. He served as music director and producer on the original album.

During the same period, Schwartz contributed additional texts to Leonard Bernstein’s Mass (CBS, 1971), one of his few collaborations as a lyricist on a work he did not originate.

Broadway Peak (1972–1978)

Pippin (1972), with book by Roger O. Hirson and direction by Bob Fosse, was Schwartz’s first full Broadway musical. The cast album was released on Motown (cat. M760L) — an unusual pairing for a Broadway show — and gave Schwartz his first song to cross into pop territory (“Corner of the Sky”). Godspell was simultaneously adapted into a feature film (1973) with Schwartz playing keyboards and producing the soundtrack on Bell Records.

The Magic Show (1974, Bell Records, cat. 9003) was a vehicle built around magician Doug Henning; commercially successful on Broadway but less critically noted. The Baker’s Wife (1976, Take Home Tunes, cat. THT-772/773) was a troubled tryout that never reached New York but became a cult item among theatre fans and received a studio cast recording years later on TER (1990). Working (1978, Columbia Masterworks, cat. JS 35411) was an ambitious adaptation of Studs Terkel’s oral history, which Schwartz adapted, directed, and produced, playing keyboards on the cast recording. It ran only briefly on Broadway.

Mid-Period and Children of Eden (1980s–1991)

The 1980s were a relatively fallow period for Schwartz on Broadway. He contributed lyrics to Rags (1991, Sony Masterworks), and wrote Children of Eden — a large-scale musical based on the Book of Genesis — which had a workshop production at the Paper Mill Playhouse in 1987 and a full London production in 1991. The London cast recording (London Records, cat. 828 234-2) is the primary available recorded document of that show.

Disney Collaborations (1995–2007)

Schwartz’s Disney work began with Pocahontas (1995), partnering with composer Alan Menken to write all songs for the animated feature. The soundtrack (Walt Disney Records, cat. WDR 36028-2) credits Schwartz on lyrics and production of the songs, with Chris Montan as executive producer. The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1996, Walt Disney Records, cat. 60893-7) continued the Menken partnership, with Schwartz adapting the Latin lyrics and writing the song lyrics. Both films were central entries in the late phase of the Disney Renaissance.

The Prince of Egypt (1998, DreamWorks Records, cat. DRMD-50041) was a non-Disney animated feature produced by DreamWorks; Schwartz wrote the songs (music and lyrics both, including “When You Believe” which became a hit for Mariah Carey and Whitney Houston) while Hans Zimmer handled the orchestral score.

Enchanted (2007, Walt Disney Records, cat. D000092502) saw Schwartz writing songs for a live-action/animated Disney hybrid — his last major Disney film to date. The sequel Disenchanted (2022, Walt Disney Records) also carried song credits by Schwartz.

Geppetto (2000, Walt Disney Records, cat. 60679-7) was a Disney Channel TV film for which Schwartz wrote music, lyrics, and served as album producer.

Wicked and Stage Legacy (2003–present)

Wicked (2003, Decca Broadway, cat. B0001682-02) became Schwartz’s greatest commercial success. Based on Gregory Maguire’s novel with a book by Winnie Holzman, it has run continuously on Broadway and in the West End since opening, generating dozens of cast recordings in multiple languages (German, Japanese, and others). Schwartz produced the original Broadway cast album. International editions include a German cast (Polydor, 2007) and Japanese Shiki Theatre Company cast (Decca, 2007).

The 2024 film adaptation of Wicked (Universal/Republic Records) generated two new commercial releases: Wicked (The Soundtrack) (cat. 602475112181) and a separate orchestral score, Wicked (Original Motion Picture Score) (Verve Records, cat. 602475466468), on which Schwartz is credited as composer and producer. The film’s Part Two, Wicked: For Good (2025), produced further soundtrack and score releases on Republic Records.

Schwartz also released Uncharted Territory (2002, Fynsworth Alley, cat. 302 062 119 2), an album of his own songs in a singer-songwriter context, and The Stephen Schwartz Album (1999, Varèse Sarabande) — a survey of his work.

Scene Connections

  • Alan Menken — primary Disney collaborator; composed music for Pocahontas and The Hunchback of Notre Dame while Schwartz wrote lyrics
  • Howard Ashman — preceded Schwartz as Menken’s lyricist on the Disney Renaissance; established the model Schwartz inherited
  • John-Michael Tebelak — conceived and directed Godspell; Schwartz supplied music and new lyrics to Tebelak’s script
  • Leonard Bernstein — Schwartz contributed additional texts to Bernstein’s Mass (1971)
  • Danny Troob — recurring orchestrator across Broadway and film work
  • Michael Starobin — orchestrator; credited on Wicked OBC and related recordings
  • Bruce Botnick — Disney sound engineer / mastering; present across all major Disney soundtrack albums
  • Chris Montan — Walt Disney Records executive producer; key production figure on all Disney features
  • Peggy Gordon — original Godspell cast, co-writer of “By My Side”
  • Robin Lamont — original Godspell cast, performed “Day By Day”
  • Walt Disney Records — primary label for Disney film soundtracks (Discogs ID: 30630)
  • Bell Records — released Godspell and the original cast recordings of The Magic Show and the Godspell film (Discogs ID: see vault)
  • Motown — released Pippin OBC (1972), unusual venue for a Broadway show
  • Decca Broadway — released Wicked OBC (2003)
  • TER — released London cast recordings including Godspell (1993) and The Baker’s Wife (1990)
  • The Pipe Dream — early group affiliation listed on Discogs (artist ID: 1723372)
