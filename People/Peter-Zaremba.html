Peter Zaremba

Discogs ID: 445036
Real name: Peter Michael Zaremba
Role: Vocalist, keyboardist, harmonica, designer, producer
Known for: Frontman and co-founder of The Fleshtones; side project Peter Zaremba’s Love Delegation; cover designer across multiple releases

Biography

Formation of The Fleshtones (1976–1979)

Peter Zaremba co-founded The Fleshtones in Queens, New York in 1976, alongside guitarist Keith Streng, bassist Jan Marek Pakulski, and drummer Bill Milhizer. The band’s aim was to revive the stripped-down energy of 1950s and early ’60s rock and roll, filtered through the fuzz guitar and Farfisa organ aesthetics of mid-’60s garage — a sound they eventually christened “super rock.” They quickly embedded themselves in the New York punk and new wave scene, playing CBGB and developing the high-energy live reputation that would define the group for decades. Saxophonist Gordon Spaeth became a frequent live and studio presence, though never a formal member.

The band’s first recording, the single “American Beat” b/w “Critical List,” appeared on Red Star Records in 1979 (Discogs release 1904214). Zaremba designed the sleeve. The label was run by Marty Thau, former New York Dolls manager, who had already established Red Star with Suicide’s debut. The “American Beat” single brought the Fleshtones wider attention in the nascent NY underground.

Red Star and Early I.R.S. Years (1980–1983)

In 1980 Zaremba designed the cover art for Marty Thau’s landmark NYC new wave compilation Marty Thau Presents 2x5 (Red Star RED 100; Discogs release 1093880). The Fleshtones contributed two tracks: “Shadow-Line” and “F-F-Fascination,” both co-written by Zaremba and Streng. Other contributors to 2x5 included Student Teachers, Revelons, Bloodless Pharaohs, and The Miamis (2); producer of the compilation was Jimmy Destri (Blondie). Zaremba’s cover art credit sits alongside engineers Cliff Hodsdon and Jim Bonnefond.

The band signed to I.R.S. Records, releasing the Up-Front EP in 1980 (Discogs release 1375988). Zaremba handled jacket design alongside art direction from Carl Grasso; producer was Paul Wexler. The full lineup on that record — Zaremba, Streng, Pakulski, Milhizer — is documented in the release credits. Roman Gods followed in 1981 (Discogs release 1138896), produced by Richard Mazda, with Zaremba credited for Vocals, Jacket Design, and Artwork By. That album is notable for including Gordon Spaeth on alto saxophone and harmonica, and John Weiss on tenor saxophone, alongside the core lineup.

Meanwhile, sessions recorded for Red Star in 1978 were finally released in 1982 by ROIR as Blast Off! (Discogs release 445194), produced by Marty Thau with engineering by Larry Alexander. Zaremba contributed vocals, harmonica, organ, and percussion, and provided the cover art concept. The album also documented a slightly different lineup, with Lenny Calderone II on drums rather than Milhizer.

Also in 1982, the “Shadow-Line” single appeared in the UK and Europe on I.R.S. (Discogs release 853477). The Hexbreaker! album followed in 1983 (Discogs release 372084), produced again by Richard Mazda. Zaremba’s artwork contribution is credited alongside Mazda, Carl Grasso, and photographer George DuBose. His performance credit on that album is listed as “Vocals [Uncredited], Maracas” — the group’s whimsical approach to their own credits being something of a house style. Hexbreaker! is widely considered the Fleshtones’ finest studio album, expanding the group’s sonic palette with horns and a wider pop sensibility.

Mid-1980s: Love Delegation and Moving Target (1984–1989)

During the mid-’80s, when recording opportunities for the Fleshtones proper were limited, Zaremba launched Peter Zaremba’s Love Delegation (Discogs ID 766363), a side project with a “garage disco” bent. The group orbited the NYC Pyramid Club/Lower East Side scene and featured vocalist Wendy Wild, drummer/percussionist Ricky Rothchild, and collaborator Robert Burke Warren (who later joined the Fleshtones). Love Delegation also appeared under the name-variations “Love Delegation” and “The L.D.’s.”

The project released two singles on Moving Target (2) in 1986: I’m Gonna Knock You Out! (Discogs release 1510766) and Spread The Word (Discogs release 2497179). Both were co-produced by Zaremba and James A. Ball. I’m Gonna Knock You Out! featured editing by The Latin Rascals and additional production by Stephane Gerbier; Zaremba designed the cover. Spread The Word is the more ambitious record: it brought in jazz saxophonists Hamiet Bluiett (baritone sax) and Tony Orbach (tenor), Barrence Whitfield as guest vocalist, and additional handclap/percussion vocals from Dave Faulkner, Bill Milhizer, and Pat Dinizio. Zaremba’s cover concept for Spread The Word was realized by designer Mr. Falls; cover photography was by Marilla Palmer Zaremba.

The Love Delegation album Delegation-Time appeared in 1989 on the French label Accord (Discogs release 1846949), recorded in New York and produced by Zaremba. The album’s lineup includes Keith Streng (guitar/vocals), Robert Burke Warren (bass), Ricky Rothchild (drums/bongos), Bill Milhizer (hi-hat), Michael Ullmann, and Wendy Wild (vocals), with Urban Blight NYC on horns. George DuBose shot the front cover. Zaremba wrote most of the material and designed the jacket.

Also in 1986 the Fleshtones released Fleshtones Vs. Reality on the European label Emergo (Discogs release 1895188), on which Zaremba took full artwork/design credit and performed vocals, harmonica, piano, organ, and marimba.

A second Zaremba side project, Peter Zaremba’s Rock Delegation (Discogs ID 5910848), featured him backed by members of the Canadian garage band Crummy Stuff (2).

The Fleshtones also played a role in the 1991 “supergroup” The Action Dogs (Discogs ID 7707871), a 12-person collaboration with members of The Plimsouls, guitarist Danny B. Harvey, pianist Uncle John Herron, and producer Jeff Eyrich. The Fleshtones contingent in The Action Dogs included Zaremba, Streng, Milhizer, Pakulski, and Spaeth.

1990s: Powerstance, Beautiful Light, and Compilations

In 1985 Zaremba curated the Rhino Records compilation Pharaohization! (The Best of Sam the Sham & the Pharaohs) (Discogs release 3384133), taking Compiled By, Concept By, Liner Notes, and Sleeve Notes credits — signaling a deep engagement with ’60s garage/R&B precursors.

The 1991 Fleshtones album Powerstance! (Discogs release 1795416), released on Big Beat Records, saw Zaremba credited with vocals, harmonica, and — in keeping with Fleshtones self-mythologizing — “Acme Thunderer” (a brand of whistle). He contributed to the writing across the album.

Beautiful Light (1993, Naked Language Records; Discogs release 2077420) kept the core lineup of Zaremba, Streng, Milhizer, and the then-new bassist Ken Fox, who had replaced Pakulski by this point. The Angry Years 84-86 Spanish compilation (Imposible Records, 1993; Discogs release 2769094) documents the mid-period with Zaremba credited as Executive Producer, lead vocalist, and liner notes writer. The Forever Fleshtones French compilation (Danceteria, 1993; Discogs release 2077422) also carries Zaremba’s design credit.

In 1995, the Greek album Laboratory Of Sound (Hitch-Hyke Records; Discogs release 1261459) lists Zaremba as Artwork By and Photography By — an extension of his visual practice beyond pure cover design. The More Than Skin Deep album (Epitaph, 1997; Discogs release 1799706) keeps the stable Zaremba/Streng/Fox/Milhizer lineup.

2000s–2020s: Yep Roc Era

The Fleshtones signed to Yep Roc Records in 2003, beginning a long partnership. Do You Swing? (Discogs release 615613), produced by Zaremba, was recorded by Rick Miller and mixed by Miller and Mark Williams. The stabilized quartet of Zaremba, Streng, Fox, and Milhizer appears on all subsequent releases.

Beachhead (2005, Yep Roc; Discogs release 2754558) was produced in part by Jim Diamond (2), Detroit garage rock figure, with Zaremba credited for Concept, Art Direction, vocals, keyboards, harmonica, and songwriting. Take A Good Look! (2008; Discogs release 1887498) and Brooklyn Sound Solution (2011; Discogs release 2909131) continued the run.

Face Of The Screaming Werewolf (2020, Yep Roc; Discogs release 15932971) is notable for Zaremba’s expanded production role: he took Album Art Concept and Direction, photography, vocals, keyboards, and harmonica credits. The album also featured guest contributions from Michael Giblin (guitar, keyboards, chorus) and Sara Milonovich (violin). It’s Getting Late (…And More Songs About Werewolves) (2024, Yep Roc; Discogs release 31940023) carries a “Design [Floralist]” credit for Zaremba — his horticultural-themed contribution to sleeve design.

The 2017 album Budget Buster (Discogs release 11194613) added “Illustration” to Zaremba’s standard credits, showing ongoing expansion of his visual contributions.

Television and Media Work

Zaremba hosted the VHS compilation The Best Of The Cutting Edge Volume I & II (Rhino Video, 1989; Discogs release 17089986), a document of the MTV-era underground, and provided an introductory hosting credit on Norton Records’ Do You Feel It Baby? (1998; Discogs release 2510222).
