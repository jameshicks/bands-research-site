Jim Staley

Trombonist, improviser, producer, and new music performer. New York City-based. Discogs artist ID: 275461. Name variations: James Staley, James Stalley, J. Staley.

Overview

Jim Staley is one of the recurring trombone voices in the downtown NYC experimental scene from the early 1980s onward. He plays on recordings across a wide range — Rhys Chatham’s minimalist brass works, John Zorn’s game-piece and downtown sessions, Elliott Sharp’s Carbon ensemble, Fred Frith’s compositional work for dance and theatre — while also leading his own albums and running production work out of the Roulette Intermedium club and recording space in New York City.

His two main identities in the Discogs record: performer (trombone, bass trombone) on other people’s records, and producer-bandleader on a smaller set of his own releases, most notably the duo OTB (1984) with John Zorn and the solo LP Mumbo Jumbo (1987).

He is listed as a member of four groups: Tone Road Ramblers, Carbon (23) (Elliott Sharp’s large ensemble), Alchemical Theatre, and The Contemporary Chamber Players of the University of Illinois.

Discography and Key Credits

As Primary Artist

OTB (1984, Lumina Records Ltd.) — Discogs ID: 1258006 A duo record with John Zorn. Staley plays trombone and co-produces; Zorn plays alto saxophone, clarinet, and game calls on Side B. Engineered by David Weinstein; produced by Roma Baran and Staley. Cover and design by Laurie Szujewska. Pressed in the US. 63 pressings variants logged in Discogs — a heavily circulated item.

Mumbo Jumbo (1987, Rift) — Discogs ID: 1227841 Solo LP, produced by Staley. Four separate sessions with different players, each occupying one side (or partial side):

  • Sides A: Elliott Sharp (double-neck guitar, bass, soprano sax), Wayne Horvitz (piano, synth, drum machine). Engineered by Eric Liljestrand.
  • Sides B: Shelley Hirsch (vocals), Samm Bennett (drums, electronic percussion). Engineered by Don Hünerberg.
  • Sides C: Bill Frisell (guitar), Ikue Mori (drums, drum machine). Engineered by Don Hünerberg. Co-produced by Fred Frith.
  • Sides D: Fred Frith (electric and acoustic guitar, vocals), John Zorn (alto saxophone). Engineered by Don Hünerberg. Mastered by Jim Shelton.

Tone Road Ramblers (self-titled, 1985, self-released) — Discogs ID: 5496751 Group record; Staley is a member. The Tone Road Ramblers are associated with Morgan Powell (the group’s principal organizer, per the band’s URL). Other members: David Sasaki, Eric Mandat, Howie Smith, John Fonville, Michael Udow, Phoebe Legere, Ray Sasaki, Steve Butters.

Groups

Carbon (23) — Elliott Sharp’s large ensemble. Members include Bobby Previte, Zeena Parkins, Ikue Mori, Samm Bennett, David Weinstein, David Linton, Lesli Dalaba, and many others. Staley appears on Fractal (1986) and Larynx (1988), both on the Dossier and SST labels respectively.

Tone Road Ramblers — Ensemble organized around Morgan Powell. Discogs group ID: 3737716. Recorded one self-titled LP in 1985.

Alchemical Theatre — Discogs group ID: 16933243. No releases in Discogs.

The Contemporary Chamber Players of the University of Illinois — Discogs group ID: 1356683. Academic new-music ensemble.

Roulette Connection

Staley’s Discogs profile notes he was “very active as a producer, mainly involving recordings in New York City club The Roulette.” Roulette Intermedium (also Roulette Intermedium, Inc.) was and is the prominent NYC new music venue and presenter — originally on Spring Street in SoHo, later in Brooklyn. Staley’s production work there is not well documented in Discogs itself (the Roulette Intermedium label entries carry no releases), but his engineering and recording credits across the 1980s–2000s suggest sustained activity as a recording-side producer for the venue’s concert documentation.
