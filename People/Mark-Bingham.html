Mark Bingham

Discogs artist ID: 80474 Name variations: Bingham, M. B., M. Bingham, M.B., Mark B. Born: 1949, Bloomington, Indiana, USA

Overview

Mark Bingham is an American music producer, composer, musician, and recording engineer whose career spans over five decades and threads together two distinct geographical phases — the New York downtown avant-rock scene of the late 1970s and the New Orleans recording and music community from 1982 onward.

In Bloomington, Indiana, Bingham signed a publishing deal with Elektra Records in 1966, releasing one single on Warner Bros.–Seven Arts before returning to Indiana, attending Indiana University, and co-founding the avant-rock group The Screaming Gypsy Bandits. He also founded his own indie imprint, Bar-B-Q Records, through which he released Screaming Gypsy Bandits recordings and records by Caroline Peyton and MX-80 Sound.

In 1975 Bingham moved to New York City, where he formed Social Climbers with bassist-singer Jean Seton Shaw and keyboardist-composer Dick Connette (who also recorded as A. Leroy). The Social Climbers released one self-titled LP on Hoboken Records in 1981. During this New York period, Bingham also co-produced the 1979 The Static 7” Theoretical Record on Theoretical Records alongside conceptual artist Dan Graham, and produced landmark releases for MX-80 Sound and Glenn Branca — including Branca’s Lesson No. 1 (1980) on 99 Records. He also produced tracks on the Ralph Records compilation Subterranean Modern (1979) and was an associate producer on Hal Willner’s Stay Awake (A&M, 1988).

In 1982 Bingham relocated to New Orleans, where he established The Boiler Room recording studio and, in 2001 (with partners including ex-REM manager Jefferson Holt and engineer John Fishbach), founded Piety Street Recording at 728 Piety Street in the Bywater / 9th Ward. The studio — built around a 48-channel SSL 4064 G+ — hosted sessions by Dr. John, Ryan Adams, Dave Matthews Band, Elvis Costello & Allen Toussaint, Cassandra Wilson, John Scofield, Steve Earle, and many others, and was featured in the HBO series Treme. Bingham closed Piety Street in August 2013. Since 2014 he has worked at Low Heat Records / Nouveau Electric Records studio.

Bingham has released two solo albums under his own name: I Passed For Human (1989, Dog Gone Records, produced alongside Hal Willner) and Psalms Of Vengeance (2009). He is a long-standing member of Michot’s Melody Makers, the Cajun electric string band led by Louis Michot.

Bands / Groups

Social Climbers (Discogs ID: 336482)

New York City new wave band, active late 1979–c.1982. Formed by Bingham, Jean Seton Shaw (bass, vocals), and Dick Connette (organ [Farfisa], drum machine [Korg], also credited as A. Leroy). Played early shows at the Mudd Club. Their sole album was recorded at Celebration studio (NYC) between 1980 and 1981 and released in July 1981 on Hoboken Records (HOBO 101). Writing credits on the album are shared between Bingham, Shaw, and Connette (credited as Leroy). Genres: Minimal, New Wave, Synth-pop.

The Screaming Gypsy Bandits (Discogs ID: 896646)

1970s avant-rock group from Bloomington, Indiana. Bingham joined after returning from his Elektra stint in Los Angeles. The band released two records on Bar-B-Q Records: Everything Is Changed (7”, 1970, Vision (21)) and In The Eye (LP, 1973, BRBQ 3). Name variations: “Screamin’ Gypsy Bandits”, “Screaming Gypsy Bandits.”
