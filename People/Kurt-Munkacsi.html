Kurt Munkacsi

American sound designer, recording engineer, and producer. Discogs artist ID: 224855. Active NYC from 1970 onward. Co-founder of Orange Mountain Music. Long-term production partner of Philip Glass; also a key figure in the early No Wave scene through his work on No New York (1978).

Overview

Kurt Munkacsi joined the The Philip Glass Ensemble in autumn 1970 after being introduced to Glass by Jon Gibson (2). From that point he became Glass’s primary engineer, mixer, and co-producer across five decades — one of the longest-running artist/engineer partnerships in American music. He is listed as a member of the Philip Glass Ensemble on Discogs.

On the No Wave side, Munkacsi engineered the landmark No New York compilation (Antilles, 1978), produced by Brian Eno, capturing all four bands — The Contortions, Teenage Jesus And The Jerks, Mars, and DNA (4) — in a single session. He is credited on that pressing as “Kurt Munkasci.” He subsequently worked on several projects adjacent to the No Wave scene: James Chance (ZE Records sessions, 1979–80), Love of Life Orchestra (Extended Niceties, 1980, with Peter Gordon and David Byrne), and Polyrock (co-produced with Philip Glass, RCA Victor, 1980).

Beyond Glass and No Wave, Munkacsi engineered records for Anthony Braxton, Don Cherry, Yoko Ono (Approximately Infinite Universe, Apple Records, 1973), and Lydia Lunch / Jody Harris via the Exposed (Virgin, 1979) sessions. He also co-produced the debut album of The Waitresses (Wasn’t Tomorrow Wonderful?, Polydor, 1982) with Chris Butler.

In 2002, Munkacsi co-founded Orange Mountain Music (Discogs label ID: 40760) as the primary Philip Glass catalog imprint, and continued as executive producer on the label through at least 2017.
