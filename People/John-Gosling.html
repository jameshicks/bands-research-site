Overview

John Gosling is a British industrial musician, DJ, and electronic producer whose career spans industrial/experimental performance in the early 1980s through to mainstream UK dance music in the 1990s–2000s and beyond. He came up in the orbit of the early-’80s UK industrial/occult underground — co-founding Zos Kia alongside Geoff Rushton (John Balance) and Min, working extensively with Psychic TV — before reinventing himself as Mekon, a big-beat and hip-hop influenced DJ/producer best known for his relationship with Wall Of Sound Records. His career is remarkable for bridging two almost incompatible worlds: the TOPY/industrial underground of the Throbbing Gristle generation and the mid-’90s UK indie dance boom.

Era 1: Industrial Underground & Zos Kia (1982–1985)

Gosling’s earliest recorded work was under the Zos Kia name, which he formed with Geoff Rushton (then John Balance) and a performer known as Min. This trio — with Peter Christopherson handling sound — performed concerts and recorded material in 1982–83 under both the Zos Kia and Coil names. When Balance and Christopherson departed to focus on Coil full-time in 1983, all subsequent Zos Kia recordings were primarily Gosling’s work alone.

The Transparent split release with Coil (Zos Kia / Coil split, Nekrophile Rekords, 1984) is the main document of the pre-split lineup, featuring Gosling (as Joan D’Arc), Balance, Min, and Christopherson. The material has been reissued multiple times — Cold Spring issued a version in 2017 (13 pressings across various editions).

Gosling appeared on Rape (All The Madmen Records, 1984), a release credited to Zos Kia, alongside Min. He also contributed Be Like Me (Temple Records, 1985) under the Lorec alias as both performer and producer.

Era 2: Psychic TV (1984–1997)

Gosling was a member of Psychic TV from at least 1984, credited across their touring and live output through 1997. His credited roles varied: tape operation, guitar, vibraphone, percussion, and drums.

The Southern Comfort session (1986) credits him with guitar, vibraphone, and tape alongside Alex Fergusson, Mouse (2), and Monte Cazazza. Those Who Do Not (1997) is one of the few late PTV releases to bring him back into the fold, alongside Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson and Alex Fergusson.

Era 3: Bassomatic & Early Dance Scene (1990–1991)

In 1990–91 Gosling was a member of Bassomatic, William Orbit’s groundbreaking UK rave/house band, alongside Sharon Musgrave, Laurie Mayer, Sindy Finn, and Inna One Step. Bassomatic’s debut single “In The Realm Of The Senses” (Guerilla, 1990) was the first release on Guerilla Records, and their “Fascinating Rhythm” (Virgin/Guerilla, VS 1274, 1990) crossed into the mainstream.

Gosling was also involved as a coordinator (alongside William Orbit and Disc O’Dell) on the Guerrilla Grooves Volume One compilation (Guerilla, 1991), a label showcase that demonstrates how central he was to the early Guerilla operation.

He also contributed breaks and cuts to Hypnotone’s Ai album (Creation Records, 1991) — a marker of his cross-pollination between the indie label world and the emerging electronic scene.

His Sugardog project was active during this period. All Inclusive (Guerilla / Tribal America, 1991) lists Gosling as co-writer alongside Jim Whelan, Tim Whelan (2), and Kruger. Sugardog’s members included Fritz Catlin, Gila Gröger, Jim Whelan, Tim Whelan (2), and Gosling.

Era 4: Wall Of Sound / Mekon (1994–2013)

The Mekon alias became Gosling’s primary vehicle from the mid-1990s onward, and his output as Mekon is his most commercially significant work. Wall Of Sound (ID: 341) was the home for essentially the entire Mekon discography — a British indie big-beat/electronic label also home to Propellerheads, Bentley Rhythm Ace, and Wiseguys.

The Welcome To Tackletown (1996) credits reveal Mekon as a loose collective at this stage: “Mekon Was” credited to Sugar J (Gosling’s own alias), Evil B, Jah Norris, Dan Peppe, Chris Blackman, Robert Hill (2), and Paul Wainwright. Gosling produced and mixed alongside Robert Hill (2).

The high-profile Marc Almond collaboration “Please Stay” (2000) was a significant mainstream crossover moment. The Afrika Bambaataa collaboration D-Funktional (2003) brought him into contact with hip-hop royalty.

Late Mekon releases were produced in close collaboration with Fritz Catlin and Emre Ramazanoglu. Piece Of Work (2013, The End Records) explicitly credits “Mekon Is: John Gosling” and lists Raymond Watts (PIG) as co-producer on several tracks, Fritz Catlin for additional production, and Emre Ramazanoglu for additional production and mix.

Agent Provocateur (1995–1997)

Agent Provocateur was a band in which Gosling participated alongside Danny Saber, Matthew Ashman, Dan Peppe, and Cleo Mathews. They released:

  • Red Tape (Wall Of Sound, 1995) — Gosling produced and co-wrote
  • Where The Wild Things Are (Wall Of Sound / Epic, 1997) — full LP; Gosling produced all tracks, played keyboards and breaks, scratched on several

The Where The Wild Things Are sessions also featured Ged Lynch (drums), Cleo Torres (vocals), and engineers John X, Robert Hill (2), Russell Kearney.

Jam Nation (1993)

Jam Nation was a project initiated by Mark Rutherford and Gosling (as Sugar J) bringing together musicians from around the world. They released an album on Real World Records in 1993. Rutherford also plays guitar on the Mekon single “Revenge Of The Mekon (Remix)” (1995), suggesting an ongoing working relationship.
