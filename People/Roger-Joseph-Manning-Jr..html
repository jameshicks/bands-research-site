Roger Joseph Manning Jr.

American keyboardist, vocalist, songwriter, arranger, and producer. Born 27 May 1966, Los Angeles. Discogs artist ID: 8591.

One of Los Angeles’s most prolific session keyboard players since the late 1980s. Co-founding member of Jellyfish (2), Imperial Drag, and The Moog Cookbook. Long-running core member of Beck’s recording band. More recently a member of The Lickerish Quartet. His keyboard arsenal spans piano, harpsichord, Wurlitzer, Rhodes, Hammond B3, Mellotron, Moog synthesizer, clavinet, and celesta — instruments that define his period-accurate, studio-craft approach to arrangement and performance.

He uses his full name professionally to distinguish himself from the folk musician Roger Manning.

Aliases: Malibu, Uli Nomi (used on Moog Cookbook releases).

Era 1: Beatnik Beatch and Early LA Scene (1987–1989)

Manning’s first recorded work came with Beatnik Beatch (Discogs ID: 2134791), a Los Angeles pop-rock band. Their self-titled debut (1988, Atlantic Records) features him on keyboards. The band was a rehearsal room for the melodic sensibility and studio ambition he would fully develop with Jellyfish.

Era 2: Jellyfish (1990–1994)

Manning co-founded Jellyfish (2) (Discogs ID: 317001) alongside vocalist/drummer Andy Sturmer, guitarist Jason Falkner, bassist Chris Manning (his brother), and briefly Tim Smith (5) and Eric Dover.

Jellyfish were a Los Angeles-based band whose sound drew equally on late-1960s British pop (The Beatles, The Beach Boys, Badfinger), early Queen, and glam-inflected American rock. Manning contributed piano, harpsichord, keyboards, vocals, and co-writing on both albums. He also co-produced the second album.

Bellybutton was produced by Jack Joseph Puig and Albhy Galuten. Spilt Milk (1993) is widely regarded as one of the decade’s most elaborately constructed pop albums; Manning co-produced and is credited as primary keyboards/vocals/writer. The band dissolved in 1994 after touring Spilt Milk; Jason Falkner departed before the second album was completed, and Eric Dover filled in on guitar.

Jellyfish reissue and archival work continued through Omnivore Recordings: Live At Bogart’s (2012), Stack-A-Tracks (2012, instrumental versions produced by Manning), and Radio Jellyfish (2013, a radio broadcast from the Bellybutton era).

Era 3: Imperial Drag and The Moog Cookbook (1996–1997)

After Jellyfish, Manning co-founded two projects with recurring bandmate Eric Dover.

Imperial Drag (Discogs ID: 352438) — a glam-pop quartet with Dover on lead vocals/guitar, Manning on keyboards/vocals, Eric Skodis on drums, and Joseph Karnes on bass. Their self-titled Columbia/Work Records debut (1996) was co-produced by Manning and Brad Jones, and mixed by Manning and Jones. The record has a highly polished British glam-pop sound. One single, Boy Or A Girl, charted in the UK.

The Moog Cookbook (Discogs ID: 45679) — a comedic side project with keyboardist Brian Kehew, recording rock radio hits entirely on vintage synthesizers. Manning is credited as “Supervised By” under his alias Uli Nomi. Two albums: The Moog Cookbook (1996, Restless Records) and Plays The Classic Rock Hits (1997, Restless Records).

Era 4: Beck’s Band and Session Peak (1998–2006)

By the late 1990s Manning had joined Beck’s core recording band alongside Justin Meldal-Johnsen (bass), Joey Waronker (drums), and Smokey Hormel (guitar). This quartet (sometimes credited as “The Players”) appears on Beck’s most celebrated records of the era.

The Blink-182 connection is notable: Manning appears on Enema Of The State (1999), Take Off Your Pants And Jacket (2001), and the self-titled (2003), as well as the Box Car Racer side project. He also appears on the 2016 Blink-182 career box set crediting his work across all four records.

The AIR session (tracks A3, A5, B4 of 10 000 Hz Legend) was produced by Tony Hoffer and found Manning working alongside Justin Meldal-Johnsen (bass) and Brian Reitzell (drums) — the same rhythm section he’d been using with Beck.

The Johnny Cash credit on American IV: The Man Comes Around (Rick Rubin, American Recordings/Lost Highway) is a featured appearance on three tracks.

Logan’s Sanctuary (2000) is a joint record with Brian Reitzell (known for scoring Sofia Coppola films), engineered and mixed by Tony Hoffer. Roman Coppola contributed photography.

The Land Of Pure Imagination (2006, Cordless Recordings) draws its title from the Willy Wonka song — it’s a piano-led pop record with trumpet added by Mike McGuffey on one track and engineering/mastering by John Paterno.
