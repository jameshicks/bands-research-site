Stephen Hague

Producer, songwriter, keyboardist. Born 1954/55 in Portland, Maine, USA. Discogs ID: 23029.

Overview

Stephen Hague is an American record producer who became one of the defining forces in British synth-pop and electronic pop during the 1980s and 1990s. Starting out as a session keyboardist and member of New York pop-rock band Jules And The Polar Bears, he transitioned to production in the early 1980s, initially working with US acts before crossing over to the UK scene. His career-making credits came in quick succession: he produced Orchestral Manoeuvres In The Dark’s Crush (1985), co-produced and co-wrote “West End Girls” on Pet Shop Boys’ debut Please (1986), co-produced and co-wrote “True Faith” for New Order (1987), and produced Erasure’s breakthrough LP The Innocents (1988). Through the early 1990s he remained central to the New Order camp, producing and co-writing on Republic (1993), and continued working with Pet Shop Boys across multiple albums and singles. He also worked extensively with Dubstar, James, Siouxsie And The Banshees, Pere Ubu, Public Image Ltd, The House Of Love, and Jimmy Somerville. His name-variations on Discogs are numerous (Steve Hague, Steven Hague, Stephan Hague, S. Hague, etc.) reflecting widespread international licensing of his productions. He also played accordion, keyboards, guitar, bass, and programming on many of his production credits — a working musician as much as a studio architect.

He had no solo releases under his own name on Discogs; his entire footprint is as a credited contributor.

Early Career (1976–1984)

Hague appears in Discogs credits as early as 1976, playing keyboards and synthesizer for Jules And The Polar Bears (with bandleader Jules Shear). The group released albums on Columbia including Got No Breeding (1978, Columbia), on which Hague is credited as engineer, keyboards, mixed by, and producer — indicating a hands-on role from early on.

By the early 1980s he was producing independent and art-rock US acts (Slow Children on Ensign, Posh Boy Records bands) and doing synthesizer programming for established artists including Gordon Lightfoot (Dream Street Rose, Warner Bros., 1980) and Dolly Parton (Dolly, Dolly, Dolly, RCA, 1980).

His first major breakthrough into UK pop came via Malcolm McLaren: Hague co-produced the Fans LP (1984, Charisma/Virgin), which included the operatic-meets-hip-hop experiments that prefigured UK dance-pop crossovers. On that same label he produced several Malcolm McLaren singles including “(Hey You) The Rock Steady Crew” (1983), “Madam Butterfly” (1984), “Ready For Battle” (1984), “She’s Fresh” (1984), and “Uprock” (1984), also taking writing credits on several of them. He co-wrote and produced “We Love You” (1986, Virgin) for Malcolm McLaren.

The Synth-Pop Peak (1985–1989)

Orchestral Manoeuvres In The Dark

Hague produced Orchestral Manoeuvres In The Dark’s Crush (1985, Virgin), their US breakthrough LP. He returned for The Pacific Age (1986, A&M), where he also played keyboards and additional guitar alongside engineer Tom Lord-Alge who mixed the record.

Pet Shop Boys

Hague was the producer of Pet Shop Boys’ debut album Please (1986, EMI America). The album is credited to engineer David Jacob and designer Mark Farrow, with Hague as sole producer. On it was “West End Girls” (re-recorded from the Bobby Orlando original), for which Hague is credited as Producer and co-Written-By on the associated single (master 32200). He also produced the Please single “Love Comes Quickly” (1986) and “Suburbia.” His writing credit on “West End Girls” is confirmed across Discogs entries. He continued with the PSB world sporadically — additional production on It’s A Sin (1987), Very (1993), and many associated singles and compilations.

New Order / True Faith

In 1987, Hague co-produced and co-wrote “True Faith” with New Order, released on Factory Records (cat. FAC 183). The track appeared on the Substance compilation (1987), where Hague is credited as Producer and Written-By for track D3 (True Faith). It became one of New Order’s defining singles, reaching no. 4 in the UK.

He also contributed an additional production co-credit to the “Round & Round” single (1989, Factory) and the “World In Motion…” track (1990, Qwest/Warner Bros.).

Erasure

Hague produced Erasure’s third studio album The Innocents (1988, Mute), handling tracks 1 through 11, with Erasure self-producing the remaining two. The album contained “A Little Respect,” “Chains Of Love,” and “Ship Of Fools” — all Hague productions — and reached no. 1 in the UK. Writing is credited to Clarke/Bell throughout. He also produced “Chains Of Love” and “A Little Respect” singles.

Public Image Ltd

Hague co-produced five tracks on Public Image Ltd’s 9 (1989, Virgin), also taking a writing credit on one track and mixing several. Other producers on the album were Eric Thorngren and PIL themselves.

Pere Ubu

Hague produced seven of the fourteen tracks on Pere Ubu’s Cloudland (1989, Fontana), playing keyboards on the LP. Other tracks were produced by Paul Hamann, Dave Meegan, Rico Conning, and Daniel Miller (Mute).

Jimmy Somerville / Bronski Beat / The Communards

Hague produced several tracks on Jimmy Somerville’s debut solo album Read My Lips (1989, FFRR/London), alongside producers Pascal Gabriel, Jess-E, and Avril Mackintosh.

The 1990s

New Order — Republic

Hague co-produced and co-wrote the entire New Order album Republic (1993, London Records), one of the band’s most commercially successful records. He is credited as Producer and Written-By alongside the band members Bernard Sumner, Gillian Gilbert, Peter Hook, and Stephen Morris. Engineers on the album included Mike “Spike” Drake, Owen Morris, Richard Chappell, and Simon Gogerly. Additional musicians included Andy Duncan, Audrey Riley, David Rhodes, and Dee Lewis.

Siouxsie And The Banshees

Hague produced Siouxsie And The Banshees’ Superstition (1991, Polydor/Wonderland), with engineer Mike “Spike” Drake and assistant engineers including a young Nigel Godrich. Percussion by Talvin Singh. Other singles from the Banshees in this period he produced include “Kiss Them For Me” (1991) and “Fear (Of The Unknown)” (1991).

Dubstar

Hague produced and mixed Dubstar’s debut album Disgraceful (1995, Food/EMI), working alongside engineer Sam Hardaker and co-producer Graeme Robinson. He mixed several tracks with Mike “Spike” Drake. He continued with Dubstar on Goodbye (1997, Food/EMI) and associated singles.

The House Of Love

Hague was one of several co-producers (with Dave Meegan, Paul Staveley O’Duffy, and Tim Palmer) on The House Of Love’s self-titled LP (1990, Fontana/PolyGram). Executive producer was David Bates.

James — Whiplash

Hague produced James’ Whiplash (1997, Fontana), also playing additional keyboards and backing vocals and mixing the album alongside Mike “Spike” Drake. Brian Eno was credited as co-producer (“Frequent Interference And Occasional Co-production”). Engineer was Richard T. Norris.
