Robert Glasper

Discogs ID: 340568 Born: 6 April 1978, Houston, Texas, USA Active: 2000–present Genres: Jazz, Hip Hop, Neo Soul, R&B Labels: Blue Note (2005–2018), Loma Vista (2019–present), Columbia/Legacy

American jazz pianist, composer, arranger, and record producer. Central figure in 2010s neo-soul and jazz-hip-hop crossover; his Black Radio series (2012–2022) fused acoustic jazz technique with hip-hop beats and R&B vocal guests, winning multiple Grammy Awards. Long-standing Blue Note artist who has produced Miles Davis archival projects, scored films, and led several band configurations.

Biography

Early Life and Training

Robert Andre Glasper was born in Houston, Texas on 6 April 1978. He grew up in a musical household — his mother was a gospel singer. He studied at the High School for the Performing and Visual Arts in Houston (the same institution that produced Erykah Badu and Chris Dave), then moved to New York to study at the New School for Jazz and Contemporary Music. In New York he became embedded in the jazz session scene and developed relationships with artists that would define his career.

Early Sideman Work and Debut (2000–2006)

Glasper’s first recorded credit is the 2000 album Raw (Transparent Music), a sideman date. The following year he appeared on Bilal Oliver’s breakthrough debut 1st Born Second (2001, Interscope) — an early link in the Houston–New York neo-soul network. He landed a Blue Note deal quickly: Canvas (2005) was his debut as leader, a straight-ahead trio record with Vicente Archer on bass and Damion Reid on drums, produced by Eli Wolf. The album established the core piano-trio lineup that would underpin much of his acoustic work.

The Trio Years and Blue Note Deepening (2007–2009)

In My Element (2007, Blue Note) deepened the trio relationship with Archer and Reid, with Glasper also assuming co-producer credit alongside Wolf. Double Booked (2009, Blue Note) was a pivotal structural statement: the first half features the acoustic trio (Archer on double bass, Chris Dave on drums), while the second half introduces the electric band with Derrick Hodge on bass, Casey Benjamin on vocoder/sax, and Chris Dave — presaging the Experiment.

Robert Glasper Experiment and Black Radio (2011–2014)

The Robert Glasper Experiment — with Hodge, Chris Dave, and Benjamin — released Black Radio in 2012 on Blue Note (recorded 2011). The album was a genre landmark: jazz instruments, hip-hop drum programming, and vocal features from Erykah Badu, Lupe Fiasco, Mos Def (Yasiin Bey), Me’Shell NdegéOcello, Lalah Hathaway, Ledisi, and Bilal. Cover versions of Sade’s “Cherish The Day,” David Bowie’s “Letter to Hermione,” and Nirvana’s “Smells Like Teen Spirit” reframed the record as commentary on the shared emotional terrain of jazz and rock. It won the Grammy for Best R&B Album.

Black Radio 2 followed in 2013, extending the guest-feature format. The Black Radio Recovered (The Remix EP) (2012) collected remixes including Kaytranada. Mark Colenburg replaced Chris Dave on drums during this era for some configurations.

Covered and the Miles Davis Project (2015–2016)

Covered (2015, Blue Note) returned to the acoustic trio setting — recorded live at Capitol Studios with Damion Reid on drums and Vicente Archer on bass — covering songs associated with Radiohead, John Legend, Kendrick Lamar, and others, demonstrating Glasper’s belief that jazz improvisation could reshape any repertoire.

Everything’s Beautiful (2015, Columbia/Legacy) was a critically important production project: a full album built from Miles Davis archival recordings, with Glasper as producer alongside Miles Davis’s estate, credited as “Miles Davis & Robert Glasper.” Glasper looped, layered, and reimagined Miles fragments into contemporary beats, enlisting contributors including Erykah Badu, Bilal, and Stevie Wonder. It fit into the Miles Ahead film promotional cycle (2016), for which Glasper also produced soundtrack material.

Supergroups and Broadening Production (2017–2019)

Glasper became a node in several collective projects. R+R=NOW (2017) — with Terrace Martin, Christian Scott (2), Derrick Hodge, Taylor McFerrin, and Justin Tyson — released Collagically Speaking (2018, Blue Note), a live-documented jazz-funk collective responding to contemporary politics. August Greene (2018) brought together Glasper, Common (Lonnie Rashid Lynn), and Karriem Riggins, releasing a self-titled debut on their own August Greene LLC imprint — an album centered on Black consciousness and poetic text.

Fuck Yo Feelings (2019, Loma Vista) was a career summary / double LP collecting material with frequent collaborators including Denzel Curry, Herbie Hancock, James Poyser, Taylor McFerrin, and Terrace Martin.

Dinner Party and Black Radio III (2020–2022)

Dinner Party (2) — Glasper with Terrace Martin, Kamasi Washington, and 9th Wonder (Patrick Douthit) — released a self-titled EP in 2020 on Sounds of Crenshaw, a compact jazz-soul collaboration aligned with the wave of politically engaged Black music.

Black Radio III (2022, Loma Vista) concluded the trilogy as a solo Glasper release (not credited to the Experiment), again built around guest vocalists and producers, released on his new label home.

Recent Work (2023–2025)

In December (2024, Loma Vista) and Code Derivation (2024, Loma Vista) continued his prolific pace. Keys To The City Volume One (2025, Loma Vista) extended his catalog. He contributed liner notes to reissue projects and continues to appear as a sideman and producer on sessions ranging from Denzel Curry to jazz instrumentalists. He scored Barbershop: The Next Cut (2016) and contributed to the Yasuke soundtrack (2021, Warp).
