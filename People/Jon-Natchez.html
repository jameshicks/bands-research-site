Biography

Early Career: Ska and New York (1995–2005)

Natchez’s earliest Discogs-documented work is as a saxophonist and songwriter in the ska/third-wave scene. He played alto, baritone, and soprano saxophone on Skavoovie and the Epitones’ debut Fat Footin’ (Moon Ska, 1995), also taking a songwriter credit. He returned for the follow-up Ripe (Moon Ska, 1997) on alto and baritone saxophone and melodica. Skavoovie and the Epitones were a New Jersey-based ska band with a large rotating membership; Natchez was among the founding generation.

Further ska and punk-adjacent credits in the late 1990s–early 2000s show Natchez placing baritone saxophone on Playtime by an unnamed artist (Hellcat Records, 1999) and The Growler (Shanachie, 1999). By 2003 he was working in indie rock contexts, including After The Wrecking Ships (Ten Good Records, 2003) and Let’s Get Serious (Dim Mak, 2003) where he is listed under the “Hard-On Horns” credit.

By 2005 Natchez was embedded in the New York experimental/indie scene; he appeared on Quarter Century (2005, Not On Label) on saxophone and flute, and was listed as a guest performer on The Silent League’s Seaside Sessions Volume One (2005, self-released). Through this period he built the range of instruments and collaborators that would define his session work in the following decade.

Beirut: The Core Years (2006–2011)

The central association of Natchez’s career is with Beirut, Zach Condon’s project rooted in Balkan brass and chamber pop aesthetics. Natchez joined the expanded band lineup on the Lon Gisland EP (Ba Da Bing!, 2006), playing ukulele, baritone saxophone, clarinet, and glockenspiel alongside Kelly Pratt (trumpet, euphonium, flugelhorn), Perrin Cloutier (accordion, cello), Nick Petree (percussion), Paul Collins (2) (ukulele, percussion, organ), and Kristin Ferebee (violin). This lineup became the core touring and recording band.

On the UK pressing of the debut Gulag Orkestar (4AD / Ba Da Bing!, 2006), Natchez is credited with ukulele, baritone saxophone, and clarinet on the second disc (the Lon Gisland EP material, tracks 2-1 to 2-5); the original Ba Da Bing! LP pressing of Gulag Orkestar was recorded by Condon alone.

For The Flying Club Cup (4AD, 2007), Natchez contributed clarinet (tracks 8, 13) and flute (tracks 8, 13) — a more limited role than on the EP, as the album featured Owen Pallett as its primary outside collaborator for string arrangements. The French-themed second album was recorded in Albuquerque and Montreal.

On The Rip Tide (Pompeii Records (2), 2011) Natchez received a specific arranger credit: “Arranged By [Brass]” on track 1 alongside Ben Lanz, Kelly Pratt, and Zach Condon — indicating a compositional contribution to the album’s brass writing, not just performance. This was Natchez’s final studio album with Beirut.

Stars Like Fleas (2007–2010s)

Natchez was a member of Stars Like Fleas, a New York-based experimental ensemble with an unusually large and shifting membership. The group included Sam Amidon, Shannon Fields, Shelley Burgon, Ryan Sawyer, Matt Lavelle, Gerald Menke, Montgomery Knott, and Tianna Kennedy. Their work falls loosely within the avant-folk / chamber pop area that overlapped with Natchez’s Beirut period.

Stars Like Fleas released The Ken Burns Effect (Talitres Records, 2007) — on this album Natchez received one of the most expansive multi-instrument credits in his discography: accordion, banjo, bass, flute, glockenspiel, “coil-o-horn,” saxophone (baritone and tenor), synthesizer (analog), trumpet, ukulele, vocals, and penny whistle. The group also credited themselves collectively as “Trichordia” for the performance. A second album, First Light (BB*ISLAND / Boy Scout Recordings / b.y_records, 2008), featured Natchez on clarinet, bass clarinet, saxophone, trumpet, glockenspiel, and Wurlitzer electric piano.

The ensemble’s broad instrumentation and New York avant connections made them an ideal laboratory for Natchez’s multi-instrument range.

Bishop Allen (2007)

Natchez is listed as a Discogs member of Bishop Allen and contributed saxophone, clarinet, flute, and horns to Bishop Allen & The Broken String (Dead Oceans, 2007). Bishop Allen were a Brooklyn indie pop band led by Justin Rice and Christian Rudder.

Yellow Ostrich (c. 2009–2013)

Natchez is listed in the Discogs membership of Yellow Ostrich, a band led by Alex Schaaf and including Jared Van Fleet, Michael Tapper, and Zach Rose. Yellow Ostrich moved from lo-fi folk toward indie rock over the course of their run. Natchez contributed to their output but does not appear to have a prominent individual credit on their key Discogs-listed releases.

The War On Drugs (c. 2014–present)

The most commercially prominent phase of Natchez’s career is his membership in The War On Drugs, the Philadelphia-based rock band led by Adam Granduciel. Natchez joined the War on Drugs lineup alongside Dave Hartley (bass), Charlie Hall (drums), Anthony LaMarca (guitar/synth), and Robbie Bennett (keyboards).

He is credited on the War on Drugs’ live album Live Drugs (Super High Quality Records, 2020), where he is listed as a full performer with baritone saxophone, Juno 106 synthesizer, Vox Jaguar organ, and Hammond organ — indicating a multi-keyboard and woodwind role live. He contributed saxophone to I Don’t Live Here Anymore (Atlantic, 2021), the Grammy-winning studio album. The band won a Grammy Award in 2018 (for A Deeper Understanding, 2017), and Natchez’s participation is confirmed by Discogs credits on the 2017 single releases “Pain” and “Up All Night” (both Atlantic) where he plays baritone saxophone.

He also appeared on the Live Drugs Again (Super High Quality Records, 2024) live release.
