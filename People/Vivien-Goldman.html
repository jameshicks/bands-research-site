Vivien Goldman

Writer, broadcaster, musician, academic. Born Vivien Anne Goldman. British; based in New York and London. Discogs artist ID: 53036.

Overview

Goldman is one of the key journalists who documented the post-punk, reggae, and African popular music scenes from within, writing for NME and Sounds in the late 1970s and 1980s. Her position was never simply critical: she contributed vocal and songwriting work to several key post-punk and dub releases, operated as a producer on her own material, and embedded herself in the networks of the On-U Sound collective, the Flying Lizards circle, and New York’s No Wave fringe. Later she became a professor at NYU’s Clive Davis Institute, teaching punk, reggae, and African popular music — a curriculum that maps almost exactly onto her career.

Aliases / name variations used across releases: Viva Chantage, V. Goldman, Goldman, Vivien Gold, Viva, Vib G. Also credited under the group name Chantage (with Eve Blouin and Moona).

Musical Career

”Launderette” / “Dirty Washing” (1981)

Goldman’s principal recording as a primary artist. The 7” single “Launderette” b/w “Private Armies” was released in the UK on Window label (cat. WIN 1) — not Rough Trade or 99 Records. A US 12” EP titled Dirty Washing (cat. 99-05EP) appeared on 99 Records and adds the dub version “P.A. Dub” as a third track.

“Launderette” (A-side) was co-produced by John Lydon (credited as Lydon), Keith Levene (credited as Levene), and Goldman herself, and written by Goldman and George Oban (credited as George (Oban) Levi). Musicians on the A-side: George Oban (bass/percussion), Shooz (drums), Keith Levene (guitar/bass), Robert Wyatt (percussion), Steve Beresford (toy piano/bass), Vicky Aspinall (violin), Goldman (vocals). “Private Armies” (B-side) and its dub were produced by Adrian Sherwood, written by Goldman alone. Goldman also co-produces on the B-side with Levene and Beresford on bass, Aspinall on violin.

The 2016 compilation Resolutionary (Staubgold, staubgold 141) collected both tracks plus the dub variant, tracks from the Flying Lizards, and Chantage material. Goldman wrote the uncredited liner notes and appears in a bonus interview track.

New Age Steppers (1981)

Goldman contributed vocals to the debut self-titled LP by New Age Steppers (1981, On-U Sound, cat. ON-U-L.P. 1), produced by Adrian Sherwood. She sings on “Private Armies” (track B4), which also appears on her own Dirty Washing EP. Other vocalists on the LP included Ari-Up and Mark Stewart. Personnel included Bruce Smith, Viv Albertine, Steve Beresford, George Oban, Style Scott, and Vicky Aspinall.

Chantage (early 1980s)

Goldman was a member of Chantage alongside Eve Blouin and Moona. Chantage tracks “Same Thing Twice” and “It’s Only Money” appear on the 2016 Resolutionary compilation; “It’s Only Money” was produced by Sherwood, Alan Jakoby, and Carroll Thompson. The “Same Thing Twice” production credits go to Chantage and Chris Thomas. “Tu M’Fais Rire” (a brief track) also attributed to Chantage with Goldman credited as Viva. The group name is Discogs ID 674628. Goldman’s individual Discogs alias Viva Chantage (ID 5060384) appears as a distinct entry.

The 49 Americans (1979–1982)

Goldman was a member of The 49 Americans, a British DIY post-punk art-collective assembled by Andrew “Giblet” Brenner in London, active 1979–1982. The roster also included David Toop, Steve Beresford, Viv Albertine, Lol Coxhill, Max Eastley, Peter Cusack, and others from the London Musicians’ Collective. She is credited as Performer on E Pluribus Unum (1980, Choo Choo Train Records) and on the 2013 Staubgold reissues. She contributed backing vocals to We Know Nonsense (2002, Saidera Records, Japan).

The Funk Queens

Goldman was a member of The Funk Queens alongside Ann Boyle and Lisa Lee (and B-Side). Discogs ID 494539. No studio releases in the DB — a live/performance unit.

Flying Lizards — Songwriting Credits

Goldman co-wrote three tracks on the The Flying Lizards debut LP (The Flying Lizards, 1980, Virgin):

  • “Her Story” — co-written with David Cunningham, Deborah Evans-Stickland, and General Strike (pseudonym); performed by the Flying Lizards with David Toop (guitar, backing vocals), Steve Beresford (bass, organ), Dave Solomon (drums), and Goldman (voice/written-by)
  • “TV” — same co-writers
  • “The Window” — written by Goldman alone; musicians included George Oban (bass), Viv Albertine (guitar), Bruce Smith (hi-hat), David Cunningham (prepared piano), Steve Beresford (piano, percussion), Goldman (voice)

Goldman also contributed voice to the Flying Lizards / Fourth Wall reissue CD (2010, RPM retrodisc).

”Hey Hey” (2010)

Goldman co-wrote and sang on “Hey Hey” with Dennis Ferrer and Dominique Keegan, released on Objektivity (541 / N.E.W.S. label). Multiple pressings and remix 12”s through 2010, and a further remix EP in 2024 (Objektivity). This is one of her most widely distributed releases.

Next Is Now (2021)

Solo album Next Is Now (Youth Sounds), 10-track digital release. Goldman co-wrote and sang all tracks; Discogs credits her as primary artist with co-written-by credits on at least some pressings. Genres: Rock/New Wave/Punk.

Lizzy Mercier Descloux Reissues

The 16 shared Discogs credits between Goldman and Lizzy Mercier Descloux all derive from Goldman writing liner notes for the 2015–2016 Light In The Attic reissue campaign of Descloux’s catalog. The releases span multiple pressings:

  • Press Color (2015, Light In The Attic) — liner notes
  • Press Color Remixes & Edits (2015, Light In The Attic) — liner notes
  • Suspense (2016, Light In The Attic) — liner notes
  • One For The Soul (2016, Light In The Attic) — liner notes
  • Mambo Nassau (2016, Light In The Attic) — liner notes
  • Zulu Rock (2016, Light In The Attic) — liner notes

Goldman and Descloux moved in overlapping New York / post-punk circles (both associated with ZE Records territory and the early 1980s No Wave/new music scene); Goldman’s selection as annotator for the entire reissue campaign reflects that personal history.

Written-By Credits (Non-Performance)

  • Massive Attack — “Sly” (1994, Circa/Wild Bunch/Virgin) and “Protection” (1994/1995, Circa) — Written-By credit, appearing across all major pressings of Protection and the Sly single
  • Collected (2006, Virgin) — Written-By
  • City Beach (2007, Vinyl Films) — Written-By

The Massive Attack songwriting credits are particularly striking — Goldman has a co-written-by credit on both “Sly” (single) and “Protection” (title track), which appear across 10+ individual pressings/territories in the Discogs database.

Academic Work

Professor at NYU’s Clive Davis Institute of Recorded Music. Her curriculum covers punk, reggae, and African popular music — mirroring her own career research interests precisely.

August Darnell / Kid Creole Collaboration

Goldman wrote a book about Kid Creole And The Coconuts and co-wrote the book (with August Darnell) for the Off-Broadway musical Cherchez La Femme: A Musical Excuse, world premiere at La MaMa Ellen Stewart Theatre, New York, May 19 – June 12, 2016. Music by Stony Browder Jr. and Darnell; directed by Angie Kristic; choreography by Kyndra Reevey. Goldman also wrote liner notes for Going Places – The August Darnell Years 1974–1983 (2008, Strut).

Open Questions

  • The Massive Attack “Sly”/“Protection” written-by credits are well-documented in Discogs but the nature of Goldman’s contribution (lyrical, compositional?) is not specified in the DB — worth a web search.
  • Goldman’s “General Strike” pseudonym used in the Flying Lizards LP writing credits: the Discogs entry General Strike (ID 156118) is the Cunningham/Toop/Beresford trio — it appears Goldman used the same name as a songwriting credit alias but was not a member of that group.
  • The Window label (WIN 1, “Launderette” UK 7”) — Discogs top_labels lists “Window” for Goldman’s years_active. Needs its own label page; unclear if it’s a self-release or an imprint.
