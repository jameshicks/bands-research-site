Nick Jameson

Nick Jameson

Aug 16, 20267 min read

Nick Jameson

Discogs ID: 276802 Real name: Nicholas Andrew Jameson Aliases: Eli Jenkins, Franz Leipkin Name variations: Jameson, N. Jameson, Nick, Nick Jamison Active: 1970–2010s Roles: Bassist, multi-instrumentalist, engineer, producer, actor

American musician, engineer, and producer based in the Woodstock, NY scene in the early 1970s. Best known as a core figure in the Bearsville Records orbit — engineering the Paul Butterfield’s Better Days albums and producing Foghat’s best-selling records — and for a brief tenure as the fourth member of Foghat (1974–1976). Later pivoted substantially into acting.

Overview

Jameson came up as a performer and studio hand in the early Bearsville ecosystem. His first traceable credit is as guitarist and pianist on The American Dream’s 1970 self-titled debut on Ampex Records/Bearsville, produced by Todd Rundgren — placing him inside the label’s founding circle from the outset. By 1972 he was engineering sessions at Bearsville Sound studio, working on Todd Rundgren’s double-album Something/Anything? (two tracks, credited as “Engineer [Bearsville Sound]”) and on the debut LP by Hungry Chuck.

His most celebrated early engineering work came in 1973 on the two Paul Butterfield’s Better Days albums — Better Days and It All Comes Back — where he engineered both records and received a co-production credit on the second alongside Paul Butterfield and Geoff Muldaur. These albums are considered among the finest roots-rock recordings to emerge from the Woodstock scene.

From 1974 Jameson moved into a more active production and performance role with Foghat, eventually joining the band as bassist for the recording and touring cycle around Fool For The City (1975) before departing. He produced several of Foghat’s key mid-70s releases. His 1977 solo debut Already Free appeared on Bearsville and was largely self-performed and self-produced. A second solo album, A Crowd Of One, followed on Motown in 1986. From the late 1980s onward his music activity gave way increasingly to an acting career.

Bearsville Era (1970–1977)

The American Dream (1970)

Jameson played first guitar, piano, and vocals on the self-titled debut by The American Dream (Ampex Records / Bearsville), produced by Todd Rundgren. This places him at Bearsville from the label’s founding year.

Membership

On Fool For The City (Bearsville, 1975) Jameson is credited as a full band member, playing bass, keyboards, guitar, and vocals alongside Dave Peverett (lead vocals, guitar), Rod Price (guitar), and Roger Earl (drums). Tony Stevens, who had been the original Foghat bassist, was replaced by Jameson for this cycle. Discogs lists Jameson as a member of Foghat (artist ID: 265836).

Fool For The City is one of Foghat’s best-known albums, containing “Slow Ride,” which became the band’s biggest hit single. Jameson produced both the album and the single.

He also contributed percussion and production to Foghat Live (1977), one of the best-selling live albums of the era. By the time of Girls To Chat & Boys To Bounce (1981) he was back as outside producer/engineer rather than a touring member.

Already Free (Bearsville, 1977) is the most Bearsville-flavored of the solo records — largely Jameson playing everything himself, with Paul Butterfield contributing harmonica on one track. The album design was by Peter Corriston (who also designed Foghat’s Live) and mastering by Bob Ludwig.

A Crowd Of One (Motown, 1986) marks a stylistic departure toward polished mid-80s pop/rock. Session musicians include saxophonists Jon Marett, Kim Parks, and Tony Carere; oboist Sandy Arenz; drummer David Anderson.

Acting Career

Per the Discogs profile: Jameson has appeared in film (Road Trip, Helter Skelter) and television (Criminal Minds, Alias, The Practice, The West Wing, Days of Our Lives), and contributed voice work to Shark Tale, Star Wars: Clone Wars, and The Critic.
