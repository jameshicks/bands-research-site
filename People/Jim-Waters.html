Jim Waters

Discogs ID: 136241 Also credited as: J Waters, J. Waters, James “Jim” Waters, James J. Waters, Jimmy Waters, Waters URL: https://waterworksrecordingstudio.com/

Engineer, producer, and mixer. Owner-operator of Waterworks West (Tucson, AZ) and formerly of Waterworks (NYC). His work spans No Wave–adjacent NYC underground (late 1980s–early 1990s) through blues-rock, alt-country, and garage rock across three decades. Also a performer in Dairyland Jubilee and The Baby Flies.

Career Arc

New York City — Waterworks Studio (c. 1984–1993)

Waters ran Waterworks, a recording studio in New York City, from at least 1984. The studio served the downtown noise, no wave, and indie rock underground: early clients include Missing Foundation (1990), Action Swingers (1991, co-produced with the band), and Dim Stars (1991, recorded). He also did track-level engineering on Sonic Youth’s Goo (1990, Geffen) — uncredited on the initial pressing but confirmed in later archival releases.

By the early 1990s Waterworks was a go-to room for the NYC-adjacent indie sphere: Gumball (1990), the Gin Blossoms (early UK material, 1991), and the Posies’ Frosting On The Beater (1993, DGC) — a mainstream-leaning power-pop record on which Waters shared recording duties with Adam Kasper.

Waters also co-produced and engineered the debut LP by The Moles (1992, Ringers Lactate) — an Australian band recording in New York — as well as Coelacanth (1992), and handled photography on several early releases alongside his engineer credits.

Relocation to Tucson — Waterworks West (1993–present)

Waters moved the studio to Tucson, Arizona in 1993, renaming it Waterworks West (address: 1120 S. Highland, Tucson, AZ). The original NYC operation continued briefly under Greg Talenfeld until around 2000 before closing.

From Tucson, Waters built a long-running relationship with The Jon Spencer Blues Explosion and its extended orbit — engineering Extra Width (1993, Crypt), producing Orange (1994, Matador), co-engineering Now I Got Worry (1996, Matador/Capitol), and mixing R.L. Burnside’s A Ass Pocket Of Whiskey (1996, Matador) — a Burnside/JSBX collaboration album. He also produced JSBX’s 2Kindsa Love (1996, Mute) and Acme (1998, Mute), and mixed Acme (Sampler) (1998) and Extra Width / Mo’ Width (2000, Mute). The studio tag “Waterworks” appears explicitly on several of these Matador/Mute credits.

He became a primary engineer for Fat Possum Records acts through this period: R.L. Burnside’s A Ass Pocket Of Whiskey (mixed), Worried (1999, mixed), and the label’s Fat Possum: Not The Same Old Blues Crap compilation (produced). He mixed and produced Twenty Miles’ self-titled debut (1997, Fat Possum/Epitaph), a side project of Judah Bauer from JSBX.

In 1997 Waters recorded and mixed [[Bands/Jonathan FireEater]]‘s major-label debut Wolf Songs For Lambs (DreamWorks Records, 1997) and the UK companion release When The Curtain Calls For You (Deceptive, 1997). He also handled recording and mixing for a Jonathan FireEater track on the 2019 Third Man Records reissue Tremble Under Boom Lights.

Other notable 1990s work: The Chrome Cranks (1994, PCP Entertainment), Bipolar Cowboy (1995, Alias), and Unboxed (1995, Wiiija). He produced and recorded Quit While You’re Ahead (1994, Caroline Records) and the Stop Rock And Roll! compilation (1997, Estrus Records). Waters also worked extensively with French label Rosebud’s roster, recording and producing Noir Désir’s Grand Public (1996), In My Coffin (1996), La Piscine (1998), and Yeah! (1998).

2000s–2020s — Continued Studio Work

Waters continued mixing and recording into the 2000s and beyond, with clients spanning garage rock, psych, and Americana:

  • Black Sun Ensemble (remix, 2000, Camera Obscura)
  • No Other Love (2002, Blue Rose/New West) — co-producer and engineer at Waterworks West
  • Dead Man On Campus soundtrack (1998, DreamWorks) — recorded and mixed
  • Lenguas Largas (2011, Recess Records) — recorded and produced
  • The Resonars’ The Greatest Songs Of The Resonars (2013, Trouble In Mind) — mixed
  • The Butterscotch Cathedral (2015, Trouble In Mind) — mixed and produced
  • Masculin Féminin (2016, Numero Group) — recorded
  • Swing From The Sean DeLear (2021, In The Red) — recorded and mixed
  • Ball Of Confusion (2023, I-94 Recordings) — mixed and recorded
  • Various In The Red Recordings releases (2023–2024)
  • Heavy Steady Go! (2024, Sweet Gee Records) — engineered and mixed
