Damo Suzuki

Born Kenji Suzuki (鈴木健二, Suzuki Kenji), 16 January 1950, Kōchi, Japan. Died 9 February 2024, Cologne, Germany, age 74. Japanese vocalist best known as the lead singer of Can during their most celebrated period (1970–73), and subsequently as the driving force behind the open-ended improvisation network known as Damo Suzuki’s Network. Discogs artist ID: 110310.

Biography

Early Life and the Discovery at a Munich Café (1950–1970)

Kenji Suzuki grew up in Kōchi on the island of Shikoku, Japan. By the late 1960s he had relocated to Europe and was living a peripatetic existence, busking across the continent. In 1970, in Munich, he was discovered by Holger Czukay and Michael Karoli while busking outside a café. The two invited him to perform with Can that same evening; within days he had moved into their communal living and recording space in Schloss Nörvenich near Cologne.

Suzuki had no formal vocal training. His approach — a stream of syllables, invented language, keening melodic fragments, and extended phoneme-sequences — meshed naturally with Can’s group improvisation method, which the band called “instant composition”.

Can: The Damo Era (1970–1973)

Suzuki appeared on Can’s second album Soundtracks (1970), a collection of film cues. The proper studio albums that followed represent the peak of Can’s creative arc and are among the most influential records in post-rock, punk, and experimental music history:

  • Tago Mago (1971, United Artists Records) — double LP; side-long pieces “Halleluwah” and “Aumgn” define the group’s approach to trance, repetition, and free improvisation
  • Ege Bamyasi (1972, United Artists Records) — tighter and funkier; includes “Vitamin C” and “Spoon”, the latter a surprise German chart hit
  • Future Days (1973, United Artists Records) — more ambient, beatific; widely cited as a proto-ambient/post-rock touchstone; Suzuki’s final studio album with the band

The lineup on all three albums was Can as a collective: Holger Czukay (bass, engineering), Jaki Liebezeit (drums), Michael Karoli (guitar), and Irmin Schmidt (keyboards), with the band self-producing at their own Inner Space studio.

Suzuki left Can in 1973, converting to the Jehovah’s Witnesses and stepping back from music entirely for roughly a decade.

Dunkelziffer and the Return to Music (1982–late 1980s)

By the early 1980s Suzuki had returned to Germany and to performing. He joined Dunkelziffer, a Cologne-based post-punk/funk group that also featured former Can drummer Jaki Liebezeit. Dunkelziffer released several albums on the FünfUndVierzig label:

Damo Suzuki Network (1990s–2020s)

From the mid-1990s onwards Suzuki developed a novel touring model: the Damo Suzuki Network. Rather than maintain a fixed band, he would arrive in a city and perform with a handpicked group of local musicians he called “sound carriers” — performing without rehearsal, in real-time improvisation. Every show was unique; no two lineups were the same. The concept formalised an approach rooted in Can’s instant-composition practice but extended it to a global distributed model.

The entity appears on Discogs under two related names:

  • Damo Suzuki Band (Discogs ID: 1122161) — earlier iteration, active c.1986–2000
  • Damo Suzuki’s Network (Discogs ID: 1112929) — the core Network project, active c.1997–2020s

Key Network releases include:

  • Damo’s Net Work (1997, Damo’s Net Work label) — foundational release establishing the format
  • Metaphysical Transfer (2001, Captain Trip Records) — live recording documenting the Network
  • Floating Element (2016, Purple Pyramid)
  • Ausland (2017, The Tapeworm)

Cancer, Recovery, and Late Period (2004–2024)

In around 2004 Suzuki was diagnosed with colon cancer. Treatment took him away from touring for a period, but he returned to performing after recovery, and the Network continued expanding. He appeared on dozens of releases in the 2010s across Europe, North America, and Japan, collaborating with an enormous range of musicians.

Later notable releases included:

  • Start From Zero (2014, Important Records) — Damo Suzuki primary credit
  • Damo Suzuki Møder Oresund Space Collective (2014, Space Rock Productions)
  • Live At Roadburn Festival (2019, Fuzz Club Records)
  • Arkaoda (2022, Akuphone)
  • Live In Paris 1973 (2024, Mute/Spoon Records) — posthumous Can live release

Damo Suzuki died on 9 February 2024 in Cologne, aged 74.
