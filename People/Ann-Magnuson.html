Ann Magnuson

Discogs ID: 267296
Real name: Ann Eleanor Magnuson
Born: January 4, 1956, Charleston, West Virginia
Active: 1980–present
Inducted: West Virginia Music Hall of Fame, 2018

Performance artist, vocalist, actress, and singer. Fixture of the NYC downtown scene in the early 1980s — Mudd Club regular, early member of Pulsallama (1981 large ensemble), and co-founder/frontwoman of Bongwater (with Kramer (2), on Shimmy Disc, 1987–1993). Also worked with Lydia Lunch and contributed a liner note micro-essay to the Bush Tetras retrospective Rhythm and Paranoia (Wharf Cat Records, 2021).

Mudd Club (c. 1979–1982) — Co-Performers

Magnuson was a DJ and performer at the Mudd Club from approximately 1979 through the early 1980s. Her official bio names four performers she “collaborated frequently with … in numerous shows” across the downtown club circuit (Mudd Club, Danceteria, The Pyramid, CBGB) — these names are given as a cluster, not as Mudd Club-exclusive:

NameNotes
Eric BogosianPerformance artist / monologist; downtown NYC fixture

John SexCamp performer; Club 57 regular; confirmed at “Club 57 Night” at Mudd Club c. 1981
Joey AriasSinger/performer; Club 57 and Mudd Club regular

Club 57 Ladies Auxiliary at the Mudd Club. The Museum of the City of New York collection holds a photograph captioned “Ann Magnuson and the Club 57 Ladies Auxiliary performing at the Mudd Club” — confirming the crossover act performed there. The Ladies Auxiliary was the women’s performance/social offshoot of Club 57, and the members who can be named are April Palmieri, Wendy Wild, and Dany Johnson (all also founding members of Pulsallama). MCNY collection (access restricted; caption confirmed via search index)

Steve Mass / Keith Haring connection. Mudd Club owner Steve Mass saw Magnuson’s Playboy Bunny night at Club 57 in 1981 and asked her to recreate it at the Mudd Club; he also engaged Keith Haring to curate the Mudd Club’s second-floor gallery at the same time, making this a documented cross-venue moment. annmagnuson.com/bio | Wikipedia, Club 57, as of 2026-08-21

Klaus Nomi context. Magnuson directed the 1978 New Wave Vaudeville Show that launched Klaus Nomi’s career — this was a Club 57 production, not a Mudd Club event, but Nomi subsequently performed at both venues. annmagnuson.com/bio

Caveat on venue specificity. The sources (Magnuson’s official bio, Wikipedia, Red Bull Music Academy interview) name the four performers above as collaborators across the whole downtown circuit; no source reviewed here isolates any single performance to the Mudd Club exclusively (except the MCNY photo caption for the Ladies Auxiliary). Treat these as confirmed co-performers in the shared Mudd Club / Club 57 ecosystem, not as a strict Mudd Club-only roster.

The Luv Show (1995) — Session Notes

Producers: Don Fleming and Ann Magnuson
Released: November 1995, Geffen Records (GED 24817)

Origin: After Bongwater dissolved (1993), Magnuson relocated to Los Angeles. She began improvising with a loose ensemble around visual artist Mike Kelley — playing drums in an ad-hoc group called Supersession (also featuring Art Byington on guitar and Richie Lee, bassist from cult band Acetone, on bass). Several of those jams developed into songs that became the core of The Luv Show. annmagnuson.com | Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-30

Studios:

  • Los Angeles — “Chéz Kelley” (Mike Kelley’s Eagle Rock home studio); remote recording at Your Place Or Mine; assistant engineer Tom Nellen
  • New York City — Sear Sound; engineer Bil Emmons

Engineer (main): Adam Kaspar
Mastering: Greg Calbi at Masterdisk, New York

Songwriting: Magnuson wrote the majority of the album; several tracks co-written with Mike Kelley, including “This Nothing Life,” “It’s a Great Feeling,” “L.A. Donut Day,” and “Man With No Face.” A handful of tracks carried over from her Bongwater-era catalogue. annmagnuson.com

Concept: Magnuson describes the record as “folk, psychedelic, lounge, glam and rock into a pastiche of musical theater and spoken word dreamscapes,” influenced by rock operas and exploitation films of the 1960s–70s. The album tells the story of a young woman from the sticks who moves to Hollywood and sells her soul to the devil in exchange for stardom. annmagnuson.com

Live context: The Luv Show played as a theatrical residency at Luna Park (Los Angeles), Fez (New York City), and the Great American Music Hall (San Francisco) before and around the album release. Dallas Observer

Label support: Geffen provided funding for a 14-minute promotional video but gave the album little further promotional push; it was commercially unsuccessful. Dallas Observer | Wikipedia

Signing context: No primary-source documentation of how Magnuson secured the Geffen deal has been located (no contemporary interview or label statement found online). The most plausible inference from available sources is that her profile from Anything but Love (ABC, 1989–1992) and the live Luv Show residencies attracted label interest, but this is not confirmed. The Mike Kelley connection is to the recording of the album (his studio, his band Supersession), not to brokering the deal.

Mike Kelley credit note: Kelley is credited as a musician (drums/percussion/effects) and co-songwriter, not as producer. The Mike Kelley Foundation for the Arts lists The Luv Show in his discography as a collaboration with Supersession. mikekelleyfoundation.org

Acting & Screen Career

Screen actress from 1981 onward, active across independent film, Hollywood features, and television. The New York Times described her in 1990 as “an endearing theatrical chameleon.” IMDb lists 62+ credits. Wikipedia, as of 2026-01-04 | Official site bio
