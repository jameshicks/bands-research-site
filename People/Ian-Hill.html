Ian Hill

Aug 19, 20268 min read

Ian Hill

Discogs ID: 268331
Name variations: Hill; Ian; Obvious Ian
Groups: Family Fodder, People In Control
Active: documented c. 1979–2015

Multi-instrumentalist (vocals, keyboards, harp, percussion, accordion, piano) and founding-era member of the Family Fodder collective. One of the earliest contributors to the group’s experimental post-punk recordings, Hill contributed to all the key Family Fodder releases between 1979 and 1981 and remained a documented participant through the 2015 Staubgold reissue sessions. Beyond Family Fodder, he worked as a session accordion player across an eclectic range of recordings — dub reggae, cabaret-art-song, new wave, and post-punk — spanning the early 1980s through the mid-1990s.

Not to be confused with Ian Hill (2) (Judas Priest bassist, a different person), Ian Hill (3) (engineer), or Ian Hill (4) (Australian indie musician).

Biography

The Sheep’s Pig / Family Fodder Origins (c. 1975–1979)

Ian Hill entered the Family Fodder orbit in the mid-to-late 1970s as part of the evolving experimental collective that preceded the group’s formal incarnation. According to the Family Fodder article on the Discogs page, the pre-history runs: student group The Ideologically Pure Nude (Alig, Mick Hobbs, Felix Fiedorowicz, Mark Doffman) → expanded collective The Sheep’s Pig, which added Ian Hill (keyboards, vocals, tape-loops) and Martin Harrison (bass, guitar). Hill and Alig established Studio San Carlo, a basement studio intended to “own the means of production,” which functioned as the group’s primary creative hub for overdubs and early experimentation.

Hill is credited across the earliest stratum of Family Fodder’s studio work. He brought keyboards, harp, tape-loop work, and vocal contributions into what was always a rotating-cast collective rather than a fixed band lineup.

Family Fodder Core Period (1979–1981)

Hill is present on all three major Family Fodder releases from the Fresh Records / Crammed Discs period.

Monkey Banana Kitchen (Fresh Records, FRESH LP 3, 1980) — the group’s debut album and their most fully documented collective recording — credits Hill on vocals, keyboards, and harp, and also co-credits him (alongside Felix Fiedorowicz and Judy Carter) for the cover artwork. The album was produced by People In Control, a production collective whose Discogs membership listing includes Hill alongside Charles Bullen, Judy Carter, Martin Harrison, and Rick Wilson. The album was recorded at four separate London studios (Berry St., Elephant, Mobile, Vineyard) — the multi-studio approach reflecting the sprawling collective’s logistics as much as its sonic ambitions.

Greatest Hits (Crammed Discs, CRAM 016, 1981) — an LP of original material (not a compilation, despite the title) recorded for Marc Hollander’s Brussels-based label — credits Hill on vocals and percussion alongside the wider ensemble: Alig, Dominique Levillain, Charles Bullen, Felix Fiedorowicz, Graham Painting, Bazz Smith, Rick Wilson, Mick Hobbs, Mark Doffman, and Martin Harrison (billed as Martin Frederix).

ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy ! (Fresh Records, FRESH 37/12, 1981) — a 12” mini-album and the most concentrated statement of Hill’s role. The lineup here is stripped to four players: Alig (keyboards, voice), Ian Hill (keyboards, voice), Graham Painting (bass, guitar, percussion, voice), and Bazz Smith (drums, percussion, voice), with Lynn Alice adding voice. Hill is credited as co-writer on four tracks (A2, B1, B3, B4) — his most substantial songwriting credit in the Family Fodder catalog. Producer credit went to Chris Mansbridge and the group collectively.

The Discogs profile lists Hill’s name variation “Obvious Ian” without explanation — this informal handle is not explained in available sources.

People In Control (Production Collective)

In parallel with his Family Fodder role, Hill was a member of People In Control, the production entity that credited several recordings outside the Family Fodder orbit. Beyond producing Monkey Banana Kitchen, People In Control also produced a track on Hermine Demoriane’s Lonely At The Top (1984, Salomé Discs) — a release on which Hill also appeared individually as session accordionist (see below).

Session Work: Accordion (1983–1995)

After the concentrated 1979–1981 Family Fodder period, Hill’s documented external credits are as a session accordion player — a different instrument from his primary Family Fodder role, suggesting either a parallel musical identity or that accordion work simply generated more documented credits.

Amos & Sara — “Go Home Soldier” (It’s War Boys, 1983). Ensemble also included Martin Harrison (billed as Martin Fredericks) on percussion, producer, and baritone saxophone — a direct carry-over from the Family Fodder network.

Hermine Demoriane — Lonely At The Top (Salomé Discs, 1984). A singular cabaret-art-song record from the French-born, London-based tightrope walker and performer. The ensemble assembled here also included Felix Fiedorowicz (bassoon), Tim Hodgkinson (baritone saxophone), Stephen Kent (French horn), and Cass Davies / Dominic Weeks (producers), placing Hill at the intersection of the Family Fodder and Henry Cow / This Is Not circles. One track (B2) was produced by People In Control. Recorded by both John Fryer and Tim Hodgkinson.

Annie Anxiety Bandez — Giacomo (One Little Indian, 1987). A politically charged record produced and mixed by Adrian Sherwood (among others), featuring Doug Wimbish (bass), Skip McDonald (remix), and Bonjo Iyabinghi Noah (percussion) — the Tackhead / African Head Charge circle. Hill contributed accordion and piano; also on the session was Martin Frederix (guitar), another Family Fodder associate. The record’s collision of dub, industrial politics, and avant-cabaret placed Hill’s contribution within one of the more adventurous independent records of the period.

Linton Kwesi Johnson — Tings An’ Times (Fnac Music / LKJ Records, 1991). The most prominent external session credit in Hill’s Discogs record — a full LP by the Jamaican-British dub poet, produced and engineered by Dennis Bovell. The ensemble for this record included Bovell (bass, percussion), John Kpiaye (guitar), Nick Straker (keyboards), Paget King (organ/piano/synthesizer), Steve Gregory (tenor saxophone), Jeff Scantlebury (percussion), and a brass section. Hill’s accordion role among this heavyweight ensemble suggests a working connection between his circle and the LKJ / Bovell network, possibly mediated through the overlapping South London post-punk / reggae scenes.

Stiltskin — Rest In Peace (White Water Records, 1995). The final documented session accordion credit. Stiltskin were the Scottish rock act formed by guitarist Peter Lawlor, who wrote and produced this release.

Staubgold Reissue Era (2015)

Hill appears on the 2015 Staubgold release Sunday Girls (Director’s Cut) (staubgold, cat. not confirmed), credited on keyboards, percussion, and vocals — the same ensemble roles as his 1979–1981 period. This was an extended reissue of the early Family Fodder mini-album Sunday Girls (1979), with the “Director’s Cut” designation matching the Staubgold reissue campaign that also produced Monkey Banana Kitchen (staubgold 130, 2014) and Schizophrenia Party (Director’s Cut) (staubgold 131, 2014). The 2015 release suggests Hill remained connected to the Family Fodder network through that campaign, though the Discogs entry indicates this is a Staubgold / Germany release.
