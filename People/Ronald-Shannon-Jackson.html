Ronald Shannon Jackson

American free jazz drummer and bandleader. Born 12 January 1940, Fort Worth, Texas. Died 19 October 2013, Fort Worth, Texas. Discogs artist ID: 271714.

Early Life and Career (1960s–1970s)

Jackson grew up in Fort Worth in a musical household and relocated to New York in the 1960s to pursue a career in jazz. He came of age inside the most turbulent wing of the New York avant-garde, playing drums in situations that had no interest in swing convention. His earliest documented Discogs credit is with the Charles Tyler Ensemble (1966, ESP-Disk’), alongside the free-jazz altoist who had worked closely with Albert Ayler.

Jackson played with Albert Ayler in the Albert Ayler Quintet (Discogs group ID: 284314) — a unit whose members at various points included Donald Ayler, Gary Peacock, Henry Grimes, Milford Graves, and Sunny Murray. This was the crucible period: Ayler’s approach to collective improvisation, rhythm as ritual, and the dissolution of meter were foundational for everything Jackson would build afterward.

He subsequently joined The Cecil Taylor Unit (Discogs group ID: 317894), led by pianist Cecil Taylor. The Unit, which at various points included Jimmy Lyons, Andrew Cyrille, Leroy Jenkins, and William Parker, occupied a different frontier of free music — dense, pianistic, architecturally complex. Jackson appears on two New World Records documents: The Cecil Taylor Unit (1978) and 3 Phasis (1979), plus Live In The Black Forest (MPS, 1979). These associations placed Jackson firmly in the first rank of the post-Coltrane drum tradition.

His sideman credits from the late 1970s also include Ornette Coleman’s electric-period work: Dancing In Your Head (A&M/Horizon, 1977) and Body Meta (Artists House, 1978), both now recognized as foundational documents of Prime Time’s harmolodic approach. Jackson is credited on drums on both.

The Decoding Society (1979–1996)

In 1979–1980 Jackson founded Ronald Shannon Jackson And The Decoding Society (Discogs group ID: 862460), his primary vehicle for the next decade and a half. The Society was not a fixed lineup but a revolving cast — its Discogs member list is unusually long: Akbar Ali, Bern Nix, Billy Bang, Byard Lancaster, Cary Denigris, Charles Brackeen, David Fiuczynski, Eric Person, Jef Lee Johnson, John Moody, Khan Jamal, Lee Rozie, Masujaa, Melvin Gibbs, Onaje Allan Gumbs, Ramon Pooser, Reggie Washington, Reverend Bruce Johnson, Robin Eubanks, Vernon Reid, Zane Massey, and others.

The concept was tribal and polyrhythmic, with Jackson layering his drumming against electric guitars, reeds, and strings in configurations that owed as much to funk and rock as to free jazz. He often described the project in terms of a “harmolodic blues” vision not far from Coleman’s, but with a harder edge and more explicit engagement with African rhythm.

The early records were produced by figures like Kathelin Hoffman (who also produced When Colors Play) and Burkhard Hennen (the Moers Music records). From Red Warrior onward, Laswell’s production hand is prominent. The DIW releases (What Spirit Say, Shannon’s House) were produced by Kazunori Sugiyama and mixed by Joe Ferla, and represent Jackson’s final major document as a bandleader before his health declined.

Jackson himself played drums, flute, and on later records a shawm (called “boschorn” or “schalmei”) — an instrument whose raw, reedy timbre suited the tribal quality of the music. He composed and arranged all material on Decoding Society recordings.

Last Exit (1986–1994)

Jackson was a founding member of Last Exit, the avant-garde supergroup also featuring Sonny Sharrock (guitar), Bill Laswell (bass), and Peter Brötzmann (saxophones). The group released its self-titled debut on Enemy Records in 1986; further documents include The Noise Of Trouble (Live In Tokyo) (1986), From The Board (1987), Into The Outlands (1988), Iron Path (Virgin, 1988), and Köln (ITM Records, 1990), plus the posthumous Low Life / Last Exit (Jazzwerkstatt, 2007).

Last Exit operated as a pure collective — no arrangements, no meters, high volume, total improvisation. Jackson’s drumming here is less polyrhythmic framework-builder and more weather event, reacting to Brötzmann’s overblown reeds and Sharrock’s white-noise guitar in real time. Engineer Robert Musso was closely associated with the group’s recordings and appears across most Last Exit credits.

Other Collaborative Projects

Music Revelation Ensemble (Discogs group ID: 341074) — Jackson appears on the 1988 DIW release Music Revelation Ensemble, a group also featuring James Blood Ulmer, David Murray, and Amin Ali, among others. See Music Revelation Ensemble.

Power Tools (Discogs group ID: 898221) — See Power Tools.

The Golden Palominos (Discogs group ID: various) — Jackson is listed as a member of The Golden Palominos, Anton Fier’s rotating collective. See also Anton Fier.

Prime Time (5) (Discogs group ID: 535384) — Jackson’s continued involvement with Ornette Coleman’s electric ensemble.

Third Rail (3) (Discogs group ID: 225209) — documented membership.

Mooko (Discogs group ID: 617360) — Jackson appears on Mooko = 蒙古 (NEC Avenue, Japan, 1988), a Japanese-market release where he is credited for drums, percussion, shawm, and production.

Wadada Leo Smith’s Golden Quartet (Discogs group ID: 1034747) — documented membership.

Albert Live In Montreux! (MPS, 1981) — Jackson appears as a primary artist on this MPS document recorded live at Montreux, alongside Albert Mangelsdorff and J.-F. Jenny-Clark.

Solo Records

Pulse (Celluloid, 1984) — A more singular document, with Jackson credited for drums and vocals, Ron St. Germain and David Breskin producing. Onaje Allan Gumbs appears on piano (one track) and poet Michael S. Harper contributes voice.
