Brad Nowell

Discogs ID: 493638 Real name: Bradley James Nowell Also known as: Texas (22) (early Skunk Records alias) Born: February 22, 1968, Long Beach, California Died: May 25, 1996, San Francisco, California (heroin overdose) Instruments: Vocals, guitar, B3 organ, SH-101 synthesizer, bass, percussion, sampler, drum programming

American vocalist, guitarist, and primary songwriter. Co-founder of Sublime (2) and Skunk Records (2) (with Marshall Goodman). Died two months before the self-titled major-label album was released; it went on to sell over five million copies in the US. His son Jakob Nowell joined the reformed Sublime in 2023.

Biography

Early Life and Formation of Sublime (1968–1991)

Bradley James Nowell was born February 22, 1968, in Long Beach, California. He formed Sublime (2) in Long Beach in 1988 with bassist Eric Wilson and drummer Bud Gaugh (also known as Floyd Gaugh). The band’s sound drew from ska, reggae, dub, hardcore punk, folk, and hip-hop, reflecting the eclectic tastes of the Long Beach scene and Nowell’s own record collection.

In 1990, Nowell and Marshall Goodman (a.k.a. “Field Marshall”) co-founded Skunk Records (2), a Long Beach independent label that gave Sublime full artistic control and became the primary vehicle for their early recordings. Goodman also played drums and turntables on early records and co-wrote several tracks.

The earliest Skunk single “Jah Won’t Pay The Bills” (1991, master 986355) credits Nowell as “Brad” (guitar only), Wilson on bass, and Gaugh on drums, produced by “Elephant Levitation” — a pseudonym for Michael -Miguel- Happoldt, who became a central production collaborator throughout Nowell’s career. Happoldt went by aliases D.J. Spock and Nice Mike; he and Nowell were close friends from the Long Beach scene.

Skunk Records Era (1992–1994)

40oz. To Freedom (1992, Skunk Records (2), Discogs master 18580) — self-released on Skunk and became a regional word-of-mouth hit. The album finds Nowell credited as “Brad” for vocals, guitar, percussion, sampler, bass, and congas. The extended ensemble included Michael -Miguel- Happoldt (sampler, guitar, vocals, piano, organ, electronics), Marshall Goodman (drums, turntables, vocals), Todd Foreman (saxophone), horn players Nick Martin (2) (trombone) and Chris Hauser (2) (trumpet), Jack Maness (vocals, guitar), and Adam Bausch (vocals, congas). The album was produced by the band themselves, with original pressings crediting “Elephant Levitation” as producer. Brian Gardner (“Big Bass Brian”) mastered later pressings.

Robbin’ The Hood (1994, Skunk Records (2), Discogs master 158166) — recorded primarily at Nowell’s own home in a lo-fi style influenced by cassette-culture underground rap and DIY punk. Nowell is credited for vocals, guitar, and drum programming, and serves as one of the primary “Recorded By” credits alongside Happoldt, with Donnell Cameron and Joe Peccerillo recording a subset of tracks. The lo-fi aesthetic was intentional rather than a limitation.

Also released during this period: the split single Work That We Do (1994, Insta-Noise Records / Skunk) and appearances on compilations. In 1994 Nowell also had a contributing credit (“Vocals, Guitar”) on the Memories comp (gp records (9)).

The Beacon Street Collection and No Doubt Connection (1995)

The Beacon Street Collection (1995, Beacon Street Records) — a No Doubt album on which Nowell appears credited as “Bradley” for “Musician [With], Vocals” on track 3. This was a connection between the Long Beach ska-punk scene and No Doubt’s Anaheim operation; Gwen Stefani handled art on the release. Nowell and No Doubt ran in overlapping Southern California alternative/ska circles.

Also in 1995, Sublime were credited as Producer on the Australian release Living In A Boring Nation (Liberation Records / Skunk).

Major Label Recording: Sublime (1996)

After signing to Gasoline Alley (a sublabel of MCA Records), Sublime recorded their self-titled major-label debut. The album’s production was split between two producers working on distinct track groups:

  • Paul Leary (guitarist of Butthole Surfers) produced and mixed the majority of the album — tracks 1, 3, 4, 6–9, 11–14, 16 — with Stuart Sullivan engineering.
  • David Kahne produced and mixed four tracks — 2 (“What I Got”), 5 (“April 29, 1992 / Miami”), 15 (“Caress Me Down”), and 17 (“Doin’ Time”) — with Eddie Ashworth engineering.
  • Track 10 (“Paddle Out”) was co-produced by Nowell himself and Michael -Miguel- Happoldt, with Ashworth mixing.

Nowell is credited across the full album for vocals, guitar, and synthesizer (B3 and SH-101). On individual tracks he co-wrote “What I Got” (track 2), “April 29, 1992” (track 5), the reprise (track 16), and “Doin’ Time” (track 17) in collaboration with Goodman and Happoldt. “Doin’ Time” interpolates the Gershwin / Heyward standard “Summertime” from Porgy and Bess.

Songwriting credits show Nowell as primary writer on most tracks. Notable exceptions: “The Ballad Of Johnny Butt” (track 11) written by Secret Hate; several tracks credited to “Sublime (2)” collectively.

Brian Gardner mastered the album. Additional session players: Field Marshall (percusion and turntables, tracks 1/2/5/8/12/14–17), Todd Foreman (saxophone, track 5), Jon Blondell (trombone, track 3), Dave Aron (programmed, track 5).

Death (May 25, 1996)

Nowell died of a heroin overdose on May 25, 1996, in San Francisco, California. He was 28. He had struggled with drug addiction throughout the band’s career. The self-titled album was released approximately two months later, on July 30, 1996, and became a multi-platinum posthumous success. Singles “What I Got,” “Santeria,” “Wrong Way,” and “Doin’ Time” all received significant radio play.

His dog Lou Dog (a Dalmatian who often appeared with the band) gave his name to L.D.R. (Lou Dog Records), the label that released the posthumous Black Album (1999, Discogs master 3338218) featuring recordings of Nowell with Wilson and Gaugh alongside a spoken cameo by Snoop Dogg.
