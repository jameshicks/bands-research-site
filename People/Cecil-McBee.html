Cecil McBee

American jazz double bassist. Born May 19, 1935, Tulsa, Oklahoma. Discogs artist ID: 258511.

One of the most prolific and versatile post-bop sidemen of the late 1960s through 1990s, McBee played on an extraordinary range of sessions spanning hard bop (Blue Note, Atlantic), spiritual jazz (Impulse!), free jazz, and world-influenced post-bop. His most intensive associations were with Charles Lloyd (the landmark 1966–68 Atlantic quartet), Pharoah Sanders (multiple Impulse! albums 1970–72), and Alice Coltrane (Journey In Satchidananda, 1971). As a leader he recorded sparingly but significantly, particularly on Strata-East, India Navigation, and Enja Records.

Career Overview

Early Work and First Recordings (1962–65)

McBee’s earliest Discogs-documented credits are from 1962–65. He appeared on:

  • Jewell (1962) — very limited release
  • Boss: Bossa Nova (1963, Workshop Jazz) — early sideman work
  • Jackie McLean’s It’s Time! (1964, Blue Note) and Action (1967, Blue Note) — entry into the Blue Note milieu
  • “In” Jazz For The Culture Set (1965, Impulse!) with the Dannie Richmond ensemble
  • Wayne Shorter’s Some Other Stuff (1965, Blue Note) and Compulsion (1967, Blue Note)

The Charles Lloyd Quartet (1966–68)

McBee’s highest-profile association in this period was with Charles Lloyd’s Atlantic-era quartet alongside pianist Keith Jarrett and drummer Jack DeJohnette. The quartet produced two landmark live albums: Forest Flower (1967, Atlantic) — recorded at the Monterey Jazz Festival, one of the first jazz records to cross into rock audiences — and Charles Lloyd In Europe (1968, Atlantic). Also: Dream Weaver (1966, Atlantic) and The Flowering (1971, Atlantic).

Blue Note and Atlantic (1965–70)

During this period McBee was deeply embedded in the Blue Note and Atlantic networks:

  • Wayne Shorter: Some Other Stuff (1965), Compulsion (1967)
  • Andrew Hill: Compulsion (1967) — Hill was a close associate, with Discogs showing 34 shared releases across pressings
  • Jackie McLean: multiple sessions, 59 shared release entries on Discogs (includes many reissues)
  • Yusef Lateef: The Blue Yusef Lateef (1968, Atlantic), The Complete Yusef Lateef (1968), Detroit Latitude 42° 30’ (1969, Atlantic)
  • Sam Rivers: Odyssey Of Iska (1971, Blue Note); extensive later collaborations

He also appeared on Alice Coltrane’s Journey In Satchidananda (1971, Impulse!) — the devotional masterwork featuring Rashied Ali on drums, Pharoah Sanders on soprano sax and percussion, and Tulsi on tambura.

Strata-East and the Black Self-Determination Scene (1971–76)

McBee was significantly involved in Strata-East, the musician-owned cooperative label founded by Charles Tolliver and Stanley Cowell:

  • Music Inc. (1971, Strata-East) — as member of Charles Tolliver’s Music Inc. ensemble
  • Live At Slugs’ Volume 1 (1972) and Volume II (1973, Strata-East) — with Charles Tolliver
  • Izipho Zam (My Gifts) (1973, Strata-East) — Pharoah Sanders
  • The Eighth Wonder (1974, Strata-East)
  • Freedom Of Speech (1975, Strata-East)
  • Impact (1976, Strata-East)
  • As leader: Mutima (1974, Strata-East) — his debut as primary leader; McBee composed, arranged, and produced; personnel included Dee Dee Bridgewater (vocals), Onaje Allan Gumbs (piano/electric piano), Billy Hart (percussion/cymbal), Michael Carvin (gong/percussion), Tex Allen (flugelhorn), Art Webb (flute)

India Navigation (1975–84)

McBee was a key artist on India Navigation, the free jazz / loft jazz label:

  • Valley Of Search (1975) — McBee credited as composer
  • Chico Freeman: Chico (1977)
  • Alternate Spaces (1979) — leader
  • Spirit Sensitive (1979, India Navigation) — as sideman
  • Flying Out (1982) — leader
  • Kings Of Mali (1978, India Navigation) — as sideman
  • The Search (1983, India Navigation)
  • The Outside Within (1981) — as sideman

Enja Records (1974–95)

Enja Records was his most-used label as leader and prominent sideman, accounting for 19 releases:

  • African Space Program (1974, Dollar Brand / Abdullah Ibrahim)
  • Music From The Source (1978, Enja) — producer credit
  • Compassion (1979, Enja) — producer credit
  • Inside Story (1981, Enja)
  • Live At The Jazz Showcase Vol. 1 (1981) — as leader
  • Old Bottles - New Wine (1985, Enja)
  • What If? (1986, Enja)
  • Live At Fat Tuesdays (1988, Enja)
  • Live At The Jazz Showcase Vol. 2 (1989) — as leader
  • Live At Sweet Basil (1989) — with Uli Lenz and Joe Chambers
  • There Was A Time - Echo Of Harlem (1990, Enja)
  • When I Was At Aso-Mountain (1993, Enja)
  • It Don’t Mean A Thing… (1994, Enja)
  • Retroflection (1994, Enja)
  • Calling Card (1995, Enja)

Dollar Brand / Abdullah Ibrahim (1974–80)

McBee played on multiple albums with South African pianist Abdullah Ibrahim (then known as Dollar Brand), including African Space Program (1974, Enja), Children Of Africa (1976, Enja), and African Marketplace (1980, Elektra). The Discogs data shows 29 shared release entries, indicating a substantial sustained association.

Wider Sideman Network (1975–95)

Beyond his core associations, McBee’s sideman work spanned:

  • Arthur Blythe — 25 shared releases; Blythe’s Columbia and India Navigation sessions
  • Leon Thomas — Spirits Known And Unknown (1969, Flying Dutchman), Facets (1973), Astral Traveling (1973)
  • Lonnie Liston Smith — multiple Impulse! sessions; 28 shared releases
  • Joanne Brackeen — extended collaboration; member of multiple Brackeen ensembles
  • David Murray — 11 shared masters; key 1980s associate
  • Chico Freeman — appears prominently both on Alternate Spaces and the India Navigation roster; 104 shared release entries (includes reissues)
  • John Hicks — 8 shared masters; regular trio partner
  • McCoy Tyner — 8 shared masters
  • Jack DeJohnette — 4 shared masters
  • Elvin Jones — 7 shared masters; Power Trio (1991)
  • Mal Waldron — 3 shared masters
  • Grachan Moncur III — 28 shared release entries
  • Charles Tolliver — Strata-East associations; 17 shared release entries
  • Yosuke Yamashita (Japanese pianist) — 16 shared release entries; significant Japan circuit presence
  • Arthur Blythe — Lenox Avenue Breakdown (1979, CBS); 25 shared releases

One Night With Blue Note (1985)

McBee appeared at the One Night With Blue Note concert series (Blue Note 25th anniversary event, February 1985), documented across multiple volumes.

The Leaders (1984–91)

McBee was a member of The Leaders (3), a cooperative ensemble with Chico Freeman, Arthur Blythe, Kirk Lightsey, Don Moye, and Lester Bowie. They released on Black Saint and other European labels.

Later Career (1990–2024)

McBee remained active into the 2010s and beyond:

  • Power Trio (1991, Novus) with John Hicks and Elvin Jones
  • Tribal Ghost (2013, NoBusiness Records) with John Tchicai, Charlie Kohlhase, Billy Hart
  • Emanation (2019, NoBusiness Records) — Sam Rivers Trio featuring McBee and Norman Connors (archival / previously unreleased material)
  • Continued reissue presence on Impulse!, Blue Note, and Atlantic through to 2024 (The Carnegie Hall Concert)
