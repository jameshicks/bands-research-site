Overview

Giggles is a Portland, Oregon duo of Madelyn Villano and Alieta Herrera-Train, both members of the long-running Portland/Los Angeles free-improvisation collective Smegma. The “(4)” disambiguation on Discogs indicates there are multiple unrelated entries under the name Giggles.

Their sole documented release is Delusional (2012), a split cassette on Pigface Records — Smegma’s house imprint — paired with The Tenses, another Portland experimental outfit that shares the Smegma circle (The Tenses members Ju Suk Reet Meate and Oblivia (3) are both Smegma figures). The split places Giggles squarely within the Pigface Records / Portland noise-improv scene of the early 2010s, a period when the label was unusually active with new acts alongside its core Smegma output.

Giggles’ contribution to Delusional — “First Meeting” (Side A, 18:39, shared with The Tenses as a joint performance) — is the only track on the release credited to the duo. Side B (“Live In Studio”, 16:57) is The Tenses alone. Ju Suk Reet Meate is credited for photography on the release.

The release is catalogued under genres Electronic and Rock with styles Avantgarde, Experimental, and Free Improvisation — consistent with the broader Pigface Records / Smegma aesthetic.

Scene Context

Giggles operates entirely within the Smegma circle in Portland. Both members are listed in the Smegma Discogs roster, and the label — Pigface Records, run from 2123 NE Rodney, Portland — is Smegma’s primary imprint, operated by Michael Lastra.

The early 2010s were a productive period for Pigface: after a gap from 1999 to 2011 the label released a dense run of cassettes by Smegma-adjacent acts including The Tenses, MSHR, Lee Rockey, Guzo, and Arttu Partinen. Delusional (015, 2012) sits in the middle of this run. The label’s aesthetic across this period is consistent: long-form improvisation, cassette format, hand-numbered or small-run editions.

Madelyn Villano went on to release as Guzo on Pigface (2013–2017) and appear on Smegma’s Wild Blue Yonders (iDEAL Recordings, 2012), Mutant Stomps (Helicopter, 2014), and the 40 Years Of Smegma retrospective (Otoroku, 2015). Alieta Herrera-Train’s Discogs credits outside Giggles are limited to her Smegma membership; no additional solo or side-project releases appear in the database.
