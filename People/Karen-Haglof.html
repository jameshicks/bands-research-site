Karen Haglof

Guitarist, vocalist. From Minneapolis, Minnesota; active in New York City. Discogs artist ID: 452626. Name variation: K. Haglof.

Biography

Minneapolis Roots — The Crackers (3) (c. 1981)

Haglof came up in the Minneapolis rock scene. The earliest Discogs trace is Sir Crackers! (1981, Twin-Tone Records, cat TTR 8122), a four-track 12”/45 RPM maxi-single by The Crackers (3). The Crackers were a trio: Steve Almaas (bass, vocals, songwriter), Jay Peck (drums, vocals), and a guitarist/vocalist credited on the sleeve as “Karen Indiana” — widely understood to be Haglof under a stage name. The Discogs group page for The Crackers (3) lists Karen Haglof as a member. Steve Almaas was active across Minneapolis punk and power-pop; he had also played bass on the Suicide Commandos’ Make A Record (1978, Blank Records) and the The Commandos Commit Suicide Dance Concert (1979, Twin/Tone).

New York — Rhys Chatham Guitar Ensemble (1983)

By 1983 Haglof was in New York, appearing in Rhys Chatham’s electric guitar ensemble. She is credited on Factor X (1983, Moers Music, Germany) — a double-sided LP combining a large brass/percussion ensemble on the A-side with a guitar-ensemble piece on the B-side. Haglof plays electric guitar on tracks B1–B3 alongside Craig Kafton, Susan Springfield, Tim Schellenbaum, and Chatham himself (on B3); James Lo plays drums. This places her squarely in the NYC downtown experimental scene at a formative moment.

Fellow players on that session who also reappear across the wider scene: Anton Fier (percussion on side A), George Lewis (trombone), Jim Staley (trombone), Olu Dara (trumpet), Bob Stewart (tuba). Producer: Burkhard Hennen; Hennen and Chatham co-mixed.

Band Of Susans — Hard Light and Love Agenda (1989)

Haglof joined Band Of Susans alongside Page Hamilton as part of a significant lineup change for their second major phase (replacing Susan Lyall and Susan Tallman). She appears on two releases from 1989:

  • Hard Light (1989, Blast First, US release ID 2995722): guitar and vocals. The core lineup on this recording was Robert Poss, Susan Stenger, Ron Spitzer, Haglof, Hamilton. Produced by Robert Poss; engineered by Jim Klein.

  • Love Agenda (1989, Blast First / Mute, UK release ID 393949): guitar and vocals. Same core lineup; produced by Robert Poss. Engineered at Planet (Russ Landis) and Platinum Island (Jim Klein). Mastered by Chris Gehringer. Back cover photo by Beth B.

Both were central records in the Blast First guitar-rock moment, connecting Band Of Susans to Sonic Youth’s UK label home.

Haglof’s tenure with Band Of Susans ended after Love Agenda; Page Hamilton departed around the same time to form Helmet (2). The Peel Sessions release (1992, Dutch East India Trading) captures BBC recordings split between the Hamilton/Haglof lineup (tracks 1–4) and the later Husick/Lonergan lineup (tracks 5–6), with Haglof credited on guitar across the full set — though the Discogs release credits her without track-scoping.

Haglof is also credited on Wired For Sound: 1986–1993 (the band’s retrospective compilation on Blast First / Mute / World Service, no year given on Discogs) in a guitar and vocals capacity.

Rhys Chatham — Die Donnergötter (2006)

Haglof returned to the Rhys Chatham ensemble context over two decades later, playing electric guitar on track 1 of Die Donnergötter (2006, Radium (3)). That track also featured Robert Poss, Bill Brovold, Ben Neill, Mitch Salmen, Tim Schellenbaum, Conrad Kinard (electric bass), and J.P. (7) on drums — mixed by J.P. (7) and Martin Bisi. Chatham conducted. The presence of both Haglof and Poss on this recording marks a reunion of the 1989 Band Of Susans guitar partnership within the Chatham ensemble frame.

The Wad (2)

Discogs also lists Haglof as a member of The Wad (2), a six-member ensemble including Steve Almaas (reuniting the Minneapolis connection), Frank Berry, Jan King, Dave Phelps (4), and Mark Goldstein (4). No releases appear in the Discogs database under The Wad (2).

Later Activity — Executive Producer (2021)

Haglof is credited as executive producer on Everywhere You’ve Been (2021, Lonesome Whippoorwill, cat LON-003), a CD by Steve Almaas — again reconnecting with her Minneapolis bandmate of forty years prior. The album was produced and performed largely by Almaas, with guests including Mitch Easter (guitar, organ, mixing), Gary Louris (vocals), Tony Garnier (acoustic bass), and Jon Graboff (guitar, pedal steel).

Open Questions

  • “Karen Indiana” (Discogs ID 3168523) is listed on the Sir Crackers! sleeve as guitarist/vocalist; The Crackers (3) group page explicitly lists Karen Haglof (ID 452626) as the corresponding member. These are confirmed to be the same person; “Karen Indiana” was a stage name used specifically for this release. The alias relationship is not formally linked in the Discogs DB (no realname entry on the Karen Indiana page, no alias record connecting the two IDs).
  • Exact period and circumstances of joining / leaving Band Of Susans (between Hope Against Hope 1988 and Love Agenda 1989; departure point unclear).
  • Whether any recordings exist under The Wad (2) — Discogs carries no releases for that group.
  • Post-2006 activity beyond the 2021 executive-producer credit.
