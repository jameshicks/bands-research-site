Andy Diagram

Discogs ID: 42283

Born: London, July 1959
Name variations: A. Diagram · Andrew Diagram · Andy · Diagram
Website: andydiagram.com
Instrument: Trumpet (Shilke), flugelhorn, electronics (harmonizers, echo loops, whammy pedal), keyboards, piano, synthesizer

British trumpeter and musician. Born in London, July 1959; moved to Manchester in early 1980; returned to London in 1992 where he remains based. Best known for James (1989–92, rejoined 2007), Spaceheads (long-running duo with Richard Harrison), and work with David Thomas (2) of Pere Ubu as David Thomas And Two Pale Boys and Two Pale Boys. Earlier: founding member of Dislocation Dance (1980–86) and The Diagram Brothers (1980–82). Has also worked with The Pale Fountains, Michael Head & The Red Elastic Band, Kalima, A Certain Ratio, Yargo, Eric Random And The Bedlamites, and many improvisers and post-punk/jazz acts. Has taught Music Production at City Lit College, London.

Developed a distinctive approach of building layered brass soundscapes using basic electronic effects — harmonizers and echo loops are central, catching breathy tones, spitting, and melodic lines and spinning them back. Credited on Discogs with Trumpet (Shilke), flugelhorn (Vincent Bach), various synths (ARP, Roland), and frequently vocals or electronics alongside.

Biography

Grew up in central London; parents were activist folk musicians who encouraged music-making from an early age. Began playing trumpet around age seven.

In early 1980 moved to Manchester, contacted the Manchester Musicians Collective, and within weeks was recruited as replacement bassist for the The Diagram Brothers (Jason Pitchers had returned to his home town of Bristol after the first single). Although he took on the “Diagram” surname, he was not a blood relation — he was brought in through the Collective network. He brought trumpet, piano, and keyboards alongside the bass role.

Through the overlapping New Hormones / Manchester Musicians Collective network, he joined Dislocation Dance by late 1980/early 1981 as trumpeter and vocalist, while continuing with the Diagram Brothers simultaneously. Both bands operated in the New Hormones orbit, playing shared gigs at venues like the Beach Club alongside Ludus, A Certain Ratio, and related acts.

During the Manchester years (1980–89) he worked extensively in the scene around Factory Records and New Hormones, playing with Kalima, A Certain Ratio, Yargo, Eric Random And The Bedlamites, and others (see Other Notable Credits below).

Joined James in 1989: was living in the same house as the person printing James T-shirts, attended Gold Mother recording sessions alongside saxophonist Vinnie Corrigan — the sax didn’t suit the band’s sound but the trumpet did. He was invited to live shows initially, then offered a retainer. His trumpet through harmonizer, fuzz, and wah pedals became central to the James sound on Gold Mother and Seven. Left in summer 1992 to pursue free-form jazz and Spaceheads. Made a guest return at Manchester G-Mex in December 1993, and appeared as special musical guest on the farewell tour (Getting Away With It…Live, 2002).

James reformed in early 2007; Andy rejoined for the festival tour beginning late April 2007, including T in the Park and V Festival that summer. Played on the reunion album Hey Ma (released 7 April 2008) and has remained a member since.

James (1989–1992; 2007–present)

Manchester indie rock band. Discogs ID: 12094. Andy joined in 1989 during the band’s run-up to mainstream UK success and left in summer 1992 to pursue free-form jazz and Spaceheads. Made a guest return at Manchester G-Mex in December 1993 (not a full member); appeared as special musical guest on the farewell tour 2001 (credited on Getting Away With It…Live). Rejoined as full member from late April 2007 — the reformation tour that included T in the Park and V Festival — and has remained since. Core members include Tim Booth (vocals), Jim Glennie (bass), Larry Gott (guitar), Mark Hunter (keyboards), Saul Davies (violin, guitar), David Baynton-Power (drums). Full article: James.

Discogs credits his role across James releases as: Trumpet, Flugelhorn, Backing Vocals, Electronics/Performer. The 1992 live release credits him specifically as “Trumpet [Through Harmonizer, Fuzz And Wah Pedals]” — a precise description of his layered-loop technique applied in a stadium rock context.

Connections

  • Dislocation Dance and Spaceheads share the Richard Harrison axis — the Dislocation Dance rhythm section drummer became Andy’s permanent duo partner.
  • The Pale Fountains connection places Andy in Liverpool’s early-1980s indie-bossa orbit alongside Michael Head (later Michael Head & The Red Elastic Band and Shack).
  • Two Pale Boys / David Thomas: working with David Thomas (2) connected Andy to the American art-rock tradition running through Pere Ubu and the broader avant-rock scene; the albums were released on Cooking Vinyl spanning 1996–2004.
  • James used Andy’s looped-brass sound as a defining textural element in their commercial peak (Gold Mother, Seven), then re-integrated it in the reform period from 2007. He is the only member to straddle both the original Dislocation Dance post-punk moment and James’s Madchester/stadium era.
  • Other groups listed on Discogs with limited documentation: Mud Hutters, Put Put, Smart Bombs, Strings Of Consciousness, The Honkies, The Pale Orchestra, Tom Jenkinson & Friends.
