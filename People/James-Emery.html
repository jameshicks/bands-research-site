James Emery

James Emery

Oct 20, 20269 min read

James Emery

Discogs ID: 314875 Born: December 21, 1951, Youngstown, Ohio, USA Active: 1975–2023 (per Discogs credits) Instruments: Guitar (acoustic, electric, soprano guitar), guitar synthesizer, electronics Name variations on record: Emery, J. Emery, Jim Emory

American guitarist and composer active in free jazz and avant-garde improvised music. Co-founder of the String Trio of New York, one of the most documented free improvisation groups in the Discogs database. Has operated across the full spectrum from solo guitar to small-group free jazz to large-ensemble orchestral work. Website: http://www.james-emery.com/

Biography

Origins: Youngstown and Cleveland

Emery was born in Youngstown, Ohio, in December 1951. Before his entry into the New York avant-garde scene, he played with the Mr. Stress Blues Band in Cleveland — a blues-based outfit that also included other musicians who would later surface in the downtown New York improvised music world. The exact period of his Cleveland tenure is not captured in Discogs; his first traceable recording credit appears in 1975.

New York, 1975: Early Large-Ensemble Work

Emery’s first Discogs credit is as guitarist on Leroy Jenkins’s 1975 JCOA Records release For Players Only, a large-ensemble project organized by Leroy Jenkins with The Jazz Composer’s Orchestra. The session was a who’s-who of then-current free jazz: Anthony Braxton (contrabass clarinet), Dave Holland (bass), Dewey Redman, Sirone, Charles Bobo Shaw, Jerome Cooper, Wadada Leo Smith, Joseph Bowie, Charles Brackeen, and others. This dates Emery’s New York activity to at least 1975.

Also in 1975, he appears on Sound Craft ‘75 Fantasy For Orchestra (Anima Productions), another large-ensemble orchestral project.

Late 1970s: Human Arts Ensemble, New Dalta Ahkri, Free Improvisation

By 1978 Emery was credited on The Human Arts Ensemble Live Vol. II (Circle Records), a European performance by Charles Bobo Shaw’s ensemble, alongside Joseph Bowie and John Lindberg — the same John Lindberg who would shortly become his partner in the String Trio of New York.

Emery was a member of New Dalta Ahkri — a collective improvisation group whose membership overlapped heavily with the late-1970s New York downtown scene. NDA included Anthony Davis (2), Bobby Naughton, Dwight Andrews, Marty Ehrlich, Michael Gregory Jackson, Oliver Lake, Pheeroan Aklaff, Thurman Barker, Wadada Leo Smith, and Wes Brown. Ehrlich appears as a recurring collaborator on Emery’s own records through the 2000s.

Also in 1979: Emery appears on Nauxtagram (Otic Records) by vibraphonist Bobby Naughton, alongside Wes Brown and Robert Pozar.

1977–1990: String Trio of New York

The String Trio of New York was founded in 1977 by Billy Bang (violin). Emery and John Lindberg (bass) rounded out the original trio. The group’s Discogs core album run spans 1979–1997 on Black Saint, with later releases on Stash, West Wind, and Music & Arts.

Lineup shifts over time — Discogs member credits include Charles Burnham (violin, replacing or rotating with Bang), Diane Monroe (violin), and Regina Carter (violin on the final Music & Arts album Ellington / Monk / Mingus / Davis, 1997, which also featured Anthony Davis (2) on piano).

1981–1988: Solo and Small-Group Work

Emery’s first solo release as primary artist was Artlife (1982, Lumina Records Ltd.) — guitar, soprano guitar, and electronics, all compositions his own, with guest violin from Leroy Jenkins on two tracks. Produced by Emery and Ned Rothenberg; engineering split between Michael Lytle and Naux (John “Crawlin’ Snake” Mac).

Exo Eso (1988, FMP) is his sole guitar release on the important European free-improvisation label FMP — recorded and produced by Jost Gebers.

1988–1991: Spectral/Septet Era, Turbulence

Turbulence (1991, Knitting Factory Works) was co-credited to Emery and the Iliad Quartet — a working unit whose lineup here includes Marty Ehrlich (soprano/alto sax, clarinet), Michael Formanek (bass), Gerry Hemingway (drums), and Rob Schwimmer (keyboards). All compositions by Emery. Produced by Emery; liner notes by Kevin Whitehead.

1995–1999: Braxton Reconnection, Enja Recordings

Emery appeared on Anthony Braxton’s Creative Orchestra (Köln) 1978 (hat ART, 1995) — a reissue/archival release of a 1978 performance — playing electric guitar. He also appears on Braxton’s Orchestra (Paris) 1978 (Braxton Bootleg, 2011), a later archival release of the same tour.

In 1999, Emery was part of Braxton’s Composition No. 94 For Three Instrumentalists (1980) (Golden Years Of New Jazz), alongside Ray Anderson (trombone/cornet/trumpet). This documents one of Braxton’s numbered compositions with a three-person small-group setting.

Standing On A Whale Fishing For Minnows (1997, Enja Records) — his second full Enja solo album — features Marty Ehrlich (alto sax, clarinet, flute), Michael Formanek (bass), and Gerry Hemingway (drums, marimba, vibraphone). This is the working combo that also appears on Spectral Domains.

Spectral Domains (1998, Enja Records) is credited to the James Emery Septet, expanding to a seven-piece: Ehrlich, Formanek, Hemingway, Chris Speed (tenor sax, clarinet), Kevin Norton (vibraphone, marimba, timpani, percussion), and Mark Feldman (violin).

2000–2003: Between The Lines, Large-Format Composition

Luminous Cycles (2000, between the lines) — James Emery Sextet — carries the Ehrlich/Formanek/Hemingway core plus Chris Speed and Kevin Norton.

Fourth World (2001, between the lines / EFA) is an unusual co-leader outing with Joe Lovano (tenor/soprano/alto sax, various instruments), Judi Silvano (flute, voice), and Drew Gress (bass). Emery and Lovano share the composing credits.

Transformations (2003, between the lines) is the most ambitious release in Discogs: Emery writing for and performing with Klangforum Wien, conducted by Emilio Pomárico, with artistic direction by Franz Koglmann. Full chamber ensemble — strings, winds, brass, vibraphone, piano. Emery plays guitar and wrote the compositions, and also contributes liner notes.

2023: Late Work

Inside Outlier (2023, Jamem Pub. Co.) — a spare two-person record: Emery (guitar) and Cyrus Campbell (bass). Self-released on his own publishing imprint.
