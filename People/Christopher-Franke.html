Christopher Franke

German electronic musician, composer, and film scorer. Born Christoph Franke, April 6, 1953, Berlin. Member of Tangerine Dream c. 1970–1987. Discogs artist ID: 34347. Real name: Christoph Franke.

Principal synthesizer player during TD’s defining Virgin Records era — credited on Phaedra, Rubycon, Stratosfear, Sorcerer, and most albums through Livemiles (1988). Co-wrote compositions with Edgar Froese and Peter Baumann throughout the Virgin years. After leaving TD he relocated to Los Angeles, founded Sonic Images Enterprises (Studio City, CA), and became a prolific TV/film scorer — most notably the entire run of Babylon 5 (1994–1999).

Biography

Early Years — Berlin (1953–1969)

Franke grew up in West Berlin. In 1965 he co-founded a beat band called The Tigers, which renamed itself The Sentries. In 1967 parts of the band merged with another group called The Ugly Things to form Agitation, and Franke became the drummer, billed as “Chrille.” In 1969 that band renamed itself Agitation Free (Discogs artist ID: 117565) to avoid confusion with another Berlin group of the same name. During this period he was simultaneously studying classical music and composition at the Berlin Conservatory.

Agitation Free and Entry into Tangerine Dream (1969–1971)

Agitation Free and Tangerine Dream (Discogs ID: 10343) shared rehearsal space in Berlin. Franke was active alongside TD as early as 1970 — Discogs credits him as a performer on Alpha Centauri (1971) and the TD profile notes he was already active in 1970. He formally joined TD in 1971, taking over drum parts while expanding into synthesizer and percussion. Discogs lists his group membership in both Agitation Free and Tangerine Dream.

TD membership dates per Discogs: The artist profile states 1970–1987 (“between 1970 and 1987”) but also notes he “left Agitation Free and switched to his rehearsal neighbours Tangerine Dream” in 1971. The brief summary of 1971–1988 appears to be a conventional rounding; his last credited live release is Livemiles (Tangerine Dream In Concert) (1988), though he departed before that release.

Tangerine Dream — Virgin Era (1974–1983)

Franke’s most celebrated contributions came during TD’s Virgin Records period. He is credited with Moog, VCS3, Synthi A, double Moog, Mellotron, sequencers, and electronic drums across this stretch.

Franke was co-writer on virtually all TD output from 1973 onward; alongside Edgar Froese (Discogs ID: 10361) he was the most consistent compositional voice in the band. The trio of Froese–Franke–Baumann (with Peter Baumann, Discogs ID: 54855) is credited together on Stratosfear (1976) and Sorcerer (1977).

Later TD Years (1983–1987)

After Baumann’s departure (1977), Franke continued as TD’s primary synthesist alongside Froese and a rotating lineup. His roles expanded to include engineer and producer credits on releases like Hyperborea, Le Parc (1985), Underwater Sunlight (1986), and Tyger (1987). He is listed as producer and engineer on Underwater Sunlight and Tyger; Johannes Schmölling (Discogs ID: 148867) and Paul Haslinger (Discogs ID: 129359) cycled through the lineup during this period.

His final primary contribution to TD per Discogs is Livemiles (Tangerine Dream In Concert) (1988, Jive), on which he is credited as Producer, Composed By, Performer. The Discogs profile gives his departure as 1987.

Solo Career and Sonic Images Enterprises (1991–2003+)

After leaving TD, Franke founded The Berlin Symphonic Film Orchestra (Discogs ID: 404702) as a vehicle for combining electronic and orchestral scoring. In 1993 he opened a recording studio for film soundtracks in West Hollywood. He also founded Sonic Images Enterprises (Discogs ID: 753572, Studio City, CA) — the corporate entity behind the Sonic Images label (Discogs ID: 164977), Sonic Images Records (Discogs ID: 6189), Earthtone Records (Discogs ID: 6188), and Sonic Images Jazz. Sonic Images became the primary home for his solo and TV soundtrack releases throughout the 1990s.

His debut solo album Pacific Coast Highway (1991, Virgin/Sonic Images) featured the Berlin Symphonic Film Orchestra conducted by Brynmor Jones, with Richard E. Roth (aka Edgar Rothermich, Discogs ID: 260875) as co-producer and recording engineer — a collaboration that continued across many subsequent releases.

The Babylon 5 franchise generated the largest single body of work in his solo career — over a dozen individually released episode soundtracks plus compilation volumes spanning 1994–2007, all on Sonic Images. The series was created by J. Michael Straczynski (Discogs ID: 4604030), who appears as a co-credit on several Babylon 5 releases.

Deepak Chopra collaboration: Franke co-credited with Deepak Chopra on at least two releases (shared on 2 releases per collaborator data), corresponding to the Transformation Of Mind project described in his Discogs profile.
