Sebastian Meissner

Sebastian Meissner

Jun 23, 20269 min read

Sebastian Meissner

Born: September 27, 1969, Częstochowa, Poland
Based: Frankfurt (late 1990s) → Berlin
Discogs ID: 109442 (main); also credited under alias IDs below
Genres: Ambient, microsound, click-and-cut, glitch, dark ambient

German electronic music producer and (since c. 2010) radio feature writer/director for Deutschlandradio and WDR3. Meissner was one of the minor but consistent figures in the Frankfurt/Berlin experimental electronic scene of the late 1990s and 2000s, working across a shifting roster of project aliases that tracked successive aesthetic moves: early glitch/microsound, ambient click-and-cut, then darker ambient and sound-design work.

Primary aliases: Klimek, Random Industries, Random_Inc, Bizz Circuits, aUTOkoNTRasT
Groups: Autopoieses, b.Z_ToneR, Solid State Transmitters, Open Source (5)

Biography

Frankfurt — Force Inc. Origins (late 1990s)

Meissner became involved with electronic music while living in Frankfurt, where he met Achim Szepanski, founder of the Force Inc label collective and its offshoots, including Mille Plateaux and Ritornell. This placed him in the centre of what would become the German click-and-cut and microsound movement — a Frankfurt network that also included Ekkehard Ehlers, Alva Noto, and Stephan Mathieu.

His recording debut came in 1999 as half of the duo Autopoieses (with Ekkehard Ehlers), releasing La Vie à Noir on Mille Plateaux (1999). A follow-up remix/transposition album, La Vie à Noir Transposed, appeared on Mille Plateaux the following year. Autopoieses had a website (autopoieses.de) but no further studio releases traced in Discogs — the project was likely more live/installation-oriented than recorded.

2000–2002 — Random Industries, Random_Inc, Bizz Circuits

In 2000 Meissner issued Selected Random Works on Ritornell (Mille Plateaux’s sublabel) under the name Random Industries (Discogs ID: 87076). The album included liner notes by US microsound theorist Kim Cascone, placing it squarely in the PureData/laptop-music discourse of the period.

Under Random_Inc (Discogs ID: 58336), he released two albums:

  • Jerusalem: Tales Outside the Framework of Orthodoxy (Ritornell, 2001)
  • Walking in Jerusalem (Mille Plateaux, 2002)

These were both rooted in the click-and-cut / digital-glitch aesthetic the Mille Plateaux constellation was then defining.

Also in 2002, and in apparent contrast, he released The Very Best of Bizz Circuits (Deluxe, 2002) under the alias Bizz Circuits (Discogs ID: 54142) — the ironic title playing on the “best of” compilation format.

In the same year, Meissner appeared on the collaborative release Music for William Forsythe (Whatness, 2002), co-credited with Ekkehard Ehlers and Thom Willems. This is the main evidence of a substantive Meissner/Ehlers co-creation outside Autopoieses.

2002–2006 — Klimek / Kompakt years

From 2002, Meissner’s most sustained alias was Klimek (Discogs ID: 34008), a project described in the press as “violently sad sounding music.” The Klimek project had an extended run on Kompakt, the Cologne label known for minimal techno and its annual Pop Ambient compilation series.

  • Milk & Honey (Kompakt, 2002) — debut 12” under the Klimek name
  • Milk & Honey (Kompakt, 2004) — full-length LP; two 12”-sides expanded to album
  • Solo Swim (Alien Transistor, 2004) — split release with The Notwist and Console; Alien Transistor is the Notwist’s own label
  • Listen, The Snow Is Falling (Kompakt, 2005) — 12”; arrangement credited as “Concept / Sound-process / (re)-arrangements”; incorporated harp recordings by Katja Leber, recorded by Peter Fey
  • Music to Fall Asleep (Kompakt, 2006) — full-length; artwork by Sensodyne

Klimek tracks also appeared on Kompakt’s Pop Ambient series, specifically the 2003, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2007, and 2008 installments.

During this period, Meissner also appeared on the Into the Void compilation (Sub Rosa, 2006), co-credited with Ran Slavin (his later b.Z_ToneR collaborator) and Eran Sachs. Meissner handled extraction, processing, and arrangement for tracks 1–11; mastering was by Aviad Albert.

The aUTOkoNTRasT alias (Discogs ID: 385773) appears in Discogs with an active year of 2004 but no primary releases traced there.

2007–2009 — Anticipate Recordings and Film Soundtrack Work

In 2007, the Klimek project moved from Kompakt to Anticipate Recordings (New York, run by Ezekiel Honig):

  • Dedications (Anticipate Recordings, 2007) — composed by Sebastian Meissner; cover drawings by Jan Rohlf; mastered by Twerk
  • Movies Is Magic (Anticipate Recordings, 2009) — including an extended version released in 2010

Also in 2009, Klimek contributed music to Elevator No. 3, a release on Not On Label (Ran Slavin self-released), under the b.Z_ToneR project name alongside Ran Slavin.

2015 — GOULDEN burst and late Klimek work

In 2015 a large cluster of Klimek releases appeared on the GOULDEN label (ID: 890680), apparently including multiple standalone album-length works. The Discogs unique-master count collapses most of these to one master (Lapidarium, GOULDEN 01), but individual release IDs document titles including: American Darkness, Live at Berghain, Live in Ramallah, Songs of Love and Fear Vol. 1 & 2, Fleischwerk, Palimpsest, Excitement & War, Pop Crimes Vol. 2 & 3, and Lapidarium. Excitement & War (GOULDEN 07) is documented as a 6-track vinyl LP with abstract/dark ambient material (“landscape of war” I–VI).

Post-2010 — Radio and Broadcasting

Since approximately 2010, Meissner has been writing and directing radio features for German public broadcasters Deutschlandradio and WDR3. He maintains active Mixcloud and Bandcamp (randomindustries.bandcamp.com) and YouTube presences, and the Random Industries name has been revived for current online activity.

Scene Context — Frankfurt/Berlin Microsound

Meissner’s entry point was the Force Inc. / Mille Plateaux constellation in Frankfurt, which through the late 1990s and early 2000s operated as one of the primary European nodes for what Kim Cascone (who wrote liner notes on Selected Random Works) theorised as the “aesthetics of failure” — laptop music, glitch, the productive use of digital artefact. Key contemporaries in the same network include Ekkehard Ehlers, Alva Noto, Stephan Mathieu, and Marcus Schmickler.

The move to Kompakt (Cologne) for the Klimek project from 2002 shifted his affiliations into the ambient/minimal-techno sphere. Kompakt’s Pop Ambient series, curated by Wolfgang Voigt, was the primary annual document of that aesthetic territory, and Klimek’s recurring presence there placed him alongside artists like Triola, Languis, and the Kompakt inner circle. The label’s Cologne base and the Alien Transistor connection (via The Notwist, based in Bavaria) broadened the geography.

The Anticipate Recordings move (2007) brought him into a New York independent ambient/electronic context built by Ezekiel Honig.

The 2015 GOULDEN cluster — all labelled under the Klimek name — represents a late activity burst after an apparent quieter period, coinciding with the shift toward radio broadcasting work. GOULDEN appears to be a self-release or micro-label vehicle.

Connections

  • Ekkehard Ehlers — Autopoieses duo partner and co-credit on Music for William Forsythe (2002); both were in the Force Inc. Frankfurt network
  • Mille Plateaux / Force Inc — first label home; critical to Meissner’s entry into experimental electronic music
  • Kompakt — six-year relationship as Klimek (2002–2008 including Pop Ambient appearances)
  • Anticipate Recordings — post-Kompakt home run by Ezekiel Honig in New York
  • Kim Cascone — US microsound theorist who wrote liner notes for debut; situates Selected Random Works in the glitch-music intellectual frame
  • Ran Slavin — Israeli artist/filmmaker; collaborated on Into the Void (Sub Rosa, 2006) and b.Z_ToneR project
  • The Notwist — Bavarian post-rock/electronic band; shared the Solo Swim split on Alien Transistor (2004)
