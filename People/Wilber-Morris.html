Wilber Morris

American free jazz double bassist, born November 27, 1937, Los Angeles, California. Died of cancer August 8, 2002, Livingston, New Jersey. Discogs artist ID: 339192. Real name: Wilbur Morris. Older brother of cornetist/conductor Butch Morris.

Biography

Wilber Morris was a central figure in the downtown New York free jazz scene from the late 1970s through the late 1990s, functioning primarily as a sideman of exceptional range who moved with equal fluency between free improvisation and post-bop. He is best known as a mainstay of the David Murray Octet, appearing on several of its landmark Black Saint recordings, and as a member of a free-jazz trio with German reedist Thomas Borgmann and drummer Denis Charles.

Morris grew up in Los Angeles. His younger brother Butch Morris would become a significant figure in his own right as a cornetist and pioneer of conduction. By the late 1970s Wilber was working in New York, appearing on the debut recording by the Billy Bang Sextet (Sweet Space, 1979, Anima Productions), alongside Butch Morris on cornet, Frank Lowe on tenor, and Steve McCall on drums — a recording that places him squarely at the origins of the downtown loft-jazz revival.

Through the early 1980s Morris became embedded in the David Murray organization. He played bass on Murray’s Octet recordings Ming (1980, Black Saint) and Murray’s Steps (1983, Black Saint), sessions that stand among the definitive statements of the post-Coltrane/Ayler avant-garde. The Octet included Henry Threadgill, Butch Morris, George Lewis, Olu Dara, and Steve McCall, forming one of the era’s most powerful ensembles. Morris also appeared on Murray Quartet recordings Home (1982, Black Saint) and Hope Scope (1991, Black Saint).

In 1984 Morris led his own trio date, Wilber Force (DIW Records, Japan), co-led with David Murray and Denis Charles — a spare, intense free-trio document he also produced himself. The same year he appeared on the Billy Bang Quintet’s Rainbow Gladiator (1981, Soul Note) and several Frank Lowe dates.

The 1982 record Untitled Gift (Anima Productions) co-led with Billy Bang, Don Cherry, and Denis Charles is another key document of the era, recorded by engineer Martin Bisi.

Lucky Four (1988, Tutu Records) is a quartet date with David Murray, Dave Burrell, and Victor Lewis, recorded in Germany.

Morris also worked regularly with alto/soprano saxophonist Noah Howard, violinist Billy Bang, saxophonist Charles Gayle, reedist Arthur Doyle, alto saxophonist Charles Tyler, and drummer Denis Charles across numerous ensemble configurations. The Borgmann/Morris/Charles Trio (with Thomas Borgmann) documented their partnership on several recordings including Nasty & Sweet (2013, NoBusiness Records), released posthumously from sessions recorded before Morris’s death in 2002.

Late-career recordings include The Spell: The Vincent Chancey Trio Live, 1987 (released 2020 on NoBusiness Records) with French horn player Vincent Chancey and drummer Warren Smith, and Queen Mary (Silkheart) with the Denis Charles Triangle.

Morris was also a member of Prima Materia, The Celestrial Communication Orchestra, and various other ensembles associated with the downtown New York scene. His Prima Materia credits are on Live at Tonic (2001, DIW — a trio with Rashied Ali and Louis Belogenis, live at Tonic, NYC) and Configurations (The Music of John Coltrane) (2009, Blue Music Group — alongside Ali, Belogenis, Allan Chase, Greg Murphy, and Joe Gallant). He contributed compositions to several recordings, including a track on the Dennis Charles Triangle’s Queen Mary.

He died of cancer on August 8, 2002, in Livingston, New Jersey.
