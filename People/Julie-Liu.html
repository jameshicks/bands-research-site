Julie Liu

Julie Liu

Oct 13, 20267 min read

Julie Liu

Discogs ID: 254026
Name variations: Julie, Julie Lu
Instruments: Violin, Viola, Bass, Trumpet, Vocals

Strings player — violin, viola, and occasional bass — who circulated through the Chicago and broader Midwest post-rock/experimental underground from the mid-1990s through the mid-2000s. She is best documented as a member of Doug Scharin’s large collective HiM and Phil Spirito’s chamber folk ensemble Orso, but her credits span a wider network: Rex (the Scharin project on Southern Records), Seam, Tortoise, Songs Ohia, and Chris Mills (7). She also appears as a songwriter credit on two tracks of Out In Worship’s Sterilized (1998), a Scharin/Goldring project on Perishable Records.

Discogs lists her as a member of six bands: HiM, Orso, Rex, Mint Aundry, Sweeder, and Team Xiaoping. The latter three are lightly documented in the database with no releases attributed to them.

Biography

Chicago Underground: Seam, Rex, and Tortoise Guest Spots (1995–1998)

Liu’s earliest Discogs credit is on Seam’s EP Are You Driving Me Crazy? (1995, City Slang), where she plays trumpet, violin, and backing vocals on two tracks (“4” and “7”). Seam at that point was led by Sooyoung Park with Bundy K. Brown (guitar on select tracks) and John McEntire among its members.

She appears throughout Rex’s album 3 (1997, Southern Records), playing viola on four tracks (A2, B1, B4, B5) and violin across the record. Rex was the post-rock project centred on Doug Scharin (drums), Phil Spirito (bass guitar), and Curtis Harvey (guitar/vocals), produced by Bundy K. Brown and Brian Deck.

In 1998 she was credited as a performer on Tortoise’s landmark TNT (Thrill Jockey), listed under the “With Help From” designation alongside Rob Mazurek (cornet), Caitlin Horsmon (bassoon), Sara P. Smith (trombone), and Popahna Brandes (cello). Her role is violin. The record was recorded and mixed by John McEntire.

The same year, she co-wrote two tracks on Out In Worship’s Sterilized (1998, Perishable Records), a project by Scharin and Joe Goldring — her only published songwriting credit in the database.

HiM and the Scharin Collective (1999–2005)

Liu is a full credited member of HiM (Discogs ID: 13240), the large rotating collective built around Doug Scharin. On the two most heavily documented HiM records:

  • Sworn Eyes (1999, Bubble Core Records): violin on track B1. The record also featured Bundy K. Brown (bass), Rob Mazurek (cornet), Jeff Parker (guitar), and Martin Perna (alto sax).
  • Many In High Places Are Not Well (2003, FatCat Records): violin on track A3. Large ensemble session including Griffin Rodriguez, Scharin, Fred Erskine, Matt Schneider, Joshua Larue, Joe Goldring, Josh Berman (cornet), Rob Mazurek (cornet), Carlo Cennamo (alto sax), Abdou M’Boup (kora), Kristín Anna Valtýsdóttir (voice), Adam Pierce (voice/acoustic guitar), and Christian Dautresme (voice). Mastered by Alan Douches.
  • Peoples (2005, Bubble Core Records): violin and viola on tracks 4 and 10, credited as “Featuring [With]“. The core unit for this record contracted to Scharin, Griffin Rodriguez, and Rob King (keyboards), with a guest roster including Martin Perna (saxophones/flute), Tomeka Reid (cello), Abdou M’Boup (kora/drums), Jordan McLean (trumpet), Nick Glöckner (guitar), Adam Pierce, Joshua Larue, Christian Dautresme, and Fumie Tsuji.

Orso (2000–2006)

Liu is a named member of Orso (Discogs ID: 75969), the chamber folk/experimental ensemble led by Phil Spirito. Her credits across Orso releases:

  • Long Time By (2000, Perishable Records): viola. Core lineup here was Spirito, Brian Deck, Gillian Lisee, Ben Massarella, Tim Rutili, with Liu as guest strings.
  • My Dreams Are Back And They Are Better Than Ever (2004, Perishable Records): viola, violin, and vocals — her most expansive Orso role. The record featured Spirito, Griffin Rodriguez, Carlo Cennamo, Joshua Larue, Matt Schneider; recording/mixing split between Rodriguez and Scharin.
  • Roveretto (2005, Hinah): violin and viola.
  • Is Xmas Tomorrow? (2006, self-released): violin, viola, vocals. Personnel expanded to include Joe Adamik, Ben Massarella, Duke Lee, Libby Reed, and Matthew Graham.
  • First Egg: Part One Transmission, Transference, Transformation (undated, self-released): viola across eight tracks in a large ensemble with Jim Becker (banjo), Dave McGranahan (baritone horn), Nick Macri (bass), Dylan Ryan (vibraphone/keyboards), Bundy K. Brown (guitar), Karl Runge (violin), and others.

Songs: Ohia and Other Chicago Sessions (1999–2000)

Liu appears as a performer on Songs Ohia’s Axxess & Ace (1999, Secretly Canadian), a Jason Molina session that drew on a rotating Chicago cast including David Pavkovic, Edith Frost, Geof Comings, Joe Ferguson, and Michael Krassner (producer).

She contributed viola and violin to Chris Mills (7)‘s Kiss It Goodbye (2000, Sugar Free Records) on tracks 5 and 10, a Brian Deck- and Jon Langford (2)-produced Chicago record with Fredrick Lonberg-Holm (cello) and Jeb Bishop (trombone) among the ensemble.
