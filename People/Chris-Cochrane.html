American songwriter, guitarist, and vocalist at the center of the downtown New York experimental scene since the mid-1980s. Known for an abrasive, densely layered guitar approach inflected equally by no-wave noise, blues grit, and free improvisation. Active across four decades as a bandleader (No Safety, solo), collaborator (Curlew, Weird Little Boy, Dim Sum Clip Job, Alphawhore), and producer/engineer for others. Also credited as a visual contributor — cover art and design — on several releases.

Biography

No Safety and early NYC (1985–1991)

Cochrane emerged in 1985 as co-primary artist on the cassette No Safety (CNLF), co-credited with No Safety and vocalist Robert Hinrix. The release documents a sprawling, multi-instrument project: Cochrane plays guitar, bass, drums, flute, lute, steel drums, tape, organ, percussion, and voice across its 17 tracks. The core duo of Cochrane and Hinrix is joined on several tracks by Sue Ann Harkey (swarmandel, mandolin, voice), Paul Hoskin (contrabass clarinet), and Sharon Gannon (voice).

In 1987 Cochrane appeared as a guest on Kramer (2)-produced records: Bongwater’s Breaking No New Ground! (Shimmy Disc) — alongside fellow guests Fred Frith, Davey Williams, and Samm Bennett — and Krackhouse’s The Whole Truth (Shimmy Disc) with George Cartwright on saxophone. The Bongwater association put him on the Shimmy Disc compilation Double Bummer + (1989, Shimmy Disc Europe) and its later reissues.

Also in 1987, Cochrane is credited on a Tellus Audio Cassette Magazine (Tellus #15 – The Improvisors, 1986) — an early appearance in the organized Knitting Factory / downtown improv documentation.

1988 sees him on Exquisite Corpses From The Bunker (Heartpunch) and the Swiss recital tour document This Lost Leg (1989, RecRec Music) — the Swiss RecRec connection indicating early European avant-music circulation for No Safety.

What Stops Us and Solo Debut (1989)

What Stops Us (1989, self-released) is Cochrane’s first primary-credited solo album. He plays guitar, drum machine, vocals, and produces; the record features what would become his core collaborators: Ann Rupel (bass, piano), Pippin Barnett (drums), Tim Spelios (drums), Doug Seidel (guitar, sampler), Zeena Parkins (piano, harp), and Tom Cora (piano, organ, guitar; also co-producer). Paul Haines contributes words to three tracks; George Cartwright co-writes two. The record is a snapshot of the entire No Safety / downtown orbit in a single session.

No Safety records (1992–1993)

No Safety’s studio LP Spill (1992, Knitting Factory Works) gathers the same constellation: Cochrane on guitar, sampler, vocals; Rupel on bass and vocals; Parkins on keyboards, accordion, vocals, and electric harp; Seidel on guitar; Spelios on drums. David Shea guests on turntables and sampler; the record is co-produced by David Weinstein and No Safety; engineer John Siket and Jon Rosenberg record it. Spill was simultaneously released on Swiss RecRec Music (cat. RecDec 28). An earlier RecRec pressing exists as RecDec 28.

Live At The Knitting Factory (1993, Knitting Factory Works) documents a concert performance with the same lineup — Cochrane also writes the liner notes (credited as “C. Cochrane”). Executive-produced by Michael Dorf (Knitting Factory founder).

Around this period Cochrane contributes guitar to the Exquisite Corpses From P.S. 122 compilation (1990, ¿What Next? Recordings) — a dense gathering of the P.S. 122 performance space community including Ikue Mori, Parkins, Cartwright, Anthony Coleman, Seidel, Spelios, Fredrick Lonberg-Holm, Catherine Jauniaux, David Watson, and Doug Henderson. He also plays on Knitting Factory Goes To The Northwest (1991, Knitting Factory Works), the Ursa’s Door compilation (1992, Les Disques Victo), and Doing The Don’t (1993, Rift).

Solo work and Avant/Tzadik orbit (1994–1998)

Dot (1994, Cuneiform Records) — a compilation or release Cochrane contributes guitar to. Split (1994, Coat-Tail Records) — co-billed or split release. Restore (1995, Fang Records) — Cochrane as electric guitarist and producer.

Bath (1995, Avant) is Cochrane’s most focused early solo statement: 18 tracks, guitar and vocals throughout, co-produced with Marc Anthony Thompson (aka Chocolate Genius; also plays tambourine, percussion, and music on track 1). Ann Rupel plays bass on six tracks; Hanna Fox plays drums and percussion on six tracks; Zeena Parkins plays harp across most of the album. Executive-produced by John Zorn and Disk Union — placing Bath squarely in the Avant label orbit (Zorn’s joint imprint with Disk Union). Harmolodic Jeopardy (1995, Avant) includes Cochrane in an additional guitar role.

3 Heads (1996, Knitting Factory Works / Factory Outlet) is co-credited to “Chris Cochrane’s Suck Pretty” — a band consisting of Cochrane (guitar, sampler, organ, voice), Dominic Richards (bass), Dudley Saunders (voice), and Ed Ware (drums, percussion). Cochrane and Bryce Goggin co-produce; Goggin also records, engineers, and mixes. Anthony Coleman guests on piano. Words and music are largely Cochrane’s, with one track (“15”) credited to Brian Wilson. Executive-produced by Dorf and Mark Perlson.

Also in 1998, Cochrane contributes guitar to Fabulous Drop (1998, Cuneiform Records). Weird Little Boy (1998, Avant) — his band by that name — is a full album on Avant with John Zorn (keyboards, saxophone, sampler; executive producer), Mike Patton (vocals, drums), Trey Spruance (guitars, drums, keyboards), William Winant (percussion), and Dennis Cooper (texts). Produced and designed by Ikue Mori. Mastered by Allan Tucker. A true downtown-meets-downtown-meets-Mr. Bungle collision.

Cochrane appears in the Bongwater compilation reissues through 1999 (Knitting Factory Records).

The Opium War (1999, Einstein Records) — Cochrane on guitar and voice. He also contributes slide guitar and songwriter credit to Eszter Balint’s Flicker (1999, Scratchie Records), produced by JD Foster, with Marc Ribot and other downtown figures.

As I Hack This Rumpus Of Shapes (1997, self-released Dim Sum Clip Job) documents the Dim Sum Clip Job project.

Curlew Paradise (1996)

Cochrane joins Curlew on Paradise (1996, Cuneiform Records) — guitar and vocals alongside Davey Williams, saxophonist George Cartwright (also producer), Ann Rupel (bass, vocals), Samm Bennett (drums, percussion), and guest Jim Spake (baritone/soprano sax). Recorded and mixed by Rusty McFarland. Cover art by Anne Elias; design by Ruth Peyser. Executive-produced by Steven Feigenbaum (Cuneiform founder). He continues in later Curlew lineups; see also the archival Cuneiform releases Live In Italy 1994 (2021) and Live At Phantasmagoria And WFMU (2022), on which he appears.

Them, Geologic Time, and later solo work (2011–2024)

Them (2011, Tzadik) is co-primary credited to Cochrane, Dennis Cooper (writer), and choreographer Ishmael Houston-Jones. Cochrane composes and plays guitars, percussion, bass, accordion, keyboards, tapes, and co-produces. Kato Hideki plays bass, percussion, keyboards, mandolin, and co-produces. Mastered by Scott Hull (2). Executive-produced by John Zorn; associate produced by Kazunori Sugiyama. The work was originally a live performance collaboration with Houston-Jones and Cooper.

Derek Bailey Tribute Band (2013, Ictus Records) — Cochrane on electric guitar in a tribute ensemble including Anders Nilsson (5), Marco Cappelli, Andrea Centazzo, David Beals, and documentary photography by Roberto Masotti.

Geologic Time (2017, Infrequent Seams) is a primary duo with Stuart Popejoy — Cochrane on guitar, Popejoy on bass and mixed by; Kato Hideki masters. Photography by Rick Shupper. Layout by Alessandro Urso.

Excavation (2022, Astral Editions) is a primary duo with John Thayer (drums, percussion, electronics, field recording). The record features an ensemble cast on specific tracks: Zeena Parkins (harp, track 3), Stuart Popejoy (organ, electric bass, tracks 3 and 6), Kato Hideki (electric and double bass, tracks 1, 3, 7), Graham Haynes (cornet, track 1), Stan Zenkov (tenor saxophone/woodwind, tracks 3, 4, 7, 8), Sarah Bernstein (violin, track 7), Gelsey Bell (voice/vocoder, tracks 1, 5, 7).

Artemisia (2024, Infrequent Seams) is a primary duo with Loren MazzaCane Connors — both on guitar. The larger ensemble for the record includes James Ilgenfritz (bass), Stephen Haynes (brass), Gabby Fluke-Mogul (violin). Mastered by Elliott Sharp. Photography by Peter Gannushkin; design by Dustin Krcatovich.

In 2024, Cochrane also contributes cover art and electric guitar/tape to Curlew’s archival Was It 1984 Or 1985? (Cuneiform Records).

Also active with Alphawhore (guitar, mixed by; self-released 2019; Zoar Records 2024) and contributing guitar to HOME (2019, Gold Bolus Recordings) and Pala Pala (2016, Bad Friend Records).

He produced and co-mixed Gordon Beeferman’s Organ Trio (2019, Minor Amusements), alongside Anders Nilsson (5) (guitar), Ches Smith (drums), and Kato Hideki (mastered).
