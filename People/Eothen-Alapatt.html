Eothen Alapatt

Discogs IDs: 902322 (as Eothen Alapatt) · 104275 (as Egon)
Also known as: Egon
Real name: Eothen Aram Alapatt
Born: July 12, 1978, Bridgeport, Connecticut, US
Active: 1998–present

American record executive, reissue producer, compiler, DJ, and writer. Founder and president of Now-Again Records. Creative director of the J Dilla estate. Partner with Madlib in Madlib Invazion. Served as general manager of Stones Throw Records from 2000 to 2011. Operates Sinecure Books, an independent publishing house for books and records focused on 20th/21st-century popular art, music, and photography.

Biography

Early Work and Arrival at Stones Throw (1998–2001)

Alapatt’s first documented Discogs credit dates to 1998: a compilation entry for 113 Audio Resources volume two on 113 Audio, reflecting an early interest in archival and curatorial work. In 1999 he produced a live recording for Nashville’s college radio station WRVU (91 WRVU). By 2001 he was working at Stones Throw Records — founded by Peanut Butter Wolf — as general manager and project coordinator, taking on roles ranging from label administration to liner notes and reissue production.

His first visible Stones Throw credits include project coordination on The Live Mix Part 2 (2000) and reissue production on Poppin’ Pop Corn b/w The Funky Sixteen Corners (2000). By 2001 he was the credited compilation producer and liner-notes author on The Funky 16 Corners, a pivotal soul-funk archival release that established both Stones Throw’s reissue credentials and Egon’s voice as an annotator. Simultaneously he began building Now-Again Records as a Stones Throw subsidiary dedicated to soul, funk, and psychedelic reissues.

Stones Throw A&R and Label Management (2002–2011)

Through the 2000s Alapatt was one of the two or three people most responsible for the operational identity of Stones Throw. His roles across 222 releases on the label span coordinator, A&R, executive producer, project manager, and management. Key projects in this period:

  • Madvillainy (Madvillain, 2004) — project coordination. The Madlib/MF DOOM collaboration became one of the most celebrated underground hip-hop albums of the decade.
  • Champion Sound (Jaylib, 2003) — label management credit (as Egon). The Madlib/J Dilla collaboration produced via an intercontinental tape exchange.
  • The Third Unheard: Connecticut Hip Hop 1979–1983 (Stones Throw, 2004) — produced, coordinated, annotated, and researched. An early example of his deeper archival compiler work, focused on pre-commercial East Coast hip-hop recordings.
  • The Funky Side of Life (2005), Chrome Children (2006), Ode To The Ghetto (2008) — A&R, project coordination, and production credits across Stones Throw’s mid-period hip-hop and beat-music catalog.
  • Ruff Draft (J Dilla, Stones Throw reissue, 2007) — coordinated the reissue.
  • Good Things (Aloe Blacc, 2010) — executive producer, A&R, coordinator; 15 pressings across territories.
  • Madvillainy 2: The Madlib Remix (2008) — A&R.

He also served as general manager throughout this period, meaning he oversaw the label’s day-to-day operations, licensing, and release scheduling while simultaneously running Now-Again.

Founding Now-Again Records (2002–2011)

Now-Again Records launched in 2002 as a Stones Throw subsidiary. Alapatt founded and ran it as an outlet for soul, funk, psychedelic rock, and global music reissues that were too specialized for the Stones Throw main catalog. The label was independent from 2012 onward.

Early Now-Again work in the Stones Throw years includes the Soul 7 compilation (2003), the Lamp Records reissues Everybody Who Wants To Be Free and Hum-Bug (2003), Cold Heat: Heavy Funk Rarities 1968–1974 (2004), and Texas Thunder Soul 1968–1974 (2006). By the late 2000s Now-Again was releasing compilations spanning funk, jazz, psychedelic rock, Brazilian music, and what would become Alapatt’s signature archival territory: African rock and Zamrock.

Zamrock and World Reissue Work (2010–2020)

From around 2010, Alapatt’s most distinctive and celebrated archival work concentrated on African rock — especially Zambian rock (Zamrock), Nigerian rock, and Ethiopian music. The Welcome To Zamrock! series (two volumes, 2016–2017) compiled the output of Zambia’s 1970s rock underground. We Intend To Cause Havoc! (Witch (3), 2012) was one of the first major Western reissues of the genre. Dark Sunrise (Rikki Ililonga & Musi O Tunya, 2010) was an early contribution. The Wake Up You! two-volume Nigerian rock anthology (2016) was produced alongside scholar Uchenna Ikonne.

Other significant global archival projects in this period:

  • The Story of Zamrock! (2020) — a comprehensive retrospective of the Zambian scene
  • Back From The Brink: Pre-Revolution Psychedelic Rock from Iran 1973–1979 (2011)
  • Beautiful Rivers And Mountains: The Psychedelic Rock Sound of South Korea’s Shin Joong Hyun 1958–1974 (2011) — research and photos credit (for Light In The Attic)
  • Those Shocking Shaking Days: Indonesian Hard, Psychedelic, Progressive Rock and Funk 1970–1978 (2011)
  • Ethiopian music projects (Che Belew, Good Aderegechegn, 2020)

Dave Cooley (Discogs ID: 219192) was the near-universal mastering and restoration engineer for this work. Errol F. Richardson (Discogs ID: 2159823) handled art direction on a large portion of the Now-Again catalog.

Madlib Invazion and Freddie Gibbs (2010–2019)

Following the end of his formal role at Stones Throw (2011), Alapatt became a partner in Madlib Invazion, Madlib’s self-run imprint. His Madlib Invazion credits include:

  • Piñata (Freddie Gibbs & Madlib, 2014) — “Producer [Madlib Invazion]” — 27 pressings, one of the most critically recognized hip-hop albums of the decade.
  • Bandana (Freddie Gibbs & Madlib, 2019) — A&R and producer alongside Ben “Lambo” Lambert, Rich Gains, and the artists themselves.
  • Fly Tunes / Channel 85 Presents Nittyville (2011), various Madlib Invazion singles and EPs through 2024.

His role at Madlib Invazion runs parallel to Now-Again and represents the contemporary hip-hop side of his work as distinct from the archival reissue side.

J Dilla Estate Work (2007–2018)

Alapatt’s relationship with the J Dilla legacy extended well past Dilla’s 2006 death. Key credits:

  • Ruff Draft (STH reissue, 2007) — coordinated the first formal reissue of Dilla’s 2003 Dilladelph/Hookage EP.
  • Anthem / Trucks (Pay Jay, 2013) — reissue producer on Dilla’s Jay Dee-era 7-inch.
  • The Diary (Mass Appeal / Pay Jay, 2016) — “Produced For Issue” — the posthumous album compiled from recordings Dilla made in his final years. A&R on the project also included House Shoes and J Rocc.
  • Ruff Draft (Dilla’s Mix) (Pay Jay, 2018) — reissue producer on the expanded reissue restoring Dilla’s original intended mix sequence.

He is credited as creative director of the J Dilla estate, a role that encompasses licensing, archival decisions, and long-term legacy management.

Now-Again Independent Period and Tribe Records (2012–present)

After Now-Again became fully independent in 2012, its catalog continued to expand across funk, soul, jazz, and world music reissues. Notable later projects include:

  • Lamp Records: It Glowed Like The Sun (2018) — the Indianapolis soul label’s story, a project Alapatt had begun researching with the early Lamp reissues in 2003–2004.
  • Tribe (3) reissues (2021–2022) — a run of reissues from the early Detroit jazz/post-bop label Tribe Records, whose alumni include Harold McKinney and Doug Hammond: Farewell To The Welfare, A Message From The Tribe, An Evening With The Devil, The Time Is Now!, Vibes From The Tribe.
  • Memphis rap reissues (2024–2025) — a series through Now-Again’s The Origins of Memphis Rap sublabel: reissues for Gangsta Blac, DJ Screw-era Memphis artists, Gimisum Family, and related acts.
  • Black Fire Music reissues (2019) — Live At The East 1973 and Space Jungle Luv, from the Washington DC jazz imprint.

Scene Context

Alapatt’s career spans two partly overlapping worlds: the early-2000s Los Angeles hip-hop underground (centered on Stones Throw) and the post-2002 global music reissue circuit. At Stones Throw he was present for the label’s most consequential period — the years that produced Madvillainy, Champion Sound, and J Dilla’s transition to the West Coast — functioning as the operational glue between the label’s founder (Peanut Butter Wolf) and its talent. His departure from Stones Throw in 2011 coincided with the label moving in a more contemporary direction.

Now-Again’s reissue catalog is eclectic by design: 1960s–70s American funk and soul sit alongside Zambian rock, Nigerian Afrobeat-adjacent rock, Indonesian psych, Iranian prog, and Ethiopian music. The common thread is Alapatt’s role as a researcher and annotator who writes or edits the liner notes across nearly every release. Dave Cooley’s consistent presence as audio restorer and mastering engineer gives the catalog a sonic coherence across its geographic diversity.

His editorial and publishing work through Sinecure Books (label ID: 544663 in Discogs profile) extends the annotation work into standalone volumes.
