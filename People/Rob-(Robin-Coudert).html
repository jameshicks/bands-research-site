Rob (Robin Coudert)

French composer, session musician, and film/television scorer. Real name Robin Coudert. Discogs artist ID: 2275. Also credited under his real name “Robin Coudert” (Discogs ID: 6726898, same person). Active from the late 1990s to present, across Paris indie-electronic, session work for French rock bands, and a substantial career composing for French cinema and television from the 2010s onward.

Overview

Rob is a French multi-instrumentalist who first appeared on record in the late 1990s as a solo artist on the Paris label Source (the same imprint behind AIR and Phoenix). His early releases were in an instrumental, electronic-inflected vein. He built a parallel career as a session player, most notably contributing clavinet to Phoenix’s United (2000) and marimba to Wolfgang Amadeus Phoenix (2009), and returning for keyboards on Alpha Zulu (2022/23).

From roughly 2012 onward he pivoted decisively into film and TV scoring. His best-known score is the remake horror film Maniac (2012, dir. Franck Khalfoun), released on Hamburger Records and later reissued by Death Waltz Recording Company. He composed the score for the American horror film Gretel & Hansel (2020, dir. Oz Perkins), released on Waxwork Records. In France he scored the espionage TV series Le Bureau des Légendes (Canal+, five seasons) and multiple features for director Coralie Fargeat, including Revenge (2018). His long-running creative partnership with engineer-producer Jack Lahana threads through both the pop and film-score strands of his career.

Career Narrative

Early solo work on Source (1999–2002)

Rob’s first releases appeared on the French electronic/indie label Source (Discogs label ID: 22255), home to AIR, Phoenix, and others in the Parisian electronic-pop scene. His debut, Musique Pour Un Enfant Jouet (1999), is a solo instrumental work. He followed it with Amours (2000), Don’t Kill (2000), Power Glove (2001, also co-released on Virgin), and Satyred Love (2002, credited as “Performer, Producer”). These releases place him squarely in the late-1990s / early-2000s Paris electronic scene centered around Source.

During the same period he appeared as a session musician:

  • Shirley Bassey meets Booster — “Light My Fire” (Blue Note, 1998): Korg MS-20 synthesizer
  • Phoenix — “If I Ever Feel Better” (Virgin, 2000): Clavinet. This single is drawn from Phoenix’s debut album United; the credits here confirm Rob’s role on that album as well.

Institubes and the Dodecalogue series (2009–2010)

Rob released five volumes of the Dodecalogue series (Vols. I–V, 2009–2010) on the Paris electronic label Institubes (with the sublabel Unsunned). Discogs lists Vols. I–V, all credited to Rob as primary artist. This represents a substantial body of solo work straddling experimental electronic and contemporary classical territory. Collaborator Sanghon Kim appears across Vols. I–III.

Session credits (2006–2023)

Beyond Phoenix, Rob contributed session keyboards and percussion to a range of French and international releases:

  • Tahiti 80 — Fosbury (additional keyboards, Lucky Number, 2006)
  • Sébastien Tellier — Narco (additional arranger, Record Makers, 2007)
  • La Fiancée — Deux (keyboards and vibraphone, Polydor, 2010; produced by Jack Lahana)
  • Scratch Massive — Confection (synthesizer, Record Makers, 2013)
  • My Dreams Dictate My Reality (keys/noise, Because Music, 2015)
  • Phoenix — Wolfgang Amadeus Phoenix (marimba, 2009) and Alpha Zulu (keyboards on track B3, 2022/23)

The Air credit on Pocket Symphony is notable: “Narco” is listed with “Arranged By [Additional]” for Rob, placing him inside the same Versailles-adjacent French electronic world as Phoenix.

The Gretel & Hansel soundtrack (Waxwork Records, 2020) is his most internationally distributed score: he composed, performed, and produced the entire album, with Jack Lahana as engineer/mixer and Moritz Reich on cello.

Le Bureau des Légendes is his most sustained TV project — the Canal+ espionage thriller ran five seasons. Music Box Records (2) (Discogs label ID: 252361) is the primary French OST label for his film work throughout 2016–2019.
