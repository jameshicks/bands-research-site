Glen Johnson

Discogs ID: 249091. British musician, songwriter, and label owner. Founder and creative constant of Piano Magic (1996–2016). Also records as Textile Ranch. Co-founded Second Language Records. Worked at Rough Trade Records (A&R, 1994–2003) and Proper Records/Navigator Records (from c. 2012); took on day-to-day management of Topic Records (world’s oldest independent label) when Proper entered a label management agreement with Topic in January 2017.

Background

Grew up on a council estate in a pit village on the Nottinghamshire/Derbyshire border near Alfreton. Working-class family. Older brother, four years his senior, worked in a factory and introduced Johnson to music by sending him to record shops with £50 to buy records — Echo and the Bunnymen, Teardrop Explodes. By age 12 was experimenting with electronic sounds, buying items from Tandy and amplifying percussion through contact microphones. Early influence: Cabaret Voltaire.

Teenage ambition was to be a short story writer, drawn to surreal fantasy. Around age 13, placed an advertisement in Record Mirror seeking “experimental avant-garde music” for a proposed label, inspired by Some Bizarre Records. The response overwhelmed him with cassette submissions but the project stalled.

Moved to London; met Dominic Chennell (fellow Nottingham exile) and founded Piano Magic at Brecknock Road, North London in 1996.

Piano Magic

Sole constant member throughout the band’s existence, writing all lyrics and the majority of music. Originally credited on debut material as G.A. Johnson. The band’s name came from “a kitsch, junkshop piano medley 10-inch” he found. His original artistic vision of the project — as a collective vehicle with rotating contributors — was realized most completely in Writers Without Homes (2002, 4AD) and found a final expression in Closure (2016, Second Language), particularly on “Attention To Life,” featuring Peter Walsh of The Apartments, which Johnson considered the album’s highlight.

Closing song at the final Piano Magic concert (The Lexington, 3 December 2016): “The Last Engineer” — dedicated to his recently deceased father.

Saint Marie EP (2004) — Alan Sparhawk collaboration

The Saint Marie EP (June 2004) featured guest contributions from Vashti Bunyan, Alan Sparhawk of Low, and Ben Ayres of Cornershop. Sparhawk’s collaboration took the form of a studio vocal contribution: he recorded a version of the title track “Saint Marie” at The Fortress studio (near Old Street, London). In his Piano Magic Diaries essay on The Troubled Sleep of Piano Magic, Johnson wrote of Sparhawk’s version: “The Alan Sparhawk version on the ‘Saint Marie EP,’ also recorded at The Fortress, was somewhat superfluous in hindsight, much as I love his voice.” [Source: arcane-delights.com/2022/04/12/piano-magic-diaries-the-troubled-sleep/, as of 2026-08-15; confirmed by piano-magic.co.uk/biography/]

The collaboration was a one-off studio guest appearance on a single EP track; no touring, side project, or further recording between Johnson and Sparhawk is documented.

Textile Ranch

Solo electronic/experimental project. Albums:

  • Bird Heart In Wool (Sep 2004)
  • Murderer/Gardener (Jan 2007)
  • The Rest, I Leave To The Poor (Oct 2008)
  • Tombola (launched Second Language label, 2009)
  • Ombilical + Prostheses (bonus disc) (May 2019) — features Amanda Butterworth, Oliver Cherer, Ola Szmidt, Franck Alba
  • Unbosoming (Jan 2020)

Future Conditional

Electro-pop project with Cédric Pin (former Piano Magic member). Releases:

  • We Don’t Just Disappear (2007, also ID’d as “Future Conditional” in Discogs credits on Part Monster)
  • The Burning Skull (2018)
  • Isotech (Sep 2022)

Second Language Records

Co-founded around 2009 with David Sheppard and Martin Jensen. The label’s aesthetic: “a rainy, wet night in Brussels, in 1982” — drawing on Les Disques du Crépuscule and Durutti Column. Notable releases include Mark Fry, Sharron Kraus, Oliver Cherer, The Home Current. Early compilations benefited Birdlife International.

Career Before Piano Magic / Label Work

Before founding Piano Magic, Johnson worked at Rough Trade Records as A&R Director and Label Manager (1994–2003), including the Tugboat Records imprint. During this period he worked on releases by The Strokes, The Libertines, Belle & Sebastian, Sufjan Stevens, Low, British Sea Power, and Life Without Buildings. [Source: LinkedIn profile, linkedin.com/in/glen-johnson-b6408935 — treat as self-reported; not independently corroborated.]

After leaving Rough Trade, Johnson joined Proper Records (the distribution and label group) around 2012, managing their in-house folk imprint Navigator Records as well as Proper Records itself. [Source: LinkedIn profile; dates 2012–2025.]

Topic Records

In January 2017, Proper Records entered a label management agreement with Topic Records, with Topic’s co-owners Tony Engle (majority shareholder, joined Topic in the 1970s) and David Suff seeking help with day-to-day operations. Johnson has described the approach as: “David and Tony from Topic approached us and said ‘We need someone else to take over the day-to-day running of it… we’re sort of exhausted’. So we volunteered, and I was the person to do it.” [Source: Haunted Generation interview, May 2020, hauntedgeneration.co.uk/2020/05/22/glen-johnson-second-language-and-textile-ranch/]

Johnson has noted that he does not consider himself a folk enthusiast but respects the label’s historical significance and the quality of its artists. He maintains relationships with key Topic artists including Martin Carthy, Norma Waterson, Eliza Carthy, Martin Simpson, and Anne Briggs. He describes his Topic role as his “day job,” distinct from his creative work on Second Language. [Source: Haunted Generation interview, as above.]

The Proper-Topic deal was announced 17 January 2017 and framed as preserving Topic’s independence while Proper handles operations. Tony Engle remained majority shareholder; David Suff moved to an advisory capacity. Drew Hill (MD, Proper Records) was named in the announcement; Johnson was not mentioned in the Music Week report of the deal. [Source: Music Week, musicweek.com/labels/read/proper-strikes-deal-with-veteran-folk-label-topic-records/067162]

His LinkedIn lists the role as “Project Manager, Topic Records / Navigator Records / Proper Records, 2012–2025.” [Source: LinkedIn, as above.]
