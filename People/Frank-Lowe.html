Frank Lowe

Frank Lowe

Jul 15, 20268 min read

Frank Lowe

Discogs ID: 145244
Born June 24, 1943, Memphis, Tennessee. Died September 19, 2003, New York City. American free jazz tenor saxophonist and composer. A central figure in the NYC loft jazz scene of the 1970s and one of the most prolific post-Coltrane tenormen of his generation. Recorded primarily for ESP-Disk’, Black Saint, Soul Note, and CIMP across a career spanning three decades.

Also known under name variations: F. Lowe, Freedom Frank Lowe, Frank Loew.

Biography

Memphis Roots and Early New York (to 1972)

Lowe grew up in Memphis before moving to the West Coast and eventually settling in New York, where he became embedded in the downtown avant-garde. His earliest documented Discogs credit is as a sideman on Alice Coltrane’s World Galaxy (1972, Impulse!), a major orchestral session that also featured Leroy Jenkins and Reggie Workman.

The ESP-Disk’ Debut and Loft Jazz (1973–1976)

1973 was a breakthrough year. Lowe released Black Beings on ESP-Disk’ — his debut as a leader — with a band comprising Joseph Jarman (soprano and alto sax), a violinist credited as “The Wizard” (later identified as Raymond Lee Cheng), Rashid Sinan (drums), and William Parker (bass). Parker’s appearance here is among his earliest on record. The record’s energy is raw and collective, in the lineage of Ayler and late Coltrane.

That same year he co-led Duo Exchange (Survival Records SR 101) with drummer Rashied Ali — one of the inaugural releases on Ali’s self-run Survival Records imprint. Lowe plays tenor sax, Japanese flute, and percussion; the session is fully improvised. He also appeared as a sideman on Relativity Suite (1973, JCOA Records), a large-ensemble date led by Don Cherry and the Jazz Composer’s Orchestra, alongside Charlie Haden, Carla Bley, Paul Motian, Ed Blackwell, Dewey Redman, and Leroy Jenkins.

Fresh (1975, Freedom / Arista) was his second leader date and showed a broader palette: Lester Bowie on trumpet, Joseph Bowie on trombone, Abdul Wadud on cello, Charles Bobo Shaw and Steve Reid sharing drums duties. Lowe produced the record himself and wrote the sleeve notes. Executive production was by Alan Bates (Freedom Records).

In 1975 he also appeared on Don Cherry’s Brown Rice (EMI Italy pressing), a funk-inflected free session.

The Flam (1976, Black Saint) was Lowe’s Italian-label debut: a quartet with Wadada Leo Smith (trumpet/flugelhorn/woodflute), Joseph Bowie (trombone), and Alex Blake (bass). Produced by Giacomo Pellicciotti.

Late 1970s: Europe and the Quartet (1977–1979)

Lowe was active in Europe through the late 1970s, recording The Other Side (1977, Palm, France) with his Frank Lowe Quartet — the session featured Butch Morris on cornet and French bassist Didier Levallet. Tricks Of The Trade (1977, Marge, France) was another French-label appearance.

Lowe And Behold (1977, Musicworks) is his most expansive release of the period: Discogs lists it as a sideman credit but it carries 36 pressings, suggesting wide circulation and probable co-leadership. The same year he released The Frank Lowe Orchestra (1977, Musicworks) under that ensemble name.

Positions 3 6 9 (1978, Kharma) was a collective trio record with Jerome Cooper (drums, multi) and Kalaparusha (Maurice McIntyre, tenor sax, clarinet, flute). Cooper composed and arranged.

Don’t Punk Out (1979, QED Records) is a co-leadership date with guitarist Eugene Chadbourne, recorded by Martin Davidson. A sax-and-guitar duo in the improvised tradition.

1980s: Soul Note and the Mature Voice (1982–1989)

Exotic Heartbreak (1982, Soul Note) was a sideman credit. Also in 1982 he played soprano saxophone on Billy Bang’s large-ensemble Outline No. 12 (Celluloid, France), alongside David Murray, Butch Morris (conductor), Khan Jamal (vibraphone), Jason Hwang (violin), Charles Tyler, Sunny Murray, and Wilber Morris. Decision In Paradise (1985, Soul Note) is the summit of his Soul Note output as leader — a quintet with Don Cherry (pocket trumpet), Grachan Moncur III (trombone), Geri Allen (piano), and the father-son rhythm section of Charles Moffett and Charnett Moffett. Carmen Lowe contributed textile artwork (a recurring collaborator, likely family). Produced by Giovanni Bonandrini.

In 1986 he appeared on Current Trends In Racism In Modern America (Sound Aspects Records, Germany), a politically charged free jazz statement.

1990s: CIMP, Billy Bang, and the Late Period (1990–2003)

The 1990s saw Lowe working frequently with violinist Billy Bang. They shared membership in the Billy Bang Quintet, the Frank Lowe / Billy Bang Quartet, The Frank Lowe Orchestra, and The Jazz Doctors — a unusually durable cross-leader pairing. Above & Beyond: An Evening In Grand Rapids (2003, Justin Time), credited to Billy Bang featuring Frank Lowe, was released the year Lowe died, and stands as one of his final recordings.

Vision Blue (1997, CIMP) is the key late-period leader date: Frank Lowe Trio with Steve Neil (bass) and Anders Griffen (drums), recorded and produced by Bob Rusch of Cadence/CIMP. Lowe composed most of the tracks.

He also appeared on Vietnam: The Aftermath (2001, Justin Time), a Billy Bang-led suite dealing with the Vietnam War and its aftermath, recorded with a large ensemble including veterans.

The Loweski (2012, ESP-Disk’) is a posthumous release restoring the Black Beings group — William Parker, Rashid Sinan, Joseph Jarman, and “The Wizard” (Raymond Lee Cheng on violin) — with liner notes by Clifford Allen dated September 2011. The release documents a reunion session completed before Lowe’s death or draws on archival material.
