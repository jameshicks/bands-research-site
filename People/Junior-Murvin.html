Junior Murvin

Jamaican roots reggae vocalist (born Murvin Junior Smith, c. 1946, Saint James, Jamaica; died 2 December 2013, Port Antonio, Jamaica). Best known for the 1976 single “Police and Thieves,” produced by Lee Perry at the Black Ark studio and released internationally on Island Records. Discogs artist ID: 94501. Real name: Murvin Junior Smith.

Overview

Junior Murvin grew up in Port Antonio and Montego Bay, studying mechanics before moving to Trenchtown, Kingston in 1967. He was deeply influenced by Curtis Mayfield and began performing with Kingston’s leading live bands — the Hippy Boys, the Mighty Falcons, and the Tornadoes — in nightclubs and tourist hotels. His earliest recording work was as “Junior Soul” for Sonia Pottinger’s Gayfeet label, then for Derrick Harriott’s Crystal label, where “Solomon” became a minor hit in 1972.

In 1976 he auditioned for Lee Perry with the song “Police and Thieves,” written in the context of Jamaica’s police violence and street-gang conflicts. Perry agreed to record it at his Black Ark studio in Washington Gardens, Kingston, and the single became an international hit — peaking at #23 on the UK Singles Chart in 1980, partly on the strength of its use in the film Rockers (1978). The full album, Police & Thieves (Island Records, 1977), produced by Perry with the Upsetters as backing band, is regarded as one of Perry’s defining productions and part of what Wikipedia describes as the Black Ark “holy trinity” alongside the Heptones’ Party Time and Max Romeo’s War Ina Babylon.

The title track was covered by The Clash on their 1977 debut album — one of the most prominent reggae-to-punk transmissions of the era.

Murvin continued recording through the 1980s with multiple Jamaican producers: Joe Gibbs and Errol Thompson (the “Cool Out Son” LP, 1979), Henry “Junjo” Lawes and the Roots Radics (Muggers In The Street, Greensleeves, 1984), Prince Jammy / King Jammy (Apartheid, 1986), and Mikey Dread (Bad Man Posse, Dread At The Controls, 1982). He also self-produced at least two singles on his own small Murvin label in Port Antonio in later years. His last notable LP was Signs & Wonders (Live & Learn Records / Live & Love, 1988–89), with the Roots Radics rhythm section. He appeared on the acoustic collective Inna De Yard (Makasound, France, 2007), produced by Earl “Chinna” Smith.

He died 2 December 2013 of complications from diabetes and hypertension in Port Antonio, where he had lived since the 1990s. (Sources: Wikipedia — Junior Murvin; Discogs profile.)

Aliases / Name Variations

Murvin recorded under several names:

  • Junior Soul (2) — earliest recordings, Gayfeet and Crystal labels
  • Murvin Junior Smith — Discogs alias, also used as a co-writer credit on Inna De Yard
  • Common spelling variants on sleeves: J. Murvin, Junior Mervin, Junior Marvin, Murvin, Mervin
