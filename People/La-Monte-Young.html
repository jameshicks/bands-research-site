La Monte Young

Composer, performer, drone music pioneer. Full name: La Monte Thornton Young. Born October 14, 1935, Bern, Idaho. Discogs ID: 32203.

Generally recognized as one of the first minimalist composers and a founding figure of American avant-garde music. Grew up in Los Angeles; played saxophone in a high school group with Billy Higgins, Dennis Budimir, and Don Cherry. Met Terry Riley at UC Berkeley. Moved to New York, where he curated a series of concerts at Yoko Ono’s loft (112 Chambers St, 1960–61) — the event conventionally cited as the opening of Downtown Music Scene as a cultural space.

Studied electronic music with Richard Maxfield; became associated with Fluxus artists. Published An Anthology (1963) with Jackson Mac Low. Formed the Theatre Of Eternal Music with John Cale, Tony Conrad, Angus MacLise, and Marian Zazeela — performing sustained-tone, drone-based works. Cale and MacLise’s work in this group directly shaped the drone aesthetics they brought to The Velvet Underground. Long-term partner and collaborator: Marian Zazeela. Both became disciples of Pandit Pran Nath and devoted effort to preserving Kirana-style Indian classical music. Top labels: Gramavision; Not On Label (self-released).
