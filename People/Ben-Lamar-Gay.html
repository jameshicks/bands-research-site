Ben Lamar Gay

Discogs ID: 4005894 Also credited as: Ben LaMar Gay, Benjamin Lamar Gay, Ben Lamar, BLG, LaMar Gay, Ben LaMarGay

Composer, cornetist, vocalist, and multi-instrumentalist based in Chicago. A significant figure in the city’s experimental jazz and improvised-music scene, Gay plays in the orbit of the The AACM Great Black Music Ensemble and has released three solo LPs on International Anthem Recording Company (2018, 2021, 2022) plus a fourth in 2025. His instrument palette spans cornet, flute, voice, synth, diddley bow, balafon, citara, pandeiro, melodica, electronics, organ, percussion, and beatbox — a range that marks him as a composer-performer in the broadest sense rather than a specialist.

Background and Early Work

Gay’s Discogs footprint begins with a 2010 live album (At Umbria Jazz 2009, Musica Jazz), where he is credited on trumpet. He is a member of the The AACM Great Black Music Ensemble (Discogs ID 4005893), the long-running collective of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians — placing him in direct lineage with one of Chicago’s most important experimental institutions. Fellow AACM Ensemble members include Tomeka Reid, Nicole Mitchell, Douglas Ewart, George Lewis (2), and Ernest “Khabeer” Dawkins.

Two-person group Juba Dance (Discogs ID 912507), with William Waheed Freyman, is an early ensemble credit. Gay also appears in Greg Ward & 10 Tongues (Discogs ID 6670270, led by saxophonist Greg Ward (2)), and in Matthew Lux’s Matthew Lux’s Communication Arts Quartet (Discogs ID 6038584) alongside Jayve Montgomery and Mikel Avery.

El Is A Sound Of Joy and Bottle Tree

Two tightly-knit ensembles anchor Gay’s mid-2010s period:

El Is A Sound Of Joy (Discogs ID 5022025) is a trio with Rob Frye and Will Faber (3). All three appear consistently through the solo discography.

Bottle Tree (Discogs ID 5715041) is a trio with Rob Frye and Tommaso Moretti, Gay’s single most frequent Discogs collaborator (17 shared releases). Bottle Tree released its self-titled album on International Anthem Recording Company in 2017, with Gay credited as Producer and Arranger.

Downtown Castles Can Never Block The Sun (2018)

Gay’s debut solo LP is a compilation-style assemblage of recordings, co-compiled and sequenced with Scott McNiece (International Anthem label figure). Personnel across tracks include Rob Frye (bass clarinet, flute), Jayve Montgomery (tenor saxophone), Tommaso Moretti (drums, flute), Will Faber (3) (guitar, synth, flute), Hanna Brock (viola, voice), Joshua Sirotiak (tuba, flute), Gira Dahnee (voice), Zuzu Fé (voice), Edinho Gerber (co-producer), Alex Inglizian (mixed), M’rald Calhoun (violin), Polyphonic (5) (synth/drum programming), Richard Malstrom (recorded/mixed), Ed Bornstein (performer), and Stephen Ptacek (mixed). Mastered by David Allen (10).

Open Arms To Open Us (2021)

A denser, more orchestrated solo album. Core band: Tommaso Moretti (drums, percussion, xylophone), Rob Frye (flute, percussion), Ayanna Woods (electric bass, voice), Matthew Davis (4) (trombone, tuba), Adam Zanolini (oboe, soprano saxophone), Johanna Brock (viola, violin), Tomeka Reid (cello, voice). Additional voices: Dorothee Munyaneza, Macie Stewart, Onye Ozuzu, Sima Cunningham, A.Martinez. Produced with Dave Vettraino (engineer, mix, co-producer). Mastered by Dave Cooley.

Certain Reveries (2022)

More intimate — Gay’s core instruments plus Tommaso Moretti (drums). Recorded and mixed by Neil Strauch. Mastered by David Allen (10).

Yowzers (2025)

Returns to the ensemble format. Band: Tommaso Moretti (drums, percussion, voice), Rob Frye (flute, bass clarinet), Will Faber (3) (guitar, bells, voice, ngoni), Matthew Davis (4) (tuba, piano, bells, voice), Ayanna Woods (voice), Tramaine Parker (voice), Ugochi Nwaogwugwu (voice). Sequenced by Scott McNiece. Recorded/mixed by Dave Vettraino. Mastered by David Allen (10).

Scene Context

Gay is a product of Chicago’s post-AACM experimental jazz ecosystem. His career overlaps significantly with the International Anthem Recording Company cohort — Jaimie Branch, Makaya McCraven, Jeff Parker, Tomeka Reid, Jason Ajemian — and extends outward to European avant-garde networks (The Notwist, We Jazz, Warp/Duophonic) and US free-improv nodes (Astral Spirits, Eremite). The breadth of his instrument credits and his consistent self-production suggest an artist who controls the entire compositional and sonic chain of his records.
