Diego Cortez

Born: September 30, 1946, Geneva, Illinois (real name: James Allan Curtis) Died: June 21, 2021, Burlington, North Carolina (kidney failure), aged 74 Role: Filmmaker, art curator, publicist, scene organizer Discogs artist ID: 177968

Central figure in the New York downtown scene. Co-founded the Mudd Club (77 White Street, Tribeca) in 1978 with Anya Phillips and Steve Mass — the primary social hub of the no wave/downtown scene 1978–1983. Advised Brian Eno to attend the “Bands at Artists Space” festival (1978) and document the scene — the direct catalyst for the No New York compilation. Served as Brian Eno’s production advisor on the No New York compilation LP.

In 1981, curated New York/New Wave at P.S. 1 Contemporary Art Center — a landmark survey featuring Andy Warhol, David Byrne, William S. Burroughs, Robert Mapplethorpe, Nan Goldin, Keith Haring, Fab 5 Freddy, Futura 2000, Ann Magnuson, and Jean-Michel Basquiat; the show is credited with bringing Basquiat to mainstream gallery attention. Declined an offer to manage Madonna in 1982.

Grew up in Wheaton, Illinois; earned a BA from Illinois State University, then studied Performance Art and Film/Video at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago under Stan Brakhage and Nam June Paik (MFA, 1973). Moved to NYC in 1973 adopting the alias “Diego Cortez” (honoring his childhood’s Hispanic neighborhood); worked as studio assistant to Dennis Oppenheim and Vito Acconci.

Directed music videos for Talking Heads and Blondie. Co-edited Private Elvis (1978), a book of rare army photographs of Elvis Presley. Performed with Hermann Nitsch, Kathy Acker, and Laurie Anderson. Archive — No Wave Collection (1972–1981) and Mudd Club Collection (c. 1977–1979) — held at Cornell University.
