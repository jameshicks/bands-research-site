Siggi Baldursson

Siggi Baldursson

Oct 13, 20264 min read

Siggi Baldursson

Full name: Sigtryggur Baldursson
Discogs ID: 133611
Born: October 2, 1962, Oslo, Norway (Icelandic)
Role: Drums, percussion
Aliases: Bogomil Font; Trix (11) (ID 1214464); Tryggur (ID 2831554)

Icelandic drummer, central figure in the Reykjavík post-punk/new wave scene from its earliest formations. Played on virtually every significant record to emerge from that scene: Þeyr (1980–82), Kukl (1983–87), and The Sugarcubes (1988–92). After the Sugarcubes dissolved, continued as a session player and collaborator across Icelandic and international projects into the 2020s.

Era 1: Þeyr (1980–1982)

Baldursson’s first recorded work is with Þeyr, one of the founding bands of the Icelandic punk and new wave scene. Þeyr operated on the Reykjavík labels SG-Hljómplötur, Eskvímó (distributed via Fálkinn), and Mjöt; their recordings were among the first Icelandic post-punk documents. Masters were believed lost for years — a 2001 compilation, Mjötviður til Fóta, drew on partially recovered recordings.

Era 2: Kukl (1983–1987)

Kukl formed from Þeyr’s ashes and brought in Björk on vocals. Released on the Gramm label in Iceland and on Crass Records in the UK — the Crass connection placed the band in the anarcho-punk / experimental orbit even as the music was more song-based art-rock. Kukl toured Europe and built an international profile that paved the way for the Sugarcubes.

Era 3: The Sugarcubes (1988–1992)

The major international chapter. The Sugarcubes signed to One Little Indian in the UK and Elektra in the US, releasing three albums between 1988 and 1992.

Era 4: Dip and Post-Sugarcubes (1997–2003)

After the Sugarcubes dissolved in 1992, Baldursson’s most sustained new project was Dip, a studio duo with composer Jóhann Jóhannsson (later known for film scores including Arrival and Blade Runner 2049). Dip began in 1997 in the US, recorded at Madison Smart Studios using loop-based production methods, and invited Icelandic vocalists to contribute. Released on the Thickface label.

In 1999, Baldursson contributed writing credits to Björk’s “Ladyshave” (4AD) and performed on her “(This Is Normal).” The same year he received a writing/co-writing credit on Emiliana Torrini’s album Love In The Time Of Science (One Little Indian).

In 2001, he contributed to the Angels Of The Universe film soundtrack on FatCat Records.

In 2003, he guested on Ghostigital (Honest Jon’s Records).

In parallel during the late 1990s, Baldursson was a member of Everplastic, a Chicago-based alt/funk/electronic ensemble featuring Jim Marcus, Mars Williams, Stella Katsoudas, Greg Lucas, Vince McAley, and Xmas Smits (ID 273891).

Connections

  • Einar Örn — co-founder of The Sugarcubes; also in Ghostigital
  • Þór Eldon — Sugarcubes guitarist; Björk’s former partner
  • Bragi Ólafsson — Sugarcubes bassist; also a novelist
