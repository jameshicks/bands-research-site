Rhys Chatham

Composer, guitarist, trumpeter. Born 19 September 1952, Manhattan, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 584

Biography

Early Formation (1960s–1970)

Studied piano as a child; moved into experimental music through encounters with La Monte Young and Morton Subotnick. Both became formative influences — Young’s drone minimalism and Subotnick’s electronic synthesis left clear marks on Chatham’s later approach. Also studied with La Monte Young directly.

The Kitchen Years (1971–1980)

Appointed Music Director at The Kitchen (NYC) in 1971, a role he held until 1980. In this position he programmed the avant-garde, minimalist, and emerging downtown scene at what had become the key institutional platform for new music and performance art in New York. The Kitchen brought him into sustained contact with the entire spectrum of the NYC experimental scene — from Fluxus-aligned composers to what would become No Wave.

The Guitar Works (1977–1989)

The compositional breakthrough came in 1977 with two landmark pieces written within months of each other:

Guitar Trio (1977): Three electric guitars, electric bass, drums. First performed at Max’s Kansas City with Glenn Branca, Nina Canal, David Rosenblum, and Wharton Tiers. The piece applies La Monte Young’s drone logic to rock instrumentation — a foundational text for both No Wave and the minimalist-rock overlap. It was performed throughout the late 1970s and early 1980s and later became the repertoire for the Rhys Chatham & His Guitar Trio All-Stars (active 2007–2008 per Discogs).

Tone Death (1977): For tenor saxophone, two electric guitars, drums; 35 minutes. First performed at Franklin Furnace, NYC, with Peter Gordon (saxophone), Nina Canal (guitar), and Robert Appleton (drums). The ensemble took the piece’s name; they performed at The Kitchen (1978) and the Artists Space five-night festival (May 1978).

Artists Space, May 1978: Chatham presented two groups — Tone Death and The Gynecologists — at a five-night festival co-organized by Robert Longo and Michael Zwack. Glenn Branca’s Theoretical Girls and Daily Life also performed. Brian Eno attended; the festival directly fed the No New York documentation moment. Chatham’s ensembles were not included on that compilation.

An Angel Moves Too Fast to See (for 100 electric guitars, electric bass, and drums, 1989): The massed-guitar concept scaled dramatically upward. A recording of a Paris performance was released in 2006 on Radium (3) (release ID 1030063), mixed by Chatham and Martin Wheeler. Personnel on that release: Jonathan Kane (drums), Ernie Brooks (electric bass), plus four guitarists including Dominique Pichon, Jean-François Pauvros, Kent Condon, Robin Lyon.

Downtown Connections

Chatham was a member of The Flying Hearts, the ensemble with Arthur Russell, David Byrne, Peter Gordon, and Jerry Harrison that recorded Instrumentals (1975–79, released 2017 on Audika Records). He appeared on the Instrumentals release playing flute and wrote liner notes.

He was also part of Love of Life Orchestra (Peter Gordon’s ensemble).

In 1977 he co-produced the debut LP by jazz multi-instrumentalist Keshavan Maslak, self-titled on Atman Records (2) (release ID 9845782) — an early avant-jazz record.

Germany: Moers Music and Dossier (1983–1987)

Factor X (1983, Moers Music, cat: momu 02008, release ID 1043437): Chatham’s primary German release, produced by Burkhard Hennen. Two distinct side-long works:

  • Side A (“For Brass”): George Lewis (trombone), Jim Staley (trombone), Anton Fier (percussion), Olu Dara (trumpet), Ron Tooley / Sinclair Acey / Frank Gordon (trumpets), Bob Stewart (tuba). Bass trombone: Steve Freeman. Chatham mixed by co-credit with Hennen; recording engineer Don Hünerberg.
  • Side B (“Guitar Ring”): Karen Haglof, Susan Springfield, Craig Kafton, Tim Schellenbaum (electric guitars); James Lo (drums); Chatham (electric guitar). The guitar-ensemble approach applied to a smaller ensemble format.

Die Donnergötter (1987, Dossier Records, release ID 814040): Composed 1984–86. Recorded at BC Studio (Brooklyn, NY), April 1986. [source: search snippet corroborated by multiple web results, 2026-06-10] Composed and annotated by Chatham. Cover concept by Robert Longo; layout by Elisa Marshall. The European release was on Dossier; the US release was on Homestead Records. [source: Wikipedia Rhys Chatham, as of 2026-06-10]

The LP documents three works: “Die Donnergötter” (6 electric guitars + bass + drums; 21:36, Side A), “Waterloo No. 2” (brass ensemble; 7:50, Side B1), and “Guitar Trio” (1977; 3 guitars + bass + drums; 8:05, Side B2). Mixed by Chatham, Martin Bisi, and J.P. (7) (Side A); Wharton Tiers (Side B1); Peter Gordon and Chatham (Side B2 — “Guitar Trio”).

The Ntone Period and New Directions (1997–2002)

Two releases on London’s Ntone label demonstrate a turn toward electronics and downtown jazz fusion:

Neon (1997, Ntone, release ID 57396): Collaborative LP with Martin Wheeler (3) — the two are co-primary artists. Minimal Discogs credits beyond design (Openmind).

Septile (1997, Ntone, release ID 254641): Three-way primary credit — Chatham (trumpet), Jonathan Kane (drums, turntables, electronics), DJ Elated System / Danny Hamilton (keyboards, turntables). Photography by Art Kane.

An Angel Moves Too Fast To See: Selected Works 1971–1989 (2002, Table Of The Elements, release ID 700882): Retrospective compilation. Liner notes by Lee Ranaldo and Tony Conrad; cover photo by Robert Longo.

Large-Scale Works and Later Career (2010–2024)

A Crimson Grail (2010, Nonesuch, release ID 2544710): For 400 electric guitars. Performed outdoors at the Trocadéro, Paris. The Discogs release credits Chatham as composer and conductor; concertmaster David Daniell; produced by Regina Greene; mixed by Eric Block. The roster of 200+ guitarists is documented in full in the release credits. Notable participants include Ernie Brooks, Chris Forsyth, Robert Poss, Sarah Lipstate (Noveller), Bill Brovold, Chris Brokaw, Doug Gillard, Kevin Micka (Animal Hospital), among many others.

Outdoor Spell (2011, Northern Spy, release ID 2835674): Trumpet, voice. Produced by Regina Greene; mastered by James Plotkin. Personnel include Kevin Shea (drums), Jean-Marc Montera (electric guitar), Beatriz Rojas (cajón). Photography by David Daniell.

Harmonie Du Soir (2013, Northern Spy, release ID 5227265): No additional Discogs credits on file.

Youuu + Mee = Weeee (2014, Sub Rosa, release ID 6375178): Collaboration with Charlemagne Palestine — Chatham (guitar, trumpet, effects/loop pedal), Palestine (Bösendorfer piano, Yamaha organ, voice). Mixed and mastered by Gabriel Séverin.

Pythagorean Dream (2016, Foom, release ID 8605226): Solo self-produced, self-engineered, self-mastered. Chatham as composer, performer, producer, engineer, mastering engineer.

What’s Your Sign? (2016, Northern Spy, release ID 9339816): Collaborative LP with Oneida. All composition, recording, and mixing shared. Colin Marston (recorded by/mixed by); mastered by James Plotkin.

Discogs activity extends to 2024 (years_active field).

Downtown Scene Connections

  • Artists Space, May 1978 — Five-night festival; Chatham presented Tone Death and The Gynecologists; Glenn Branca’s Theoretical Girls also on bill; Brian Eno in attendance; led to No New York context (Chatham’s groups not included on that compilation)
  • La Monte Young — direct study; drone minimalism influence
  • Arthur Russell — Flying Hearts co-member; Russell’s First Thought Best Thought and Instrumentals
  • David Byrne — Flying Hearts co-member
  • Arto Lindsay — downtown scene overlap
  • The Kitchen — Music Director 1971–1980; institutional hub connecting all strands

Band Of Susans Connection

Band Of Susans cited Chatham as a primary influence alongside Glenn Branca. Karen Haglof (BOS guitarist on Love Agenda, 1989) had appeared in Chatham’s guitar ensemble on Factor X (1983). Robert Poss (BOS founder) later appeared as a musician on A Crimson Grail (2010, Nonesuch).
