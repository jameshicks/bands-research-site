Otto Von Schirach

Miami-based IDM/breakcore producer and live performer (real name: Otto Augusto Von Schirach). Of Cuban/German descent, active since 1998. Central figure in Schematic’s 2000s catalog — 20 primary releases on the label between 2001 and 2015, making him one of its most prolific single artists. His work fuses IDM and breakcore with Miami bass, noise, and a theatrical heavy metal visual aesthetic. Appeared on two Skinny Puppy albums and toured with the band; later moved into Miami bass revival work and developed a sustained relationship with Modeselektor’s Monkeytown Records.

Biography

Origins and Early Period (1998–2000)

Von Schirach began producing under the Docnuke.com alias, with an early credit appearing on the Tektonikal Turbulence EP (Komadose, 1998) where he is listed as Producer and Written-By. His background roots him in Miami’s bass culture but his sound quickly assimilated the abstract, heavily processed IDM aesthetic coming out of labels like Warp and Planet Mu.

He is described in his Discogs profile with characteristic irreverence: “infused with life energy healing force from the Bermuda Triangle and is capable of teleportation. His Bass is unrivaled among contemporaries.” The self-mythology is integral to his persona — the Bermuda Triangle has remained a recurring visual and conceptual motif throughout his career, eventually becoming the name of his own label imprint.

He appeared in the 2002 documentary Electro-Dziska, an early marker of his visibility in the experimental electronic scene.

The Schematic Era (2001–2008)

Von Schirach debuted on Schematic in 2001 with 8000 B.C. (SCH017), a full-length produced entirely by himself, with artwork handled by Arnold Steiner. The same year brought Escalo Frio (SCH 020 CD). These early releases established the compressed, violent rhythm structures and noise-saturated bass that defined his output.

The years 2002–2005 were prolific: Pelican Moondance (2002), Chopped Zombie Fungus (2003, also functioning as a Schematic compilation), Global Speaker Fisting (2004, SCH.044 — credited “All Music Snuffed, Eyeballed And Tongued By”), and Armpit Buffet (2005, co-released on Schematic and Detroit Underground). Each title pushed his aesthetic of grotesque and hyperbolic naming into the album format.

He was also active in the Miami underground scene during this period, releasing El Golpe Avisa on Rice And Beans in 2003 — a label with a Latin American focus — followed by a Japanese reissue on Palm Tree Snuff / Arepaz / RL66 in 2005.

During this period, his live performances incorporated outfits, masks, and props, pushing the visual element of his act toward industrial and metal theatrics in contrast to the typical laptop-and-cables setup of IDM contemporaries.

Skinny Puppy Collaboration (2004–2007)

Von Schirach contributed to two Skinny Puppy albums, landing him his most prominent non-Schematic credits:

  • The Greater Wrong Of The Right (Synthetic Symphony, 2004): Programmed By (track 5), Sound Designer (tracks 1, 9), Synthesizer (track 5). He worked alongside co-producers cEvin Key and Mark Walk, and alongside touring players including Danny Carey (Tool) on drums for track 8.
  • Mythmaker (Synthetic Symphony, 2007): Guest credit.
  • The Greater Wrong Of The Right Live (2005): Listed as Live Crew / Sound Designer (Vibe).

He also toured with Skinny Puppy during this period — a significant exposure outside the IDM underground.

Diversification (2006–2009)

Pick Your Perversion (Touchin’ Bass, 2006, UK) expanded his label relationships internationally, mastered by Mike Marsh. The same year he appeared as co-primary artist on Pimps Of Gore with German goregrind band Gut (Supreme Chaos Records) — an unlikely crossover that reflected the noise/metal overlap in his aesthetic.

Oozing Bass Spasms (Cock Rock Disco, 2008) was his German imprint debut under his own name; photography on the release was by Sarah Sitkin.

Magic Triangle (Sub-Conscious Communications, 2009) was executively produced by cEvin Key, connecting his second Skinny Puppy collaboration into a broader relationship.

In 2009 he also activated Miami Bass Warriors — a Miami bass-focused project with Jose el Rey, El Tigre, and Debbie D (Debbie Attias of Avenue D). Their debut video “Going Back to Calle 8” received placement on Pitbull’s show La Esquina, marking a genuine reach into mainstream Latin/Miami pop culture. The group released its debut on Palm Tree Snuff / ¿Que Pasa M.I.A.?

He is also credited with lyric contributions to Secret Chiefs 3 / Quintron split release Purple Pickle Eater (Schematic, 2008) — an unusual credit that shows cross-pollination with Schematic’s roster beyond his own solo work.

Triangle Earth and Monkeytown (2011–2018)

Von Schirach founded his own label, Triangle Earth (later also Bermuda Triangle Records (3)), through which he issued several releases including Otto And The Electric Eel (2012), Cadillac Culo (2013), and Alien Culo (2021).

In parallel, Modeselektor signed him to their Monkeytown Records imprint for an extended run of releases:

  • Supermeng (2012) — written and produced by von Schirach
  • Salpica / Skeleton Search (Incl. Modeselektor Edit) (2013)
  • Tipo Tropical / La Plancha (2014)
  • Draculo (2018)

The Modeselektor connection was not incidental — Otto is credited on their albums Happy Birthday! and Monkeytown as a collaborator. This relationship brought him into the Berghain-adjacent European club circuit audience.

In 2011 he toured the US with Atari Teenage Riot, another high-profile industrial/noise crossover.

Breakcore Beefcakes (2018, Bermuda Triangle Records (3)) was a collaborative release with Doormouse, connecting two nodes of the American breakcore underground.

Recent Work (2019–2025)

Ottobrus (Serendip Lab, France, 2021) continued his international label releases. Alien Culo (Triangle Earth / Bermuda Triangle Records (3), 2021) marked a return to his own imprint.

In 2025, he received a drum programming credit (Additional) on a release on Ipecac Recordings / Ramseur Records / Thirty Tigers — Ipecac being Mike Patton’s label, suggesting continued crossover into experimental rock/metal circles.

Scene Context

Von Schirach’s career sits at the intersection of several scenes:

Schematic / Miami IDM: He is one of Schematic’s five most prolific artists by release count, alongside Phoenecia, Dino Felipe, Richard Devine, and Takeshi Muto. The Miami angle distinguished Schematic from the UK-centric IDM mainstream — bass weight and rhythmic aggression were always present beneath the glitch surface.

Breakcore / Noise Underground: His releases on Cock Rock Disco and Supreme Chaos Records placed him in the European breakcore/noise circuit. The Gut collaboration (Pimps Of Gore) and the Doormouse partnership (Breakcore Beefcakes) both reflect this orbit.

Industrial / EBM crossover: The Skinny Puppy work was not a one-off — touring with the band and with Atari Teenage Riot shows sustained connections to the industrial rock world. The heavy metal visual aesthetic of his performances reinforces this overlap.

Miami Bass Revival: The Miami Bass Warriors project and its Pitbull/TV placement suggest a genuine attempt to bridge the experimental and regional pop worlds in his home city. His name variations include “Otto ‘El Santo’ Von Schirach,” nodding to lucha libre imagery common in both Latin music and the grindcore/noise underground.

Bermuda Triangle mythology: His Triangle Earth / Bermuda Triangle Records (3) imprint reflects a consistent personal cosmology around Miami’s geography and mysticism, separate from the Schematic institutional identity.
