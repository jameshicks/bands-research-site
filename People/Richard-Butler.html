Richard Butler

Discogs ID: 78757 Real name: Richard Lofthouse Butler Born: 5 June 1956, Kingston upon Thames, Surrey, England Name variations: Butler, Butler Rep, Lofthouse Butler, R Butler, R. Lofthouse Butler

Vocalist, lyricist, and visual artist. Co-founder and permanent lead singer of The Psychedelic Furs alongside his younger brother Tim Butler (born 7 December 1958, Teddington, Middlesex). Also co-founded Love Spit Love with Tim during the Furs’ early-1990s hiatus, and released one solo album in 2005. His voice — raw, sandpaper baritone with a distinct slur — is one of the most recognisable in post-punk.

Era 1 — Formation and CBS/Columbia years (1977–1991)

The Psychedelic Furs (debut, 1980)

The Furs formed in 1977 London. The original lineup crystallised around Richard and Tim Butler, John Ashton (guitar), Roger Morris (guitar), Vince Ely (drums), and Duncan Kilburn (saxophone).

Talk Talk Talk (Columbia, 1981)

Produced again by Steve Lillywhite; Phil Thornalley engineering. Same core lineup. “Pretty In Pink” and “Into You Like A Train” appeared here first. The 1981 original of “Pretty In Pink” was pulled into John Hughes (17)‘s 1986 film of the same name — the band re-recorded it for the soundtrack, giving them their widest commercial exposure.

Forever Now (Columbia, 1982)

Produced by Todd Rundgren at his Utopia Sound Studio. Rundgren also engineered and played keyboards/sax on “No Easy Street.” Howard Kaylan and Mark Volman (ex-Turtles) contributed backing vocals. Duncan Kilburn was gone; Mars Williams joined on horns and saxophone. Key tracks: “Love My Way,” “President Gas.”

Mirror Moves (Columbia, 1984)

Produced by Keith Forsey (also played drums). Thommy Price handled drums on tracks A2–A3. Mars Williams on saxophone throughout. Richard Butler took a design co-credit “after Barney Bubbles” alongside Da Gama. Key tracks: “The Ghost In You,” “Heaven,” “Heartbeat.”

Midnight To Midnight (CBS, 1986)

Produced by Chris Kimsey. Paul Garisto replaced Vince Ely on drums; Jon Carin joined on keyboards; Marty Williamson on second guitar. Mixed by Steve Thompson & Michael Barbiero (most tracks) and Kimsey (tracks B3, B5). “Heartbreak Beat” became the band’s biggest US chart hit.

World Outside (EastWest, 1991)

Produced by Stephen Street and The Psychedelic Furs. Lineup: Richard Butler, Tim Butler, John Ashton, Knox Chandler, Joe McGinty, Don Yallech. Sleeve design by Vaughan Oliver (of 4AD). This was the band’s final studio release before the hiatus. Key tracks: “Valentine,” “Until She Comes,” “Don’t Be A Girl.”

Era 2 — Love Spit Love (1992–1999)

After The Psychedelic Furs went on hiatus in 1991, Richard Butler formed Love Spit Love in New York City. Core lineup: Richard Butler (vocals), Richard Fortus (guitar), Frank Ferrer (drums). Tim Butler appeared on the debut but not the second album.

Love Spit Love (Imago, 1994)

Produced by Dave Jerden; mastered by Eddy Schreyer. Jon Brion appeared as guest (Chamberlin, Optigan, piano). John Philip Shenale arranged strings on “Green.” Key tracks: “Seventeen,” “Am I Wrong,” “Change In The Weather.”

Trysome Eatone (Maverick, 1997)

Tim Butler did not appear; Chris Wilson (8) joined on bass or additional role. Co-produced, co-mixed, and co-written by Richard Butler, Richard Fortus, and Ben Grosse. Jon Carin returned as guest (keyboards). “Stars” is co-written by Carin, Butler, and Fortus. Mastered by George Marino.

Era 3 — Solo album (2005)

Richard Butler (Koch Records, 2005, KOC-AD-9987):

A solo record built largely around the collaboration with Jon Carin, who produced, engineered, mixed, and played all instruments. Richard Butler contributed vocals, concept, and associate production. Tim Butler co-wrote tracks 5 (“Broken Aeroplanes”) and 9 (“Last Monkey”). Mastered by Mark Wilder.

Era 4 — Electronic side project and Furs return (2000–present)

Reunion and live recordings

The Psychedelic Furs reformed around 2000 with live releases: Beautiful Chaos: Greatest Hits Live (Columbia/Legacy, 2001) and Live From House Of Blues (Eagle Vision, 2001).

”You Need The Drugs” with WestBam (2013)

Butler collaborated with German techno/electro DJ WestBam (Maximilian Lenz) on You Need The Drugs (Vertigo Berlin, 2013) — a full releases package with multiple remixes: Thomas P. Heckmann (DJ Hell Remix), Kobosil (Kartell Mix), Public Possession (&ME Remix). This is Butler’s most extensive venture outside rock context.

Made Of Rain (Cooking Vinyl, 2020)

The Psychedelic Furs’ first new studio album in 29 years. Produced by Richard Fortus and The Psychedelic Furs; additional production by Tim Palmer and David Maurice (tracks 4, 6, 8, 12). Mixed by Tim Palmer. Mastered by Justin Shturtz.

Lineup: Richard Butler (vocals/lyrics), Tim Butler (bass), Richard Good (guitar), Amanda Kramer (keyboards), Paul Garisto (drums), Mars Williams (saxophone).

Writing is heavily collaborative, with Butler co-writing most tracks with Richard Good. Jon Carin co-wrote “Stars.”

Notable co-writing credits

Discogs credits Richard Butler with “Words By, Music By” on the Counting Crows album August And Everything After (Geffen, master 76466) and a “Written-By” credit on the single “Round Here” (Geffen, master 248078, 1994 Australia). The nature of this attribution — whether it reflects a genuine co-write with Adam Duritz or a Discogs data error — is unverified.

Visual art

Richard Butler designed or co-designed several Psychedelic Furs sleeves, often credited with an “After Andy Warhol” note (e.g. Talk Talk Talk). On Mirror Moves he designed the sleeve “after Barney Bubbles.” He contributed screenprint images to World Outside alongside Vaughan Oliver. The solo album cover art is his own concept.
