Paul Leary

Real name: Paul Leary Walthall. Guitarist, vocalist, producer. Born Austin, Texas. Best known as lead guitarist and co-founder of Butthole Surfers. Also known as alias “Paul Walthall.” Discogs artist ID: 45088.

Biography

Origins and Butthole Surfers (1981–present)

Paul Leary formed the Butthole Surfers in San Antonio/Austin in the early 1980s alongside vocalist Gibby Haynes. The band’s Discogs profile lists him as a member alongside Haynes, Jeffrey Pinkus, Teresa Taylor, and others. The band’s earliest releases appeared on Alternative Tentacles (self-titled EP, 1983; Live PCPPEP, 1984) before moving to Touch And Go, where they recorded their core catalog through the late 1980s.

Butthole Surfers discography on Touch And Go:

  • Psychic… Powerless… Another Man’s Sac (Touch And Go, 1984)
  • Cream Corn From The Socket Of Davis (Fundamental, 1985, UK)
  • Rembrandt Pussyhorse (Touch And Go, 1986)
  • Locust Abortion Technician (Touch And Go, 1987)
  • Hairway To Steven (Touch And Go, 1988)
  • Piouhgd (Rough Trade, 1991)

The band transitioned to major labels in the 1990s. Independent Worm Saloon (Capitol Records, 1993) was their Capitol debut; Leary is credited with “Art Direction [Art Master]” on that release. Electriclarryland (Capitol, 1996) was produced by Leary (tracks 1, 2, 5, 8, 11–13) in tandem with Steve Thompson (tracks 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10), with Leary also mixing his tracks alongside engineer Stuart Sullivan.

The band’s final studio album, Weird Revolution (Hollywood Records/Surfdog, 2001), was produced by Leary (all tracks), with Rob Cavallo co-producing selected tracks (2, 3, 5, 7, 8) and Chris Lord-Alge mixing.

Leary participated in the 2024 Matador reissue campaign, credited as “Remastered By [With Guidance From]” on reissues of Psychic… Powerless… Another Man’s Sac, Rembrandt Pussyhorse, and Locust Abortion Technician, and as “Performer [Associated Performer]” on the Locust Abortion Technician reissue.

The band also has an alias: 100 Foot Worm Makes Own Food.

Solo Work

Leary released a solo album under his own name:

  • The History Of Dogs (Rough Trade, 1991) — fully self-produced; credited as Writer, Arranger, Performer, and Producer. UK release, 12 pressings documented.

  • Born Stupid (Shimmy Disc, 2021) — solo album; Leary wrote, produced, mixed, and handled artwork. Drums by Josh Freese; mastered by Kramer (2). 3 pressings. His most recent solo studio record as of this research.

  • We Wish You A Merry Christmas (Joyful Noise Recordings, 2021) — primary artist credit, role not specified in the DB.

Production Career (1991–present)

Leary became a prolific outside producer from the early 1990s onward. His production work spans punk, alternative rock, country-adjacent, and indie contexts. Recurring engineer collaborator: Stuart Sullivan of Austin’s Wire Recording.

Bad Livers

  • Lust For Life (Fist Puppet, 1991) — Producer, Engineer
  • Delusions Of Banjer (Quarterstick Records, 1992) — Producer; also appeared on Vocals and Percussion. Other credits: Danny Barnes (mandolin, accordion, banjo, vocals, guitar, dobro), Mark Rubin (tuba, vocals, double bass), Champ Hood (fiddle), Ralph White (fiddle, accordion). Engineer: Stuart Sullivan.
  • Dust On The Bible (Quarterstick Records, 1998) — Mastered By

Daniel Johnston

  • Fun (Atlantic, 1994) — Producer, Engineer, Mixed By. One of Johnston’s most produced records; Leary engineered and mixed alongside Stuart Sullivan and others.

Meat Puppets

  • Backwater single (London Records, 1993) — co-produced with the Meat Puppets
  • Too High To Die (London Records, 1994) — co-produced with the Meat Puppets; Leary mixed tracks 4, 8, 11–13. A platinum-selling breakout record. Engineers: Stuart Sullivan, Brad Vosburg.
  • No Joke! (London Records, 1995) — co-produced with the Meat Puppets; Leary also played lead guitar on track 11.
  • We Don’t Exist (London Records, 1994) — Producer
  • Scum (London Records, 1995) — Producer
  • Born With A Tail (Sub Pop, 1995) — Producer

Supersuckers

  • The Sacrilicious Sounds Of The Supersuckers (Sub Pop, 1995) — Producer
  • The Greatest Rock And Roll Band In The World (Sub Pop, 1999) — co-produced tracks 3, 6, 9, 23 alongside Jack Endino (other tracks)

Sublime

  • Sublime (MCA/Gasoline Alley, 1996) — Producer (tracks 1, 3, 4, 6–9, 11–14, 16), Mixed By (same tracks), Electric Guitar [Lead] (track 16), Slide Guitar [Lead] (track 14). David Kahne produced tracks 2, 5, 15, 17; Brad Nowell and Miguel Happoldt produced track 10. Engineer for Leary’s tracks: Stuart Sullivan. The album was released posthumously after Brad Nowell’s death; it became one of the best-selling albums of the 1990s.
  • Second Hand Smoke (Gasoline Alley, 1997) — Producer (tracks 1, 14, 15); compilation/rarities set
  • What I Got: The 7 Song EP (Gasoline Alley, 1997) — Producer
  • Wrong Way (MCA Records, 1997) — Producer; single
  • Sublime Acoustic (Bradley Nowell & Friends) (Gasoline Alley, 1998) — Producer
  • Greatest Hits (MCA Records, 2002) — Mixed By; Producer
  • Icon (Gasoline Alley/Geffen/Skunk Records, 2020) — Producer

Sublime With Rome

  • Yours Truly (Fueled By Ramen, 2011) — Producer. Eric Wilson and Bud Gaugh on bass and drums; engineer Stuart Sullivan.

Refreshments / Roger Clyne

  • The Bottle & Fresh Horses (Mercury, 1997) — Producer, Mixed By (alongside Stuart Sullivan)

Reverend Horton Heat

  • Spend A Night In The Box (Time Bomb Recordings, 2000) — Producer, Mixed By (Stuart Sullivan engineered/mixed)

Melvins

  • Hold It In (Ipecac Recordings, 2014) — Performer and Recorded By. Leary appears as a full performer on this album alongside King Buzzo, Dale Crover, and former Butthole Surfers bassist Jeffrey Pinkus (“JD Pinkus”). This reflects the Discogs group listing of Leary as a Melvins member.

Gibby Haynes (solo)

  • Gibby Haynes And His Problem (Surfdog Records, 2004) — Mixed By [5 Songs]. Haynes’s solo outing; Nathan Calhoun (bass) and other Butthole Surfers-adjacent personnel appear uncredited.

Open Questions

  • The Melvins group listing on Discogs places Leary as a member — this appears to be specifically for the Hold It In (2014) album lineup; not a permanent member status.
  • Alias “Paul Walthall” — no separate Discogs artist page found; appears to be a name variant used on some releases.
  • Name variations in DB include “Gibson Jerome Haynes” — this looks like a data error (that is Gibby Haynes’ real name), worth flagging.
  • The Butthole Surfers operated their own self-release imprint, Latino Bugger Veil, used for Double Live (1989) and later archival/compilation releases.
  • Stone Temple Pilots’ Purple (Atlantic, 1994) — Leary played the end guitar solo on one track; no production credit, just a session appearance.
  • The “Happy Time” (Seed Records, 1994), “Sunny Hours” (DreamWorks, 2002), and various other productions in the DB are not readily identifiable without web research.
