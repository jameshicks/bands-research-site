Stevie Nicks

American singer-songwriter. Born Stephanie Lynn Nicks, May 26, 1948, Phoenix, AZ. Discogs artist ID: 13735.

Overview

Stevie Nicks is one of the defining voices of American rock and pop. She came up in the early 1970s as half of the duo Buckingham Nicks with Lindsey Buckingham, releasing a debut album on Polydor in 1973 before both joined Fleetwood Mac in early 1975. Her arrival, alongside Buckingham, transformed the band from a British blues outfit into a global soft-rock phenomenon. She has sustained a parallel solo career from 1981 onward, releasing eight studio albums across Modern Records, Atlantic, and Reprise, and remains one of the best-selling artists in either capacity.

Her solo work is characterized by mystical imagery, signature shawls-and-tambourine stage presence, and a long-running creative partnership with producer Jimmy Iovine (first three solo albums), followed by turns with Rupert Hine, Rick Nowels, and ultimately Dave Stewart (Eurythmics). She co-produced much of her own output from Rock A Little (1985) onward.

Vault connection: Trouble In Shangri-La (Reprise, 2001) features “Love Changes” produced by David Kahne — the track that linked her into this vault through the David Kahne research thread.

Also credited under the alias Madame X (7) on Discogs.

Pre-Solo Career: Buckingham Nicks & Fleetwood Mac

Buckingham Nicks (1973)

  • Label: Polydor (ID: 1610)
  • Discogs release ID: 1778025
  • Produced and engineered by Keith Olsen
  • Musicians include Waddy Wachtel (guitar), Jim Keltner (drums), Ron Tutt (drums), Richard Dashut (assistant engineer)
  • Not a commercial success at the time; now considered a cult record

Joining Fleetwood Mac (1975–)

Both Nicks and Buckingham joined Fleetwood Mac (Discogs ID: 47333) in 1975, replacing Bob Welch. The Rumours-era lineup — Nicks, Buckingham, Christine McVie, John McVie, Mick Fleetwood — became one of the most commercially successful bands in history. Nicks wrote Fleetwood Mac staples including “Rhiannon,” “Gold Dust Woman,” “Sara,” and “Dreams.”

Solo Discography

Bella Donna (1981)

  • Label: Modern Records / WEA (Discogs release ID: 575955)
  • Producer: Jimmy Iovine
  • Key personnel: Waddy Wachtel (guitar), Mike Campbell (guitar), Benmont Tench (organ), Russ Kunkel (drums), Bob Glaub (bass), Roy Bittan (piano), Shelly Yakus (engineer)
  • Backing vocalists throughout career begin here: Sharon Celani, Lori Perry
  • Debut single “Stop Draggin’ My Heart Around” (duet with Tom Petty & The Heartbreakers)
  • “Leather and Lace” duet with Don Henley

The Wild Heart (1983)

  • Label: Modern Records / ATCO (Discogs release ID: 723670)
  • Producer: Jimmy Iovine
  • Engineers: Shelly Yakus, Tom “Gordo” Gondolf; mixed by Greg Edward / Shelly Yakus
  • Includes “Stand Back” (co-written with Prince) and “If Anyone Falls”

Rock A Little (1985)

  • Label: Modern Records (Discogs release ID: 636438)
  • Producers: Jimmy Iovine (select tracks), Rick Nowels (select tracks), Stevie Nicks
  • Key personnel: Waddy Wachtel (guitar), Benmont Tench (piano/organ), Rick Nowels (keyboards), Greg Phillinganes (keyboards), Steve Jordan (drums)
  • First album on which Nicks takes producer credit

The Other Side Of The Mirror (1989)

  • Label: Modern Records / Atlantic (Discogs release ID: 444190)
  • Producer: Rupert Hine
  • Engineer / mixer: Stephen W. Tayler
  • A departure toward more atmospheric production; Rupert Hine (known for work with Howard Jones, Rush) brings a different sonic palette

Street Angel (1994)

  • Label: Modern Records / Atlantic (Discogs release ID: 1810495)
  • Producers: Thom Panunzio, Roy Bittan (tracks 10, 13), Stevie Nicks
  • Key personnel: Waddy Wachtel, Mike Campbell, Andy Fairweather-Low, Bernie Leadon, Ethan Johns, Glyn Johns (drum programming), Bob Dylan (guitar, harmonica on track 11), David Crosby (harmony vocals, track 3), Benmont Tench
  • Widely considered her commercial low point; took seven years before the next solo studio album

Trouble In Shangri-La (2001)

  • Label: Reprise Records (Discogs release ID: 1624747)
  • Multi-producer: John Shanks (tracks 1, 4, 5, 12), Sheryl Crow (tracks 2, 3, 6–8), Mike Campbell (track 6), Rick Nowels (track 10), Jeff Trott (tracks 7, 8), Pierre Marchand (track 13), David Kahne (track 9 — “Love Changes”), Stevie Nicks
  • Mixed by Chris Lord-Alge throughout
  • Mastered by Doug Sax and Robert Hadley
  • A commercial comeback; Sheryl Crow’s involvement prominent

In Your Dreams (2011)

  • Label: Reprise Records (Discogs release ID: 2860267)
  • Producers: Dave Stewart (Eurythmics), Glen Ballard, Mike Campbell, Rob Cavallo, Stevie Nicks
  • Key personnel: Lindsey Buckingham (vocals, guitar), Mick Fleetwood (drums), Steve Ferrone (drums), Waddy Wachtel (guitar), Greg Leisz (mandolin), Chris Lord-Alge (mixed by)
  • Co-directed as a documentary film with Dave Stewart
  • Notable for featuring Buckingham and Fleetwood as guest contributors

24 Karat Gold – Songs From The Vault (2014)

  • Label: Reprise Records (Discogs release ID: 6164727)
  • Producers: Dave Stewart, Waddy Wachtel, Stevie Nicks
  • Mixed by Chris Lord-Alge
  • A collection of previously unrecorded or unreleased songs from her archives, dating back to the 1970s and 80s

The Lighthouse (2024)

  • Label: Warner Records (Discogs release ID: 32169192)
  • Producers: Dave Cobb, Sheryl Crow, Stevie Nicks
  • Sheryl Crow also contributes backing vocals
  • Chris Isaak credited for illustration

Connections to Vault Scenes

Nicks sits at the periphery of several vault-relevant scenes rather than at their center:

  • Post-punk / indie adjacency: virtually none directly — her work is mainstream AOR/pop-rock. She enters the vault through David Kahne, whose career spans post-punk production (Romeo Void, Translator, The Bangles) and mainstream pop (Nicks, Sugar Ray). “Love Changes” on Trouble In Shangri-La is the sole contact point.
  • Tom Petty & The Heartbreakers: Deep overlap — Mike Campbell and Benmont Tench both play extensively on her solo records. First single “Stop Draggin’ My Heart Around” (1981) was a full Heartbreakers collaboration.
  • Traveling Wilburys adjacency: Bob Dylan played on Street Angel; Tom Petty was a close collaborator.
