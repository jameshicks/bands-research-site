Steve Turre

Jazz trombonist and conch-shell pioneer. Born Omaha, Nebraska. Real name: Stephen Johnson Turre. Discogs artist ID: 174447.

Long-tenured member of the Saturday Night Live band; one of the most recorded trombonists in post-bop jazz; principal force in establishing the conch shell as a serious jazz instrument.

Biography

Early Career — Sideman Years (1969–1985)

Turre came up through the North Texas State University big band scene (credited on NTSU lab recordings from 1969–70), then moved into the professional jazz world as a sideman on major label dates. His early mentors define the arc: Rahsaan Roland Kirk (member of the Vibration Society, credited on Kirkatron, 1977, and Boogie-Woogie String Along For Real, 1978), Woody Shaw (recorded extensively with Shaw’s various Columbia-era groups — Woody Three, 1979; United, 1981; Lotus Flower, 1982 Enja; Master Of Suspense, 1987 Blue Note), and Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers (Master Of The Art, 1982, Elektra Musician; Night Music, 1983, Elektra Musician).

He also appeared with Horace Silver (Rejoice, 1981, Theresa Records) and with Mongo Santamaría in the Latin jazz orbit that would define a recurring strand of his career. His conch-shell work dates from this period: Discogs credits him on conch as early as 1976 (a session on East Wind, Discogs release 2679083) and on the Jerry Gonzalez/Fort Apache Ya Yo Me Curé (1980, American Clavé).

In 1985 he appeared on Deadline’s Down By Law (prod. Bill Laswell, Celluloid) — playing didgeridoo on the Makosaa Rock single (alongside Jaco Pastorius, Manu Dibango, Bernie Worrell, Paul Butterfield, Olu Dara) and receiving a co-writing credit on Down By Law itself.

Around this period he began his long tenure as trombone/conch player in the Saturday Night Live band.

SNL Band (mid-1980s onward)

Turre joined the Saturday Night Live house band during the mid-1980s and remained a fixture for decades. The SNL credit does not appear in Discogs release data (TV appearances are out of scope), but is confirmed by his own website and Wikipedia. Exact start and end years are an open question in this vault.

Early Solo Career on Stash (1986–1990)

Turre’s solo debut, The Music Of Rahsaan Roland Kirk (1986, Stash Records), was an explicit tribute to his mentor. Two further Stash albums followed: Viewpoints And Vibrations (1987) and Fire And Ice (1988), the latter crediting him as producer, illustrator, and liner-note writer as well as trombonist and conch player. These set the format of his solo work: hard-bop trombone at the center, conch shells woven through as texture and featured solo voice, Latin rhythmic elements, and a consistent ensemble from New York’s post-bop world.

Dedication (1989, JMT 834 433-2) was a co-lead record with Robin Eubanks — his most structurally ambitious early work, featuring Mulgrew Miller on piano/synthesizer, Charnett Moffett on bass, and Tommy Campbell/Tony Reedus on drums.

Antilles Period (1991–1995)

Moving to Antilles Records (a PolyGram jazz imprint), Turre recorded four albums that consolidated his reputation:

  • Right There (1991) — 19 pressings, his most widely distributed release
  • Sanctified Shells (1993, 314 514 186-2) — the definitive conch-shells statement; guests include Dizzy Gillespie (trumpet on “Toreador”) and a large percussion ensemble: Andy Gonzalez, Milton Cardona, Kimati Dinizulu, Badal Roy, Herlin Riley, Ignacio Berroa
  • The Time Is Right (1993, Red Record) — live in Europe
  • Rhythm Within (1995, 314 527 159-2) — features Pharoah Sanders, Herbie Hancock, and an expanded trombone/conch ensemble; recorded and mixed by Rudy Van Gelder

Verve Records (1997–1999)

Steve Turre (1997, Verve Records, 314 537 133-2) is his most star-studded session: J.J. Johnson, Jon Faddis, Randy Brecker, Robin Eubanks, Cassandra Wilson (vocals), Mongo Santamaría (congas on “Mongo ‘N’ McCoy”), Stefon Harris, Regina Carter, and Akua Dixon. Multiple trombone sectionals and conch ensembles appear throughout.

Lotus Flower (1999, Verve) followed in the same vein.

Telarc Period (2000–2003)

Three albums for Telarc: In The Spur Of The Moment (2000), TNT (2001), One4J (2003, tribute to J.J. Johnson). Turre produced all three.

HighNote Records (2004–2013)

The Spirits Up Above (2004, HighNote Records), Woody’s Delight (2012, HCD 7228 — tribute to Woody Shaw; features Jon Faddis, Wallace Roney, Claudio Roditi), and The Bones Of Art (2013).

Smoke Sessions (2018–present)

The Very Thought Of You (2018), Generations (2022), Sanyas (2024).

Conch Shells — Instrument Pioneer

Steve Turre is the leading force in establishing the conch shell as a serious jazz instrument, building ensembles around it and composing specifically for its timbral properties. Earliest Discogs conch credit: 1976. The 1993 album Sanctified Shells is the fullest statement — three “African Shells” interludes structure the record; multiple shell players featured alongside djembe, tabla, and West African percussion; title track and several compositions written by Turre.
