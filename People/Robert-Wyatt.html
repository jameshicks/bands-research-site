Robert Wyatt

Discogs ID: 5049
Real name: Robert Wyatt-Ellidge
Aliases: Robert Ellidge, Shirley Williams, Whack Skins

Drummer, vocalist, composer, pianist. Born January 28, 1945, Bristol, England. Co-founder of Soft Machine and Matching Mole. Following a fall at a party in June 1973 that left him paralyzed from the waist down, he continued as vocalist and composer — no longer drumming on stage but performing and recording prolifically for four more decades. His work spans Canterbury prog, post-punk (Rough Trade singles), experimental electronics (Brian Eno collaborations), jazz-adjacent improvisation, and politically engaged song.

Career Narrative

Wyatt co-founded Wilde Flowers in Canterbury in 1964 with Kevin Ayers and Daevid Allen; from that nucleus Soft Machine formed in 1966. The band opened for the Jimi Hendrix Experience on a US tour in February 1968. After leaving Soft Machine in 1971, he formed Matching Mole (a pun on Machine Molle, French translation of Soft Machine). In June 1973 he fell from a window at a party at Gong drummer Pip Pyle’s flat, and was left paralyzed below the waist.

Rock Bottom (1974, Virgin) was recorded in the immediate aftermath and is widely considered his masterpiece: Nick Mason of Pink Floyd produced it using the band’s Manor Mobile studio. He married Alfreda Benge (born 1940), his lyricist and cover artist — she had previously worked as assistant editor on Nicholas Roeg’s Don’t Look Now — in 1974. His cover of I’m A Believer (Neil Diamond) charted in the UK the same year.

His Rough Trade period produced some of his most explicitly political work: Shipbuilding (1982, words Elvis Costello, music Clive Langer) addressed the Falklands War; Old Rottenhat (1985) and Dondestan (1991) continued in that vein. He also appeared on early Scritti Politti records during their Rough Trade avant-pop phase.

Shleep (1997) inaugurated a late-career resurgence on Hannibal, with Paul Weller mixing two tracks and Jamie Johnson becoming his regular engineer/co-producer. Cuckooland (2003) and Comicopera (2007, Domino) followed. His final studio album to date is ‘…For The Ghosts Within’ (2010, Domino).
