Heather Woods Broderick

Multi-instrumentalist, vocalist, arranger, and producer. Portland, Oregon. Sister of Peter Broderick.

Biography

Heather Woods Broderick emerged from the fertile Portland, Oregon folk and chamber-pop scene of the mid-2000s. She plays piano, cello, acoustic guitar, electric guitar, organ, synth, flute, and percussion — a range that made her indispensable as both a bandmember and session contributor. She and her brother Peter Broderick are both listed as members of Horse Feathers and Loch Lomond, two key Portland folk-ensemble projects.

Her early session work placed her on records across the Portland indie scene: she provided strings and string arrangements for Blitzen Trapper’s Destroyer of the Void (Sub Pop, 2010), co-written and arranged with Peter Broderick, and contributed backing vocals and flute to the Alela Diane orbit from 2013 onward. By 2012 she was appearing on Yann Tiersen’s Skyline (Anti-, 2012), credited as “Heather Woods” for vocals on one track.

Her first solo release under her own name was From The Ground (Preservation, 2009), co-credited with Peter Broderick. The first fully solo album proper was Glider (Western Vinyl, 2015), on which she played virtually every instrument herself — vocals, guitar, keyboards, flute, drums — with Peter Broderick handling overdub recording, mixing, and additional instrumentation (strings, drums, slide guitar, keyboards, bass). Contributors included Dave Depper and David Allred on bass/trumpet and Eric Early on Hammond organ.

A 7-inch, Home Winds / Shoreline (Planthouse, 2017), bridged the gap to her second album. Invitation (Western Vinyl, 2019) marked a creative step up: she produced it alongside longtime collaborator Daniel J. Goodwin, who also recorded, mixed, and mastered. The album features a broader cast including Mirabai Peart (violin, viola), Ryan Francesconi (string arrangements), Andrew Carlson (2) (bass, baritone guitar, synth), Jason Bemis Lawrence (drums), Peter Broderick (synth, vocals), and a choir of Alicia Walter, Jessica Larabee, Laura Stevenson, and Maia Friedman.

Domes (Dauw, 2022) was a more minimal release — written, performed, and recorded entirely by Heather, with Peter Broderick handling additional arrangement, processing, mixing, and Daniel J. Goodwin mastering. It was issued on the Belgian label Dauw.

Labyrinth (Western Vinyl, 2023) saw her and Daniel J. Goodwin co-producing once again; she played beats, cello, guitar, keyboards, percussion, piano, synth, and vocals, with Goodwin adding bass, beats, drums, guitar, and more.

Throughout this period she remained active as a collaborator and session player. She appears on releases by Yann Tiersen, Blitzen Trapper, Sharon Van Etten (Remind Me Tomorrow, Jagjaguwar, 2019 — backing vocals), and various Sonic Pieces releases including the Mirall compilation (2018). She appears on a 2025 release on No Coincidence Records (Everything Must Go) for cello.

Her work with Alela Diane is the most sustained creative partnership on record. She joined as a key contributor on About Farewell (Believe Recordings / Rusted Blue Records, 2013), handling flute, keyboards, and arrangements. She became a co-primary artist on Live At The Map Room (AllPoints, 2021), a live album credited to Alela Diane, Heather Woods Broderick, and Mirabai Peart, where she played backing vocals, piano, cello, acoustic guitar, flute, and percussion and served as co-photographer. On Looking Glass (Believe / Naïve, 2022) she handled full arrangements and played vocals, acoustic guitar, cello, flute, and piano. Producer Tucker Martine helmed that record; other contributors included Carl Broemel, Scott Avett, Anna Fritz, and Peter Broderick.
