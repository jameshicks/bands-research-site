Jim Welton

Discogs ID: 2283274
Aliases: L. Voag (ID: 547573), Amos (3) (ID: 282974), Xentos, Atom Of Doubt, D.J. Taj And The New Language, Dave Marsh (6), King Kobra (3), Narky Brillans, The Tennis Ball’s Bigger Than The Golf Ball, Vic Serf & The Villains, Appel Singh Bankboy’s Five O’ Clock Train
Name variations: Jim Xeltos Jones
Origin: London, UK
Active: 1976–present (intermittent)

London-based musician, radio artist, and producer who came out of the punk squat scene. Had an ultra-prolific period in the early 1980s releasing lo-fi tape experiments, cut-ups, sound collage, and post-punk on his own label It’s War Boys under an extensive network of aliases. Resurfaced in the late 1990s working in experimental radio art, circuit bending, and sample-based collage. Subject of the 2003 documentary Xentos (Fray Bentos) Jones: Found Documents Concerning The Life And Work Of Xentos Jones by Luke Fowler and Kosten Koper.

Biography

Origins: Punk Squat Scene (1976–1979)

Jim Welton’s story starts before he even went by that name. In 1976, while still a teenager, he was recruited as bassist by Bruno Wizard (born Bruno McQuillan) for a South London punk group called The Rejects (7) — formed at a university in New Cross and playing their first gig in January 1977 supporting The Damned and The Vibrators. Over the following months they played alongside Wire, Generation X, The Jam, Eater, 999, and Sham 69. In the summer of 1977, Anton Hayman joined on guitar, and the group changed their name to The Homosexuals. No recordings exist under The Rejects name.

The Homosexuals — initially just Wizard and Hayman, formed properly in Battersea, South London in 1978 — became Welton’s main outlet through the late 1970s. He appears on the debut single Hearts In Exile (Lorelei No. 1, 1978) and the Homosexuals EP (Black Noise (2), 1979). Around the same time, he began releasing material under the alias L. Voag: the 7” Move (self-released as “Move 1,” 1979) and the LP The Way Out (Not On Label, “No 9,” 1979), the latter a conceptually structured LP divided into “House X-axis” and “Chat Y-axis” — rooms and conversations mapped across the two sides of a record, tagged by Discogs as Art Rock / Avantgarde / Experimental / Lo-Fi.

L. Voag also appeared as bassist (tracks B1 and B6) on the Homosexuals LP Masai Sleep Walking (Black Noise (2), 1980), released under the band’s alias George Harrasment.

It’s War Boys: The Alias Factory (1981–1983)

The early 1980s were Welton’s most prolific period. Operating from North London (London N5 per label contact info), he founded It’s War Boys as a micro-label running releases catalogued with pound signs (£1, £2, £3…). The label issued at least 13 documented releases between 1981 and 1984, primarily on cassette and vinyl, with most material credited to aliases that were either Welton alone or Welton plus a shifting cast of collaborators from the same squat scene.

The full intended catalogue (per the label’s own memory document) ran to at least £31 and included numerous items presumed lost or unreleased, including “Skim Nomrom & The Corolettes – Copydex Wonderland (lost - presumed erased).” The label announced a relaunch in Spring 2026 to release archival material.

Amos & Sara (Discogs ID: 560218) was the primary vehicle across three releases. Welton appeared as “Amos (3)” with Sara (21) (ID: 1077045). The 1981 cassette Amos And Sara Sing The Private World Of Amos (£2) featured an expanded roster including Lepke Buchvalter (as “Lepke Buchvater”), Chris Gray (2) (as “C. D. Greyt”), Andrew Camelot, Sally Nunn, Noel Broun, and Seamus Luttman-Luttman-Luttman — names that recur repeatedly across the It’s War Boys network.

The Just Measurers (ID: 1210069) — the trio of Welton, Lepke Buchvalter, and Chris Gray (2) — issued the LP Flagellation (£5, 1983) on It’s War Boys, 16 tracks of post-punk experimental material. Performers on that record include Narky Brillans (another Welton alias, ID: 1362015), Yakko Banovic (a Lepke alias), and Chris Gray under the spelling “C.D. Greyt.” Production credited to “The Infiltrators.”

Narky Brillans (as a named project, ID: 1362015) appears both as a Welton alias and as a named performer — the 1981 cassette Goes Into Orbo… (£13) is the sole Narky Brillans release on Discogs.

The Tesco Bombers and the Y Records Connection (1982)

The Tesco Bombers (ID: 531543) was a London post-punk project with a floating lineup including Welton, Keith Allen (actor and musician, ID: 56831), Richard Dudanski (drummer associated with The Clash / Public Image Ltd. orbit, ID: 519954), Neal Brown (ID: 928923), Inigo Batterham, and others. Their single Hernando’s Hideaway (Y Records, Y14, 1982) — featuring a tango cover and B-sides — was produced by the group collectively. The full performer credits for that session also include Chris Gray (2), Lance Hunter, and Sara (21). Y Records (run by Dick O’Dell) was the label also associated with The Pop Group and Maximum Joy.

The Work (1982)

Welton appears as a member of The Work (ID: 163768) on Discogs. His documented credit is on the live LP Live In Japan (Recommended Records Japan, RRJ 05, 1982), where he is credited as Amos (3) playing bass guitar and voice — substituting for regular bassist Mick Hobbs. The Live In Japan session also brought in Chris Cutler on drums. The Work core at that point was Tim Hodgkinson, Bill Gilonis, Mick Hobbs, and Rick Wilson.

Die Trip Computer Die (1994–)

Die Trip Computer Die (ID: 244937) is described on Discogs as initially a duo of Lepke Buchvalter and “Xentos Fray Bentos” (Welton under the Xentos alias), with Ted Barrow joining in 1994 to complete the circle. Their sole Discogs release is the cassette Sibling Family Research (self-released, 1995), credited to Lepke Buchvalter (as “Lepke Buchwater”) and Xentos (as “Xentos (Flycraken) Jones”). Fourteen tracks of experimental / sound collage, with track titles like “XXentos Peeps The Septick Veil” and “Frying Baby Newton’s B..B..Brains.” Die Trip Computer Die have a Bandcamp presence (dietripcomputerdie.bandcamp.com).

Later Work: Radio Art, Circuit Bending, Film Subject (1990s–)

Welton’s Discogs person credits for the post-1990 period are sparse by comparison to the It’s War Boys years. He appears on the Hyped To Death / Messthetics compilation Astral Glamour (2004) with lead vocals and bass/guitar credits — the Messthetics series documented UK DIY music of the 1978–1984 era, and The Homosexuals’ catalog was a major focus (the companion An Introduction To Astral Glamour, Messthetics #204, runs 27 tracks of Homosexuals archival material). He is also credited as engineer on a 2006 Hong Kong release (70 mph (You Have No Friends) / 73 mph (There Is No Spoon), Fine Time Recordings) and as engineer/mixer on The Many Moods Of Bread And Shed (The Orchestra Pit Recording Co., 2012, UK).

Perfect Vacuum (ID: 2257197) is another late-period group: a duo with Lukas Simonis (Dutch musician, ID: 508445), though no releases appear on Discogs under that name.

Modern Shit (ID: 1440695), a group with Lepke Buchvalter, is represented by the undated It’s War Boys cassette £21 (Will Make You Sick), reissued by Alga Marghen as Will Make You Ill (2008, plana-M 14TES.071) — 37 untitled tracks, produced by Emanuele Carcano. Welton appears as Performer (as “Xentos Bentos”).

Milk From Cheltenham (ID: 618090) — Welton is not listed as a formal member on Discogs, but is credited as Producer (as “Xentos Bentos”) and Recorded By (as “L. Voag”) on the 2005 Alga Marghen reissue of Triptych Of Poisoners. His connection to the 1983 original is undocumented. See Milk From Cheltenham.

The 2003 documentary by Luke Fowler and Kosten Koper — titled Xentos (Fray Bentos) Jones: Found Documents Concerning The Life And Work Of Xentos Jones — is linked from Welton’s Discogs page to UbuWeb, situating him firmly within the tradition of experimental audio/visual documentary filmmaking. Fowler is a Glasgow-based artist and filmmaker known for films about marginal figures in British experimental music.

The Homosexuals Redux (2016)

The Homosexuals resurfaced as an active group. Important If True (self-released, 2016) — a nine-track digital album — featured Bruno Wizard on lead vocals, Mike Dos Santos on bass, guitar, vocals, piano, and co-production, Dave Siegal on guitar, Travis Harrison on drums and production, with backing vocals from Neon Music and Verushka George. Anton Hayman wrote two tracks (“Pentecost 2016” and “Snapshots Of Nairobi”). No Jim Welton credit appears on this release; it reflects the Wizard-led incarnation rather than the full original lineup.

It’s War Boys Label

Discogs ID: 101085
Contact: iwb@studioshitless.com
Location: London N5
Reissue/relaunch: Spring 2026 (archival releases)

The label Welton founded and ran under the L. Voag / Amos alias. All releases carry pound-sign catalogue numbers (£1, £2, etc.) — a characteristically deadpan gesture. The label’s primary aesthetic was lo-fi cassette and vinyl production, collage sensibility, and a rotating cast of collaborators operating under aliases. The full intended catalogue ran to at least £31, with several items described as “lost” or “presumed erased.” The label’s own memory document (part of the Discogs profile) is itself presented as if output by “a refurbished Sinclair ZX Spectrum.”

See It’s War Boys.

The Way Out (Not On Label, catalogue “No 9”) is the most substantial solo L. Voag document: 21 tracks across two sides organised as “House X-axis” (rooms: Front Door, Hall, Kitchen, Toilet, Living Room, Bedroom, The Way Out) and “Chat Y-axis” (conversations: “The Lengthy Pause,” “Franco’s Prayer,” “Boxing and Sparring (With The Third Dimension),” etc.). Tagged Avantgarde / Experimental / Lo-Fi. The matrix runout inscriptions read “GOD IS BRUNO ! ONEST MATE ! KEEP PEDLIN IM RIGHT BEHIND YOU !” — another oblique Homosexuals reference.

L. Voag releases have been reissued by Superior Viaduct and Alcohol (per top-labels data), suggesting archival interest in the 1979 recordings.

Open Questions

  • Whether he appears on the 2016 Important If True LP under any alias — that release names only Wizard, Dos Santos, Hayman (writer), Siegal, and Harrison.
  • The fate of the many listed-but-unreleased It’s War Boys catalogue items (£7, £8, £12, £14–16, £19–20, £22–31 and unnumbered items), most described only in the label’s own internal memory document.

  • Kosten Koper (co-director of the 2003 documentary): no Discogs presence found; likely a non-music-industry collaborator.
