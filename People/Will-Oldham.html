Will Oldham

American singer-songwriter, actor, and photographer. Born December 15, 1970, Louisville, Kentucky (real name Joseph William Oldham). Discogs artist ID: 68468. Primary recording alias since 1999: Bonnie “Prince” Billy (Discogs ID: 66089). Earlier recordings under Palace Brothers, Palace Music, Palace Songs, and Palace — all consolidated under the Discogs artist entry for Palace (ID: 135307).

Brother of Ned Oldham and Paul Oldham, both of whom appear throughout his recordings. Known for a DIY aesthetic, emotional directness, and an unusually prolific output spanning Americana, folk, country, punk, and indie rock. Married to photographer Elsa Hansen; father of artist Hollis Mae Sparhawk.

Biography

Louisville Origins and the Palace Era (1991–1997)

Oldham emerged from the Louisville underground alongside Slint, Squirrel Bait, and The For Carnation. In 1991 he photographed the cover of Slint’s Spiderland (Touch And Go) — his first significant public credit. He also took inner-sleeve photos for the Slint Tweez 35th Anniversary Edition (Touch And Go, 2024).

The Palace Brothers debut, There Is No-One What Will Take Care of You (Drag City, 1993), was recorded by Grant Barger and featured personnel from the Louisville scene: Britt Walford and Brian McMahan from Slint, Todd Brashear, and Paul Greenlaw alongside Oldham on guitar and vocals. The self-titled Palace Brothers LP followed in 1994 with minimal additional credits (Grant Barger on bass). Both were released on Drag City.

The project name cycled rapidly through aliases as Oldham resisted fixed identity:

  • Palace Brothers — debut LP (1993) and second LP (1994)
  • Palace Music — Viva Last Blues (Drag City/Domino, 1995); An Arrow Through The Bitch EP (Domino, 1994)
  • Palace Songs — EP releases
  • Palace — Arise Therefore (Drag City, 1996), recorded by Steve Albini and featuring David Grubbs on piano/organ and brother Ned Oldham on bass
  • Palace — Joya (Drag City, 1997), a more experimental, lo-fi direction produced and largely performed by Oldham himself

All these aliases are represented under the single Discogs artist entry Palace (ID: 135307), with Bonnie “Prince” Billy as a separate but linked alias (ID: 66089).

I See A Darkness and the BPB Transition (1998–2003)

The name Bonnie “Prince” Billy debuted in 1998 on I See A Darkness (Palace Records). The album was recorded and mixed by brother Paul Oldham, co-mixed with David Pajo and Colin Gagon, and features a core band including Bob Arellano, Peter Townsend, and Oldham himself on vocals and guitar. Artwork included skull drawings by Joanne Oldham (Will’s mother). The album is widely considered his most celebrated work — Johnny Cash covered the title track on American III: Solitary Man (2000).

Ease Down the Road (Palace Records, 2001) — the first full Bonnie “Prince” Billy LP — was recorded in Louisville with Paul Oldham producing and Oldham providing voice, guitar, synthesizer, and percussion.

Master And Everyone (Drag City/Palace Records, 2003) was recorded at Beech House in Nashville by Mark Nevers (who became a key collaborator), with William Tyler on guitar, Tony Crow on keyboards, Paul Oldham on bass/guitar, and Marty Slayton on backing vocals. One of the most stripped-down records of the BPB catalog.

The Prolific Middle Period (2004–2012)

Sings Greatest Palace Music (2004, produced with Mark Nevers) was an exercise in country reinterpretation of older Palace songs, using Nashville session players including Stuart Duncan (fiddle/mandolin), Hargus Robbins (piano), and Eddie Bayers (drums).

Superwolf (Drag City/Palace Records, 2005) was a full collaboration with guitarist Matt Sweeney. Sweeney produced and played guitar/bass/keyboards; Paul Oldham recorded. A second Sweeney collaboration, Superwolves (Domino, 2021), followed sixteen years later.

The Letting Go (Domino, 2006) was recorded in Iceland by producer Valgeir Sigurðsson, with string arrangements by Nico Muhly and vocalist Dawn McCarthy as a prominent duet partner. The Dirty Three’s Jim White (2) and Mick Turner both appear on multiple tracks.

Beware (Drag City/Palace Records, 2009) was the most expansive BPB release, featuring a Chicago-heavy ensemble: Rob Mazurek (cornet), Nicole Mitchell (flute), Joshua Abrams (bass guimbri), Michael Zerang (percussion/marimba), Emmett Kelly (guitar/keys/voice), Leroy Bach (organ), Azita Youssefi (piano), Greg Leisz (mandolin/pedal steel), and Jennifer Hutt (violin). Jon Langford added voice. Recorded and mixed by Neil Strauch.

Wolfroy Goes To Town (Domino, 2011) and Singer’s Grave A Sea Of Tongues (2014, produced by Oldham) continued the BPB catalog into the 2010s.

Later Work (2013–present)

I Made A Place (Domino, 2019) was recorded by Chris Greenwell, with Nathan Salsburg on guitar, Cheyenne Mize on fiddle, and Joan Shelley providing guest voice — a return to intimate acoustic recording.

Keeping Secrets Will Destroy You (Drag City/Palace Records, 2023) continues the active BPB catalog.

The Purple Bird (No Quarter, 2025) is the most recent release as of research date.

We Are Together Again (No Quarter, 2026) is listed in Discogs with a forthcoming release date.

Actor Credits / Photography

Oldham has been an actor since the early 1990s (films include Matewan, 1987, for director John Sayles). His Discogs credits don’t capture film work directly. As a photographer he shot:

  • Slint, Spiderland cover (Touch And Go, 1991) — the defining image of that record
  • Slint, Tweez inner sleeves (Touch And Go, 35th Anniversary Edition, 2024)
