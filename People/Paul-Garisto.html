Paul Garisto

Paul Garisto

Oct 04, 20268 min read

Paul Garisto

Discogs ID: 267793
Real name: Paul John Garisto
Origin: Brooklyn, New York, USA
Role: Drummer, session and touring musician

New York City-based drummer originally from Brooklyn. Active as a touring and session player since 1981; best known for his twenty-year-plus association with The Psychedelic Furs, whom he first joined in 1984.

Career Overview

Early Work — Ricochet (1981)

Garisto’s earliest traceable Discogs credit is the 1981 single “Baby, It’s You” by Ricochet, a New York-based act on Arona Records. This predates his Furs recruitment by three years and establishes him as working in the New York scene well before he broke through to major-label sessions.

The Psychedelic Furs — First Tenure (1984–1991)

Garisto joined The Psychedelic Furs in 1984, replacing Vince Ely (who had himself replaced Phill Calvert). His first album with the band was Mirror Moves (1984, Columbia), where he played alongside Richard Butler (vocals), Tim Butler (bass), John Ashton (guitar), Mars Williams (horns/saxophone), and Jon Carin (keyboards).

He remained the band’s drummer through their commercial peak. Midnight To Midnight (1987, CBS) — credited to “Psychedelic Furs” — is the most prominent Discogs credit from this period, listing him alongside the same core lineup plus Ed Buller on additional keyboards. The album was produced by Chris Kimsey and partly mixed by Steve Thompson & Michael Barbiero.

World Outside (1991, Columbia) closes his first Furs stint. The Discogs credits on the main pressing list the performers collectively without individual names, but Garisto is credited by the band’s Discogs entry as a member through this period. Stephen Street produced.

He also appears on the compilation All Of This And Nothing (Columbia) playing drums on track 13 — the only Midnight To Midnight-era track on a compilation otherwise drawing from earlier records, confirming his tenure started in that transitional moment between eras.

Iggy Pop — Instinct and related work (1988)

In 1988, concurrent with his Furs duties, Garisto played drums on Iggy Pop’s album Instinct (A&M Records), produced by Bill Laswell. The band assembled for the record was tight and New York-rooted: Leigh Foxx on bass, Steve Jones (2) (Sex Pistols guitarist) on guitar, and Seamus Beaghen on keyboards. Garisto also appears on the associated single “Cold Metal” (1988, A&M), same lineup.

An undated Iggy Pop release, World On Fire (Troublemaker Enterprises), features Garisto alongside Steve Jones, Andy McCoy (of Hanoi Rocks), and Alvin Gibbs — likely a live or unofficial release from the Instinct touring band.

Garisto also appears on the live albums Live At The Channel, Boston M.A. 1988 and the Iggy Pop release Live On The King Biscuit Flower Hour (1997, King Biscuit Flower Hour Records), a live document from the same Instinct tour era. A further live document, Search And Destroy - Live In Chicago 1988 (2015, Echoes), also captures the same touring configuration.

Fat Boys — On And On (1989)

An unlikely session credit: Garisto played live drums on the Fat Boys’ final studio album On And On (1989, Polydor/Tin Pan Apple), alongside programmed drums from members of the group. This is the rare non-rock context in an otherwise rock-dominated session career.

Shooting Gallery (1992)

After the first Furs period ended, Garisto co-founded Shooting Gallery with Finnish guitarist Andy McCoy (Hanoi Rocks), bassist Dave Tregunna (The Brats, Lords of the New Church), and vocalist Billy G Bang. Their self-titled debut (1992, Mercury) featured guest appearances from Nicky Hopkins (piano) and Mars Williams (saxophone) — the latter a Furs alumni connection. Garisto played drums, percussion, and backing vocals.

Slumlords (1993)

A Japanese-market release, Har-Dee-Harhar (1993, InsideOut), credited to Slumlords (2), reunited Garisto with Dave Tregunna and Andy McCoy’s associate Billy G Bang, plus guitarist Takashi O’Hashi. Garisto sang as well as drummed. The Slumlords lineup is essentially a Shooting Gallery variant.

Dragmules (1995)

Garisto was a member of Dragmules (also known as Jackass (8)), a New York band that released 2A (1995, Atlantic). Other members included Josh Weinberg, Johnny McNabb, Marty Sarandria, and Trippy Thompson.

Session Work — Mid-to-Late 1990s

Closer (4) — Garisto played on Don’t Walk (1997, Revolution), with production by Ed Buller — another Psychedelic Furs alumni connection. Buller had been a Furs keyboard player and later became a prominent producer (Suede, etc.).

Buster Poindexter — The discography entry for Love Is A Dog From Hell (1997, Mercury/Mouth Almighty Records) credits Garisto with drums.

The Psychedelic Furs — Reformed Lineup (2000–present)

Garisto rejoined The Psychedelic Furs for their reformed lineup from around 2000. The live album Live In NYC (2000, King Biscuit Flower Hour Records) documents this return. The reformed lineup that recorded Made Of Rain (2020, Cooking Vinyl) — the band’s first studio album in 29 years — includes Garisto on drums and percussion alongside Richard Butler, Tim Butler, John Ashton, Amanda Kramer (keyboards), Richard Good (guitar), and Mars Williams (saxophone). Richard Fortus co-produced. Tim Palmer mixed and co-produced.

Jesse Malin (2002–2007)

Garisto was a core member of Jesse Malin’s New York rock band for at least three albums:

  • The Fine Art Of Self Destruction (2002, One Little Indian) — produced and partly mixed by Ryan Adams, with Melissa Auf der Maur on backing vocals and Richard Fortus on lead guitar for one track
  • The Heat (2004, One Little Indian) — Garisto on drums, percussion, and backing vocals
  • Glitter In The Gutter (2007, One Little Indian) — produced by Eddie Wohl and Rob Caggiano; band included Sam Yaffa (Hanoi Rocks) on bass

Dave Gahan — Paper Monsters (2003)

Garisto played drums on three tracks (CD-4, CD-9, CD-10) of Dave Gahan’s solo debut Paper Monsters (2003, Mute), produced by Ken Thomas. Other musicians on the album included Knox Chandler (guitar, keyboards, bass), with Victor Indrizzo sharing drum duties.

Ryan Adams — Love Is Hell (2003)

Garisto appears as part of the “New York Band” on Ryan Adams’s Love Is Hell Pt. 1 (2003, Lost Highway), playing drums on tracks 2–4. The New York Band also included Johnny McNabb (guitar), Joe McGinty (piano), and Johnny Pisano (bass). He is also credited on Love Is Hell Pt. 2 and the combined Love Is Hell Pts. 1&2.

Bree Sharp (1999)

Session credit on A Cheap And Evil Girl (1999, Trauma Records), playing drums on selected tracks. Marty Sarandria — also in Dragmules — played bass on the same tracks, suggesting a shared NYC session pool.

Mercury Retrograde (2008)

Mercury Retrograde (2008, One Little Indian) is another Garisto session credit, though the primary artist isn’t specified in the available credits — likely related to Jesse Malin’s wider orbit.
