Jim Dickinson

Discogs ID: 135966

American musician, producer, and session player based in Memphis.

Produced The True Believers’ self-titled LP (EMI America, 1986). Also known for production work with Big Star, The Rolling Stones (Exile on Main St.), and Ry Cooder, among many others. Father of Luther and Cody Dickinson.

Notes (web)

Stax Records

Jim Dickinson’s confirmed Stax connection: session musician on Gus Cannon’s (Cannon’s Jug Stompers) final album, reportedly the first album released on the Stax label, c. 1963. His exact instrument on the session is not specified in sources consulted (piano is his primary instrument but not stated for this session). Dickinson’s main Memphis production base was Ardent Studios, not Stax — his early 1960s session work pre-dates his Ardent relationship. Americana-UK; Encyclopedia of Arkansas

Royal Studios

No source consulted documents Dickinson working at Royal Studios (Willie Mitchell’s Memphis facility). One source notes he attended Willie Mitchell’s live band performances in West Memphis as a young man, but no studio session credit at Royal has been confirmed. Memphis Music Hall of Fame

Main studio associations

  • American Studios (Chips Moman) — session work
  • Ardent Studios — producer from mid-1960s onward; Big Star, early Memphis rock sessions
  • Muscle Shoals Sound Studio — session work (Rolling Stones, Aretha Franklin)
  • Zebra Ranch (family studio, Hernando/Coldwater, MS) — late-career solo albums and Sons of Mudboy session (2009)
