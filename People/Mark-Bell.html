Mark Bell

Real name: Mark Peter Bell
Born: February 22, 1971, Leeds, UK
Died: October 8, 2014 (complications following an operation)
Discogs ID: 167

British electronic musician, producer, and DJ. Co-founder of LFO (Low Frequency Oscillation), one of the foundational acts of UK bleep techno. His parallel career as a record producer brought him into sustained collaboration with Björk across six albums from 1995 to 2011, and a full-album production credit on Depeche Mode’s Exciter (2001). He was active under the LFO name until his death at 43.

LFO

Bell met Gez Varley (Gerrard Varley, Discogs ID 1066506) while both were part of rival breakdancing crews at the Merrion shopping centre in Leeds in 1984. They reconnected at college in 1988 while studying photography and graphic design, and formed LFO that year. A third founding member, Martin Williams (ID 45786), was involved in the earliest period but left by 1990.

The name LFO stood for Low Frequency Oscillation, taken from the LFO component of synthesizers. The group were early movers in the Leeds/Sheffield bleep-and-bass scene emerging around Warp Records. Their self-titled single “LFO” (1990, Warp) became an instant anthem; Frequencies (1991, Warp; US release on Tommy Boy) is the defining album of the bleep techno era. The follow-up Advance (1996, Warp) was the last release credited to both Bell and Varley — Varley left the group around this time. From 2003 onward Bell operated LFO as a solo pseudonym, releasing Sheath and Freak (both 2003, Warp).

A 2019 Warp release compiled Bell’s Peel Session from October 20, 1990.

Production Work — Björk

Bell’s production partnership with Björk began with Post (1995, One Little Indian) and continued across her most critically considered run of albums. He was often co-producer alongside Björk herself, and his programming is prominent throughout Homogenic (1997), the album most defined by the collision of electronic production and string arrangements.

Production Work — Other

Depeche Mode — Exciter (2001, Mute): Bell produced the entire album, their tenth studio LP. His credit runs across all four singles released from it: “Dream On,” “I Feel Loved,” “Freelove,” and “Goodnight Lovers.”

Deltron 3030 (2000, 75 Ark): “Music By” credit on the debut album by Del tha Funkee Homosapien and Dan the Automator. Bell’s contribution sits alongside Dan the Automator’s main production role.

R&S Records productions (1994–1996): Several releases under aliases — Storm (1994), Surge (1996), Blue Bossa (1996) — representing his UK techno production activity outside Warp.

Further production credits:

Aliases

Bell released under multiple pseudonyms: Clark, Counterpoint, Fawn, Space Monkey, Speed Jack. The Counterpoint alias is associated with releases on Harthouse.
