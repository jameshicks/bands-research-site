Andy Haas

Discogs ID: 210517
Canadian saxophonist and multi-instrumentalist (soprano/alto saxophone, trombone, didgeridoo, piri, fife, taepyungso, electronics). Born Toronto, ON; later based in New York City. Key member of Martha And The Muffins (1977–1983 era), then a long-term collaborator with NYC experimental/no-wave circle God Is My Co-Pilot. Maintained a solo career on his own Resonant Music (2) imprint from the mid-1990s onwards, and from the 2010s embedded in the Toronto experimental-jazz scene around The Cosmic Range.

Name variations on Discogs: A. Haas, Andy ‘Muffin’ Haas, Andy H., Haas.

Biography

Toronto / Martha and the Muffins era (1977–1983)

Haas was a founding member of Martha And The Muffins, the art-pop/new-wave band that emerged from the Ontario College of Art scene on Toronto’s Queen Street West. He played saxophone (and occasionally trombone) on the band’s first three studio albums for Dindisc (Virgin’s UK subsidiary):

  • Metro Music (1980) — produced by Mike Howlett; personnel also included Martha Johnson, Martha Ladly, Mark Gane, Carl Finkle (bass), Tim Gane (2) (drums). Cover design by Peter Saville (2).
  • Trance And Dance (1980)
  • This Is The Ice Age (1981) — co-produced with Daniel Lanois; Haas credited alto saxophone and trombone.

The band scored an international top-ten hit with “Echo Beach” (1980). Haas appears credited on the 7” single as well. By 1983, the band contracted to the duo of Martha Johnson and Mark Gane (rechristened briefly as M+M), and Haas’s involvement ended. His Discogs name variant “Andy ‘Muffin’ Haas” recalls this period.

His earliest Discogs credit is the Martha and the Muffins debut single Insect Love / Suburban Dream (1978, Muffin Music (2)), where he plays saxophone and a “squawk box.”

NYC experimental scene (mid-1980s–early 1990s)

After Martha and the Muffins, Haas relocated to New York and became active in the downtown experimental scene. He is not on Discogs as a primary artist for this period, but by the early 1990s had developed a close association with God Is My Co-Pilot (Craig Flanagin and Sharon Topper’s rotating collective, operating out of their own The Making Of Americans imprint).

He also appeared on John Zorn’s Filmworks II: Music For An Untitled Film By Walter Hill (1995, Avant) playing didgeridoo, alongside Marc Ribot, Anthony Coleman, Cyro Baptista, and David Shea.

Solo work and Resonant Music (1997–2013)

Haas launched his own label, Resonant Music (2) (later catalogue styled as “Resonantmusic”), and released a series of politically and musically adventurous solo records.

Arnhem Land (1997, Avant, Japan) — his debut solo album and arguably his most acclaimed. Structured as a series of improvised duets: Haas on didgeridoo against a different guest on each track. Guests: Ikue Mori (drum machines, tracks 1 and 10), Cyro Baptista (berimbau, percussion — tracks 2, 7, 11), Keiji Haino (voice — tracks 3, 9, 12), Fred Frith (guitar — tracks 4, 8), Anthony Coleman (organ — track 5), Makigami Koichi (voice — track 6). Executive-produced by John Zorn and Disk Union. Design by Ikue Mori.

Humanitarian War (2006, Resonant Music (2)) — solo album, Haas on shofar, raita, fife, electronics, tabla taal tarang. Titles name specific munitions (CBU 87 Steel Rain, Depleted Uranium, White Phosphorus, etc.). Recorded by Perkin Barnes.

The Ruins Of America (2008, Resonant Music (2)) — solo album mixing original compositions with reworked American Popular Song, traditional Arab/Islamic music, and fado. Instruments: saxophone, piri, fife, live electronics, prepared loops. Recorded by Perkin Barnes.

Haas - Moreno (2013) — duo album with David Moreno (7) (modular synth, Moog Voyager). Haas: saxophone, piri, fife.

He also participated in other NYC-adjacent projects during this period. A session with noise/improv band Gawk (1995, Invisible) on didgeridoo and a “bucket.”

Later Toronto-scene connections (2006–present)

From the mid-2000s Haas extended his reach back into Canada via a series of Toronto-based experimental and avant-jazz projects:

Radio I-Ching — trio with Dimitri Papadopoulos and Don Fiorino. One known release: Last Kind Words (2006, Resonantmusic).

Tertiary Trio — trio with Don Fiorino and Paul Corio. Title Goes Here (2003, rentcontrolrecords).

Ask The Oracle — quintet also featuring Matthew “Doc” Dunn, Brandon Valdivia, Colin Fisher (2), Aaron Lumley. Ask The Oracle (2011, Resonantmusic) — all tracks written by Haas.

GOTHgsb (Ghosts Of The Holy Ghost Spermic Brotherhood) — NYC noise trio with David Grollman (acoustic snare, objects, balloons) and Michael Evans (2) (amplified snare, objects, pedals). Haas: soprano sax, flute, jew’s harp, bird calls, pedals. For (2016, Resonantmusic).

Paradise Of Ashes (2011), Notes On The Miasms (2012) — solo/leadership recordings on Resonantmusic, with Perkin Barnes recording and Scott Friedlander engineering/involved. Saxophone, electronics, producer credits.

The Cosmic Range — Toronto experimental-jazz collective with Matthew “Doc” Dunn, Brandon Valdivia, Jonathan Adjemian, Kieran Adams, Isla Craig, others. Haas joined for:

  • New Latitudes (2015, Idée Fixe) — as credited member
  • In A Poem Unlimited (2018, 4AD) — saxophone; this is U.S. Girls’ album with The Cosmic Range as backing group
  • The Gratitude Principle (2019, Idée Fixe) — saxophone, electronics
  • Scroll (2021, Telephone Explosion Records) — performer

Kim (2018, Cosmic Range / Resonant Music (2)) — duo LP with Matthew “Doc” Dunn, produced by Haas, Dunn, Jeff McMurrich, and David Millar (6). Saxophone, piri, electronics.

American Nocturne (2018, self-released) — duo with Don Fiorino (guitar, glissentar, lap steel). Haas: saxophone, drum machine, electronics, producer.

Future Moons (2022, Ansible Editions) — trio LP with Kieran Adams and Matthew “Doc” Dunn. Haas: saxophone, fife, electronics, taepyungso.

Taballah (2014, Resonant Music (2)) — solo LP. Genres: Electronic/Jazz/Experimental. No additional credits listed. (A Taballah II also exists on Discogs, undated, credited to Haas as producer.)
