Klaus Dinger

Oct 17, 20265 min read

Klaus Dinger

Born: 24 March 1946, Scherfede, Germany
Died: 21 March 2008, Zeeland, Netherlands (heart attack, aged 61)
Discogs ID: 388633
Alias: Nikolaus van Rhein (also separate Discogs ID 639292, used for keyboard credits)

German drummer, guitarist, vocalist, composer, and producer. Inventor of the motorik beat. Co-founder of NEU! and La Düsseldorf. Brother of Thomas Dinger.

Biography

Early Career and Kraftwerk (1970–1971)

Dinger came up in the Düsseldorf avant-garde scene and joined Kraftwerk in 1970, appearing on their debut LP. He departed alongside Michael Rother shortly after — both found Kraftwerk’s direction incompatible with what they wanted to do. Conny Plank, who had engineered the Kraftwerk debut, would go on to produce virtually everything Dinger made for the next decade.

NEU! (1971–1975)

Dinger and Rother co-founded NEU! in 1971. The band was essentially a two-man project — Dinger on drums, guitar, vocals, and miscellaneous instruments; Rother on guitar, bass, and electronics — with Conny Plank as a near-constant third presence in the studio. Rother has described Plank as something close to an unofficial member.

NEU! released three albums on Brain Records before dissolving, each commercially marginal but retrospectively regarded as foundational texts of what came to be called krautrock. Dinger’s playing on these records — specifically the locked, motorik pulse that opens NEU! — established a rhythmic template that post-punk, new wave, and eventually electronic music would draw on for decades. “Motorik” was not Dinger’s own word for it; he reportedly called it “Apache beat,” but motorik stuck.

By NEU! ‘75 the band had split into two factions within a single record: Side A is the Dinger/Rother duo in close-harmony guitar mode; Side B brings in Thomas Dinger and Hans Lampe and turns harder and more aggressive, anticipating punk. The two sides barely cohere as one album — which was part of the point.

After NEU! ‘75 Rother departed to pursue solo work and his collaboration with Cluster in Harmonia. NEU! was finished as an active band, though Dinger would continue to invoke the name.

La Düsseldorf (1975–1984)

Dinger formed La Düsseldorf in 1975 with his brother Thomas and Hans Lampe. The first two albums — La Düsseldorf (1976) and Viva (1978) — were produced by Conny Plank and constitute their best-regarded work: expansive, motorik-driven, more melodic and accessible than NEU! without abandoning the pulse. Viva’s Side B is almost entirely occupied by the 20-minute “Cha Cha 2000.”

Conny Plank was absent from the third album Individuellos (1981), and the critical consensus is that it shows. Hans Lampe left the band in 1984. Dinger continued working on material he considered a fourth La Düsseldorf album, but legal disputes with former members blocked its release under that name. It eventually appeared as Klaus Dinger + Rheinita Bella Düsseldorf material; in Japan it was briefly issued as La-Duesseldorf.de.

Post-La Düsseldorf Projects (1985–2008)

Dinger remained active through the 1980s and 1990s in various configurations:

  • Néondian — solo alias used on a 1985 Teldec EP
  • Die Engel Des Herrn — 1992 project on the LSD label
  • La! NEU? — later-period project (Discogs ID 204114)
  • Dinger-Brothers — collaboration with Thomas Dinger (Discogs ID 2040959)

In the 1990s NEU! was rediscovered by a new audience via the post-punk and indie rock scenes. A 1995 Captain Trip Japan release of material billed as NEU! 4 caused controversy — Rother disputed the release and its official status remains contested.

Dinger spent his final years working on a project called Japandorf, a collaboration with Japanese musicians. He died of a heart attack on 21 March 2008 — three days before his 62nd birthday — while preparations for the record were underway. The album was completed posthumously and released in 2013 on Grönland Records.

Legacy

NEU!‘s rhythmic vocabulary is audible across an enormous range of subsequent music. Dinger’s motorik beat shows up explicitly in post-punk, krautrock-influenced indie, kosmische-derived electronic music, and beyond. Quentin Tarantino used “Super” from NEU! 2 on the Kill Bill Vol. 1 soundtrack; The Killers interpolated NEU! material on Imploding The Mirage (2020). A 50th anniversary NEU! box set appeared in 2022.
