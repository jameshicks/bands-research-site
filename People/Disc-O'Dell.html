Oct 21, 20264 min read

Disc O’Dell

Label owner, manager, producer. Discogs ID: 30733. Real name: Richard O’Dell (consistently referred to in all web sources as “Dick O’Dell” — see name note below). Also credited as: Dick O’Dell, Dirk Mephisto.

Owner and founder of Y Records (early 1980s Bristol UK indie, distributed by Rough Trade). Manager of The Pop Group, The Slits, William Orbit, Ed Harcourt, and Bat for Lashes. Co-owner of Guerilla Records. Produced Maximum Joy’s “White And Green Place” (Y Records, 1982) and appeared as producer/engineer across much of the Y Records catalog.

Guerilla Records

Co-founded Guerilla Records in 1990 with William Orbit. Discogs label ID: 621 (“Guerilla”). The label ran from 1990 to ~1994 (company dissolved 2003), releasing progressive house and electronic dance music from London. O’Dell compiled and produced the label’s flagship compilation Dub House Disco (GRLP 004, 1992) — credited as “Dick O’Dell” on that release. His management company O’Dell Artists is credited on 7 label releases.

US distribution was handled via I.R.S. Records and Rob Di Stefano’s TRIBAL America imprint (1991–1994). When Di Stefano declined to renew the deal in 1994, Guerilla lost its US revenue stream and folded.

Post-Guerilla Management Career

After the collapse of Guerilla Records (~1994), O’Dell continued working as an artist manager. Web sources consistently name three post-Guerilla clients: Ed Harcourt, and Bat for Lashes (Natasha Khan), in addition to his earlier clients The Pop Group and The Slits.

Ed Harcourt

O’Dell was managing singer-songwriter Ed Harcourt by at least 2002. This is confirmed by Simon Reynolds’s Rip It Up and Start Again footnotes blog (2008), which notes O’Dell’s management of Harcourt when describing a failed attempt to interview O’Dell on Portobello Road around that time. No further specifics (duration, label context, how the relationship ended) have been confirmed from web sources. The Wikipedia article on Ed Harcourt does not mention O’Dell. [source: Rip It Up and Start Again: Footnotes ch. 5]

Bat for Lashes (Natasha Khan)

O’Dell discovered Natasha Khan at an early Brighton performance. In his own words (Gulf News interview, 2007): “I was actually at the bar while she was playing. Then these two kids came out of the venue. One said to the other, ‘That girl’s weird.’ My ears pricked up when I heard that.” He approached Khan after the set to discuss management. [source: Gulf News, 2007]

As her manager, O’Dell secured Khan’s first publishing deal and introduced her to producer David Kosten, with whom she made the demos that became the debut album Fur and Gold (2006). O’Dell also acted as intermediary during creative tensions between Khan and Kosten during recording. [source: Talkhouse/Life of the Record — Fur and Gold making-of]

Khan described the relationship in the Life of the Record podcast transcript: “my first manager, Dick O’Dell, who I was with for seven years, he found me one night or sort of approached me and helped me get my first publishing deal.” This places the management relationship at approximately 2000–2007 (or thereabouts, if the Mercury Prize nomination in July 2007 marks the transition point — O’Dell identified that nomination as “a turning point in her career”). [source: Life of the Record — Notes and Transcript]

O’Dell’s Gulf News profile (2007) describes him as having “enthusiasm remarkably undimmed for someone who has spent years in the music industry.” [source: Gulf News, 2007]

Name note

The brief for this web research referred to O’Dell as “Ed O’Dell.” All web sources (Bristol Archive Records, Gulf News, Talkhouse, Life of the Record, Rip It Up footnotes, The Vinyl Factory) consistently use “Dick O’Dell.” The Discogs entry gives the real name as “Richard O’Dell,” of which “Dick” is the standard informal form. No source found supports “Ed O’Dell” as a name variant. This may be an error in the research brief; flag for user review.
