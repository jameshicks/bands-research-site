Harold Battiste

Full name: Harold Raymond Battiste, Jr.
Born: October 28, 1931, New Orleans, Louisiana
Died: June 19, 2015, New Orleans, Louisiana
Discogs ID: 292367
Roles: Saxophonist, pianist, composer, arranger, producer, bandleader, label founder, educator

New Orleans-rooted multi-instrumentalist and creative organizer who built one of the earliest Black-owned record labels (A.F.O. Records, 1961), served as a key architect of the Sonny & Cher sound from 1965 onward, and co-produced and arranged the first three Dr. John albums (1968–1969). Among the most versatile and under-documented figures in American popular music from the 1950s through the 1970s.

Biography

1950s: New Orleans Session Work

Battiste came up in the New Orleans R&B session world. By 1956 he was playing uncredited saxophone on sessions for Modern Records (a 1956 Modern single credited to another act). In 1958 he produced and played saxophone — again uncredited — on a session for Specialty (the New Orleans Specialty roster). His work as session bandleader for Specialty in this period is documented on the 1989 Ace/Specialty box set The Specialty Sessions (Little Richard), where he is credited as leader for one session block.

The Specialty connection positioned him inside the New Orleans scene’s core creative infrastructure alongside reed players Alvin Tyler and cornetist Melvin Lastie, drummer John Boudreaux, and bassist Peter Badie — the nucleus that would become the AFO Executives.

1961–1963: A.F.O. Records

In 1961 Battiste founded A.F.O. Records (All For One) at 712 N. Claiborne Avenue, New Orleans — one of the earliest Black-owned record labels in the United States. The label released twelve singles and three albums between 1961 and 1963, distributed in part by Sue Records Inc..

The label’s first release was Barbara George’s “I Know (You Don’t Love Me No More)” (A.F.O. 302, 1961), a regional smash that crossed nationally. Battiste co-wrote and produced it. Other AFO acts included Prince La La, Willie Tee (debut single “Always Accused”), Tami Lynn, and the AFO Executives house band. The 1963 album A Compendium (A.F.O. LP 0002) documents the Executives: Battiste on piano and saxophone, Alvin Tyler on saxophone, Melvin Lastie on trumpet, John Boudreaux on drums, and Chuck Badie on bass.

Also in 1961 Battiste was arranging for Atlantic (he arranged and conducted the Irma Thomas single “Talk About You” on Atlantic) and producing and arranging on Ric Records in New Orleans. He arranged Lee Dorsey’s “Ya Ya” (Fury Records, 1961) and other New Orleans hits.

Distribution problems stalled AFO, and Battiste relocated to Los Angeles. The label went dormant until the 1990s.

1964–1968: Los Angeles — Spector, Sonny & Cher, and the Sunset Strip

In Los Angeles Battiste established himself quickly as a session pianist and arranger with deep connections to the Philles and Imperial/Liberty orbit. He played piano on The Ronettes’ debut album …Presenting The Fabulous Ronettes Featuring Veronica (Philles Records, 1964) — a Phil Spector production alongside the Wrecking Crew (Hal Blaine, Carol Kaye, Barney Kessel, Leon Russell, Don Randi). He arranged for SAR Records (Sam Cooke’s label) in 1964, credited on Johnnie Taylor’s “Oh How I Love You / Run But You Can’t Hide” (SAR Records, 1964).

His most sustained mid-1960s relationship was with Sonny Bono and Cher. He arranged Cher’s debut album All I Really Want To Do (Imperial, 1965) and played piano on it, and continued as arranger and/or conductor on nearly every major Sonny & Cher and solo Cher release from 1965 through 1968:

Mac Rebennack played guitar (credited as “Mack Rebennack”) on Cher’s 1966 self-titled LP — one of many Battiste-adjacent situations where the New Orleans expatriate community coalesced on Hollywood sessions.

Battiste also arranged for other Imperial/Liberty acts in this period, including Jackie De Shannon (“Comin’ Through,” 1965), and Hanna-Barbera Records (1966). He arranged The Cake’s debut LP (Decca, 1967) and its follow-up, and played piano on it.

In 1967 he arranged “Lookin’ Through The Eyes Of Love” (Pala, cat. 175), the debut single by Larry Marks (3), produced by Greene & Stone. The session also involved engineer Doc Siegel. This puts Battiste in the New Orleans–to–LA orbit that connected the Pala label network to Hazlewood-adjacent California pop.

1968–1972: Dr. John Collaborations

Battiste’s most artistically significant work from this period is his collaboration with Mac Rebennack as Dr. John. He co-produced, arranged, and played on the Night Tripper’s first three albums:

1. Gris-Gris (ATCO Records, 1968) — The foundational voodoo-psych record. Battiste is credited as bass (under alias “Dr. Battiste”), producer, arranger, and co-writer (on “Jump Sturdy” and “I Walk On Guilded Splinters”). The band assembled for this record was largely New Orleans expatriates in LA, with Battiste as the organizer.

2. Babylon (ATCO Records, 1969) — Battiste sole-produced and arranged; the Night Tripper persona continued in its surrealist vein.

3. Dr. John’s Gumbo (ATCO Records, 1972) — Co-produced with Jerry Wexler; Battiste arranged horns and vocals, played clarinet and saxophone. Band included Melvin Lastie (cornet), Alvin Robinson (guitar/backing vocals), Tami Lynn (backing vocals), Lee Allen (saxophone), and Ronnie Barron (electric piano, backing vocals). This is the record that broke Dr. John commercially and critically.

He continued through the Dr. John catalog: Let the Good Times Roll (ATCO, 1972), Iko Iko (ATCO, 1972), and is credited on The Ultimate Dr. John (Warner Special Products, 1987) retrospective with horn arrangements, vocal arrangements, bass, clarinet, saxophone.

Also in this period: Battiste arranged the Beyond the Valley of the Dolls soundtrack (20th Fox, 1970; additional arrangements) and worked on Pulsar Records, a short-lived LA label where he arranged and produced several 1968–1969 releases.

Early 1970s: Session Work Continues

Outside the Dr. John axis, Battiste played baritone saxophone on Gram Parsons’ GP (Reprise Records, 1973) — a session indicating his reach into the country-rock circle. He played alto saxophone on Don Preston’s Phew! (Warner Bros., 1973). He appeared as keyboardist on Tom Waits’ Blue Valentine (Asylum Records, 1978, Australia pressing) and on Motown’s High On Sunshine (1978) and the Commodores orbit. He played piano on the Phil Spector retrospective Back To Mono (1958-1969) (ABKCO, 1991) and on multiple Crystals and Ronettes compilations.

The Plimsouls self-titled debut (Planet Records, 1981) credits him for horn arrangements — a late appearance in a New Wave context.

1976: Solo Record

Battiste’s sole headliner LP: Alone Together (Opus 43, 1976) — arranged, played bass, organ, piano, saxophone on, and produced. Personnel included Melvin Lastie, Cornell Dupree (guitar), Chuck Rainey (bass), Lowell Jordan, and New Orleans musicians.

1989–2015: Return to New Orleans, AFO Revival, and Education

Battiste returned to New Orleans in 1989. He played soprano saxophone in the horn band on the Johnny Handsome soundtrack (Warner Bros., 1989). He revived A.F.O. Records in the 1990s, issuing previously unreleased early recordings and new work by students at the University of New Orleans jazz-studies program. The 1997 release The Classic / Ellis Marsalis (A.F.O. Records) documents this revival phase. The label also issued The Sounds Of Harold’s Horn (A.F.O. Records, 2010), a compilation of his own recordings.

He collaborated with Ellis Marsalis throughout the late career. The AFO revival included Lagniappe: The 2nd 50 Years (The Future Of Our Past) featuring New Orleans musicians including Nicholas Payton, Donald Harrison, Johnny Adams, Willie Tee, and Tami Lynn.

Name Variations

Battiste recorded under a large number of credited name variants. On Gris-Gris he appears as “Dr. Battiste.” Other common forms: Harold Battiste Jr., H. Battiste, Hal Battiste, Harold R. Battiste Jr., H. R. Battiste Jr., Batiste, Baptiste Jnr, Bazz, H. X. Bazz, H. Bazzaka.

Connections

  • Dr. John — co-produced and arranged first three Night Tripper albums; both New Orleans men in exile in LA
  • Mac Rebennack — same person as Dr. John; also played guitar (as “Mack Rebennack”) on Cher’s 1966 LP arranged by Battiste
  • Larry Marks (3) — arranged his 1967 debut single on Pala
  • Ellis Marsalis — AFO Records original LP (1963); collaborated again on the 1990s AFO revival
  • Melvin Lastie — founding AFO Executives member; played on Gumbo and Alone Together
  • Tami Lynn — AFO recording artist; reappeared on Gumbo backing vocals and 1990s material
  • Willie Tee — debuted on AFO Records; reunited in the late-career AFO revival projects
  • Phil Spector — session pianist on Ronettes LP and Crystals sessions, embedded in the Wall of Sound
  • Gram Parsons — baritone saxophone on GP (Reprise, 1973)
  • Greene & Stone — production team who employed Battiste for the Larry Marks session
  • Jessie Hill — New Orleans songwriter; connected through the Mac Rebennack/AFO circle
