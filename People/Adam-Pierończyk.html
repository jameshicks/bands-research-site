Adam Pierończyk

Polish jazz saxophonist, born January 24, 1970 in Elbląg. Discogs artist ID: 671130. Name variations: Adam Pieronczyk, A. Pieronczyk, Pierończyk.

Website: adampieronczyk.com

Overview

Adam Pierończyk is one of the central figures of the Polish jazz revival that emerged in the 1990s. A multi-horn player — soprano, alto, and tenor saxophone — he came to wider attention through his duo partnership with pianist Leszek Możdżer, which produced Not Two Records’ inaugural release (Live In Sofia, MW 701-2, 1998). His career spans free jazz, post-bop, and projects that range from avant-garde improvisation to orchestrated tributes and cross-genre collaborations with spoken-word poets.

His six releases on Not Two Records (1998–2002) place him at the label’s founding moment, making him one of the few artists whose work literally defines the imprint’s opening chapter. From the early 2000s he continued recording on international labels including PAO Records (Austria), JRC Jazz (Ukraine), Jazzwerkstatt (Germany), For Tune (Poland), Anaklasis (Poland), and Jazz Sound (Poland), while maintaining an active sideman presence across Polish jazz.

He is also active as a composer and arranger — his 2010 Komeda tribute (Komeda – The Innocent Sorcerer) on Jazzwerkstatt is a significant entry in the Krzysztof Komeda commemoration literature.

Biography

Formation (to 1997)

Born in Elbląg in northern Poland, Pierończyk developed as a saxophonist during the post-communist opening of Polish jazz. By the mid-1990s he was working with the pianist Leszek Możdżer — then already establishing himself as one of Poland’s most prominent jazz pianists — and with the rhythm section of bassist Adam Kowalewski and drummer Jacek Olter.

His first documented recording is the Anniversary Concert For Hestia (1996, Hestia Insurance / HST 03), a co-billed date with Możdżer that predates the Not Two era. In 1997 he recorded Few Minutes In The Space (GOWI Records, CDG-43) as leader of the Adam Pierończyk Trio with Kowalewski and Olter — his first major label date and his first appearance on the Polish jazz imprint GOWI Records, which was co-run by Marek Winiarski, the future founder of Not Two.

Not Two period (1998–2002)

When Marek Winiarski launched Not Two Records in Kraków in 1998, Pierończyk and Możdżer’s duo recording Live In Sofia (MW 701-2) was chosen as the label’s first release — a live document of a concert in Bulgaria. The album exists in both CD (MW 701-2) and cassette (MW 701-4) formats.

Over the next four years, Pierończyk remained a core Not Two artist:

  • Plastinated Black Sheep (1999, MW 710-2) — trio with American bassist Ed Schuller and Polish drummer Jacek Kochan
  • Interzone Plays With Adam Pierończyk (2000, MW 716-2) — collaboration with the Romanian-rooted Interzone Jazzorchestra (Mircea Tiberian, piano; Horst Nonnenmacher, bass; Maurice De Martin, drums)
  • Gray Days (2001, MW 726-2) — with brothers Marcin Oleś and Bart Oleś
  • Transylvanian Grace (2002, MW 740-2) — Interzone Jazzorchestra featuring Pierończyk, Vladimir Karparov, Marta Hristea, Horst Nonnenmacher, and Topo Gioia; produced by Maurice De Martin and Ulf Drechsel; executive-produced by Marek Winiarski

He also made a sideman appearance on the Not Two compilation landscape of this era. A second duo recording with Możdżer — 19-9-1999 — came out in 2000 on the Ukrainian label JRC Jazz (JRC JAZZ 0003-2000) and was later reissued on GOWI Records in 2003 (CDG57).

Parallel work and Austrian connections (2001–2007)

Running alongside the Not Two catalog, Pierończyk developed relationships with the Austrian label PAO Records. Digivooco (2001, PAO 10230) — credited to Adam Pierończyk Digivooco — brought together Christoph Burgstaller, Gary Thomas, Tadeusz Sudnik, Paul Zauner, and Gunnar Geisse for an electronics-inflected jazz project. Amusos (2003, PAO 10900) continued this direction, featuring Henning Sieverts, Mina Agossi, Rainer Robben, Daniel Schröteler, and Christoph Burgstaller (who appears across multiple Digivooco-related releases as Pierończyk’s most-frequent collaborator, with 7 shared releases in Discogs).

In 2005 Pierończyk appeared on a Romanian compilation, Notes on Music and Music Notes (Charmides), placing him in a broader Central/Eastern European jazz context.

Village Time (2007, BCD Records) continued his Polish recordings, and in 2009 came Gajcy Szyc Pierończyk (Muzeum Powstania Warszawskiego), a release connected to Polish wartime poet Tadeusz Gajcy and actor Borys Szyc — an unusual literary-theatrical project.

Komeda tribute and international work (2010–2019)

Komeda – The Innocent Sorcerer (2010, Jazzwerkstatt, Jw104) is Pierończyk’s most internationally distributed recording. A tribute to Polish jazz legend Krzysztof Komeda, the album features:

  • Nelson Veras — guitar
  • Anthony Cox — double bass
  • Łukasz Żyta — drums and percussion
  • Gary Thomas — tenor saxophone
  • Adam Pierończyk — soprano and tenor saxophone, arranger

The recording’s pairing of Pierończyk with American musicians Gary Thomas and Anthony Cox, and Brazilian-French guitarist Nelson Veras, signals his reach into the wider international jazz world. The album was reissued by Universal Music Polska on both CD and vinyl in 2019 (775 955 2 / 775 955 1).

In 2013 The Planet Of Eternal Life appeared on Jazzwerkstatt (jw 149), continuing the German label relationship.

2014–2015 brought a busy recording period on the Polish For Tune label: A-Trane Nights (2014, sideman), then two 2015 leaders dates — Wings (For Tune, 0084 054), with Miroslav Vitous (double bass), Rafał Smoleń (drums), and others; and Migratory Poets (For Tune, 0061 039), a co-led project with Trinidadian-British spoken-word poet and novelist Anthony Joseph (2), featuring Nelson Veras (acoustic guitar), Robert Kubiszyn (double bass), and John B. Arnold (drums).

19-9-1999 Live At NOSPR (2019, Jazz Sound, JS 021) documents a concert performance with Miroslav Vitous, Takao Fujioka, Izabela Lechowicz, and Piotr Grabowski.

2020s

I’ll Colour Around It (2021, self-released) — trio with Jean-Paul Bourelly (guitar), Orlando Le Fleming (bass), and Bartłomiej Kapłoński.

Great Encounters (2021, Anaklasis, ANA 075 LP) — large ensemble; roster includes Adam Bałdych (violin), Aukso chamber orchestra, Adam Sztaba, Krzysztof Herdzin, Krzysztof Dys, Marcel Comendant, Igor Herbut, Marek Moś.

Szymanowski / X-Ray (2022, Anaklasis / PWM Edition, ANA 022) — trio with Nelson Veras (electro-classical guitar) and Dominik Wania (piano); a response to Karol Szymanowski’s music.

On The Way (2023, Jazz Sound, JS 024) — with Maciej Olejniczak.
