Overview

A recording engineer and producer who began his career in the late 1970s at Pink Floyd’s Britannia Row Studio, Michael Johnson became one of the most sought-after technical figures in British post-punk, working extensively with New Order, Joy Division, Soft Cell, and the Skids. His catalogue stretches from 1980 through the 2010s with over 1,200 credits spanning synth-pop, new wave, and indie rock. Factory Records accounts for the densest portion of his label work, and his two most frequent collaborators — New Order (745 shared releases) and Martin Hannett (219) — place him at the heart of the Manchester Factory scene. He now operates Tankfield Recording Studio in Wirral, UK.
