Albert Hammond Jr.

American singer, songwriter, and guitarist. Born Albert Louis Hammond, Jr., April 9, 1980. Rhythm guitarist in The Strokes from their formation. Son of British-Spanish pop songwriter Albert Hammond (Discogs ID: 252786), known for “It Never Rains in Southern California.” Solo career running parallel to The Strokes since 2006. Discogs artist ID: 430104.

Career

The Strokes

Hammond joined The Strokes alongside Julian Casablancas, Nick Valensi, Fabrizio Moretti, and Nikolai Fraiture before their debut. The band’s first documented Discogs release is a self-released demo/promo from 2000. Their commercial breakthrough came with the debut LP Is This It (2001, RCA/BMG), one of the most-pressed records in the Discogs database (54 pressings across territories). Hammond is credited as Guitar on all Strokes recordings through The New Abnormal (2020), with one credit as “Band, Guitar” on the Reptilia single (2003) and “Rhythm Guitar” on Angles (2011).

Solo Career

Hammond launched his solo output in 2006 with the Rough Trade 7” 101, recorded while The Strokes were on hiatus. The B-side “Postal Blowfish” features a co-write credit by Robert Pollard (of Guided By Voices), with Gus Oberg producing the track and Sean Lennon contributing piano and backing vocals to the A-side. Drummer Matt Romano and bassist Josh Lattanzi rounded out the band on this session.

The debut LP Yours To Keep followed on Rough Trade later in 2006, a ten-track indie rock record. The follow-up ¿Cómo Te Llama? (2008, Rough Trade America) expanded to thirteen tracks, marking a move to the US arm of the label. A 7” single, GfC (2008, Rough Trade), was co-produced by Hammond, Gus Oberg, and Matt Romano — the first Discogs credit explicitly naming Hammond as producer on his own material.

After a five-year gap coinciding with The Strokes’ Angles campaign, Hammond returned with the digital EP AHJ (2013, Cult Records), produced solely by Gus Oberg. This was his first release on Cult Records, the label run by Julian Casablancas. Momentary Masters (2015) — his third LP proper — followed on various licensees internationally (Liberator Music in Australia; Vagrant Records in the US per Discogs top-label data). The album carries no additional production credits in Discogs.

Francis Trouble (2018, Red Bull Records) was the most autobiographical release to date, exploring themes of Hammond’s twin who died in utero. An accompanying single/EP release Etchings From Francis Trouble appeared simultaneously in a USA & Europe pressing. Both are on Red Bull Records.

Off Cycle (2019, Red Bull Records) is a short release (two pressings, US only). Melodies On Hiatus (2023, Red Bull Records) is his most recent full-length — a 19-track album featuring guest appearances by GoldLink (rapper, track “100-99”), Matt Helders of Arctic Monkeys (“Thoughtful Distress”), Steve Stevens (guitarist, “Thoughtful Distress”), and Rainsford (“Remember” and “Alright Tomorrow”).

Production Work Outside Solo Career

Hammond and Gus Oberg co-produced Ropewalk (2015, Cooking Vinyl) for Scottish indie band The View (2), a full production credit alongside engineering. This is the clearest documented instance of Hammond working as producer for a third-party artist.

Hammond is also credited “Written-By” on one track of the Hardenough release Say Goodbye to Memory Den (2017, Japan) — likely a licensed compilation — and holds a single “Written-By” credit on Strange Days (2020, Interscope), which appears to be a compilation.

Hammond received a “Written-By” credit on Barely Legal (2013, Interscope/Polydor), indicating a publishing/co-write placement outside rock.
