Jack Tracy

Discogs ID: 353749 Born: July 27, 1926, Minneapolis, Minnesota Died: December 21, 2010, Nooksack, Washington Name variations: J. Tracy, Jack Tracey

American jazz producer, A&R executive, and journalist. Editor of Down Beat magazine from 1953 to 1958. One of the most prolific behind-the-scenes figures in postwar American jazz, Tracy worked as producer and A&R executive at Mercury Records, Argo (6), EmArcy, Limelight, and Liberty Records across a career spanning from the mid-1950s through the 1980s. His credit count on Discogs — over 1,500 release appearances — makes him one of the most-documented label-side figures in jazz history.

Biography

Down Beat Years (1953–1958)

Before entering production, Tracy was a working journalist. He served as editor of Down Beat, then the most authoritative jazz trade publication in the US, from 1953 to 1958. During this period he developed relationships with virtually every working jazz musician, which directly enabled his later A&R career. His liner note writing — which would span decades — began here, rooted in the prose style of a working jazz critic.

Mercury and Argo / Chess (1957–1963)

Tracy joined Mercury Records and its jazz subsidiary EmArcy in the late 1950s, initially as a Recording Supervisor or “Recording Director” before taking full producer credit. Mercury’s parent structure at the time included the Argo (6) imprint (distributed through Chess Records), and Tracy supervised and produced records for both.

His Mercury/Argo client list through 1963 reads as a who’s-who of the hardbop era:

  • Sarah Vaughan — No Count Sarah (Mercury, 1959, producer)
  • Max Roach — Max Roach + 4 At Newport (EmArcy, 1958, liner notes); Rich Versus Roach (Mercury, 1959, liner notes)
  • Quincy Jones — The Birth Of A Band! (Mercury, 1959, producer); The Great Wide World Of Quincy Jones (EmArcy, 1960, producer)
  • Rahsaan Roland Kirk — Introducing Roland Kirk (Argo, 1960, supervised by); Rip, Rig & Panic (Limelight, 1965, producer)
  • Ahmad Jamal Trio — Count ‘Em 88 (Argo, 1956, liner notes); In Chicago (Chess/Argo, 1960, producer original recordings)
  • The Jazztet (Art Farmer / Benny Golson) — Meet The Jazztet (Argo, 1960, recording supervisor)
  • Muddy Waters — Muddy Waters At Newport 1960 (Chess, 1960, sleeve notes)
  • Ramsey Lewis Trio — In Chicago (Argo, 1960, supervised by); Stretching Out (Argo, 1960, producer)
  • James Moody — Happy Moods (Argo, 1960, supervised by/liner notes); multiple Argo records
  • Lee Hazlewood — Trouble Is A Lonesome Town (Mercury, 1963, supervised by/liner notes)

Trouble Is A Lonesome Town is an outlier in Tracy’s jazz-centric catalog — a country-noir concept LP by a singer-songwriter, self-produced by Hazlewood. Tracy’s role was Mercury’s executive supervisor, not creative producer, and he provided the sleeve text.

He also produced the spoken-word LP How To Speak Hip (Mercury, 1961) by Del Close and John Brent — a Chicago comedy record aimed at the hip set.

Limelight and the Liberty Move (1964–1968)

In 1964, Tracy moved to Limelight, Mercury’s jazz sublabel launched that year, and took on a producing role. His Limelight output includes several significant records:

  • Rahsaan Roland Kirk Quartet — Rip, Rig & Panic (Limelight, 1965) with Elvin Jones, Jaki Byard, Richard Davis; recorded by Rudy Van Gelder
  • Buddy Rich — multiple sessions (EmArcy/Argo/Limelight, 1959–1965)
  • Gerry Mulligan — records from 1965 onward

Around 1966–67, Tracy transitioned to Liberty Records and its related imprints (Imperial, World Pacific), where he produced an eclectic range including pop, easy listening, and jazz. Key Liberty credits:

  • The Johnny Mann Singers — We Can Fly! Up-Up And Away (Liberty, 1967) and many subsequent volumes; Tracy produced 45+ Mann Singers releases
  • Jack Wilson — Something Personal (Blue Note, 1967), produced with engineer Rudy Van Gelder
  • The Three Sounds and Oliver Nelson Orchestra — Coldwater Flat (Blue Note, 1968)
  • Tim Rose — Through Rose Colored Glasses (Liberty/CBS, 1969)
  • Various pop-jazz orchestral records on Liberty/Imperial

Fantasy and Later Career (1974–1981)

Tracy produced a cluster of releases on Fantasy Records in the mid-1970s, including work with Woody Herman (Thundering Herd, 1974; Children Of Lima, 1975; King Cobra, 1976) and Feelin’ So Blue (Fantasy, 1981). He also curated/executive-produced for the jazz archival label Jazzz Records (1976–1977), pulling together historical material.

His final significant credits in the Discogs database are from the mid-1980s and later reissue work for Verve Records (1989–2001), where he received “Original Recordings Produced By” credits on compilations of his Mercury-era masters.

Peak Activity

By release count per year, Tracy’s active producing years peaked sharply:

  • 1960: 111 releases (heaviest single year — Mercury/Argo hardbop apex)
  • 1963: 77 releases
  • 1967: 109 releases (Liberty/Limelight transition era)
  • 1965: 84 releases
  • 1959: 73 releases (early Mercury period)

The dominant era was 1957–1968, accounting for the great majority of original productions. Post-1970 activity was lighter and concentrated on Fantasy.
