Mulgrew Miller

Jazz pianist. Born 13 August 1955, Greenwood, Mississippi. Died 29 May 2013, Allentown, Pennsylvania, age 57. Discogs artist ID: 272493.

Post-bop pianist in the tradition of McCoy Tyner and Bill Evans. One of the most-recorded sidemen of his generation, appearing on sessions across nearly every major post-bop and hard-bop label from the mid-1980s through the 2000s. Led his own groups on Landmark Records and Novus, and taught at William Paterson University.

Biography

Early Career (to 1981)

Born and raised in Greenwood, Mississippi. Moved to Memphis, Tennessee, where he came up in a local jazz scene that also produced James Williams (2), Donald Brown, and Harold Mabern — all pianists who later formed the nucleus of a Memphis jazz piano tradition. Little pre-1981 Discogs credit trail exists.

Art Blakey’s Jazz Messengers (c. 1981–1983)

Miller joined Art Blakey’s Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers in the early 1980s, the band’s last great incubating period. He appears on live documents from this era including New York Scene (1984, Concord Jazz) — recorded during his tenure. The Messengers at this time also included Wynton Marsalis and Bobby Watson, establishing Miller in the highest-visibility hard-bop context of the period.

Woody Shaw and the Mid-1980s Sideman Peak (1983–1987)

After leaving the Messengers, Miller became a fixture on the New York session circuit. His most significant long-term association was with trumpeter Woody Shaw, including appearances on:

  • United (1981, Columbia) — early credit
  • Lotus Flower (1982, Enja) — before his Messengers tenure
  • Master Of The Art (1982, Elektra Musician)
  • Night Music (1983, Elektra Musician)
  • Time Is Right - Live In Europe (1983, Red Record)
  • Live appearances with the Woody Shaw Quartet and Woody Shaw Quintet

He also appeared on Tony Williams’s Foreign Intrigue (1985, Blue Note) — a key Blue Note comeback session with Wallace Roney, Donald Harrison, Ron Carter, and Bobby Hutcherson.

Landmark Records Leader Sessions (1985–1991)

Miller signed with Landmark Records (ID: 147404), then run by producer Orrin Keepnews, and recorded a series of well-regarded leader dates:

Wingspan (1987) is his most compositionally ambitious Landmark date — he wrote or co-wrote most of the program and added Steve Nelson on vibraphone. Producer on all: Orrin Keepnews. Engineer: Tom Mark. Mastering throughout: George Horn.

Parallel Sideman Work: Blue Note, Enja, JMT (1985–1993)

Alongside the Landmark dates, Miller appeared constantly as a sideman:

  • Tony Williams: Foreign Intrigue (1985, Blue Note), Civilization (1987, Blue Note), Verses (1987, Muse) — three Blue Note-era sessions with Williams’s small group
  • Kenny Garrett: Introducing Kenny Garrett (1985, Criss Cross), Garrett 5 (1989)
  • Blakey/Messengers live docs: New York Scene (1984, Concord), Live At Sweet Basil (1985, GNP Crescendo), Live At Ronnie Scott’s (1985), Blue Night (1985, Timeless), Live At Kimball’s (1986, Concord)
  • Steve Turre: Viewpoints And Vibrations (1987, Stash), Dedication (1989, JMT, Piano + Synthesizer) — two key sessions
  • Elvin Jones: African Exchange Student (1990, Atlantic Jazz)
  • Countin’ On The Blues (1987, Enja) — co-led session with John Stubblefield and Hamiet Bluiett, Charnett Moffett (b), Victor Lewis (dr)

Novus Period and Later Leadership (1993–2000)

Moved to Novus/RCA for the 1990s leader sessions:

Hand In Hand is his most star-studded date — a collective all-star session bringing in two Henderson players and the young McBride. Miller co-produced alongside Keepnews.

Piano duo work: The Duets (1999, Bang & Olufsen) with Danish bassist Niels-Henning Ørsted Pedersen.

2000s: Teaching and Continued Recording

Miller taught at William Paterson University in Wayne, New Jersey, where he became a central figure in jazz education. He continued to record as a sideman on sessions for Blue Note, Concord, HighNote, Telarc, and others. Later leader dates include:

  • Promised Land (2000, Audiophoric)
  • San Sebastian (2012, In+Out Records) — with the Golden Striker Trio

Posthumous Release

In Harmony (2021, Resonance Records) — a live duo recording with Roy Hargrove, released eight years after Miller’s death, received wide attention and garnered liner notes from Ron Carter, Kenny Barron, Sonny Rollins, Christian McBride, and others.
