JFDR

Real name: Jófríður Ákadóttir
Discogs IDs: 5627476 (as JFDR); 2394231 (as Jófríður Ákadóttir)
Born: 4 August 1994, Reykjavík, Iceland
Active: 2009–present
Also known as: Jófríður (name variation)

Singer, songwriter, and multi-instrumentalist from Reykjavík. Twin sister of Ásthildur Ákadóttir. Daughter of Áki Ásgeirsson. Her solo work operates under the JFDR alias; earlier and collaborative work is credited to her full name. She has released records on her own White Sun Rec imprint, Morr Music (Berlin), Bedroom Community (contributor credit), and Houndstooth (London, fabric Records sublabel).

Biography

Early work and Pascal Pinon (2009–2016)

Jófríður formed Pascal Pinon with her twin sister Ásthildur Ákadóttir and two classmates — Halla Kristjánsdóttir and Kristín Ylfa Hólmgrimsdóttir — in Reykjavík in 2009. Their self-titled debut (2009/2010) was initially self-released in Iceland before being picked up by Morr Music in Germany. The band’s name references the early 20th-century circus performer Pasqual Pinon. Jófríður functioned as the primary songwriter and lead vocalist within the group, also playing guitar, clarinet, and keyboards; Ásthildur typically sang harmony and played keyboards, guitar, and bassoon.

Pascal Pinon released Twosomeness on Morr Music in 2012/2013 and Sundur in 2016, also on Morr Music. By 2017, Jófríður announced the band would be on hiatus, with a final European tour that year standing in for a formal break-up statement.

Alongside Pascal Pinon, she was a member of Samaris (Discogs ID: 2394232), an electronic-folk trio with Þórður Kári Steinþórsson and Áslaug Rún Magnúsdóttir, active 2011–2016 and signed to One Little Indian (UK) and 12 Tónar (Iceland). She also appears in Gangly (Discogs ID: 5476177).

Her non-JFDR credits during this period include: arranging and songwriting contributions to the Samaris albums Silkidrangar (12 Tónar, 2014) and Black Lights (One Little Indian, 2016); a clarinet contribution on Iron & Wine’s Sermon On The Rocks (Till It’s All Forgotten EP, Full Time Hobby, 2015); and a songwriting/production credit on Empire by Ásthildur’s project Muted (2018).

JFDR and White Sun (2016–2018)

She launched the JFDR alias in 2016 with White Sun, a solo debut released on her own White Sun Rec imprint from Iceland. The debut was spare — no additional credits are documented in Discogs for that release. It was followed immediately by the full-length Brazil (White Sun Rec, 2017), co-produced with Shahzad Ismaily (New York multi-instrumentalist). Ismaily remains her most consistent recurring collaborator across three albums. Jófríður wrote all material on Brazil; additional credits include photography by Cary Tijerina.

In 2018, Morr Music released White Sun Live Part 1: Strings, a live document produced and recorded by Albert Finnbogason and mastered by Francesco Donadello. The strings arrangement was handled by Ian McLellan Davis. The session featured an Icelandic string ensemble (violin, viola, cello, double bass) and saw Ásthildur Ákadóttir contributing piano and vocals.

New Dreams and pandemic-era work (2019–2020)

New Dreams (White Sun Rec, 2020) was her most collaboratively dense production. Jófríður served as primary songwriter, producer, performer, and engineer — alongside a network of New York-based collaborators. Production credits are shared across Josh Wilkinson (the largest single engineering/production contributor across seven tracks), Paul Corley, Jake Aron, Jeremy Malvin (also known as Chrome Sparks), and Shahzad Ismaily. String arrangement on one track is credited to Albert Finnbogason; Nico Muhly appears as a guest performer (credited in collaborators). Mastering was by Taylor Deupree (New York ambient/electronic artist); mixing by Paul Corley. Design by DM Stith (also David Stith). A Dream On EP was also issued in 2020 on Krúnk.

The New Dreams credits also include Gyða Valtýsdóttir (musician, member of múm), Daníel Friðrik Böðvarsson, Logan Coale, Clyde Lawrence, Anna Bikales, Grey McMurray, Friðfinnur Sigurðsson, and Daníel Friðrik Böðvarsson.

Guest credits (2019–2022)

Jófríður contributed vocals to Sam Slater’s I Do Not Wish To Be Known As A Vandal (Bedroom Community, 2022), alongside Hildur Guðnadóttir (halldorophone, cello), Yair Elazar Glotman (double bass), Hilary Jeffery (brass), Sam Dunscombe (clarinet), and James Ginzburg (mix). The record connected her to the Bedroom Community network beyond the label’s usual orbit.

Her credit on Flying Lotus / Brainfeeder-adjacent release Amnioverse (Brainfeeder, 2019) — written-by credit on a release in that orbit — appears under her full name in the DB.

Museum (2023)

Museum (Houndstooth, 2023 — Houndstooth is a sublabel of Fabric Records, Discogs ID: 509824) marked her move to a UK electronic label and a shift in production approach. She co-produced and co-mixed the album with Josh Wilkinson, with additional engineering from Albert Finnbogason and Hafsteinn Þráinsson (also known as Ceasetone). Mastering again went to Taylor Deupree. Shahzad Ismaily is credited on mix for some tracks. Songwriting/lyrics are credited jointly under both “JFDR” and “Jófríður Ákadóttir” in the Discogs entries.
