Michael Gerzon

Michael Anthony Gerzon (4 December 1945, Birmingham — 6 May 1996, Oxford). British audio engineer, mathematician, and high-fidelity pioneer. Discogs artist ID: 293820. Name variations on sleeves: Michael Gerson, Michael Gerzin.

Best known technically for inventing Ambisonics surround sound and co-developing Meridian Lossless Packing (MLP, later Dolby TrueHD). In parallel, he was a dedicated field recordist who documented dozens of UK free improvisation and contemporary music sessions from the mid-1980s through the mid-1990s, leaving a recorded legacy on Incus Records, Leo Records, Slam Productions, ReR Megacorp, and several other labels.

Background and Education

Gerzon read mathematics at Corpus Christi College, Oxford University, earning his MMath degree in 1967. In that same year he joined the Oxford University Tape Recording Society (OUTRS), which became the practical base for his lifelong work in audio.

Ambisonics and Acoustic Research

Working with graduate student Peter Craven and Peter Fellgett (professor at the University of Reading), Gerzon designed the Soundfield microphone — a tetrahedral array of four capsules that captures a full three-dimensional sound field. He then developed the Ambisonic UHJ encoding/decoding format to store and reproduce that signal via conventional loudspeakers.

Ambisonics was conceived in direct opposition to the competing matrix-based quadraphonic formats of the early 1970s — CBS’s SQ, Sansui’s QS Regular Matrix, JVC/RCA’s CD-4 — and argued for a psychoacoustically correct approach tuned to human aural perception. Gerzon published openly critical articles in 1974. He left Oxford in 1972 to continue research under the National Research Development Corporation (NRDC).

In 1979 he demonstrated an Ambisonics prototype to Dolby Laboratories, Inc., which explored licensing it for Dolby Surround; the deal collapsed due to NRDC bureaucratic delays.

In May 1984 Gerzon presented a hardware Ambisonic Mastering System developed with Geoffrey Barton of Audio & Design (Recording) Ltd, which allowed downmixing conventional stereo multitrack masters to UHJ Ambisonic. In 1992 Gerzon and Barton presented custom “2,2,3” decoders for enhancing two-channel stereo via three L/C/R speakers; Barton later patented this as “Trifield” and licensed it to Meridian Audio.

He became an Audio Engineering Society fellow in 1978 and received the AES Gold Medal in 1991. He published over 120 articles in academic journals and consumer magazines including the Journal of the Audio Engineering Society, Wireless World, and Gramophone.

Digital Signal Processing and Software

In the early 1990s Gerzon worked with Waves Audio, an Israeli DSP/VST developer, developing tools including the Q10 Equalizer and one of the first prediction limiters on the market, the L1 Ultramaximizer.

He also developed noise-shaped dither filtering techniques. Collaborating with J. Robert “Bob” Stuart of Meridian Audio, he worked on multi-channel PCM audio compression. The resulting MLP (Meridian Lossless Packing) was presented in June 1999 — three years after Gerzon’s death — and became the default compression method for DVD-Audio and later Blu-ray (as Dolby TrueHD).

Field Recording Work — UK Free Improvisation

Alongside his technical research, Gerzon maintained an active practice as a field recordist and live recording engineer, specifically in the UK free improvisation scene. He documented sessions by Evan Parker, Derek Bailey, Anthony Braxton, Lol Coxhill, and others from the mid-1980s onward. The British Library is reportedly in the process of digitizing his comprehensive 400-hour archive of recordings.

Key Releases — Detail

The Snake Decides (1986) — Evan Parker, Incus

Solo soprano saxophone. Gerzon engineered. One of Evan Parker’s landmark solo documents; Gerzon’s credit here sits alongside a woodcut by George Murphy and photography by Caroline Forbes. Incus label ID: 1264835.

The Coventry Concert (1987) — Anthony Braxton Quartet, West Wind

Live recording of the quartet with Marilyn Crispell, Mark Dresser, Gerry Hemingway. Credited on sleeve as “Michael Gerzin.” West Wind (German label).

Han (1988) — Derek Bailey / Han Bennink, Incus

Gerzon credited for post-production and recording alongside Chris Atton and Eddie Klak (who recorded one track apiece). Post-production handled with John Hadden.

The Holywell Concert (1990) — Lol Coxhill / George Haslam / Howard Riley / Paul Rutherford, Slam Productions

Recorded live at the Holywell Music Room, Oxford — almost certainly facilitated by Gerzon’s Oxford base. He provided the digital recording; George Haslam produced and handled design. This is the sole Slam Productions credit in the Discogs database. Slam Productions label ID: in vault.

Figuring (1990) — Barre Phillips / Derek Bailey, Incus

Duo session with Barre Phillips (bass) and Derek Bailey (guitar). Post-production with John Hadden; design by Karen Brookman.

Live Improvisations (1991) — Fred Frith / Tim Hodgkinson, Megaphone Records / Woof Records

Gerzon recorded this duo of Fred Frith (guitar, violin, vocals) and Tim Hodgkinson (keyboards, alto sax, clarinet). The sophomore release on Jason Willett’s Megaphone Records (3).

Quartet (Birmingham) 1985 and Quartet (Coventry) 1985 (1991, 1993) — Anthony Braxton, Leo Records

Two related releases documenting the same 1985 quartet — Anthony Braxton, Marilyn Crispell, Mark Dresser, Gerry Hemingway — at two UK venues. Gerzon recorded both. Produced by Leo Feigin for Leo Records.

Conic Sections (For Kunio Nakamura) (1993) — Evan Parker, Ah Um

Solo soprano sax. Gerzon digitally recorded. Released on Ah Um (ID: 65069).

Downtime (1998) — Peter Blegvad, Locus Solus / ReR Megacorp

Peter Blegvad singer-songwriter album. Gerzon’s credit is for live recording of tracks 15–16 only, within a larger production featuring Chris Cutler, John Greaves, Tim Hodgkinson, and others. Issued on ReR Megacorp.
