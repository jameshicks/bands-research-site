Ted Niceley

Ted Niceley

Oct 12, 20269 min read

Ted Niceley

Discogs ID: 263544
Origin: Washington, DC area, USA
Active as producer/engineer: c. 1979–present
Also known as: Ted Meanley (alias), Ted Alan Niceley, Ted “911” Niceley, Ted “Che Caruso” Niceley, Ted Crooner Niceley, Mr. Ted Niceley

Producer, engineer, and bassist from Washington, DC. Best known for co-producing the first four Fugazi records with Don Zientara at Inner Ear Studios, establishing the sonic template for DC post-hardcore in the late 1980s and early 1990s. Career spans early DC power pop in the late 1970s through major-label production work in the mid-1990s and continued independent work into the 2010s.

Biography

Early Career: DC Power Pop (Late 1970s–Early 1980s)

Niceley began his career as a bassist in the late 1970s DC music scene. He played bass for power pop band Razz (5) (also credited as Razz), appearing on their 1977 release C.Redux / 70’s Anomie (O’Rourke Records) and 1979 Air Time (Limp Records). Around this time he also moved into production, producing the Third EP (1979) for what appears to be the same Razz/O’Rourke circle.

He subsequently played bass with Tommy Keene as Keene transitioned to his solo band — a connection documented through the 1982 Strange Alliance (Avenue Records), on which Niceley played bass and percussion and also produced, and the 1984 Places That Are Gone (Dolphin Records), another bass/producer double credit. He appears again on the 1986 Tommy Keene album Songs From The Film (Geffen Records) in a bass role. A 2010 Tommy Keene retrospective (Tommy Keene You Hear Me: A Retrospective 1983–2009) also credits him on bass.

During this period Niceley was also producing other DC-area acts. He produced Negative Approach-affiliated material and the hardcore-adjacent The Brain That Refused To Die / (Are You Gonna Be There At The) Love-In (Dacoit Records, 1980) and Here To Stay / Porcelain Butter Kitten (Dacoit Records, 1981). The 1985 War Of The Superbikes for Meatmen (Homestead Records) is another early production credit, where he co-produced with engineer Jim Crenca alongside players including Brian Baker and Lyle Preslar — both ex-Minor Threat.

The Fugazi Years (1988–1993)

Niceley’s most consequential association began with Fugazi’s self-titled debut EP in 1988 (Dischord Records). The production split across that record and the following releases is consistent: Niceley and the band share the Producer credit; Don Zientara engineers at Inner Ear Studios in Arlington, Virginia.

Fugazi EP (1988, Dischord DIS 30) — Producers: Fugazi, Ted Niceley. Engineer: Don Zientara. Band: Ian MacKaye (vocals/guitar), Guy Picciotto (vocals/guitar), Joe Lally (bass), Brendan Canty (drums).

13 Songs (1989, Dischord DIS 36) — A compilation of both EPs. Tracks 1–7 produced by Fugazi and Ted Niceley, engineered by Don Zientara; tracks 8–13 produced and engineered by John Loder (Southern Studios, London) with Paul Gadd.

Repeater (1990, Dischord DIS 44) — Producer: Ted Niceley. Engineer: Don Zientara. Niceley receives sole outside-producer credit here, with the band not listed as producers on this pressing.

In On The Kill Taker (1993, Dischord DIS 60) — Producers: Fugazi, Ted Niceley. Engineer: Don Zientara. Graphic concept and texts by Jem Cohen; cover by Jason Farrell; band photo by The Spectra System; “carry out” photo by Cynthia Connolly.

Notably, Steady Diet Of Nothing (1991) was produced entirely by the band without Niceley — the one Fugazi studio album in this period on which he does not appear.

The Niceley/Zientara/Inner Ear pairing became one of the defining sonic identities of the Dischord scene: dry, close-miked, minimal reverb, with powerful low-mid presence. Other Dischord work from this period includes Of Two Minds (1991) and Curiosity And Relief (1992), and a mixing credit on the New Orleans Opera release (1989).

Outside DC: Independent and Major-Label Production (1989–1998)

Parallel to the Fugazi work, Niceley built a broader production practice spanning punk, alt-rock, and international acts.

Mudhoney / Sub Pop — Produced the Mudhoney single Song #1 b/w Joe #1 plus Break In (Sub Pop, 1989), an early Sub Pop release featuring the same Fugazi lineup engineered by Zientara — an unusual crossover between the DC and Seattle scenes.

Girls Against Boys (Touch And Go) — Produced three records for GVSB: Venus Luxure No.1 Baby (1993), Cruise Yourself (1994, on which he also took the band photo), and House Of GVSB (1996). Also mixed two additional Touch And Go 12-inches for the band: Super-fire (1996) and Sexy Sam / I’m From France (1994). Engineer on the GVSB records was Eli Janney.

Jawbox (Atlantic) — Produced For Your Own Special Sweetheart (1994), the band’s Atlantic debut. Engineer/mixer was Drew Mazurek; band led by J. Robbins.

Noir Désir (Barclay) — French rock band. Produced Tostaky (1992) and continued through several further releases including 666.667 Club (1996) and 4 (a later Pyromane Records release, 2010). Also mixed the Dies Irae live record (1994). Engineer on Tostaky was Eli Janney.

Shudder To Think (Epic) — Produced Pony Express Record (1994) and 50,000 B.C. (1997), as well as the single Mind Science Of The Mind (1996) and Red House (1997). Also produced the related Chad Clark material including Connected (1981, Limp Records — possibly an earlier, separate project).

Jawbreaker (Pony Records / DGC) — Produced For Your Own Special Sweetheart is Jawbox; for Jawbreaker specifically, Niceley is not credited in the Discogs data reviewed. (Verify: the Atlantic credit above is Jawbox, not Jawbreaker.)

Cows / Tar (Touch And Go) — Produced Cows’ Pony Express Record credit is Shudder To Think (see above). For Touch And Go releases specifically, Niceley’s credits include the Cows single Sexy Sam (1994) and work for The Jesus Lizard (not confirmed in this dataset — do not infer).

Thinking Fellers Union Local 282 (Matador/Mammoth) — Mixed and provided additional production on Shape (1996, Attic/Mammoth).

Later Career (2000s–2010s)

Production work continued at a lower volume. Credits include:

  • You Can’t Fight What You Can’t See — Girls Against Boys (Jade Tree, 2002)
  • Future Kings Of Spain — (The Red Flag Recording Co., 2003)
  • Le Debemos EP — (The Red Flag Recording Co., 2004)
  • Back To Zero Now — (12XU, 2013) — also bass credit
  • Set It Right — (Arctic Rodeo Recordings, 2019) — also bass credit

He continued playing bass in groups including Nightman (2) and the earlier Razz (5).

Connections

  • Don Zientara — Primary engineering partner throughout the Fugazi run; both rooted at Inner Ear Studios in Arlington, VA. The Niceley/Zientara pairing defines the sonic character of early Dischord post-hardcore.
  • Fugazi — Four records across 1988–1993; Niceley absent from Steady Diet Of Nothing (1991).
  • Tommy Keene — Pre-Fugazi career as bassist and producer in DC power pop.
  • Girls Against Boys — Longest single-band production relationship outside Fugazi; five records and mixing credits across 1993–2002.
  • Shudder To Think — Three Epic records 1994–1997; Niceley helped broker the band’s major-label sound.
  • Noir Désir — Transatlantic French rock relationship beginning 1992; continued for nearly two decades.
  • Jawbox — Co-produced the band’s Atlantic debut For Your Own Special Sweetheart (1994) with J. Robbins; see also J. Robbins.
  • Meatmen — Early DC-scene production work (1985) alongside ex-Minor Threat members.
