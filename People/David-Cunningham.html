David Cunningham

Producer, composer, multi-instrumentalist, and label founder. Born in Ireland, 1954. Lives and works in London. Discogs artist ID: 41910. Name variations: Cunningham, D Cunningham, D. Cunningham, D.C., DC, David.

Founder of the Piano label (est. 1976). Guiding force and primary creative behind The Flying Lizards. One of the key producers of the British post-punk and experimental scene 1979–1985, with credits on landmark records by This Heat, Palais Schaumburg, and Michael Nyman. Later academic: AHRB Research Fellow in Fine Art, University of Newcastle.

Biography

Early Work and Piano Label (1976–1978)

Cunningham founded his own imprint, Piano, in 1976, and released his debut LP Grey Scale (Piano 001) the following year — one of the earliest self-released British experimental records. The album is a solo multi-tracking exercise across piano, violin, bass guitar, glockenspiel, synthesizer, percussion, and tape — organised around “Error System” variations and tape-loop pieces titled after South American countries. It establishes his working method: systematic exploration of acoustic/electronic material through repetition and process.

He produced This Heat’s self-titled debut (1979, Piano THIS-1) in his own label’s first major statement beyond his own work — a co-production with the band and co-engineering credit alongside Chris Blake, Frank Bryan, Kevin Harrison, Rik Walton, and Anthony Moore.

Early Production Credit: The Electric Chairs (1977–78)

Cunningham produced “So Many Ways” for The Electric Chairs (Wayne/Jayne County’s band), appearing as track 7 on Punk 45 - I’m A Mess! D-I-Y Or Die! (Soul Jazz, 2022). This predates his Piano label work and Flying Lizards recordings — an early punk-era production credit in the Discogs record.

The Flying Lizards and Virgin Records (1979–1984)

The Flying Lizards were Cunningham’s primary vehicle for art-pop experimentation on major/significant independent labels. Their version of “Money (That’s What I Want)” (Virgin, 1979) was an international hit — deconstructions of pop and R&B originals through studio manipulation. The band was a loose collective; the Discogs membership list includes David Toop, Steve Beresford, Patti Palladin, Deborah Evans-Stickland, Julian Marshall, Michael Upton, Bob Black, and Sally Peterson. Cunningham wrote most originals, played guitar/keyboards/percussion/tape, engineered, mixed, and produced all Flying Lizards releases.

The Flying Lizards discography continued through the Statik Records years (1984): Dizzy Miss Lizzie, Sex Machine, Top Ten compilation. Cunningham played drums, keyboards, percussion, instruments across these.

The Secret Dub Life of the Flying Lizards (1996, Piano PIANO 501). Cunningham restructured and produced a dub rework of Flying Lizards material in collaboration with reggae producer Jah Lloyd, who provided basic tracks. Design co-credit with Jon Wozencroft.

Post-Punk Production Work (1979–1984)

Cunningham’s studio work in this period was unusually wide-ranging — connecting British post-punk, German Neue Deutsche Welle, French punk, and experimental music.

This Heat — self-titled (1979, Piano THIS-1). Co-produced with the band. Engineers included Cunningham alongside Chris Blake, Frank Bryan, Kevin Harrison, and Rik Walton. Members: Charles Bullen, Gareth Williams, Charles Hayward.

Family Fodder — “Savoir Faire” single (1980, Fresh Records / Crammed Discs). Cunningham co-produced the “Savoir Faire” single with Charles Bullen of This Heat, under the joint production alias “Executives for Executives.” The single was recorded at Cold Storage in Brixton. Cunningham is also credited as Executive Producer on the track as it appears on Savoir Faire: The Best Of (1998). This is the only confirmed Family Fodder connection in the web record. (source: factmag.com, 2016)

General Strike — Danger In Paradise (1984, Touch TO: 2). General Strike was a trio — Cunningham, David Toop, and Steve Beresford. The album is experimental/dub on Touch, with guest appearances by Lol Coxhill (soprano/tenor saxophone). All three co-produced.

Collaboration with John Greaves (1991–1995)

Cunningham formed a sustained creative partnership with bassist/vocalist John Greaves (ex-Henry Cow, National Health).

Greaves, Cunningham (1991, Eva WWCX 2030, Japan). A co-billed album. Cunningham composed the majority of tracks and played guitar, effects/treatments, percussion, keyboards; produced the record. Greaves contributed voice, bass, percussion, keyboards, and lyrics. Guests: Yasuaki Shimizu (tenor sax, clarinet), Jakko M. Jakszyk (vocals), Kristoffer Blegvad (acoustic guitar). Post-production handled by Richard Dowling and Noel Summerville.

The Yellow Box (1996, Piano). Cunningham’s solo work — guitars, loops, effects/treatments, prepared piano, percussion, keyboards. Engineers: Tom Newman, Aquarius/Geneva and Cold Room/London sessions. Guests include Peter Gordon, David Van Tieghem (David Van Tieghem), Anton Fier, Stephan Mathieu, Theo Krieger.

Songs — John Greaves (1995, Resurgence RES112CD). Cunningham appears as electric guitarist. A John Greaves–led album with an exceptional cast including Robert Wyatt (vocals, percussion), Elton Dean (saxello), Mireille Bauer (vibraphone), Sophia Domancich (piano), Caroline Loeb (vocals), Kristoffer Blegvad (vocals), Peter Blegvad (lyrics), François Ovide (acoustic guitar). Produced by Alain Cluzeau and Greaves.

Solo, Installations, and Later Work (1991–present)

Cunningham released solo works on his Piano label throughout the 1990s:

  • Voiceworks (1992, Eva) — composer, engineer, performer, producer
  • Water (1992, Made To Measure, Belgium) — performer, composer, producer
  • Ghost Dance (1996, Piano) — loops/treatment, guitar, percussion; written-by. Film score for Ken McMullen, co-credited alongside Michael Giles and Jamie Muir
  • Ext. Night (1997, Piano) — 60-minute solo electronic work
  • One Hundred (2008, Staubgold, Germany) — editor; features Yasuaki Shimizu

From 1993 he developed a series of site-specific acoustic installations, most notably “The Listening Room,” shown at the Biennale of Sydney (1998). Two installation works appeared in “Days Like These” (Tate Triennial, Tate Britain, 2003). Further installations at ICC Tokyo and Ikon Birmingham.

Academic post: AHRB (Arts and Humanities Research Board) Research Fellow in Fine Art, University of Newcastle.

Also a member of The Suspicions (Discogs ID: 912625), a smaller side project.
