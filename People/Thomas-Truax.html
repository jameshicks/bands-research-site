Thomas Truax

Oct 19, 20267 min read

Thomas Truax

Discogs ID: 384769
Origin: New York City, USA
Active: 1988–present
Also Credited As: Thomas; Thomas Truax & His Dark Tricks; Truax

American musician, singer-songwriter, and maker of homemade instruments. Truax (pronounced “troo-aks”) builds his own mechanical and electronic devices — most famously the Hornicator and Mother Superior (9) — and uses them alongside loop pedals, guitar, and imaginative lyrical storytelling. Sometimes labelled steampunk, though his work sits closer to experimental indie-folk. Member of Like Wow alongside Scott Hartley and Carlos Vivanco.

Early Career — New York, 1988

Truax’s earliest documented release is Spook Show (1988, Psycho Teddy, catno PT-1001), a 12” EP credited to Thomas Truax & His Dark Tricks — one of the name variations he used before settling on a single identity. The record is tagged New Wave and marks the founding of his own Psycho Teddy imprint, which he would return to throughout his career. Like Wow, the band he formed with Scott Hartley and Carlos Vivanco, issued one record on the label: Whacked In Waco! (1993, Psycho Teddy).

Full Moon Over Wowtown Era — 2004

Full Moon Over Wowtown (2004, Breakin’ Beats, BBEATSCD004) is Truax’s earliest widely documented solo album in Discogs, released in Europe. It is a recording of exceptional personnel density — rather than a fixed band, Truax worked with shifting small ensembles across individual tracks, producing, writing, and engineering alongside multiple collaborators.

UK Period — SL Records / Akoustik Anarkhy, 2006–2011

Truax relocated to the UK and built a steady presence there. A run of releases came out on SL Records and the short-lived Akoustik Anarkhy Recordings:

  • Have We Been Left Behind? (2006, Akoustik Anarkhy Recordings) — CDr promo single; credited performers include Meredith Yayanos and Erin Gillgannon and Sister Spinster.
  • Why Dogs Howl At The Moon (2007, SL Records) — promo single; Truax credited on Hornicator and vocals. Styles: Art Rock, Avantgarde, Experimental, Leftfield.
  • Songs From The Films Of David Lynch (2009, Psycho Teddy / SL Records, PT1010) — limited/numbered CD. Ten covers of songs from Lynch films, including “Wicked Game,” “Twin Peaks (Falling),” “Blue Velvet,” “I’m Deranged,” and “In Heaven (Lady In The Radiator Song).” Truax credited on all conventional and invented instruments. Mastered by Dirk T. Jambor; cover co-designed with Philip Lethen, who also plays contrabass and piano on “I Put A Spell On You.”
  • Sonic Dreamer (2010, Psycho Teddy / SL Records, PT-1011) — 11-track CD album; Truax sole credited performer.
  • Grandmother’s Advice (2011, SL Records) — CDr single; solo.

Monthly Journal and Blang, 2012

Monthly Journal (2012, Blang / Psycho Teddy, BLANG 41) — 12-track concept album structured around the months of the year, released in Europe. Truax sole credited performer. The album includes a version of “Grandmother’s Advice” from the 2011 single.

Jetstream Sunset, 2015

Jetstream Sunset (2015, Psycho Teddy!, PT-1028) — 11-track CD. Key departure: Brian Viglione (of The Dresden Dolls) drums on the album. Mixed by Markus Maria Jansen; mastered by Noah Mintz. Pete Harvey plays cello on “Written In Your Blood.” Truax plays “Naive Drums” alongside Viglione — a characteristic designation for his lo-fi home percussion. Also includes a cover of “You Are My Sunshine.”

All That Heaven Allows, 2018

All That Heaven Allows (2018, Psycho Teddy Records, PT-1032) — 10-track LP. Sole-credited release; no additional performers listed in Discogs. Styles: Alternative Rock, Experimental. Notable titles include “Humane Train,” “Precarious Waltz,” and “International Homeland Security.”

Dream Catching Songs — Collaboration with Budgie and Mother Superior (9), 2023

Dream Catching Songs (2023, Blang / Psycho Teddy, BLANG104LP) — 10-track LP, a genuine co-billed collaboration. Primary credits: Thomas Truax with Budgie (2) and Mother Superior (9). Budgie (2) is Peter Clarke, long-time drummer for Siouxsie And The Banshees (Discogs ID 295874). Mother Superior (9) is a homemade instrument built by Truax (Discogs entity ID 13786693), treated as a co-performer — a conceptual credit consistent with his instrument-maker practice.

Instruments

Truax’s homemade instruments are central to his identity and credited explicitly on releases:

  • The Hornicator — credited by name on Full Moon Over Wowtown (2004) and Why Dogs Howl At The Moon (2007)
  • Mother Superior (9) — Discogs entity ID 13786693; billed as a co-performer on Dream Catching Songs (2023)
  • Naive Drums — his term for lo-fi home percussion, credited on Jetstream Sunset (2015)
  • Various unnamed “uncredited sounds,” noisemakers, triwave generators, tin whistle, toy piano
