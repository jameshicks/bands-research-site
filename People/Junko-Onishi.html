Junko Onishi

Junko Onishi

Aug 22, 202613 min read

Junko Onishi

Born: April 16, 1967, Kyoto, Japan Real name: 大西順子 Instruments: Piano, keyboards (Rhodes, Clavinet) Roles: Performer, bandleader, producer, arranger Discogs artist ID: 518834 Name variations: J. Onishi, 大西順子

Japanese jazz pianist who emerged from the Tokyo scene in the early 1990s and built a sustained international career. Best known for her trio’s two live albums recorded at the Village Vanguard in 1994 — flagship releases on Blue Note Japan — she has since returned to active recording under her own Somethin’ Cool imprint, expanding into orchestral and large-ensemble formats.

Biography

Emergence and Early Recordings (1991–1993)

Onishi’s first known Discogs credit is as a sideman pianist on Ja-Hed (1991, Lob Inc.), an early Tokyo jazz session. Her profile rose quickly: by 1993 she was leading her own trio, the Junko Onishi Trio, releasing two albums in rapid succession. Cruisin’ (1993, Blue Note / Somethin’ Else) featured Rodney Whitaker on bass and Billy Higgins on drums — two American jazz veterans in a New York-recorded session. Wow (1993, Somethin’ Else) was recorded in Tokyo with a different lineup: Tomoyuki Shima (bass) and Dairiki Hara (drums). On Cruisin’ Onishi is credited as Piano and Co-producer; on Wow she is sole producer. She wrote original material on both albums; Cruisin’ also features Duke Ellington compositions.

The executive producer across nearly all her 1990s records was Hitoshi Namekata (Discogs ID: 1353644), with A&R directed by Yoshiko Tsuge (Discogs ID: 594490), both of Somethin’ Else. Recording and mixing for most of the Blue Note period was handled by engineer Jim Anderson (Discogs ID: 282077), a recurrent technical collaborator throughout the decade.

The Village Vanguard Records (1994)

The twin live albums are the centerpiece of Onishi’s early career. Live At The Village Vanguard (1994, Blue Note / Somethin’ Else) and Live At The Village Vanguard II (1994, Blue Note / Somethin’ Else) were both recorded by the same trio lineup:

Junko Onishi Trio — Village Vanguard personnel:

InstrumentPlayer
PianoJunko Onishi
BassReginald Veal
DrumsHerlin Riley

Reginald Veal and Herlin Riley were both alumni of Wynton Marsalis’s band at the time, bringing a direct lineage from the Marsalis-era Blue Note aesthetic. Both albums were recorded by Jim Anderson, mixed by Masuzo Iida (Discogs ID: 1055715), and mastered by Yoshio Okazaki (Discogs ID: 518826). Onishi is credited as Piano and Co-producer on both.

Piano Quintet Suite and Blue Note Period Expansion (1995–1997)

Piano Quintet Suite (1995, Blue Note) was Onishi’s first leader album under her own name rather than the trio. The expanded quintet lineup brought in:

InstrumentPlayer
PianoJunko Onishi
BassRodney Whitaker
DrumsTony Rabeson (Discogs ID: 317358)
Alto SaxophoneEiichi Hayashi (Discogs ID: 706584)
Trumpet + VocalsMarcus Belgrave

Belgrave’s presence is notable: the Detroit trumpet veteran was already a key figure in the US jazz-to-Detroit-hip-hop crossover world. Recording and mixing by Jim Anderson. Executive producer again Hitoshi Namekata.

Also in 1995 Onishi contributed liner notes for a Blue Note Japan reissue of At The Hickory House Volume 1, signalling her role as a commentator within the jazz tradition alongside her performing career.

In 1996, the trio recorded live again in Europe: Play, Piano, Play - Junko Onishi Trio In Europe (1996, Somethin’ Else) with Shigeo Aramaki (Discogs ID: 6043057) on bass and Dairiki Hara on drums — a different trio lineup from the Vanguard recordings. The same year, Junko Onishi Trio At The Montreux Jazz Festival (1996, Toshiba EMI) was captured with the same Aramaki/Hara configuration; produced by Claude Nobs, the Montreux festival director.

Hat Trick (1996, Somethin’ Else) is co-billed as “Jackie McLean meets Junko Onishi,” placing her alongside the legendary Blue Note alto saxophonist Jackie McLean. The full quintet for that date:

InstrumentPlayer
PianoJunko Onishi
Alto SaxophoneJackie McLean
BassNat Reeves (Discogs ID: 1232821)
DrumsLewis Nash

McLean also co-produced. Recorded and mixed by Jim Anderson.

In 1997, Tenor Time (Somethin’ Else) credits Onishi as pianist on a session led by Joe Lovano, with Rodney Whitaker on bass and Al Foster (Discogs ID: 271001) on drums. Lovano produced alongside Michael Cuscuna (Discogs ID: 252960).

Self Portrait and Late-90s Transitional Work (1998–1999)

Fragile (1998, Somethin’ Else) is an eclectic Onishi leader date featuring rotating drummers — Karriem Riggins (billed as “Ol Skool Jamz”), Motohiko Hino (Discogs ID: 322166), and Tamaya Honda (Discogs ID: 1585443) — over Reginald Veal on bass and keyboards from Onishi. Vocalist Peace (Discogs ID: 70391) appears. Jim Anderson recorded and mixed. This record marks the first appearance of Riggins in her orbit.

Self Portrait (1998, Somethin’ Else) is a summit of the decade’s collaborators, featuring different rhythm sections track by track:

  • Herlin Riley (drums, tracks 2–3) with Reginald Veal (bass)
  • Rodney Whitaker (bass, tracks 4, 8) with Billy Higgins (drums, track 4) and Tony Rabeson (drums, track 8)
  • Shigeo Aramaki (bass, tracks 6–7) with Dairiki Hara (drums)
  • Motohiko Hino (drums, track 1) with Yasushi Yoneki (Discogs ID: 557446) on bass
  • Guest appearances: Eiichi Hayashi (alto sax, track 8), Tetsuro Kawashima (tenor sax, track 7), Yoshiro Okazaki (trumpet, track 7), Marcus Belgrave (trumpet + vocals, track 8)

In 1999, Cool Woods (Somethin’ Else) placed Onishi at the piano in a session led by saxophonist Phil Woods, with Ron Carter on bass and Bill Goodwin on drums. The session was recorded by Kurt Lundvall and mixed by Jim Anderson.

Hiatus and Return: Musical Moments and Baroque (2009–2010)

After the Cool Woods session, Onishi’s recording activity goes quiet in the Discogs data for roughly a decade. She returned with Musical Moments (2009, Blue Note / Somethin’ Else Records) — reissued in Europe in 2009 — and the leader album Baroque (2010, Verve Records). Baroque is a richly orchestrated large-ensemble record:

Recorded and mixed by Jim Anderson. Co-produced with Kuninori Tamamori (Discogs ID: 6076014), mastered by Alan Silverman (Discogs ID: 476080). The Verve credit is notable as a departure from Blue Note / Somethin’ Else.

Somethin’ Cool era: Sextet and orchestra work (2017–2021)

Onishi relaunched under the Somethin’ Cool label (Discogs ID: 995785, a sublabel of DIW Products Group, Discogs ID: 82511). Her production work became more central in this period.

Very Special (2017, Somethin’ Cool) — leader album, Onishi as producer and pianist.

Glamorous Life (2017, Somethin’ Else) — Onishi writes liner notes and is credited as producer and pianist on what appears to be a tribute or collaborative project.

XII (2018, Somethin’ Cool) — billed as “Junko Onishi Presents The Sextet” (The Sextet, Discogs ID: 6077360). Full personnel:

Recorded and mixed by Shinya Matsushita (Discogs ID: 2249635). Co-produced by Ryoko Sakamoto (Discogs ID: 3912105) alongside Onishi and Hitoshi Namekata. Mastered by Akihito Yoshikawa (Discogs ID: 4391144). This session establishes Onishi’s core 2010s Japanese ensemble.

Jatroit: The Japan-Detroit Bridge (2019)

Junko Onishi Presents Jatroit (2019, DIW Products Group / Somethin’ Cool) is a three-way primary artist credit — Onishi, Robert Hurst (bass), and Karriem Riggins (drums). The title conflates “Japan” and “Detroit.” Full credits:

InstrumentPlayer
PianoJunko Onishi
BassRobert Hurst
DrumsKarriem Riggins

Produced by Onishi; co-produced by Hitoshi Namekata and Ryoko Sakamoto. Recorded and mixed by Shinya Matsushita. The collaboration with Riggins and Hurst — both core to the Detroit jazz tradition — extends a thread that began with Onishi’s use of Riggins on Fragile (1998). The concept of bridging Tokyo and Detroit jazz became a defining project-statement for her later career.

Out Of The Dawn and Large-Ensemble Conducting (2021)

Out Of The Dawn (2021, Somethin’ Cool) is billed as “Junko Onishi Presents The Orchestra” — an ambitious large-ensemble project with Onishi as sole producer. Key orchestra members:

  • Trumpet: Miki Hirose, Yusuke Sase (Discogs ID: 6297165)
  • Trombone: Mitsuhiro Wada (Discogs ID: 5383261), Shigetaka Ikemoto; Bass Trombone: Hiroyuki Aochi
  • Tuba: Atsushi Matsunaga (Discogs ID: 2763081)
  • Saxophone: Akihiro Yoshimoto (soprano/tenor/flute), David Negrete (alto/flute, Discogs ID: 7286527), Yasuki Sogabe (tenor/soprano/flute, Discogs ID: 9455803), Yu Kuga (baritone/clarinet)
  • Clarinet: Yoshie Sato (Discogs ID: 6126496)
  • Bass: Yosuke Inoue; Guitar: Takayoshi Baba (Discogs ID: 1427492)
  • Drums: Sota Kira, Shogo Hamada (2) (Discogs ID: 8868004); Percussion: Ogimi Gen (Discogs ID: 217670)
  • Piano: Kazuhiro Takemoto (tracks 2–4, 6–14), Dairo Suga (tracks 1, 5)
  • Oboe/English Horn: Shu Homma

Compositions credited to Akihiro Yoshimoto (tracks 1, 7, 8), Miki Hirose (tracks 2–6, 10, 14), and Yosuke Inoue (tracks 9, 11, 12) — indicating ensemble members wrote much of the book. Recorded and mixed by Shinya Matsushita. Cover illustration by jazz trumpeter Terumasa Hino (Discogs ID: 303402).

Also in 2021: Grand Voyage (Somethin’ Cool) — Onishi on piano and as producer, alongside an ensemble.

American Classics (2026)

The most recent Discogs entry: American Classics (2026, DIW Products Group / Somethin’ Cool) — under her own name, three pressings, produced and played piano.
