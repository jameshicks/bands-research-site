Mark Cunningham

Born: 7 April 1952, New Jersey, USA Instruments: Trumpet, bass, vocals, guitar, synthesizer Discogs ID: 198178 Website: markcunningham.cat

Trumpeter, bassist, vocalist, producer, and multi-instrumentalist. One of the core members of No Wave band Mars, which contributed four tracks to No New York (Brian Eno / Antilles, 1978) — the foundational document of the No Wave movement. After Mars dissolved, Cunningham continued through the 1980s in New York with Don King and produced Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s final studio album Suspense (Polydor, 1988). He relocated to Barcelona in 1991, where he formed or contributed to a series of experimental and avant-rock bands: Raeo (2), Convolution (2), Bèstia Ferida, Blood Quartet, and occasional projects with Bel Canto Orchestra. Also active in duo formats including Aleatory Grammar and Cunningham - Volt - Serra.

Early Life and Eckerd College

Attended Eckerd College in St. Petersburg, Florida, where he met Connie Burg (later China Burg) and Arto Lindsay. All three relocated to New York together in 1974. This Eckerd pipeline seeded a significant portion of the No Wave scene: Cunningham and Burg went on to form Mars; Lindsay went on to form DNA.

Mars (1975–1981)

Mars formed in December 1975 (originally as China; renamed Mars in 1977 after a naming conflict). The core lineup throughout: Mark Cunningham (trumpet, bass, vocals), Connie Burg (aka China Burg; guitar, vocals), Sumner Crane (guitar, vocals), and Nancy Arlen (drums). Rudolph Grey was also briefly a member per Discogs.

Mars were widely considered the most extreme of the No Wave bands: non-standard tunings, near-atonal structures, shattered rhythmic forms. Their four tracks on No New York appear on side B.

Later guitarist Rudolph Grey also appears in the Discogs member roster but his tenure and role are not fully documented there.

John Gavanti (1978–c.1980)

Post-Mars project formed by three of Mars’s four members: Cunningham, Connie Burg, and Sumner Crane, with Ikue Mori (of DNA (4)) on drums in place of Nancy Arlen. The project centered on a chamber-operatic concept by Crane.

  • John Gavanti (Hyrax Records, 1980): Cunningham credited as Horns and Producer. Also featured Crane (lyrics, vocals, guitar, piano, percussion), Mori (strings), Connie Burg (bass clarinet, as “Don Burg”), Walter Sear and Bob Schwall (engineers).

Don King (1983–1987)

Post-Mars/post-Gavanti ensemble with significant overlap. Members included Cunningham, Arto Lindsay, Connie Burg, Duncan Lindsay, Toni Nogueira, and Tony Maimone. Engineer/producer Martin Bisi on the 1985 LP. The band worked with Spanish label 4 Sellos for their later release, bridging Cunningham’s New York and Barcelona lives.

Production Work: Lizzy Mercier Descloux — Suspense (Polydor, 1988)

Cunningham’s most prominent production credit. Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s fifth and final studio album, recorded in Oxfordshire and Wales. He co-produced with John Brand and Descloux herself, and played trumpet and bass. The album also featured Bruce Smith (drums; The Pop Group, Rip Rig + Panic, PiL), Kim Burton (accordion), Stuart Gordon (viola, violin, cello), Constanza Burg (bass clarinet), and Julian Stewart-Lindsay (keyboards/Fairlight).

A companion single, Gueule D’Amour (Polydor, 1988), lists Cunningham as producer.

Barcelona Era (1991 onward)

Cunningham relocated to Barcelona in 1991. The following projects document his activity there.

Raeo (2) (c. early 1990s)

Spanish-based trio with Anton Ignorant and Gat (Gat, Discogs ID 509207).

  • XV El Diablo (G3G Records, 1992): sole Raeo (2) release found in DB.

Bel Canto Orchestra

Large ensemble led by Pascal Comelade, based in the Perpignan area, with revolving membership. Cunningham is listed as a member. Discogs credits one primary release: Dans Le Cadre De Casino (Ding Dong Records and Tapes, 1985, Netherlands).
