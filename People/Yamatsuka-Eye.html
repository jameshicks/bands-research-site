Yamatsuka Eye

Discogs ID: 170037
Real name: 山塚徹郎 (Yamatsuka Tetsurō)
Name variations: EYE, EYƎ, Yamantaka Eye, Yamataka Eye, Tetsuo Yamatsuka (and dozens of project aliases — see below)
Origin: Osaka, Japan

Vocalist, visual artist, producer, electronics manipulator. Founder of Boredoms and Hanatarash. Core member of Naked City (John Zorn’s all-genre ensemble, 1988–1993). Prolific collaborator across noise, free jazz, experimental rock, and avant-pop. Also known for visual art and album sleeve work — most notably art direction and collage for Beck’s Midnite Vultures (1999).

Career

Hanatarash (1983–early 1990s)

Eye founded Hanatarash (ハナタラシ, “sniveler”) in Osaka in 1983. The project was built around extreme noise music and notoriously dangerous live performances: Eye threw junk with a backhoe inside a venue, operated an electric saw mid-show (inflicting a deep leg wound on himself), and on at least one occasion severed a dead cat on stage. At a 1985 Tokyo show (Superloft), the audience was required to sign liability waivers; the performance ended just before Eye was to throw a lit molotov cocktail onto a gasoline-soaked stage. That show caused ¥600,000 in venue damage. Hanatarash’s reputation eventually made it impossible to book shows.

Early releases under the Hanatarash name appeared on cassette via the Condome Cassex imprint (1984–85): Take Back Your Penis!! (1984), ハナズマリ (1984), Cock Aktion (1985). A live album from 1984 was released in 1993 (Live!! 84 Dec. 16 Zabo-Kyoto, Mom ‘N’ Dad Productions).

Other Hanatarash members included Ikuo Taketani and Ohmiya Ichi. Discogs ID for the band: 78093.

Boredoms (1986–present)

Eye founded Boredoms (ボアダムス) in Osaka in 1986. The early Boredoms fused noise, hardcore punk, and art-rock chaos. The core lineup that recorded the first major releases included Eye (vocals, electronics), Yoshimi P-We (drums, percussion, trumpet, keyboards), Seiichi Yamamoto (guitar), Hira (bass), and various rotating members.

Early US releases on Shimmy Disc:

  • Soul Discharge (1989, Shimmy Disc shimmy 035) — first US LP; Eye credited “Vocals [Aaaahg]”; lineup included Yoshimi, Seiichi Yamamoto, Hira, and others

Major label era (Reprise Records / WEA Japan):

  • Pop Tatari (1992, Reprise) — Eye credited with artwork
  • Chocolate Synthesizer (1994, Reprise 9 45814-2) — lineup: Eye, Yoshimi, Seiichi Yamamoto, Hira, ATR (Kazuya Nishimura), Toyohito Yoshikawa; Eye produced, mixed, and contributed artwork alongside Dirty Rotten Graphucks, Ishiguroo, and Shoohey!; engineered by Ohji Hayashi

Late-period WEA Japan / A.K.A. Records era:

  • Super æ (1998, WEA Japan / A.K.A. Records WPC6-8442) — Eye produced, mixed, wrote lyrics and music; track-by-track personnel: Eye on tape manipulation, synthesizer, vocals, bongos, electronics, loops; Yoshimi, ATR, E-Da (drums/perc), Seiichi Yamamoto (guitar), Hira (bass); mixed by Eye and Ohji Hayashi
  • Vision Creation Newsun (1999, WEA Japan / A.K.A. Records WPC6-10044-5) — double CD box set; Eye as 1st and 2nd mix engineer, remix, editor, and performer (vocals, synthesizer, sampler, turntables, open reel tape, vocoder, computer, electronics); live disc included a November 1998 Osaka City University outdoor concert; additional synthesizer by Kiyoshi Izumi

Naked City (1988–1993)

Eye was the vocalist in John Zorn’s Naked City, formed 1988. The ensemble combined jazz, metal, noise, and film music with extreme stylistic switching. Members: Zorn (alto saxophone), Bill Frisell (guitar), Fred Frith (bass), Joey Baron (drums), Wayne Horvitz (keyboards), Eye (vocals/guest).

Key releases:

  • Naked City (1990, Elektra/Nonesuch 9 79238-2) — self-titled debut; 26 tracks; Eye credited “Vocals, Guest [Special]”; engineered and mixed by Roger Moutenot; illustrated by Suehiro Maruo; mastered by Bob Ludwig
  • Torture Garden (1990, Shimmy Disc) — Eye “Guest, Vocals”
  • Heretic: Jeux Des Dames Cruelles (1992, Avant) — Eye credited “Voice”
  • Grand Guignol (1992, Avant) — Eye “Vocals”
  • Radio (1993, Avant) — Eye “Vocals”
  • BlackBox (Torture Garden / Leng Tch’e) (1997, Tzadik) — compilation; Eye credited on Torture Garden material

Ground Zero (3) (guest, early 1990s)

Eye appeared as guest vocalist on releases by Ground Zero (3) (Discogs ID 136992), Otomo Yoshihide’s jazz-noise unit. Ground Zero also included Sachiko M, Kazuhisa Uchihashi, and others in its 1994–98 lineup. Eye’s credit: “Guest [Special Guest], Arranged By” on Ground Zero (1993, God Mountain).

Solo and Collaborative Work

TV Shit (1994, Ecstatic Peace! E# 38) — EP co-credited to Sonic Youth and Yamatsuka Eye. Sonic Youth’s label (Thurston Moore’s Ecstatic Peace!). Four-part “No II” suite; uncredited features by J Mascis (Dinosaur Jr.) and Mark Arm (Mudhoney) on Part 1.

Nani Nani (1995, Tzadik TZ 7206) — collaborative album by Dekoboko Hajime (John Zorn alias, Discogs ID 735183) & Yamatsuka Eye. Personnel: Zorn (saxophone, harmonium, guitar, sitar, samples), Eye (vocals, drums, toys); designed by Ikue Mori; executive-produced by Zorn; associate produced by David Newgarden and Kazunori Sugiyama; mastered by Allan Tucker; recorded and mixed by Jason Baker.

Z-Rock Hawaii (1996, Nipp Guitar) — collaboration between Boredoms members (Eye, Yoshimi, Seiichi Yamamoto, Toyohito Yoshikawa) and Ween members (Aaron Freeman, Mickey Melchiondo, Andrew Weiss, Claude Coleman). Part of the Nipp Guitar Heavy series.

50¹º (2005, Tzadik TZ 5010) — collaborative album by Yamataka Eye & John Zorn. Personnel: Zorn (alto saxophone), Eye (voice, electronics), Taeji Sawai (computer/Powerbooks), Fred Frith (guitar on “Sun See Soon”); produced by Zorn; recorded by Daniel Goldaracena; mastered by Scott Hull (2); designed by Heung-Heung Chin (Chippy).

Musica Transonic & Yamataka Eye (1996, La Musica Records) — Eye credited “Voice.”

  • “Sexx Laws” single — Beck (1999, DGC) — artwork/collage

Notes (web)

Battles Gloss Drop — “Sundome” collaboration (2011)

Eye is credited as featured vocalist and co-writer on “Sundome”, the closing track of Battles’ Gloss Drop (2011, Warp Records).

How the collaboration came about. In a 2011 interview with Exclaim!, Ian Williams said the band’s choices of guest vocalists were mostly based on “vague connections” rather than close friendships, and cited Eye specifically: “[Boredoms’ Yamantaka] Eye actually stayed at my house about 20 years ago in Pittsburgh, in a different band UFO or Die.” This places the prior acquaintance in the early-to-mid 1990s, when Eye toured with UFO Or Die. The wording implies Williams reached out on the basis of that personal history rather than via Warp or a management introduction.

How the track was made. Dave Konopka described the working method in press coverage surrounding the album’s release: Eye sent the band a raw vocal track and said, in Konopka’s paraphrase, “Do whatever you want to it.” Konopka also noted that the vocals sounded as though Eye was speaking Japanese but were in fact invented syllables — “making up his own stuff and he’s repeating stuff that he’s making up, which is so hard to do.” The band then built the final arrangement around that material.
