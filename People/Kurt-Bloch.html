Overview

Kurt Bloch is a Seattle-based songwriter, guitarist, engineer, and producer who has been a fixture of the Pacific Northwest indie and punk scene since 1980. He is best known as a core member of the Fastbacks, the long-running Seattle power-pop act he shares with Kim Warnick and Lulu Gargiulo, and has also played in Filthy Friends, Cheaters, Bad Scene, and “S” Box. Beyond performing, Bloch has accumulated close to a thousand production and engineering credits, working extensively with Scott McCaughey, the Young Fresh Fellows, and Peter Buck — a cluster of collaborators that also brought him into Filthy Friends. His home studio, Strictly Vintage, has served as a recording base for much of this work, with output spread across Sub Pop, Yep Roc Records, and the long-running family imprint No Threes Records.

Notes (web) — Filthy Friends recruitment

How Bloch came to join Filthy Friends / Super Earth

The clearest account comes from Peter Buck in a 2017 Stereogum interview (stereogum.com). Buck described the initial Filthy Friends lineup as an existing ensemble, not a fresh recruitment:

“It was my solo band, basically. Bill I’ve played with for 15 years, Scott for 35.”

On Bloch specifically:

“Kurt, I’ve played with him, but I’ve never been in a band with him. But that was my solo band when we did the solo records.”

In other words, Bloch was already embedded in Buck’s extended musical circle before Filthy Friends existed. The route in ran through Scott McCaughey: Bloch joined Young Fresh Fellows (McCaughey’s band) in 1989, replacing Chuck Carroll, giving him an ongoing working relationship with McCaughey that preceded the Filthy Friends project by 25 years. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Young_Fresh_Fellows) McCaughey and Bloch also both appear extensively on Minus 5 recordings alongside Buck, deepening that three-way overlap.

Corin Tucker’s entry into the project followed a different path. Buck and Tucker had both relocated to Portland, Oregon by 2012; that year Buck was working on solo material and invited Tucker to contribute. The songwriting partnership that grew from those sessions in 2012–2013 became the basis for Filthy Friends. (Consequence, April 2014; Riff Magazine interview with Tucker)

The ensemble — Buck, Tucker, McCaughey, Bloch, and William Rieflin — first convened in the studio in 2014 under the tentative name Super Earth, debuting live at Secret Society Ballroom in Portland in April 2014. (Consequence, April 2014) Krist Novoselic dropped by a later session and played bass on two tracks of Invitation. (Magnet Magazine, November 2017) Linda Pitmon replaced Rieflin for the recorded albums. The band renamed itself Filthy Friends in 2016.

Summary of the recruitment chain:

  • Bloch → McCaughey: via Young Fresh Fellows (1989–ongoing) and Minus 5 sessions
  • Bloch/McCaughey → Buck: via Buck’s post-R.E.M. solo band and Minus 5
  • Tucker → Buck: via Portland proximity and 2012 solo album collaboration
  • All five → Super Earth / Filthy Friends: studio session, 2014
