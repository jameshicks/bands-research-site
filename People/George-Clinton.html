George Clinton

Discogs ID: 29262
Origin: Kannapolis, North Carolina / Detroit, Michigan, USA
Born: July 22, 1941

Founder and leader of the P-Funk collective (Parliament and Funkadelic). Brought Junie Morrison into the collective as musical director in 1977. Worked with Morrison across both Parliament and Funkadelic catalogs, 1977–1981, and on later solo Capitol Records albums (Computer Games 1982, You Shouldn’t-Nuf Bit Fish 1983). Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inductee (1997, as Parliament-Funkadelic).

Parliament — Casablanca Era

Produced all eight Casablanca Parliament studio LPs (1974–1980), from Up For The Down Stroke (NBLP 7002) through Trombipulation (NBLP 7249). Wrote or co-wrote the majority of the catalog alongside Bootsy Collins, Bernie Worrell, and Garry Shider. The P-Funk stage spectacle during the Earth Tour (1976–77) — including the Mothership landing prop — was his theatrical vision. See Parliament for full discography.

Post-Casablanca Solo Work

After Parliament’s Casablanca run ended (label went bankrupt 1980), Clinton signed to Capitol Records as a solo artist:

  • Computer Games (1982, Capitol)
  • You Shouldn’t-Nuf Bit Fish (1983, Capitol)
  • Some Of My Best Jokes Are Friends (1985, Capitol)

“Atomic Dog” (1982) became a solo hit and one of the most-sampled records in hip-hop.

Kamasi Washington connection

Featured on “Get Lit” from Kamasi Washington’s Fearless Movement (2024), alongside D Smoke. Part of Washington’s explicit outreach to the P-Funk lineage on that record.
