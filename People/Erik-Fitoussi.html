Erik Fitoussi

French guitarist, vocalist, and songwriter; co-founder of the Paris post-punk group Marie Et Les Garçons and a member of New York no-wave collective Rosa Yemen. Active primarily 1977–1990; also credited under the alias Erik Elliasson for his production and performance work with Lizzy Mercier Descloux.

Biography / Career

Origins: Marie Et Les Garçons (1977–1980)

Fitoussi co-founded Marie Et Les Garçons in Paris, playing guitar and sharing songwriting duties with vocalist Patrick Vidal and bassist Jean-Pierre Charriau, with Marie Girard on drums. The group recorded with manager/producer Michel Esteban from the start. Their debut single Rien A Dire (1977, Rebel Records) already showed the core lineup; the 1978 Spy Records single Attitudes brought in John Cale on piano and marimba, engineered by Mike Young. Fitoussi is credited on vocals and guitar across these early singles and is co-writer on most Marie Et Les Garçons material.

By 1979 the group had shifted to the New York orbit of ZE Records, co-produced by Michel Esteban and Michael Zilkha. The singles Divorce and French Boy (Part 1 & 2) — credited to the stripped-down name Garçons — were recorded in New York with arranger Carlos Franzetti and engineering from Bob Blank and Joe Arlotta. Fitoussi co-wrote both. The self-titled 1980 LP Marie Et Les Garçons (Celluloid), produced by Michel Esteban, collects earlier work; Fitoussi is credited as primary songwriter on most tracks (A1–A5, B1–B3, B5), with Vidal and Charriau sharing credits across the record.

Rosa Yemen and New York (1979)

In New York, Fitoussi was a member of Rosa Yemen, a side project led by Lizzy Mercier Descloux and involving D.J. Banes (Didier Esteban). The 1979 Rosa Yemen EP (Celluloid) listed Fitoussi as a group member, though performance credits on the EP itself went to Descloux (lead vocals, guitar), D.J. Banes (guitar), and engineer Bob Blank; Michel Esteban produced. Fitoussi’s primary Rosa Yemen contribution comes through his alias work.

The Elliasson Alias: Production for Lizzy Mercier Descloux (1979)

As Erik Elliasson, Fitoussi co-produced, arranged, and mixed Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s early solo recordings (1979), alongside D.J. Banes and Descloux herself, engineered by Bob Blank. These sessions appear on the 2003 compilation Press Color (ZE Records), where he is credited as “Erik Eliasson” for guitar, bass, keyboards, production, mixing, and co-writing (tracks 1–8). This is the most detailed documentation of his production role — he was integral to shaping Descloux’s minimal, guitar-driven sound on her earliest New York recordings.

1980 and the Garçons continuation

The 1980 Celluloid single Les Deux Amants, credited to Garçons (featuring Les Garçons), placed Fitoussi alongside Charriau, Chris Levrat, and Eric Melon, produced by Michel Esteban. By this point the various Marie Et Les Garçons / Garçons incarnations were winding down.

Late credits (1988–1990)

Fitoussi returned to Marie Et Les Garçons material in the late 1980s. The 1988 single Rebop! (Le Café Wha?) lists him and Vidal as co-writers. The 1989 compilation 76/77 (Instant Records) documents the early live lineup, with Fitoussi on guitar alongside Vidal, Charriau, and Girard. He also contributed guitar and piano to Patrick Vidal’s 1990 solo LP Histoires D’Aventures (New Rose Records), working alongside an extended band including Charriau, Jean Christophe Vautier, Yann Lina, and Philippe Zamora.

Scene Connections

Fitoussi sits at the intersection of the Paris post-punk scene and the New York no-wave/ZE Records milieu of the late 1970s. Marie Et Les Garçons were part of the loose French punk/new wave wave managed by Michel Esteban and his partner Lizzy Mercier Descloux, with ties to the early ZE Records circle (Esteban co-managed ZE alongside Michael Zilkha). The Garçons’ New York recordings brought them into contact with Bob Blank’s studio world and the downtown scene. As Erik Elliasson, Fitoussi bridged the French and New York downtown contexts directly — co-producing Descloux’s earliest solo work, which became foundational to the ZE Records / no-wave-adjacent sound. The Rosa Yemen EP is a primary document of that French-NY crossover.
