Jay Karpinski

Co-founder and drummer of The Six Parts Seven, based in Kent, Ohio. Discogs artist ID: 590718. Name variations: “Jay”, “Jay K”, “Jay K.” on credits.

Overview

Jay Karpinski co-founded The Six Parts Seven in Kent, Ohio in 1995 with his brother Allen Karpinski (guitar). He is the drummer on every documented Six Parts Seven release across the band’s active period (1995–2006) and their 2024 archival release Kissing Distance (with Goodmorning Valentine). He also appeared on the 2021 and 2025 reissues/archival compilations that drew on the back catalog.

Outside Six Parts Seven, he is a member of Noah (2) (with Allen and Ryan Rinella) and Old Hearts Club (with Allen, Kyle Thompson, and Mary Mazzer), the pre-Six Parts Seven band whose dissolution around 1997 directly led to the formation of Six Parts Seven.

Discogs shows no solo releases, no side projects beyond the above, and no compilation appearances. His entire documented output is collaborative.

Pre-Six Parts Seven: Old Hearts Club (1996–1997)

Before The Six Parts Seven existed, Jay and Allen were both in Old Hearts Club, an indie/post-rock band from Kent, OH. The lineup: Kyle Thompson (vocals, guitar), Allen Karpinski (bass, sampler), Mary Mazzer (lap steel), Jay Karpinski (drums), and Matt E. (drums — listed as former member). Their only release is the self-titled Old Hearts Club LP (1997, Happy Go Lucky / Old Hearts Recordings). Jay is credited as “Jay K.” under percussion.

According to the Discogs profile for Old Hearts Club, the band became The Six Parts Seven when Kyle Thompson moved away, forcing a reshaping of the project. Allen moved from bass to guitar; Jay retained drums. This origin story makes Old Hearts Club the direct prototype for Six Parts Seven’s instrumental post-rock sound.

Noah (2) (1995)

Jay and Allen also appeared under the name No@h (rendered “Noah (2)” on Discogs, artist ID 251494) as early as 1995. The only documented No@h release is a split 7” with The Man I Fell In Love With (1995, And Then The Bear Left), where the Karpinski brothers and Ryan Rinella are credited collectively as “the no@h speedsters are.” This split predates Old Hearts Club’s LP and represents the earliest documented Jay Karpinski recording.

Guest / Session Appearances

Boundary Problems — Time Pieces (2022, Porchlight Records)

Jay contributed drums to one track (B1) on the Boundary Problems LP by Time Pieces, a project on Porchlight Records. Allen Karpinski also appears on the same record (guitar, all tracks). This is the only known non-Six Parts Seven session credit in Jay’s discography.

The Hidden Years 2000-2007 — Brian Straw (2025, Wax Mage Records)

Jay appears as a session player on this archival album by Brian Straw, released in 2025. The record also draws on the extended Six Parts Seven family: Allen Karpinski, Tim Gerak, Mike Tolan, Steve Clements, Ben Vaughan, and Erik Koltnow all appear. Straw is credited as writer, engineer, and mixer. The record is on Wax Mage Records.
