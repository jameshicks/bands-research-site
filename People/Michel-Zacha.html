Michel Zacha

French singer, guitarist, and record producer. Discogs artist ID: 403966. Active from at least 1970 through the 2010s. His career spans two distinct phases: a run as a solo recording artist (under his own name and the alias Michel Zacka) on Pathé and Saravah in the early 1970s, followed by a longer career as a working producer/engineer serving the French rock and pop mainstream from 1978 onwards.

Overview

Michel Zacha began recording as a singer-songwriter in 1970 with a single on Saravah, Pierre Barouh’s prestige folk-jazz label, credited as “Michel Zacka.” He then released a trilogy of albums for Pathé under his own name — the Promesses d’Atlantides series (Vol. 1, 1972; Vol. 2 Le Vol d’Icare, 1974; Vol. 3 Inutile, 1977) — which locate him in the progressive/poetic French rock milieu of that decade, using a large ensemble of session players and literary album concepts.

From 1978 he pivoted decisively to production, becoming the house producer for Pathé’s French rock roster. His most sustained client was Starshooter, the Lyon punk-rock group whose run of albums from 1978 to 1980 Zacha produced in full (Starshooter, 1978; Get Baque, 1978; Mode, 1979; Chez Les Autres…, 1980; plus compilations). He also produced the Electric Callas debut 12” EP So Chic (Pathé, 1979), one of the key documents of the Lyon new-wave scene, credited on that sleeve as “Zacha.”

Beyond Pathé, he produced Jean-Louis Murat’s debut EP Murat (Pathé, 1982) and worked across CBS, Virgin, Barclay, and Philips through the 1980s–90s. A notable late-career thread is his longstanding association with humorist/satirist Pierre Desproges — Zacha engineered and/or produced several of Desproges’s live and radio recordings (on Epic and Tôt Ou Tard, 1986–2001). The Georges Moustaki live album La Scène (Epic, 1995) is also credited to Zacha as producer and recording engineer.

He appears in the Discogs database under several name variations: Zacha, Zacharopoulos, Michel Zacka (earliest credit), Michel Zachanasta (12°5, 1980), M. Zacha, Michael Zacha, Michel ‘vieil escroc’ Zacha, and the playful self-designation Michel Zaccharopoulos. The alias “Big Zak” is listed as a name variation in his profile but no releases are indexed under that name as a standalone Discogs artist entry. There is a separate Discogs entry “Michel Zaccharopoulos” (ID: 1240916) — this is a different person (one 2005 world-music credit only).

Career by Era

1970–1977: Solo Artist (Saravah / Pathé)

Zacha’s debut single “Feuille Brûlée / On Peut Rêver” (Saravah, 1970) was produced by Olivier Lorsac and credited to “Michel Zacka.” He then signed to Pathé and released three albums under the Promesses d’Atlantides banner:

  • Vol. 1: La Nuit Des Cigales (Pathé, 1972) — ensemble production with Jean-Pierre Alarcen (electric guitar), Marc Chantereau (percussion), Georges Rabol (piano), Thomas Guthrie (2) (drums), Ric Dodd (flute/sax), Maria Popkiewicz (backing vocals)
  • Vol. 2: Le Vol d’Icare (Pathé Marconi EMI, 1974) — adds Olivier Bloch-Lainé (bass), Georges Rodi (piano/mellotron/synthesizer); Zacha plays guitar, piano, backing vocals
  • Vol. 3: Inutile (Pathé, 1977) — more stripped-back; Zacha on acoustic guitar, keyboards, vocals; Michel Ripoche on electric violin; Claude Wagner engineering; Danièle Chadelaud on vocals; Georges Rodi on electric piano/organ/synthesizer

A separate release Inutilebingalo (Pathé Marconi EMI, 1977) appears to be a companion single/EP from the same sessions.

1978–1985: Pathé House Producer / New-Wave Era

Zacha became the primary in-house producer for Pathé’s French rock acts, most prominently Starshooter (Lyon punk-rock), for whom he produced five releases between 1978 and 1980. He also produced the Electric Callas So Chic EP (1979) alongside Claude Wagner (recording engineer), and produced Jean-Louis Murat’s debut EP Murat (Pathé, 1982) with Georges Rodi on keyboards and Claude Wagner mixing.

During this period he also stepped outside Pathé:

  • 12°5 (Ballon Noir, 1980): producer + synthesizer (credited as “Michel Zachanasta”)
  • Jungle A Ferraille (CBS, 1980): engineer and producer
  • Kent (7): produced Amours Propres (CBS, 1982) and Tiny Tinto (CBS, 1983), playing guitar and backing vocals on both; also produced Passions Privées (Pathé, 1984)
  • Jean-Louis Murat: Murat EP (Pathé, 1982) — Murat’s debut; Zacha produced
  • Elisabeth Wiener: Plume Cassée (Virgin, 1983)
  • Halouf Nar (Mosquito, 1982): producer

1986–2000s: Mainstream Pop / Radio / Live Work

Zacha’s work expanded into mainstream French pop and comedy/spoken-word recordings:

  • Pierre Desproges: Pierre Desproges Se Donne En Spectacle (Speedi, 1986) — engineer/realization; Les Réquisitoires Du Tribunal Des Flagrants Délires volumes (Epic, 1993–1998) — editor/engineer; En Scène Au Théâtre Fontaine 1984 (Tôt Ou Tard, 2001) — engineer
  • Gérard Blanchard: Amour De Voyou (Barclay, 1987 and 1988), Moi, Nous, Vous, Ils (Barclay, 1989), Au Zénith (Barclay, 1990) — producer
  • Etienne Auberger: Amis A Vie (Philips, 1987)
  • Georges Moustaki: La Scène (Epic, 1995) — producer and recording engineer
  • Various: Night To Night (Real World Records/Virgin, 1996) — backing vocals, programming, editing, mastering
  • Bye Bye Lady Dame (Virgin, 1996) — programming, backing vocals

2000s–2010s

Remastering work for Seventeen Records (2006), engineering for Tôt Ou Tard Desproges archival releases, and a recording/mixing credit on Made In China (Thoobett, 2011).

Connections

  • Electric Callas — produced So Chic (Pathé, 1979), the Lyon new-wave EP; one of Zacha’s most cited production credits in post-punk retrospectives
  • Starshooter — primary production client 1978–1980; Zacha produced the band’s full Pathé run, including the landmark self-titled debut (1978) with vocalist Kent (7)
  • Pathé — effectively Zacha’s home base as both recording artist (1972–1977) and producer (1978–1984)
  • Jean-Louis Murat — Zacha produced Murat’s debut EP in 1982, early in what became one of the most significant careers in 1980s–2000s French rock
  • Pierre Desproges — sustained six-release association with France’s leading satirist; Zacha’s engineering and editorial work preserved Desproges’s live and radio performances
  • Saravah — Pierre Barouh’s label; Zacha’s 1970 debut places him in the orbit of the French singer-songwriter/jazz scene before his Pathé move
  • Claude Wagner — recurring engineer throughout Zacha’s production work; Wagner appears on Zacha’s own Atlantides albums, Starshooter, Electric Callas, and Jean-Louis Murat
