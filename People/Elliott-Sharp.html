Elliott Sharp

Composer, guitarist, multi-instrumentalist. NYC experimental/avant-garde. Born March 1, 1951, Cleveland, Ohio. Discogs ID: 63308.

Overview

Elliott Sharp is one of the central figures of the downtown New York experimental music scene from the late 1970s onward. He plays guitar (including custom doubleneck guitarbass and fretless guitar), bass clarinet, soprano and tenor saxophone, lap steel, and a range of invented instruments he calls “slabs,” “pantars,” “tubinets,” and “violinoids.” He leads Carbon (23) — his primary large-format ensemble — as well as Orchestra Carbon, the expanded orchestral version of the same project. He is also a member of Mofungo and has produced extensively for The Scene Is Now, Mofungo, and other downtown bands.

Sharp studied anthropology at Cornell (where he took an electronics class with synthesizer inventor Robert Moog), then worked with free jazz pioneer Roswell Rudd at Bard College (classmates included the future Lounge Lizards, John and Evan Lurie). He completed graduate study in Buffalo under Morton Feldman before moving permanently to New York in 1979. By the early 1980s he was performing at the Mudd Club and other underground venues.

His output spans avant-garde jazz, noise, heavy-guitar rock, contemporary classical composition, electronics, and cross-genre experimentation. He has worked throughout his career with Martin Bisi (his primary engineer at BC Studio, Brooklyn), Zeena Parkins (harp, 26 shared releases), Bobby Previte (drums), Bill Laswell (bass, early 1980s), Wayne Horvitz, and John Zorn.

Name variations used on releases: E#, E. Sharp, E. Sharp, Elliot Sharp, Elliott Sharp Ensemble, Elliott Sharp’s Orchestra Carbon.

Early Work and Zoar Records (1978–1982)

Sharp’s first recorded appearance is Hara (1978, Zoar Records, ZOAR 01), a co-primary LP with guitarist David Fulton. Sharp plays 6-string and 12-string guitar and soprano saxophone; the tracklist pays tribute to political prisoners (Gary Tyler, Steve Biko) and indigenous land rights. Engineer: Steve Rosenthal.

A second solo LP, Resonance (1979, Zoar), documents extended acoustic guitar techniques. Sharp also produced and designed the Zoar compilation State Of The Union (1982), which documents the wider downtown improv community.

Nots (1982, Glass Records (2), Glasslp 020) is the first major LP to show his range. Multiple drummers (Phillip Wilson, Mark E. Miller, Charles K. Noyes, Diana Meckley), Bill Laswell on bass (several tracks), and Olu Dara on cornet. Different tracks used different engineers: Alan Fierstein, Rod Hui, Doug Epstein, and Martin Bisi (who handles the B-side recording and mixing — his earliest confirmed credit on a Sharp release). Mark Kamins and Sharp split production duties.

Moving Info (Zoar Records, ZCS 3, 1981; cassette EP) was recorded live at the Mudd Club and Hurrah in 1981. Band: Bill Laswell and Percy Jones (bass), Phillip Wilson (drums), Mark E. Miller (percussion), Art Baron (trombone), Olu Dara (trumpet). Documents the Mudd Club’s overlap with downtown jazz-improv.

Carbon and the Dossier / SST Era (1984–1990)

Carbon (1984, Atonal Records, ST 3003)

Sharp’s first primary solo LP under the Carbon name. Engineer: Martin Bisi. Personnel: David Linton (electronic drums), Charles K. Noyes (bass drum, cymbal), Mark E. Miller (snare, congas). Lesli Dalaba (trumpet) appears on two tracks; Sharp plays guitar, bass, saxophone, clarinet, trombone, and custom instruments. Self-produced.

Dossier Records block (1985–1988)

Sharp released five primary LPs on Dossier Records (Berlin), making him the label’s most prominent NYC artist alongside Wayne Horvitz and Rhys Chatham. All recorded with Martin Bisi at BC Studio, Brooklyn.

Virtual Stance (1986) is largely a solo record — Sharp plays doubleneck guitarbass, voice, drum programming, Mirage keyboard, and drum machine. Bobby Previte plays live drums; Wayne Horvitz co-engineers. Side A is a single 21-minute piece.

In The Land Of The Yahoos (1987, Dossier ST 7536) is a song-format LP featuring a rotating cast of vocalists and collaborators per track: Paul Garrin, Christian Marclay (turntables), Sussan Deyhim, Elizabeth Fischer, Shelley Hirsch, Christoph Anders. Multiple studios: BC Studio (Bisi), 6/8 Studio (JZ), Demolition Sound (Michael Beinhorn), and David Fulton’s Hothouse studio.

Sharp also appears as guitarist/bassist on Wayne Horvitz’s Dinner At Eight (Dossier ST 7514, 1986) — guitar and bass on tracks A1–A3, B3.

SST Records block (1987–1988)

Sharp signed to SST Records in Lawndale, CA — the label of Black Flag and the Minutemen — for three primary releases and a compilation:

Tessalation Row (SST 129, 1987) is a co-primary release with the Soldier String Quartet — a chamber music crossover, with Sharp composing and producing. Engineer: Martin Bisi. The quartet: David Soldier, Laura Seaton (violin), Ron Lawrence (viola), Mary Wooten (cello). Ratzo B. Harris (double bass) appears on one track.

Larynx (SST 194, 1988) co-credited to Carbon (23) — a large-ensemble LP with the full Carbon roster: Previte, Noyes, Linton, Samm Bennett (four drummers), Lesli Dalaba (trumpet), David Fulton and Ken Heer (trombone), the Soldier String Quartet (strings). Engineer: Martin Bisi. Sharp plays doubleneck guitarbass, sampler, pantar, soprano and tenor saxophone, bass clarinet.

Enemy Records (1990–1991)

Two landmark LPs on Enemy Records (distributed in Germany through EFA):

In New York (1990, Enemy Records, EMY 114) — co-primary with Gnawa musician Bachir Attar. Sharp plays guitar, bass, drum programming; produces and designs. Jane Tomkiewicz plays bendir. An unusual blend of Moroccan traditional music and Sharp’s layered guitar/bass work.

Datacide (1990, Enemy Records, EMY 116) — co-credited to Carbon (23). Large ensemble: David Linton (drums), Zeena Parkins (electric harp, keyboards), Samm Bennett (percussion, drums), with engineers Bryce Goggin, Gil Abarbanel, Steve McAllister (Baby Monster studio), and Steve Rosenthal (Magic Shop). Michael Knuth (executive producer). Styles listed as Avantgarde and Heavy Metal.

Tocsin (1991, Enemy Records, EMY 134-2, CD) — co-credited to Carbon (23). Ensemble: Marc Sloan (bass), Joe Trump (drums, percussion), Zeena Parkins (electric harp), David Weinstein (sampler). Engineer: Bryce Goggin. Executive producer: Michael Knuth.

Semantics (1986, Review Records)

Sharp appears on the self-titled LP by the group Semantics, credited for double-neck guitarbass, vocals, violinoid; also has writing credits. Discogs ID for the release: 1266611.

John Zorn — Cobra (1987, hat ART)

Sharp plays doubleneck guitar/bass and soprano guitar in John Zorn’s game-piece sessions. One of the most significant documentation records of the downtown scene.

Lounge Lizards / Wayne Horvitz / Bobby Previte

Sharp’s name appears repeatedly in the credits of peers from the downtown axis. His co-writing and producing relationship with Mofungo and The Scene Is Now runs through the mid-1980s (see below).

Later Work: Intakt Records and Recent Releases (2013–2022)

Sharp has released a series of records on Intakt Records (Zurich), typically produced by label head Patrik Landolt:

TitleYearCat#Co-primaries
Crossing The Waters2013Intakt CD 215Melvin Gibbs (bass), Lucas Niggli (drums)
Err Guitar2017Intakt CD 281Mary Halvorson, Marc Ribot
Transmigration At The Solar Max2018Intakt CD 311Carbon (23), Zeena Parkins, Bobby Previte
Kumuska2019Intakt CD 328Saadet Türköz (voice)

Err Guitar (2017) gathers three guitarists — Sharp, Mary Halvorson, and Marc Ribot — for 12 pieces with overlapping track assignments. Sharp mixes and masters; Patrik Landolt produces.

Transmigration At The Solar Max (2018) is the fullest recent statement of the Carbon (23) formation, with Zeena Parkins on electric harp and Bobby Previte on drums. Sharp plays 8-string guitarbass, soprano saxophone, electronics, and samples; he also composes, produces, records, remixes, and masters.

Kumuska (2019, Intakt CD 328) is a duo with Kazakh-Swiss vocalist Saadet Türköz. Sharp plays analog synthesizer, bass clarinet, and glissentar; he mixes and masters.

Sharp has also released on his own Zoar Records (including a 1997 reissue Rheo~Umbra and the SyndaKit LP, 1999), Infrequent Seams (multiple releases from 2014 onward), and Karlrecords (Oneirika, 2017).
