Isaac Brock

Vocalist, guitarist, songwriter, producer. Real name: Isaac Kristofer Brock. Born July 9, 1975, Issaquah, Washington. Discogs ID: 260715. Alias: Ugly Casanova (2).

Frontman, primary songwriter, and driving creative force of Modest Mouse from 1992 to present. Also operates as a producer and label head (Glacial Pace Recordings). His work outside Modest Mouse spans two distinct threads: the Ugly Casanova alias (home recordings, folk-gothic character) and a production/mentorship role channeled through Glacial Pace acts, most consistently Mimicking Birds.

Modest Mouse

Founded Modest Mouse in Issaquah as a teenager with Eric Judy (bass) and Jeremiah Green (drums). Remained the constant across all lineup changes — the only original member present on every release. Contributes guitar, vocals, occasional bass, occasional banjo, production co-credits, photography, and sole authorship of all lyrics. Co-produced The Lonesome Crowded West (1997, Up Records) with Calvin Johnson. Co-produced Good News For People Who Love Bad News (2004, Epic) and We Were Dead Before The Ship Even Sank (2007, Epic) alongside Dennis Herring. Creative director for The Golden Casket (2021, Epic).

Also functions as a visual artist — photography credits on The Lonesome Crowded West and The Moon & Antarctica, and art direction on The Golden Casket.

See Modest Mouse for full discography and personnel.

Ugly Casanova

Recording alias predating the 2002 Sub Pop release. “Ugly Casanova” appears as early as the 1997 Modest Mouse EP The Fruit That Ate Itself (K Records) — the band is there credited to a production entity of that name. Brock has said he wrote a song called “Ugly Casanova” around 1993. The name was applied to home-recorded material made under the fictional persona of Edgar E. Graham, a folk savant supposedly discovered at a Denver show in 1998 and whose “tapes” were released as Sharpen Your Teeth — a fiction Brock later admitted was invented to avoid press attention.

1997 Early Appearance

On the “White” compilation 7” (Magic Eye Singles, 1997), Brock appears on track “To Roads To Go To Roads To Go” — credited under the Ugly Casanova name — with Steve Dukage (slide steel, percussion) and John Atkins (vocals, percussion). Same Steve Dukage and John Atkins who appear on Sharpen Your Teeth (2002).

Sharpen Your Teeth (Sub Pop, 2002)

Full album assembled from Brock’s home recordings at Glacial Pace Studios, Oregon, with Brian Deck and Tim Rutili as core studio collaborators. Pall Jenkins (The Black Heart Procession) co-wrote and sang on two tracks; John Orth (Holopaw) on four tracks. Released May 21, 2002.

See Ugly Casanova for full personnel table and track-level breakdown.

180° South Soundtrack (Brushfire Records, 2010)

Eight new Ugly Casanova songs appeared on the soundtrack to the documentary 180° South: Conquerors of the Useless. Brock credited with guitar, bass, banjo, and vocals across multiple tracks. First new Ugly Casanova material since 2002.

2026

Ugly Casanova announced for Modest Mouse’s 2026 fan cruise — first public announcement of UC activity in roughly 15 years.

Glacial Pace Recordings

Brock’s own label, operating out of Portland. Home to:

  • Mimicking Birds — project of Nate Lacy (songwriter); Brock co-produced the debut (2010) with Clay Jones and contributed instruments and backing vocals; returned for Eons EP (2014) and Layers Of Us (2018).
  • Modest Mouse reissues — Building Nothing Out Of Something reissued on Glacial Pace in 2015–2016.

Production Work

Wolf Parade — Apologies To The Queen Mary (2005, Sub Pop)

Brock produced tracks 1–3, 6, 8–12 of Wolf Parade’s debut LP. The album was mixed by Jace Lasek and Wolf Parade self-produced tracks 4, 5, and 7. This was a significant production credit — it helped launch Wolf Parade (Dan Boeckner, Spencer Krug, Arlen Thompson, Hadji Bakara, Timothy Kingsbury) into wide visibility on Sub Pop.

Mimicking Birds — Self-Titled (2010, Glacial Pace)

Co-produced with Clay Jones. Contributed electric guitar, kalimba, and backing vocals across several tracks.

Mimicking Birds — Eons EP (2014, Glacial Pace)

Contributed electric guitar.

Mark Kozelek / Sun Kil Moon Overlap

Brock’s connection to Mark Kozelek (Sun Kil Moon) appears on two records:

  • Presents Lee Hazlewood’s Trouble Is A Lonesome Town (2013, SideOneDummy): Brock sings on Kozelek’s cover of a Lee Hazlewood track
  • Jesu / Sun Kil Moon (2016): guest vocals on Kozelek’s collaboration album with Justin Broadrick (Jesu)

Kozelek also covered Modest Mouse material — the connection runs in both directions. Note that Jimmy LaValle (The Album Leaf) collaborated with Kozelek on Perils From The Sea (2013, Caldo Verde). Both Brock and LaValle orbit Kozelek’s world in the same period, independently.

Gainesville / Florida

After finishing The Moon & Antarctica in Chicago (summer–autumn 1999 at Brian Deck’s Clava Studios), Brock moved to Gainesville, FL with his girlfriend, who was from there. He has said: “my girlfriend was from Gainesville and I was like maybe I should take her to a place where she knows people.” He stayed roughly one to two years, living in relative obscurity — departure from Seattle was also partly driven by a sexual assault allegation that had circulated there (later withdrawn).

This Gainesville period is almost certainly when he encountered John Orth. Orth — born Jacksonville, FL; FSU fine arts grad — was embedded in the Gainesville scene by this time. When Brock began assembling the Ugly Casanova project in 2001 at his home in Cottage Grove, Oregon, he invited Orth out. Orth co-wrote four tracks on Sharpen Your Teeth (Sub Pop, 2002).

Sub Pop’s own description of the Holopaw signing is direct: Holopaw “came to our attention by way of Isaac Brock (he and Holopaw’s John Orth co-wrote some of our favorite songs on the Ugly Casanova record).” Brian Deck, who produced both Sharpen Your Teeth and the Holopaw debut, is the professional bridge — but the relationship appears to predate Deck’s involvement; it’s Brock who brought Orth in, not the other way around.

The song “Florida” (tr.4, We Were Dead Before The Ship Even Sank, 2007) draws on the Gainesville period: Brock confirmed in a 2021 interview that the stay “feeds into that song.” The song’s themes — failed escape, deflated expectations, Florida as metaphor — are layered rather than purely autobiographical. Not the Andrew Cunanan track — that’s “Pistol (A. Cunanan, Miami, FL. 1996)” from Strangers To Ourselves (2015), a separate piece based on crime mythology and Miami Vice aesthetics, not personal experience.

A Miami visit around 2002 to see Sam Beam (Iron & Wine) performing at a gallery is documented; no musical significance.

Key source: Tape Op issue 36 (Brian Deck interview) — confirms Deck produced both Ugly Casanova and Holopaw debut, refers to Orth as “the other singer in Ugly Casanova.”
