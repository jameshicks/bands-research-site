Biography

Fred Peterkin is a New York City-born deep house producer and DJ who records primarily as Fred P. and under the alias Black Jazz Consortium. He has been active in music since at least the early 1990s — the Discogs credit trail shows drum programming work on jazz-funk violinist Noel Pointer’s posthumous 1993 LP Never Lose Your Heart (Shanachie/Cachet Records), which indicates professional studio work well before his house output emerged. An early saxophone credit appears on a 1999 release (Goin’ To A Party With The Confidentials, SJS Music), suggesting broad musicianship rather than a single-instrument background.

His house career began in earnest around 2006–2007. Under the Black Jazz Consortium alias he debuted on Italian label Gotta Keep Faith Records in 2006 (Looking Forward E.P.), then moved to his own imprint Soul People Music, where the bulk of his production through the 2010s would appear. The Fred P. alias launched at Soul People Music in 2008 with 4th Dimension E.P. and No Looking Back EP.

Peterkin’s core aesthetic is stripped, contemplative deep house — long-form tracks with subtle percussion, jazz-chord harmonics, and a meditative pace that draws comparisons to the Chicago-influenced end of the New York underground. His self-run label Soul People Music (with sublabels Arthouse, Boards, and Energy Of Sound) has been the main vehicle for both the Fred P. and Black Jazz Consortium output.

Beyond self-releases, he has appeared on Underground Quality (the Jus-Ed label, a 2008 split with DJ Jus-Ed titled Real House Music…), Ibadan (Energy Soul, 2016), Finale Sessions (Finale Revisited Vol 1, 2012; Two Worlds collaboration with Terrence Dixon, 2014), and UK labels including Modern Dialect, Shall Not Fade, and Aus Music.

His widest-reaching collaboration is the track “Building Bridges” co-written and co-produced with Move D (David Moufang), which appeared as track D1 on Move D’s 2019 album Building Bridges (Aus Music, cat. AUSLP010). The album is credited solely to Move D as primary artist; Peterkin is listed as Written-By and Producer on that one track.

From 2018 onward he has collaborated regularly with SMBD (Simbad / P.L. Stanislas-Renouf), releasing the Mantras For The Travelling Souls Vol. 1 LP on Modern Dialect (UK, 2018) as Fred P & SMBD.

His Mule Musiq appearances (2015–2017, as Fred Peterkin, credited producer on entries 5, 6, and 7 in an artist series) suggest a significant Japanese underground house following.

Scene Context

Fred P. sits at the centre of a New York underground deep house network that emerged in the mid-2000s and is loosely defined by long tracks, minimal swing, and a conscious anti-commercial positioning. His closest scene peers — Jus-Ed (Underground Quality), DJ Qu (Quintessentials), and Terrence Dixon (whose work straddles NYC house and Detroit techno) — all appear as direct collaborators. The Underground Quality connection is significant: Jus-Ed’s label was a key organising structure for this NYC underground, and Peterkin’s appearance on it in 2008 signals an early scene alignment.

His Soul People Music imprint, alongside its sublabels (Arthouse, Boards, Energy Of Sound), functioned as a self-sufficient infrastructure for a decade, releasing work by Peterkin and his associates without requiring major-label or distributor involvement.

The European dimension of his output — Ibadan (Germany), Syncrophone (France), Private Society (Germany), Mule Musiq (Japan-facing UK) — reflects the deep house underground’s geography: small quantities of vinyl pressed for a collector and DJ market centred in Germany, Italy, and Japan as much as New York.

The Mantras For The Travelling Souls collaboration with SMBD (Simbad), a Martinique-born, UK-based producer whose aliases include Loose Ensemble and Mowgly, extends his orbit into the UK-European deep house/broken-beat crossover world.
