Michele Rosewoman

Born: 19 March 1953, Oakland, California, USA. Discogs artist ID: 1676651. Real name: Donna Michele Rosewoman.

American jazz pianist and composer. A foundational figure in the New York avant-garde since the early 1980s, equally conversant in post-bop, free improvisation, and Afro-Cuban jazz. Known above all for New Yor-Uba, a large ensemble she formed in 1983 that synthesized New York jazz with Yoruba-derived Cuban music.

Biography

Oakland Origins

Rosewoman grew up in Oakland, California. She relocated to New York City in the late 1970s, entering the downtown avant-garde scene that centered on lofts, the Village Vanguard, and the Soul Note / Black Saint recording network out of Milan.

1980s: New York Avant-Garde and First Records

Her first documented recording appearance on Discogs is as pianist on the Billy Bang Quintet’s Rainbow Gladiator (Soul Note, 1981) — alongside Charles Tyler (alto/baritone sax), Wilber Morris (bass), Denis Charles (drums), and Bang on violin. The session was produced by Giovanni Bonandrini and recorded at Pick Up Studios, Italy.

In 1984 she led her first date under her own name: The Source, billed as the Michele Rosewoman Quartet (Soul Note). The band featured Baikida Carroll on trumpet/flugelhorn and Pheeroan Aklaff on drums, with Roberto Miranda on bass. All compositions were credited to Rosewoman.

She also founded New Yor-Uba around 1983 — a large ensemble that incorporated Yoruba-based Afro-Cuban traditions (bata drums, conga, vocal chanting) into a jazz context. The ensemble documented its 30th anniversary in 2013 on Advance Dance Disques with Pedrito Martinez (vocals, bata, congas) and production by Liberty Ellman.

The mid-to-late 1980s saw Rosewoman active as a sideman and in her own ensembles simultaneously. She played piano and synthesizer on Greg Osby’s debut Greg Osby And Sound Theatre (JMT, 1987), alongside Lonnie Plaxico (bass), Terri Lyne Carrington (drums, guest), and Kevin McNeal (guitar). The same year she released Quintessence (Enja, 1987) as a leader — her strongest early statement — with Steve Coleman (alto sax), Greg Osby (soprano sax), Anthony Cox (bass), and Terri Lyne Carrington (drums). The album was produced by Matthias Winckelmann and engineered by Jim Anderson.

In 1988 she appeared on Oliver Lake’s Otherside (Gramavision), sharing piano duties with Geri Allen across different tracks.

1989–1993: Quintessence Era

Contrast High (Enja, 1989), billed as Michele Rosewoman And Quintessence, is a key document: Rosewoman produced it herself, played piano, synthesizer, and sang, with Greg Osby (alto/soprano sax), Gary Thomas (tenor sax, flute), Lonnie Plaxico (bass), Cecil Brooks III (drums), and Eddie Bobé (bongos, guiro on one track).

Also in 1989 she appeared as pianist on Dick Griffin / Jean-Paul Bourelly / Pheeroan Aklaff’s Hippo Stomp (Sound Aspects Records).

Occasion to Rise (Somethin’ Else, 1991) appeared under the Michele Rosewoman Trio with Ralph Peterson on drums and Rufus Reid on bass.

Harvest (Enja, 1993) is her second produced leader date for Enja — a quintet session with Steve Wilson (alto/soprano sax), Gary Thomas (tenor sax, flute), Kenny Davis (bass), Gene Jackson (drums), and Eddie Bobé (congas, bajo quinto on select tracks). Rosewoman again produced and contributed vocals on one track.

She also appears as pianist on the Ralph Peterson Quintet’s Art (Somethin’ Else, 1993), alongside Steve Wilson, Graham Haynes (cornet), Phil Bowler (bass), Craig Handy (tenor), and Frank Lacy (trombone).

1996–2000: Spirit and Guardians

Spirit (Somethin’ Else, 1996) is a trio session under her own name, again with Kenny Davis (bass) and Gene Jackson (drums) — a notably stable rhythm section across her Enja/Somethin’ Else period.

Guardians Of The Light (Enja, 2000), again as Michele Rosewoman And Quintessence, brought in Steve Wilson (alto/soprano), Craig Handy (tenor), Kenny Davis (bass), and Gene Jackson (drums) — Rosewoman produced and sang.

2013: New Yor-Uba Thirtieth Anniversary

Michele Rosewoman’s New Yor-Uba 30 Years (A Musical Celebration Of Cuba In America) (Advance Dance Disques, 2013) documents the ensemble’s three-decade run, featuring Pedrito Martinez and production by Liberty Ellman.
