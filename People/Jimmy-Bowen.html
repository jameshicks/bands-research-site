Jimmy Bowen

Real name: James Albert Bowen Born: November 30, 1937, Santa Rita, New Mexico, USA Discogs ID: 276426 Alias: J.B. Pickers (Discogs ID: 282001) Married: Keely Smith (1965–1969)

American singer, songwriter, bassist, and record producer. Started as a rockabilly performer in the late 1950s, then pivoted to production at Reprise Records in 1963, becoming one of the most commercially successful producers of the 1960s pop era (Dean Martin, Frank Sinatra) before transitioning to Nashville country in the 1970s–80s where he produced and ran major labels, working with George Strait, Reba McEntire, Hank Williams Jr., and others. Founded Amos Records (1968) and MCA Nashville (1988).

Early Career: Rockabilly Singer (1956–1962)

Bowen launched as a performer on Triple-D Records in 1956, recording “Party Doll / I’m Stickin’ With You.” He was a member of The Rhythm Orchids alongside Buddy Knox, and the two shared songwriting credits on several early Roulette releases. “I’m Stickin’ With You” (1957, Roulette) was his only notable chart success as a performer. He also wrote “Swingin’ Daddy” (1958) and accumulated early writing credits on Roulette and Capehart labels through 1961.

His recording alias J.B. Pickers (Discogs ID: 282001) surfaced during this period; its sole primary-artist release in Discogs is “Super Soul Theme / Nobody Knows” (1971, London Records), suggesting it was used selectively.

Reprise Records and the Rat Pack Era (1963–1969)

In 1963 Bowen was hired by Frank Sinatra as a producer at Reprise Records. This was the most concentrated and commercially successful phase of his production career. His core clients were Dean Martin, Frank Sinatra, Sammy Davis Jr., and associated Reprise artists.

Amos Records (1968–1971)

Bowen founded Amos Records in 1968 (Discogs label ID: 83495). Artists on the label included Johnny Tillotson (Tears On My Pillow, 1969), Kim Carnes (Rest On Me, 1971), Frankie Laine (Frankie Laine’s Greatest Hits, 1970), and a soundtrack release for Beneath The Planet Of The Apes (Leonard Rosenman, 1970). Amos was a West Coast pop/MOR label; it closed around 1971.

Nashville Transition (1971–1988)

In the early 1970s Bowen moved to Nashville and shifted entirely into country music. He served as president of multiple labels in succession — MCA Records Nashville operations, Elektra/Asylum Nashville, Warner Bros. Nashville, and eventually founded MCA Nashville (1988). His Nashville production output was prolific.
