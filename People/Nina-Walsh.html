Nina Walsh

Also known as: Obscura, c-pij 01 Discogs ID: 45807 Role: Musician, Songwriter, Producer, Engineer, Composer, Performer Based: UK Active: 1994–present (credited from 1994; active as of 2025) Associated Acts: Slab, The Woodleigh Research Facility, C-PIJ, Rootmasters, The Rabettes, W.O.T-, Fireflies (5) Key Labels: Sabrettes, Hydrogen Dukebox, Malicious Damage, Rotters Golf Club, Byrd Out, Facility 4

Biography

Nina Walsh is a UK composer, performer, engineer, and producer. Her brother is Craig Walsh.

Early career and Sabres of Paradise connection (1990s)

Walsh’s first commercially recorded vocal performance was on Primal Scream’s track “Original Sin,” remixed by DJ and producer Andrew Weatherall. From this early encounter with Weatherall’s orbit, she became connected to the infrastructure around The Sabres Of Paradise — the group Weatherall ran with Jagz Kooner and Gary Burns. Walsh and Weatherall co-founded two electronic dance labels: Sabrettes (Discogs ID: 664, a sublabel under Sabres Of Paradise Ltd.) and a second imprint also in that infrastructure.

Her earliest production credits appear on releases for Slab — a group she was a member of alongside Lol Hammond. She co-wrote and produced Slab’s Atomsmasher (1995, Hydrogen Dukebox) and Rampant Prankster (1995, Hydrogen Dukebox), and appeared as a performer on their Freeky Speed (1996, Hydrogen Dukebox). Further credits during this period: writing and production on Red 4 (Skin) (1996, Sabrettes) and Sonic Grudge (1996, Sabrettes), plus Lidpopper (1995, Hydrogen Dukebox).

She also worked under the group name C-PIJ (Discogs ID: 5056) — a project in which she was the only credited member — releasing Once / Mongolian Remodelling (1999, C-Pij label) and Seven (2000, C-Pij). Her Discogs aliases “c-pij 01” and “Obscura” relate to this and adjacent work.

The Orb and Rootmasters (late 1990s–2000s)

Walsh met Alex Paterson of The Orb in the early 1990s. Together they co-wrote “Ghost Dancing” and “Plum Island,” both of which appear on The Orb’s album Cydonia (2000, MCA Records) — she is credited as Walsh on tracks 3 and 9. She and Paterson also record together under the name Rootmasters (Discogs ID: 926524); in that capacity she appeared on Push Once (2007, Malicious Damage) and on Orbsessions Volume One (2005, Malicious Damage) as a performer.

She is also a member of The Rabettes (Discogs ID: 23768, with Kris Needs) and W.O.T- (Discogs ID: 144460, with Anita Hurst), and later Fireflies (5) (Discogs ID: 4695416).

Solo album: Bright Lights and Filthy Nights (2009)

Walsh released her solo debut album Bright Lights And Filthy Nights (2009, Malicious Damage) under her own name. She self-produced the bulk of the album (tracks 1–7, 9–12), with production contributions from Charlie May (Charles May, track 9), Erick Legrand (tracks 3, 11), and Matt Rowlands (Mat Rowlands, track 8). She wrote the majority of the material, sharing writing credits with Charlie May on track 9 and with Max Garrett on track 8. Prior to the LP, she had released the EP Memo’s (2005, C-Pij label) as a primary artist.

She also co-wrote “Occasionally In A Way” (2005) for Bio Muse, alongside Mauro Ruvolo and Stefano Curti.

Engineering work (2010–2014)

Beyond songwriting and performance, Walsh worked as a recording engineer on a number of high-profile releases:

  • Warpaint’s The Fool (2010, Rough Trade) — engineer on tracks 3 and 7 (Weatherall mixed those same tracks)
  • Fuckbuttons’ Slow Focus (2013, ATP Recordings) — initial tracks recorded by Walsh
  • Fuckbuttons’ Brainfreeze (2013, ATP Recordings) — initial tracks recorded by Walsh
  • Pete Molinari’s Theosophy (2014, Cherry Red) — engineer
  • Additional mixing and engineering on Qualia (2017, Höga Nord Rekords) by Andrew Weatherall

She also co-engineered and co-programmed the Guilty Of Love (Andrew Weatherall Remixes) (2015, Unloved Records).

Weatherall final-period collaboration (2015–2020)

Walsh was Weatherall’s primary creative partner in his final period of work. The Discogs DB documents at least 10 co-writing credits with him across multiple releases spanning 2015–2020:

  • The Phoenix Suburb (And Other Stories) (2015, Rotters Golf Club, RGCLP021) — co-written with Weatherall (and Martin Glover on track D8); released under The Woodleigh Research Facility
  • Convenanza (2016, Rotters Golf Club, RGCCD022P) — co-written with Weatherall; his credited solo LP
  • Consolamentum (2016, Rotters Golf Club, RGCCD023) — co-written with Weatherall; his other 2016 LP
  • Kiyadub EP (2017, Byrd Out) — co-written with Weatherall
  • Blue Bullet EP (2018, Byrd Out) — co-written with Weatherall
  • 127 To Facility 4 (2018, Rotters Golf Club) — co-written with Weatherall; released as The Woodleigh Research Facility
  • Unknown Plunderer / End Times Sound (2020, Byrd Out) — co-written with Weatherall (Andy Bell of Ride plays guitar)
  • Pamela #1 (2020, Pamela Records (2)) — co-written and co-produced with Weatherall; his final release before his death in February 2020

She also contributed “Loops” to A Very British Coup (2019, Cadiz Music), a collaboration between Weatherall and Youth featuring Jah Wobble, Keith Levene, Mark Stewart, and Richard Dudanski.

She provided backing vocals on both Two Lone Swordsmen albums Wrong Meeting (2007, Rotters Golf Club) and Wrong Meeting II (2007, Rotters Golf Club), appearing on the Japanese compilation Wrong Meetings (Beat Records, 2007).

The Woodleigh Research Facility (2015–present)

The Woodleigh Research Facility (Discogs ID: 4689420; also abbreviated W.R.F.) is a duo of Walsh and Weatherall. The project began appearing on Discogs from 2015 and released extensively on Rotters Golf Club and Byrd Out. After Weatherall’s death in February 2020, Walsh appears to have continued the project: Phonox Nights (2023, Facility 4) is credited as WRF and carries Walsh and Weatherall’s writing credits. The Vous Du EP (2024, Byrd Out) is also credited to The Woodleigh Research Facility. The project’s current operational status is not fully resolved from Discogs data.

WRF releases include:

  • Gardens Dub (2015, Moine Dubh) — download
  • The Phoenix Suburb (And Other Stories) (2015, Rotters Golf Club)
  • 127 To Facility 4 (2018, Rotters Golf Club)
  • Facility 4 series — at least 9 volumes (2020, Rotters Golf Club) — download releases; large body of material released in 2020
  • Phonox Nights (2023, Facility 4)
  • Vous Du EP (2024, Byrd Out)

Post-2020 solo and other work

Walsh released Sail We Must (2025, Facility5) as a primary solo artist. She contributed backing vocals to Youth’s Spinning Wheel (2021, Cadiz Music) and co-wrote and produced Phonox Nights (2023) as WRF. She contributed “Loops And Ideas” to the 2025 remaster of Celtic Vedic (LSD - Liquid Sound Design).
