George Harrison

Born 25 February 1943, Liverpool, England; died 29 November 2001, Los Angeles, California. Lead guitarist of The Beatles; solo artist from 1968 onward. Co-founder of Dark Horse Records (1974). Co-founder of Traveling Wilburys (1988). Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame posthumously as a solo performer in 2004 (previously inducted as a Beatle in 1988). Discogs artist ID: 243955.

Known aliases on record: L’Angelo Misterioso, Hari Georgeson, George O’Hara, Nelson Wilbury, Spike Wilbury, P. Roducer, Son Of Harry, Buzz Trilby.

Early Life and The Beatles (1958–1970)

Harrison joined John Lennon and Paul McCartney in the skiffle group The Quarrymen in 1958, later evolving through Johnny and the Moondogs and the Beat Brothers into The Beatles. As the band’s lead guitarist throughout their recording career (1962–1970), he contributed increasingly as a songwriter — his compositions include “Something,” “Here Comes the Sun,” “While My Guitar Gently Weeps,” and “Within You Without You.” By the mid-1960s he had become a devoted student of Indian music and culture, studying sitar with Ravi Shankar; this shaped his compositional voice and introduced the Beatles to Indian sounds on Revolver and Sgt. Pepper’s.

Solo Career: Apple Records Era (1968–1975)

Harrison was the first Beatle to release a solo album: Wonderwall Music (Apple Records, 1968), a film soundtrack incorporating Indian instrumentation. Electronic Sound (1969, Apple/Zapple) followed as an experimental synthesizer record.

The triple-LP All Things Must Pass (Apple, 1970) was his commercial and critical breakthrough — co-produced with Phil Spector, it featured an extraordinary cast of session players (see credits below) and yielded the #1 single “My Sweet Lord.” It established the template for his solo work: large ensemble recordings, spiritual themes, and a rolling cast of top-tier Los Angeles and New York session musicians.

The Concert for Bangladesh (Madison Square Garden, 1 August 1971) was a landmark charity event organized by Harrison at the request of his friend Ravi Shankar. The live album (The Concert For Bangladesh, Apple, 1971/1993) co-produced with Phil Spector, featured Bob Dylan, Eric Clapton, Ringo Starr, Billy Preston, Badfinger, and a horn section including Jim Horn. It was one of the earliest large-scale rock charity concerts.

Living in the Material World (Apple, 1973) continued the spiritual direction, self-produced with a core band including Jim Keltner (drums), Klaus Voormann (bass), Gary Wright and Nicky Hopkins (keyboards), John Barham (strings), and Jim Horn (flute, saxophone). The Indian classical dimension was supplied by Zakir Hussain (tabla).

Dark Horse (Apple, 1974) was recorded partly during and partly after a grueling 45-city North American tour with a large touring band (including Jim Horn). The album is often cited as rushed; Harrison was suffering from laryngitis throughout the tour period.

Extra Texture (Read All About It) (Apple, 1975) was the last Apple album, self-produced, with engineer Phil McDonald handling the basic tracks. The band personnel was leaner; notable for Harrison’s introspective writing in the post-Beatle legal period.

Dark Horse Records Era (1976–1989)

Harrison founded Dark Horse Records in 1974, initially distributed through A&M Records and later through Warner Bros. Records. The label released his own records alongside other artists including Ravi Shankar.

Thirty Three & 1/3 (Dark Horse/Warner Bros., 1976): Self-produced with assistance from Tom Scott (saxophone, flute, lyricon; also co-producer). Rhythm section: Willie Weeks (bass), Alvin Taylor (drums), Richard Tee (piano, organ), David Foster (Fender Rhodes). Engineer: Hank Cicalo.

George Harrison (Dark Horse, 1979): Continued the mid-tempo California rock direction.

Somewhere in England (Dark Horse, 1981): Co-produced with Ray Cooper. Personnel included Jim Keltner and Ringo Starr (drums), Tom Scott (horns, lyricon), Al Kooper and Gary Brooker (keyboards), Alla Rakha (tabla). Contained “All Those Years Ago,” a tribute to John Lennon (released as a single, co-produced with Ray Cooper; featured Ringo Starr and Paul McCartney).

Gone Troppo (Dark Horse, 1982): Co-produced with Phil McDonald and Ray Cooper. Harrison largely withdrew from public life after this release, not touring or releasing new material until 1987.

Cloud Nine (Dark Horse, 1987): Comeback album, co-produced with Jeff Lynne. Personnel: Jim Keltner and Ringo Starr (drums), Ray Cooper (drums, percussion), Eric Clapton (guitar), Jim Horn (baritone and tenor saxophone), Elton John (piano), Gary Wright (piano). Co-written in part with Jeff Lynne. Yielded “Got My Mind Set on You” (#1 US).

Best of Dark Horse 1976–1989 (Dark Horse/Warner Bros., 1989): Compilation produced primarily by Harrison, with individual tracks co-produced by Jeff Lynne, Phil McDonald, Ray Cooper, and Russ Titelman.

Traveling Wilburys (1988–1990)

Harrison co-founded the supergroup Traveling Wilburys with Bob Dylan, Tom Petty (see Tom Petty And The Heartbreakers), Roy Orbison, and Jeff Lynne. Harrison recorded under the alias Nelson Wilbury (and Spike Wilbury on Vol. 3). Both albums — Volume One (1988) and Vol. 3 (1990) — featured Jim Horn on saxophone. Roy Orbison died in December 1988 before Vol. 3 was recorded.

Later Work and Posthumous Release (1992–2002)

Live in Japan (Dark Horse/Warner Bros., 1992): Recorded on his 1991 Japanese tour with Eric Clapton and band — Harrison’s only full solo concert tour since 1974.

Brainwashed (Dark Horse/Parlophone, 2002): Posthumous album, recorded in the late 1990s and completed after Harrison’s death by his son Dhani Harrison and Jeff Lynne. A co-cover of “Between the Devil and the Deep Blue Sea” (Harold Arlen / Ted Koehler) is included alongside original compositions.
