Lincoln Fong

Recording engineer, mixer, musician, and bass player. Discogs artist IDs: 49266 (Lincoln Fong), 6623935 (Lincoln Fook — alternate Discogs entry with same realname). Active from at least 1985 through the 2020s. Name variations on credits: “Lincold Fong”, “Lincoln”. Bandcamp: lincolnfong.bandcamp.com.

Overview

Lincoln Fong is a London-based recording engineer (later also based in or connected to Chicago — see Open Questions) whose career spans four decades of independent and major-label work. He cut his teeth as an assistant/second engineer at 4AD and its orbit in the late 1980s, working on landmark records for The Wolfgang Press, A.R. Kane, Harold Budd, and Lush alongside house producers John Fryer, Flood, and Robin Guthrie. Simultaneously he engineered the global smash “Pump Up The Volume” for MARRS (4th & Broadway, 1987) and the debut LP for The Gun Club offshoot The Weather Prophets.

By the early 1990s he had stepped into co-production and production roles — most significantly as a full member, bassist, producer, engineer, and mixer for The Veldt (Marigolds, Mammoth/Stardog, 1992) and for UK indie band Moose (2) (multiple Setanta and Hut releases, 1991–1996). His most-cited credit is recording and mixing Dirty Three’s Whatever You Love, You Are (Touch And Go, 2000). Other notable production work includes The Inbreds’ It’s Sydney Or The Bush (Murderecords, 1996) and a technical engineering role on The Who’s Endless Wire (Universal Republic, 2006).

Throughout his career Fong also played bass and guitar as a performer, not just a technician. He is credited as a member of five bands on Discogs: Critical Mass (5), For.Mat-, Moose (2), Polar Aim, and The Veldt.

Career by Period

1985–1988: 4AD Orbit, Early Studio Work

Fong’s earliest Discogs credit is as producer on “Like A Virgin” for an act on Illegal Records (2) (1985, UK). By 1986 he appears as re-recording assistant on Out Of The Trees (4AD, re-recording of This Mortal Coil material) and as recording/mixing assistant on Extractions (4AD Japan pressing, 1987). From there he becomes a full engineer across 4AD’s core early roster:

  • The Wolfgang Press — Bird Wood Cage (4AD, 1988): engineered tracks 1–3, 5, 9, alongside John Fryer (tracks 10–13) and Hugo Nicolson. Producers: Flood and John Fryer.
  • Harold Budd — The White Arcades (Opal Records, 1988): engineered track B3 alongside Brian Eno (B2) and Robin Guthrie (A2, A4). Harold Budd sole producer and performer.
  • A.R. Kane — “i” (Rough Trade, 1989): one of many engineers on this sprawling double LP.
  • The Weather Prophets — Mother Juno (Red Rhino, 1987): sole engineer. Also Breaking Hands (Fundamental/Red Rhino, 1988).

The M|A|R|R|S session is particularly notable: “Pump Up The Volume” (4th & Broadway, 1987) became one of the first UK number-one house/sampling records. Fong was the session engineer across all tracks; producers were Martyn Young and Steven Young (Colourbox); CJ Mackintosh scratched, Dave Dorrell performed, A.R. Kane contributed guitar, and John Fryer added samples. Design by Vaughan Oliver.

1988–1991: 4AD Continues, Expanding Roster

Fong’s 4AD work deepens through 1990:

  • Lush — Gala (4AD, 1990): engineered tracks 4–7 (Robin Guthrie produced those tracks; John Fryer and Tim Friese-Greene produced others). Also engineered the Mad Love EP (4AD, 1990), co-engineered with Robin Guthrie.
  • Cocteau Twins — Spooky (4AD, 1992): assistant engineer.
  • Pixies / related — additional engineering on Four-Calendar Café (Fontana, 1993, Cocteau Twins), Bluebeard (Capitol, 1993), Evangeline (Capitol, 1993), Milk & Kisses (Fontana, 1996), Tishbite (Fontana, 1996), Snow single, Summerhead single — these are all Cocteau Twins releases; Fong’s additional engineering role places him as part of Robin Guthrie’s extended team in this period.

He also engineers for acts outside 4AD in this window: 69 (Rough Trade, 1988, artist unknown), Up Home! (Rough Trade, 1988), Rem’i’xes (Rough Trade Germany, 1989), King Of Soul (4AD, 1988), Raintime / Bottom Drawer (4AD, 1989).

By 1991–1992 Fong transitions into performer/bassist roles alongside engineering: credited on Hut Recordings releases Reprise (1991), This River Will Never Run Dry (1991), and …XYZ (1992) — bass and vocals — as well as mixed by on …XYZ. These are Moose (2) releases (Hut/Virgin).

1992–1996: Moose, The Veldt, Production Work

The early-to-mid 1990s are Fong’s most active as both musician and producer-engineer.

Moose (2) (Discogs ID: 256671) — Fong is a band member (bass) and engineer/producer on their catalog. UK indie/shoegazer band. Hut Recordings (Virgin), then Setanta:

  • Reprise (Hut, 1991) — bass
  • This River Will Never Run Dry (Hut, 1991) — bass
  • …XYZ (Hut, 1992) — bass, vocals, mixed by
  • Marigolds (Mammoth/Stardog, 1992) — Note: this is The Veldt, not Moose
  • Shifting EP (Setanta, 1994) — engineer, mixed by
  • Cotton Dress (Setanta, 1994) — engineer, mixed by (for band “Catchers”, also a Setanta act)
  • Mute (Setanta France, 1994) — engineer, mixed by
  • The Frog Princess (Setanta, 1996) — mixed by
  • Bring Trouble (Setanta, 1999) — additional engineer
  • Songs In The Key Of E (Midnight Music, 1991) — engineer and producer
  • BBC Sessions (Bella Union, 1999) — engineer

The Veldt (Discogs ID: 540963) — Fong is a full band member (bass) and produced, engineered, and mixed their debut EP and album:

  • Marigolds (Mammoth Records/Stardog Records, 1992) — bass, producer, engineer, mixed by. The Veldt are a shoegaze/dream-pop band from Durham, NC; members include Daniel Chavis (vocals), Danny Chavis (guitar), and Marvin Levi (drums).

The Inbreds (2) — It’s Sydney Or The Bush (Murderecords, Canada, 1996) — producer, mixed by, engineer. Canadian indie rock duo (Mike O’Neill and Dave Ullrich).

Bespoke (Atlantic, 1997) — producer and engineer on this US release.

Other 1993 credits: Honey Bee (Play It Again Sam, 1993) — performer and producer; Uptown (Play It Again Sam, 1993) — producer; Liquid Make Up (Cool Badge, 1993) — producer and engineer; Why Can’t I Wake Up With You? (RCA, 1993) — engineer.

2000–2006: Dirty Three, Live Mixing, The Who

Dirty Three — Whatever You Love, You Are (Touch And Go, 2000) — Fong’s most-cited standalone credit. He recorded and mixed the entire album; design by David Babbitt and Mick Turner. The album features Warren Ellis (violin), Mick Turner (guitar), and Jim White (2) (drums), all credited as composers. 18 pressings across multiple territories.

Live mixing — Live At The Royal Albert Hall (Image Entertainment/MCY Entertainment, 2001) — mixed by (likely a concert film or live album). A second live RAH release in 2003 (Steamhammer, Germany) credits him for “Additional Mixes Assisting.”

Smiling & Waving (Virgin, 2001) — assistant engineer.

The Who — Endless Wire (Universal Republic, 2006) — technical engineer (studio tracks) and mixed by (live DVD tracks). Pete Townshend produced and mixed the studio album; Fong handled technical engineering on CD1 and co-mixed the live DVD material with Myles Clarke.

2008–2023: Sporadic Credits

Open Questions

  • Melbourne or Chicago? The brief asked this question. Discogs gives no location for Fong. His 4AD credits are clearly London-based (4AD operated from Wandsworth/London throughout). By 2000 he was recording Dirty Three in what would have been their Australian sessions or US sessions (the band was Melbourne-based but toured the US extensively; Touch And Go is Chicago). The Inbreds (1996, Murderecords) is a Canadian label. Without a web search this cannot be resolved from Discogs alone — needs web confirmation.
  • Russell Fong (Moose member, Discogs ID: 898372) — surname match raises question of whether Lincoln and Russell Fong are related (brothers?). Discogs has no data beyond the band-member listing.
  • Critical Mass (5), For.Mat-, Polar Aim — three of Fong’s five listed band memberships have no detail in his artist record. Discogs has no releases attributed to these acts in his credit trail. Possibly very small/unreleased projects.
  • “Pump Up The Volume” session location — given 4th & Broadway is a London-UK-US joint label and the M|A|R|R|S project involved London-based artists (Colourbox, A.R. Kane), the session was almost certainly London. Consistent with a London base in 1987.
  • The 2023 credit (Illuminated 1989, guitar/programmed by) is unusual for an engineer — suggests Fong’s performer identity continues late in his career.
  • Full personnel on It’s Sydney Or The Bush beyond Fong, O’Neill, and Ullrich is not documented in these credits.
