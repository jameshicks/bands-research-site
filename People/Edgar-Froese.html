Edgar Froese

German electronic musician, composer, visual artist, and founder of Tangerine Dream. Born Edgar Wilmar Fröse, 6 June 1944, Tilsit, East Prussia (now Sovetsk, Kaliningrad region, Russia); died 20 January 2015, Vienna, Austria, from a pulmonary embolism. Father of Jerome Froese. Discogs artist ID: 10361.

Biography

Early Life and Formation (1944–1967)

Froese was born in Tilsit in the final year of the Second World War. His father and some relatives were killed under the Nazi regime; he and his mother fled to Berlin after the war. He began working at age 15, and at 18 was accepted to the Berlin Academy of Arts (Hochschule für Bildende Künste), studying painting, graphics, and sculpture — training that shaped his lifelong involvement in cover design and visual art. He financed his studies through acting, advertising, and writing work. Although he initially intended to become a pianist, he turned to guitar as his primary instrument. He completed his studies in 1966.

His musical career began in 1965 with a short-lived rock group called The Ones (2), an eight-piece band including Charlie Prince, Detlef Stegen, Hacky Hornbach, Kurt Herkenberg, Lanse Hapshash, Michael Auerbach, Ron Perrit, Volker Hombach, and Will Stegen. After that group disbanded in 1967, Froese co-founded Tangerine Dream within the same year. The group began performing live in 1968 but Froese suspended activities for a six-month hiatus in 1969 before reconvening with a new lineup.

Also listed in the Discogs group memberships is Ed Jefro (artist ID: 666180), an obscure early band entry.

Tangerine Dream — Ohr Period (1970–1973)

With the reactivated Tangerine Dream, Froese invited Steve Jolliffe (soon to leave) and in his place Conrad Schnitzler and Klaus Schulze to form the trio that recorded the debut album Electronic Meditation (Ohr, 1970). Froese played guitar, organ, piano, and glass-shard noises (“Glasscherben”), and is credited for composition (“Musik & Manuskript”). After both Schnitzler and Schulze departed, Froese rebuilt the lineup with Christopher Franke (percussion, synthesizer) joining for Alpha Centauri (Ohr, 1971), alongside Steve Schroyder and Roland Paulick. Recording was handled by Dieter Dierks.

Peter Baumann joined for Zeit (Ohr, 1972), forming the classic Froese–Franke–Baumann trio that would define Tangerine Dream’s most celebrated era. On Zeit Froese played gliss guitar and audio generator; his cover painting appeared on the sleeve. Atem (Polydor, 1973) — the last Ohr-era record — was voted album of the year by John Peel at BBC Radio 1, catalyzing the British attention that led to the Virgin deal.

Throughout these years Froese was also designing and painting the band’s sleeves — a visual practice he maintained for decades across both Tangerine Dream and his solo work.

Tangerine Dream — Virgin Records Classic Period (1974–1983)

Signed to Virgin Records in 1974, the Froese–Franke–Baumann trio recorded Phaedra (1974), arguably the most influential album in the Berlin School canon. The sequencer-driven title track became an archetype; the album reached #15 in the UK charts. Froese played mellotron, guitar, bass, VCS3, and organ, and painted the cover. He continued as composer, producer, and multi-instrumentalist across the core Virgin run:

Film Scoring (1977–1990)

Froese and Tangerine Dream became the pre-eminent European synthesizer film scorers of the late 1970s and 1980s. Key entries documented on Discogs:

Froese’s Kamikaze 1989 (1982) is a solo soundtrack credit, scored for the German film directed by Wolf Gremm and starring Rainer Werner Fassbinder.

Jive Electro, Private Music, and TDI Era (1984–2001)

After Virgin, Tangerine Dream moved through Jive Electro (1984–1988, releases including Poland (The Warsaw Concert) and Tyger), then Private Music — Peter Baumann’s own label — for Optical Race (1988), Lily On The Beach (1989), and Melrose (1990). Jerome Froese (Edgar’s son, Discogs ID: 447638) joined in 1990, and the father-son duo became the nucleus of Tangerine Dream for the next 24 years.

In the mid-1990s Froese founded TDI Music (Discogs ID: 14081), which had four sublabels (Ground Liftaz, TDC, TDI Production, TDP International). TDI issued the band’s catalog from roughly 1996 onward: Tournado (1997), TimeSquare: Dream Mixes II (1997), Ambient Monkeys (1998), Mars Polaris (1999), The Seven Letters From Tibet (2000), and many archival and remix packages. Froese designed cover art for virtually all of these (often credited “Zoom-O-Graphic” or similar).

Solo releases during this period include Dalinetopia (Eastgate, 2005, engineered and mastered by Thorsten Quaeschning), Orange Light Years (Eastgate, 2005), and The Epsilon Journey (Eastgate, 2008).

Eastgate Era and Late Activity (2002–2015)

TDI Music was renamed/succeeded by Eastgate (Discogs ID: 57822), Froese’s continuing imprint under Eastgate Music And Media Ltd. The Eastgate years produced a torrent of releases: the Dante trilogy (Inferno 2002, Purgatorio 2004, Paradiso 2006), Madcap’s Flaming Duty (2007), Springtime In Nagasaki (2007), Views From A Red Train (2008), Chandra (The Phantom Ferry — Part I) (2009), The Endless Season (2010), Finnegans Wake (James Joyce) (2011), The Angel From The West Window (2011), Machu Picchu (2012), Franz Kafka The Castle (2013), Cruise To Destiny (2013), Chandra (The Phantom Ferry Part II) (2014), and Phaedra Farewell Tour 2014. Froese handled composition, production, keyboards, guitar, artwork, and often direction of video releases throughout.

Thorsten Quaeschning (synthesizer/keyboards/engineering) became an increasingly prominent collaborator during the 2000s, eventually becoming the continuity figure for Tangerine Dream after Froese’s death.

A notable late credit: Froese composed music for Grand Theft Auto V (Rockstar Games, 2014), credited as written-by on the official soundtrack release (The Music Of Grand Theft Auto V, Mass Appeal/Rockstar Games, 2014).

Quantum Key and Quantum Gate were reportedly being worked on with Peter Baumann (who briefly returned in June 2015) and Quaeschning before Froese’s sudden death on 20 January 2015. Froese died in Vienna from a pulmonary embolism.

His posthumous release Final Movement (Not On Label / Jerome Froese self-released, 2015) credited him on sequencer and keyboards alongside Jerome Froese.

Visual Art Practice

Froese maintained an active visual art career throughout his life, rooted in his formal training at the Berlin Academy. He painted album covers for multiple Tangerine Dream records (notably Phaedra, Zeit), designed or contributed design to dozens of releases, and in later years developed a “Zoom-O-Graphic” series of digital artworks used consistently across Eastgate-era packaging. He is listed with an IMDB entry (nm0296209), reflecting both his early acting work and documentary/film music connections.

Name Variations

Credited on records as: E. Froese, E.W. Froese, Edgar W. Froese, Edgar Willmar Froese, Froese, E. Foese, EF, Edgar, e.froese, and several other variations. Real name: Edgar Wilmar Fröse.
