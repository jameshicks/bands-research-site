Nick Heyward

UK singer, songwriter, guitarist. Born Nicholas Heyward, 20 May 1961, Beckenham, Kent. Discogs artist ID: 111936. Also credited as N. Heyward, N.H., Nicholas Heyward, Nick Heyward And The 13 Satellites.

Overview

Nick Heyward came to prominence as the founder, lead vocalist, and principal songwriter of Haircut One Hundred, the new wave / pop-funk band he formed in Beckenham in 1980 with school friends Graham Jones and Les Nemes. The band signed to Arista Records in 1981 and scored four UK top 10 singles between 1981 and 1982 before their debut album Pelican West (1982) peaked at No. 2. Heyward departed in early 1983 — officially to go solo, though he later acknowledged pressure and stress had effectively led to his dismissal. He has described his feelings toward the band warmly in subsequent years, and Haircut One Hundred reformed intermittently from 2004 onward, releasing new material from 2024.

His solo career, launched on Arista in 1983, yielded three top-20 UK singles and the Gold-certified debut LP North of a Miracle (No. 10, UK). Subsequent albums on Arista, Reprise, Epic, and Creation Records charted with diminishing commercial returns through the 1990s, though From Monday to Sunday (Epic, 1993) reached No. 4 on Billboard’s Hot Modern Rock Tracks with the single “Kite.” His Creation Records period (1997–98) produced the album The Apple Bed, the last major-label release. Since then Heyward has worked independently: Open Sesame Seed (2001, with actor/singer Greg Ellis), The Mermaid and the Lighthouse Keeper (2006, with India Dupre), and Woodland Echoes (2017, co-produced with his son Oliver Heyward and Ian Shaw). Haircut One Hundred released new material in 2024–25 on the Map Of England label. (Sources: Wikipedia; Discogs)

Haircut One Hundred

Pre-band history: Heyward, Jones, and Nemes first played together as early as 1977 under names including Rugby, The Boat Party, Captain Pennyworth, and Moving England before settling on Haircut One Hundred. The band’s first incarnation as “Moving England” predates the Discogs entity Moving England (a different 1980 project). See Haircut One Hundred for the full band article.
