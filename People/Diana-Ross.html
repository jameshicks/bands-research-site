Overview

Diana Ross (born March 26, 1944, Detroit, Michigan) is an American singer, actress, and producer who established herself as the lead voice of The Supremes before launching one of Motown’s most enduring solo careers. Across the 1970s and 1980s she moved fluidly through soul, R&B, disco, and jazz, with Motown as her primary label home for six decades alongside stints on RCA and EMI. Her most consistent creative relationships were with the songwriting and production team Ashford & Simpson and with engineers Russ Terrana and Hal Davis, while her 1980 collaboration with Bernard Edwards and Nile Rodgers marked a significant stylistic pivot toward disco-funk. She is the sister of Motown songwriter Arthur Ross.
