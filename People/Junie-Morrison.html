Junie Morrison

Real name: Walter B. Morrison, Jr.
Discogs ID: 67807
Born: January 28, 1954, Dayton, Ohio
Died: January 21, 2017, London, UK
Aliases: J.S. Theracon, BoyInSea, Captain America (3), Jettzonn, Micronagual, Tekadelic, The Algorithm (3)
Groups: Ohio Players, Parliament, Funkadelic

American producer, arranger, multi-instrumentalist, and vocalist. A central figure in the P-Funk universe across two decades: founding member of the Ohio Players, then musical director of Parliament-Funkadelic at the height of their powers (1977–1981). Solo career on Westbound Records and Columbia produced three highly regarded LP/single runs as “Junie.” Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997 as a member of Parliament-Funkadelic. In the late 1980s relocated to London, where he founded the Akashic label and continued writing and producing. Died in London, January 21, 2017.

Note: The previous stub listed birth date as November 26, 1951 / death as January 27, 2021 — Discogs gives January 28, 1954 / January 21, 2017 in London. The 2017 date aligns with widely reported accounts; the 1951 date appears to be an error.

Ohio Players (1970–1974)

Morrison joined the Ohio Players as keyboardist, vocalist, producer, and writer in 1970, when the band was still recording for Westbound. He appears on Pain (1972, Westbound) as performer and writer, and Ecstasy (1973, Westbound) as arranger. Left in 1974 to pursue a solo career.

Parliament-Funkadelic (1977–1981)

Joined George Clinton’s P-Funk collective in 1977 as musical director. Key during the period of the collective’s greatest commercial and artistic peak. He wrote, produced, and performed across both the Parliament (Casablanca) and Funkadelic (Warner Bros.) catalogs. Also used the pseudonym J.S. Theracon (Discogs ID 736231) on selected credits, reportedly to avoid contractual restrictions.

London Era and Independent Work (late 1980s–2017)

Relocation to London and Akashic Records

In the late 1980s Morrison relocated permanently to London, England. There he founded the Akashic record label together with his wife Kate Garner (a photographer and one-third of the 1980s new wave act Haysi Fantayzee). Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-25

Production and Songwriting (1990s)

During the 1990s Morrison wrote material recorded by several artists including Sounds of Blackness, Soul II Soul, and God’s Property. Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-25 / rockandrollparadise.com

Specific confirmed credits from this period:

  • Sounds of Blackness — “I Believe” (from Africa to America: The Journey of the Drum, 1994, Perspective/A&M): Morrison credited as co-songwriter alongside Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis. Wikipedia “I Believe (Sounds of Blackness song)”
  • James Ingram — produced recordings for Ingram across the 1990s; Ingram’s 1999 album Forever More (Love Songs, Hits and Duets) is cited, with the John Tesh/James Ingram track “Give Me Forever (I Do)” mentioned in connection with Morrison’s work. rockandrollparadise.com (source reliability: mid-tier fan/tribute site — confirm against album credits)
  • Soul II Soul — production/writing contribution to the Believe era cited in multiple obituaries, but specific song or album credit not yet confirmed. ambrosiaforheads.com

Juniefunk Label (2000s onward)

Dam-Funk Collaboration (2015)

Morrison appeared on Dam-Funk’s double/triple LP Invite The Light (Stones Throw, September 4, 2015):

  • “Junie’s Transmission” (ft. Junie Morrison) — opening track
  • “Junie’s Re-Transmission” (ft. Junie Morrison) — closing track
  • “Magic” — outtake from the same sessions; released separately October 1, 2015 via Okayplayer

Dam-Funk described the collaboration: “His Spirit Was So Unique and Interesting.” Morrison also served as a co-producer on the sessions. Vice/Dam-Funk interview / Okayplayer

Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (1997)

Inducted as a member of Parliament-Funkadelic in 1997, alongside George Clinton, Bernie Worrell, Bootsy Collins, and fifteen other P-Funk members.

Death

Morrison died January 21, 2017, in London, at age 62. His family did not announce the death publicly until approximately one month later (the announcement came around February 16, 2017). The specific cause of death was kept private. Dam-Funk confirmed the death and noted he had been in regular contact with Morrison in the period before his death. Rolling Stone obituary, Feb 16 2017 / Billboard

George Clinton’s tribute: “the most phenomenal musician on the planet.” georgeclinton.com

Sampling Legacy

“One Nation Under A Groove” (written w/ Clinton / Shider) and “Funky Worm” (Ohio Players) were extensively sampled. Notable samples: DJ Jazzy Jeff & The Fresh Prince’s “Boom! Shake The Room” (1993, Jive) samples “Funky Worm”; Naughty By Nature (1993) and Tribe Called Quest’s Beats, Rhymes And Life (1996) also sample Morrison-associated works.
