Adrian Sherwood

British record producer, label founder, and dub mixologist. Born Adrian Maxwell Sherwood, 20 January 1958, London. Founder of On-U Sound Records and the central figure in the UK post-punk/dub crossover of the early 1980s. Discogs artist ID: 4969.

Biography

Early Career and Hitrun (1977–1979)

Sherwood’s production work began as a teenager in the late 1970s, operating out of London with an early label called Hitrun (Discogs label ID: 910231). His first documented credit is a 1977 liner note for a K&B Records release; by 1978 he was producing and arranging for Hitrun, including Creation Rebel’s Dub From Creation (1978) and Close Encounters Of The Third World (1978), among the first releases in what would become his dub-led aesthetic.

He also worked briefly in a technician/production capacity on sessions that circulated through UK independent channels (Rough Trade, Y Records), mixing releases such as “Man Next Door” (1980) for Y Records — a connection that tied him into the post-punk/reggae crossover scene centred around Y Records and the then-emerging UK DIY label network.

Founding On-U Sound (1980–1982)

Sherwood founded On-U Sound Records around 1980, and the label’s earliest releases began appearing that year. Key productions from the founding period:

  • Creation Rebel — Starship Africa (1980, 4D Rhythms) — producer, arranger; one of the earliest full Sherwood dub productions
  • New Age Steppers — The New Age Steppers (1981, On-U Sound) — producer, mixer; a rotating collective including Ari-Up, Bruce Smith, and others from the post-punk orbit
  • New Age Steppers — Action Battlefield (1981, Statik Records) — producer
  • Creation Rebel — Psychotic Jonkanoo (1981, Statik Records) — producer
  • Creation Rebel — Threat To Creation (1981, Cherry Red) — producer, “disassembled by”
  • Singers & Players — War Of Words (1981, On-U Sound; licensed to 99 Records for US) — producer; a Jamaican artist collective Sherwood convened including Prince Far I, Mikey Dread, and Bim Sherman
  • Vivien Goldman — “Launderette” / “Private Armies” (1981, Window label UK / 99 Records US) — produced “Private Armies” (B-side); “Launderette” A-side co-produced by John Lydon and Keith Levene. Note: not Rough Trade or Virgin — UK 7” was on Window (WIN 1), US 12” EP on 99 Records.
  • Medium Medium — “Hungry, So Angry / Nadsat Dream” (1981, Cherry Red) — producer
  • The Fall — Slates (1981, Rough Trade) — producer; an early outside commission from Mark E. Smith

The 1981–1982 period established On-U Sound’s identity: a rolling roster of Jamaican vocalists, UK post-punk musicians, and session players anchored by Sherwood’s processing-heavy studio approach — extreme echo, tape manipulation, unconventional rhythm editing.

Key 1982 productions:

  • African Head Charge — Environmental Studies (1982, On-U Sound) — producer, arranger; debut LP for African Head Charge, the project anchored by percussionist Bonjo Iyabinghi Noah
  • African Head Charge — My Life In A Hole In The Ground (1981, On-U Sound) — producer, arranger
  • Creation Rebel — Across The Red Sea (1982) — producer
  • Creation Rebel — The Pounding System (Ambience In Dub) (1982, On-U Sound) — producer
  • Singers & Players — Revenge Of The Underdog (1982, On-U Sound) — producer
  • Singers & Players — Lows & Highs (1982, Cherry Red) — producer
  • Judy Nylon — Pal Judy (1982, On-U Sound) — producer
  • Mark Stewart And The Maffia — Learning To Cope With Cowardice (1983, On-U Sound) — producer

Expansion and Mark Stewart / Tackhead (1983–1988)

The mid-1980s saw Sherwood’s studio extended into industrial, funk, and hip-hop territory, largely through his association with Mark Stewart and the formation of Tackhead.

Mark Stewart — Sherwood produced most of Stewart’s solo and Maffia-credited releases:

  • Learning To Cope With Cowardice (1983) — producer
  • As The Veneer Of Democracy Starts To Fade (1985, Mute) — Maffia member/performer
  • Mark Stewart (1987, Mute) — producer
  • Metatron (1990, Mute) — recorded by, mixed by, producer
  • Hysteria (1990, Mute) — producer, mixer, performer

Tackhead — Sherwood is the fourth member of this industrial/hip-hop unit, credited as “mixologist.” The core trio of Skip McDonald, Doug Wimbish, and Keith LeBlanc — formerly the rhythm section behind Sugarhill Records — combined with Sherwood’s production on releases including:

  • Tackhead Tape Time (1987, Nettwerk) — performer
  • Friendly As A Hand Grenade (1989, World Records) — engineer, mixologist
  • Strange Parcels — Sherwood listed as a band member

Ministry — Twitch (1986, Sire) — producer; Sherwood produced this early Ministry album, bridging industrial and EBM sounds for Al Jourgensen before Ministry’s heavier direction.

Cabaret Voltaire — Code (1987, Parlophone) — producer (tracks 1–8, 10, 11); Sherwood brought his On-U Sound processing style to Cabaret Voltaire’s major-label debut, working with Richard H. Kirk and Stephen Mallinder. He also produced related Cab Volt singles including “Here To Go” (1987) and “Don’t Argue (Dance)” (1987).

Gary Clail — Sherwood produced and mixed extensively for Gary Clail and the Gary Clail & On-U Sound System throughout the late 1980s, including:

  • The English Disease (1989, On-U Sound) — producer
  • Songs Of Praise (1990, On-U Sound) — mixer, producer
  • Emotional Hooligan (1991, Perfecto/RCA) — producer (multiple pressings including US)
  • Who Pays The Piper? (1992, Perfecto) — producer

Nine Inch Nails and Mainstream Crossover (1989–1993)

Perhaps the most unexpected credit in Sherwood’s discography is as one of four producers on Nine Inch Nails’ Pretty Hate Machine (1989, TVT Records), alongside Flood, John Fryer, and Keith LeBlanc. Sherwood also reproduced and remixed several NIN singles:

  • “Down In It” (1989, TVT) — reproduced by, remix
  • “Head Like A Hole” (1990, TVT) — mixer, producer
  • “Sin (Long, Dub & Short)” (1990, TVT) — remix

Further mainstream remix work in this period included Depeche Mode — “Enjoy The Silence” (1990, Mute) mixer; Sinéad O’Connor work; and a remix of The Fall’s Extricate (1990, Fontana/Cog Sinister) as producer and mixer.

Lee Perry — Time Boom X De Devil Dead (1987, Syncopate) — engineer, producer; a landmark collaboration with the legendary Jamaican producer, bringing two of dub’s defining figures together.

Gary Clail, Pop Crossover, and On-U in the 1990s (1990–1999)

Sherwood continued a steady production output through the 1990s, balancing On-U Sound catalog releases with outside commissions:

  • Primal Scream — Echo Dek (1997, Creation Records) — mixer, additional producer; dub reworking of Vanishing Point with Andrew Weatherall-adjacent material
  • The Cure — “The Big Man And The Scream Team Meet The Barmy Army Uptown” (1996, Creation Records) — Barmy Army collaboration, mixer/producer
  • African Head Charge — continued producing throughout, including In Pursuit Of Shashamane Land (1993), Vision Of A Psychedelic Africa (2005)
  • Dub Syndicate — on-label productions throughout the decade
  • Sinéad O’Connor — Faith And Courage (2000, Atlantic) — producer
  • Audio Active (Japanese band) — series of productions through Beat Records 1994–1999
  • Barmy Army — political dance-punk project active from 1988 onwards

On-U Sound released the ongoing Pay It All Back compilation series documenting the label’s output: Vol. 1 (1984), Vol. 2 (1988), Vol. 3 (1991), Vol. 4 (1993), Vol. 5 (1995), Vol. 6 (1996), Vol. 7 (2019), Vol. 8 (2022) — a continuous archive spanning four decades.

Skinny Puppy — Sherwood mixed Addiction (1991, Nettwerk) and related releases.

2000s–Present

After On-U Sound’s 2002 relaunch (kicked off with Little Axe’s Never Trust A Hippy on Real World Records), Sherwood resumed steady output:

  • Little Axe — Never Trust A Hippy (2003, Real World) — producer; Champagne & Grits (2004); Stone Cold Ohio (2006); Bought For A Dollar/Sold For A Dime (2010)
  • Lee Perry — Rainford (2019, On-U Sound) — producer; Perry’s return album produced by Sherwood shortly before Perry’s death
  • Lee Perry — The Mighty Upsetter (2008, On-U Sound) — producer
  • Horace Andy — extensive production on the late On-U Sound catalog
  • Roots Manuva — On-U Sound releases including Bangarang (2011 via On-U)
  • Mark Stewart — Edit (2008, Crippled Dick Hot Wax!) — performer as The Maffia, producer, recorded by, mixed by
  • Tackhead — Survival & Resistance (2012, Beat Records) — producer, arranger, mixer (as primary)
  • Asian Dub Foundation — More Signal More Noise (2015, ADF Communications) — mixer, producer
  • Pinch — co-productions at the On-U/dubstep crossover point 2013–2019
  • Man Vs. Sofa (2017, On-U Sound/Tectonic) — Sherwood’s collaboration with Pinch
  • Spoon — “Lucifer On The Moon (Spoon Vs On-U Sound)” (2022, Matador) — remix
  • Bob Vylan — The Collapse Of Everything (2025, On-U Sound) — producer, mixer

On-U Sound Records

Discogs label ID: 2409
Parent entity: On-U Sound Records And Music Ltd. (ID: 469461)
Total releases: 781 (primary label); sublabels include Sound Boy, Pay It All Back, Reggae Archive, Noah Records, Sherwood At The Controls, On-U Sound MasterRecordings, and ON-U Sound 10” Disco Plate

Founded around 1980 in London, On-U Sound was initially distributed through independent channels (Cherry Red, Statik, Rough Trade); by the mid-1980s it operated with international distribution through EFA (Germany). The label relaunched in 2002 with new catalog numbers starting at ON-U CD 1001.
