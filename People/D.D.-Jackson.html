D.D. Jackson

Born Robert Cleanth Kai-Nen Jackson, 25 January 1967, Ottawa, Canada. Jazz pianist, composer, arranger, educator, and TV/film composer. Two-time Emmy Award winner; Juno Award-winning jazz pianist. Canadian–American citizenship.

Overview

D.D. Jackson built his early career as a leader on Justin Time (Montreal), recording five albums between 1995 and 1999 that established him as a technically accomplished post-bop pianist with eclectic influences running from Don Pullen to Duke Ellington. His debut Peace-Song (1995) featured David Murray on tenor saxophone, an association that deepened through membership in the David Murray Octet and David Murray Quartet, with whom Jackson contributed original compositions as well as piano.

As a sideman he was active across multiple orbits: the avant-jazz scene around David Murray and Kip Hanrahan’s American Clavé/Yellowbird productions; the New York post-bop world (James Carter (3), Vincent Chancey, Billy Bang); and a later venture into hip-hop production with The Roots (…And Then You Shoot Your Cousin, Def Jam, 2014, track 8).

His solo work on RCA Victor (So Far, 1999) was a solo piano recording dedicated to pianists including Ornette Coleman, Claude Debussy, Vladimir Horowitz, and John Hicks. In the 2000s he transitioned significantly into composing for television and other media.
