Steve Beresford

Born Stephen Beresford, Wellington, Shropshire, UK, 1950. Multi-instrumentalist, composer, arranger, improviser. One of the central figures in the British free improvisation scene since the mid-1970s, working across free jazz, post-punk, electro-acoustic music, experimental pop, and film/TV composition. Discogs artist ID: 123904.

Biography

Early Career and the Incus Circle (1975–1979)

Beresford emerged in the mid-1970s from the orbit of the Incus label, which had been established by Derek Bailey and Evan Parker as the key British free improvisation imprint. His first significant recorded appearance is Teatime (1975, Incus), a collectively composed release with guitarist John Russell, percussionist Dave Solomon, and violinist Nigel Coombes — a five-way improvising unit. The same period saw his involvement with the loose post-Fluxus collective that would eventually be labelled Three Pullovers (with Tony Orton and David Toop), documenting cassette-format minimalist work on Quartz-Mirliton.

By 1979 he was a member of The Flying Lizards, the avant-cabaret group assembled by David Cunningham. The Flying Lizards had an unexpected mainstream hit with a deadpan cover of “Money” on Virgin, but their albums — including Fourth Wall (1981) — showcased Beresford on keyboards and bass in stranger territory.

Post-Punk Networks: The Slits, On-U Sound, New Age Steppers (1980–1984)

The early 1980s saw Beresford operating at the intersection of British free improvisation and the post-punk/dub underground. He appears on Return Of The Giant Slits (1981, CBS) credited as “Plays A Lot On This Record,” produced partly by Dennis Bovell. He played bass, piano, and percussion on the debut self-titled LP by New Age Steppers (1981, On-U Sound), an On-U Sound collective assembled by Adrian Sherwood featuring vocals from Mark Stewart, Ari-Up, and Vivien Goldman.

On-U Sound would remain a recurring connection through the decade: he contributed keyboards and horns to records in their catalog including Sherwood productions for Prince Far I (Cry Tuff Dub Encounter Chapter III, 1980), Crucial Tony’s sessions, and the label’s various compilation volumes.

General Strike — the trio with Cunningham and David Toop — produced their key statement Danger In Paradise (1984, Touch). Beresford plays the full range: bass, piano, glockenspiel, organ, trumpet, synthesizer, flugelhorn, toy piano, euphonium, percussion, vocals, melodica, and noises. He also co-produced. The album sits at the juncture of early electronic pop, free improvisation, and art music.

Simultaneously, he became the central musical architect for Frank Chickens, the Japanese duo of Kazuko Hohki and Kazumi Taguchi based in London. He produced and arranged their debut We Are Frank Chickens (1984, Kaz Records) and the hit single We Are Ninja (Not Geisha) (1984), co-directed their musical direction across several years.

The Nato / Chabada Years and International Collaborations (1985–1991)

The French labels Nato (run by Jean Rochard in Nantes) and Chabada became Beresford’s primary outlets for a prolific sequence of solo and collaborative albums. Jean Rochard produced most of these dates.

  • Dancing The Line (1985, Nato) — with vocalist Kazuko Hohki, clarinetist Alan Hacker, and David Toop on flutes and guitar
  • Deadly Weapons (1986, Nato) — quartet with David Toop, John Zorn, and vocalist Tonie Marshall; Beresford on keyboards, tape, trumpet, guitar, sampler, drum programming
  • Directly To Pyjamas (1987, Nato) — duo with Dutch percussionist Han Bennink; Beresford on piano, euphonium, pocket trumpet, Casio SK
  • L’Extraordinaire Jardin De Charles Trenet (1988, Chabada) — conducted and arranged by Beresford as Steve Beresford His Piano & Orchestra, featuring Han Bennink on drums, Louis Sclavis on baritone saxophone, Tony Coe on tenor, and an ensemble of British and French players

Through the Chabada catalogue he also recorded multiple albums with The Melody Four, documenting work that had begun in France’s experimental pop orbit.

Alterations — Beresford’s longest-running free-improv group (active from the late 1970s), with David Toop, Peter Cusack, and Terry Day — worked steadily through this period. A 2023 archival release documents a 1980 Logos Foundation concert.

He appeared on Celluloid Records via the It’s Only Money session (1983, with Fred Frith), linking him to the New York downtown network.

Mid-Period: Free Improvisation, Film Work, and Scatter (1992–2003)

Through the 1990s Beresford concentrated increasingly on two tracks: continued free improvisation (with the British and European scenes) and composing for independent British film and television. The Discogs profile notes his compositional work for film/TV as a major focus of the decade, though these credits appear largely outside Discogs’s scope.

On the improvisation side he remained active in the ecology around London Improvisers Orchestra (he is listed as a member) and continued duos and small-group work. Fish Of The Week (1996, Scatter) documents a quartet with John Butcher on saxophones, Clive Bell on shakuhachi, and Mark Sanders on drums.

He contributed liner notes and production support to several Ogun releases through the 1990s, including work on South African jazz documentation (Spirits Rejoice, 1992; Ixesha (Time), 1994) as line producer and music director.

A solo self-titled CD appeared in 2002 (released in Japan on Saidera).

2004–2015: Italian Improv, Reissues, and Continuing Ensembles

I Shall Become A Bat (2004, Qbico) pairs Beresford with John Butcher on saxophones in an electronics and objects duet. Ointment (2004, Rossbin) is a solo record of objects, electronics, toy instruments, and trumpet.

He was involved in the formation or continuation of numerous ensembles including Four Pullovers, Kontakte Trio, Foxes Fox, Frequency Disasters, Consorts, SFE, and Will It Float?. A 2015 Norwegian release on Va Fongool, Will It Flow, documents the latter group.

The Nato/Chabada catalogue saw periodic CD reissues; Deadly Weapons and Dancing The Line received new pressings in the 2000s and 2010s.

Recent Work (2016–2025)

Beresford has maintained a prolific recording output into his seventies. Key releases:

  • No Relation (2020, Amplify 2020) — duo with David Toop, with overdubs by Taku Unami; Beresford on East German Zimmermann piano
  • Trwst (2020) — with Welsh violinist Angharad Davies
  • The Phantom Sunrise (2020, Aural Detritus) — with Kontakte Trio
  • Stovelit Lines (2020, Weight Of Wax) — solo piano and electronics
  • Iklectik Recordings (2022, Shrike Records) — trio with vocalist Steph Horak and percussionist Gino Robair, recorded at the Iklectik venue
  • Untitled / Logos Improvisations (2023, Sub Rosa / Logos) — with various collaborators at the Logos Foundation in Ghent
  • Elusive (2024, Shrike Records) — piano, objects, electronics
  • Be S-Mart (2025, Confront) — prepared piano, electronics, objects

A 2023 release documents the Alterations group at Logos Foundation, Ghent, 1980 — an archival recovery of the earliest period.
