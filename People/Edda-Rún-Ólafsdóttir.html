Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir

Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir

Oct 29, 20266 min read

Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir

Discogs ID: 419790 Instrument: Violin, Viola Origin: Iceland Active: c. 1999–present (as session/guest contributor)

Icelandic violinist and violist. Founding member of Amiina (then Amína), the Reykjavík string quartet that became the core touring and studio ensemble for Sigur Rós. Active with Amiina from the group’s formation until 2015, she has continued as a session player and appears on a wide range of Icelandic and international records across modern classical, post-rock, ambient, and folk-adjacent contexts.

Name variations on Discogs: E. R. Ólafsdóttir · Edda Rùn Ólafsdóttir · Edda Rún Ílafsdóttir · Edda Rún Ólafsdottir

Biography

Amína / Amiina: Formation and Sigur Rós (c. 1999–2006)

Edda was one of the four founding members of the string quartet that formed in Reykjavík as Amína — alongside María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir, Hildur Ársælsdóttir, and Sólrún Sumarliðadóttir, with Ólöf Júlía Kjartansdóttir appearing on some early recordings as viola. The group came to wider attention as the uncredited string quartet on Sigur Rós’s ( ) (FatCat Records, 2002), where Edda plays violin and the quartet also arranged the string parts. The credit is listed individually on Discogs: she is credited “Violin [Uncredited]” alongside Ólöf Júlía (viola), Sólrún (cello), and María Huld (violin).

The same year, Edda appeared as second violin on La Chanson Dada (Raft Recordings, 2002), a release by The Hafler Trio featuring cello from Hildur Guðnadóttir and harmonium from Jóhann Jóhannsson.

The group’s first release under their own name was AnimaminA (The Worker’s Institute, 2005), credited as “Amina,” with Edda, Hildur Ársælsdóttir, María Huld, and Sólrún listed as Performers. In March 2006 the group renamed to Amiina; Edda is credited as co-writer and performer on the “Seoul” single (Amiina self-released, 2006).

Amiina’s Solo Catalog and Continued Sigur Rós Work (2006–2012)

Edda remained a core member through Amiina’s active recording period: Kurr (Bláskjár Records, 2007), Puzzle (Amínamúsík Ehf., 2010), and The Lighthouse Project (Sound Of A Handshake, 2013). Discogs credits her as a performer on Amiina’s main releases during this period.

Alongside the group’s solo work, she continued as a session player for Sigur Rós:

  • Heima (EMI, 2007) — credited violin (individually listed alongside Amiina’s other members)
  • Með Suð Í Eyrum Við Spilum Endalaust (EMI/Krúnk, 2008) — strings on tracks 2–5, 8, and 9, credited as “E. R. Ólafsdóttir”
  • Valtari (Krúnk/Parlophone, 2012) — strings arranger and performer on the majority of tracks (all except the one arranged by Daníel Bjarnason); this is her most substantial individual credit on a Sigur Rós record

She also appeared on Efterklang’s Tripper (Leaf, 2004) as violin on several tracks (A1, B1, C1, C2), playing alongside Hildur Ársælsdóttir.

Session Work Outside Amiina (2010–2014)

During this period Edda accumulated a range of session credits as part of the recurring Amiina string unit, often operating as “Quartet Iceland” or under the Amiina collective name:

  • Julianna Barwick — Nepenthe (Dead Oceans, 2013): strings credited “Strings [Amiina]”
  • Spiritualized — Sweet Heart, Sweet Light (Double Six, 2012): “Strings [Quartet Iceland]” alongside Hildur, María Huld, and Sólrún
  • Alcest — Shelter (Prophecy Productions, 2014): viola, with strings arranged and recorded by Birgir Jón Birgisson
  • Sólstafir — Ótta (Season of Mist, 2014): strings alongside the other three Amiina members; produced and mixed by Birgir Jón Birgisson
  • Borko — Born To Be Free (Kimi Records / Sound Of A Handshake, 2012): viola

Departure from Amiina and Post-2015 Work

Both Edda and Hildur Ársælsdóttir left Amiina in 2015, per the Discogs profile note. The remaining lineup (María Huld, Sólrún, Magnús Trygvason Eliassen, Kippi Kaninus) continued as Amiina.

Despite the departure, Edda and Hildur both appear as featured guests on track A1 of Fantômas (Mengi, 2016), Amiina’s double-LP soundtrack to the 1913 silent film serial by Louis Feuillade. The record was mixed and edited by Gunnar Örn Tynes, Silli Geirdal, and Ívar Ragnarsson alongside Amiina.

Post-Amiina, her Discogs credits show continued activity as a session player:

  • Throws — Throws (Thrill Jockey, 2016): “Strings [String Quartet]” with Hildur, María Huld, and Sólrún — the four former Amiina string players reuniting as a unit for hire
  • Alex Somers — Here We Are: Notes For Living On Planet Earth (Lakeshore Records, 2020): viola; also listed alongside Amiina as a credited ensemble
  • Alex Somers — Siblings (Krúnk, 2021): viola; same pattern, working with Sólrún, Hildur, and María Huld again

The Siblings credit is notable: released on Krúnk in 2021, it places all four ex-Amiina string players together in the same ensemble context even six years after the official departure, suggesting the “Quartet Iceland” unit continues operating informally for sessions regardless of the Amiina name.
