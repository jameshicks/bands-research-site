Johanna Went

Discogs ID: 149537
Active: c. 1979–present
Base: Los Angeles, California
Name variations: J. Went, Johanna, Johanna Went’s Box, Went

LA-based performance artist and musician. One of the most extreme figures in early-1980s LA underground, known for visceral, confrontational live shows involving props, costumes, animal organs, and chaotic theatrical staging. Documented on two releases from 1981–82 and collected on the 2007 Soleilmoon retrospective Club Years.

Biography

Origins and Early LA Underground (c. 1979–1981)

Went emerged from the Los Angeles experimental/punk underground in the late 1970s, developing a performance practice that sat outside the standard band format. Her shows drew on elements of body art, noise, and spectacle — widely described as involving raw meat, elaborate props, and physically intense stage behavior. She performed at key LA venues including Club Lingerie, the Starwood, and On Klub, placing her squarely in the same scene as early hardcore, No Wave–adjacent art-noise acts, and the LA free jazz/experimental community.

Her core collaborators from this period were the Wheaton brothers: Mark Wheaton (synth, piano, tape, engineer, co-producer) and Brock Wheaton (drums), who appear on every release. The guitarist Kerry McBride plays on the first 7-inch and appears in early live footage.

Recordings (1981–1982)

Her debut 7-inch, Slave Beyond The Grave / No U No (Graybeat Records, 1981), was self-produced with Mark Wheaton. The front cover photograph was shot by Ed Colver, the LA hardcore scene’s signature photographer. The two tracks are co-written by Went, Mark Wheaton, and Brock Wheaton, with Kerry McBride on guitar.

The follow-up LP Hyena (Posh Boy, 1982) expanded the lineup significantly — Karl Precoda (then of The Dream Syndicate) plays bass on four tracks, Greg Burk (2) (saxophone) and Jeff McLane (Chapman Stick, later engineering duties) join the core group. Multiple performers contribute backing vocals and percussion track-by-track: Crystal Cross (violin, percussion, vocals), Evan Sebor (guitar), Keith Mitchell (3) (drums/percussion), Andre Champagne (percussion), Aldo Hernandez (3) and Killer (8) (backing vocals). Went and Mark Wheaton co-composed all music; Stan Ross mastered. The LP was released on Posh Boy (cat# PBS 139) — label best known for early LA hardcore (Circle Jerks, TSOL, Adolescents) but also home to outsider material.

A second pressing of Hyena exists and the release has a Discogs master (1790179).

Live and Multimedia Work (c. 1980–1986)

Several live performances were captured on video during this period:

  • Live at UCLA (filmed by Shirley Clarke, the New American Cinema filmmaker)
  • The Box (scored by Mark Wheaton and Joseph Berardi; features Hans Christian on bass/cello and Jonathan Gold on cello — the future Pulitzer Prize–winning food/music critic)
  • New Wave Theater (Peter Ivers’s public access TV show)
  • Knifeboxing and Primate Prisoners (the latter featuring Annie Iobst and Lucy Sexton of Dancenoise)
  • On Klub and Hollywood Central Theater (the latter with Z’EV on percussion and Paul M. Young on Serge Modular synthesizer)
  • John’s Place
  • A 21-minute live recording from The Star System, Phoenix, Arizona, 1980

These videos were collected on the DVD component of Club Years (2007).

Club Years Retrospective (Soleilmoon, 2007)

Club Years (Soleilmoon SDVD 6, 2007) is a CD+DVD compilation/retrospective collecting the bulk of her documented work. The CD contains remastered versions of both 7-inch sides and all of Hyena, plus additional tracks (“Benny’s Nightmare,” “The Box,” “Bad End,” “The Worm,” and the 21-minute Phoenix live recording). Mark Wheaton handled remix and remastering throughout. The DVD collects 8 video documents with audio commentary by Went and Wheaton.

Additional performers on the CD tracks not previously credited: Hans Christian (bass, CD-10/11), Steve Brenner (engineer, CD-10/12), Robin Ryan (drums, CD-12), The Hot Chilean (guitar, CD-12), John Dentino (keyboards, CD-12), and the group Strong Silent Types (backing vocals/percussion, Hyena-era tracks).

Design for Club Years was by Liz Young (credited as Liz Y). Cover photo by Lynda Burdick. Video editing by Bob Klonarides and Mark Wheaton.
