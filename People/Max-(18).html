Overview

Max (18) is the organ player and a founding member of Band of Holy Joy, the South London post-punk collective whose roots reach back to 1983. Their recorded credits are concentrated in the 1980s, spread across a loose network of independent imprints — Ministry of Power, What’s So Funny About.., Flim Flam Productions — alongside occasional major-label appearances on Columbia. Beyond the band’s own indie-rock orbit, Max (18) accumulated an unusually large number of shared credits with Test Dept., pointing to crossover between Band of Holy Joy’s milieu and the South London industrial underground.

Notes (web)

Real name

Max (18) is Max Davies, Johny Brown’s sister. She is identified by name in multiple sources as one of the band’s co-founders alongside Johny Brown, Brett Turnbull, and photographers Big John Jenkins and Martine Thoquenne. (Louder Than War, 40th anniversary piece; Transpontine blog, December 2008)

Band of Holy Joy / Test Dept. collaboration — nature and extent

The shared Discogs credits reflect Max Davies’s membership in Band Of Holy Joy during the same period that Test Dept. were active contemporaries, not a formal recording collaboration between the two bands.

Shared living, not a shared project. In the mid-1980s, Band of Holy Joy and Test Dept. were housemates in a squat at Nettleton Road, New Cross, South London. Johny Brown described it as “chalk and cheese”: Test Dept. were “incredibly masculine… polemic… in training upstairs being militant,” while Band of Holy Joy were “downstairs on drugs writing these really weird dirges… extremely feminine… about romance.” (Transpontine, December 2008)

Live touring — Test Dept. brought Band of Holy Joy on the road. Test Dept. took Band of Holy Joy out on tour with them, resulting in very early live performances in Manchester, Sheffield, and Retford, circa 1983–1984. Max Davies was a founding and performing member at this time, so she was present for those dates. (Search snippet / Transpontine; Louder Than War)

No joint studio recordings documented. No source identifies a recording that credited both bands jointly or Max Davies working in-studio with Test Dept. members. The shared Discogs credits almost certainly reflect separate releases by each act on overlapping indie imprints (Ministry of Power, Some Bizzare) rather than collaborative recordings.

Departure. Max Davies left Band of Holy Joy after their live tours of 1987. (Louder Than War)
