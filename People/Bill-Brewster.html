Overview

Bill Brewster (born Nigel Peter Brewster) is an English DJ, writer, and music historian, best known as co-author (with Frank Broughton) of Last Night A DJ Saved My Life (1999, revised 2006), widely regarded as the definitive history of the disc jockey. His Discogs credits span 1995–2025, encompassing liner notes for over 50 releases, DJ compilations, label management, production work, and performance across multiple group projects.

Brewster ran the New York office of DMC for two years in the 1990s and co-founded several labels with Broughton, including DJhistory.com and Sugar Daddy Records (New York). In the UK he was involved with Twisted United Kingdom and Forensic Records. As a DJ and compiler his most substantial primary release is Praxis (2002, Hooj Choons), a double-disc compilation/mix, followed by the three-volume After Dark series (2013–2015) and After Dark (Vespertine) (2023) on LateNightTales. He has also been active as a producer and arranger under group names including The Popular People’s Front, Fat Camp, and World Discothéque Movement.
