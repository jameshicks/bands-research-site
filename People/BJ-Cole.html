BJ Cole

Discogs ID: 61104 Real name: Brian John Cole Born: 17 June 1946, Enfield, Middlesex, England Instrument: Pedal steel guitar (also dobro, lap steel, resonator guitar, cello)

British pedal steel guitarist; one of the most recorded session players in UK history, active from the late 1960s through the 2020s. His range spans early-70s folk-rock, glam, country-tinged pop, post-punk, drum and bass, ambient, and experimental composition.

Biography

Early Career and Cochise (1969–1972)

Cole came up in the British session world at the tail end of the 1960s. His earliest Discogs credit is pedal steel on Country Meets Folk (Beacon, 1969). He quickly embedded himself in the emerging UK folk-rock circuit, playing on sessions for artists as various as Elton John (Madman Across The Water, 1971 — steel guitar on “Tiny Dancer”), Nazareth (self-titled debut, 1971), Humble Pie (self-titled, 1970), and Roger Daltrey’s first solo record (Daltrey, 1973).

From 1970 he was a founding member and key instrumentalist in Cochise (7), a UK rock band on United Artists Records. Their self-titled 1970 debut LP captures Cole playing dobro, guitar, pedal steel, cello, and steel guitar across the full record. Other Cochise members included Rick Wills, Mick Grabham, Stuart Brown (7), John Wilson (97), and Willie Wilson.

Solo Work and The New Hovering Dog (1972)

In 1972, still signed to United Artists, Cole put out two records under his own name and the alias The New Hovering Dog:

  • The New Hovering Dog (1972, United Artists) — his debut LP under his own name (credited to “B.J. Cole”). Cole produced and played pedal steel, dobro, cheng (Chinese zither percussion), synthesizer, and vocals. Session cast included Danny Thompson (double bass), Michael Giles (drums), Robert Kirby (arrangements, piano), Graham Preskett (electric violin), Francis Monkman (harpsichord), Tristan Fry (percussion), and Crispian Steele-Perkins (trumpet).

  • Up On The Hill Where They Do The Boogie (1972, United Artists) — a second record released the same year, credited to “The New Hovering Dog” (one of Cole’s aliases). Cole produced and played pedal steel; Robert Kirby arranged and played piano.

These early records show Cole as a composer/producer rather than pure session hand — they draw on British folk, country, and early ambient tendencies simultaneously.

Heavy Session Years (1972–1985)

Through the 1970s Cole was in constant demand as a session player. Key credits from this period:

  • Marc Bolan / T. Rex — Zinc Alloy And The Hidden Riders Of Tomorrow (1974), a glam-rock landmark; Cole credited as “Performer” alongside Danny Thompson, Gloria Jones, and Mickey Finn.
  • Elton John — pedal steel on Madman Across The Water (1971).
  • Procol Harum — Exotic Birds And Fruit (1974, Chrysalis), pedal steel.
  • Joan Armatrading — self-titled 1976 A&M debut, steel guitar.
  • Kiki Dee — I’ve Got The Music In Me (1974, Rocket) and Loving And Free (1973), steel guitar.
  • The Alan Parsons Project — I Robot (1977, Arista), steel guitar. One of the most-pressed credits in his discography.
  • Cat Stevens — Foreigner / related sessions (1973).
  • Roger Daltrey — Daltrey (1973, Track Record), steel guitar.
  • Gerry Rafferty — City To City (1978, United Artists), steel guitar and dobro.
  • Shakin’ Stevens — multiple Epic singles (1980–1983); Cole was also producer on Get Shakin’ (1981) and You Drive Me Crazy (1982).
  • Paul Young — The Secret Of Association (1985, CBS), pedal steel; a top-selling UK LP.
  • Everything But The Girl — Love Not Money (1985, Blanco Y Negro), pedal steel.
  • Deacon Blue — Raintown (1988), pedal steel.
  • Bill Nelson — Quit Dreaming And Get On The Beam (adjacent era, see below).

He also produced and played on Hank Wangford’s self-titled 1980 debut (Cow Pie), and was a member of The Hank Wangford Band, a UK country-oriented group with which he maintained a long-term association.

Transparent Music and 1989 Pivot

Transparent Music (1989, Hannibal Records) is Cole’s most significant solo statement of the era. He produced and played pedal steel across a set of nine pieces that merge ambient, jazz-inflected, and new-age textures. Key contributors:

Hannibal was run by Joe Boyd and gave the record strong distribution in the UK and US.

Drum and Bass Crossover: Luke Vibert Collaborations (1999–2000)

In the late 1990s Cole formed an unlikely but productive partnership with Luke Vibert, the Devon-born electronic producer behind Wagon Christ, Plug, and related aliases. Together they released:

  • Drum’n’Bass’n’Steel (1999, Law & Auder) — co-primary credit, no additional credits listed beyond the two main artists.
  • Stop The Panic (1999/2000, Cooking Vinyl / Law & Auder) — the more fully realised album. Multiple pressings including a Japanese edition on Beat Records. Features collaborators including Bobby Valentino (violin), Ben Davies, Martin Giles, Jeremy Simmonds, Andy Hamill, Tom Jenkinson (Squarepusher), and Holly Penfield.
  • Spring Collection (2000, Cooking Vinyl) — Cole co-primary credit with Vibert.

These records placed Cole’s pedal steel inside breakbeat and liquid drum and bass frameworks and brought him to a new audience. The Stop The Panic LP in particular became a cult document of late-90s UK electronic music.

2000s Sessions

Cole continued in high demand through the 2000s:

  • Spiritualized — Ladies And Gentlemen We Are Floating In Space (1997, Arista/Dedicated), pedal steel. One of the era’s most acclaimed UK albums.
  • Depeche Mode — Ultra (1997, Mute/RCA), pedal steel. A surprise credit.
  • R.E.M. — Additional musician on BBC sessions compiled on R.E.M. At The BBC (2018, Craft Recordings) and The Best Of R.E.M. At The BBC.
  • Tom Jones — Praise & Blame (2010, Island), steel guitar.
  • Trouble In Paradise (2004, Cooking Vinyl) — a solo release.

Norwegian Collaboration: 1982 + BJ Cole (2012)

1982 + BJ Cole (2012, Hubro) is a full album collaboration with the Norwegian group 1982 (2). Cole plays pedal steel throughout; the ensemble includes Nils Økland (Hardanger fiddle, violin), Sigbjørn Apeland (harmonium), Øyvind Skarbø (drums), and Morten Lund (mastering). Hubro is the prestige Norwegian jazz/experimental imprint; the record places Cole in a Nordic folk-chamber context.

Apollo and Other Ensemble Work (2012)

Apollo (2012, Cantaloupe Music) — a concert arrangement of Brian Eno’s Apollo: Atmospheres & Soundscapes album, performed by Icebreaker (5), the British new-music ensemble, with BJ Cole as co-billed performer. Composers credited: Brian Eno, Daniel Lanois, and Roger Eno. This sits alongside the Hannibal/ambient lineage and signals Cole’s ongoing connection to ambient-adjacent work.

Shebang and Experimental Contexts (2022)

Shebang (Oren Ambarchi, Drag City, 2022) — Cole plays pedal steel on tracks A2 and B2 and is credited for additional recording. The record’s ensemble also includes Julia Reidy (12-string acoustic guitar, tracks B1/B2), Chris Abrahams (piano), Jim O’Rourke (synth), Johan Berthling (acoustic bass), Sam Dunscombe (bass clarinet), and Joe Talia (mix, mastering). Design by Stephen O’Malley. The Shebang session places Cole inside the extended Oren Ambarchi circle, bridging his deep session history with contemporary experimental music.

Recent Work (2022–2025)

Cole remained active into the mid-2020s:

  • Red Sunset Dreams (2022, Sonic Cathedral) — pedal steel and co-writer credit.
  • San Vito (2024, Little World Records) — pedal steel.
  • Time Machine (2024, Esoteric Antenna) — pedal steel.
  • Angebot Und Nachfrage (2025, Meadow Lake Music, Germany) — pedal steel.

Connections

  • Oren Ambarchi — session contribution to Shebang (2022, Drag City); connects Cole to the contemporary experimental/improv world
  • Julia Reidy — co-contributor on Shebang
  • Luke Vibert — most substantial solo-record collaboration; crossover between UK session tradition and electronic music
  • Danny Thompson — recurs across decades; both were central UK session players of the 70s
  • Brian Eno — connected via the Apollo arrangement; Eno’s ambient register and Cole’s pedal steel find common territory
  • Spiritualized — Ladies And Gentlemen We Are Floating In Space is one of the UK’s most loved 90s albums; Cole’s pedal steel is part of its sound
  • Robert Kirby — orchestrator and arranger on Cole’s 1972 solo records; Kirby was also Nick Drake’s arranger
  • Scott Walker — Cole’s Hawaiian pedal steel appears on Bish Bosch (2012, 4AD), Walker’s most extreme studio work
