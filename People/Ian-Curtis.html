Ian Curtis

Lead vocalist and primary lyricist of Joy Division. Born Ian Kevin Curtis, 15 July 1956, Macclesfield, Cheshire, England. Died 18 May 1980, Macclesfield, by suicide, age 23.

Discogs artist ID: 236149

Biography

Early Life and Warsaw (1976–1977)

Curtis grew up in Macclesfield and moved to Manchester in his late teens. He worked at Rare Records in Manchester before taking civil service positions — first at Manpower Services Commission in Manchester, then at the Mansfield Job Centre helping disabled people find work. His day-job stability ran alongside his musical ambitions throughout the Joy Division years.

He formed the group that would become Joy Division under the name Warsaw (3) in 1977 alongside Bernard Sumner (guitar), Peter Hook (bass), and briefly Steve Brotherdale on drums before Stephen Morris joined. The band played under the Warsaw name through 1977 before renaming to Joy Division. An early demo session was recorded at Pennine Sound Studios; these recordings were later collected on The Ideal Beginning (Anonymous Records, 1981) and the Warsaw compilation.

Prior to Warsaw, Curtis had a still earlier outfit — credited on Discogs as Stiff Kittens (2) — which represents the very first incarnation of the group.

Joy Division: Rise (1978–1979)

Joy Division signed to Factory Records — Tony Wilson’s newly founded Manchester label — and recorded their debut EP An Ideal For Living (1978, Enigma/Anonymous Records ANON1, pre-Factory) with Paul Adshead engineering. The lineup on this release: Curtis (vocals), Peter Hook (bass), Stephen Morris (drums), Bernard Sumner (guitar, credited as Bernard Albrecht).

Their debut album Unknown Pleasures (Factory FACT 10, 1979) was produced by Martin Hannett, engineered by Chris Nagle, with sleeve design by Peter Saville. Curtis wrote all lyrics; the band wrote the music collectively. Hannett’s production — treating each instrument with layers of effects and space — became as definitive as Curtis’s vocal delivery. Unknown Pleasures established Joy Division’s international profile.

The single “Transmission” (FAC 13, 1979) and a John Peel session (recorded at BBC; released commercially on Strange Fruit, 1986) documented the live band. Curtis played guitar on the Peel sessions in addition to vocals.

Curtis’s epilepsy was first diagnosed during this period, around 1978. The condition worsened across 1979–80, exacerbated by medication side effects (phenobarbitone) and the physical demands of performing — his on-stage convulsive dancing could not always be distinguished from a seizure.

Joy Division: Final Phase (1980)

Closer (Factory FACT 25, 1980) was recorded at Britannia Row Studios and produced again by Martin Hannett, with engineering by Jon Caffery and Martin Hannett, and design by Peter Saville and Martyn Atkins. The sleeve photograph was by Bernard Pierre Wolff. Released posthumously in July 1980, Closer is regarded as Curtis’s most fully realized lyrical statement — its themes of alienation, dissolution, and mortality in direct correspondence with his deteriorating mental and physical state.

“Love Will Tear Us Apart” (FAC 23, 1980) — produced by Martin Hannett — was the band’s commercial breakthrough single and became one of the most-pressed Factory releases. Recorded in early 1980; released posthumously. Sleeve designed by Peter Saville and Ben Kelly (uncredited).

“Atmosphere” (FACUS 2, 1980) was originally released on the French label Sordide Sentimental in a limited edition; later issued by Factory.

“Komakino” (FAC 28, 1980) was a free flexi-disc given away with the Factory Fact sheet.

Curtis also produced — uncredited — the Section 25 single “Girls Don’t Count” (FAC 18, 1980) alongside Rob Gretton. This is one of only two known production credits outside Joy Division.

Death

Curtis died on 18 May 1980 in his Macclesfield home, the night before Joy Division were due to depart for their first North American tour. He was 23. The surviving members — Bernard Sumner, Peter Hook, and Stephen Morris — formed New Order with the addition of Gillian Gilbert.

Posthumous Legacy

Curtis’s lyrics continued generating royalties and songwriting credits for decades after his death — across tribute albums, remixes, and cover versions including Therapy?’s Troublegum (1994), Moby’s “Feeling So Real” (Mute, 1994), and numerous others documented in the Discogs credits list. The Still double album (Factory FACT 40, 1981) collected live recordings and outtakes. Substance (1988) gathered the singles. Heart and Soul (London Records, 1997) was the comprehensive 4-CD box set.

Deborah Curtis’s memoir Touching From A Distance (1995) remains the primary biographical source. Anton Corbijn’s film Control (2007) is based on Deborah’s book.
