Debbi Peterson

Full name: Deborah Mary Peterson Born: 22 August 1961, Northridge, Los Angeles, California Role: Drummer, vocalist, songwriter, producer Discogs artist ID: 452205

American drummer, vocalist, and songwriter. Co-founder and drummer of The Bangles, and sister of Vicki Peterson. One of the primary creative architects of the Bangles from their 1981 formation through the 2003 reunion album Doll Revolution. Post-Bangles, she has worked as a producer and continued performing across multiple side projects and guest appearances.

Biography

Origins and Early Recordings (1981–1982)

Debbi Peterson co-founded The Bangles in Los Angeles in 1981 alongside her sister Vicki Peterson and Susanna Hoffs. The band initially performed as The Bangs, and their earliest document in Discogs is the 1981 single “Getting Out of Hand” (Down Kiddie Records), on which Debbi is credited for vocals, drums, and bass — the band’s lineup was still fluid at this stage, with all three founding members multi-tracking across instruments. Engineer on the session was Ethan James; photographs were by David Roback (later the Rain Parade guitarist and Mazzy Star co-founder).

In 1982 the band released The Bangs EP (Faulty Products), produced by Craig Leon — their debut proper. Debbi is credited for drums, backing vocals, and written-by on tracks A2 and B1, making her one of the band’s primary early songwriters alongside Hoffs and Vicki Peterson. Original bassist Annette Zilinskas played bass and harmonica. The lineup — Hoffs on rhythm guitar, Vicki on lead guitar, Zilinskas on bass, and Debbi on drums — was the founding four-piece.

Classic Era: The Bangles on Columbia (1984–1989)

Annette Zilinskas departed in 1983, replaced by Michael Steele. The new lineup signed to Columbia Records and recorded All Over The Place (1984), produced by David Kahne. Debbi played drums and sang; Kahne also engineered the sessions, with Joe Chiccarelli mixing. Songwriting on the album was concentrated in Hoffs and Vicki Peterson, though Debbi’s drum work throughout is central to the band’s jangle-rock sound.

Different Light (1985/1986), again produced by Kahne, became their commercial breakthrough — reaching #2 on the US Billboard 200. It produced “Walk Like An Egyptian” (US #1, on which Debbi holds a written-by credit on the Discogs single release) and “Manic Monday.” Engineers on the album included Tchad Blake and Peggy McLeonard; David Leonard handled the mix.

The third Columbia album, Everything (1988), marked a change in producer: Davitt Sigerson took over from Kahne. Debbi played drums and percussion and sang. The record featured a large supporting cast of session players: Phil Shenale (keyboards/programming), David Lindley (lute, dobro, bouzouki, classical guitar), Paulinho Da Costa (percussion), Tommy Morgan (harmonica), and Jim Snodgrass (tabla). Mixing was by Frank Filipetti; mastering by Doug Sax. “Eternal Flame” (co-written by Hoffs with Tom Kelly and Billy Steinberg) reached US #1. The band dissolved following the Everything cycle, with tensions around Hoffs’s solo profile a contributing factor.

The Interregnum: Kindred Spirit (3) and The Harmony Pigs (1992–1998)

During the Bangles’ hiatus, Debbi formed two side projects alongside vocalist Siobhan Maher and, in the first group, Go-Go’s drummer Gina Schock.

Kindred Spirit (3) (Discogs ID: 1023254) — a three-piece of Debbi Peterson, Gina Schock, and Siobhan Maher — released two records on I.R.S. Records: the single “Here In My Eyes” (1992) and the full album Kindred Spirit (1994). Debbi co-produced both releases alongside Siobhan Maher; the single was co-produced with Christian Nesmith (who also engineered and played guitar on the album). The album featured orchestral arrangements by Phil Shenale, session guitarists including Michael Thompson and Hank Linderman, and mixing by Ray Kennedy. Debbi’s producing role here — credited alongside Maher on the full LP — marks her formal emergence as a record producer.

The Harmony Pigs (Discogs ID: 1155939) — a duo of Debbi Peterson and Siobhan Maher — provided background vocals on the Kindred Spirit LP, making it a satellite project to the main band rather than a separately released act.

A 1998 Sony compilation In Your Room also appeared during this period, collecting Bangles material.

Reunion: Doll Revolution (2003)

The Bangles reunited in the late 1990s and recorded Doll Revolution (2003), released on the band’s own Down Kiddie Records imprint (Canada) and Liberty EMI (UK). The album was self-produced by The Bangles alongside engineer/mixer Brad Wood. Debbi played drums, percussion, acoustic guitar, and sang — an expanded instrumental role compared to earlier albums. Additional players included Peter Holsapple (accordion, keyboards, mandolin), Greg Leisz (lap steel, pedal steel), and Dillon O’Brian (electric guitar, Wurlitzer). A “Bangles Boys Choir” featuring Dave Grohl, Chick Wolverton, and John Crooke appeared on at least one track.

The Minus 5 and Later Guest Work (2012–present)

Debbi has been a credited member of The Minus 5 (Discogs ID: 288329), the rotating collective led by Scott McCaughey and featuring a rotating cast including Peter Buck (R.E.M.), Ken Stringfellow (Posies), Glenn Kotche (Wilco), William Rieflin, and others. She appeared on the 2025 Minus 5 album Oar On, Penelope! (Yep Roc Records) in a band and vocals capacity.

Other later credits include:

  • Guest appearance on Elvis Costello’s The Return Of The Spectacular Spinning Songbook!!! tour (2012, Hip-O Records/UMe)
  • Guest background vocals on Fanny’s Fanny Walked The Earth (2018, Blue Élan Records)
  • Backing vocals on the Empty Hearts / Wicked Cool single “It’s Christmastime” (2019)
  • Harmony vocals and lead vocals on the project My Best Evil Friend (Yep Roc, 2023)
  • Liner notes for the Omnivore Recordings release Complete Third (2022)
  • Performer on Matthew Sweet’s Tomorrow Forever (2017, Honeycomb Hideout), alongside Gary Louris, Ric Menck, Rod Argent, and others

She is also credited on two Bangles archival and live releases: Ladies And Gentlemen… The Bangles! (2016, Omnivore Recordings) and The Ritz New York ‘84 (2017, Echoes).
