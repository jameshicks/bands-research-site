Robert Whatley

Robert Whatley

Oct 28, 20268 min read

Robert Whatley

Drummer, programmer, and co-songwriter. Discogs artist ID: 563207. Also credited as “Rob Whatley,” “Robert Whateley,” and “Whatley.” Core member of Disco Inferno from formation (1989) through dissolution (1995–96). Post-Disco Inferno: co-founded Floorshow with Ian Crause; later co-founded Transformer (3) with Paul Willmott.

Biography

Disco Inferno: formation (1989–1991)

Robert Whatley was a founding member of Disco Inferno, formed in Essex in January 1989, initially as a four-piece alongside Ian Crause (guitar, vocals), Paul Willmott (bass), and keyboardist Daniel Gish. The quartet was teenage at formation. Gish departed around 1991–92, subsequently joining Bark Psychosis and later Boymerang; the remaining trio — Crause, Willmott, and Whatley — became the band’s fixed lineup.

Whatley’s role throughout was drums and drum programming. He is credited as a co-songwriter on every Disco Inferno release, confirming that compositional credit was shared equally across the trio rather than centralised around Crause. On Summer’s Last Sound (1992), his Discogs credit reads “Drum Programming [Drum Triggers], Programmed By [Bass Patterns], Songwriter [All Songs Written By]” — a precise description of the band’s core technique: drum pads triggering pre-loaded environmental samples in real time, functioning both as percussion and as melodic/textural events.

Crause developed the approach by running his guitar through a Roland S-750 sampler and spending approximately six months programming toward the new sound before Summer’s Last Sound. Whatley applied the same principle on drum pads, so neither instrument functioned in its conventional role: guitar triggered samples, pads triggered different samples, and Willmott’s bass grounded the texture acoustically. The samples themselves were sourced from environmental recordings — running water, wind, boots, car crashes, angry voices — rather than existing music.

ché Records period (1991–1992)

The band’s first releases — Entertainment (1991, 7”), Open Doors • Closed Windows (1991, LP), and Science (1991, 12” EP) — were on ché Records, a small independent label. Charlie McIntosh produced and engineered Entertainment and co-produced the subsequent releases. Richard Charlton co-produced Science and In Debt (1992), the latter a compilation drawing on the ché catalogue. Artwork across the ché period was handled by Cactus Design.

Whatley holds primary-artist credit (no specific role listed in Discogs) on all ché releases, consistent with the band operating as a collective unit with shared songwriting credit.

Cheree Records: the technique consolidated (1992)

Summer’s Last Sound (1992, Cheree Records, CHEREE 28 CD) is the release that most explicitly documents Whatley’s function. Two tracks — “Summer’s Last Sound” (5:43) and “Love Stepping Out” (6:21) — with the following personnel:

Rough Trade years (1993–1995)

Disco Inferno moved to Rough Trade for their most celebrated run. Whatley holds primary-artist and co-songwriter credits on every release.

A Rock To Cling To / From The Devil To The Deep Blue Sky (1993, R2983) — debut Rough Trade release. Produced and engineered by Charlie McIntosh. The nine-minute “From The Devil To The Deep Blue Sky” became one of their most-referenced pieces.

The Last Dance (1993, R3030) — 12” EP. The title track and “The Long Dance” produced by Michael Johnson, engineered by John A. Rivers; “D.I. Go Pop” and “Scattered Showers” produced by Charlie McIntosh.

D. I. Go Pop (1994, R3072) — debut full-length album. Eight tracks, all written by Disco Inferno. Produced by Charlie McIntosh. Design by Fuel (9); photography by David Spero (2). Tracklist: “In Sharky Water” / “New Clothes For The New World” / “Starbound: All Burnt Out & Nowhere To Go” / “A Crash At Every Speed” / “Even The Sea Sides Against Us” / “Next Year” / “A Whole Wide World Ahead” / “Footprints In Snow.”

Second Language (1994, R3153) — four-track CD EP. Produced by John Coxon; engineered by Mads Bjerke. Tracks: “Second Language” / “The Atheist’s Burden” / “At The End Of The Line” / “A Little Something.”

It’s A Kid’s World (1994, R3353) — three-track CD EP. Self-produced (the band). Tracks: “It’s A Kid’s World” / “A Night On The Tiles” / “Lost In Fog.”

Technicolour (1995) — second and final studio album. Recorded and mixed by John Coxon and Mads Bjerke; produced by Disco Inferno. Nine tracks. Whatley is credited Written-By, Producer, Performer — the self-production role confirms the band had taken full control of the recording process by this point. Released posthumously in 1996 after the band dissolved.

BBC sessions

Two documented BBC sessions exist:

  1. “Mixing It” session — recorded 19 December 1994 at Maida Vale Studios for BBC Radio 3’s Mixing It. Six all-animal-named instrumental tracks: “Shark” / “Elephant” / “Tortoise” / “Shrew” / “Bird” / “Rats.” Produced by Philip Tagney. Commercially released 13 September 1999 on Tugboat Records as The Mixing It Session (Tug CD 011). Whatley is credited as performer (as “Rob Whatley”).

  2. Second session, 27 March 1995 — tracks: “It’s A Kid’s World,” “Technicolour,” “When The Story Breaks,” “Don’t You Know.” The specific BBC programme has not been confirmed in any available source.

Dissolution and Floorshow (1995–1996)

Disco Inferno split acrimoniously in 1995, while or just after recording Technicolour. The rift was primarily between Crause and Willmott — not between Crause and Whatley. Crause had proposed an arrangement whereby he would record alone with Willmott and Whatley adding parts independently afterwards, which Willmott rejected. Financial pressure, years of commercial failure despite critical acclaim, and (in some accounts) equipment theft had also taken their toll.

Whatley was not a target of the acrimony. He and Crause moved together to form Floorshow immediately after the split, recording an album also titled The Vertical Axis. That album was shelved by a label — Crause later described the sessions as “catastrophic” and referred to “a major label” without naming it. No label name has been confirmed in any public source. Tracks from the Floorshow sessions were subsequently salvaged by Crause for his solo singles Elemental (2000, Tugboat Records) and Head Over Heels (2001, Acuarela), both featuring drummer Ritchie Thomas (Dif Juz, The Jesus and Mary Chain) rather than Whatley.

Transformer (3) (c.1996)

Following the Floorshow period, Whatley co-founded Transformer (3) with Paul Willmott, with additional members George Chesterton and Lee Walker. No releases under the Transformer (3) name appear in the Discogs database. Walker’s principal solo project, The Mellowtrons, released Evacuate (1996, Chill Out Label) with Willmott as co-producer on three tracks, suggesting the Transformer (3) circle was active as a collaborative unit in 1996 even without formal releases.

Whatley’s precise instrument and contribution within Transformer (3) is not documented in Discogs beyond his listed membership.

Later

Whatley appeared as performer on The Mixing It Session (1999), released on Tugboat Records — the posthumous commercial issue of the 1994 BBC session. This is his last documented credit in Discogs.

Disco Inferno’s The 5 EPs (2011, One Little Indian, TPLP1082CD) — a remastered compilation of the five Rough Trade EPs — credits Whatley as “Composed By” alongside Crause and Willmott. Compiled by Ned Raggett and Brian Macdonald (5); liner notes by David Howell (2).
