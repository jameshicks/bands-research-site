Kamasi Washington

American jazz saxophonist, composer, arranger, and bandleader born February 18, 1981 in Los Angeles, California. Full name: Kamasi Tii Washington. Son of jazz flautist Rickey Washington. Central figure in the 2010s West Coast jazz renaissance; best known for his 2015 triple album The Epic on Brainfeeder. Discogs artist ID: 324293.

Name variations on record: Camasi Washington, K. Washington, KW, Kamashi Washington, カマシ・ワシントン.

Biography

Early life and apprenticeship (1981–2011)

Washington grew up in Los Angeles and came up through the city’s jazz education infrastructure. His father, Rickey Washington (Discogs ID: 1073779), is a jazz flautist and saxophonist active since the 1970s with groups including Raw Soul Express; Rickey appears on several of Kamasi’s own records (flute on Heaven And Earth, Harmony Of Difference, tenor saxophone on Heaven And Earth disc 3). The family connection is a recurring thread across the catalog.

Washington became a member of Gerald Wilson Orchestra (Discogs ID: 341422), one of Los Angeles’s foundational big-band institutions. He can be heard on the orchestra’s 2007 75th Birthday Bash Live! (Blue Note) and other Wilson releases. This apprenticeship placed him in a lineage of West Coast big-band jazz stretching back decades.

He also participated in the Young Jazz Giants collective (Discogs ID: 2044296), a loose LA group that included future collaborators Stephen Bruner (Thundercat), Ryan Porter, Cameron Graves, Miles Mosley, Ronald Bruner Jr., and others. A Young Jazz Giants live recording exists on Birdman Records (2004). This circle of musicians would eventually become the core band on all of Washington’s major records.

Other early group affiliations: The Gathering (4) (Discogs ID: 1942980), The Next Step (5) (Discogs ID: 3616203), and Throttle Elevator Music (Discogs ID: 4551953) — a jazz-funk unit led by Gregory Howe that released self-titled material on Wide Hive Records in 2012. The Next Step released a live album, Live At 5th Street Dick’s and The Proclamation, both key early Washington documents.

Early session work includes tenor saxophone on We Are Scientists’ Brain Thrust Mastery (Virgin, 2008) and percussion/horns on Broken Bells’ After The Disco (Columbia/Sony, 2014), demonstrating crossover reach before his breakthrough.

Breakthrough: The Epic and the Brainfeeder connection (2012–2016)

Washington’s relationship with Flying Lotus (Steven Ellison, Discogs ID: 365248) and Brainfeeder proved the catalyst for his international profile. Flying Lotus is a Los Angeles producer, Warp Records artist, and Alice Coltrane’s great-nephew — his own music orbits the same spiritual-jazz and cosmic-soul territory as Washington’s.

The Epic (Brainfeeder, 2015; cat. BFDNL050; master ID: 837501) is a 17-track triple album running approximately three hours. It was recorded in a single five-day session. The album draws on Coltrane-influenced extended improvisation, orchestral string arrangements, choir, and R&B-tinged vocals — a broad synthesis that was received as a statement about the vitality and ambition of contemporary jazz. Genres credited: Funk/Soul, Jazz; styles: Contemporary Jazz, Fusion, Psychedelic, Soul-Jazz.

Also in 2015, Washington contributed string arrangements to Kendrick Lamar’s To Pimp A Butterfly (Aftermath/Interscope/Top Dawg, 2015; master ID: 810214) — working alongside Terrace Martin and Sounwave on string arrangements. This credit connected him to the most commercially visible strand of the West Coast neo-soul/rap renaissance.

The Gaslamp Killer Experience: Live In Los Angeles (2015) documents a session with DJ/producer The Gaslamp Killer; Washington is credited on tenor saxophone.

Harmony Of Difference and Truth EPs (2017)

Two releases on Young Turks in 2017 preceded Heaven And Earth.

Truth (Young Turks, digital single, 2017; master ID: 1164532; release ID: 10124082) is a single 13:30 track. The full core band appears — Cameron Graves, Miles Mosley, Ronald Bruner Jr., Tony Austin, Brandon Coleman, Ryan Porter, Dontae Winslow — augmented by Thundercat (electric bass), Terrace Martin (alto saxophone), Rickey Washington (flute), Nick Mancini (vibraphone), string quartet (Paul Cartwright, Jen Simone, Molly Rogers, Andrea Whitt, Chris Woods (2), Tylana Renga), and a choir: Dawn Norfleet, Dexter Story, Dustin Warren, Jimetta Rose, Mashica Winslow, Patrice Quinn, Steven Wayne (3), Taylor Graves, Thalma de Freitas. Engineer: Russell Elevado. Mastered by Daddy Kev.

Harmony Of Difference (Young Turks, EP, 2017; cat. YTCD171; master ID: 1228516; release ID: 10756685) comprises five shorter pieces (“Desire,” “Humility,” “Knowledge,” “Perspective,” “Integrity”) plus the extended title track “Truth” (13:30). All six tracks use the same core octet. Washington credited as Music By, Producer, Conductor. Mixed by Russell Elevado; mastered by Daddy Kev (Kevin Moo). Recording engineer: Michael Harris (10), with Benjamin Adamson (2) and Christopher Cerullo as additional/assistant engineers. Artwork: Amani Washington.

Heaven And Earth (2018)

Heaven And Earth (Young Turks, 2018; cat. YT176CD; master ID: 1379564; release ID: 12135022) is a double album structured in three discs: “Earth,” “Heaven,” and “The Choice.” 24 tracks total. Genres: Jazz; styles: Contemporary Jazz, Fusion, Soul-Jazz.

Washington composed and produced nearly all material. Covers include a Freddie Hubbard song (“Hub-Tones”), a Goffin and King standard (“Will You Love Me Tomorrow”), and a track originally associated with James Wong / Joseph Koo / Ku Chia Hui (“Fists Of Fury”). Guest contributions from Chris Dave (drums, on “Agents Of Multiverse”), Robert “Sput” Searight (drums), Terrace Martin (alto saxophone, on “Tiffakonkae”), Thundercat (electric bass), Carlitos Del Puerto (upright bass), Gabe Noel (bass), Jamael Dean (piano on “The Next Step”). Choir contributors include Patrice Quinn, Taylor Graves, Thalma de Freitas, Dawn Norfleet, Nia Andrews, Mashica Winslow, and others. Full SATB choir plus orchestra on orchestral tracks.

Becoming soundtrack and Dinner Party (2020)

Becoming (Music From The Netflix Original Documentary) (Young Turks, 2020; master ID: 1853640; release ID: 16317351) — original score composed by Washington for the Michelle Obama Netflix documentary. The release has no additional credits in the Discogs record beyond Washington as primary artist; the album drew on the same core band.

Dinner Party (Sounds Of Crenshaw, 2020; master ID: 1819186; release ID: 15650467) — debut EP by Dinner Party (2) (Discogs ID: 8218434), the jazz-soul supergroup comprising Washington, Robert Glasper (piano), Terrace Martin (saxophone, keys), and 9th Wonder / Patrick Douthit (production). All four credited as Producer, Written-By, Lyrics By. Artwork by Amani Washington. The group operates under the Sounds of Crenshaw imprint.

Also in 2020: appeared on Flying Lotus’s It Is What It Is (Brainfeeder; master ID: 1709557) — listed as “Performer [With].”

Fearless Movement (2024)

Fearless Movement (Young (5) label, 2024; cat. YO350LP; master ID: 3474511; release ID: 30564577) — 12-track double LP. Genres: Funk/Soul, Hip Hop, Jazz; styles: Contemporary Jazz, Fusion, Psychedelic, Trip Hop. Released on Young (5) — a label distinct from Young Turks.

Features significant guest appearances across the tracklist:

The album marks an explicit extension into hip-hop and funk territory, with George Clinton (Parliament/Funkadelic) and Andre 3000 (OutKast) serving as generational anchors.

Lazarus soundtrack (2025)

Lazarus (Original Series Soundtrack) (Milan / [Adult Swim], 2025; master ID: 4044550; release ID: 35642734) — Washington credited as Tenor Saxophone; Written-By, Arranged By, Producer. The core ensemble reappears: Ryan Porter, Cameron Graves, Miles Mosley, Ronald Bruner Jr., Tony Austin, Brandon Coleman, Dontae Winslow, Allakoi Peete (percussion), plus an orchestra and choir. Trumpet guest Josef Leimberg appears on several tracks. Mastered by Alex DeTurk.
