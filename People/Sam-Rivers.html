Sam Rivers

American jazz multi-instrumentalist (tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, flute, piano, bass clarinet, harmonica) and composer. Full name Samuel Carthorne Rivers. Born September 25, 1923; died December 26, 2011, in Orlando, Florida. Discogs artist ID: 244170.

Discogs lists his birthplace as El Reno, Oklahoma; most printed sources give El Dorado, Arkansas — see Open Questions below.

Biography

Early Life and Boston Years

Rivers grew up in a musical family and came of age in Boston, where he became a central figure in the local jazz community during the 1950s and early 1960s. In Boston he befriended and mentored a teenage Tony Williams, who would go on to redefine jazz drumming with the Miles Davis Quintet. Rivers and Williams worked together extensively before Williams’s departure for New York.

Miles Davis Tour (1964)

In 1964, Miles Davis invited Rivers to tour Japan as a temporary replacement for tenor saxophonist George Coleman. The tour is documented on Miles In Tokyo (Miles Davis Live In Concert) (CBS/Sony, 1969/1973), on which Rivers is credited with tenor saxophone. The engagement was brief — Wayne Shorter took the chair permanently — but it brought Rivers to wider attention.

Blue Note Era (1965–1967)

Alfred Lion signed Rivers to Blue Note Records (Blue Note), where he made three landmark albums as a leader:

ReleaseYearKey Personnel
Fuchsia Swing Song1965Tony Williams (drums), Ron Carter (bass), Jaki Byard (piano)
A New Conception1966Steve Ellington (drums), Herbie Lewis (bass), Hal Galper (piano)
Contours1967Joe Chambers (drums), Ron Carter (bass), Herbie Hancock (piano), Freddie Hubbard (trumpet)

All three were recorded by Rudy Van Gelder at his Englewood Cliffs studio. Rivers wrote all his own material throughout. A posthumous vault release — Involution (Blue Note, 1975/1986) — drew on sessions from this era paired with a second date featuring Andrew Hill (piano), Donald Byrd (trumpet), Julian Priester (trombone), James Spaulding (alto sax, flute), and Cecil McBee (bass). Dimensions & Extensions (Blue Note, 1986) was another posthumous issue from this period.

He also appeared as a sideman on Andrew Hill’s Dialogue (Blue Note, 1965) — among the most advanced Blue Note sessions of the decade.

The Impulse! and Shandar Sessions (1970s)

Rivers appeared with the free-jazz ensemble assembled around the Fondation Maeght in France — documented on the Nuits De La Fondation Maeght series (Shandar, 1971–72), which captured the scene around George Lewis, Anthony Braxton, and allied improvisers.

He then signed to Impulse! (ABC/Impulse! and ABC Impulse! imprints), producing a run of quartet and trio sessions:

Conference of the Birds and the ECM Connection (1973, 1980)

In 1973, Rivers appeared on Dave Holland’s Conference Of The Birds (ECM Records), one of the most revered albums in free jazz. The quartet — Holland (bass and composer), Rivers (reeds/flute), Anthony Braxton (reeds/flute), and Barry Altschul (percussion/marimba) — recorded the session entirely from Holland’s compositions, produced by Manfred Eicher. The ensemble is listed on Discogs as the David Holland Quartet (ID: 263427).

Rivers returned to ECM in 1980 for Contrasts, this time leading a trio with Holland (bass) and George Lewis (trombone) — further extending the improvised language he and Holland had been developing.

Studio Rivbea and the Loft Jazz Scene (1970s)

Rivers and his wife Bea opened Studio Rivbea (the name combines “Riv” and “Bea”) in a SoHo loft at 24 Bond Street, New York City, sometime in the early 1970s. The space became the center of what became known as the New York Loft Jazz scene — a circuit of artist-run spaces where musicians performed outside the commercial club system. Studio Rivbea hosted hundreds of concerts and sessions.

Rivers was one of five producers (alongside Michael Cuscuna and Alan Douglas) on the Wildflowers: The New York Loft Jazz Sessions compilation series (Casablanca/Douglas, 1977), a five-volume document of the loft scene:

  • Wildflowers 1 (The New York Loft Jazz Sessions) (1977)
  • Wildflowers 2 (The New York Loft Jazz Sessions) (1977)
  • Wildflowers 3 (The New York Loft Jazz Sessions) (1977)
  • Wildflowers 4 (The New York Loft Jazz Sessions) (1977)
  • Wildflowers 5 (The New York Loft Jazz Sessions) (1977)

The “Studio Rivbea” name also appears on Discogs as a label (ID: 293089, zero releases catalogued) and under “Riv Bea Sound” (ID: 1114432, one release — the Trio’s Offering, 1995). Rivers also controlled a set of publishing entities: Rivbea Music, Rivbea Music Productions, Rivbea Sound Company, and Rivbea Publishing Co.

Italian Labels and International Touring (1976–1977)

Rivers appeared extensively on Italian improvised music labels in the mid-1970s:

  • Capricorn Rising (Black Saint, 1976) — with Don Pullen (piano), featuring Alex Blake (bass) and Bobby Battle (drums)
  • The Quest (Red Record, 1976)
  • Black Africa! Villalago (Black Africa 1) (Horo Records, 1977) and Black Africa! Perugia (Black Africa 2) (Horo Records, 1977) — both live Italian concert recordings

The Trio and Quartet (Late 1970s–1990s)

Sam Rivers Quartet (Discogs ID: 2129616) had a fluid membership that over its history included Dave Holland, Thurman Barker, Joe Daley (tuba), Steve Ellington, Stephen McCraven, and others. The Quartet recorded “Crosscurrent” — Live At Jazz Unité (Blue Marge, 1982) and Lazuli (Timeless Records, 1990).

The Sam Rivers Trio (Discogs ID: 4384974) included in various periods: Dave Holland (bass), Barry Altschul (drums), Cecil McBee (bass), Norman Connors (drums), Anthony Cole (3) (drums/tenor sax), and Doug Mathews (bass/violin). The Trio released:

  • Offering (Riv Bea Sound, 1995) — trio of Rivers, Anthony Cole (3), and Doug Mathews
  • Trio Live (Impulse!, 1998)

Waves (Tomato, 1979) featured the Quartet configuration of Rivers, Holland, Thurman Barker (drums), and Joe Daley (tuba).

The Rivbea All-Star Orchestra (1980s–1990s)

Sam Rivers’ Rivbea All-star Orchestra (Discogs ID: 1109205) was a large ensemble drawing from Rivers’s extended network. Documented on two BMG France releases:

  • Culmination (BMG France, 1999)
  • Inspiration (BMG France/RCA Victor, 1999)

Also documented on Jazzbühne Berlin ‘82 (Repertoire Records, 1990). The Orchestra’s membership was large and shifting; it included Greg Osby, Steve Coleman, Hamiet Bluiett, Ray Anderson, Ralph Alessi, Chico Freeman, Joseph Bowie, George Lewis, and many others. Sam Rivers also led the Sam Rivers Winds Of Manhattan (Discogs ID: 2976380), a related wind ensemble including Steve Coleman and Bobby Watson (2).

Later Career and Pi Recordings Reunion (2004–2012)

Rivers continued performing and recording into his late eighties:

  • Celebration (Posi-Tone, 2004)
  • Purple Violets / Violet Violets (Stunt Records, 2005) — two concurrent releases on the Danish label
  • Reunion: Live In New York (Pi Recordings, 2012) — released posthumously; reunited Rivers, Dave Holland, and Barry Altschul in a fully improvised live set at (mixed and mastered by Liberty Ellman; produced by Yulun Wang)

Rivers died December 26, 2011, in Orlando, Florida.
