Jerry Finn

Discogs ID: 253669 Born: 1966 Died: 2008 Role: Mixer, producer Also credited as: “Huckle” Jerry Finn, Jerry “Finnbad” Finn, Jerry “The Wizard” Finn, Jerry “Geezer” Finn

Jerry Finn was an LA-based mixer and producer who became the dominant technical voice of mainstream American pop-punk in the late 1990s and 2000s. Starting as an assistant engineer at Devonshire and Conway Studios in the early 1990s, he rose through Green Day’s Reprise-era singles to co-mix Dookie (1994) with Rob Cavallo, then moved into full production work that defined the commercial peak of the genre: Blink-182’s Enema of the State (1999) and Take Off Your Pants and Jacket (2001), both multi-platinum; Rancid’s …And Out Come The Wolves (1995); Alkaline Trio’s Wiretap Scars (2002), Good Mourning (2003), and Crimson (2005); AFI’s Sing The Sorrow (2003) and Decemberunderground (2006); and Morrissey’s You Are The Quarry (2004) and Years of Refusal (2009). He died in 2008 after suffering a brain hemorrhage in July of that year. His last completed productions — including Morrissey’s Years of Refusal and AFI’s Crash Love — were released posthumously.

Career

Early Years: Assistant Engineer (1992–1994)

Finn’s earliest Discogs credits are as an assistant or second engineer at Los Angeles studios. In 1993 he assisted on The Muffs’ self-titled debut (Warner Bros.) and on Lea Salonga’s Atlantic album. In 1994 he took second-engineer roles on Body Count’s Born Dead (Virgin) and on Narada Equinox’s Arctic Blue, crediting “Devonshire” studios in the role string — a facility that was one of the main punk-adjacent recording rooms in the Valley at the time.

The breakthrough came in 1994 when he mixed the Green Day singles “Basket Case,” “Longview,” and “Welcome to Paradise” on Reprise, working within the production team of Rob Cavallo. Those singles came off Dookie (1994), where Finn shared a “Mixed By” credit with Cavallo and the band itself. The Dookie connection opened the door to becoming the default mixer for Reprise’s punk-adjacent roster for the rest of the decade.

Mid-1990s: Mixing the Epitaph and Reprise Punk Wave

With Cavallo producing and Finn mixing, Green Day’s Insomniac (1995) was their second collaboration. That same year Finn shifted from mixing to co-producing: he and Rancid co-produced …And Out Come The Wolves (1995) and About Time (1995) for Epitaph — the former becoming one of the canonical releases of street punk/ska-punk. He also mixed Jawbreaker’s Dear You (1995, DGC), a record that Jawbreaker produced themselves alongside Rob Cavallo; Finn’s mix is the defining sound of that album’s more polished departure from the band’s earlier recording aesthetic.

Also in 1995: production on the Rancid singles “Time Bomb” and “Roots Radicals” (Epitaph); mixing the Green Day singles “Geek Stink Breath,” “Brain Stew / Jaded,” and the Angus soundtrack (Reprise); and mixing for The Muffs’ Blonder and Blonder (Reprise) and Ash singles “Jack Names The Planets” / “Kung Fu” (Reprise).

By 1996–1997 he was mixing and/or producing for The Offspring (II, Columbia), Strung Out (Suburban Teenage Wasteland Blues, Epitaph), Propagandhi (Less Talk, More Rock, Fat Wreck), and a scattering of Hollywood Records acts. The Conway Studios credit appears regularly from this period onward — Conway (later renamed) was a primary West Hollywood facility for major-label punk and alternative work.

Late 1990s: Engineering Identity and Transition to Producer

From 1997 onward Finn accumulated full producer credits alongside mixing. He produced Mest’s Destination Failure (1997, Capitol), and the self-titled Nerf Herder debut. In 1998 he mixed Rancid’s Life Won’t Wait (Epitaph), the Vandals’ Hitler Bad, Vandals Good (Nitro), and the Australian band The Living End’s self-titled debut (EMI/Modular), the last a signal of his growing reach beyond the US market.

1999–2001: Commercial Peak — Blink-182 and Pop-Punk Mainstream

The production of Blink-182’s Enema of the State (1999, MCA) marked his arrival as a top-tier pop-punk producer. He produced the full album; mixing was split with Tom Lord-Alge on some tracks. The record sold over 15 million copies worldwide. Finn followed with Blink-182’s live album The Mark, Tom and Travis Show (2000) and the studio album Take Off Your Pants and Jacket (2001, MCA), again multi-platinum. He also produced Sum 41’s All Killer No Filler (2001, Island) and New Found Glory’s self-titled (2000, Drive-Thru/MCA) in this period, cementing his role as the go-to producer for major-label pop-punk.

Recurring engineer on his sessions during this period: Joe McGrath (who appeared on Alkaline Trio’s Good Mourning, Sum 41’s All Killer No Filler, Sparta’s Wiretap Scars, AFI’s Sing The Sorrow, and Morrissey’s You Are The Quarry). Drum tech Mike Fasano appeared on a remarkable number of Finn productions from Insomniac through the early 2000s. Mastering often went to Brian Gardner (Bernie Grundman’s facility).

2002–2004: Diversification — Sparta, Alkaline Trio, Morrissey

Box Car Racer (the Tom DeLonge / Travis Barker side project), Box Car Racer (2002, MCA) — Finn produced. Sparta’s Wiretap Scars (2002, DreamWorks) — a more post-punk/alternative direction, with engineer Joe McGrath and assistant Jeff Moses. Alkaline Trio’s Wiretap Scars (2002, DreamWorks) and Good Mourning (2003, Vagrant) — co-produced with the band (Joe McGrath engineered). Alkaline Trio’s From Here to Infirmary (2001, Vagrant) — mixed only.

For AFI he co-produced Sing The Sorrow (2003, DreamWorks) alongside Butch Vig, a pairing that brought post-punk and gothic-tinged production textures to what had been a more straightforward hardcore band. Finn handled most tracks; Vig handled two.

The Morrissey connection was unexpected: Finn produced and mixed You Are The Quarry (2004, Attack), Morrissey’s commercial comeback after years of label difficulties. Engineer Joe McGrath and second engineer Seth Waldmann were on the sessions; keyboardist Roger Joseph Manning Jr. appeared (as he did on several Finn productions). The album reached the UK top 10 and US top 20.

2005–2008: Final Productions

Alkaline Trio’s Crimson (2005, Vagrant) — produced and mixed; Ryan Hewitt engineered. AFI’s Decemberunderground (2006, Interscope) — Finn produced and mixed the majority (Butch Vig produced two tracks; Chris Lord-Alge mixed several). Finn also sang backing vocals on the album. Plus 44’s When Your Heart Stops Beating (2006, Interscope) — co-produced and mixed.

In 2007 he produced Rancid’s Music From Regions Beyond (Hellcat). His final completed productions — Morrissey’s Years of Refusal (Polydor, 2009) and AFI’s Crash Love (Interscope/DGC, 2009) — were released the year after his death.

Finn suffered a brain hemorrhage in late July 2008 and died on August 21, 2008, at age 41.
