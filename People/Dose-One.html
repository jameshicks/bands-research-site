Oct 23, 20265 min read

Dose One

Born: 1977
Real name: Adam Drucker
Discogs IDs: 28560 (Dose One), 404042 (Adam Drucker)
Aliases: Big Mud, Gun Dolo
Origin: Cincinnati, OH → Oakland, CA

Rapper, producer, and poet. One of the founding figures of Anticon and its associated experimental hip hop scene. Known for a rapid, stream-of-consciousness vocal style, abstract/surrealist lyricism, and a prolific output of solo and collaborative work spanning the late 1990s through the present. Core member of cLOUDDEAD, Themselves, Subtle, 13 & God, and Deep Puddle Dynamics.

Formation and Early Work (1997–2001)

Drucker began releasing material independently in the late 1990s. His debut Untitled (1997) and Hemispheres (1998) were self-released, featuring production from J. Rawls, Jel, Mr. Dibbs, Mr. Len, and others; cover artwork by Yoni Wolf (then known as Jonathan Wolf, later of Why?).

The critical breakthrough came with Circle (2000, Mush), a collaborative album with Boom Bip (Bryan Charles Hollon). The record’s blend of abstract hip hop, glitch, and experimental electronics — with additional production from Robert Curcio and mastering by Phil Austin — established Dose One and Boom Bip as central figures in the emergent Anticon/underground experimental hip hop scene.

Both Drucker and Hollon were from Cincinnati; according to Boom Bip’s Last.fm biography they met “in the local clubs and open mic nights” in that city. [last.fm/music/Boom+Bip/+wiki — user-edited; treat as weak source pending a named interview] Boom Bip described the project’s ambition: “I wanted to do a project with no boundaries, my fellow musicians and my sampler. So Dose and I discussed doing a record together with live instrumentation and vocals that no one has ever attempted before.” [theleaflabel.com/releases/circle/] The album was recorded August–October 1999 and released August 1, 2000 on Mush — its first full-length release (preceded only by the Boom Bip & DJ Osiris Low End Sequence EP in 1998). A European reissue followed on The Leaf Label in May 2002. The collaboration was short-lived: one album plus a joint Peel Session with cLOUDDEAD and an appearance on the Mush Tour DVD (2002).

Around the same time, Dose One co-founded cLOUDDEAD with Odd Nosdam (David Madson, Discogs ID 32301) and Yoni Wolf (Discogs ID 5053036). Originally based in Cincinnati, the trio relocated to the Bay Area. Six 10” EPs released 2000–2001 were compiled into the self-titled cLOUDDEAD LP (2001, Mush / Big Dada UK). A second album, Ten (2004, Mush), followed before the group disbanded. Their collage-heavy, lo-fi approach is widely cited as an influence on cloud rap, hauntology, and lo-fi hip hop.

Also during this period: Deep Puddle Dynamics, an Anticon supergroup pairing Dose One with Alias (3) (Brendon Whitney), Slug (Sean Daley of Atmosphere), and Sole (Tim Holland). Their LP The Taste Of Rain… Why Kneel (1999, Anticon) is a foundational document of the label’s early aesthetic.

Themselves and Collaborative Projects (2001–2010)

Themselves — Dose One’s ongoing duo with Jel (Jeffrey Logan, Discogs ID 1044585) — is his primary long-term creative partnership. Based in Oakland. Active since approximately 1998, with key releases including CrownsDown (2009, Anticon) and theFREEhoudini (2009, Anticon).

Subtle was a full band project (2002–2008) on Lex Records, featuring Drucker alongside Alexander Kort, Dax Pierson (Discogs ID 115707), Jel, Jordan Dalrymple (Discogs ID 172461), and Marty Dowers.

13 & God (2005–2011) brought Themselves together with members of German indie rock band The Notwist: Markus Acher (Discogs ID 137761), Martin Gretschmann / Console (Discogs ID 97925), and Micha Acher (Discogs ID 31168). Released on both Anticon and Alien Transistor (The Notwist’s label).
