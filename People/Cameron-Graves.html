Cameron Graves

Los Angeles jazz pianist, composer, and producer. Discogs artist ID: 441339. Name variations: C. Graves; Cameron “Wïrm” Graves.

Biography

Early LA Scene

Cameron Graves is a Los Angeles-based jazz pianist who came up through the city’s tight-knit jazz underground in the late 1990s and early 2000s. He was a founding member of Young Jazz Giants, an early-2000s youth collective that united the core musicians who would later define the West Coast Get Down movement — including Kamasi Washington (saxophone), Thundercat / Stephen Bruner (bass), Ronald Bruner Jr. (drums), Miles Mosley (bass), and Ryan Porter (trombone). The group released a live album on Birdman Records in 2004, with Graves credited on piano and electric piano (Rhodes).

Before establishing himself in the jazz world, Graves had an unlikely detour: he was a member of Wicked Wisdom, the nu-metal/hard rock band fronted by Jada Pinkett Smith, appearing on the band’s 2005 self-titled debut on 100% Womon / Suburban Noize Records. His credits there included band member, guitar, keyboards, vocals, and producer — an early indication of his versatility across genres.

West Coast Get Down and The Epic (2015)

By the mid-2010s, Graves was a core member of The Next Step (5), the nine-piece recording band at the center of the West Coast Get Down collective. The ensemble — alongside Kamasi Washington, Ronald Bruner Jr., Miles Mosley, Ryan Porter, Brandon Coleman, Tony Austin, Patrice Quinn, and Thundercat — recorded a large body of work in marathon sessions at various Los Angeles studios.

This work appeared most prominently on Kamasi Washington’s The Epic (Brainfeeder, 2015), a landmark triple album that drew international attention to the West Coast Get Down scene. Graves played piano and organ throughout and also contributed choir vocals. The album went on to be one of the most celebrated jazz debuts of the decade.

Solo Career on Mack Avenue Records

Graves launched his solo career with Planetary Prince (Mack Avenue Records, 2017), taking full production and writing control. He played piano, sang, produced, arranged, and mixed the record himself. The album featured the West Coast Get Down inner circle: Ronald Bruner Jr. on drums, Hadrien Feraud / Thundercat alternating on bass, Kamasi Washington on tenor saxophone, Ryan Porter on trombone, and Philip Dizack on trumpet.

He followed it with Seven (Mack Avenue Records, 2021), a return to solo leadership with a new rhythm section: Max Gerl on bass, Mike Mitchell (aka Blaque Dynamite; Discogs: Michael Mitchell (8)) on drums, and Colin Cook on guitar. Graves co-produced with engineer Gary Lux (2).

A live document, Live From The Seven Spheres (Mack Avenue, 2022), captured the touring band.

Kamasi Washington — Heaven And Earth through The Prophet And The Madman

Graves continued as a central figure in Washington’s recording band through the 2010s and 2020s. On Heaven And Earth (Young Turks, 2018) he played piano across virtually the entire triple LP and contributed choir vocals alongside the extended West Coast Get Down choir. On Fearless Movement (Young (5), 2024) he returned on piano. On The Prophet And The Madman (Brainfeeder, 2025), co-credited to Ami Taf Ra (Fatima Washington), he played piano and sang in the choir alongside Taylor Graves (his sibling, a recurrent choir presence across Kamasi’s records).

Other Notable Session Credits

  • Miles Mosley — Uprising (World Galaxy Records, 2017): grand piano alongside the West Coast Get Down horn section
  • Kamasi Washington — Truth (Young Turks, 2017): piano on the Harmony of Difference EP
  • Kamasi Washington — Becoming (Young Turks, 2020): piano and Rhodes on the Netflix documentary soundtrack
  • The Stanley Clarke Band — listed member; Clarke’s working band in this era also included Ronald Bruner Jr. / Michael Mitchell on drums
  • Ami Taf Ra — The Prophet And The Madman (Brainfeeder, 2025): piano and choir
