Claudia Sarne

Musician, vocalist, and multi-instrumentalist. Discogs artist ID: 106258. Married to Atticus Ross; sister-in-law of Leopold Ross.

Name variation: “Claude” (credited on 12 Rounds releases).

Biography

Early Career — UK Pop and Electronic Scene (1991–1995)

Sarne’s earliest Discogs credit is as a backing vocalist on Stress (Eternal, 1991), the debut album by UK R&B group Stress (6). She shared that session with Claudia Fontaine, in a production overseen by Guy Chambers (later the chief collaborator of Robbie Williams). The record also featured Ian Mussington on drums — a name who would reappear on 12 Rounds recordings.

No credits are documented between 1991 and 1995, but by mid-decade she was a founding member of 12 Rounds (Discogs ID: 170295), a UK electronic rock group formed with Atticus Ross, his brother Leopold Ross, Adam Holden, Andy Crisp, and Kirk Hellie. The band briefly appeared on an NME promotional cassette titled Past Forward under the name “12 Rounds With Jesus” before signing to Polydor.

12 Rounds — Polydor Period (1995–1997)

The band released the 12 Rounds E.P. on Polydor in 1995. Sarne is credited under her nickname “Claude” as “Vocals, Performer [Everything]” — a total performer credit alongside Atticus and Adam Holden, who are credited “Performer [Everything Bar Vocals]“. Additional musicians on the EP included cellist Stan de Mea Beer, violist Sarah Shandi, and guitarist Mark de Lane Lea, all of whom recur across later releases.

The debut full-length Jitter Juice (Polydor, 1996) documents Sarne’s range across the band: she plays piano on tracks 5–7 and 13, takes full vocals, and co-writes tracks 4, 6, 8, and 10 (alongside Adam Holden and the collective 12 Rounds credit). Atticus handles bass, programming, and additional songwriting; the band self-produced. Recording was by Dave Pine.

Singles released from Jitter Juice include “Business / Pleasant Smell” (Polydor, 1996), where all core members — Atticus, Sarne, and Holden — are credited as “Performer [12 Rounds Are]”, and the Personally E.P. (Polydor, 1996, European release).

Following an initial run of shows, Kirk Hellie departed the band. The band was dropped by Polydor and spent the summer of 1997 recording new material in their own basement studio.

12 Rounds — Nothing Records Period (1998)

The basement recordings caught the attention of Trent Reznor, who offered the band a deal on his Nothing Records imprint. Two releases followed: My Big Hero (Nothing Records, 1998) — their US-market compilation album — and the single Pleasant Smell (Nothing Records, 1998).

On My Big Hero, Sarne’s contributions expanded: she plays bass on five tracks (1, 3, 4, 8, 9) and keyboards on six tracks (3, 6–10), alongside her vocals and shared written-by credit on the whole record. Atticus handles drum programming and instrumental programming; Andy Crisp plays live drums on three tracks. The record was mixed by Tim Simenon (Bomb the Bass) and mastered by Tom Baker (3). Art direction was by Vaughan Oliver (the 4AD designer known for Pixies and Cocteau Twins sleeves).

On the Pleasant Smell single (Nothing, 1998), Barry Adamson plays bass — connecting the 12 Rounds circle directly with Atticus’s parallel Mute Records work with Adamson. Dave Ogilvie (Skinny Puppy) appears as mixer on several tracks alongside Simenon. Sarne is credited for vocals and songwriting.

A third album was subsequently recorded with in-studio input from Trent Reznor but was never released, as Nothing Records collapsed. The band went on indefinite hiatus. Atticus and Claudia married.

nearLY (2006)

After the 12 Rounds hiatus, Sarne had a period as a vocalist for nearLY (Discogs ID: 531097), the project of Jerome Dillon (ex-Nine Inch Nails drummer). She is credited for vocals on tracks 2, 3, and 5–12 of the nearLY album Reminder (KUFALA Recordings, 2006), and for keyboards and vocals on the live document Reminder Live (La Casa Nostra, 2006). The Reminder session also featured Petra Haden (violin), Tanya Haden (cello), and Keith Hillebrandt (mix) — all figures in the NIN extended circle. Leopold Ross appears on Reminder Live as guitarist. Sarne is not credited on the 2006 single “Straight To Nowhere” (also on KUFALA) except on the main artist page, but it falls in the same period.

Film Scores with Atticus Ross — Collaborator Credits (2000–present)

Sarne’s film score work begins with Der Krieger + Die Kaiserin (The Princess and the Warrior, Motor Music, 2000) — a Tom Tykwer film scored primarily by Pale 3 (Klimek, Heil, Tykwer). Sarne and Atticus Ross are credited “Music By” for track 6 alongside Alison Goldfrapp and Michael Brook, who contribute individual cues. This is their first documented score collaboration.

The full transition into Atticus Ross’s film scoring world arrives with The Book Of Eli (Reprise Records, 2010) — Atticus’s solo debut as film composer, directed by the Hughes Brothers. Sarne is credited as Written-By and Performer alongside Atticus and Leopold Ross, who are credited Recorded By, Written-By, and Performer. It is a family score: all three write and perform. Orchestral sessions at Abbey Road are credited separately.

From 2013 onward she appears as a primary artist on several Ross-family score releases:

  • Broken City (Metropolis Movie Music, 2014) — primary artist alongside Atticus Ross, Kirk Hellie, and Leopold Ross. Directed by Allen Hughes (co-director of Book Of Eli). No additional session credits documented in Discogs.
  • Earthquake Bird (Lakeshore Records, 2019) — primary artist alongside Atticus Ross and Leopold Ross. Netflix film directed by Wash Westmoreland.
  • Dispatches From Elsewhere (Music From The Jejune Institute) and Dispatches From Elsewhere (Music From The Elsewhere Society) (both Lakeshore Records, 2020) — primary artist on both volumes of the TV series score alongside Atticus and Leopold Ross.

She also appears in contributor roles on:

  • Der Krieger + Die Kaiserin (Motor Music, 2000) — Music By (track 6, with Atticus Ross)
  • Music From Love & Mercy (Capitol Records, 2015) — Additional Vocals, Additional Keyboards (Brian Wilson biopic; Atticus solo credit, with Leopold Ross as score collaborator)
  • Almost Holy (Sacred Bones Records, 2016) — Cello and Piano on track B9 (scored by Atticus/Leopold Ross and Bobby Krlic)
  • Shōgun (Mutant (6), 2024) — Keyboards [Additional] (scored by Atticus Ross, Leopold Ross, and Nick Chuba)

Other Notable Credits

  • Barry Adamson — “Can’t Get Loose” (Mute, 1998) — Backing Vocals. Atticus Ross produces, Flood adds additional production. This is her only Mute-adjacent credit outside of 12 Rounds.
  • Nojahoda — Jahoda Witness (Sony Soho Square, 1999) — Keyboards. Atticus Ross produces and programs; Leopold Ross plays guitar and vocals; Milo Ross (another member of the Ross family) plays harmonica and kazoo. Robbie Furze plays bass.
  • Most Folks Know (Sony Soho Square, 1999) — Bass and Keyboards (same orbit; Atticus production).
