Bernard Rhodes

Manager and creative collaborator of The Clash. Discogs artist ID: 644872.

Overview

Associate of Malcolm McLaren; managed The Clash across two distinct stints (1976–1978 and 1981–1986), with a gap during which Caroline Coon and Blackhill Enterprises held the role. Co-credited as writer on Cut The Crap (1985) — the album is widely seen as partially his project, with the pseudonym “Jose Unidos” attributed to a Rhodes/Strummer collaboration. His management style was confrontational and politically motivated, pushing the band toward overt socio-political messaging. The power struggle between Rhodes, Strummer, and Jones contributed to the band’s fracturing.

Management Tenures with The Clash

First stint: 1976 – October 1978

Rhodes was instrumental in The Clash’s formation in 1976, emerging from the London SS dissolution. He signed the band to CBS Records on 25 January 1977 and co-produced (with Mickey Foote) their self-titled debut. His first stint ended on 21 October 1978, when The Clash terminated the management relationship — reportedly because Rhodes felt the band were drifting from their street-politics ideals, and the band resented his opposition to working with producer Sandy Pearlman on Give ‘Em Enough Rope. (The firing date of 21 October 1978 is from musicianguide.com; the Spanish-language site Planeta Clash places it on 28 October 1978, which may be when Melody Maker published the news rather than the actual date.)

Gap period: October 1978 – February 1981

Two interim managers in sequence:

  1. Caroline Coon — hired immediately after Rhodes was sacked, late 1978. Dismissed by the band in June 1979 (after the Cost of Living EP). Wikipedia – The Clash
  2. Blackhill Enterprises (Peter Jenner / Andrew King) — managed the band from mid-1979 through the London Calling (1979) and Sandinista! (1980) period. The Clash fired and sued Blackhill; Kosmo Vinyl (ex-Blackhill employee) stayed on as press agent and guide. musicianguide.com, encyclopedia.com

Second stint: February 1981 – 1986

Joe Strummer demanded Rhodes’s return, threatening to quit if he refused. Strummer and Simonon persuaded the rest of the band, and Rhodes was rehired in February 1981. musicianguide.com His first major act on return was organising the Bond International Casino residency in New York (first two weeks of June 1981). He remained through Combat Rock (1982) and Cut the Crap (1985, which he produced as “Jose Unidos”), until the band’s dissolution.

Date conflict note: The Wikipedia article on The Clash states “In January 1980, Rhodes was reinstated as the band’s manager,” which would place his return during the Sandinista! era. This appears to be an error: multiple other sources place the rehire in early/February 1981 (after Sandinista! had already been released in December 1980), and Blackhill Enterprises is consistently described as managing the band through the London Calling and Sandinista! sessions. The February 1981 date is more consistent with the evidence. See Wikipedia – The Clash vs. encyclopedia.com.

Other Management / Production

  • Subway Sect — managed and produced What’s The Matter Boy? (MCA, 1980). Rhodes’s involvement placed Subway Sect directly in the Clash orbit from their earliest days.
  • Also produced one track (B6) on Subway Sect A Retrospective (1977–81) (Rough Trade, 1984).
