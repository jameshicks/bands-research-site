Ravi Coltrane

American post-bop tenor and soprano saxophonist, composer, and producer. Discogs artist ID: 316502. Born August 6, 1965, Long Island, NY. Son of John Coltrane and Alice Coltrane. Cousin of Flying Lotus (Steven Ellison, aka Captain Murphy). Named after Indian sitarist Ravi Shankar.

Overview

Ravi Coltrane plays tenor and soprano saxophone, with some sopranino work in later recordings. He emerged in the early 1990s as a sideman — most visibly through an extended partnership with Steve Coleman’s M-Base orbit — before building a solo discography under RCA Victor and later Blue Note and ECM. He is also a significant producer and steward of his parents’ legacy: he produced and mixed Alice Coltrane’s Translinear Light (2004, Impulse!), her first studio recording in nearly thirty years; and has served as producer on multiple posthumous John Coltrane archival releases for Impulse! and UMe.

His core working quintet across the 2000s–2010s features Ralph Alessi (trumpet), Luis Perdomo (piano), Drew Gress (bass), and E.J. Strickland (drums), with James Genus and Eric Harland also rotating through across multiple albums. The 2016 ECM release In Movement, recorded with Jack DeJohnette and Matthew Garrison, marks a major departure: a politically engaged trio album produced by Manfred Eicher.

Translinear Light (2004) — Detail

Alice Coltrane’s return to secular jazz recording after nearly three decades devoted to her Vedantic ashram. Ravi produced, mixed (with Steve Genewick), and performed on multiple tracks. The session featured two distinct rhythm sections:

  • Tracks 3, 5, 8, 9, 10: Charlie Haden (bass), Jack DeJohnette (drums)
  • Tracks 2, 4, 7: James Genus (bass), Jeff “Tain” Watts (drums)
  • Track 6: Oran Coltrane (alto saxophone, Ravi’s brother)
  • Track 11: The Sai Anantam Ashram Singers (vocals)
  • John Coltrane compositions appear on tracks 8 and 9; Alice Coltrane compositions on tracks 3, 4, 6, 7, 10

Steve Coleman Orbit (1994–1997)

Ravi appeared as tenor saxophonist across multiple Steve Coleman releases in the mid-1990s, including A Tale Of 3 Cities, The EP (Novus/RCA, 1994), Def Trance Beat (BMG Victor, 1995), The Sign And The Seal (BMG France, 1996), and Genesis & The Opening Of The Way (BMG France/RCA Victor, 1997). The first RCA Victor solo album Moving Pictures (1998) was co-produced by Coleman and shares personnel with the M-Base world: Coleman plays on tracks 9 and 11; drummer Jeff “Tain” Watts and bassist Lonnie Plaxico are featured throughout.

Spirit Fiction (2012) — Blue Note Signing

Spirit Fiction was Ravi’s Blue Note debut, executive produced by Bruce Lundvall. It uses two distinct rhythm sections across different track groups, and Joe Lovano serves as co-producer and mixing engineer on tracks 2, 5, 8–10, in addition to playing tenor saxophone on tracks 9 and 10. Ralph Alessi appears on trumpet on tracks 2, 5, 8, 9.

Open Questions

  • CalArts connection (1990): A CalArts Jazz Ensemble release from 1990 (California Institute of the Arts) includes Ravi Coltrane on saxophone. Whether he formally studied at CalArts, and for how long, is not determinable from Discogs alone.
  • Earliest solo recording: The Tenor Conclave (Alfa Jazz, 1995) has primary credit for Ravi Coltrane, but is a Japan-only release under Grand Central (10). The Long Time Coming (1996, Not On Label) is listed as his primary credit on a Canadian release, though it’s a group project led by New York Jazz Guerrilla. The first unambiguous solo leader date is Moving Pictures (1998, RCA Victor). Clarification of the 1995–96 period would require web sources.
  • Ravi Shankar naming: The Discogs profile states Ravi Coltrane was named after sitarist Ravi Shankar (Discogs artist ID 299241). This is well-attested biographical fact, but any direct relationship or meeting between the two is not documented in Discogs.
  • M-Base depth: The exact dates and scope of Ravi’s involvement with Steve Coleman’s M-Base Collective (beyond the credited releases) are not traceable from Discogs credits alone.
