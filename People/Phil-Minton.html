Phil Minton

Born: 2 November 1940, Torquay, England
Discogs artist ID: 202316
Name variations: Minton · P. Minton · P. Miton · Phil
Website: philminton.co.uk

British vocalist, free improviser, and trumpeter. One of the most distinctive vocal voices in European free improvisation, known for an extreme range of extended vocal techniques — multiphonics, ululation, noise, fragmented language, and extreme dynamic contrast. Trained first as a trumpeter (from age 15), Minton has combined both instruments across a fifty-year career spanning the Mike Westbrook Orchestra, solo improvised voice records, large choral projects, and a web of duo and small-group collaborations across Britain, continental Europe, Russia, the USA, and Australia. Named Best Male Singer in Europe by the International Jazz Forum Magazine in 1988.

Biography

Origins and early career (1950s–1971)

Phil Minton grew up in Torquay, Devon. He began learning trumpet at 15 and played in the Brian Waldron Quintet (Torquay) from 1959 to 1961. After moving to London in 1962 he joined the Mike Westbrook Orchestra, with whom he would work intermittently for almost three decades (1962–64, 1971–1990), touring internationally and appearing at major festivals in Adelaide, Edinburgh, New York, Strasbourg, and Berlin.

First recordings: the Westbrook years (1972–1982)

Minton appears on the Solid Gold Cadillac LP (RCA Victor, 1972) — the Westbrook-affiliated jazz-rock group — and subsequently on a series of Mike Westbrook Orchestra releases: Plays “For The Record” (Transatlantic, 1976), Goose Sauce (Original Records, 1978), Mama Chicago (Telefunken, 1979), Rags (Arc / ReR, 1980), The Paris Album (Polydor, 1981), The Cortège (Original Records, 1982), and The Westbrook Blake (Bright As Fire) (Impetus Records, 1987).

His first solo credit — as leader — is Voice (Ogun, 1977), a recording by Voice (8), the vocal group he co-founded with Julie Tippetts and Maggie Nicols. The group was dedicated to collective vocal improvisation; the record was produced and mixed by Keith Beal with the group itself.

Solo voice records and the Rift period (1981–1988)

A Doughnut In Both Hands (Solo Singing) (Rift, 1981) is Minton’s first fully solo document — unaccompanied improvised voice. The US-based label Rift also released Voice Of America (Rift, 1982), a multi-artist release including Bob Ostertag that Minton contributed written pieces to. Both records prefigure the solo vocal practice that became central to his identity.

The Berlin Station (FMP, 1988) — Minton’s landmark FMP solo album — captures his unaccompanied voice live in Berlin. Produced by Jost Gebers, it is one of the defining documents of extended vocal improvisation in European free music.

The Norwegian LP Brazzy Landscapes (Odin, 1987) was his first primary leader credit outside solo performance, documenting a large-ensemble/trumpet project.

International festival and duo work (1988–1999)

By the late 1980s Minton was a fixture at international festivals: Victoriaville, Willisau, Vancouver, Linz, Ars Electronica, Musica Strasbourg, and many others. He developed an extensive series of duo improvisations with Fred Frith, Roger Turner, Peter Brötzmann, Günter Christmann, George Lewis, Radu Malfatti, and others.

His partnership with pianist Veryan Weston became one of his most sustained. Releases under the Phil Minton - Veryan Weston duo credit include:

  • Ways (ITM Records, 1987)
  • Ways Past (ITM Records, 1992)
  • Songs From A Prison Diary (Leo Records, 1993) — settings of Ho Chi Minh prison poems for 22 voices, which won the Cornelius Cardew Composition Prize and was premiered at Musica’91 in Strasbourg
  • Ways Out East - Ways Out West (Intakt, 2005)
  • Ways - A Songbook (Jazzwerkstatt, 2007)

Working with GrubenKlangOrchester (Georg Gräwe’s ensemble), Minton appeared on Songs And Variations (hat ART, 1989) and Flavours, Fragments (ITM Classics, 1994).

His duo and trio work with Roger Turner is extensively documented. Releases under the Phil Minton & Roger Turner credit span from 1984 (Ammo, Leo Records) through the 2020s: Dada Da (Leo, 1993), Drainage (Emanem, 2003), Slur (Emanem, 2007), Scraps Of Heard (Confront, 2018), and I Dream I Was An Earopean (ezz-thetics, 2023), among others.

The FMP label produced three further key Minton solo documents: A Doughnut In One Hand (FMP, 1998) — a 1992 live recording, produced by Jost Gebers with Veryan Weston as recording engineer — and Two Concerts (FMP, 1998), a double set of solo voice and voice/trumpet performances.

The Emanem reissue A Doughnut In Both Hands (Emanem, 1998) compiled early solo material alongside new recordings.

With Bob Ostertag, Minton appeared on Say No More (RecRec Music, 1993), Verbatim (Rastascan Records, 1996), and Verbatim, Flesh And Blood (Rastascan / Seeland, 1998–99), which combined voice and Ostertag’s live electronics.

At the Rectangle label (run by Lol Coxhill’s associate Jean Rochard), Minton led two releases: My Chelsea (Rectangle, 1997) and Xmas Songs (Rectangle, 1998) — both shared with Coxhill, part of their 11 shared releases across the decade.

The Ferals and ensemble projects

The Ferals — quartet with Hugh Davies (live electronics), Alan Tomlinson (trombone), and Roger Turner (percussion) — produced Ruff (Leo Records, 1987). Minton also sang in The Dedication Orchestra (Spirits Rejoice, Ogun, 1992; Ixesha (Time), Ogun, 1994), the large ensemble assembled to honour South African exile musicians.

He was a member of London Improvisers Orchestra from its founding and joined Roof (with Tom Cora) for European tours in 1997. The Roof collaborations documented on The Untraceable Cigar (Red Note, 1996) and Trace (Red Note, 1999).

2000s–present

Minton continued an active international schedule. Apples Of Gomorrah (GROB, 2002) documents him in duo with Veryan Weston. (For) Friends And Neighbo(u)rs (Concepts of Doing / Edition Explico, 2002) is a composed choral piece. Anicca (Dancing Wayang, 2011) — a Buddhist-themed choral project — is among his most ambitious large-ensemble work. A Doughnut’s End (Fataka, 2015) and A Duo(s) (Bocian, 2015) document him in duo with various partners.

Scatter (FMR Records, 2007) launched his long association with Scatter (4), the large improvising ensemble, documented further on Scatter (On A Clear Day Like This) (577 Records, 2023). Recent work extends to More Metal, More Breath (2025, with Carl Ludwig Hübsch).

He is also a member of No Spaghetti Edition, Toot, Vladimir Estragon, Vario 12, Vario 19, fORCH, 4Walls, Dirty Songs, Skatgobs, Sean Bergin’s Song Mob, Simon Nabatov Octet, Simon Nabatov Quartet, Trio Raphiphi, The Feral Choir, Speak Easy, Tony Oxley’s Celebration Orchestra, Madame Luckerniddle, René Bottlang Quintet, and Axon (2).
