John Maher

Drummer. Born 21 April 1960, Manchester, UK. Discogs artist ID: 440262.

Original drummer of Buzzcocks from 1976, present through the band’s dissolution in March 1981 and credited on all three classic United Artists albums. After the Buzzcocks split he played with Steve Diggle’s side project Flag Of Convenience and contributed session drums to Pauline Murray and the Invisible Girls, Penetration (2), and The Things (5). Returned to the Buzzcocks for the 1989 reformation tour and for early-1992 European dates. In later life he pursued a parallel career developing high-performance racing engines and became a professional scenic and landscape photographer (flyingmonk.co.uk / @johnmaher.photo / @flyingmonkphoto).

Name variants on Discogs: Maer, Maher, Mather, Mayer.

Biography

Buzzcocks (1976–1981)

Maher joined the nascent Buzzcocks in mid-1976, becoming the fourth member alongside Pete Shelley, Howard Devoto, and Steve Diggle. This lineup played the famous Lesser Free Trade Hall support for the Sex Pistols (4 June 1976, Manchester) and went on to record Spiral Scratch (New Hormones, January 1977) — one of the earliest British DIY punk EPs — with Martin Hannett (credited as Martin Zero) producing and Richard Boon behind the camera. Devoto left in February 1977; by late 1977 Steve Garvey had settled in as bassist, completing the classic four-piece.

Maher played on the full run of United Artists material from 1977 to 1981: “Orgasm Addict” (1977), Another Music in a Different Kitchen (1978), Love Bites (1978), A Different Kind of Tension (1979), and most of the singles between. All three studio albums were produced by Martin Rushent with Doug Bennett engineering. The Buzzcocks disbanded in March 1981.

Post-Buzzcocks session work (1980–1982)

While still a Buzzcocks member Maher played drums on the debut LP by Pauline Murray and The Invisible Girls (Pauline Murray and the Invisible Girls, Illusive, 1980), a Martin Hannett–produced record that also featured Vini Reilly and Robert Blamire. He returned for the follow-up single “Searching for Heaven” / “Dream Sequences” (Illusive, 1981), on which Wayne Hussey played guitar — one of Hussey’s earliest significant credits.

In 1979 he contributed drums to Patrik Fitzgerald’s album Grubby Stories (Small Wonder / Polydor), alongside Blamire on bass and Peter Wilson producing.

In 1980 he appeared as a member of The Things (5) on the single “Pieces Of You” / “Imperial” (Imperial (9)), a short-lived project with Dave Holmes (guitar/vocals), Joe Brehony (bass), Ella Metcalfe (keyboards), and Timothy Lyons (vocals). This is The Things (5)‘s only Discogs release.

In 1981 Maher played on the Steve Diggle solo EP 50 Years of Comparative Wealth (Liberty), alongside Steve Garvey on bass — the Buzzcocks rhythm section intact even after the band’s dissolution.

Flag Of Convenience (1982 onward)

After the Buzzcocks split, Diggle formed Flag Of Convenience, retaining Maher on drums. The debut FOC single “Life On The Telephone” (PVC Records, 1982) featured Diggle on guitar/vocals, Maher on drums, David Farrow on bass, and D.P. (5) on keyboards — effectively a post-punk continuation of the Buzzcocks core. Maher’s involvement in later FOC recordings is not individually credited on Discogs; the 1987 LPs Northwest Skyline and Should I Ever Go Deaf! (both M.C.M. Records) list John Caine as drummer.

Buzzcocks reformed (1989, 1992)

The Buzzcocks reconstituted for touring in 1989 with the classic Maher / Garvey / Diggle / Shelley lineup — a tour-only reformation producing no new studio recordings. Maher is credited on the 1989 compilation and live release Product (EMI, 6-LP box) with drums, vocals, and written-by credits, and on the 1989 The Fab Four release (EMI). He also appears on Live At The Roxy Club April ‘77 (Absolutely Free Records, 1989).

The same classic lineup appeared on Entertaining Friends – Live At The Hammersmith Odeon March 1979 (EMI, 1992), indicating Maher played the 1992 European dates as well (the Buzzcocks.md lineup table records early-1992 dates with Maher before Mike Joyce took over for 1990–1991, and the lineup again shifted in mid-1992). After this reformation Maher did not return to Buzzcocks recordings.

Penetration (2) (2015–2017)

Maher joined the reformed Penetration (2) lineup for two releases: Resolution (Polestar, 2015) and the single “Shake Some Action” (Polestar, 2017). The Resolution lineup was Pauline Murray (vocals), Robert Blamire (bass, producer), Paul Harvey (guitar), Steve Wallace (guitar), and Maher on drums — his most recent confirmed recording credit in the DB.

Photography (later career)

Maher is documented on Discogs in one photography credit: cover photography for Badbea (AED, 2019) by an unrelated artist. His photography practice centres on landscape / scenic work (flyingmonk.co.uk, Instagram @johnmaher.photo, Flickr outerhebrides).
