Henry Lewy

American recording engineer and producer. Discogs artist ID: 259822. Born May 31, 1926 in Magdeburg, Germany; died April 8, 2006 in Prescott, Arizona. Known in the studio community for his warmth and sensitivity — credits routinely spell out his full character on the sleeve: “Henry ‘Inspirational’ Lewy,” “Henry — More Than An Engineer — Lewy,” “The Fifteen Flying Fingers Of Henry Lewy.” Staff engineer at A&M Studios in Hollywood for much of his career, he became one of the defining recording engineers of the West Coast singer-songwriter era. His deepest association is with Joni Mitchell, for whom he engineered every album from Clouds (1969) through Mingus (1979) and continued into the 1980s.

Overview

Lewy came up through the Los Angeles studio circuit in the late 1950s, working at Liberty Records on instrumental pop and novelty records — The Ventures, Jan and Dean, Gary Lewis and the Playboys — and doing early sessions for Dolton Records and Liberty subsidiaries. By the mid-1960s he had planted himself at A&M Studios on Sunset, the facility that Jerry Moss and Herb Alpert had built out of the old Charlie Chaplin lot. He would remain the house’s go-to engineer for the next decade and a half.

His pivotal connection arrived through Reprise and Asylum. He engineered Joni Mitchell’s Clouds (1969) and stayed with her through her entire artistic peak: Ladies Of The Canyon (1970), Blue (1971), For The Roses (1972), Court And Spark (1974), Miles Of Aisles (1974), The Hissing Of Summer Lawns (1975), Hejira (1976), Don Juan’s Reckless Daughter (1977), Mingus (1979), and the live album Shadows And Light (1980). On several of these he received unusual production credit — “Producer [Guidance]” on For The Roses, “Produced By” on Mingus — acknowledging that his role had expanded well beyond technical execution.

At the same time Lewy was one of A&M Records’s key engineers for non-Mitchell work: Joe Cocker’s self-titled debut (1969), Procol Harum’s A Salty Dog (1969), The Flying Burrito Bros’s debut The Gilded Palace Of Sin (1969) and follow-up Burrito Deluxe (1970) — where he also shared producer credit with Larry Marks — and many projects for Sergio Mendes, Roger Nichols, England Dan and John Ford Coley, and countless A&M singles through the decade.

His work extended beyond A&M into adjacent circles. He co-produced Judee Sill’s two Asylum albums (Judee Sill, 1971; Heart Food, 1973), receiving the tongue-in-cheek credit “Audio Alchemy.” He engineered Neil Young’s If I Could Only Remember My Name sessions (additional engineering), recorded the live Time Fades Away (1973), and was the engineer behind the Harvest (1972) mixes — credited as “Producer” on the Reprise master. He recorded and mixed Leonard Cohen’s Recent Songs (1979), co-producing with Cohen, and was the live recording engineer for the 1979 world tour, later released as Field Commander Cohen (2000). He engineered Van Morrison’s Common One (1980). He produced multiple Joan Baez albums for A&M in the mid-1970s: Diamonds & Rust (1975), Gulf Winds (1976), Blowin’ Away (1977), and the compilation The Best Of Joan C. Baez.

From the mid-1970s onward Lewy also had a substantial production career outside his core Mitchell/Cohen axis. He produced Minnie Riperton’s Minnie (1979) and subsequent Capitol singles, and produced Joan Armatrading’s How Cruel (1979) and Track Record (1983). He co-produced or engineered a string of ABC Records releases in the mid-1970s including John Stewart’s Cannons In The Rain / Careless and Stephen Bishop’s Careless (1976). His earlier A&M work included engineer credits on the Mamas and Papas’ debut (assistant engineer), Sergio Mendes’s Fool On The Hill (1968), and Herb Alpert’s orchestral records.

Lewy died in April 2006. Retrospective boxsets and reissues by Rhino in the 2000s–2020s continued to surface his credit across Neil Young’s Archives volumes and Joni Mitchell’s Asylum and Reprise retrospectives, cementing his legacy as one of the key engineers of a specific, precious decade in California rock.
