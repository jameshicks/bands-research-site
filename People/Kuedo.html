UK electronic musician. Real name Jamie Teasdale. Formerly one half of Vex’d (as Jamie Vex’d). Solo Planet Mu career began 2009; 27 releases through 2022 including Severant (ZIQ309, 2011) and Slow Knife (ZIQ380, 2016). Also records as Dakimh.

Vex’d — End of the Duo

Vex’d (Teasdale and Roly Porter) dissolved around 2007, with the split attributed to geographic separation rather than a falling out. Teasdale explained in the Bandcamp notes for the posthumous album Cloud Seed (Planet Mu, 2010): “we had moved away from each other, to different cities, in different countries. We wanted to keep writing together, but geography and work made it too hard.” source

Cloud Seed was compiled by Planet Mu’s Mike Paradinas from unfinished material the duo had been building for a second album during late 2006 and early 2007; those tracks were left on the shelf when Vex’d dissolved and the duo moved on to individual projects. In mid-2009 the label approached them about releasing the material unfinished, resulting in the 2010 album. source

Teasdale has characterised the end of the project as a natural divergence rather than a dramatic break. In a 2011 Quietus interview he described Vex’d as “just a cool, interesting phase” and noted that his intensive interest in that sound “didn’t hang around very long”; he acknowledged Porter’s stronger imprint on the duo’s spirit, saying “a lot of the defining character of Vex’d, in terms of its energy, its spirit was to do with Roly.” source

Porter’s own trajectory after the split corroborates the mutual-divergence reading: he moved to Bristol (c. 2010), took an extended break from music to focus on carpentry and family, then re-emerged as a solo artist with Aftertime (2011), deliberately distancing himself from the Vex’d sound palette. source

The two later collaborated again: Porter guested on Kuedo’s EP Assertion of a Surrounding Presence (Knives, 2015) and the duo performed as Vex’d at the Planet Mu 20th anniversary concert in Belgium. source
