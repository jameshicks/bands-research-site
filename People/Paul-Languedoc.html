Aug 02, 20266 min read

Paul Languedoc

Sound engineer, luthier, and guitar tech. Discogs artist ID: 537191. Phish’s primary live recording engineer and guitar technician from the early 1990s through the present; credited on the vast majority of the band’s official live catalogue across Elektra, Livephish.com, and Jemp Records. He also builds custom guitars — most famously the instruments played by Trey Anastasio — making him a uniquely dual-role figure in Phish’s organization: both the person who captures the sound from the front-of-house position and the person who designs and maintains the guitars producing it.

Career

Languedoc’s earliest Discogs credits place him in the Phish touring organization by 1992, when he is listed as “House Sound” crew on A Picture Of Nectar (Elektra, 1992) — Phish’s major-label debut. By 1993’s Rift he is listed under “Road Crew,” suggesting a broad early role before his responsibilities became more specialized.

His first “Recorded By” credit appears on A Live One (Elektra, 1995), Phish’s first official live double-album. From that point forward, Languedoc is the constant presence at front-of-house (FOH) on virtually every Phish live release. The credit trail is consistent across three distinct phases of the band’s output:

Elektra / LivePhish CD series (1995–2004). The original LivePhish numbered series (volumes 01–20, released 2001–2003) relied on Languedoc’s FOH recordings as source material. He is credited as “Recorded By” across every volume. On several volumes — 13, 14, 15, 16, and 20 — he also received “Mixed By” credit, indicating that his board tapes were used directly as the final mixes. Hampton Comes Alive (Elektra, 1999), the landmark 6-disc Halloween box set, lists him as “Recorded By,” “Mixed By,” and additionally as “Road Manager” — underscoring how his role extended beyond audio duties.

Livephish.com download era (2003–2012). As Phish moved to selling individual show downloads, Languedoc’s “Recorded By” credit follows throughout. Individual download releases for dates ranging from 1993 through 2008 consistently carry his name, covering shows at small theatres (Murat Theatre, Indianapolis; Pershing Auditorium, Lincoln NE) through arena and amphitheatre runs.

Jemp Records physical catalogue (2005–present). Jemp Records — Phish’s own imprint — became the primary vehicle for archival and new live releases from the mid-2000s onward. Languedoc appears on 46 releases under this label’s “other” credit bucket per the label’s full credit summary, carrying roles including “Recorded By,” “Audio Recorded By,” “Engineer [FOH Engineer],” and “Technician [Sound Reinforcement By].” Notable Jemp releases crediting him include Vegas 96 (2007), New Year’s Eve 1995 – Live At Madison Square Garden (2005, also as “Engineer [FOH Engineer]”), Chicago ‘94 (2012), Hampton/Winston-Salem ‘97 (2011), At The Roxy (2008), The Clifford Ball (2009), and the ongoing Live Bait series (volumes 3–18, 2010–2021) plus the LP on LP series extending through 2025.

On Phish’s studio albums he appears in non-recording roles: “Technical Assistance” on Hoist (1994) and Undermind (2004), and “Technical Support” on The Siket Disc (2015). The Lawn Boy reissues (Jemp, 2013 and later) credit him as “Technician [Sound Reinforcement By],” acknowledging his live PA work during the period when that album’s material was performed.

Outside Phish. His sole confirmed non-Phish recording credit in the Discogs database is a single track on Son Seals’s Lettin’ Go (Telarc, 2000) — he is listed as “Engineer [Recording]” on track 14 only, alongside primary engineers Joel Moss and Michael Bishop. This appears to be a one-off session, not an ongoing relationship.

He also received a “Recorded By [Additional]” credit on Trey Anastasio’s self-titled solo debut (Elektra, 2002), reflecting his continued involvement during the band’s 2000–2004 hiatus period, and a similar credit on The Horseshoe Curve (Rubber Jungle Records, 2007), a side project associated with Trey Anastasio.

His credit activity in the Discogs database runs from 1992 through releases dated as late as 2025, spanning more than three decades of continuous involvement.

Connections

  • Phish — primary employer; Languedoc has been their live engineer and guitar tech since the early 1990s
  • Trey Anastasio — builds and maintains Anastasio’s custom guitars (the “Languedoc guitar”); also ran FOH on solo projects including Live In Brooklyn and Live In Chicago
  • Jemp Records — Phish’s own label; Languedoc is one of its most consistently credited individuals across the entire catalog
  • Livephish.com — download platform that distributed his FOH recordings
  • Elektra — major label that released Phish’s commercial albums and the original LivePhish series
