Pete Heller

Pete Heller

Oct 27, 20269 min read

Pete Heller

Real name: Peter Kenneth Heller Discogs ID: 1379 Role: DJ, Producer, Remixer Origin: Brighton, UK Active: 1990–present Associated Acts: Bocca Juniors, Heller & Farley, Fire Island, Roach Motel, The Cooler Kings, Sleepers (2) Aliases: Pete Heller’s Big Love; Stylus Trouble (as Discogs artist 5012) Labels: Junior Boy’s Own, Phela Recordings, Bedrock Records

Biography

Early life and Boy’s Own origins (late 1980s–1990)

Pete Heller was born in Brighton. He began DJing while at Manchester University, where he started promoting parties. He came to wider attention when he was handed the warm-up slot when the legendary Shoom acid house night relocated to Busby’s club. This placed him at the heart of the UK acid house scene in its formative moment.

Heller co-founded the Boy’s Own collective alongside Andrew Weatherall, Terry Farley, and Cymon Eckel (ID 6923564). Boy’s Own was initially a fanzine and club night operation, central to the British acid house and early rave explosion of 1988–89. It eventually launched Boy’s Own Productions (ID 680176) as a label under London Records, with Junior Boy’s Own (ID 283) as its enduring sublabel.

Within Boy’s Own, Heller, Farley, Weatherall, and vocalist Anna Haigh also recorded together as Bocca Juniors (ID 6515). Their debut 12-inch “Raise (63 Steps To Heaven)” (1990, Boy’s Own Productions, cat. BOIXDJ 1) was co-written and performed by Heller, Farley, Weatherall, and Hugo Nicolson, with additional rap from Protege and vocals from Haigh. It captured the breathless early-period Boy’s Own sound — a combination of balearic, house, leftfield, and hip hop influences. A second Bocca Juniors single, “Substance / Substantially Soulful,” followed on Boy’s Own Productions in 1991.

Heller & Farley and Fire Island / Roach Motel (1992–1998)

Heller’s main production partnership from the early 1990s was with Terry Farley, working under several project names. As Fire Island (Discogs ID 9075), they released on Junior Boy’s Own from 1992 onward. Their debut, “Fire Island” (Junior Boy’s Own, 1992, cat. JBO 03-12), featured rapper Ricardo Da Force and was engineered by Gary Wilkinson. As Roach Motel (ID 13962), the same core duo recorded a series of house 12-inches exclusively for Junior Boy’s Own between 1993 and 2003: “The Right Time / Movin’ On” (1993), “Transatlantic / Afro Sleeze” (1993), “Happy Bizzness / Wild Luv” (1994), “Work 2 Doo” (1995), and “The Night” (1996). Roach Motel material was revived with the retrospective “Wild Luv (Tom Stephan Re-Edit)” (Junior Boy’s Own, 2003) and “JBO Revisited Vol.1 EP2” (2008). The project returned again in 2023 for “Faith Presents Ain’t That A Groove Volume 1.”

The umbrella Heller & Farley name (Discogs ID 5010) covered remix work and production credits that didn’t fit neatly into either Fire Island or Roach Motel. Under this guise or close variants (Farley & Heller, Farley + Heller), they remixed a wide range of artists including The Farm (“All Together Now,” Jive, 1990), Praga Khan (“Spartacus Re-mixes,” 1991), Babybird, Everything But The Girl, and Michael Jackson (“Blood On The Dance Floor,” Epic, 1997). The duo also issued mix compilations as Heller & Farley: DJ Power Vol: 2 (Escapade, 1994), Journeys By DJ Presents Musicmorphosis (Music Unites, 1996), Anthology (AM:PM, 1997), and Perceptions Of Pacha Vol 1 (Pacha Recordings UK, 2000).

Fire Island — credited as a distinct project from Roach Motel — was more explicitly song-based. Aside from the 1992 debut, key Fire Island releases include “In Your Bones / Wake Up” (Junior Boy’s Own, 1992), “There But For The Grace Of God” (Junior Boy’s Own, 1993), “If You Should Need A Friend” (Junior Boy’s Own, 1995), “White Powder Dreams” (Junior Boy’s Own, 1997) with vocalist Ms. Joan Hillery, and “Shout To The Top” (JBO/V2, 1998). A 2015 reunion single, “Baby Wants To Ride” (Junior Boy’s Own), continued the Fire Island name.

During the mid-1990s Heller also co-wrote several tracks credited under Junior Boy’s Own’s broader orbit. He co-wrote and produced “Ultra Flava” (Junior Boy’s Own/AM:PM, 1996) — a track with 10 pressing variants — and “The Night” (Junior Boy’s Own, 1996). He contributed keyboards and programming to Junior Boy’s Own releases by other artists including the sub-label’s 1992–93 period 12-inches.

”Big Love” and peak solo profile (1998–2001)

Heller’s defining solo moment was the track “Big Love” (1998), released as Pete Heller’s Big Love. This was initially issued as a white label (cat. PEASHE 901) before commercial release, and became one of the most-pressed UK house singles of the late 1990s with 26 pressing variants logged on Discogs. The A-side was mixed by Dave Darlington; the B-side “Big Love (The Dronez Mix)” was remixed by The Dronez with vocalists Connie Harvey, La-Rita Gaskin, and Octavia Lambertis.

Alongside “Big Love,” two tracks from the “Junior (5)” sublabel (Junior Boy’s Own’s sub-imprint, ID 318357) appeared in 1999: “Deep Sensation” and “The Rising Sun,” both written and produced by Heller. These formed the foundation of his stronger solo identity as a house producer.

Heller also produced “Shout To The Top” for Fire Island (JBO/V2, 1998), co-produced remixes of Fatboy Slim’s “Song For Shelter” (Skint, 2001), Chemical Brothers’ “Star Guitar” (Freestyle Dust/Virgin, 2001), and contributed to Primal Scream’s “Only The Strongest Will Survive” (Creation, 1999).

His sole major-label DJ mix CD from this period is Nite:Life 014 - Dirty Grooves (NRK Sound Division, 2003).

Phela Recordings and ongoing production (2001–2010)

Heller founded and ran his own label, Phela Recordings (ID 28177), in the mid-2000s. Under the Phela imprint he released a sequence of his solo productions: “Timewarp” (PHL002, 2004), “Amerika” (2005) — presented as Darren Price & Pete Heller Present The Cooler Kings — “You Love This Ass” (2005), “Simpler” (2006), and “Vibrate” (2006). The label also released “Stellar” (2004), a co-production with Darren Price credited elsewhere.

His Stylus Trouble alias (Discogs artist 5012, an alias shown also as Pete Heller’s alias in the collaborator data) produced “Sputnik” (Junior London, 2001) and “Future Child / Stylus Chant” (Junior London, 2001), both with co-writer and production associate John Dent (ID 147827) and engineer Gary Wilkinson.

Heller continued remixing into the late 2000s: Basement Jaxx (“Good Love,” [PIAS] 2001), Röyksopp (“Eple” / “Remind Me” era; “Swimming Places,” Defected, 2006), Faithless (“Music Matters,” Toolroom, 2009), and Moloko (“Yes We Can,” Upside, 2008).

In 2009 Heller released “Sabotage” (Bedrock Records, ID of label not confirmed), followed by “Nu Acid” and “Overtime” (both 2010), the latter on Buzzin’ Fly Records.

2010s–present

Activity slowed after 2010 but did not stop. Heller contributed additional production to a 2015 Junior Boy’s Own revival: “Baby Wants To Ride” (Fire Island, Junior Boy’s Own, 2015). He is credited with additional production on The Farm’s “All Together Now (Remixes)” (BMG, 2022). He continues to DJ and runs Phela Recordings.
