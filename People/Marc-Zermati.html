Marc Zermati

Marc Zermati

Oct 06, 20264 min read

Marc Zermati

Discogs ID: 331639
Aliases: Doctor Z Wild Rock’n’Roll
Born: 21 June 1945, Algiers
Died: 13 June 2020

French music entrepreneur, label founder, concert promoter, and record shop operator based in Paris. Founded Skydog in the early 1970s, which became one of the first European labels to document and promote the American proto-punk underground. Key early European supporter of The Flamin’ Groovies, releasing Grease (1973) and related singles. Also involved in releasing early Stooges, MC5, and New York Dolls material in France.

Open Market Record Shop

Zermati opened the Open Market record shop in October 1972 on rue Quincampoix in the Les Halles district of Paris. It sold US garage rock, pub rock, and punk imports from London, Amsterdam, and New York. The shop became a cultural hub for the emerging French proto-punk scene — regulars included critics Alain Pacadis and Yves Adrien. Beyond retail, the shop also functioned as a rehearsal space for several early Parisian punk bands. It closed in 1977. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-05; OpenEdition/Volume journal, as of 2026-07-05)

Distribution

In 1976 Zermati co-founded Bizarre Distribution in London with Larry Debay — one of the first independent record distribution networks in France and the UK. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-05)

Concert Promotion and Management

Zermati organised the first European punk rock festival at Mont-de-Marsan, France, in August 1976 (headlined by The Damned and Eddie and the Hot Rods), and a second edition in 1977 at the Corrida Arena drawing approximately 7,000 attendees per day (The Clash, The Jam, The Damned). (I-94 Bar, as of 2026-07-05)

He organised French tours and concerts for: The Clash, Dr. Feelgood, The Heartbreakers, Eddie and the Hot Rods, the Ramones, and Talking Heads. He also managed bands including Stinky Toys and Les Dogs (Wikipedia also cites “the Lou’s”). (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-05)

Venues: Opéra (Unidentified) and Club Sept — No Connection Found

Multiple sources were searched for any connection between Zermati and Paris venues named “Opéra” or “Club Sept.” None was found. An extended second-pass web search (July 2026) covering French-language punk history sites, Wikipedia (French/English), Gonzaï, OpenEdition, andrewgallix.com, paris70.free.fr, and academic sources confirmed this null result.

  • “Opéra” as a Paris punk venue: No Paris concert venue specifically called “Opéra” or “l’Opéra” has been identified in any source. The Gonzaï Zermati profile lists Palais des Glaces, Mogador, and Mont-de-Marsan as his key venues, not any “Opéra”. The vault’s reference to this name is unresolved and may be an error or misattribution in the research queue’s original brief. French punk venue history consistently names Le Gibus, Palais des Glaces (37 rue du Faubourg-du-Temple, 10th arr.), Le Bataclan, Le Palace, and Le Rose Bonbon — but no “Opéra”. (andrewgallix.com, as of 2026-07-13; Gonzaï Zermati profile, as of 2026-07-13; fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_à_Paris, as of 2026-07-13)
  • Palais des Glaces (37 rue du Faubourg-du-Temple, 10th arr.) is the confirmed Paris punk venue most associated with Zermati: on 28 March 1977 he organised the “Nuit punk” there, featuring Wayne County and the Electric Chairs, Stinky Toys, The Jam, Generation X, and The Police. Further punk nights at this venue followed in April 1977 (The Clash, The Damned, The Stranglers). (evelynecoutas.net, as of 2026-07-13; andrewgallix.com, as of 2026-07-13)
  • Club Sept (7, rue Sainte-Anne) was a disco and fashionable nightclub founded in 1968 by Fabrice Emaer, noted for its gay clientele, disco music (DJ Guy Cuevas), and celebrity crowd (Jagger, Bowie, Saint Laurent models). It closed in 1980 and was the direct predecessor to Le Palace. No sources connect Zermati to this venue. (Wikipedia: Le Sept, as of 2026-07-05)

The Gonzaï obituary references “electric evenings” Zermati organised into the early 1980s without naming specific venues beyond the above. (Gonzaï, as of 2026-07-05)

Skydog Records (Later Activity)

In the 1990s Zermati created the Kind of Groove sublabel under Skydog for experimental and electronic music. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-05)
