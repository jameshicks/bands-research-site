Phil Puleo

American composer, drummer, percussionist, illustrator, and visual artist from New York. Discogs artist ID: 250170.

Overview

Phil Puleo has maintained a presence in New York’s noise-rock and avant-garde circles across four decades, moving between extreme industrial rock and chamber-folk textures with equal ease. He first emerged as the drummer for Cop Shoot Cop, the dual-bass NYC provocateurs who were a fixture of the early-1990s industrial/noise underground on Interscope Records and Big Cat. When Cop Shoot Cop dissolved (c. 1996), Puleo’s trajectory bent toward Swans — he joined their final 1996/97 touring incarnation and has remained a core member through the reformed era (2010–present), appearing on every Swans studio album from My Father Will Guide Me Up A Rope To The Sky (2010) through Birthing (2025). Between those chapters he played on Angels of Light’s debut New Mother (1999), and in 2020 co-founded Human Impact alongside Cop Shoot Cop’s Jim Coleman, Swans’ Chris Pravdica, and Unsane’s Chris Spencer.

A distinctive feature of Puleo’s musicianship is the breadth of instruments he brings to recording situations. Alongside standard percussion kit he regularly contributes hammer dulcimer, melodica, flute, Jew’s harp, Casio organ, fujara, and duduk — a range that reflects both the orchestral ambitions of the Gira projects he has inhabited and his independent work as a composer and visual artist. He is also credited as illustrator, layout designer, and visual artist on numerous Young God Records releases, with his drawings appearing on Live Rope (2024) among others.

Name variations on Discogs: P. Puleo, P.Puleo, Phil Puelo, Philip Carey Puleo, Philip Cary Puleo, Philip Puleo, Puleo, Pulso.

Career Arc

Cop Shoot Cop (1988–1996)

Puleo was the drummer for Cop Shoot Cop from their earliest recordings. The band’s lineup was built around dual basses (Tod Ashley and Jack Natz), no guitar, and the electronics of Jim Coleman — an unusual tonal base that required Puleo’s percussion to anchor the low-end aggression. He appears on every major Cop Shoot Cop release, from the self-released cassette Live Demonstration: (1988) through the final LP Any Day Now (1995).

The Cop Shoot Cop discography moved through several label homes: early records on Supernatural Organization (Japan) and Circuit Records (2), then a sustained run on Big Cat (UK) and Interscope Records (US). On Ask Questions Later (1993, Interscope), Puleo is credited not only for drums and metal percussion but also for layout and back cover photography — an early sign of his parallel visual practice. The band’s recording home for much of this period was Martin Bisi’s BC Studio in Brooklyn; Bisi co-produced Ask Questions Later alongside the band.

Cop Shoot Cop core lineup:

  • Tod Ashley (Tod A.) — high-end bass, vocals
  • Jack Natz — low-end bass
  • Jim Coleman — electronics, sampler
  • Phil Puleo — drums, percussion

Swans (1996–1997 and 2010–present)

Puleo joined the final touring lineup of Swans in 1996/97, and is credited as part of “Swans 1996/7” on the live single I Am The Sun (1997, Die Stadt/Überschall Records). On Swans Are Dead (1998, Young God Records), the double-live farewell document, he is credited on Disc 1 (the 1996 tour material) for percussion and hammer dulcimer — his first documented use of dulcimer with Swans. Disc 2 (the 1997 performances) featured a different lineup (Mullins, not Puleo).

After Gira reformed Swans in 2010, Puleo became a fixture of the new core lineup alongside Norman Westberg, Kristof Hahn, Chris Pravdica, Thor Harris, and William Rieflin. He appears on all five studio albums of the reformed era, contributing an expanding instrumental palette and co-writing music on several records.

Angels of Light (1999, 2007)

Puleo appeared on two Angels of Light albums. On New Mother (1999) — the first Angels of Light LP — he is one of three drummers alongside Thor Harris and Larry Mullins, reflecting Gira’s maximalist percussion aesthetic for this project. His credited role is the broadest of the three: drums, percussion, hammer dulcimer, melodica, flute, Jew’s harp, Casio organ, and backing vocals. On We Are Him (2007), the final Angels of Light LP, his contribution is narrowed to hammer dulcimer only.

Human Impact (2) (2018–present)

Human Impact formed c. 2018, assembling a quartet from the New York noise-rock world: Chris Spencer (guitar/vocals; Unsane), Jim Coleman (electronics; Cop Shoot Cop), Chris Pravdica (bass; Swans), and Puleo (drums). Their self-titled debut (Ipecac Recordings, 2020) was recorded at BC Studio by Martin Bisi, with additional recording by Coleman and producer/mixer Alan Camlet. The band thus directly reconnects Puleo with Coleman — his Cop Shoot Cop bandmate — nearly 25 years after CSC dissolved. Discogs ID: 7531920.

Solo and Other Work

Puleo released C4C5 (2020) on a self-released imprint credited as “Not On Label (Phil Puleo Self-released),” Discogs ID 15135905 — his sole primary-credited solo release documented on Discogs.

In 2025 he co-credited What You Could Not Visualise (Original Soundtrack Music) (Inflammable Material) alongside Italian composer Teho Teardo, guitarist Karl Bonnie, and UK post-punk group Rema-Rema. The release was compiled and produced by Gary Asquith (Rema-Rema) and Marco Porsia (film director), and appears to be soundtrack music for a Porsia film. This is a distinct project from the ongoing Swans work.

He is also listed as a member of several smaller or less-documented groups: Dig Dat Hole (Discogs 2835289), Excop (Discogs 7145975), F.I.L. (Discogs 645949), Red Expendables (Discogs 859923), and The Children… (28) (Discogs 14746594).

Open Questions

  • What year exactly did Puleo join Cop Shoot Cop? Discogs has him on the 1988 cassette Live Demonstration: but his first appearance on a label-released record is Headkick Facsimile (1989). Whether he was in the original lineup or joined slightly after formation would require a web source or liner-note confirmation.
  • Was he part of the Cop Shoot Cop 1991 lineup for White Noise, or did he join between Consumer Revolt and White Noise? Both are credited to him, but the transition from Circuit Records to Big Cat may have coincided with a lineup change — this is answerable only from a contemporaneous source.
  • What is What You Could Not Visualise (2025)? The film director is Marco Porsia; the soundtrack is on Inflammable Material (UK). Confirming the film’s title and context (documentary? fiction?) would require a web source.
  • Are Dig Dat Hole, Excop, F.I.L., Red Expendables, and The Children… (28) active projects or historical ones? Discogs lists Puleo as a member but provides minimal release data; none appear to have substantial release histories documented there.
  • Puleo is described on Discogs as a “composer” as well as drummer and visual artist. Whether he has released composed/scored work outside the 2020 solo release and the 2025 soundtrack is not documented on Discogs.
