Vikku Vinayakram

Vikku Vinayakram

Aug 25, 20269 min read

Vikku Vinayakram

Real name: Thetakudi Harihara Vinayakram (Tamil: தேத்தாக்குடி ஹரிஹர விநாயக்ராம்) Discogs ID: 350232 Born: 1942, Thetakudi, Tamil Nadu, India Instrument: Ghatam (earthen pot percussion), Mridangam, Kanjira, Jew’s Harp (Moorsing) Active: 1967–present (Discogs credits from 1967 to 2024)

Indian Carnatic percussionist; one of the foremost ghatam masters in South Indian classical music. Founding member of Shakti (2) and a key link between the Carnatic tradition and Western jazz-fusion. Father of V. Selvaganesh, V. Umashankar, and V. Umamahesh, who perform together as The Vinayakrams.

Biography

Early Career and Classical Roots (1960s–1970s)

Thetakudi Harihara Vinayakram — known universally as “Vikku” — was born in 1942 in Thetakudi, Tamil Nadu. He is a product of the Carnatic tradition and one of the principal exponents of the ghatam, a clay pot percussion instrument used as a secondary accompaniment in South Indian classical music, which he transformed into a solo voice. Among the many name variants that appear on his recordings: T. H. Vinayakram, T.H. Vikku Vinayakram, Vidwan Vikku Vinayakram, and variations thereof — over forty documented on Discogs alone.

His earliest Discogs credit dates to 1967 (an Odeon release of an M. S. Subbulakshmi UN concert recorded in 1966), but his defining early document as a leader is Sri Jaya Ganesh Talavadya Vidyalaya Presents Ghata Mala (Columbia, 1968) — a showcase for ghatam ensemble work. Throughout the late 1960s and early 1970s he appeared as accompanist on a dense run of South Indian classical recordings: veena recitals, violin concerts, flute sessions — the standard Carnatic accompanist circuit. Artists in this period include violinist Lalgudi Jayaraman and a long association with bansuri flautist Hariprasad Chaurasia.

Shakti Era (1975–1977)

In 1975 British guitarist John McLaughlin, fresh from dissolving the Mahavishnu Orchestra, formed Shakti (2) in London with a core South Indian classical lineup. The founding members were McLaughlin (acoustic guitar), L. Shankar (violin), Zakir Hussain (tabla), Ramnad Raghavan (mridangam), and Vikku Vinayakram (ghatam, mridangam). The ensemble’s music fused South Indian Carnatic structures — raga, tala, improvisation — with McLaughlin’s jazz-trained ear, creating a sound that had no real precedent.

Three studio albums documented the original Shakti run, all on Columbia/CBS:

ReleaseYearLabelVinayakram’s roles
Shakti With John McLaughlin1976ColumbiaGhatam, Mridangam
A Handful Of Beauty1977CBSGhatam, Mridangam
Natural Elements1977CBSGhatam, Dholak, Kanjira, Moorsing, Vocals

By Natural Elements, Vinayakram’s role had broadened well beyond ghatam — he contributed multiple rhythmic and melodic percussion instruments, with vocal contributions. McLaughlin produced Natural Elements himself; he had produced A Handful Of Beauty as well. The band dissolved after 1977, with the three studio albums remaining foundational documents of the jazz-world fusion genre.

A BBC In Concert recording from 1977 (UK, BBC Transcription Services) was also documented from this period, capturing the band live.

The 1980s: Carnatic Collaborations and International Sessions

Through the 1980s Vinayakram remained primarily within the Carnatic concert circuit while beginning to appear on international projects. In 1980 he appeared on Jazzmine (Polydor, India), a rare meeting of jazz and Carnatic idioms on an Indian major label. His 1989 leader record Panca Nadai Pallavi (CBS India) — co-credited with Shankar, Zakir Hussain, and vocalist Caroline (2) — documented his ECM-adjacent work of the period; the release was engineered by Jan Erik Kongshaug (a regular ECM recording engineer) and produced by Manfred Eicher of ECM Records. This trio of Shankar/Hussain/Vinayakram became a recurring working relationship.

Also in 1989 he appeared on Shankar’s Nobody Told Me (ECM Records), which marks his first crossover onto the European jazz label.

Bill Laswell and Material (1990s)

In the early 1990s Vinayakram became part of the extended orbit of bassist and producer Bill Laswell, contributing ghatam to two Material albums:

ReleaseYearLabelOther key players
Mantra1993Axiom / IslandLaswell (bass/producer), Zakir Hussain (tabla), Shankar (arrangements)
Hallucination Engine1994Axiom / IslandLaswell, Zakir Hussain, Shankar, Nicky Skopelitis, Jonas Hellborg, Bootsy Collins, Wayne Shorter, Trilok Gurtu, Sly Dunbar, Bernie Worrell, William S. Burroughs (voice)

Hallucination Engine was one of Axiom’s most densely-crewed productions, drawing on virtually every strand of the label’s global network. Vinayakram’s ghatam sits alongside a cast spanning P-Funk, dub, downtown New York, and the Arab world.

He also contributed ghatam to Shankar’s Soul Searcher (Axiom, 1990), produced by Laswell, with Peter Gabriel on keyboards and Zakir Hussain on tabla. And in 1991, Mickey Hart brought him in as a co-producer (alongside Zakir Hussain, Airto Moreira, Babatunde Olatunji, and others) on Planet Drum (Rykodisc) — the Grammy-winning percussion world-music project.

Remember Shakti (1999–2006)

The Shakti reunion crystallised in the late 1990s under the name Remember Shakti. The lineup now featured McLaughlin, Zakir Hussain, Hariprasad Chaurasia (bansuri flute), and Vikku Vinayakram — with Shankar no longer present but V. Selvaganesh (Vinayakram’s son) joining later. Three live albums appeared on Verve Records:

ReleaseYearLabelNotes
Remember Shakti1999Verve RecordsLive; Vinayakram on ghatam (partial set); Chaurasia on bansuri
The Believer2000Verve RecordsV. Selvaganesh on kanjira/ghatam; U. Srinivas on mandolin
Saturday Night In Bombay2001Verve RecordsLive, Mumbai

Vinayakram contributed liner notes to Remember Shakti (1999) alongside McLaughlin, Hussain, and Chaurasia. The Believer session is significant for featuring his son V. Selvaganesh as a fully-credited member of the reformed lineup, continuing the family connection. The band has continued releasing albums into the 2020s as Shakti (The Way Of Beauty 2006, This Moment 2023, Mind Explosion 2025, the latter on Abstract Logix), though Vinayakram’s primary participation was concentrated in the 1976–77 and 1999–2001 windows.

Family and The Vinayakrams

Three of Vikku Vinayakram’s sons became professional musicians:

MemberDiscogs IDInstrument(s)Active (per Discogs)
V. Selvaganesh959762Kanjira, Ghatam, Mridangam1994–2025
V. Umashankar1101310Percussion1994–2009
V. Umamahesh1101311Percussion1994–2006

All three perform together as The Vinayakrams (Discogs ID: 1101312), which released at least one album: Paris (Bardo Records, 2004). V. Selvaganesh has had the most extensive individual career, joining the Remember Shakti lineage on The Believer and continuing into the 2020s. Vinayakram and his sons also recorded together on various South Indian percussion ensemble releases including A Percussion Ensemble Of Magical Brilliance (Live) and Swara Laya Mela.
