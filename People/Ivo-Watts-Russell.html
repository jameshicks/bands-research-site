Ivo Watts-Russell

Label founder, A&R, producer, mixer. UK. Discogs ID: 254112.

Co-founder of 4AD (with Peter Kent, 1980; not Martin Mills — Mills bought the label from Watts-Russell in 1999). The creative driving force behind 4AD’s aesthetic: signed Cocteau Twins, Dead Can Dance, Bauhaus, Pixies, Throwing Muses, and His Name Is Alive. Mixed the first two His Name Is Alive LPs (Livonia, 1990; Home Is In Your Head, 1991) alongside John Fryer. Left 4AD in 1999 due to mental health difficulties; label passed to Beggars Group / Martin Mills.

Post-4AD (1999–present)

Departure from 4AD

Watts-Russell sold his half of 4AD to Martin Mills (Beggars Group) in 1999. The circumstances were a long deterioration: he suffered a nervous breakdown in 1994 and had been spending increasing time in Los Angeles, becoming progressively disengaged from day-to-day label operations. His depression — described by 4AD designer Vaughan Oliver as a “black dog he carried every day” — was a central factor, though it was not widely known publicly at the time. The fullest account of this period is in Martin Aston’s 2013 authorised history Facing the Other Way: The Story of 4AD (HarperCollins), which draws on interviews with Watts-Russell himself. [source: thequietus.com/articles/13633-4ad-facing-the-other-way; drownedinsound.com/in_depth/4146923-4ad-week-facing-the-other-way]

The Hope Blister (1997–1999)

His final music project while still nominally at 4AD. He “directed” (his preferred framing — he disliked “producer”) a fixed trio: vocalist Louise Rutkowski, bassist Laurence O’Keefe, and cellist/string arranger Audrey Riley. Additional players on …Smile’s OK included violinists Chris Tombling and Leo Payne, viola Sue Dench, saxophone and drums Ritchie Thomas. The Hope Blister was conceptually a successor to This Mortal Coil but with a stable lineup rather than revolving guests, and focused on cover versions.

Discography:

  • …Smile’s OK (4AD, 1998)
  • Underarms (4AD, 1999) — vocals on one track by Momus
  • Underarms and Sideways (4AD, 2005) — expanded reissue of Underarms with a second disc of seven remixes by Markus Guentner

The project ended with Watts-Russell’s exit from the industry in 1999. [source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hope_Blister; 4ad.com/artists/thehopeblister]

Relocation to New Mexico

After leaving 4AD, Watts-Russell moved first to Los Angeles (he had been based there intermittently since 1993), then settled in Lamy, New Mexico — a small outpost near Santa Fe. As of 2016 he was still resident there. [source: newmexicomusic.org/2016/04/13/watts-russell-ivo/ (New Mexico Music Commission entry)]

Post-music life

Following the sale of 4AD, he turned to art and photography. He has lived a deliberately low-profile life in Lamy: listening to music, reading, watching films. He has volunteered to transport dogs from local shelters to a no-kill rescue facility in Albuquerque. (These personal details derive from sources of varying reliability — see caveat below.)

Source caveat: The specific details about photography, dogs, and daily routine appear in the New Mexico Music Commission entry (newmexicomusic.org, April 2016) and aggregator pages (Grokipedia). The NM Music Commission entry is a primary public record but brief. The 2014 radio interview (conducted May 26, 2014, with Watts-Russell at his New Mexico home) is the most recent confirmed direct-to-Watts-Russell source found; the audio is at radiobombast.podbean.com but no transcript is publicly available. [source: radiobombast.podbean.com/e/full-interview-ivo-watts-russell-of-this-mortal-coil-and-the-hope-blister/]
