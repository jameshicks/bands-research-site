Roy Dodds

British drummer and percussionist. Discogs artist ID: 256966. Name variations: Dodds, R. Dodds, Roy “Spike” Dodds, Roy Dodd.

Biography

Early session work (1977–1986)

Dodds appears on Discogs as far back as 1977, with a credit on the Steps LP (EMI France). Through the late 1970s and into the 1980s he was active in the London session circuit, turning up on records for Working Week (Virgin, 1985), Kirsty MacColl (Titanic Days, 1993 — though he appears on a small number of tracks only), and a range of UK pop and post-punk projects. His earliest confirmed UK studio credits include the feminist post-punk band Jam Today (Drum Beat For Baby, Rough Trade 1982) and a 1982 Rough Trade LP La Varieté. He was also part of the early acid jazz milieu: a credit on the Acid Jazz release Mind How You Go (1990) places him adjacent to that scene.

By the mid-1980s Dodds was a regular contributor to Working Week, the British jazz-pop/Latin ensemble led by Simon Booth. He is listed as a member of Working Week on Discogs alongside Kim Burton, Nic France, Larry Stabbins, Juliet Roberts, and Simon Emmerson.

Fairground Attraction (1987–1990, 2024–present)

Dodds co-founded Fairground Attraction with guitarist/songwriter Mark E Nevin, singer Eddi Reader, and bassist Simon Edwards (playing guitarrón). The band formed around 1987 and released their debut album The First of a Million Kisses (RCA, 1988), a UK number one. Dodds is credited on drum kit and percussion throughout. The album was produced by the band alongside Kevin Moloney.

Fairground Attraction’s second album Ay Fond Kiss (RCA, 1990) followed the same acoustic-folk-pop template. After the band split (c. 1990), Dodds moved back to session work and production.

In 2024 the original quartet reunited: Beautiful Happening (Absolute, 2024) and a live album Beautiful Happening Live In Japan (Sony Records Int’l, 2025) are credited to Fairground Attraction with the same core lineup.

Post-FA session career (1990–present)

After Fairground Attraction’s dissolution Dodds remained closely aligned with Eddi Reader’s solo career. He plays on:

  • Mirmama (Eddi Reader With The Patron Saints Of Imperfection, RCA 1992)
  • Eddi Reader (Blanco Y Negro, 1994) — on tracks 1, 3, 7, 10; same session as Mark E Nevin
  • Candyfloss And Medicine (Blanco Y Negro, 1996)
  • Angels & Electricity (Blanco Y Negro, 1998) — also credited as Recorded By and co-writer
  • Sings The Songs Of Robert Burns (Rough Trade, 2003)
  • Love Is The Way (Rough Trade, 2009)
  • Live In Japan (Eddi Reader, 2010) — as backing band member

His session breadth in the 1990s–2000s extended to:

  • Kirsty MacColl — Titanic Days (1993), congas on track 6, drums on tracks 4, 5, 11; alongside Simon Edwards and Mark E Nevin
  • Cara Dillon — self-titled debut (Rough Trade, 2001), full album drums and percussion; produced by Sam Lakeman
  • Donal Lunny — Coolfin (Metro Blue, 1998), percussion
  • Loreena McKennitt — A Midsummer Night’s Tour (Highlights) live disc (Quinlan Road, 2014), touring drummer
  • Karine Polwart — A Quiet Eye (Topic Records, 1999) and After The Morning (Rough Trade, 2006), percussion
  • Christine Tobin — listed as member of Christine Tobin Band
  • Theo Travis — Double Talk (33 Records, 2008), co-producer (tracks 1–3, 6–7) and drums; notable session as Robert Fripp appears on the same record

Dodds also pursued production and engineering work from the early 2000s. He co-produced, recorded, and wrote material for Simple Soul (Compass Records, 2001) and Driftwood (Not On Label, 2001), and produced/engineered Leaves & Feathers (Hatsongs Records, 2013) and Big Guitar (Quake Records, 2017).

Graham Parker & The Goldtops (2017–2021)

Dodds became the drummer in Graham Parker & The Goldtops, playing on Cloud Symbols (100% Records, 2018) — with Simon Edwards on bass again — and the live album Five Old Souls (Live In Southampton With The Rumour Brass) (100% Records, 2021). The Goldtops lineup on these records pairs Dodds with Edwards, Martin Belmont, and Geraint Watkins.
