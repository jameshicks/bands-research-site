John Fothergill

Oct 19, 20266 min read

John Fothergill

Discogs ID: 236325
Realname: John Fothergill
Groups: Nurse With Wound, The Murray Fontana Orchestra

Co-founder of Nurse With Wound alongside Steven Stapleton and Heman Pathak. Described by Stapleton (2009 box set liner notes) as “a genuinely eccentric intellectual; an aesthete of the avantgarde” who became Stapleton’s musical mentor. The three met through shared record hunting in London second-hand shops.

Fothergill performed uncredited on the first three NWW albums (1979–1980) and co-compiled the “Categories Strain” NWW list insert on the debut. His instrument on the debut was electric guitar with tremolo booster and ring modulator; he reportedly laid the guitar on the floor and played it with a toilet brush.

Discogs credits extend into the early 1990s (last confirmed: Psilotripitaka, United Dairies, 1990). Stapleton’s 2009 dedication “to John and Heman, wherever you are now” suggests he had lost contact with both by 2001.

Departure from NWW / The Fothergill–Stapleton split: The rift was personal and operational simultaneously. Stapleton in a 1997 Wire interview:

“I fell out with John due to The Lemon Kittens. Basically, he fell in love with Danielle (Dax) and that kind of split us up permanently, and for a year I ran a United Dairies and he ran a United Dairies. The only time we would converse would be whenever we were putting something out, just so we didn’t get the catalogue numbers mixed up.”

[source: https://www.brainwashed.com/nww/words/wire1997.html]

Danielle Dax was one half of Lemon Kittens, the Karl Blake / Danielle Dax duo signed to United Dairies (debut LP We Buy A Hammer For Daddy, UD 02, 1980). Dax had also contributed cover artwork and appeared on some tracks of the Bombay Ducks LP Dance Music (UD 05, 1981). The Bombay Ducks were a United Dairies side project (Paul Hamilton and Joseph Duarte), reinforcing Fothergill’s involvement with Dax-adjacent releases during this period.

The two parallel United Dairies operations (c.1980–1981): During the estrangement, Fothergill continued to operate United Dairies from his address at P.O. Box 18, Richmond, Surrey (TW10 6ND) — the address registered to UD releases UD 02 through UD 07, as well as the associated “Experimental Records” sublabel (Fothergill’s rock-oriented offshoot) and the “Anthony & Paul” imprint. Stapleton ran a separate United Dairies operation in parallel. Their only coordination was catalogue number sequencing.

Stapleton was explicit that he would not have sanctioned two of the releases Fothergill put out during this period: “He put out The Bombay Ducks album I mentioned and The Lemon Kittens Cake Beast album — I wouldn’t have released Cake Beast, I didn’t like it very much, and I certainly wouldn’t have put out The Bombay Ducks album.” [source: https://www.brainwashed.com/nww/words/wire1997.html]

Fothergill therefore controlled the releases that bore the United Dairies imprint during the parallel period, including: Lemon Kittens — Cake Beast (UD 07, 1981) and Bombay Ducks — Dance Music (UD 05, 1981). Stapleton ultimately retained the United Dairies name and continued it after Fothergill’s withdrawal; the Experimental Records and Anthony & Paul sublabels appear to have ceased with Fothergill’s departure.

Karl Blake’s position: No documented account from Blake or Dax describes any tension between Blake and Fothergill over the romance. The Lemon Kittens’ next and final release (Those That Bite The Hand That Feeds Them…, 1982) appeared on Illuminated Records — not United Dairies — consistent with the band losing their label connection as the UD split resolved in Stapleton’s favour. Blake later formed Shock Headed Peters. No interview with Blake or Dax addressing these label circumstances has been located as of June 2026.

David Keenan’s England’s Hidden Reverse (2003; expanded ed. 2023, MIT Press): Keenan interviewed Fothergill for the book. The relevant passage is cited specifically regarding the NWW list dispute (Fothergill rebutting Stapleton’s claim that some names on the list were invented), but the book’s coverage of the Fothergill/Dax/Stapleton split has not been independently extracted from the text in available web sources. Secondary citations confirm Keenan addresses the split and the parallel UD operations, but the full passage is not reproduced online. [sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse_with_Wound_list, as of 2026-05-22; search result summaries citing Keenan 2003]

NWW list — invented names dispute: In a 1997 Wire interview, Stapleton claimed that some names on the Categories Strain list “don’t exist.” Fothergill disputed this in David Keenan’s England’s Hidden Reverse (SAF Publishing, 2003); Keenan, having researched the matter independently, sided with Fothergill’s assertion that all names on the list are real artists. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse_with_Wound_list, as of 2026-05-22; https://www.brainwashed.com/nww/words/wire1997.html; Keenan, England’s Hidden Reverse, 2003]

Post-1990 life: No documented activity found in web sources after last Discogs credit (Psilotripitaka, 1990) and the 2001 Chance Meeting CD reissue appearance. Whereabouts after the early 1990s remain unknown from public sources as of June 2026.
