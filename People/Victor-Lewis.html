Victor Lewis

American jazz drummer. Born 20 May 1950, Omaha, Nebraska. Discogs artist ID: 298245.

One of the most in-demand sidemen on the New York hard-bop circuit from the mid-1970s through the 1990s, Lewis appeared on well over 150 releases across that span. He is best known for his long associations with Woody Shaw, Kenny Barron, Stan Getz, and Bobby Watson, and for co-leading sessions under his own name on Enja Records. His Discogs name variations include “Victor Lewis Trios” and “V. Lewis.”

Career Overview

Lewis came up through the mid-1970s New York scene, appearing early on recordings by Woody Shaw (The Moontrane, 1975, Muse; Love Dance, 1976; Rosewood, 1978, Columbia; Woody Three, 1979, Columbia) and David Sanborn (Sanborn, 1976, Warner Bros.). The Woody Shaw association was particularly deep — Lewis appeared on multiple Shaw albums through the early 1980s and is credited on the 1992 Mosaic box The Complete CBS Studio Recordings Of Woody Shaw (arranger and drums).

Through the late 1970s and early 1980s he worked with Stan Getz — appearing on The Dolphin (1981, Concord Jazz), Pure Getz (1982, Concord Jazz), Line For Lyons (1983), and Anniversary (1989, EmArcy) — and with the Carla Bley ensembles (Sextet, 1987, ECM/WATT; The Very Big Carla Bley Band, 1991, ECM/WATT). He also drummed on Paul Bley’s Heavy Heart (1984, ECM/WATT) and Night-glo (1985, ECM/WATT).

In the second half of the 1980s Lewis began recording as a leader on Enja Records. Bushman Song (1986) was a co-leader date with John Stubblefield, Geri Allen, Charnett Moffett, and Mino Cinelu. Countin’ On The Blues (1987) was a quintet co-date with Stubblefield, Hamiet Bluiett, Mulgrew Miller, and Moffett — the most prominent of his several co-leader associations with Miller. His first pure solo leader credit was High Standards (1984, Polydor Japan).

Lewis also contributed to Bobby Watson’s Blue Note period, co-producing and co-leading Post-Motown Bop (1991, Blue Note) alongside Watson’s group Horizon, with Edward Simon on piano and Melton Mustafa on trumpet. He self-produced and played on Family Portrait (1992, AudioQuest Music), a larger ensemble date with Cecil McBee, Stubblefield, Bobby Watson, Don Alias, and Jumma Santos — on which Lewis also played piano and sang.

Through the 1990s Lewis appeared frequently with Kenny Barron (Kenny Barron Trio and Quintet), and participated in the Lewis, Watson, Bergonzi, Barron, Fink, Lundy collective session (1997, e.f.s.a. collection) alongside Jerry Bergonzi, Barron, David Finck, and Curtis Lundy. He was active as sideman on the Kansas City film soundtracks (1996–97, Verve Records) and toured with Sonny Rollins in the 2000s (appearing on Road Shows Vol 1, 2008; Road Shows, Volume 3, 2014; Holding The Stage / Road Shows Vol. 4, 2016).

Later-career leader sessions include Eeeyyess! (1997, Enja), a trio date with Seamus Blake and Terell Stafford; Grey (2002, Quinton), with Wolfgang Puschnig, Steve Swallow, and Don Alias; and A Letter To Dexter (2006, Kind Of Blue Records), a co-date with George Cables Trio and Rufus Reid.
