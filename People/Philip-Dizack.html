Biography

Philip Dizack is an American jazz trumpeter and flugelhorn player born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin in 1985. He moved to New York City in 2003, where he attended the Manhattan School Of Music on a full-tuition scholarship. His time at MSM placed him inside one of New York’s most active conservatory big-band ecosystems — he appears on a 2009 Jazzheads live album by the Manhattan School of Music Jazz Orchestra, led by Justin DiCoccio and featuring David Liebman as guest.

Dizack has since built a career that spans multiple roles: leader of small-group sessions on prestige jazz labels, first-call sideman across a wide stylistic range, and a working member of several New York-based orchestras and large ensembles.

Leader Recordings

Dizack’s debut as a leader, Beyond A Dream (Fresh Sound New Talent, 2005, FSNT 234), was released while he was still a student or recent graduate. The album was co-produced with veteran trumpeter Brian Lynch and features a strong post-bop quintet: Patrick Cornelius (alto saxophone), Greg Tardy (tenor/soprano saxophone), Miro Sprague (piano), Joe Sanders (3) (bass), and Obed Calvaire (drums). Executive production came from Jordi Pujol of Fresh Sound New Talent. The record connects Dizack to the Barcelona-based new talent pipeline that has launched many New York-based jazz musicians.

His second leader date, End Of An Era (Truth Revolution Records, 2012, TRR006), saw him expand the palette: the rhythm section splits between two configurations — Aaron Parks (piano), Linda Oh (bass), and Kendrick Scott (drums) on several tracks; Sam Harris (8) (electric piano), Joe Sanders (3) (bass), and Justin Brown (4) (drums) on others. Jake Saslow plays tenor saxophone; strings (Brooke Quiggins Saulnier, Maria Jeffers, Matt Aronoff, Nora Krohn) appear on selected tracks. Dizack produced and played trumpet throughout.

His third leader date, Single Soul (Criss Cross Jazz, 2013, CRISS1363CD), was produced by label founder Gerry Teekens and recorded/mixed by Michael Marciano. The band: Eden Ladin (piano), Joe Sanders (3) (bass), Eric Harland (drums), Ben Wendel (tenor saxophone). The Criss Cross imprimatur placed him squarely in the hard-bop/post-bop mainstream New York lineage.

Joe Sanders (3) appears on all three leader albums, making him Dizack’s most persistent collaborator by shared credit count (3 releases).

Sideman Work

Dizack has been a consistent sideman in contemporary jazz settings spanning mainstream, large-ensemble, and crossover contexts. Notable engagements include:

Miho Hazama — Journey To Journey (Verve Records, 2012, UCCJ-2107): Dizack plays trumpet and flugelhorn across 8 of the 9 tracks for the Japanese composer-conductor’s debut, a large-ensemble record featuring guest spots from Steve Wilson (2) and Stefon Harris.

Ben Van Gelder — Among Verticals (BvG Music, 2016): A Dutch alto saxophonist’s NY-recorded session; the band includes Sam Harris (8) (piano) and Rick Rosato (bass).

Cameron Graves — Planetary Prince (Mack Avenue Records, 2017, MAC1123): The most high-profile context of Dizack’s career to date. He plays trumpet on the debut solo album by West Coast Get Down pianist Cameron Graves, in a horn section also including Kamasi Washington (tenor saxophone) and Ryan Porter (trombone). The rhythm section is Ronald Bruner Jr. (drums), Hadrien Feraud and Thundercat alternating on bass.

Eric Berman — אהבה ואלוהים אחרים (NMC United, 2017): Dizack appears on this Israeli singer-songwriter’s Hebrew-language record alongside pianist Eden Ladin, demonstrating his reach into the New York–Tel Aviv axis that Eden Ladin also occupies.

Michael Leonhart Orchestra — Suite Extracts Vol.1 (Sunnyside Communications, 2019, SSC 1555): A trumpet section chair in this large ensemble, alongside Dave Guy, Jordan McLean, Andy Bush, and Carter Yasutake. Other featured players include Nels Cline, Donny McCaslin, Chris Potter (2), and Eric Harland.

Shai Maestro — Human (ECM Records, 2021, ECM 2688): Produced by Manfred Eicher, this Israeli pianist’s ECM debut features Dizack on trumpet alongside Jorge Roeder (bass) and Ofri Nehemya (drums). One of his most prominent international credits.

Orlando Le Fleming — Romantic Funk: The Unfamiliar (Whirlwind Recordings, 2020, WR4763LP): A UK bassist’s hybrid jazz/funk project; Dizack plays trumpet on most tracks alongside Will Vinson (alto saxophone), Sean Wayland (keys), Kush Abadey and Nate Wood (drums).

Orlando Le Fleming & Romantic Funk — Wandering Talk (Whirlwind Recordings, 2024, WR4825): A follow-up to the above, with the Romantic Funk band, recorded and released in the UK.

Outside jazz, Dizack appears in commercial session contexts: a horn section role on Phillip Phillips — The World From The Side Of The Moon (Interscope/19 Recordings, 2012) and on Breaking Benjamin — Aurora (Hollywood Records, 2020), where he contributed trumpet alongside an orchestral arrangement by Chuck Palmer.

Groups and Ensembles

Discogs lists Dizack as a member of seven groups: Philip Dizack Quintet, JC Hopkins Biggish Band, Max Von Mosch Orchestra, Remy Le Boeuf’s Assembly Of Shadows, The Awakening Orchestra, Daniel Jamieson’s Danjam Orchestra, and Imagine (21). The Quintet released Live In Barcelona (Fresh Sound New Talent, 2008) — a live document from his early career.
