Tommy Mottola

Music manager and industry executive, born Thomas Daniel Mottola, July 14, 1948, in The Bronx, New York. Founder of Champion Entertainment Organization, Inc. (Discogs ID: 4342088). Brief recording career as “T.D. Valentine” on CBS Records. Later served as Chairman and CEO of Sony Music Entertainment. Discogs artist ID: 1012741.

Overview

Mottola began as an artist manager in the late 1960s/early 1970s. His major breakout client was Daryl Hall & John Oates, whose commercial success through the RCA years established Champion Entertainment’s profile — Mottola appears in Discogs credits as “Coordinator [Of Chaos]” on Abandoned Luncheonette (Atlantic, 1973) and as “Management [Management And Direction]” on releases through Big Bam Boom (RCA, 1984). He subsequently took on Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band as clients (circa 1975–76), leveraging his RCA relationship to secure their record deal. August Darnell later recalled: “It was luck Tommy Mottola was our manager… he was a great manager.” Darnell wrote Mottola’s name into the lyric of “Cherchez La Femme” (1976) — Mottola described hearing his name on radio five times a day as “almost surreal.” (Mottola, Hitmaker, cited in Longreads 2019)

By 1979, following disarray within the Savannah Band after their third album failed to chart, Mottola broke off that management relationship. He renewed his management of August Darnell through the early Kid Creole And The Coconuts years; his credit appears on Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places (ZE/Island, 1981) and Doppelganger (ZE/Sire, 1983), both credited to “Tommy Mottola” alongside Champion Entertainment Organization, Inc.. On Doppelganger his credit reads: “Management And Direction.” Ongoing friction between Mottola and Michael Zilkha of ZE Records was described in period reporting as “long and legendary.” Darnell ultimately bought Mottola out and ended the management relationship, likely circa 1983–84. Darnell said: “I don’t want to sound ungrateful, but it had to happen. I just couldn’t deal with it anymore.”

During his Champion Entertainment years Mottola also managed or was credited on releases by Carly Simon (A&M, 1980–1981), Sandy Linzer’s artist Odyssey (3) (RCA, 1977), Cory Daye’s Cory And Me (1979), and provided musical supervision for the Ruthless People soundtrack (Epic, 1986) and The Secret Of My Success soundtrack (MCA, 1987).

Mottola subsequently rose to Chairman/CEO of Sony Music. Discogs credits document his Executive-Producer role on early Mariah Carey recordings starting with Mariah Carey (CBS, 1990) and Emotions (Columbia, 1991). He and Carey were married 1993–1998. He continued as executive producer through her early 1990s singles and albums. He also served as Executive-Producer on the Ruffin & Kendrick album (RCA Victor, 1987), the Ruthless People soundtrack, and later Latin artists on Sony Music Latin including Thalia through the 2000s and 2010s. Darnell later named signing to Sony (Columbia) in the early 1990s “one of the biggest mistakes of my career,” partly because by that point Mottola had moved on to larger priorities. (Quietus interview with August Darnell)

Champion Entertainment Organization, Inc.

Discogs lists Champion Entertainment Organization, Inc. (ID: 4342088) as an entity separate from Mottola himself, with its own credits on many of the same releases. Other named members of the Champion organization appearing in Discogs credits include Al Smith (16), Amy Levin, Bonnie Slifkin, Brian Doyle (2), Jan Mullen (2), John Sykes (3), Mark Arrow, Myrna Suarez (2), Randy Hoffman (3), and Rick Winward (2). On Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places, Champion staffers Al Smith (16) and Randy Hoffman (3) are each separately credited for Management alongside Mottola.
