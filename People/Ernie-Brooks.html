Ernie Brooks

American bassist and occasional producer. Born 1949. Discogs artist ID: 352219. Name variations: Ernest Brooks, Ernest Brooks III, E. Brooks, EB.

Co-founder of The Modern Lovers (Boston, 1970–1974) and a charter member of The Necessaries (New York, 1978–1982). A key figure in the downtown New York ecosystem of the late 1970s and 1980s through his work with Peter Gordon / Love of Life Orchestra, Arthur Russell, and Rhys Chatham.

The Modern Lovers (1970–1974)

Brooks was one of the founding members of The Modern Lovers, the Boston-area proto-punk band formed around Jonathan Richman in 1970. The classic early lineup — Richman (vocals, guitar), Brooks (bass), Jerry Harrison (keyboards), and David Robinson (3) (drums) — recorded a set of demos in 1972 produced by John Cale (then at Warners) that became one of the foundational documents of proto-punk and new wave.

Those recordings were not released at the time; they eventually came out as The Modern Lovers (Home Of The Hits / Beserkley, 1976). A second set of demos, recorded after Harrison departed, appeared as The Original Modern Lovers (Disc’ AZ International, 1981). Both LPs credit Brooks on bass and backing vocals throughout.

The band disbanded in 1974. Harrison went on to join Talking Heads; Brooks relocated to New York City.

The Necessaries (1978–1982)

After arriving in New York, Brooks joined The Necessaries, a power-pop/new wave band founded in 1978 by guitarist Ed Tomney. The initial lineup also included drummer Jesse Chamberlain (recruited from Harry Toledo And The Rockets) and vocalist Randy Gun. Following encouragement from John Cale, the band secured a deal with SPY Records (Cale’s own label).

Their debut single, You Can Borrow My Car b/w “Driving and Talking at the Same Time” (SPY Records / I.R.S. Records, 1979), was produced by John Cale. The band went through several lineup changes: guitarist Chris Spedding joined after Randy Gun departed (late 1979), then Arthur Russell replaced Spedding in mid-1981, playing keyboards, guitar, cello, and vocals.

The Necessaries recorded two albums for Sire. The first, Big Sky (1981), was withdrawn by the label without the band’s consent — Sire had released unfinished mixes over the band’s objection. The second, Event Horizon (Sire, 1982, UK), is considered the authorized record; it was produced by Bob Blank and credits Brooks on bass and vocals alongside Tomney, Chamberlain, and Russell. A full retrospective, Completely Necessary (Anthology 1978–1982), was released by Omnivore Recordings in 2026.

Peter Gordon / Love of Life Orchestra (1980–2016)

Brooks became a recurring collaborator in Peter Gordon’s Love of Life Orchestra orbit. His earliest documented credit is bass on two tracks of the Extended Niceties EP (Infidelity Records, 1980), the LOLO release that also featured David Byrne and Arto Lindsay on guitar, David Van Tieghem on drums and synth, and Kurt Munkacsi as producer/engineer. (Laurie Anderson contributed cover concept.)

Decades later Brooks appeared on Same Language Different Worlds (O Genesis, 2016), credited as a co-artist alongside Tim Burgess, with Brooks playing electric bass on track 2. Other contributors on that album include Peter Zummo (trombone), Arto Lindsay alumna Nik Colk Void, and Larry Saltzman.

Discogs records 52 shared release credits between Brooks and Gordon across their careers.

Arthur Russell (archive production, 2005–2008)

Brooks played a central role in the posthumous rehabilitation of Arthur Russell’s recorded legacy through Audika Records.

  • Producer credit on World Of Echo (Audika, 2005): Brooks is credited as producer on track C3 of this Audika reissue of Russell’s solo cello/voice album. The reissue was overseen by Steve Knutson and Tom Lee (3).
  • Co-compilation producer, First Thought Best Thought (Audika / Rough Trade, 2006): a two-disc archival set drawing on Russell’s work with his loft-music ensemble (which included Brooks on bass for disc 1, alongside Rhys Chatham on flute, Peter Gordon on keyboards, Julius Eastman conducting, Jon Gibson on reeds, David Van Tieghem on percussion, and Andy Paley on drums). Brooks is also credited on liner notes.
  • Co-compilation producer, Love Is Overtaking Me (Rough Trade, 2008): country and folk-leaning outtakes from Russell’s archive. Co-produced with Steve Knutson and Tom Lee (3).

Rhys Chatham Guitar Orchestra works

Brooks contributed electric bass to two major Rhys Chatham guitar-orchestra works:

  • “An Angel Moves Too Fast To See” (For 100 Electric Guitars, Electric Bass, And Drums) (Radium, 2006): Brooks credited as “Ernest Brooks III” on electric bass alongside drummer Jonathan Kane. The work was written for massed guitars.
  • “A Crimson Grail” (Nonesuch, 2010): a 200-guitar orchestra work performed at Notre-Dame de Paris; Brooks is listed as a Musician among the ensemble.

Solo and Other Session Credits

Brooks’ sole Discogs credit as a primary artist is “Falling, They Get You” (New Rose Records, 1994), a French release where he appears as vocalist, guitarist, bassist, and co-producer alongside Roger Robindoré.

Additional notable session work includes:

  • Jerry Harrison: Casual Gods (Sire, 1988): assistant co-producer — reflecting the ongoing connection from the Modern Lovers years, with Harrison producing and Brooks in a supporting production role. The album also featured Bernie Worrell.
  • Arthur’s Landing (Strut, 2011): bass and vocals for this Arthur Russell tribute collective, alongside Peter Zummo (trombone), Bill Ruyle (drums), Mustafa Ahmed (percussion), Joyce Bowden (clarinet/guitar), and Steven Hall (guitar).
  • “Master Mix — Red Hot + Arthur Russell” (Yep Roc, 2014): text credits.
  • Arthur Russell Instrumentals (Audika, 2017): bass; liner notes/thanks.

He also appeared on various French rock releases throughout the 1980s–1990s, primarily through New Rose Records, the French indie label, and on records by artists in its orbit.
