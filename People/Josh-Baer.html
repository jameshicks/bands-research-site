Josh Baer

Art dealer, arts administrator. New York City. Discogs ID: 305055.

Co-founded Neutral Records with Glenn Branca in 1982. Was director of White Columns non-profit art space (1979–1983); his departure from White Columns roughly coincided with the label’s main release cluster. His art world network directly explains many of Neutral’s non-music acts: Cindy Sherman (cover of N5), Jack Goldstein (N-7), Beth B & Scott B (N-SIX), Eric Bogosian (N-10). Exec-produced N-3 (Native Dance) and N5 (Tidal) alongside Branca. Later became a prominent contemporary art dealer.

Why Neutral Records wound down — web research findings

No documented first-hand account of why the label ended has been found. The following is what the available record shows circumstantially.

Baer’s career pivot, 1983–1985. Baer left the directorship of White Columns in 1983. In 1985 he opened the Josh Baer Gallery, a commercial gallery where he represented Chris Burden, Lorna Simpson, Nancy Spero, and Leon Golub. The Baer Faxt newsletter launched in 1994 as a continuation of that commercial art-world focus. His own bio frames Neutral Records as part of his “downtown moments” rather than a sustained enterprise. The timing — Neutral’s catalog clusters 1981–1984, Baer’s gallery opens 1985 — is consistent with the label having served roughly the same window as his White Columns tenure, with no apparent intention of continuing it past that phase. (thebaerfaxt.com; oneartnation.com)

The label’s active window was short and apparently complete. Discogs and the Northwestern University finding aid (Glenn Branca papers, Box 6 Folder 16) both date the Neutral Records materials to 1981–1984. The label released 14 records total. No source mentions a planned continuation that was cut short; the impression across multiple retrospective accounts is that it ran its natural course. (findingaids.library.northwestern.edu)

Branca’s own accounts omit the closure. Interviews in The Wire (Marc Masters) and The Believer both describe how the label was founded — Baer asked Branca what he most wanted to do, Branca said start a record label, Baer agreed — but neither interview addresses why it stopped. No financial difficulties, distributor problems, or falling-out between Branca and Baer are mentioned in any published source found.

Archive finding aid accessed (October 2026). The Glenn Branca Collection finding aid at Northwestern University is online but its pages return 403 (Cloudflare-blocked) to automated fetches; the full container list cannot be read remotely. What can be confirmed from the indexed archival object entry is that Box 6, Folder 16 is titled “Neutral Records (independent label of Glenn Branca and Joshua Baer), 1981–1984” and classed as Mixed Materials — the finding aid entry itself carries no further description of the folder’s specific contents (no itemized letters, no summary of what the business papers contain). (findingaids.library.northwestern.edu/repositories/3/archival_objects/720769)

Possible additional folder: “New Neutral,” 1987–1990. Multiple Google-indexed search snippets from the Northwestern finding aid reference a separate folder described as “‘New Neutral’: Neutral Records correspondence, inventory, finances, announcement of cessation of new productions, 1987–1990.” If this description is accurate (it comes only from AI-generated search result summaries, not from directly reading the finding aid page, so caution is warranted), it would indicate: (a) a later incarnation of the label existed under the name “New Neutral” in the late 1980s, distinct from the original 1981–1984 run, and (b) there is a document explicitly announcing cessation of new productions in that folder. The box/folder number for this item has not been confirmed — one search summary mentioned “Box 9, Folder 17” but this has not been verified against the actual finding aid. This is a research queue item requiring physical or remote access to the Northwestern archive. (findingaids.library.northwestern.edu/repositories/3/resources/1893/collection_organization)

Summary (updated October 2026). No documented account of the original label’s 1984 closure exists in the published record, and the Box 6 Folder 16 finding aid entry does not describe its contents at the item level. The circumstantial picture is that Neutral wound down organically around 1984 as Baer transitioned fully into commercial gallery work and Branca moved on to large-scale orchestral composition. However, the finding aid also appears to contain a separate “New Neutral” folder (1987–1990) with materials including an “announcement of cessation of new productions” — suggesting the label may have had a later phase not reflected in the 14-release Discogs catalog, and that the actual cessation documentation may relate to that later phase rather than the 1984 wind-down. This remains unconfirmed and requires archive access to resolve.
