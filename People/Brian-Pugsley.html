Overview

Brian Pugsley is a British recording engineer, keyboard player, and member of Cheapglue, best known for his work at Pyramid Studios in Luton, England. His Discogs credits run from 1982 into the 2010s — over 650 in total — spanning engineering, production, mixing, and programming across indie rock, synth-pop, house, and leftfield styles. The shape of his catalogue points to a close, sustained relationship with One Little Indian Records: his three densest collaborator clusters are Björk, label head Derek Birkett, and the Sugarcubes, suggesting he served as a preferred house engineer for that roster through the late 1980s and 1990s. The Shamen and a wider circle of alternative and electronic artists account for secondary threads across the same period.

Notes (web)

Pyramid Studios, Luton — background

A July 1983 article in Electronics & Music Maker profiled Pyramid Studios as a Luton facility managed by a “Managing Director Mick Ilka.” It was located in premises formerly occupied by Don Larking Audio Sales, about one hour from London and minutes from Luton railway station. In 1983 it was an 8-track facility (MCI, 1-inch, 30 ips) equipped for electronic music production (Jupiter 8, Emulator, Linn LM-1, Lexicon 224). No mention of Pugsley appears in that article. [source: https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/studio-focus/6089 — Electronics & Music Maker, Jul 1983]

Earliest traceable Pugsley / One Little Indian connection

The strongest candidate for Pugsley’s point of entry into the One Little Indian orbit is Flux’s Uncarved Block (1986) — the very first release on One Little Indian Records, produced by Adrian Sherwood. Brian Pugsley is listed among the credited personnel on the sleevenotes alongside Adrian Sherwood, Ray Shulman, Style Scott, and others; his specific role (musician vs. engineer) is not specified in the Wikipedia article for the record or in available web sources. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncarved_Block_(album) — as of 2026-10-28]

Because Uncarved Block was Derek Birkett’s own band (Flux of Pink Indians, operating as Flux) and the label’s debut release, a credit on that record would place Pugsley inside the Birkett circle from the very start of One Little Indian. Whether Pugsley came to that session via Pyramid Studios or through an independent connection to Birkett/Sherwood is not documented in any available source.

By the time of the Sugarcubes’ Life’s Too Good (1987–1988), Pugsley appears as one of several engineers (alongside Gail Lambourne, Gerard Johnson, Ken Thomas, and others). That album was recorded at Studio Sýrland (Reykjavík), Berry Street Studio (London), and Orinoco Studios (London) — not Pyramid Studios. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life%27s_Too_Good — as of 2026-10-28]
