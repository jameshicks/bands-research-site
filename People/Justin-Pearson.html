Justin Pearson

Musician, vocalist, bassist, label founder. San Diego, California. Discogs artist ID: 696365 (listed as “Justin Pearson (2)”). Real name: Justin Pearson.

Prolific underground figure in the San Diego noise/hardcore/grindcore/punk scene, active from the early 1990s to the present. Founder and operator of Three One G (est. mid-1990s). Member of over a dozen bands, with The Locust as his primary long-running project. Known for collaborations across punk, metal, experimental, and electronic territory.

Name variations on record: J. Pearson, JP, JP - Razorgoat Gargler, Justin, Mister Justin Pearson, Mr. Justin Pearson, Pearson.

Biography

Early career — Struggle, Swing Kids (1992–1996)

Pearson first appeared on record in 1992 with Struggle (2), contributing bass to their self-titled 7” on Ebullition Records and a split with Undertow (Bloodlink Records). Both appeared in a posthumous 21-pressing run on Discogs, indicating the material has circulated widely.

By 1994 he was playing in Swing Kids (2), a San Diego post-hardcore band whose members also included Jimmy LaValle, Eric Allen, John Brady, and Jose Palafox. Swing Kids released a self-titled record in 1994 on Kidney Room Records and a split with Fly By Wire in 1995 on Three One G — the label’s earliest releases. The Three One G name itself derives from the chorus of the Joy Division song “Warsaw,” which Swing Kids had covered.

The band issued a discography compilation in 1997, also on Three One G.

The Locust (1994–present)

The Locust (Discogs ID: 125425) is Pearson’s primary band and the project most closely associated with him. Formed in San Diego in the mid-1990s, the group developed a signature sound: grindcore speed, synth-driven textures, costumes, and extremely short songs. Pearson plays bass and contributes vocals.

Full membership across the band’s history includes Gabe Serbian (drums), Joey Karam (keyboards), Bobby Bray (guitar), David Astor, Jimmy LaValle, and others.

Three One G label (1994/1995–present)

Pearson founded Three One G in San Diego, with the earliest documented release being the Swing Kids / Fly By Wire split in 1995. The label has grown into a substantial underground catalog spanning hardcore, noise, punk, grindcore, and experimental music.

Side projects and collaborations (1998–2009)

The Crimson Curse (1763489): San Diego hardcore band. Pearson is listed under “Band” credit on their 1999 split with The Festival of Dead Deer (Three One G) and their 2001 Greatest Hits compilation. The Crimson Curse was a significant early Three One G act.

Holy Molar (Discogs ID: 305716): Side project of Locust members, also including Gabe Serbian, Bobby Bray, Mark McCoy, and Maxamillion Avila. All releases on Three One G, 2001–2007. Live album recorded at the San Diego Metropolitan Correctional Center.

Head Wound City (Discogs ID: 576482): Supergroup with Serbian (drums), Cody Votolato and Jordan Blilie of The Blood Brothers, and Nick Zinner of Yeah Yeah Yeahs. Pearson credited as bass (uncredited on sleeve) and as a “Head Wound City Is” performer (“JP”). Self-titled 2006 debut on Three One G; returned in 2016 with A New Wave Of Violence and Scraper on Vice Records.

Some Girls (3) (Discogs ID: 446202): Noise-punk/hardcore band co-led with Wes Eisold (vocals). Released on Deathwish, Three One G, and Epitaph, 2001–2006. See Some Girls (3) for full detail.

Ground Unicorn Horn (1038387): Two releases on Three One G (2007).

Leg Lifters (1907471): Later project, four Three One G releases (2013–2014).

Dead Cross (2017–present)

Dead Cross (Discogs ID: 5806102) is a hardcore supergroup with Mike Patton (vocals, Faith No More / Mr. Bungle), Dave Lombardo (drums, ex-Slayer), Mike Crain (guitar), and Gabe Serbian. Pearson on bass. Released on Ipecac Recordings (co-founded by Patton) and Three One G.

Notes (web)

Anal Trump

Active project, 2016–present. Core duo: Rob Crow (Pinback) on guitar/bass/drums + Travis Ryan (Cattle Decapitation) on vocals, performing as “Rob Trump” and “Travis Trump.” Pearson participates as live bassist and secondary vocalist (“Justin Trump”) — he is not the primary creative force.

Formed as a political grindcore tribute to Anal Cunt, with all releases donating proceeds to charity (ACLU, Planned Parenthood, Immigrant Defense Project, National Immigration Law Center).

Physical releases not on Discogs:

  • That Makes Me Smart! (2016) — digital-only debut EP (30 tracks, ~3 minutes)
  • The First 100 Days EP series (2017) — several EPs, all proceeds to ACLU / Planned Parenthood
  • The First 100 Songs (2018, Joyful Noise Recordings) — compilation LP; formats: green “Drain The Swamp” vinyl (sold out) + CD. Joyful Noise product page ([archive.org snapshot advised for permanent record])
  • Fuckin’ Bitch EP (January 28, 2026, self-released via Bandcamp) — lathe-cut 7”, ICE-colored, 616 hand-numbered copies, sold out. Proceeds to Immigrant Defense Project and National Immigration Law Center. Bandcamp

The 2026 EP confirms the project is active as of the date of this research (2026-07-03). No Discogs entries for any of these releases; Discogs has only the artist page (ID 5361249) with no release credits.

Brain Tourniquet (2)

Defunct early-1990s San Diego hardcore/punk band. Pearson’s project between Struggle and Swing Kids. Confirmed by Pearson himself in interview: “soon after I was in this band called Brain Tourniquet. So both of the bands toured a bit on the West Coast.” Described as a “younger thrash band” in the San Diego scene, inspired by Born Against and Crossed Out.

The band appears on Discogs as “Brain Tourniquet (2)” (ID 7640659) — the “(2)” disambiguation distinguishes it from a separate DC-area Brain Tourniquet (Discogs ID 7304603) active in the 2020s, which is unrelated.

Possible physical release: A 1991 split cassette released as “Gravity Records #0” is documented online pairing “Slug” (sometimes spelled “Sloog”) with a band called Brain Tourniquet — but it is ambiguous whether this is Pearson’s band or a different unit. The Forced Exposure description of a recent Sloog/Brain Tourniquet split LP (L.G. Records, LG.009LP) says Brain Tourniquet “members went on to bigger things” without naming them. An eBay listing confirms a “Brain Tourniquet / Slug split 1991 Private hardcore/punk cassette tape + Insert” exists as a physical artifact. This cassette is not on Discogs and its connection to Pearson’s Brain Tourniquet (2) is plausible but not confirmed by a named source.

No other releases identified. The project is definitively defunct (Pearson moved on to Swing Kids by ~1993–1994).

Sources: Fecking Bahamas — Three One G oral history (as of 2026-07-03); Forced Exposure — Sloog/Brain Tourniquet LP (as of 2026-07-03); eBay listing — 1991 cassette
