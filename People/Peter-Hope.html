Background

Peter Hope is a Sheffield vocalist and lyricist whose career stretches from the early 1980s post-punk scene through to contemporary industrial and experimental electronic music. His voice — rawboned, sardonic, rooted in a tradition of northern English art-rock alienation — has appeared across more than sixty releases on Discogs under various names. He is not a studio name only: his live work is documented through multiple recordings and the Exploding Mind moniker in particular carries an ongoing performance dimension.

He has no published biographical profile text in the Discogs database, so the picture assembled here is entirely from credits, group memberships and release data.

The Box (3): Clock DVA’s Aftermath, 1981–1985

Hope’s first documented significant work is as vocalist for The Box (3), a Sheffield group that emerged directly from the ruins of the classic Clock DVA lineup. Founding members Paul Widger (guitar), Charlie Collins (saxophone/clarinet), and Roger Quail (drums) linked up with bassist Terry Todd and briefly worked with Ken Thomas as producer, and with vocalists including Ken Bingley and Stephen Mallinder (Cabaret Voltaire), before settling on Hope as their main singer.

The Box signed to Go! Discs — the first act on that label — releasing two LPs and several singles before moving to the Cabaret Voltaire’s own Doublevision imprint. They split in 1985.

The Kirk Collaboration: Leather Hands to Black&White Medicine, 1985–2018

Hope’s most sustained creative partnership is with Richard H. Kirk (Discogs ID 2238), core member of Cabaret Voltaire. They share 15 releases in Discogs, making Hope Kirk’s most frequent vocal collaborator after Stephen Mallinder. The collaboration spans three distinct phases:

Phase 1 — Doublevision and Native Records, 1985–1988

Leather Hands (Doublevision DVR 15, 1985) — a 12” in three mixes (Master Mix / Radio Mix / Crash Mix), co-composed and co-written by Hope and Kirk. Kirk handled instruments, drum programming, and production; Hope provided voice and words. Design by Naked Art; photography by Phil Barnes (2); cut by Porky (5). Genres: Electronic, Electro, Industrial. This is the founding document of the pairing.

Dry Hip Rotation (Native Records NTVLP 14, 1986) — LP credited to Peter Hope & The Jonathan S. Podmore Method (i.e., Jono Podmore, later known as Kumo). Hope provided words; Podmore the music. Additional: Alan Fisch (featuring, engineer); design by Naked Art and Artwork (7); lacquer cut by Porky (5).

Hoodoo Talk (Native Records NTVCD 28, 1987) — CD album, the core Peter Hope & Richard H. Kirk full-length. Eight tracks: “Intro,” “Numb Skull,” “N.O.,” “Cop Out,” “Surgeons,” “50 Tears,” “Leather Hands” (re-recorded), “50 Tears (Reprise).” Kirk: instrumentation, drum programming, production. Hope: voice, words, co-writing. Design by The Designers Republic (additional), original concept by Naked Art; photography by Phil Barnes (2). Later issued on Wax Trax! (US, 1988) and reissued on The Grey Area / Mute (2000).

Surgeons / N.O. (Native Records 12NTV 36, 1988) — 12” drawing two tracks from Hoodoo Talk into extended remix versions (three mixes each of “Surgeons” and “N.O.”). Kirk: instrumentation, drum programming, production, remix. Hope: voice, words. Additional credits: DJ Ping Pong (acid scratch formation); Jo Cammack (additional vocals); artwork by Dr. No (18). Genres include Dub and Downtempo — a notably different approach to the same material.

Phase 2 — Hiatus and reissues, 1988–2011

After Surgeons / N.O. the primary collaboration went quiet for over two decades. Hoodoo Talk received a Wax Trax! US pressing in 1988 and a Mute/The Grey Area reissue circa 2000, keeping the records in circulation.

Phase 3 — Wrong Revolution revival, 2018

Black&White Medicine (Wrong Revolution WRNG35, 2018) — digital album (7 tracks), the primary reunion document. Tracks: “Sugar Can You Feel The Drums,” “Workhorse,” “Hot Words,” “Upbeat,” “Hard Times,” “Surgeons Mixxx,” “Men&Women.” Kirk: instruments, production. Hope: vocals. Guest contributors: Jo Cammack (backing vocals on track 6); DJ Ping Pong (turntables on track 6); pH2 (4) [Hope’s own alias] (xtra sonic voodoo); Toby Morrow (mastering); David Bocking (photography); Faux Pax (design).

“Surgeons Mixxx” on this album is a 2018 revisitation of the “Surgeons” track from Hoodoo Talk, making it one of the longest-running musical obsessions in either artist’s catalog.

Other Projects and Collaborations

Sufferhead / Sufferhead (3), 1985–2020

Sufferhead EP. (Ink Deutschland RTD-INK 1, 1985) — 12” EP credited to Peter Hope / David Harrow. Four tracks: “Too Hot,” “Buckle Down,” “(Excerpt From) Bright Boys,” “Snakes Washed In.” Hope: voice, treated piano, lyrics, producer, sleeve design. Harrow: keyboards, sampler, electronics, producer. Additional musicians: Stephan Bevan (acoustic guitar), Simon Grayling (trumpet). Engineers: Tony Harris (Livingston Studios), Steve Collis (The Influence), SNAKE (9) (Vibrasound). Released on German label Ink Deutschland; later reissued digitally (Workhouse Digital, 2013) and physically (Kinetik Records, as Sufferhead 85/13, 2020). The pairing of Hope and Harrow forms their own band credit, Sufferhead (3).

Chain, 1986

Chain (Discogs ID 86265) — Sheffield group whose members include Hope, Mark Estdale (SNAKE/producer), Alan Russell (2), and Nort. One release documented: Banging On The House / Chains (Native Records NTV 2, 1986). The A-side “Banging On The House” features backing vocals by Elaine McLeod, bass by Simon DC Markham, and piano by Robin Markin. SNAKE (9) produced and engineered.

Hoodoo, 1993–

Formed 1993. Members: Peter Hope, Richard Barratt (a.k.a. DJ Parrot, Crooked Man, member of Sweet Exorcist and The All Seeing I), and Dave Lloyd (4). Hoodoo is a later vehicle, distinct from the Hope/Kirk Hoodoo Talk era — the band name is coincidental with that LP.

Flex 13, 1994–

Flex 13 reunites Hope with Charlie Collins (his Box bandmate). After The Box split in 1985, the two reconnected in 1994, releasing tracks on compilations before signing to Liquid Records in 1998 for the album Paint My Legs. Guest musicians on subsequent work include John Wills (Loop / Hair & Skin Trading Company), Mark Flunder (Television Personalities / Sportique), and Jono Podmore (Kumo). Discogs description describes their aesthetic as applying hip-hop ethics to rock formats.

Credits: Paint My Legs (Liquid Records, 1998) — Hope: keyboards, voice, instruments, producer, writer.

The Impossible

A group linking Hope, Charlie Collins, and Jon Wood (ID 881238). No profile data available in the DB.

White Trash (2)

Short-lived project with Richard Barratt (Parrot), Ross Orton (Fat Truckers), and Freekspert. Also listed member: Alicia Bostock.

Exploding Mind / Pete Hope’s Exploding Mind, 2011–

Hope’s most prolific solo-era vehicle. Also spelled “Pete Hope’s Exploding Mind” and “Peter Hope’s Exploding Mind” on various releases. Members per DB: Luna Menta, Natascha Schampus, and Hope. The Exploding Mind has its own name variations: Ex.Mind, The Exploding Mind.

First release under this flag: Loud/Wrong/Proud (Wrong Revolution WRNG01, 2011) — cassette, Limited Edition. Released on Hope’s own label.

Key Exploding Mind release: Peter Hope’s Exploding Mind Presents Hoodoo Dance (Klanggalerie, 2011).

Shangri-lies

Group shared by both Hope and Richard Barratt (Discogs ID 3017601). No profile data.

Soup (13), Chester Hope & Stealin’ Bo Hog, The Bone Orchestra, Collins/Hope/Infanti

Additional groups per Discogs membership data, with limited release data visible.

Wrong Revolution (label)

Hope runs Wrong Revolution (Discogs ID 296448, https://www.wrongrevolution.co.uk/), which has served as the primary release vehicle for the Exploding Mind and associated projects from 2011 onwards. The label has issued over 58 releases. Catalog numbers run from WRNG01 onwards.

Wrong Revolution releases include the Kirk collaboration (Black&White Medicine, WRNG35, 2018), as well as solo and collaborative work with Faux Pax, Iv/An, Toby Borrow, Toby Morrow, VX (16), John McBain, Ashley Reaks, Derek Saw, and others.
