Val Valentin

Discogs ID: 339187 Real name: Luis Pastor Valentin Alias: Luis Valentin (2) (Discogs ID: 2990182) Name variations: Val Valentine, Val Valentín, Val Valetin, Val Valintin, Valentin, Van Valentin Born: January 6, 1920, Puerto Rico Died: March 24, 1999 Wikipedia: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Valentin

Overview

Val Valentin (Luis Pastor Valentin) was an American recording engineer of Puerto Rican birth who worked from the early 1950s through the 1970s, primarily for Verve Records and MGM Records. Over a career spanning more than two decades, he served as staff engineer and eventually Director of Engineering for the MGM/Verve family of labels, touching some of the most significant jazz, pop, rock, and experimental records of the era. His credit on The Velvet Underground’s White Light/White Heat (1968) and their self-titled third album (1969), and on The Mothers’ Freak Out! (1966) and Absolutely Free (1967), reflects the overlap of the Verve/MGM catalog at a particular moment when the label pivoted from jazz toward rock. He is one of the few engineers credited on both canonical jazz masterworks and watershed avant-rock records of the 1960s.

Career Narrative

Early Work: West Coast Jazz (1953–1955)

Valentin’s earliest documented credits are from 1953 on the Los Angeles-based Contemporary Records, where he engineered sessions for the Howard Rumsey Lighthouse All-Stars and Shelly Manne. Simultaneously he worked for the short-lived Trend (3) label, recording a variety of jazz and pop sessions. By 1954–55 he was also engineering for Pacific Jazz (the Chico Hamilton Quintet, Bob Gordon) and Tampa Records, a West Coast independent that issued Art Pepper, Marty Paich, and related players throughout the decade.

These years established him as a go-to engineer for West Coast cool jazz sessions. His first MGM Records credit comes in 1955 (Hank Williams archive material); his first Verve/Norgran credit appears the same year, on a blues compilation.

Verve Records and the Jazz Mainline (1956–1965)

From 1956 onward Valentin became the house engineer for Norman Granz’s Verve Records. The volume of sessions is enormous — by rough count, several hundred distinct releases across a decade. Key clients during this phase:

  • Ella Fitzgerald — Rodgers & Hart Songbook Vol. 2 (1957–60 across multiple pressings), Jerome Kern Songbook (1963), Ella Swings Gently With Nelson (1962), Ella and Basie! (1963), Ella Sings Broadway (1963)
  • Oscar Peterson — Night Train (1963), Affinity (1963), Bursting Out With The All-Star Big Band (1962)
  • Stan Getz — Jazz Samba Encore! (1963), Stan Getz With Guest Artist Laurindo Almeida (1963), Another Time, Another Place (1966)
  • Bill Evans — Conversations With Myself (1963), Bill Evans Trio With Symphony Orchestra (1966)
  • Jimmy Smith — Hobo Flats (1963), Blue Bash! with Kenny Burrell (1963), Any Number Can Win (1963), Christmas Cookin’ (1966)
  • Gene Krupa — Drummer Man Gene Krupa In Highest-FI (1956)
  • Coleman Hawkins — Hawkins! Alive! At The Village Gate (1962), The Genius of Coleman Hawkins (1958)
  • Count Basie — Ella and Basie! (1963), Li’l Ol’ Groovemaker…Basie! (1963), On My Way and Shoutin’ Again (1963), Basie’s Beatle Bag (1966)
  • Wes Montgomery, Cal Tjader, Walter Wanderley, Astrud Gilberto, Antonio Carlos Jobim, João Gilberto (all Verve, 1963–66)

His credit title shifted during this period from plain “Engineer” to “Director of Engineering” (and related variants: “Engineer [Director]”, “Recorded By [Director Of Engineering]”), reflecting an institutional seniority role rather than just session engineering.

He also engineered the Jazz at the Philharmonic concerts for Verve in the early 1960s, and Ella Fitzgerald’s extended live sessions.

MGM Records: Pop, Rock, and Experimental (1962–1969)

MGM was Verve’s parent company, and from the early 1960s Valentin’s Director of Engineering title spanned both labels. His MGM credits are eclectic:

  • Connie Francis — multiple albums 1963–64 (Follow the Boys, Greatest American Waltzes, Mala Femmena)
  • Bill Evans (MGM side) — “Theme From The V.I.P.s” and Other Great Songs (1963)
  • Hank Williams archive releases (1962–66)
  • Hank Williams Jr. — several albums (1966)
  • The Animals — Animalism and Animalization (both 1966)
  • Herman’s Hermits — Both Sides Of Herman’s Hermits (1966)
  • Sandy Posey — Born a Woman (1966)
  • Herbie Hancock — Blow-Up soundtrack (1966)
  • Roy Orbison — MGM-era albums (1965 onward)
  • The Righteous Brothers — MGM releases
  • The Blues Project — Blues Project (Verve Folkways, 1966)
  • Richie Havens — Verve Folkways releases (1966–67)

Lee Hazlewood (1966–1968)

Valentin is credited as Director of Engineering on Lee Hazlewood’s key MGM albums — his most prominent single-artist association in the rock/country-pop sphere:

  • The Very Special World Of Lee Hazlewood (MGM, 1966)
  • Lee Hazlewoodism — Its Cause And Cure (MGM, 1967)
  • This Is Lee Hazlewood (MGM, 1967/68)

He appears on 36 Hazlewood releases in Discogs, including later reissues. These are the releases that, in the context of MGM’s Director of Engineering role, made his name recognizable to later researchers of the Hazlewood catalog.

The Velvet Underground and the Mothers (1966–1969)

Among the most historically significant entries in Valentin’s discography are the Verve and MGM sessions for experimental rock acts:

  • The Mothers (Freak Out!, Verve, 1966; Absolutely Free, Verve, 1967; Mothermania, 1969) — Director of Engineering
  • The Velvet Underground (The Velvet Underground & Nico, Verve, 1967; White Light/White Heat, Verve, 1968; The Velvet Underground, MGM, 1969) — Director of Engineering; additionally credited as Mixed By on some pressings of the 1969 self-titled LP

These were not sessions Valentin necessarily “produced” in a creative sense — his role was institutional oversight of MGM/Verve’s recording operations. Nevertheless his credit appears on all three VU studio albums that were released on the label, as well as on Frank Zappa’s first two Verve LPs with The Mothers.

Later Career: Pablo Records and Beyond (1972–)

Discogs credits extend into the Pablo Records era (Norman Granz’s post-Verve label, founded 1973) through the 1970s and later, suggesting Valentin maintained an ongoing relationship with the Granz production world through at least the mid-to-late 1970s. Pablo accounts for 420 release entries in his Discogs credit count, indicating a substantial late-career phase in jazz production.
