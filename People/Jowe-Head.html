Jowe Head

Discogs ID: 90163 Real name: Stephen John Bird Aliases: Matilda Tank, Stefan Vogel (2), Stephen Bird (Discogs ID: 806181) Origin: UK

British bassist, vocalist, multi-instrumentalist, and visual artist. Co-founder of Swell Maps, one of the defining British post-punk bands. After Swell Maps dissolved in 1980, built a decades-long parallel career: solo recordings from 1981 onward, a sustained role as bassist/co-producer in Television Personalities (member 1983/84–c.1996, returned 2017), work with the German-connected underground (Constrictor label), and an ongoing stream of collaborative and solo releases through to the 2020s. Also active as a visual artist, producing cover artwork for his own and others’ records.

Swell Maps (1977–1980)

Jowe Head joined Swell Maps — the Coventry-formed, Birmingham-raised post-punk group led by brothers Nikki Sudden (guitar/vocals) and Epic Soundtracks (drums) — as bassist. The band operated their own Rather Records and licensed through Rough Trade.

Solo Career

Phase 1: Hedonics / Rough Trade (1981–1982)

Immediately after Swell Maps, Head released two primary solo works:

Pincer Movement (1981, Hedonics Record HEDON 5) — debut LP. Self-produced with John A. Rivers (billed as “John ‘Baz’ Rivers”) engineering and co-producing. Performers included Phones B. Sportsman, Prince Empire (Discogs: 409523), and Joe-From-Hendon (Joe Foster). Entirely self-written except “Wimoweh” (Campbell/Ray, trad.) and “Crawfish” (Ben Weisman/Fred Wise). 21 tracks across two sides, mixing short vignettes with longer pieces.

Rain, Rain, Rain (1982, Rough Trade RT 104) — 12” single co-credited to Epic Soundtracks & Head. Both produced and wrote. Recorded by John Rivers, Jon Dee, and Martin Daley. Featured Carmel McCourt on vocals (side A), Ben Coleman on violin, Chopper (3) on cello, Nikki Sudden on back photography. Avant-garde/new wave.

Phase 2: Constrictor / Germany (1984–1989)

Head built a significant relationship with the German label Constrictor through the mid-to-late 1980s:

Chocolat-Art (A Special Tribute To James Last) (1984, Pastell/Principe Logique) — contributed bass, vocals, and cover artwork.

Strawberry Deutsche Mark (1986, Constrictor CON-00001) — solo LP produced entirely by Head. Key personnel:

  • Epic Soundtracks — drums (tracks A4, B2, B3)
  • Mark Empire (Discogs: 1092717) — drums (A1, A3)
  • John A. Rivers — organ, piano, percussion (A1, A3)
  • Phones B. Sportsman — bass, saxophone, vocals (B1)
  • Carmel McCourt — vocals (B2)
  • Curt Replies (Discogs: 409524) — horns

Also produced The Day Before Tomorrow / What A Wonderful World (1986, 53rd & 3rd) for another artist, and contributed to the Constrictor compilation Dump (1986) as lyricist/vocalist.

Personal Organizer (1989, Constrictor) — solo LP. Head composed all material, played all main instruments, wrote sleeve notes. Supporting cast:

  • Lucy The Holy Ghost (Discogs: 1075948) — saxophone (A3, A4)
  • Jean Elmes (Discogs: 847194) — synthesizer (A4, AA1, AA4, AA5)
  • Mercedes Mole (Discogs: 741312) — piano and vocals
  • Trudi Holt (Discogs: 543848) — keyboards (A1, A5)
  • Martin Giles — engineer

Phase 3: The Legendary EP / Get Happy!! Records (1991)

The Legendary EP (1991, Get Happy!! Records SMALL 01 / Roman Cabbage Vinyl GREY 4) — 4-track 7” EP. Personnel: Lucy The Holy Ghost on saxophone, Jean Elmes on synthesizer (B2), Mercedes Mole on photography. Self-written tracks “Marzipan” and “Exhibition (Coda)” on B-side; covers of “Constantinople” and “Frenzy” on A-side. Engineered by Martin Giles.

Phase 4: Collaborative Work (1990s)

In 1992, contributed bass guitar, bowed strings, and “trumpaphone” to Nikki Sudden’s Privilege (Fire Records). Contributed to Television Personalities’ We Will Be Your Gurus (1992, Seminal Twang) and How I Learned To Love The Bomb (Overground, 1992, featured).

The split single Cake Shop Girl / Only Fades Away (2004, Cape Shok Records) credited to Jowe Head & The NoMen / Cause Co-Motion.

Phase 5: Widdershins and Later (2019–present)

Widdershins (2019, Easy Action EARS150LP)** — double LP, the most substantial recent solo statement. Head played: vocals, guitar, zither, bass guitar, percussion, thumb piano, double bass, analog synthesizer. Multiple mix engineers (Constantin Groenert, Rico Conning, Xabier Garcia). Supporting contributors: Heath Stanley (electric guitar on A4, B4, D2), Catherine Gerbrands (vocals on A1, A5). Two tracks feature Maria Moraru on vocals and arrangement. Draws on folk, traditional, and experimental strands.

Mayday Signals (2021, Easy Action) — compilation/archival release for which Head contributed edited-by, compiled-by, liner notes, and cover collage artwork.

Active into 2025–26: appeared on the Swell Maps John Peel Sessions anthology (Mute/The Grey Area, 2025) and on the new Swell Maps (C21) release C21 (2026, Rather Records/Tiny Global Productions) in lead vocals and lead guitar.

Television Personalities (1984–1996, returned 2017)

Head joined Television Personalities (led by Daniel Treacy) in 1983–1984 — his first confirmed Discogs credit on a TVPs release is Chocolat-Art (A Special Tribute To James Last) (1984, Pastell/Principe Logique). Wikipedia dates the join to 1983. He is not credited on any TVPs release from 1978 through 1983, confirming he was not an original member.

First period (1984–1996): Head appears consistently on TVPs releases from 1984 through 1996, playing bass and vocals. The 1985 album How I Learned To Love The Bomb and the 1987 era are not documented with specific individual credits on Discogs, but the 1989 Privilege (Fire Records) and Salvador Dali’s Garden Party EP firmly establish him in the band by that point. His continuous credited run covers 1984, 1989–1996.

Departure circa 1996: The 1996 TVPs single “Where’s Jowe Head Now?” (Overground Records) — a title that pointedly asks after him — credits no individual musicians, and Head is absent from all TVPs releases from 1997 through 2016. This strongly implies he left the band around 1996. He appears in a “Featuring” role on just two tracks of the 1998 album Don’t Cry Baby…It’s Only A Movie, suggesting only an occasional guest arrangement after departure.

Return (2017): Head returned as the principal creative force on Beautiful Despair (2017, Fire Records) — his most substantial TVPs contribution, credited as producer, multi-instrumentalist (bass guitar, electric and acoustic guitars, synthesizer, autoharp, bowed psaltery, drum machine, percussion), recorded by, and arranged by. Daniel Treacy provided lead vocals and all songwriting. He is also credited as performer on the 2019 Fire Records anthology series Some Kind Of Happening Singles 1978–1989 and Some Kind Of Trip Singles 1990–1994, and on the 2021 live set Another Kind of Trip Live 1985–1993 and the 2025 radio sessions compilation.

Wikipedia (Jowe Head article) states he joined in 1983 and “stay[ed] with the band for the next ten years” — consistent with the Discogs evidence of a mid-1990s departure.

On Closer To God (1992), the lineup was: Daniel Treacy (guitar, vocals, keyboards, banjo, melodica, tambourine), Jowe Head (bass, vocals), Jeffrey Bloom (drums), Bekka Jones (fiddle), Derek Pascoe (saxophone), Kuljit Bhamra (tabla/percussion). Produced by Phil Vinall.

On Beautiful Despair (2017), Head effectively ran the production entirely — recording, arranging, playing most instruments — with Daniel Treacy supplying lead vocals and all songwriting. Mixed by Cos Chapman and Xabier Garcia.

Visual Art

Listed as visual artist on Discogs. Contributed cover artwork and illustrations to: Pincer Movement (1981), Chocolat-Art (1984, “Cover Artwork + Fishplate”), Schmalookas (1991, front cover illustration). Also produced the front cover collage for Mayday Signals (2021).
