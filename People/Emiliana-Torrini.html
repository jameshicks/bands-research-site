Emiliana Torrini

Icelandic singer-songwriter (b. Emilíana Torrini Davíðsdóttir). Best known internationally for “Gollum’s Song” from The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (2002). Discogs artist ID: 23910.

Icelandic Collaborations

Torrini’s early career was rooted in the Reykjavík scene before her international breakthrough. The following are confirmed Icelandic musician collaborations from Discogs.

Early Icelandic Period (1994–1996)

Spoon (15) — Spoon (1994, Iceland). Torrini appears as Lead Vocals / Backing Vocals on this Icelandic band’s album. Other musicians: Höskuldur Ö. Lárusson (vocals, guitar), Jón Ólafsson (keyboards, arranger, recording supervisor), Ingi S. Skúlason (bass), Friðrik Júlíusson (drums), G. Hjörtur Gunnlaugsson (guitar, mandolin), Sigurður Örn Jónsson (backing vocals), Stefán Hjörleifsson (acoustic guitar). Release ID: 4541153.

Crouçie D’Où Là (1995, Iceland) — Torrini’s own debut album (ETJÓ label) with an all-Icelandic band:

  • Jón Ólafsson — piano, vibraphone
  • Jóhann Hjörleifsson — drums, percussion, marimba
  • Róbert Þórhallsson — contrabass, bass
  • Haraldur Þorsteinsson — bass
  • Guðmundur Pétursson — guitar
  • Sigurður Sigurðsson — harmonica
  • Snorri Valsson — trumpet

Release ID: 1100747.

Fjallkonan — Partý (1995). Torrini sings on track 2 of this Icelandic group’s release. Shared personnel with Crouçie D’Où Là: Jón Ólafsson (keyboards, vocals), Jóhann Hjörleifsson (drums), Róbert Þórhallsson (bass), Stefán Hjörleifsson (guitar). Release ID: 8135337.

Lhooq — Untitled (Lhooq) (1995). Torrini contributes vocals to this release by the Icelandic group Lhooq, which also features Jóhann Jóhannsson (electronics) and Pétur Hallgrímsson (guitar). This is one of Jóhannsson’s earliest documented recording appearances. Release ID: 16908630.

Stone Free - Love (1996). Another release featuring Torrini alongside the same core Reykjavík session group: Jón Ólafsson (music director, keyboards, organ, piano, vocals), Jóhann Hjörleifsson (drums), Róbert Þórhallsson (bass, trumpet), Guðmundur Pétursson (guitar). Release ID: 6571008.

International Albums with Icelandic Personnel

Siggi Baldursson — The most sustained Icelandic collaboration of Torrini’s career, across both of her major-label albums.

  • Love In The Time Of Science (One Little Indian, 1999): Written-By credit on four tracks — “Easy” (6), “Fingertips” (7), “Telepathy” (8), and “Tuna Fish” (9) — all co-written with Torrini and Eg White. He also played Drums/Percussion on tracks 3 (“Baby Blue”), 7 (“Fingertips”), and 9 (“Tuna Fish”). (UK release ID: 380690; US pressing ID: 103650.)

  • Fisherman’s Woman (Rough Trade, 2005): Credited as both Lyrics By and Written-By on track 4, “Lifesaver” — co-written with Torrini and Dan Carey. His involvement on this album is confined to that single track. (Brazilian pressing ID: 6919096 is the only Discogs entry with full credits; other pressings have no additional credits listed.)

Jóhann Jóhannsson (also credited as Jóhann G. Jóhannsson, ID: 2055832 / 52220) — Co-wrote and played keyboards on Love In The Time Of Science (1999/2000). Appeared on the Lhooq release with Torrini in 1995.

One-Off Collaborations

Björk — Shared a split single All Is Full Of Love / Dead Things (1999) — a UK promo pairing Björk’s track with Torrini’s single. Not a performance collaboration but a notable co-release. Release ID: 15938609.

múm — Provided additional production on Torrini’s “Heartstopper” EP track (track 3), Rough Trade, 2005. Release ID: 455241.

Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson — Played trumpet on “Heartstopper” EP (track 2), 2005.

Valgeir Sigurðsson, Héðinn Unnsteinsson, Prinspóló (Svavar Pétur Eysteinsson) — All co-credited as primary artists on Vertu Úlfur - Plays By Unnur Ösp Steánsdóttir (2021), an Icelandic tribute/compilation. Also featured Marketa Irglova. Release ID: 19024321.

Sturla Þórisson (alias: Mio (3)) — Recorded Racing The Storm (Bella Union, 2023). Discogs ID: 557292.

Helgi Hrafn Jónsson — Co-credited on Crossroads (2019, release ID: 14472648).
