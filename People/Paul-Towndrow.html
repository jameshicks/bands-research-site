Paul Towndrow

Scottish saxophonist, composer, arranger, and educator. Born 13 December 1978. Discogs artist ID: 1171235.

Overview

Paul Towndrow is a Glasgow-based saxophonist with a wide footprint across Scottish jazz, indie, and folk-rock. He is a core member of the Scottish National Jazz Orchestra (SNJO) — the ensemble directed by Tommy Smith — and a founding member of the brass quintet Brass Jaw. As a session player he has worked across an unusually broad range of genres, from ECM jazz recordings to Matador-label indie, Celtic folk, and Rock Action indie-rock.

His most prominent recent session credits are on two Sacred Paws albums — Run Around The Sun (2019) and Jump Into Life (2025) — both produced by Tony Doogan on Rock Action Records. He also appeared across both of Belle & Sebastian’s 2022–23 Matador albums and on Hue & Cry’s Xmasday (2010), Hot Wire (2012), and Pocketful Of Stones (2017), the latter also crediting him as strings arranger. He has a longstanding Discogs primary release: Colours (2003, FMR Records), credited under his own name and produced by Ross Hamilton.

He also performs as part of the Paul Towndrow & Steve Hamilton Duo with pianist Steve Hamilton (3) (Discogs ID: 1231092).

Scene Connections

Towndrow’s session footprint maps onto three overlapping Scottish music scenes:

Scottish jazz: SNJO and Brass Jaw are the main ensembles. The ECM release Celebration (2012, produced by Manfred Eicher) places him in a significant international jazz context. Other SNJO credits include the Spartacus Records release Culloden Moor Suite (2014, feat. Bobby Wellins).

Glasgow indie/Rock Action orbit: The Sacred Paws connection runs through Tony Doogan and Rock Action Records. The brass trio of Towndrow / Owers / Quigley is a recurring unit on Glasgow indie sessions — they appear together on both Sacred Paws albums and on Capercaillie’s At The Heart Of It All (mixed by Tony Doogan at C.O.D. Studios, Glasgow).

Scottish folk and Celtic music: The Band From Rockall (Calum and Rory Macdonald of Runrig), Capercaillie, and Ross Ainslie (Pool, 2024, Great White Records) represent his folk-adjacent session work.

Glasgow/Matador indie: Belle & Sebastian’s A Bit Of Previous (2022) and Late Developers (2023) — both on Matador — used a brass section of Towndrow, Owers, and Quigley, with arrangements credited to Ryan Quigley and Sarah Martin (2).
