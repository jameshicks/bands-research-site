Levon Helm

American drummer, vocalist, and multi-instrumentalist. Born Mark Lavon Helm, 26 May 1940, Marvell, Arkansas (often cited as Helena, Arkansas); died 19 April 2012, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 252930. Father of Amy Helm.

The only American-born member of The Band, Helm was the group’s drummer and one of its primary vocalists throughout their career from 1968 to 1977. Before The Band, he backed Ronnie Hawkins as part of The Hawks (1958–1963). His post-Band career included the RCO All-Stars (1977–1978), three solo albums for ABC and MCA (1978–1982), and a late-career renaissance with two Grammy-winning Vanguard albums — Dirt Farmer (2007) and Electric Dirt (2009) — anchored by his Woodstock Midnight Ramble concert series.

Early Career: Ronnie Hawkins and The Hawks (1958–1966)

Helm began playing with Ronnie Hawkins around 1958, appearing on Hawkins’ early Roulette Records material (Ronnie Hawkins, 1959; “Mr. Dynamo”, 1960) and on the earliest Quality Records singles. By the mid-1960s, the Hawks — which had evolved to include Robertson, Danko, Manuel, and Hudson alongside Helm — were operating independently as Levon And The Hawks and, briefly, the Canadian Squires. This unit became Bob Dylan’s backing band for the 1965–66 electric tour; Helm left the touring band during that period due to the hostile crowd reactions but rejoined his bandmates in Woodstock by 1967 for the informal recordings that would become The Basement Tapes.

The Band (1968–1977)

The Band’s classic five-member lineup — Helm, Robbie Robertson, Rick Danko, Richard Manuel, and Garth Hudson — recorded eight studio albums for Capitol Records between 1968 and 1977. Helm sang lead on some of the group’s most celebrated tracks (“The Weight,” “Up on Cripple Creek,” “Rag Mama Rag,” “The Night They Drove Old Dixie Down”) and played drums, mandolin, and occasional guitar throughout.

The Band’s farewell concert (Thanksgiving 1976, Winterland, San Francisco) was filmed by Martin Scorsese and released as The Last Waltz (Warner Bros., 1978) — co-produced by Rob Fraboni, Robbie Robertson, and John Simon. Helm contributed drums, mandolin, vocals, and co-wrote “Out of the Blue” on the soundtrack. The guest roster included Bob Dylan, Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Van Morrison, Muddy Waters, Eric Clapton, and Neil Diamond, among others.

The Band also recorded and toured with Dylan on Planet Waves (Asylum, 1974) and Before The Flood (Asylum, 1974), both engineered by Fraboni, and appeared on the posthumously released The Basement Tapes (CBS, 1975).

Band Reunions (1983–1999)

Following Robertson’s departure after The Last Waltz, Helm continued The Band in various reunion configurations. The reconstituted lineup (with Jim Weider on guitar replacing Robertson, and later Randy Ciarlante as a second drummer) recorded Jericho (1993), High on the Hog (1996), and Jubilation (1998).

RCO All-Stars (1977–1978)

Helm formed The RCO All-Stars (named for RCO Studios, his Woodstock recording facility) as a supergroup vehicle for two ABC Records albums. The lineup on Levon Helm and the RCO All-Stars (1977) was a remarkable assembly of players:

  • Paul Butterfield — harmonica, vocals
  • Donald “Duck” Dunn — bass
  • Steve Cropper — guitar
  • Booker T. Jones — keyboards
  • Mac Rebennack (Dr. John) — keyboards, guitar, vocals
  • Howard Johnson (3) — baritone saxophone, tuba
  • Lou Marini — saxophone
  • Tom Malone — trombone
  • Alan Rubin — trumpet
  • Fred Carter, Jr. — guitar
  • Garth Hudson — accordion (on one track)
  • Robbie Robertson — guitar (on one track)

The album was recorded and produced by Helm, with Eddy Offord as recording engineer. A second album, Levon Helm (1978), followed on ABC with a largely different session lineup drawn from Muscle Shoals — produced by Donald “Duck” Dunn and featuring Roger Hawkins, David Hood, Barry Beckett, Steve Cropper, Jimmy Johnson, and Willie Hall.

The Levon Helm Band / Midnight Ramble

From around 2004, Helm began hosting Midnight Ramble concerts at his barn in Woodstock, New York — informal public performances in the tradition of traveling medicine shows. The concerts became a significant venue for late-career activity and attracted prominent guests. The Levon Helm Band, the ensemble that backed these shows and his studio records from Dirt Farmer onward, included:

  • Larry Campbell — guitar, fiddle, mandolin, vocals; music director and producer
  • Amy Helm — vocals, drums (Levon’s daughter)
  • Brian Mitchell — piano, organ, accordion, vocals
  • Teresa Williams — vocals, guitar, autoharp
  • Byron Isaacs — bass, vocals
  • Erik Lawrence — saxophone, trombone, tuba
  • Jay Collins — saxophone, vocals
  • Clark Gayton — trombone, tuba
  • Steven Bernstein — trumpet, cornet
  • Justin Guip — engineer (also recorded and mixed the studio albums)

The Midnight Ramble Sessions were documented on official releases; It’s Showtime: The Midnight Ramble Sessions Vol. 3 (Vanguard, 2014) appeared after his death.
