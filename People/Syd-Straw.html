Overview

Syd Straw (born Susan Straw Harris) is an American vocalist and songwriter who relocated from Los Angeles to New York in 1978 and became embedded in the city’s experimental and alternative rock circles. She first gained notice as a backup singer for Pat Benatar before joining The Golden Palominos — the fluid Anton Fier-led collective — from 1985 to 1987, a tenure that connected her with Bill Laswell, Marc Ribot, Bernie Worrell, and Jody Harris, who remain her most frequent collaborators by shared credits. Her solo catalogue, concentrated in the late 1980s and early 1990s and spanning Alternative Rock, Folk Rock, and Country Rock, appeared on Virgin and later Capricorn Records; she has since moved to Vermont.

Departure from The Golden Palominos and the Road to Virgin (web sources)

No single dramatic break is documented anywhere in the available record. The picture across multiple sources is consistent: Straw left because the Palominos period crystallised a desire to write and lead her own project, not because of a falling-out.

How Fier found her. Fier first encountered Straw in 1983 at the Bottom Line in New York, where she was performing with Van Dyke Parks. He was “struck by the beauty of her singing and personality, very intrigued.” He later invited her in as a background vocalist on Visions of Excess (1985), then gave her lead vocal duties after her early contributions impressed him — including writing lyrics for Jack Bruce’s tracks. [encyclopedia.com, https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/golden-palominos; The Wire / Anton Fier interview, https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/interviews/anton-fier-a-toast-to-the-restless-drummer]

Her stated reason for leaving. In her own words: “The coolest thing that came out of my time with the Palominos was a desire to begin writing my own songs. For the first time, I started thinking of myself as a writer, and not just an interpreter.” [musicianguide.com, https://www.musicianguide.com/biographies/1608001513/Syd-Straw.html; encyclopedia.com, https://www.encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/straw-syd — both citing the same quote; Indyweek.com profile, https://indyweek.com/music/thirty-years-cult-hero-syd-straw-stood-strong-cusp-fame/]

The relationship remained warm. Fier appeared as a guest on Surprise (1989), Straw’s debut, and as late as 2010 they were in discussions about a reunion project (a proposed “Silver Palominos” with members from the 1985–87 lineup). [Indyweek profile; The Wire / Fier interview]

The gap years (1987–1989). Between leaving the Palominos and releasing Surprise, Straw worked steadily as a background vocalist — clients included Richard Thompson, Rickie Lee Jones, and Leo Kottke. [musicianguide.com, https://www.musicianguide.com/biographies/1608001513/Syd-Straw.html]

The Virgin deal. No source documents how the signing was specifically brokered. What is known: the album Surprise was released on Virgin Records America in 1989; it was recorded “over a year in a variety of studios” across multiple locations; Straw described it as “an excuse to travel all over the world and visit people, and I called it a record.” The album featured Michael Stipe, Richard Thompson, Van Dyke Parks, Ry Cooder, Daniel Lanois, John Doe, Marshall Crenshaw, Dave Alvin, and former Palominos members Jody Harris, Bernie Worrell, and Fier. [encyclopedia.com Straw entry; Trouser Press review, https://trouserpress.com/reviews/syd-straw/; Wikipedia Surprise article, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surprise_(Syd_Straw_album) as of 2026-09-28]

Critical reception of Surprise. Ray Rogers of Interview called it “dazzling”; Entertainment Weekly’s Tony Scherman called it a “first-rate debut”; The Sacramento Bee listed it among 1989’s best debut albums. It sold poorly. [Wikipedia Surprise article; encyclopedia.com]
