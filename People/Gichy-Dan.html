Jul 13, 20267 min read

Gichy Dan

Real name: Frank Passalacqua (also credited as Frank ‘Pago Pago’ Passalacqua) Discogs ID: 30095 Active: 1979–2005 (releases); died of AIDS (date unknown) Origin: Staten Island, New York Roles: Vocalist, performer

Silken-voiced Staten Island crooner whose name came from “Beachwood #9,” an old 1950s rock and roll number. He was a founding figure in the downtown New York scene around August Darnell and Coati Mundi, predating Kid Creole And The Coconuts as an outlet for Darnell’s songwriting. In Darnell’s own words: “Gichy Dan was the precursor to Kid Creole and the Coconuts; had I known it, he would have been Kid Creole. I knew he could handle the material.” Adriana Kaegi described him as a potential “new Johnny Mathis” who died prematurely from AIDS.

Biography

Origins and Beachwood #9 (1979)

Passalacqua took the stage name Gichy Dan from the title of a 1950s rock and roll song. He became part of the circle around August Darnell (Thomas Browder) and Andy “Coati Mundi” Hernandez in the late 1970s, at the moment the ZE Records downtown New York scene was taking shape. Darnell had written songs that were rejected by Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band; those songs became the raw material for the Beachwood #9 project.

His primary vehicle was Gichy Dan’s Beachwood - 9, the band project that released a full LP on RCA Victor in 1979. The album was produced by Darnell and Ron Rogers, with all songs written by Darnell and horn/string arrangements by both Darnell and Hernandez — effectively a showcase for Darnell’s compositional style with Gichy Dan as the featured voice. The record was pressed and released in France (cat# PL 12938), in the Disco/Latin styles that would define the early ZE sound.

The LP was recorded at Blank Tape Studios (New York City), the studio run by engineer Bob Blank, who served as recording engineer. It was subsequently mixed at RCA Recording Studios (New York) by Warren Schatz, Howard (“Doctor”) Lindeman, and Al Garrison. Darnell later described the situation bitterly: “Warren Schatz, who was the head of RCA at that time, had raped my Gichy Dan album by mixing it himself, which was a very painful experience.” (Schatz was National VP of A&R at RCA from 1976.) The album was credited to Gichy Dan Music Productions, Inc. (source)

The LP found an audience through college radio in New York City and through play by DJs at the Mudd Club. (source)

Coati Mundi described the project: “We would concoct this romance thing, then make up some profound story.”

Live appearances with the Darnell circle

Passalacqua was not primarily a touring artist, but he did appear on stage as a special guest at “The August Darnell Revue” at the ’80s club in New York (March, date uncertain — likely early 1980s). (source) Whether Gichy Dan’s Beachwood - 9 ever performed as a full live band is not documented in any available source; available evidence points to it being a studio project.

He also appeared as a vocalist on the Kid Creole And The Coconuts live circuit in a backing role, and participated in the Elbow Bones and the Racketeers project (1983), described as a “sort of Coconuts reserves team.” (source)

ZE Records orbit (1980–1983)

When Kid Creole And The Coconuts formed and began recording for Antilles/ZE, Gichy Dan appeared as a guest artist. He is credited on the 1980 single Me No Pop I (Antilles AN 807), the debut Kid Creole/Coati Mundi release, alongside the full ensemble. He also provided harmony vocals on the Kid Creole debut LP Off The Coast Of Me (Antilles/ZE, 1980) — specifically on tracks “Yolanda,” “Bogota Affair,” and “Calypso Pan American.”

He appears on the 1983 Kid Creole album Doppelganger (Sire/ZE) with a “Special Appearances By” credit, and provided backing vocals on Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s Piranana (Island/ZE, 1983).

Solo release on Celluloid/ZE (1981)

Gichy Dan released the 12” single Cowboys & Gangsters on Celluloid Records / ZE Records (cat# CEL 6593) in France in 1981. The single (with b-side “Action Man” and an extended “Version Longue”) was written and produced by Ron Rogers. No contemporary chart position or press review has been located. The track later became a cult dance floor item in the 2000s through remixes and DJ interest, described in ZE Records’ own reissue notes as having become a cult hit. (source) It was edited and reissued on the small US label Editions Disco (2005) as a split 7” with Roy Ayers’s “Chicago.”

Continued backing vocal work (1985–1993)

Gichy Dan continued as a backing vocalist on Kid Creole releases through the late 1980s and into the 1990s:

  • In Praise Of Older Women And Other Crimes (Sire, 1985)
  • I, Too Have Seen The Woods (Sire, 1987)
  • Private Waters In The Great Divide (CBS, 1990)
  • To Travel Sideways (Atoll/Ascot Music, 1993) — compilation appearance

Gichy Dan’s Beachwood - 9 also appeared as cameo performers on Cristina’s 2004 ZE Records reissue Doll In The Box.

Open Questions

  • Exact year/circumstances of death (died of AIDS — the official Kid Creole website states the year “is kept anonymous”; no date in any public source found as of 2026-05-17). One web commenter (The New Vinyl Villain, 2024) speculates “somebody online says he died of AIDS in the early 80s,” but the same author notes this contradicts his credit on To Travel Sideways (1993), making any pre-1993 death date impossible. Death must therefore be post-1993. No obituary, memorial quilt entry, or named-byline source gives a year. NYC death records, Legacy.com, Find A Grave, quiltindex.org (NAMES Project), aidsmemorial.org, AllMusic, Wikipedia, Last.fm, and multiple August Darnell interviews (Quietus 2011, Classic Pop, The Afterword, Don Shewey, Rolling Stone) all checked — none contain a year.
