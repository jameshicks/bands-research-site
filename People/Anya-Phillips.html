Anya Phillips

Fashion designer, club co-founder, scene organizer. Born February 1955, Taipei County, Taiwan. Died June 19, 1981, Valhalla, New York (brain cancer), age 26.

Overview

Central figure in the late-1970s NYC No Wave / downtown Manhattan scene — as fashion designer, Mudd Club co-founder, and music manager. Her role was structural to the scene beyond any single band.

Biography

Grew up in Taiwan and on US military bases; stepfather was Wade Phillips, a US military attaché. Her brother is singer Fei Xiang (birth name Kris Phillips). Attended Taipei American School, where she befriended Sylvia Reed (later Sylvia Morales, who married Lou Reed).

Moved to New York City in 1974 to attend Parsons School of Design on a full scholarship, but left almost immediately due to aversion to formal training.

Worked as a photojournalist for New York Rocker and Punk magazine; her byline at Punk was “Anya Chowmein.”

Fashion: Designed clothes by knotting and tying latex and fabric strips (she did not sew). Debbie Harry wore her pieces on the cover of Blondie’s Plastic Letters (1978) and on the 1983 single “Rush Rush.” Phillips shot and designed the cover for James Chance and the Contortions’ debut LP Buy (1979). Her approach has been cited as an influence on Anna Sui. Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-25

Mudd Club: Co-founded the Mudd Club at 77 White St, lower Manhattan, in 1978 with filmmaker Steve Maas and art curator Diego Cortez. She envisioned it as “the Molotov Cocktail Lounge.” Left following a dispute with Maas. The club became a central social hub for No Wave and the downtown arts scene until 1983. Wikipedia — Mudd Club

Management: Managed The Contortions / James Chance and briefly managed Lydia Lunch. She also helped organize the “Bands at Artists Space” event (May 1978) that produced No New York.

Within the Contortions, Phillips’s management created internal tension. According to Pat Place, Phillips “had visions of James being this big star” and pushed to pay band members session fees rather than treating them as equal partners — a shift that fractured the band’s collective dynamic. The final break came when Phillips and Chance reportedly spent the Paris gig money (Spring 1978) on drugs rather than distributing it. Place and the other members departed; Place chose to leave with the band rather than remain in Chance’s orbit. Perfect Sound Forever — Pat Place interview

Other: Performed a dominatrix act at CBGB with Terence Sellers. Made a rare vocal appearance on the Contortions’ 1980 Rotterdam concert recording (released 1990). Appeared in Amos Poe’s 1978 film The Foreigner.

Illness and Death

Diagnosed with cancer in 1979. A benefit concert at Bond’s International Casino in May 1981 — featuring Debbie Harry, Nile Rodgers, and Bernard Edwards — was organized to fund her chemotherapy. She died June 19, 1981, in Valhalla, New York, age 26. James Chance dedicated his 1982 album Sax Maniac to her.

Connections

  • James Chance — partner and management client
  • Pat Place — Contortions bandmate of Chance; Phillips’s management approach (solo-star strategy, session-fee pay structure) was the immediate cause of Place’s departure from the band
  • Lydia Lunch — management client (briefly)
  • Diego Cortez — Mudd Club co-founder
  • Debbie Harry — friend; wore Phillips’s designs
  • No Wave — central organizer
