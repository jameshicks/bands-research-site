Chad Taylor

Drummer, percussionist, composer, and educator. Born March 19, 1973, in Tempe, Arizona; based in Chicago, IL. Discogs artist ID: 304285. One of the most active drummers in Chicago’s creative music and free jazz scenes from the late 1990s onward. Co-founder of the Chicago Underground Duo with Rob Mazurek and its several ensemble extensions (Trio, Quartet, Orchestra); core member of Jaimie Branch’s fly or die quartet across all four records; has also led his own small groups and recorded widely as a sideman in free jazz, avant-garde, and indie rock contexts. Name variations in Discogs credits include C. E. Taylor, C. Taylor, CT, Chad, and Chad (Leonardo).

Biography

Early Chicago Scene (1995–2003)

Taylor’s earliest Discogs credit is a 1995 German release (Yorksters In Boston, Troll Records) where he appears on guitar — suggesting multi-instrumentalism from early on. By 1998 he was embedded in Chicago’s experimental scene, playing on Alan Licht / Loren Mazzacane-Connors’ Hoffman Estates (Drag City) and appearing on two foundational Chicago Underground records: Playground (the Chicago Underground Orchestra debut on Delmark Records) and 12° Of Freedom (the Duo debut on Thrill Jockey).

In this period Taylor also appeared on Sam Prekop’s self-titled 1999 LP (Thrill Jockey), with co-writing and arrangement credits — an early link to the Thrill Jockey post-rock/experimental circle that included John McEntire and Jeff Parker.

The Taylor / Parker / Chris Lopes trio emerged as a distinct working unit, releasing Like-coping (2003, Delmark Records) as co-leaders. The record was co-produced by Taylor, Parker, Lopes, John McEntire, and Robert G. Koester.

The Sticks and Stones trio (Discogs ID: 1011993) with Matana Roberts (saxophone) and Joshua Abrams (bass) also operated in this period, performing at Chicago’s Velvet Lounge.

Chicago Underground Years (1998–2025)

The Chicago Underground Duo, Trio, Quartet, and Orchestra have been Taylor’s most sustained single collaborative relationship — a partnership with Rob Mazurek that spans nearly three decades and a dozen releases.

Marc Ribot / Spiritual Unity (2005)

Taylor drummed on Marc Ribot’s Spiritual Unity (2005, Pi Recordings) alongside Henry Grimes (bass) and Roy Campbell (trumpet/pocket trumpet). An Albert Ayler tribute record — Taylor appearing among the key players in that project links him directly to the downtown New York free jazz circle.

fly or die Quartet (2017–2023)

Taylor was the drummer in Jaimie Branch’s fly or die quartet for all four records on International Anthem Recording Company, and is credited with co-composition and liner notes on the later albums.

Broader Sideman Work

Taylor’s sideman credits span indie rock (Iron and Wine’s Kiss Each Other Clean, 4AD, 2011; Sam Prekop’s 1999 LP on Thrill Jockey), free jazz (Marc Ribot’s Spiritual Unity; Fred Anderson Quartet and Trio on Delmark), and international sessions (Aruán Ortiz Trio, Intakt Records).
