Coati Mundi

Coati Mundi

Real name: Andy Hernandez (also credited as “Sugar-Coated” Andy Hernandez, Coati M. Hernandez) Discogs ID: 30097 Active: 1979–2021 (Discogs range) Origin: New York, NY (Latin-American community) Roles: Percussionist, vibraphone, marimba, flute, keyboards, lead vocals, producer, arranger

Coati Mundi is a New York multi-instrumentalist and vocalist best known as a founding co-lead of Kid Creole And The Coconuts, the tropical / Latin-tinged dance-theater group he co-created with his cousin August Darnell (Kid Creole). He released solo material on ZE Records and Virgin in the early 1980s, and later produced for Rong Music in the 2000s. A cousin of August Darnell (born Thomas August Darnell Browder), Hernandez was credited across ZE Records’ downtown New York catalog as both a performer and co-producer, and was a key musical architect of the Kid Creole sound.

Biography

Origins and ZE Records (1979–1983)

Andy Hernandez grew up in New York and was part of the extended Browder/Darnell family network that formed the core of the Kid Creole enterprise. When August Darnell and Michael Zilkha co-founded ZE Records circa 1978–79, Hernandez was immediately embedded in the operation — first appearing as a featured player on Aural Exciters’ “Spooks In Space” (ZE, 1979), an early downtown New York disco-funk hybrid. He co-wrote “Maladie D’Amour” on the first Kid Creole album.

In 1980 Hernandez launched his solo career on ZE / Antilles with the 12” “Me No Pop I” (ZE/Antilles AN 807), a Latin-disco track on which he sang lead, played vibraphone and flute, and co-produced with August Darnell. The single was credited jointly to Kid Creole & The Coconuts and Coati Mundi, establishing the dual-identity framework that would persist through the ZE years. Multiple pressings appeared internationally (Spain, France) through 1981, including the single “I’m A Wonderful Thing (Baby)” (Phonogram, France) — a UK Top 10 for Kid Creole — and “You, You, You / Que Pasa” (Ariola, Spain).

Kid Creole Albums (1980–1983)

As a core member of Kid Creole And The Coconuts, Hernandez played on all the ZE-era albums:

  • Off The Coast Of Me (Island/ZE, 1980) — vibraphone and orchestration on the majority of tracks; engineered by Bob Blank and Julian McBrowne
  • Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places (Island/ZE, 1981) — co-producer and arranger; “Musica Americana” and “I Am” written by Hernandez; the album’s theatrical concept credited to Darnell, with Hernandez co-directing
  • Doppelganger (Sire/ZE, 1983) — credited as associate producer and “The Boys In The Band” performer alongside the full Kid Creole ensemble

Solo Albums on Virgin (1983)

By 1983 Hernandez had signed as a solo artist to Virgin Records and released:

  • “Como Esta Usted?” (How Are You?) (Virgin VS 585-12, UK, 1983) — 12” single, engineered by Julian McBrowne, produced by Hernandez as “Sugar Coated Andy Hernandez”
  • “Oh! That Love Decision” (Virgin, Europe, 1983) — single
  • The Former 12 Year Old Genius (Virgin 205 396, Germany, 1983) — his full-length solo debut, credited to “Little Coati Mundi.” Produced and arranged by Hernandez himself; he played lead vocals, vibraphone, marimba, flute, percussion, and keyboards throughout. Personnel included recurring Kid Creole players: Carol Coleman (bass), Mark Mazur (guitar), Peter Schott (piano), Lori Eastside (backing vocals), Gichy Dan (backing vocals), and guest appearances by Don Van Vliet (Captain Beefheart, written-by credit on “Tropical Hot Dog Night”) and Ruben Blades (cameo on “Tropical Hot Dog Night”). Also featured string sections led by Jill Jaffe and Marin Alsop, and horn arrangements by Charlie Lagond. The album was mixed by The Hit Squad (2) and engineered by Julian McBrowne.

Later Work and Film Soundtracks (1984–2010)

Hernandez appears on Hall & Oates’ Big Bam Boom (RCA, 1984) in a spoken “Dissertation In Español.” He contributed a remix to Gloria Estefan’s “Hey Mambo” (Arista, 1988) and appeared on the all-star reggae compilation (HBO Video, 1988) with a featured vocal credit.

He wrote “Que Pasa Contigo,” co-written with The Sherman Brothers (2), which appeared on the Kid Creole track “I, Too Have Seen The Woods” (Sire, 1987) and was re-recorded for various pressings through 2007.

In 1998 Hernandez produced both volumes of the 54 (Music From The Miramax Motion Picture) soundtracks (Tommy Boy), serving as “Miramax Soundtrack Album Producer” for the Studio 54 biopic. He also contributed vocals to “Subalo Parriba (Move It Up)” (Jellybean Recordings, 1998).

His later solo work appeared on Rong Music: the 12” No More Blues (Rong 029, UK, 2009), co-composed with Elan Polushko and Lee Douglas, and the album Dancing For The Cabana Code In The Land Of Boo-Hoo (Rong CD07, US, 2010), co-produced with Elan Polushko (credited as “E-Love (2)”). Rong Music, run by Todd Terje associate Todd Terje network affiliates, positioned this as a disco / Latin dance revival release.
