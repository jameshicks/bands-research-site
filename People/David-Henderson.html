David Henderson

Scottish vocalist, guitarist, and songwriter. Discogs artist ID: 272672. Also credited as “Davy Henderson,” “Davey Henderson,” “Dave Henderson,” and “D. Henderson.”

Wikipedia: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davy_Henderson

Biography

Origins and the Glasgow Post-Punk Moment (1979–1981)

David Henderson emerged from the Scottish post-punk scene centred on Edinburgh and Glasgow in the late 1970s. He became vocalist and guitarist of Fire Engines, which also featured Graham Main (bass), Murray Slade (guitar), and Russell Burn (drums). The band’s earliest recorded output was the double A-side “Get Up And Use Me / Everything’s Roses” (1980, Codex Communications), produced without an outside producer and engineered by Wilf Smarties. This was followed in 1981 by two releases on Bob Last’s Pop Aural imprint — the single “Big Gold Dream” and the mini-LP Lubricate Your Living Room — both produced by Bob Last, with Henderson and the full band co-credited as songwriters. “Big Gold Dream” featured additional keyboards from Simon Best and backing vocals from Karen Brown (2) and Hl Ray.

Pop Aural was the sister outlet to Fast Product, Bob Last’s influential Edinburgh-based label (whose URL on the Pop Aural Discogs page links directly to an archived Fast Product discography page). Fire Engines therefore sit at the Fast Product nexus alongside Josef K, The Human League (early releases), and Gang of Four — the post-punk network that Fast Product helped broker.

Win (1985–1989)

After Fire Engines dissolved Henderson joined Win, a Glasgow pop-soul group also featuring former Fire Engine Russell Burn, alongside Ian Stoddart, Mani Shoniwa, Willy Perry, and Simon Smeeton. Win signed to Swamplands (1985 debut) and then London Records and Virgin Records, releasing a run of singles and at least one LP. Henderson is credited on Win releases as vocalist, guitarist, keyboardist, and songwriter.

Win occupied a mainstream pop trajectory quite different from Fire Engines’ abrasive minimalism — the London/Virgin move placed them in the realm of chart-oriented UK pop. Henderson’s written-by and multi-instrument credits suggest a central compositional role.

Pie Finger and Heartbeat (7) (early 1990s)

Around 1992, Henderson was a listed member of two short-lived projects:

  • Pie Finger (Discogs 900214): also included Nick Prescott and Russell Burn (again). Their only Discogs release is “A Dali Surprise” (Creation Records, 1992) — though Henderson does not appear in the release credits for that single (only Burn, Prescott, and Julio Ilonis are credited). His membership on Discogs may reflect an early lineup or uncredited contribution.
  • Heartbeat (7) (Discogs 944950): also included Hilary Morrison and James Locke. No releases appear in the Discogs database under this band.

The Nectarine No. 9 (1992–2004)

Henderson reconvened with the Glasgow post-punk milieu through The Nectarine No. 9, which he co-formed with Gareth Sager (ex-The Pop Group, Rip Rig & Panic). The full lineup also included Ian Holford (drums), Simon Smeeton (guitar/vocals), John (Todd) Thompson, and King Edward (3).

The band’s debut was “A Sea With Three Stars” (Postcard Records, 1992), with sleeve design credited to Alan Horne (Postcard founder). Subsequent releases moved through Postcard, Sano Music, and eventually Creeping Bent / Beggars Banquet.

Henderson is credited as vocalist, guitarist, and songwriter throughout. On I ♥ Total Destruction (2004), the band is credited as playing “Fender Jaguars” — a self-description consistent with their spiky, trebly sound. Gareth Sager contributed “Black Pudding Edits” as a production credit.

The connection to Postcard Records is notable: Postcard (Alan Horne’s label, home to Orange Juice, Josef K, Aztec Camera) was the other cardinal point of Glasgow art-pop alongside Fast Product. Having debuted on Fast Product’s orbit (Pop Aural) and then landed on Postcard for Nectarine’s early releases, Henderson’s career traces the Glasgow post-punk map precisely.

The Sexual Objects (2007–present)

The Sexual Objects is Henderson’s current ongoing group, co-founded with Douglas MacIntyre (founder of Creeping Bent). The stable lineup: Henderson (vocals, guitar, written-by), MacIntyre (bass, vocals), Ian Holford (drums, vocals), and Simon Smeeton (guitar, vocals) — with Holford and Smeeton carried over from The Nectarine No. 9.

Outside Work and Guest Appearances

  • Gareth Sager — Slack Slack Music (2018, Creeping Bent, BENT 088): Henderson appears as lead vocalist and backing vocalist on tracks 4, 8, and 11 of Sager’s solo album, alongside Ian Holford (drums, backing vocals). The album was produced by Howard Gray and engineered by Ashley Krajewski. Sandy Dillon sings on track 6.
  • Jock Scot — My Personal Culloden (2015, Forever Heavenly): Henderson produced this album for spoken-word poet Jock Scot and played as a musician, bringing the full Nectarine/Sexual Objects rhythm section (Holford, Thompson, Smeeton). Henderson wrote lyrics for 13 of the 18 tracks.
  • The Secret Goldfish — Petal Split (2017, Creeping Bent): Henderson contributed a co-write on track A1 (alongside Simon Smeeton). MacIntyre produced the whole LP.
  • Gareth Sager And The Hungry Ghosts — various: Henderson’s continued involvement in Sager’s projects reflects the deep continuity of the Nectarine / Creeping Bent scene into the 2010s.
  • The Maskatonians All Stars & Friends (2007, Pepe Lobo Rekords, Mexico): Henderson credited as Trumpet — likely a one-off guest appearance.
  • Primal Scream — More Light (2013): Henderson credited as Lead Guitar on one pressing (Russia). His exact role on this session is not fully documented in Discogs.
  • Compilations: Henderson appears on Big Gold Dream (2007, Acute Records) — the definitive Fast Product / Pop Aural / Postcard compilation — and Hungry Beat (2007, Acute Records) — a broader Scottish post-punk survey.

Scene Context

Henderson’s career maps the full arc of Scottish post-punk and its afterlives:

Fast Product / Pop Aural era (1980–1981): Fire Engines sat at the heart of Bob Last’s Edinburgh operation, alongside Josef K, Gang of Four (released via Fast), and The Human League’s early output. The Pop Aural imprint (whose archived URL connects directly to the Fast Product discography site) was essentially Fast’s Glasgow-facing outlet. Henderson was the vocalist at the sharp end of the scene’s most wiry, repetitive, post-funk wing.

Postcard adjacency (1992–1995): The Nectarine No. 9 launched on Postcard Records, putting Henderson in direct relationship with Alan Horne — who not only ran the label but designed the sleeve for their debut single. Postcard had earlier launched Orange Juice, Josef K, and Aztec Camera. The Nectarine’s arrival on Postcard a decade after its heyday was both a revival and a continuation of the Glasgow art-pop thread.

Creeping Bent era (2001–present): From the Nectarine’s Beggars co-release onwards, Henderson’s primary scene context has been Creeping Bent — Douglas MacIntyre’s label, which positioned itself as a living archive and ongoing creative project for the Glasgow lineage. The Sexual Objects, the Gareth Sager collaborations, and guest appearances on label releases by the Secret Goldfish and Jock Scot all anchor him here.

Gareth Sager connection: The alliance with Sager (ex-The Pop Group, Bristol) extends Henderson’s network beyond Scotland into the wider UK post-punk formation. Sager and Henderson have worked together across Nectarine and solo contexts for over thirty years.
