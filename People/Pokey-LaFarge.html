Pokey LaFarge

Real name: Andrew Heissler Born: 1983 Location: Nashville, TN (based) Genres: American roots, country, jazz, Western swing, hokum blues Active: 2003–present Discogs ID: 2180810

American musician and songwriter specialising in pre-war American roots styles — country, ragtime jazz, Dixieland, and Western swing — delivered with vintage instruments and a period-appropriate bandstand sensibility. His core touring group, The South City Three, anchors his sound; the roster expands into horn sections on larger productions. Came to wider attention through Third Man Records (2011–2013), then moved into Americana mainstream via Rounder Records and New West Records. In 2017 he played Hank Snow in the CMT television series Sun Records.

Career Overview

Early Years and Free Dirt Period (2003–2011)

LaFarge first appears on Discogs as a member of The Schwillbillies, contributing mandolin and fiddle to their 2003 recording Ghost Train (The Schwillbillies l.l.c.). His solo trajectory began in earnest with Free Dirt Records, which released his debut solo LP Riverboat Soul (2010, master 2113750) — produced by LaFarge himself — and the follow-up Middle Of Everywhere (2011, master 465747), credited jointly to Pokey LaFarge and The South City Three and recorded with that trio’s vintage-instrument lineup plus additional brass. A live set, Live In Holland (2012, Free Dirt), documented the early touring configuration.

Third Man Records Period (2011–2013)

Jack White (2) produced the Third Man debut, Chittlin’ Cookin’ Time In Cheatham County (2011, TMR, master 454438), co-credited to The South City Three. White and Vance Powell handled mixing; the session captures the stripped-down parlour-blues end of LaFarge’s range.

The self-titled LP Pokey LaFarge (2013, TMR, cat. TMR 206, release ID 4618924) was co-produced with Ketch Secor of Old Crow Medicine Show — an important alignment with the Nashville old-time revival. A second Third Man LP, Central Time (2013, TMR, master 871392), was engineered by Morgan Jahnig and co-produced with Secor, with Adam Hoskins serving as associate producer; all songs written and arranged by LaFarge.

Rounder Records Period (2015–2017)

Something In The Water (2015, Rounder, master 835682) was co-produced by LaFarge and Jimmy Sutton, and features the largest assembled cast of the period: core band members Adam Hoskins, Joey Glynn, and Ryan Koenig, plus Chicago jazz players Alex Hall (4), Joel Paterson, Scott Ligon, Paul Asaro, Andy Schumm, and Chloe Feoranzo.

Manic Revelations (2017, Rounder, master 1178972) marks a stylistic shift — produced by the South City Collective (Discogs ID 5670684) with Tony Hoffer mixing, moving away from acoustic roots toward a fuller band sound. LaFarge also appeared on the Still The King: Celebrating The Music Of Bob Wills And His Texas Playboys tribute compilation (2015, Proper Records).

New West Records Period (2020–present)

Rock Bottom Rhapsody (2020, New West, master 1711315) was produced by Chris Seefried (also on guitar, piano, organ, and Mellotron), with Alex Hall (4) as recording engineer and drummer. The album adds string arrangements by Paul Cartwright and features backing vocals from Casey McDonough, Marley Munroe, and Marieme Diop.

In The Blossom Of Their Shade (2021, New West, master 2312956) was produced and arranged jointly by LaFarge and Seefried, with Hall again engineering and mixing. Mastering split between Fred Kevorkian (most tracks) and Ryan Smith (2).

Rhumba Country (2024, New West, master 3483460) continues the LaFarge/Seefried/Hall production team, with Elliot Bergman added as co-producer and engineer, and Kennie Takahashi handling the majority of the mixing.

LaFarge also appeared in the American Epic (The First Time America Heard Itself) documentary soundtrack (2017, PBS).
