Stephan Eicher

Swiss singer-songwriter, born 17 August 1960 in Münchenbuchsee, near Bern. Discogs artist ID: 164282.

Overview

Stephan Eicher is the rare Swiss artist who broke into the French popular market entirely — a career arc that starts in the punk/wave underground of early-1980s Switzerland, passes through an anomalous production alliance with Ed Bahlman (the NYC no-wave/post-punk producer behind 99 Records, ESG, and Liquid Liquid), and ends up at Barclay and Universal France where he became a major Francophone pop figure. He records and performs in French, German, and English, and has been active continuously from 1980 to the present.

He is the brother of Martin Eicher, who co-founded Grauzone with him.

Early Career and Swiss Underground (1980–1982)

Eicher’s first credited release is the 1980 7” Stephan Eicher Spielt Noise Boys on Off Course Records (catno ASL-19), an all-instruments solo effort on which he also handled artwork. The songs were written by Noise Boys (2) — a project he was a member of.

Simultaneously, Eicher was a core member of Grauzone, the Swiss post-punk/new wave band formed in Basel around 1980–81. In Grauzone he sang, played guitar, bass, synthesizer, saxophone, drum machine, and contributed cover design. Grauzone released material on both Off Course Records and EMI Electrola / Welt-Rekord, their biggest track being “Eisbær” (1981). The group also appears in a 1982 EMI release Träume Mit Mir on which Eicher contributed artwork.

The 1982 EP Souvenir (Off Course Records, ASL-1225) marks his first solo production credit. He co-produced and co-arranged it with Etienne Conod, a Swiss composer and studio owner (Sunrise Studios, Switzerland), and also played all instruments. Conod was the session’s engineer and contributed piano, Hammond organ, and chorus.

The Ed Bahlman Sessions (1983)

The pivot point of Eicher’s early catalog is the LP Les Chansons Bleues (1983, Off Course Records, ASL 3310) and the 7” Sweetheart (1983, ASL-29), both co-produced and co-arranged by Ed Bahlman — the New York-based founder of 99 Records (2) and the key producer behind ESG, Liquid Liquid, and Bush Tetras.

The mastering at Masterdisk NYC (Bill Kipper) is documented in the matrix runout: ASL 3310 A² MI MASTERDISK BK EDP SWISS MADE. The matrix suffix BK is Bill Kipper’s initials. This locates at least the mastering process in New York, consistent with Bahlman’s presence there at the same time he was finishing the final ESG and Liquid Liquid records (Bahlman’s 99 Records credits for Come Away With ESG and Optimo are also dated 1983).

NYC infrastructure on both releases

Conod is Swiss-based and the likely recording studio contact. Bahlman’s role appears to be incoming from New York — the same year he produced Come Away With ESG and Optimo for Liquid Liquid in his last 99 Records burst. The mechanism of how they connected is not documented in Discogs.

1988 compilation as postscript

Les Chansons Bleues / Souvenir / Noise Boys (1988, Off Course Records, ASL-CD 10-2) compiles the 1983 sessions (tracks 1–9, produced/arranged/mixed by Bahlman + Eicher) alongside the 1982 Souvenir EP and Noise Boys material (tracks 10–13, produced by Etienne Conod, Urs Steiger, and Eicher). This is Bahlman’s last production credit in Discogs — 1988, five years after the original sessions. Its appearance here may simply reflect reissue credits applied to original work.

Transition to Major Labels (1984–1985)

In 1984 Eicher released the “Nice” 7” single on Metronome (Germany, catno 821 145-7), self-produced and self-arranged. The mastering on this single was done at Frankford/Wayne, New York (matrix: MASTERING BY FRANKFORD/WAYNE NEW YORK) — another NYC mastering house, suggesting Eicher’s management continued to route mastering through New York-connected facilities even after the Bahlman sessions.

In the same year, his song “Les Filles Du Limmatquai” (which had appeared on Les Chansons Bleues) was released in France on Polydor with over 60 pressings catalogued — his first substantial French commercial breakthrough.

For the 1985 album I Tell This Night (Barclay, France), Eicher hired New York-based producer Harvey Goldberg (who worked at a New York label) and recording engineer Don Wershba. The session also credits Klaudia Schifferle as co-writer on several tracks. Eicher played all instruments himself; mixing assistants included Don Peterkofsky and Fernando Kral.

Barclay Period: Major Francophone Career (1987–1993)

The Silence LP (1987, Barclay) was fully self-produced by Eicher, with David M. Allen mixing (Allen is best known for his work with The Cure and Depeche Mode). The record features Madeline Bell on backing vocals, and sessions recorded with engineers Sid Wells and Christian “Djoum” Ramon.

Engelberg (1991, Barclay/Mercury) was produced with Dominique Blanc-Francard, the French recording engineer and producer (father of Boom Bass and Sinclair (3), brother of Patrice Blanc-Francard). The Engelberg sessions brought in Pino Palladino on bass and Manu Katché on drums — two of the most-used session musicians in European pop/rock of the period. Max Lässer contributed guitar. Mixing was also by Blanc-Francard. Engelberg gave Eicher major singles in French-language markets (“Hemmige,” “Déjeuner En Paix,” “Engelberg,” “Pas D’ami (Comme Toi)”).

Carcassonne (1993, Barclay) continued with Blanc-Francard producing and mixing, and brought in Richard Lloyd (Television) on electric guitar, Pino Palladino on bass, Manu Katché on drums, and Sonny Landreth on slide/acoustic guitar. The album reached medievally-inflected territory with hurdy gurdy, cornemuse, crumhorn, oud, and psaltery — the 1993 Occitan sessions.

Open Questions

  • The Bahlman connection: How Eicher came to work with Ed Bahlman is not documented in Discogs. Bahlman’s Swiss credits appear sandwiched between his final 99 Records productions (1983: Come Away With ESG, Optimo) and the store’s collapse (1985–86). No other Swiss or European credits appear in Bahlman’s entire discography — only Eicher. This is a prime candidate for web/archival research.
  • Noise Boys (2): Only Eicher is listed as a member in Discogs, but the 1988 comp credits the group as “Written-By” for several tracks. Were there other members? The 1980 solo EP Spielt Noise Boys (“plays Noise Boys”) implies Eicher was performing Noise Boys songs, possibly as a solo interpreter of a group he co-wrote with.
  • “Zorro Schlubowitz” alias: A 1981 release credited to this alias appeared on Solid & Elegant Kassetten (Switzerland). Discogs has no profile text for this alias and no additional releases under it.
  • Starter: Listed as a group Eicher belongs to (ID 145009). A 1981 Germany release on Repertoire Records is credited to Starter with Eicher’s role as Written-By. Needs investigation.
