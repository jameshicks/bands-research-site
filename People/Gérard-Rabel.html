Overview

Gérard Rabel is a creative figure whose Discogs credits are concentrated in the deathrock and goth rock sphere of the early-to-mid 1980s, appearing primarily in a “Concept By” capacity. His closest association is with Christian Death and that band’s core membership — Rozz Williams, Gitane Demone, Valor, David Glass, Eric Westfall, and Constance Smith — who together account for the majority of his 36 catalogue credits. The bulk of his work is tied to the French label L’Invitation Au Suicide, which carries nine of his credits between 1982 and 1985, with further appearances on Closer Records and Contempo Records through the late 1980s. A single isolated credit dates to 1974, a decade before his main activity.

Notes (web)

Connection to Christian Death — via L’Invitation Au Suicide

Web research conducted 2026-10-19 on the question of whether Rabel’s Christian Death credits came through L’Invitation Au Suicide or a prior direct connection to Rozz Williams.

Summary finding: All available evidence points to the L’Invitation Au Suicide pathway. No source places Rabel in contact with Williams outside the label context.

What is established:

  • L’Invitation Au Suicide was run by Yann Farcy from Le Havre, Normandy, France (founded 1981, closed 1985). Wikipedia’s Christian Death article confirms: “After Williams was approached by Yann Farcy of French record label L’Invitation au Suicide to record another Christian Death album and tour Europe…” — the initiative came from Farcy, not from Williams. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Death, as of 2026-10-19.
  • Rabel is described in search-result snippets as a graphic designer from Rouen (neighbouring city to Le Havre in Normandy) who worked with Yann Farcy and Marie Lemeur on L’IAS releases. The Discogs credits confirm he was the label’s go-to designer across all nine of his L’IAS credits (1982–1985), not just for Christian Death. His earliest L’IAS credit (1982, Entrave et Étouffement / Heresie) predates Christian Death’s first L’IAS release (Only Theater Of Pain, 1983), which means he was already embedded in the label before Christian Death entered the picture. discogs.com (via trawler data).
  • Search snippets from multiple sources (including a BnF/Gallica interview with Yann Farcy that was inaccessible at time of research — 403) describe Rabel, Farcy, and Lemeur as the creative core of L’IAS. Whether Rabel was a formal co-founder or a close collaborator of Farcy’s from the outset could not be confirmed from accessible sources.

What was not found:

  • No interview, biography, or press source names Rabel in connection with Williams or Christian Death outside the L’IAS context.
  • No source suggests Rabel and Williams had contact before Farcy brokered the Christian Death–L’IAS relationship circa 1982–1983.
  • Rabel has no Discogs profile text and no accessible biography; his real name is not recorded on Discogs.

Sources checked (dead ends):

  • thebluehour.free.fr/rozzarticles-ias85.htm — Christian Death / L’IAS 1985 interview; returned socket hang-up, inaccessible.
  • perteetfracas.org — L’IAS history page; returned socket hang-up, inaccessible.
  • gallica.bnf.fr/selections/fr/html/entretien-avec-yann-farcy — Yann Farcy interview at BnF; returned 403.
  • Wikipedia articles on Christian Death, Rozz Williams, Heresie EP — none mention Rabel.
  • David Glass (ex-Christian Death) interview, Echoes and Dust (2026) — page returned CSS/template only, no article text accessible.

Conclusion: The connection is through L’Invitation Au Suicide. Farcy approached Williams; Rabel was already the label’s graphic designer in Normandy before Christian Death joined the roster. There is no evidence of a prior or independent Rabel–Williams relationship.
