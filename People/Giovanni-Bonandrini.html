Giovanni Bonandrini

Giovanni Bonandrini

Jun 18, 20265 min read

Giovanni Bonandrini

Discogs ID: 358475 Role: Record producer, label founder/director Based: Milan, Italy Active (labels): 1977–c. 2004, then retired

Italian music producer who founded and ran two of the most important independent jazz labels in history: Black Saint and Soul Note. Over roughly 25 years he built a catalog of ~500 albums documenting avant-garde jazz, free jazz, post-bop, and experimental improvised music at a moment when major American labels were retreating from those genres.

Biography

Bonandrini was born in Italy and developed an interest in jazz as a teenager, buying his first shellac records in 1945. By the 1970s he was co-owner of the Hi-Fi & Record Center in Milan.

In 1977 he took over Black Saint as a partner and co-owner. The label had been founded in 1975 by Giacomo Pellicciotti and by that point had released 13 records in the avant-garde jazz field. Bonandrini expanded the label aggressively through the late 1970s, signing American avant-garde players — Anthony Braxton, Muhal Richard Abrams, Don Pullen, David Murray, Max Roach, Archie Shepp, World Saxophone Quartet, Charlie Haden, Don Cherry — at a time when US major labels had largely abandoned this music.

In 1979 he founded Soul Note as a sister label oriented toward more bop-influenced modern jazz, though the line between the two catalogs was not always rigid. Soul Note became the home for Steve Lacy, Bill Dixon, George Russell, Chet Baker, Paul Motian, Enrico Pieranunzi, Giorgio Gaslini, Max Roach, Art Blakey, Ran Blake, and many others.

Bonandrini maintained the labels’ independence deliberately — at one point canceling a contract with Polygram when they sought input into his program. By 2004 the combined catalog reached approximately 500 albums, with 12–15 new releases per year still being issued. He won the Down Beat Critics Poll as producer in 1982–1985 and 1987–1989.

Bonandrini retired at some point after 2004. In 2008 both labels were acquired by CAM (a Rome-based company, Discogs parent ID 47332), which began reissuing the catalogs under the CAM Jazz imprint. His son Flavio Bonandrini succeeded him in label management.

Labels

Black Saint (Discogs ID: 30835)

Founded 1975 by Giacomo Pellicciotti; Bonandrini took over in 1977/1978. Specializing in free jazz, improvised music, and avant-garde. First releases included Billy Harper, Archie Shepp, Sam Rivers, and Malachi Favors / Muhal Richard Abrams (BSR 0001–0002, 1975). Under Bonandrini the label signed the core American free jazz community. Catalog reached over 190 albums.

Key sub-communities on the roster:

  • AACM / Chicago: Anthony Braxton, Muhal Richard Abrams, Joseph Jarman, Roscoe Mitchell, Henry Threadgill, George Lewis, Famoudou Don Moye
  • New York loft scene: David Murray, Don Pullen, Julius Hemphill, Hamiet Bluiett, World Saxophone Quartet, String Trio of New York, Leroy Jenkins, Oliver Lake, Chico Freeman
  • Old And New Dreams collective: Don Cherry, Charlie Haden, Ed Blackwell, Dewey Redman
  • The Sun Ra Arkestra: 20 releases, 1987 onward

Distributed internationally; from July 1982, US distribution through a separate distributor.

Soul Note (Discogs ID: 26659)

Founded 1979 by Bonandrini as a companion to Black Saint. Based in Tribiano, Italy. Oriented toward modern jazz, post-bop, and more structured improvised music, though with significant crossover with Black Saint’s avant-garde sensibility. Label Code: LC 11120. Catalog over 350 albums.

Key artists by release count: Steve Lacy (25 releases), Charlie Haden (24), Andrew Cyrille (23), Bill Dixon (21), Enrico Pieranunzi (21), Giorgio Gaslini (21), Max Roach Quartet (21), Paul Motian (20), Archie Shepp (17), Chet Baker (15), Cecil Taylor (14), Ran Blake (14), George Russell (13).

US distribution from July 1982 through same US distributor as Black Saint.
