Jean-Louis Rizet

Discogs ID: 483546 | Alias: Pistamouche (Discogs ID: 5442832) | Name variations: J.L. Rizet, Jean-Lou, Professeur Pistamouche, Rizet

French recording engineer, producer, and multi-instrumentalist (keyboards, synthesizers, flute, trumpet, guitar). Active from the mid-1970s through the 2020s, Rizet operated primarily out of Ramsès (Studio Ramsès) in Paris, where he served as chief engineer and executive producer on a wide range of French recordings spanning experimental electronics, jazz, prog, world music, and cinema soundtracks. He is best known in the international experimental canon for his work with the Richard Pinhas / Heldon circle — most prominently as arranger and engineer on Pinhas’s L’Ethique (1982) and mixer on DWW (1992) — and as a founding half of the synthesizer duo Besombes - Rizet alongside Philippe Besombes. His alias Pistamouche (and variant Professeur Pistamouche) appears in the mixing credit on Flarenasch’s Toujours Plus Haut (1983).

Career

Origins — Pôle Records and Besombes - Rizet (1975)

Rizet’s Discogs credit trail begins in 1975. That year he and Philippe Besombes released a double LP as Besombes - Rizet simply titled Pôle on the short-lived experimental imprint Pôle Records (run by Paul Putti). The record — keyboards, synthesizers, flute, trumpet, guitar, and vocals from both principals, with drums from Jacky Vander Elstraete and saxophone from Alain Petit — sits between kosmische Krautrock and Heldon-adjacent electronics and is one of the cult touchstones of the French experimental underground. The Pôle Records context is important: Rizet and Besombes later maintained that the Pôle (2) branding on compilations of that label’s output was applied without their consent by Putti for marketing purposes, and that they were studio collaborators rather than a formal collective.

Also in 1975, Rizet contributed synthesizers as a session player to Philippe Grancher’s 3000 Miles Away, also on Pôle Records, with Besombes handling recording and mixing duties.

Studio Ramsès — Chief Engineer (1979–1992)

From 1979 onward Rizet’s credit trail thickens into a sustained run as house chief engineer at Ramsès / Studio Ramsès, Paris. The studio operated as a production and recording facility (the Discogs artist entity “Ramses (12)” aggregates its non-label credits); the associated imprint Ramses released a handful of albums. During this period Rizet served variously as recording engineer, mixer, and executive producer — often jointly credited with the studio itself and with associate engineer Laurent Peyron, who functions as his closest recurring collaborator.

Key recordings from the Studio Ramsès phase include:

  • Odeurs — 1980: No Sex (Polydor, 1979): Engineer and assistant producer alongside Ramses (12) and Ramon Pipin. The session brought together a remarkable cross-section of the Paris underground: Bernard Paganotti, Patrick Gauthier, Richard Pinhas (synthesizer), Jean-Philippe Goude (Minimoog/arrangements), Klaus Blasquiz, and Stella Vander.
  • Jean-Philippe Goude — Drones (Polydor / Ramsès, 1980): Engineer and co-executive producer (with Alain Ranval under the “Producteurs Exécutifs Ramsès” credit). Laurent Peyron was assistant engineer.
  • Georges Grünblatt — K-Priss (Polydor / Ramsès, 1980): Chief sound engineer; Richard Pinhas and Laurent Peyron as engineer-assistants.
  • Renaud — Le Retour De Gérard Lambert (Polydor, 1981): Recording and mixing; Goude as arranger; Alain Ranval co-arranger; session featured Blasquiz, Stella Vander, and Coluche on backing vocals.
  • Patrick Gauthier — Bébé Godzilla (Cy Records, 1981): Engineer, mixer, and producer on track B1 (with Laurent Peyron); Richard Pinhas executive producer on the full album.
  • Richard Pinhas — L’Ethique (Pulse, 1982): Engineer on the full album; arranger jointly with Goude on tracks A1, B1, B3, B5. The session also featured Bernard Paganotti (bass), Clément Bailly (drums), Patrick Gauthier (additional Minimoog).
  • Richard Pinhas — DWW (Cuneiform, 1992): Mixer on tracks 1, 8, and 10. Laurent Peyron was executive engineer and assistant producer; Goude and Gauthier performed on other tracks.

Jazz and World Music Sessions (1979–1986)

Parallel to the prog-electronics work, Rizet was the go-to engineer for several French jazz and world-music projects of this era, mostly on the Sun Records (2), Impro, Marge, and JMS labels:

  • Archie Shepp Quintet — Bird Fire (Tribute To Charlie Parker) (Sun Records / Impro, 1979): Recording and mixing.
  • Session In Paris, Vol. 1 “Song Of Soil” (Paddle Wheel, 1979): Recording engineer.
  • Ailton — The Awakening (Marge, 1979): Recording and mixing.
  • Zikir (Sun Records, 1979): Engineer.
  • Christian Escoudé Group Featuring Toots Thielemans (JMS, 1983): Engineer.
  • Trio (JMS, 1983): Engineer.
  • Night Bird (JMS, 1983): Engineer.
  • Didier Lockwood Group (JMS, 1984): Recording, mixing, and special effects (alongside Laurent Peyron as assistant).
  • La Flute De Pandit Hari Prasad Chaurasia (Disques Espérance, 1986): Recording.
  • Space — Space (Le Chant Du Monde, 1982): Sound engineer.

International Sessions (1979–1989)

Rizet’s reach extended beyond French labels into international productions recorded in Paris:

  • John McLaughlin — Belo Horizonte (Warner Bros., 1981): Chief engineer (credited “Engineer [Cheif]”); Laurent Peyron assistant. Session included Paco De Lucía, Katia Labèque, and others.
  • Pat Metheny — Music Spoken Here (Warner Bros., 1982): Engineer.
  • The Kid (MPS Records, 1983): Engineer.
  • Mahavishnu (WEA, 1984): Chief engineer and mixer on John McLaughlin’s Mahavishnu Orchestra album.
  • West Side Story — Symphonic Dances And Songs (CBS, 1989): Engineer.
  • Concerto For Guitar & Orchestra “The Mediterranean” (CBS, 1990): First engineer.

Film and Pop Sessions (1980–1996)

Rizet also did considerable soundtrack and pop work:

  • Claude Zidi’s Clara Et Les Chics Types (WEA, 1980): Engineer and recording.
  • Luc Besson’s Subway soundtrack (Gaumont Musique, 1985): Engineer; Eric Serra composer and performer; Laurent Peyron co-engineer.
  • La Musique Des Films (Virgin, 1996): Mixer.

Late Career — Magma Live (2021)

The most recent credit in the DB is Rizet appearing as recording engineer (“Enregistré Par”) on specific live tracks of Magma (6)‘s Retrospektïẁ I-II-III (Seventh Records, 2021), alongside Laurent Peyron on the same tracks (3-4). This late appearance — in company with Peyron, Benoît Widemann, and Bernard Paganotti — reaffirms his continued ties to the Zeuhl/prog world.
