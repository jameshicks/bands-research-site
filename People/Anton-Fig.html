Anton Fig

Drummer and percussionist, born 08 August 1952 in Cape Town, South Africa. Lives in New York City. Discogs artist ID: 328193

One of the most in-demand session drummers of the 1980s–2000s, best known as the house drummer for Paul Shaffer’s band on Late Night with David Letterman / Late Show with David Letterman (1986–2015). Also a co-founding member of Frehley’s Comet and a long-running contributor to Joe Bonamassa’s touring and recording band.

Biography

Early Career — South Africa and the New York Entry (1971–1977)

Fig’s earliest Discogs credit is a 1971 South African live album (Live!, Cameo (7)), suggesting active gigging in Cape Town in his late teens. By the mid-1970s he had relocated to New York City, where he embedded in the city’s session world. His first high-profile credit came via the mid-to-late 1970s KISS solo-album sessions.

The KISS / Ace Frehley Era (1978–1980)

Fig appeared as drummer and percussionist on the 1978 Ace Frehley solo LP (Casablanca), part of the simultaneous quadruple KISS solo release. He also played on Kiss’s Unmasked (1980). The Frehley connection proved lasting: he would reunite with Ace in Frehley’s Comet nearly a decade later.

During this same period Fig accumulated a dense run of New York session work: Joan Armatrading’s Me Myself I (A&M, 1980), the soundtrack Bullshot (RCA, 1979), the Spider (12) group (he was a core member), and Link Wray’s live set recorded at Amsterdam’s Paradiso (1980).

Spider and the New York Rock Scene (1980–1982)

Fig was a full member of Spider (12), the American hard-rock group whose self-titled debut (Dreamland, 1980) and Between The Lines (Dreamland, 1981) he co-wrote on as well as played. He also played on Shanghai (4)‘s Shanghai (Chrysalis, 1982) as a group member with co-writing credits.

The Breakthrough Session Period (1983–1986)

This is when Fig became a first-call New York studio drummer:

  • Cyndi Lauper — She’s So Unusual (Portrait, 1983): Full-album drums/percussion alongside producer Rick Chertoff, Rob Hyman, and Eric Bazilian of The Hooters. The album went multi-platinum and produced “Girls Just Want to Have Fun” and “Time After Time.” Fig returned for Lauper’s follow-up, True Colors (Portrait, 1986), where he shared drum duties with programmed tracks.
  • Bob Dylan — Empire Burlesque (CBS, 1985): Fig played drums on one track alongside Jim Keltner, Sly Dunbar, and Don Heffington.
  • Mick Jagger — She’s The Boss (CBS, 1985): Two tracks, produced by Nile Rodgers and Bill Laswell, alongside Jeff Beck, Pete Townshend, Herbie Hancock, and Sly Dunbar.
  • The Rolling Stones — Dirty Work (Rolling Stones Records, 1986): Credited as Performer.
  • Bob Dylan — Knocked Out Loaded (CBS, 1986): Drums.
  • Paul Butterfield — The Legendary Paul Butterfield Rides Again (Amherst Records, 1986): Full-album drums, alongside Paul Shaffer (keyboards) and Blondie Chaplin (guitar/vocals). Produced by Thom Panunzio.

Letterman Band / World’s Most Dangerous Band (1986–2015)

In 1986 Fig joined Paul Shaffer’s band for Late Night with David Letterman, later renamed The World’s Most Dangerous Band and, after the CBS move in 1993, The CBS Orchestra. He remained the house drummer for nearly three decades. The band’s core lineup alongside Fig included Will Lee (bass), Sid McGinnis (guitar), Tom Malone (horns/trombone), Aaron Heick (saxes/flute), Felicia Collins (guitar, later years), Frank Greene (2) (trumpet), and David Sanborn (sax, early years). Also in the early orbit: Hiram Bullock (guitar).

The band released one studio album, The World’s Most Dangerous Party (SBK Records, 1993), produced by Todd Rundgren, featuring celebrity guests including David Letterman, Ringo Starr, Leonard Cohen, Lou Reed, George Clinton, Bootsy Collins, Joan Jett, Tony Bennett, LL Cool J, and Aaron Neville. A second album, Paul Shaffer & The World’s Most Dangerous Band (Sire, 2017), followed after the show ended.

Frehley’s Comet (1987–1989)

Fig was a founding member of Frehley’s Comet, Ace Frehley’s post-KISS band. He played drums on the self-titled debut (Atlantic/Megaforce, 1987), produced by Eddie Kramer, with John Regan on bass and Tod Howarth on guitar/keys/vocals. He also played on the live album Live + 1 (Atlantic, 1988). He later appeared on the Trouble Walkin’ LP (1989) as well.

Session Work — 1990s

While anchored to the Letterman band, Fig maintained a robust session career:

  • Madonna — Erotica (Maverick/Sire, 1992): Drums.
  • Al Kooper — Rekooperation (Music Masters Rock, 1994): Drums on the majority of tracks alongside Harvey Brooks, Jimmy Vivino, Hank Crawford, and Randy Brecker. Liner notes by Stephen King.
  • Al Kooper — Soul Of A Man: Live (Music Masters Rock, 1995): Drums.
  • The Blues Brothers Band — Blues Brothers 2000 OST (Universal, 1997): Band drummer.
  • The Concert For New York City (Columbia, 2001): Drums with the Orchestra For New York City at the post-9/11 benefit concert.

Joe Bonamassa and the Blues Circuit (2007–present)

From 2007 onward, Fig became a near-constant presence in Joe Bonamassa’s studio and live recordings across more than a dozen albums produced by Kevin Shirley for Provogue/J&R Adventures. Key entries:

  • Sloe Gin (2007) — first Bonamassa collaboration
  • The Ballad of John Henry (2009)
  • Black Rock (2010)
  • Don’t Explain (2011) and Seesaw (2013) — with Beth Hart
  • Different Shades of Blue (2014)
  • Blues of Desperation (2016)
  • Black Coffee (2018) — with Beth Hart
  • Royal Tea (2020)
  • Easy To Buy - Hard To Sell (2020)
  • Heavy Soul (2024)
  • Multiple live albums from the Royal Albert Hall, Sydney Opera House, Greek Theatre, etc.

The Bonamassa touring band also typically included Carmine Rojas (bass).
