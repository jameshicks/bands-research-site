Martin Rushent

Oct 08, 20263 min read

Martin Rushent

British record producer and engineer. Produced The Decorators’ Twilight View (ORG 5, 1980) on New Hormones — an early credit. Best known for producing the Human League’s Dare (1981, Virgin), which became one of the definitive British synth-pop albums. Also worked with Buzzcocks, The Stranglers, and others.

The Decorators / New Hormones connection

Rushent’s relationship with New Hormones and label boss Richard Boon pre-dates The Decorators: he was the staff producer at United Artists who was assigned to Buzzcocks when they signed to UA in August 1977, producing Another Music in a Different Kitchen (1978) and Love Bites (1978). That working relationship with Boon is the most plausible route through which he came to produce The Decorators in 1980, though no source states this explicitly.

The Decorators’ signing to New Hormones was itself via family connection: Boon has stated “It was nepotism: my brother-in-law” — Joe Cohen, the band’s saxophonist, was Boon’s brother-in-law. Boon qualified this by adding: “We wouldn’t have put it out if we didn’t like the record, even if it was family.” (newhormonesinfo.com — Richard Boon tag as of 2026-10-08). This explains how the band got on New Hormones, but does not address how Rushent was selected as producer.

The Twilight View session was recorded at Eden Studios — a step up from the cheap facilities (Drone Studios, Pennine Studios) New Hormones typically used. Boon acknowledged the contrast himself, reportedly noting: “Factory had Hannett, we had Stuart” — referring to Stuart James, the label’s usual in-house producer — which flags the Decorators session as a deliberate exception. Rushent exerted creative control over the session: according to band member Joe Cohen, “Martin Rushent wanted to do that track, so Richard went along with his choice: ‘Let’s do a ballad.‘” The band had mixed feelings about the result. Cohen said the production “was not really representative of our style,” and drummer Allan Boroughs reflected: “In hindsight I’m not sure the results were that great. One of the things we struggled to do was to capture on record the sound we had live.” The Decorators recorded four tracks with a view to a second single, but it never materialised; Twilight View was their only New Hormones release.

On the specific engagement mechanism: No interview or liner-note source — including the newhormonesinfo Decorators and Richard Boon tag pages, the LTM Auteur Labels liner notes, the Louder Than War Boon interview, and the Julie Hamill Boon interview — explains who first brought Rushent to the session: whether it was Boon’s initiative (drawing on the prior Buzzcocks/UA relationship), the band’s request, or Rushent’s own approach. The Buzzcocks/UA working relationship remains the only documented prior link between Rushent and Boon, and the most likely origin. No Rushent interview discussing New Hormones or The Decorators has surfaced. (Web research exhausted 2026-10-08.)
