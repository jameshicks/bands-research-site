Overview

Günter Körber (1946–2013) was a German producer who shaped the institutional backbone of the Krautrock era through two successive label launches. After leaving Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser’s Ohr imprint alongside Bruno Wendel, he founded Brain Records under Metronome in 1971, establishing it as a home for Krautrock and progressive rock. When he departed Metronome in 1975 he founded Sky Records, which carried the catalogue into ambient and new wave territory through the following decade. His deepest production partnerships were with the band Jane and its core members Klaus Hess and Peter Panka — over a hundred shared credits each — with Conny Plank and Charly Maucher also prominent among his recurring collaborators.

Notes (web) — Körber/Kaiser split and Brain Records founding

Sources consulted: Wikipedia (Brain Records article), Wikipedia (Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser article), Jan Reetze blog post on Cosmic Couriers / Kaiser (2011), eurock.com profile of Kaiser.

What drove the departure: The consistent picture across sources is that Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser’s personal behaviour — specifically his escalating LSD use and increasing eccentricity — eroded working conditions at Ohr to the point where Körber and Bruno Wendel decided to leave. The Brain Records Wikipedia article phrases it as Kaiser’s “management style” and his “increasing eccentricity and LSD experiments.” The Kaiser Wikipedia article says departure was “partly as a result of Kaiser’s increasing LSD experiments.” The Jan Reetze blog (2011) adds that the two men “both felt that things had gone downhill,” situating the departure in a broader frustration: an over-proliferation of mediocre or obscure artists, promotional activities becoming disconnected from musical substance, and a general sense of declining quality. https://janreetze.blogspot.com/2011/05/rolf-ulrich-kaiser-gille-lettmann_1460.html

No source names a single precipitating business dispute or specific incident — the picture is one of accumulated dissatisfaction with Kaiser’s behaviour and the label’s direction rather than a clean, documented break over one event.

Brain Records structure: Wendel and Körber approached Metronome (Ohr’s Hamburg-based distributor) and established Brain as a Metronome subsidiary. They brought Guru Guru with them from Ohr and quickly signed Cluster. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_Records (as of 2026-11-22)

Date conflict: Discogs and the Brain Records Wikipedia article both say Brain was founded in 1971 (the Brain Records article specifically says “middle of 1971” / “late 1971”). The Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser Wikipedia article places the departure in 1972. The Reetze blog also uses 1972. The discrepancy is unresolved in available sources; the founding/registration of Brain under Metronome may have occurred in 1971 while the operational departure of Körber and Wendel is placed at 1972 by some sources.

What could not be confirmed: No primary source (interview with Körber, internal correspondence, contemporary press account) is available in accessible web sources that describes the split from Körber’s own perspective or names a specific business dispute.
