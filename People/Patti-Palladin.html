Patti Palladin

Vocalist, songwriter, producer. Discogs artist ID: 267514. Name variations: P. Palladin, Pat Palladin, Patt Palladin, PP.

Overview

American-born vocalist who relocated to London in the late 1970s and became embedded in the UK post-punk and punk-adjacent scenes. She is best known for two sustained partnerships: as one half of the art-punk duo Snatch (3) with Judy Nylon (1977–1983), and as the lead vocalist on The Flying Lizards’ second album Fourth Wall (1981), for which she co-wrote lyrics on most of the album’s tracks. Her longest creative collaboration was with New York Dolls guitarist Johnny Thunders, producing two LPs together — Crawfish (1985) and Copy Cats (1988) — and remaining active in that partnership through the late 1980s. She also took producer credits on those records, demonstrating a production role that went well beyond frontwoman duties. Her solo output is sparse: a 1982 single (“Trial By Fire / Siamese Lovers”) and a 2000 single covering “Memo From Turner” (Jagger-Richards) on Creation Records.

Snatch (with Judy Nylon)

Palladin and Judy Nylon formed Snatch in New York and subsequently operated out of London. Discogs credits them as a “Legendary Punk girl duo from New York, associated with the Art-Rock scene.” Their recorded output is small but sits at the intersection of punk and art-rock.

The Flying Lizards

Palladin joined The Flying Lizards — the experimental pop project led by producer David Cunningham — as vocalist for Fourth Wall (1981), contributing lyrics to most of the album (credited tracks A1 through B3 and B6). She is also listed as Voice and Tape on the group’s debut LP (1979). In the Flying Lizards, she sat alongside a rotating cast of improvisers and new-music figures.

Johnny Thunders Collaborations

Palladin’s most extensive body of work came through her partnership with Johnny Thunders, former New York Dolls guitarist. She co-wrote, sang on, and produced two full albums alongside him.

Key Producers and Engineers

Ken Thomas engineered across multiple Palladin releases — the Trial By Fire single (1982), Crawfish (1985), and one track of Copy Cats (1988) — making him the most consistent technical collaborator in her recorded output.

David Cunningham produced and mixed the Flying Lizards material. Keith Hancock engineered the bulk of Copy Cats.
