Overview

Lindsay Gravina is a Melbourne-based audio engineer, producer, and mastering engineer who has been one of the city’s most prolific behind-the-scenes figures in rock and alternative music since the early 1990s. He spent much of his career as an in-house producer at Birdland Studios, Melbourne, which gave him a central position in recording the Australian underground and guitar-oriented rock scene.

His credits span the rough garage punk of Cosmic Psychos and Venom P. Stinger, the alt-rock of The Living End (including their breakthrough 1998 debut), two solo albums for Rowland S. Howard (Teenage Snuff Film, 1999; Pop Crimes, 2009), the post-punk of Mick Harvey’s solo work, and later the Melbourne post-punk revival through New War. He also co-produced the 2024 Birthday Party documentary Mutiny In Heaven.

As a performer he was guitarist in The Spleens (members: Lindsay Gravina, Ross Jackson, Greg Hynes), who appear on the 1992 Velvet Underground tribute compilation The Velvet Down Underground (Birdland / 3RRR / Shock, BIRDCD 001) — a comp Gravina himself co-produced and engineered. This was one of his earliest significant production credits.

The alias The Count (11) refers to a separate Discogs performer identity for Gravina (ID: 3292923), active 2001–2019, with at least three producer credits: Who Flung (2001, Shock), the Glorius Barsteds reunion records (2011, 2019), and Change Of Heart (2012–13).

Scene Context

Gravina’s career maps almost exactly onto the arc of Melbourne’s independent and underground rock scene from the late 1980s through to the present. His early 1990s work centred on 3RRR-connected compilations and Au Go Go Records bands. Through the mid-1990s he was embedded in the Australian garage-punk circuit (Cosmic Psychos, Bored!, Venom P. Stinger). The 1998 Living End debut represented his one major mainstream breakthrough — a commercial Australian rock album that sold heavily.

His most critically significant work came in 1999 and 2009: producing both of Rowland S. Howard’s solo albums, widely considered high points of Australian post-punk. His 2009 work with HTRK (on Marry Me Tonight, released via Blast First Petite in the UK) extended his reach into the Melbourne noise/drone scene. Around 2012–2013 he worked extensively with New War, a key band in the Melbourne post-punk revival of that period.

From roughly 2015 onward his output includes more mastering-only credits and work with Mick Harvey on Mute-released translations of Serge Gainsbourg songs.

The “Mastered At Birdland” credit appears explicitly on the Thirsty Merc debut (2004, WEA); Birdland Studios is consistently the physical space behind Gravina’s engineering credits.
