Cory Daye

Born 25 April 1952, New York, NY. Disco, soul, and boogie vocalist. Lead singer of Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band. Discogs artist ID: 81944. Known name variations: Corey Daye, Cory Dave, Cory Day, Daye, The Inimitable Cory Daye.

Overview

Cory Daye was the primary lead vocalist of Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band, the eclectic New York dance band that fused big-band swing, disco, and theatrical kitsch in the late 1970s. Her voice was central to the band’s identity through three albums on RCA Victor (1976–1979). Alongside band co-founders Stony Browder (music, guitar, piano, musical director) and August Darnell (bass, lyrics), she helped shape the group’s retro-anachronistic sound — her vocals on tracks like “Cherchez La Femme” became emblematic of the era.

In 1979 she launched a solo career under her own name, releasing two albums on New York International Records (an RCA sublabel): Cory And Me and Green Light, both produced by Sandy Linzer. Her single “Pow Wow” was a significant dancefloor hit. A second solo phase followed in 1986–1987 on Blue Chip Records (2), yielding a boogie-soul album and several singles without major commercial traction.

She remained closely associated with August Darnell’s subsequent project Kid Creole And The Coconuts, appearing as a “Special Guest Star” on their landmark Tropical Gangsters (ZE Records, 1982) and returning in contributor roles on Doppelganger (1983), In Praise Of Older Women And Other Crimes (1985), I, Too Have Seen The Woods (1987), Private Waters In The Great Divide (1990), and To Travel Sideways (1993).
