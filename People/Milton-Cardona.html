Milton Cardona

Aug 25, 202611 min read

Milton Cardona

Born: 21 November 1944, Mayagüez, Puerto Rico Died: 19 September 2014 Role: Conguero, percussionist, vocalist Discogs artist ID: 104679

Puerto Rican-born, New York-based Latin percussionist who worked across salsa, Latin jazz, and downtown avant-garde contexts from the mid-1960s until the 2000s. Best known as a fixture on Fania Records sessions with Willie Colón, Rubén Blades, and Héctor Lavoe, and as a recurring presence in the American Clavé orbit around Kip Hanrahan. A rare player equally at home on rumba-circuit dance-band sessions and the downtown New York experimental scene. His solo output was minimal — one proper LP (Bembé, 1986) and a handful of subsequent releases under his name — but his contributor credits span well over 150 releases across six decades, with instruments including congas, batá drums, shekere, claves, bongos, and bajo quinto, as well as vocals in coro and occasional lead contexts.

Career

Mid-1960s–1969 — Early New York Salsa Scene

Cardona’s earliest documented Discogs credits place him in the New York Latin dance-band world, appearing on Cotique Records sessions: Boogaloo ‘67 (Cotique, 1967) on congas, and Boogaloo Blues (Cotique, 1967) on coro. He was playing in Willie Colón’s band by the late 1960s — Discogs places him on Colón’s Wanted By FBI / The Big Break - La Gran Fuga (Fania, 1970), an album on which Héctor Lavoe sang. The Discogs profile notes he was a member of Colón’s outfit when Rubén Blades first encountered Colón in Panama in 1969.

1970s — Fania and the Golden Age of Salsa

Through the 1970s Cardona became a core session player in the Fania Records stable, appearing on a high density of landmark recordings:

  • El Juicio (Fania, 1972) — Rubén Blades; congas
  • Lo Mato Si No Compra Este LP (Fania, 1973) — Willie Colón; congas (24 pressings — one of Colón’s most-issued records of the era)
  • Cada Loco Con Su Tema (Vaya, 1974) — batá
  • Salsa (Fania, 1974) — the documentary / compilation; batá
  • La Voz (Fania, 1975) — Héctor Lavoe; congas (32 pressings)
  • The Gardens Of Harlem (JCOA, 1975) — Clifford Thornton & The Jazz Composer’s Orchestra; percussion (batá, tumba) — an early downtown-jazz crossover credit, also featuring Jerry Gonzalez and Andy Gonzalez
  • Metiendo Mano! (Fania, 1977) — Rubén Blades / Willie Colón; congas, quinto, claves, talking drum, coro (30 pressings — one of the key Blades/Colón collaborations)
  • De Ti Depende (Fania, 1976) — Willie Colón; congas, chorus, percussion (26 pressings)
  • Maestra Vida (Fania, 1980) — two-part Rubén Blades concept album produced by Colón; congas, claves, backing vocals

Also active in parallel Vaya Records sessions: There Goes The Neighborhood (Vaya, 1975), Only They Could Have Made This Album (Vaya, 1977), and Celia Y Willie (Vaya, 1981), the Willie Colón / Celia Cruz collaboration.

Cardona also served as music director on the Tribute To Chano Pozo - Campanero album (Velvet, 1977) and on Los Rebeldes (Cotique, 1979).

1980 — Crossover into Downtown New York

1980 brought a notable crossover appearance: Cristina’s self-titled debut LP (Island/ZE Records, 1980), produced by August Darnell (Kid Creole). This placed Cardona inside the ZE Records world alongside Darnell and Andy Hernandez (Coati Mundi). The session was engineered by Bob Blank.

1983–1990 — American Clavé and the Kip Hanrahan Sessions

The most distinctive thread of Cardona’s career outside the salsa mainstream was his sustained collaboration with producer-composer Kip Hanrahan on the American Clavé label. He appears across Hanrahan’s entire run of critically regarded downtown-jazz-meets-Latin releases:

  • Desire Develops An Edge (American Clavé, 1983) — a sprawling all-star session including John Zorn, Jack Bruce, Arto Lindsay, John Scofield, Jody Harris, Jamaaladeen Tacuma, Steve Swallow, and others; Cardona on congas and shekere across multiple tracks
  • Music For The Texts Of Ishmael Reed (American Clavé, 1984) — congas
  • Vertical’s Currency (VeraBra, 1985) — congas, bongos
  • A Few Short Notes From The End Run (American Clavé, 1986) — congas, chéquere, quinto; alongside Giovanni Hidalgo and Jack Bruce
  • Days And Nights Of Blue Luck Inverted (American Clavé, 1987) — congas; alongside Andy Gonzalez, Jack Bruce, Steve Swallow, David Murray, George Adams, Lew Soloff, Jerry Gonzalez, Ignacio Berroa, Robby Ameen
  • Cab Calloway Stands In For The Moon (American Clavé, 1988) — congas
  • Tenderness (American Clavé, 1990) — percussion, congas
  • Exotica (American Clavé, 1992) — performer (some cuts)
  • All Roads Are Made Of The Flesh (American Clavé, 1995) — congas
  • A Thousand Nights And A Night (1996–98) — congas / chéquere
  • Beautiful Scars (American Clavé/Yellowbird, 2008) — percussion
  • At Home In Anger (Enja/Yellowbird, 2011) — congas, percussion
  • Crescent Moon Waning (American Clavé, 2018 Japan reissue) — congas

Jon Fausty was the regular recording and mixing engineer across most of these American Clavé sessions.

1986 — Bembé (Solo LP)

Cardona’s sole proper solo LP, Bembé (American Clavé, 1986), was his most significant release as a primary artist. The album has no additional credited personnel in Discogs — it is essentially a solo percussion and vocal statement. It has had six pressings, indicating a sustained cult profile. The Discogs profile describes Cardona’s voice as “unique” and notes this remained his only proper solo album.

1987–1999 — Widening Contexts

Through the late 1980s and 1990s Cardona’s name appeared with increasing frequency outside the salsa and American Clavé orbits:

  • Not Just The Girl Next Door (Atlantic, 1986) — Phyllis Hyman
  • Humble People (George Wein Collection, 1985) — Flora Purim & Airto Moreira; batá
  • Obatalà (Enja, 1989) — Jerry Gonzalez & The Fort Apache Band; percussion
  • Beauty (Virgin America, 1989) — Dave Valentin; percussion
  • Megadrums (veraBra, 1990) — iyabata, quinto, shekere; the ensemble project Megadrums appears in his Discogs group memberships
  • Electric Landlady (Virgin, 1991) — Kirsty MacColl; congas on several tracks; engineer Jon Fausty again
  • Uh-Oh (Luaka Bop/Sire/Warner, 1992) — David Byrne; batá on track 4
  • Llegó La India… Via Eddie Palmieri (Ritmo/Soho Sounds, 1992) — Eddie Palmieri; choir, congas, shekere
  • The Mambo Kings (Elektra, 1992) — film soundtrack; congas (17 pressings)
  • Musical Portraits (GRP, 1992) — Dave Valentin; bata, backing vocals
  • Sanctified Shells (Antilles, 1993) — Steve Turre; congas, shekere
  • The Latin Side Of John Coltrane (Astor Place, 1996) — Andy Gonzalez; vocals, batá, congas, percussion
  • Songs From The Capeman (Warner, 1997) — Paul Simon; backing vocals, bongos, maracas, claves, congas, guiro
  • Steve Turre (Verve, 1997) — congas, shekere
  • The Brass Orchestra (Verve, 1997) — Steve Turre; percussion

Also during this period: Layers Of Time (Ellipsis Arts, 1996), a release credited to Afro-Latin Jazz Orchestra where Cardona played full batá set (iyá, itótele, okónkolo), shekere, congas, rattle, and vocals — a showcase for his liturgical percussion command.

1999 — Cambucha (Carmen) (Solo LP)

His second proper solo record, Cambucha (Carmen) (American Clavé / in-akustik, 1999), was a fuller artistic statement: Cardona arranged, composed six of twelve tracks, produced, and is the primary artist. Traditional material accounts for the remaining six tracks. Engineer: Jon Fausty; executive producer: Kip Hanrahan. Released in the US and Germany (3 pressings).

2000s — Later Session Work and Ebioso

  • Masterpiece / Obra Maestra (Universal, 2000) — Celia Cruz; batá, chanter
  • Shadows In The Air (Sanctuary, 2001) — Rubén Blades; congas (9 pressings)
  • You Are #6: More Music For Six Musicians (Blue Note, 2001) — congas, percussion, vocals
  • Original Music For Piñero (American Clavé/EWE, 2002) — film score; congas
  • Bebo De Cuba (Calle 54, 2004) — Bebo Valdés; congas, shekere
  • Una Noche Inolvidable (Palmetto, 2005) — Chucho Valdés; percussion
  • Ebioso (Un-Restricted Access, 2006) — his third release as primary artist; produced and wrote; reproduced by Quentin Harris and Timmy Regisford (house music remixes)
  • Every Child Is Born A Poet (American Clavé, 2006) — the Piri Thomas tribute; percussion, coro
  • 65 Infanteria (Grymes Hill Records, 2013) — congas

Edward Simon’s 1995 debut on Kokopelli Records (Edward Simon, Kokopelli Records) features Cardona on percussion alongside Mark Turner, Larry Grenadier, and Adam Cruz.

Groups and Ensembles

Cardona is listed as a member of several groups in Discogs:

  • Jerry Gonzalez And The Fort Apache Band — one of the most important Afro-Latin jazz units of the 1980s–90s
  • Libre — Eddie Palmieri’s ensemble
  • Afro-Latin Jazz Orchestra — conducted by Arturo O’Farrill
  • Megadrums — percussion ensemble, veraBra Records
  • Mingus Big Band — percussion guest
  • The Jazz Composer’s Orchestra — early downtown free-jazz context (1975)
  • Azuquita Y Su Melao
  • “Killer” Ray Appleton Melvin Rhyne Quartet
