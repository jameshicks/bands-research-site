Ted Templeman

Real name: Edward John Templeman
Born: October 24, 1944, Santa Cruz, California, USA
Discogs ID: 239304
Name variations on sleeves: T Templeman, T. Templeman, T. Templman, T.Templeman, Ted, Ted “Champagne” Templeman, Ted Tempelman, Ted Templebean, Ted Templeton, Teddy Templeman, Templeman

American record producer, drummer, and vocalist. Long-time house producer at Warner Bros. Records from the late 1960s through the 1990s, best known for his extended associations with The Doobie Brothers and Van Halen. Originally a member of the pop group Harpers Bizarre (and before that, The Tikis). Collaborated regularly with engineer Donn Landee across hundreds of sessions.

Bio

Early Career — The Tikis and Harpers Bizarre (1965–1970)

Templeman began his career as a drummer, songwriter, and vocalist with The Tikis (later renamed Harpers Bizarre), a San Jose soft-pop vocal group signed to Warner Bros. Records. His earliest Discogs credits date to 1965 — songwriting credits on Autumn Records releases (“If I’ve Been Dreaming,” 1965; “Lost My Love Today,” 1966).

Harpers Bizarre had their biggest success with the 1967 pop hit “59th Street Bridge Song (Feelin’ Groovy)” (a Simon & Garfunkel cover), produced by Lenny Waronker. The group recorded four albums for Warner Bros./Warner Bros.-Seven Arts (1967–1969): Feelin’ Groovy (1967), Anything Goes (1967), The Secret Life of Harpers Bizarre (1968), and Harpers Bizarre 4 (1969). Templeman’s roles across these records included vocals, guitar, drums, and vocal arrangements. The Secret Life LP carries a co-production credit (“Producer [Assisting]”) for him alongside Waronker.

Members included Dick Scoppettone (Discogs ID: 935225), Dick Yount (1668271), Ed James (3) (412068), John Petersen (3) (1387720), and Templeman.

Transition to Production at Warner Bros. (1971–1972)

After Harpers Bizarre wound down, Templeman shifted fully to production at Warner Bros. His first major independent productions came in 1971:

  • The Doobie Brothers — self-titled debut LP (Warner Bros., 1971). A loose, country-inflected rock band from San Jose; this would grow into one of the decade’s most durable commercial relationships.
  • Van Morrison — Tupelo Honey (Warner Bros., 1971) and Saint Dominic’s Preview (1972), Hard Nose the Highway (1973), It’s Too Late to Stop Now (live, 1974). Templeman produced Morrison’s most commercially successful mid-period Warner run, while also playing organ on Tupelo Honey.
  • Little Feat — Sailin’ Shoes (Reprise, 1972), produced with Lowell George. The next Little Feat LP, Dixie Chicken (1973), also carried a Templeman credit.
  • Captain Beefheart and His Magic Band — Clear Spot (Reprise, 1972), one of Beefheart’s most accessible records. Also produced the companion set released as Double Dynamite: The Spotlight Kid / Clear Spot.

Van Morrison Productions (1971–1974, 1998)

In addition to the studio LPs, Templeman produced Morrison’s live double album It’s Too Late to Stop Now (Warner Bros., 1974) — widely considered one of the great live rock records. He later worked on Morrison’s The Philosopher’s Stone (Polydor, 1998) and received producer credit on the major Morrison compilations and box sets.

Montrose (1973–1974)

Templeman produced the debut LP Montrose (Warner Bros., 1973) and Paper Money (Warner Bros., 1974) for the hard rock group featuring Sammy Hagar. The debut is considered a foundational American hard rock record, pre-dating Van Halen’s commercial rise but laying similar sonic ground.

Van Halen (1978–1984)

Templeman’s most commercially successful run. He produced Van Halen’s first six studio albums, all on Warner Bros., almost always with engineer Donn Landee:

Van Halen (1978) is the record that established Templeman’s commercial reputation at the highest level. Combined pressings across the debut and 1984 on Discogs run to 500+ variants internationally.

He also produced the David Lee Roth solo EPs and LPs during Roth’s departure from Van Halen: Crazy from the Heat (EP, Warner Bros., 1985) and Eat ‘Em and Smile (Warner Bros., 1986), Skyscraper (1988).

Other Significant Productions (1975–1994)

Eric Clapton: Templeman is credited as executive producer on several Clapton compilations and produced Behind the Sun (Duck Records/Warner Bros., 1985) alongside Lenny Waronker.

Sammy Hagar — VOA (Geffen, 1984) and I Can’t Drive 55 (single). Done with Mirrors (Geffen, 1985) with Aerosmith (an executive producer credit). The Van Halen to Hagar solo pipeline is notable — Templeman produced both.

Aerosmith — Done with Mirrors (Geffen, 1985); Templeman produced the band’s Warner/Geffen-era reunion LP.

Cheap Trick — The Essential Cheap Trick compilation (Epic/Legacy, 2004) lists his production on earlier materials.

Nicolette Larson — Nicolette (Warner Bros., 1978), which contained “Lotta Love” (written by Neil Young); Templeman produced it and is also credited as producer on the “Lotta Love” single (1978).

The Beau Brummels — reunion LP (Warner Bros., 1975); connection to his Harpers Bizarre/Warner Bros. soft-rock origins.

Bette Midler — Bathhouse Betty (Warner Bros., 1998); late-career production.

Little River Band — Time Loves a Hero (Capitol, 1977); Templeman produced this Australian pop-rock group’s US-oriented LP.

BulletBoys — self-titled debut (Warner Bros., 1988); late-1980s hard rock act.

Carly Simon — Another Passenger (Elektra, 1976); one of his few non-Warner credits from that era.

Lorraine Ellison — produced 1974 LP on Warner Bros.

Lauren Wood — produced 1979 LP on Warner Bros. (“Please Don’t Leave”).

Chunky, Novi & Ernie — novelty/pop LP (Reprise, 1974).

Boyz II Men offshoot: small pop credits in the early 1990s.

Later Career (1988–2026)

After the Van Halen/Roth split and the close of the classic WB rock era, Templeman’s credits became less frequent but continued across hard rock and pop: Arcade (Elektra, 1992, co-production), BulletBoys debut (1988), Bette Midler Bathhouse Betty (1998), and scattered compilation/reissue productions through the 2010s and 2020s. His most recent Discogs credit as producer is from 2024 (Van Halen reissue material on Warner Records).

He also appeared as a performer on several Warner Bros. sessions — percussion, tambourine, cowbell, maracas, organ, and backing vocals are the recurring instrumental credits across Doobie Brothers and Roth-era albums.
