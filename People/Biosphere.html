Biosphere

Real name: Geir Jenssen Born: 1962, Norway Discogs artist ID: 3956 (Biosphere) / 25441 (Geir Jenssen) Aliases: Bleep, Cosmic Explorer, E-Man (7), Levi (28) Active: 1991–present Primary labels: Origo Sound (1991–1997), Apollo / R&S (1992–1999), All Saints Records (1997), Touch (2000–2015), Biophon Records (2012–present), Smalltown Supersound (2016), AD 93 (2021–2025)

Overview

Biosphere is the primary recording name of Geir Jenssen (born 1962), a Norwegian musician who has become one of the central figures of the ambient electronic canon. Working across synthesizers, sequencers, and field recordings, he developed a distinctive “arctic ambient” and “ambient techno” idiom across a body of work that spans more than three decades. His track “Novelty Waves” was used in Levi’s 1995 advertising campaign; his 1997 album Substrata was voted by Hyperreal.org as one of the all-time classics of the ambient genre.

Jenssen came up through the Norwegian independent electronic scene in the late 1980s, contributing to the Bel Canto project and working as a synthesist before establishing the Biosphere alias in 1991. His first two decades in the Biosphere project coincided with the flowering of the Apollo label (the R&S ambient sub-label) as a major venue for European ambient music; after moving to Touch in 2000 he became that label’s most prolific primary artist, racking up 72 primary release credits. His output has grown only more expansive since leaving Touch — recent albums have appeared on Smalltown Supersound, Biophon Records, and AD 93.

Early Career and Pre-Biosphere Work (1984–1990)

Bleep and early electronics

Jenssen appears on Discogs under the alias E-Man (7) on a 1984 Norwegian release on the Likvidér label, credited for electronics and production — one of his earliest documented appearances. His trajectory through the Norwegian and European scene in the mid-to-late 1980s is tracked through his work as a collaborator and session musician:

  • Bel Canto (2) (1987–1989): Jenssen contributed synthesizer, sequences, piano, drum machine (TR-808), and programming to the Norwegian dream-pop act Bel Canto (2). Credits appear on their LP White-Out Conditions (Crammed Discs, 1987, where he is credited for “Written-By” on the Norwegian pressing), and on Birds Of Passage (IRS / Nettwerk, 1989), where he is credited for keyboards, programming, piano, TR-808, photography (cover), lyrics, and music — a substantial co-writing role. His compositions also feature on Blank Sheets (Crammed Discs, 1988).

  • The Fires Of Ork (1993): Jenssen contributed written-by credits to a release on the Fax +49-69/450464 label (the Frankfurt ambient/techno imprint run by Pete Namlook), suggesting early connections to that continental ambient techno network.

The Origo Sound and Apollo Period (1991–1999)

Microgravity and debut (Origo Sound, 1991)

The Biosphere project launched in 1991 with Microgravity (Origo Sound, OCD 9105), produced and written by Geir Jenssen with digital editing by Alexander Stojanovic. The album’s mix of ambient atmospheres and rhythmic pulse points toward the ambient techno direction he would develop at Apollo. A 12” single “The Fairy Tale” appeared on Origo Sound the same year as a promo.

Microgravity was picked up by Apollo (the ambient sub-label of R&S Records) and reissued in 1992, marking the beginning of Biosphere’s main international phase.

Apollo years (1992–1999)

Patashnik (1994, Apollo, cat# AMB 3927 CD) is the defining Apollo-era statement. Produced and written by Jenssen, with digital edits by Alexander Stojanovic. Sleeve design by The Designers Republic. “Botanical Dimensions” features a co-writing credit for Karsten Brustad. The album demonstrated the sonic template that would carry through Substrata: extended ambient pieces, rhythmic undertow, and a sense of deep, cold space.

The Designers Republic (Sheffield) provided sleeve design for Patashnik, Seti Project, and Novelty Waves — a significant design relationship.

Collaboration: Polar Sequences (1996)

Polar Sequences (Beyond, 1996) is a collaborative album with The Higher Intelligence Agency (Bobby Bird), released on the Beyond label. The six-track album — credited jointly as “HiA | Biosphere” — runs to nearly 57 minutes of deep ambient techno. Jenssen is credited for written-by and performer; Bird likewise. Subsequent live recordings from this period were compiled on Birmingham Frequencies (Headphone, 2000).

Substrata (All Saints Records, 1997)

Substrata (All Saints Records (2), 1997, cat# ASCD33) is the landmark album. Produced and written by Geir Jenssen, with digital editing by Alexander Stojanovic and additional EQ by Audun Strype. Design by David Coppenhall. Mastered by Nimbus. 11 tracks, running approximately 61 minutes; all tracks named for botanical or geographical terms (“Chukhung,” “Kobresia,” “Poa Alpina,” “Antennaria,” etc.). The album was voted one of the all-time classic ambient albums by Hyperreal.org. It was distributed in various territories through Thirsty Ear (US), Paradise Island / For Life Records (Japan), and Origo Sound (Norway).

Insomnia (Origo Sound, 1997)

Insomnia (Origo Sound, 1997, cat# SOUND 15) is a soundtrack album. 16 short tracks plus a remix of “Insomnia” by Alanïa (Ole Mjøs and Rune Lindbæk plus Torbjørn Brundtland). The album features vocal contributions from Mia Kaarinadotter. Design by Harald Lervik and Peter Tubaas; photography by Torunn Nilsen.

Nordheim Transformed (Rune Grammofon, 1998)

Biosphere contributed to Nordheim Transformed (Rune Grammofon, 1998), a tribute / reinterpretation album dedicated to Norwegian composer Arne Nordheim, alongside Deathprod (Helge Sten) and Kim Hiorthøy among others. This situates Jenssen within the broader Norwegian experimental/avant-garde scene.

Touch Period (2000–2015)

Biosphere became the primary commercial artist on Touch, releasing a run of albums across 15 years. The label counted 72 primary release credits for Biosphere, a figure inflated by multiple pressings, vinyl variants, and CDR releases but nonetheless reflecting his dominance in the label’s output. Jon Wozencroft handled photography and design across the Touch catalogue.

Departed Glories (Smalltown Supersound, 2016)

Departed Glories (Smalltown Supersound, STS281LP) is a double-vinyl / CD release conceptually structured around historical photographs by Sergei Prokudin-Gorskii (Russia’s early color photographer, 1863–1944). Mastered and cut by Stefan Betke (Pole). Design by Jacob Grönbech Jensen. 17 tracks.

Open Questions

  • Full scope of the Bleep, Cosmic Explorer, and Levi (28) alias discographies — not systematically traced in Discogs; partial overlap with known releases likely
  • The Geir Jenssen Discogs entry (ID: 25441) is treated as the same person as Biosphere (ID: 3956) by Discogs; both entries are consistent aliases of the same artist. No separate stub needed — cross-link only.
  • Substrata was released on All Saints Records (2) in the UK (ASCD33), not on Biophon Records; the connection to Biophon is that Insomnia (1997) was later reissued on Biophon in 2024, and Biophon is Jenssen’s own label from the 2010s onward
  • Film soundtracks (Im Schatten, Kill By Inches, Nokas, all 2012, Biophon Records) credited primarily to Geir Jenssen rather than Biosphere — full filmographic scope not confirmed in DB
  • The number “72 primary releases on Touch” (seen in Touch.md) includes all format variants, pressings, and reissues; the master-deduplicated primary album count is substantially smaller (approx. 12 distinct album titles on Touch)
  • AD 93 relationship (2021, 2025) and whether this label represents a sustained new chapter or an incidental home — web sources would clarify
