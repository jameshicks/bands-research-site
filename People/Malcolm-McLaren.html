Malcolm McLaren

British impresario, artist, and musician. Born Malcolm Robert Andrew McLaren, 22 January 1946, Stoke Newington, London; died 8 April 2010, New York. Discogs artist ID: 24495.

Biography

Early Career and Management (1970s)

McLaren’s entry into music came through management and image-making rather than performance. In the early 1970s he became involved with New York Dolls, briefly managing and restyling them in a communist-red leather phase (1975) that did not extend their career. Back in London he co-ran the King’s Road boutique SEX with Vivienne Westwood, which became a gathering point for what would become punk.

He assembled Sex Pistols (Discogs ID: 31753) in 1975–76 from among his shop customers and associates, managing them through their entire lifespan. The Sex Pistols’ records were released on Virgin and A&M (briefly); McLaren credited himself as “Advisor [Ideas]” on Some Product - Carri On Sex Pistols (1979, Virgin) and has a Vocals credit on The Great Rock ‘N’ Roll Swindle soundtrack (1979, Virgin). He is also the primary artist on the Sex Pistols-era cash-in “You Need Hands” (1979, Sex Pistols Records).

After the Sex Pistols disintegrated McLaren approached Adam Ant with interest in managing Adam and the Ants. Instead of managing Ant, he poached Ant’s entire backing band and created Bow Wow Wow (1980) around teenage vocalist Annabella Lwin. On Bow Wow Wow’s early releases (EMI, 1980–81) McLaren held Producer and Written-By credits, including “C·30 C·60 C·90 Go” (1980) and the Your Cassette Pet cassette EP. He also managed Adam Ant briefly before their falling out.

Duck Rock and Hip-Hop Pioneer Phase (1982–1985)

McLaren’s solo debut proper came with “Buffalo Gals” (1982, Charisma), produced by Trevor Horn with co-writing from Anne Dudley. It was among the first UK singles to incorporate hip-hop scratching, produced in New York with the World’s Famous Supreme Team (Discogs ID: 81951). The album Duck Rock (1983, Charisma; UK master: release ID 29852) assembled South African township sounds (with Mahlathini And The Mahotella Queens uncredited on backing vocals), Appalachian square-dance music, and New York hip-hop. Production was by Trevor Horn; string arrangements and keyboards by Anne Dudley; additional keyboards by Thomas Dolby; engineer Gary Langan (later of The Art Of Noise); graffiti artwork by Dondi White; cover illustration by Keith Haring. Duck Rock was reissued on The state51 Conspiracy in 2023 (cat. MMDR1).

Singles from Duck Rock included “Double Dutch” (1983, Charisma), “Soweto” (1983, Charisma), and “D’ya Like Scratchin’” / “World’s Famous” (both on Island Records in the US). The US edition of Duck Rock and its associated EPs appeared on Island.

Opera and Fans (1984–1985)

Fans (1984, Charisma/Virgin; release ID 76880) pivoted to opera-derived source material — Puccini, Bizet — reassembled in pop productions. Producers were Stephen Hague (on the majority of tracks) and Robbie Kilgore. The “Carmen” single (1984, Charisma/Virgin) and “Madame Butterfly” (1984, Charisma/Virgin, the album’s biggest European hit) came from this period. “Swamp Thing” (1985, Charisma/Virgin) continued in a similar direction.

Waltz Darling and the Bootzilla Orchestra (1989)

McLaren assembled Malcolm McLaren And The Bootzilla Orchestra (Discogs ID: 74412) for the album Waltz Darling (1989, Epic). Members included Bootsy Collins, Jeff Beck, and vocalists Lourdes and Pretty Fatt alongside McLaren. Producers included Bootsy Collins and Phil Ramone. The Waltz Darling sessions also generated “Deep In Vogue” (1989, Epic), “House Of The Blue Danube” (1989, Epic), and “Something’s Jumpin’ In Your Shirt” (1989, Epic) — the last credited to Malcolm McLaren And The House Of McLaren (Discogs ID: 174628).

World’s Famous Supreme Team / Operaa House (1990)

McLaren presented the compilation Round The Outside! Round The Outside! (1990, Virgin Music Canada) and the Operaa House single (1990, Virgin) credited to “Malcolm McLaren Presents The World’s Famous Supreme Team Show.” Both drew on the Supreme Team connection and his house/vogue-scene interest.

The Ghosts of Oxford Street and RCA (1991)

“Magic’s Back (Theme From ‘The Ghosts Of Oxford Street’)” (1991, RCA) featured vocalist Alison Limerick. The production was co-credited to Stock & Waterman. McLaren is also associated with the Channel 4 TV special The Ghosts of Oxford Street (1991, dir. Julian Temple), though this does not appear in Discogs as a primary credit.

Paris (1994)

Paris (1994, Disques Vogue; release ID 134262) was co-produced and arranged by McLaren with Leigh Gorman (ex-Bow Wow Wow) and Robin Millar. The UK version was titled Malcolm McLaren’s Paris (No! label; release ID 168462) and starred Catherine Deneuve. US release was on Island as The Largest Movie House In Paris. Companion project Fantastic Planet (Discogs ID: 144836) united McLaren with Leigh Gorman and Harrison Sacco.

Late Career (1995–2010)

“Revenge Of The Flowers” (1995, Gee Street/No!) and “The Bell Song” (1997, Red Parrot Recordings) continued his output. “Buffalo Gals Back To Skool” (1998, Virgin) and Buffalo Gals Stampede (1998, Virgin) revisited his hip-hop origins — the latter credited McLaren alongside the World’s Famous Supreme Team, Rakim, and Roger Sanchez.

McLaren’s songwriting credits became widely sampled and covered: “Buffalo Gals” and “World’s Famous” / “Hobo Scratch” are credited on De La Soul’s Stakes Is High (2003), Eminem’s The Eminem Show (2002, as a “Written-By” credit, likely for a sample), and the Kill Bill Vol. 2 soundtrack (Maverick). His written-by credit appears on Mariah Carey’s “Honey” (1997, Columbia) and multiple other pop releases of the late 1990s–2000s.
