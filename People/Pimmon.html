Australian experimental electronic musician working in glitch, EAI (electro-acoustic improvisation), and laptop composition. One of the core figures in the Sydney/Australian experimental digital underground of the late 1990s and 2000s, closely linked to Oren Ambarchi and the European Mego/Ritornell scene.

Biography

Early Work and US/Australian Debut (1997–1999)

Pimmon’s first recorded appearance is Copper Hats (1997, Freedom From), a US release with no additional credits on Discogs — a spare debut that places him among the earliest Australian artists in the laptop/glitch genre internationally. In 1998 came Zwischen Die Sprunge / Endlose Reise on the Australian label Snawklor, a cassette or limited release representing his domestic foothold. Both releases belong to the formative glitch-laptop moment before the genre crystallised around Vienna’s Mego label and its international satellites.

International Connections and the Ritornell Moment (2000)

2000 was Pimmon’s breakout year internationally, producing three significant releases:

(while) (Fällt, 2000, Ireland) — a solo record on the Dublin experimental label Fällt (Discogs ID: 5822), which positioned him alongside contemporaries in the European digital music scene. Credits are minimal on Discogs; the release carries no additional credited personnel.

Split Series #8 (FatCat Records, 2000, UK) — split LP with James Plotkin, issued on FatCat’s Split Series alongside Matmos and Motion (2). Pimmon contributed tracks B1–B3, credited to Paul Gough as “Sounds”. Mastered by Mandy Parnell (Pimmon’s side) and Tim Dennen (Plotkin’s side). This is one of his earliest UK appearances and signals FatCat’s awareness of the Australian experimental scene.

Afternoon Tea (Ritornell, 2000, Germany) — the pivotal document. A five-way collaborative improvisation with Oren Ambarchi (guitar, editing, mixing), Fennesz (computer), Peter Rehberg (computer), and Keith Rowe (guitar). Pimmon and his alias Paul Gough are both credited for “Computer”. Cover by Alorenz (Angela Lorenz). Engineered by Chris Townend. Released on Ritornell (Discogs ID: 761), the Frankfurt sublabel of Mille Plateaux under Achim Szepanski. This recording joins Pimmon to the core Viennese Mego circle and the broader transatlantic EAI conversation of that moment.

Afternoon Tea also appeared in the FatCat promo compilation Split Series #11 / Split Series #8 (2000), alongside Matmos and Motion (2), giving the record broader UK distribution context.

The Mort Aux Vaches Period (2003)

Mort Aux Vaches (Mort Aux Vaches, 2003, Netherlands) — solo release on the Dutch Mort Aux Vaches live-recording series (associated with Staalplaat), produced by Berry Kamer, Jan Hiddink, and Maurice Woestenburg, with Berry Kamer also as recording engineer. Paul Gough credited for all music. Staalplaat handled artwork and design concept. The Mort Aux Vaches series documented international experimental and noise artists in live/radio contexts, placing Pimmon in a roster that included many of the same European avant-garde figures he’d been collaborating with.

The 2003 Australian compilation Grain (Dorobo Limited Editions) also featured Pimmon alongside Byron Scullin (aka Spiderface), David Brown (3) (aka Candlesnuffer), Darrin Verhagen (aka Shinjuku Filth / E.P.A.), and Philip Samartzis — a useful snapshot of the Sydney/Melbourne experimental electronic scene at the time.

Cromim and Late Output (2007–2013)

From 2007 onward Pimmon self-released on his own Cromim imprint:

  • Decoration: Underline (Cromim, 2007, Australia) — no additional Discogs credits
  • Sounds Of Perth (Cromim, 2010, Australia) — no additional Discogs credits
  • Just Because You’re Spatial (Cromim, 2011) — no additional Discogs credits

The Oansome Orbit (Room40, 2011, Australia) — released on Room40 (Discogs ID: 16420), Lawrence English’s Brisbane label. Written By credited to Paul Gough. Design by whenstudio.com. The Room40 connection aligns Pimmon with the Australian experimental label that had become internationally prominent through artists like Lawrence English and Grouper.

Procrastination (Home Normal, 2013, Japan) — collaboration with Italian sound artist Fabio Orsi (Discogs ID: 402050). Pimmon credited for “Loops, Synth, Arranged By, Edited By [Layered], Mixed By”; Fabio Orsi for “Guitar, Effects [Filters], Synth” and cover art. Mastered by Giuseppe Ielasi. The most fully documented production credit in his discography; issued on the Japanese ambient label Home Normal.
