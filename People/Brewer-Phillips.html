Brewer Phillips

American blues guitarist and singer. Born November 16, 1924 in Youngsburg, Mississippi. Died August 30, 1999 in Chicago, Illinois. Discogs artist ID: 622079.

Biography

Brewer Phillips grew up in Mississippi and relocated to Chicago in his mid-thirties, arriving in the city that was the center of postwar electric blues. Once in Chicago he worked the South Side club circuit, playing as a sideman for various artists including Roosevelt Sykes and Bill Harvey before landing the role that would define his recorded legacy.

In the late 1960s Phillips became the second guitarist in Hound Dog Taylor’s trio, Hound Dog Taylor & The House Rockers. The band’s lineup was lean and raw: Taylor on lead slide guitar, Phillips on rhythm guitar, and Ted Harvey on drums. No bass. The two-guitar interplay — Taylor’s slashing Delta-inflected slide against Phillips’s driving rhythm and occasional lead fills — was the signature sound of the group.

When Bruce Iglauer founded Alligator Records in 1971, the House Rockers were his first signing. Hound Dog Taylor And The House Rockers (AL 4701) was Alligator’s debut release, placing Phillips at the foundation of the entire label’s history. The band recorded two studio albums for Alligator before Taylor’s death from lung cancer in December 1975.

After Taylor died, Phillips continued to work with Ted Harvey and other Chicago blues figures. He appeared on sessions under J.B. Hutto’s name and toured with various configurations of former House Rockers. In the early 1980s he cut his first album as a leader, Ingleside Blues (Wolf Records, 1982), recorded in Europe with Harvey on drums. A second Wolf session, Have Some Fun (1992), was credited to The Houserockers featuring Hound Dog Taylor, Brewer Phillips, and Ted Harvey — posthumous archive material from Taylor combined with new Phillips and Harvey performances.

Through the 1980s–90s, Phillips appeared on several releases alongside rock and blues enthusiast Cub Koda (of Brownsville Station). The two collaborated in the outfit Cub Koda & The Houserockers, alongside Ted Harvey. A double-header live album, Good House-Rockin’ (Wolf Records, 1995), co-credited to Phillips and Harvey, drew on recordings from multiple eras and featured guest appearances by Hutto and Koda.

Phillips’s final recorded release under his own name was Well Alright (Black Rose, 1999), a collection produced by Ron Bartolucci in which Hound Dog Taylor, J.B. Hutto, Louis Myers, Leroy Pina, and Cub Koda are credited as accompanists — reflecting either archival recordings or the producers’ documentation of his broader career network. Phillips died in Chicago in August 1999, the same year of that release.

In Japan, P-Vine Records issued Homebrew (1995), crediting Phillips as a primary artist. Alligator compiled House Rockers material into a Deluxe Edition set in 1999.

Scene Context

Phillips was part of the raw, no-frills South Side Chicago blues tradition that Hound Dog Taylor represented: Delta influences filtered through electric amplification, played loud and fast in small clubs. The House Rockers’ three-piece, bass-less format was unusual and deliberate. Their 1971 Alligator debut helped establish that label as the premier home for this kind of unvarnished Chicago blues, setting the template for the label’s subsequent roster. Phillips’s mid-career European recordings (the Wolf Records sessions from Vienna) reflect the intense interest in this style among European blues fans in the early 1980s — a circuit many American blues veterans worked extensively. His collaborations with Cub Koda, a rock musician and blues scholar, brought the Houserockers’ sound to a slightly broader rock-informed audience in the 1990s.
