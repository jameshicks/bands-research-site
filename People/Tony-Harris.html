Tony Harris

Discogs artist ID: 208740. UK recording engineer, mixer, and occasional producer. Active from the early 1980s through the 2010s, Harris is most closely identified with Livingston Studios in North London, where he recorded a wide-ranging roster of UK independent, alternative, and mainstream artists across roughly three decades. He appears on well over 130 releases in the Discogs database.

Overview

Harris’s career spans multiple genres and eras without a single obvious genre niche. His credits run from post-punk and experimental work (Coil, Slab!, Anne Clark-adjacent acts on Ink Records) through major-label alternative rock (R.E.M., The Jesus And Mary Chain, Julian Cope, The Triffids) to pop, jazz, Celtic, and classical crossover. What unites the bulk of his work is the Livingston Studios address in North London, which appears explicitly in several of his credits (“Engineer [Livingston Studios London],” “Recorded By [At Livingston],” etc.) — suggesting he was either staff or a long-term resident engineer there.

He is listed in the Red Flame and Ink Records databases as one of the labels’ most used engineers, with 18 credited appearances across those two imprints between 1984 and 1991.

Career Phases

Early Period: Apprenticeship and First Credits (1980–1983)

Harris’s earliest Discogs credit is as assistant engineer on Girlschool’s Shuffle ‘n’ Cut (1980, Logo Records), assisting Roger Tebbut at Pebble Beach Studios. He was also engineer on the Direct Lines / Le Chorale 7” (Scratch Records, 1981). A 1980 credit as Producer on an obscure release (“Quite Like You,” Not On Label) suggests early independent production activity alongside his engineering work.

By 1983 he was credited as Assistant Producer and Assistant Engineer on Interrupted Journey (PRT, 1983), still in an apprentice capacity, alongside work on Charisma and K-Tel projects suggesting access to mainstream studio bookings.

  • Ambition — Atlantic (1987) — additional engineering

  • Big Music — Venture (1988) — additional recording

Livingston Studios Connection

Multiple Discogs credits explicitly name Livingston Studios (sometimes spelled “Livingstone Studios”) in North London as the recording location, including:

  • “Engineer [Livingston Studios London]” — David Harrow / Pinkie Maclure, 1985
  • “Engineer [Livingston Studios]” — Sufferhead EP (Fela Kuti-adjacent), 1985
  • “Engineer [Editing – Livingston Studios]; Engineer [Recording – Livingston Studios]” — Beyond The Astral Skies, 1985
  • “Recorded By [At Livingston]” — Coil, Horse Rotorvator, 1986
  • “Recorded By [Drums Recorded At Livingstone Studios By]” — Kitchens of Distinction (2024 reissue credit)

The production company “Livingston Productions” appears as a co-label on Harris’s 1987 credit for Brilliant (Final Cut Records), suggesting a closer-than-freelance relationship with the studio.
