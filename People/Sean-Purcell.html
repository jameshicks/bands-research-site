Overview

Sean Purcell (real name John Joseph Purcell) was an English singer, songwriter, and producer who died in 1996. Best known as the frontman of Cuddly Toys — a new wave and synth-pop act that evolved from the punk band Raped — he also appeared as a member of Goat (4). His most concentrated recording period ran from the late 1970s through the early 1980s, with nine releases on Fresh Records between 1980 and 1982. Closest recurring collaborators across that run included Tony Baggett, Faebhean Kwest, and Paddy Phield, with further work spanning glam and minimal styles.

Notes (web)

Name change: Raped → Cuddly Toys (January 1979)

The name change had two overlapping causes: a practical commercial problem and a specific outside prompt.

The problem: Despite generating press attention, the name “Raped” had become a commercial liability — it blocked airplay, made it impossible to secure a record contract in the UK, and created what Faebhean Kwest described as a “bad media vibe.” punk77.co.uk/band/raped/

The prompt: According to Faebhean Kwest, it was John Peel who triggered the actual decision. Peel had already been playing the band’s records on Radio 1 and wanted to record a session with them. He wrote them a letter suggesting they adopt “the silliest name possible” — offering examples including “The Fluffy Bunnies” and “Cuddly Toys.” Kwest stated he still has Peel’s letter. Sean Purcell then proposed “Cuddly Toys” from among the alternatives (others floated internally included “Chocolate Fireguard” and “The Moonrockets”). killyourpetpuppy.co.uk/news/raped-cuddly-toys-parole-overseas-records/ and punk77.co.uk/band/cuddly-toys/

Kwest’s direct quote (via Kill Your Pet Puppy / Punk 77): “The name was changed because of John Peel of all people. He had already been playing our stuff on the radio a few times. He wanted to do a session with us, and suggested a complete opposite extreme name to Raped like ‘The Fluffy Bunnies’ or ‘Cuddly Toys’ or something and we thought, yeah.”

The change was not label-driven — Fresh Records and the Japan deal with Teichiku Records came after the rename.

Sound shift: Tony Baggett confirmed the name change aligned with a musical shift already underway: “Raped didn’t fit with the sound anymore.” The new direction moved from raw punk shock-value material toward glam-influenced new wave/post-punk. punk77.co.uk/band/cuddly-toys/
