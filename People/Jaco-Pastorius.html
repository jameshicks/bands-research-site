Jaco Pastorius

Electric bassist, composer, and bandleader. Born John Francis Anthony Pastorius III, 1 December 1951, Norristown, Pennsylvania; died 21 September 1987, Fort Lauderdale, Florida, aged 35. Discogs artist ID: 10616.

Widely regarded as the most influential electric bassist of the 20th century — his fretless technique, melodic approach, and compositional ambition redefined the instrument’s role in jazz and fusion. Best known for his tenure with Weather Report (1976–1981) and his two solo studio albums on Epic and Warner Bros. Records. Also credited as Nelson (Jocko) Padron (alias).

Overview

Pastorius was raised in Fort Lauderdale. He played drums before switching to bass in his teens after injuring his right hand. He was self-taught on fretless electric bass — a choice that gave him his distinctive singing, cello-like tone. By the early 1970s he was working South Florida club circuits, briefly with Wayne Cochran & The C.C. Riders and Tommy Strand & The Upper Hand. His friend and neighbour Alex Darqui spelled his childhood nickname “Jocko” as “Jaco” by mistake; Pastorius kept the spelling.

He was inducted posthumously into Down Beat’s Jazz Hall of Fame in 1988 — one of only seven bassists so honoured, and the only electric bassist.

Family: Father of Mary Pastorius and Johnny Pastorius (with first wife Tracy) and Felix Pastorius (with second wife Ingrid Pastorius). Uncle of David Pastorius.

Solo Discography (as leader)

Jaco Pastorius (1976, Epic)

Discogs release ID: 1038489 (Europe pressing); master ID: 49071. Produced by Bobby Colomby.

His debut solo album, recorded with a remarkable cast: Herbie Hancock (piano, Fender Rhodes), Wayne Shorter (soprano saxophone on “Opus Pocus”), Lenny White (drums), Don Alias (congas), David Sanborn (alto sax on “Come On, Come Over”), Michael Brecker and Randy Brecker (horns), Hubert Laws (piccolo flute), Othello Molineaux (steel drums), and string arrangements conducted by Michael Gibbs. Solo bass showcase “Portrait of Tracy” introduced harmonic flageolet technique to a wide audience; opening track “Donna Lee” (Charlie Parker) became the de facto audition piece for a generation of bassists. Labels: Epic (EPC 81453). Genres: Funk/Soul, Jazz; styles: Fusion, Post Bop.

Key tracks: “Donna Lee,” “Come On, Come Over,” “Continuum,” “Portrait of Tracy,” “Opus Pocus,” “(Used To Be A) Cha-Cha”

Pastorius / Metheny / Ditmas / Bley (1976, Improvising Artists Inc.)

Discogs release ID: 1064011 (US); master ID: 49066. Quartet session with Pat Metheny (guitar), Bruce Ditmas (drums), and Paul Bley (piano). Recorded before any of them were widely known; documents the pre-Weather Report Jaco in a free-leaning context.

Word Of Mouth (1981, Warner Bros. Records)

Discogs release ID: 934804 (Europe); master ID: 49085.

His second and final solo studio album. Self-produced big band record; one of the most ambitious fusion albums of its era. Tracklist: “Crisis,” “3 Views of a Secret,” “Liberty City,” “Chromatic Fantasy” (Bach arr. Jaco), “Blackbird” (Lennon/McCartney), “Word of Mouth,” “John and Mary.” Labels: Warner Bros. Records (BSK 3535). Styles: Fusion, Jazz-Rock. The touring ensemble around this record became the Word of Mouth Big Band.

Invitation (1983, Warner Bros. Records)

Discogs release ID: 1044330 (Europe); master ID: 129936. Live big band recording, produced and arranged by Jaco. Massive lineup including Peter Erskine (drums), Toots Thielemans (harmonica), Don Alias (percussion), Othello Molineaux (steel drums), Randy Brecker and Jon Faddis (trumpet), Bob Mintzer, Alex Foster, Paul McCandless (reeds), and Dave Bargeron (trombone/tuba). Covers include “Invitation,” “Sophisticated Lady,” “Reza/Giant Steps,” and Jaco originals “Liberty City” and “Continuum.”

The Birthday Concert (1995, Warner Bros. Records)

Discogs release ID: 1457014 (US); master ID: 232274. Posthumously released recording of a 1981 concert in Fort Lauderdale on Jaco’s 30th birthday; features the Word of Mouth Big Band.

Truth, Liberty & Soul — Live In NYC: The Complete 1982 NPR Jazz Alive! Recordings (2017, Resonance Records)

Discogs release ID: 10131632 (US); master ID: 1175692. Complete NPR broadcast of the Word of Mouth Big Band at the Savoy, New York, 1982. One of the most complete documents of the big band at its peak.

Modern American Music…Period! The Criteria Sessions (2014, Omnivore Recordings)

Discogs release ID: 5610057 (USA & Canada); master ID: 744867. Early Fort Lauderdale studio recordings; Jaco on bass, electric piano (Fender Rhodes), and as producer/songwriter.

Weather Report (1976–1981)

Jaco joined Weather Report — founded by Joe Zawinul and Wayne Shorter — in 1976, replacing Alphonso Johnson on bass. His arrival transformed the group’s sound: he brought melodic invention, harmonic sophistication, and showmanship that turned the bass into a front-line voice.

Heavy Weather (Discogs master ID: 21748) — the commercial peak. Full band: Joe Zawinul (synths, keys), Wayne Shorter (saxophones), Alex Acuña (drums), Manolo Badrena (percussion). “Teen Town” showcases Jaco playing both bass and drums simultaneously. The Legendary Live Tapes: 1978–1981 (2015, Columbia/Legacy) draws from the Weather Report archive across this period. The Twins I & II releases (1982, Warner Bros., Japan) document the Aurex Jazz Festival with Jaco as producer and arranger.

Hejira (Discogs release ID: 1011632; master ID: 47775) is the landmark — Jaco’s fretless bass weaves through Joni Mitchell’s open-tuned guitar and vocals across four tracks. Shadows and Light (1980, Asylum) documents the Mitchell/Pastorius live band with Peter Erskine (drums), Pat Metheny (guitar), Don Alias (congas), and Herbie Hancock (electric piano).

Pat Metheny

Bright Size Life (1976, ECM Records) — Discogs release ID: 1914113; master ID: 62235. Pat Metheny’s ECM debut; trio with Jaco on bass and Bob Moses on drums. Produced by Manfred Eicher. One of ECM’s defining early recordings.

Pastorius / Metheny / Ditmas / Bley (1976, Improvising Artists Inc.) — free quartet session.

Deadline / Celluloid (1985)

Makosaa Rock (1985, Celluloid Records, CEL 704) — Discogs release ID: 1083148; master ID: 13990. 12” single by Deadline, featuring Jaco on bass (side A “Makosaa Rock,” 11:03, and B1 edit). Deadline was a Bill Laswell / Phillip Wilson project; this session also featured Manu Dibango (saxophone), Bernie Worrell (synthesizer), Olu Dara (cornet), Steve Turre (didgeridoo), Paul Butterfield (harmonica), and Aiyb Dieng (percussion). B2 (“Gammatron”) features Jonas Hellborg on bass instead. Note: the Discogs title is “Makosaa Rock”; the stub previously referred to this as “Down By Law,” which is a different Deadline release.
