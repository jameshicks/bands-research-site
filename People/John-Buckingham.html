John Buckingham

Discogs ID: 326415

John Buckingham (1940–2017) was an American tuba player active in free jazz and avant-garde music. He is best known as a core member of the Bill Dixon Quartet, alongside Bill Dixon, Mario Pavone, and Laurence Cook, and appeared on three of Dixon’s most significant Soul Note recordings from the late 1980s and early 1990s. His earliest documented credit places him in the large ensemble for Carla Bley and Paul Haines’ Escalator Over the Hill in 1971.

The tuba is a rare instrument in jazz contexts, making Buckingham’s sustained presence in the Dixon orbit notable. Dixon’s work in this period drew on extreme dynamics and the instrument’s low-frequency weight, and Buckingham’s tuba supplied a foundation that displaced the conventional bass role on some sessions while coexisting with multiple bassists on others.

Note on Vade Mecum (1994): The brief sent to research this article listed Vade Mecum as a Buckingham credit, but Discogs does not list him in that session’s personnel. The 1994 Vade Mecum features Barry Guy and William Parker on double bass, Tony Oxley on percussion, and Dixon on trumpet/flugelhorn — no tuba. He may have been present but uncredited, or the attribution in the brief was an error.

Bill Dixon Quartet

The Bill Dixon Quartet is listed on Discogs as a distinct group entity (ID: 9272440) with four members: Bill Dixon, John Buckingham, Laurence Cook, and Mario Pavone. This core unit is the group behind Son Of Sisyphus (1990) and likely the working band for much of Dixon’s late-1980s to early-1990s period. No vault page for the group exists yet — it overlaps significantly with Bill Dixon 7-Tette (the larger ensemble) and the formations behind Thoughts.
