George Haslam

Born: Preston, England, 22 February 1939 Instruments: Baritone Saxophone, Tárogató, Alto Saxophone, Bass Clarinet, Clarinet Discogs artist ID: 718264

British free jazz and improvised music saxophonist; founder and operator of Slam Productions (est. 1989). One of the most prolific figures on the British free jazz scene from the late 1980s onward, Haslam is distinguished by his extensive international touring — particularly in Argentina and Eastern Europe — and by his parallel role as a label operator who has released over 250 albums of jazz and improvised music. Voted 19th All Time World Greatest Baritone Saxist in the Japanese Encyclopaedia of Jazz.

Biography

Early Career (pre-1989)

Haslam came up through the British jazz circuit, notably appearing on the 1984 BBC-documented live recording with a Hungarian ensemble (released as Live In Hungary on Spotlite Records). By the mid-1980s he was active in British improvised music, appearing on Natural Music ‘85 (1985) and The Healing (1986), the latter crediting him on baritone and tenor saxophone, design, and illustration. He led the first British jazz group to play Cuba in 1986 and became the first British jazzman to play Argentina in 1989.

Slam Productions and the Early 1990s

In 1989 Haslam founded the SLAM CD label (later Slam Productions), issuing the debut 1989 – And All That (SLAMCD 301), a duo with trombonist Paul Rutherford (2). The label immediately became a vehicle both for his own projects and for the broader British free jazz community.

The quintet album Termite One (1990, Bruce’s Fingers) established his core UK circle: a co-led date with Lol Coxhill (soprano sax), Paul Rutherford (2) (trombone), Paul Hession (drums), and Simon H. Fell (double bass). All five contributed compositions. The same year, the quartet of Coxhill/Haslam/Howard Riley/Rutherford appeared on The Holywell Concert (SLAMCD 302), recorded live in Oxford with digital recording by Michael Gerzon and produced by Haslam.

Level Two (1993, Slam CD) expanded the personnel to include Howard Riley (piano), bassist Marcio Mattos, drummer Tony Marsh, and vocalist Liz Hodgson on two tracks, with recording by regular engineer Tim Turan.

In 1993–94, Slam released albums by Steve Lacy / Mal Waldron (Let’s Call This…. Esteem, SLAMCD 501) and the duo Waldron – Haslam (1994), a meeting of Haslam with pianist Mal Waldron that showcased the label’s ability to attract international names.

Argentine Adventures (1994–2010)

Haslam’s Argentine connections produced one of the most distinctive threads of his career. Beginning with Argentine Adventures: In Jazz, Ethnic and Improvised Musics (1994), he documented collaborations with Buenos Aires musicians including bassist Mono Hurtado, pianist/melodicist Ruben Ferrero, percussionist Horacio Straijer, saxophonist Daniel Harari, and vocalist Mirta Insaurralde. The series ran to at least three volumes (Part 2: New Dimensions – Tango, Blues and Freedom, 1996; Part 3: Travels With My Tarogato, 1999) and continued into Once Upon A Time In Argentina (2010) and a live 1997 Buenos Aires session (En Vivo En Dr. Jekyll, released 2020).

The tárogató — a Hungarian single-reed woodwind — became an increasingly prominent voice in these recordings, connecting his work to Eastern European folk strains as well as improvised jazz.

Collaborations and Travel (1997–2007)

Slam released Solos East West (1997), Haslam performing alone in different settings. Harmonance (1999) and Duos East West (1998) continued his pairing projects. He worked with Ukrainian pianist Vladimir Solyanik (Communications, 1998; Duos East West, 1998) and with Finnish musicians via the Anglo-Kuopio Quartet.

Parker/Haslam/Edwards (2001, Slam CD) brought together Evan Parker (soprano/tenor sax) and John Edwards (double bass), recorded and mastered by Tim Turan.

The Mahout (2004) placed Haslam with American free jazz pianist Borah Bergman and drummer Paul Hession, drawing on sessions recorded across multiple cities. Cool Moon (2004) and SHaK (2006) continued the trio/quartet format.

Italian Axis and the Helios Suite (2006–2012)

A sustained Italian connection developed through violinist Stefano Pastor, who became a key collaborator and co-producer. The quartet of Haslam/Pastor/Steve Kershaw (double bass)/Paul Hession produced Helios Suite (2007, Slam CD), their most substantial joint statement. The same personnel — with Eric Smith (16) and Alan Howell recording — reconvened for Holywell Session: Live In Oxford (2007).

Freedom (2010) added Italian musicians Claudio Lugo (soprano sax) and Giorgio Dini (double bass) to the Pastor/Haslam core.

Narcéte (2012) brought in Genoese vocalist-poet Erika Dagnino and trumpeter Steve Waterman, with Pastor handling recording, mixing, mastering, and artwork.

Later Work (2013–present)

Haslam continued recording prolifically into the 2010s and 2020s. Words Unspoken (2013) with Steve Waterman was recorded by Haslam himself. Dada Dandy (2014) and Ancient & Modern (2019) show his continued baritone/tárogató axis. Loveland (2021), co-credited to Friends Of George Haslam, featured a Slovak ensemble including Stefano Pastor, Jozef Láska (double bass), Jan Šikl (drums), and Jan Faix (synthesizer), co-produced with Czech engineer Miloš Latislav.

A celebration release Loveland doubled as a summary of his late-career international reach. As of 2024, he remained active on Slam Productions (Glossolalia, 2024, with Dave Bitelli and Julie Tippetts).

Slam Productions

Label: Slam Productions Discogs label ID: 89815 Founded: 1989, by George Haslam Label code: LC 5526 / LC 05526 Parent label (Discogs): My Only Desire Records (ID: 988521) Total releases (Discogs): ~266

Slam Productions is a free jazz, contemporary jazz, and improvised music label. It operates as Haslam’s primary outlet and also as a curatorial imprint for allied musicians. The label’s catalog spans from UK free improvisation staples (Lol Coxhill, Paul Rutherford (2), Howard Riley, Elton Dean) to Italian jazz-improv artists (Stefano Pastor, Erika Dagnino, Paul Dunmall) and international collaborators (Argentine, Finnish, Ukrainian, Czech, Portuguese).
