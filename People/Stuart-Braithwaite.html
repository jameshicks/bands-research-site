Stuart Braithwaite

Scottish guitarist, bassist, drummer, singer and songwriter. Born 10 May 1976. Best known as founder and primary guitarist of post-rock band Mogwai. Discogs artist ID: 295737. Also records under aliases Holy Stuarto and Plasmatron.

Biography

Early Years and Mogwai’s Formation

Stuart Leslie Braithwaite was born in Scotland on 10 May 1976. He was an early member of the Scottish indie band Eska (Discogs ID: 14353), a Glasgow band formed in 1993 that also included Colin Kearney, Christopher Mack, and Kenny MacLeod. He later briefly played drums for an early recording session — his first Discogs credit is as drummer on the 1995 UK release Trucking & Paving (Modern Independent Records).

Braithwaite co-founded Mogwai around 1995–1996 in Glasgow, alongside Dominic Aitchison, Martin Bulloch, and initially Brendan O’Hare (who departed before the debut album). The band released its first singles on the Glasgow label ché Records in 1996 — the Angels vs. Aliens / Buzzsong split and the Tour Split EP. Their debut album Young Team (1997, Chemikal Underground) used pseudonyms for the band members throughout — Braithwaite appeared as “pLasmatroN”, a nickname that became one of his long-standing aliases. Barry Burns joined on keyboards/guitar for Come On Die Young (1999).

Mogwai Peak Years (1999–2010)

Come On Die Young (1999), produced by Dave Fridmann at Tarbox Road Studios, was the first album to list all five members by name and signalled a move toward more melodic, dynamic arrangements. Paul Savage handled recording duties on early records; Tony Doogan took over as house producer from Mr. Beast (2006) onward, with Fridmann returning for major album cycles.

Mogwai launched Rock Action Records — named after the 2001 album — as their own imprint, which became the primary home for their catalog from Hardcore Will Never Die, But You Will. (2011) onward and also served as a platform for other Scottish artists.

In 2006 Mogwai contributed guitar and performance to the soundtrack of Darren Aronofsky’s film The Fountain alongside composer Clint Mansell and Kronos Quartet. Braithwaite and John Cummings are individually credited for guitar on that release.

Side Projects and Collaborations

Alongside his Mogwai work Braithwaite has maintained an active parallel life in other projects:

The Sick Anchors (2002) — a one-release project with Aidan Moffat (of Arab Strap) and Colin Macpherson, released on Lost Dog Recordings. The self-titled EP is the only Sick Anchors release in the Discogs database.

Aloha Hawaii — a duo project with Aidan Moffat. No Discogs primary releases found; noted in band membership records.

The Best-Ofs (Discogs ID: 744076) — a larger Scottish collective including Alun Woodward, Stewart Henderson, Alan Barr, James Swinburne, and others. Released How To Get To Heaven From Scotland (2009) and Knock On The Wall Of Your Womb (2009), both on Chemikal Underground.

Minor Victories (2015–2017, Discogs ID: 4971998) — British alternative rock supergroup with Rachel Goswell (Slowdive), Justin Lockey (producer/guitarist), and James Lockey. Braithwaite is credited as Band, Engineer [Additional], and Written-By on the self-titled debut (Fat Possum Records / Play It Again Sam, 2016). The orchestral reworking Orchestral Variations (Play It Again Sam, 2017), produced and orchestrated by Justin Lockey, carries a Band credit for all four members.

Silver Moth (Discogs ID: 12872573) — a more recent project built around Braithwaite alongside vocalist Evi Vine, Elisabeth Elektra, Matthew Rochford, and Steven Hill. Released the album Black Bay (2023, Bella Union); Braithwaite is credited for Guitar and Synth on five of six tracks.
