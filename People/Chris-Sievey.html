Chris Sievey

Discogs ID: 743289 (as Chris Sievey) · 267701 (as Frank Sidebottom)
Real name: Christopher Mark Sievey
Born: 25 August 1955, Ashton-on-Mersey, Sale, England
Died: 21 June 2010, Wythenshawe, Manchester, England
Also known as: Frank Sidebottom (comedic alter ego); Chris Sievey’s Pyjama Band; Little Frank And The Beastie Puppets; The Sidebottom Twins
Groups: The Freshies · The Bees Knees (4)

English musician, comedian, and artist. Frontman and primary songwriter of The Freshies, the Manchester power-pop group he ran through his own independent label Razz Records (2). From 1984, Sievey performed under the Frank Sidebottom persona — a comedic character with a papier-mâché head, obsessed with his home suburb of Timperley — who became one of the most distinctive cult entertainers in British pop history. Alongside Freshies work and the Frank persona, Sievey released solo material in his own name, including the 1986 LP Big Record on Alan Jenkins’ Cordelia Records. He died of leukaemia in 2010.

Early Work and The Bees Knees (1975)

Sievey’s earliest recorded work is with The Bees Knees (4) on Early Recordings 1975 — issued posthumously on the Sidetrack Music label in 2005 (Discogs ID: 26658548). He is credited with lead vocals, guitar, saxophone, violin, and drums on the recording. This predates The Freshies and gives some indication of the wide-ranging home-recording approach that would define his career.

The Freshies (1977–1984)

The Freshies were a Manchester power-pop group fronted by Sievey as vocalist, guitarist, and sole writer. The band operated largely on his own Razz Records (2) imprint. Sievey produced the records himself and handled sleeve and label design.

The “Manchester Megastore” single achieved significant radio play (notably Radio 1 airplay) and is considered the commercial peak of Sievey’s pre-Frank period. The Freshies dissolved in 1984 when Sievey launched the Frank Sidebottom persona as the band’s mascot, then as a solo act.

Solo Work as Chris Sievey (1983–1986)

“Camouflage” (1983)

Single on Random Records (11), cat. RND1 (Discogs ID: 2675252). Produced by Martin Hannett, engineered by Chris Nagle. Drums by Mike Doherty. A-side “Camouflage” is 3:20; B-side includes “Camouflage” (alternate), “Flying Train”, and “Ft”. Genres: Electronic/Rock, styles: Pop Rock, Synth-pop. Hannett’s production credit makes this one of only a handful of post-Joy Division Hannett productions documented on Discogs; the connection is notable given Hannett’s Manchester Factory circle.

Big Record (1986)

LP on Cordelia Records, Ericat 015 (Discogs ID: 861664). The most substantial release under Sievey’s own name.

Lisa Stansfield’s contribution predates her commercial breakthrough on Arista by several years; she appears here in an uncredited-capacity guest role on female vocals, her presence attributable to overlapping Manchester connections. Co-producer Mike Doherty was the Freshies drummer. The LP was issued on Alan Jenkins’ Cordelia label in Leicester as part of the ERICAT series (Ericat 015), which ran from 1984 to c. 1987 and covered a range of UK home-recording and US cassette-underground acts.

A second Cordelia entry exists (CD066, 2016, Discogs ID: 8483081) but this appears to be an R. Stevie Moore release (Verve) listed under an adjacent Cordelia catalog run, not a Sievey release; the overlap in the stub is an error.

Frank Sidebottom (1984–2010)

Frank Sidebottom was created by Sievey in 1984 initially as a mascot for The Freshies. The character — wearing a large papier-mâché head, speaking in a mannered falsetto, and fixated on Timperley, Greater Manchester — was first signed to Regal Zonophone in 1985 and then launched a series of independent labels: his own 11:37 (named after his stage debut time) and then In Tape distribution through the Sidetrack Music operation.

Discogs lists Frank Sidebottom as a distinct artist (ID: 267701) with realname Christopher Mark Sievey. The character’s years active in Discogs run 1982–2024, reflecting reissues extending after Sievey’s death.
