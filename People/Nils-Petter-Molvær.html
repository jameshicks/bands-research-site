Biography

Early Career and the Oslo Jazz Scene (1983–1996)

Molvær grew up on the island of Sula off the Norwegian coast and came up through the Oslo jazz scene in the early 1980s. His first documented Discogs credits are session trumpet work from 1983, including the Norwegian band Masqualero’s debut (Odin, 1983) — a group he was a member of alongside Jon Balke, Arild Andersen, Jon Christensen, and Tore Brunborg. Masqualero was one of the key jazz ensembles of that era in Oslo.

Through the late 1980s and early 1990s he accumulated substantial session credits across Scandinavian jazz and pop, appearing on ECM albums by Marilyn Mazur (Marilyn Mazur’s Future Song, 1992), Jon Christensen (So I Write, ECM, 1990), and Terje Rypdal. He was also a member of Oslo 13, a large-ensemble collective led by Jon Balke (their Off Balance appeared on Odin in 1988). His musical touchstones during this period included Miles Davis, Don Cherry, Jon Hassell, Brian Eno, and Bill Laswell — a set of references that would define his later solo direction.

A major early credit as primary composer and performer: Hastening Westward (ECM / ECM New Series, 1995) — a chamber work featuring his trumpet alongside percussion and strings, co-released on ECM’s New Series imprint. This established his relationship with Manfred Eicher and ECM.

Khmer and the ECM Breakthrough (1997–2001)

Khmer (ECM Records, 1997) is the defining release of Molvær’s career and a landmark of jazz-electronica. Produced by Manfred Eicher and Ulf Holand, it sets Molvær’s trumpet — described variously as ethereal and feverish — against programmed electronic beats, sampled textures, and treated guitar. The album was widely praised on release; it received the German Record Critics’ Award and LA Weekly named it Jazz Album of the Year for 1998. Rolling Stone later ranked it 92nd in its 2013 list of 100 best jazz albums.

Molvær also compiled and co-released Khmer (Remixes) (ECM, 1997), a separate remix album featuring contributions from Rockers Hi-Fi, The Herbaliser, Mental Overdrive, and others.

ECM follow-up Solid Ether (2000) continued the formula, again with Eicher as album producer. Molvær handled recording production himself. The Recoloured remix album (2001, EmArcy/Universal) gathered further remix contributors including Bill Laswell and Funkstörung. This period marks the transition from ECM to the EmArcy / Universal system for his studio output.

EmArcy / Universal Period (2001–2009)

Moving to EmArcy (a Universal sublabel), Molvær released a sequence of studio albums maintaining the jazz-electronics fusion:

  • NP3 (2002, EmArcy/Universal) — featured Helge Sten (Deathprod) on electronics and Audun Kleive on drums
  • Live (From Hamburg Jazzport Festival 2001) (2002) — live document
  • Streamer - Live (2004, Sula Records) — released on his own newly founded label Sula Records
  • Er (2005, EmArcy/Sula Records) — notable for featuring Sidsel Endresen and Elin Rosseland (vocals), Eivind Aarset (guitar), and Magne Furuholmen (keyboards, formerly of a-ha)
  • Remakes (2005, Sula/Universal) — remix/reworkings collection
  • Re-Vision (2008, EmArcy/Sula/Universal) — further studio material
  • Hamada (2009, EmArcy/Sula/Universal)

He also scored the German film Stratosphere Girl (dir. M.X. Oberg) and the French film Edy (2005, Wagram Music). In 1998 he received the Norwegian Gammleng Prize (jazz category); in 2003 the Buddyprisen from the Norwegian Jazz Forum.

Return to ECM and Broader Collaborations (2010–2013)

Quiet Inlet (ECM Records, 2010) brought a one-off ECM return — a duo recording with the English group Food (Iain Ballamy / Thomas Strønen), plus contributions from Fennesz. Baboon Moon (2011, Columbia/Sony Music) moved to a major-label home in Sony’s classical/jazz division; Molvær co-produced and engineered at Sula Studio Frysja.

1/1 (2013, EmArcy/Universal Music Group) saw Molvær collaborating with techno and minimal figures including Moritz von Oswald, Mala, and Ricardo Villalobos — a significant genre-crossing move.

Okeh Period and Nordub (2014–2018)

From 2014 onward Molvær’s home became Okeh, Sony’s revived jazz imprint:

  • Switch (2014, Okeh/Sony Music) — Molvær as producer, performing trumpet, acoustic guitar, synthesizer, and programming
  • Buoyancy (2016, Okeh)
  • Nordub (2018, Okeh) — co-primary credit with Sly & Robbie, Eivind Aarset, and Vladislav Delay; produced by Jan Bang

Nordub is the most discussed release from this period: a collision of Jamaican rhythm section legends Sly & Robbie with Oslo electronica, processed through dub production techniques. Bang produced and recorded at Punkt Studio, Vladislav Delay co-produced and mixed from Shark Reef Studio, with engineering at DVolante Studio. Sly Dunbar (drums) and Robbie Shakespeare (bass) anchor the rhythm section; Aarset contributes guitar and electronics; Molvær plays trumpet throughout.

Modern Recordings and Recent Work (2020–present)

Molvær’s most recent studio albums appear on Modern Recordings (2) (a BMG imprint):

  • SulaMadiana (2020) — produced by Molvær; liner notes conversation in English
  • Stitches (2021) — Molvær as producer; trumpet, voice, electronics, Minimoog
  • Certainty Of Tides (2023) — composed by Molvær; trumpet; features Kringkastingsorkestret (Norwegian Broadcasting Orchestra)

Forgive Us (2024, House of Mythology) — a collaboration with Ulver, Molvær as co-primary artist.

Khmer Live In Bergen (2025, Edition Records) — live performance of the Khmer album, documenting the work some 28 years after its studio release.

In 2021 Molvær received the European Film Award (together with Peter Brötzmann) for music in the historical drama Große Freiheit.
