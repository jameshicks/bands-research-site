Gary Numan

Born Gary Anthony James Webb, 8 March 1958, Hammersmith, London. British singer, songwriter, and electronic music pioneer. Discogs artist ID: 12588. Aliases: Gary Webb, Valeriun.

One of the architects of synth-pop. Broke through in 1979 with Tubeway Army’s “Are ‘Friends’ Electric?” and his debut solo album The Pleasure Principle, both UK number ones. Career has run continuously from 1977 through the 2020s, spanning punk, synth-pop, industrial, EBM, darkwave, and post-apocalyptic electronica.

Biography

Origins and Tubeway Army (1977–1979)

Webb started in punk in 1977 with a short-lived band called Mean Street, was sacked, then answered a Melody Maker ad to join The Lasers alongside Paul Gardiner. Both left to form Tubeway Army with Webb’s uncle Jess Lidyard on drums. Signed to Beggars Banquet in 1978, they released punk singles before Webb stumbled on a Minimoog left in the recording studio and immediately pivoted to synthesizers.

The band name was retained for the first two albums but Numan was always the creative core — sole songwriter, vocalist, primary instrumentalist, and self-producer. Lineup on Replicas (1979): Webb (keys/guitar/vocals), Paul Gardiner (bass), Lidyard (drums).

The single “Are ‘Friends’ Electric?” (1979) hit UK #1. The debut solo album The Pleasure Principle (1979) — recorded without guitars — also hit UK #1. Both records appeared on Beggars Banquet. At that point Webb adopted Gary Numan as his permanent name.

Synth-pop Peak (1979–1983)

The run of Beggars Banquet albums from The Pleasure Principle through Warriors established Numan as one of the dominant forces in British electronic pop. Core band across this period: Paul Gardiner (bass), Ced Sharpley (drums), Chris Payne (2) (keyboards/viola), with Rrussell Bell adding guitar from I, Assassin onward.

Notable outside contributor on I, Assassin (1982): Pino Palladino on fretless bass and guitar.

Dance (1981) marked a shift toward a more orchestrated, less guitar-free sound. I, Assassin (1982) brought Numan closer to a band-based production. Warriors (1983) and Berserker (1984, first on his own Numa label) saw the sound becoming denser, more complex. Paul Gardiner died of a heroin overdose in 1984, a significant personal and artistic loss.

Numa Records and the Mid-1980s (1984–1992)

In 1984 Numan founded Numa Records (parent company: Numa Records Ltd., Discogs label ID: 11376 / 477955) to maintain creative and commercial control. The label released his studio albums Berserker (1984), The Fury (1985), Strange Charm (1986), Metal Rhythm (1988, also on Illegal Records), Outland (1991), and Machine + Soul (1992), as well as associated singles and fan-club releases.

The Fury (1985) featured production collaboration with The Wave Team on most tracks, backing vocals from Tessa Niles and Tracy Ackerman, and saxophone from Dick Morrissey. The mid-1980s were commercially difficult despite a devoted fanbase.

Industrial and Dark Period (1994–2000)

Sacrifice (1994) marked a decisive reinvention: Numan handled all instruments, production, engineering, and mixing himself, moving into darker, slower, industrial territory influenced by Nine Inch Nails and American industrial music. He was candid about this influence. Exile (1997) on Eagle Records continued the direction, with some live band contributions.

Pure (2000, Spitfire Records) brought in band members from his live lineup — drummer Richard Beasley and Steve Monti, guitarist Rob Holliday, and producer/multi-instrumentalist Sulpher — and was critically reappraised as a strong industrial record. Rob Holliday also co-produced.

Cooking Vinyl / Mortal Records Era (2006–2013)

Jagged (2006, Cooking Vinyl / Mortal Records) returned Numan to wider critical attention, his darkest and most industrial-leaning album to that point. Produced entirely by Numan.

Splinter (Songs From A Broken Mind) (2013, Mortal Records) introduced a key long-term collaborator: Ade Fenton (Discogs ID: 1370), who produced, mixed, and co-played the album. Fenton became Numan’s production partner for the remainder of his output.

Also in 2011, Numan contributed guest vocals to “My Machines” on Battles’ Gloss Drop (Warp Records). The track also appeared in remixed form on Dross Glop (2012).

BMG and Late Career (2017–present)

Savage: Songs From A Broken World (2017, BMG) — Numan’s most commercially successful album in decades. Produced by Ade Fenton, with live guitarist Steve Harris (5) and bassist Tim Slade. Concept album about climate collapse. Daughter Persia Numan contributes vocals. The single “My Name Is Ruin” features Persia.

Intruder (2021, BMG) continued the same team, adding guest vocals from Gazelle Twin and Görkem Şen on yaybahar (a Turkish acoustic-electric hybrid string instrument). Daughter Raven Numan also appears.

Guts of Love (announced 2025) — in progress at time of research, credited to I Speak Machine collaboration.
