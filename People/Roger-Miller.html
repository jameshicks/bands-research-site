Roger Miller

Guitar, vocals. From Ann Arbor, Michigan. Discogs ID: 677599.

Co-founder of Mission Of Burma (1979–1983, 2002–2012). Developed tinnitus from sustained loud performance, causing the band’s 1983 dissolution. Also active in Birdsongs Of The Mesozoic (with Martin Swope), Alloy Orchestra, Sproton Layer, No Man, Binary System (2), Dredd Foole And The Din.

Alloy Orchestra

Three-piece live silent film accompaniment ensemble, Cambridge MA. Founded 1991 by percussionist/clarinetist Ken Winokur and keyboardist Caleb Sampson when the Coolidge Corner Theatre (Brookline, MA) invited them to create a new score for Fritz Lang’s Metropolis; percussionist/accordionist Terry Donahue was brought in before the first performance. The group had previously worked together from 1985 on theatrical scores but the 1991 Metropolis engagement marked their entry into silent film accompaniment. Wikipedia (as of 2026-07-15); WBUR

Caleb Sampson died by suicide on 8 June 1998 in Cambridge, MA. IMDB Winokur and Donahue asked Roger Miller to take Sampson’s place on keyboards; Miller joined in 1998. Wikipedia (as of 2026-07-15)

Lineup (Miller-era):

  • Ken Winokur — director, junk percussion, clarinet
  • Terry Donahue — junk percussion, accordion, musical saw, vocals
  • Roger Miller — keyboards (“orchestral keyboards”)

Instrumentation: Found percussion (“rack of junk” — horseshoes, plumbing pipes, bedpan, homemade instruments), accordion, clarinet, musical saw, sampling synthesizer, orchestral keyboards.

Scale of work: Over 1,000 performances across 13 countries; scores written for approximately 40 feature-length films or collections of shorts; 19 feature-length DVDs/Blu-rays commercially released. New scores premiered annually, typically at Telluride Film Festival, San Francisco Silent Film Festival, or New York Film Festival. Global Arts Live bio PDF

Notable quote: Roger Ebert: “Alloy Orchestra is the best in the world at accompanying silent films.” rogerclarkmiller.com

Disbanding and aftermath

Alloy Orchestra disbanded in 2021 after over 30 years. Ken Winokur departed the group; Terry Donahue and Roger Miller continued under the name Anvil Orchestra. The split was contentious: Winokur disputed Donahue and Miller’s use of jointly-composed Alloy scores for Anvil Orchestra performances; Donahue countered that “all of the Alloy Orchestra scores are joint works.” WBUR

Notes (web)

No Mission of Burma interim period in 1997–1998

A research query asked whether Miller “rejoined Mission of Burma around 1997–1998,” citing a Discogs credit suggesting 1998 (after a supposed Clint Conley departure) and rogerclarkmiller.com suggesting 1997. Web research finds no evidence of any such interim Burma period:

  • Mission of Burma was fully dissolved from 1983 until 2001. Their first post-1983 performance occurred in 2001 when Miller and Conley joined Peter Prescott’s Peer Group for an encore at a Wire support slot. The formal 2002 reunion followed. Salon.com (as of 2026-08-10)
  • Clint Conley did not depart Mission of Burma in 1997 or 1998; the band did not exist in those years. Conley was working in television (Boston’s WCVB Chronicle) and began writing songs again in the late 1990s, forming Consonant in 2001. Wikipedia — Clint Conley (as of 2026-08-10)
  • The 1997/1998 Discogs credits that may have prompted the question are likely credits for Rykodisc reissues (the label reissued Burma’s core catalog in 1997 with bonus material) or for Alloy Orchestra work — not a Burma lineup credit.
  • The one confirmed 1996 Miller/Conley collaboration is the one-off single Wrong Pipe, a project of the two musicians only (not Burma). earcandymag.com interview with Clint Conley (as of 2026-08-10)

Conclusion: There was no Mission of Burma interim reunion or lineup change in 1997 or 1998. The brief’s premise is not supported by any source. The rogerclarkmiller.com 1997 date and the Discogs 1998 date both appear to relate to the Alloy Orchestra, not Burma — see the “Alloy Orchestra join year” resolution note in Research Queue below.

Alloy Orchestra join year: 1998 confirmed

Multiple independent sources confirm Miller joined Alloy Orchestra in 1998, after Caleb Sampson’s death in June 1998:

  • WBUR: “Tragedy slammed into them in June of 1998 after Sampson died by suicide… Winokur asked Miller to join them.” WBUR (as of 2026-08-10)
  • Illinois Public Media: “Roger C. Miller, who succeeded Sampson after his death in 1998…” Illinois Public Media (as of 2026-08-10)
  • Wikipedia: “After Sampson’s death in 1998, the band was joined by keyboardist Roger Miller.” Wikipedia — Alloy Orchestra (as of 2026-08-10)

Conflict: rogerclarkmiller.com/alloy-orchestra/ states “Roger joined on keyboard in 1997.” This contradicts all other sources and is logically inconsistent — Sampson did not die until June 1998, so Miller could not have replaced him in 1997. The official site appears to contain a retroactive error. The Alloy Orchestra discography entry Silents (1997) was recorded before Miller joined; that recording features the original three-piece with Sampson.
