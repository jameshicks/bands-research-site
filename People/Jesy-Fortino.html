Oct 13, 20262 min read

Jesy Fortino

Discogs ID: 853296
Primary project: Tiny Vipers (Discogs ID: 820685)
Labels: Sub Pop, Luckyhorse Industries

Singer-songwriter active from 2004. One half of Mirrorring (with Liz Harris of Grouper); Foreign Body (Kranky, 2012).

Mirrorring — collaboration origins

Fortino and Harris first connected online: Harris recalled, “Jesy and I met over the internet. She got in touch to trade music about a day or two before a mutual friend recommended hers to me.” Harris was immediately drawn to Fortino’s Tiny Vipers work and said she “was in to it right away.” Over the following year or two the two played shows together and, in Harris’s words, “understood each other pretty quickly.” (Drowned in Sound interview, archived at drownedinsound.org)

The formal collaboration grew out of Fortino’s regular visits to Portland to see friends and family. Harris described the initiation: “We agreed it would be interesting to try to write some songs together. [She] came down for a couple days. It was pretty laid back, didn’t go in to it with any expectations beyond trying to write a couple songs together to see what would happen.” (Drowned in Sound, ibid.)

The Kranky label offer formalised the project. Fortino told KEXP in 2025 that when the opportunity to record for Kranky came up she “was like, ‘Oh, this is a big opportunity. I wanna do it.‘” Recording took place at Harris’s Oregon home on weekend trips Fortino made while simultaneously enrolled in engineering school and working two jobs. (KEXP, “Throwaway Style: Tiny Vipers, Seattle’s Quietest Legend,” 12 Sep 2025)

Wikipedia’s article on Mirrorring also states the pairing came about after the two toured together in Europe, citing Mark Richardson, “Grouper and Tiny Vipers Talk Mirrorring Collaboration,” Pitchfork, 8 Mar 2012, http://pitchfork.com/news/45681-grouper-and-tiny-vipers-talk-mirrorring-collaboration/ — the Pitchfork article itself could not be fetched. (Wikipedia: Mirrorring, as of 2026-10-13)

Note: the Wikipedia/Pitchfork “toured together in Europe” framing may refer to the same shows Harris describes as playing together over the first year or two after meeting online; these accounts are not contradictory.
