Derek Bailey

Derek Bailey

Aug 22, 202610 min read

Derek Bailey

Discogs ID: 41904 Born: January 29, 1930, Sheffield, England Died: December 25, 2005, London, England Instruments: Guitar (electric, acoustic, amplified acoustic), occasional VCS3 synthesizer Active: 1950s–2005

One of the most radical and influential guitarists of the 20th century; a founding figure of the European free improvisation movement. Bailey renounced idiomatic music in the late 1960s after a career as a working dance-band and commercial guitarist in Sheffield, and spent the rest of his life developing a highly personal approach to non-idiomatic improvisation — dense, percussive, harmonically unreferenced, atonal, and structurally free. He co-founded Incus Records with Evan Parker (one of the first artist-run labels in British music), and convened the rotating-ensemble project Company (2) for over two decades, making it one of the defining institutions of European free improv.

Biography

Early Career — Sheffield Dance Bands (1950s–mid-1960s)

Bailey spent the 1950s and early 1960s working as a professional guitarist in Sheffield’s commercial music scene: dance bands, television and radio work, sessions, club engagements. His reading and versatility gave him steady employment but no artistic satisfaction. He has said in interviews that this period forced him to become an exceptionally accomplished guitarist before he started dismantling everything he had learned.

In the mid-1960s Bailey began contact with improvising musicians who would become central to British free jazz and free improvisation. Key early meeting point: the Joseph Holbrooke Trio (Discogs group ID: 1844820) with drummer Tony Oxley and bassist Gavin Bryars — an experimental trio that moved from jazz-influenced improvisation toward increasingly open structures. This trio is documented historically rather than on Discogs releases, but it represents Bailey’s transition from commercial to experimental work.

London Free Improv Scene — Formation (1966–1970)

By the mid-to-late 1960s Bailey had settled into the London avant-garde. He was a member of:

  • Spontaneous Music Ensemble (ID: 207925) — John Stevens’s collective, one of the original British free jazz groups
  • The Music Improvisation Company (ID: 395057) — documented on ECM Records (The Music Improvisation Company, 1970, ECM)
  • Early recordings document him with Evan Parker on saxophone and Han Bennink on percussion

The first Incus release — and one of the most important documents of the British free improv scene — was The Topography Of The Lungs (1970, Incus INCUS.1), a trio session with Evan Parker and Han Bennink. Parker plays soprano and tenor saxophone; Bailey plays guitar; Bennink plays percussion. The record establishes the model that would define British free improvisation for a generation: no pre-composition, no heads, no standard forms, complete harmonic and rhythmic freedom.

Also in 1970: Bailey appeared on ECM’s first batch of releases — 4 Compositions For Sextet (CBS) and The Music Improvisation Company (ECM 1009).

Incus Records and Solo Guitar (1970–1978)

Bailey and Evan Parker co-founded Incus Records (label ID: 1264835) in the early 1970s — one of the first artist-run independent labels in British music, predating the punk DIY explosion by several years. Incus gave Bailey total control over how his music was recorded, packaged, and distributed.

The label’s early catalog is dominated by Bailey’s own recordings and duo/ensemble projects:

  • Solo Guitar (1971, Incus 2) — Bailey’s first pure solo record; tracks including pieces by Misha Mengelberg, Willem Breuker, and Gavin Bryars alongside improvisations; features VCS3 synthesizer on one track. Recorded with Hugh Davies.
  • Derek Bailey & Han Bennink (1972, Incus 9) — duo with the Dutch drummer Bennink, a relationship that continued across many labels
  • Lot 74 — Solo Improvisations (1974, Incus 12) — solo guitar; recorded by Martin Davidson and Bob Woolford; classic Incus document
  • The London Concert (1975, Incus 16) — Bailey/Parker duo, live; one of the key documents of the extended Bailey-Parker partnership
  • Duo (1976, Incus 20) — with Tristan Honsinger (cello)

Also in this period: Improvisations For Cello And Guitar with Dave Holland (ECM 1013, 1971) — an early and notable ECM release, Holland on cello, Bailey on guitar.

The mid-1970s saw Bailey document his solo guitar work across multiple labels. Guitar Solos 2 (Caroline Records, 1976) placed him in a compilation context alongside Fred Frith and Hans Reichel, affirming his position within the international free-guitar scene. He also recorded for Emanem (Martin Davidson’s label, ID: 38942): Duo (1974, Emanem) — a document of his work with an unnamed partner.

Company (2) — The Ensemble Years (1976–2001)

Bailey formed Company (2) (Discogs ID: 326370) in 1976 as a deliberate anti-group: a pool for collective improvisation with no fixed membership, no recurring repertoire, no aesthetic agenda beyond open improvisation. The annual Company Week (held through the 1980s and into the 1990s) brought together nine or ten musicians from diverse backgrounds — often meeting each other for the first time — for five days of intensive improvisation.

The Company roster over the decades reads as a who’s who of European, American, and Japanese free improvisation:

Core/frequent members: Evan Parker, Han Bennink, Fred Frith, George Lewis, Anthony Braxton, John Zorn, Misha Mengelberg, Peter Brötzmann, Dave Holland, Steve Lacy, Joëlle Léandre, Tristan Honsinger, Paul Rutherford, Maarten Altena, Jamie Muir, Keith Tippett, Julie Tippetts, Paul Lovens, Philipp Wachsmann, Rhodri Davies, Mark Wastell, Wadada Leo Smith, Vinko Globokar, Steve Beresford, Lol Coxhill, Yves Robert, Ernst Reijseger, and many others.

Incus documented Company extensively:

  • Company 1–5 (1977–1978, Incus 21–28)
  • Company 5 (1978)
  • Epiphany (1982, Incus 46/47) — double LP
  • Trios (1986, Incus 51)
  • Company 6 & 7 (1991, Incus; compilation)
  • Company 91 Volume 2 and Volume 3 (1994, Incus CD17/18) — late documentation
  • Company In Marseille (2001, Incus CD 44/45) — final Company document

NYC Connections and International Reach (1978–1995)

By the late 1970s Bailey was a regular presence in the downtown NYC free improvisation scene. Key documents:

  • Yankees (1982, Celluloid CEL 6706) — trio with George Lewis (trombone) and John Zorn (alto sax, soprano sax, clarinet, game calls); recorded by Martin Bisi; one of the definitive downtown NYC free improvisation records and a landmark cross-Atlantic collaboration. Side B features the 18-minute “On Golden Pond.”
  • Improvised Music New York 1981 (1992, MuWorks MU W 1007) — sextet recording from 1981 with Fred Frith, Sonny Sharrock, John Zorn, Bill Laswell, and Charles K. Noyes; a dense document of the downtown avant-garde scene at its peak.

Bailey also worked extensively with Japanese improvisers from the late 1970s onward. He recorded in Japan, appeared with Japanese musicians at various festivals, and Incus released Duo & Trio Improvisation (1978, Kitty Records) — a Japanese-market release reflecting his presence in that scene.

In 1981: Dart Drug (Incus 41) with percussionist/visual artist Jamie Muir — Bailey self-produced; Muir contributed the artwork.

The PISA 1980 Improvisors’ Symposium (Incus 37, 1981) documented the large international gathering of free improvisers in Pisa, Italy, with Bailey alongside Parker, Lewis, Barry Guy, George Lewis, Giancarlo Schiaffini, Maarten Altena, Paul Lovens, Paul Lytton, Paul Rutherford, and Philipp Wachsmann.

European sessions continued across the decade: Pleistozaen Mit Wasser (1989, FMP) with Cecil Taylor, on Jost Gebers’s Berlin label; appearances on the FMP catalog placed Bailey within the German free jazz lineage running from Peter Brötzmann and Alexander von Schlippenbach.

Tzadik Period and Late Work (1995–2005)

John Zorn’s Tzadik label gave Bailey a late-career platform in the US:

  • Saisoro (1995, Tzadik TZ 7205) — with Japanese group Ruins (Tatsuya Yoshida on drums and voice, Ryuichi Masuda on bass); a collision between Bailey’s guitar and Ruins’ ferocious free rock; produced by Zorn; recorded by Robert Musso.
  • Mirakle (2000, Tzadik TZ 7603) — trio with Jamaaladeen Tacuma (bass) and Calvin Weston (drums); executive producer Zorn; another unexpected pairing, bringing Bailey into contact with funk-affiliated rhythm players.

The 2003 David Sylvian album Blemish (Samadhisound SS001) featured Bailey on guitar (tracks 2, 5, 7 — co-composed by Bailey and Sylvian). This brought his work to a wider audience. The collaboration yielded a follow-on release: To Play (The Blemish Sessions) (2006, Samadhisound) — released posthumously, Bailey having died on December 25, 2005.

Final years also produced the Incus Solo Guitar Series (2002): In Church (SG3), South (SG4), Different Guitars (SG5); New Sights, Old Sounds (Solo Live) (2002, Incus CD 48/49); and posthumous archival releases continuing through the 2020s on labels including Confront, NoBusiness Records, Recital, and Honest Jon’s.
