Vince Ely

Vince Ely

Oct 13, 20268 min read

Vince Ely

Discogs ID: 293509
Real name / alias: Vincent Paul Davey
Origin: UK
Instrument: Drums, Percussion; also Producer, Keyboards (session)

Drummer and co-founder of The Psychedelic Furs, present on the band’s first three studio albums (1980–1982). Left after Forever Now, spent 1983–85 as a freelance producer and session drummer, then returned to the Furs fold for the 1989 reunion album Book Of Days. Outside the Furs he produced early Ministry releases and session-drummed for Robyn Hitchcock, among others.

Biography

Formation and early years with the Furs (1977–1980)

Ely was a founding member of The Psychedelic Furs, which coalesced in London from 1977 onward around singer Richard Butler and guitarist Roger Morris. The earliest confirmed Discogs release is the 1979 single “We Love You” (Epic), on which Ely is credited for drums alongside Tim Butler (bass), John Ashton (guitar), Roger Morris (guitar), and Duncan Kilburn (saxophone). Production on that single was shared between Howard Thompson, Ian Taylor, and the band itself.

The self-titled debut album The Psychedelic Furs (CBS, 1980) established the lineup and sonic identity — a dense, abrasive post-punk sound with Kilburn’s saxophone cutting through layers of guitar noise. Ely’s credit is drums. Production was split: Steve Lillywhite handled most tracks; Howard Thompson and Ian Taylor co-produced others. Phil Thornalley engineered.

Talk Talk Talk and the Lillywhite peak (1981)

Talk Talk Talk (Columbia, 1981) repeated the Lillywhite collaboration and sharpened the band’s commercial profile. Ely is credited drums and percussion. Duncan Kilburn appears on horns and keyboards; Roger Morris on guitar. This album contains “Pretty in Pink,” later a chart entry for the Pretty in Pink soundtrack (CBS, 1986), on which Ely holds a songwriting credit.

Forever Now and departure (1982)

Forever Now (Columbia, 1982) brought a dramatic change of producer: Todd Rundgren, recording at his Utopia studio. The album expanded into orchestral and psychedelic territory, with backing vocalists Howard Kaylan and Mark Volman (Flo & Eddie), cellist Ann Sheldon, and horn players Gary Windo and Donn Adams. Ely is credited drums and percussion. The “Love My Way” single (1982), drawn from the album, lists Ely as co-writer alongside Richard Butler, Tim Butler, and John Ashton — notable because most Furs writing credits default to the whole band collectively. This is the last Furs studio record with Ely as drummer.

Ely departed the Furs after the Forever Now touring cycle. The circumstances are not documented in the Discogs record. For Mirror Moves (1984), the drum chair went to Keith Forsey (who also produced several tracks) and subsequently Paul Garisto.

Freelance production and session work (1983–1985)

After leaving the Furs, Ely moved into production, often in partnership with Ian Taylor (who had engineered and co-produced the early Furs records). The most significant outside credit is his production work for Ministry during the Chicago synth-pop era, before Al Jourgensen pivoted toward industrial rock:

  • Ministry – “Work For Love” (Arista, 1983): produced by Vince Ely and Ian Taylor. Al Jourgensen and Stephen George are listed as performers.
  • Ministry – “I Wanted To Tell Her” (Arista, 1983): Ely produced the A-side; Ian Taylor produced the full single together.
  • Ministry – “Revenge (You Did It Again)” (Arista, 1983): produced by Ely and Ian Taylor.
  • Ministry – “Here We Go” (Arista, 1983, Philippines): also in this production run.

These four Ministry singles represent Ely’s most substantial production credit outside the Furs — a pivotal moment in Ministry’s career before Jourgensen repudiated the synth-pop sound.

Also in this period:

  • Robyn Hitchcock – Black Snake Diamond Röle (Armageddon Records, 1981): Ely drums on tracks A2, A3, A4, and B2. The record split drum duties between Ely and Morris Windsor; Kimberley Rew and Matthew Seligman also appear.
  • Alan Vega – Saturn Strip (ZE Records / Elektra, 1983): Ely credited as “Producer [Basic Tracks]” on track A1, alongside Ian Taylor in the same role. The album’s main producer was Ric Ocasek (The Cars); Al Jourgensen also appears as keyboards/writer on that track.
  • Shrapnel (4) – Shrapnel (Elektra, 1984): Ely produced and played keyboards and additional percussion on tracks A2 and B2. Dave Wyndorf (later of Monster Magnet) is on vocals.
  • Antonia & The Operators – “Versailles” (Mankin Records, 1984): Ely drums (French project led by Antonia De Portago).
  • Jane Wiedlin – Jane Wiedlin (I.R.S. Records, 1985): Ely produced tracks A2, A4, A6, B1, B2 on the debut solo album from the Go-Go’s guitarist. The remaining tracks were produced by Bill Payne, George Massenburg, and Russ Kunkel.
  • Dance Exponents – Expectations (Mushroom, 1985): Ely drums on this New Zealand new wave album, produced by Ian Taylor. Jordan Luck on voice.
  • Limited Warranty – This Is Serious (Ultra Beat Records, 1985): Ely produced and is credited Mastered By and Mixed By on a 12” single for this US act.

Return to the Furs: Book Of Days (1989)

The Psychedelic Furs regrouped in 1988–89 with a reformed lineup including Ely, Richard Butler, Tim Butler, and John Ashton — but without Roger Morris or Duncan Kilburn. The compilation All Of This And Nothing (Columbia, 1988) prefaced the reunion and lists Ely as drummer, alongside Ashton and Tim Butler; notably it includes tracks produced by Keith Forsey and Steve Lillywhite from the Mirror Moves era, confirming Ely’s absence from those recordings.

Book Of Days (Columbia, 1989), produced by David M. Allen, is Ely’s full return as a recording member. The Discogs credit lists the core Furs as “The Psychedelic Furs Are: John Ashton, Tim Butler, Vince Ely, Richard Butler.” Additional musicians included Knox Chandler, Joe McGinty, Jem Finer, and Anthony Thistlethwaite, among others. David M. Allen’s production style — clean, atmospheric, mid-period post-punk — suited the reunion sound.

After the Furs

Ely’s post-1989 Discogs footprint is sparse. He appears as producer on two tracks of Limited Warranty’s self-titled album (Wounded Bird Records, 2007) — a different release from the 1985 single, apparently the same US act. He is credited drums on the 2025 live broadcast release Paramount Theatre 1984 (Live Radio Broadcast) (The Vinyl Champ), documenting the Furs in concert during the Mirror Moves touring period — a year after he had already left the band, suggesting this may involve archival material from the Forever Now era tour or a brief transitional period; the exact date requires clarification.

Compilations and retrospectives list Ely’s playing across the first three albums: Should God Forget: A Retrospective (Columbia/Legacy, 1997), I Wanna Go Backwards (Yep Roc, 2007), and Playlist: The Very Best Of The Psychedelic Furs (Columbia/Legacy, 2011).

Connections

  • The Psychedelic Furs — founding drummer, 1977–c.1982; returned for Book Of Days (1989)
  • Richard Butler — Furs vocalist and primary lyricist throughout Ely’s tenure
  • Tim Butler — Furs bassist; co-credited with Ely as performer on Book Of Days
  • John Ashton — Furs guitarist; co-writer of “Love My Way” with Ely
  • Roger Morris — Furs co-founding guitarist (departed before Forever Now); present on both early studio records with Ely
  • Duncan Kilburn — Furs saxophonist on debut and Talk Talk Talk; absent from Book Of Days
  • Steve Lillywhite — produced debut and Talk Talk Talk; Ely’s primary producer on the Furs’ breakthrough years
  • Todd Rundgren — produced Forever Now (Ely’s final original-era Furs album)
  • David M. Allen — produced Book Of Days
  • Keith Forsey — successor drummer/producer on Mirror Moves; Ely’s replacement
  • Paul Garisto — also drummed for the Furs mid-1980s
  • Phil Thornalley — engineered the debut and Talk Talk Talk
  • Robyn Hitchcock — Ely session-drummed on Black Snake Diamond Röle (1981)
  • Morris Windsor — shared drum duties with Ely on the Hitchcock album
  • Knox Chandler — additional musician on Book Of Days
  • Ian Taylor — frequent production partner 1983–85 (Ministry, Alan Vega, Dance Exponents)

Open Questions

  • Precise circumstances and date of Ely’s departure from the Furs after Forever Now — Discogs has no documentation.
  • The 2025 “Paramount Theatre 1984” live release credits Ely on drums despite him having left by 1984 — the actual recording date and whether this captures a 1982/83 performance requires clarification (possibly mislabeled year on the release).
  • Whether the Vincent Paul Davey alias was a stage-name variant or a separate professional identity used for any specific work.
