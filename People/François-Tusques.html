François Tusques

François Tusques

Jul 26, 20268 min read

François Tusques

Discogs ID: 365565
Born: 27 January 1938, Paris, France
Instrument: Piano, also Fender Rhodes, balafon, guitar (bowed), saw, maracas, celesta, organ
Role: Pianist, composer, bandleader, label co-founder

French jazz pianist and composer. One of the leading figures of the first wave of European free jazz in the mid-1960s, and a sustained presence in politically engaged French music through the 1970s–80s. Co-founder of the Le Temps Des Cerises record label.

Biography

Early Career and the French Free Jazz Explosion (1964–1967)

Tusques arrived in the French jazz scene in the early 1960s. His first Discogs-documented recording is the 1964 7-inch La Maison Fille Du Soleil (Studio Scriptone Nantes), recorded as a trio with American cornetist Don Cherry and bassist Beb Guerin — a signal document of American free jazz’s early contact with the Paris scene.

His debut LP Free Jazz (1965, Disques Mouloudji) is considered a founding document of European free jazz: a tightly wound quintet date featuring Michel Portal on bass clarinet, Bernard Vitet on trumpet, François Jeanneau on saxophone and flute, Beb Guerin on contrabass, and Charles Saudrais on percussion. The cover art direction was handled by vocalist Colette Magny. The album’s track titles (“Description Automatique D’Un Paysage Désolé,” “La Tour Saint-Jacques”) signal a Surrealist influence alongside the Coltrane/Ornette-derived harmonic freedom.

Le Nouveau Jazz (1967, Disques Mouloudji) extended this work in a co-credited recording with tenor saxophonist Barney Wilen, with Beb Guerin and J.-F. Jenny-Clark sharing bass duties and Aldo Romano on drums.

Futura Records and Political Radicalism (1968–1973)

By 1968 Tusques was appearing on politically charged recordings across the French left-wing cultural scene. His piano and organ appear on Bernard Vitet’s film score Auto Jazz (Tragic Destiny Of Lorenzo Bandini) (1968, MPS Records/Germany) and as sideman on Don Cherry’s Big Chief (1969, Pathé) and other BYG Records dates (Love Rejoice, 1969).

Piano Dazibao (1970, Futura Records (2), cat. GER 14) is his most nakedly political solo statement: a solo piano LP with track titles referencing Maoist slogans, solidarity with imprisoned Breton journalist Michel Le Bris, and direct invocations of the Commune. The follow-up Dazibao N°2 (1971, Futura Records (2)) continued the series.

Intercommunal Music (1971, Shandar, SR 10 010) — a key release — expanded the political collective concept to a full ensemble: Alan Silva on cello, Sunny Murray on drums, Steve Potts on alto saxophone, Alan Shorter on trumpet, Beb Guerin and Bob Reid on contrabasses, Louis Armfield on percussion, with Tusques playing piano, bowed guitar, musical saw, and maracas. The track titles (“Les Forces Progressistes,” “La Bourgeoisie Périra Noyée Dans Les Eaux Glacées Du Calcul Égoïste,” “Portrait D’Erika Huggins”) place the music explicitly within Third Worldist and Black Panther solidarity politics.

He also appeared on The Panther And The Lash (1971, America Records), a radical political music project, playing piano, celesta, balafon, and maracas.

Intercommunal Free Dance Music Orchestra (1974–1983)

Tusques was a founding member and primary composer of the Intercommunal Free Dance Music Orchestra, a long-running left-wing collective whose membership included Beb Guerin, Michel Marre, Jo Maka, Adolf Winkler (alias Ramadolf), and Tanguy Le Doré.

The ensemble’s key recordings on Disques Vendémiaire include:

  • L’inter Communal (1978) — collective compositions blending free jazz with Breton trad music and Catalan song, featuring bombarde players and percussionists alongside the core jazz ensemble.
  • Après La Marée Noire - Vers Une Musique Bretonne Nouvelle (1979, Le Chant Du Monde, LDX 74703) — Tusques’s most explicit exploration of Breton music synthesis, with bombarde players Gabriel Kerdoncuff, Jean-Louis Le Vallegant, and Philippe Le Strat, and percussionist Kilikus, vocalist Carlos Andreu. Styles span Celtic, Modal, Big Band, Free Jazz.
  • Le Musichien (1983, Edizione Corsica) — the orchestra’s later studio document.

He also recorded solo/as leader on Le Chant Du Monde during this period: Répression (1972) was a politically charged studio work.

Label Work and Continuing Activity (1974–1994)

Tusques co-founded Le Temps Des Cerises records; Vol. 1 - Concert A Prades Le Lez (1974) was among its early releases. The label published a range of politically engaged French music and world music.

Octaèdre, Un Hommage À Cortazar (1994, AxolOtl Jazz, AXO 101) was a late-career studio album in tribute to Argentine writer Julio Cortázar — a 18-track CD with Bernard Vitet on trumpet/flugelhorn, Isabel Juanpera on vocals, and César Stroscio on bandoneon. The tracklist interweaves Cortázar-derived titles with jazz standards (“Round About Midnight,” “Embraceable You”) in a way that echoes Cortázar’s own love of jazz.

Film Scores and Late Reissues (1967–2024)

Tusques had scored the 1967 Jean Rollin vampire film La Reine Des Vampires (also known as Le Viol du Vampire), and this score remained unreleased for decades. It was finally issued in 2014 by Cacophonic (a Finders Keepers Records sublabel) as La Reine Des Vampires 1967 — alternate takes and unused cues featuring Barney Wilen on tenor sax, Beb Guerin and J.-F. Jenny-Clark on bass, and Eddy Gaumont on violin. A follow-up Cacophonic release, Alors Nosferatu Combina Un Plan Ingénieux (2019), appeared alongside La Chasse Au Snark (2020).

Finders Keepers Records issued live archive material as lathe-cut 7-inches: Dunois 1981 (2023) and Bateke (2024, a trio document with Bernard Vitet and Beb Guerin).

His late duo LP with Sunny Murray, Intercommunal Dialogue 1&2 (2019, Ni-Vu-Ni-Connu, Luxembourg), documents a reunion of two artists connected since the early 1970s Paris free jazz scene.

Connections

  • Byard Lancaster — Tusques appeared on 6 of Lancaster’s French-period recordings, including Funny Funky Rib Crib (1979), playing Fender Rhodes
  • Don Cherry — appeared together on Tusques’s 1964 debut 7-inch, one of the earliest French free jazz documents
  • Sunny Murray — appeared on Intercommunal Music (1971) and reunited on the 2019 duo LP Intercommunal Dialogue 1&2; both part of the US expatriate free jazz community in Paris
  • Alan Silva — fellow Paris-based American free jazz exile; Intercommunal Music (1971)
  • Bernard Vitet — the most consistent French collaborator, from the 1965 debut through to 2024
  • Beb Guerin — most-frequent collaborator across 60 years; member of the Intercommunal Free Dance Music Orchestra
  • Michel Portal — on the 1965 Free Jazz debut; both became central to French jazz’s engagement with improvisation
  • Barney Wilen — unusual co-lead partnership spanning Le Nouveau Jazz (1967) through the 1967 Jean Rollin film score
  • Colette Magny — left-wing chanson-jazz bridge figure; art direction on Free Jazz
  • Jean Rollin — Belgian/French horror film director; Tusques scored his 1967 debut La Reine Des Vampires
  • Futura Records (2) — key label for French free jazz alongside BYG/Actuel; Tusques among its most significant artist-composers
  • Finders Keepers Records — Andy Votel’s reissue label; championed Tusques’s archive in the 2010s–2020s
