Bill Laswell

Full name: William Otis Laswell Born: February 12, 1955, Salem, Illinois (raised Albion, Michigan) Role: Bassist, producer, label owner Discogs ID: 661 Active: 1978 – present

Biography

Background and Arrival in New York (1955–1978)

Born in Salem, Illinois and raised in Albion, Michigan. Laswell arrived in New York City in the late 1970s, embedding himself in the downtown avant-garde scene at the intersection of free jazz, punk, and funk. He became a founding member of Material around 1978–1979, the collective that would define his early career and serve as the vehicle for his first recordings.

Material and the ZE / Celluloid Era (1979–1983)

The Material collective — Laswell, Michael Beinhorn, and Fred Maher — released their debut “Temporary Music 1” on ZE Records in 1979, produced by Giorgio Gomelsky (the Georgian-born impresario who had managed the early Rolling Stones and Yardbirds before relocating to New York in 1978).

In 1980, Laswell co-established BC Studio in Brooklyn with Martin Bisi, initially operating under the name OAO Studios. The room became central to the downtown scene — it would go on to record Sonic Youth, Swans, John Zorn, and many others.

Memory Serves (Celluloid, 1981) marked a leap in ambition: Laswell assembled Henry Threadgill, George Lewis, Sonny Sharrock, Olu Dara, Fred Frith, Charles K. Noyes, and Billy Bang for an album that spanned free jazz, funk, and noise. One Down (Celluloid, 1982) expanded further — adding Nile Rodgers, Archie Shepp, Oliver Lake, Nona Hendryx, and a then-unknown Whitney Houston among its guest cast. Executive producer on One Down was Jean Karakos of Celluloid Records.

The Commercial Breakthrough and Major Label Work (1983–1986)

In 1983, Laswell co-produced Herbie Hancock’s Future Shock (Columbia), the album containing “Rockit” — the hip-hop/electro crossover hit that reached number one on the UK dance charts and won a Grammy. The same year, his own debut solo album Baselines appeared on Elektra Musician. Production credits proliferated rapidly:

  • Mick Jagger — She’s The Boss (1985, Columbia); Just Another Night (1985, Columbia); co-productions
  • Afrika Bambaataa / Time Zone / John Lydon — “World Destruction” (1984, Celluloid) — one of the era’s defining hip-hop/post-punk fusions
  • Laurie Anderson — Mister Heartbreak (1984, Warner Bros.)
  • Motorhead — Orgasmatron (1986)
  • Sly & Robbie — Language Barrier (1985, Island) and Rhythm Killers (1987, Island/4th & Broadway)
  • Yellowman — Blueberry Hill (1985, CBS/Columbia)
  • Nico — Camera Obscura (1985, Beggars Banquet) — Laswell produced the album; related to his “Army Arrangement” work
  • Last Poets — 1987 (Celluloid)
  • Iggy Pop — Instinct (1988, A&M)
  • Ramones — Brain Drain (1989, Sire)
  • Swans — The Burning World (1989, UNI Records)
  • PIL (Public Image Ltd.) — Album (1985, Elektra)

This period established Laswell as one of the most commercially adventurous producers working across genre lines — equally at home with avant-garde downtown acts and major-label rock.

Last Exit (1986–1994)

Last Exit — Laswell on bass alongside Sonny Sharrock (guitar), Peter Brötzmann (sax), and Ronald Shannon Jackson (drums) — was formed in 1986 and stands as one of the most uncompromising free-music ensembles of the era. Laswell also produced the group’s Iron Path (1988, Virgin). The group recorded prolifically through the early 1990s. Discogs ID: 265435.

Axiom Records and Island (1989–1997)

Laswell founded Axiom as an imprint of Island Records (Discogs label ID: 1072, parent: Island). The label ran roughly 1989–1997 and functioned as a platform for Laswell’s world-music and avant-jazz production work, releasing around 230 titles. Key Axiom releases include:

Orange Music and the 1990s–2000s Outreach

In 1997, Laswell opened Orange Music studio in West Orange, New Jersey, which became his primary production base. Production work continued to span an implausible range:

  • Miles Davis — Panthalassa: The Music Of Miles Davis 1969–1974 (1997, Columbia) — Laswell reconstructed and remixed Davis’s electric period sessions
  • Tabla Beat Science — collaborative project with Indian classical percussionists (Axiom/Palm Pictures)
  • William S. Burroughs — Seven Souls (1989, Virgin) — Laswell set Burroughs text to electronic soundscapes
  • Gigi (Ejigayehu Shibabaw) — self-titled 2001 Palm Pictures debut produced by Laswell; she became his wife
  • Herbie Hancock — Dis Is Da Drum (1994) and other later productions
  • Jah Wobble — various collaborative works including Radioaxiom: A Dub Transmission (2001, Axiom/Palm Pictures)
  • *Hashisheen: The End Of Law (1999, Sub Rosa) — spoken word/music collage featuring Patti Smith, Iggy Pop, Genesis P-Orridge, William S. Burroughs

M.O.D. Technologies and Late Career (2010s–present)

Laswell founded M.O.D. Technologies (Discogs label ID: 214986) as a self-operated imprint for his later work. The label has released over 60 titles from 2011 onward including work by Praxis, Ginger Baker, and numerous Laswell solo/collaborative recordings. A sub-label M.O.D. Reloaded handles some later releases.

Activity has continued at high volume: collaborations with Jah Wobble (Realm Of Spells, 2019, Jah Wobble Records), Sub Rosa (Brussels-based avant label releasing Laswell work since the mid-1990s including 2020’s The Acid Lands and 2026’s Mount Analogue), and a dense stream of self-released digital recordings via Bandcamp.
