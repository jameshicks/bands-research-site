Mel Cheren

Real name: Melvin Cheren Discogs ID: 458298 Born: January 21, 1933, Everett, Massachusetts; raised in Revere, MA Died: December 7, 2007 (AIDS complications)

Music industry executive, label founder, painter, and philanthropist. Co-founded West End Records in December 1976 with Ed Kushins, building one of the defining labels of the New York disco era and the principal document of the Larry Levan / Paradise Garage sound. Also the financial backer of the Paradise Garage itself. Died of AIDS complications after decades of AIDS activism.

Early Career — ABC-Paramount and Scepter (1959–1976)

Cheren entered the music industry in 1959 at ABC-Paramount Records (ABC Records), eventually rising to head of the label’s production department. When ABC moved operations to Los Angeles in 1970, he declined to follow and instead became head of production at Scepter Records.

At Scepter, Cheren made two contributions that would prove foundational to the DJ economy about to emerge:

  1. 12” B-side instrumental mixes — he was among the first to release extended instrumental versions of tracks on 12” B-sides, giving DJs tools they didn’t yet know they needed.
  2. Early record pools — helped form one of the first record pools alongside David Mancuso, Vince Aletti, and Steve D’Aquisto. Record pools were the distribution networks through which labels got promos to club DJs; Cheren’s involvement from the supply side gave him a deep understanding of how the new dance economy actually worked.

During this period Cheren was also an active visual artist. His paintings appeared on several major jazz LP covers — five received Grammy nominations:

West End Records (1976–2007)

Founding and First Releases

West End launched in December 1976 at 254 West 54th Street, New York. The first releases included soundtrack material (Sessomatto, Black Emanuelle) and club-oriented singles by early roster acts. The label’s catalog number sequence — WES 12xxx for 12” singles — began immediately.

Cheren produced the debut West End release himself: Sesso Matto’s Sessomatto (WES 12100, 1976). Philly U.S.A.’s “Speak Well” (WES 12106, 1977) he mixed personally.

Karen Young and Commercial Breakthrough

West End’s first major commercial success was Karen Young’s “Hot Shot” (WES 12111, 1978), produced by Andy Kahn and Kurt Borusiewicz. The single sold 800,000 copies, making it one of the biggest-selling 12” singles in history at that point.

Paradise Garage and Larry Levan

Cheren was the financial backer of the Paradise Garage — the most influential club in NYC from roughly 1977 to 1987. The club was owned and operated by his former partner Michael Brody; Larry Levan was the resident DJ. Cheren’s financial and label relationship with Levan gave West End a direct line to what the Garage crowd wanted.

Philanthropy and AIDS Activism

In 1982 Cheren became actively involved with the Gay Men’s Health Crisis (GMHC) — one of the earliest institutional AIDS relief organizations in the US, founded the same year. In 1987 he founded 24 Hours For Life, a nonprofit of media and music professionals producing fundraising events for AIDS relief and education.

West End released the Dance For Life compilation (DFL9000-2, 2002) and the Original Soundtrack From The Documentary ‘Gay Sex In The 70s’ (2006), both connecting the label’s catalog to advocacy contexts.

Later Years and Legacy

Cheren revived Garage Records in 1995. The reactivated West End released Larry Levan’s archival material:

  • Larry Levan’s Classic West End Records Remixes Made Famous At The Legendary Paradise Garage (WES 2001-1, 1999) — Cheren and Kenton Nix executive produced and produced
  • Larry Levan Live At The Paradise Garage (2000, co-released with Strut and Garage Records) — Cheren as album coordinator

In summer 2000 Cheren published his autobiography: Keep On Dancin’: My Life and The Paradise Garage (West End / Keep On Dancin’). The prologue reads: “This is a story of my gay generation, the world we built, and the world we lost.”

In 2007 the documentary The Godfather of Disco (adapted from the memoir) premiered at the 25th Minneapolis/St. Paul International Film Festival, winning the Emerging Filmmaker Award. Cheren died that same year, December 7, 2007, before the film’s wider release.

He also ran the Colonial House Inn, a B&B whose walls were hung with his paintings, lit by black lights to bring out the fluorescent paint. All profits from his paintings went to charity.
