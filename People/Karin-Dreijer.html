Karin Dreijer

Born: 7 April 1975, Nacka, Sweden
Real name: Karin Elisabeth Dreijer
Aliases / name variations: Fever Ray; Karin Dreijer Andersson (earlier releases); K. Dreijer; Dreijer Andersson; Karin Knife

Swedish vocalist, composer, and multi-instrumentalist. Co-founder and vocalist of The Knife with sibling Olof Dreijer (1999–2014). Previously a member of Honey Is Cool (1994–1999). Records and performs solo as Fever Ray. One of the most distinctive voices in European electronic music, with a catalog that runs from indie pop (Honey Is Cool) through The Knife’s decade-long arc from synth-pop to industrial opera, and into increasingly experimental solo work.

Karin has cited Miami Vice and artists including Björk and Kate Bush as major influences.

Biography

Honey Is Cool (1994–1999)

Karin was a founding member of Honey Is Cool, a Swedish indie pop band from Gothenburg, formed in 1994. She played guitar and sang lead vocals throughout the band’s run. Honey Is Cool released four full-lengths and several singles across Sunspot Records (2), MVG Records, and Rabid Records, including the debut self-titled LP (1994), Focky Focky No Pay (1995), Crazy Love (1997), and Early Morning Are You Working? (1999, Rabid Records — their final release). Karin is credited with guitar, vocals, music, lyrics, and design across those records. The band also produced a handful of EPs and singles including Nach Heart (1997), There’s No Difference (1999), and Bolero! EP (1999).

Other members at break-up: John Jern (guitar), Staffan Larsson (bass), Fredrik Wennerlund (drums). Earlier members included Håkan Hellström (drums, 1995–1997), Anders Göthberg (guitar, 1994), and several keyboard players.

The Knife (1999–2014)

Karin co-founded The Knife with Olof Dreijer in 1999. On all Knife recordings, she is credited with vocals and a wide range of instruments: accordion, alto saxophone, electric guitar, acoustic guitar, bass guitar, organ, synthesizer, sequencing, sampler, and drum programming. The duo self-produced throughout and operated through their own Rabid Records imprint.

Key albums: The Knife (2001), Deep Cuts (2003, contains “Heartbeats” and “Pass This On”), Silent Shout (2006, their critical breakthrough), the opera collaboration Tomorrow, In A Year (2010, co-created with Mount Sims and PlanningToRock), and the double album Shaking The Habitual (2013, Brille Records). The duo disbanded in November 2014 after the Shaking The Habitual tour.

Guest Credits / Collaborations (2005–2025)

Karin sang lead vocals on Röyksopp’s “What Else Is There?” (2005, The Understanding, Wall Of Sound), one of that duo’s signature tracks. She also contributed vocals to Röyksopp’s “This Must Be It” (2009) and photography to Junior (2009, EMI/Wall Of Sound), credited as “Karin Dreijer-Andersson.”

She co-wrote and produced “Who’s That Girl” for Robyn (Konichiwa Records, 2008). In 2012 she sang on “Tricky Tricky (Cristo Tricky Before Bedtime Mix)” (Full Circle Werks). In 2006 she mixed, produced, recorded, and wrote “Christmas Reindeer” (Rabid Records) with credited production. More recently, she contributed lead vocals to “What Else Is There? (True Electric)” (2025, Dog Triumph / Embassy One), and lead vocals and production to “I’m Not Done (Therapy Session)” (2025, Mute). She is also credited as composer/lyricist on “Who Said” by Nia Archives (Chess Club / RCA Victor, 2024).

Fever Ray — Solo Career

Fever Ray (2009)

The debut Fever Ray album was released on Rabid Records in 2009 (RABID 039). Karin wrote all music and lyrics. Key producers: Christoffer Berg (tracks 1, 3, 4, 5, 9), Van Rivers & The Subliminal Kid (tracks 2, 6, 7, 8, 10), and Fever Ray throughout. Cecilia Nordlund contributed co-writing, lyrics, and vocals on “Keep The Streets Empty For Me” (track 9). Artwork by Martin Ander. Peder Mannerfelt contributed guitar, keyboards, vocals, mixing, and mastering on at least one pressing. Tracklist: “If I Had A Heart,” “When I Grow Up,” “Dry & Dusty,” “Seven,” “Triangle Walks,” “Concrete Walls,” “Now’s The Only Time I Know,” “I’m Not Done,” “Keep The Streets Empty For Me,” “Coconut.”

Singles from the album included If I Had A Heart (2008, the debut single), When I Grow Up (2009), Triangle Walks (remixes, 2009), and Seven (2009). A remixes compilation (Remixes, 2009, Rabid/V2/Cooperative Music) followed, along with a live album Stranger Than Kindness / Here Before (2009).

Plunge (2017)

The second Fever Ray album was released on Rabid Records in 2017 (RABID061). All songs written by Karin Dreijer. Co-producers vary by track:

Tami T is also credited as co-writer on “A Part Of Us.” Singles included Wanna Sip, IDK About You, Mustn’t Hurry (remixes, 2018), and Plunge (DJ Marfox Remix) (2018). A full remix album, Plunge Remix, followed in 2019; This Country Makes It Hard To Fuck (Björk Remix) (2019) was also issued as a standalone. Live At Troxy (2019, Live Here Now / Rabid Records) documents the Plunge-era live show; credits include guitar and vocals.

Radical Romantics (2022)

Third Fever Ray album, released on Mute Records (MTE-1110-1, also Rabid Records). Mixed by Johannes Berglund; mastered by Sören von Malmborg. Creative direction and design by Martin Falck. Management: DEF Ltd. Tracklist: “What They Call Us,” “Shiver,” “New Utensils,” “Kandy,” “Even It Out,” “Looking For A Ghost,” “Carbon Dioxide,” “North,” “Tapping Fingers,” “Bottom Of The Ocean.” Singles: What They Call Us, Carbon Dioxide (remixes, 2023), Kandy (Sylvere Remixes) (2023), Shiver (Ivory Remix) (2023), Mama’s Hand (Radio Slave Remix) (2022). A further release The Year Of The Radical Romantics appeared in 2025 (Mute).
