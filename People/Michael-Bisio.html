Biography

Early Career and Pacific Northwest (1983–2009)

Michael Bisio began his documented recording career in 1983, appearing as a contrabassist on a Cadence Jazz Records session (The Past And Tomorrows) alongside label head Bob Rusch. His debut as a leader came in 1987 with Michael Bisio Quartet In Seattle (Silkheart, Sweden), a quartet date featuring trumpeter Ron Soderstrom, alto saxophonist Rick Mandyck, and drummer Teo Sutton. Bisio produced the record himself — a self-directing instinct that would run throughout his career.

Through the 1990s and 2000s he maintained an extensive sideman and co-leader presence across several independent jazz and improvised music labels: CIMP (Creative Improvised Music Projects), Cadence Jazz Records, OmniTone, and smaller imprints. His credits from this period are wide-ranging — bassist on dates with composers including Joe Giardullo, Tomas Ulrich, Dominic Duval, Rob Blakeslee, and Connie Crothers, among many others. He also produced and led sessions under his own name and as part of ensembles including the Michael Bisio Quartet, Bisio Trio, Bisio Quintet, and the Post Modern Jazz Quartet.

Notable early releases include MBEK™ (2000, Meniscus) — a quartet with Eyvind Kang — and Undulations (2000, OmniTone), both documenting his settled New York free improvisation practice. His connection to the Pacific Northwest remained strong: Three (2010, Clean Feed) with Rich Halley and Newman Baker was recorded there.

Post Modern Jazz Quartet and Thirsty Ear Connections (2009–2011)

By 2009 Bisio was appearing in Thirsty Ear contexts through the Post Modern Jazz Quartet, a group he co-led. He appears on the DJ Spooky album The Secret Song (2009, Thirsty Ear) in a track-level guest capacity (track CD-13), alongside Matthew Shipp on piano and Khan Jamal on vibraphone — this was likely the earliest shared recording session between Bisio and Shipp. Also in 2009 Bisio was a credited bassist on Blink Of An Eye (Thirsty Ear), a release by Scanner with the Post Modern Jazz Quartet, again featuring Shipp.

Joining the Matthew Shipp Trio (2010–2017)

Bisio became the primary bassist of the Matthew Shipp Trio around 2010, stepping into a role previously occupied by William Parker. The first formal trio release with the new lineup was Art Of The Improviser (2011, Thirsty Ear, cat. THI 57197) — a 2xCD combining a live trio set (with Whit Dickey on drums) with a solo piano disc. Bisio plays on the entire live disc (tracks 1-1 through 1-5).

His 2022 Gravity Without Airs (Tao Forms) marks a return to the Shipp Trio format on a newer label, alongside Whit Dickey. World Construct (ESP-Disk’, 2022) is likewise a Matthew Shipp Trio release with Bisio.

Bisio’s collaboration with cornetist Kirk Knuffke produced several records across the mid-2010s and into the 2020s, including as part of the Kirk Knuffke Trio (Discogs ID: 11608535). He has also worked extensively with saxophonist Joe McPhee across multiple groups and contexts (8 shared releases documented), including Joe McPhee’s Bluette and Joe McPhee & Strings.

In 2019 he appeared on Charles Gayle’s Peace Planet & Box Of Light (AUM Fidelity) as bassist for The Charles Gayle Trio, connecting him to another central figure in the New York free jazz continuum.
