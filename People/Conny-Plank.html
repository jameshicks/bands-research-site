Conny Plank

Born Konrad Plank, 3 May 1940, Hütschenhausen, Germany. Died 17 December 1987, Neuss (cancer). German record producer, sound engineer, and studio owner. Discogs artist ID: 40135. Called “the Phil Spector of Krautrock.” Married to Christa Fast; father of Stephan Plank.

Overview

Conny Plank is the central production figure of the Düsseldorf/Cologne experimental music scene from the late 1960s through the 1980s. He built a recording studio near Neuss (outside Cologne) in 1974 — known as Conny’s Studio (label/studio ID: 270188) — which became the primary laboratory for Krautrock and kosmische Musik, and later for the wave of post-punk and electronic acts that sought his particular approach to space and texture.

His technique was non-interventionist in a specific sense: he treated the recording studio as an instrument, favouring room sound, unusual microphone placement, and real-time processing over conventional overdubbing and polish. This made him the ideal partner for bands working in drone, motorik pulse, and systematic repetition. He was also a capable performer in his own right — credited on several releases as synthesizer player, guitarist, vocalist, and composer.

He worked across at least three distinct generations of artists: the founding Krautrock generation (Kraftwerk, NEU!, Cluster, Ash Ra Tempel, Guru Guru), the continental art-rock wave (La Düsseldorf, Michael Rother solo), the British post-punk/new wave crossover (Ultravox, Joy Division, Killing Joke), and the early-80s NDW and electronic body music scene (DAF, Eurythmics).

Biography by Era

Pre-Studio Freelance Work (1967–1973)

Plank’s earliest Discogs-documented credit is the 1967 LP Max Nix (Vogue Schallplatten), where he is listed as sound engineer. Through the late 1960s he accumulated engineering credits across a wide range of German sessions — folk, jazz, rock — for labels including Vogue, Philips, QUASAR, and RCA.

The decisive early breakthrough was engineering and co-producing Kraftwerk’s debut self-titled LP (Philips, 1970). He shared the producer credit with Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider-Esleben; the record featured Klaus Dinger on drums on one side, and established the Plank–Düsseldorf network that would define his career. He also engineered the Organisation LP Tone Float (RCA, 1970), another Hütter/Schneider project, and the first Cluster album (Philips, 1971), where he is credited as sound engineer, electronics, and producer.

In 1972 he produced NEU!‘s debut (Brain), co-produced Cluster II (Brain), and worked on early records for Scorpions (Lonesome Crow, Brain), Grobschnitt, and Thirsty Moon. His pace through 1972–1973 was extraordinary: half a dozen significant Krautrock albums per year, many of which became genre touchstones.

He co-produced the second and third Kraftwerk albums — Kraftwerk 2 (Philips, 1972) and Ralf And Florian (Vertigo, 1973) — though not Autobahn (1974), where he is credited only as engineer.

The Studio Years — Peak Krautrock (1974–1976)

In 1974 Plank built his own studio near Neuss, run under the name Conny’s Studio and the Aamok production label. This gave him a permanent base and full creative control over the recording environment.

From this period through the mid-70s he remained closely involved with:

  • NEU!: Neu! 2 (Brain, 1973) and Neu! ‘75 (Brain, 1975) — the latter featuring the split between Dinger’s harder rock approach (Side B, with Thomas Dinger and Hans Lampe on additional drums) and Rother’s ambient drift (Side A)
  • Michael Rother: Flammende Herzen (Sky, 1977) and subsequent solo records
  • La Düsseldorf: debut LP La Düsseldorf (Nova, 1976), with Klaus Dinger post-NEU!
  • Guru Guru: multiple albums including the self-titled (Brain, 1973)
  • Various Aamok-label productions

He also participated as a performer. On the 1978 Liliental album (Brain) he is credited with synthesizer (ARP), guitar, vocals, and writing — Liliental being a project group that included him as a member. He was also a member of Les Vampyrettes, Aamok Musikproduktion, and Wired (4).

The Eno Connection (1977–1978)

The most internationally consequential cluster of recordings at Conny’s Studio came when Brian Eno began working with Cluster (Dieter Moebius and Hans-Joachim Roedelius) there. Plank produced and engineered:

  • Cluster & Eno (Sky Records, 1977) — the foundational Eno/Cluster collaboration; nine tracks of minimal ambient-drone, including guest spots from Holger Czukay (“Ho Renomo”) and Asmus Tietchens (“One”)
  • After The Heat (Sky Records, 1978) — credited to Eno, Moebius & Roedelius; Plank produces and engineers; Czukay plays bass on “Tzima N’Arki”; represents the fullest synthesis of British tape-ambient and German kosmische

These sessions directly informed Eno’s ambient theory, and the studio was a proving ground for the processes he codified in the Ambient series. Plank also engineered Before And After Science (Polydor, 1977) and Ambient 1: Music For Airports (EG, 1979), demonstrating his role across multiple Eno projects.

British New Wave and Post-Punk (1978–1982)

From 1978 Plank became increasingly sought after by British acts looking for the particular electronic coldness and textural depth he had developed with the German bands.

Key productions in this period:

  • Devo — Q: Are We Not Men? A: We Are Devo! (Virgin, 1978): Plank engineered; Brian Eno produced. The division of labour here (Eno produces, Plank engineers) mirrors their German studio arrangement.
  • Ultravox — Systems Of Romance (Antilles/Island, 1978): Plank produced and engineered alongside Dave Hutchins; John Foxx-era lineup (Foxx, Midge Ure, Chris Cross, Warren Cann, Billy Currie). This record set the sonic template for European synth-pop.
  • Ultravox — Vienna (Chrysalis, 1980): now with Midge Ure on lead vocals; Plank produces. This became Ultravox’s commercial breakthrough and one of the defining records of UK synth-pop. He continued with Rage In Eden (1981, co-produced), The Thin Wall and All Stood Still (singles, 1981), and U-Vox (1986).
  • Eurythmics — In The Garden (RCA, 1981): produced; Annie Lennox and Dave Stewart’s debut; also Never Gonna Cry Again (single, 1981)
  • Killing Joke — Revelations (Malicious Damage, 1982): produced; plus “Ha” Killing Joke Live (EG, 1982) and Birds Of A Feather EP

He also worked with The Tourists (Annie Lennox’s pre-Eurythmics band) and a wide range of German NDW acts.

DAF and NDW (1981–1983)

His most radical post-punk production work was with Deutsch Amerikanische Freundschaft (DAF):

  • Alles Ist Gut (Virgin, 1981): Gabi Delgado-Lopez and Robert Görl; minimal electro-body music; “Der Mussolini” became one of the defining tracks of NDW
  • Gold Und Liebe (Virgin, 1981)
  • Für Immer (Virgin, 1982)
  • Numerous DAF singles through 1982–1983

The DAF productions showed Plank pushing toward maximum reduction — drum machine, one bass synth, one voice — a logical endpoint of the motorik minimalism he had been refining since the NEU! sessions.

Own Releases and Collaborations with Moebius

Plank’s closest long-term musical partner (beyond his engineering clients) was Dieter Moebius of Cluster. They collaborated on four albums under their own names:

  • Rastakraut Pasta (Sky Records, 1980) — Plank credited as performer, writer, and producer; release ID 666222
  • Material (Sky Records, 1981) — release ID 300167
  • Zero Set (Sky Records, 1983) — release ID 146655; also featuring Mani Neumeier (Guru Guru)
  • Begegnungen (Sky Records, 1984) and Begegnungen II (Sky Records, 1985) — releases ID 967943, 967979

The Moebius–Plank partnership logged 62 shared releases across the Discogs database — the largest single overlap in Plank’s collaboration graph.

Later Work (1983–1987)

Through his last years Plank remained active across a surprisingly diverse roster: Rita Mitsouko (Rita Mitsouko, Virgin, 1984; Marcia Baïla single), Gio Ferraري (Italian pop), Clannad (Crann Ull, 1980; Magical Ring, 1983), and continued Ultravox work. He died in December 1987 before several sessions he had been working on were completed.

Connections

  • Cluster — almost a third member; produced/engineered most of their 1970s catalog and all three Eno collaborations
  • Brian Eno — 16 shared releases; Plank’s studio the venue for Eno’s most important German work
  • NEU! — all three studio albums; the defining motorik aesthetic was shaped partly by Plank’s room sound
  • Kraftwerk — debut LP and first two albums; early electronic DNA
  • Devo — engineered debut; Eno produced
  • Deutsch Amerikanische Freundschaft — core DAF albums 1981–1983
  • Ultravox — Systems Of Romance through U-Vox; commercial peak with Vienna
  • Eurythmics — In The Garden (debut); introduced Annie Lennox to record
  • Killing Joke — Revelations and live album 1982
  • Joy Division — mentioned in Discogs profile (Die Kleinen Und Die Bösen, 1980, Mute, remix credit)
  • La Düsseldorf — Klaus Dinger’s post-NEU! project
  • Michael Rother — Flammende Herzen (1977) and subsequent solo work
  • Dieter Moebius — closest creative partner; four duo albums
  • Ash Ra Tempel — debut album (Ohr, 1971)
  • Guru Guru — multiple albums with Mani Neumeier
  • Holger Czukay — Can; recurring guest at Conny’s Studio
  • Christa Fast — wife
  • Stephan Plank — son; documentary filmmaker (made film about Conny)
  • Sky Records — home label for Cluster and Plank’s own releases
