Overview

Ian Brown (born 20 February 1963, Warrington, Cheshire, England) is a vocalist and songwriter best known as the frontman of The Stone Roses, whose credits trace back to 1985 and include earlier membership in The Garage Flowers. When The Stone Roses dissolved he launched a solo career on Polydor from 1997 onwards, broadening his style palette from Indie Rock into Downtempo and Leftfield territory; the bulk of his catalogue falls in the 2000s, with 24 releases in that decade against 10 in the 1990s. Longstanding Stone Roses collaborators John Squire, John Leckie, Reni, and Mani (Gary Mounfield) account for the heaviest shared-credit counts, while Paul Schroeder and Simon Dawson figure prominently in his solo-era work.

Notes (web)

1997 Polydor signing — circumstances

After The Stone Roses dissolved (October 1996) and Brown returned from a period in Morocco, he recorded solo material and took the tapes to John Kennedy, the Stone Roses’ former lawyer who by then was running Polygram. Kennedy liked what he heard and offered Brown his choice of label within the Polygram group. Brown considered Island Records — attracted by its association with Bob Marley — but rejected it because Island wanted to remix parts of the album. He chose Polydor partly because of its historical connection to Jimi Hendrix.

“So he took the tapes to John Kennedy, the old Roses lawyer, now the boss of Polygram, who liked what he heard. He offered Ian his choice of label.”

Source: Hot Press, 2001 interview — https://www.hotpress.com/music/i-m-ian-brown-i-used-to-be-in-a-band-called-the-stone-roses-416821

Brown also stated in a late-1990s NME interview (published as “BROWNED OFF!”) that before going solo he doubted himself: “I’m not strong enough to be solo, I don’t wanna be a pop star.” He reportedly considered selling Stone Roses bootlegs rather than launching a solo career. Source: NME, https://www.nme.com/news/music/ian-brown-113-1381290 (snippet only; full article not fully accessible).

The musicOMH interview confirms Brown had material already written that had been intended for a third Stone Roses album — specifically naming “My Star”, “Can’t See Me”, and “Sunshine” — and that this made the transition to solo work straightforward in practical terms. Source: https://www.musicomh.com/features/interviews/interview-ian-brown
