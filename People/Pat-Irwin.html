Pat Irwin

Discogs ID: 273196
Name variations: Patrick Irwin; P. Irwin; PI; Patrick D Irwin
Active: c. 1979–present
Role: Guitar, Saxophone, Bass, Piano, Keyboards, Clarinet, Electronics

American multi-instrumentalist, songwriter, and composer. One of the central figures of the post-punk New York downtown scene of the early 1980s — co-founder of The Raybeats, core member of 8 Eyed Spy (with Lydia Lunch), and key collaborator on Lydia Lunch’s debut Queen Of Siam. After the dissolution of The Raybeats c. 1983, Irwin moved through session and production work across the mid-1980s before joining The B-52’s in 1989 as a touring and recording member, a relationship that lasted through 2008. In parallel he built a film and television composing career, receiving ASCAP Film & Television Awards for his contributions to the SpongeBob SquarePants soundtrack. Into the 2010s and 2020s he has been an active member of the ambient/Americana group Suss (5) and continues recording as a primary artist and collaborator.

The 8 Eyed Spy LP — Studio Side Detail

The studio side (A1–A5) of the 1981 Fetish LP was recorded at Blank Tape Studios by Bob Blank, who also produced and engineered. Irwin’s role here was unusually broad: he covered bass duties (George Scott III was on the live side only), plus guitar, piano, and saxophone. Jim Sclavunos plays drums and percussion. The mixing credits are complex: Chris Stamey and Scott Litt remixed, with Sclavunos and Irwin co-credited as “Remixed With.” Sleeve design: Neville Brody.

The Raybeats — Personnel and Context

The Raybeats formed c. 1979 from overlapping personnel with The Contortions and 8 Eyed Spy. George Scott (2)‘s death from a heroin overdose (5 August 1980) led to Danny Amis replacing him on bass. The lineup for most of the recording catalog: Irwin (sax, guitar, organ), Jody Harris (guitar), Don Christensen (drums), Danny Amis (bass). The Lost Philip Glass Sessions (2013) surfaced archival recordings made with Philip Glass himself playing keyboards alongside Glass Ensemble member Michael Riesman, giving the Raybeats an unexpected canonical connection to the minimalist composer world.

Production Work (1986)

By 1986 Irwin had transitioned into production. He produced and played multiple instruments on Debora Iyall’s solo LP Strange Language (Columbia), the first post-Romeo Void project for the frontwoman. He also produced both the Love Tractor album This Ain’t No Outerspace Ship (Big Time, 1986) and their earlier release Party Train (Big Time, 1986) — an Athens, GA post-punk band adjacent to the R.E.M. scene.

The B-52’s (1989–2008)

Irwin joined The B-52’s touring band in 1989, a relationship that ran through 2008. The Discogs profile references “a242415” — The B-52’s — as the group he joined. On the band’s 1992 LP Good Stuff (Reprise), his credits are confined entirely to the Don Was-produced tracks: release-level Keyboards on tracks 3, 6, and 10 (A3, B1, B5); track-level Organ [Hammond] and Piano on track 2 (A2, “Hot Pants Explosion”); and track-level Guitar on track 10 (B5, “Bad Influence”). He has no credits on any of the five Nile Rodgers-produced tracks (1, 4, 5, 7, 8). The release-level keyboards summary (A3, B1, B5) undercounts his actual contributions — the track-level data adds A2 as organ/piano. The 30th anniversary reissue of Cosmic Thing (2019) credits him for Electric Guitar and Keyboards, confirming his recording contributions to that period. His ASCAP Film & Television Award credits for SpongeBob SquarePants place the film/TV composing career in the same era.

Suss (2018–present)

Suss (5) is an ambient/Americana collective on Northern Spy Records, with Irwin as one of five members (alongside Bob Holmes, Gary Leib, Jonathan Gregg, William Garrett (5)). The band released Ghost Box (2018, E.V.P. Recordings) and has issued four further LPs on Northern Spy through 2024, with Birds & Beasts being the most recent. Irwin mixes and records as well as performs.
