Jimmy Cleveland

Discogs ID: 252996 Real name: James Milton Cleveland Born: May 3, 1926, Wartrace, Tennessee, USA Died: August 23, 2008, Lynwood, California Aliases / pseudonyms: James Van Dyke; Jim Whatsmyname; Jimmy O’Heigho Name variations on records: Cleveland; J. Cleveland; Jim Cleaveland; Jimmie Cleveland; Jimmy Cleaveland; ジミー・クリーブランド

American jazz trombonist. One of the most-recorded trombone first-callers of the hard bop and mainstream jazz era, active continuously from the early 1950s through the 1980s. Closely associated with EmArcy and Mercury Records, and a cornerstone of the New York studio world during its peak decade (1955–1965).

Biography

Early Career & Lionel Hampton (1950–1954)

Cleveland arrived in New York in the early 1950s having already earned his trombone chops on the road. His earliest Discogs-documented credits are with Lionel Hampton’s orchestra, appearing on multiple Vogue Productions sessions from 1953 — Hampton’s big-band recordings were done in Paris, placing Cleveland in the thick of the transatlantic jazz scene that also spawned the Jazztime Paris Blue Note sessions with Clifford Brown and Gigi Gryce (1954). He is credited on Jazztime Paris Vol. 1 and Vol. 2 (Blue Note, 1954), prominent enough in those sessions to be listed as “Soloist.”

EmArcy / Mercury First-Call (1955–1960)

The mid-1950s saw Cleveland cement his position as a first-call studio trombonist on EmArcy and Mercury — the two labels ran jointly as Mercury’s jazz imprint. He appears on virtually every major EmArcy session of the period:

  • Cannonball Adderley — Julian “Cannonball” Adderley (EmArcy, 1955) and In The Land Of Hi-Fi (EmArcy/Mercury, 1956)
  • Clark Terry — Plays Swahili And Kitten (EmArcy, 1955); Clark Terry (EmArcy, 1955); Introducing Clark Terry (EmArcy, 1957)
  • Dinah Washington — For Those In Love (EmArcy, 1955); The Swingin’ Miss “D” (EmArcy, 1957)
  • Helen Merrill — Dream Of You (EmArcy, 1956/57)
  • Maynard Ferguson — Boy With Lots Of Brass (EmArcy/Mercury, 1957)
  • Quincy Jones — Jazz Abroad (EmArcy, 1957); The Birth Of A Band! (Mercury, 1959); The Great Wide World Of Quincy Jones (Mercury, 1959); Change Of Pace (EmArcy, 1959)

His parallel position as a sideman at Prestige (with Art Farmer from 1953–1956) and Savoy Records (Trombones, 1956, a multiple-trombonist showcase) shows how deeply embedded he was in both the bebop and hard bop production circuits.

Cleveland Style (EmArcy, 1958) is the key studio document for understanding his circle: Benny Golson contributes one arrangement and plays tenor; Ernie Wilkins handles the remaining charts; Art Farmer and Wynton Kelly round out the front line. This album directly intersects with the Jazztet world.

My Fair Lady Loves Jazz (Impulse!, 1963) is his most star-studded date — a co-leadership with Billy Taylor and Quincy Jones around a Lerner & Loewe songbook, with Creed Taylor producing.

Compilations / Reissues

  • I Giganti Del Jazz Vol. 35 (Curcio, 1981, Italy) — live reissue documenting a concert with Max Roach, Phil Woods, Thelonious Monk, Thad Jones, and others
  • Complete Recordings (Lone Hill Jazz, 2006) — collects the EmArcy/Mercury leader sessions
  • The Trombones Inc. (Lone Hill Jazz, 2007) — trombone ensemble compilation featuring Cleveland alongside J.J. Johnson, Frank Rosolino, Bob Brookmeyer, Melba Liston, Urbie Green, and others on the West Coast trombone circuit
  • Four Classic Albums Plus Bonus Tracks — Avid reissue combining Introducing, A Map Of Jimmy Cleveland, Tops On Trombone, and bonus tracks

Miles Davis / Gil Evans Collaborations (1957–1960)

Cleveland is credited on two of the defining large-ensemble jazz recordings of the era:

  • Miles Davis + Gil Evans — Miles Ahead (Columbia, 1957): the landmark Gil Evans orchestral arrangement for Miles; Cleveland is in the trombone section
  • Miles Davis + Gil Evans — Porgy And Bess (Columbia, 1959): follow-up Evans/Davis collaboration, again with Cleveland in the brass section
  • Gil Evans & Ten (Prestige, 1957): Evans’s own nonet/dectet session, earlier in the same partnership

Continued Studio Work (1960s–1980s)

Cleveland remained a consistent studio presence well past the hard bop peak:

  • Quincy Jones sessions for Mercury throughout the early 1960s (The Great Wide World of Quincy Jones, Walking In Space A&M 1969, Genius + Soul = Jazz, various Impulse!/Verve projects)
  • Michel Legrand — Legrand Jazz (Columbia, 1958), a critically important album with Miles Davis and others
  • Oliver Nelson — The Blues And The Abstract Truth (Impulse!, 1961), the iconic hard bop masterwork; Cleveland in the trombone chair
  • Jimmy Smith — Bashin’ — The Unpredictable Jimmy Smith (Verve); Jimmy & Wes (The Dynamic Duo)
  • Wes Montgomery — Jimmy & Wes (The Dynamic Duo) (Verve)
  • Horace Silver — Señor Blues sessions
  • Dorothy Ashby — The Fantastic Jazz Harp Of Dorothy Ashby
  • Melba Liston — Melba Liston And Her ‘Bones (Jazz Workshop)
  • Quincy Jones — Walking In Space (A&M, 1969)
  • Live concert documented in Live In Rotterdam 1967 (The Lost Recordings, 2025)

The Discogs credit trail extends continuously through reissues into the 2020s, as sessions Cleveland played on in the 1950s–1960s continue to surface in new editions.

Scene Connections

Cleveland is a hub figure in the hard bop New York studio world, with dense connections outward to:

  • The Jazztet orbit — Benny Golson and Art Farmer appear on his Cleveland Style album; the three worked together repeatedly in the late 1950s
  • The EmArcy/Mercury house band — Ernie Wilkins (house arranger), Don Butterfield (tuba regular), Ernie Royal (trumpet regular) form a recurring unit across Cleveland’s Mercury dates
  • The Gil Evans/Miles Davis circle — Miles Ahead and Porgy And Bess place him inside the most prestigious large-ensemble jazz projects of 1957–59
  • The trombone fraternity — Trombones (Savoy), Jay & Kai + 6 (Columbia), The Trombones Inc. (Lone Hill Jazz) document his place in the trombone-ensemble subgenre that ran from J.J. Johnson and Kai Winding downward through Benny Powell, Henry Coker, Frank Rosolino, Bob Brookmeyer, and Melba Liston
  • Seldon Powell co-billing — the two Roost albums (Seldon Powell Sextet Featuring Jimmy Cleveland, 1956) show an unusual co-star arrangement where Cleveland receives title billing as a featured soloist rather than simple sideman credit

Open Questions

  • The stub listed death as “August 29, 2008, Los Angeles” — Discogs gives “August 23, 2008, Lynwood, California” (Lynwood is an LA suburb; the date discrepancy needs a reliable source to resolve)
  • Tops On Trombone appears in the Four Classic Albums Plus Bonus Tracks Avid reissue listing alongside A Map Of and Introducing, but does not appear as a standalone release in Discogs under Cleveland’s primary credits — may be an alternate title for one of the Mercury sessions or an unindexed release
  • No Discogs credits found for a period between approximately 1963 and 1969 in the primary sideman record — this may reflect a gap in the DB indexing rather than inactivity (the Quincy Jones Walking In Space A&M date from 1969 resumes the trail)
  • West Coast relocation timing unclear — the Gerald Wilson Orchestra of the 80s and Bill Berry LA Band credits indicate he was based in California by the 1980s, but when exactly he moved from New York is not documented in Discogs
