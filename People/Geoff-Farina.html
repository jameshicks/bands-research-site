Geoff Farina

Discogs ID: 480330

Guitarist and vocalist; co-founder of Karate (Boston, formed 1993). Also the primary visual designer for Southern Records — 28 design credits, the most of any designer across the label’s catalog. Disbanded Karate in July 2005 after developing hearing problems; reunited 2022.

Design Background

Farina’s design work appears to be professionally acquired on the job rather than through formal design education. The clearest documentary evidence is a CWAS interview (Winter 2002) in which he states he is about to resign “after a year as a ‘User Experience Designer,’ which is a fancy word for a Web application designer” — indicating a roughly year-long stint in a UX/web-application role circa 2001–2002. The interviewer’s introduction also mentions “his web design work” as a concurrent activity alongside music, suggesting it was publicly known at the time. (CWAS #9, Winter 2002)

No sources consulted mention formal design schooling (art school, graphic design programme, or similar). His academic credentials are a BA in Songwriting from Berklee College of Music (arrived Boston 1988, graduated 1991, BM ‘91) and an MA in English Composition from the University of Massachusetts (thesis: history of analog synthesis). (DePaul University faculty page, Berklee article as of 2026-07-16)

His 28 design credits on Southern Records (the most of any designer across the label’s catalog, per Discogs) therefore appear to reflect self-taught or on-the-job practice rather than credentialed design training.

What this shows: Farina’s earliest confirmed design credit on Southern is 1998 — three years into Karate’s relationship with the label and after the label had used a dedicated designer (Andy Tidy) on the 1995 debut. His own earliest Southern credit is the less formal “artwork” role on that 1995 release. The credit gap on “Unsolved” (2000 — Jonathan Lukens designed it) shows his involvement was not continuous.

Crucially, his confirmed design credits in 2002 (“Some Boots”, “Blobscape”) are contemporaneous with the UX day job he was leaving at the time of the CWAS Winter 2002 interview. The Southern Records design work therefore overlapped with the UX stint rather than clearly preceding or following it. His Berklee years (1988–1991) predate all of this by at least four years; he was already living in Boston and leading Karate when the design work began.
