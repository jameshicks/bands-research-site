Alice Babs

Discogs ID: 322142 Real name: Hildur Alice Babs Sjöblom (born Nilsson) Born: 26 January 1924, Kalmar, Sweden Died: 11 February 2014, Stockholm, Sweden

Swedish singer, pianist, actress, and yodeler. One of Sweden’s most prominent vocalists of the twentieth century, active from 1939 to at least the 2000s. She recorded across an extraordinary breadth of styles — swing, jazz, pop, children’s music, classical arias, and liturgical music — and is internationally best known for her collaborations with Duke Ellington, especially his Sacred Concerts series. She was the first Swedish Eurovision Song Contest entrant (1958, finishing fourth), was appointed hovsångare (Royal Court Singer) in 1972, and was elected to the Royal Academy of Music in 1974.

Overview

Early Career — Swing and Yodeling (1939–1950)

Alice Babs made her first recording on 25 April 1939, releasing material on Sonora (2). These earliest sides show her double identity as a jazz-inflected pop singer and an enthusiastic yodeler — the name “Joddlarflickan” (The Yodeling Girl) was applied to her from the start, and Swiss and German audiences took to her yodeling records throughout the 1940s–50s. During this period she worked with Swedish swing orchestras including Seymour Österwalls Orkester, Eskil Eckert-Lundins Filmorkester, Thore Ehrlings Orkester, and Charlie Norman’s various groups.

Metronome Decade (1951–1958)

Her most intensive pop/swing recording period was on Metronome, for which she cut dozens of singles and several 78/LP compilations. Key backing orchestras include Harry Arnolds Orkester (55 shared releases, the most of any collaborator) and Bengt Hallbergs Orkester. The pianist Bengt Hallberg was a regular collaborator throughout this era (32 shared releases). She also recorded prolifically for Polydor’s German operation (Schlager and novelty material), and began appearing on Decca.

In 1958 she co-founded the jazz trio The Swe-Danes with Danish violinist Svend Asmussen and guitarist Ulrik Neumann. The group recorded for Warner Bros. Records and toured internationally. The same year she represented Sweden at the Eurovision Song Contest.

The Swe-Danes (1958–1962)

The Swe-Danes issued several albums on Warner Bros., including At A Georgia Camp Meeting (1960), Scandinavian Shuffle (1960), The Swe-Danes Vol. 2 (1960), and Med Swe-Danes På Berns (1961). Alice Babs’s role was primarily as vocalist alongside Asmussen’s violin improvisations and Neumann’s guitar. The group disbanded around 1962.

Duke Ellington Collaborations (1966–1975)

Ellington discovered Alice Babs in the early 1960s and was captivated by her voice’s unusual purity and range. Their first co-credited album was Serenade To Sweden (1966, Telestar), recorded in a trio setting with Ellington and Billy Strayhorn at piano alongside bassist Kenny Clarke and drummer Sam Woodyard. It remains among the most intimate recordings Ellington ever made.

Ellington wrote specifically for her voice for his Second Sacred Concert (performed 1968, issued 1970 on Fantasy). She appeared as one of “The Singers” alongside Tony Watkins, Trish Turner, Devonne Gardner, and Roscoe Gill Jr., backed by the full Ellington orchestra with multiple guest choirs including the A.M.E. Mother Zion Church Choir.

For the Third Sacred Concert, recorded at Westminster Abbey and released on RCA Victor in 1975 — the year after Ellington’s death — Alice Babs received a “Featuring” credit on the primary release. The concert had taken place in October 1973; Mercer Ellington produced the posthumous album.

The 1975 album Alice Babs Serenading Duke Ellington (Swedish Society Discofil) is her own Ellington tribute, recorded with Nils Lindberg’s Orchestra (arranger Nils Lindberg, with Arne Domnérus on alto, Red Mitchell on bass, Rune Gustafsson on guitar, Jan Allan on trumpet, and Lennart Åberg and Ulf Andersson on tenors). Producer was Frank Hedman.

Swedish Society Discofil Period (1966–1980s)

From the late 1960s through the early 1980s, Swedish Society Discofil became her primary artistic home for more serious and varied work. Key albums from this period:

  • Sånger Och Arior / Exsultate, Jubilate (1966) — arias and Mozart, with Kungliga Hovkapellet and organist Åke Levén
  • Söndag (1970) — conducted by Olle Bolander
  • Elizabethan Love Songs (1971) — with Musica Holmiæ and Olle Bolander
  • Alice In Israel (1972) — recorded in Israel
  • Music With A Jazz Flavour (1973) — with Nils Lindberg’s Orchestra, produced by Frank Hedman; personnel includes Red Mitchell, Rune Gustafsson, Arne Domnérus, Jan Allan, and Lennart Åberg
  • Om Sommaren Sköna (1974) — with Kungliga Hovkapellet
  • Alice Babs Serenading Duke Ellington (1975) — see above
  • Far Away Star (1978, Phontastic)
  • What A Joy! (1980, Bluebell Of Sweden) — also released 1991 on Swedish Society Discofil

Frank Hedman produced her work on Bluebell Of Sweden (his own label, parent: AB Frank Hedman) as well as Swedish Society Discofil releases throughout this period. Per Metérus handled layout design across 18 releases on Telestar, Swedish Society Discofil, and RCA.

Later Recordings and Legacy

She remained active into the 1990s: Bästa (1995, Scana) and Swingtime Again (1998, RCA Victor) brought her full circle to the swing repertoire. A Naxos Nostalgia compilation Swingflickan (2004) collected her 1939–1944 originals.

She was married to Nils-Ivar Sjöblom. Three of her children also had music careers: Lilleba Sjöblom, Titti Sjöblom, and Lasse Sjöblom — all appear as collaborators on Discogs releases.
