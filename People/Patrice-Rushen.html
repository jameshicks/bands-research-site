Patrice Rushen

Discogs ID: 21262
Born: September 30, 1954, Los Angeles, California, USA
Also credited as: Mumbi
Instruments: Piano, electric piano, synthesizer, clavinet, organ, flute, clarinet, various percussion
Roles: Vocalist, composer, arranger, producer, music director, educator

Grammy Award-winning American R&B, jazz, funk, and soul artist. One of the most versatile and technically accomplished musician-producers to emerge from the Los Angeles jazz-funk scene. Her career divides roughly into three phases: a jazz/jazz-funk period on Prestige (1974–1977), a pop/R&B commercial peak on Elektra (1978–1984, then Arista 1987), and a return to jazz-oriented work from the late 1990s onward. Best known for the 1982 hit “Forget Me Nots,” which has been widely sampled. Maintained a long-running acoustic jazz trio with Stanley Clarke and Leon Ndugu Chancler.

Jazz Period — Prestige (1974–1977)

Rushen won a competition at age 17 that landed her a spot at the Monterey Jazz Festival, which led to a recording contract with Prestige Records in 1973. Her debut album Prelusion (1974) was a fully self-composed and self-arranged jazz-funk record, with Ndugu on drums, Joe Henderson on tenor saxophone, George Bohanon on trombone, Oscar Brashear on trumpet, and producer Reggie Andrews overseeing the session. Already a striking debut: all originals, long-form fusion structures, and a compositional maturity unusual for a first release.

Her second album Before The Dawn (1975) expanded the palette with Hubert Laws on flute and alto flute, Lee Ritenour on guitar, Harvey Mason on drums (sharing the drum chair with Ndugu), and a wider horn section. The third Prestige LP Shout It Out (1977) moved toward funk and soul, with James Gadson on drums, Al McKay on guitar, Bill Summers on percussion, and lyrical contributions from sister Angela Rushen (credited as Angela Ehigiator on later releases). Rushen produced most of Shout It Out herself, a notable move for a 22-year-old artist in 1977.

She was also an in-demand session keyboardist throughout this period, appearing on records by Jean-Luc Ponty, Lee Ritenour (Captain Fingers, 1977; Sugar Loaf Express, 1977), and the LA jazz community generally.

Transition to Elektra and Pop/R&B Peak (1978–1987)

After three albums on Prestige, Rushen signed to Elektra in 1978. The self-titled Patrice (1978) and its successor Pizzazz (1979) bridged jazz-funk and mainstream R&B/disco, with Rushen functioning as executive producer and principal writer. She assembled a consistent studio band around Freddie Washington (bass), Leon Ndugu Chancler (drums), Marlo Henderson (guitar), Paul Jackson Jr. (guitar), and a brass section anchored by Oscar Brashear (trumpet/flugelhorn), Ray Brown (2) (trumpet/flugelhorn), Clay Lawrey (trombone), and Maurice Spears (bass trombone). Engineer Peter Chaikin and mixer F. Byron Clark were recurring technical collaborators. The horn and string arrangements were Rushen’s own throughout.

Pizzazz (1979) contained “Haven’t You Heard,” which became her first major R&B hit, and “Let The Music Take Me.” The album reached No. 1 on the Billboard Black Albums chart. Co-writers included Sheree Brown and Freddie Washington.

Posh (1980) continued in this vein. “Never Gonna Give You Up (Won’t Let You Be)” was a significant single. The band was essentially the same core unit, with David T. Walker and Paul Jackson Jr. on guitars, James Gadson and Ndugu splitting drum duties, and the large horn ensemble intact.

Straight From The Heart (1982) was her commercial apex. The opening track “Forget Me Nots” — co-written with Freddie Washington and Terri McFaddin — became an international hit, and its bass line and synthesizer hook have since been interpolated and sampled extensively (Will Smith’s “Men in Black,” George Michael’s “Fastlove,” and many others). Gerald Albright played saxophone on the track. The album also included “Breakout!” (written by Brenda Russell (2)) and “I Was Tired Of Being Alone.”

Now (1984) was the final Elektra studio album. It yielded “Feels So Real (Won’t Let Go)” and “Get Off (You Fascinate Me).” The Anthology Of Patrice Rushen (1985, Elektra) closed out her tenure there.

She then moved to Arista, releasing Anything Can Happen (1987) and Watch Out! (1987). These albums brought in a different production team; Aaron Zigman and Jerry Knight are credited on arrangements. Label president Clive Davis is credited as executive producer on the Arista releases.

Return to Jazz and Later Work (1988–present)

Old Friends - New Friends (1988, Electric Bird/Japan) was a Ndugu Chancler-led session on which Rushen appeared as keyboardist and string/horn arranger — one of several Japanese market recordings from this period.

The formal trio with Stanley Clarke and Leon Ndugu Chancler crystallized in the late 1990s. Jazz Straight Up (2001, Fine Tune / Vertical Jazz) was the trio’s first major release under joint billing, a set of bebop and cool jazz standards: “Lover Man,” “Mack The Knife,” “Salt Peanuts,” “Take Five,” “Now’s The Time,” and others. The follow-up Standards (2006, Kind Of Blue Records) was a CD+DVD release covering much of the same repertoire with a DVD of the recording session added; produced by Jeffrey Weber and Suzanne Severini.

Signature (1997, Discovery Records) was an intermediate release under her own name, featuring Kirk Whalum (saxophone), Dexter Wansel, Paul Jackson Jr., Gary Bias, Paulinho Da Costa (percussion), Stuart Matthewman and Andrew Hale of Sade’s band.

Lenny White Live (2013, BFM Jazz) features Rushen as a primary co-billed artist alongside drummer Lenny White, with Victor Bailey (bass), Mark Ledford (vocals/trumpet), Foley (bass), Don Blackman, and Bennie Maupin.

A Place In Time (2016, HighNote Records) is a Gary Bartz-led session in which Rushen appears on piano, with Buster Williams (bass), and Wallace Roney (trumpet).

She scored the 2023 film American Fiction, releasing the original soundtrack (MRC II, 2023) — an indication of her continuing activity as a composer and music director.

She has also been credited as a member of The Stanley Clarke Trio, CAB (13) (with Tony MacAlpine, Dennis Chambers, and Bunny Brunel), and appeared as a sideperson on numerous sessions through the 2010s.

”Forget Me Nots” — Sampling and Interpolation Legacy

Written by Rushen, Freddie Washington, and Terri McFaddin. Among the tracks that have sampled or interpolated it:

  • Will Smith — “Men in Black” (1997, Columbia)
  • George Michael — “Fastlove” (1996, Aegean/Virgin) — “Older” album
  • Brandy — “I Need You Tonight” (1995)
  • Cypress Hill — “Throw Your Set in the Air” (1995)
  • Wreckx-n-Effect — “Runnin’ To Your Love / Hibby” (adjacent credits)
  • Ol’ Dirty Bastard — “Niggamortis” (1994)
  • Montell Jordan — “Gettin’ Off On You” (1995)
  • Various others across 1990s–2000s hip-hop and R&B
