Mark Dawson (2)

Australian drummer and percussionist. Discogs artist ID: 410573. Alias: Bongo Fury (8) (Discogs ID: 7406585, realname Mark Dawson).

Name variations on record: Dawson, M. Dawson, Marcos Dawson, Mark “Bongo Fury” Dawson.

Biography

Early Years — Scattered Order (1981 onward)

Dawson’s earliest documented activity is as a member of Scattered Order (Discogs ID: 105479), the Sydney post-punk/experimental group whose Discogs page lists him among a large rotating cast. Scattered Order’s recorded output begins with the Screaming Tree cassette (1981, M Squared) and continues through Career Of The Silly Thing (1985, Volition), though individual personnel credits on those early releases are sparse in the database. The band also revived activity in the 2010s, releasing Odd Din (2011), Puppy Frequencies (2013, Klanggalerie), Bodram Bogota (2015), and continuing into the 2020s, suggesting Dawson has remained connected to the project over four decades.

The Kuepper Years — Ed Kuepper (1986–1995)

Dawson has a long-running association with Ed Kuepper (Discogs ID: 381695), spanning at least nine years as a credited contributor. His earliest confirmed credit with Kuepper is Rooms Of The Magnificent (1986, Hot Records), where he plays drums and percussion. He continued through Kuepper’s prolific early-solo period:

On Today Wonder Dawson works alongside engineer Adam Chapman and producer Kuepper, in what appears to be a lean trio setup. Honey Steel’s Gold adds Chris Abrahams (of The Necks) on piano and organ, and Sir Alfonso (bass). A King In The Kindness Room adds saxophone and flute by Louise Elliott.

Dawson also drummed on Kuepper’s side project Ed Kuepper & The Yard Goes On Forever (Discogs ID: 1937182).

The Aints (1991–1992, 2010)

Dawson is a member of The Aints (Discogs ID: 450870), Ed Kuepper’s Saints-repertoire project, alongside Kent Steedman, Artie Sledge, Peter Oxley, Alister Spence, Paul Larsen (2), and Tim Reeves. The Aints released Ascension (1991, Hot Records), Autocannibalism (1992), Shelf Life Unlimited!! Hotter Than Blazing Pistols!!! (1995), and a live album The Aints Live (2010, Prince Melon Records). The band resumed later activity around 2018, though the more recent releases don’t show Dawson’s individual credit.

Blackeyed Susans (1995–2017) — Primary Tenure

From Mouth To Mouth (1995, Hi-Gloss Records) onward, Dawson is the principal drummer for Blackeyed Susans (Discogs ID: 422723), joining a band whose revolving lineup had previously featured Alsy MacDonald and Jim White (2). He appears on every subsequent studio release through Close Your Eyes And See (2017, Teardrop):

The La Mascara EP is notable for the band adopting Spanish-language personas: the release credits the band as “Las Blackeyed Susans” and Dawson as “Marcos Dawson.” He co-wrote material on Spin The Bottle (1997) and La Mascara (1998) — the only two Blackeyed Susans releases where writing credits are attributed to the full band collectively. Close Your Eyes And See (2017) represents the final recorded chapter, with the band expanding to include J.P. Shilo, Evil Graham Lee, and a brass section (Matthew Habben on saxophone, Adam Hutterer on trombone, Ken Gardner (2) on trumpet), and Clare Moore on vibraphone.

The Blackeyed Susans’ other members across this period include vocalist Rob Snarski, bassist/multi-instrumentalist Phil Kakulas, guitarist Dan Luscombe, and keyboardist Kiernan Box. Writing credits on Close Your Eyes And See go primarily to Phil Kakulas.

Second Winter — Ed Kuepper duo (2012)

Second Winter (Special Tour Edition) (2012, Prince Melon Records) is credited to “Ed Kuepper with Mark Dawson (2)” — making this one of the few releases where Dawson is a co-billed primary artist. The release is produced by Kuepper and Julian Knowles, with additional contributions from Sir Alfonso (Mx88 synthesizer), Josh Watson (2) (violin, bells), and Rob Knaggs (cello). Dawson’s role is listed as “Drums, Percussion [Forks].”

Curious (Yellow) (1988–1991)

Dawson is listed among the members of Curious (Yellow) (Discogs ID: 714382), a Sydney outfit whose releases include I Am Curious (1988, Red Eye), Charms & Blues (1990, Polydor/Red Eye) — produced in part by Steve Kilbey of The Church — and Love Itself (1991). The Charms & Blues credits are focused on vocalist/lyricist Karin Jansson, with Dawson listed as a member but without a specific named credit on that LP.

Ron S. Peno And The Superstitions (2011–2021)

Dawson is a core member of Ron S. Peno And The Superstitions (Discogs ID: 2827722), the ensemble led by Ronald S. Peno (born Ronald Stephen Peno), best known as the longtime vocalist of Died Pretty. The Superstitions released Future Universe (2011, Public Bookings) and Anywhere And Everything Is Bright (2013, Public Bookings), both of which Dawson plays drums and percussion on, alongside bassist Andy Papadopoulos, guitarist Cam Butler, and keyboardist Tim Deane. More recently Guiding Light (2017, Public Bookings) and Do The Understanding (2021) continue the project.

Charlie Marshall & The Existentialists (2010)

Dawson is credited on Inordinate Fondness For Beetles (2010, Homo Sapien Records) by Charlie Marshall & The Existentialists (Discogs ID: 2880926).

The Jackson Code (1989)

Dawson is a member of The Jackson Code (Discogs ID: 1251829), the Sydney acoustic/alternative group led by songwriter Mark Snarski. His confirmed credit is on their debut LP Del Musical Del Mismo Nombre (1989, Waterfront Records, DAMP 115) — drums on tracks A2, A4, B3, B4; bongos on A1; bass drum on B1; floor tom on A3. The band self-produced and arranged the record, with Kathy Wemyss handling brass arrangements. The Jackson Code released three further albums (Strange Cargo 1992, Draggin’ The River 1993, The Things You Need 1995), though individual personnel credits on those releases are absent from Discogs.

Other Groups

Discogs lists Dawson as a member of:

  • The Coralinas (Discogs ID: 2779909)
  • The Nearly Brothers (Discogs ID: 2539524)

These groups have limited Discogs footprints and have not surfaced prominently in the release database.

Bongo Fury (8) alias

Under his alias Bongo Fury (8) (Discogs ID: 7406585), Dawson is credited on You Can’t Hide From Your Yesterdays (2010, Not On Label) — a release separate from the main Dawson discography, suggesting the alias was used selectively for at least one recording project.

John Kennedy’s Love Gone Wrong (1985)

Dawson appears on King Street (1985, Red Eye Records) by John Kennedy’s Love Gone Wrong alongside bassist Barry Turnbull and guitarist Colin Bloxsom, produced by John Kennedy and recorded by Clive Shakespeare.

Truckload Of Sky / Tribute Work (2020)

Dawson appears as a performer (credited as “The Band”) on Truckload Of Sky: The Lost Songs Of David McComb Vol 1 (2020, Lost Records And Tapes) — a tribute project drawing on the songwriting of David McComb, the Blackeyed Susans’ founding vocalist who died in 1999.

Solar and Frontierland (2022–2023)

Solar (2022, Kasumuen Records) — drums and percussion. Frontierland (2023, Prince Melon Records / Remote Control) — “Drum, Electronic Drums [Octopad]”, suggesting continued exploration of hybrid percussion.
