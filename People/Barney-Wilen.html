Biography

Early life and emergence (1937–1956)

Bernard Jean Wilen was born in Nice, France on March 4, 1937, to an American father and a French mother. As a teenager he began performing in Riviera clubs, encouraged by the writer Blaise Cendrars, a friend of his mother. By 1955 he was sitting in with visiting American musicians in Paris, appearing on sessions led by Jay Cameron and with Roy Haynes’s modern group (both on Swing). His precocious command of hard bop language drew attention from visiting Americans who recognized in him an equal.

The Paris years with Art Blakey and Miles Davis (1957–1960)

Wilen’s career intensified sharply in 1957. He appeared on the John Lewis–led Afternoon in Paris session for Atlantic — his first international-label credit — alongside Sacha Distel and Percy Heath. That same year he led his own quintet for the Guilde Du Jazz label and recorded Tilt for Swing with his quartet (Marcel Romano, producer; Al Levitt, drums; Maurice Vander, piano; Gilbert Rovère, bass).

The decisive moment came when Miles Davis arrived in Paris in late 1957 to create the music for Louis Malle’s debut feature Ascenseur pour l’échafaud. Davis assembled a Paris-based rhythm section and recruited Wilen as his sole French horn voice. The session — see dedicated section below — was recorded in a single night with eyes on the projected film. The resulting soundtrack, released on Fontana in 1958, circulated widely and placed Wilen’s name in international jazz consciousness.

In 1959 Wilen recorded Un Témoin Dans La Ville (Fontana), composing as well as playing; the score for Édouard Molinaro’s noir film starred Lino Ventura. He also appeared prominently on the MJQ-adjacent Paris Session with Milt Jackson, Percy Heath, Kenny Clarke, and René Urtreger, released on Philips.

Art Blakey then chose Wilen to interpret the music of Roger Vadim’s 1960 film Les Liaisons Dangereuses. The resulting double-lead record — credited to Art Blakey’s Jazz Messengers avec Barney Wilen — featured Lee Morgan, Bobby Timmons, Jymie Merritt, and Duke Jordan (who substituted on piano for one session). It became Wilen’s best-selling release. That same year he led Barney for RCA, with Kenny Dorham, Duke Jordan, Paul Rovère, and Daniel Humair — one of the strongest hard-bop sessions under his own name. He also played the Newport Jazz Festival in 1960 with pianist Toshiko Akiyoshi, bassist Tommy Bryant, and drummer Roy Haynes.

Hard bop to free jazz (1961–1968)

Wilen worked intermittently through the first half of the 1960s, appearing on compilation recordings and European tours. Jazz Sur L’écran / Jazz Sullo Schermo (Fontana, 1965) gathered his film-score work. Back to Back (Fontana, 1966) documented sessions from the Blakey years. Zodiac (Disques Vogue, 1966) marked a transition: a set of original compositions featuring J.-F. Jenny-Clark and Karl Berger, gesturing toward freer tonality.

The watershed came in 1967. Wilen co-led Le Nouveau Jazz with pianist François Tusques on the tiny Disques Mouloudji label. The record is a key document of French free jazz: turbulent collective improvisation with J.-F. Jenny-Clark and Beb Guerin on contrabass and Aldo Romano on drums, with liner notes by Yves Buin. That same year he participated in the SABA sessions Jazz Meets India — an ambitious fusion of German free jazz and classical Indian music with Manfred Schoof, Irene Schweizer, the Dewan Motihar Trio, and musicians from the emerging Krautrock orbit (Mani Neumeier, Uli Trepte). Produced by Joachim Ernst Berendt.

In 1968 Wilen recorded Dear Prof. Leary (MPS Records) — a dedication to the LSD theorist Timothy Leary — with the same label’s rock-and-jazz explorers, marking his explicit turn toward psychedelic rock. Auto Jazz (Tragic Destiny Of Lorenzo Bandini) (MPS, 1968) was a film score for a German production, co-credited with François Tusques.

Africa, silence, and return (1969–1986)

In 1969 Wilen traveled to Africa with his partner Caroline De Bendern and a film crew, recording in Congo and other locations. The resulting material appeared years later as Moshi (Saravah, 1972), a synthesis of jazz and African music produced by Pierre Barouh, and much later as Moshi Too — Unreleased Tapes Recorded in Africa 1969–70 (Sonorama, 2012). A long near-silence followed. Wilen composed for French cinema and television through the late 1970s and early 1980s, worked with punk musicians, and appeared occasionally on session credits (notably engineering on BYG Records’ Archie Shepp live document, 1971).

Renaissance period: IDA Records and later work (1987–1996)

Wilen’s full return to jazz recording came with IDA Records, the French jazz label, where he made a run of well-received albums:

  • La Note Bleue (IDA, 1987) — quartet with Alain Jean-Marie (piano), Riccardo Del Fra (bass), Philippe Vincent (drums); composed music for the film of the same name
  • French Ballads (IDA, 1987) — tenor and soprano saxophone standards
  • Wild Dogs Of The Ruwenzori (IDA, 1989) — with Alain Jean-Marie, Riccardo Del Fra, Sangoma Everett; cover art by Marie Möör
  • Sanctuary (IDA, 1991)

He also recorded extensively for Japanese labels in this period: Inside Nitty = Gritty (Venus Records, 1993), Talisman (IDA, 1994), and several Venus Records sessions. Late collaborations included work with Mal Waldron (as the Barney Wilen & Mal Waldron Quartet) and the graphic-novel collaboration La Note Bleue with comic artist Loustal. He also appeared at the 1988 Grenoble concert with Tete Montoliu (Barney And Tete) and in Tokyo with his quartet in 1991.

Wilen died of a heart attack in Paris on May 25, 1996.

Ascenseur pour l’échafaud (1958)

The session is one of the founding documents of jazz and cinema. Davis reportedly did not brief the musicians beforehand; they watched the film projected and improvised simultaneously. The resulting score — brooding, spare modal trumpet over steady swing — influenced a generation of European jazz musicians and cinematographers alike.

Scene Connections

  • French jazz establishment: Kenny Clarke was the central Paris-based American drummer; Pierre Michelot the key bassist; René Urtreger the key pianist — Wilen worked repeatedly with all three
  • Film music: Major collaborator with Louis Malle (Ascenseur), Édouard Molinaro (Un Témoin), Roger Vadim (Les Liaisons Dangereuses); later composed for Jean Rollin horror films (with Tusques)
  • Free jazz turn: François Tusques (co-leader on Le Nouveau Jazz, 1967), Beb Guerin, J.-F. Jenny-Clark, Aldo Romano — the Parisian free jazz network
  • German MPS/SABA axis: Jazz Meets India (SABA, produced by Joachim Ernst Berendt) linked Wilen to the German jazz-fusion and proto-Krautrock world (Manfred Schoof, Irene Schweizer, Mani Neumeier, Uli Trepte, Larry Coryell)
  • Africa: Caroline De Bendern (travel companion and co-credited), Pierre Barouh (Saravah label founder, produced Moshi)
  • IDA Records era: Alain Jean-Marie, Riccardo Del Fra, Sangoma Everett, Hervé Le Guil (engineer), Marie Möör (cover artist, vocalist)
  • Labels: Fontana (1958–65), RCA (1960), Disques Vogue (1966), Disques Mouloudji (1967), MPS Records (1967–68), Saravah (1972), IDA Records (1987–94), Venus Records (5) (Japan, 1993–95)
