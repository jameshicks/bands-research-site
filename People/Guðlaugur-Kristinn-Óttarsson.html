Guðlaugur Kristinn Óttarsson

Guðlaugur Kristinn Óttarsson

Discogs ID: 587393 (alias Godkrist (2): 713537)
Born: 1954, Reykjavík, Iceland
Role: Electric guitar, bass
Website: islandia.is/gko / YouTube: DenseTime

Icelandic guitarist active from 1981 to at least 2022. Core member of early Reykjavík new wave band Þeyr (1981–1982) and post-punk group Kukl (1983–1986), the latter featuring Björk and forming the nucleus that became The Sugarcubes. Also formed the mid-80s duo Elgar Sisters with Björk. Contributed to Björk’s solo career (1993–94) as co-writer and co-producer. Released solo album Dense Time (2005). Operates under numerous pseudonyms including Nnitsirk Rukúk, Guð.Krist, and Godkrist (2).

Þeyr (1981–1982)

Guðlaugur was guitarist in Þeyr (also spelled Theyr/Peyr), an Icelandic new wave band from the early 1980s. The group began with a dance-music orientation before pivoting to new wave. Two other future Kukl / Sugarcubes members were in the band: Siggi Baldursson (drums) and Hilmar Örn Agnarsson (bass).

Kukl (1983–1986)

Kukl formed in Reykjavík in August 1983, conceived as a project by Ásmundur Jónsson of Gramm. Members: Einar Örn Benediktsson (vocals, trumpet), Björk (vocals, woodwind), Guðlaugur (electric guitar), Birgir Mogensen (bass), Einar Melax (keyboards), Siggi Baldursson (drums). They debuted in Icelandic radio and at a We Demand a Future festival before Crass; later toured European countries. Their music was described as a mixture of punk, rock, jazz, and experimental. Lyrics in English and Icelandic. In 1986, Kukl split; the core — Björk, Einar Örn, Birgir Mogensen, Einar Melax, Siggi Baldursson — went on to form The Sugarcubes. Guðlaugur did not join The Sugarcubes.

Debut 7”. Guðlaugur credited under pseudonyms Nnitsirk Rukúk (guitar, B-side) and Guð.Krist. (guitar). Other credits: Birgir as “Birgir” (bass “Rikki”), Siggi as “Tryggur” (drums “Trymmis”), Einar Melax as “Bragadóttir” (keyboards, guitar), Björk (voice “Söngl”), Einar Örn as “Einar Ørn” (voice, trumpet). Recorded and mixed by Kjartan Kjartansson. Tracks: “Söngull” / “Pökn (Fyrir Byrjendur).”

The Eye (Crass Records, 1984)

Debut LP recorded in London, January 1984. Credited as K.U.K.L. on Crass Records (cat: 1984/1). Guðlaugur credited as God Krist (Strings [Electric]). Drums on this recording: Trix (11) rather than Siggi Baldursson. Engineer: Tony Cook (2). Artwork/design: Dada Nana.

Tracklist: “Assassin” / “Anna” / “Moonbath” / “Open The Window And Let The Spirit Fly Free” / “Dismembered” / “Seagull” / “The Spire” / “Handa Tjolla.”

Holidays In Europe (The Naughty Nought) (Crass Records, 1986)

Second and final studio album. Also on Crass Records. Guðlaugur (as Godkrist) credited Strings [Electric].

Megas and Icelandic session work (1988)

Played guitar and bass on Höfuðlausnir (Gramm, 1988) by Megas, the Icelandic singer-songwriter. The album also featured Björk Guðmundsdóttir (vocals), Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson (keyboards, bass, programming), Inga Guðmundsdóttir (vocals), and Rose McDowall (vocals). 10 tracks.

How Rose McDowall came to appear on Höfuðlausnir

McDowall’s presence on an Icelandic record is explained by the Psychic TV / Gramm network that had been building since 1984–85. The key steps:

  1. Psychic TV connection (1984–85). Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson became a Psychic TV fixture from 1984; McDowall was also in PTV’s extended orbit. On 19 May 1985, they performed together as part of an expanded Psychic TV lineup at “Thee Fabulous Feast Ov Flowering Light” (Hammersmith Palais, London). KUKL — the Reykjavík band featuring Björk and Einar Örn — also appeared at the same event. [source: https://coldspring.bandcamp.com/album/thee-fabulous-feast-ov-flowering-light-csr188cd; https://www.compulsiononline.com/ornamental_rosemcdowall.htm]

  2. Ornamental (1987–88). The shared PTV world led directly to the studio project Ornamental, a collaboration between McDowall, Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson, Einar Örn Benediktsson, and Dave Ball (Soft Cell). Ornamental released two 12”s: No Pain (1987, Gramm) and Crystal Nights (1988, One Little Indian). The debut was on Gramm — the same Reykjavík indie label behind Höfuðlausnir and Guðlaugur’s own Kukl releases. McDowall recorded in Iceland for this project and has said she found Iceland “the place to go off and write an album.” [source: https://www.compulsiononline.com/ornamental_rosemcdowall.htm]

  3. In Iceland, in the same scene. With McDowall already travelling to Reykjavík for Ornamental in 1987–88, embedded in the Gramm/Sugarcubes circle, and on personal terms with Björk (the two became close friends during this period), her appearance alongside Björk as a backing vocalist on Megas’s Gramm album was a natural extension of those ties. McDowall herself noted that she and Björk’s voices “complemented each other really, really well.” [source: https://www.compulsiononline.com/ornamental_rosemcdowall.htm]

No source confirms Guðlaugur was the direct conduit; the connection ran through Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson and the Gramm label network. Guðlaugur’s own PTV overlap (see the Psychic TV section above) makes him part of the same social web, but the specific invitation likely came from the Megas / Hilmar / Gramm axis rather than Guðlaugur personally.

Björk solo contributions (1993–1994)

After Kukl / The Sugarcubes, Guðlaugur maintained a creative relationship with Björk into her solo career.

Big Time Sensuality single (One Little Indian, 1993)

Co-wrote “Sídasta Ég” (B-side, track 1-2) with Björk Guðmundsdóttir and Þór Eldon. The song appeared in the single’s B-side sequence alongside Glóra.

Venus As A Boy US single (Elektra)

Co-produced and played guitar on “Stigdu Mig” (track 2), which he also co-wrote with Björk and Þór Eldon. Björk and Guðlaugur are jointly credited as producers of that track; Nellee Hooper produced the remainder of the single. Engineer: Jim Abbiss. The single was released on Elektra in the US, One Little Indian elsewhere.

Dense Time (solo, 2005)

Solo album released on Pronil Holdings (cat: PN2005, Iceland). 11 tracks, spanning Classical / Electronic / Jazz / Rock / Experimental. Notably includes “Stigðu Mig” (track 7) and “Síðasta Ég” (track 9) — the same compositions he co-wrote with Björk for the 1993–94 B-sides. No additional personnel credited in Discogs.

Tracklist: “Zontal” / “Seventh Seal” / “Sirius” / “Patre” / “Findings” / “Stefjahreimur” / “Stigðu Mig” / “Partir” / “Síðasta Ég” / “Essence” / “Closure.”
