Tony Barber

Bassist, guitarist, drummer, engineer, producer. UK punk/post-punk scene. Discogs artist ID: 310589. Aliases: Anthony De Large, Billy Beethoven (2). Name variations: Barber, Tony, Tony Arber, Tony B.

Member of Buzzcocks August 1992–April 2008 — the longest-serving bassist in the band’s history across five studio albums. Active from the early 1980s UK anarcho-punk scene through to the 2020s as a producer and engineer operating out of studios on both sides of the Atlantic.

Biography

Origins: Anarcho-Punk and the Crass Orbit (1982–1986)

Tony Barber surfaces in the Discogs record as a member of Lack Of Knowledge, a post-punk band from the south-east England anarcho scene. Their earliest known release is a self-titled 7” (The Uninvited / Ritual, 1982, self-released) followed by the LP Grey (1984, Crass Records), produced by Penny Rimbaud of Crass at Southern Studios with John Loder engineering. On Grey, Barber is credited (uncredited on sleeve) with guitar and songwriting alongside fellow members Philip Barker (drums), Karen Gower, Daniel Drummond, and Paul Stevens (6). The band also released Sentinel (1985, Chainsaw) and the Sirens Are Back 7” (1985, Corpus Christi). A reunion LP, White Science, appeared in 2004 on Alternative Records (3).

Lack of Knowledge is notable for being one of the few Crass Records acts to reunite and record new material two decades later. Barber and Philip Barker — both from Lack of Knowledge — went on to form the nucleus of the Buzzcocks’ post-reunion rhythm section.

Also in this period, Barber appeared as guitarist on Rubella Ballet’s Freak Box VHS (Jungle Visual Displays, 1984).

Mid-1980s: Ear Trumpet, Boys Wonder, Data Terminal Systems (1985–1989)

By the mid-1980s Barber was playing in several overlapping projects:

Ear Trumpet (Discogs ID: 898346) — a short-lived post-punk grouping whose 1987 LP Bring On The Dirt (Data Terminal Systems Records) features a remarkable lineup: Barber on bass, organ, vocals, and violin; Bruce Gilbert of Wire on guitar; Steven Severin of Siouxsie and the Banshees on guitar, organ, piano, and synthesizer; and Neil Mouat on vocals, drums, organ, and tape. The album is a notable artifact connecting the Wire/Siouxsie post-punk lineage with the anarcho scene.

Boys Wonder (Discogs ID: 765839) — Barber is listed as a member.

Data Terminal Systems Records releases (1987): Barber is credited as bassist, guitarist, producer, and/or vocalist on three 1987 releases: Blue Hour, Bring On The Dirt (Ear Trumpet), and The Program, all on Data Terminal Systems Records.

Alternative TV (Discogs ID: 252690) — Mark Perry’s long-running post-punk project. Barber is listed as a member; his specific recordings with ATV are not yet fully identified from Discogs data.

Chelsea (2) (Discogs ID: 260917) — Gene October’s punk band. Barber is listed as a member. The Faster Cheaper And Better Looking compilation (Captain Oi!, 2005) and Red With Purple Flashes (Planet, 2008) include Barber credits.

Creation Records Adjacent: Slaughter Joe (1990)

Barber contributed guitar and bass to side B of The Pied Piper Of Feedback (Creation Records, 1990), a Joe Foster / Slaughter Joe project. That side also features Philip Barker on drums — an early Barber/Barker pairing in a Creation Records context, produced by Joe Foster and Alan McGee.

Buzzcocks: Reunion Era (1992–2008)

Barber joined Buzzcocks in August 1992 alongside drummer Philip Barker (no relation), becoming the rhythm section of the reunited band fronted by Pete Shelley and Steve Diggle. He remained for the entirety of the band’s most prolific post-reunion period, appearing on five studio albums over fifteen years.

Studio Work: New Era Recorders and Toilet Studios (1988–2007)

Barber’s profile notes he was proprietor of New Era Recorders in Edmonton, London from 1988 until 2002 — covering the entire early reunion period. He then ran Toilet Studios in Bushwick, New York from 2003 until 2007, explaining his New York base during the Buzzcocks (2003) and Flat-Pack Philosophy (2006) cycles.

Production and engineering credits from this period include:

  • Casualty Street EP (Kollusion Records, 1996) — producer
  • Sense Of Solitude (Southern Records, 2000) — producer
  • Small Songs With Big Hearts (Get Back, 2001) — mixed by, producer

Poptones Projects: Selofane Seventyfour and Airport (2000–2001)

Around 2000–2001, Barber was active on the Poptones label (Alan McGee’s post-Creation imprint):

Selofane Seventyfour (Discogs ID: 2112471) — a duo of Barber and Joe Foster. Their self-titled album Selofaneseventyfour (Poptones, 2000) credits both as writers, producers, and performers, with Richard Taiter on piano/trumpet and Peter Towndrow on Moog/zither.

Airport (4) (Discogs ID: 1414863) — Barber is the sole member listed. Lift Off With Airport (Poptones, 2001) credits him as writer and producer; Joe Foster is listed for A&R.

Post-Buzzcocks: Exitstencil Studios and Continued Production (2008–present)

After departing Buzzcocks in April 2008, Barber built and ran Exitstencil Studios (also known as Tony’s Shed) at Dial House in Epping, England from 2009 to 2014. Dial House is the long-time home of Crass, connecting Barber back to his anarcho roots from the early 1980s. He subsequently established Civil Defence Studios in New York.

Post-2008 production/engineering credits include:

The Christ’s Reality Asylum credit is for a Crass-related project, again tying Barber back to that early anarcho network.

On The Hot Dog Streets (West Midlands Records, 2012) — Barber plays synthesizer, vocals, and guitar.

Last Amendment (Discogs ID: 5833165) and The Modern Folk Quintet (Discogs ID: 1182543) and The Peafish House Band (Discogs ID: 3326374) and The Creation (2) (Discogs ID: 268836) — Barber is listed as a member; specific releases not yet identified in these entries.
