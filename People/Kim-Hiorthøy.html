Kim Hiorthøy

Born March 27, 1973, Trondheim, Norway. Based in Berlin. Discogs artist ID: 19822.

Norwegian graphic designer, illustrator, filmmaker, musician, painter, and author. Best known as the sole cover and sleeve designer for Rune Grammofon across its entire catalog from the label’s founding in 1998 through the present — 714 credited appearances across 185 unique masters on that label alone, making him one of the most prolific single-label designers in contemporary music. His visual identity defines Rune Grammofon’s physical presence: spare, typographically precise, often abstract or illustrative, consistently recognizable across three decades of releases.

Also a longstanding visual collaborator with Motorpsycho, dating back to 1993 — predating the Rune Grammofon relationship — with credits on over 35 unique releases including artwork, sleeve design, photography, illustration, and film direction. Records and releases music under his own name on Smalltown Supersound.

Background

Hiorthøy grew up in Trondheim and studied at the Trondheim Academy of Fine Art (1991–96), spending a year abroad in 1994 at the School of Visual Arts in New York. He later studied at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts in Copenhagen (1999–2000). Now based in Berlin.

He has published illustrated books and exhibited visual art alongside his design and music work. His written and visual style shares a minimalist, deadpan sensibility across all media.

Musical Discography

Kim records infrequently and self-produces. His music falls broadly in electronic and ambient territory with occasional modern classical or folk elements. All lyrics and compositions are his own; his debut was self-released, subsequent records on Smalltown Supersound.

Design Career

Rune Grammofon — Exclusive Label Designer

Kim is the sole credited designer on every Rune Grammofon release from the label’s first year (1998) to the present. Over 714 credits across 185 unique masters, spanning roles including Sleeve, Design, Cover, Artwork, Art Direction, Illustration, Photography, Layout, Film Director, and Film Editor.

Rune Grammofon was founded in 1998 by Rune Kristoffersen in Oslo. Its first releases were by Supersilent (1-3, 4), Arne Nordheim (Electric), and Deathprod (Nordheim Transformed) — all with Kim’s sleeve design. He has continued without interruption through the label’s most recent releases in 2025.

Motorpsycho — Long-Term Collaboration

Kim’s design work for Motorpsycho began in 1993, several years before Rune Grammofon existed, and predates his exclusive label relationship. He designed artwork and sleeves for Motorpsycho on multiple labels (Voices Of Wonder, Columbia, Stickman Records, dBut) before the band moved to Rune Grammofon and the relationship continued there.

Stickman Records / Early Norwegian Scene

Before the Rune Grammofon era, Kim was the house designer for the German heavy/psych label Stickman Records (3), which handled Motorpsycho’s catalog for several years. He accumulated 138 credits there — the second highest label count after Rune Grammofon. Also designed for Voices Of Wonder (32 credits) and Columbia Norway (30 credits) in the mid-1990s.

Ninja Tune / International Reach

Kim designed sleeves for Jaga Jazzist’s Ninja Tune releases — A Livingroom Hush (2002), The Stix (2002), Day (2003), What We Must (2005) — carrying his visual style onto an international UK label at the same time he was working exclusively in Oslo’s Rune Grammofon world. Total: 23 credits on Ninja Tune.

Warp Records

Kim designed artwork for Jamie Lidell’s Warp period: Preparations (Prefuse 73, 2007), Another Day, Little Bit Of Feel Good, and Jim (all Jamie Lidell, 2008). 19 Warp credits total.

Biographical Depth — Context and Aesthetic Philosophy

Hiorthøy came to music while studying at the Trondheim Academy’s sound studio. Smalltown Supersound founder Joakim Haugland discovered his work and released his debut Hei in 2000 (some sources say 2001 for international release). His musical approach is described by the label as combining “weird beats, lo-fi/leftfield electronics, field recordings, electro-acoustic sounds and samples.” His cited influence includes Jan Johansson (Swedish jazz pianist) and early hip-hop aesthetics. source

On his creative process, he has spoken of deliberate obscuring of method — using digital tools to create sounds that appear handmade, “like making a 3D model on the computer of a wood sculpture made with an axe.” He designed record sleeves for local bands while still a student, publishing fanzines and developing his visual identity in parallel with his musical work. He was named graphic designer of the year by Norsk Form before his musical debut. source source

His gallery representation is through STANDARD (Oslo). Exhibition credits include shows at Kunstnernes Hus (Oslo, 2022), Bergen Kunsthall, the 14th Vilnius Painting Triennial, and Manifesta San Sebastian.
