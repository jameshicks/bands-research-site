William S. Burroughs

Born: February 5, 1914, St. Louis, Missouri. Died: August 2, 1997, Lawrence, Kansas. American novelist, essayist, painter, and spoken word artist. Discogs artist ID: 50077.

Overview

Burroughs was one of the central figures of the 1950s Beat movement, alongside Jack Kerouac and Allen Ginsberg, and went on to become one of the most significant literary presences in avant-garde and experimental music from the 1960s through the 1990s. His novels — Naked Lunch, The Soft Machine, The Ticket That Exploded, Nova Express — pioneered the “cut-up” technique developed with Brion Gysin at the Beat Hotel in Paris, in which text (and later audio) was physically cut and reassembled to generate unconscious writing. The technique was directly transposed to tape recording and influenced decades of industrial and experimental practice. His phrase “heavy metal” became a genre name; Soft Machine and Steely Dan both lifted their names from his fiction.

His music career began with Call Me Burroughs (1965), readings from Naked Lunch and Nova Express first issued in France, later on ESP. Through the 1970s–80s he appeared regularly on John Giorno’s Giorno Poetry Systems label and was celebrated at the 1978 Nova Convention at the Entermedia Theater (with Patti Smith, Philip Glass, Allen Ginsberg, Frank Zappa, and others). In 1981 Genesis P-Orridge and Peter Christopherson of Throbbing Gristle co-produced Nothing Here Now But The Recordings (Industrial Records), drawing on 1950s–60s archive tape experiments. The avant-garde maintained its links through the 1980s; Laurie Anderson featured him on Mister Heartbreak (1984); Material / Bill Laswell integrated his voice on Seven Souls (1989); and Hal Willner produced Dead City Radio (1990, Island Records), his most fully realized solo record, with John Cale, Donald Fagen, and Sonic Youth in the cast. His collaboration with Kurt Cobain on The “Priest” They Called Him (1993, Tim/Kerr Records) was widely noted. Burroughs died of a heart attack in Lawrence, Kansas in August 1997.

James Grauerholz, his longtime secretary and companion, appears as co-producer, engineer, or coordinator on virtually every significant release across three decades.
