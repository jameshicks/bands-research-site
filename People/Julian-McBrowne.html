Julian McBrowne

Julian McBrowne

Oct 21, 202610 min read

Julian McBrowne

Discogs ID: 404750. Engineer and producer based in New York, operating primarily out of Planet Sound Studios (Discogs label ID: 301835). Active from the late 1970s through the 2020s across a wide range of genres — ZE Records-era downtown New York, soca, reggae, jazz, and mainstream R&B. Most closely identified with August Darnell and Kid Creole And The Coconuts, for whom he served as primary engineer on their most significant studio run (1980–2021). Also produced and co-produced records by Coati Mundi (Andy Hernandez) and the Antiguan soca artist Arrow (2).

Credited on Discogs under multiple name variants: Jules McBrown (early ZE Records credits), J. McBrown, J. McBrowne, Julian Browne, Julian M.C. Brown, Julian MacBrowne, Julian Mc Browne, Julian McBrown, Julien Mc Brown, Julien McBrown, Jules McBrowne.

Overview

McBrowne entered the record industry in the late 1970s doing session engineering for small New York labels (Laurie Records, Willpower, Jam Sessions). His earliest notable credit is the 1978 side project release “In U.S.A.” (Makossa), where he is credited for Remix. By 1980 he was working at the ZE Records–Island Records intersection, co-engineering (as Jules McBrown) Kid Creole And The Coconuts’s debut album Off The Coast Of Me alongside Bob Blank.

From 1980 onward McBrowne became the central engineering voice on Darnell’s records, serving as Chief Engineer on Doppelganger (Sire/ZE, 1983) and engineer/mixer on In Praise Of Older Women And Other Crimes (Sire, 1985) and I, Too, Have Seen The Woods (Sire, 1987). He was also the engineer on Coati Mundi’s solo records for Virgin — The Former 12 Year Old Genius (1983) and the “Como Esta Usted?” single (1983). He appeared as a credited Performer (House of Darnell) on Private Waters In The Great Divide (CBS, 1990), suggesting his position as a core member of the extended Darnell circle beyond a purely technical role.

Parallel to the Kid Creole work, McBrowne built a substantial soca engineering practice in the late 1980s–mid-1990s, working repeatedly with Arrow (Alphonsus Cassell) — including the co-production credit on Zombie Soca (Arrow Records, 1991) and engineering/mixing on Soca Dance Party (Mango, 1990) and Classics Plus (1994). He also worked with Caribbean labels Burning Flames Music (Antigua & Barbuda), Cocoa Records, and Creole (2).

The 1990s saw him cross into mainstream New York R&B and hip-hop — recording credits on Bad Boy Entertainment’s Mase/Blackstreet release (1999), Lil’ Man Records (Finally, Girlfriend/Boyfriend), the Wild Wild West soundtrack (Interscope, 1999), and Guy’s Guy III (MCA, 2000). He also worked in jazz (Atlantic Jazz, Muse Records) and reggae contexts (EMI America’s Wanted Dread & Alive, 1981; Rebel, Island, 1990).

His relationship with Darnell continued into the 2000s (To Travel Sideways, Atoll Music, 1993; Kiss Me Before The Light Changes, Victor, 1994 — where he is credited co-producer) and the 2020s, with an engineer credit on Nothin’ Left But The Rest (2C2C, 2021), the band’s most recent release on Discogs.

Key Release Notes

Off The Coast Of Me (Island/ZE, 1980)

Kid Creole And The Coconuts debut LP. McBrowne co-engineered with Bob Blank; credited as “Jules McBrown.” Mixed by August Darnell and Bob Blank. Produced by August Darnell. Notable for its full orchestral arrangements — vibraphone by Coati Mundi, drums by Winston Grennan.

Doppelganger (Sire/ZE, 1983)

McBrowne’s most prominent Kid Creole credit — one of three Chief Engineers alongside Joe Barbaria and Michael H. Brauer (who also mixed). Multiple assistant engineers including Andy Heermans, Gary Hellman, Michael Abbott (2), Richard McClain, Steve Rinkoff. Produced by August Darnell; associate producer Coati Mundi.

The Former 12 Year Old Genius (Virgin, 1983) / “Como Esta Usted?” (Virgin, 1983)

Coati Mundi solo release and accompanying 12” single, both engineered by McBrowne. The Former 12 Year Old Genius produced and arranged by Andy Hernandez (“Sugar-Coated Andy Hernandez”); mixed by The Hit Squad (2). The single produced by Hernandez alone.

In Praise Of Older Women And Other Crimes (Sire, 1985)

Multi-engineer, multi-mixer Kid Creole LP. McBrowne credited both as engineer and as one of several mixers (alongside Argyle Neft, August Darnell, Coati Mundi, Joe Barbaria, Steven Stanley). Mastered by Bob Ludwig.

Soca Dance Party (Mango, 1990)

Arrow LP for the Island Records imprint Mango. McBrowne engineered and mixed alongside Arrow (Alphonsus Cassell) and Roland Richards. Key session players include Frankie McIntosh and Leston Paul (keyboards), Mac Gollehon (trumpet). Mastered by Ted Jensen.

Zombie Soca (Arrow Records, 1991)

McBrowne’s first production co-credit with Arrow; co-produced with Justin “Hero” Cassell and Roland Richards. Produced by Alphonsus Cassell (Arrow).

Kiss Me Before The Light Changes (Victor, Japan, 1994)

Kid Creole Japan-only release; McBrowne is credited as Producer (tracks 1–12) alongside August Darnell and Jed Becker. His most prominent production credit in the Darnell discography.

Private Waters In The Great Divide (CBS, 1990)

McBrowne appears as a Performer credited to “The House Of Darnell” — the extended collective of Darnell associates credited on this album.

Nothin’ Left But The Rest (2C2C, 2021)

Most recent Kid Creole release on Discogs. McBrowne returns as engineer alongside Gilles Sampic (who also mixed). Produced by August Darnell, Joe Hammer, and Olivier Bloch-Lainé.
