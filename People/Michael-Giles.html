Michael Giles

Born: 1 March 1942, Winton, Dorset, England. Drummer, multi-instrumentalist. Discogs artist ID: 259121.

Original drummer of King Crimson, co-founder of Giles, Giles And Fripp and McDonald and Giles. Active as a session drummer across a wide range of British rock and pop throughout the 1970s–80s. Later work included the experimental trio Ghost Dance (release) with David Cunningham and Jamie Muir.

Biography

Pre-King Crimson: The Giles Brothers (1960–1967)

Michael and his brother Peter Giles began their recording careers in the early 1960s as the rhythm section behind a string of beat-era acts: Johnny King and the Raiders (1960–61), Dave Anthony and the Rebels (1961), The Dowlands and the Soundtracks (1961–63), Sands (1963), Interns (1963), Trendsetter Limited (1964–66), Trend (1966), and Brain (1967). Credits from this period show Michael on drums (often uncredited) on Parlophone and Page One singles from 1964–67, with Joe Meek among the producers encountered in the orbit of those sessions.

Giles, Giles and Fripp (1967–1968)

In September 1967 the brothers co-founded Giles, Giles And Fripp with guitarist Robert Fripp. The trio released one album, The Cheerful Insanity of Giles, Giles and Fripp (1968, Deram), a quirky, orchestrated pop record produced by Wayne Bickerton with session keyboard contributions from Nicky Hopkins. It sold poorly but led directly to King Crimson’s formation.

King Crimson (1969)

When Ian McDonald and Greg Lake joined, the band became King Crimson. Michael drummed and sang on In the Court of the Crimson King (1969), one of the foundational progressive rock albums. The lineup on that record: Robert Fripp (guitar), Ian McDonald (keyboards/woodwind), Greg Lake (bass/vocals), Peter Sinfield (lyrics), and Michael (drums, percussion, vocals). The band self-produced. Michael departed after the debut, with some drum overdubs appearing on In the Wake of Poseidon (1970).

McDonald and Giles (1970)

Michael and Ian McDonald formed McDonald and Giles and released one studio album (1970, Island Records), co-produced and co-arranged by both. Peter Giles played bass; Steve Winwood contributed organ and piano; Peter Sinfield supplied lyrics for the suite “Birdman”. The percussion credits are characteristically eccentric: “milk bottle, handsaw, lip whistle, nutbox.”

Session Work (1971–1989)

After McDonald and Giles, Michael became a highly active London session drummer. Documented credits across this period include:

  • Roger Glover projects: The Butterfly Ball and the Grasshopper’s Feast (1974, Purple Records); Wizard’s Convention follow-ups
  • Leo Sayer: Silverbird (1973, Chrysalis), Just a Boy (1974), Another Year (1975)
  • Kevin Ayers: The Confessions of Dr. Dream and Other Stories (1974, Island)
  • Streetwalkers: Streetwalkers (1974, Reprise)
  • Grimms: Grimms (1973, Island)
  • Family / related: Burlesque-era recordings
  • Greg Lake: Greg Lake (1981, Chrysalis) — reuniting with his former King Crimson bandmate
  • Anthony Phillips (Wise After the Event, 1978, Arista; Sides, 1979)
  • Penguin Cafe Orchestra: Broadcasting from Home (1986, Editions EG)
  • Various Christian music releases (Myrrh label, 1977–79)
  • La Mezcla (1988, EMI) — producer credit

He also contributed equipment/technician credit to King Crimson’s Islands (1971).

Ghost Dance (1996)

The most significant post-KC creative project was Ghost Dance (1996, Piano), a trio record with David Cunningham and Jamie Muir. The album was a soundtrack to Ken McMullen’s film of the same name. Michael is credited on drums, kit, percussion, horns, woodwind (mouth reeds), bow, voice, and keyboards — a maximally wide instrumental palette. Jon Wozencroft handled design. The record was compiled for CD by Cunningham and Giles.

Progress and Later Projects (2001–2024)

  • Progress (2002, Voiceprint) — a solo/archival release. Michael produced, recorded, wrote all material, and contributed photography; Hugh Padgham handled post-production; Simon Heyworth mastered.
  • The Adventures of the Michael Giles MAD BAND (2009, self-released) — associated with his group The Michael Giles Mad Band.
  • 21st Century Schizoid Band — Michael participated in the KC reunion/tribute touring project (2003–04).
  • Nigel Lived (2001, Headcase) — drums and percussion.
  • Reimagining the Court of the Crimson King (2024, Purple Pyramid) — written-by credit.
