Uwe Nettelbeck

Jul 25, 20263 min read

Uwe Nettelbeck

Discogs ID: 254004 Born: 7 August 1940, Mannheim, Germany Died: 17 January 2007, Bordeaux, France Occupations: Record producer, journalist, film critic

German music journalist, film critic, and record producer; the creator and originating force behind Faust. He was one of Germany’s leading film critics in the 1960s (chief film critic for Die Zeit) before turning to music production. In 1969 he was approached by a Polydor A&R representative to assemble an experimental German rock group; he merged two Hamburg groups (Nukleus and Campylognatus Citelli) into Faust, converted a schoolhouse at Wümme into a studio, and produced the band’s core catalog. (Wikipedia: Uwe Nettelbeck)

Overview

Nettelbeck grew up near Lake Constance, studied German literature at Göttingen without completing a degree, and began film criticism in 1962 (writing for Filmkritik and then Die Zeit). His left-wing politics eventually caused friction with Die Zeit — a 1969 article on the Andreas Baader trial earned him a formal warning — and he moved toward underground journalism and the left-wing magazine konkret. His move into music production was an extension of his avant-garde cultural interests.

Beyond Faust he also produced Anthony Moore’s first two solo albums (Pieces From the Cloudland Ballroom, 1971; Secrets of the Blue Bag, 1972), which led to the formation of Slapp Happy with Peter Blegvad and Dagmar Krause. He produced Slapp Happy’s first two albums, Sort Of (1972) and Casablanca Moon / Acnalbasac Noom (1973), in which Jean-Hervé Peron, Werner Diermaier, and Gunther Wüsthoff from Faust performed. The original Casablanca Moon was rejected by Polydor; the version with Faust was not officially released until 1980 via Recommended Records as Acnalbasac Noom.

Between 1976 and 2006, Nettelbeck and his wife Petra wrote and edited 124 editions of the review magazine Die Republik. In 1992 they relocated to France, settling in an isolated farmhouse in the Gironde. He died of cancer in Bordeaux in January 2007.
