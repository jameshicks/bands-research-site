Jerry Soto

Recording engineer and studio owner. Born in New York, raised in Evanston, Illinois. Graduated from Evanston Township High School; studied at Oakton Community College and a Palatine music school before opening his own recording facility. Owner and chief engineer of Soto Sound Studio (Discogs label ID: 290814), which began in Evanston in the late 1970s, relocated to Palatine, Illinois (800 E. Northwest Hwy., Suite 60) in the late 1980s, and operated until his death. Died 2005. Discogs artist ID: 59740.

Known name variations on record: J. Soto, Jerry “High Strung” Soto, Jerry “Splicemaster” Soto, Jerry (Splicemaster) Soto, Soto, Mr. Jerry Soto.

Overview

Soto ran one of the key independent recording rooms serving Chicago’s underground and blues scenes from the late 1970s through the early 2000s. His studio work touched a wide stylistic range: avant-garde spoken-word/electronic (Algebra Suicide), Chicago blues (Buddy Guy, Fred Anderson, Eddie “The Chief” Clearwater, Lefty Dizz, Carey Bell), early house music (Trax Records, D.J. International Records), noise/industrial (ONO), and jazz. He also occasionally performed as a session musician — playing guitar, keyboards, piano, organ, or synthesizer — on some of the records he engineered.

His longest single association was with Algebra Suicide (Lydia Tomkiw / Don Hedeker): he engineered every original Algebra Suicide recording from the 1982 debut 7” through the 1987 LP. The two Dark Entries reissue compilations (2013, 2019) list him as engineer because they draw from those same original tapes, not from new sessions — his involvement predates his 2005 death.

Genre Range

Soto’s studio served a notably eclectic clientele across roughly 25 years of active work:

  • Blues — his heaviest single genre, with numerous sessions for JSP Records, Alligator Records, Rooster Blues, and independent Chicago-area blues acts (Buddy Guy, Eddie “The Chief” Clearwater, Lefty Dizz, Carey Bell mentioned in his profile)
  • Avant-garde / spoken-word / electronic — Algebra Suicide (1982–1987)
  • Chicago house / dance — Trax Records (Jesse Velez, Super Rhythm Trax), D.J. International Records, House Jam Records
  • Noise / industrial / post-punk — ONO (Machines That Kill People, 1983; shared tracking with Iain Burgess)
  • Jazz — Fred Anderson Quartet (Dark Day, 1979, with Hamid Drake)
  • Metal — Wicked World / Ravensthorn releases (2004–2005, late-career)
