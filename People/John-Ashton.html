John Ashton

John Ashton

Discogs ID: 264869
Born: 30 November 1957, East London, England, UK
Role: Guitarist, producer
Name variations on records: Ashton, Aston, J. Ashton, J. Aston, John Gez Ashton, John Geza Ashton

Founding and longest-serving guitarist of The Psychedelic Furs, present from the band’s formation in 1977 through to the present day. Alongside the Butler brothers (Richard Butler and Tim Butler), he is one of only three members who can claim continuity across the band’s entire active span. Beyond the Furs he has production credits for Sisters of Mercy (1982–1987), a side project (Satellite Paradiso, 2014), and early punk-era membership in The Unwanted (pre-Furs).

Biography

Early Days: The Unwanted (pre-1977)

Before the Furs coalesced, Ashton had a brief stint in the London punk band The Unwanted (Discogs ID: 295129), a short-lived outfit whose membership also included Olli Wisdom (later of Specimen) and Robin Wills (later of The Dentists). The Unwanted were active in the late-1977 London punk wave; the overlap with the Furs’ formation period suggests Ashton moved quickly from one project to the other.

The Psychedelic Furs: Formation and First Albums (1977–1982)

The Psychedelic Furs formed in London in 1977 around the core of Richard Butler (vocals), Tim Butler (bass), and John Ashton (guitar), later joined by Roger Morris (guitar), Duncan Kilburn (saxophone/keyboards), and Vince Ely (drums). Their first single, “We Love You” (Epic, 1979), announced a dense, aggressive post-punk sound.

The self-titled debut (CBS, 1980) was produced partly by Steve Lillywhite and partly by the band with Howard Thompson — Ashton on rhythm guitar alongside Roger Morris. Talk Talk Talk (Columbia, 1981), again with Lillywhite producing, refined the formula and included early standout “Pretty In Pink.”

Forever Now (Columbia, 1982) marked a significant production shift: Todd Rundgren took the helm, adding orchestral colour (backing vocals from Howard Kaylan and Mark Volman of The Turtles, cello from Ann Sheldon). Ashton’s guitar remained central throughout.

The Alice credit is particularly notable — it is the debut release by Sisters of Mercy (with Andrew Eldritch, Gary Marx, Ben Gunn, Craig Adams), produced by Ashton in his role as a working peer in the UK post-punk scene. The Discogs credit on Good Things appears on a Portuguese pressing, suggesting a licensing or regional release context.

Mirror Moves and the Mid-80s Furs Sound (1984–1988)

Mirror Moves (Columbia, 1984), produced by Keith Forsey, saw the Furs move toward a slicker, more commercial sound, with Mars Williams on saxophone and session drummers (Forsey himself plus Thommy Price). Ashton’s guitar adapted to the new context. The album yielded “The Ghost In You” and “Heaven.”

Midnight To Midnight (CBS, 1986) and Book Of Days (Columbia, 1989) continued the band’s major-label run. The compilation All Of This And Nothing (Columbia, 1988) collected the band’s key singles to that point.

First Hiatus and the 1991 Disbanding

The Furs released World Outside (EastWest/Columbia, 1991) and then went on an extended hiatus. The band effectively dissolved in the early 1990s, with Richard Butler forming Love Spit Love.

Reunion and Later Work (2000s–Present)

The Furs reformed around 2000, releasing live recordings including Beautiful Chaos: Greatest Hits Live (Columbia/Legacy, 2001) and Live From House Of Blues (Eagle Vision, 2001). Various compilations and retrospectives followed throughout the 2000s.

Satellite Paradiso (2014)

Ashton co-founded Satellite Paradiso with Frank Coleman and Fredric Schreck. The self-titled album (2014, self-released) credits Ashton with a full multi-role slate: Written-By, Arranged By, Engineer, Producer, Mixed By (co-mixed with Anthony Molina), and Design (co-designed with Coleman). It represents his most comprehensive production/engineering credit outside the Furs.

Made Of Rain (2020)

The Furs returned with their first studio album in 29 years, Made Of Rain (Cooking Vinyl, 2020), a critical success that re-established them as an active band.
