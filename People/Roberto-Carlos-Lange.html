Biography

Early Career — ROM (2) and Epstein (1997–2006)

Roberto Carlos Lange’s first documented Discogs credit is from 2002 on ROM (2)‘s Weekly Departure (Counterflow Recordings, CDr), a post-rock/IDM project he co-founded with Matt Crum. The duo produced, wrote, and arranged the release together. ROM (2) released one further record: Foot Signal (2009, Romsound), with Lange credited for design and performance.

Under the alias Epstein (Discogs ID: 144548), Lange began releasing solo electronic work in the early-to-mid 2000s rooted in abstract hip-hop and downtempo. The debut Puñal (2004) appeared on two labels: Beta Bodega sublabel Botanica Del Jibaro (BDJ025) and RL66, and featured additional instrumentation from Matt Crum and Ryan Batch. The title is all-Spanish and all-lowercase — a signal of the bilingual, Latin-rooted aesthetic that would carry into his later work. Epstein continued with Jibaro Times FM: WLMF 4 (2005, Arepaz — another Beta Bodega sublabel), a compilation contribution credited to Epstein; Otros (2007, Arepaz), and the final Epstein LP Sealess See (2011, Asthmatic Kitty Records, AKR080), a 17-track vinyl record of abstract electronic miniatures with artwork by Kristi Sword and design by Paul Coors.

The Epstein Y El Conjunto project (Discogs ID: 1829865) expanded the group to six members — Lange, Matt Crum, Jason Trammell, Chris Devoe, Eduardo Alonso, and Ryan Rasheed — releasing Canto De Hermanos (2007, Arepaz/RL66).

During this period Lange also worked as a producer and collaborator on releases outside his own projects. He appears on Equal Vision Records alt-rock release Horizon (2002) in a photography credit, and co-produced ROM’s Weekly Departure on Counterflow. He also contributed recording assistance to Scott Herren’s To An Ambient Hollywood (2003, Hometapes) and produced Rice and Beans label release Confetti (2004). His connection to the Beta Bodega coalition — a Miami-based independent umbrella with sublabels including Botanica Del Jibaro, Arepaz, Bandeja Paisa, Rice and Beans, and RL66 — was central to his early distribution and release infrastructure.

Savath & Savalas (2000–2010)

Savath & Savalas (Discogs ID: 3838) is a side-project trio comprising Scott Herren (Guillermo Scott Herren, a.k.a. Prefuse 73), Catalonian singer Eva Puyuelo Muns, and Roberto Carlos Lange. Active from approximately 1998–1999. Herren is the primary creative engine; Lange performs alongside Puyuelo Muns as the ensemble body.

Helado Negro — Development and Asthmatic Kitty Years (2009–2016)

The Helado Negro alias (Discogs ID: 1577970) debuted in 2009 with Awe Owe (Asthmatic Kitty Records, AKR065). The CD release has sparse credits: Lange is listed as “Roberto Carlos Lange — Performer [Helado Negro Is],” establishing the formula that the alias and the person are identical. The album blends Afro-Cuban, bossanova, and IDM across 11 tracks, all sung in Spanish or wordless vocalization.

An associated collaborative album Believe You Me (2012, Asthmatic Kitty) was credited to OMBRE (4) — the project of Helado Negro and Julianna Barwick.

4AD and Mature Period (2021–2024)

Far In (2021, 4AD + Private Energy, 4AD0399LP) was Lange’s move to 4AD and his most commercially successful album. A double LP with 15 tracks, its credits on the formal Discogs release are sparse (manufacturing/mastering trail only), though the complete credits drawn from the “Roberto Carlos Lange” ID include Lange as engineer, producer, mixed by, and multi-instrumentalist (Steinway baby grand, Roland Juno 60, Rhodes 73 MK I, steel pan, OP-1/OP-Z, Wurlitzer 200a, Casio MT-40, Fender Stratocaster, Danelectro baritone, classical guitar, Moog Grandmother, MPC 2000 XL, vibraphone, marimba, autoharp, Korg Minilogue, drums, voice). Photography also by Lange.

Kite Symphony

Kite Symphony, Four Variations (2020, self-released via Not On Label — Roberto Carlos Lange) is a standalone self-released piece for which Lange is credited on all instruments: Moog Grandmother, field recording, pigeon flute (whistle), piano, Teenage Engineering OP-1, vibraphone, Rhodes, Wurlitzer, organ, and computer-processed sounds. Performed at Ballroom Marfa, with collaborators including Rob Mazurek and Rachel Alina among the listed performers.
