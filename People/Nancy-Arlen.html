Nancy Arlen

Drummer, sculptor. Born January 29, 1942. Died September 17, 2006 (following heart surgery). Discogs ID: 306710.

The direct human link between Mars and Dark Day — drummer in both. She was also one of the catalysts of Mars’s formation: she and Connie Burg met at a Lucinda Childs dance workshop and together brought in Mark Cunningham and Sumner Crane to form the band in late 1975.

Sculpture

Worked in cast polyester resin sculpture throughout the 1970s. Sourced materials from the plastic shops of Canal Street, Manhattan. Exhibited at Robert Steffanotti Gallery and the New Museum. Made a series of glass works in 1983. Per Cunningham, she was generally more interested in her visual art practice than in any renewed attention to Mars — she declined involvement in retrospective projects before her death.

Mars

Mars performed approximately 24 times, exclusively in Manhattan. First show: CBGB’s, January 1977. Last show: Max’s Kansas City, December 10, 1978 (the band had already effectively disbanded by that point).

Rebel Records / 3 E Single — How Mars Ended Up on a French Label

The chain from the September 1977 recording session to the March 1978 French release runs through Patti Smith Group members and a Paris entrepreneur:

  1. Lenny Kaye and Jay Dee Daugherty produced the session. Kaye (guitar, Patti Smith Group) and Daugherty (drums, Patti Smith Group) heard Mars play and arranged for them to record for Mer Records — the small independent label Patti Smith and Kaye had set up (best known for Smith’s 1974 debut “Hey Joe”/“Piss Factory”). The session took place in September 1977; Daugherty and engineer Brooke Delarco recorded and mixed the tracks under Kaye’s direction. (marsnowave.bandcamp.com; Wikipedia – Rebel Records (France))

  2. Mer Records shelved it when Patti Smith went on tour. The single was never released on Mer — Smith decided the group was too busy touring to run the label, and it was wound down. The master sat unreleased. (Wikipedia – Rebel Records (France))

  3. Kaye/Daugherty sold the master to Michel Esteban. Esteban was a Paris-based entrepreneur who had studied at the School of Visual Arts in New York (1974), become a friend of Patti Smith, and had connections into the CBGB-adjacent scene. He had opened the Paris rock shop “Harry Cover” (12 rue des Halles) in 1973 and founded Rebel Records above it in 1977 — its only other significant release was Marie et les Garçons. Kaye and Daugherty sold him the Mars master, and he released it in March 1978 as Rebel RB 7802. (Wikipedia – Michel Esteban; Wikipedia – Rebel Records (France))

  4. Rebel closed in 1978; Esteban co-founded ZE Records. Immediately after, Esteban co-founded ZE Records in New York with Michael Zilkha (1978–1984), home to James White, Kid Creole, Lydia Lunch, Suicide, and others. The 3E / 11,000 Volts tracks were subsequently reissued on ZE as a 12-inch. (Wikipedia – ZE Records)

Summary: The French label connection was essentially accidental — the recording was made for a New York label (Mer) with Patti Smith Group backing, but when Mer folded, Patti Smith’s Paris friend Michel Esteban acquired the master and put it out on his imprint before relocating to New York himself.
