Lois Maffeo

Discogs IDs: 321100 (as Lois Maffeo); 459516 (as Lois (3))
Active: c. 1990–2003 (recording); broader scene involvement earlier
Based: Washington DC (origins) → Olympia, WA
Genres: Indie rock, lo-fi, acoustic pop, punk

Singer-songwriter, guitarist, and vocalist at the heart of the early 1990s Olympia, WA underground scene. Released four solo LPs on K Records (as “Lois”) and one collaborative LP on Kill Rock Stars (co-credited with Brendan Canty). Also a member of Dub Narcotic Sound System and a handful of Olympia side projects.

Biography

Washington DC Origins and Olympia Arrival (late 1980s)

Lois Maffeo grew up on the East Coast and was involved in the Washington DC punk scene before relocating to Olympia, WA. Her earliest documented recording credit is the 1990 Courtney Love (2) 7” Uncrushworthy (K Records, IPU 12) — a release credited to a pre-fame, Olympia-era iteration of Courtney Love, on which Maffeo contributed uncredited guitar and vocals alongside drummer Pat Maley. The group is credited collectively as “Are Courtney Love” on the sleeve; the K catalogue number places it within Calvin Johnson’s International Pop Underground series.

This K Records 7” established Maffeo’s place in the Olympia underground well before her solo debut.

Solo Career on K Records — “Lois” (1992–1996)

Maffeo recorded and released her solo work under the single name “Lois” via K Records across a run of four LPs. The K catalogue treated them as records by the artist “Lois (3)” in Discogs disambiguation. Her sound across these records is lo-fi acoustic indie rock — fingerpicked guitar, melodica, spare arrangements, dry studio presentation typical of the K aesthetic.

Butterfly Kiss (1992, K Records — KLP 15)

Debut LP. Personnel included Stuart Moxham (bass, producer), Molly Neuman (drums), and Stuart Hallerman (engineer). Moxham — co-founder of the Welsh post-punk group Young Marble Giants — was an unusual transatlantic presence in the Olympia world; his production credit here is notable. Photography by Monica Gesue. Eleven tracks across two sides; styles tagged as Acoustic, Indie Rock, Lo-Fi.

Strumpet (1993, K Records — KLP 021)

Second LP. Produced by Calvin Johnson. Engineered again by Stuart Hallerman. Multiple bassists — Donna Dresch (Team Dresch) on several tracks, Stephen Immerwahr (Codeine) on others. Steve Peters contributed guitar on four tracks. Photography by Monica Gesue; design by Tae Won Yu. Eleven tracks; Rod Argent’s “The Way I Feel Inside” (from The Zombies) covered uncredited on B5.

Bet The Sky (1995, K Records — KLP 036)

Third LP. Co-produced by Maffeo and Calvin Johnson; engineered and produced by Stuart Hallerman. The production credit gives Maffeo more agency in the studio than on her earlier records. Heather Dunn on drums. Track A4 (“Transatlantic Telephone Call”) features a guest appearance by Brendan Canty (guitar) and John Goodmanson (bass) — the first pairing of Maffeo and Canty on record, five years before The Union Themes. Ten tracks; styles tagged Indie Pop, Indie Rock, Lo-Fi.

Infinity Plus (1996, K Records — KLP 058)

Fourth solo LP. Sparse Discogs credits — the three performers listed are Brendan Canty, Heather Dunn, and Lois Maffeo, all credited as “Featuring.” The release proper credits the artist as “Lois (3)” with featured performers; no separate producer or engineer credit in the DB. Ten tracks.

Snapshot Radio EP (1996, K Records — KLP 57)

A five-track CD EP released the same year as Infinity Plus. Maffeo contributed guitar, melodica, vocals, cover design, and photography. Each track featured a different set of collaborators, giving it a compilation-within-an-EP character:

  • Track 1 (“My Souvenir”): Brendan Canty (guitar), Heather Dunn (drums), Steve Wold / Seasick Steve (harmonica, recorded by), Alan Sparhawk (vocals), Calvin Johnson (recorded by), Steve Fisk (mixed by)
  • Track 2 (“Northern Soul”): Tim Rutili and Tim Hurley (Califone / Red Red Meat), Brian Deck, Ben Massarella, Damon Locks
  • Track 3 (“Tell Her”): Brendan Canty (guitar, organ), Heather Dunn (drums), Steve Fisk (mixed), Calvin Johnson and Steve Wold (recorded)
  • Track 4 (“N.O. Style”): Tim Rutili, Tim Hurley, Brian Deck, Ben Massarella
  • Track 5 (“Not Funny, Ha Ha”): Calvin Johnson and Lawrence Crane (mixed), James McNew (synthesizer), Josh Gennet (recorded)

Photography credits include Brendan Canty, Kurt Calloway, and Sarah Utter.

Dub Narcotic Sound System (mid-1990s)

Maffeo was a member of Dub Narcotic Sound System — Calvin Johnson’s Olympia-based dub/experimental collective. Her vocal credit appears on Boot Party (1996, K Records — KLP 040). The full DNSS lineup on that record: Todd Ranslow (bass), Larry Butler (drums), Lindiwe Coyne (guitar), Brian Weber (guitar/organ), Calvin Johnson (synthesizer, melodica, drums, vocals), Jen Smith (vocals), DJ Sayeed (scratches), Lois Maffeo (vocals).

The Union Themes (2000, Kill Rock Stars — KRS356)

Co-credited LP with Brendan Canty. This was Maffeo’s only release on Kill Rock Stars and her only album with another artist as equal co-primary. Canty played virtually every instrument: acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, piano, drums, mellotron — and produced and mixed the record. Maffeo contributed acoustic guitar, flute, vocals, and all songwriting. Pete Chramiec contributed acoustic and electric guitar on tracks 1, 6, and 8. Stuart Hallerman engineered and mixed alongside Canty. Roger Seibel mastered.

The album was a significant stylistic shift from the K lo-fi LPs: fuller arrangements, Canty’s polyinstrumentalism, mellotron textures, and Maffeo’s flute as a prominent element. Ten tracks; tagged Indie Rock, Pop Rock. BMI rights society.

Owl & The Pussycat (2003, Kill Rock Stars — KRS 390)

Maffeo co-wrote and sang on the LP Owl & The Pussycat (2003), credited to the group Owl & The Pussycat (Discogs ID 2365230). Her co-writer and co-vocalist was Greg Moore. The record was engineered/recorded in Vancouver by David Carswell and John Collins (The New Pornographers / Mint Records circle); mastered by John Golden; design by Tae Won Yu. Thirteen tracks; styles tagged Indie Rock.
