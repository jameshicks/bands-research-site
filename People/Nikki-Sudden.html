Nikki Sudden

Nikki Sudden

Oct 19, 202613 min read

Nikki Sudden

Discogs ID: 263346
Real name: Adrian Nicholas Godfrey
Born: 19 July 1956, London, England
Died: 26 March 2006, New York, USA
Aliases: Adrian Godfrey, Edrun Kübelwagen, Nikki Mapp, Nikki Mattress, Tudor Vernon

English singer-songwriter and guitarist. Co-founder of Swell Maps with his brother Epic Soundtracks (Kevin Godfrey). Prolific solo artist from 1981 onwards; co-founder and focal figure of The Jacobites with Dave Kusworth. Died of a heart attack during a US solo tour in March 2006, on the eve of a scheduled Jacobites European tour.

Era 1 — Swell Maps (1977–1980)

Sudden co-founded Swell Maps in Birmingham with his brother Epic Soundtracks (Kevin Godfrey), initially under the name Rather (later the name of their self-run label). The band released four singles and two albums on Rough Trade between 1977 and 1980; all hit number one on the UK Independent Charts. Sudden was principal lyricist and lead guitarist. When the band dissolved in 1980, Sudden retained the Rather Records imprint for his early solo work.

Swell Maps are credited in Sudden’s own Discogs bio as formative influences on US DIY/post-punk, specifically cited by Sonic Youth.

Key releases (as contributor):

  • A Trip To Marineville (1979, Rough Trade) — co-writer, inner cover
  • …In “Jane From Occupied Europe” (1981, Rough Trade) — bass, guitar, vocals, organ, piano
  • Collision Time (1982, Rough Trade) — posthumous compilation

Era 2 — Early Solo Work (1981–1983)

After Swell Maps dissolved, Sudden issued three solo albums in quick succession through independent labels, largely self-produced at John A. Rivers’s Woodbine Street Studios. His debut single Back To The Start (1981, Rather Records) was followed by the first proper solo albums.

Key personnel across this period:

  • John A. Rivers — engineer and co-producer (all three albums)
  • Anthony Thistlethwaite — saxophone (later of The Waterboys)
  • Mike Scott — co-producer on two tracks of The Bible Belt
  • Lizard (6) (Max Edie) — backing vocals, multiple albums
  • Phones B. Sportsman (David Barrington) — bass, guitar, co-producer (Waiting On Egypt)
  • Dave Kusworth — guitar (The Bible Belt, A1, A2, A5, B1–B3, B5)
  • Kevin Wilkinson — drums (The Bible Belt)

Era 3 — The Jacobites (1983–1986, reformed 1993–1998)

First Run (1983–1986)

Sudden and Dave Kusworth formalised their partnership as The Jacobites in 1983, initially releasing on Glass Records (2). Kusworth brought a glam-inflected rock sensibility to complement Sudden’s more literary, folk-punk approach. The band’s second album Robespierre’s Velvet Basement (1985) reached number one on the German Independent Charts and led to a German tour; Sudden subsequently relocated to Germany for a period.

Jacobites core lineup:

  • Nikki Sudden — vocals, guitar, organ, synthesizer
  • Dave Kusworth — vocals, guitar, bass
  • Epic Soundtracks — drums, percussion, piano (Robespierre’s Velvet Basement)
  • Mark Lemon — bass, guitar, tambourine, backing vocals (Robespierre’s Velvet Basement)
  • Andy Wickett — keyboards, harmonica, backing vocals (Robespierre’s Velvet Basement)
  • Bob Lamb — percussion, producer, recorded/mixed (Robespierre’s Velvet Basement)
  • Tyla — slide guitar (Robespierre’s Velvet Basement)

Era 4 — Creation Records Solo Period (1986–1990)

Signing and relationship with Alan McGee

When the Jacobites dissolved in 1986, Sudden moved from Glass Records (2) to Creation Records, the independent label founded in 1983 by Alan McGee, Dick Green, and Joe Foster. The transition is confirmed by October 1986 — Texas (CRELP 012) was released that month — but no publicly available source documents precisely how the signing came about or what personal contact, if any, preceded it. McGee ran Creation on an ethos of providing “an outlet for like-minded musicians” rather than systematic A&R scouting, and it is probable the signing grew out of the overlapping post-punk / indie underground that connected Glass Records artists to the Creation orbit, but this is inference rather than confirmed fact. (Wikipedia: Creation Records, as of 2026-09-28)

One contemporary observer — writing a fan biography — noted that after the signing, “Nikki’s now with Alan McGee’s Creation Records, where he’s maintained his now near legendary levels of productivity, whilst simultaneously languishing somewhat, as McGee, to my mind misguidedly, directs his more industrious efforts on the likes of, well, we’ll go into that shortly.” The passage implies Sudden was not a priority signing for McGee even though the label relationship was active. (whatanicewaytoturn17.blogspot.com)

A separate account of the concurrent 1987 sessions at Dave Pegg’s Woodworm studio (which produced Dead Men Tell No Tales and Kiss You Kidnapped Charabanc) specifies that both albums “were financed by Creation Records — purse strings of same being controlled by Yvonne, the then Mrs. McGee,” suggesting that day-to-day label administration for Sudden’s recordings during this period was handled by McGee’s wife Yvonne rather than McGee himself. (outsideleft.com — “I Was Buffalo Bill” by Jeremy Gluck) Sudden himself commented on the multi-label funding situation for those concurrent sessions: “It’s a great way to confuse record companies — no one knew who was paying for what.” (ibid.)

The Wikipedia article on Glass Records confirms a broader pattern: it explicitly names both The Jazz Butcher and Nikki Sudden & The Jacobites as Glass Records artists who later moved to Creation Records, suggesting the Glass–Creation pipeline was a recognisable feature of the mid-1980s indie landscape rather than an isolated move by Sudden. (Wikipedia: Glass Records, as of 2026-09-28)

A broader survey of Creation’s mid-1980s signing pattern supports the same reading. An overview of the label’s history groups Sudden explicitly with a cohort of similarly-positioned artists — Felt, The Jazz Butcher, Momus, and Ed Ball (The Times) — as artists from “like-minded labels and scenes” who joined Creation during this period, rather than as a special or targeted signing by McGee. No source positions Sudden as someone McGee actively pursued or sought out personally. (theshfl.com — Creation Records guide)

On the question of how the Creation relationship ended: Sudden’s posthumous autobiography The Last Bandit: A Rock’n’Roll Life (published 2014; written in the final years of his life) contains a passage in which Sudden reflects that despite McGee’s stated willingness to continue releasing his albums, Sudden himself decided it was time to move on — “despite Alan’s protestations that he was willing to release my albums forever, it was probably time for me to be moving on.” This places the departure in 1990 as Sudden’s own decision, not a label drop. The quote surfaced in search results attributed to the autobiography but the specific passage has not been verified against the physical text; treat as provisionally sourced. (Goodreads: The Last Bandit — search snippet, not confirmed from full text)

No direct McGee quote about Sudden and no named interview where either party discusses the signing have surfaced in any publicly available source searched as of September 2026. The Creation Records official site history (creation-records.com/history/) does not mention Sudden at all. The official Creation Records discography page lists only CRE 033 (“Jangle Town”) and CRE 040 (“Wedding Hotel”, with Rowland Howard) as Sudden’s singles in their catalog (creation-records.com/discography/).

Creation Records output

Sudden released five records on Creation across 1986–1990, with a sixth compilation. His first Creation album, Texas (October 1986, CRELP 012), is widely considered his solo masterwork; it was recorded June 16–July 4, 1986. He also contributed “Kiss at Dawn” to the 1987 Creation compilation Doing God’s Work, alongside Biff Bang Pow!, Momus, The House of Love, and The Jasmine Minks. (Wikipedia: Doing God’s Work)

Key personnel:

  • John A. Rivers — engineer/producer throughout
  • Tim Matyear — engineer (Dead Men Tell No Tales, Kiss You Kidnapped Charabanc)
  • Rowland S. Howard — co-primary artist, co-producer (Kiss You Kidnapped Charabanc)
  • The French Revolution (2) — co-primary on Groove (1989)
  • Günter Pauler — lacquer cut/engineer (Dead Men Tell No Tales, Kiss You Kidnapped Charabanc, Groove)

Era 5 — The Jewel Thief / Athens, Georgia Sessions (1990–1991)

In 1990, Sudden recorded in Athens, Georgia with members of R.E.M.: Peter Buck (producer), Mike Mills, and Bill Berry (2). The resulting album was released as The Jewel Thief (1991, UFO Records) and later reissued as Liquor, Guns & Ammo. Engineer: John Keane.

Era 6 — Glitterhouse and Late Career (1995–2006)

After the Jacobites’ second dissolution, Sudden continued solo with Glitterhouse Records (Germany) and later Secretly Canadian (US). His final decade saw extensive touring — approximately 100 shows per year — with his backing band The Last Bandits (Discogs artist ID: 1693427).

The Last Bandits — confirmed Discogs lineup:

  • John C. Barry (ID: 2654518) — bass, handclaps, backing vocals
  • Stéphane Doucerain (ID: 3382280) — drums, percussion
  • Johnny Connors (ID: 602081; real name John Connor, IRL; also in The Golden Horde and Messyheads) — listed in Discogs group membership but no release-level instrument credits confirmed

Barry and Doucerain are confirmed by credits on Treasure Island (2004, Secretly Canadian pressing credited to “Nikki Sudden & The Last Bandits”) and Live In Moscow, Central House Of Artists (2004, same co-primary credit). Connors’ specific role and instrument remain unconfirmed in Discogs.

Treasure Island (2004, Secretly Canadian) featured a particularly star-studded guest roster:

  • Ian McLagan (Small Faces / Faces) — organ
  • Mick Taylor (Rolling Stones) — guitar
  • Anthony Thistlethwaite (The Waterboys) — mandolin
  • Dave Kusworth — guitar
  • Darrell Bath (Dogs D’Amour) — guitar
  • John A. Rivers — recorded/mixed
