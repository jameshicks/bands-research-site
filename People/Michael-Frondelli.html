Michael Frondelli

Recording engineer, mixer, and producer. Discogs ID: 73358. Known name variations on records include Mike Frondelli, M. Frondelli, Michael Frandelli, Michael Frondoli, and Michel Frondelli.

Shure Artists & Engineers page: http://www.shure.com/americas/artists/engineers/mike-frondelli

Overview

Michael Frondelli is a New York-based recording engineer whose career spans the mid-1970s through the 2010s. He began as an assistant engineer in the mid-1970s at Electric Lady Studios in New York, accumulating credits on a wide range of jazz-funk, soul, and rock sessions for labels including Blue Note, CTI, Polydor, A&M, Buddah, and Atlantic. By the late 1970s he had moved into a full engineer role, most notably on Chic’s debut self-titled LP (1977, Atlantic) alongside Bob Clearmountain — one of the earliest recordings by the disco-funk duo of Nile Rodgers and Bernard Edwards.

His most celebrated early work came in the ZE Records orbit: he served as Chief Engineer on Kid Creole And The Coconuts’s Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places (1981, Island/ZE) and Tropical Gangsters (1982, ZE) — the two LPs that established the group’s tropical/Latin-tinged new wave sound under August Darnell’s direction. On Tropical Gangsters he shared the Chief Engineer credit with Bob Blank. He also served as recording engineer on Klaus Nomi’s self-titled debut (1981, RCA/RCA Victor), engineered the Kid Creole singles Christmas In B’Dilli Bay (1982) and Dear Addy / No Fish Today (1982), and handled the soundtrack album for the French poet/musician Poèmes Rock on Island (1981).

From the early 1980s onward Frondelli’s remit expanded: he moved into mixing, co-producing, and eventually full production. He engineered Billy Idol’s Rebel Yell (1983, Chrysalis) with producer Keith Forsey and mixed the Crowded House debut (1986, Capitol) produced by Mitchell Froom. He contributed engineering to The Breakfast Club OST (1985, A&M), Joe Jackson’s Big World (1986, A&M), and mixed Pat Benatar’s Seven The Hard Way (1985, Chrysalis). He co-produced several tracks credited to the Waitresses (Polydor, 1982–1984) and produced the debut by Funkapolitan (Pavillion, 1982).

By the late 1980s and 1990s Frondelli was active as a full producer across AOR, rock, and country-adjacent projects, working with Michael Bolton, Poison (remix), Henry Lee Summer (CBS Associated), and others. Later credits include mixing Brian Wilson / Van Dyke Parks’s Orange Crate Art (1995, Warner Bros.) and recording strings on Rufus Wainwright’s debut (1998, DreamWorks).
