Michael Karoli

Oct 19, 20267 min read

Michael Karoli

Guitarist and co-founder of Can. Born 29 April 1948, Straubing, Germany. Died 17 November 2001, Essen, Germany, age 53. Discogs artist ID: 120164.

Biography

Michael Karoli was the youngest member of Can when the group formed in Cologne in 1968, reportedly still a teenager when he began playing with Holger Czukay — who had been his guitar teacher. That teacher-student relationship connecting Czukay to the teenage Karoli is one of the odder founding facts of the Cologne scene: a bassist/composer trained under Karlheinz Stockhausen introducing his young guitar student into what would become one of the most influential experimental rock groups in history.

Karoli’s background was as a rock guitarist rather than a conservatory musician, which gave Can’s sound much of its propulsive, blues-inflected energy alongside the more academic frameworks that Czukay and Irmin Schmidt brought from their classical and avant-garde training. He is credited on Discogs under a range of name variations — Caroli, Karoll, M. Karoli, Michel Karoli, Miki, Mischa — reflecting the various pressings and territories in which Can’s records appeared.

Can: The Full Run (1968–1978)

Karoli played on every major Can studio album from the debut through the group’s dissolution.

Monster Movie (1969, Music Factory) — Can’s debut, recorded as “The Can.” Karoli is credited as “Guitar [Sonar & Radared Guitar Pilot]” — one of the whimsical role descriptions the band gave themselves on that record. The lineup was the original five: Czukay (bass), Schmidt (organ), Jaki Liebezeit (drums), Karoli (guitar), and Malcolm Mooney (vocals).

Soundtracks (1970, Liberty) — The transitional album, mixing tracks with Mooney and early sessions with Damo Suzuki, who Karoli and Czukay had discovered busking in Munich. Karoli: guitar.

Tago Mago (1971, United Artists Records) — Double album widely considered Can’s peak. Lineup: Czukay (bass), Liebezeit (drums), Karoli (guitar), Schmidt (organ, electric piano), Suzuki (vocals). Self-produced and arranged.

Ege Bamyasi (1972, United Artists Records) — Karoli’s most instrumentally diverse credit in the Can catalog: guitar, twelve-string guitar, acoustic guitar, and shenai (a North Indian double-reed wind instrument). Schmidt also played violone and steel guitar on this record, the band pushing well past standard rock instrumentation.

Future Days (1973, United Artists Records) — Damo Suzuki’s final album with Can. Karoli: guitar. Recording credits shared between Czukay, Chris Sladdin, and Volker Liedtke.

Soon Over Babaluma (1974, United Artists Records) — First Can album without a conventional lead vocalist; the group shared vocal duties. Karoli contributed guitar, electric violin (credited on tracks A1–A3), violin (A1), and backing vocals (A2) and lead vocals (A1, B1, B2). This was the record where his range as a multi-instrumentalist became most explicit in the credits.

Landed (1975, Virgin) — Guitar, violin, vocals. First Can LP on Virgin after leaving United Artists.

Flow Motion (1976, EMI Electrola / Harvest) — Karoli’s only credit here is Photography By; this was the album that produced Can’s only UK chart single (“I Want More”). The band’s direction was shifting toward a more accessible sound, and Czukay was increasingly sidelined.

Saw Delight (1977, EMI Electrola / Harvest) — Guitar, electric violin (credited as “E-violine”), vocals. New members Rosko Gee (bass, vocals) and Rebop Kwaku Baah (percussion, vocals) had been brought in from Traffic, fundamentally altering the group’s dynamic.

Can (1978, EMI Electrola / Harvest) — The final studio album under the Can name during this original run. Karoli: guitar, vocals, and co-written credits alongside Liebezeit and Schmidt.

Unlimited Edition (1976, Caroline Records) — Outtakes and unreleased material compiled during the active period. Guitar credit.

Delay 1968 (1981, Spoon Records) — Archive release of very early recordings predating Monster Movie. Karoli: guitar, written-by.

Post-Can Solo Work

After Can’s dissolution Karoli remained active but rarely as a primary artist under his own name.

Deluge (1984, Spoon Records) — His most substantial solo statement. A duo record co-credited to Karoli and vocalist Polly Eltes. Karoli played all instruments, wrote and arranged all tracks, produced, and engineered. Czukay contributed additional master processing. The record was issued on Spoon Records — Can’s own archival label — suggesting ongoing ties to the Can infrastructure. This is the only release in Discogs where Karoli appears as a primary artist.

The Can Reunion: Rite Time (1989)

Rite Time (1989, Mercury) — Can’s one proper reunion album, bringing back Malcolm Mooney as vocalist. Karoli’s credit is notably expansive: chorus, organ (pocket organ), bass, guitar, producer, mixed by. He and Czukay shared production and mixing duties. This was a much fuller creative role than his standard guitar credit across the original Can run.

Later Can Archival Work

Karoli remained involved in Can’s archive activities through the 1990s. On the Can Box (1998, Spoon Records) — the comprehensive box set — he is credited as compiled by, engineer (soundprocessing), mastered by, composed by, and performer, indicating hands-on curatorial work on the Can legacy in the years before his death. He was also credited as engineer on the Villa Wunderbar compilation (2013) and other late-period archival releases, though these were assembled posthumously.

The live releases that have emerged since his death — Live In Stuttgart 1975, Live In Brighton 1975 (both 2021), Live In Cuxhaven 1976 (2022), Live In Paris 1973, Live In Keele 1977 (both 2024) — all document Can performances from the 1970s with Karoli as guitarist.

Sofortkontakt!

Karoli was also a member of Sofortkontakt!, a group whose Discogs entry lists members including Mark Spybey and three others (Alexander Schönert, Felix A. Gutierrez, Thomas Hopf). Discogs has no primary releases under that name, suggesting it was either a live project or released under other configurations.

Songwriting Legacy and Samples

Karoli’s compositions continued to circulate after Can’s original run:

  • “I Want More” (co-written) has been reissued across multiple compilations and reworked on the Wax Trax! label (1987) and Finiflex (1993).
  • The Jesus and Mary Chain covered or interpolated Can material — “April Skies” (1987, Blanco Y Negro) carries a Karoli written-by credit.
  • Sacrilege (1997, Mute) — a remix/reinterpretation album — lists Karoli as written-by on tracks that drew from Can’s catalog.
  • A Tribe Called Quest’s We Got It From Here… Thank You 4 Your Service (2016) carries a Karoli written-by credit, as does The Killers’ Imploding the Mirage (2020) and Snow Patrol’s Wildness (2018) — all presumably sampling Can material.
  • Ry Cooder’s Gormenghast soundtrack (2000, Mute/Spoon) credits Karoli for “pre-recorded” guitar.
