Kommissar Hjuler

Real name: Detlev Hjuler Born: 1967, Flensburg, Germany Discogs artist ID: 413164 Genres: Experimental, Sound Art, Noise, Fluxus, Mail-Art, Avant-Garde Primary labels: Der Schöne Hjuler-Memorial-Fond, Psych.KG, Malmlund Records, Domestic Violence Recordings, Feeding Tube Records

Overview

Kommissar Hjuler (born Detlev Hjuler, 1967) is a sound recordist, visual artist, and mail-art figure based in Flensburg, Germany. He is one of the most prolific artists in the international cassette, vinyl, and mail-art network, with well over 800 primary-credited release entries on Discogs spanning from 2000 into the 2020s, the vast majority in limited handmade editions.

His career began in sound in 1999 and visual art in 2006. Before his artistic life, he served as a police officer from 1983 to 2013 — a biographical fact that inflects many of his project names and pseudonyms (Kommissar, Polizeileutnant, Polizei-Diensthund, etc.). Almost all of his work is made in Flensburg, typically in close collaboration with his wife Mama Bär (Andrea Katharina Ingeburg Hjuler, née Göthling), with whom he records primarily as Kommissar Hjuler Und Frau (Discogs ID 526739) and Kommissar Hjuler & Mama Baer (Discogs ID 2058684).

The couple operate two key imprints out of their home address (Weizenstieg 11, Flensburg): Der Schöne Hjuler-Memorial-Fond (their self-release label, with handmade limited editions) and co-run Psych.KG (a broader mail-art/sound-art label that is his primary vehicle for collaborative releases with external artists). The scale is extraordinary: Psych.KG alone has 859 releases on Discogs, with Hjuler and Mama Bär as the top-billed artists (274 and 172 releases respectively on that label alone).

Labels

Der Schöne Hjuler-Memorial-Fond (Discogs ID: 63362)

Hjuler’s own label, run from the Flensburg address. Described in its Discogs profile as “mainly self-releases in very limited editions with handmade artworks.” Catalog numbers for official releases start with SHMF-; items given informally to friends are catalogued as SHMF-none. It has 931 releases on Discogs and four sublabels (Bayerischer-Staatsstreich-Schallfolien AG, Der Schoene-Hjuler-Memorial-Fond, Edition Kepler-452b, MILF-Kuh Productions). The label website is at asylum-lunaticum.de.

Psych.KG (Discogs ID: 127571)

German label with 859 Discogs releases. Contact information lists both Matthias Horn (c/o Kleine Hoehle 12, Euskirchen) and Kommissar Hjuler (Flensburg). Parent label: Multi National Disaster Records (ID 17801). Sublabels include Dych.KG, E-Klageto, Jack’s’Quokka, Nagrania Brutalnie Domowy, and la bois Records. Psych.KG functions as the principal vehicle for Hjuler’s collaborative releases with outside artists — it is simultaneously his home and a broad network label, with Family Fodder (42 releases), The New Blockaders (33), Steve Dalachinsky (29), Franz Kamin (28), and many others appearing in substantial quantity.

Scale and Prolificacy

The scope of Hjuler’s output defies standard discographic summary:

  • 848 primary-credited release entries on Discogs under his own name (Kommissar Hjuler, ID 413164 alone)
  • Mama Bär has 172 primary credits on Psych.KG alone, and their joint projects (Kommissar Hjuler Und Frau, Kommissar Hjuler & Mama Baer) add hundreds more
  • Active continuously from at least 2000 to 2025 (the Discogs record extends across this full span)
  • Many releases are handmade vinyl editions in single-digit or very small-number runs
  • The releases span dozens of countries, reflecting the international mail-art network model: Poland (Nagrania Brutalnie Domowy), UK (Heart Fine Art), Sweden (Obskyr Records, Malmlund Records, Hate Poem Self-Released Split Series), Finland (Malmlund Records), Greece (Shamanic Trance), Russia (Cack Tapes), Italy (Ultramarine, Ricerca Sonora), Ukraine, France, Austria, and more

His approach is essentially that of Fluxus and mail-art practice applied to the vinyl/cassette medium: constant output, international postal exchange, collaboration as primary mode of production, and the limited handmade object as the art form.

Family Fodder Collaborations

From approximately 2014 onward, Family Fodder became one of Hjuler’s most sustained external collaborative partners, with 24 shared releases and 42 Family Fodder releases appearing on Psych.KG. The collaboration connects Hjuler’s Fluxus/sound-art practice to Family Fodder’s UK post-punk/experimental tradition.

Other Significant Release Contexts

Early output (2000–2008): All through Der Schöne Hjuler-Memorial-Fond, with titles referencing literary figures (Albert Vigoleis Thelen, Will Quadflieg, Forvandlingen af Franz Kafka) and abstract sound works. Debut Discogs-listed release: Fuer Die Perlen (2000, SHMF).

Feeding Tube Records (US): Amerikanische Poesie Und Alkoholismus (2009) — one of his few US independent label appearances.

Intransitive Recordings (US): Asylum Lunaticum (2009) — credited jointly with Mama Bär; the Asylum Lunaticum name recurs as the Bandcamp/website name for their joint operation.

Malmlund Records (Finland): A significant run from 2017–2021, with titles largely named after pornographic performers (Lea Martini, Rebecca Volpetti, Arwen Gold, Jessi Palmer, and many others) — a recurring Hjuler conceptual strategy that uses the collision of explicit titling with sound-art content.

Obskyr Records (Sweden): Karawane #1 & #2 (2017) — Hjuler is credited with Artwork for Side B, and as primary artist; the “Karawane” title is a reference to Hugo Ball’s Dada sound poem.

Edition Amt Eisenberg (Austria): Zusammenarbeit In Bild Und Ton: Assemblage & 2 Musikstücke (2021), Sitzplatzzwang Und Anschnallpflicht +/- (2022).

Goodiepal connection: Goodiepal (Kristian Vester, ID 612) is based in the same general area (Flensburg/Denmark border region) and appears in 19 shared releases, including through the joint project GP&PLS (Goodiepal & Pals, ID 6749625). This is one of the geographically proximate relationships in an otherwise predominantly postal/mail-based network.

Conceptual Orientation

Hjuler’s practice sits at the intersection of several traditions:

  • Fluxus: The “Fluxus” title appears across dozens of releases (Fluxus In The Kitchen, FLUXUS Villach, FLUX!Knast-Mix, Fluxporn-Forvandlingen af Franz Kafka, etc.), positioning the work within the tradition of event scores, process-based art, and the dematerialized art object.
  • Mail art / cassette culture: The Psych.KG / SHMF model operates on the same postal-exchange logic as the international mail-art network of the 1970s–80s, with Hjuler functioning as a hub connecting European, American, Asian, and Australian practitioners.
  • NO!art: The NO!art Performance project (ID 8887903) connects to the New York-based NO!art movement (Boris Lurie, Dietmar Kirves) — both of whom appear as Psych.KG collaborators.
  • Sound poetry / Dada: Repeated references to Hugo Ball’s “Karawane,” Franz Kafka, and other Dada/literary sources in titles. “Nein-Sonate” echoes Schwitters’ “Ursonate.”
  • Police officer biography: Many project names incorporate police vocabulary (Kommissar, Polizeileutnant, Polizei-Diensthund), a self-ironic deployment of his pre-artistic career.
