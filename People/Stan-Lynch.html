Stan Lynch

Born: 21 May 1955 in Cincinnati, Ohio, USA. Drummer, songwriter, and record producer. Discogs artist ID: 292784

Best known as founding member and original drummer of Tom Petty And The Heartbreakers, a role he held for eighteen years (1975–1994). After leaving the band he built a second career as a songwriter and producer, working extensively with Don Henley and others in the mainstream rock world. Active into the 2020s as a multi-instrumentalist and producer.

Early Career and Tom Petty & The Heartbreakers (1975–1994)

Lynch joined Tom Petty’s band in Gainesville, Florida in 1975, becoming the founding drummer of what would be billed as Tom Petty And The Heartbreakers (Discogs artist ID: 269534). He remained through every studio album from the self-titled debut (1976) to Into the Great Wide Open (1991) and the subsequent Greatest Hits (1993), providing drums and backing vocals throughout.

The band’s core lineup alongside Lynch included Tom Petty (vocals, guitar), Mike Campbell (guitar), Benmont Tench (keyboards), Ron Blair (bass, later replaced by Howie Epstein), and Phil Jones (percussion). Lynch’s tenure covered the band’s commercial peak: Damn the Torpedoes (1979), Hard Promises (1981), Long After Dark (1982), Southern Accents (1985), Let Me Up (I’ve Had Enough) (1987), and Into the Great Wide Open (1991). All principal Heartbreakers records were produced with Jimmy Iovine (first three albums) and later various collaborators including Tom Petty himself.

Lynch departed in 1994 and was replaced by Steve Ferrone.

Session Work (1979–1998)

Alongside his Heartbreakers duties Lynch appeared as a session drummer on a significant number of outside records across the 1980s and 1990s.

Rank & File — Long Gone Dead (1984)

Produced by Jeff Eyrich and released on Slash Records, this is Lynch’s most-noted outside session of the early period. He played drums and percussion on the Kinman brothers’ cowpunk record. Other contributors included Richard Greene (fiddle), Pete Grant (pedal steel, slide guitar, autoharp, banjo), and vocalists / instrumentalists Chip Kinman and Tony Kinman. The Kinmans wrote all the material.

Stevie Nicks — Bella Donna (1981)

Lynch played drums on the Fleetwood Mac singer’s debut solo album, alongside a large cast of Heartbreakers-adjacent players: Mike Campbell (guitar), Tom Petty (guitar), Benmont Tench (organ, piano), Phil Jones (percussion), and Jimmy Iovine as producer. Stevie Nicks is credited as lead vocalist and primary songwriter.

Don Henley — The End of the Innocence (1989)

Lynch co-produced two tracks (A4 and B3) alongside John Corey on Henley’s fourth solo album. The album’s production was otherwise handled by Danny Kortchmar, Greg Ladanyi, Bruce Hornsby, Mike Campbell, and Henley himself. Shelly Yakus engineered.

Don Henley — Building the Perfect Beast (1984)

Lynch contributed a songwriting credit to “All She Wants to Do Is Dance” (track B5). Mike Campbell and Benmont Tench also have songwriting credits on this album. Produced by Danny Kortchmar, Greg Ladanyi, and Don Henley.

Don Henley — Actual Miles: Henley’s Greatest Hits (1995)

Lynch played drums and produced tracks 7, 11, 12, and 13. Mike Campbell and Benmont Tench also appear as musicians, as do Jeff Porcaro, Vinnie Colaiuta, Bruce Hornsby, and Timothy B. Schmit.

Eagles — Hell Freezes Over (1994)

Lynch played percussion on this live reunion album for Geffen Records. The recording captures the Eagles’ 1994 reunion concert.

Don Henley — Cass County (2015)

Lynch co-produced this country-inflected solo album with Don Henley, released on Capitol Records. One of his most substantial post-Heartbreakers production credits.

Post-Heartbreakers Production Work (1989–2020)

From the late 1980s Lynch increasingly worked as a songwriter and producer for mainstream rock and country artists.

  • Don Henley, “The Last Worthless Evening” (1989, Geffen) — producer and co-writer.
  • Don Henley, “The Garden of Allah” (1995, Geffen) — producer.
  • Don Henley, “Learn to Be Still” (1995, Geffen) — producer.
  • Don Henley, “You Don’t Know Me at All” (1996, Geffen) — producer and co-writer.
  • Don Henley, Cass County (2015, Capitol) — co-producer (full album).
  • Inside Job (Don Henley, Warner Bros., 2000) — producer.
  • Everything Is Different Now (Warner Bros., 2000) — producer.
  • Taking You Home (Warner Bros., 2000) — producer and co-writer.
  • They’re Not Here, They’re Not Coming (Warner Bros., 2000) — producer and co-writer.
  • All The King’s Men (Sweetfish Records, 1997) — producer.
  • Notes From the Underground (Last Resort Records, 2004) — drums, percussion, listed as “High Mojo.”

Later Bands and Projects

The Harshed Mellows

Loose supergroup featuring Lynch alongside Benmont Tench, Mike Campbell, Dan Baird, Brendan O’Brien, Mauro Magellan, and Michelle Malone. Discogs artist ID: 946048. No formal album release documented in Discogs.

The Speaker Wars

Duo project with Jon Christopher Davis. Released The Speaker Wars (2025, Frontiers Music SRL) — Lynch plays drums, percussion, vocals, guitar, bass, keyboards, harmonica; also credited as engineer and co-writer. Discogs artist ID: 14224492.
