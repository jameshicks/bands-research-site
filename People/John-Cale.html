John Cale

Multi-instrumentalist, composer, producer. Born John Davies Cale, 9 March 1942, Garnant, Wales. Discogs artist ID: 66358.

Co-founder of The Velvet Underground with Lou Reed. Pioneer of drone-based minimalism through his work with La Monte Young’s Theatre Of Eternal Music. Prolific producer of downtown NYC acts in the late 1970s and 1980s: produced Model Citizens (3)‘s Shift The Blame EP (Spy Records, 1979) and mixed Lester Bangs’s “Let It Blurt” / “Live” single (SPY 003, 1979) — reportedly after losing out to Brian Eno on the chance to produce Television. Also produced Patti Smith’s Horses (1975), The Stooges’ The Stooges (1969), and many others.

VU Role

In the Velvet Underground, Cale played electric viola, bass guitar, organ, and piano, and sang vocals. His drone-instrument training from Theatre Of Eternal Music shaped the band’s avant-garde edge. He departed the band in September 1968 — fired, not voluntary: Reed gave Morrison and Tucker an ultimatum (fire Cale or he quits); they reluctantly complied. Reed cited Cale’s increasingly extreme ideas (e.g. recording with amplifiers underwater) and a desire to make the band more accessible. Final gig at the Boston Tea Party. Replaced by Doug Yule — per Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cale). His final VU album was White Light/White Heat (1968, Verve Records).

Cale was central to the band’s drone and noise aesthetic; his departure coincided with a softer, more introspective direction on the 1969 self-titled album.

Solo / Production

Three studio albums on ZE Records: Music for a New Society (1982), Caribbean Sunset (1983), Comes Alive (1984). He was also connected to ZE Records as the original SPY Records link that introduced Michel Esteban to Michael Zilkha — ZE’s first release was Cristina’s “Disco Clone” (12 ZE 001, 1978). Cale produced the original version, which was pressed in a limited run of approximately 1,500 copies with male vocals by Anthony Haden-Guest (English and French versions). A subsequent re-recorded version — co-produced by Zilkha with engineering by Bob Blank, and male vocals by Kevin Kline — became the more widely distributed pressing; Cale is not credited on that re-recording. The creative friction is characterised by Zilkha as: “Cristina wanted something Brechtian and John wanted Monster Mash.” The broader SPY/ZE split is attributed by Michel Esteban to SPY being “John’s baby” where Cale wanted to produce every artist himself — see Cristina for full account. [Source: Wikipedia “Disco Clone” https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Clone; ZE Records site https://www.zerecords.com/release/135/disco-clone.html; post-punk.com Zilkha interview https://post-punk.com/ze-records-revived-with-the-future-is-mine-interview-with-legendary-producers-michael-zilkha-and-john-robie/]

Reunited with Reed, Morrison, and Tucker for 1993 European tour → Live MCMXCIII (Sire/Warner Bros.).
