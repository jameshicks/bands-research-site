Biography

Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson is an Icelandic trumpet player, improviser, composer, and arranger born in Reykjavík. He has been based in Utrecht, the Netherlands for an extended period, positioning himself at an intersection between the Icelandic indie and experimental scene and European contemporary/improvised music circles.

Reykjavík roots and múm (late 1990s–2007)

Eiríkur’s earliest traceable Discogs credit is on múm’s debut Yesterday Was Dramatic - Today Is OK (TMT Entertainment, 1999/2000), where he plays trumpet on the central long-form piece “Awake On A Train.” He became a de facto core member of múm — not formally listed among the band’s five official members (the Valtýsdóttir twins, Örvar Þóreyjarson Smárason, Gunnar Örn Tynes, and later Sigurlaug Gísladóttir) — yet shared 31 releases with the band across their entire FatCat and Morr Music periods. On Finally We Are No One (FatCat, 2002) he is credited as trumpet player alongside Valgeir Sigurðsson (producer/mixer) and Samuli Kosminen. On Summer Make Good (FatCat, 2004) his credit expanded considerably: trumpet, piano [pianette], synthesizer, and whistling — reflecting his broader role in the band’s live and studio work. On Go Go Smear The Poison Ivy (FatCat, 2007) he is credited as musician, songwriter (tracks 8 and 10), and arranger of strings — an expansion into compositional territory within the múm frame.

During the same period he was also performing and recording with several other Reykjavík acts. He appears on the Kippi Kaninus album Huggun (Kitchen Motors / ÓMI, 2002) and contributed trumpet to Benni Hemm Hemm’s records from the mid-2000s onward.

In 2004 he appeared on Borko’s Dreams Never Die (FatCat) on trumpet and handclaps — a further link into the Morr-adjacent Icelandic scene.

Icelandic scene connections and Sigur Rós (2005–2014)

Eiríkur’s reputation as a go-to session trumpeter in the Reykjavík scene is confirmed by two major credits: he plays trumpet on Sigur Rós’s Takk… (Geffen, 2005) and later co-arranged brass on their Kveikur (XL Recordings, 2013). These are among the most widely distributed records in his credit list. He is also credited on the Sigur Rós-related Hoppípolla single (EMI, 2006).

He played flugelhorn on Bonnie ‘Prince’ Billy’s The Letting Go (Domino, 2006) — his most notable excursion into American indie-folk territory. The Domino compilation pairing (The Letting Go / Little Lost Blues) also features him.

Move to Netherlands and Skeylja (2011–2014)

By the early 2010s Eiríkur was based in Utrecht, and his credits began to diversify into European contemporary and improvised music. He played trumpet on the The Kilimanjaro Darkjazz Ensemble’s From The Stairwell (Denovali Records, 2011) and on Jessica Sligter’s Fear And The Framing (Hubro, 2012). He contributed strings arrangement to Múm’s collaborator Kira Kira and Hermigervill’s project, and took part in Rutger Zuydervelt’s (Machinefabriek) large-scale drone-composition project Stay Tuned (Baskaru, 2014) — a record with over 150 credited contributors from across the international improvised/experimental music scene.

The centrepiece of this period is Skeylja (The Alvaret Ensemble, Kira Kira, Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson, Ingi Garðar Erlendsson, Borgar Magnason; Denovali Records, 2014), on which Eiríkur is a co-primary artist. The album is a cross-Icelandic-Dutch collaboration: The Alvaret Ensemble is the Dutch drone/minimalist collective of Jan Kleefstra, Romke Kleefstra, Sytze Pruiksma, and Greg Haines, while Kira Kira (Kristín Björk Kristjánsdóttir), Ingi Garðar Erlendsson (tuba, trombone, “thranophone”), and Borgar Magnason (contrabass) are Icelandic collaborators. Eiríkur plays trumpet and electronics. The record was released on Denovali Records in 2014 and represents his sole primary-credited studio release to date.

Later work (2016–present)

After Skeylja, Eiríkur maintained an active session and guest profile:

  • Slow Healer (Near Gale Records, Belgium, 2016): trumpet credit
  • Lanzarote (Jo Berger Myhre & Ólafur Björn Ólafsson; Hubro, 2019): featured trumpet on tracks 2, 3, and 6
  • Motions Like These (Eskmo & Kira Kira; Ancestor, 2019): trumpet, whistle, flugelhorn
  • Sumarbörn (Kira Kira & Hermigervill; Time Released Sound, 2019): trumpet, flugelhorn
  • Just A Closer Walk With Thee (The Emotional Carpenters; Radio Bongo, 2020): primary artist role
  • Palm Trees In The Snow (KÁRI; Alda Music, 2023): trumpet, flugelhorn

He is a member of Hist Og, a trio with Magnús Trygvason Eliassen and Róbert Sturla Reynisson (also in Benni Hemm Hemm). Their debut Days Of Tundra appeared on Reykjavík Record Shop (2019); a second release Just A Closer Walk With Thee followed on Radio Bongo (2020), with Submission on Radio Bongo and Reykjavík Record Shop (2023). The Emotional Carpenters released Submission (Radio Bongo, 2023).

Seabear — his other long-running ensemble membership — spans 2004–2022, with albums on Morr Music (The Ghost That Carried Us Away, 2007; We Built A Fire, 2010; In Another Life, 2022). Seabear includes Sindri Már Sigfússon and Örvar Þóreyjarson Smárason among its members.

Scene Connections

Eiríkur sits at the nexus of two distinct scenes:

Reykjavík indie/experimental (late 1990s–2010s): He is part of the extended múm collective centered around Örvar Þóreyjarson Smárason, Gunnar Örn Tynes, and Valgeir Sigurðsson. This web connects outward to Sigur Rós, Hildur Guðnadóttir, Ólöf Arnalds, Gyða Valtýsdóttir, Kristín Anna Valtýsdóttir, Samuli Kosminen, and the labels FatCat and Morr Music.

European improvised/contemporary music (2010s–present): His Utrecht base placed him within Dutch and pan-European improv/contemporary circles. The Alvaret Ensemble connection — with the Kleefstra brothers (Jan and Romke, from Friesland), Sytze Pruiksma, and Greg Haines — is the clearest bridge. The Baskaru Stay Tuned release (Machinefabriek project) places him alongside a global network of improvisers including Nils Frahm, Oren Ambarchi, Jenny Hval, Thomas Ankersmit, Peter Broderick, and many others. His work on Jessica Sligter’s Fear And The Framing (Hubro, produced by Randall Dunn) connects him to Norwegian new jazz circles.
