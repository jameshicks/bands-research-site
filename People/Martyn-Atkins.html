Martyn Atkins

British graphic designer and occasional music video director. Discogs artist ID: 482104. Active primarily in the 1980s–1990s; closely associated with Factory Records and Mute Records.

Designed Robert Görl’s Night Full Of Tension LP (Mute, 1984). 11 shared releases with Görl in the Discogs database, all related to that album and its various pressings.

Studio Affiliation

Atkins ran (or co-ran) a design studio called T&CP Associates (also referred to as “Town & Country Planning” or T+CP). Named collaborators within T&CP include David A. Jones and Mark Higenbottam. The studio is credited on multiple Depeche Mode releases from 1982–1987. [source: classicpopmag.com/features/the-cover-art-of-depeche-mode/]

Earlier in his career (c. 1980–1982) Atkins worked as a freelance or associate designer alongside Peter Saville on Factory Records releases, before T&CP was formalised. [source: factoryrecords.org/martyn-atkins.php]

Atkins is described on the official Factory Records site as “an early Factory era designer who worked with Minny Pops, Section 25, A Certain Ratio and others.” [source: factoryrecords.org/martyn-atkins.php]

Zoo Records / Zoo-era Liverpool acts (c. 1980–1984)

Atkins stated in a 2015 comment (Post-Punk Monk) that he worked for Zoo Records, Echo & the Bunnymen, and the Teardrop Explodes. [source: postpunkmonk.com/2015/05/13/simple-minds-sidebar-1-ranking-the-cover-art-part-13/]

The first four Echo & the Bunnymen studio albums were designed by Atkins in collaboration with photographer Brian Griffin, forming a coordinated series of elemental landscape images:

Depeche Mode (T&CP Associates period, 1982–1987)

Atkins (via T&CP Associates) took on regular design responsibilities for Depeche Mode from A Broken Frame through Music for the Masses, working closely with photographer Brian Griffin and later illustrator Ian Wright and photographer Peter Ashworth. He art-directed shoots and “ran sleeve ideas by” band members directly, without management intermediaries. He also directed the “Little 15” and “Strangelove (Version 2)” music videos. [source: classicpopmag.com/features/the-cover-art-of-depeche-mode/]

Johnny Cash / American Recordings (1990s–2000s)

Atkins met Rick Rubin in 1990 and went on to serve as art director and photographer across the American Recordings series with Johnny Cash. He is credited with creating the Cash logo used across the series. [source: postpunkmonk.com/2015/05/13/simple-minds-sidebar-1-ranking-the-cover-art-part-13/]

Confirmed credits include:

  • American Recordings (1994) — art direction and photography alongside cover photographer Andy Earl [source: discogs.com/release/696268]
  • American VI: Ain’t No Grave — additional photography [source: discogs.com/release/5485921]
