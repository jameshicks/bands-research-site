Bobby Bray

Guitarist and vocalist. San Diego, California. Discogs artist ID: 821458. Full name: Robert Bray (on record as “Robert Bray,” “Bobby Bray,” “Mr. Robert Bray,” “Mr. Bobby Bray,” “Mister Robert Bray,” “Bray”).

Core guitarist of The Locust for their entire recording career (1997–2012), and a founding member of the Locust side project Holy Molar. Later founded Inus (2) (also known as INUS), a San Diego trio on Three One G.

See also: Robert Bray (duplicate stub — same person, consolidate)

Biography

Origins and The Locust (1994–1997)

Bobby Bray was part of the San Diego underground scene that coalesced around Three One G and the city’s hardcore/noise network in the early-to-mid 1990s. He appears on The Locust’s first proper studio recording, the 1997 self-titled 7” on Gold Standard Laboratories (GSL 09), credited as “Mr. Robert Bray” alongside Jimmy LaValle (who played in the band’s early lineup), Justin Pearson, and David Astor. Notably, neither Gabe Serbian nor Joey Karam appear on the 1997 7” — this appears to be an earlier four-piece configuration of the band.

By the 1998 self-titled LP (GSL 15), the lineup had solidified into the classic five-piece with Gabe Serbian and Joey Karam joining Bray, Pearson, and Astor.

The Classic Locust Lineup (1998–2007)

Bray remained the constant guitar presence throughout The Locust’s run on Gold Standard Laboratories, Anti-, and Ipecac. He is credited on every significant studio release:

  • 1997 Locust 7” (GSL 09) — credited Musician
  • 1998 The Locust LP (GSL 15) — credited Musician
  • 2000 Well I’ll Be A Monkey’s Uncle 12” (GSL 32) — credited Guitar
  • 2001 Flight Of The Wounded Locust 7” (GSL 41) — credited Musician
  • 2003 Plague Soundscapes (Anti-) — credited Band (as “Bobby Bray”)
  • 2005 Safety Second, Body Last EP (Ipecac) — credited Band
  • 2007 New Erections (Anti-) — credited Musician

On the 2012 career retrospective Molecular Genetics From The Gold Standard Labs (Anti-), Bray is credited with Guitar, Vocals, Synthesizer (track 13), and Songwriter across the compiled material.

Holy Molar (2001–2007)

Holy Molar was a side project overlapping substantially with The Locust’s membership, also drawing in Mark McCoy and Maxamillion Avila. Bray played keyboards on their first release, Live At The San Diego Metropolitan Correctional Center (Three One G, 2001) — recorded at the actual San Diego detention facility. He is credited as “Bobby Bray” (Performer) on the 2007 album Cavity Search (Three One G), alongside Pearson, Serbian, McCoy, and Avila. The band’s self-titled 2003 LP and The Whole Tooth, And Nothing But The Tooth… (also 2003) complete the core catalog.

More Songs About Girls And Work (2010)

A release credited to an unspecified project (UK release, Not On Label), Bray is credited across an unusually wide range of instruments: Bass, Drums, Songwriter, Engineer, Producer, Vocals, Guitar, Ukulele, Banjo, Mandolin, Glockenspiel, Mellotron, Percussion. The Discogs record dates this to 2010. This appears to be a solo or near-solo effort; details are sparse in the DB.

INUS / Inus (2) (2011–2019)

After the apparent wind-down of active Locust touring, Bray formed Innerds around 2011 with drummer Brandon Relf, initially as a two-piece. The project was renamed INUS (full name: The Institute for Navigating the Universal Self, described as “pre-postapocalyptic, post-honky-tonk-prog-skronk, space Tropicalia, math-lounge powersilence”) and eventually expanded to a trio with the addition of bassist Chad Deal in 2016. The debut album Western Spaghettification was released in 2019 on Three One G (Discogs ID: 14024378), completing the recording after Deal joined.

INUS represents a significant stylistic departure from The Locust’s grindcore brevity, with the descriptors suggesting extended, eclectic compositions drawing from prog, Tropicália, and noise traditions.
