Overview

John Anderson is a producer and mixing engineer best known for his work on early recordings by Warsaw (3) and Joy Division. His 23 Discogs credits are dominated by connections to those groups — shared credits with Ian Curtis, Peter Hook, Stephen Morris, Bernard Sumner, Bernard Albrecht, and Martin Hannett — placing him at the centre of foundational Manchester post-punk activity. Bob Auger is his single largest collaborator by shared releases (30), pointing to a sustained working relationship likely centred on Pennine Sound Studios. His catalogue is thin but spans the 1980s through 2000s, surfacing mainly through reissues on London Records, Rhino, and Vinyl Passion.

Notes (web)

Anderson’s background and route into the Warsaw/Joy Division sessions

Anderson was not a studio engineer or an employee of Bob Auger — his background was as a Northern Soul collector and rare record importer. He founded Soul Bowl, a UK import business that supplied DJs and collectors with 1970s American soul releases, and was based in Kings Lynn, Norfolk (Glasgow-born). In June 1977 he co-founded Grapevine Records with Philadelphia businessman Bernie Binnick; Grapevine was manufactured and distributed by RCA and concentrated on legitimate UK reissues of obscure US soul material. source: soul-source.co.uk/forums/topic/20954-history-of-grapevine-records-1977-80 (fetched 2026-11-01)

His route into the Joy Division sessions was through the Grapevine/RCA commercial arrangement, not through Pennine Sound Studios. Richard Searling, Anderson’s co-director on Grapevine and a Northern Soul DJ working in RCA’s Manchester promotions department, brought Joy Division into the orbit of RCA in early 1978. Searling has stated he personally funded a demo session; the resulting Arrow Studios sessions (3–4 May 1978) credited Anderson, Auger, and Searling jointly as producers. source: gigslutz.co.uk/interview-richard-searling (fetched 2026-11-01)

Anderson’s involvement in post-production was explicitly editorial: the joydiv.org history records that “John Anderson decided to put synthesisers on the final mix in an effort to produce a more ‘professional’ sound” — a decision the band strongly objected to and which contributed to the deal collapsing. source: joydiv.org/hist2.htm (fetched 2026-11-01)

Key distinction — Pennine vs Arrow: The original 1977 Pennine Sound Studios demo (18 July 1977, Oldham) is a separate session from the 1978 Arrow/RCA sessions where Anderson is credited. Anderson does not appear to have been involved in the 1977 Pennine recording; that session features Bob Auger and Paul Adshead on the studio side. Anderson appears in shared-release credits on Discogs alongside Auger because both are credited on the later reissues and compilations that package both sessions together (e.g., Warsaw 1989, Heart and Soul 1997). The 30 shared Discogs releases between Anderson and Auger therefore reflect co-credit on compiled and reissued product, not a pre-existing studio partnership or employer–employee relationship.

No source found establishing a prior personal or professional connection between Anderson and Auger before the 1978 sessions. Their collaboration appears to have been circumstantial: Auger as the studio/engineering side at Arrow for RCA, Anderson as the Grapevine label figure who had brought the band into the RCA orbit via Searling.
