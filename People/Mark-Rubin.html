Mark Rubin

Multi-instrumentalist (upright bass, tuba, vocals), bandleader, and producer. Real name: Mark Daniel Rubin. Born in Oklahoma; lived in Dallas and Austin, TX; now based in New Orleans. Performs under the alias “Mark ‘Bigwood’ Rubin” and “Mark Rubin (Jew Of Oklahoma).” Discogs artist ID: 1107101. Website: jewofoklahoma.com

Core member of the Bad Livers (Austin, 1990–2000) and leader of several klezmer and roots ensembles including Rubinchik’s Orkestyr and Mark Rubin & His Jews Of The Golden West.

Biography

Early Career — Dallas Roots (late 1980s)

Rubin’s earliest Discogs credit is as double bass player on Alive From The City Of Hate In The Lone Star State (1989, Not On Label), a live document credited to Killbilly, the Dallas-based “bluethrash” band mixing bluegrass, rockabilly, and punk. Also in Killbilly’s ranks were Danny Barnes and future Old 97’s members Rhett Miller and Murry Hammond, as well as Alan Wooley, Craig Taylor, Harris Kirby, Mike Schwedler, Richard Hunter (6), Richie Vasquez, Stephen Trued, and Steve Lutke. Killbilly released a second record, Stranger In This Place (1998, Flying Fish), after the band had formally dissolved.

Austin Roots Scene — The Bad Livers Era (1990–2000)

The Bad Livers were the central project of Rubin’s Austin years, a string-band unit drawing on bluegrass, old-time, punk, and experimental traditions. The lineup: Danny Barnes (banjo, guitar, vocals), Ralph E. White (fiddle, accordion), Rubin (upright bass, tuba, vocals), and later Bob Grant (10) (mandolin, guitar).

The band recorded for Quarterstick Records (a Touch and Go imprint) before moving to Sugar Hill Records (2). Key albums:

The Delusions Of Banjer session brought in Paul Leary of the Butthole Surfers as producer and additional vocalist/percussionist — an early sign of the Austin underground’s cross-pollination. Stuart Sullivan engineered.

Alongside the Bad Livers, Rubin appeared on the Austin scene in 1990 as acoustic bass and backing vocals on Jean Caffeine (2)‘s Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop (Porch Music, 1990), a session that also featured Ponty Bone (accordion), Fred KRC (drums), Bobby Birdsong (lap steel), Mick Buck (guitar), JD Foster (mixed, guitar), and Lisa Pankratz (percussion).

The Newton Boys Soundtrack (1998)

Rubin co-produced and played tuba and double bass on The Newton Boys — Music From The Motion Picture (Epic/Sony, 1998), the soundtrack to Richard Linklater’s Texas-set crime film. He also co-wrote the liner notes. The album drew a wide circle of Austin players: Danny Barnes, Erik Hokkanen, Steve James (7), Floyd Domino (piano), Carl Sonny Leyland (piano), Patty Griffin (vocals), Guy Forsyth (vocals), Abra Moore (vocals), Elias Haslanger (tenor sax). Executive producers: Linklater and Anne Walker-McBay.

Klezmer and Jewish Music — Rubinchik’s Projects

Rubin developed a parallel career in Austin klezmer under the “Rubinchik” banner, eventually leading multiple ensembles. Rubinchik’s Orkestyr (Discogs ID: 4856404) began as Rubinchik’s Kapelye with Erik Hokkanen (fiddle), Michael Maddux (accordion), Mike Stinnett (clarinet), and Rachel Rhodes (2) (vocals), then renamed as they built a residency at Flipnotics in Austin. Later lineup added Jake Zuckerman (guitar), Lisa Schneider (2) (fiddle replacing Hokkanen), and Ben Saffer (clarinet replacing Stinnett).

Rubinchik’s Orkestyr released one document: Flipnotics Freilachs (1998, Rubinchik Recordings — Rubin’s own imprint). Rubin produced, engineered tracks 8–11, and played tuba, 6-string bass, and balalaika. Jerry Tubb edited and mastered; Walter Morgan engineered tracks 1–7; Kamran Hooshmand appeared on oud.

The broader Rubinchik’s Yiddish Ensemble (Discogs ID: 6584964) is a related but distinct entity.

Rubin was also a member of Frank London’s Klezmer Brass Allstars (Discogs ID: 426404), the New York-based klezmer brass band led by Frank London. That ensemble released Di Shikere Kapelye (Jewish-Oriental Village Brass From NYC’s Lower East Side) (Piranha, 2000, Germany).
