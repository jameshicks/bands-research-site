Martin Ditcham

Discogs ID: 252445 Real name: Martin Russell Ditcham Born: February 22, 1951, Ilford, Essex, England Role: Percussionist, Drummer Based: UK Active: 1979–present Associated Acts: Talk Talk, Mark Hollis, .O.Rang, Henry Cow, Held By Trees, Nucleus (3)

Biography

Martin Ditcham is a British session percussionist and drummer who has been one of the most consistently employed specialists in UK recording across five decades. Born in Ilford, Essex in 1951, he worked across jazz, rock, pop, and experimental contexts. His career divides into three overlapping threads: early jazz and art-rock involvement in groups including Henry Cow and Nucleus (3); a long peak as a first-call session percussionist for major artists throughout the 1980s and 1990s; and a later phase connecting him to post-Talk Talk successor projects, including Held By Trees.

Early career and group work (1970s–early 1980s)

Ditcham is listed as a member of Henry Cow, the Canterbury-adjacent British art-rock collective around Fred Frith, Chris Cutler, Tim Hodgkinson, John Greaves, and Geoff Leigh. He also appears as a member of Nucleus (3), the jazz-rock group. His earliest Discogs-documented session credit is 1979 (The Joke’s On You, Harvest label, Scandinavia), and by 1981 he was active on multiple European and UK records — percussion on releases by artists issued on Frituna (Scandinavia), Deram (UK), and Vertigo (Germany).

The early 1980s saw him drawn into the orbit of the Some Bizzare label, appearing on records by Marc Almond (Stories of Johnny, 1985; A Woman’s Story compilation, 1986), The The (Infected-era connections), and other Some Bizzare-adjacent releases. He is listed as a performer on the Some Bizzare Singles 1984–1987 compilation with a “Thank You To The Willing Sinners With” credit alongside Marc Almond.

Session peak: the mid-1980s to mid-1990s

By 1983–84 Ditcham was appearing on some of the best-selling albums of the era. His most notable landmark session from this period is Sade’s debut album Diamond Life (1984, Epic), where he added percussion alongside the band. He returned for Promise (1985, Portrait/Epic) — where, unusually for a session player, he is credited as co-writer of one track (“Cherry Pie,” credited “Written-By: Ditcham”) — and for Stronger Than Pride (1988, Epic), the latter with a “Drum, Percussion” credit. He also co-wrote “The Sweetest Taboo” (co-written with Stuart Matthewman and Sade Adu), which appeared on Promise; this credit tracks through numerous Sade compilation pressings into the 2010s.

Other notable 1983–84 credits include The Rolling Stones’ Undercover (1983, Rolling Stones Records) — a large percussion ensemble session including Sly Dunbar, Ian Stewart, and others — and Nik Kershaw’s Human Racing (1984, MCA).

Through the mid-1980s he worked across a wide stylistic range:

  • Chris Rea — a long and recurring association, beginning with Shamrock Diaries (1985, Magnet) and continuing through On the Beach (1986), The Road to Hell (1989, where he is credited as a full band member “The Band”), Auberge (1991, where he plays drums and percussion on all tracks), God’s Great Banana Skin (1992), Espresso Logic (1993), King of the Beach (2000), The Blue Cafe (1998), and beyond. This is the most sustained long-term professional relationship in Ditcham’s discography outside Talk Talk.
  • The Beautiful South — percussion on Welcome to the Beautiful South (1989, Go! Discs) and subsequent releases through the 1990s and 2000s, including their major UK compilations.
  • Everything But The Girl — percussion on Amplified Heart (1994, Blanco y Negro), the critical turning-point album preceding their drum’n’bass breakthrough.
  • Mike & The Mechanics — percussion on Living Years (1988, WEA), the Genesis-adjacent group’s biggest-selling album.
  • Tanita Tikaram — percussion on Ancient Heart (1988, Reprise), her breakthrough debut.
  • Lush — percussion on Split (1994, 4AD/Reprise).
  • Marc Almond — multiple credits across Some Bizzare/Virgin releases in 1985–86.
  • Manic Street Preachers — percussion on Everything Must Go (1996, Epic) and This Is My Truth Tell Me Yours (1998, Epic), the band’s major commercial albums.
  • Kevin Rowland (Dexys Midnight Runners) — tambourine and congas on My Beauty (1999, Creation).

Talk Talk and the inner circle (1986–1998)

Ditcham’s most critically significant work came from his association with Talk Talk and the small ensemble that Mark Hollis and Tim Friese-Greene assembled for their later records. He appears on all three of Talk Talk’s post-commercial albums:

  • The Colour of Spring (1986, EMI) — Percussion on tracks A1, A3, B1, B2, B4. The album’s large ensemble session; he appeared alongside Morris Pert, Steve Winwood, Robbie McIntosh, and Danny Thompson.
  • Spirit of Eden (1988, Parlophone) — Percussion. One of approximately twenty musicians on the entire album, alongside Nigel Kennedy (violin), Hugh Davies (shozygs), Danny Thompson (double bass), and a cathedral choir.
  • Laughing Stock (1991, Polydor/Verve) — Percussion. The Talk Talk inner-circle ensemble was even smaller here: Harris, two acoustic bassists (Simon Edwards and Ernest Mothle), a seven-viola section, Henry Lowther on trumpet, Mark Feltham on harmonica, and Ditcham.

He also appears on .O.Rang’s Herd of Instinct (1994, Echo) — the post-Talk Talk project of Lee Harris and Paul Webb — playing percussion on tracks 2, 3, and 7. His presence in the album’s art-installation photo series (credited “Fig. IX”) signals his inclusion in the broader Talk Talk extended family. Engineer Phill Brown co-produced several tracks on the same album.

The final link in this chain is Mark Hollis’s self-titled 1998 solo album (Polydor), where Ditcham is credited with Drums and Percussion. The album also featured Robbie McIntosh (guitar), Mark Feltham (harmonica), Henry Lowther (trumpet), Phill Brown (engineer), and Dominic Miller (guitar, arrangements on one track), alongside classical players Lawrence Pendrous (piano, harmonium), Warne Livesey (arrangements), and others. Ditcham’s drums-and-percussion role — rather than the narrower “percussion” label from the Talk Talk albums — reflects the relative rhythmic centrality of his contribution to the record.

Held By Trees and later work (2021–present)

In 2021 Ditcham appeared on Solace (Tweed Jacket Music Ltd), the debut album by Held By Trees, a group explicitly constituted around the Talk Talk alumni network. The lineup included Robbie McIntosh (guitar), Lawrence Pendrous (piano, harmonium), Simon Edwards (bass, double bass), Andy Panayi (2) (flute, clarinet), and James Marsh (logo artwork) — an almost complete reconstitution of the Mark Hollis solo album ensemble, with Phill Brown again mixing several tracks. Ditcham plays drums and percussion on six of the album’s tracks.

The Held By Trees discography as of 2025 includes: In the Trees EP (2021, Circular Ritual Records), Solace (2021), Eventide / Solace – Live at Real World Studios (2023, Sound Canyon), and Hinterland (2025, Lasgo).

His other later credits extend into the 2010s and 2020s: Soldier of Love by Sade (2010, Epic/RCA/Sony), where he plays drums and percussion; a 2022 live Bark Psychosis release (Glastonbury and Elsewhere, ReR Megacorp); and various Chris Rea late-career projects.
