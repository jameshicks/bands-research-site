Charlie Watts

Discogs ID: 299325 | Alias: Chris Winters (9) — Discogs ID: 10546741 Born: 2 June 1941, Kingsbury, London, England Died: 24 August 2021, London, England Role: Drummer, percussionist

English drummer, best known as the backbone of The Rolling Stones for nearly six decades (January 1963 – August 2021). Beyond the Stones, Watts was a dedicated jazz enthusiast who led or co-led a series of big band and small ensemble projects from the mid-1980s onward, including the Charlie Watts Orchestra, the Charlie Watts Quintet, and Charlie Watts And The Tentet. He received his first drum kit in 1955, met Alexis Korner in 1961, and played briefly with Blues Incorporated before Brian Jones recruited him into the embryonic Rolling Stones in January 1963. He played Gretsch drums throughout his career. In July 2006 he was voted into the Modern Drummer Hall of Fame.

The Rolling Stones

Watts joined in January 1963 and appeared on every studio album from the 1964 debut through A Bigger Bang (2005). Two tracks recorded before his death appeared on Hackney Diamonds (2023). His drumming — rooted in jazz timekeeping, spare and precise — was the rhythmic anchor for the band’s entire catalog.

Jazz and Solo Projects

The Charlie Watts Orchestra

Large ensemble formed in the mid-1980s. Watts co-produced and co-led with percussionist John Stevens (2). The orchestra featured many of the leading names of British jazz.

Other Group Projects and Session Work

Rocket 88 (1981)

Blues super-session band, Atlantic Records (ID: 1467881). Members included Alexis Korner (guitar), Jack Bruce (bass), Ian Stewart (piano), Bob Hall (3) and George Green (piano), Don Weller (tenor sax), Hal Singer (tenor sax), John Picard (trombone), Colin Smith (trumpet). Produced by Ian Stewart. Watts also designed the cover.

Willie and the Poor Boys (1985)

Willie And The Poor Boys — charity project featuring Watts alongside other rock names. Mercury Records (ID: 2049318). Multiple singles and a video release.

Jamming With Edward! (1972)

Rolling Stones Records (ID: 1213127). Jam session featuring Watts, Mick Jagger, Keith Richards, Nicky Hopkins, and Ry Cooder, credited as “Edward.”

The London Howlin’ Wolf Sessions (1971)

Chess Records (ID: 6272452). Watts appears as part of the British rock/blues contingent backing Howlin’ Wolf alongside Eric Clapton, Ringo Starr, Klaus Voormann, and others.
