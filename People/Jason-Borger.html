Jason Borger

Discogs ID: 424049
ANV / alias: The Professor (9) (Discogs ID: 1646387)
Name variations: Jason “Borge” Borger, Jason “Borgé” Borger, Jason “The Professor” Borger

San Francisco–based multi-instrumentalist and keyboardist. Active from at least 1999 as a session player and arranger across indie rock, singer-songwriter, and Americana. Known for a deep kit of vintage keyboards — Hammond B-3, Farfisa, Wurlitzer, Rhodes, Mellotron, Clavinet, harmonium — and occasional string arrangement work. No primary-artist releases on Discogs. He is a member of American Music Club’s post-reunion lineup and appears on their 2008 and 2021 releases.

Overview

Borger surfaces first on a pair of 1999 San Francisco indie records — Highwater Rising’s Highwater Rising and Matt Nathanson’s Still Waiting for Spring — playing a wide range of keyboards and organ. In the same year he became a recurring player for Chuck Prophet, appearing on The Hurting Business (1999), Age of Miracles (2004), and Aidan Hawken’s Pillows & Records (2005, produced by Chuck Prophet’s circle). His string arrangement work begins around 2004 across Chuck Prophet’s Age of Miracles and the Soviet album Spies in the House of Love.

From 2008 onward his profile widens: he joins American Music Club for The Golden Age (2008) and stays with the band into the 2021 releases A Toast To You and Our First Trip to Nottingham. Simultaneously he plays piano and Mellotron for Joshua Radin on Simple Times (2008) and The Fall (2017), and contributes organ to Patrick Park’s Come What Will (2010). His most notable out-of-town credit is Hammond organ and piano on Roy Harper’s Man & Myth (Bella Union, 2013), a late-period Harper album co-produced by Jonathan Wilson that attracted significant critical attention.

His alias “The Professor (9)” appears to be a sleeve/ANV handle used informally; it has its own Discogs entry (ID 1646387) pointing back to Jason Borger but no separate discography of its own.
