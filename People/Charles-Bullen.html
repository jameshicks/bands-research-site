Charles Bullen

Guitarist, clarinetist, viola player, vocalist, multi-instrumentalist. UK. Discogs artist ID: 283932. Alias: Billy Bagg (credited separately as Discogs artist ID: 384625).

Full name: Charles Sylvester Bullen. Co-founder of This Heat. Before This Heat he played in the early group Ning (2) in Coventry, and after the band dissolved in 1982 he went on to record under his own name (as Lifetones), as Circadian Rhythms (with Gwen Jamois), and sporadically as People In Control. Has run his own studio and operated as a producer and engineer for a wide range of UK post-punk and experimental acts.

Early Career: Ning and the Path to This Heat

Bullen’s first documented group is Ning (2), a Coventry-based band also featuring Jim Edwards (3) and Mick Ross. He subsequently formed the duo Friendly Rifles with Charles Hayward, which was the nucleus that grew into This Heat.

This Heat (1976–1982)

This Heat formed in London with Bullen, Charles Hayward (drums, keyboards, voice, tape), and Gareth Williams (keyboards, guitar, bass, voice, tape). The trio self-produced and self-engineered much of their work from their South London rehearsal space/studio, Cold Storage — a rented meat locker in Brixton.

Bullen’s role throughout: guitar, clarinet, viola, voice, tape/loops. On later releases he also took on engineering and editing duties.

The debut This Heat (1979, Piano) was co-produced by David Cunningham and Anthony Moore alongside the band. Deceit (1981, Rough Trade) was produced by David Cunningham. For the 2006 archival releases Live 80/81 and Out Of Cold Storage, Bullen and Hayward jointly edited, mastered, and remastered the material — Bullen’s studio role extending to the band’s legacy material.

Peter Bullen (Discogs ID: 534596) — a separate individual, possibly a relative — appears on several This Heat releases in tape operation and projection roles.

Post-This Heat: Lifetones (1983)

After This Heat split, Bullen made one LP under the name Lifetones: For A Reason (1983, Tone Of Life Records). He played voice, guitar, bass, keyboards, percussion, and clarinet; also produced and engineered it, and wrote the majority of the material. Co-writer and co-performer Julius Cornelius Samuel (J. Samuel (2)) contributed drums, percussion, and keyboards, and co-wrote tracks A1–A3. Engineering assistance from Islaine Jamois.

Lifetones (Discogs ID: 304113) also lists Frank Byng as a member, though he does not appear in the LP credits.

People In Control

A loose collective including Bullen, Ian Hill, Judy Carter, Martin Harrison, and Rick Wilson. They appear as producers on Family Fodder’s Monkey Banana Kitchen (1980, Fresh Records) — Bullen himself played drums, guitar, vocals, percussion, and viola on the album. The collective also released When It’s War (1981, Crammed Discs) as a primary release.

Producer/Engineer Work (1979–2017)

Beyond his own projects, Bullen had an extensive parallel career as a producer and engineer across the London post-punk/experimental scene. His Billy Bagg alias was used on at least one production credit — the Family Fodder single “Playing Golf (With My Flesh Crawling)” (1979, Fresh Records / Parole Records), where he is credited as Billy Bagg on the AA-side and as Charles Bullen on the A-side.

The Woof Records cluster (1982–1989) suggests a sustained working relationship with that label. His two credits for Ut on Out Records (1984, 1985, 2017) bracket a 33-year connection with that band.

Circadian Rhythms (1998)

Bullen’s later alias/project Circadian Rhythms was a duo with Gwen Jamois. The sole release is Internal Clock (1998, Language label, Europe). Bullen played instruments and co-produced; Jamois programmed and played instruments. Additional players on the album: Brian Lion (cymbal), Steve Blaggy (djembe), Jean-Dominique Nkishi (flute), Spike (5) (melodica), Winston Rose (saxophone), Tom Chant (soprano saxophone), Denny Rose (trombone), Wolfy (synth). Nigel Laybourne engineered.
