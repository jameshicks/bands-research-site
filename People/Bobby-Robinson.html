Bobby Robinson

Harlem record producer, label owner, songwriter, and record shop owner. Real name Morgan Clyde Robinson. Born 16 April 1917, Union, South Carolina; died 7 January 2011, Harlem, New York City (aged 93). Discogs artist ID: 119103.

Brother of Danny Robinson (3) (Discogs ID: 1919482). Father of Ronnie Robinson. Uncle of Spoonie Gee.

Overview

One of the most significant independent label operators in postwar American music, Robinson ran a cluster of Harlem-based imprints from the late 1950s through the 1980s — Fire Records (2), Fury Records (2), Everlast (2), Red Robin, and most durably Enjoy Records. He operated out of his Harlem record shops (“Bobby’s Records And Tape Center,” “Bobby’s Happy House”) and was active as both producer and songwriter across R&B, blues, soul, and eventually hip-hop.

Discogs credits him as producer on 155+ releases and as writer/co-writer on 71+ releases — a body of work spanning from Elmore James and King Curtis in the early 1960s through Grandmaster Flash, the Treacherous Three, The Fearless Four, and Doug E. Fresh in the early 1980s.

Career by Era

1950s–Early 1960s: R&B and Blues

Robinson built Fire and Fury Records into significant independent operations during the early 1960s. Key Fire productions include Elmore James sides (“The Sky Is Crying,” “It Hurts Me Too,” “Mojo Hand”), Bobby Marchan, and numerous blues and R&B artists. Fury had early recordings of Gladys Knight And The Pips and Lee Dorsey (“Ya Ya,” 1961 — a number-one hit produced by Robinson). He also produced for outside labels during this period (Josie, Philips, Mercury, Capitol, Vee Jay, Smash).

1962: Enjoy Records Launch

Founded Enjoy Records with the first release being “Soul Twist” by King Curtis And The Noble Knights (cat. 1000, 1962) — a number-one R&B hit produced by Robinson and his brother Danny Robinson. The label also issued Elmore James blues sides (1965), doo-wop, and R&B through the mid-1960s.

1966–1978: Gap Period

Robinson continued producing across a range of labels (Josie, Capitol, Mercury, Philips) but Enjoy released very little through this period. He reissued Fire Records material through Trip Records in the mid-1970s and ran a Front Page Records imprint briefly around 1970.

1979–1985: Hip-Hop Pioneer

Recognizing the new music emerging from Harlem and the Bronx block-party circuit, Robinson launched a new catalog series at Enjoy (starting cat. 6000) and became one of the first producers to commit hip-hop to vinyl commercially. He produced nearly every hip-hop release on the label, typically crediting himself as both producer and co-writer. Key productions:

The Treacherous Three were the most prolific Enjoy hip-hop act under Robinson (31 Discogs pressings). Spoonie Gee was Robinson’s nephew.

1985–2011: Late Career and Legacy

After the hip-hop roster moved on (Grandmaster Flash to Sugar Hill, The Fearless Four to Elektra), Enjoy continued releasing sporadically until 1993. Robinson’s catalog was widely reissued: Collectables Records in the US and P-Vine Records in Japan issued numerous compilations of both the Fire Records blues period and the Enjoy hip-hop catalog. Robinson was recognized in his later years as a foundational figure in both postwar R&B and old-school hip-hop.
