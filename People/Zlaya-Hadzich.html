Zlaya Hadzich

Producer, recording engineer, mixer, and mastering engineer from Bosnia & Herzegovina (real name Zlatan Hadžić), based in Amsterdam. Discogs artist ID: 253076. Also credited on Discogs under the alias Zlaya Loud (ID: 5565376), reflecting his long association with Loud (3) (Studio Loud, Amsterdam), where he works. Active on record from 1989 through to at least 2026.

Overview

Zlaya Hadzich is the in-house engineer and mastering engineer most closely identified with the Konkurrent “In The Fishtank” series — the Dutch label’s programme of short-notice, fixed-location sessions pairing artists who don’t normally record together. He worked on at least eight volumes of the series between 1998 and 2009, across a roll-call of experimental and post-rock names: Tortoise, The Ex, Sonic Youth, Dirty Three, Low, The Black Heart Procession, Karate, Isis, Aereogramme, Sparklehorse, and Fennesz.

Alongside the Fishtank work, his credit trail runs through Dutch indie and experimental scenes — Dox Records, Kindred Spirits, Katzwijm, Subroutine — plus heavy/doom credits (Amenra’s Mass III) and a background in Croatian and Bosnian recording from the 1990s. He also functions as composer and performer on some releases, not only as technical crew.

The Discogs alias note instructs editors: “Mastered by Zlaya at Loud” should be entered as Mastered At — Loud (3) [company] plus Mastered By — Zlaya Hadzich [person], clarifying the studio/engineer split.

Background

Hadzich’s earliest Discogs credit is as uncredited drummer on a 1989 Yugoslav release (During Wartime, SCH (3)). His mid-1990s credits in Croatia and Bosnia (drum programming, sampling, engineering, co-production) show him working across genres in post-Yugoslav recording infrastructure before relocating to the Netherlands. By 1998 he was engineering at Konkurrent’s studio in Amsterdam, including co-engineering the Tassilli Players’ At The Cowshed (Konkurrent, 1998) alongside Colin Cod and with Zion Train producing.

The Fishtank Years (1998–2009)

The “In The Fishtank” series is the spine of his discography. Each volume is a collaboration record made by bands placed together in a Dutch studio with minimal preparation time. Hadzich’s role varied across volumes — engineer only, engineer + mastering, or full producer/mixer/mastering.

The Fishtank 9 session (Sonic Youth + ICP + The Ex, 2002) is among the most notable — Hadzich recorded, mixed, and mastered a one-off gathering of Thurston Moore, Lee Ranaldo, Jim O’Rourke, Steve Shelley, Han Bennink, Luc Ex, Terrie Ex, and others. The Fishtank 15 session (Sparklehorse + Fennesz, 2009) was recorded near the end of Mark Linkous’s life; he died in 2010.

The Amenra credits are significant: Hadzich mixed Mass III (the Belgian doom/post-metal band’s self-released 2005 record) and was involved in production and mastering for the Japanese and European reissues of Mass III-II + Mass IIII (2011). He has no writing credit on the original Mass III but is credited as Written-By on the Japanese compilation, suggesting his involvement deepened on the reissue.

The Gathering’s If_then_else (Century Media, 2000) is his highest-profile non-Fishtank credit by commercial reach — a progressive metal/rock album by the Dutch band, 27 pressings tracked on Discogs.

Studio

Hadzich is based at Loud (3) (also known as Studio Loud, Loud!, Loud Amsterdam) in Amsterdam. The studio functions as both a recording and mastering facility. His Zlaya Loud alias was specifically created to capture “Mastered by Zlaya at Loud” credits, which Discogs editors previously entered in various inconsistent ways.

Open Questions

  • Konkurrent studio location: the label is Amsterdam-based but it is unclear whether the Fishtank sessions all took place at Loud (3) or at a separate Konkurrent facility
  • The Gathering / If_then_else (2000): his “Co-producer, Engineer” credit on a Century Media release is notable but details of his specific contribution vs. the primary producer are not on Discogs
  • Croatian/Bosnian phase (1989–1998): credits suggest active work as drummer and producer in the former-Yugoslav recording scene before relocating; little detail on those projects in the Discogs entries
  • During Wartime (1989, Yugoslavia): uncredited drums — context of the recording unknown
  • The alias “Zlaya Loud” was created separately on Discogs; some credits may still be split between IDs 253076 and 5565376 — the Zlaya Loud ID (5565376) has 7 separate credits not captured under the main ID, including a Sony Music release (Charl, 2017) and a Whirlwind Recordings release (Polyphony 3, 2023)
  • No birth year or biographical detail on Discogs
