Tim Palmer

Tim Palmer

Oct 11, 20268 min read

Tim Palmer

Born: Timothy J. Palmer
Discogs artist ID: 86622
Based: Originally London, UK; later Austin, Texas
Active: 1981–present

British record producer, mixer, and engineer. One of the key production figures behind the British post-punk and alternative rock crossover to American rock radio in the late 1980s and 1990s. Began as an assistant engineer at Utopia Studios (London) in the early 1980s, became a full producer mid-decade, and by the 1990s had shifted toward mixing and co-production for a wide range of alternative, hard rock, and mainstream acts. Credits span The Mission, The Mighty Lemon Drops, The House Of Love, Gene Loves Jezebel, Tin Machine (David Bowie), Pearl Jam, Tears For Fears, The Psychedelic Furs, U2, Robert Plant, and Ozzy Osbourne.

Biography

Utopia Studios and Early Assistant Work (1981–1985)

Palmer began his career at Utopia Studios, London (Discogs label ID: 266218), one of the city’s major commercial studio facilities. His earliest Discogs credits are as tape op and assistant engineer on sessions for Donovan (1981), Classix Nouveaux (1982), Heatwave (1982), and Mark Knopfler (including Local Hero, 1983, and several other Knopfler releases into the mid-1980s). He also assisted on sessions for Dead Or Alive (1983 onward) and John Foxx (1983). These credits place him in the thick of British new wave and synth-pop recording activity in the early 1980s.

A notable early credit: co-producing the Identity LP (1984, EMI) for Zee (3) — the project by Pink Floyd’s Richard Wright and Dave Harris — alongside engineer work.

First Production Work and New Wave Pop (1983–1986)

Palmer’s first full productions emerged from pop acts associated with the Utopia milieu. His most commercially prominent early production was Kajagoogoo — White Feathers (1983, EMI America), the debut album containing “Too Shy.” He also produced the solo career of Limahl (Kajagoogoo’s former singer): Only For Love (1983, EMI), Don’t Suppose (1984, EMI), and several singles. These were chart productions aimed squarely at pop radio.

Also in this period: production work for Violent Blue (1985–1986, Magnet) and early singles for various UK acts.

British Post-Punk and Alternative: Core Production Era (1986–1992)

Palmer became closely associated with a cluster of UK post-punk and gothic-adjacent acts breaking into the US alternative market via labels like Mercury, Vertigo, Beggars Banquet, and Fontana. This is the work that defines his reputation in the vault’s scene:

The Mission — Palmer’s deepest and longest production relationship. He produced their entire run of core studio albums:

  • God’s Own Medicine (1986, Vertigo) — debut LP; first chart success
  • The First Chapter (1987, Mercury) — B-sides/rarities comp
  • Children (1988, Mercury)
  • Carved In Sand (1990, Mercury) — their commercial peak
  • Grains Of Sand (1990, Mercury)
  • Deliverance (1990, Vertigo)
  • Sum And Substance (1994, Vertigo) — best-of with new recordings Palmer also produced numerous The Mission singles throughout. He worked with the band into the 2010s on later releases.

The Mighty Lemon Drops — Produced World Without End (1988, Reprise), Laughter (1989, Reprise), Fall Down (Like The Rain) (1988, Chrysalis), and additional singles. These records brought the band to US college radio.

The House Of Love — Produced their self-titled Fontana album (1990) and singles including Shine On (1990). Guy Chadwick’s primary band. Palmer gave the album its polished-yet-atmospheric sound.

Gene Loves Jezebel — Produced Kiss Of Life (1990, Beggars Banquet) and Tangled Up In You (1990, Beggars Banquet).

Wire Train — Produced Ten Women (1987, CBS / 415 Records), the band’s third and most fully realized album. Also produced their single She Comes On (1987, CBS). Wire Train were a San Francisco post-punk band on the 415 Records roster; this was a significant US indie-to-major crossover production.

Texas — Produced their debut Southside (1989, Mercury) — which launched “I Don’t Want A Lover” — and Mothers Heaven (1991, Mercury), as well as numerous singles through the early 1990s. Texas were a Glasgow act; Palmer was their primary producer for the formative phase.

Ned’s Atomic Dustbin — Produced Brainbloodvolume (1995, Sony Soho Square) and associated singles including Stuck (1995).

Ocean Colour Scene — Produced their self-titled debut (1992, Fontana) and several singles, before the band refined their sound under different production for their breakthrough.

An Emotional Fish — Produced their self-titled debut (1990, EastWest/WEA), the Irish band that had a US MTV moment with “Celebrate.”

Tin Machine (1989–1991)

Palmer produced and mixed both Tin Machine albums — the debut Tin Machine (1989, EMI USA) and Tin Machine II (1991, Victory) — as well as associated singles. These were David Bowie’s heavy-rock group with Tony Sales, Hunt Sales, and Reeves Gabrels. The productions are notably raw and guitar-forward relative to Bowie’s 1980s solo work, which was intentional; Palmer emphasized the band’s live, scrappy energy. He also played additional piano and percussion on some tracks.

Robert Plant (1985–ongoing)

Palmer’s relationship with Robert Plant is one of his longest-running. He engineered and co-produced Little By Little (1985, Es Paranza) and was subsequently involved as producer, engineer, or mixer across Plant’s solo catalog from the mid-1980s into the 2020s, spanning over 240 release entries on Discogs. Key produced albums include Now And Zen (1988, Es Paranza). He remains among Plant’s go-to mixing engineers.

Tears For Fears (1991–1995)

Palmer co-produced several tracks and albums for Tears For Fears during their post-Seeds of Love period:

  • Elemental (1993, Mercury) — full producer credit, with Palmer also playing all instruments on tracks
  • Raoul And The Kings Of Spain (1995, Epic) — producer
  • Woman In Chains (1991, Fontana, with Oleta Adams) — producer
  • Multiple singles including Break It Down Again (1993), Cold (1993), God’s Mistake (1995).

Pearl Jam (1991–present)

Palmer mixed Ten (1991, Epic Associated) — Pearl Jam’s debut and one of the best-selling rock albums of the 1990s. The Discogs credit, while listed in an unusual way (he appears with novelty credits for “fire extinguisher” and “pepper shaker” in addition to Mixed By), represents a significant mixing credit. He has continued as a mixer for Pearl Jam releases and live material across 170+ entries on Discogs.

Major Mainstream Crossover Work (1995–2010s)

By the mid-1990s Palmer had become a mixer-for-hire across hard rock and alternative:

  • U2 — Mixed several tracks on All That You Can’t Leave Behind (2000, Island), their commercial comeback album. Also involved in later U2 releases.
  • Ozzy Osbourne — Produced and played on Down To Earth (2001, Epic), including contributions of rhythm guitar, acoustic guitar, keyboards, drums, and backing vocals. A notably hands-on production role.
  • HIM (2) (Finnish goth-metal) — Produced and mixed Dark Light (2005, Sire), Venus Doom (2007, Sire), and many associated singles. HIM is one of his most prolific Discogs relationships (163+ entries).
  • Porcupine Tree — Mixed various releases (2002–2023, 30+ entries).
  • Sepultura — Mixed releases (1998–2022).
  • The Cranberries — Production/recording credit on To The Faithful Departed (reissue, Island).
  • Goo Goo Dolls — Produced their work around 2010 (16 entries).

The Psychedelic Furs — Made Of Rain (2020)

Mixed and provided additional production on The Psychedelic Furs’ Made Of Rain (2020, Cooking Vinyl) — the band’s first studio album in 29 years. The album was produced by Richard Fortus (who also played guitar). Palmer’s mixing role was one of the most prominent credits on a post-punk-adjacent record in his later career.
