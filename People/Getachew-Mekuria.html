Getachew Mekuria

Discogs ID: 6035111 Real name: ጌታቸው መኩሪያ Born: March 14, 1935, Yifat, Ethiopia Died: April 4, 2016, Ethiopia Also spelled: Getatchew Mekuria; Getatchew Mekurya; Géhonetatchèw Mèkurya; Gétachèw Mékurya; Gétatchèw Mèkurya; Gétatchèw Mékurya

Ethiopian tenor saxophonist; a central figure in the golden era of Ethio-jazz. Key collaborator with The Ex.

Overview

Getachew Mekuria was a veteran Ethiopian saxophonist and composer, born in Yifat, Ethiopia in 1935. He rose to prominence as a member of two landmark orchestras in Addis Ababa: the Police Orchestra (also known as the Police Force Band) and the Haile Selassie I Theatre Orchestra, a government-sponsored ensemble that performed internationally in the 1960s and early 1970s — including appearances in Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Senegal (at the 1966 Festival Mondial des Arts Nègres), and Mexico (at the 1968 Summer Olympics). Other Police Orchestra members included Alemayehu Eshete and Fekade Amde Maskal; the Theatre Orchestra included Bereket Mengistaab and Beyene Fire.

His earliest solo recordings date to 1972 on the Ethiopian imprint of Philips, including the single Antchi Hoye / Shilela and the album Getatchew Mekuria And His Saxophone. He was rediscovered internationally when Francis Falceto — producer and curator of the Éthiopiques series — compiled his work for Éthiopiques 14: Negus Of Ethiopian Sax (2003, Buda Musique), drawing on his 1972 recordings and featuring musicians from those original sessions including Alemayehu Eshete (bass), Girma Beyene (organ, piano), and Getachew Degefu (organ, piano).

The second act of his international career came through his association with Dutch post-punk group The Ex. The two parties recorded Moa Anbessa (2006, Terp Records) — a landmark cross-cultural collaboration that brought together Mekuria’s saxophone and compositions with The Ex’s instrumentation and English lyrics, supplemented by an ensemble of guests. A follow-up, Y’Anbessaw Tezeta (2012, Terp Records), expanded the cast. A 2007 French DVD document, 11 Ethio-Punk Songs, captured the group in concert. Mekuria died in Ethiopia on April 4, 2016.
