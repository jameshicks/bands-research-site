Amon Tobin

Aug 22, 20268 min read

Amon Tobin

Discogs ID: 322
Real name: Amon Adonai Santos de Araújo Tobin
Born: 7 February 1972, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Also records as: Cujo (Discogs ID: 654), Only Child Tyrant, Figueroa (3), Player (2), Stone Giants

Brazilian-Canadian electronic musician and producer. Signed to Ninja Tune from 1996 to 2015; subsequently self-releasing on his own Nomark Records. Began his career in Brighton, UK under the alias Cujo on the independent label NINEBARecords; moved from jazz-sample-heavy breakbeat to increasingly cinematic, sound-design-driven electronic music across two decades.

Biography

Origins and the Cujo Years (1995–1996)

Tobin grew up in Rio de Janeiro and relocated to Brighton, UK in the mid-1990s. Under the alias Cujo he released a series of singles and EPs on NINEBARecords starting with the Curfew EP (1995, catno NOZB002). The debut Cujo album Adventures In Foam followed in 1996 (NOZACD003), a record that reached the US through Shadow Records. The Cujo material was rooted in breakbeat and jungle, built around aggressive sample manipulation.

Ninja Tune Signing and Early Albums (1996–2000)

In late 1996 Tobin switched to recording under his own name and signed to Ninja Tune. The debut single Creatures (1996) introduced the new alias, and Bricolage (1997, zenCD29) followed shortly after — its title (French for “DIY construction from available materials”) signalling the approach: layered jazz and bossa nova samples reconstructed into textural breakbeat. The sleeve credits designer Openmind and sources a Claude Lévi-Strauss text for liner notes, an early indicator of Tobin’s interest in framing his work within broader aesthetic and intellectual contexts.

Piranha Breaks E.P. and Chomp Samba (both 1997) followed on Ninja Tune. Permutation (1998, zen36) deepened the jazz-sampling approach, with Openmind again on design. 4 Ton Mantis (2000) and Supermodified (2000, zen CD48) concluded this first phase; Supermodified was mastered by Voda, with Openmind credited for the “Supermodified Architecture” artwork.

Around this period Tobin co-produced “Samba Da Benção” for Bebel Gilberto’s breakthrough album Tanto Tempo (Ziriguiboom, 2000) and later contributed a remix to Tanto Tempo Remixes (Ziriguiboom, 2001).

Cinematic Turn and Splinter Cell (2002–2004)

Out From Out Where (2002, ZEN CD70) marked a significant shift toward orchestral, cinematic textures — less obvious sample-chop, more composed density. Openmind continued as visual collaborator.

Chaos Theory — The Soundtrack To Tom Clancy’s Splinter Cell (2004, Ninja Tune ZENCD 100 FAN) was a landmark commission: a full video game score that brought Tobin’s sound to an enormous mainstream audience and demonstrated his approach could carry extended, functional narrative music.

Solid Steel Presents Amon Tobin Recorded Live (2004, Ninja Tune) documented his live performance context for the Solid Steel radio series.

Foley Room and Field Recording (2007)

Foley Room (2007, ZENCD 121 X) represented a methodological turning point. Where earlier work sampled jazz recordings, Foley Room was built around original field recordings and live performance sessions. Engineer Vid Cousins handled field recording, foley, and tracking. Performers included Patrick Watson (vocals/piano), Pietro Amato (french horn), Sarah Pagé (harp), KJ Sawka (drums), Evan Cranley, and various chamber players. Mastered by Sarah Register. The result was a fully original sound-source record where jazz influence remained structural rather than sample-based.

Kitchen Sink Remixes (2007) and the Bloodstone EP (2007) appeared alongside it on Ninja Tune.

ISAM and Immersive Live Work (2011–2012)

ISAM (2011, ZENDNL168) — mastered by Shawn Hatfield — was conceived alongside an elaborate live audio-visual performance that used large-scale projection-mapped set design. The ISAM Live release (2012) documented the theatrical version. Surge (2012) was a related EP. Chaos Theory Remixed (2011) revisited the Splinter Cell material.

A self-titled compilation Amon Tobin (2012, Ninja Tune) assembled career highlights.

Dark Jovian and Ninja Tune Exit (2015)

Dark Jovian (2015, ZEN12387) — mastered by Twerk — was Tobin’s final release on Ninja Tune, concluding a near-twenty-year relationship with the label.

Two Fingers (2008–2012)

Alongside the Ninja Tune catalog, Tobin co-formed Two Fingers (also listed as 60hz (2) on Discogs, ID 2297320) with Joe Chapman (ID 807230). The project released on Big Dada Recordings (a Ninja Tune sublabel):

  • Two Fingers (2009, Big Dada)
  • Stunt Rhythms (2012, Big Dada)

Two Fingers produced and wrote for rapper Dose One on Instrumentals (Paper Bag Records, 2009) and produced “That Girl” for Shlohmo-adjacent releases on Big Dada.

Eskamon (c. 2010)

Tobin collaborated with Brendan Angelides (ID 799656) as Eskamon (Discogs ID 1775129), releasing Fine Objects (2010, Ancestor).

Nomark Records and Post-Ninja Tune Period (2019–present)

From 2019, Tobin released on his own imprint Nomark Records. Output has been prolific and eclectic:

  • Fear In A Handful Of Dust (2019, Nomark No. 1) — mastered by Shawn Hatfield
  • Long Stories (2019, Nomark No. 3)
  • Fine Strips Of Violence (2019, Nomark)
  • Deep In Time (2020, Nomark)
  • Cruel Like Flint (2020, Nomark)
  • How Do You Live (2021, Nomark No. 8)
  • A Living Room — Music From Meow Wolf’s Omega Mart (2022, Nomark No. 9) — site-specific installation score
  • Nomark Selects, V.1 (2023, Nomark No. X) — curated compilation
  • Nomark Selects, V.2 (2025, Nomark NOXIV)

Also appeared on Brainfeeder (Loophole, 2020) and Vision Recordings (Ithaca, 2020). Scored the film Hole In The Ground (2023, Nomark No. 12). Contributed to a CS:GO music pack (All For Dust, 2020).
