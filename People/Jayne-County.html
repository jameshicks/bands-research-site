Jayne County

American singer, songwriter, actress, visual artist, and pioneer transgender rock performer. Born Wayne Vernoy Rogers, 13 July 1947, Dallas, Georgia. Also credited as Wayne County (Discogs ID: 698219), Wayne Rogers (4), and in various combined credits as Jayne/Wayne County. Primary Discogs artist ID: 343032 (Jayne County).

Overview

Jayne County’s career spans six decades and two stage names that mark a personal and historical turning point in rock music. She came to prominence in the early 1970s as Wayne County, a fixture of the Andy Warhol Factory scene and the proto-punk underground of New York City — playing at Max’s Kansas City and CBGB with her band Wayne County And The Back Street Boys (Discogs ID: 304608), which included Greg Van Cook and Eliot Michaels. In 1972 she formed Queen Elizabeth, one of the earliest proto-punk acts, and was briefly signed to MainMan Artistes, David Bowie’s management firm. No records were produced under that arrangement, though County has maintained that the 1974 stage show “Wayne at the Trucks” influenced Bowie’s Diamond Dogs tour.

In 1977 County relocated to London and formed The Electric Chairs, releasing on Safari Records as Wayne County & The Electric Chairs through 1979. The core lineup on their principal Safari output (1978–79) was County on vocals, Greg Van Cook on lead guitar, Eliot Michaels on guitar, Val Haller on bass, and J.J. Johnson (3) on drums. Henry Padovani — fresh from his departure from The Police — played rhythm guitar on the Storm The Gates Of Heaven sessions and the Blatantly Offenzive EP. The two main Safari LPs were produced by Martin Birch; the singles “Berlin (Long Version)” and “So Many Ways” were produced by David Cunningham of The Flying Lizards. The Electric Chairs appeared in Derek Jarman’s 1978 punk film Jubilee, with County playing the character Lounge Lizard.

In 1979, while in Berlin, County adopted the stage name Jayne County and publicly identified as a woman — making her rock’s first openly transgender performer. The 1980 live album Rock ‘n’ Roll Resurrection (In Concert) was the first release under the Jayne County name.

Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, County continued recording as Jayne County and Jayne County & The Electric Chairs, self-producing most of her later work with a Manchester-based lineup that included Mark Pearson (5) on bass, Paul Wainwright on guitar, and rotating drummers. Her 1993 album Goddess Of Wet Dreams was co-released by ESP-Disk (the legendary New York experimental label) and ZYX Music. Cover graphics for that album were by Derek Jarman (painting by Michael Spencer Jones). She published her autobiography, Man Enough to Be a Woman (Serpent’s Tail), in 1995/1996; it was republished in 2021.

Note on the Ric Ocasek/ZE Records connection: The brief states County recorded for ZE Records/Elektra produced by Ric Ocasek. However, this is a conflation: Saturn Strip (1983), Just A Million Dreams (1985), and Video Babe were albums by Alan Vega (of Suicide) on ZE/Elektra, not Jayne or Wayne County. No Wayne County or Jayne County releases appear in Discogs on ZE Records. The connection between County and Ocasek is not confirmed by Discogs data.

Research Queue

  • The Ric Ocasek/ZE Records connection listed in the earlier stub (Saturn Strip, Video Babe, Just A Million Dreams) is an error: all three are Alan Vega albums on Elektra/ZE. Confirm whether Wayne County or Jayne County had any actual involvement with ZE Records or Ric Ocasek — is there an interview or contemporaneous press record connecting them as collaborators?
  • Things Your Mother Never Told You (1979) is listed in the Discogs discography for Wayne County (ID: 698219) only on a Canada pressing (Attic, ID: 2870540). The main LP appears to have been a German release on Crystal (5). Confirm whether there was a simultaneous UK Safari Records pressing of this album.
  • The 1974 stage show “Wayne at the Trucks” (later released on Munster Records in 2006 as At The Trucks!) was reportedly filmed by MainMan; the footage has reportedly never been released. Confirm if this film material is documented in any archive or press.
  • County claims “Queenage Baby” was a prototype for Bowie’s “Rebel Rebel” — confirm whether any contemporaneous Bowie interview or press supports or dismisses this claim.
  • Amerikan Cleopatra (1986, Discogs ID: 3809062) appears in the SQL query but was not returned by get_artist_discography for either ID. Confirm whether this is a distinct LP from Private Oyster or an alternate title/pressing.
  • Jools Holland’s appearance on “Hot Blood” (The Electric Chairs, 1978) — confirm whether this was a paid session booking or an informal cameo, and whether Holland has discussed it in any interview.
  • The Blatantly Offenzive EP (Canada, Attic, 1978) appears to be a Canada-only variant distinct from the UK Safari pressing — confirm track listing and whether it is the same master.
