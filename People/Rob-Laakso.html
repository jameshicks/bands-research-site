Rob Laakso

Real name: Robert D. Laakso
Born: 1979
Died: May 5, 2023 (aged 44)
Discogs ID: 173675
Also known as: Doc, Doc Laakso, Doc Rob Laakso, Dr. Rob, Dr. Rob Laakso, R. Laxo, RL

American multi-instrumentalist, record producer, and engineer. Best known as a long-standing member of Kurt Vile’s backing band Kurt Vile & The Violators, and as a contributor to Swirlies. Also played in Diamond Nights and The Wicked Farleys.

Biography

Laakso came up through the Boston-area indie underground, first appearing on record in 1997 with The Wicked Farleys — an indie rock group who released two records on Big Top Records (2) (Ken Theory, 1997; Make It It, 1999). He is credited as “R. Laxo” on Ken Theory, playing guitar and synthesizer alongside Michael Brodeur (guitar/vocals), Mike Cory (bass), and Ken Bernard (drums).

By 2003 he was embedded in the Boston/New York indie collective around Swirlies, contributing engineering, mixing, and performance to Cats Of The Wild: Volume Two (2003, Bubble Core Records). That album was a sprawling collective effort bringing together a large roster of players from the extended Swirlies/Mice Parade orbit.

In parallel, Laakso became part of Diamond Nights, a hard-edged New York rock band (Morgan Phalen, Seth Rumsey, Tim Traynor plus Laakso on lead guitar/synth, credited as “Doc”). Diamond Nights released Popsicle on Kemado Records in 2005.

His most sustained collaboration was with Kurt Vile, beginning no later than 2011 when he appeared as an additional player on Smoke Ring For My Halo (Matador, 2011). He became a full member of Kurt Vile & The Violators — the touring/recording band — and his credits on Vile’s records span performance, engineering, production, mixing, overdubs, and photography across multiple albums from 2011 to 2022. On the live LP It’s A Big World Out There (And I Am Scared) (Matador, 2013) — credited to Kurt Vile & The Violators — he co-produced and mixed several tracks alongside Vile and served as engineer and tracking engineer on specific songs.

Outside the Vile orbit, Laakso contributed additional guitar to Mice Parade’s self-titled 2007 LP (FatCat Records), and again to Lapapọ (2022), playing guitar on the closing track “Good Night” (track 13, the final track of the album). The Lapapọ credit closes out his documented Discogs record — he died on May 5, 2023.
