Britt Walford

American drummer. Louisville, Kentucky. Discogs ID: 135341. Also known as John Britton Walford (full name).

Principal drummer of Slint and Squirrel Bait, two of the defining bands of the Louisville post-hardcore/post-rock lineage. Played drums and sang on The Breeders’ debut Pod (1990) under the pseudonym “Shannon Doughton” (Discogs ID: 515202). Used the alias “Mike Hunt (3)” (Discogs ID: 539485) on the Breeders’ Safari EP (1992). One of the most influential drummers in American underground music of the late 1980s–1990s despite an exceptionally sparse discography as leader.

Biography

Louisville Beginnings: Squirrel Bait (1983–1988)

Walford emerged in Louisville’s hardcore scene in the early 1980s as a teenager. He played drums on the debut self-titled EP by Squirrel Bait (Homestead Records, 1985), though some tracks on that record were credited to fellow drummer Ben Daughtrey — Walford is credited specifically on tracks A4 and B3. By the band’s Kid Dynamite 7” (1986) and Skag Heaven LP (1986/87, Homestead), Daughtrey was the credited drummer and Walford had moved on, though the two shared the kit across that initial period.

Squirrel Bait also connected Walford with Brian McMahan (guitar/vocals) and David Grubbs (guitar), relationships that would shape his entire career. The band’s membership also included Peter Searcy (vocals), Clark Johnson (bass), and Richard Schuler.

Slint (1986–1994 and reunions)

Slint formed out of the Squirrel Bait orbit around 1986, with Walford, McMahan, and David Pajo (guitar) at the core. The debut album Tweez (Jennifer Hartman Records, 1989) was recorded with Ethan Buckler on bass — Buckler was later replaced by Todd Brashear, who appears on Spiderland. The engineer on Tweez was credited under the pseudonym “Some Fuckin’ Derd Niffer.”

Spiderland (Touch And Go, 1991) is the band’s defining document and one of the foundational texts of post-rock. Walford’s credits on the original pressing were uncredited — he is listed as “Drums [Uncredited], Vocals [Uncredited]” in the Discogs record, an unusual choice reflecting the band’s anti-hierarchical approach. The cover photograph was taken by Will Oldham.

Slint disbanded after Spiderland but issued an untitled EP (Touch And Go, 1994) and a live recording. They reunited for All Tomorrow’s Parties in 2005, and in 2014 issued LSXX (4AD), a 2xLP anniversary reissue, and a 7” single Breadcrumb Trail / Good Morning, Captain (Touch And Go, 2019) which showed the core four-piece (McMahan, Walford, Pajo, Brashear) still capable of recording together.

King Kong (3) (c. 1989)

Overlapping with Slint’s early years, Walford was part of the Louisville experimental collective King Kong (3). The lineup on the Movie Star 7” (1989, self-released) included Walford (drums, vocals), Pajo (drums, guitar, cover art), Buckler (bass, guitar, vocals), and McMahan (guitar, vocals) — essentially a shuffled Slint with additional members. The King Kong (3) roster at various times also included David Grubbs, Darren Rappa, Ray Rizzo, Peter Townsend, and Todd Hildreth, making it more of an open collective than a fixed band.

The Breeders Sessions (1990–1992)

Walford played drums on Pod (4AD, 1990), the debut album by The Breeders, credited as “Shannon Doughton.” The record was engineered by Steve Albini and featured Kim Deal and Tanya Donelly on guitars/vocals, Josephine Wiggs on bass, and Carrie Bradley on violin. The pseudonym is thought to reflect Walford’s desire not to be publicly associated with the project; the name has never been officially explained.

He returned for the Safari EP (4AD, 1992) under a second alias, “Mike Hunt (3).” The Safari lineup also included Kelley Deal, marking a shift toward what would become the Last Splash configuration — though Walford did not continue with that record.

Post-Slint: The For Carnation, Evergreen (5), Various (1993–2000s)

After Slint’s dissolution, Walford appeared in a loose network of Louisville/Chicago-adjacent projects:

  • Palace Brothers — Credited on There Is No-One What Will Take Care Of You (Drag City, 1993) playing drums, electric guitar, and bass, alongside Will Oldham and others. Also credited as a featured player on Lost Blues And Other Songs (1997).
  • The For Carnation — Credited as “Written-By [Additional Writers], Performer [Performers]” on the self-titled LP (Touch And Go, 2000) by The For Carnation (Brian McMahan’s post-Slint project). The core band did not include Walford as a regular member, but he contributed.
  • Evergreen (5) — Louisville band (also recorded earlier as Cinderblock (3)). Walford credited for drums [uncredited] and keyboards [uncredited] on the self-titled LP (Hi-Ball Records, 1996) and drums on the Pants Off / The Queen Song 7” (Hi-Ball Records, 1994). Other members: Sean McLoughlin (vocals/guitar), Tim Ruth (guitar), Troy Lee Cox (bass). Recorded by James Murphy and Nicolas Vernhes.
  • King Kong (3) continued — The compilation Funny Farm (Drag City, 1993, keyboards credit) shows continued activity. Note: Pants Off / The Queen Song (Hi-Ball Records, 1994) is an Evergreen (5) release, not a King Kong (3) release.

Watter (2) (c. 2014–2017)

Walford was a founding member of Watter (2), an ambient/post-rock group on Temporary Residence Limited. The core members were Walford (drums), Zak Riles (guitars, oud, synthesizers, drum machine), and Tyler Trotter (keyboards, effects, lap steel). The band released two LPs:

  • This World (Temporary Residence, 2014) — also featured guest appearances by Tony Levin (bass on one track), Rachel Grimes (piano), Todd Cook (bass), Cheyenne Mize (violin), and Dane Waters (voice).
  • History of the Future (Temporary Residence, 2017) — expanded cast including Bundy K. Brown (bass), Nathan Salsburg (guitar), Rachel Grimes (piano), Jacob Duncan (tenor sax), and Shawn Trail (marimba).

Watter (2)‘s music sat in the quieter, textural end of the post-rock spectrum — contemplative rather than dynamic.

Maurice (51)

Maurice (51) (Discogs ID: 4252570) is a later configuration reuniting Walford with Brian McMahan, David Pajo, Ned Oldham, Mike Bucayu, and Sean Garrison. Minimal Discogs documentation; no release credits found in database.

Recent Activity (2024–2025)

Walford appears on Laura Cantrell’s Nobody Loves You More (4AD, 2024), back in a Breeders context (that album’s Breeders lineup). He is also credited as “Guest Drums” on After Alter (Pelagic Records, 2025), showing continued session activity.
