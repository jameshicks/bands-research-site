Janel Leppin

Discogs artist ID: 2427162. Cellist, multi-instrumentalist, vocalist, and composer based in the Washington DC area. Active on record since at least 2006. Primary projects: the duo Janel & Anthony with guitarist Anthony Pirog (Discogs ID: 2726356), and the punk/post-punk band Priests (2). Has also released solo and collaborative records on Ideologic Organ and Atlantic Rhythms, and contributed string arrangements and session cello to releases on Dischord Records, Sacred Bones Records, and Sub Pop.

Name variations on Discogs include “Janel Leppin-Pirog,” indicating she and Pirog are personal partners as well as musical collaborators.

Overview

Leppin’s recorded work spans freely improvised chamber music, experimental electronics, contemporary classical, and punk. Her baseline instrument is cello — played acoustically, modified, and looped — but she also plays piano, synthesizer, koto, hammered dulcimer, violin, and rhythm guitar across different projects.

Her longest-running recording partnership is with Anthony Pirog in Janel & Anthony, a duo that began releasing music in 2006 and signed to Cuneiform Records for their 2012 album Where Is Home. The duo’s 2024 album New Moon In The Evil Age expanded the core duo sound with additional players (Devin Hoff on bass, Dr. Ali Analouei on daf/tonbak).

In parallel, Leppin is a member of Priests (2), the Washington DC post-punk band, and appears in the credits as a co-songwriter on their 2019 album The Seduction Of Kansas (Sister Polygon Records, produced by John Congleton). She is also a member of Stylus (15), a DC-area improv/experimental group also featuring Pirog, Chester Hawkins, and Jeff Bagato; and of Mellow Diamond (Discogs ID: 5701170), for which she wrote all music and lyrics on the 2017 album American God.

Her 2020 work was particularly dense: she appeared as a primary artist on two collaborative albums — Sister Mirror (with Susan Alcorn and Meghan Habibzai, on Atlantic Rhythms) and The Heart Sutra (with Susan Alcorn, on Ideologic Organ, which she also curated and arranged). The latter featured an ensemble including Anthony Pirog, Eyvind Kang, Skúli Sverrisson, Doug Wieselman, and Jessika Kenney.

Her solo debut under her own name, To March Is To Love (2024, Cuneiform Records), features cello and piano from Leppin alongside Sarah Hughes (alto saxophone), Luke Stewart (2) (bass), Larry Ferguson (4) (drums), and Anthony Pirog (guitar).
