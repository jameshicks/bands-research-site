August Darnell

Real name: Thomas August Darnell Browder Aliases: Kid Creole (2), Thomas Browder Discogs ID: 55005 Born: August 12, 1950, The Bronx, New York City (source; Wikipedia corroborates, as of 2026-06-01). His father was from Savannah, Georgia; his mother from South Carolina with Caribbean and Italian heritage. The “born in Montreal” backstory is a fiction tied to the Kid Creole persona. Active: 1965 (first band, The In-Laws, with Stony Browder Jr.) – present

Multi-instrumentalist (bass, guitar), vocalist, lyricist, producer, and theatrical conceptualist. The creative force behind Kid Creole And The Coconuts and before that a core member of Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band. Half-brother of Stony Browder (Dr. Buzzard’s bandleader); described on Discogs as cousin of Coati Mundi (Andy Hernandez) — see note below under Web sources. The primary commercial engine of ZE Records from 1980 onward, where his work as both artist and producer defined the label’s Latin-funk-Caribbean aesthetic.

Biography

Early career and Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band (1971–1979)

Darnell performed from 1965 in a group called The In-Laws alongside Stony Browder Jr. He later attended Hofstra University and worked as an English instructor at Alverta B. Gray Schultz Middle School in Hempstead, New York, before committing fully to music. He began writing songs under the name Thomas Browder; his earliest Discogs credit is a 1971 songwriter appearance on a P.I.P. Records single (“Oh, Black Day / The First Good Thing”). The project that brought him to wider attention was Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band, a retro big-band/disco ensemble anchored by his half-brother Stony Browder Jr., co-founded in 1974. Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-01

The group’s self-titled debut (RCA Victor, 1976) was produced by Sandy Linzer, with orchestrations by Charlie Calello and Stony Browder. Darnell played bass and sang lead, with Cory Daye and Stony also on vocals, Coati Mundi on vibraphone, Don Armando Bonilla on percussion, and Mickey Sevilla on drums. The single “Cherchez La Femme” (written by Darnell and Stony Browder) became a disco-era hit and remains the song most associated with that band. The debut album charted in the Top 40, received gold certification, and earned a Grammy nomination. Longreads, 2019

Tommy Mottola of Champion Entertainment (then best known for managing Hall & Oates) was the band’s manager from the outset — he leveraged his RCA relationship to secure their record deal after the label had initially rejected them. Darnell wrote Mottola into the lyric of “Cherchez La Femme” five times; Mottola later recalled the experience in his autobiography Hitmaker. Darnell said of Mottola: “We clicked immediately. He too was from the Bronx. He was a great manager.” Stony Browder Jr. was less enthusiastic about the arrangement. Longreads; Musical Urbanism, Vassar

The second album, Meets King Penett (RCA Victor, 1978), saw Darnell as associate producer and bass player alongside Stony Browder’s production and arrangements. A third album, James Monroe H.S. Presents Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band Goes To Washington (Elektra, 1979), was produced by David Wolfert and Gary Klein with Darnell contributing as songwriter but moving away from a production role — the seeds of an independent path were already in place. By this point Mottola had broken off his management ties with the Savannah Band: “There was so much disarray around the Savannah Band [by 1979] that I broke off management ties,” he later said. Longreads, 2019

Between Dr. Buzzard albums Darnell also produced Gichy Dan’s Beachwood - 9 (RCA Victor, 1979, with horn and string arrangements credited to him) and had a production/writing credit on Machine’s “There But For The Grace Of God Go I” (RCA Victor/Hologram, 1979) — that track became a cult disco-funk item.

ZE Records and Kid Creole And The Coconuts peak years (1979–1985)

By 1979 Darnell was aligned with ZE Records, the New York boutique co-founded by Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban. His first production credit there was Don Armando’s Second Avenue Rhumba Band (ZE, 1979), executive-produced by Darnell. He also produced and played on Gichy Dan’s Beachwood - 9’s album that year.

Under the name Kid Creole (2) and fronting Kid Creole And The Coconuts, Darnell released a run of albums that made him one of the more distinctive figures of early-1980s pop:

Off The Coast Of Me (Island/ZE, 1980) The debut Kid Creole album. Produced and mixed by Darnell. Core band: Darnell himself credited as “Tommy Browder” on bass, Franz Krauns (guitar), Peter Schott (piano), Winston Grennan (drums), Andrew Lloyd (2) (percussion), Coati Mundi as “Coatimundi” (vibes). Vocals: Adriana Kaegi, Brooksie Wells, Fonda Rae, Lourdes Cotto. Orchestrations: Andy Hernandez (most tracks), Stony Browder (one track). Engineering: Bob Blank and Julian McBrowne. Label directed by Michael Zilkha.

Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places (Island/ZE, 1981) Produced by Darnell; co-produced and associate-produced by Andy Hernandez (“Sugar-Coated Andy Hernandez”). Darnell and Hernandez co-arranged and co-wrote the music. Executive producer: Michael Zilkha. Chief engineer: Michael Frondelli. The album was conceived as a theatrical narrative (“based on a story” by Darnell). Cast listed includes Cheryl Poirier, Carol Coleman, Lori Eastside, Yogi Horton, Steve Kroon, Libre (Conjunto Libre), Adriana Kaegi, Andrew Lloyd (2), Peter Schott, Winston Grennan, and many others. Management at this time: Tommy Mottola / Champion Entertainment Organization Inc. Despite having stepped away from the Savannah Band in 1979, Mottola renewed his management of Darnell through the early Kid Creole years. His credit appears on Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places (1981) and is confirmed through Doppelganger (1983). The relationship was marked by ongoing friction between Mottola and ZE’s Michael Zilkha — described in period reporting as “long and legendary” feuding. Darnell ultimately had to buy Mottola out: “I don’t want to sound ungrateful, but it had to happen. I just couldn’t deal with it anymore.” Longreads, 2019; Kid Creole/Carol Cooper, 1983

Tropical Gangsters (ZE Records, 1982; released as Wise Guy in the US) Produced and directed by Darnell; horn and string arrangements by Darnell and Carlos Franzetti, who also conducted. Engineers: Bob Blank and Michael Frondelli (chief), Michel Sauvage and Bruce Buchhalter (assistant). Executive producer: Michael Zilkha. Vocals/featured players include Coati Mundi, Cheryl Poirier, Winston Grennan, Yogi Horton, Adriana Kaegi, Carol Coleman, Andrew Lloyd (2); special guest star Cory Daye (a nod to the Dr. Buzzard origins). Photography: George DuBose. Sleeve design: Bruno Tilley. The album contains “Annie, I’m Not Your Daddy,” “Stool Pigeon,” and “I’m A Wonderful Thing, Baby” — the singles that peaked Kid Creole’s UK chart presence.

Doppelganger (ZE/Island/Sire, 1983) Produced by Darnell; associate producer Andy Hernandez (“Sugar Coated Andy Hernandez”). Recorded at Electric Lady Studios and Daily Planet Studios, New York. Engineers: Joe Barbaria, Michael H. Brauer, Julian McBrowne (chief). Mixed by Michael H. Brauer at Media Sound for MHB Productions. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Photography: Peter Ashworth. Management and Direction: Tommy Mottola / Champion Entertainment Organization Inc. Core band (“Boys in the Band”): Kid Creole, Adriana Kaegi, Coati Mundi, Peter Schott, Carol Coleman, Cheryl Poirier, Charles Lagond, Dave Span, Ken Fradley, Lee Robertson, Mark Mazur, Taryn Hagey, Bongo Eddie. Session musicians (“Extras”) include: Andy Gonzalez, Buddy Williams, Dave Friedman, Francisco Centeno, Jimmy Rippetoe, Joe Mannozzi, Jose Madera, Manny Oquendo, Phillipe Saisse, Steve Kroon. Special appearances: Cory Daye, Conjunto Libre, Lori Eastside, Perri Lister, Gichy Dan, Dutch Robinson, and Daryl Hall (on “Bongo Eddie’s Lament”). Singles: “There’s Something Wrong in Paradise” (Sep 1983), “The Lifeboat Party” (Nov 1983), “If You Wanna Be Happy” (US only, Oct 1983). Source: Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-01

In Praise Of Older Women… And Other Crimes (Sire, 1985) Produced by Darnell. Engineers: Joe Barbaria, Michel Sauvage (assistant). Mixed by Argyle Neft and Steven Stanley. Mastered by Bob Ludwig.

During this period Darnell also produced several other ZE artists:

  • Cristina — self-titled debut (Island/ZE, 1980): produced and wrote all material; Andy Hernandez arranged and conducted; Bob Blank engineered; Michael Zilkha directed; band included Carol Coleman (bass), Ralph Schuckett (piano), Milton Cardona (percussion), Mickey Martinez (drums).
  • Funkapolitan — produced their single for London Records (1982)
  • Various ZE singles and albums through Antilles/ZE and Island/ZE in 1980–81

ZE Records and Michel Esteban

ZE Records was co-founded by Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban. Their distribution arrangement with Chris Blackwell’s Island Records (facilitated partly through Esteban’s connection to Anna Wintour, then Blackwell’s girlfriend) gave ZE global reach. The label’s influence peaked around 1981–82. Esteban left New York in 1982 following a personal dispute with Zilkha, and ZE formally closed in 1984. Esteban continued producing artists (including Lizzy Mercier Descloux and Lio) and later revived ZE as a reissue platform in France from 2003. Wikipedia ZE Records, as of 2026-06-01; TIDAL Magazine. The closure of ZE corresponded with Darnell’s transition to Sire (from 1985) for North American distribution, though Island continued in the UK and Europe.

Mid-career and label transitions (1985–1993)

Kid Creole And The Coconuts continued releasing through the late 1980s and early 1990s on Sire (the parent-distributed ZE partner), then moved to Arista (Hey Mambo, 1988), CBS/Columbia (Private Waters In The Great Divide, 1990; The Sex Of It, 1990; You Shoulda Told Me You Were…, 1991), and then Atoll Music/Ascot in France (To Travel Sideways, 1993). Throughout, Darnell retained production control. Activity slowed in the mid-1990s; 1996 saw two releases on Initial (2), a French label. Darnell later described signing to Sony (Columbia) as “one of the biggest mistakes of my career,” noting that by that point Mottola (then Sony Music’s chairman) “didn’t have time for August Darnell — he’d gone on to bigger things.” Quietus interview

A notable later production: People Will Talk (Elektra Musician, 1989) and Too Cool To Conga (2001).

1996 French releases (The Kid And I / Are You Bad Enough)

In 1995 Darnell traveled internationally as subject of a Canal Plus documentary film, Le Grand Voyage (dir. Frédéric Hadengue). The songs written during that journey were released the following year in France only as The Kid And I on Initial (2), effectively a solo August Darnell album rather than a full Kid Creole And The Coconuts production. Co-producers were Olivier Bloch-Lainé (French film composer) and Joe Hammer (drums, also serving as producer). Core session personnel: Danny Blume (electric guitar), Nicolas Fiszman (bass), Eddie Folk (percussion), Matt Clifford (keyboards), Dwight Brewster (background vocals). The single Are You Bad Enough (b/w other tracks) was also released separately on Initial (2) the same year; songwriting credited to Darnell and Eddie Folk. Rights to the album later reverted to Darnell, who rediscovered the recordings during the pandemic and reportedly prepared them for wider release. Soul and Jazz and Funk interview

Later career (1996–2021)

Activity became more intermittent. The late 1990s brought SPV-distributed releases in Germany (The Conquest Of You, 1997). A 2001 release (Too Cool To Conga) on Orpheus/Pyramid Records is credited as written and produced by Darnell. The 2011 album I Wake Up Screaming (Strut) was the most recent full studio album with significant label backing; Darnell contributed lyrics, music, and production. A final entry in the Discogs database under the Kid Creole banner is Nothin’ Left But The Rest (2C2C, 2021).

The band’s Discogs-documented live record includes a 1982 concert at The Ritz in New York (Island Pictures VHS, 1982), a 2001 Live In Paris video (Classic Pictures Entertainment), and the Live At Rockpalast 1982 release (MIG, 2012).

Darnell relocated to Europe during the 1980s and subsequently lived in Denmark, Sweden, and Maui. His marriage to Adriana Kaegi (a Coconuts vocalist) lasted 1978–1991; he married Eva Tudor-Jones in 2019. Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-01

Theatrical work

Darnell’s theatrical ambitions were woven into his music from the beginning — Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places (1981) was conceived as a narrative theatrical piece and received a stage production at the New York Public Theater (under Joseph Papp), receiving rave reviews. Papp originally proposed mounting it on Broadway in 1982 but was held back by script concerns and musical feasibility questions; the project stalled at that stage. Longreads, 2019; Carol Cooper, 1982

In 2016 Darnell premiered Cherchez La Femme: A Musical Excuse at La MaMa Experimental Theatre Club (Ellen Stewart Theatre, 66 East 4th Street, New York), 19 May – 12 June 2016. Book by Darnell and Vivien Goldman; music by Stony Browder Jr. and Darnell; directed by Angie Kristic; choreography by Kyndra Reevey; costumes by Adriana Kaegi. The show is a fictional account of a band called Caufy Keeps and the Lemon Drops, drawn from Darnell’s real-life experiences; set in New York and the Caribbean in the 1980s; themes include abandonment, sibling rivalry, and unrequited love. Cast included Jacira Araujo, Aja Downing, Isaac Gay, Christopher Hanford, Kristina Hanford, Julius Hollingsworth, Traci Michelle, Molly McCloskey, CB Murray, Julia Neveu, Antonio Rowland, Carey Segal, Nerses Stamos, Jenna Velichko, and Skyler Volpe. TheaterMania; La MaMa blog, 2016
