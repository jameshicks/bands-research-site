Overview

Chris Thomson is a Washington D.C. punk musician who served as bassist for Ignition and co-founded Circus Lupus, where he became the primary singer and lyricist. Active since 1986, he has worked almost exclusively within the Dischord Records orbit, with additional releases on Touch and Go and Simple Machines. Beyond Ignition and Circus Lupus, he has appeared in Coffin Pricks, Fury, and Las Mordidas, accumulating 83 credits alongside frequent collaborators Don Zientara, Eli Janney, and Ian and Alec MacKaye.

Notes (web)

Circus Lupus disbanding and aftermath

Circus Lupus split in the summer of 1993. Sources describe the cause in general terms: “constant touring and the normal hardships of being an underground band finally took its toll.” No public statement from Thomson or other members giving a more specific reason has surfaced in interviews or press. (Trouserpress snippet; Amoeba Music bio snippet via search result)

After the split the band divided into two short-lived offshoots: Casebolt, Lorinczi, and Hamley stayed together as Antimony, while Thomson formed Las Mordidas — described as a “one-single groove-punk quartet” — with ex-Fidelity Jones members Dug E. Bird and Jerry Busher. (Trouserpress snippet) Thomson and Hamley later reunited in The Monorchid and then Skull Kontrol.

Fury (7) — a pre-Circus Lupus project, not a subsequent one

Note on the brief’s framing: Fury predates Circus Lupus; it is not a project Thomson joined after the band. Fury was a Washington D.C. one-off in 1989, formed as a “loose experiment with no intentions beyond being a diversion” by members of Swiz and Ignition (5). The lineup was Thomson on vocals (his “singing debut”), Jason Farrell on guitar, Shawn Brown on bass, and Alex Daniels on drums. The band existed for a few months, wrote six songs, played two shows, and produced a 12-minute recording later released as the Resurrection EP on Jade Tree. (Jade Tree band page; Tony Rettman interview Pt. IV, in which Thomson confirms: “When Fury and Ignition wrapped up, I went off to Madison thinking I would take a long break from music and focus on school. After about three or four months, I fell in with the Circus Lupus folks and that started going.“)

Coffin Pricks — post-DC, Chicago period

After stints in The Monorchid and Skull Kontrol, Thomson relocated from Washington D.C. to Chicago, where he fronted Red Eyed Legends. Coffin Pricks was then initiated by guitarist Ryan Weinstein (Cavity, Red Eyed Legends) as a vehicle to work with Thomson, Jeff Rice (Ottawa, Calvary, Daylight Robbery), and Chay Lawrence (Bob Tilton, Tribute) on bass for live shows. Thomson’s stated attraction was the band’s solid musical foundation: “Ryan is the musical generator and Chay and Jeff are rock-solid… I just focus on what I can bring to it and hopefully take the lessons I’ve learned.” He has described his particular strength as knowing “how to do one thing really well and that’s singing to this kind of music,” suggesting the aggressive, direct sound was the draw. (Tim Kinsella blog interview, 2012; Chicago Reader — “Coffin Pricks come together” snippet)

The band released a single on Stationary Heart Recordings in 2012, played several Chicago shows, and wrote an album’s worth of material — much of it unrecorded before the band went dormant. Semi-Perfect Crimes was eventually released in 2024 on L. G. Records (5). (Scenepointblank, March 2024)

Rettman interview series — scope confirmed (2026-10-20)

The Tony Rettman / No Idols Substack series on Chris Thomson comprises exactly four parts (Parts I–IV, published April–June 2021). A site:rettman.substack.com search returns only those four URLs; no Part V or later installment exists. (Pt I · Pt II · Pt III · Pt IV)

Chronological coverage of each part, per fetch:

  • Pt I–II: Thomson’s childhood, early DC scene entry, Lunch Meat formation (fall 1984), Soulside split.
  • Pt III: Closes at the formation of Circus Lupus — Thomson describes how the band formed in Madison, WI, and his transition from guitarist to singer.
  • Pt IV: Covers the Fury/Ignition period and ends at the moment Thomson joined Circus Lupus: “When Fury and Ignition wrapped up, I went off to Madison thinking I would take a long break from music and focus on school. After about three or four months, I fell in with the Circus Lupus folks and that started going.”

The series ends before the Circus Lupus active years. Neither Part III nor Part IV nor any subsequent part addresses why the band disbanded in 1993 — the series stops just as Circus Lupus is getting started and no further installments were published. The question of Thomson’s own account of the 1993 breakup cannot be answered from this source series.
