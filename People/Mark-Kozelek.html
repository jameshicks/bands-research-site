Mark Kozelek

Born January 24, 1967, Massillon, Ohio. Vocalist, guitarist, songwriter. Discogs ID: 264250. Name variations on Discogs: Kozelek, M. Kozelek, M.Kozelek, Mark Kozalek.

Led Red House Painters (4AD, 1992–2001), a slowcore/folk-rock group formed in San Francisco; later continued under the name Sun Kil Moon (2002–present), primarily through his own imprint Caldo Verde Records. Also listed as a “member” of Stillwater (Discogs ID: 652044) — the fictional band from Cameron Crowe’s film Almost Famous (2000); Kozelek appeared as an actor playing bassist Larry Fellow, not as a musician on the recordings. Co-wrote material on His Name Is Alive’s Stars On E.S.P. (1996, 4AD) — credited at album level (tracks 1–14) and with a specific track-level co-write credit on track 13 “Across The Street” — connecting two 4AD acts at their labelmate peak.

Era 1 — Red House Painters on 4AD (1989–2001)

Red House Painters formed in San Francisco in 1989 around Kozelek as vocalist and principal songwriter. The group signed to 4AD and released their debut Down Colorful Hill in 1992. The classic lineup comprised:

  • Mark Kozelek — vocals, guitar
  • Gordon Mack — guitar
  • Anthony Koutsos — drums
  • Jerry Vessel — bass
  • Phil Carney — guitar

The 4AD years produced six studio albums plus the Shock Me EP, all characterized by long-form slowcore arrangements, Kozelek’s elliptical confessional lyrics, and a debt to Tim Buckley’s ghostly tenor. Two albums were released in the same year under the identical title Red House Painters (1993): the “Rollercoaster” album (4AD 9 45256-2) and the “Bridge” album (4AD 9 45441-2), distinguished by their cover images. For Songs For A Blue Guitar (1996) the band moved briefly to Island Records / Supreme Recordings before returning to 4AD for the posthumous-feeling Old Ramon (2001, Sub Pop).

Label Shifts: 4AD → Island/Supreme → Sub Pop (Web sources)

Why Red House Painters left 4AD for Songs For A Blue Guitar (1996):

Songs For A Blue Guitar was recorded while the band were still under contract to 4AD, but 4AD declined to release it and terminated the contract. Two accounts circulate:

  1. The guitar-solo story: Label founder Ivo Watts-Russell was reportedly unhappy with the lengthy guitar solos on “Make Like Paper” and the Paul McCartney cover “Silly Love Songs.” Kozelek allegedly offered to release the album under his own name rather than as a Red House Painters record, but this was also rejected. (Wikipedia — Songs for a Blue Guitar, as of 2026-07-13)
  2. The U.S. branch friction story: A more structural explanation points to strained relations between Kozelek and 4AD’s American operation, which was under Warner Bros. control at the time. Other 4AD artists (including Warren Defever of His Name Is Alive) reportedly voiced similar frustrations with U.S. management during this era. (Wikipedia — Songs for a Blue Guitar, as of 2026-07-13)

Watts-Russell later said informally that dropping Red House Painters was “the worst decision that 4AD ever took” (or, in another reported formulation, “probably the stupidest decision I made at 4AD”). (Wikipedia — Songs for a Blue Guitar, as of 2026-07-13)

Following the split, the band signed with Supreme Recordings, a new label owned by filmmaker John Hughes and distributed by Island Records. Songs For A Blue Guitar was released July 22–23, 1996.

Why Old Ramon ended up on Sub Pop (2001):

Old Ramon was recorded fall 1997–spring 1998 — i.e. before Songs For A Blue Guitar was commercially available — and was ready for release in 1998. However, the major-label consolidations of the late 1990s (Island/PolyGram being absorbed into the Universal Music Group empire) left the band effectively without a functioning label home. The album sat unreleased for approximately three years, becoming known as a “lost” album. Kozelek eventually purchased the master recordings back from Island, and Sub Pop then signed the band to release Old Ramon on April 10, 2001. (Wikipedia — Old Ramon, as of 2026-07-13)

The return of Old Ramon to 4AD was not an option: 4AD had severed the contract in 1995–96, and the masters were held by Island/Universal, not by 4AD. Sub Pop was a straightforward independent home willing to release a completed album once Kozelek reacquired the rights.

Era 2 — Solo and Transitional (2000–2003)

During the Red House Painters’ dissolution, Kozelek released covers collections and live material under his own name via Badman Recording Co. (Discogs ID: 24590):

  • Rock ‘N’ Roll Singer (2000) — AC/DC covers, Discogs ID: 491534
  • If You Want Blood (2001) — further AC/DC covers, Discogs ID: 887957
  • What’s Next To The Moon (2001) — more AC/DC covers, Discogs ID: 694994

These releases established a pattern of covers LPs (John Denver covers followed later) alongside the Sun Kil Moon project.

Era 3 — Sun Kil Moon Launch (2002–2007)

Sun Kil Moon formed in 2002 and released the debut on Vinyl Films and Jetset Records (ID: 3327). The Discogs-listed members of Sun Kil Moon are: Anthony Koutsos, Ben Boye, Chris Connolly, Geoff Stanfield, Jerry Vessel, Mark Kozelek, Mike Stevens (9), Neil Halstead, Nick Zubeck, Phil Carney, Ramon Fermin, Steve Shelley, Tim Mooney.

Era 4 — Caldo Verde and Prolific Output (2008–present)

From 2008 onward Kozelek released prolifically through Caldo Verde — often multiple albums per year, including live records, collaboration LPs, and covers projects. Key studio albums:

  • April (2008) — Discogs ID: 1291344
  • Admiral Fell Promises (2010) — Discogs ID: 2350442; nylon-string guitar focused
  • Among The Leaves (2012) — Discogs ID: 3492346; criticized at the time for stream-of-consciousness lyrics

His Name Is Alive Connection

On His Name Is Alive’s Stars On E.S.P. (4AD CAD 6010, 1996), Kozelek is credited as “Written-By” at the album level for tracks 1–14 alongside Denise James, Ian Masters (Pale Saints), Erika Hoffmann, the ESP Beetles, and Matthew Smith. At the track level, he receives a specific “Written-By [Cowritten, Uncredited]” credit on track 13, “Across The Street.” The nature of the collective co-write — all these 4AD-connected names pooled across the album — suggests a session or artist exchange rather than a formal band membership. See His Name Is Alive for full personnel on that release.
