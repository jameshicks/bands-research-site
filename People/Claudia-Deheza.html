Claudia Deheza

American musician. Also known as C. M. Deheza, Claudia Maria Deheza. Born October 18, 1978, Guatemala. Twin sister of Alejandra Deheza. Discogs ID: 285568.

Member of On!Air!Library! (2000–2004), School Of Seven Bells (2007–2010), and A Cloud Mireya (with Scott Herren / Prefuse 73). Left SVIIB in October 2010; minimal confirmed activity since.

Background

See Alejandra Deheza for shared biography — the sisters are twins, raised in South Florida after moving from Guatemala at age nine, and relocated to New York City together in 1999. Claudia’s roles across all her projects: vocals, guitar, keyboards.

On!Air!Library! (2000–2004)

Co-founded OAL with Alejandra after a gig slot was offered under a case of mistaken identity. See On!Air!Library! for full discography.

A Cloud Mireya (c. 2002–2006)

Collaborative project with Scott Herren (Prefuse 73; also Savath & Savalas, Delarosa & Asora). Both wrote and played all songs, recording together “for over two years” before release. The sole album, Singular (2006, Eastern Developments EDM-015), is 12 tracks, IDM/ambient in style. The track titles suggest an intimate, introspective register.

Claudia is also credited on Prefuse 73’s Everything She Touched Turned Ampexian (2009, Warp) as an additional player.

School Of Seven Bells (2007–2010)

Co-founded SVIIB with Alejandra and Benjamin Curtis in 2007 in Brooklyn. Left the band in October 2010, mid-tour, for “personal reasons” — the announcement was a brief statement via Facebook; no further public elaboration has been made by Claudia, Alejandra, or Benjamin Curtis. The band continued as a duo.

Contributed vocals to “Music Takes Me” on the posthumous final SVIIB album SVIIB (2016) — cited by Alejandra as meaningful support during that recording.

Also contributed voice on track C4 (“The Only Guitar To Die Alone”) of Prefuse 73’s The Only She Chapters (2011, Warp WARPLP208), alongside Alejandra and Benjamin Curtis on guitar; produced by Roberto Carlos Lange.

See School Of Seven Bells for full discography.

Notes (web) — researched 2026-06-29

SVIIB departure: full picture from press coverage

The departure occurred mid-tour: Claudia was last on stage at the San Francisco date on 2 October 2010. The band’s Facebook page announced on 12 October 2010: “We just wanted to let you all know that Claudia has left the band for personal reasons. In the meantime, we are excited to continue to write and tour as usual, and we’re very much looking forward to seeing you all at our upcoming shows.” No other statement was issued by Claudia, Alejandra, or Benjamin Curtis. (Live4ever, October 2010; Dallas Observer, 2010)

Benjamin Curtis gave no comment on the reasons at the time of departure. A Miami New Times interview (2010) focused on the addition of live drummer Zach Saginaw; Curtis sidestepped questions about the lineup and the band’s publicists described Claudia as “still a part of the band” even after the Facebook post suggested otherwise.

Alejandra Deheza addressed the departure in a Ghostory-era interview (Drowned in Sound, ~May 2012) but gave no substantive reason: “The whole thing of losing a member didn’t affect it at all, if anything it was like now we can make our records.” Curtis added: “It’s a lot more fun when everyone wants to be doing it.” These comments frame the departure positively but say nothing about cause.

In a separate Consequence of Sound interview (March 2012), Alejandra denied that Ghostory lyrics were written about or for Claudia: “Not intentionally. I wasn’t really thinking about that. Not on the surface, anyway.”

A Stereogum long-read (2015) on the posthumous SVIIB album describes the band as a trio that became a duo by 2012 but contains no quotes elaborating on the departure.

A Northern Transmissions interview with Alejandra (~2016) — conducted around the release of SVIIB — mentions that Alejandra and Claudia were in contact and discussing working together again, described as something that hadn’t happened since Disconnect from Desire. Alejandra did not elaborate on the departure in that piece.

Verdict: No public elaboration on the reasons for Claudia’s departure has ever been made by Claudia, Alejandra, or Benjamin Curtis, across any interview or statement found in a thorough search of press coverage from 2010 to 2026. The departure is universally described as “personal reasons” and left unexplained.

Post-SVIIB activity

No solo releases, new band memberships, or credited studio work by Claudia after October 2010 are recorded in any music database (Discogs, AllMusic, RateYourMusic, SecondHandSongs) or reported in press. The Dork credits aggregator lists 39 total credits for Claudia Deheza but all are traceable to OAL, A Cloud Mireya, Prefuse 73/Savath & Savalas, and SVIIB. (Dork/readdork.com)

One previously unrecorded credit surfaced: Claudia Deheza is listed as a featured vocalist on “Era Tu” from Savath & Savalas – Golden Pollen (2007, Eastern Developments). This predates the SVIIB departure and is consistent with the A Cloud Mireya / Scott Herren working relationship, but is not currently in the Other Credits table on this page — see below.

The Northern Transmissions (~2016) reference to Alejandra and Claudia discussing future collaboration produced no confirmed release as of the research date.
