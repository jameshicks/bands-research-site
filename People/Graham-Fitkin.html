Graham Fitkin

Discogs ID: 294479 Real name: Graham Fitkin Born: 1963, Cornwall, UK Role: Composer, pianist, keyboardist Website: fitkin.com

UK contemporary composer and pianist working at the intersection of contemporary classical, minimalism, and electronic music. Known for dense, rhythmically driven works for multiple pianos and mixed ensembles. Member of the duo Fitkin Wall (with harpist Ruth Wall) and of The Will Gregory Moog Ensemble.

Biography

Education and Early Career (to 1990)

Fitkin studied at Nottingham University and subsequently at the Koninklijk Conservatorium in The Hague, Netherlands. He founded Nanquidno, a four-pianist group whose name references a hamlet in West Cornwall near Land’s End — a recurring marker of his Cornish identity.

In 1990 he won the Composer for Dance Award, and his debut solo album Flak appeared that year on Factory Classical, the contemporary-music imprint of Tony Wilson’s Factory Records. The record featured a quartet of pianists performing Fitkin’s compositions: alongside himself, Eleanor Alberga, Errollyn Wallen, and Shelagh Sutherland. Production was handled by John H. West (credited also as editor). The same year, Fitkin’s Flak material was included on the five-disc Factory Classical sampler box set alongside works by I Fagiolini, Rolf Hind, Red Byrd, and others — placing him squarely in Factory’s brief but notable classical-music push.

1990s: Argo Years and Expanded Collaborations

Through the early-to-mid 1990s Fitkin released three further albums on Argo (2), Decca’s contemporary-music subsidiary:

  • Log · Line · Loud (1992) — co-credited with the six-piano ensemble Piano Circus, whose members at the time included Max Richter alongside Ginny Strawson, Kate Heath, Kirsteen Davidson Kelly, Michael Haslam, and Richard Harris. Liner notes by Laurence Crane; produced by Andrew Cornall.
  • Slow / Huoah / Frame (1992) — co-credited with The Smith Quartet and Shelagh Sutherland.
  • Hook · Mesh · Stub · Cud (1993) — co-credited with Ensemble Bash, Icebreaker (5), Delta Saxophone Quartet, and the John Harle Band.
  • Hard Fairy (1994, US pressing) — solo album; engineered by Neil Hutchinson, produced by Christopher Pope.

In 1994–96 he held the position of Composer in Association with the Royal Liverpool Philharmonic Orchestra.

Fitkin Wall

At some point Fitkin formed the ongoing duo Fitkin Wall with harpist and synthesizer player Ruth Wall (Discogs artist ID: 960817). The duo has no releases in the Discogs database beyond their group-member listing, suggesting their output may be primarily live/commission-based or documented elsewhere.

The Will Gregory Moog Ensemble (2024)

Fitkin appears as a member — playing Moog Voyager synthesizer — on Heat Ray (2024, Mute), the release by The Will Gregory Moog Ensemble. The ensemble is led by Will Gregory (of Goldfrapp) and also includes Adrian Utley (Portishead), Ruth Wall, Simon Haram, Hazel Mills, Eddie Parker (3), Viv Hope Scott, Ross Hughes (2), and Simon Clark. The album was co-produced by Will Gregory and Adrian Utley, conducted by Ben Foster, with the BBC National Orchestra of Wales featured. Mastered by Stefan Betke (Pole).
