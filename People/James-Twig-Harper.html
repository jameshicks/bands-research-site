Biography

James Twig Harper (also credited as Twig Harper, T. Harper, Jmstwghrpr, and a handful of other variants) is an American noise and experimental musician based in the Maryland/Baltimore area. He is a central figure in the underground US noise scene that crystallized around the Hanson Records and Heresee labels in the late 1990s and 2000s, and a founding member of Nautical Almanac — a group whose membership also included Carly Ptak, Mat Brinkman, Nate Young, Max Eisenberg, and Sol Meltzer.

Origins and early work (1997–2004)

Harper’s earliest documented release is a 1997 split with Carly Ptak on Hanson Records — Banjo With Ping Pong Electronics (Discogs release 1030709), credited as T. Harper / C. Ptak. The title encodes the working method that would persist throughout his career: acoustic instruments (banjo) combined with homemade or found electronics (ping pong ball amplification, circuit-bent devices, salvaged hardware).

In 2001 he contributed Commodore 64 programming and circuit electronics to the Nautical Almanac release Fluorescent Towers, also on Hanson Records. That same year he appeared as performer on Team Heat (Gods Of Tundra), and his involvement in the group’s operations deepened through the decade.

Under the alias Brain Dead (3) he released Rotting Phases (2004, American Tapes, cat# AM354), a CDr split with Dead Brain — nine untitled tracks of industrial/noise. The same year, as Toiletman (2), he co-released Garbageman Y Toiletman Presenta Remixtro De La Wolf Eyes Extreme / Supra! (2004, Not On Label), a cassette-style CDr split with Garbageman (3) (i.e. Mat Brinkman).

Mid-period: Heresee, Nautical Almanac, and collaborations (2004–2012)

Harper’s own label, Heresee (Discogs label ID: 29549, website: heresee.com), became the primary home for his solo and collaborative output. He mixed Aaron Dilloway’s Dog Jaw (2004, Heresee), credited as “Mixed By [Uncredited].” In 2005 he released a split with Raionbashi on Heresee.

By 2008 Harper was appearing on AA Records releases alongside Nautical Almanac associates: Pitted Water (2008, AA Records) credits him on electronics alongside Max Eisenberg and Nate Young, with the primary artist listed as Hatred (3). The 2009 Nautical Almanac album Melding Linearality (Tusco/Embassy) names the performing lineup as Carly Ptak, Twig Harper, Jungil Hong, and Mat Brinkman, with Harper also handling the final mix and mastering cut.

His two 2009 Heresee solo releases — Music For Higher Dimensional Consciousness and Possible Last Unknowns — were credited to “Twig Harper” and represent the core of his self-released catalog. Both are CDr or tape releases in the small-edition format typical of the noise underground.

In 2010 Harper and Daniel Higgs (of Lungfish, The Skull Defekts) released Clairaudience Fellowship Omphalos/Baltimore on Thrill Jockey — a collaboration that placed Harper on a significantly larger label alongside an artist from the post-hardcore world. The same release documents a shared interest in spirituality and trance-state sound making.

Harper contributed “Infinite Psychic Assistance” to Aaron Dilloway’s Modern Jester (2012, Hanson Records), credited alongside Nate Young, Darksmith, and John Mannion. He also recorded John Olson’s Jazz Mind (2012, Load Records).

His collaboration with Rubber O Cement appeared on Shameful, Effectual, Masterbattalion!! (Brutal Sound Effects, undated), and he produced the Ehse Records release Greezy Man And Stinky Man Meets Smutty Ranks On Tarantula Hill (2011).

Later work (2020–2024)

Harper participated in the large-ensemble Thrill Jockey release The Consuming Flame: Open Exercises In Group Form (2020), credited among the participants alongside a wide range of experimental artists. In 2024 he returned to Hanson Records with the solo release Ha Ha Laughing Record and contributed a remix — “Companion Rises (Twig Harper Remix)” — to a Drag City release. He also contributed photography to Matador’s worldwide compilation The Collective (2024).

Scene Connections

Harper sits at the intersection of several overlapping US underground noise communities:

  • Baltimore/Maryland noise scene: Long-term presence in the Baltimore experimental underground. Heresee (his label) and its website heresee.com served as a hub for his own and associated releases. Key links: Daniel Higgs (Baltimore post-hardcore → free music), Nautical Almanac (Maryland noise collective).
  • Hanson Records orbit: Aaron Dilloway, Nate Young, John Olson, Wolf Eyes adjacent — the Ohio/Ann Arbor noise community overlapping with the Baltimore scene via shared label affiliations and touring.
  • Improvised/experimental crossover: Connections to John Wiese, Bill Nace, Quintron, Robert Turman, and the broader free improv/noise circuit visible in the Rozart Mix release.
  • Thrill Jockey: Two appearances (with Daniel Higgs, and the 2020 group project) place him in the label’s experimental roster alongside post-rock and avant-garde artists.
  • Wolf Eyes / Michigan noise network: The 2004 Toiletman CDr remixing Wolf Eyes material, and the Nautical Almanac / Hanson Records connections, tie him closely to the Michigan noise scene.
