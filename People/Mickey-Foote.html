Mickey Foote

Record producer and engineer. Real name Michael Foote. Born 8 September 1951, Aberdeen, Scotland. Died 2 March 2018, Aberdeen, aged 66. Discogs artist ID: 78722. Also known as “Micky Foote.”

Overview

Produced The Clash’s self-titled debut album (1977, CBS). An early associate of the punk scene, credited as the producer of one of the defining first-generation UK punk records. His raw, garage-level production aesthetic suited the band’s early DIY ethos. Also worked with the band Foote & Mouth (artist ID: 480931).

Also produced Subway Sect’s “Ambition” (Rough Trade, RT 004, 1978) — one of Rough Trade’s earliest releases — and multiple tracks on the Subway Sect A Retrospective (1977–81) (Rough Trade, 1984). His involvement with Subway Sect, like with the Clash, ran through the Bernard Rhodes management connection.

Background and entry into recording

Foote grew up in Aberdeen and attended Newport College of Art (later incorporated into the University of Wales). In early 1973, Joe Strummer — then still John Mellor — came to Newport to visit a friend from the Central School of Art & Design and ended up living with that friend and Foote, who was studying fine art there. [source: theclash.org.uk/joetheearlyyears.htm, via web search snippet — page unreachable directly]

When Strummer later fronted The 101ers in London, Foote joined as the band’s live soundman and road manager. He was present at the May 1976 gig where Strummer saw the Sex Pistols perform — an encounter that catalysed Strummer’s decision to form The Clash. [source: mixonline.com; louderthanwar.com]

There is no documented formal recording or engineering training on record. His entry into production appears to have been entirely through proximity to Strummer and the live-sound work with the 101ers.

The Clash debut album (1977)

When The Clash signed to CBS and entered Whitfield Street Studio No. 3 in February 1977, Foote was brought in as producer. The album was recorded and mixed in approximately three weeks for around £4,000. CBS staff engineer Simon Humphrey handled engineering duties; Foote produced. [source: wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clash_(album)]

The approach was deliberately minimal — no overdubs per Strummer’s insistence, aimed at capturing the live band sound. Foote also mixed The Clash’s live sound on the Sex Pistols’ chaotic Anarchy Tour of the UK (December 1976), which preceded the recording sessions. [source: mixonline.com]

Departure from The Clash

In late 1977, Foote was fired after an incident with the single “Clash City Rockers.” Manager Bernard Rhodes felt the finished mix sounded “a bit flat,” and Foote used varispeed to raise the tape speed, pitching the track up one semitone. Strummer and Mick Jones were in Jamaica when the altered version was released; on hearing it they dismissed Foote. Subsequent reissues reverted to the original-speed version, with the exception of the 2000 US reissue of The Clash, which used Foote’s sped-up master. [source: wikipedia.org/wiki/Clash_City_Rockers; mixonline.com]

Strummer later reconciled with Foote, who contributed to archival Clash releases. The Bagging Area blog notes that Strummer was “still grumbling” about having fired him years later. [source: baggingarea.blogspot.com]

Later life

Foote relocated to Aberdeenshire in the mid-2000s. He became a prominent local activist opposing Donald Trump’s golf resort development on the Menie Estate. He was a member of Sustainable Aberdeenshire and appeared in Anthony Baxter’s 2011 documentary You’ve Been Trumped. [source: mixonline.com; imdb.com/name/nm8520703/]

He died in Aberdeen on 2 March 2018 after a short undisclosed illness.
