Julius Hemphill

Born: January 24, 1938, Fort Worth, Texas
Died: April 2, 1995, New York City (complications from heart disease and diabetes, age 57)
Real name: Julius Arthur Hemphill
Instruments: Alto saxophone, soprano saxophone, flute
Discogs artist ID: 593607
Also credited as: Hemphill; J. Hemphill; Julius A. Hemphill; Julius Arthur Hemphill; Julius Hempill

Avant-garde jazz composer and alto saxophonist. Co-founding member of Black Artists Group (BAG) in St. Louis, 1968–1972. Co-founder of World Saxophone Quartet (1976). One of the defining composer-performers of the American free jazz and avant-garde post-BAG scene.

Birth city correction: The existing stub said “St. Louis-born.” Discogs confirms he was born in Fort Worth, Texas — St. Louis was where he built his career (BAG), not where he was born.

Biography

Fort Worth and Early Life

Hemphill was born in Fort Worth, Texas, on January 24, 1938. Details of his early musical education are not documented in Discogs; he eventually settled in St. Louis, where his career took shape.

St. Louis: Black Artists Group (1968–1972)

In St. Louis, Hemphill became a co-founding member of the Black Artists Group (BAG), incorporated in 1968 and active until approximately 1972. BAG was modelled on Chicago’s AACM and extended beyond music to theater, dance, visual art, and poetry — but its musical core was free jazz, with Hemphill as one of its key saxophonists alongside Oliver Lake, Hamiet Bluiett, J.D. Parran, and trumpeters Baikida Carroll and Floyd LeFlore.

Hemphill’s own first recording, Dogon A.D. (1972, Mbari), dates from this period. That debut — with Baikida Carroll on trumpet, Abdul Wadud on cello, and Phillip Wilson on percussion — became a landmark of the St. Louis avant-garde. He also produced an early solo theatre-music work, The Collected Poem — For Blind Lemon Jefferson (1971, Mbari), where he contributed flute and saxophone alongside Carroll.

By 1972–1973 BAG had dissolved institutionally; key members relocated to Paris and then New York. Hemphill’s trajectory led directly into his prolific solo recording career and, eventually, the World Saxophone Quartet.

Solo Career: Mbari, Freedom, Sackville (1972–1978)

After BAG, Hemphill recorded prolifically as a leader on small independent labels. His catalog from this period spans several essential documents:

  • Dogon A.D. (1972, Mbari) — debut; Baikida Carroll, Abdul Wadud, Phillip Wilson
  • ‘Coon Bid’ness (1975, Arista/Freedom) — with Arthur Blythe (as “Black Arthur Blythe”), Hamiet Bluiett, Abdul Wadud, Barry Altschul on drums; producer on Side B
  • Blue Boyé (1977, Mbari) — solo (Hemphill performs uncredited); produced and composed; Tim Berne served as assistant producer under the name “Bruce Berne”
  • Roi Boyé & The Gotham Minstrels (1977, Sackville) — solo multi-instrument performance; produced, composed, and wrote liner notes
  • Buster Bee (1978, Sackville) — duo with Oliver Lake; both on alto and soprano saxophone
  • Live In New York (1978, Red Record) — live performance; composed and wrote liner notes
  • Raw Materials And Residuals (1978, Black Saint) — co-billed with Abdul Wadud and Famoudou Don Moye; alto and soprano saxophone

World Saxophone Quartet (1976–early 1990s)

In 1976 Hemphill co-founded World Saxophone Quartet with Oliver Lake (alto/soprano), Hamiet Bluiett (baritone), and David Murray (tenor). All four had connections to the St. Louis/BAG and New York loft jazz scenes. The WSQ performed without a rhythm section — four saxophones only — and became one of the defining ensembles of the era.

Hemphill appeared on all of the WSQ’s Black Saint recordings through the 1980s, beginning with Steppin’ With The World Saxophone Quartet (1979) and running through Live At Brooklyn Academy Of Music (1986), Plays Duke Ellington (1986, Nonesuch) and Dances And Ballads (1987, Elektra Nonesuch). He left the group in the early 1990s before Rhythm And Blues (1989) was the last confirmed Discogs credit. His compositions remained central to the WSQ repertoire throughout.

Big Band and Sextet Period (1987–1994)

In parallel with WSQ and after his departure from it, Hemphill led larger ensembles:

Julius Hemphill Big Band — Julius Hemphill Big Band (1988, Elektra Musician). A 16-piece ensemble including Bill Frisell (guitar), Marty Ehrlich (alto sax/soprano sax/flute), John Purcell (alto/soprano/flute), John Stubblefield (alto/soprano/flute), J.D. Parran (baritone/flute), Jerome Harris (electric bass), Ronnie Burrage (drums), Frank Lacy (trombone), David Taylor (bass trombone), Vincent Chancey (French horn). Hemphill composed and arranged all material.

The Julius Hemphill Sextet — Hemphill’s primary vehicle in his final years, an all-saxophone ensemble:

  • Fat Man And The Hard Blues (1991, Black Saint) — with Marty Ehrlich (soprano/alto/flute), Carl Grubbs (alto/soprano), Sam Furnace (baritone/flute), Andrew White (tenor), James Carter (3) (tenor); Hemphill as alto saxophone, producer, and composer
  • Five Chord Stud (1994, Black Saint) — with Tim Berne (alto), Marty Ehrlich (soprano/alto), Sam Furnace (soprano/alto), Andrew White (tenor), James Carter (3) (tenor), Fred Ho (baritone); Hemphill as composer and conductor (health precluded playing by this point)

By Five Chord Stud, Hemphill was too ill to perform; he composed and conducted but did not play. The record is his final credited release before his death in 1995.

Sideman Credits

Beyond his own projects, Hemphill appeared as a sideman on a handful of significant sessions:

  • Fast Last! (1974, Muse Records) — Archie Shepp; alto saxophone
  • New York, Fall 1974 (1975, Arista) — soprano saxophone
  • Çonceré Ntasiah (1976, Universal Justice) — soprano saxophone
  • Point Of No Return (1977, Moers Music) — WSQ debut
  • Jungle Cowboy (1987, JMT) — alto saxophone
  • Before We Were Born (1989, Elektra Musician) — Bill Frisell; alto saxophone as “Soloist For The Bill Frisell Band”
  • Streets Of St. Louis (1978, Moers Music) — alto saxophone (a 30-pressing release)

Death and Legacy

Hemphill died April 2, 1995 in New York City, of complications from heart disease and diabetes, aged 57. His compositions continued to be performed and recorded after his death. Notable posthumous activity in Discogs:

  • The Julius Hemphill Sextet posthumously released material: The Hard Blues: Live In Lisbon (2004, Clean Feed); various Sextet performances
  • Diminutive Mysteries (Mostly Hemphill) (2004, Winter & Winter) — John Zorn ensemble performing Hemphill compositions
  • Lowlife — The Paris Concert I (2005, Winter & Winter) — further Hemphill material performed by others
  • The Hemphill Stringtet — string ensemble devoted to his repertoire; released The Hemphill Stringtet Plays Music Of Julius Hemphill (2025, Out Of Your Head Records) and Yikes Too (2025, Out Of Your Head / Screwgun Records), with Tomeka Reid on cello
  • Father Of Origin (2011, Eremite Records) — archival alto saxophone recording
  • Frequency Equilibrium Koan (2021) — archival alto saxophone session

His NYU papers are archived: https://wp.nyu.edu/library-hemphill_papers/

  • The Hemphill Stringtet — ensemble devoted to his compositions; active 2020s with Tomeka Reid (cello)
