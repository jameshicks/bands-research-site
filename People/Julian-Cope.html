Julian Cope

British singer-songwriter, author, archaeologist-obsessive, and cult figure. Born Julian David Cope, 21 October 1957, Deri, Monmouthshire, Wales. Raised in Tamworth, moved to Liverpool in the late 1970s, now based near Avebury, Wiltshire. Discogs artist ID: 114566.

Husband of Dorian Cope; father of Avalon Cope (also a credited collaborator on later releases). Aliases on record include Double De Harrison, Droolian (2), Hugo De Klee, The Arch Drude, and several others.

Overview

Cope co-founded The Crucial Three in May 1977 — a short-lived Liverpool proto-post-punk trio with Ian McCulloch and Pete Wylie that dissolved within weeks. In 1978 he formed The Teardrop Explodes, which became the flagship band of the Zoo/Eric’s scene, signed to Bill Drummond and David Balfe’s Zoo Records, and later to Mercury. The Teardrop Explodes released two studio albums — Kilimanjaro (1980) and Wilder (1981) — before dissolving in late 1982.

Solo from 1983, Cope remained on Mercury through two albums before moving to Island Records for his commercial peak (1986–1992), then to Echo (1994–1996), and finally to his own imprint Head Heritage from 1999 onward. Across all eras he has been prolific and uncompromising, moving freely between hook-driven pop, neo-psychedelia, Krautrock-informed drone, and heavy rock — often within the same record. He is also a published author: Head On (1994) and Repossessed (1999) chronicle his career; The Modern Antiquarian (1998) documents megalithic sites.

Discography by Era

Mercury Era (1983–1985)

The solo debut came with the single “Sunshine Playroom” (Mercury, 1983), followed by World Shut Your Mouth (Mercury, 1984) — produced by Cope himself; Donald Ross Skinner on slide/electric guitar — and Fried (Mercury, 1984). Fried was produced by Steve Lovell, engineered by Paul Watkins, with Chris Whitten on drums, Kate St. John on cor anglais, and Skinner again on guitar. Both albums are cult items, especially Fried, known for its surrealist artwork (Cope photographed naked with a turtle-shell shield).

The compilation Songs To Learn & Sing (Korova/Zoo, 1985) gathered Teardrop Explodes material. The “Sunspots EP” (Mercury, 1985) closed out the Mercury chapter.

Island Records Era — Commercial Peak (1986–1992)

Island reissued World Shut Your Mouth in 1986 with a new band lineup — James Eller (bass), Chris Whitten (drums), Donald Ross Skinner (electric guitar), Double De Harrison (organ/clavinet); Cope on vocals and Electric Clavioline. That version charted. Saint Julian (Island, 1987) was produced in two parts: Ed Stasium (tracks A1, A4, B2) and Warne Livesey (remainder), with the same core band. My Nation Underground (Island, 1988) was produced and recorded by Ron Fair, engineered by Hugo Nicolson, mixed by Hugh Padgham.

The early 1990s produced Cope’s most ambitious Island work. Peggy Suicide (Island, 1991) is a sprawling double album largely co-produced with Donald Ross Skinner, engineered by multiple engineers including Hugo Nicolson, Tim Bran, Paul Tipler, and Tony Harris; Mike Joyce of The Smiths plays drums on three tracks. Jehovahkill (Island, 1992) is another double album — co-produced with Skinner, recorded and mixed by Hugo Nicolson, Paul Corkett, and Shaun Harvey; Rooster Cosby on drums and saxophone. Fear Loves This Place EP (Island, 1992) was engineered by Paul Corkett (track 1), Shaun Harvey (tracks 3–4), and Tim Bran (track 2).

Cope founded the side project Queen Elizabeth in 1992, releasing their self-titled album on Echo Special Projects (1994) — Cope credited with piano, synthesizer, effects, percussion, guitar, and bass; also producing and directing.

Ma-Gog / Independent (1990, 1992–1993)

The self-distributed lo-fi album Skellington (CopeCo/Zippo Records, 1990) — produced by Cope, acoustic guitar and vocals — was released outside the Island contract. Rite (Ma-Gog Records, 1992) and The Skellington Chronicles (Ma-Gog, 1993) continued in this mode; Paul Corkett is credited as engineer on Rite.

Echo Era (1994–1996)

Post-Island, Cope signed to Echo. Autogeddon (American Recordings/Echo, 1994) is a collaborative, ensemble-textured album mixed and “synthesized” by Thighpaulsandra, featuring Donald Ross Skinner, Rooster Cosby, Sid Moon-Eye, Thighpaulsandra, and others. 20 Mothers (Echo, 1995) has Thighpaulsandra recording, mixing, and playing synthesizer and piano; Terry Edwards arranging horns; string arrangements also by Thighpaulsandra. Interpreter (Echo, 1996) again features Thighpaulsandra on mix and synthesizers, with Rooster Cosby and Sid Moon-Eye.

Cope also wrote sleeve notes for the Castle/Essential! reissue series of Tangerine Dream albums in 1996 (Electronic Meditation, Atem, Zeit, Alpha Centauri), reflecting his longstanding interest in Krautrock.

Head Heritage Era (1999–present)

From 1999 Cope released material on his own Head Heritage imprint, including collaborations with Brain Donor (founded 1999) and solo records. Odin (Head Heritage, 1999) was produced by Thighpaulsandra, recorded by Cliff Cheerio; Cope: vocals, Mellotron 400, Starchamber keyboards. An Audience With The Cope (Head Heritage, 2000) — a live/studio hybrid — features Doggen, Cliff Cheerio, Thighpaulsandra, Kevin Bales; recorded by David Wrench, Terry Dobbin, Thighpaulsandra.

You Gotta Problem With Me (Head Heritage, 2007) saw Cope playing lead guitar, bass, Mellotron 400, Moog/Korg synthesizers, and producing. Citizen Cain’d (Head Heritage, 2005) features Donald Ross Skinner, Doggen, Holy McGrail, Mitch Razor, recorded by David Francolini and Terry Dobbin. Drunken Songs (Head Heritage, 2017) — an album of rearranged traditional drinking songs — was self-produced; design by daughter Avalon Cope.

Recent records include Cold War Psychedelia (Head Heritage, 2021, credited to Julian Cope & The Teardrop Explodes), England Expectorates (Head Heritage, 2022), and Friar Tuck (Head Heritage, 2024).

Cope also appeared on Sunn O)))‘s White1 (Southern Lord, 2003) as a featured guest.

Written Work and Sleeve Notes

Beyond music, Cope wrote sleeve notes for Castle/Essential!‘s 1996 Tangerine Dream reissue series (Electronic Meditation, Atem, Zeit, Alpha Centauri) and contributed liner notes and booklet text to numerous other releases. His books include Head On (autobiography), Repossessed (autobiography vol. 2), The Modern Antiquarian (megalithic sites), and Japrocksampler (Japanese rock). He runs the Head Heritage website (headheritage.co.uk), which includes reviews and essays.
