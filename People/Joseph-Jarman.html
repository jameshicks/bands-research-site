Joseph Jarman

Joseph Jarman

Aug 11, 20268 min read

Joseph Jarman

Discogs artist ID: 254749

American multi-instrumentalist and composer. Born September 14, 1937, Pine Bluff, Arkansas; died January 10, 2019, Silver Spring, Maryland. A founding and core member of The Art Ensemble Of Chicago and a pivotal figure in the AACM. Known also by the name Shaku Joseph Jarman. His instrumental range was exceptional: alto, soprano, tenor, sopranino, baritone, and bass saxophone; oboe; bassoon; flute (C, alto, bass, bamboo, piccolo); clarinet (B-flat, alto, bass); marimba; vibraphone; congas; percussion; voice; guitar; harpsichord; piano; accordion; synthesizer.

Overview

Jarman came up in Chicago’s South Side music scene in the early 1960s and was a founding member of the AACM when it was established in 1965 by Muhal Richard Abrams and others. Before joining what would become the Art Ensemble, he led his own AACM ensembles and recorded two landmark solo albums for Delmark Records: Song For (1967) and As If It Were The Seasons (1968). These recordings placed him among the most distinctive voices in the first wave of AACM-affiliated music — combining free improvisation, extended techniques, text, and theatrical elements.

In 1969 he joined Roscoe Mitchell’s ensemble (which included Lester Bowie, Malachi Favors, and later Famoudou Don Moye) and traveled to Paris, where the group became the Art Ensemble Of Chicago. The Paris period (1969–71) was intensely productive: multiple albums for BYG Records, Pathé, America Records, and Saravah documented the ensemble live and in the studio.

Back in the US through the 1970s and 80s, Jarman recorded both as an AEoC member (ECM, Atlantic, DIW) and under his own name on India Navigation, Black Saint, and AECO Records. He also explored duo and trio configurations — most consistently with AEoC drummer Famoudou Don Moye, with whom he made several co-leader records for Black Saint. He retired from music around 2003 after becoming a Zen Buddhist priest, though he made at least one further recording in Japan in 2004.

Solo Discography

Song For (1967, Delmark Records)

Jarman’s debut as leader. Recorded with an ensemble assembled from the early AACM community.

One of the earliest Delmark recordings to fully document the AACM’s multi-instrumental, text-inclusive approach. Charles Clark and Christopher Gaddy both died young (1969 and 1968 respectively), making this a key document of a short-lived but important grouping.

As If It Were The Seasons (1968, Delmark Records)

Second Delmark album; more chamber-like, stripped to a quartet. Notable for the use of voice alongside extended instrumental textures.

Together Alone (1974, Delmark Records)

Co-led with Anthony Braxton. Both credited as “Recording Supervisor,” produced by Robert G. Koester. An unusual two-horn dialogue within the AACM tradition; Jarman and Braxton are the sole performers.

Egwu-Anwu (Sun Song) (1978, India Navigation)

Co-led with Famoudou Don Moye. A percussion-and-reed duo that marked Jarman’s first step beyond AEoC-credited recordings toward a sustained duo partnership with Moye. Self-produced on the India Navigation label (label ID: 39624).

Sunbound Volume One (1978, AECO Records)

Solo release on the Art Ensemble’s own AECO Records imprint. Jarman plays alto saxophone, bass clarinet, flute, sopranino saxophone, cymbals, gong, bells, vibraphone — self-produced. A solo multi-tracking project presenting Jarman without ensemble context.

The Magic Triangle (1979, Black Saint)

Co-leader date with Don Pullen (piano, vocals) and Famoudou Don Moye (drums, congas). Recorded in Italy; produced by Giovanni Bonandrini for Black Saint (label ID: 30835).

Black Paladins (1980, Black Saint)

Trio co-led with Famoudou Don Moye and Johnny Dyani (piano, bass, vocals). Jarman on sopranino, tenor, baritone saxophones, shell horn, flutes, bass clarinet, voice. A late appearance for Dyani; the album features a poem by Henry Dumas.

Earth Passage – Density (1981, Black Saint)

Jarman–Moye duo expanded with Donald Garrett (bass, flute, clarinet, conch) and Craig Harris (trombone, didgeridoo, voice). Recorded in Italy. Garrett appears under the name “Rafael Garrett.”

Poem Song (Joy In The Universe) (2004, Videoarts Music)

Jarman’s last documented solo leader date, recorded in Japan on Videoarts Music label. He plays soprano saxophone, bass clarinet, voice, bass flute, bells, and conch shell alongside a Japanese ensemble including koto players Kazue Sawai and Hideaki Kuribayashi, trombonist Hiroshi Itaya, and two bassists. Represents a late-period Japanese connection after his years as a Zen priest.

Open Questions

  • The death date in Discogs is January 10, 2019; the brief cites January 9. Discogs is the source of record here; web verification would clarify.
  • The Discogs DB does not document Jarman’s period as a Zen Buddhist priest or his formal ordination; this is not in the profile text beyond a brief reference elsewhere.
  • Together Alone (1974) lists both Jarman and Braxton as “Recording Supervisor” — the actual performers are not listed in Discogs credits, though it is understood to be a two-horn unaccompanied session. Would benefit from release-level confirmation.
  • DIW-era AEoC releases (1985–91) are well-documented in the discography but individual personnel credits on each title were not pulled; Jarman’s instrument credits from the discography table are used.
  • Jarman’s connection to Africanasia (1969, BYG) is listed as “Flute” for Archie Shepp — could be investigated further as a Paris-period side credit.
  • Several late releases (The Meeting, Sirius Calling, both Pi Recordings 2003–04) appear to be AEoC projects but are credited differently in Discogs; the exact lineup warrants checking.
