Von LMO

Discogs ID: 952553
Real name: Frank Cavallo
Born: March 10, 1951, Brooklyn, NY
Name variations: Lmo
Active: c. 1977–2018+

Guitarist, vocalist, multi-instrumentalist (guitar, electronics, shortwave radio, organ). Co-founder of Red Transistor with Rudolph Grey (NYC no wave, 1977–78). Best known for the 1981 LP Future Language, a self-produced cosmic-punk/space-rock record on his own StraZar imprint. Cavallo has described his aesthetic variously as “future music,” cosmic punk, and extraterrestrial rock — occasionally claiming origins from a planet called “Strazar” (the source of his label name).

Biography

Pre–Red Transistor (to 1977)

Before forming Red Transistor, Cavallo played in two earlier groups: Funeral of Art (with bassist Sal Maida, who later played with Roxy Music and Sparks) and Kongress (described as Velvet Underground-influenced). These groups were active in the mid-1970s Brooklyn/NYC underground before the no wave explosion.

Red Transistor (1977–1978)

In September 1977 Cavallo co-founded Red Transistor with Rudolph Grey. The band played the SoHo loft and gallery circuit through 1978 and performed at the “Bands at Artists Space” festival alongside the four acts later documented on No New York — but were passed over by Brian Eno for the compilation. Red Transistor recorded two tracks (“Not Bite” / “We’re Not Crazy”) at Sorceror Sound in December 1977 for Marty Thau’s Red Star Records; the release was shelved and sat unreleased until Thurston Moore issued it on Ecstatic Peace! in 1990.

By August 1978 Red Transistor had dissolved. Drummer Jim Sclavunos moved to Teenage Jesus And The Jerks; Rudolph Grey continued with Blue Humans.

Solo career — Future Language era (1979–1982)

After Red Transistor, Cavallo launched a solo career and began performing at Max’s Kansas City. In 1979 he recorded five demo tracks at Skyline Studios. In 1981 he released Future Language as an LP on StraZar, his own imprint — a record he produced, arranged, composed, and designed the cover for, playing electronic guitar, lead vocals, and special effects. The band on the album was: Mike Gee (2) (electronic guitar), George Matthewson (electronic bass), Bobby Ryan (drums), and Juno Saturn (electronic saxophone, backing vocals). Executive producers were Diane Rose and Elliott Sewell. Mastered by Howie Weinberg.

After a show at the Palladium drew minimal attendance, and a Max’s Kansas City gig in November 1981, Cavallo “disappeared from the music scene” until 1991.

Absence and return (1982–1993)

Cavallo moved to Long Island, married, and had a son (born 1984). He struggled with heroin addiction during this period. He returned to New York City in 1991.

Variant Records years (1994–1999)

Reconnecting with the underground, Cavallo recorded and released material on Variant Records (Redwood City, CA). The 1994 single Cosmic Interception (prod. Peter Crowley) contained three tracks — “Cosmic Interception,” “Ultraviolet Light,” and “Inside Shadowland.” Also in 1994, he recorded a split 7” with Gorls on Bad Vibe Records, contributing “Outside of Time.”

The 1996 CD Red Resistor (Variant Records, prod. Peter Crowley) was four extended noise-rock tracks, drawing praise from Julian Cope and Alan Vega. The lineup featured David Tamura (guitar), Robert Lee Oliver II (bass), and Howard “Crash” Valentine (drums).

In 1999, Cavallo self-released the compilation CD Advance Yourself (cat. VL-1999-ZE), gathering reworked and older material.

Reissues and recognition (2000–2006)

In 2000, Future Language was reissued on CD by Flemish Masters (cat. fm 001/fm 13001), reportedly with assistance from Weasel Walter. Spanish label Munster Records issued a 7” single (“Be Yourself” b/w “Cosmic Interception”) in 2001, then the double-LP retrospective Tranceformer (Future Language 2.001) in 2003 (MR 217 / MRCD 217), compiling both the original Future Language sides and a second disc of other material (“Smudge Head,” “Transformer,” “Womb of Eternity,” “Freeze Frame Mode,” and others). Also in 2001, Munster issued a Spain CD pressing of Future Language.

In 2006, Cavallo produced the CD The Original Unreleased 1979 Album for punk band Disgrace (5) on Welfare Records (WEL020).

Legal trouble and Avant Duel (2007–2018)

In 2007, Cavallo was convicted of second-degree robbery and sentenced to three and a half years. He was released in 2010 and immediately began performing with Otto Von Ruggins (real name Frank Peter Faconti) as Avant Duel — a space rock/industrial duo based in Brooklyn.

Avant Duel released Beyond Human (2012, self-released FLAC), on which both members played instruments, composed, produced, and engineered, with Cavallo also handling vocals, lyrics, and artwork. The follow-up We Are The Aliens appeared in 2016 (self-released FLAC), again a full co-production between Cavallo and Von Ruggins.

In 2018, Cavallo self-released Songs From Galaxy Zero as a digital compilation (WAV), collecting thirteen tracks including “Tranceformer,” “Black Noise Toys,” “Disco Zombie,” and “Zivoid Is Cuming.”

Identity and Mythology

Cavallo has made varying claims about his origins, including a stated birth year of 1924 and the assertion of extraterrestrial origins from a planet called “Strazar” — the same name (differently spelled) as his StraZar record label. These claims have been a consistent part of his public persona since the late 1970s.

Pre-Red Transistor (supplementary)

Before Red Transistor, Cavallo played in Funeral of Art (with bassist Sal Maida, later Roxy Music/Sparks) and Kongress (VU-influenced). (Bandcamp Daily)

Future Language and StraZar

StraZar was Von LMO’s own imprint, created solely for Future Language. No other releases on the label identified. The album blends no wave, punk, and space rock with lyrics centered on cosmic themes and human psychic advancement. (Wikipedia — Future Language; Bandcamp Daily)
