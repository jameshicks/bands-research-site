David Longstreth

David Longstreth

Oct 22, 20269 min read

David Longstreth

Discogs ID: 816296 Name variations: Dave Longstreth, David Donnell Longstreth, D. Longstreth Role: Vocalist, guitarist, multi-instrumentalist, producer, songwriter Active: 2001–present Twitter: https://twitter.com/davidlongstreth

American singer, songwriter, producer, and multi-instrumentalist. Founder and sole constant member of Dirty Projectors. Has written and produced virtually every Dirty Projectors record since 2003, handling vocals, guitars, drums, arrangement, mixing, and art direction. Outside the band he has production and co-writing credits for Björk, Solange (see Solange), Rihanna / Kanye West / Paul McCartney, Bombino, and Jacob Collier, among others. Credited on 17 unique masters released on Domino Records across 79 individual pressings — his dominant label home since Bitte Orca (2009).

Biography by Era

Yale Years and Early Recordings (2001–2003)

Longstreth began releasing music while attending Yale University. His first Discogs-traceable release is The Graceful Fallen Mango (2001, This Heart Plays Records), a solo record on which he is the sole primary artist. He appeared as a vocalist on the K Records compilation Future Fusion Vision Wave (2003). The first Dirty Projectors LP, The Glad Fact (2003, Western Vinyl), credits him as Performer and for Artwork.

Orchestral Society and Conceptual Albums (2004–2007)

To record Slaves’ Graves & Ballads (2004, Western Vinyl), Longstreth assembled a small chamber ensemble he called the Orchestral Society for the Preservation of the Orchestra (Discogs ID: 1027624), described in the group’s profile: he recruited two violins, cello, flute, clarinet, oboe, horn, and percussion, rehearsed and recorded scores from midnight over three consecutive nights in December, then overdubbed his own singing. He acted as conductor, vocalist, guitarist, and recording engineer. Members included Adam Bloniarz, Elana Arian, Emily Rostetter, Ezra Seltzer, Hank Miller, Jon Zalben, Sam Bernstein, and Yasemin Schatz.

The Orchestral Society also performed on The Getty Address (2005, Western Vinyl) — a conceptual album for which Longstreth arranged, produced, engineered, played drums and guitar, and sang. That record also featured the Brown Finch Choir (Discogs ID: 1027623) and vocalist Lucy Greene (Discogs ID: 1027622), and trombone/trumpet by Joseph Grimm.

Rise Above (2007, Dead Oceans) is Dirty Projectors’ recomposed version of Black Flag’s Damaged, from memory. The lineup included Nat Baldwin (bass), Brian McOmber (drums), Chris Bear (drums on one track, electronics), Sam Hillmer (flute), Charlie Looker (guitar), Jessica Pavone (viola), Amber Coffman (vocals/guitar), and Susanna Waiche (vocals). Co-produced and co-mixed with Chris Taylor (7) of Grizzly Bear. Greg Ginn is credited Written-By throughout. Paul Gold (mastering) — see Paul Gold.

Breakthrough and Domino Period (2008–2013)

Bitte Orca (2009, Domino) marked the beginning of Longstreth’s relationship with Domino and the classic five-piece live lineup. Credits: Amber Coffman, Angel Deradoorian, Brian McOmber, and Nat Baldwin as core Dirty Projectors performers; Haley Dekle on additional harmonies; a string quartet of Jordan Dykstra (viola/director), Andrew Todd (violin), Caleb Russell (violin), and Anna Fritz (cello); co-mixed and co-produced with Nicolas Vernhes; recorded by Robby Moncrieff. The album generated widespread critical attention.

Ascending Melody (2009, Domino) — standalone single, written, produced, and mixed by Longstreth.

Mount Wittenberg Orca (2010, self-released) — collaborative EP with Björk. Primary artists: Dirty Projectors + Björk. Performers: Amber Coffman, Angel Deradoorian, Björk, Brian McOmber, David Longstreth, Haley Dekle, Nat Baldwin. Mixed by all three (Björk, Longstreth, Nicolas Vernhes). Produced and written by Longstreth.

Swing Lo Magellan (2012, Domino) — expanded lineup adds chamber ensemble yMusic on four tracks (CJ Camerieri, Nadia Sirota, Alex Sopp, Hideaki Aomori, Clarice Jensen, Rob Moose). Additional mixing by Carl Barc and Donato Paternostro. Art direction and design by Longstreth and Rob Carmichael (2). Cover photo by Jake Longstreth (Discogs ID: 2044627). Amber Coffman co-wrote track 10.

Get Free (2012, Mad Decent) — Longstreth credited for guitar and written-by on this Major Lazer / Amber Coffman track. Also a co-writer on Free The Universe (2013, Mad Decent / Secretly Canadian).

Outside Productions and Collaborations (2013–2016)

Azel (2016, Modiba / Partisan Records) — Longstreth produced this Tuareg rock album by Bombino (Omara “Bombino” Moctar), also playing Hammond organ. Mixed by David Wrench; mastered by Heba Kadry. A notable instance of Longstreth working outside indie rock entirely.

FourFiveSeconds (2015, Roc Nation) — co-written by Longstreth (credited as “Dave Longstreth”) alongside Kanye West, Paul McCartney, Dallas Austin, Elon Rutberg, Kirby Lauryen, Mike Dean, Noah Goldstein, and Ty Dolla $ign. Primary artists: Rihanna, Kanye West, Paul McCartney.

Divers (2015, Drag City) — Joanna Newsom album; Longstreth credited for Orchestral Arrangements.

Keep Your Name (2016, Domino EP) — Dirty Projectors single, Longstreth as lyrics, music, and producer; Tyondai Braxton (see Tyondai Braxton) on additional music; mixed by Jimmy Douglass.

The Self-Titled Album and Solo Turn (2017)

Dirty Projectors (2017, Domino) — widely understood as a breakup album following Longstreth’s split from Amber Coffman. Credits: Longstreth as primary creator (music, lyrics, production, mixing, art direction/design shared with Rob Carmichael); Tyondai Braxton on additional production and music across multiple tracks; Jimmy Douglass as primary mixer; additional mixing by Sonny DiPerri and David Tolomei; engineering by Sonny DiPerri (drums/percussion), Ryan Tuttle, Mikaelin Bluespruce, Jamie Walters (4), and Derek Bergheimer (Dawn Richard’s vocals). Solange Knowles co-wrote “Cool Your Heart” (track C2); Ewan MacColl co-wrote “Up In Hudson” (track A3). Samples from Bernard Herrmann (“Scene D’Amour”) performed by the Royal Scottish National Orchestra; sample of Dan Deacon’s “Sheathed Wings.” Teresa Eggers co-wrote lyrics on “Winner Take Nothing.”

Cool Your Heart (Ludwig Goransson Remix) (2017, Domino) — Longstreth mixed, produced, and is credited Music By; co-written with Solange.

City Of No Reply (2017, Columbia) — Longstreth produced and arranged this track for Dawn Richard (additional music co-writing credit too).

Lamp Lit Prose and Collaborative Records (2018–2023)

Lamp Lit Prose (2018, Domino) — Dirty Projectors’ most explicitly pop-oriented record. Engineering spread across David Tolomei, Robby Moncrieff, Sonny DiPerri, Ryan Tuttle, and Miro Mackie; mastered by Greg Calbi; Manny Marroquin mixed track 2. Guest co-writers: Nat Baldwin and Mike Johnson (18) on track 10. Guest vocal appearances include Robin Pecknold (Fleet Foxes) on track 9 and Rostam Batmanglij on track 9 (see Robin Pecknold and Fleet Foxes).

5 EPs (2020, Domino) — a suite of five EPs released serially, each featuring a prominent guest collaborator (Empress Of, D∆WN, Rostam, Haile Supreme, Olof Arnalds). Longstreth credited Art Direction; design by Rob Carmichael (2); mastered by Greg Calbi.

On And Ever Onward (Live From Housing Works 2009) (2023, Domino) — Longstreth credited as Composer.

Song of the Earth (2025)

Song Of The Earth (2025, Nonesuch) — joint primary credit for David Longstreth, Dirty Projectors, and Stargaze (4) (s t a r g a z e; Discogs ID: 4771951), the Amsterdam contemporary classical ensemble. Longstreth credited Words By, Music By, Producer, and Mixed By (with additional mixing by Danny Reisch). Conducted by André de Ridder; mastered by Max Lorenzen. Longstreth’s first release on Nonesuch.

Other Notable Credits

  • I Dreamed I Saw St. Augustine (2010, Levi’s Revival Recordings) — produced by Longstreth; part of a Levi’s-branded project.
  • Explorations In Sound Vol. 1 & 2 (2020, Vinyl Me, Please / Marantz) — Longstreth credited Music Librarian.
  • The Story Of The Grateful Dead (2020, Warner Records) — Longstreth contributed liner notes.
  • Djesse Vol. 4 (2024, Decca / Interscope) — Longstreth contributes gang vocals on this Jacob Collier album.
  • Come Along (2002, States Rights Records) — Longstreth credited for hand silk-screened artwork; early design work.
