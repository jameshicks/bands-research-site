Danny Barnes

Banjo player, guitarist, vocalist, songwriter, and producer. Born Edward Daniel Barnes, 21 December 1961. From Texas; now based in Irondale, Washington. Discogs artist ID: 324127. Also credited as D. Barnes, D.Barnes, Edward D. Barnes, Barnes.

Co-founder of Killbilly (Dallas, ~1988–1994) and Bad Livers (Austin, 1990–2000). Principal songwriter, lead vocalist, and self-producer across his entire career. Owner and operator of Minner Bucket Studio (Austin, TX) and the Minner Bucket label. Website: dannybarnes.com

Biography

Texas Origins and Killbilly (1988–1994)

Barnes’s Discogs trail begins in 1988 on the Killbilly record Even Trolls Have Moms (Shadow Play Records), a Dallas-based band mixing bluegrass, rockabilly, and punk — described at the time as “bluethrash.” The Killbilly roster at various points included Mark Rubin (upright bass, tuba), Rhett Miller and Murry Hammond (both later Old 97’s), plus Alan Wooley, Craig Taylor, and several others. A second Killbilly record, Bahamas Pajamas, appeared on Shadow Play in 1990. The band dissolved around 1994 though a final archival release (Stranger In This Place, Flying Fish, 1998) appeared posthumously.

Barnes and Rubin crossed paths during this Dallas period in a way that would define the next decade: both relocated to Austin and co-founded what became the Bad Livers.

Bad Livers Era (1990–2000)

The Bad Livers were the central project of Barnes’s career and one of the key acts in the 1990s American roots underground. The core trio was Barnes (banjo, guitar, lead vocals), Mark Rubin (upright bass, tuba, vocals), and Ralph E. White (fiddle, button accordion). Bob Grant (10) (mandolin, guitar) joined by the time of Hogs On The Highway (1997) to form a fourth-piece lineup.

Barnes was the band’s principal songwriter, engineer, and producer on the early records. The first release, Dust On The Bible (1991, self-released), was produced and engineered by Barnes. Delusions Of Banjer (Quarterstick, 1992) brought in Paul Leary (Butthole Surfers) as outside producer — the kind of cross-scene Austin underground move that characterized the period — with Quarterstick Records (Touch and Go’s imprint) as the label home for the first three LPs. Barnes played tuba’s worth of stringed instruments on that record: 5-string banjo, tenor banjo, mandolin, dobro, acoustic guitar, accordion, xylophone.

From Horses In The Mines (1994) onward, Barnes reclaimed full production control, engineering and producing himself. The move to Sugar Hill Records (2) for Hogs On The Highway (1997) broadened the sonic palette — Lloyd Maines and Bobby Arnold co-mixed, guests appeared (Erik Hokkanen, Steve James, Toby Torres), and a fourth member solidified the lineup.

The Bad Livers’ catalog drew on old-time, bluegrass, punk, gospel, and experimental music in ways that made them a touchstone for the alt-country and insurgent country movements of the 1990s without ever sitting comfortably in any of those bins.

The Newton Boys Soundtrack (1998)

Barnes appeared on The Newton Boys — Music From The Motion Picture (Epic/Sony, 1998), the soundtrack to Richard Linklater’s Texas bank-robbery film, playing 5-string banjo, tenor banjo, tenor guitar, and guitar. The album drew a wide Austin circle including Mark Rubin, Erik Hokkanen, Steve James (7), and others.

Solo Career Begins (1998–2008)

Barnes launched his solo work under his own Minner Bucket label in 1998 with Minor Dings — early steps into self-released recordings. The 2001 album Things I Done Wrong (Terminus Records), credited to Danny Barnes & Thee Old Codgers, was produced by Wayne Horvitz and engineered by Tucker Martine, with Jon Parry on violin/viola and Keith Lowe on bass. Barnes wrote most of the material; one track covered Marc Bolan.

Dirt On The Angel (Terminus, 2003) was the breakthrough solo statement, featuring an exceptional supporting cast: Bill Frisell (guitar), Chuck Leavell (piano, organ), Dirk Powell (banjo, guitar, accordion), Darol Anger (violin), Amy Denio (voice), Steve Moore (5) (trombone), Garey Shelton (bass), Carlton Jackson (drums), Mark Graham (7) (harmonica), and Ben Mish (voice). Barnes played voice, banjo, guitar, resonator guitar, harmonica, percussion, and sampler.

Get Myself Together (Terminus, 2005) found Barnes producing himself again, playing tuba and singing all parts (lead, bass, baritone, tenor vocals), with Garey Shelton on electric bass and Brittany Haas on violin.

Barnyard Electronics (Minner Bucket, 2007) was a solo-format record on his own label — 5-string banjo and electric guitar.

ATO Records Period and Broader Collaborations (2009–2020)

Pizza Box (ATO Records, 2009) brought Barnes into a collaboration with Dave Matthews and Matt Chamberlain (drums, loops, percussion). John Alagia produced and mixed; Barnes handled all stringed instruments, bass harmonica, piano, organ, loops, and voice. Dave Matthews contributed artwork and “artillery.” Rashawn Ross (trumpet) appeared.

Rocket (ATO Records, 2011) was also produced by John Alagia.

ambient works vol. 1 (Minner Bucket, 2014) marked a solo ambient/experimental turn — self-released, no outside credits.

Man On Fire (ATO Records, 2020) was the most star-studded collaboration of Barnes’s career: John Paul Jones (Led Zeppelin) on bass and mandolin, Dave Matthews on vocals, organ (Wurlitzer), and executive producer, Matt Chamberlain on drums and percussion, Bill Frisell on guitar, and Tim Kerr contributing artwork. Geoff Stanfield produced and engineered alongside Barnes. The album marked a clear convergence of Barnes’s Austin roots world and the broader Americana/rock sphere.
