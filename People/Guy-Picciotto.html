Guy Picciotto

Discogs ID: 279521
Born: September 17, 1965, Washington, DC
Role: Vocalist, guitarist, producer, engineer
Labels operated: Peterbilt Records / (related imprint)

American singer, guitarist, songwriter, and record producer. Best known as co-vocalist and co-guitarist of Fugazi (1987–2002). Pre-Fugazi, he founded and fronted Rites Of Spring (1984–1986) and played in One Last Wish and Happy Go Licky (both 1986–1988). Post-Fugazi, he has worked primarily as an outside producer and engineer for artists including Blonde Redhead, The Gossip, The Blood Brothers, and Vic Chesnutt, and has collaborated with members of Godspeed You Black Emperor!.

Biography

Rites Of Spring (1984–1986)

Picciotto formed Rites Of Spring in Washington DC in 1984 alongside guitarist Eddie Janney, bassist Mike Fellows, and drummer Brendan Canty. The band were central to the “Revolution Summer” moment in DC hardcore — a conscious turn away from macho violence toward emotionally direct songwriting. Their sole LP, Rites Of Spring (1985, Dischord Records DIS 16), was produced by Ian MacKaye and Michael Hampton (3) and engineered by Don Zientara. Picciotto sang and played guitar; he also wrote the lyrics. The record is widely cited as a founding text of what later became known as “emo.”

The band dissolved in 1986.

One Last Wish (1986)

After Rites Of Spring, Picciotto formed One Last Wish with Janney, Canty, and Michael Hampton. The band recorded a demo in 1986 that was released posthumously as 1986 (1999, Dischord / Peterbilt Records). Picciotto sang and played lead guitar; he is credited as songwriter on 12 of the songs.

Happy Go Licky (1986–1988)

Happy Go Licky formed from the same core as Rites Of Spring: Picciotto, Janney, Fellows, and Canty. They were a transitional band, recording and performing but releasing little at the time. Their self-titled release appeared on Peterbilt Records in 1988 — by which point Picciotto had already joined Fugazi. The compilation 1986 (1999) also gathered Happy Go Licky material.

Fugazi (1987–2002)

Picciotto joined Fugazi in fall 1987, initially as a stage presence without an assigned instrument, then assuming co-vocal and guitar duties alongside founder Ian MacKaye. The four-piece core — MacKaye (vocals/guitar), Picciotto (vocals/guitar), Joe Lally (bass), Brendan Canty (drums) — remained stable for the band’s entire existence. Fugazi operated entirely on Dischord Records, recorded at Inner Ear Studios with engineer Don Zientara, and maintained strict DIY principles (all-ages shows, $5 ticket cap, no merchandise table).

Post-Fugazi Production Work (1996–present)

Following Fugazi’s hiatus, Picciotto pivoted to production. His production style tends toward clean, uncluttered recordings that preserve the natural sound of a band performing together — an approach consistent with Fugazi’s own Inner Ear ethic.

Other Groups and Appearances

Discogs lists Picciotto as a member of several additional groups: Brief Weeds, Getaway Car, Pigeons, and The Black Light Panthers — all minor or informal projects. The Black Light Panthers is a Picciotto/Canty duo whose sole release is 1982–1997 (1998, Peterbilt 5) — a 12” EP presenting early-1982 material on one side and a 1997 track on the other. It is the fifth catalog entry on Picciotto’s own Peterbilt Records.

He appeared on Vic Chesnutt’s North Star Deserter (2007, Constellation) as a guitarist and backing vocalist alongside Efrim Menuck, Thierry Amar, Howard Bilerman, and members of A Silver Mt. Zion.

Web sources

On his production approach and how he selects projects, Picciotto stated in a 2011 interview: “It’s just something I fell into out of my love of music and through relationships made through music.” He distinguishes between working with long-term friends (Blonde Redhead, Casual Dots) and cases where he was “more just a fan of what they were doing like the Gossip or the Blood Brothers” — i.e. not a prior personal connection. He records only when “it’s a good fit and that both the band and he will have a good experience working together.” (source, as of 2026-06-25)

His Kill Rock Stars relationship began with The Casual Dots debut (2004), co-produced with Don Zientara. That record’s band included his wife Kathi Wilcox. This KRS relationship preceded his work with The Gossip on Standing In The Way Of Control (recorded April 2005 at Bear Creek Studio, Woodinville, WA), suggesting KRS facilitated the Gossip introduction.

Production career began at “Pirate House” (DC group house) where Fugazi had set up an 8-track analog studio in the basement; he started recording friends there. Early credits included Slant 6, Make-Up, Metamatics, Crainium, and early Blonde Redhead sessions. (source, as of 2026-06-25)
