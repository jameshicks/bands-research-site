Seth Tiven

Singer/songwriter, guitarist, multi-instrumentalist (lap steel, bass, acoustic guitar). Originally from Connecticut; based in Austin, TX. Discogs artist ID: 1323819. Active on record from 1978 through at least 2018. Name variations: S. Tiven, Tiven.

Amy Tiven (alias Amy Farris; Discogs ID: 1714620) — violinist who appears alongside Seth on several recordings — is presumed to be a close relation (sibling or spouse).

Biography

Early years — Connecticut / New Haven (late 1970s–early 1980s)

Seth Tiven’s earliest Discogs credit is a photography credit on the Big Sound label compilation Are You Serious? (1978), suggesting he was active in the CT/New England scene from the late 1970s. That same year he contributed guitar to Saucers (ID: 1123975), a CT-based act whose membership overlapped with Mark Mulcahy (later of Miracle Legion) and Craig Bell (Rocket from the Tombs). The band’s one release, What We Do (1979), also credited Malcolm Marsden and Katherine Cormack.

In 1980 he received an uncredited performer credit on “A Certain Kind Of Shy / She’s Alright” (Gustav Records) — a single with 22 pressings, suggesting a widely distributed release.

Dumptruck (1983–1995)

Tiven is a founding member and primary creative force behind Dumptruck, a Connecticut-formed jangle-rock/indie-rock band that became his main vehicle for roughly fifteen years. Dumptruck recorded for Incas Records (first LP), then Big Time Records for a run of mid-1980s albums, before a final LP on Unclean Records in 1995.

  • D Is For Dumptruck (1983, Incas Records) — Tiven played guitar, bass, and vocals; co-wrote all material with Kirk Swan. Mark Mulcahy and James Clements (2) shared drum duties.
  • Positively Dumptruck (1986, Big Time) — guitar, vocals; produced by Don Dixon
  • For The Country (1987, Big Time) — guitar, vocals; recorded at Rockfield and Master Rock Studios; produced by Hugh Jones; notable guests include BJ Cole (pedal steel) and Tommy Eyre (Hammond organ)
  • Going Nowhere (1987, Big Time) — UK release; written-by credit
  • Island (1987, Big Time) — written-by credit
  • Days Of Fear (1995, Unclean Records) — Tiven served as guitarist, vocalist, engineer, mixer, and cover photographer; guests included Juliana Hatfield (backing vocals, track 5), Mark Mulcahy (backing vocals, tracks 4 & 8), and Amy Tiven (violin, backing vocals); co-produced with Adam Lasus and Scott Herzog
  • Wrecked (2018, Rae Dell Records) — Tiven as producer and sole songwriter; George Duron (drums), Chris “Frenchie” Smith (engineer/mix)

Austin scene (1992–present)

By 1992 Tiven had relocated to Austin, TX. That year he played lap steel guitar on Jean Caffeine’s All-Nite Truckstop’s Hard Work And A Lot Of Hairspray! (Porch Music (2)), alongside Amy Tiven (violin/backing vocals), Mick Buck (slide/lead guitar), Dennis Ku (guitar), Gurf Morlix (producer/acoustic guitar), and Jean Caffeine (2) (vocals). This recording ties him firmly into the Austin alt-country/roots scene centered around Jean Caffeine.

He joined Blind Willie’s Johnson (ID: 1594316) — Austin blues/punk hybrid — on bass, also designing the sleeve for Oops! (1994, Unclean Records). Other BWJ members: Mick Buck (guitar/vocals), Dennis Ku (guitar), Ron Polito (harp), Jason Vargo (drums).

Also in 1996 he produced and made a “Guest [Extra Special]” appearance on Pork’s Slop (Emperor Jones), an Austin label release also featuring Adam Wiltzie (engineer) and Walter Daniels (guest).

Solo work and Dunebuggy (2000s–2010s)

Solitude (2007, Rae Dell Records) is Tiven’s solo album. He wrote, produced, sang, and played guitar; the session features a notable cast of Austin regulars:

In 2017 Tiven served as lead/acoustic guitarist, engineer, and producer for Dunebuggy (4) (ID: 6512271), an Austin group whose members included Abra Moore (drums/vocals) and Donnie Poston (vocals/rhythm guitar/songwriter).
