Garo Yellin

Cellist and songwriter based in New York City. Discogs artist ID: 260076. IMDB: nm1325728.

Known professionally as Garo Yellin or D. Garo Yellin (full name David Garo Yellin). Active as a session and ensemble cellist from the mid-1980s through the 2020s, appearing across indie rock, art rock, film soundtracks, funk-soul revival, and experimental contexts.

Overview

Garo Yellin came up in the early-1980s New York no wave / experimental rock scene as a member of The Ordinaires, a nine-piece chamber-punk ensemble founded around 1982 and active through 1991. The Ordinaires combined guitars, saxophones, strings, and drums in a post-punk framework with influences from no wave and noise rock; Wikipedia describes them as “both an outgrowth of and a reaction against the shapeless dissonance of their peers” (Wikipedia: The Ordinaires). Yellin played cello throughout the band’s run, appearing on their 1985 self-titled debut (Dossier, Germany) and the 1989 LP One (Bar/None / Restless).

His relationship with David Thomas (2) began even before the Pere Ubu years: Yellin co-wrote the track “A3” on Monster Walks The Winter Lake (1986), the Rough Trade LP credited to David Thomas / The Wooden Birds — alongside Daved Hild and Thomas himself. That connection led to his recruitment into Pere Ubu for the early-1990s, where he is listed as a full member. He appears on Worlds in Collision (1991, Fontana) — credited as violin — and more extensively on Ray Gun Suitcase (1995, Tim/Kerr), where he plays electric cello on tracks 3, 6, 13, and 15 and shares co-writing credit on the same four tracks with Thomas, Jim Jones, Michele Temple, Robert Wheeler, and Scott Krauss. He also contributed to Pere Ubu’s Monster EP/compilation (1997, Cooking Vinyl / Tim/Kerr).

Beyond the avant-garde world, Yellin has had a prolific career as a session cellist — film soundtracks (Elliot Goldenthal’s Frida, 2002), major-label records (Sheryl Crow’s Globe Sessions, 1998; Counting Crows’ Recovering the Satellites / Hard Candy-era releases), indie-rock dates (The Silos self-titled, 1990; The Silos’ Ô Seasons Ô Castles, 1994; RCA), funk-soul revival (El Michels Affair’s Enter the 37th Chamber, 2009, Fat Beats; Leon Michels-produced projects), and roots-soul sessions into the 2020s (Big Crown Records releases through 2020; Easy Eye Sound’s Electrophonic Chronic, 2023).

He is also a member of Les Chauds Lapins, a New York chanson / cabaret ensemble that includes Kurt Hoffman and Jessica Pavone, which released Parlez-moi D’amour (2007, Hot Rabbit Records).
