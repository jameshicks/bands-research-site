Jerome Arnold

Discogs ID: 774577 Real name: Romeo Maurice Arnold Active: 1965–1976 (Discogs-sourced credits) Roles: Bassist, vocalist, guitarist, songwriter

American blues bassist from Chicago. He played bass in Howlin’ Wolf’s band before being recruited alongside drummer Sam Lay to join The Paul Butterfield Blues Band in 1964. He appeared on the band’s first two albums for Elektra Records — the self-titled debut (1965) and East-West (1966) — and was part of the group that backed Bob Dylan at the 1965 Newport Folk Festival during Dylan’s controversial electric debut. Brother of blues harmonica player Billy Boy Arnold.

Biography

Chicago roots and Howlin’ Wolf

Jerome Arnold (real name Romeo Maurice Arnold) came up in Chicago’s South Side blues scene. He is the brother of Billy Boy Arnold, the veteran Chicago harmonica player and Bo Diddley associate. Discogs credits Arnold on bass for Billy Boy’s 1965 Prestige LP More Blues On The South Side (Prestige PRST 7389), which places him in active session work in the mid-1960s Chicago blues world before the Butterfield connection.

Prior to joining the Butterfield Blues Band, Arnold played bass in Howlin’ Wolf’s working band. The only Discogs-sourced session that directly documents a shared credit between Arnold and Howlin’ Wolf is the 1990 compilation Blues Roots Vol. II (Peerless), but his tenure in Wolf’s band is corroborated by his Wikipedia entry and is the established biographical context for his recruitment into the Butterfield outfit.

Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1964–1967)

In 1964, Paul Butterfield and guitarist Mike Bloomfield were building an integrated Chicago blues band on the city’s North Side. Butterfield recruited Arnold and drummer Sam Lay directly out of Howlin’ Wolf’s band — an unusual crossing of the South Side–North Side and Black–white divides in Chicago’s blues world. The band’s other key members at this stage were guitarist Elvin Bishop and organist Mark Naftalin.

The band signed to Elektra Records and released their debut, The Paul Butterfield Blues Band, in 1965. Arnold played bass throughout; the album was produced by Paul A. Rothchild and featured the classic “Born In Chicago” lineup. The record is credited as a foundational document of blues-rock, fusing Chicago electric blues with the energy of rock and roll for a predominantly white college audience.

Also in 1965, Arnold and the full band (with Sam Lay on drums) were recruited to back Bob Dylan at the Newport Folk Festival. On July 25, 1965, this lineup — Arnold (bass), Lay (drums), Bloomfield (guitar), Al Kooper (organ), and Barry Goldberg (organ) — played the infamous electric set that drew booing from part of the folk crowd. The performance is documented on the 2007 Columbia/Legacy DVD The Other Side Of The Mirror (Live At The Newport Folk Festival 1963–1965), where Arnold is credited on tracks 17–19 (the electric set).

The band’s second album, East-West (Elektra, 1966), is generally considered their high-water mark. By this point Sam Lay had been replaced by Billy Davenport on drums, and the album’s 13-minute title track became a landmark in extended psychedelic blues improvisation. Arnold is credited on bass throughout. Producer credits went to Paul A. Rothchild and Mark Abramson.

Arnold departed the band before the recording of the third album, The Resurrection of Pigboy Crabshaw (Elektra, 1967). That record features Bugsy Maugh on bass and Phillip Wilson on drums, confirming Arnold’s exit from the lineup. His time with the band therefore spans the 1965 debut through the East-West period, roughly 1964–1967.

The Original Lost Elektra Sessions (Elektra Traditions, 1995) — unreleased material from the Rothchild sessions — also credits Arnold on bass, representing pre-debut and outtake recordings from the same period.

After Butterfield: session work and later projects

Following his departure from the Butterfield Blues Band, Arnold remained active in Chicago-area blues and roots music. In 1969 he played bass on three tracks (A4–A6) of the Testament Records compilation Goin’ To Chicago (Testament T-2218), a survey of Chicago blues acts that includes Billy Boy Arnold Band, the Sam Lay Blues Band, J.B. Hutto & The Hawks, and Floyd Jones–Eddie Taylor Band. Arnold appears on the Billy Boy Arnold Band tracks.

Also in 1969, Arnold appeared as a primary artist on Southern Comfort (Sire SES 97011), a blues-rock collaboration credited to Walter Horton / Martin Stone / Jessie C. Lewis / Jerome Arnold. The record was made in the UK and features Arnold on bass and vocals, including a co-writing credit on the closing raga-influenced instrumental “Netti-Netti.” This is the only release in his discography where he is a top-billed primary artist.

The 1976 bootleg LP Passed Over And Rolling Thunder (The Amazing Kornyfone Record Label, TAKRL 2982) — an unofficial Bob Dylan compilation documenting recordings from the Rolling Thunder Revue and earlier sessions — lists Arnold on bass for five tracks (A1–A5), alongside Bloomfield on guitar and Sam Lay on drums, confirming this as material from the 1965 Newport period rather than 1976.

Also in 1976, Arnold appeared as a member of Smoky Mountain Special, a country/rockabilly outfit, on the LP Totally UnCalled For? (Southsong Records NR7165). Arnold is credited on guitar, harp, and vocals, and wrote three of the album’s ten tracks (“Just Passing Thru,” “Rag For Les,” and “Heavy Duty Mama”). This represents a striking stylistic departure from his blues work.
