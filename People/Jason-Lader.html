Jason Lader

American musician, producer, and engineer. Discogs artist ID: 359731.

Overview

Jason Lader is a New York-based multi-instrumentalist and record producer who has worked across major-label pop, rock, and alternative music from the late 1990s onward. He is best known as the primary collaborator on Julian Casablancas’ solo debut Phrazes for the Young (2009), where he co-produced the album and performed all instruments alongside Casablancas. He also served as engineer and co-mixer on The Strokes’ The New Abnormal (2020), produced by Rick Rubin.

His credit history places him in a recurring orbit around Rick Rubin productions — appearing as additional engineer on Rubin projects including RHCP’s Stadium Arcadium (2006), Kesha’s Gag Order (2023), and RHCP’s Unlimited Love (2022) and Return Of The Dream Canteen (2022) — while simultaneously building an independent production career with artists including Jenny Lewis, Elvis Costello, Nightmare Of You, and Jake Bugg.

Career

Early Work (1998–2003)

Lader’s earliest credit in Discogs is a 1998 “Music By” role on Adventure (Meanwhile…/Virgin), a UK release. By 2001 he appeared as digital engineer and additional musician on The Golden Hum by Rilo Kiley (Elektra, Discogs release ID: 1229783), his first prominent placement. He contributed digital editing roles throughout the early 2000s on a number of high-profile productions — Avril Lavigne’s Under My Skin (Arista/RCA, 2004; bass, programming), Kelly Clarkson’s Breakaway (19 Recordings/RCA, 2005; bass, programming), and Mötley Crüe archival releases.

His first full producer/engineer/mixer credit was Night Like My Head by Jason Darling (Yep Roc Records, 2003, Discogs release ID: 5991349), a small-scale record where he held the entire production role.

Bass and Programming Sessions (2004–2006)

Through the mid-2000s Lader accumulated a substantial body of bass and programming session work on major-label productions:

  • Avril Lavigne — Under My Skin (Arista, 2004): bass, programming, digital editing on tracks; Discogs release ID: 434231
  • Gwen Stefani — Love.Angel.Music.Baby. (Interscope, 2004): programmed by; Discogs release ID: 352861
  • Kelly Clarkson — Breakaway (19 Recordings/RCA, 2005): bass, programming; Discogs release ID: 9113086
  • Mötley Crüe — Red, White & Crüe (Hip-O/Mötley, 2005): digital engineering; Discogs release ID: 1345152
  • System Of A Down — Mezmerize / Hypnotize (American Recordings/Columbia, 2005): digital editing; Discogs release IDs: 458707, 569648
  • Jay-Z — “99 Problems / My 1st Song” (Roc-A-Fella, 2004): programmed by; Discogs release ID: 268242

These credits suggest a New York studio position where Lader was frequently brought in for digital editing and programming on records otherwise produced by others. The System of a Down and Jay-Z connections both fall within Rick Rubin productions, indicating his early relationship with that production world.

Production Career Builds: Jenny Lewis, Johnathan Rice, Elvis Costello (2007–2008)

By 2007 Lader had become a full-service producer and mixer. Key productions:

Johnathan Rice — Further North (Reprise, 2007, Discogs release ID: 6088015) Co-produced with Rice and Farmer Dave Scher. Jenny Lewis contributed songwriting. Mastered by Greg Calbi.

Jenny Lewis — Acid Tongue (Warner Bros., 2008, Discogs release ID: 1488405) Co-produced with Lewis, Johnathan Rice, and Farmer Dave Scher. Mixed by Lader. Mastered by Stephen Marcussen. Photography by Autumn DeWilde. Lewis’s second solo album following Rabbit Fur Coat; more country-influenced and warmly received.

Elvis Costello & The Imposters — Momofuku (Lost Highway, 2008, Discogs release ID: 1345128) Co-produced with Costello. Lader also served as engineer, mixer, and studio photographer. The Imposters lineup: Davey Faragher (bass), Pete Thomas (drums), Steve Nieve (keys). Lyrics on two tracks by Loretta Lynn and Rosanne Cash. Mastered by Stephen Marcussen.

Maroon 5 — “The Moneymaker” / Under The Blacklight session (Warner Bros., 2007): produced “The Moneymaker” single; also credited as additional musician on Rilo Kiley’s Under The Blacklight.

Red Hot Chili Peppers — Stadium Arcadium (Warner Bros., 2006): additional engineer; Discogs release ID: 681721. Rick Rubin produced; this appears to be the earliest entry point into the Rubin production system.

Nightmare Of You (2005)

Nightmare Of You — Nightmare Of You (EastWest/The Bevonshire Label, 2005, Discogs release ID: 3185295) Produced and engineered by Lader. He also played bass on most tracks and mixed several tracks. Adam MacDougall (keyboards, piano, harmony vocals), Sammy Siegler (drums). This was Nightmare Of You’s debut album; lead singer/songwriter Brandon Reilly. A tight indie rock record that received positive alternative press coverage.

Phrazes for the Young — Julian Casablancas (2009)

Lader’s highest-profile credit. Julian Casablancas’ debut solo album (Cult Records (3)/RCA, 2009, Discogs release ID: 1993310) was produced by Lader, with both Casablancas and Lader credited with performing all instruments. Lader’s role was comprehensive — he co-performed, co-produced, and shaped the album’s synth-forward, New Wave-influenced aesthetic.

Additional production on one track (“Tourist”) by Mike Mogis. Mixed by Brian Sperber. Mastered by Brian Gardner and Steve Fallone.

Lader also mixed the live promotional version “11th Dimension (Live From Electric Lady Studios For WRXP, New York)” (2010, Discogs release ID: 2237010).

The album’s success and Casablancas’ profile made this the most visible entry point for Lader’s name in music press. The two’s relationship appears to have deepened: Lader is also credited as songwriter on the Julian Casablancas+The Voidz album Tyranny (2015, Discogs release ID: 6792844), an unusual credit for an engineer/producer.

The New Abnormal — The Strokes (2020)

The Strokes’ fifth studio album, produced by Rick Rubin (Cult Records (3)/RCA, 2020, Discogs release ID: 15049564). Lader served as principal engineer and co-mixer, mixing most tracks (A4 to B7, B9) while Ben Baptie mixed the remainder. Additional engineering by Pete Min and assistant engineers Dylan Neustadter, Kevin Smith (63), and Rob Bisel. Mastered by Stephen Marcussen and Stewart Whitmore.

This was a significant re-entry into the Strokes/Casablancas orbit, and a prominent studio pairing with Rubin. The album received strong critical reception, winning the Grammy Award for Best Rock Album (2021).

RHCP Return and Kesha (2022–2023)

Lader appeared on multiple major Rick Rubin productions in quick succession:

  • Red Hot Chili Peppers — Unlimited Love (Warner Records, 2022): engineer; Discogs release ID: 22694642
  • Red Hot Chili Peppers — Return Of The Dream Canteen (Warner Records, 2022): engineer; Discogs release ID: 24813257
  • Kesha — Gag Order (Kemosabe/RCA/Sony, 2023): co-producer (tracks 4, 7, 9, 11), recorded by, mixed by; Discogs release ID: 27180522. Rick Rubin produced overall; Lader was one of the key co-producing collaborators on the record.
