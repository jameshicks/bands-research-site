Don Zientara

Don Zientara

Oct 16, 20268 min read

Don Zientara

Discogs ID: 265662
Origin: Arlington, Virginia (Washington DC area), USA
Active: 1978–2021 (Inner Ear Studios closed October 2021)

Recording engineer, producer, and studio owner. Sole operator of Inner Ear Studios in Arlington, Virginia. The central recording figure for the Washington DC hardcore and post-hardcore scene across four decades, responsible for most of the Dischord Records catalog. Also a musician: member of Underheaven in the early 1980s, and solo artist (Sixteen Songs, 2003, on Ian MacKaye’s Northern Liberties label).

Biography

Early Work and Inner Ear Origins (1978–1980)

Zientara’s engineering career began in 1978, working on sessions for local DC-area bands on labels including Limp Records (2) and Round Raoul Records. His first documented credits are from that year. Inner Ear Studios — initially in Zientara’s own basement in Arlington, Virginia — opened around 1980. The Dischord Records profile describes it as operating “in continuous operation for over 25 years” from that point. The physical address is 2701 South Oakland Street, Arlington, VA 22206.

His first documented Dischord session was Minor Disturbance E.P. (1980, Dischord), marking the beginning of a relationship with the label that would define his career.

DC Hardcore Era (1981–1985)

Inner Ear became the default recording site for the first wave of Washington DC hardcore. The speed and intensity of the scene required fast, affordable, hands-on recording — and Zientara’s basement studio delivered. Key sessions from this period:

  • Minor Threat — Minor Threat EP (1981), In My Eyes EP (1981), Out Of Step (1983), Salad Days EP (1985), Complete Discography (1989). Engineer on all. Producer on some alongside the band and Skip Groff.
  • Minor Threat — Minor Threat (1981): alongside Jeff Nelson (drums/graphics), Lyle Preslar (guitar), Brian Baker (bass), Ian MacKaye (vocals); Skip Groff mixed.
  • Flex Your Head (1982, Dischord) — the definitive DC hardcore comp. Zientara engineered tracks A7–A16 and B5–B16. Engineer on the session that captured Minor Threat, SOA, Government Issue, Teen Idles, Youth Brigade, and others.
  • The Faith / Void split (1982, Dischord) — engineer and co-producer. Zientara produced and mixed both sides alongside the bands and Ian MacKaye/Jeff Nelson.
  • Rites Of Spring — Rites Of Spring (1985, Dischord). Engineer. Produced by Ian MacKaye and Mike Hampton. Often cited as a founding document of “emo”; Zientara’s room captured the band’s raw emotional intensity. Members included Guy Picciotto (guitar/vocals), Brendan Canty (drums), Mike Fellows (bass), Eddie Janney (guitar).
  • Government Issue, Beefeater, Scream, and other Dischord and Dischord-adjacent bands recorded throughout this period.

Also during this era: Bad Brains sessions. The Black Dots album (recorded late 1970s–early 1980s, released 1996 on Caroline Records) captures Bad Brains before their landmark Rock For Light, with Zientara engineering. The lineup: H.R. (vocals), Dr. Know (guitar), Darryl Jenifer (bass), Earl Hudson (drums).

Post-Hardcore and Fugazi Era (1986–2001)

Fugazi’s entire recorded output — from their 1988 self-titled debut EP through The Argument (2001) — was tracked at Inner Ear with Zientara as engineer. This is the most sustained creative partnership in his career.

Fugazi’s lineup throughout: Ian MacKaye (vocals/guitar), Guy Picciotto (vocals/guitar), Joe Lally (bass), Brendan Canty (drums). Ted Niceley co-produced most early Fugazi records with Zientara.

Zientara also engineered Rites Of Spring-adjacent projects, Embrace (1987, Dischord — Ian MacKaye post-Minor Threat band), and numerous Dischord roster acts including Lungfish, Holy Rollers, Black Eyes, Jawbox-era material, and Q And Not U.

The The Dismemberment Plan’s Emergency & I (1999, DeSoto Records) is another landmark from this period, co-engineered with J. Robbins and produced by Chad Clark and J. Robbins.

Zientara also recorded Bikini Kill’s self-titled cassette (1992, Kill Rock Stars) — bridging the DC and Olympia riot grrrl scenes.

Later Indie and Crossover Work (2002–2021)

Zientara continued working extensively through the 2000s and 2010s, still primarily through Inner Ear:

  • The Walkmen — A Hundred Miles Off (2006, Record Collection). A notable outlier: a New York indie rock band recording with a DC engineer. Zientara is credited as “Recorded By” and “Recorded By, Mixed By” on the majority of tracks. The Walkmen self-produced much of the album but routed several sessions through Inner Ear. Band members: Hamilton Leithauser (vocals/guitar), Paul Maroon (guitar), Walter Martin (bass), Matt Barrick (drums), Peter Bauer (organ/keys). Fred Kevorkian mastered.
  • The Evens — The Evens (2005) and Get Evens (2006) on Dischord. Ian MacKaye and Amy Farina duo.
  • Coriky — Coriky (2020, Dischord). MacKaye’s post-Evens trio (with Joe Lally and Amy Farina). Recorded and mixed by Zientara and the band. One of the last major sessions before Inner Ear’s closure.
  • Foo Fighters — Sonic Highways (2014) credits Zientara as “Inner Ear Guru,” acknowledging the studio’s role in DC music history.
  • Continued work with Youngblood Records bands, Revelation Records artists, and a scattering of European hardcore acts.

Inner Ear Studios closed October 1, 2021, per Zientara’s announcement in June 2021.

Open Questions

  • The Walkmen connection: How did a NYC band (whose home base was Marcata Recording in upstate New York and Sear Sound in Manhattan) end up routing sessions through Inner Ear for A Hundred Miles Off? The Discogs credit is partial (tracks 1–3, 6–8 recorded/mixed by Zientara; other tracks by Walter Martin, Pete Bauer, Hamilton Leithauser themselves). Possible angle: the band may have wanted a specific room sound for part of the album, or had a DC connection through the indie circuit. Needs web research to confirm.
  • Studio founding date: The Dischord profile says “over 25 years” as of some unspecified date. The first credit appears 1978 (not yet at Inner Ear), and Dischord sessions begin 1980. Exact year Inner Ear opened is unconfirmed.
  • Underheaven: Band is listed in Discogs as “Underheaven” (artist ID 2175134). Zientara’s membership dates and what they recorded are unknown.
  • Solo album: Sixteen Songs (2003) on Northern Liberties — not in the DB as a Zientara primary credit. Details unknown from Discogs alone.
  • Bad Brains Black Dots recording date: Released 1996, but the sessions were from ca. 1978–1979. The exact relationship (were these Zientara’s basement sessions?) is unconfirmed from DB data alone.
