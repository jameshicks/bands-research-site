Q-Tee

Discogs ID: 15887 Real name: Tatiana Mais Also credited as: Girl 7, Tatiana Mais Name variations on record: Q Tee, Q. Tee, QTee, Qtee

UK female rapper and vocalist, active from around 1990. Best known for guest appearances on early Saint Etienne releases and a handful of solo and collaborative singles through the mid-1990s. Also appeared on two albums by Babble (the post-Thompson Twins project of Tom Bailey and Alannah Currie). Contributed lyrics to a 1997 UK hip-hop compilation under her own name.

Biography

Origins and early career (1990–1992)

Q-Tee surfaced in 1990 as a featured vocalist on “Afrika” by History (Discogs ID: 25864) on SBK Records. The single was co-produced by Archie & Aurra and Louie Louie (DJ/producer), with Q-Tee co-writing the A-side (credited as “Mais”). The production team represented a mid-tier UK dance-pop world orbiting major-distributed indie labels.

In 1991 she appeared as a co-billed artist on “Filthy” (Heavenly HVN 13, UK), alongside Saint Etienne (credited as “St Etienne” on the sleeve). The release was early in Saint Etienne’s run on Heavenly, before Sarah Cracknell had fully consolidated the lead vocalist role. Q-Tee is credited “Vocals [Featuring].” Discogs release ID: 8483.

Also in 1991, she released “Free The People” as a solo single on EMI (Discogs release ID: 1383446), produced by Dean Boylan. The sleeve was designed by Abrahams Pants.

In 1992 she issued “U Can’t Play Me” (Discogs release ID: 6671969) — a self-released / Not On Label UK & Europe single. No additional credits are on record for this release.

Babble and mid-period work (1993–1994)

Q-Tee (credited as “Q. Tee”) appears as a featured vocalist on two releases by Babble — the project formed by Tom Bailey and Alannah Currie after the break-up of Thompson Twins:

  • “The Stone” (Reprise Records, 1993, US) — Q-Tee sings on track 7. Produced by Tom Bailey and Alannah Currie; engineered by Keith Fernley. Discogs release ID: 12956854.
  • “Beautiful / Tribe” (Reprise Records, 1994, US) — Q-Tee featured on tracks 1, 3, 4, and 7. Again produced by Bailey and Currie, with Keith Fernley as assistant producer and engineer. Discogs release ID: 843105.

These appearances represent Q-Tee’s most sustained studio collaboration outside the UK indie scene — Babble were signed to a major US label and aimed at a post-synth-pop adult contemporary market.

”Gimme That Body” and late activity (1995–1997)

“Gimme That Body” (Heavenly, 1995, UK) was Q-Tee’s second solo release on Heavenly, produced by Kevin Best and engineered by Dave Pemberton. Q-Tee co-wrote with Best (credited as “Mais”). Discogs release ID: 478016. The single ran to eleven pressings across formats, suggesting reasonable commercial interest.

In 1997 Q-Tee contributed lyrics to The London Convention (Utmost Records, Discogs release ID: 506897), a UK hip-hop compilation by The London Allstars. Other contributors included Rodney P, Phoebe One, Funky DL, Skinnyman, TY, MCD, and Fallacy — placing her among the central figures of the mid-1990s UK rap underground.

Later credit (2024)

Q-Tee receives a “Vocals” credit on a 2024 European reissue of Saint Etienne’s So Tough (Heavenly, Discogs release ID: 29535286), consistent with her original appearance on “Calico” and “Filthy” from that album.
