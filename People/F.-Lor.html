F. Lor

Full name: Fabrice Laureau
Also credited as: F. Lor, F/Lor, Fabrice Laureau
Name variations on Discogs: F-Lor, F.LOR, F.Lor, F.lor, f. lor, f.lor, F.Lor Fabrice Laureau, Fabrice ‘Flor’ Laureau
Discogs IDs: 309687 (F. Lor) / 255530 (Fabrice Laureau) — same person, two entries; 255530 is the more complete record

French musician, producer, recording engineer, and mixer based in France. Active from the mid-1990s through the 2020s across a wide range of French independent and international artists. Core member of the French experimental trio NLF3 and the earlier post-rock group Prohibition (2). Most internationally prominent credit: producer and mixer on Dirty Three’s She Has No Strings Apollo (Touch And Go, 2002). Also mixed the Amélie (Die Fabelhafte Welt Der Amélie) soundtrack for Yann Tiersen (2001, 34 pressings documented), making it one of his most widely distributed credits.

Alias Confirmation

Confirmed. Both Discogs entries explicitly cross-reference each other as aliases:

  • ID 309687 (“F. Lor”): realname listed as “Fabrice Laureau”; aliases list includes “Fabrice Laureau” and “F/Lor”
  • ID 255530 (“Fabrice Laureau”): aliases list includes “F. Lor” and “F/Lor”

The two IDs share overlapping credits (e.g. Echotropic, 2008, appears in both credit trails). “F. Lor” appears to be the studio/production alias while “Fabrice Laureau” is used for performer/musician credits.

Biography

Mid-1990s — Prohibition and early bass work

Fabrice Laureau’s earliest Discogs credits are as a bass player and performer. He was a member of the French post-rock/indie group Prohibition (2) alongside his brother Nicolas Laureau, Ludovic Morillon, and Quentin Rollet. Prohibition (2) released four records on Prohibited Records between 1993 and 1998: Turtle (1993, on Distorsion), Cobweb-day (1995), Towncrier (1996), and 14 Ups And Downs (1998). On Cobweb-day he also contributed a front painting — an early sign of multi-disciplinary involvement.

2000–2003 — Production emerges; Dirty Three and Amélie

By 2000, Fabrice Laureau had moved into recording and mixing alongside his performing work. Under the F. Lor alias he engineered and mixed the Herman Düne records Between The Little Houses and Turn Off The Light (both 2000, Atmosphériques/Prohibited Records). He also produced Françoiz Breut’s Vingt A Trente Mille Jours (2000, Labels).

The most commercially far-reaching credit of this period came through the “Fabrice Laureau” name: the mix of Yann Tiersen’s Die Fabelhafte Welt Der Amélie (2001, Labels), the European release of the Amélie film soundtrack. With 34 pressing variants documented, this is Fabrice Laureau’s most widely distributed single credit.

In 2002, as F. Lor, he produced, recorded, and mixed tracks 1–6 on Dirty Three’s She Has No Strings Apollo (Touch And Go, US). Track 7 was recorded and mixed by Adam Rhodes. This was his only Touch And Go / US indie rock credit; the collaboration’s origin is undocumented in Discogs.

Also in 2002, he recorded and mixed Yann Tiersen’s C’était Ici (Ici D’Ailleurs / Labels), a live album.

2003–2008 — NLF3 and the Yann Tiersen partnership

NLF3 (Fabrice Laureau + Nicolas Laureau + Jean-Michel Pirès + Ludovic Morillon, with Mitch Pires also credited) released Viva! (2003, Prohibited Records), an elaborate multi-instrument record on which Fabrice played bass, double bass, Rhodes, Casiotone keyboards, drums, synthesizers, sampler, percussion, loops, melodica, guitar, and vocals. NLF3 became the primary vehicle for his performing work across the 2000s–2020s.

As F. Lor, he continued extensive work with Yann Tiersen:

  • Good Bye Lenin! (2003) — recorded by, mixed by
  • Yann Tiersen & Shannon Wright (2004, Ici D’Ailleurs / Vicious Circle) — recorded by, mixed by
  • Les Retrouvailles (2005, Labels) — co-produced and mixed with Tiersen, with Orchestre National de Paris
  • On Tour (2006, Labels) — produced, recorded, mixed
  • Tabarly (2008, Ici D’Ailleurs) — recorded (pianos) and mixed

Other credits in this period include:

  • Switzerland Heritage (2001, Prohibited Records/Wagram) — recorded by, mixed by
  • The Belgian Kick (2004, Ici D’Ailleurs/Wagram) — recorded by, mixed by
  • Mountain Mouton (2006, Active Suspension) — produced, recorded
  • Mentors Menteurs ! (2007, Prohibited Records) — mixed by, produced

NLF3’s Echotropic (2008, Prohibited Records) appears in both ID credit trails: as F. Lor he mixed and mastered it; as Fabrice Laureau he played bass, percussion, kalimba, electronic drums, and vocals.

Ma Lueur Clown (2008, Polydor) — arranged, produced, mixed, recorded — is a notable mainstream French label credit.

2010–2024 — Continued NLF3; Yann Tiersen on Mute

NLF3 continued releasing: Ride On A Brand New Time (2008), Beautiful Is The Way To The World Beyond (2010), Pink Renaissance (2014), Waves Of Black And White (2017), ABCDEFG HI ! (2020), Days (2024) — all on Prohibited Records except the 2010 self-release.

As F. Lor he recorded drums and choirs on Yann Tiersen’s Dust Lane (2010, Mute) and Skyline (2011, Mute), marking his only credits on a major/near-major label.

Other later credits:

  • La Chirurgie Des Sentiments (2012, Caramel Beurre Salé) — mastered by, recorded by, mixed by
  • La Bar Mitzvah Du Chien / Don’t Cry For Me, Mamma (2015, Bisou) — mastered by
  • Headgearalienpoo (2018, Ici D’Ailleurs) — mixed by
  • A Beautiful Cloud (2021, Prohibited Records) — mixed by
  • Cover Songs In Inferno (2023, Prohibited Records) — mixed by
  • Good Bye Lenin! (2023, Warner Music France reissue) — recorded by, mixed by
