Keith Levene

Keith Levene

Oct 12, 20263 min read

Keith Levene

Discogs ID: 121607

Guitarist. Co-founder of Public Image Ltd (PiL) with John Lydon and Jah Wobble. Played guitar on Singers & Players’ War Of Words (1981) — one of the key post-punk/dub crossover connections on that album, alongside Ari-Up (The Slits). 8 shared credits with Singers & Players.

The Clash (1976)

Levene was introduced to Mick Jones by manager Bernie Rhodes in April 1976 and became a co-founder of The Clash alongside Jones and Paul Simonon. He helped persuade Joe Strummer to leave the pub rock band the 101ers and join the new group. (fodderstompf.com, as of 2026-06-25)

First show: 4 July 1976, Black Swan pub, Sheffield — The Clash supporting the Sex Pistols. At this gig Levene met John Lydon, and the two found common ground over feeling alienated within their respective bands; Levene suggested that if the Pistols ever split they should start a band together. (fodderstompf.com)

Departure: Levene quit in late August / early September 1976 — approximately five months after joining. He was not fired; he walked out during rehearsals, reportedly leaving his guitar feeding back against an amplifier. The last confirmed performance with the band was the Roundhouse, 5 September 1976. By the 100 Club Punk Festival on 20 September 1976, the band played as a four-piece without him. (The Arts Desk, “Music Reissues Weekly: Keith Levene and The Clash”)

Reasons given: Multiple overlapping factors are documented (from Marcus Gray, Last Gang in Town, 1995, and Pat Gilbert, Passion is a Fashion, 2005, as synthesised by The Arts Desk):

  1. Refused to play “White Riot” — objected to the title after Strummer brought the song to rehearsal following the Notting Hill Carnival riots (late August 1976).
  2. Friction with manager Bernard Rhodes and his “constant programming-style verbiage.”
  3. Strummer persistently haranguing him about the band’s political mission.
  4. General disillusionment with rock ‘n’ roll as a musical direction.

The Arts Desk notes: “Any or all of these resulted in him walking out.” Some secondary sources additionally cite drug use (amphetamines) as a factor, though this does not appear in the more detailed accounts. The story is not definitively settled from Levene’s own first-person accounts in these sources.

Levene’s own account: He wrote about the period in his autobiography I WaS a TeeN GuiTariST 4 the CLaSH! (2015). (Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-25)

Recordings: No studio recordings were made with the band. Three live recordings from his tenure exist: Screen on the Green (29 August 1976), 100 Club (31 August 1976), and the Roundhouse (5 September 1976). Despite no studio work, he received a co-writing credit on “What’s My Name” on The Clash (1977). (The Arts Desk)

Bridge to PiL: After leaving The Clash, Levene played briefly in Flowers of Romance (late 1976) alongside Sid Vicious and a pre-Slits Viv Albertine. He co-founded PiL with Lydon in May 1978 — roughly two years after their Sheffield meeting, following the Sex Pistols’ disbandment. (fodderstompf.com; Wikipedia/Public Image Ltd, as of 2026-06-25)

Conflict note: Some obituary sources (e.g. search aggregations) state Levene was fired. The more detailed secondary sources (fodderstompf.com biography; The Arts Desk piece drawing on Gray and Gilbert) consistently describe a voluntary walkout. The discrepancy likely reflects the ambiguity of a messy split rather than a clear-cut firing or resignation.
