Lee Hazlewood

Discogs ID: 225403 Real name: Barton Lee Hazlewood Born: July 9, 1929, Mannford, Oklahoma, USA Died: August 4, 2007, Las Vegas, Nevada, USA Aliases: “The Cowboy”, Grandpa (2), Mark Robinson (18)

American singer, songwriter, producer, and actor. One of the defining figures of the late-1950s to 1970s crossover between country, pop, and what would become country rock. Best known for his duo recordings with Nancy Sinatra and for writing “These Boots Are Made For Walkin’.” Founder of LHI Records.

Early Career: Producing the Twang (1955–1962)

Hazlewood began as a songwriter and radio DJ in the mid-1950s before moving into production. His first significant production work was with guitarist Duane Eddy at Jamie Records starting in 1958, where he engineered and produced Eddy’s signature “twangy” guitar sound on Have ‘Twangy’ Guitar Will Travel (1958) and subsequent releases. These records established Hazlewood’s production sensibility — dry reverb, low vocal register, cinematic space — that would define his solo work.

He wrote “Soda Fountain Girl” in 1955 (one of his first documented writing credits, uncredited on a label credited to Eb X. Preston). By the early 1960s he was producing in Los Angeles.

Mercury and Solo Debut (1963)

Trouble Is A Lonesome Town (Mercury SR60860, 1963)

His first solo LP. Self-produced with engineers Chuck Britz and Jim Malloy. A stark, concept-adjacent set of country-noir originals — ten songs depicting life in a small fictional town. Featured no name musicians in Discogs credits; Jack Tracy (Discogs ID: 353749) provided liner notes and supervised the session for Mercury. Tracy and Hazlewood share 16 releases in Discogs.

MGM Era and Nancy Sinatra (1964–1968)

Hazlewood moved to Reprise Records and then MGM through the mid-1960s, producing records for Nancy Sinatra (notably Friday’s Child, 1965) and recording his own LPs. Arranger Billy Strange and engineer/Director of Engineering Val Valentin were his principal studio collaborators at MGM.

Nancy & Lee (1966–1971)

Hazlewood’s most commercially successful work came from his partnership with Nancy Sinatra, Frank Sinatra’s daughter. Key singles:

  • “Summer Wine” (1966, Reprise)
  • “Jackson” (1967, Reprise)
  • “Some Velvet Morning” (1967, Reprise) — their most celebrated recording
  • “Lady Bird” (1967, Reprise)
  • Nancy & Lee album (1968, Reprise; 122+ pressings documented in Discogs)
  • “Did You Ever?” (1971, Reprise)

The duo also recorded Nancy & Lee 3 in 2004 (Warner Strategic Marketing Australia).

Hazlewood wrote “These Boots Are Made For Walkin’” and gave it to Nancy Sinatra; her 1966 recording reached #1. He co-wrote most of the duo’s material.

LHI Records (1967–1971)

Hazlewood founded Lee Hazlewood Industries and its label, LHI Records, in 1967. Originally called Eden Records, renamed LHI. Address: 9000 Sunset Boulevard, Los Angeles. Distributed by Decca (1967–68), then ABC Records (1968–71). Closed 1971; revived 1990s/2000s.

Notable LHI releases beyond Hazlewood’s own work: International Submarine Band Safe At Home (1968), considered by some the first country rock album. Honey Ltd. Honey Ltd. (LHI-12002, 1968) — a female vocal quartet from Detroit that Hazlewood produced and whose sessions brought in arrangers Ian Freebairn-Smith and Jack Nitzsche.
