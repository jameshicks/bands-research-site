David Daniell

NYC-based guitarist and musician. Active from the mid-1990s across experimental, post-rock, and drone contexts. Known for long-form electric guitar work, sustained collaborations with Douglas McCombs and Fennesz, and a central role in Rhys Chatham’s large-ensemble guitar works. Discogs artist ID: 81144

Name variations used in credits: DWD, Daniell, David D., David Daniel.

Biography

1990s — Early Work and Noise Scene

Daniell’s earliest Discogs credit is a guitar performance on a 1995 not-on-label release, Pine And Cotton, Sand. By 1999 he was appearing on releases across two distinct currents: a connection to the experimental folk-blues world via a collaboration with Loren MazzaCane Connors (see below), and engineering work for Table Of The Elements, Jeff Hunt’s Atlanta-based label focused on minimalist and drone composition.

In 1999, the quartet of Suzanne Langille, Andrew Burnes, Daniell, and Loren MazzaCane Connors released Let The Darkness Fall on Secretly Canadian — an all-guitar improvisation drawing on American Primitive and blues abstraction. The same year Daniell handled photography for Connors’s Primordial Lift on Table Of The Elements.

2000s — Solo Records and Thrill Jockey

In 2000 Daniell contributed a performance to Folk Songs For Trains, Trees And Honey on Warp Records — a compilation curated by the Brokeback / Tortoise axis.

His first solo album proper, I-IV-V-I, appeared in 2008 on Table Of The Elements (release ID 1545300). Credited with electric guitar, acoustic guitar, and bass across all parts, the record was produced with art direction by Table founder Jeff Hunt, etchings by Savage Pencil, and mastering by James Plotkin.

Also in 2008 he contributed mixing to Telescope on Table Of The Elements.

2009–2012 — Peak Collaborations

Sycamore (2009, Thrill Jockey, release ID 1924481) was a co-release with Douglas McCombs — both credited on electric guitar, with McCombs adding acoustic guitar and lap steel. John McEntire mixed; Jeremy Lemos recorded. Cover art by Arik Roper.

A Crimson Grail (2010, Nonesuch, release ID 2544710) was Rhys Chatham’s 200+ guitar ensemble piece, for which Daniell served as concertmaster — a significant organisational and musical role in one of the largest guitar orchestrations in recorded music.

Knoxville (2010, Thrill Jockey, release ID 2383877) was a trio record with Fennesz (Christian Fennesz, electric guitar and computer) and Tony Buck (drums). Daniell handled electric guitar, photography, and design. No external producer or engineer is credited — a lean, self-contained release.

Origins & Evolutions (2012, Install label, release ID 4848542) placed Daniell in a multi-guitarist ensemble curated by Aidan Baker, alongside Mick Barr and others — four long-form pieces running drone and percussive guitar textures.

Versions (2012, Thrill Jockey, release ID 3614830) was a second Daniell / McCombs duo album: electric guitar and pedal steel (Daniell), electric guitar and lap steel (McCombs). Mastered by Paul Gold.

2010s — Engineering and Photography Credits

Alongside solo and collaborative records, Daniell worked as engineer and/or mix assistant on several releases in this period: Sea Changes & Coelacanths (2006, Table Of The Elements, a John Fahey tribute); Offstrings: Inventions For Guitar (2010, Complacency); and mix assistance on Cleared (2011, Immune). He also contributed photography to Ocean Parkway (2012, Three Lobed Recordings) and cover design to Harmonie Du Soir (2013, Northern Spy).

2020 — Apiary

Daniell is a member of Apiary (2) (Discogs artist ID 8262902), a quartet with Jason Stein (2) on bass clarinet, Joseph Clayton Mills on electronics, and Steven Hess on drums. Their self-titled debut appeared in 2020 on The Tapeworm (UK). Daniell and Joseph Clayton Mills are co-credited for editing, mixing, and guitar/electronics respectively; mastering by Helge Sten.
