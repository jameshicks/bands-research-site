Willie Mitchell

Trumpeter, arranger, bandleader, and record producer. Born 3 March 1928, Ashland, Mississippi; died 5 January 2010, Memphis, Tennessee (heart attack). Discogs artist ID: 145293. Real name: William Lawrence Mitchell.

Mitchell was the defining architect of the Memphis soul sound of the 1970s. As in-house producer and co-owner at Hi Records (co-owner 1970–1979), he built and ran Royal Recording Studios on Willie Mitchell Boulevard in Memphis and constructed the sonic signature — warm, cushioned bass, stinging guitar, swirling organ, lush Memphis strings — that made Al Green’s run of albums on Hi the template for a decade. He also produced Ann Peebles, Syl Johnson, O.V. Wright, Otis Clay, and a roster of Hi acts before branching out to work with Paul Butterfield and founding his own Waylo label in the 1980s.

Biography

Early Career and Hi Records (1958–1968)

Mitchell began recording instrumentals for Home Of The Blues in 1961 before settling at Hi Records, where his first credited release appeared in 1962. Through the mid-1960s he was a performing bandleader issuing soul-jazz instrumental sides for Hi — Hold It!!! (1965), It’s Dance-Time With Willie Mitchell (1965), Solid Soul (1967), The Hit Sound Of (1967), On Top (1968), Up Hard (1968), Soul Serenade (1968), Willie Mitchell Live (1968) — distributed internationally by London Records. His single “20-75” (1964) was a regional hit, and the instrumental style he developed in this period — horn-heavy but rhythmically very precise — foreshadowed the Hi soul productions to come.

Alongside performing, Mitchell began producing other Hi artists in 1967: early credited production work includes sessions for Back Beat acts and smaller labels in this period.

The Hi Soul Peak — Al Green (1969–1979)

The partnership with Al Green began with Green Is Blues (1969, Hi Records) — Green’s debut on the label, on which Mitchell is credited as producer. The run of albums that followed through the mid-1970s constitutes Mitchell’s central legacy:

On these albums Mitchell served simultaneously as producer, engineer, mix engineer, string arranger (with James Mitchell, his brother), and co-writer on many tracks. His role was total: he controlled the studio sound from tracking to mastering. The rhythm section he used — the Hi Rhythm Section — was invariant: Leroy Hodges (bass), Mabon “Teenie” Hodges (guitar), Charles Hodges (organ/piano), Howard Grimes and Al Jackson Jr. (drums). The horn section came primarily from The Memphis Horns: Wayne Jackson (trumpet), Andrew Love (tenor saxophone), James Mitchell (baritone saxophone), Ed Logan (tenor saxophone), Jack Hale, Sr. (trombone). String arrangements were prepared by James Mitchell and Charles Chalmers, with the ensemble billed as The Memphis Strings. Background vocals across the run came from Rhodes, Chalmers & Rhodes.

Mitchell also engineered Ann Peebles’ debut This Is Ann Peebles (1969), though that album’s production credit went to Gene Miller.

Earlier, he produced O.V. Wright sessions for Back Beat Records through 1969–1973, capturing the same Hi musicians on a subsidiary label that fed into the Hi family. He also arranged for Westbound Records acts including Denise LaSalle (Trapped By A Thing Called Love, 1971; Now Run And Tell That, 1972) and for ATCO and Epic artists in one-off sessions.

The Bearsville Period — Paul Butterfield and Others (1979–1981)

After Hi’s sale to Al Bennett’s Cream Records in 1977 disrupted the label, Mitchell moved work to Bearsville Records for a run of productions:

  • Paul Butterfield, North South (1980) — recorded at Royal Recording Studios, Memphis. Mitchell produced, engineered, and co-arranged strings and horns; also played percussion. The Memphis Horns (Wayne Jackson, Andrew Love) and Memphis Strings appear on the session; James Mitchell arranged. This is the only known Bearsville session linking the entire Hi production apparatus to an outside artist.
  • Elizabeth Barraclough, Hi (1979) — produced and engineered by Mitchell; The Memphis Horns appear.
  • Various Bearsville sessions 1979–1981 under Mitchell’s production included Movin’ On A Feelin’ (1980) and Sugar (1980); his own album Willie…Mitchell…Listen…Dance appeared on Bearsville in 1981.
  • Rod Stewart, Atlantic Crossing (1975) — Mitchell is credited as engineer on this Bearsville/Warner release, a notable outside session.

Waylo Records (1982 onward)

Mitchell founded Waylo (parent: Waylo Records, Inc., Discogs label ID: 111477) in the early 1980s as his own imprint. He produced artists including Bobby McClure, Good Woman Go Bad, and released his own material. He continued running Royal Recording Studios, which remained active for outside productions. His son Boo Mitchell (Lawrence Mitchell) took over operation of Royal Studios and continued the family presence in Memphis recording.

Mitchell produced a Memphis Sessions album for The Precious Organisation in 1988, returned to produce Al Green’s I Can’t Stop (Blue Note, 2003) and Everything’s OK (Blue Note, 2004), and contributed to various compilations of Hi material issued through the 1990s–2000s.

Research Queue

  • Discogs credits show Rod Stewart’s Atlantic Crossing (1975, Bearsville/Warner) with Mitchell as engineer — what tracks did he work on, and was he the sole engineer or one of several at Royal Studios for that session?
  • Ann Peebles’ debut This Is Ann Peebles (1969) credits Gene Miller as producer and Mitchell only as engineer — at what point exactly did Mitchell take over sole production of the Peebles catalog?
  • The Denise LaSalle arrangement credits (Westbound, 1971–1972) — did Mitchell arrange those sessions at Royal Studios or did he provide arrangements that were recorded elsewhere?
  • Willie Mitchell Live (1968, London Records) — what is the documented venue and date for this recording? Discogs gives no location.
  • Discogs lists a “Directed By” credit for Mitchell on a 1968 Decca single (“I Can’t Put You Down / We Must Have Love”) — what does that role entail in this context, and who was the artist?
  • O.V. Wright’s Back Beat sessions (1969–1973): were all of these recorded at Royal Studios with the same Hi Rhythm ensemble, or did some use different personnel?
  • The Memphis Strings (Discogs ID: 389960) appear as a billed ensemble on Hi sessions — who organized and led this ensemble, and was it a standing group or assembled per session?
