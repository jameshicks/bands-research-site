Michael Steele

American bassist, guitarist, songwriter, and singer. Born Susan Thomas, June 2, 1955, Pasadena, California. Discogs artist ID: 452206. Also credited as Micki Steele in early career work.

Best known as bassist and vocalist of The Bangles from 1984 through the band’s 2003 reunion album, replacing original bassist Annette Zilinskas. Prior to The Bangles she was a member of The Runaways and a number of smaller Los Angeles bands.

Early Career and The Runaways

Before her tenure in The Bangles, Steele (then known as Micki Steele) passed through several bands in the Los Angeles rock underground. Her most notable early association was with The Runaways, the all-female hard rock group led by Joan Jett and managed by Kim Fowley. Steele appears on the Runaways’ posthumous archival release Born To Be Bad (1991, Marilyn) in the role of bass and vocals, credited as Micki Steele. The Born To Be Bad compilation drew on demo recordings and outtakes from the band’s mid-to-late 1970s period.

She also served as a member of:

  • Slow Children (2) — a Los Angeles new wave/pop act also featuring Pal Shazar and Andrew Chinich. The band released records on Ensign (UK/US) between 1979 and 1982, including the album Spring In Fialta (1981) and several singles.
  • Snakefinger’s Vestal Virgins — a project associated with guitarist Philip Charles Lithman (Snakefinger), also featuring Eric Drew Feldman among others.
  • Nadia Kapiche — an early-1980s Los Angeles group that also included Toni Childs, Jack Sherman, Larry Zee, and Steve Schiff. Released “Africa” (M.A.O. Records, 1981), on which Steele played bass and sang; produced by Toni And The Movers with engineering by Dave Jerden.
  • Toni And The Movers / Elton Duck — further pre-Bangles LA associations also involving Toni Childs and Jack Sherman.

The Bangles (1984–2003)

Steele joined The Bangles in 1984 in time for the band’s major-label debut on Columbia Records, replacing Annette Zilinskas on bass. She remained with the band through their commercial peak and their 2003 reunion album, contributing bass, occasional electric and acoustic guitar, vocals, and percussion throughout.

All Over The Place (1984)

The major-label debut, produced by David Kahne and engineered by Kahne with additional engineering from Andrew Berliner; mixed by Joe Chiccarelli. Steele played bass and sang. Songwriting on the album was split among the Peterson sisters and Hoffs, with no Steele-penned writing credits on this release.

Everything (1988)

Produced by Davitt Sigerson. Steele credited for bass guitar, occasional guitar, percussion, and vocals. The album featured a range of session contributors including David Lindley (lute, dobro, bouzouki, classical guitar), John Philip Shenale (keyboards/programming), Paulinho Da Costa (percussion), and Tommy Morgan (harmonica). Mastered by Doug Sax; mixed by Frank Filipetti. “Eternal Flame” reached US #1.

Doll Revolution (2003)

Reunion album, self-produced by The Bangles with Brad Wood (also engineer and mixer). Released on Down Kiddie Records (Canada) / Liberty EMI (UK). Steele played acoustic guitar, electric guitar, bass, and sang. The album is notable for including a “Bangles Boys Choir” featuring Dave Grohl (Foo Fighters/Nirvana), Chick Wolverton, and John Crooke. Additional players included Peter Holsapple (accordion, keyboards, mandolin), Greg Leisz (lap steel, pedal steel), and Dillon O’Brian (electric guitar, Wurlitzer).
