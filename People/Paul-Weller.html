Oct 25, 20267 min read

Paul Weller

Discogs ID: 14906 Real name: John William Weller Jr Born: 25 May 1958, Sheerwater (near Woking), Surrey, England Aliases: Jake Fluckery, The Boy Wonder

British singer, songwriter, and guitarist. Founder and frontman of The Jam (1972–1982) and The Style Council (1983–1989), then a solo artist from 1992 to present. Central figure in the UK Mod revival of the late 1970s and one of the most persistently influential British rock songwriters of the past five decades.

Era I — The Jam (1972–1982)

Weller formed The Jam in Woking, Surrey, in 1972 with Bruce Foxton (bass), Rick Buckler (drums), and briefly Steve Brookes (guitar). The group emerged from the pub-rock/punk circuit and signed to Polydor in 1977. Weller was the primary songwriter and creative force throughout the band’s existence.

The Jam’s debut album In The City (1977) arrived in the first wave of British punk, but the band’s sharp dress, Rickenbacker guitars, and Weller’s Mod obsessions set them apart from the Sex Pistols school. All Mod Cons (1978) is widely regarded as the breakthrough: it marked Weller’s emergence as a social-realist lyricist, drawing on the Kinks’ Ray Davies (who contributed one track) and Pete Townshend. Setting Sons (1979) continued in the same vein, incorporating a loose concept of disillusionment among old school friends and featuring orchestral arrangements.

Sound Affects (1980) showed Weller absorbing post-punk and funk while retaining melodic songcraft. By 1982’s The Gift, the band had achieved the rare status of regularly reaching #1 in the UK charts; “Town Called Malice” / “Precious” debuted at #1. Weller abruptly dissolved The Jam in October 1982, citing artistic restlessness. Their final single “Beat Surrender” also went straight to #1.

The Jam’s catalogue remains on Polydor.

Era II — The Style Council (1983–1989)

After dissolving The Jam, Weller formed The Style Council with Mick Talbot (keyboards). Drummer Steve White (3) and Weller’s partner Dee C. Lee (vocals) became key collaborators. The band was signed to Polydor for most territories, with Polydor subsidiary Geffen handling US releases.

The Style Council was a deliberate reinvention: where The Jam had been guitar-driven and Mod, the Style Council embraced jazz-inflected pop (Café Bleu, 1984), soul and funk (Our Favourite Shop, 1985), and eventually house and dance music (Confessions Of A Pop Group, 1988). The band toured extensively, issuing numerous singles and EPs.

Polydor rejected the house-influenced album Modernism: A New Decade in 1989; the album sat unreleased until 1998. The Style Council dissolved in 1990.

Era III — Solo Career (1992–present)

Weller relaunched as a solo artist in 1992, signing to Go! Discs. The self-titled debut (1992) was produced with Brendan Lynch as co-producer — a relationship that defined his sound through the mid-to-late 1990s. The albums Wild Wood (1993) and Stanley Road (1995) consolidated his critical and commercial standing; both reached #2 in the UK album chart. Stanley Road in particular became a defining statement, featuring cover artwork by painter Peter Blake (4) and guest bass work from Dr. Robert, Marco Nelson, and Yolanda Charles.

After Go! Discs, Weller moved to Island Records for Heavy Soul (1997), Heliocentric (2000), and Days Of Speed (2001). Later albums appeared on Independiente (Illumination, 2002), V2 (Studio 150, 2004; As Is Now, 2005), Island again (22 Dreams, 2008; Wake Up The Nation, 2010), and then Parlophone/Polydor for his more recent work. 22 Dreams (2008) marked an experimental expansion, featuring guest appearances from Graham Coxon and Bobby Gillespie, and is Weller’s most sprawling record. On Sunset (2020) and Fat Pop (Volume 1) (2021) both reached #1 in the UK.

In 2024 he released 66 on Polydor and Soul Wandering; in 2025, Find El Dorado (Parlophone).

Production Work — The Nips Single (1981)

Weller produced Happy Song / Nobody To Love by The Nips (Shane MacGowan’s pre-Pogues band), released on Test Pressings (cat. T.P.5., 1981). Engineered by Peter Wilson. This was an outside production credit during the final years of The Jam.

Connection to Robert Wyatt — Shleep (1997)

Weller mixed tracks 5 and 10 on Robert Wyatt’s album Shleep (1997, Hannibal Records), assisted by Charles Rees and Jamie Johnson (who co-mixed). Robert Wyatt mixed the remaining tracks himself with the same two engineers. The connection reflects Weller’s long-standing ties to the British art-rock and experimental left; he has appeared on several Wyatt-adjacent projects.
