Hamiet Bluiett

Jazz baritone saxophonist, clarinetist, and composer. Born September 16, 1940 in Lovejoy (or Brooklyn), Illinois; died October 4, 2018 in St. Louis, Missouri. Discogs artist ID: 296988.

One of the foremost baritone saxophonists in jazz history. Co-founder of Black Artists Group (BAG) in St. Louis. Founding member of World Saxophone Quartet. Led a parallel solo career across five decades on India Navigation, Black Saint, Soul Note, Enja Records, and CIMP. Known as “Bunny” to colleagues; also recorded as Hamiet Bluiett, Jr.

Biography

Early Life and Education

Bluiett was born in 1940 in Lovejoy, Illinois (some sources say Brooklyn, Illinois), near East St. Louis. He came up in the St. Louis jazz and R&B scene, studied music formally, and played in military bands. He developed a deep command of the baritone saxophone at a time when the instrument was rarely heard outside of big band sections; he also became a serious multi-wind player, working extensively on clarinet (alto, soprano, bass, contrabass, contra-alto), flute (including bamboo flute and alto flute), and bass and contrabass saxophone.

Black Artists Group (BAG), St. Louis, c. 1968–1972

Bluiett was a founding member of the Black Artists Group, the St. Louis collective that paralleled Chicago’s AACM. BAG brought together musicians, dancers, poets, and visual artists in a community arts umbrella. Alongside Julius Hemphill, Oliver Lake, Charles Bobo Shaw, Baikida Carroll, Joseph Bowie, Lester Bowie, Floyd LeFlore, Shirley LeFlore, and Phillip Wilson, Bluiett helped define the St. Louis strand of free jazz. BAG’s activities are documented on the collective’s releases (see Black Artists Group) and in Bluiett’s early appearance with The Human Arts Ensemble.

New York Arrival, 1970s

After BAG dissolved around 1972, most of its core musicians relocated to New York. Bluiett embedded himself in the loft jazz scene almost immediately. His session credits through the mid-1970s show him working across a wide range of New York free and post-bop contexts:

  • Charles Mingus — appeared on Mingus At Carnegie Hall (1974, Atlantic), one of Mingus’s large-scale concert events alongside Roland Kirk, Don Pullen, George Adams, Jon Faddis, and Dannie Richmond.
  • Dollar Brand (Abdullah Ibrahim) — African Space Program (1974, Enja), a large-ensemble session featuring John Stubblefield, John Purcell, Cecil McBee, and Enrico Rava.
  • The 360 Degree Music Experience — In:Sanity (1976, Black Saint), Beaver Harris’s collective; Bluiett served as arranger, conductor, and multi-instrumentalist alongside Dave Burrell and Cecil McBee.

His first recording as leader, Endangered Species (1976, India Navigation), came out of this milieu: the group featured Olu Dara on trumpet, Phillip Wilson on drums, Juini Booth on bass, and Jumma Santos on balafon.

World Saxophone Quartet, 1976–

In 1976 Bluiett co-founded the World Saxophone Quartet with Julius Hemphill, Oliver Lake, and David Murray. The WSQ performed and recorded without a rhythm section, placing four saxophones in direct dialogue. Bluiett anchored the ensemble from below as the permanent baritone voice. The group’s discography runs from Point Of No Return (1977, Moers Music) through a long stretch on Black Saint (six albums, 1979–1994), a major-label run on Elektra/Nonesuch (four albums, 1986–1994), and later releases on Justin Time and Jazzwerkstatt. See World Saxophone Quartet for full discography and personnel detail.

Solo Career as Leader

Bluiett maintained a continuous solo recording career running in parallel with WSQ. His leader discography shows a consistent aesthetic: baritone saxophone at the center, small groups, frequent use of the full clarinet and flute family, and a preference for rhythm sections that understood both blues and free improvisation.

Bluiett Baritone Nation / Baritone Saxophone Group

Bluiett also led Bluiett Baritone Nation and the Bluiett Baritone Saxophone Group, projects centered on multiple baritone saxophones — an extension of the all-reeds philosophy he developed in WSQ into a single-instrument ensemble context.

Instruments

Primary: baritone saxophone. Also documented on: alto clarinet, soprano clarinet, bass clarinet, contrabass clarinet, contra-alto clarinet, flute, alto flute, bamboo flute, bass saxophone, contrabass saxophone. His work on The Power (2000) has him on flute, bass saxophone, and contrabass saxophone — none of which is the baritone, underscoring his range.

Open Questions

  • BAG’s specific chronology (founding year, dissolution) and Bluiett’s exact role within it are not resolved by Discogs alone — his Discogs profile does not contain a bio section with this level of detail.
  • The Japanese Apple Cores (1979, Baystate) has no personnel listed in Discogs.
  • In The Name Of… (1994, DIW) — the Discogs listing shows this as a solo release with Bluiett as featured artist; full personnel not confirmed here.
  • The precise recording date of Kahil El’Zabar’s America the Beautiful (issued 2020) relative to Bluiett’s October 2018 death needs confirmation.
  • Discogs has no entry for Bluiett’s early 1960s credit on Direct From The Blue Note Club (1963) in a primary role; this session appears in his credits list with the role “Baritone Saxophone” — may be an early pre-BAG session requiring web verification.
