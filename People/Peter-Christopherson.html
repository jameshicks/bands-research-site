Peter Christopherson

Born: 27 February 1955, Leeds, England Died: 25 November 2010, Bangkok, Thailand Also known as: Sleazy Discogs ID: 34069

Overview

Peter Martin Christopherson (“Sleazy”) was an English musician, music video director, commercial artist, designer, and photographer. He was a founding member of Throbbing Gristle (the band credited with creating industrial music), Psychic TV, and the experimental electronic duo Coil. Before his musical career took off he was a partner in the celebrated album cover design agency Hipgnosis (2), responsible for some of the most iconic artwork of 1970s rock (Pink Floyd’s Wish You Were Here, Animals; three Peter Gabriel solo LPs). After John Balance’s death in 2004, Christopherson relocated to Bangkok, Thailand, where he founded the solo project The Threshold HouseBoys Choir and the duo SoiSong with CoH (Ivan Pavlov). He died in his sleep in Bangkok on 25 November 2010 at age 55. His father Derman Christopherson (1915–2000) was a professor of engineering and master of Magdalene College, Cambridge. (Wikipedia)

Career by Era

Hipgnosis — Design and Photography (1974–1982)

Christopherson joined the Hipgnosis design agency (Discogs: Hipgnosis (2), ID: 1826972) in 1974 as a junior assistant; he became a full partner alongside Storm Thorgerson and Aubrey Powell. Hipgnosis created sleeve artwork for Pink Floyd, Peter Gabriel, Led Zeppelin, 10cc, Al Stewart, and dozens of other major artists. After Hipgnosis disbanded in 1982, Christopherson became a partner in Greenback Films. He also shot the first promotional photographs of the Sex Pistols (1976) and did design work for Malcolm McLaren/Vivienne Westwood’s SEX boutique. He designed the BOY London logo. (Wikipedia)

COUM Transmissions and Throbbing Gristle (1975–1981)

In March 1974 Christopherson attended a COUM Transmissions performance and befriended Genesis P-Orridge and Cosey Fanni Tutti. By March 1975 he was performing with COUM Transmissions; in September 1975 the trio joined with Chris Carter (2) to form Throbbing Gristle. Christopherson played tapes, electronics (“machines”), cornet, harmonica, vibraphone, and vocals. He co-wrote most TG material alongside the other members.

Psychic TV (1981–1984)

Following TG’s dissolution in 1981, Christopherson worked with Genesis P-Orridge in Psychic TV and the Temple Ov Psychick Youth. His lover Geoff Rushton (later John Balance) joined the PTV circle during this period. Christopherson played keyboards, electronic percussion, tubular bells, and contributed music on the first two PTV albums before departing to form Coil.

Coil (1983–2004)

Coil was the defining project of Christopherson’s musical life, formed with Geoff Rushton (John Balance) after their break from Psychic TV. The duo — occasionally augmented by Stephen Thrower, Drew McDowall, Thighpaulsandra, Ossian Brown, and others — released across their own Threshold House label and its sublabels Eskaton and Chalice.

After John Balance died in November 2004, Christopherson continued to issue archival Coil material and oversaw posthumous releases. He also released a 16-DVD box set Colour Sound Oblivion (tour photographs, Balance’s funeral program).

The Threshold HouseBoys Choir (2005–2010)

After relocating to Bangkok in 2005, Christopherson launched the solo project The Threshold HouseBoys Choir (Discogs alias). The debut album Form Grows Rampant (2007, Threshold House) consists of five pieces with a DVD of Thai ritual footage filmed in Krabi. Danny Hyde co-produced and “semi-conducted.” Mastered by Mark Godwin (2).

SoiSong (2008–2012)

In 2008 Christopherson and CoH (Ivan Pavlov) formed SoiSong (Discogs ID: 1132285). The project premiered in Tokyo on 9 March 2008 and performed across Europe, including a live soundtrack to Derek Jarman’s Blue in Rovereto, Italy. Their debut LP xAj3z (2009, soisong.com) featured “Melodic Primitives And Vocalists.” Christopherson died before completing his portion of the Soisong Split (2012); his four unpublished sketches were released posthumously alongside a CoH EP. (Wikipedia)

Electric Sewer Age (2010)

The last recordings Christopherson made were with Electric Sewer Age (Discogs ID: 2615190), a project also involving Danny Hyde and John Deek. These sessions were intended as a “Moons Milk” series; an album In Final Phase was eventually issued in 2013 in an edition of 999 numbered copies. (Wikipedia)

Video Direction

Christopherson directed over 40 music videos. Notable credits include:

  • Broken (1993) — Nine Inch Nails; a short horror film; directed by Christopherson (uncredited on the Australian pressing)
  • Rage Against the Machine — Live At The Grand Olympic Auditorium (2003); also Live At Kezar (1981, Japan)
  • Robert Plant — Now Voyager (1985, LaserDisc)
  • Sepultura — Il Demonio Nera (2005)
  • The Innocents (Goldfrapp) — The Innocents EPK (2009)
  • Closure (1996) — Nine Inch Nails home video; film director and editor
  • Train Of Thought — video (1984, Japan TOEMI Video)
  • Stories — music video direction for A&M Records (1995)

He was also involved in producing the Desertshore / The Final Report TG album (2012, posthumous), where he had been working on a reworking of Nico’s Desertshore at the time of his death.

Throbbing Gristle Reform (2004–2010)

TG reformed for concerts in 2004 and released TG Now (2004, Industrial Records/Mute) and Live December 2004 (A Souvenir Of Camber Sands) (2004). A new studio album Part Two — The Endless Not followed in 2007. Christopherson contributed design, art direction, and sampler sounds. The band disbanded again in 2010. A posthumous TG project X-TG (Discogs ID: 2020530) assembled surviving members for Desertshore / The Final Report (2012) and the แฝดนรก (Faet Narok) album (2012).
