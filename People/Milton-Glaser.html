Milton Glaser

American graphic designer, born 1929, New York City; died 26 June 2020, New York City. Discogs artist ID: 1131756. Groups/studios: Push Pin Studios (Discogs ID: 1853299), Milton Glaser, Inc. (Discogs ID: 4037153).

Overview

Glaser was one of the most prolific and influential American graphic designers of the twentieth century, best known outside the music world for the “I ♥ NY” logo (1976) and the psychedelic poster included with original pressings of Bob Dylan’s Greatest Hits (1967). Within the music industry he worked continuously from the early 1950s through the 2000s, covering classical, folk, jazz, blues, rock, and avant-garde releases across more than 200 credits on Discogs.

In 1954, Glaser co-founded Push Pin Studios with Reynold Ruffins, Seymour Chwast, and Edward Sorel — a design collective that shaped American graphic illustration through the 1960s and 1970s. In 1974 he established Milton Glaser, Inc. as a separate Manhattan studio. He was also co-founder of New York magazine with Clay Felker (1968), serving as president and design director until 1977.

Career Phases in Music

Early Classical Work — Westminster, Columbia Masterworks, Epic (1953–1966)

Glaser’s earliest music credits, starting in 1953, are almost entirely classical LP covers for Westminster, Columbia Masterworks, and Epic — Bartók, Stravinsky, Debussy, Mozart, Brahms, Grieg, Rachmaninoff and others. These covers established his relationship with Columbia’s classical division (CBS/Columbia Masterworks), which would recur throughout his career. He also picked up a 1955 cover for RCA Victor (Tony Scott Septet).

Folk and Pop — Warner Bros., Kapp, Columbia (1962–1967)

In 1962 Glaser designed the debut album cover for Peter, Paul and Mary on Warner Bros. Records — one of his highest-profile early pop commissions. He returned to Peter, Paul and Mary for Peter, Paul and Mommy (1969) and the compilation (Ten) Years Together (1970), among others.

He did heavy work for Kapp Records through the mid-1960s, producing covers and illustrations for folk, children’s, and novelty records including Tom Glazer’s On Top Of Spaghetti and various seasonal releases. Also during this period: Columbia covers for the jazz and blues anthologies The Sound Of Harlem, The Sound Of New Orleans, The Sound Of Chicago, Blues Roots.

The Bob Dylan connection arrived in 1967: Glaser created the iconic psychedelic silhouette poster included with Bob Dylan’s Greatest Hits on Columbia — now one of the most reproduced images in rock design history.

Poppy Records — the Core Relationship (1968–1972)

Glaser’s most sustained label relationship in rock/folk was with Poppy Records, a boutique label managed through the Bearsville/Albert Grossman orbit. He designed nearly every Poppy release, including:

  • Music From Big Pink — The Band, Capitol Records, 1968 (album design; the cover painting itself was by Bob Dylan)
  • For The Sake Of The Song — Townes Van Zandt, 1968
  • Our Mother The Mountain — Townes Van Zandt, 1969
  • Townes Van Zandt (self-titled), 1969
  • Lightnin’! — Lightnin’ Hopkins, 1969
  • Medium — The Mandrake Memorial, 1969
  • Blues Roots — compilation, 1969
  • Dick Gregory On:, Dick Gregory Live At The Village Gate, Dick Gregory At Kent State — 1969–1971
  • Puzzle, The Late Great Townes Van Zandt, 2nd Right 3rd Row, various others through 1972

Bearsville Records (1971–1975)

When Bearsville Records launched as a separate entity (still under Albert Grossman), Glaser moved with it. Key Bearsville credits:

  • Lazarus (1971), Jesse Frederick (1971)
  • Runt. The Ballad Of Todd Rundgren — Todd Rundgren, 1971 (cover design)
  • Better Days — Paul Butterfield’s Better Days, 1973
  • It All Comes Back — Paul Butterfield’s Better Days, 1973
  • M. Frog — Malcolm Rebennack (Dr. John), 1973 (as M. Frog Labat)
  • Put It In Your Ear — 1975

Major Rock/Pop Commissions (1973–1978)

  • There Goes Rhymin’ Simon — Paul Simon, Columbia, 1973 (cover design)
  • Portrait Gallery — Harry Chapin, Elektra, 1975 (design + illustration)
  • The First Seven Days — Kansas, Nemperor, 1975 (cover illustration)
  • The Wiz (original cast recording) — Atlantic, 1975 (illustration)
  • Masekela Introducing Hedzoleh Soundz — Hugh Masekela, Blue Thumb Records, 1973 (cover design)
  • Chick Corea, Herbie Hancock, Keith Jarrett, McCoy Tyner — Atlantic, 1976 (illustration)
  • Mary — Mary Travers, Warner Bros., 1971
  • Reunion — Dave Mason / Cass Elliot, Warner Bros., 1978 (art direction + design)
  • Carnival — Natalie Cole, Columbia, 1978
  • I Love New York — Salsoul Records, 1978 (cover design — the “I ♥ NY” logo applied to a disco compilation)

Tomato Records — Extended Design Partnership (1977–2003)

Glaser became the house designer and illustrator for Tomato Records, a blues/jazz/experimental label launched circa 1977. He designed dozens of Tomato releases, including:

  • John Cage — 1978 (cover design)
  • Einstein On The Beach — Philip Glass / Robert Wilson (2), 1979 (box and booklet design, box cover illustration)
  • Flyin’ Shoes — Townes Van Zandt, 1978
  • Live At The Old Quarter, Houston, Texas — Townes Van Zandt, 1977
  • Sonatas And Interludes / A Book Of Music — John Cage, 1977
  • Earthquake Island — John Abercrombie, Ariola/Tomato, 1978
  • Waves — 1979; X-Dreams — 1979; Space Minds — 1979
  • Kiss My Axe — Clarence “Gatemouth” Brown, 1991
  • Dozens of blues reissues and compilations through the 1990s and 2000s

For later Tomato releases (1993 onward), Glaser’s credit typically combines “Design, Illustration” — he was creating the full visual identity of the label’s catalog reissues.

Other Notable Credits

  • Etudes Australes For Piano — John Cage, Ariola, 1979 (cover design)
  • Chase (self-titled) — Epic, 1971 (cover lettering/typography)
  • The Mandrake Memorial — Poppy, 1968 (album design)
  • Songs From The Trilogy — Emerson, Lake & Palmer, CBS, 1989 (Einstein illustration credited by name)
  • First Impressions Of Earth — The Strokes, RCA, 2006 (artwork credited for specific tracks: “Ask Me Anything” music notes and “Ize Of The World” gun barrel eyes)
  • WTC 9/11 / Different Trains — Steve Reich, Megadisc, 2016 (“I ♥ NY More Than Ever” logo)
  • Live At Montreux 1976 — 2005 reissue (credited for original Montreux Jazz Festival poster from 1976)

Connections

  • Push Pin Studios — co-founder, 1954; co-founders include Seymour Chwast and Edward Sorel
  • Albert Grossman — manager of The Band, Townes Van Zandt, Janis Joplin; ran both Poppy Records and Bearsville Records; Glaser was house designer for both labels
  • Bob Dylan — painted the cover of Music From Big Pink (which Glaser designed); Glaser created the poster for Greatest Hits
  • Townes Van Zandt — designed five or more Poppy/Tomato albums; one of the most consistent artist-designer pairings in his career
  • Todd Rundgren — Bearsville cover designs including Runt. The Ballad Of Todd Rundgren
  • Philip Glass — designed the box and booklet for Einstein On The Beach (Tomato, 1979)
  • Paul Simon — designed There Goes Rhymin’ Simon (Columbia, 1973)
  • Harry Chapin — Portrait Gallery, Elektra, 1975
  • Hugh Masekela — Masekela Introducing Hedzoleh Soundz, Blue Thumb, 1973
  • The Band — designed Music From Big Pink (Capitol, 1968); Bob Dylan painted the cover
