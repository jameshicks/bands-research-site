Overview

Nobukazu Takemura is a Japanese musician, DJ, producer, and artist whose career has spanned hip-hop, nu-jazz, glitch, IDM, drum and bass, chamber music, and performance art. He grew up listening to radio and began making music at home with a tape recorder and keyboard; a record store job in high school exposed him to jazz and hip-hop, and he started regular DJ gigs in Osaka and Kyoto clubs before launching his production career.

In 1987 he formed the DJ team Cool Jazz Productions, and in 1988 he joined Audio Sports, the hip-hop unit led by Yamatsuka Eye of Boredoms, also featuring Aki Onda. Around 1993 he branched into solo work under the moniker Child’s View, a name reflecting a core thematic preoccupation — melodies that capture a sense of childlike wonder and innocence. He simultaneously developed Spiritual Vibes, a nu-jazz collective active throughout the early to mid-1990s.

His first US release was Funfair (1999, Bubble Core) — a slightly altered version of the Yoru No Yuenchi album already released in Japan on his own Childisc label. This was followed by Scope (1999, Thrill Jockey), his most critically noted work, a long-form glitch/minimal electronics album featuring voice samples by longtime collaborator Aki Tsuyuko. The Thrill Jockey relationship continued through Sign (2000) and the Assembler alias releases. He founded the Lollop and Childisc labels in the 1990s to release music by artists who lacked label homes.

Takemura is also known for commercial commissions: he created all sounds for Sony’s robotic dog AIBO, and composed music for two seasons of Issey Miyake’s Naoki Takizawa-directed Men collections (1999 Milano and Finale albums). He toured the US opening for Tortoise and Plaid in the early 2000s, and toured the UK with a full live band on a British Arts Council sponsorship. He moved from Kyoto to Berlin in 2008. In 2014 he released Zeitraum, a compilation of material from 2004–2013; Einheit followed in 2015. As of 2025 he returned to Thrill Jockey with Knot Of Meanings.

The vast majority of his music was recorded in his home studio in his Kyoto apartment, referred to as “Moonlit Studio.”

Key Release Credits

Child’s View (1994) — Bellissima / 99 Records

Debut album. Recorded across Tokyo, London, and Paris. Vocals by Kikuko Nonaka (“Kiku”), Maki Hiraga, Reichi, Stephen A. Elliott, Dee C. Lee, Nadja (4), Monika Linges, Seth Borden. Guitar by Uchihashi Kazuhisa. Drums by Yasuhiro Yoshigaki. Percussion by Takero Ogata. Synthesizer by Toshiyuki Mori. Mixed by Howie B. (4 tracks), Masashi Yabuhara, Tokehiko Kamada. Recorded in Japan by Masashi Yabuhara, Ohji Hayashi, Ryoichi Fukumoto, Takehiko Kamada; in London by Howie B.; in Paris by Philippe Cerboneschi (alias of Philippe Zdar). Genres: Electronic, Jazz, Latin.

Changing Hands (1997) — Flavour of Sound / Medium Productions Limited

Three-way collaboration with Steve Jansen (Japan, Rain Tree Crow) and Richard Barbieri (Japan, Porcupine Tree). All three co-wrote, performed, and produced. Recorded at Moonlit Studio (Kyoto) by Ohji Hayashi, and London by Jansen and Barbieri. Mastered by Masaaki Kato.

Scope (1999) — Thrill Jockey

Solo electronics album. Five extended tracks (longest: 22:29 “On A Ball”). Voice sample by Aki Tsuyuko. All composed by Takemura. Styles: Experimental, Glitch, Minimal.

Sign (2000) — Thrill Jockey

Includes “Souvenir In Chicago” (side B) recorded with Tortoise members: John McEntire (drums), Douglas McCombs (bass), Bundy K. Brown (guitar), recorded by Casey Rice. Styles: Abstract, Experimental, IDM.

Songbook (2001) — Childisc / Bubble Core

Multi-instrumentalist album (Takemura: guitar, piano, organ, bass, synthesizer). Features Aki Tsuyuko (voice, harp, words), Hirono Nishiyama / Gutevolk (voice, words), Jim O’Rourke (guitar, track 18), Tadashi Sato (cello), Jun Nagami (drums), Hironao Nishida (trumpet). Recorded and mixed by Takemura and Ohji Hayashi.

10th (2002) — Childisc / Thrill Jockey

Solo — all composition, programming, arrangement, performance, mixing, and mastering by Takemura. Lyrics by Aki Tsuyuko. Design by Kohei Osato. Illustration by Nakaban.

Identification With The Enemy: A Key To The Underworld (2007) — Atavistic

Collaboration with Italian experimental trio Zu (Luca T. Mai — baritone saxophone, Massimo Pupillo — bass, Jacopo Battaglia — drums/electronics). Takemura on electronics. Produced and mixed by Zu / Matteo Spinazzè. Styles: Experimental, Rhythmic Noise.

Groups and Collectives

Audio Sports

Hip-hop group formed 1988 in Osaka. Members: Nobukazu Takemura (Discogs ID: 1777), Yamatsuka Eye (Discogs ID: 170037) of Boredoms, Aki Onda (Discogs ID: 136990). Released on All Access (2). Discography includes 3·6·9 (1990), Eat & Buy & Eat (1992), Ooh I Feel Horny (1994), Strange Emotion (1995). After Takemura departed, Onda took primary control. Wikipedia cites Era of Glittering Gas (1992) as first album.

Spiritual Vibes

Nu-jazz collective formed 1992. Members: Nobukazu Takemura, Gaku Nakamura, Hiroshi Funato, Honzi, Kazumi Totaka, Kenji Kamon, Kikuko Nonaka, Kohei Osato, Takero Ogata, Takeshi Habu, Tomoo Kawagoe. Discography: Spiritual Vibes (1993, Bellissima Records), Newly (1994, 99 Records), Tender Blue (1995, Bellissima), Yawaraka Na Kaze (1995, Idyllic), ことばのまえ (1996, Idyllic). Also released as Spiritual Vibes — Alternative Tracks on 99 Records (1993/1994).

Kool Jazz Productions

DJ team formed 1987. Members: Nobukazu Takemura, Aki Onda, MC Maga, Momo (91), Tatsuya Hirano (2). One Discogs release: Hoping For The Sun (1992, Global Dept Records, UK 12”).

Artwank

Duo: Nobukazu Takemura + Kiyoshi Izumi. No Discogs releases found (only member credits).

Childisc Label

Takemura founded Childisc (Discogs ID: 8218) in Japan in the late 1990s. 84 releases in Discogs. Primarily a home for Takemura’s own releases and his extended circle of collaborators. Key Childisc artists include Aki Tsuyuko (Sprout 1998, Ongakushitsu 1999, Hokane 2006), Child’s View EPs, Lullatone, Suppa Micro Pamchopp. The label operated using Tokuma Japan Communications (TKCA-) catalog numbers for several releases from 2001–2002.
