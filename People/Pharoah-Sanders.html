Pharoah Sanders

Born: Ferrell Lee Sanders, 13 October 1940, Little Rock, Arkansas, USA Died: 24 September 2022, Los Angeles, California, USA Discogs ID: 21147 Instrument: Tenor saxophone (also soprano sax, alto sax, piccolo flute, bass clarinet, percussion, vocals)

American jazz tenor saxophonist; one of the defining voices of spiritual jazz and free jazz. Active from the early 1960s until his death in 2022. Known for a physically overwhelming tone — overtone-rich, multiphonic, veering from blistering sheets of sound into passages of extreme tenderness. A central figure in the late work of John Coltrane, then a prolific leader for Impulse!, Strata-East, and Theresa Records across the late 1960s through the 1980s. A late-career collaboration with Floating Points and the London Symphony Orchestra on Promises (2021) brought him to a new generation of listeners shortly before his death.

Early Life and Apprenticeship (1940–1964)

Born in Little Rock, Arkansas. Learned to play saxophone in high school; moved to Oakland, California, as a young adult and subsequently to New York City, where he fell into the orbit of the experimental jazz scene in the early 1960s. He played with Sun Ra and recorded his debut LP Pharaoh in 1964 for ESP-Disk’ (released 1965, Discogs ID 1894971) — an early document of his approach, credited as “Little Rock” on that session. A second early document, In The Beginning 1963–1964, was later released on ESP-Disk’ in 2012 (ID 4143805).

With John Coltrane (1965–1967)

Sanders joined John Coltrane’s group in 1965, a period when Coltrane was moving decisively into free improvisation. Sanders appeared on the landmark Ascension session (Impulse!, recorded June 1965, released 1966) — a sprawling collective improvisation for an eleven-piece ensemble that is among the most radical recordings in jazz history. He played tenor saxophone alongside Coltrane and a large group that included Archie Shepp, Marion Brown, John Tchicai, and Freddie Hubbard, with Elvin Jones and McCoy Tyner anchoring the rhythm section.

He continued with Coltrane’s working group through 1966–1967, appearing on:

  • Live At The Village Vanguard Again! (Impulse!, 1966) — tenor sax and flute
  • Meditations (Impulse!, 1966) — his most intensive on-record collaboration with Coltrane alongside Rashied Ali’s second drum kit
  • Coltrane In Japan (recorded 1966, released 1976) — tenor and alto sax
  • Expression (Impulse!, recorded 1967) — piccolo flute

Coltrane died in July 1967. Sanders’s association with him was one of the formative relationships of his career, and the spiritual orientation he absorbed from that period defined his output as a leader.

Impulse! Years as Leader (1966–1975)

Tauhid (1967)

Debut as a headlining artist on Impulse! (ID 376668). Produced by Bob Thiele. Recorded with:

  • Sonny Sharrock — guitar
  • Dave Burrell — piano
  • Henry Grimes — bass
  • Roger Blank — drums
  • Nat Bettis — percussion
  • Rudy Van Gelder — engineer

A strong statement of his early solo direction: raucous and experimental but also attentive to structure. He played tenor sax, alto sax, and piccolo flute, and contributed vocals (credited as “vocalese/vocalise”).

Karma (1969)

The pivotal record. (Impulse! ID 873261; ABC Records co-credit.) Produced by Bob Thiele; engineered by Bob Simpson. The centerpiece is “The Creator Has A Master Plan” — a 32-minute piece with vocalist Leon Thomas, whose yodelling technique became inseparable from the Sanders sound of this era. Among the most celebrated pieces in spiritual jazz. Leon Thomas also co-wrote the composition. (72 pressings in Discogs across all variants, the highest of any Sanders release.)

Jewels Of Thought (1970)

Impulse! ID 1019118. Produced by Ed Michel. Lineup: Lonnie Liston Smith (piano, African flute, thumb piano, percussion), Cecil McBee (bass), Idris Muhammad (drums), Leon Thomas (vocals, percussion). Sanders played tenor sax, contrabass clarinet, and an array of percussion instruments. A session that consolidated his core working group of this period.

Ptah, The El Daoud (1970)

Impulse! ID 385862. Co-billed with Alice Coltrane as a side-long co-led session; Alice Coltrane on harp and piano.

Summun Bukmun Umyun (Deaf Dumb Blind) (1970)

Impulse! ID 513736. Produced by Ed Michel. Notable for the expanded ensemble: Lonnie Liston Smith (piano), Cecil McBee (bass), Gary Bartz (alto sax), Woody Shaw (trumpet), Clifford Jarvis (drums). Sanders played soprano sax and an arsenal of percussion and found instruments.

Thembi (1971)

Impulse! ID 932779. Produced by Ed Michel and Bill Szymczyk. Key personnel: Lonnie Liston Smith (piano, Fender Rhodes, Bailophone), Cecil McBee (bass), Roy Haynes (drums, B-side), Clifford Jarvis (drums/maracas, A-side), Michael White (violin), and a large percussion ensemble. Among the most-pressed of his Impulse! albums (50 variants). A balance of introspection and kinetic energy; African percussion runs throughout.

Journey In Satchidananda (1971)

Impulse! ID 549847. Primarily an Alice Coltrane album but co-billed (“Alice Coltrane featuring Pharoah Sanders”). Produced by Alice Coltrane and Ed Michel. Personnel: Alice Coltrane (harp, piano), Cecil McBee (bass), Rashied Ali (drums), Tulsi (tambura). Sanders on soprano sax and percussion.

Black Unity (1972)

Impulse! ID 933644. A single side-long piece; the band expanded to include Stanley Clarke and Cecil McBee on two basses, Norman Connors and Billy Hart on two drum kits, Carlos Garnett (tenor sax), Hannibal Marvin Peterson (trumpet), Joe Bonner (piano), and large percussion. Sanders composed and produced. One of the most politically charged records of his Impulse! run — the liner notes were written by his wife Thembi Sanders.

Live At The East (1972) and Village Of The Pharoahs (1973)

Both on ABC/Impulse!; document his live performances of the period. Izipho Zam (My Gifts) (1973, Strata-East, ID 618576) appeared on the independent Strata-East label, outside the Impulse! arrangement.

Elevation (1974) and Love In Us All (1974)

His final records for the ABC/Impulse! configuration before the label was restructured. By this point his ensemble approach was well established; these recordings represent a consolidation of the 1969–1973 peak.

Theresa Records Period (1977–1987)

After a brief and commercially minded detour on Arista (1978’s Love Will Find A Way, Got To Give It Up, As You Are — a period generally regarded as an anomaly in his discography), Sanders returned to a smaller-label, artist-controlled model.

Pharoah (1977, India Navigation, ID 1135773) — issued on the independent New York label India Navigation, with Tisziji Muñoz (guitar), Steve Neil (bass), Greg Bandy (drums). A leaner, more intimate record than the Impulse! output.

Journey To The One is considered the strongest of the Theresa run — a return to sustained melodic improvisation after the Arista interlude. Self-produced by Sanders; he had full creative control.

Rejoice (1981) is notable for the personnel: Billy Higgins (drums on two sessions), Joe Bonner (piano), John Hicks (piano), Art Davis (bass), Steve Turre (trombone), Danny Moore (trumpet).

Late 1980s and International Collaborations

Africa (1987, Timeless Records, ID 1067107) and This Is For You, John (1987, Timeless) — the latter a tribute to John Coltrane, also on Timeless.

Sun Ra And His Arkestra Featuring Pharoah Sanders (1976, El Saturn Records, ID 7987607) documents a mid-career encounter with Sun Ra’s Arkestra.

A Tribute To John Coltrane / Blues For Coltrane (1988, Impulse! reissue, ID 2530922) returned him to the Impulse! banner via a compilation context.

The Trance Of Seven Colors (1994, Axiom, ID 395993) — a collaboration with Maleem Mahmoud Ghania, the Gnawa musician from Morocco, produced by Bill Laswell on the Axiom label. An encounter between Sanders’s tenor and the Gnawa tradition (guimbri, karkabas, call-and-response vocal chorus). One of the most distinctive records of his later period. Released on Bill Laswell’s Axiom imprint.

Evidence Records and Venus Records (1990s)

Ed Kelly & Pharoah Sanders (1993, Evidence, ID 8068921) — duet with the Oakland pianist Ed Kelly.

Solomon’s Daughter (1994, Evidence, ID 3473824).

Message From Home (1995, Verve, ID 13193029) — a more mainstream Verve production.

The Japanese Venus Records label documented several quartet sessions: Crescent With Love and Ballads With Love (both 1999). The Pharoah Sanders Quartet (Discogs ID 2227202) was his primary working unit for this period.

Promises (2021)

The late-career release that achieved the widest circulation of his career. Discogs ID 18037798. Primary credits: Floating Points (Sam Shepherd), Pharoah Sanders, and the London Symphony Orchestra. Released on Luaka Bop.

A nine-movement piece built around a repeated chord motif developed by Floating Points, with Sanders’s tenor weaving through an orchestral framework. Recorded over approximately two years (partly during the COVID-19 pandemic). No additional performer credits appear in the Discogs entry — the collaboration structure placed the orchestral writing with Floating Points, with Sanders improvising over it.

The record reached #4 on the UK Albums Chart, a figure unprecedented for Sanders and unusual for free/spiritual jazz generally. It won the Mercury Prize shortlist and introduced his work to listeners primarily familiar with electronic and post-club music. He died in September 2022, the year after its release.
