Felix Fiedorowicz

Multi-instrumentalist (keyboards, bassoon, violin, vocals, percussion, autoharp, recorder, tape) and core member of Family Fodder and Officer!. Discogs artist ID: 360282.

Name variations on record: F. Fiedorowicz, FF, Felix, Felix Fiederowicz, Fiedorowicz.

Biography

Background and Early Life

Fiedorowicz has described himself as having lived “at the very edges of the alternative music scene since the mid 1970s.” [source: https://felixf.bandcamp.com/album/120-days] As a schoolboy in the late 1960s and early 1970s he was engaged with contemporary classical music — collecting records on budget labels (Heliodor Wergo, DGG, Philips), purchasing scores from London’s music publishing houses, and attending concerts. Composers he engaged with in that period included Stockhausen, Cage, Berio, and Ligeti. He created electronic, concrete, and electro-acoustic compositions using his school’s physics laboratory facilities, and composed pieces for his school’s wind quartet. He also experimented with 8mm film and video. [source: https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html, accessed 2026-06-09]

He attended meetings of the Scratch Orchestra — Cornelius Cardew’s experimental ensemble — though found the factionalism unsustainable. He later visited Cardew at his North London home, where Cardew played and encouraged his piano compositions. During the same period he was involved in Marxist-Leninist organizations. [source: https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html, accessed 2026-06-09]

His home base during the Family Fodder and early Officer! years was Southall, West London — the location of his home studio where much of All Styles (1983) was recorded. [source: https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html, accessed 2026-06-09]

He stopped composing around 1993 and did not resume until 2016, following his relocation to the island of Ithaka, Greece, specifically the near-deserted village of Exoghi (which he describes as translating roughly as “the alien land”). He continues to be based there. [source: https://felixfiedorowicz.bandcamp.com/album/felix-fiedorowicz-and-the-sacred-band-of-thebes, accessed 2026-06-09; https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html]

Nationality is not stated in any available source; the surname is Polish in origin and the early-life context is London-based.

1980–1983: Family Fodder’s Classic Albums

Fiedorowicz appears on all three of Family Fodder’s studio albums on Fresh Records (14) and affiliated labels. He was part of the early Family Fodder lineup alongside Alig, Mick Hobbs, Martin Harrison, and sleeve designer Ian Hill. According to Alig Fodder, “Felix played me John Cage and Harry Partch” — Fiedorowicz was a conduit for introducing experimental classical composers to the group’s aesthetic. [source: https://www.factmag.com/2016/08/28/family-fodder-guide-post-punk/, accessed 2026-06-09]

His involvement with Family Fodder was ultimately described in a 2022 interview as “fleeting” — he was eventually dropped from the collective. [source: https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html, accessed 2026-06-09]

On Monkey Banana Kitchen (1980, Fresh Records) he plays keyboards, bassoon, violin, vocals, and percussion — one of the most broadly instrumental contributions in the ensemble, which on that record also included Alig, Martin Harrison, Mick Hobbs, Charles Bullen, Graham Painting, Rick Wilson, Mark Doffman, Ian Hill, and vocalists Dominique Levillain and Judy Carter. He also co-designed the album cover from original artwork alongside Ian Hill and Judy Carter.

On Greatest Hits (1981, Crammed Discs) his credit narrows to keyboards, bassoon, and violin in a slightly reconfigured ensemble that now included Bazz Smith, Mark Doffman, Rick Wilson, Charles Bullen, Alig, Mick Hobbs, Martin Harrison (as “Martin Frederix”), Ian Hill, Dominique Levillain, and Jan Beetlestone.

All Styles (1983, Jungle Records) is the record on which Fiedorowicz’s compositional role is most prominent. He is listed as one of the principal songwriters and his instrumental palette expands to keyboards, vocals, autoharp, percussion, recorders, bassoon, and tape. The ensemble on All Styles contracts to a smaller core: Bazz Smith, Alig, Lynn Alice, and Fiedorowicz. The album was largely recorded at Fiedorowicz’s home studio in Southall. [source: https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html, accessed 2026-06-09] The album also features his arrangement of Franz Schubert’s “Der Leiermann” for the group. The track appears in a lyrics database attributed to Family Fodder with songwriters listed as Schubert, W. Müller, and others (no separate arrangement credit visible in that listing); whether Fiedorowicz’s arrangement is a track-level or sleeve-note credit cannot be confirmed from available sources — only the Discogs credits tables surface it. [source: https://www.cancioneros.com/lyrics/song/2272666/der-leiermann-the-organ-grinder-family-fodder]

1983–1984: Officer! and the Paris-London Axis

Fiedorowicz is a founding member of Officer!, the post-Family Fodder group centered on Mick Hobbs and operating across Paris and London. The Café OTO artist page states Officer! “formally surfaced in ‘82 with Eight New Songs By Mick Hobbs,” while the Discogs entry dates the cassette to 1983. The Psychedelic Baby Magazine interview (2022) also describes the 1983 tape as Officer!‘s first release; the Officer! Bandcamp page gives 1984 as the group’s “inception.” The most consistent reading across sources is that the project began taking shape in 1982–1983 with 8 New Songs, and the core lineup of Hobbs, Fiedorowicz, and Bill Gilonis was established from the start. [sources: https://www.cafeoto.co.uk/artists/officer/, accessed 2026-06-09; https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html, accessed 2026-06-09; https://officer-official.bandcamp.com/album/paragraphs-and-principles, accessed 2026-06-09]

In the 2022 interview, Fiedorowicz emphasized that the first Officer! tape was “fully developed” and not a demo — “the most pure release” of the catalog. [source: https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2022/06/officer-the-work-interview.html]

Officer!‘s debut release 8 New Songs by Mick Hobbs (1983, AAA) was produced by Dominique Diebold, and Fiedorowicz served as lead engineer (with Bill Gilonis and Andy Bole as assistants). The label AAA was a small French independent associated with the Recommended Records network.

Officer!‘s first LP proper, Ossification (1984, Ayaa), is the record on which Fiedorowicz’s songwriting contribution to the Officer! catalog is most visible: he is credited as co-writer on five tracks (A4, B1, B2, B3), sharing or splitting credits with Mick Hobbs and Bill Gilonis. Production assistance on Ossification was handled collectively by Gilonis, Charles Bullen, Tim Hodgkinson, and others associated with This Heat and the Recommended Records orbit.

Also in 1984, Fiedorowicz plays bassoon on Lonely at the Top by Hermine Demoriane (Salomé Discs), a cosmopolitan cabaret-leaning record produced by People In Control (on one track) with recording by John Fryer, alongside Tim Hodgkinson on baritone saxophone and Stephen Kent on French horn.

He also contributes bassoon that year to The Lo Yo Yo (self-released, 1984) by The Lo Yo Yo, an informal ensemble featuring Mick Hobbs, Alig, and others from the same circle.

1988: Officer! Live Document

Yes Yes No No Yes No Yes (1988, self-released) documents a later Officer! configuration. The sleeve credits a collective of performers rather than assigning individual roles: alongside Fiedorowicz the participants include Mick Hobbs, Bill Gilonis, Antoine Gindt, Claudia Schmid, Daniel Koskowitz, and Keisuke Matsui.

2008–2015: Family Fodder Returns

Fiedorowicz appears on More Great Hits! (2008, Jungle Records), a Family Fodder reunion/compilation credited to nearly the entire classic-era roster. He then appears on Sunday Girls (Director’s Cut) (2015, Staubgold), released under the credit “Family Fodder and Friends,” playing bassoon and recorder alongside the core Family Fodder membership.

2010: Up Murphy Street

Fiedorowicz plays bassoon on Up Murphy Street by Up Murphy Street, originally released as a vinyl LP in 1986 (UK, Not On Label) and reissued on CDr by El Frenzy Productions (Spain) around 2010. The record reconnects him with Mick Hobbs and Bill Gilonis in a loose ensemble also featuring Richard Grace on trombone, vocalists including Marie-Jeanne F., and a cast drawn from the Murphy Working Stiffs and It’s War Boys networks.

2019–2025: Arranger and Producer

Fiedorowicz arranges one track (track 5) on Never Not (2019) by The Nature Centre (self-released UK), a band unrelated to the Family Fodder / Officer! world.

Officer!‘s Paragraphs and Principles (2021, Klanggalerie) finds Fiedorowicz in a full producer/engineer/arranger role for the first time across the majority of the album (tracks 1–10, 12–14), with Mick Hobbs handling one track independently. He shares production and engineering credits with Hobbs and Bill Gilonis.

Dead Right (2025, Klanggalerie) is the most recent Officer! release. Fiedorowicz is credited for composition, arrangement, keyboards, mixing, programming, and mastering — effectively the primary studio architect of the record alongside Mick Hobbs.

Solo Work

After stopping composition around 1993 and resuming in 2016 following his move to Ithaka:

  • 120 Days (2017, Bandcamp) — solo release exploring themes around Pasolini’s Salò. [source: https://felixf.bandcamp.com/album/120-days]
  • Felix Fiedorowicz and The Sacred Band of Thebes (July 2024, Bandcamp) — 15 tracks referencing ancient Roman and Greek theatrical works and philosophical concepts. [source: https://felixfiedorowicz.bandcamp.com/album/felix-fiedorowicz-and-the-sacred-band-of-thebes]
  • Active on SoundCloud under “Ἱερὸς Λόχος” (Sacred Band). [source: https://soundcloud.com/user-65845628]

Open Questions

  • No realname or birthdate confirmed in any source.
  • Nationality not explicitly stated anywhere. Surname is Polish in origin; all early-life context is London. Bandcamp profile lists location as “London, UK” despite current residence in Greece.
  • The Schubert “Der Leiermann” arrangement credit on All Styles: a lyrics database lists the track as a Family Fodder track with Schubert/Müller/others as songwriters, with no separate Fiedorowicz arrangement credit visible there. Whether the credit is track-level or only in sleeve notes is not resolved.
  • Relationship to The Nature Centre (2019) unclear — likely a one-off arrangement session rather than membership.
  • Officer! formation year: sources conflict (1982 per Café OTO; 1983 per release date on Discogs and Psychedelic Baby interview; 1984 per Officer! Bandcamp). The 1983 tape is the first documented release; 1982 may refer to when the project began taking shape.
  • Whether Fiedorowicz ever had a non-music professional life is unknown from any source found.
