Norman Spinrad

Aug 10, 20266 min read

Norman Spinrad

Discogs ID: 573487 Born: 1940, USA Based: Paris, France (as of Discogs profile) Role in music: Lyricist, Vocalist Years active in music (Discogs): 1994–2005 Name variations on releases: N. Spinrad, Spinrad

American science fiction author best known for Bug Jack Barron (1969) and The Iron Dream (1972). Acquired a secondary role as occasional vocalist and lyricist within the French electronic rock orbit of Richard Pinhas, contributing to releases by Richard Pinhas and Heldon across a decade.

Biography

Author First

Spinrad is primarily a novelist and essayist, not a musician. He belongs to the generation of American New Wave SF authors who brought literary and countercultural concerns into speculative fiction. Bug Jack Barron appeared in 1969 (serialized in New Worlds) and drew controversy for its direct political satire; The Iron Dream (1972) is a metafictional tour de force presenting a pulp fantasy novel supposedly authored by Adolf Hitler in an alternate history where he emigrated to America and became a genre writer. Both remain landmarks.

What Discogs records is narrower but specific: he moved to Paris at some point and entered the orbit of Richard Pinhas, the philosopher-guitarist who had been citing Spinrad’s fiction since the early days of Heldon — the band’s name is drawn from the totalitarian entity in Bug Jack Barron, and Pinhas has consistently acknowledged the novel as a foundational reference.

Entry into the Pinhas Circle (1994)

Spinrad’s first credited musical appearance is on the Cyborg Sally album (1994, Nexus (3), cat. KICP 423), a Japan-pressed release credited to Richard Pinhas & John Livengood. He contributes Voice on track 2. The album features a wide cast including Didier Batard (bass), Antoine Paganotti (drums), Patrick Gauthier (synthesizer), and Duncan Nilsson (guitar). This is the earliest confirmed musical credit for Spinrad on Discogs.

Two years later he returned for The Cyborg Sally Song / Stand By (Live) (1996, AMP Records, cat. AMP-CD S002), again a Pinhas & John Livengood release. Here he is credited both as Backing Vocals on the studio track and as Composed By on tracks 1 and 2 — indicating deeper creative involvement than a simple guest performance. This single/EP also features David Korn (composed track 1), Frédéric Pelloie / Freddy Pelloie (track 2), Didier Batard (bass), and Antoine Paganotti (drums).

Only Chaos Is Real — Heldon (2001)

The central and most substantial credit. Only Chaos Is Real (2001, 25pm, cat. 25pm001) is credited to Heldon — a formal reunion of the band name after nearly two decades. Spinrad contributes Lyrics By and Vocals at the album level (not track-scoped). He shares the lyric credit with Maurice G. Dantec, the French-Canadian cyberpunk novelist and sometime Pinhas collaborator.

East West — Richard Pinhas (2005)

Spinrad’s final Discogs credit is on East West (2005, Cuneiform Records, cat. Rune 31 CDX), a Richard Pinhas solo record reissuing early material. He is credited as Voice on tracks 1 and 9. This is a reissue/archival release coordinated by Steven Feigenbaum; the track personnel for tracks 1 and 9 also includes François Auger (drums/percussion), Patrick Gauthier (Polymoog), and Georges Grünblatt (Polymoog on tracks 4 and 5). The reissue context suggests Spinrad’s voice was recorded earlier and the credit here is archival rather than new studio work.

Scene Connections

  • Heldon — band whose name is taken from Spinrad’s novel Bug Jack Barron; Spinrad provided lyrics and vocals on their 2001 reunion album
  • Richard Pinhas — guitarist/composer at the centre of all four Spinrad credits; the connection spans 1994–2005
  • Maurice G. Dantec — French-Canadian cyberpunk novelist; shares the lyric credit on Only Chaos Is Real; both operate as literary figures pulled into Pinhas’s musical world
  • 25pm — Pinhas’s imprint; released Only Chaos Is Real
  • Cuneiform Records — US label (Steven Feigenbaum) that handled Heldon/Pinhas reissues in the 2000s; issued East West
  • Benoît Widemann — synthesist on Only Chaos Is Real; longstanding Heldon collaborator
  • Olivier Manchion — bassist on Only Chaos Is Real
