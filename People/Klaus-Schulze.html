Klaus Schulze

Klaus Schulze

Oct 30, 20269 min read

Klaus Schulze

Born: 4 August 1947, Berlin, Germany
Died: 26 April 2022 (aged 74, following a long illness)
Discogs artist ID: 10360
Real name: Klaus Schulze (also credited Klaus-Jürgen Schulze)
Aliases: Richard Wahnfried · Heinz Schultze Berlin · Schulzendorfer Groove Orchester
Role: Composer, synthesist, producer, drummer (early work)

One of the central figures of Berlin-school electronic music. Began as a drummer, passed through both Tangerine Dream and Ash Ra Tempel, then launched one of the most prolific solo careers in electronic music — more than 50 years of releases spanning kosmische, ambient, symphonic synth, dark-trance, and collaboration with classical singers.

Biography

Early Career: Drums and the Berlin Scene (1969–1971)

Schulze began playing drums with the short-lived Berlin group Psy Free before joining the nascent Tangerine Dream in 1969–70. He plays drums and “noises” on their debut album Electronic Meditation (Ohr, 1970), recorded alongside Edgar Froese and Conrad Schnitzler. Both Schulze and Schnitzler departed after that single album; Froese carried Tangerine Dream forward.

Schulze then joined Ash Ra Tempel, the group founded by guitarist Manuel Göttsching and bassist Hartmut Enke. He appears on the first two Ash Ra Tempel albums — Ash Ra Tempel (Ohr, 1971) and Schwingungen (Ohr, 1972) — and on the Klaus Schulze-sessions-focused archival release Join Inn (Ohr, 1973), before departing to pursue his solo path. The Ash Ra Tempel period also overlapped with the loose collective The Cosmic Jokers, featuring Göttsching, Harald Grosskopf, Dieter Dierks, and others.

Solo Beginnings: Pure Electronics (1972–1974)

In 1971 Schulze launched his solo career, leaning entirely into electronics and moving away from drums. Irrlicht (Ohr, 1972) is a wholly self-produced work: Schulze plays organ, guitar, zither, voice, electronics, and percussion, and also engineers and photographs the sleeve. The music was built on orchestral overdubs and tape manipulation rather than synthesizers — the VCS 3 did not arrive until Cyborg (Kosmische Musik, 1973), which he later described as his first “real” synthesizer record.

Blackdance (Brain/Metronome, 1974) broadened the palette further: synthesizers, organ, piano, 12-string guitar, and orchestra, with bass vocalist Ernst Walter Siemon as the only outside contributor of note.

Peak of the 1970s: The Berlin School Sound (1975–1979)

This is the period of Schulze’s most celebrated solo work, built around long-form sequencer patterns and analogue synthesis. Key synthesizers documented on Moondawn (1976) include the Big Moog, ARP 2600, ARP Odyssey, EMS Synthi, and Farfisa Synthorchester.

Timewind (Virgin/Caroline, 1975) was a turning point: two side-long pieces dedicated to Richard Wagner, credited compositionally under the “Wahnfried” pseudonym. The album won the Grand Prix du Disque in France.

Moondawn (Isadora, 1976) introduced drummer Harald Grosskopf as a recurring collaborator; Grosskopf appears on drums through the late 1970s period, including on the Body Love film soundtracks (1977) and Mirage (1977).

X (Ariola, 1978) is a six-part suite dedicated to six composers; Schulze produced, composed, arranged, performed, recorded, and mixed it entirely himself.

Dune (Brain, 1979) adds cellist Wolfgang Tiepold and vocalist Arthur Brown, who sings on the B-side track.

During this same period Schulze was a member of the collective Stomu Yamash’ta’s Go, recording with Japanese percussionist Stomu Yamash’ta, Steve Winwood, Al Di Meola, and others on the live album Go Live From Paris (Island, 1976). He also recorded Lord Krishna Von Goloka and Join Inn under the Ash Ra Tempel umbrella, and appeared on several Cosmic Jokers releases produced by Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser and Dieter Dierks at Dierks’ studio.

Innovative Communication and the 1980s (1981–1989)

In 1978 Schulze founded the label Innovative Communication (IC; Discogs label ID: 4293), later under the parent company IC/DigIt Music GmbH. It became a home for German electronic and new-age music with twelve sublabels including J.U.IC.E., Erotic Music, Galaxy, and Sounds of Planet Earth. He sold IC in summer 1983.

His first albums on IC were Trancefer (1981) — with cellist Wolfgang Tiepold and percussionist Michael Shrieve — and Audentity (1983), which added Rainer Bloss on glockenspiel and Michael Shrieve on Simmons drums and computer. The label years also saw the live document Dziękuję Poland (Live ‘83) (IC, 1983).

After selling IC, Schulze moved briefly to Inteam GmbH for Angst (1984, also a film soundtrack), Aphrica, and Drive Inn (1984). He “went digital” in 1986, rebuilding his studio and releasing Dreams (Brain, 1986) and Babel (Venture, 1987), a UK label that also issued several of his early-1990s records.

Also in 1979 Schulze launched the Richard Wahnfried alias/project (Discogs artist ID: 18791), initially for more atmospheric or concept-driven music. Wahnfried releases — including Tonwelle, Miditation, Megatone, and Time Actor — run through to 2022.

Classical and Vocal Collaborations (1990–2003)

Through the 1990s Schulze became increasingly interested in classical interpretation. He produced several large-scale recordings engaging with Wagner — most notably Das Wagner Desaster – Live (ZYX, 1994) and Rheingold (Live At The Loreley) (Synthetic Symphony, 2008) — and worked with opera singers and classical orchestras. A feature of these projects is the Colloquium Musica Orchestra / Symphonieorchester aus Berlin, which had already appeared on Irrlicht and Cyborg.

He also recorded Le Moulin De Daudet (Virgin, 1994), his lone Virgin solo release post-Timewind.

Concurrently Schulze began his most prominent 1990s collaboration: The Dark Side Of The Moog series with Frankfurt producer Pete Namlook on Namlook’s Fax +49-69-450464 label. Starting in 1995 and running to at least eleven volumes (some repackaged later), the series is pure ambient/drone electronics. On Discogs, the project is listed as a separate artist entity (ID: 29599). The collaboration ran across most of the decade; both are co-credited as producers (uncredited on early volumes).

Bill Laswell appears as a contributor on at least three Dark Side of the Moog volumes (vols 4, 7, and the Evolution compilation), providing bass.

Later Period and Synthetic Symphony / SPV (2005–2022)

An intensive reissue program began in 2005 via Revisited Rec. and then MIG, eventually producing the enormous La Vie Electronique archive series (at least sixteen volumes) documenting previously unreleased recordings from the full career span. New studio releases continued alongside these on Synthetic Symphony and then SPV.

The most notable late collaboration is with vocalist Lisa Gerrard (of Dead Can Dance). They are co-primary artists on Farscape (Synthetic Symphony, 2008) and Rheingold (Live At The Loreley) (Synthetic Symphony, 2008), with Gerrard providing voice. She also appears on the live documents Dziękuję Bardzo – Vielen Dank (2009) and Dziękuję Bardzo – Warsaw 25 Years Later (2009).

Other later collaborations include electronic duo partner Christopher von Deylen (aka Schiller) on Zenit (2008), guitarist Günter Schickert on The Schulze-Schickert Session (2013), and vocalist Chrysta Bell on Shadowlands (SPV/Oblivion, 2018).

Deus Arrakis (SPV, 2022) was released shortly after Schulze’s death in April 2022, making it his final solo record. A posthumous live album, 101, Milky Way (SPV, 2024) followed, and Bon Voyage (SPV, 2025) appeared as a further archival release. Schulze’s wife Elfi Schulze is credited on the later archival works (In Blue, 101, Milky Way).

Also notable is Schulze’s membership in Din A Testbild and Software (Discogs artist IDs: 165931 and 89241), minor projects listed among his group memberships.
