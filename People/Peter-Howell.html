Peter Howell

Discogs ID: 15808 Born: 1949 (Wikipedia) Alias / name variation: John Ferdinando (Discogs alias — Howell used this name in early psychedelic folk work) Name variations on record: Howell · P. Howell Active: late 1960s–present BBC Radiophonic Workshop: 1974–1997 (original run); 2009–present (live reunion)

British musician and composer. Howell’s career falls into three broad phases: early psychedelic folk with John Ferdinando (late 1960s–early 1970s), membership of the BBC Radiophonic Workshop (1974–1997) during which he produced some of British television’s most recognisable electronic scores, and a continuing presence in reunion performances and soundtrack reissues. He is best known for his 1980 re-arrangement of the Doctor Who theme — realised on a Yamaha CS-80, an ARP Odyssey Mk3, and a Roland Jupiter-4 — which accompanied the programme for five years (1980–1985) and for roughly ten full incidental scores within that period. He published a memoir, Radiophonic Times (Obverse Books, 2021). (Sources: Discogs profile; Wikipedia, “Peter Howell (musician)“)

Psychedelic Folk Era (late 1960s–1973)

Howell’s career began in the late 1960s working with John Ferdinando in various psychedelic and folk groups. Together they recorded as Peter Howell & John Ferdinando, as Agincourt (with Lee Menelaus), as Ithaca (a larger group also including Brian Hussey, Lee Menelaus, and Martin Garrett), and as the duo Friends (10). The pair set up their own label, H&F Recordings, to issue their private-press and acetate output.

BBC Radiophonic Workshop (1974–1997)

Howell joined the Workshop in 1974, part of the new generation of composers (alongside Malcolm Clarke, Paddy Kingsland, and Roger Limb) who shifted the unit from tape-and-oscillator techniques toward synthesisers. According to Wikipedia, the Workshop was contributing to over 300 programmes per year by the late 1970s.

Doctor Who Theme (1980)

When producer John Nathan-Turner decided to update Doctor Who’s title music in 1980, he commissioned Howell to provide a new arrangement of Ron Grainer’s original theme (first realised by Delia Derbyshire in 1963). Howell’s version used analogue synthesisers — principally a Yamaha CS-80, an ARP Odyssey Mk3, and a Roland Jupiter-4 — in contrast to Derbyshire’s musique concrète approach. It first appeared in The Leisure Hive (1980) and was used throughout Tom Baker’s final season, all of Peter Davison’s tenure, and Colin Baker’s first season. It was replaced in 1986 by a new arrangement by Dominic Glynn. (Source: Wikipedia, “Peter Howell (musician)”)

The 1980 Doctor Who single (Discogs ID: 256990) is credited to Peter Howell And The BBC Radiophonic Workshop. Additional credit: Mike Brown (3), lacquer cut.

Research Queue

  • The 1975 acetate LP Peter Howell, John Ferdinando & Marcia Jackson is listed without a master ID on Discogs (private-press, no commercial release). Who is Marcia Jackson — was she a regular collaborator or a one-off vocalist? A single contemporary source (liner notes, interview, or press piece) should resolve this.
  • Wikipedia states Howell’s first Doctor Who work was “some accompanying incidental music for Revenge of the Cybermen” and “special sound for Planet of Evil” (both 1975). Discogs credits him as arranger on the 2023 Revenge of the Cybermen Silva Screen release but does not list a 1975 credit for Planet of Evil — confirm whether his Planet of Evil contribution was credited anywhere in the original broadcast or any contemporary release.
  • Howell’s Doctor Who Radio work — The Paradise of Death (1993) and The Ghosts of N-Space (1996) — is noted in Wikipedia but has no release-level Discogs entry under his ID. Does either have a commercial audio release, and if so, are they listed under the BBC or Big Finish catalog?
  • Wikipedia attributes the K-9 and Company theme to Ian Levine and Fiachra Trench, arranged by Howell — confirm whether Howell received a composer credit or arrangement-only credit on any commercial release of the theme.
  • Howell’s contribution to The Isness & The Otherness (2004, Future Sound of London Recordings) is listed as “Written-By” — what is his specific creative connection to Future Sound of London, and is it a sample clearance or an original composition?
