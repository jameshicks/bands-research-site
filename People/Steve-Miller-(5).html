‘Handsome’ Steve Miller (not to be confused with that OTHER Steve Miller) is an Australian musician, vocalist, raconteur, bar owner and personality. He grew up in the small South Australian town of Mount Gambier (known primarily as being a comfort stop between Melbourne and Adelaide or Adelaide and Melbourne). Miller moved to Adelaide along with friend and bandmate Dave Graney, where they released a single as Sputniks before moving again to Melbourne with The Moodists, eventually trying their luck in the UK. Upon returning to Australia, Miller dipped into the world of hospitality, with a string of idiosyncratic bars, tour managing, and co-ran the record label W.Minc Productions Records. He has also guested on a number of records by his contemporary artists. More recently he has returned to the live music world with his combo, logically but a bit cheekily named The Steve Miller Band (no, not Steve Miller Band, as confusing as that may indeed be), also known by the acronym The SMB.

Overview

Steve Miller — known in Australian music circles as ‘Handsome’ Steve Miller to distinguish him from his famous American namesake — is a guitarist, vocalist, and raconteur from Mount Gambier, South Australia. He came up through the post-punk scene alongside Dave Graney, first as Sputniks in Adelaide and then as a member of The Moodists, who relocated to Melbourne and later to the UK in the early 1980s, releasing on Red Flame, Virgin, Creation, and Island Records. His catalogue is concentrated almost entirely in the 1980s, with Clare Moore, Graney, and Chris Walsh his most consistent collaborators, while credits shared with The Triffids and Mick Turner reflect the interconnected Melbourne indie scene. After returning to Australia he moved into hospitality and tour management, co-running W.Minc Productions Records, before returning to live performance years later with his own group, The Steve Miller Band (The SMB).

Notes (web) — The Moodists’ end and immediate aftermath

Clarification on the “Island Records period”

Steve Miller’s two Island Records credits in Discogs (1987, 1989) are not Moodists releases. They are Triffids albums: Calenture (1987, credited as “Costume Designer [Lamé]”) and The Triffids Present The Black Swan (1989, credited as “Performer [Tour Escort]”). The Moodists themselves were never on Island — their labels were Au Go Go, Red Flame, Virgin, Creation, and T.I.M. The brief’s framing of an “Island Records period” conflates Miller’s parallel work with the Triffids with his Moodists membership.

Why The Moodists wound down

The band did not formally disband in a single decisive moment. By mid-1986 the group had run out of momentum in the UK. Dave Graney’s explanation, quoted in a Christopher Hollow article hosted on davegraney.com: “Steve was tour managing the Triffids a lot, going away with them. Their success and easy engagement with the UK business seemed to be a trick we could never manage.” Clare Moore recalled there was no band meeting to decide it — it was a gradual, undramatic dissolution. Graney’s phrase was simply: “We ran out of gas.” (davegraney.com/moodists/Moodistspress1.htm)

Wikipedia dates the final disbandment to 1987 and notes their last performance was in London. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Moodists, checked 2026-11-10)

The final lineup was: Dave Graney (vocals), Clare Moore (drums), Steve Miller (guitar), David McClymont (bass, ex-Orange Juice).

Immediate aftermath — Dave Graney and Clare Moore

Graney and Moore, along with Malcolm Ross (also ex-Orange Juice), formed Dave Graney with the Coral Snakes in late 1987 and played London pubs and clubs. They recorded an EP for Fire Records produced by Barry Adamson. By late 1988 Graney and Moore returned to Australia, reportedly ordered out of the UK by immigration authorities by 1989. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Graney; davegraney.com/moodists/Moodistspress1.htm)

Immediate aftermath — Steve Miller

Miller did not follow Graney into the Coral Snakes. His post-Moodists activity in the late 1980s was primarily as a tour manager: he tour-managed both The Triffids and The Go-Betweens through the late 1980s. During his time with The Moodists he had also played in a rockabilly/country project called “Tall Dark and Handsome” and an acoustic duo called “the Starliners.” After returning to Australia he moved into hospitality (bar ownership) and continued tour managing, and co-ran W.Minc Productions with Graham Lee of The Triffids. (davegraney.com search result snippet; australianmusictrade.50webs.com snippet via web search, 2026-11-10 — this last site is a fan resource and should be treated as lower-confidence)
