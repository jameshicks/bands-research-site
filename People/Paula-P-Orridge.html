Paula P-Orridge

Born: Paula Jean Brooking, 23 February 1963
Discogs ID: 479849
Aliases: Alaura; Mistress Mix; Miss P. Ornot; Ornot; P P-Orridge; P Porridge; P-Orridge; P. P-Orridge; P.P-Orridge; Paula P. Orridge; Paula P.Orridge; Porridge
Groups: Psychic TV, 23 Skidoo (member of record), The Anti-Heroin Project

English musician and performer. Married to Genesis P-Orridge (later divorced); mother of Caresse P-Orridge and Genesse P-Orridge. Core member of Psychic TV throughout their foundational Temple Records era (1982–1991), appearing on the band’s first studio album, all major live documents, and the transition into acid house on Towards Thee Infinite Beat (Wax Trax!, 1990). Also listed as a formal Discogs member of 23 Skidoo, though her connection to that band is through the Genesis P-Orridge production nexus rather than performance credits on their own releases.

Biography

Early Involvement — Some Bizzare and TOPY (1982–1983)

Paula’s first Discogs credit appears on First Transmission (TOPY, 1982) — a cassette release on Thee Temple Ov Psychick Youth imprint — credited for “Instruments” alongside Genesis P-Orridge, Peter Christopherson, David Tibet, and Stan Bingo. This was effectively Psychic TV’s first document as they coalesced out of Throbbing Gristle’s dissolution.

Her second credit is Force The Hand Of Chance (Some Bizzare, 1982) — Psychic TV’s debut album on Stevo’s Some Bizzare label — credited “Instruments [Played By].” The album appeared on the label alongside early work from Soft Cell, Marc Almond, Coil, and others in the Some Bizzare orbit.

Dreams Less Sweet (Some Bizzare, 1983) is the most instrumentally specific early credit: Drums, Vibraphone, Percussion. The album, produced by Psychic TV and Ken Thomas, featured an unusually large cast — Andrew Poppy arranged choral and orchestral parts; Geoff Rushton (later Geoff Rushton) played bass and vibraphone; Peter Christopherson handled synthesizer and loops; Alex Fergusson played guitar; a string of outside orchestral players contributed. Paula’s role as percussionist and vibraphonist was central to the album’s unusual textural palette.

Temple Records Core Period (1984–1987)

From 1984 onward, Paula appears consistently on Temple Records releases — the label Genesis P-Orridge ran as an extension of the TOPY network.

N.Y. Scum (Temple Records, 1984) documents a New York performance: the credited PTV lineup was Alex Fergusson, Genesis P-Orridge, Geoff Rushton, John Gosling, Paula P-Orridge, and Peter Christopherson — the mid-period core six.

Unclean (Temple Records, 1984) — the same lineup, produced by Psychic TV and re-mixed by Ken Thomas.

Godstar (Temple Records, 1985) — a tribute to Brian Jones, co-credited to Psychic TV and The Angels Of Light (2). Paula is listed in the Psychic TV performer block alongside Alex Fergusson, Genesis P-Orridge, Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson, Monte Cazazza, and Mouse (2). Produced by Genesis P-Orridge and Ken Thomas; written by Alex Fergusson and Genesis P-Orridge.

Mouth Of The Night (Temple Records, 1985) — Paula credited for “Vocals [Additional Vocal Treatments]” alongside Akiko Hada. The release also carries a design credit to Derek Jarman and engineer credits to Danny Hyde. This is one of the few releases where Paula’s contribution is specifically vocal rather than percussive.

Themes 2 (Temple Records, 1985) and Descending (Sordide Sentimental, 1985) — both carry performer credits within the PTV collective listing.

Je T’Aime… (Sub Rosa, 1985) — a Genesis P-Orridge & The Angels Of Light release, not a Psychic TV primary credit. Paula is credited for “Vocals [Spoken And Sexual].” Drew McDowall contributed drum programming and keyboards; Alex Fergusson played guitar. This is one of the more intimate credits in her catalog, and one of the few times she appears on a non-PTV-primary release within the Temple era.

The Acid House Turn and Transition (1988–1991)

By 1988 the PTV lineup had shifted significantly. The Temple Records live album series continues but the band’s US touring had become associated with a new configuration. Paula’s credit on the 1988 US live documents changes to “Tape, Vocals” — indicating a shift in her live role from acoustic percussion toward tape manipulation and vocals as the band moved toward acid house and rave music.

Acid Tablets Volume One (Castalia Recordings, 1988) — a Jack The Tab release, with Genesis P-Orridge as the primary creative force. Paula is credited as “Words By” and “Written-By” on track 5, under the alias Miss P. Ornot and Ornot respectively. Richard Norris also contributed. This is the sole credit using the “Ornot” alias in the Discogs database.

A Real Swedish Live Show (TOPY Scandinavia, 1989) — produced by Carl Abrahamsson; Paula credited Percussion, Tape.

Live At Thee Ritz (Temple Records, 1989) — Performer.

Towards Thee Infinite Beat (Wax Trax! Records, 1990) — PTV’s most commercially prominent release and their entry into the acid/rave world. Paula is credited for Sampler, Tape, and Vocals — a three-way credit reflecting the album’s construction from samples, tape loops, and layered voices. The core team was Genesis P-Orridge, Fred Giannelli, Matthew Best, and Daniel Black (keyboards); Paula alongside Genesis as both sampler and vocalist.

The album appeared on Wax Trax! Records in the US and reached audiences far outside the industrial/experimental sphere. It produced the single “Ultrahouse (The L.A. Connection)” (1991), where Paula is credited as co-writer.

City Ov London / City Ov Glasgow (1991) and Thee City Ov Tokyo / Thee City Ov New York (1991) — live documents from two different PTV configurations. Paula plays “Percussion, Drums” and “Vibraphone [Vibes], Percussion” in the respective sets; on the Tokyo/New York recording she is specifically credited for vibes on tracks 8–13.

Maple Syrup (Jettisoundz, 1991) — Percussion and Tape.

Post-1991 Appearances

Pagan Day (Cleopatra, 1994) — Paula credited “Performer [Uncredited].” This late appearance in the Cleopatra-era catalog suggests she continued to appear on archival/compilation releases drawn from earlier sessions.

MeinGoettIn*Gen (1994) — listed as Performer within the PTV collective.

Instrument Profile

Across her Discogs credits Paula’s primary contribution is percussion and vibraphone — a distinctive combination that gave Psychic TV’s live sound a ritualistic, non-rock quality distinct from standard rock drumming. Credited instruments across the corpus:

  • Percussion — the most consistent single credit
  • Vibraphone (often listed as “Vibes”) — particularly prominent in the mid-1980s live recordings
  • Drums — on studio and live recordings
  • Tape — from 1988 onward, reflecting the shift toward electronic/sample-based performance
  • Vocals — occasional; includes spoken/sexual vocal treatments (Je T’Aime, Mouth Of The Night), as well as full vocals on Towards Thee Infinite Beat
  • Sampler — on Towards Thee Infinite Beat (1990)

She was also credited for Words By / Written-By on Acid Tablets Volume One (1988) and Written-By on “Je T’Aime” (Temple Records, 1989) and “Ultrahouse (The L.A. Connection)” (1991).

23 Skidoo Membership

Paula is listed as a formal member of 23 Skidoo in the Discogs group_member table (artist ID 479849 in group artist ID 21148). However, no Discogs release credits her as a performer on a 23 Skidoo primary release. Her connection to the band appears to derive from the proximity of the Genesis P-Orridge production network: Genesis P-Orridge (alongside Peter Christopherson) produced Seven Songs (Fetish Records, 1982) under the pseudonym “Tony, Terry and David.” Paula was part of that immediate circle. The 23 Skidoo file in this vault notes her as “listed due to the Seven Songs production credit; not a formal member.”

Family

Two daughters: Caresse P-Orridge and Genesse P-Orridge — both referenced in dedications on PTV releases (Album 10, 1988: “Dedicated To: Elita, Genesse, Zelta”). Caresse P-Orridge has a separate Discogs artist entry.
