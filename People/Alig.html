Alig

Real name: John Pearce (also J. R. Pearce) Discogs IDs: 236307 (Alig) / 1837976 (John Pearce (2)) Active: c. 1975 – present Origin: UK

Songwriter, multi-instrumentalist, vocalist, and producer. Principal creative force behind Family Fodder from 1975 onwards; also a core member of The Lo Yo Yo, session contributor to Cuddly Toys and others, and participant in Swiss and European collaborative projects.

Biography

Origins and Family Fodder (c. 1975–1978)

Alig (John Pearce) began what would become Family Fodder around 1975 in North London. Together with Mick Hobbs (bass) and Felix Fiedorowicz (electric bassoon, Farfisa organ), all Dada enthusiasts, he formed a student group called The Ideologically Pure Nude — the name an irreverent suggestion for what the Worker’s Revolutionary Party might include in their newspaper. The early group’s influences ranged from “Syd’s Floyd, the Velvets and Beatles to Partch, Cage and Stockhausen,” and Mark Doffman joined shortly after on drums. (source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk)

This evolved into a larger collective called The Sheep’s Pig, which added Ian Hill (keyboards, vocals, tape-loops) and Martin Harrison (bass, guitar). Harrison’s influence introduced the group to dub reggae. At some point, Alig and Ian Hill established Studio San Carlo — a basement studio representing the group’s attempt to own their own means of production — where early overdub experimentation and writing took place. The earliest Family Fodder gigs were assembled around this period, with Dominique Levillain joining as lead vocalist. Alig has described the project as “initially my solo project, but came out of a sprawling London collective.” (source: factmag.com, 2016) (source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk)

When punk exploded in 1976, the collective already had experimental credentials; rather than identifying as punks, they absorbed punk’s energy while viewing This Heat and Eno as heroes. By the time the group formally signed to Fresh Records (14) in 1979, the collective included at minimum Alig (guitar, keyboards, bass, saxophone, vocals), Dominique Levillain (lead vocals), Ian Hill (keyboards, harp, vocals), Felix Fiedorowicz (keyboards, bassoon, violin), Graham Painting (cello, bass), Rick Wilson (drums), and Mick Hobbs (bass, percussion).

Fresh Records Period (1979–1981)

Family Fodder’s debut single “Playing Golf (With My Flesh Crawling) / My Baby Takes Valium” (FRESH 1 / PURL 4, 1979) was produced on the A-side by Charles Bullen (of This Heat) under his alias Billy Bagg. The debut placed Family Fodder immediately within the London post-punk experimental underground.

Alig’s first significant outside session credit also dates to 1979: he appeared on Cuddly Toys’ Guillotine Theatre LP (Overseas Records, Japan, SUX-176-V), credited as “Alig ‘Fodder’” — playing piano on tracks A1, A4, A6 and organ on B2 and B6. The Cuddly Toys were a London glam/new wave group centred on Sean Purcell; the connection suggests Alig was active in the same circuit of London clubs and studios.

The debut album Monkey Banana Kitchen (Fresh Records, FRESH LP 3, 1980) documented the full collective in action. Alig played guitar, keyboards, bass, saxophone, vocals, and percussion. Production was credited to “The People In Control” — a collective alias for Charles Bullen and associates, including Ian Hill and Judy Carter. Recording took place at four London studios (Berry St., Elephant, Mobile, Vineyard). The album has been cited by Wire magazine as one of the 100 records that “Set The World On Fire (While No One Was Listening).”

Greatest Hits (Crammed Discs, CRAM 016, 1981) — despite its title, an album of original material — was released in Belgium and documented the ensemble at its broadest, featuring Alig on guitar, keyboards, saxophone, and vocals alongside Fiedorowicz, Hobbs, Wilson, Bazz Smith, Mark Doffman, Bullen, Dominique Levillain, Ian Hill, Jan Beetlestone, and Martin Frederix (Martin Harrison).

ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy ! (FRESH 37/12, 1981) was a 12” mini-album on which Alig played keyboards and contributed voice. It was co-produced with Chris Mansbridge.

During this period Alig also contributed to several Crammed Discs productions in a production capacity, credited as producer on:

  • Soul Kitchen / Revenge (Crammed Discs, 1981) — producer
  • Lunga Notte (Crammed Discs, 1982) — producer
  • Les Tueurs De La Lune De Miel (Crammed Discs, 1981) — recorded and mixed at Berry Street Studio (credited as “with the collaboration of”)
  • Made To Measure Vol. 1 (Crammed Discs, 1984) — recorded by

These credits demonstrate Alig’s role not only as a performer but as a working producer within the Crammed Discs circle in the early 1980s. Specifically: Soul Kitchen / Revenge was a By Chance (Discogs ID 314544) release; Lunga Notte was by Des Airs (Discogs ID 190650), a Francophone act that also featured Bob Vanderbob (saxophone, guitar, bass, voice) and vocalist Catherine Jauniaux.

Jungle Records Period (1982–1983)

Family Fodder moved to Jungle Records as one of the label’s earliest signings. Alig’s principal instrument credits on the All Styles double LP (Jungle Records, FREUD 2, 1983) are piano, keyboards, bass, vocals, and tape. The album featured a reduced core of Alig, Fiedorowicz, Bazz Smith, and Lynn Alice (who had taken over lead vocal duties from Dominique Levillain).

The Lo Yo Yo (1984–1986)

After Family Fodder’s main activity slowed, Alig co-founded The Lo Yo Yo in 1984 with Mick Hobbs, Carrie Brooks (drums), Joey Stack (vocals, keyboards, bass), and Steve Wright (6) (bass, guitar). The group recorded a self-titled home demo cassette on an eight-track reel-to-reel (Calypso Now / Not On Label, 1984) and appeared on the split cassette Double Dog Dare, Summer ‘84 (Calypso Now, NOW-37, 1984) alongside Belgian collective Look De Bouk.

The mini-album Extra Weapons (Floppy Discs (2), 1985) was the group’s main studio document. Alig played bass, guitar, percussion, keyboards, and vocals; production was shared between the band and Charles Bullen. The ensemble expanded to include Annie Hunt (cello, glockenspiel), Rick Wilson (percussion), and Bob Vanderbob (saxophone, whistle). The group dissolved around 1986 following a live cassette documenting a Würzburg performance.

The Lo Yo Yo catalog received a series of reissues from 2022 onwards: Klanggalerie (Vienna) compiled Extra Weapons and Double Dog Dare, Summer ‘84 in 2022; Staubgold reissued Extra Weapons on vinyl in 2023; and Concentric Circles (Portland, US) reissued the self-titled home demo on LP in 2025 (catalog CNTR.011). Mick Hobbs died 3 January 2025, making the Concentric Circles release posthumous for him.

Other 1980s Collaborations

Alig appeared on the Staubgold-released Made To Measure Vol. 1 (Crammed Discs, 1984, Germany) as a recording engineer.

B Goes (Switzerland, c. 1990–2002)

Alig (credited as John Pearce (2)) is listed as a member of B Goes (Discogs ID 2207250), a Swiss alternative rock group led by Andi Hoffmann and releasing on By The Way Records (Switzerland). Their catalog runs from 1990 (Denise) through 2002 (Living In The Big Wide World), covering eight releases.

The band was formed in Switzerland in 1989 when Hoffmann assembled a group of Swiss musicians for a recording project. Early releases note Alig Pearce (accordion) as a member, alongside Chessy Weaver (vocals), Andy Hug (drums), and Jüre Schmiedhauser (bass). The group is described as playing “organic rock” with a strong emphasis on exquisite vocal harmonies. Hoffmann later relocated to New Orleans (c. 1993) and rebuilt the band around American musicians, making Alig’s accordion contributions most likely concentrated in the Swiss-based early period (1990–c.1993). (source: bgoes.com)

This Swiss association represents activity during a period otherwise less documented in Alig’s UK-based discography — roughly contemporaneous with the gap between The Lo Yo Yo’s dissolution (c. 1986) and the Family Fodder revival (Water Shed, 2000).

V-Human

V-Human (Discogs ID 69181) was a duo of Alig and Viv Dogan Corringham (Vivienne Dogan Corringham). No releases are listed in the Discogs database under the V-Human name. However, the recordings made for this project were eventually released in 2016 as the Family Fodder album Foreverandever (Jungle Records, FREUDCD119). Per Jungle Records’ release notes, the recordings date from “a couple of decades” before the 2016 release — i.e. approximately the late 1990s or early 2000s. They were originally assembled for an unreleased album; some tracks were subsequently released under Alig’s alter-ego Johnny Human on an obscure Italian label (name not confirmed in available sources), while others were donated to a cooperative project. Jungle Records gathered and renovated the full set for official release under the Family Fodder name. Personnel: Alig Fodder (keyboards, guitar, vocals), Viv Corringham (vocals), Rick Wilson (drums and percussion), Dick Jude (drums). (sources: jungle-records.net; familyfodder.bandcamp.com)

Viv Corringham is a British vocalist, composer, and sound artist based in New York, known for her project Shadow-walks (audio installations and soundwalks) and certified as a teacher of Deep Listening via Pauline Oliveros. She is a 2006 and 2012 McKnight Composer Fellow. Her prior musical career began in the late 1970s; she has since worked in improvised music, sound installation, and field recording. (source: konsztruktingsoundz.co.uk)

Idol Fodder

Idol Fodder (Discogs ID 1940809) is a project alias used by Alig, credited alongside vocalist Darlini Singh-Kaul. Darlini is one of the daughters of Dominique Levillain, the original Family Fodder lead vocalist — making this a second-generation Family Fodder project. Idol Fodder is also registered as an alias of Family Fodder on Discogs; Vox Humana (8) is a further alias.

The only release under the Idol Fodder name is Bäbytalk (Slender Means Society, SMS008 / States Rights Records, US, 2008), released as Volume VI of the Pregnancy Series curated by the Slender Means Society (a Portland-based independent label founded in 2004 by Zac Pennington of Parenthetical Girls). The EP contains seven tracks exploring themes of pregnancy and parenthood. Musical elements include post-punk, classical chamber music, jazz, funk, and ambient passages. The oud features prominently — an instrument Alig favours. Key tracks: “The Onliest Thing” (Alig and Darlini in duet over sawing cello), “Infamy,” “Death and the Maiden,” “Analyse my life?,” and the Indian-influenced instrumental “Earlimusix.” The EP was gifted with the involvement of Jungle Records and bears the co-credit to Alig Fodder and Jungle Records. (sources: vivonzeureux.blogspot.com; slendermeanssociety.com; wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender_Means_Society)

Family Fodder Returns (1998–present)

The Savoir Faire: The Best Of compilation (Jungle Records / Dark Beloved Cloud, 1998) brought renewed attention to the early catalog. Alig is not specifically credited on this release beyond the primary “Family Fodder” credit.

Water Shed (Dark Beloved Cloud, DBC 226, 2000) was a full return as a recording act, with Alig credited as keyboards, vocals, and accordion (and listed on the release as “Alig Fodder”). The album was co-produced with Andy Le Vien and Martin Harrison. Other contributors included Dominique Levillain (vocals), Martin Harrison (bass, sampler), Rick Wilson (drums), Cécile Rabhi (percussion), and Ricky Edwards (bass clarinet, alto saxophone).

More Great Hits! (Jungle Records, 2008) was the second retrospective, with Alig credited as performer alongside the full historical ensemble.

Sunday Girls (Director’s Cut) and Staubgold (2014–2015)

Alig played guitar, keyboards, vocals, and saxophone on Sunday Girls (Director’s Cut) (Staubgold, 2015), credited as “Alig Fodder.” The ensemble included Hobbs, Harrison, Fiedorowicz, Wilson, Doffman, Hill, Levillain, Jan Beetlestone, Monelle Jouet, and Naresh Singh Kaul.

In 2014 Alig also worked on the Staubgold reissues: he handled mastering on the Monkey Banana Kitchen CD reissue (staubgold 130), credited as John Pearce (2).

Later Work (2011–2023)

Alig contributed additional keyboards to Shakatak’s Snowflakes & Jazzamatazz (The Christmas Album) (Secret Records, Russia, 2014), a guest credit.

Family Fodder’s later catalog through the 2010s and into the 2020s includes collaborations with Kommissar Hjuler and the Psych.KG label (Germany), as well as the Crammed Discs release Ex-Futur Album (2014) and Une Aventure De VV (Songspiel) (Crammed Discs, 2023), on which Alig is credited for performances and vocals.

Connections

  • Family Fodder — founder, principal songwriter, guitarist, keyboardist, vocalist, and producer throughout the group’s history
  • The Lo Yo Yo — co-founding member 1984; bass, guitar, keyboards, vocals on all releases
  • This Heat / Charles Bullen — Bullen produced Family Fodder’s debut single and co-produced the “Savoir Faire” single with David Cunningham; also produced The Lo Yo Yo’s Extra Weapons
  • People In Control — production collective (Bullen-associated) that produced Monkey Banana Kitchen
  • The state51 Conspiracy — Family Fodder’s digital-era releases including the 2011 Singularity series
  • Crammed Discs — not only released Family Fodder material but credited Alig as producer on associated Crammed acts in 1981–1984
  • Cuddly Toys — session keyboard/organ work, 1979 (Guillotine Theatre)
  • Shakatak — guest keyboards, 2014
  • B Goes — Swiss alternative rock group; member c. 1990–2002

Open Questions

  • Pre-1979 precursor names now confirmed (The Ideologically Pure Nude, 1975; The Sheep’s Pig, mid-late 1970s). Specific gig venues and dates from 1975–1978 remain undocumented — no contemporary press coverage or set lists have been located.
  • V-Human: the Foreverandever (2016) LP appears to be the primary document of the Alig / Viv Corringham recordings, but whether V-Human also operated as a live or performance art project (separate from the album recordings) is not confirmed.
  • B Goes: Alig’s instrument is confirmed as accordion (from bgoes.com). Specific session dates and whether he contributed songwriting or only performance credits are not detailed. His involvement appears concentrated in the early Swiss-based period (c.1990–1993) before Hoffmann’s move to New Orleans.
  • The alias “Henry Fodder” is shared between Alig and Graham Painting on Discogs — whether this was intentional credit-sharing, an informal collective tradition, or a cataloguing error is not confirmed by any web source.
  • The “obscure Italian release” of V-Human / Foreverandever tracks under the Johnny Human alias: the label name has not been identified in available web sources.
