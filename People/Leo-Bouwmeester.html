Biography

Leo Bouwmeester is a Dutch pianist and keyboardist whose career spans at least three decades, from a 1993 album credit through a 2023 Jacques Brel tribute. Discogs documents him solely as “Pianist” with no further biographical detail. He has a Dutch Wikipedia article and maintains a personal website at leobouwmeester.com.

1990s — Session Work and Ensemble Membership

Bouwmeester’s earliest Discogs credit is on Boys (1993, Ariola), an alternative rock album by the Dutch band Shine (17). He contributed piano to three tracks (1, 9, 10); the session was otherwise dominated by bandleader Richard Janssen (R.J.), who played guitar, bass, vocals, and produced. The album was mixed by Dick Meaney.

By 1995 he was keyboardist in the Astrid Seriese Band, appearing on Trouble Of The World (Brigadoon, BIS 012). Track-level credits on “Wake Me Up” confirm his role as keyboards alongside bassist Harm Wijntjes, drummer Theo Bodewes, guitarist Martijn van Agt, percussionist Peter Meuris, and saxophonist Marc Mommaas. “Wake Me Up” was written by George Michael.

He was simultaneously a member of I Compani, a large Amsterdam-based chamber jazz ensemble with a roster of some sixty-plus rotating musicians. I Compani had released two Nino Rota film-music albums on BV Haast Records before Bouwmeester joined — Music To The Films Of Federico Fellini (1985) and Ecco! — More Filmmusic By Nino Rota (1988) — though Bouwmeester himself does not appear credited on those pressings. The 1985 album features Frank van Merwijk on piano; the 1988 on Frank van Merwijk as well. Bouwmeester’s specific I Compani credits are not documented in the Discogs release database.

The mid-1990s also saw him in The Rembrandt Orchestra, a Dutch chamber/pop orchestra that released National Anthems Of The E.U. (1995, Disky, DCD 5427) — a Benelux-market CD of EU national anthems arranged by John May and produced by Cat Music. Bouwmeester is listed as a member of the ensemble in Discogs but carries no named role credit on the release itself.

He is also a member of Maiden Voyage (Discogs ID 2364906), a large Dutch jazz ensemble. Their sole documented Discogs release is Night In Torremolinos (1999, BV Haast Records, CD 9918), a jazz CD with compositions by Paul Stocker, Tony Heimer, and Jacko Schoonderwoerd. Bouwmeester is credited as piano (sharing the piano chair with Tony Heimer on tracks 2 and 7). The ensemble is notably large — over eighteen members including saxophonists Sean Bergin and Paul Stocker, trombonist David Rothschild, and tenor saxophonist Tobias Delius.

A fourth ensemble membership is the Wouter Kiers Quartet, a Dutch jazz quartet whose sole documented Discogs release is The ‘Café Alto’ Repertoire Volume 2 (2005, 16Mb Limited Edition Records, cat. 300104), a CD of jazz standards featuring Wouter Kiers on tenor saxophone. The quartet’s other members are Hans Ruigrok and Maarten Kruijswijk.

2000s

The Wouter Kiers Quartet recording (Café Alto vol. 2, 2005) is the only Discogs-documented credit from this decade. Active performing life during this period is not documented in the database.

2010s — Theatre and Jacques Brel

In 2018 Bouwmeester played piano on Jeroen Willems Zingt Jacques Brel (Concerto Records, CRLP5049), a double-LP by Dutch actor and singer Jeroen Willems (2). The album is a Dutch-language Jacques Brel program with translations by Ernst van Altena, Peer Wittenbols, and Rob Klinkenberg. The band also includes Oleg Fateev (accordion), Peter Bjornhild (bass), Victor De Boo (drums), with Wim Selles producing and mixing. The release was mastered in two sessions by Bastiaan Kuijt (sides A–C) and Mikel Le Roy (side D).

2020s — Jacques Brel Continued

In 2023 Bouwmeester played piano on Beatrice Zingt Brel — Laat Me Niet Alleen (Moerasgas, MG23004), a thirteen-track CD by Dutch singer Beatrice van der Poel (who also produced). The ensemble includes Gert Wantenaar (accordion), Sanne Van Delft (contrabass), Maripepa Contreras (oboe), Daniel van Dalen (percussion), and Micha Molthoff (violin). The album was engineered by Bart Wagemakers and mixed/mastered by Frans Hendriks. Translations are primarily by Ernst van Altena with some by Willem Wilmink (adjusted by Beatrice van der Poel).
