Adele Bertei

Vocalist, keyboardist, songwriter, filmmaker, author. Born 1955, Cleveland, Ohio. Discogs ID: 32010. Real name: Adele Marie Bertei. Alias: Randy Marlowe.

Overview

One of the central figures of the New York no wave scene and its aftermath. Original keyboardist in The Contortions (playing Ace Tone organ rhythmically, not melodically); briefly the first vocalist of Bush Tetras; co-founder of The Bloods (the first openly queer all-female rock band in the US); member of Aural Exciters (ZE Records); session vocalist for Thomas Dolby, Tears for Fears, Arthur Baker, Ornette Coleman, and others; and solo artist on Geffen and Chrysalis. Later became a filmmaker (directing for Showtime) and published author (five books, with a sixth on no wave due 2026).

The thread connecting her career is queer identity made explicit at a time when that was still radical, and a particular restlessness — she moved between the most experimental end of no wave and mainstream pop production without ever settling in either.

Formation: Cleveland

Born 1955 to an Italian father (Umberto Bertei) and Irish/French-Canadian mother. Her childhood was marked by institutional displacement — foster homes, a Catholic convent school, and a juvenile reformatory. Before music she worked at a Veterans Hospital, the Salvation Army, and on a Ford assembly line. Entirely self-taught.

Peter Laughner

Met Peter Laughner — guitarist, co-founder of Rocket from the Tombs and Pere Ubu — at a blues jam where she was singing “Piece of My Heart.” He introduced her to Television’s debut single and his record collection; they became roommates and formed a band called The Wolves. Laughner died in June 1977 from pancreatitis (alcohol and drug abuse). His death prompted her move to New York: “When Peter passed away, there was nothing left for me in Cleveland so I did what he and I had planned to do together — I moved to New York in 1977, and immediately dove into the No Wave scene.”

She also knew Nan Goldin from a Cleveland gay bar; Goldin was already in the downtown Manhattan scene. Bertei later wrote about Laughner in her memoir Peter and the Wolves (Hat & Beard Press, 2013; reissued 2021).

No Wave Era (1977–1982)

The Contortions (1977–c.1979)

Recruited by James Chance to play Ace Tone organ despite having never played keyboards. Chance had her play rhythmically rather than melodically — the organ as percussion and texture. Also contributed electric guitar.

Credited as Acetone Organ on No New York (Antilles AN-7067, 1978), the defining document of the scene, produced by Brian Eno.

The Eno connection: In 1978, while working as Eno’s personal assistant, Bertei took him to a series of concerts at Artists Space, New York — directly resulting in him producing No New York. The stub in the DB at Artists Space records this; the moment is one of the most consequential in no wave’s documented history.

Off White — James White & The Blacks (1979)

Played piano on tracks A1 and A4 of Off White (Island Records / ZE Records, France, 1979), the James White & The Blacks album. A session that concentrated much of the no wave world in one record:

Aural Exciters (1979–1982)

Member of Aural Exciters, a ZE Records project conceived by producer Bob Blank and August Darnell (Kid Creole). An ensemble that drew from the same downtown milieu but aimed at mutant disco; other members included Lizzy Mercier Descloux, Andy Hernandez (Coati Mundi), Taana Gardner, Ron Rogers, Christine Wiltshire, and James Siegfried.

Discography on ZE Records:

  • “My Boy Lollipop” (ZE / Buddah, 1979)
  • “Spooks In Space” (ZE / Buddah, 1979)
  • “Chinese Rap” (Top Flight, 1982)

Vortex Soundtrack (1982)

Co-producer (tracks A3 and B6) on Beth B & Scott B’s Vortex (The Motion Picture Soundtrack) (Neutral Records, 1982) — a primary artist credit alongside Lydia Lunch, Richard Edson, Kristian Hoffman, and Beth B & Scott B. Engineered by Don Hünerberg.

Bush Tetras (briefly, 1979/1980)

After leaving The Contortions, briefly became the first vocalist of Bush Tetras — formed by her ex-Contortions bandmate Pat Place. Left after their debut performance following poor press. No recordings; tenure was weeks. Cynthia Sley then joined as vocalist.

Film: Vivienne Dick

Appeared in three films by Vivienne Dick during this period:

  • Guerillère Talks (1978) — with Pat Place, Lydia Lunch, Anya Phillips, Ikue Mori
  • Beauty Becomes the Beast (1979) — with Lydia Lunch and Klaus Nomi

And in The Offenders (Scott B and Beth B, 1979/80), playing character “Laura.”

The Bloods (c.1980–1983)

Co-founded with guitarist Kathy Rey. Consistently described as the first openly queer all-female rock band in the United States. Post-no wave punk-funk. Toured the US and Europe; opened for The Clash in New York; supported Gang of Four, Bush Tetras, Richard Hell, Johnny Thunders, the Slits, and Lydia Lunch.

Solo Career (1983–1988)

“Build Me A Bridge” (Geffen Records, 1983)

One of the first solo acts signed to Geffen. Produced by Thomas Dolby; remixed by Mark Kamins (the DJ who discovered Madonna at Danceteria) and Harvey Goldberg. Co-written with Lesley Woods (vocalist/guitarist of Au Pairs) and Raymond Jones.

Did not chart. No album emerged from the Geffen deal.

Thomas Dolby (1984)

Sang backing vocals on “Hyperactive!” (Parlophone, from The Flat Earth, 1984) — UK #17 / US Hot 100 #62 / US Dance Chart #37. Co-wrote “Marseilles” (B-side of “Hyperactive!”). Performed with Dolby on The Old Grey Whistle Test. Appeared on Dolby’s Retrospectacle: The Best Of (EMI, 1994).

Tears for Fears (1985)

Sang backing vocals on Songs from the Big Chair (Mercury, 1985) — confirmed via her credit on the Scenes From The Big Chair DVD reissue (Universal Music DVD Video, Belgium, 2005). The sub’s unconfirmed “wrote for Tears for Fears” is separate from this; the Discogs credit is for vocals, not songwriting.

Phil Pickett — 1984 Olympic Theme (MCA Records, 1984)

Backing vocals on “Destiny (The Olympic Theme)” by Phil Pickett (ex-Sailor). Other backing vocalists: Helen Terry (Culture Club), Terry Britten, Thereza Bazar (Dollar). Drums: Jon Moss (Culture Club).

Why Bertei was in this session: By 1984 Bertei had relocated to London (after touring Europe with The Bloods she spent time in Rotterdam and Amsterdam before settling in the UK) and had established herself in the London session circuit as a backing vocalist for Culture Club — both live and in the studio. Per Wikipedia: “During her years in London, Bertei sang backing vocals with various groups live and in the studio, including Culture Club and The Passions.” (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele_Bertei, as of 2026-08-16). The “Destiny” session was essentially a Culture Club circle production: Phil Pickett was Culture Club’s keyboard player and co-wrote “Karma Chameleon” (Ivor Novello winner); Jon Moss was Culture Club’s drummer; Helen Terry was their regular backing vocalist. Bertei’s presence follows directly from her established role in that network. No press interview naming her specifically for this session has been located.

Jamaaladeen Tacuma / Cosmetic — So Tranquilizin’ (Gramavision, 1985)

Backing vocals on So Tranquilizin’ (Gramavision, US, 1985) by Cosmetic with Jamaaladeen Tacuma. A dense studio production featuring Ornette Coleman (saxophone), Anton Fier (DMX drum programming), John Robie (Emulator), and Maripol (backing vocals alongside Bertei).

”When It’s Over” / “Every Little Bit Hurts” (Chrysalis CHS 2907, 1985)

Produced by David Gamson (Scritti Politti), Fred Maher (Scritti Politti), and John Potoker. The Scritti Politti connection is confirmed via Gamson and Maher; Green Gartside backing vocal is claimed in external sources but not credited in Discogs.

”Just a Mirage” (Chrysalis, 1988)

Co-wrote and sang lead on “Just a Mirage” — credited “Jellybean featuring Adele Bertei”; on Jellybean’s Just Visiting This Planet (Chrysalis, 1987). Co-written with Toni C. (Antoinette Colandero). UK #13 — her biggest commercial chart position.

Little Lives LP (Chrysalis FV/FVT/VK 41634, 1988)

Solo debut album. Co-produced by Bertei and Ian Prince. Recorded at Chelsea Sound, Science Lab (NY), Seller Sound (Detroit), Sorcerer Sound (NY), Westside Sound and Why Not (LA).

Post-1988

”Kiss the Ground (You Walk On)” (RCA, 1991)

Featured vocalist on Arthur Baker featuring Adele Bertei — “Kiss The Ground (You Walk On)” (RCA, 1991). Co-written and co-produced by Baker, Eric Kupper, and Lenny Dee.

Imagination — Medium Cool (Rough Trade, 1991)

Featured vocalist on Medium Cool’s Imagination (Rough Trade, US, 1991). Bertei sings tracks A1, A4, and B5. Full credits:

Phantascope (Rhythmbank Entertainment, 2004)

Member of Anubian Lights; credited Lead Vocals, Backing Vocals, Ace Tone Organ, Keyboards, Written-By on Phantascope — released on Nona Hendryx’s Rhythmbank Entertainment label.

”Smoke In The Shadows” (2004/2006)

Lyrics and music credit on “Smoke In The Shadows” — Breakin’ Beats (2004) and Mule Musiq reissue (Germany, 2006).
