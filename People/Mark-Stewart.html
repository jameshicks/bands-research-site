Mark Stewart

Vocalist, songwriter, artist, producer. Bristol, UK. Discogs ID: 19111. Born 10 August 1960; died 21 April 2023. Aliases: Angel Salvador, Control Data.

Co-founder and vocalist of The Pop Group (1977–1980). After the band split, fronted Mark Stewart And The Maffia and New Age Steppers, releasing on Adrian Sherwood’s On-U Sound and on Mute Records. One of the defining voices in British post-punk and industrial dub. His death in April 2023 — followed by John Waddington’s in June — effectively ended any prospect of a further Pop Group revival.

New Age Steppers

Discogs lists Stewart as a formal member of New Age Steppers (Discogs ID: 23698), the post-punk dub collective formed in 1980 by Adrian Sherwood and Ari-Up. His actual recorded appearances with the group are concentrated on one release:

On the self-titled LP he is credited as “Mark” (first name only) under Vocals, alongside Ari-Up and Vivien Goldman. He also has an uncredited songwriting credit on track A4, “Crazy Dreams And High Ideals.” He does not appear on Action Battlefield (1981), Foundation Steppers (1983), or the debut 7” Fade Away (1980).

Track-level vocal credit

Multiple sources confirm Stewart sings on “Crazy Dreams And High Ideals” (track A4) — described variously as “a rallying cry against inequality” and “an early version of his track later re-versioned with the Maffia.” (Bandcamp NAS page; On-U Sound LP reissue review, Test Pressing, as of 2026-06-27.) No other track on the LP is attributed to Stewart by any source. The album-wide vocalist credit (“Ari, Viv, Mark”) does not break down by track; reviewers assign the reggae interpretations (“Fade Away”, “Love Forever”) to Ari Up and the album closer “Private Armies” to Vivien Goldman. Tracks “Radial Drill”, “State Assembly”, “Abderhamane’s Demise”, and “Animal Space” have no publicly documented lead vocalist.

Assessment: core member or guest?

Discogs formally lists him as a member, and he is credited on the group’s debut LP — the release that established the collective. Multiple sources confirm that after The Pop Group split (1981) Stewart “headed off to London to hook up with the emerging On-U Sound conspiracy” and contributed to NAS before forming Mark Stewart And The Maffia (Wikipedia: Mark Stewart, as of 2026-06-27). The personnel on NAS releases shifted substantially from release to release (the group operated as an open collective rather than a fixed band). Stewart’s participation appears to have been front-loaded at the 1981 LP, after which he directed energy into Mark Stewart And The Maffia, his primary On-U Sound vehicle. The 1994 compilation credit reflects archival material rather than new involvement.

How he came to work with Sherwood

Sherwood first encountered Stewart at Revolver Records in Bristol in the mid-1970s — Sherwood was approximately 17–18, Stewart approximately 14–15. Stewart and his friends would gather at Revolver to receive new reggae imports Sherwood brought down weekly from London. After The Pop Group disbanded, Stewart relocated to London and the two began recording together; their primary collaboration was Learning To Cope With Cowardice (1983). (Caught by the River, Revolver Records feature, as of 2026-06-27; Test Pressing / Dr Rob interview with Sherwood, as of 2026-06-27.)

Aliases: Angel Salvador and Control Data

Discogs lists two aliases on Stewart’s artist page: “Angel Salvador” and “Control Data.” Web research (2026-06-27) clarifies the following:

Control Data is the title of a 1996 Mute Records LP (Control Data, released June 1996), not a release pseudonym. Stewart did not release material under “Control Data” as a name. The Discogs alias entry likely reflects a database convention where album titles can be mistakenly entered as aliases, or may reflect a Japan-market release variant. (Wikipedia: Control Data (album); Mute Records page, as of 2026-06-27.)

Angel Salvador is documented as a second Discogs artist page (ID: 686791) linked to Mark Stewart via MusicBrainz external links (the MusicBrainz entry lists both “Discogs (as Mark Stewart)” and “Discogs (as Angel Salvador)”). This confirms Angel Salvador is a pseudonym Stewart used for at least some releases. However, no specific releases credited to Angel Salvador could be confirmed from public web sources — the Discogs artist page is Cloudflare-blocked and no review, interview, or database mirror surfaces the titles. The specific releases remain unidentified pending Discogs DB access. (MusicBrainz: Mark Stewart, as of 2026-06-27.)
