Harry Connick Jr.

Jazz pianist, vocalist, composer, arranger, actor, and television host. Born Joseph Harry Fowler Connick, Jr., September 11, 1967, New Orleans, Louisiana. Discogs artist ID: 352440. Real name: Joseph Harry Fowler Connick, Jr.

Active 1978–present. Primary label: Columbia Records (1987–2019+). Later: Verve Records (2019–). Married to former Victoria’s Secret model Jill Goodacre.

Biography

New Orleans Roots and Early Mentors (1967–1986)

Harry Connick Jr. was born into a musical family in New Orleans. His father, Harry Connick Sr. (Discogs: 1661493), served as the District Attorney of Orleans Parish and had a long association with the city’s cultural institutions. Connick began playing piano at age three and performing publicly by five. His early mentors included two foundational figures of New Orleans jazz:

  • Ellis Marsalis — Connick studied piano privately with Marsalis throughout his teenage years, an apprenticeship that grounded him in the tradition of bebop and New Orleans jazz. Ellis Marsalis later appeared on piano alongside Connick on track 1 (“Stardust”) of the 1992 album 25.
  • James Booker — the idiosyncratic New Orleans pianist was another formative influence on Connick’s keyboard approach.

Connick appears to have first recorded commercially as early as 1978 as part of Dixieland Plus (a group release, Discogs: 3856391 / master 478505, on Studio A Productions). He moved to New York City in his teens to attend the Manhattan School of Music and Hunter College.

Columbia Debut and the Marsalis Connection (1987–1989)

His self-titled debut Harry Connick, Jr. (1987, Columbia FC 40702) was a solo piano album produced by Delfeayo Marsalis — younger brother of Wynton Marsalis — and released through George Butler’s Columbia jazz roster. The album features:

  • Ron Carter — acoustic bass (tracks A5, B1, B3)
  • Reginald Veal — bass (track B4)
  • Herlin Riley — drums (track B4)
  • Executive Producer: George Butler

A promotional interview LP, Interchords (Interview By Wynton Marsalis) (1987, Columbia), features Wynton Marsalis conducting the interview — reflecting the close mentorship connection. His second album, 20 (1988, Columbia), followed quickly.

Breakthrough: When Harry Met Sally… (1989)

The soundtrack When Harry Met Sally… (Music From The Motion Picture) (1989, Columbia CK 45319) made Connick a mainstream star. Produced by Connick and Marc Shaiman, it reached the top 20 of the Billboard 200 and introduced his big-band vocal style to a mass audience. Personnel include:

  • Marc Shaiman — arranger (big band tracks), co-producer
  • Ben Wolfe — bass
  • Jeff Tain Watts — drums (credited as “Jeff ‘Tain’ Watts”)
  • Jay Berliner — acoustic guitar
  • Frank Wess — tenor saxophone

Columbia Years: Big Band and Vocal Jazz (1990–1999)

Connick became Columbia’s flagship jazz-pop artist through the 1990s, releasing a string of major albums with a working big band and trio. His long-running creative partnerships solidified:

  • Tracey Freeman — producer on virtually all 1990s–2000s Columbia albums (307 shared release credits); also co-writer, vocal coach, and backing vocalist
  • Gregg Rubin — recording and mixing engineer across the same period (216 shared credits)
  • Marc Shaiman — arranger and co-producer, especially on big band material (115 credits)

Film, Television, and Broadway (1990s–2010s)

Connick built a parallel career in entertainment alongside his recording work:

  • Acting: appeared in Memphis Belle (1990), Little Man Tate (1991), Independence Day (1996), Hope Floats (1998), The Iron Giant (1999, voice), P.S. I Love You (2007), among others
  • Broadway: wrote music for and appeared in Thou Shalt Not (2002, original cast recording released on Papa’s June Music); appeared in The Pajama Game revival (2006, collected on Harry On Broadway, Act 1, Columbia)
  • Television: hosted a daytime talk show Harry (2016–2018); regular judge on American Idol (2014–2016)

The 1994 album She reflects a broader New Orleans funk direction. The Discogs credits list Connick as songwriter (music) and multiple lyricists (Ramsey McLean, Jonathan Dubose, Tony Hall); specific session musicians are not fully documented in Discogs. Connick connections to Meters alumni (George Porter Jr., Joseph Modeliste) may be sourced from press/liner notes not captured in Discogs.

Marsalis Music Period (2003–2007)

In the mid-2000s, Connick released a trilogy of solo piano albums on Marsalis Music (Wynton Marsalis’s label, distributed by Rounder Records):

AlbumYearLabel / Cat#
Other Hours – Connick On Piano 12003Marsalis Music / Rounder 116 613 304-2
Occasion (Connick On Piano, Vol. 2)2005Marsalis Music / Rounder
Chanson Du Vieux Carré (Connick On Piano 3)2007Marsalis Music / Universal

These albums consist entirely of original Connick compositions played solo; the Discogs entry for vol. 1 lists no personnel credits beyond him as primary artist. They represent a return to his improvisational jazz roots.

The 2007 album Oh, My Nola (Columbia) was a tribute to New Orleans in the wake of Hurricane Katrina.

Later Columbia and Verve Years (2008–2021)

  • Your Songs (2009, Columbia) — covers of pop standards
  • Every Man Should Know (2013, Columbia)
  • That Would Be Me (2015, Columbia)
  • True Love: A Celebration Of Cole Porter (2019, Verve Records B0030829-02) — produced by Connick and Tracey Freeman; recorded and mixed by Ryan Hewitt (who had previously served as assistant engineer on 30 in 2001); moved to Verve after decades at Columbia
  • Alone With My Faith (2021, Capitol Christian Music Group / Verve B0033520-02) — gospel and sacred music album; Connick played all instruments and produced solo; photography by Georgia Connick
