Ann Margret

Discogs ID: 115000 Real name: Ann-Margret Olsson Born: April 28, 1941, Oscars parish, Stockholm, Sweden Alias: Rusty Martin (7) (Discogs ID: 7449030)

Swedish-born American actress and singer. She grew up in Wilmette, Illinois after emigrating with her family in 1946. She was discovered singing in a nightclub by comedian George Burns. Known in the early 1960s as the “female Elvis,” she was one of the most commercially successful pop singers on RCA Victor during the label’s early 1960s peak, simultaneously building a film career. Her most distinct recording chapter outside the RCA years was a run of duets and solo singles on Lee Hazlewood’s LHI Records (1968–69), including the LP The Cowboy & The Lady (1969). Later recordings appeared on Polydor, Ocean/Ariola America, MCA, and First American. She married actor Roger Smith (Discogs ID: 2248322) in 1967; he remained her manager until his death in 2017.

Early RCA Victor Years (1961–1967)

Ann-Margret’s recording career began immediately after her discovery by George Burns. Her debut LP And Here She Is (RCA Victor, 1961) was produced by Dick Peirce, arranged and conducted by Marty Paich, and engineered by Al Schmitt — a polished mix of pop standards and uptempo numbers that established her as a serious pop commodity. Burns contributed liner notes.

She followed with a series of singles and LPs through RCA Victor, including:

  • On The Way Up (1962) — continued the Peirce/Schmitt/Sholes production team
  • Bachelors’ Paradise (1963) — produced by Steve Sholes, arranged and conducted by Hank Levine, engineered by Al Schmitt
  • Beauty And The Beard (1964) — duet LP with trumpeter Al Hirt (RCA Victor); one of her biggest commercial releases

She also appeared on the State Fair (1962) movie soundtrack on Dot Records alongside Pat Boone, Bobby Darin, Pamela Tiffin, Tom Ewell, and Alice Faye — a rare non-RCA Victor outing in this period.

By mid-decade she was receiving high-profile arrangements: Songs From The Swinger And Other Swingin’ Songs (RCA Victor, 1966) featured arrangements by John Williams (4) (credited as “Johnny Williams”) on the A-side and Quincy Jones on B4.

LHI Records / Lee Hazlewood Era (1968–1969)

Ann-Margret’s most creatively idiosyncratic recording period came when she signed with Lee Hazlewood’s independent label LHI Records, based at 9000 Sunset Boulevard, Los Angeles. The connection made natural sense: Hazlewood was already a proven duet partner (most famously with Nancy Sinatra), and his country-noir, cinematic production style offered a sharp contrast to her polished RCA Victor sound.

She appears on four primary LHI releases:

  1. LHI-1 — You Turned My Head Around (7”, 1968, solo)
  2. LHI-2 / Release 4482382 — Sleep In The Grass (7”, 1968) — both billed as “Ann Margret & Lee Hazlewood”; produced, arranged, and written by Hazlewood; orchestra conducted by Jimmie Haskell
  3. LHI S-12007 / Release 1687897 — The Cowboy & The Lady (LP, 1969) — their main duo album
  4. LHI-5 — The Dark End Of The Street / Victims Of The Night (7”, 1969)
  5. LHI-11 — Walk On Out Of My Mind (7”, 1969)

The Cowboy & The Lady (LHI S-12007, 1969)

The central document of the Ann-Margret / Hazlewood collaboration. Hazlewood produced and arranged the album, with Charlie McCoy contributing additional arrangements. Associate producers were Donnie Owens and Joe Nixon.

Film Work: Tommy (1975)

Ann-Margret took a significant acting/singing role in Ken Russell’s film adaptation of The Who’s Tommy (1975, Polydor). She appeared on the original soundtrack recording as a vocalist across multiple tracks.

MCA and First American Era (1979–1981)

After the LHI chapter, Ann-Margret worked with producer Paul Sabu for a late-1970s/early-1980s pop-rock run, signing to Ocean/Ariola America and then MCA:

  • Love Rush E-Minor (Ocean/Ariola America, 1979) — produced, arranged, and written by Paul Sabu; Marc Kreiner and Tom Cossie as executive producers; Steve Pouliot engineering
  • Ann-Margret (MCA Records, 1980) — again produced by Paul Sabu; elaborate string arrangements; Marc Kreiner executive producer
  • Everybody Needs Somebody Sometimes (First American, 1981) — Paul Sabu producing

Later Career

Ann-Margret returned to RCA for a 1996 compilation (Let Me Entertain You) and continued to appear on stage and in film through the 2000s. She appeared on the Newsies (1992) soundtrack on Walt Disney Records as part of the “Additional Singing Cast” group Newsies Additional Singing Cast (Discogs ID: 5850092). In 2022 and 2023 she released new material on Cleopatra Records — Splish Splash (2022) and Born To Be Wild (2023) — the latter featuring guest appearances from Joe Perry, Steve Cropper, Cliff Richard, Rick Wakeman, Brian Auger, and others.
