David Sanborn

American jazz and R&B alto saxophonist (also soprano, baritone). Born 30 July 1945, Tampa, Florida; died 12 May 2024, Tarrytown, New York. Discogs artist ID: 185751.

Overview

David Sanborn was one of the defining voices of the alto saxophone in popular music from the mid-1970s onward. His signature sound — raw, gutbucket at the bottom, soaring and keening at the top — bridged blues, R&B, jazz-funk, and the smooth jazz that he helped define commercially. He came up in blues (a teenage polio survivor who took up the horn on a doctor’s advice), joined The Paul Butterfield Blues Band in 1967, and spent 1967–1971 recording with them before launching a solo career on Warner Bros. that ran through most of the 1980s, then shifted to Reprise and Elektra through the 1990s.

His session footprint is enormous — he appears on landmark records by David Bowie, Stevie Wonder, James Taylor (2), Carly Simon, Elton John, The Rolling Stones, Aretha Franklin, Gil Evans, and many others. As a solo artist his Grammy-winning album Voyeur (1981) and the Bob James collaboration Double Vision (1986) are his commercial peaks. He was also a TV fixture as co-host/bandleader of the NBC late-night show Night Music (1988–1990).

The Paul Butterfield Blues Band (1967–1971)

Sanborn joined The Paul Butterfield Blues Band around 1967 after the departure of several original members. He appears on four studio albums: The Resurrection of Pigboy Crabshaw (1967, alto sax), In My Own Dream (1968, soprano/alto/baritone sax), Keep On Moving (1969, alto sax), and Sometimes I Just Feel Like Smilin’ (1971, alto sax). He also appears on the posthumous compilation Golden Butter (1972) and the reissue Paul Butterfield’s Better Days (1990, Sequel Records). His work with Butterfield brought him into the orbit of the Bearsville/Woodstock circle and the New York studio world.

Selected Session Credits

Sanborn’s session discography spans hundreds of releases. The most significant by artist:

David Bowie

  • David Live (RCA, 1974) — alto saxophone, flute; live tour band including Michael Kamen, Earl Slick, Herbie Flowers
  • Young Americans (RCA, 1975) — saxophone; band includes Luther Vandross (vocal arrangements), Carlos Alomar, Mike Garson
  • Later Bowie compilations (Sound+Vision, Changesbowie, Who Can I Be Now? box set) credit his contributions from this period

Stevie Wonder

  • Music Of My Mind (Tamla Motown, 1972) — saxophone
  • Talking Book (Tamla, 1972) — alto saxophone

James Taylor
