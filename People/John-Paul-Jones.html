Bassist for Led Zeppelin. Born: January 3, 1946, Sidcup, Kent, England.

John Paul Jones became a prolific musical director and arranger of a huge range of prominent sixties artists, including the Rolling Stones, Billy Nichols, Nico, the Andrew Oldham Orchestra, Herman’s Hermits, Donovan, Lulu, the Yardbirds, Cliff Richard, and Dusty Springfield, before forming Led Zeppelin with Jimmy Page in 1968.

Overview

John Paul Jones (born John Paul Baldwin, 3 January 1946, Sidcup, Kent) spent the early 1960s building one of the most extensive session and arrangement careers in British pop, contributing to records by the Rolling Stones, Donovan, Dusty Springfield, the Yardbirds, and many others before co-founding Led Zeppelin with Jimmy Page in 1968. As the band’s bassist, organist, and keyboard player, he was its rhythmic and harmonic anchor; his 3,600-plus Discogs credits cluster overwhelmingly around the Page–Plant–Bonham core alongside engineers Eddie Kramer, Glyn Johns, and Andy Johns, with Atlantic and Swan Song as the primary label homes. After Led Zeppelin dissolved following John Bonham’s death in 1980, his catalogue shows scattered solo and session output through the 1990s and 2000s, including a stint in the supergroup Them Crooked Vultures with Dave Grohl and Josh Homme.
