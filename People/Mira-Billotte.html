Mira Billotte

Washington, DC musician, songwriter, and visual artist. Discogs artist ID: 707839. Sister of Christina Billotte. Co-founder, drummer, vocalist, and guitarist of Quix-o-tic (1997–2002). After Quix-o-tic dissolved she continued as the primary creative force behind White Magic, a psychedelic folk project based in Brooklyn, releasing records on Drag City through the mid-2000s and into the 2010s. Name variation in credits: “Billotte.”

1997–2002: Quix-o-tic

Quix-o-tic (Discogs: Quixotic, ID 558111) was a Washington, DC alternative/indie band described as blending R&B and doo-wop with a gothic outlook. Founded with her sister Christina Billotte (guitar, vocals) and Brendan Majewski (bass, vocals; ID 1316080). Mira played drums on all three releases and expanded into guitar and lead vocals on the final album.

Mick Barr plays bass on tracks 1–3, 5–7, 9–12 and drums on track 8; Christina plays bass on track 8 and drums on tracks 2 and 12. Cover photo by Barbara Billotte. The album includes covers of Billy Stewart’s “Sitting In The Park,” Aaron Neville’s “Tell It Like It Is,” and Black Sabbath’s “Lord Of This World.”

2004–2015: White Magic

White Magic (Discogs ID 400533) is a psychedelic folk rock project led by Mira Billotte, formed in Brooklyn. Discogs describes it as invoking “both traditional and experimental folk,” ranging from “loud psychedelia to meditative trance.” Mira performs under the White Magic name both with accompaniment and solo, using a daf, shruti box, and a cappella singing. All studio albums on Drag City except the final release (Leaving Records, 2015).

Core duo of Mira Billotte and Douglas Shaw (ID 707838). Shaw contributes guitar, vocals, organ, melodica, vibraslap, handlettering, and artwork across multiple releases.

Visual Art

Beyond music, Mira is credited as a visual artist on her own releases. She painted the cover of Dat Rosa Mel Apibus (2006) and is credited for “Performer, Artwork” on I’m Hiding My Nightingale (2015). The Quix-o-tic cover photos across all three releases were handled by her mother Barbara Billotte (ID 4490133).
