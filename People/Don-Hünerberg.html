Don Hünerberg

American recording engineer, born and based in New York City. Discogs ID: 480296. Also credited as Don Hunerberg, Donald Hünerberg, Don Hunerburg, and variants.

Career arc per his Discogs profile: staff engineer at Plaza Sound Studios (September 1976 – June 1980); chief engineer at Radio City Music Hall Studio (1980–1989); freelance producer/engineer from 1989 onward.

Overview

Hünerberg is a key but largely uncelebrated figure in the New York underground and avant-garde scenes of the late 1970s through the 1990s. His mid-career decade at Radio City Music Hall Studio (which he ran as chief engineer from 1980 to 1989) placed him at the intersection of no wave, downtown jazz, post-punk, and experimental composition. He engineered the debut Sonic Youth EP, three 99 Records releases (ESG, Liquid Liquid), the original Golden Palominos album, John Zorn’s Spillane, The Big Gundown, and Cobra, Alvin Lucier’s Lovely Music LP, and several Rhys Chatham pieces — a cross-section that maps the entire CBGB/loft-jazz/99 Records downtown New York ecosystem of the era.

His earliest documented work as a session musician appears on Phase Murmur 7-11-73 (1974), a self-released collaboration with Peter Gillespie, on which he performed flute, organ, bass, and balloon (listed as “Performer [Balloon]”), and wrote the liner notes and illustrated the sleeve. This suggests he was active in the New York experimental milieu before his engineering career started in earnest.

Career Phases

Mid-1970s: Plaza Sound Studios and CBGB-era work

As a staff engineer at Plaza Sound Studios, Hünerberg worked as assistant engineer on several landmark punk and new wave debut albums:

  • Ramones, Ramones (Sire, 1976) — assistant engineer under Rob Freeman; produced by Craig Leon
  • Richard Hell & The Voidoids, Blank Generation (Sire, 1977) — engineer (with Rob Freeman) on the majority of tracks; produced by Richard Gottehrer and Richard Hell
  • Blondie, Blondie (Chrysalis, 1977) — assistant engineer under Rob Freeman

These were among the defining documents of the nascent CBGB scene. His role was assistant or co-engineer throughout, working alongside senior engineers Rob Freeman and Jerry Solomon and with producers Craig Leon and Richard Gottehrer.

1980–1989: Chief Engineer, Radio City Music Hall Studio

This is the central chapter of his career. Taking over as chief engineer at Radio City Music Hall Studio around 1980, Hünerberg became the go-to room for a wide range of downtown New York projects:

99 Records / No Wave

  • ESG, ESG Says Dance To The Beat Of Moody (99 Records, 1982) — engineer; produced by Ed Bahlman
  • Liquid Liquid, Optimo (99 Records, 1983) — tape engineer; produced by Ed Bahlman
  • ESG, Come Away With ESG (99 Records, 1983) — engineer; produced by Ed Bahlman
  • Liquid Liquid, Dig We Must (99 Records, 1985) — engineer; mixed and remixed by Ed Bahlman

Sonic Youth and the Neutral Records orbit

  • Sonic Youth, Sonic Youth (Neutral Records, 1982) — engineer on the band’s debut EP; the band self-produced
  • Souls of Chaos (Neutral Records, 1983) — engineer

The Golden Palominos (Celluloid Records)

  • The Golden Palominos, The Golden Palominos (Celluloid, 1983) — engineer (with Martin Bisi); produced by Anton Fier and Bill Laswell; co-produced by Arto Lindsay and Martin Bisi; Jean Karakos executive-produced
  • The Golden Palominos, Visions Of Excess (Celluloid, 1985) — primary engineer (“Engineered By”); produced by Anton Fier

John Zorn projects

  • John Zorn / Michihiro Satoh, Ganryu Island (Yukon Records, 1985) — recording engineer; produced by Zorn
  • John Zorn, The Big Gundown (Elektra Nonesuch, 1986) — recording engineer; mixing by Robert Musso; Arto Lindsay and others performed
  • John Zorn, Spillane (Elektra Nonesuch, 1987) — recorded and mixed tracks 1–3; produced by Zorn; mastered by Bob Ludwig
  • John Zorn, Cobra (hat ART, 1987, recorded live in Geneva) — recorded the first two discs (A1–B6 sides); Bill Frisell, Arto Lindsay, Wayne Horvitz, Zeena Parkins, Elliott Sharp, Jim Staley, Christian Marclay among the performers

Celluloid Records / Avant-garde

  • JG Thirlwell (Foetus), Blow Your Top (Animal Records, 1982) — engineer
  • John Lurie / Lounge Lizards, Joyeux Noël (Nato, 1987) — recorded by
  • Lydia Lunch, The Uncensored Lydia Lunch (note: see also Strange Meeting, Antilles New Directions, 1987) — engineer; release featured Thurston Moore
  • The Golden Palominos, Blast Of Silence (Axed My Baby For A Nickel) (Celluloid, 1986) — additional recording at Radio City Music Hall Studio
  • The Golden Palominos, Visions Of Excess — see above
  • The Animal Speaks (Celluloid, 1986) — engineer
  • The Golden Palominos, Scenes From A Mirage (Review Records, 1987) — engineer, Radio City Music Hall; also mixed

Alvin Lucier / Lovely Music

  • Alvin Lucier, Still And Moving Lines Of Silence In Families Of Hyperbolas, Part 2 (Lovely Music, Ltd., 1983) — engineer; mastered by Howie Weinberg

Rhys Chatham

  • Credited on the Essaïde session (side A) as recording engineer, per the Rhys Chatham vault page.

Other downtown/experimental

  • Astor Piazzolla, The Rough Dancer And The Cyclical Night (Tango Apasionado) (American Clavé, 1988) — recorded at Radio City Studio
  • Anthony Davis, Trespass (Lumina Records, 1986) — engineer
  • Elliott Sharp, Semantics (Review Records, 1986) — engineer
  • Elliott Sharp, Bone Of Contention (SST Records, 1987) — engineer
  • Sonny Sharrock, Mumbo Jumbo (Rift, 1987) — engineer
  • Live Skull (Massive Records, 1984) — recorded by
  • Antarctica: New Music From Antarctica, Volume 1 (Antarctica Records, 1982) — engineer on track A3; produced by Peter Gordon

Mainstream and outside work

Radio City’s studio also brought in commercial and Broadway work:

  • Kiss (Ace Frehley solo album) (Casablanca, 1978) — engineer
  • The Mystery Of Edwin Drood (Original Broadway Cast Recording, Polydor, 1986) — recorded and mixed
  • Various Polydor sessions (Stay, Open Up, Gambled On Your Love)

1989–Present: Freelance

After leaving Radio City Music Hall Studio, Hünerberg continued as a freelance engineer:

  • Astor Piazzolla, The Late Masterpieces: The Complete Work On American Clavé (American Clavé, 1993) — engineer
  • John Zorn, The Classic Guide To Strategy – Volumes One & Two (Tzadik, 1996) — recorded by
  • John Zorn, The Bribe: Variations And Extensions On Spillane (Tzadik, 1998) — recorded by
  • John Zorn, Godard / Spillane (Tzadik, 1999) — mixed by; sampler; recorded by
  • Glenn Branca, Lesson No. 1 (Acute Records, 2004, reissue) — recording and mixing engineer on track 3 (“Bad Smells”); Ed Bahlman credited as assistant producer
  • Rhys Chatham, Interference (The Social Registry, 2010) — recorded by

Personal Projects

  • Intense Molecular Activity — New York experimental group; Hünerberg is listed as a member. The group self-released I.M.A. (1980), on which he played Votone Selector (electronics).
  • Phase Murmur 7-11-73 (Not On Label, 1974) — co-released with Peter Gillespie; Hünerberg played flute, organ, bass, and balloon; also wrote liner notes and drew illustrations.
