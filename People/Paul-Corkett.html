Biography

Paul Corkett is an Australian mixing engineer, recording engineer, and producer who built his career primarily in the UK. He began working as a studio assistant in 1985, appearing on sessions at Jacobs Studio (UK) and elsewhere in secondary roles — assistant engineer on ABC’s How To Be A Zillionaire! (Mercury, 1985), Strange Times by The Chameleons (Geffen, 1986), and a Twelfth Night album for Charisma/Virgin that same year.

By the late 1980s Corkett had graduated to lead engineer and mixing roles, working with German new wave acts (credited on multiple pressings of releases on Metronome) and expanding into UK indie production in the early 1990s. His credit on Björk’s debut Debut (Elektra, 1993) — credited as engineer alongside Nellee Hooper — places him at the centre of one of that era’s most significant records. That same year he engineered Julian Cope’s Jehovahkill (Island Records, 1992), a double album widely considered among Cope’s most ambitious works.

The mid-1990s saw Corkett’s relationship with The Cure begin: he mixed the single “Mint Car” and contributed mixing to the multi-producer Wild Mood Swings (Fiction Records/Elektra, 1996). He then became the primary producer and engineer for Bloodflowers (Fiction Records/Elektra, 2000), co-producing and mixing the album with Robert Smith — and has remained The Cure’s closest production collaborator ever since, working as FOH sound engineer on the 2018 Hyde Park anniversary concert, producing and mixing the live records from that event, and co-producing and mixing Songs Of A Lost World (Fiction Records/Polydor, 2024), The Cure’s first studio album in sixteen years. This is by far his longest and deepest single-artist relationship in the Discogs data: 19 shared masters.

Alongside The Cure work, Corkett became the central producer for Strangelove — a Bristol band on Food Records in the mid-to-late 1990s — producing and engineering their albums Time For The Rest Of Your Life (1994), Love And Other Demons (1996), and several singles. He also produced Placebo’s Black Market Music (Hut/Virgin, 2000) and contributed additional engineering to their earlier Without You I’m Nothing (Hut/Virgin, 1998). The Placebo connection runs to 7 shared masters. For The Cooper Temple Clause he produced and mixed See This Through And Leave (Morning Records, 2002) and the earlier Let’s Kill Music single/EP (2001), along with the Hardware EP (additional producer/mix) — his involvement ended after the debut; the second and third TCTC albums were produced by Dan Austin and Chris Hughes respectively.

He engineered Catherine Wheel’s albums Happy Days (Fontana, 1995) and Like Cats And Dogs (Mercury, 1996), and was part of recording sessions for Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds — credited on The Boatman’s Call (Mute, 1997) and The Best Of Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds (Mute/Reprise, 1998).

Into the 2000s and 2010s, Corkett diversified across indie rock, post-hardcore, and roots acts: producer/engineer on Biffy Clyro’s Blackened Sky (Beggars Banquet, 2002); producer of Fiction Plane’s Left Side Of The Brain (Bieler Bros., 2007); ongoing mixing work for Danish jazz and pop acts (Kira Skov, Stunt Records). Recent credits include producing The Cure’s Songs Of A Lost World (2024) and its associated live releases, as well as engineering and mixing A Fragile Thing (Polydor, 2024).

His Discogs profile names four primary clients: Nick Cave [a31844], Björk [a1373], Placebo [a9436], and The Cure [a28972] — confirming the profile’s own framing of his career.
