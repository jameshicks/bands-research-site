Burkhard Hennen

German record label founder, producer, and publisher. Born 1946. Discogs artist ID: 361202. Founder and artistic director of Moers Music (and its predecessor Ring Records), the principal German independent label for free jazz and avant-garde improvisation, based in Moers, Germany. Ran the label from its founding in 1974 until 2005. Also the proprietor of Burkhard Hennen Publishing (Discogs label ID: 301112), the publishing arm behind Moers Music releases.

Overview

Burkhard Hennen built one of the most significant European platforms for free jazz and experimental music through his label work in Moers. Starting in 1974 with Ring Records — directly tied to the annual Moers Festival — he documented live performances and studio sessions by a remarkable cross-section of American and European avant-garde musicians. When a naming conflict with a Canadian label forced a rename in 1977, the imprint became Moers Music, under which it continued for nearly three more decades.

Hennen operated primarily as a producer-presenter: he initiated and financed recordings (often live-at-the-festival), handled production logistics, and gave extraordinary latitude to the artists. His roster ranged from the AACM wing of American free jazz (Anthony Braxton, World Saxophone Quartet) through the post-Ornette New York scene (Ronald Shannon Jackson, Music Revelation Ensemble) to European improvisers (Vienna Art Orchestra, Rhys Chatham, Curlew) and beyond. The label code for both Ring Records and Moers Music is LC 6059 / LC 06059, reflecting their shared identity.

Ring Records (1974–1977)

Ring Records was founded in 1974 by Hennen in Moers, Germany, address 4130 Moers, in direct association with the Moers Festival. The earliest releases were live documents from the 1976 festival edition, establishing the label’s core format: full concert captures in relatively unadorned production.

Moers Music (1977–c. 2005)

Under the Moers Music banner, Hennen expanded scope while keeping the festival-documentation model alive. Catalog numbers ran from the 01xxx series (vinyl era, continuing from Ring Records) into the 02xxx series (which accommodated CDs from the mid-1980s onward). He served as producer on the vast majority of releases through the mid-1980s, then shifted toward Executive Producer credits as the label matured.

Late 1970s — American Free Jazz

The first Moers Music releases in 1977 immediately established the transatlantic ambition. World Saxophone Quartet’s Point Of No Return (momu 01034, 1977) — featuring Julius Hemphill, Oliver Lake, Hamiet Bluiett, and David Murray — was an early WSQ recording and remains a landmark of the saxophone-quartet form. Anthony Braxton’s Seven Compositions 1978 (momu 01066, released 1980) documented a trio with John Lindberg (bass) and Thurman Barker (drums, marimba).

John Carter’s work appeared in two releases: A Suite Of Early American Folk Pieces For Solo-Clarinet (momu 02014, 1979; Hennen produced and mixed) and Variations with the John Carter Quintet (momu 01056, 1979). Charles Bobo Shaw and the Human Arts Ensemble contributed Streets Of St. Louis (momu 02020, 1978). Sunny Murray Trio appeared on Live At Moers Festival (Momu 01054, 1979).

1980–1981 — New York Post-Ornette Wave

Hennen made a decisive push into the New York electric free jazz scene in these years.

Music Revelation Ensemble – No Wave (momu 01072, 1980) The James Blood Ulmer-led group with Ronald Shannon Jackson on drums, David Murray on tenor, and Amin Ali on electric bass. Recorded by Hans Schlosser. Hennen produced.

Ronald Shannon Jackson And The Decoding Society – Nasty (momu 01086, 1981) Personnel: RSJ (drums), Vernon Reid (guitar), Melvin Gibbs (bass), Reverend Bruce Johnson (bass), Byard Lancaster (alto/baritone sax, piccolo flute), Charles Brackeen (soprano/tenor sax), Lee Rozie (soprano/tenor sax), Khan Jamal (vibraphone). Produced by Hennen. One of the earliest RSJ Decoding Society documents.

Ronald Shannon Jackson And The Decoding Society – Street Priest (momu 01096, 1981) A second RSJ album the same year, same core band (Reid, Gibbs, Johnson) plus Zane Massey and Lee Rozie on saxophones. Produced by Hennen.

Rova Saxophone Quartet – This, This, This, This. (momu 01080, 1980) West Coast free jazz ensemble; Hennen as Executive Producer.

1982–1983 — Broadening Scope

Odean Pope – Almost Like Me (momu 01092, 1982). Produced by Hennen.

Tavil-Nadasvaram Group – Music From South-India (momu 01090, 1982). South Indian classical ensemble; Hennen as producer — an early signal of the label’s non-jazz reach.

Rhys Chatham – Factor X (momu 02008, 1983) The major Downtown NYC crossover moment for Moers Music. Chatham’s large-ensemble work: Side A an orchestral piece with George Lewis and Jim Staley (trombones), Olu Dara and others (trumpets), Anton Fier (percussion), Bob Stewart (tuba); Side B his guitar-ensemble pieces with Karen Haglof, Craig Kafton, Susan Springfield, Tim Schellenbaum (guitars) and James Lo (drums). Hennen co-produced and co-mixed with Chatham.

Mathias Rüegg / Vienna Art Choir – From No Art To Mo-(Z)-Art (momu 02002, 1983) First Vienna connection on the label. Wolfgang Puschnig (reeds), George Lewis (trombone), Lauren Newton (vocals).

Vocal Summit – Sorrow Is Not Forever – Love Is (momu 2004, 1983) Bobby McFerrin, Jay Clayton, Jeanne Lee, Lauren Newton, Urszula Dudziak. Produced by Hennen.

1984–1988 — Maturity and the CD Era

David Moss – Full House (momu 2010, 1984) Solo percussion/voice record. Moss produced himself; guests include Arto Lindsay (vocals/guitar) and Christian Marclay (turntables). Hennen as label Executive Producer. Engineered and mixed by Martin Bisi at BC Studio.

David Moss – Dense Band (MoMu 02040, 1985) Follow-up with full ensemble. Hennen as Executive Producer.

Curlew – North America (Moers Music 02042, 1985) George Cartwright (sax), Fred Frith (bass), Tom Cora (cello), Mark Howell (guitar), with Rick Brown and Pippin Barnett sharing drum duties. Produced by Frith and Martin Bisi; Hennen as label.

Vienna Art Orchestra – Swiss Swing (Moers Music 02060, 1986) and Inside Out – Live ‘87 (Moers Music 02062/63, 1988), Two Little Animals (02066, 1988). Hennen as label for the VAO’s Moers output; Vienna Art Orchestra became a sustained presence on the label through the late 1980s.

Phalanx – Got Something Good For You (Moers Music 02046, 1986). Produced by Hennen.

Reichlich Weiblich – Live At Moers Festival ‘87 (Moers Music 02064, 1987). Produced by Hennen.

Sublabel: Baff

Moers Music had one sublabel, Baff (Discogs ID: 80918).

Production Style and Aesthetic

Hennen was not an interventionist producer in the studio-craft sense. His role was to initiate sessions, fund them, and give artists space. Most Moers Music records were recorded live at the festival or in straightforward studio sessions — the label’s value was curatorial and logistical, not sonic. The consistent use of Jürgen Pankarz for cover design across many 1980s releases gave the catalog a visual coherence.

Hans Schlosser appears repeatedly as recording engineer on the early Ring Records and Moers Music sessions, establishing a house-sound baseline. Later releases brought in engineers specific to the artists’ own networks (Martin Bisi for the NYC-Downtown sessions; Thomas Kern and Don Hünerberg for the Chatham sessions).
