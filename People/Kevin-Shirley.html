Jun 08, 202610 min read

Kevin Shirley

Music producer, recording engineer, and mixer. Born 29 June 1960 in Johannesburg, South Africa. Currently based in California, US. Discogs artist ID: 254419

Known professionally as “The Caveman” (alias: The Caveman (4) and The Slasher on Discogs). Owner and operator of The Cave Studios in Malibu, California. Member of The Barnestormers (recorded 2023).

Biography

1982–1989 — South Africa: Apprenticeship

Shirley began his career in Johannesburg as an assistant engineer and producer in the early 1980s, working on South African recordings across a range of genres. His earliest Discogs credit is as assistant engineer and assistant producer on Unravished Brides (1982, Warner Bros. Records). Through the mid-1980s he accumulated credits on Mountain Records (2) releases — recording, editing, and engineering — including jazz, folk, and rock titles out of South Africa. Notable early work includes Juluka Live (The Good Hope Concerts) (1986), a live document of Juluka, the pioneering South African multiracial band. By 1986–1988 he had moved into full producer/engineer roles on South African and Australian records.

1990–1994 — Australia: Breaking Through

Shirley relocated to Australia and became a sought-after producer for the country’s rock scene. He produced Baby Animals’ self-titled debut (1991, Imago / Epic) — engineered and mixed, the album broke the band internationally. He worked across multiple Australian acts on Mushroom, Chrysalis, EMI, and CBS imprints. His production and mix work during this period is voluminous: more than two dozen releases across Australia, Germany, and the UK.

1995–1998 — International Breakthrough: Silverchair, Aerosmith, Journey

The mid-1990s brought Shirley into the first rank of mainstream rock production.

Silverchair — Frogstomp (1995, Murmur/Epic): Shirley produced, recorded, and mixed the debut album of the teenage Australian trio. The record — tracked fast and raw — became a global hit on the strength of “Tomorrow” and “Pure Massacre.” He continued with the band through the Mint 400 EP (1995) and Neon Ballroom (1999, mixed by Shirley).

Aerosmith — Nine Lives (1997, Columbia): Shirley co-produced with Aerosmith (additional producer credit), recording and mixing the album. It reached #1 in the US and spawned “Falling Into Infinity” and the Grammy-winning “I Don’t Want to Miss a Thing.” The Aerosmith relationship continued: Falling Into Infinity (Dream Theater, 1997, EastWest) — Shirley engineered, mixed, and co-produced — and further work on the Nine Lives promo singles and compilation packages.

Journey — Trial By Fire (1996, Columbia/Sony): Shirley produced, recorded, and mixed the band’s reunion album with Steve Perry.

Dream Theater — Falling Into Infinity (1997, EastWest): Produced, engineered, and mixed. A disputed production where the label pushed for a more commercial sound; credited as full producer alongside the band.

1998–2002 — Rocket Scientists and Rock Workhorses

Shirley remained prolific in the hard rock/metal space. Key credits:

  • Black Crowes — By Your Side (1998, Columbia/American Recordings): Full producer.
  • Rocket From The Crypt — RFTC (1998, Interscope): Produced, recorded, mixed.
  • Liquid Tension Experiment (1998 and 1999, Magna Carta): Mixed both self-titled albums (these were instrumental prog records featuring members of Dream Theater).
  • Dream Theater — Metropolis Pt. 2: Scenes From A Memory (1999, EastWest); Live Scenes From New York (2001); Six Degrees Of Inner Turbulence (2002, Elektra); Train Of Thought (2003, Elektra): Ongoing mixing relationship; the band’s production notes acknowledge a creative tension with Shirley that nonetheless produced some of their most acclaimed records.
  • HIM — Deep Shadows And Brilliant Highlights (2001, RCA/Terrier): Produced and engineered.

2000–2006 — Iron Maiden and the Classic Rock Establishment

The most significant long-term relationship of this era was with Iron Maiden.

Iron Maiden — Brave New World (2000, EMI): Shirley engineered and mixed; co-produced with Steve Harris. This marked the reunion of Bruce Dickinson and Adrian Smith with the band. The Maiden partnership extended through: Rock In Rio (live, 2002), Dance Of Death (2003), Death On The Road (live, 2005), A Matter Of Life And Death (2006), and further into the 2010s (see below). For A Matter Of Life And Death Shirley also contributed liner notes — unusual for a producer.

Led Zeppelin — How The West Was Won (2003, Atlantic): Shirley engineered and mixed this three-disc live set drawn from 1972 concert recordings. The original recordings were by Eddie Kramer; Shirley was brought in by Jimmy Page to restore and mix them. He also mixed The Song Remains The Same soundtrack (2007 reissue, Swan Song) and No Quarter: Jimmy Page & Robert Plant Unledded (surround and stereo mixes, Atlantic).

Aerosmith continued: Additional production and remix work on compilations through the mid-2000s (O, Yeah! Ultimate Aerosmith Hits, 2002; Devil’s Got A New Disguise, 2006).

2006–2013 — Joe Bonamassa and the Blues-Rock Empire

Beginning with Sloe Gin in 2007, Shirley became the house producer for Joe Bonamassa, a relationship that has yielded over 20 studio and live albums and has continued without break to the present day.

Key releases in the Bonamassa run:

  • Sloe Gin (2007, Provogue) — first collaboration
  • The Ballad of John Henry (2009, Provogue)
  • Black Rock (2010, Provogue)
  • Live From The Royal Albert Hall (2009, Provogue)
  • Don’t Explain (2011, Provogue) — with Beth Hart
  • Dust Bowl (2011, Provogue)
  • Seesaw (2013, Provogue) — with Beth Hart
  • Tour De Force – Live In London (multiple volumes, 2013–2014)
  • An Acoustic Evening At The Vienna Opera House (2013, Provogue)

In parallel, Shirley formed Black Country Communion (BCC) with Bonamassa, Glenn Hughes, Jason Bonham, and Derek Sherinian. He produced and mixed Black Country Communion (2010, J&R Adventures), 2 (2011, Mascot), Afterglow (2012, Mascot), and BCCIV (2017, J&R Adventures). All BCC records were tracked at The Cave.

Iron Maiden work continued through: A Matter Of Life And Death (2006), Flight 666 (2009 soundtrack), The Final Frontier (2010, EMI), En Vivo! (2012 live, EMI), Maiden England ‘88 (2013 reissue — Shirley compiled, edited, and produced new mixes), The Book Of Souls (2015, Parlophone), and Senjutsu (2021, Parlophone).

2014–Present — Sustained Output

Shirley has maintained an exceptionally high release cadence across blues, hard rock, and heavy metal:

  • Bonamassa: Different Shades Of Blue (2014), Blues Of Desperation (2016), Redemption (2018), Royal Tea (2020), Time Clocks (2021), Live At The Hollywood Bowl (2024), V (2024), Breakthrough (2025)
  • Beth Hart: Ooh Yea “The Betty Davis Songbook” (2015), Live At Radio City Music Hall (2015), British Blues Explosion Live (2018, also Music Director), A New Day Now (2020), Walking To New Orleans (2019), Live At Carnegie Hall (2017)
  • Iron Maiden: The Book Of Souls (2015), Speed Of Light single (2015), Empire Of The Clouds single (2016), Senjutsu (2021), The Writing On The Wall single (2021), Stratego single (2021)
  • Cold Chisel (Australia): The Best Of Cold Chisel All For You (2011), Live Tapes Vol. 2 (2014), Armistice Day: Live At The Domain, Sydney (2018), Blood Moon (2019)
  • Jimmy Barnes (Australia): multiple solo releases including My Criminal Record (2019), Flesh And Blood (2021), Blue Christmas (2022), I Only Trust Rock N Roll (2025)
  • The Barnestormers — band Shirley himself performs in, 2023 self-titled album (Bloodlines)

On Royal Tea (2020) Shirley is credited as playing acoustic guitar and percussion in addition to producing. On BCCIV (2017) he plays additional percussion. These performance credits recur across several late-career productions.

Open Questions

  • Exact year of emigration from South Africa to Australia, and then to the US, is not documented in the Discogs DB. The transition appears to be: South Africa 1982–late 1980s → Australia c.1990–mid-1990s → US (California) mid-1990s onward, but this needs web confirmation.
  • The Discogs profile references two label IDs (l1144662 and l272994) as studios Shirley owns in addition to The Cave; the specific names are not resolved in the DB profile text. Web check would clarify.
  • The nature of the dispute over Falling Into Infinity (Dream Theater) — reportedly the label overrode band/producer preferences — is not documented in the DB. Widely reported in DT fan literature but would need web/interview sourcing.
  • Whether Shirley has any formal production credits on Led Zeppelin original studio albums vs. purely archival/live remixes — the DB shows only the archive remix work; he has not produced any original LZ studio material.
