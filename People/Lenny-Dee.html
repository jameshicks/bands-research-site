Biography

Early Career & House Music Roots (mid-1980s – 1990)

Leonardo Didesiderio — known as Lenny Dee — emerged from the Brooklyn, New York DJ scene in the mid-1980s. His earliest documented credit is a scratches contribution on “Knights Of The Turntables” (25 West Records, 1985). By 1987–88 he was producing and writing for Fourth Floor Records, a key early New York house imprint, with credits on tracks like “The Morning After” and “Bamboo (I Can’t Quit).”

His partnership with Frankie Bones defined this early period. The two co-produced and co-wrote the Looney Tunes series on Nu Groove Records, starting with Looney Tunes - Volume One (1989, with additional engineering by Victor Simonelli under the alias Vicsimone, Tommy Musto, and Nicolai Vorkapich). The series continued with Looney Tunes II (1990) and became a touchstone of the New York underground house sound.

During 1989–91, Dee’s credits spread widely: productions and mixes for Nu Groove, Fourth Floor, and a notable production role on the UK-released “Just As Long As I Got You” (XL Recordings, 1989). He contributed breaks and samples to KLF’s “What Time Is Love? (Live At Trancentral)” (1990) and worked on The Shamen’s En-Tact (One Little Indian, 1991) as mix/edit contributor.

Founding Industrial Strength Records & Pivot to Hardcore (1991–1993)

In 1991, Lenny Dee co-founded Industrial Strength Records at 69 Havemeyer Street, Brooklyn — a defining moment for New York hardcore techno. The label’s stated mission was to put NYC on the map for the emerging hardcore sound. Its first significant release was the Planet Core Productions Special Double AA Side (1991), for which Dee served as US executive producer alongside co-exec Jennifer Williams (3), bringing German hardcore artists Mescalinum United and The Mover to a US label.

The label’s initial sampler series launched in 1992: Industrial Strength Sampler (English Muffin / Crowd Control), Industrial Strength Sampler Vol. II (DX-13 / Disintegrator), and quickly established a dense roster. Dee wore multiple hats — executive producer across nearly every early ISR release, and hands-on producer/engineer on many. His collaboration with Glitch on the Trauma EP (1993) is representative: Dee co-produced, programmed, and co-wrote alongside Glitch’s members.

Trax From The Darkside (Industrial Strength Records, 1993) was Dee’s landmark compilation, compiled and digitally edited by him, establishing ISR’s aesthetic: brutal, compressed hardcore with New York attitude. The same year he produced and presented Mokum Traxxx (Mokum Records, Netherlands), showing his European reach early in the hardcore era.

ISR Peak Years & International Reach (1993–1998)

Through 1994–96, Industrial Strength Records and its sublabels (IST Records, Bastard Loud Records, Ruff Beats Records, LD Records (3), Industrial Strength Limited) defined the US hardcore techno sound. Dee remained executive producer across dozens of releases on these imprints covering the entire scene: DJ Skinhead, English Muffin, Disintegrator, Industrial Terror Squad, Ralphie Dee, Caustic Visions, Glitch, and many more.

His own releases in this period include:

  • Round One / Round 2 (Rotterdam Records, 1994/1996) — Dutch gabber market releases written and produced by Dee
  • Spontaneous Combustion (Ruff Beats Records, 1994) and A Weekend In Bklyn Part I (Ruff Beats Records, 1995) — Brooklyn hardcore records with Jennifer Williams (3) on vocals
  • Emotional Response (LD Records, 1996) — his own imprint debut under his name
  • Funky Twisted (Industrial Strength Limited, 1996) — showcasing a persistent funk undercurrent in his hardcore

He also contributed a remix to Earache metal act Napalm Death’s I, Destructor (The Lenny Dee Mixes) (1994), and the Industrial Strength compilation (Earache/ISR, 1995) served as a label overview released through the UK metal imprint, giving ISR significant mainstream exposure.

The Industrial F**kin Strength 2 - Unleash The Brutality (The Music Cartel/ISR, 1998) found Dee himself on DJ mix duties for the second volume of the label’s flagship compilation series.

European connections deepened throughout: appearances at Mokum Records in the Netherlands, Jurassick Terror E.P. (Terror Traxx, Netherlands, 1994), a Swiss tarot club mix (1994/95), collaborations with French hardcore figures Manu Le Malin and Radium, and later with Dutch artists Paul Elstak and the Rotterdam Records camp.

Later Periods (1999–present)

Industrial Strength Records continued through the 2000s–2020s with Dee as figurehead and executive producer. The label expanded from vinyl to digital while maintaining its Brooklyn-based hardcore identity. Key milestones include the IFS numbered compilation series (IFS3 through IFS6, 2001–2009), the Calibrate Volume One (2010), and the 20 Years / 30 Years retrospective releases (2012, 2023).

Dee maintained his DJ profile through mix compilations (Rez VI, UK’s Rezerection, 1996; Murder One, 1996; IFS5 Cold Fusion, 2008) and collaborated with the European hardcore underground: the Grandmaster Dee & The Furious Fish release with Hellfish (Deathchant, 2014), work with Tieum and French acts like Radium on Headbanger Boogie (Psychik Genocide, 2004).

A late-period hook-up with Italian label Randy 909% Records produced vocal appearances on Boomshakalaka (2007) and Brrr Stick’em (2009) with The Sickest Squad.

Releases continued into the late 2010s and 2020s on ISR: Party Animal (2017), Call The Man (2019), Liberate The Aggression (2021).
