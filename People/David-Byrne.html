David Byrne

Singer, guitarist, composer, filmmaker, visual artist. Born 14 May 1952, Dumbarton, Scotland; raised in Hamilton, Ontario and Baltimore, Maryland. New York City from the early 1970s. Discogs artist ID: 3918. Alias: Absalm el Habib.

Overview

Co-founder and frontman of Talking Heads (with Chris Frantz, Tina Weymouth, Jerry Harrison). Talking Heads were active 1975–1991, one of the central bands bridging the CBGB scene, New Wave, funk, and African music. Byrne’s parallel activities in the NYC downtown experimental scene situate him as much in the avant-garde as in rock — he was crediting alongside Arthur Russell, Rhys Chatham, and Peter Gordon long before Talking Heads broke mainstream.

After Talking Heads he maintained an unusually varied solo career: world music (Latin dance on Rei Momo, 1989), film scores (The Catherine Wheel, 1981; Young Adam, 2003), Broadway (Here Lies Love, 2010; American Utopia, 2019), continued Eno collaboration (Everything That Happens Will Happen Today, 2008; American Utopia, 2018), and a joint album with St. Vincent (Love This Giant, 2012). He also founded the world-music label Luaka Bop in 1988.

Early NYC / Downtown Scene (1975–1981)

Byrne arrived in New York in the early 1970s and fell into the CBGB / downtown experimental orbit. His non-Talking Heads credits from this period are among the most revealing in the Discogs DB:

  • Arthur Russell / Dinosaur — “Kiss Me Again” (Sire, 1978, release ID 95382): Byrne on guitar. Co-produced by Nicky Siano. The record sits at the intersection of disco and avant-garde that Russell inhabited.
  • The Flying Hearts — a fluid ensemble also including Russell, Peter Gordon, Rhys Chatham, and Jerry Harrison. Performed and recorded Instrumentals 1975–1979 (released Les Disques du Crépuscule, 1984).
  • Peter Gordon / Love of Life Orchestra — Extended Niceties (Infidelity, 1980, release ID 863400): Byrne on guitar alongside Arto Lindsay and Ernie Brooks.

These credits place Byrne inside the same network as the Love of Life Orchestra and Kitchen Center circles, not merely adjacent to them.

Talking Heads (1975–1991)

See Talking Heads for the full band article. Key facts relevant to Byrne’s role:

  • Primary songwriter throughout; all lyrics on Talking Heads: 77 (1977), Fear of Music (1979), Speaking in Tongues (1983), and Little Creatures (1985) are solely his.
  • Remain in Light (1980) is credited collectively (“All songs written by Talking Heads”) with Eno contributing to structures, but Byrne held the vocal arrangements credit.
  • Directed the 1986 film True Stories (also the band’s album of the same year), the 1989 documentary Ilé Aiyé (The House of Life), and co-directed the 1988 live film Storytelling Giant.
  • Stop Making Sense (1984): directed by Jonathan Demme; Byrne is credited for the stage concept (“Conceived For Stage By”). Package design by Byrne.
  • Designed cover art for multiple albums; ongoing visual practice alongside music.

Film / Theater Scores (Solo, 1981–2003)

Songs From “The Catherine Wheel” (Sire, 1981) — release ID 236409

Score for Twyla Tharp’s Broadway dance production. Byrne sole primary artist; sole producer. Guest performers: Brian Eno and Adrian Belew (both “Featuring”). Mastered by Greg Calbi.

The Complete Score From The Broadway Production Of “The Catherine Wheel” (Sire, 1981) — release ID 1401518

Expanded two-LP version. Byrne plays bass, guitar, synthesizer (Prophet), and sings; co-writes with Eno (A3, A5) and John Chernoff (A4, B2, B3, B5). Percussion: John Chernoff. Drums: Yogi Horton.

Music For The Knee Plays (Zonophone, 1985) — release ID 655205

Score for Robert Wilson’s theater work. Byrne as composer, producer, mixer, and voice. Brass-heavy ensemble: Los Angeles session players including Fred Wesley (trombone), Don Myrick, Ernie Watts, Pete Christlieb, Jackie Kelso (saxophones), Chuck Findley, Harry Kim (trumpets). Conductor: David Blumberg.

Lead Us Not Into Temptation (Music From “Young Adam”) (Thrill Jockey, 2003) — release ID 1077172

Film score for director David Mackenzie. Byrne arranged strings and played guitar. Issued on Thrill Jockey.

My Life In The Bush Of Ghosts (Sire, 1981) — release ID 27566

Co-credited with Brian Eno. Grew directly out of the Remain in Light sessions. Both credited as multi-instrumentalists (guitar, bass, synthesizer, drums, percussion, found objects) and producers. Engineers: Dave Jerden (lead), Eddie Korvin, John Potoker, Neal Teeman, Stacy Baird, and five assistant engineers. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Typography: Peter Saville (2). The album incorporates found vocal samples (Arabic and evangelical radio broadcasts) over dense polyrhythmic backing — a foundational text for sampling practice.

Solo Career

Rei Momo (Luaka Bop / Sire / Warner Bros., 1989) — release ID 657251

First full solo album proper. A dense Latin dance record drawing on salsa, merengue, and cumbia. Co-produced with Steve Lillywhite; Jon Fausty mixed. Songwriting collaborators on individual tracks: Arto Lindsay (track 11), Johnny Pacheco (tracks 3, 6, 9), Willie Colón (track 5). First Luaka Bop release credited primarily to Byrne.

Uh-Oh (Luaka Bop / Sire / Warner Bros., 1992) — release ID 1002841

Second solo album. Produced by Nick Launay. Rhythm section: George Porter Jr. (bass), Oscar Salas (drums / percussion). Backing vocals: Dolette McDonald. Horns and strings arranged and conducted by Angel Fernandez. Design by M&Co. Mastered by Bob Ludwig. Cover painting by Brian Dewan.

David Byrne (Luaka Bop / Sire / Warner Bros., 1994) — release ID 1002851

Self-titled album. Co-produced with Arto Lindsay and Susan Rogers (Prince’s longtime engineer). Bass: Paul Socolow. Drums: Todd Turkisher. Guitar: Byrne throughout. Mastered by Bob Ludwig.

Feelings (Luaka Bop / Warner Bros., 1997) — release ID 431800

Co-produced with Morcheeba (tracks 1, 4–6, 8, 11) — brothers Paul and Ross Godfrey. Backing vocals: Skye Edwards (Morcheeba’s vocalist). Dobro and acoustic guitar by Byrne. Mixed by Mark Saunders and Marc Ramaer. Mastered by Ted Jensen.

Look Into The Eyeball (Luaka Bop / Virgin, 2001) — release ID 466969

Orchestral pop record. Strings, cello, viola, violin. Percussion: Mauro Refosco (bass: Paul Frazier, both recurring collaborators). Mixed and co-produced by Ken Lewis. Mastered by Greg Calbi.

Grown Backwards (Nonesuch, 2004) — release ID 370674

Moved to Nonesuch. Strings arranged by Stephen Barber. Recurring personnel: Patrick Dillett (producer / mixer / engineer, most tracks), Mauro Refosco (percussion), Paul Frazier (bass). Also includes theremin (Pamelia Kurstin) and harp (Elaine Barber). Byrne drew the illustrations in the packaging. Mastered by Greg Calbi.

Here Lies Love (Nonesuch / Todomundo, 2010) — release ID 2217810

Double album co-credited with Fatboy Slim (Norman Cook). Disco/house concept album about Imelda Marcos, later adapted as a Broadway musical (2014). Mixed by Patrick Dillett. Mastered by Greg Calbi. The Broadway production ran at the Public Theater, New York.

Everything That Happens Will Happen Today (Todomundo, 2008) — release ID 1438426

Second Eno / Byrne collaboration, 27 years after My Life In The Bush Of Ghosts. Both credited as producers and writers. Additional producer: Leo Abrahams. Mixed by Patrick Dillett. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Design: Sagmeister Inc.

Love This Giant (4AD, 2012) — release ID 3857635

Co-credited with St. Vincent (Annie Clark). Heavy brass arrangement throughout (Tony Finno, Kelly Pratt, Lenny Pickett). Co-producers: John Congleton, Patrick Dillett, Annie Clark, David Byrne. Mixed by Patrick Dillett. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Released on 4AD.

American Utopia (Nonesuch / Todomundo, 2018) — release ID 11660886

Solo album drawing on earlier Eno / Byrne collaboration material (Eno credited “Composed By” on A1–A4, B1–B4; Daniel Lopatin / Oneohtrix Point Never on A5, B5). Producers: Byrne, Patrick Dillett, Rodaidh McDonald. Engineers: Patrick Dillett, Alex Epton, various mix engineers (Marta Salogni, Jack Sugden, Tuck Nelson, David Wrench). Mastered by Greg Calbi. The record was followed by Spike Lee’s concert film David Byrne’s American Utopia (2020).

Luaka Bop Label

Byrne founded Luaka Bop in 1988. The label’s early catalog focused on world music compilations — particularly Brazilian (Beleza Tropical series, 1989; O Samba, 1989; Brazil Classics 3–4, 1991), Cuban (Cuba Classics series, 1991–1992), West African (Love’s A Real Thing, 2005), and Afro-Peruvian (Afro-Peruvian Classics: The Soul of Black Peru, 1995) music. Key artist discoveries: Tom Zé (whose catalog Byrne helped reissue), Susana Baca, Os Mutantes (whose catalog was compiled for Western release), and later Floating Points (Elaenia, 2015; Promises with Pharoah Sanders, 2021). Yale Evelev served as executive producer throughout. Byrne founded the label in 1988; Evelev joined in 1990 when Byrne hired him to run it, becoming president and co-owner — he did not co-found it. (Wikipedia: Yale Evelev, as of 2026-06-27) Label Discogs ID: 3264.
