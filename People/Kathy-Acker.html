Kathy Acker

Real name: Karen Lehmann. Born 18 April 1947, New York City. Died 30 November 1997. Discogs artist ID: 228197.

Overview

Kathy Acker was a foundational figure in American postmodern and punk literature — her work is characterised by collage, appropriation, explicit content, and radical politics. She is best known as a novelist (Blood and Guts in High School, Don Quixote, Empire of the Senseless), but her relationship with downtown New York’s avant-garde music world was sustained and genuine rather than peripheral. She was part of the founding lineup of Peter Gordon’s Love of Life Orchestra in 1977, appeared on Discogs-documented recordings from 1984 to 1999, and contributed text and voice to releases on TOPY, AKT Production (France), New Tone Records (Italy), CodeX (UK), Quarterstick Records, and Kill Rock Stars. Her second husband was Peter Gordon (though Discogs notes their partnership ran 1971–1977, with the Leningrad Xpress lyrics credit coming later in 1990). She moved through the same downtown NYC / No Wave circles as Lydia Lunch, Richard Hell, Julius Eastman, David Van Tieghem, and Peter Zummo.

Acker died of breast cancer on 30 November 1997 in Tijuana, Mexico. Her music recordings are a small but specific documentation of the poet/text-artist crossover into experimental music — she spoke her texts over noise, post-punk, and jazz-inflected avant-garde rather than writing conventional song lyrics.
