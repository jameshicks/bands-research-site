Larry Marks (3)

Real name: Lawrence Markowitz Discogs ID: 8313549 Alias: Rabbitt (2) (Discogs ID: 1685133) Name variations: L. Marks, Marks

Singer, songwriter, producer, and arranger based in California from 1966 onward. Best known for his work in the LHI Records orbit alongside Lee Hazlewood, and for singing the Scooby-Doo theme song. Died 21 October 2020.

Note: Do not confuse with Larry Marks (Discogs ID: 285134), a producer/songwriter/executive who worked for Mercury, Columbia, and other major labels; or with a harmonica player and former member of a separate act (Discogs ID: 8313558).

Biography

Mid-1960s: Solo Singles and Early California Work

Larry Marks (Lawrence Markowitz) entered the music business as a singer-songwriter on the California soul circuit. His 1967 debut single “Lookin’ Through The Eyes Of Love” (Pala, cat. 175) was produced by Greene & Stone and arranged by Harold Battiste — a New Orleans-connected arranger and A&R man who had worked with Sam Cooke and the early AFO collective. Marks co-wrote the A-side himself (credited as “L. Marks”). The B-side “Eye to Eye” was written by Jessie Hill and Mac Rebennack (Dr. John), confirming the New Orleans connection in his early material.

His 1968 single “Sandy” / “Don’t Slam the Door in My Face” (VMC, cat. V717) marks a significant association: it was produced by Barry White and arranged by Gene Page — both early in their careers, years before their string-drenched 1970s success. Marks co-wrote the B-side with Vernon Jones (5).

1969–1970: LHI Records and the Hazlewood Circle

By 1969 Marks had linked up with Lee Hazlewood at LHI Records, Hazlewood’s Los Angeles-based independent. Under his alias Rabbitt (2), he released the LP “Rabbitt 1” (Bell Records, 1969), produced by both Marks and Hazlewood, with arrangements by Marks. Recording engineers Eddie Brackett and Mickey Crofford — regulars in the LHI studio — were credited, along with recordist Steve Francisco.

The alias continued with the LHI single “Bubble Gum Train” (1970), credited to Rabbitt (2) and co-produced by Marks and Hazlewood.

In 1970, Marks was credited as arranger on the Joe Cannon (2) LP “Numbers” (LHI Records, Australia), alongside producers Donnie Owens and Joe Nixon.

1970: Hazlewood Album Productions

Marks served as associate producer (alongside Donnie Owens, Jack Robinson, and Shel Talmy) on Hazlewood’s “Cowboy In Sweden” (LHI Records, 1970) — a major Hazlewood LP recorded partly in Sweden with arrangers Craig Doerge, David Whitaker, Johnny Arthey, and Hazlewood himself. Other vocalists on the record include Nina Lizell and Suzi Jane Hokom.

Also in 1970, Marks took a full producer credit (shared with Hazlewood) on the Hazlewood LP “No Train To Stockholm” (LHI Records).

1972: Arranger on 13

Marks arranged the Hazlewood album “13” (Viking (3), 1972, Sweden), one of Hazlewood’s key Scandinavian-period records. Executive producer on that album was Suzanne Jennings.

Later: Scooby-Doo and Legacy

Outside his recorded discography, Marks is noted for singing the Scooby-Doo theme song. He died on 21 October 2020.

In 2021 his production work received renewed attention when Light In The Attic issued “The LHI Years: Singles, Nudes & Backsides (1968–71)” — a Hazlewood retrospective compiled by Hunter Lea and Matt Sullivan (4), on which Marks is credited as producer for at least one track (C3).

Connections

  • Lee Hazlewood — primary creative partner through the LHI years; co-producer on Rabbitt 1, associate/full producer on multiple Hazlewood albums
  • Barry White — produced Marks’ 1968 “Sandy” single before White’s own career took off; Gene Page arranged the same session, a pairing that would define White’s signature sound years later
  • Harold Battiste — arranged Marks’ 1967 debut; Battiste was a central figure in the New Orleans music world and had founded AFO Records
  • Greene & Stone — production duo behind Marks’ debut single; their company Greene-Stone Enterprises had a stake in the Pala/York-Pala label network
