Larry Grenadier

American jazz double bassist, born 6 February 1966 in San Francisco, California. Discogs artist ID: 289532. Brother of jazz trumpeter Phil Grenadier. One of the most sought-after acoustic bassists in contemporary jazz, best known as the anchor of the Brad Mehldau Trio from its inception. Has also led his own projects on ECM Records and appeared as a sideman across a wide range of jazz idioms from the mid-1980s onward.

Career

Mid-1980s — Early Session Work

Grenadier’s earliest documented Discogs credits appear in the mid-to-late 1980s, placing him in a variety of contexts. He appears on an obscure UK release, Blues For Red (Hot House Records, 1985), and on Larry Vuckovich’s In The Spirit (Quartet Records, 1988) and Thoughts Of A Gentleman (Quartet, 1988), indicating early activity in California-based and European sessions.

By the late 1980s he was appearing in fusion contexts: Steve Smith’s Vital Information — the percussive jazz-fusion group that also cycled through Mike Stern, Frank Gambale, and others — lists Grenadier among its members. His Vitalive! credit (VeraBra Records, 1990) is among the earliest documented large-ensemble appearances.

Late 1980s–Early 1990s — Sideman Circuit and First Leader Credits

Grenadier’s early-1990s credits show him active across multiple registers: the Latin-leaning projects Music Of The Brazilian Masters (Concord Picante, 1989) and Tres Palabras (Concord Jazz, 1990); Standards (And Other Songs) (Atlantic Jazz, 1992); and an Austrian recording, Black & Blue (Amadeo, 1992), with further Amadeo work (In & Out, 1993) and European appearances throughout.

His first leader credit on Discogs is the Torsten De Winkel Acoustic Quartet (Hot Wire Records, Germany, 1991), a co-leader situation in a German acoustic quartet context.

His most distinctive early appearance as co-leader is Home (Golden Horn Records, 1997), appearing alongside a companion release Our Song on the same label from 1998 — both early statements in a more personal vein.

Also from 1997: Touchdown! (Storyville, Denmark) — another co-led or leader date in a Scandinavian setting.

The Brad Mehldau Trio — Foundational Partnership (1997–ongoing)

The defining association of Grenadier’s career is the Brad Mehldau Trio, the piano trio he co-founded with pianist Brad Mehldau and original drummer Jorge Rossy, subsequently with Jeff Ballard taking over the drum chair. The trio became one of the most documented and critically significant small jazz groups of the late 1990s and 2000s.

The Art Of The Trio series established a new benchmark for the piano trio format, with Grenadier’s bass consistently cited for its melodic invention and rhythmic flexibility. Personnel across the series remained stable: Mehldau on piano, Grenadier on bass, Rossy on drums. Producer Matt Pierson handled the early volumes; James Farber was the recurring engineer.

The trio also appears in the 2011 box set The Art Of The Trio (Recordings: 1996–2001) (Nonesuch).

Beyond trio work, Grenadier played on Mehldau’s large-ensemble project Highway Rider (Nonesuch, 2010), an orchestral double album co-produced by Jon Brion, with Grenadier on specific tracks alongside Matt Chamberlain, and Joshua Redman on saxophone.

Grenadier also appears on Mehldau’s Quartet (Nonesuch, 2007) and on the Mehldau film score Ma Femme Est Une Actrice (Warner Jazz France, 2001). A final Nonesuch credit, Largo (2023), lists Grenadier as a writer on the session.

Fly Trio — ECM Collaboration with Turner and Ballard (2004–2012)

Grenadier is a founding member and co-leader of Fly (24), the saxophone-bass-drums trio he formed with tenor saxophonist Mark Turner (2) and drummer Jeff Ballard. The group worked under the name “FLY (Turner / Grenadier / Ballard)” and recorded three albums:

ReleaseYearLabel
Fly2004Savoy Jazz
Sky & Country2009ECM Records
Year Of The Snake2012ECM Records

The ECM move gave the trio a distinctly spacious, open-sounding aesthetic characteristic of that label’s production. Both ECM volumes were produced by Manfred Eicher. The group brought together three musicians who also share a long Brad Mehldau Trio connection — Ballard is the Trio’s drummer post-Rossy, and Turner has appeared on multiple Mehldau-adjacent recordings.

The Gleaners — Solo ECM Debut (2019)

Grenadier’s debut solo album, The Gleaners (ECM Records, 2019), stands as his most significant statement as a leader. An unaccompanied double bass recital, it was produced by Manfred Eicher, mixed by Grenadier and Eicher together alongside Gérard de Haro, and engineered by James Farber. The liner notes in English were written by Grenadier himself. The album’s ECM release positioned it firmly in the tradition of solo bass recordings the label has championed.

Release ID: 13223738. Label: ECM Records.

Driftwood — Co-Leader Date with Muthspiel and Blade (ECM, 2014)

Driftwood (ECM Records, 2014) is a co-led trio of Wolfgang Muthspiel (guitar), Grenadier (bass), and Brian Blade (drums). Recorded at Rainbow Studio, Oslo, and produced by Manfred Eicher with Jan Erik Kongshaug engineering. All compositions credited primarily to Muthspiel, with one piece jointly credited to all three. This was an early and significant ECM trio date for Grenadier outside the Mehldau and Fly contexts.

Release ID: 5701407.

Hudson Supergroup (2017)

Grenadier is a co-founding member of Hudson (10), a supergroup comprising Jack DeJohnette (drums), Grenadier (bass/vocals), John Medeski (piano/organ), and John Scofield (guitar). The group’s self-titled debut Hudson (Motéma, 2017) was collectively produced and features all four members on vocals. Grenadier is credited as “Acoustic Bass, Vocals; Producer [Produced By].” The album engages with American roots music traditions and was conceived partly around the Hudson Valley musical community those players share with DeJohnette.

Key Sideman Roles Beyond Mehldau

Charles Lloyd (ECM): Grenadier appears on Lloyd’s The Water Is Wide (ECM, 2000), a major ECM release featuring Lloyd, Brad Mehldau (piano), Billy Higgins (drums), and John Abercrombie (guitar). This session was one of the first substantial cross-connections between Grenadier’s Lloyd and Mehldau associations.

Joshua Redman (Nonesuch): Grenadier plays bass on Redman’s Compass (Nonesuch, 2009), with Brian Blade and Gregory Hutchinson sharing drum duties. He also appears on Back East (Nonesuch, 2007) and further Redman sessions.

Enrico Rava / Paul Motian (ECM): New York Days (ECM, 2009) brought together Rava, Paul Motian (drums), Mark Turner (2) (tenor), Stefano Bollani (piano), and Grenadier — a striking pan-European/American ECM encounter. Grenadier contributed music to the recording.

John Scofield: Beyond the Hudson group, Grenadier appears on Scofield’s Past Present (Impulse!, 2015), alongside Joe Lovano and Bill Stewart.

Edward Simon: Appears on Simon’s debut album Edward Simon (Kokopelli Records, 1995) — an early encounter with what became a recurring network of collaborators including Mark Turner (2).

Wolfgang Muthspiel Sextet/Group: Grenadier is listed as a member of both Wolfgang Muthspiel Sextet and Wolfgang Muthspiel Group on Discogs, indicating a longer-running relationship with the Austrian guitarist beyond the Driftwood date. He appears on Street Scenes (Columbia, 1996) and Awareness (Warner Bros., 1997).

Pat Metheny: Grenadier is a member of the Pat Metheny Trio entity on Discogs (Discogs group ID: 356914), indicating trio-format work with the guitarist. He appears on the compilation/project With A Little Help From Our Friends (Lightyear Entertainment, 2005) in this context.

Paul Motian Trios: Grenadier is listed as a member of both Paul Motian Trio 2000 + One and Paul Motian Trio 2000 + Two, Motian’s late-period rotating groups. He appears on Once Around The Room (A Tribute To Paul Motian) (ECM, 2022), featuring him alongside other Motian associates.

Richard Galliano New York Trio: Grenadier is listed in Richard Galliano New York Trio alongside the French accordionist.

Bruce Barth Quintet / Seamus Blake groups / Kevin Hays Trio / Jon Gordon Quintet / Jonathan Kreisberg Trio / Patrick Zimmerli Quartet / Steve Wilson Quartet / Wycliffe Gordon Quintet: All Discogs group memberships, attesting to the depth of Grenadier’s involvement across the New York scene through the late 1990s and 2000s.

2019–2024 — Continued Activity

Grenadier appeared on Sanctuary (Sunnyside, 2019) and Reflections (Deutsche Grammophon, 2019). The Terrible Animals (Criss Cross Jazz, 2019) date co-leads with Lage Lund, Sullivan Fortner, and Tyshawn Sorey — a contemporary pianoless quartet session on Criss Cross.

ECM released Where The River Goes (2018) featuring Grenadier. He appears on Warna (Verve, 2020), Every Note Is True (Blue Note, 2022), and further sessions through 2024 including Solid Jackson (Criss Cross Jazz, 2024) and The Sky Will Still Be There Tomorrow (Blue Note, 2024).

A live document with the Brad Mehldau Trio, Live At The Village Vanguard (Loveland Records, Denmark, 2025), shows the trio still active into 2025.
