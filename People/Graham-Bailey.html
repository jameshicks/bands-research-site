Graham Bailey

UK bassist and multi-instrumentalist. Discogs IDs: 400406 (Graham Bailey) and 452803 (Graham Green) — same person; two separate entries. Also credited as: Bailey, Bayley, G. Bailey, Graham “Green” Bailey, Green, Graham Greene, G. Green, W. Green.

Born: Graham John Bailey. Linked site: myspace.com/bassaxist (archived).

Overview

Graham Bailey was bassist across every studio release by Adrian Borland’s South London bands from the late 1970s through the final dissolution of The Sound (2) in 1988. He appears on all seven Sound studio albums, both original Second Layer EPs, and the sole Second Layer full-length, as well as the pre-Sound punk recordings with The Outsiders (2) and The Crazies (3).

Name history: Bailey used the pseudonym Graham Green for his first three Sound albums — Jeopardy (1980), From The Lions Mouth (1981), and All Fall Down (1982). From Shock Of Daylight (1984) onward he appeared under his real surname. Discogs maintains two distinct artist entries (400406 “Graham Bailey” and 452803 “Graham Green”) that are confirmed to be the same person.

In Second Layer — the duo he co-founded with Borland — Bailey’s role expanded beyond bass: he handled keyboards, bass guitar, and drum programming, providing the machine architecture that gave Second Layer its harder, more industrial character. The drum-machine-locked synth bass of World Of Rubber (1981) is substantially his contribution. He also took a photography credit on In The Hothouse (1985) and wrote credits on selected Sound material.

Post-Sound, Bailey contributed bass to A Simple Vision (2021), the posthumous Crazies (3) album, and to The Sound’s archive release Will And Testament (2021), participating actively in the Borland estate’s archival projects decades after the band ended.

Chronological History

The Outsiders (2) — 1976–1979

Bailey’s first documented band. The Outsiders (2) (Discogs ID: 131374) was a South London punk act featuring Borland (vocals, guitar), Adrian Janes (drums, lyrics), Bob Lawrence (bass), and Bailey (bass). The presence of two bassists suggests Bailey joined partway through the band’s run, replacing or supplementing Lawrence; on the 2021 archival box set Count For Something (Cherry Red, 2021), Lawrence plays bass on discs 1–4 tracks 1–13 and all of disc 5, while Bailey plays bass specifically on disc 4 tracks 14–23 — the later recordings.

Songwriting on the Outsiders was split between Borland and Janes. The Close Up LP (1979) engineer was Gary Lucas (2).

The Crazies (3) — c. 1977–1978

The Crazies (3) (Discogs ID: 10381858) was a pre-Outsiders or concurrent South London unit featuring Borland, Janes, Bailey, Bi Marshall (born Benita Biltoo), and a fifth member credited as Lord Sulaco (Discogs ID: 683979). The recordings are raw punk demos; Nick Robbins is credited as Recording Engineer (‘78) on the posthumous 2021 release, indicating material dates from 1978.

Their sole official release is A Simple Vision (LP, Optic Nerve Recordings, 2021, cat. OPT4.043), which compiled tracks recorded in 1978, remastered for release in 2020. The tracklist: “Scorch Torch,” “Human Pie,” “Strontium,” “Flipper,” “Bodybag,” “The Shakes,” “When We’re Dead.” Bailey plays bass; Janes plays drums; Borland plays guitar; Bi Marshall plays clarinet and designed/drew the artwork; Lord Sulaco provides vocals. Written-By credit goes to The Crazies (3) collectively.

This release is notable for reuniting the pre-Sound South London scene under one document: it’s the same core people who would split into The Outsiders, Second Layer, and The Sound, here captured at the earliest stage.

Second Layer — 1979–1981

Bailey co-founded Second Layer (Discogs ID: 59911) with Borland in 1979, launching the self-run Tortch Records imprint simultaneously. Second Layer was the experimental, machine-driven counterpart to The Sound, running concurrently. See the Second Layer article for full detail.

Bailey’s role in Second Layer: keyboards, bass guitar, drum programming, written-by — the project’s drum-machine architecture was his domain. All three original releases (two EPs plus World Of Rubber) credit him as keyboards/bass/drum programming.

The Sound (2) — 1979–1988

The main body of Bailey’s recorded career. The Sound (2) (Discogs ID: 171148) released seven studio albums over nine years on three labels. Bailey appears on all of them.

From The Lions Mouth (1981, Korova KODE 5):

All Fall Down (1982, WEA, Netherlands):

  • Bailey credited as: Graham Green (ID 452803), role “Bass, Effects [Wasp], Drum Machine, Percussion [Metal]”
  • Lineup: Borland (vocals, guitar), Max Mayers (keyboards), Graham Green/Bailey (bass, Wasp effects, drum machine, metal percussion), Mike Dudley (drums, percussion, metal percussion)
  • Produced by Nick Robbins and The Sound; engineered by Flood, Craig Milliner, Steve Prestage
  • Bailey’s most expanded Sound credit — operating Wasp synthesizer effects and drum machine alongside conventional bass, reflecting Second Layer’s influence on the album’s harder texture

The Cold Beat LP (2009, unofficial) documents the original Sound lineup — Borland, Bi Marshall, Bailey, and Dudley — and is notable for Bi Marshall’s return, playing keyboards and saxophone alongside Bailey’s bass.

Open Questions

  • Bailey’s activities between The Sound’s dissolution (1988) and his reappearance on the 2009 Cold Beat unofficial LP are not documented in Discogs. Whether he played in other bands, worked outside music, or continued any recording activity in the 1990s–2000s is unknown from DB sources alone.
  • “Lord Sulaco” (Discogs ID: 683979) — the vocalist on The Crazies (3) A Simple Vision — is an otherwise undocumented figure. Whether this is a pseudonym for one of the other members or a separate person outside the core circle is not determinable from Discogs.
  • The No Reason (2023, EMI/Virgin) release credits Bailey as “Composed By, Lyrics By” — unusual given Borland’s longstanding status as primary lyricist. This may be a posthumous archival compilation with new credits, or a previously uncredited co-write.
  • Whether Bailey has given any interviews or public accounts of the Sound years is not answerable from Discogs.
