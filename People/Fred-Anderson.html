Fred Anderson

Discogs ID: 582509
Real name: Fred Anderson Jr.
Born: March 22, 1929
Died: June 24, 2010 (heart attack, age 81)
Origin: Chicago, Illinois, USA

American tenor saxophonist and Chicago jazz institution. Founder of the Velvet Lounge (Chicago venue/club). Key figure in the AACM orbit and Chicago free jazz. Long-running recording partnership with Hamid Drake (10 shared releases as co-leaders); their most prominent document is From The River To The Ocean (Thrill Jockey, 2007), recorded by John McEntire. Co-leader on Fred Anderson / DKV Trio (1997, Okka Disk), produced by Bruno Johnson and Ken Vandermark.

The Velvet Lounge

Anderson founded the Velvet Lounge in 1982 by converting an existing tavern at 2128½ South Indiana Avenue (South Loop, Chicago) into a jazz and creative music club. [source: https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.VELVETLOUNGE] [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Lounge, as of 2025-07-05]

Note on founding year: Most sources cite 1982 (UChicago finding aid, Chicago Magazine, Jazz Institute of Chicago). Wikipedia’s Velvet Lounge article states 1983. The 1982 date is treated as primary given the archival finding aid.

2006 relocation

The original building was slated for demolition, forcing a move. In summer 2006 the club relocated approximately two blocks to 67 East Cermak Road (between South Michigan Ave. and South Wabash Ave.), funded by community contributions and performance revenue. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Lounge, as of 2025-07-05] [source: https://www.organissimo.org/forum/topic/63068-chicagos-velvet-lounge-closes-forever/]

Post-Anderson history and closure

Fred Anderson died June 24, 2010. His two granddaughters took over operations but a family dispute (involving Anderson’s two sons) led the staff to walk out by late November 2010. The venue never fully reopened as a jazz club; by January 2011 it was reported that “the Velvet as we knew it is now gone forever” (Neil Tesser, Chicago Examiner). The bar at 67 E. Cermak continued under new ownership that shifted programming to DJs, hip-hop, and comedy; creative music programming is cited as having ended by 2012. The building finally closed permanently in 2019. [source: https://www.organissimo.org/forum/topic/63068-chicagos-velvet-lounge-closes-forever/] [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Lounge, as of 2025-07-05]

Role in Chicago’s creative music scene

The Velvet Lounge was a primary performance space for AACM-adjacent and free improvisation musicians on the South Side during a period of sharp decline in Black jazz clubs (beginning 1960s). Anderson began with bi-weekly Sunday afternoon jam sessions (four-dollar cover), which grew into regular performances Tuesday through Saturday evenings. The venue was described as “a spiritual home to many musicians who shared the uncommercial player’s perennial need for an intimate space” and “the dusty epicenter of the Midwest’s free form jazz scene.” Tatsu Aoki noted it was “the only place” for Asian-American and other marginalized creative musicians in Chicago during the late 1990s through 2012. [source: https://news.jazzjournalists.org/the-velvet-lounge-then-and-now-show-and-tell-at-hyde-park-jazz-fest/] [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Anderson_(musician), as of 2025-07-05]

Anderson mentored hundreds of younger musicians who launched careers at the club. A regular house ensemble in the early 1990s included trumpeter Billy Brimfield, saxophonist Art Taylor, pianist Jim Baker, bassist Mike Cristol, and drummer Gerald Donovan. By the mid-1990s a “Velvet Graduates” ensemble featured saxophonists David Boykin, Aaron Getsug, and Greg Ward II, bassist Karl E. H. Seigfried, and drummer Isaiah Spencer. Other documented associates include Hamid Drake, Nicole Mitchell, Avreeayl Ra, Ari Brown, Ernest Dawkins, Henry Grimes, and Von Freeman. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Lounge, as of 2025-07-05] [source: https://news.jazzjournalists.org/the-velvet-lounge-then-and-now-show-and-tell-at-hyde-park-jazz-fest/]

Anderson also co-helped initiate the Vision Festival in 2005, dedicated to avant-jazz. The Velvet Lounge was a Chicago Jazz Festival fixture from the 1990s onward. Its closure was described by the UChicago archival finding aid as “the close of an era — the era of Black jazz clubs on the South Side.” In 2013, drummer Mike Reed founded Constellation nightclub partly to fill the void. [source: https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.VELVETLOUNGE]

Archival collection: Guide to the Velvet Lounge Collection 1980–2010 held at the University of Chicago Special Collections Research Center. [source: https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.VELVETLOUNGE]

Delmark Connection

Koester produced Timeless (2006, DE-569) — a live document of Anderson and his working group, released on Delmark Records. Anderson was a longtime presence in the Chicago creative music community but recorded relatively infrequently as a leader; the Delmark release is the most visible document on a major independent. Produced by Robert G. Koester.
