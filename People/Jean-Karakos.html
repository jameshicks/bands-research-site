Jean Karakos

Full name: Jean Georgakarakos (also credited as Jean Karakos, J. Karakos, Uljana Karakos) Born: June 26, 1940, Malestroit, Brittany, France Died: January 22, 2017, Paris, France Role: Record label founder, executive producer, distributor Discogs artist ID: 403913 (Jean Karakos); 253029 (Jean Georgakarakos — same person, fuller credit history); 8380536 (Uljana Karakos — sleeve variation, one Celluloid credit)

Biography

Early Career and French Labels (1960–1966)

Jean Georgakarakos was born in Malestroit, Brittany, France, the son of Greek immigrant parents (hence the Greek-derived surname). He entered the music industry around 1960, founding his first label, Star Success (Discogs: 938758), which released French pop and twist singles (1961–1962 — Gilbert Brun, Billy Watch). In 1964 he founded a second imprint, Joc (Discogs: 163390), oriented toward imported folk and blues licensing for the French market — releases included Pete Seeger, Lightnin’ Hopkins, Big Bill Broonzy, Sonny Terry, Charlie Parker, and Pentangle. These were French-pressed licensed editions, not original recordings, but they positioned Karakos as a specialist importer and distributor during a period when American vernacular music was difficult to find in France.

Alongside his label work, Karakos ran a network of French record shops called Pop Shop, through which he sold imported American LPs. This retail infrastructure gave him both market knowledge and a distribution channel that he would later deploy for Celluloid.

BYG Records and the Free Jazz Moment (1967–1976)

In 1967, Karakos co-founded BYG Records (Discogs: 13232) in Paris with Jean-Luc Young and Fernand Boruso. BYG became one of the most significant documentary free jazz labels of the late 1960s, recording a concentrated burst of American free and avant-garde jazz during the Actuel Festival period (1969). The label’s core output — nearly all recorded in 1969 under what became the Actuel sublabel imprint — included:

  • Don Cherry (Mu Parts 1 and 2; Eternal Rhythm)
  • Archie Shepp (Yasmina, A Black Woman; Blasé; Poem for Malcolm)
  • Sun Ra (The Solar-Myth Approach, Vols 1 and 2)
  • Anthony Braxton (debut recordings)
  • A.A.C.M. (Art Ensemble of Chicago, AACM members)
  • Carla Bley, Roswell Rudd, Alan Silva, Burton Greene

BYG also released rock and blues through sublabels including Rock Generation (the Yardbirds, Animals, Brian Auger, Rod Stewart early recordings) and managed a large Archive of Jazz compilation series of historical reissues (Louis Armstrong, Duke Ellington, Sidney Bechet, Bix Beiderbecke, Fats Waller).

Karakos produced or supervised the core BYG Actuel recordings; his name appears on well over 100 releases credited at this label under the Jean Georgakarakos alias. BYG was eventually acquired by Charly Acquisitions Ltd and the masters have since been extensively reissued (including via Charly and BYG’s own reissue program, still active as of 2024–2025).

Celluloid Records: Co-Founding and No Wave Era (1976–1981)

In 1976, Karakos co-founded Celluloid Records (Discogs: 5195) in New York, alongside a short-lived imprint called Tapioca (Discogs: 48269). Celluloid was structured under the US corporate entity Celluloid Inc. (Discogs: 468602) and the intermediate entity Celluloid Records (2) (Discogs: 109358).

The label’s early catalog under the LTM catalog-number prefix (1979–1981) consisted primarily of French pressings of UK post-punk and industrial acts: Throbbing Gristle (D.o.A.), Cabaret Voltaire, Stiff Little Fingers, Henry Cow. These were licensed French-market editions, establishing Celluloid’s distribution reach before the label developed its own original roster.

Under the CEL prefix the label began signing original downtown New York acts directly:

  • James Chance — early no-wave singles (1980)
  • Teenage Jesus And The Jerks — Pre Teenage Jesus And The Jerks (1980)
  • Was (Not Was) — debut single “Wheel Me Out” (1980)
  • Lydia Lunch — Queen Of Siam (1981, CEL 2-6561)
  • Suicide — French reissues, Dream Baby Dream single (1981)
  • Material — Temporary Music Compilation EP (1980), Memory Serves (1981)

ZE Records Co-Release Arrangement

In the early 1980s, Celluloid entered a co-release arrangement with ZE Records (Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban). This operated on an artist-specific rather than exclusive basis, connecting Karakos’s French distribution infrastructure with ZE’s Island Records transatlantic deal. Alan Vega’s 1980 solo debut carried both Celluloid and ZE logos; Was (Not Was)‘s debut single appeared on Celluloid before the LP moved to ZE.

Bill Laswell / Material and the Hip-Hop Pivot (1982–1985)

Bill Laswell’s involvement with Celluloid from 1982 onward transformed the label’s direction. Laswell brought in Material’s One Down (1982) — on which Karakos served as executive producer (credited as “J. Karakos”) — and opened connections to New York hip-hop, electro, and cross-genre experimentation.

Notably, Karakos is also credited on Bill Laswell’s Baselines (1983, Elektra Musician) as executive producer — an unusual off-label credit suggesting his managerial relationship with Laswell extended beyond Celluloid releases alone.

The hip-hop series that ran under CEL 150s–190s catalog numbers (1982–1985) included landmark records: Change The Beat (B-Side / Fab 5 Freddy, CEL 156), Crazy Cuts and The Grand Mixer Cuts It Up (D.St.), The Wildstyle and World Destruction (Time Zone / Afrika Bambaataa). These releases placed Celluloid at the center of early international hip-hop distribution.

African and World Music Expansion (1984–1989)

From the mid-1980s, Celluloid’s heaviest activity was in West and Central African music. Touré Kunda (Senegalese) became the label’s single most-released artist with over 100 catalog entries. Fela Kuti’s Celluloid catalog ran to 81 entries from 1985 onward. Other African artists included Manu Dibango (Electric Africa, 1985), Youssou N’Dour (Immigrés / Bitim Rew, 1985), Ladysmith Black Mambazo, and Foday Musa Suso.

Kaoma and “Lambada” (1989)

In 1989, together with co-creator Olivier Lorsac (Discogs: 581694), Karakos assembled the Brazilian-French group Kaoma (Discogs: 226971), fronted by singer Loalwa Braz. The group’s single “Lambada” became a worldwide commercial phenomenon, reaching number one in dozens of countries. Released through Epic/CBS (not Celluloid), the record generated enormous revenue — and immediate controversy over its uncleared sampling of Bolivian folk music.

By 1989, Karakos sold Celluloid, ending the label’s original run (operations ceased, though the masters passed to subsequent owners).

Later Career: Karakos Productions and Distance (1995–2005)

After Celluloid, Karakos established Karakos Productions & Publishing (Discogs: 3214637), which became the parent structure for several later imprints:

  • Distance (Discogs: 57) — dance label, founded 1995; sublabels included Melting Pop, Question Mark ?ecords, Substance, Polyester Records (3)
  • Les Disques Caractere (Discogs: 512320)
  • Sonodisc (Discogs: 53478) — merged with Musisoft Next in 1998; the combined operation ran until approximately 2005

He also maintained production credits on BYG Records reissue programs through Charly Acquisitions; his Jean Georgakarakos credit appears on BYG/Charly reissues as recently as 2025.

Notes (web) — BYG, the Algiers Pan-African Festival, and the Actuel Series

The Pan-African Cultural Festival, Algiers, July 1969

The First Pan-African Cultural Festival of the Organisation of African Unity took place in Algiers in July 1969, organised by the Algerian government. The festival drew a broad political and cultural convergence: Eldridge Cleaver (Black Panthers information minister), Stokely Carmichael, Senegalese anthropologist Cheikh Anta Diop, and a substantial cohort of American free jazz musicians were all present. pan-african-music.com

Archie Shepp performed there on 29–30 July 1969 with an all-star ensemble — Clifford Thornton (cornet), Grachan Moncur III (trombone), Dave Burrell (piano), Alan Silva (bass), Sunny Murray (drums), and poets Ted Joans and Don Lee — complemented by Algerian and Tuareg musicians playing shawms and percussion. The concerts took place at the Ketchaoua mosque forecourt and the Atlas Cinema in Algiers. Wikipedia — Live at the Pan-African Festival; pan-african-music.com

How BYG Connected to the Algiers Cohort

BYG’s link to the Algiers festival was brokered by Jacques Bisceglia, a jazz photographer and BYG collaborator who attended the festival specifically to recruit musicians for the label. After the festival, the ensemble traveled to Paris. pan-african-music.com; 1960sdaysofrage.wordpress.com

The BYG connection to Actuel magazine was through Claude Delcloo, a French drummer and editor-in-chief of the avant-garde magazine Actuel, who provided backing for the recording venture. BYG acquired or was closely associated with the magazine, lending the sublabel its name. 1960sdaysofrage.wordpress.com

BYG Recordings Directly from Algiers

BYG recorded one album at the Algiers festival itself:

  • Archie Shepp — Live At The Panafrican Festival (BYG Actuel): recorded 29–30 July 1969, at the Ketchaoua mosque forecourt and Atlas Cinema, Algiers. Produced by Jean Georgakarakos and Jean-Luc Young. Released on BYG/Actuel. This is the only confirmed on-site Algiers recording in the Actuel catalog. Wikipedia; Discogs release 1980625

Paris Sessions Arising from the Algiers Connection (August 1969)

Following the festival, the Algiers cohort traveled to Paris and recorded an intensive burst of sessions — primarily in August 1969, primarily at Studio Saravah (Passage des Abbesses) and later Davout Studios (Porte de Bagnolet). The artists, having earned fees at the Algiers festival, used those funds to finance the Paris travel. pan-african-music.com

Albums arising directly from the Algiers-to-Paris pipeline include:

  • Archie Shepp — Yasmina, A Black Woman (recorded 12 August 1969) — Bandcamp Daily
  • Archie Shepp — Blasé (with Jeanne Lee) — pan-african-music.com
  • Clifford Thornton — Ketchaoua (recorded 18 August 1969, Studio Saravah): named after the Algiers district where the festival performances took place; personnel overlaps directly with the Algiers concert (Shepp, Moncur III, Burrell, Murray). Wikipedia — Ketchaoua
  • Don Cherry — Mu (Parts 1 & 2): recorded 22 August 1969 with Ed Blackwell. Cherry was part of the wider Algiers-connected cohort though his Algiers participation is less directly documented. Red Bull Music Academy Daily

Other musicians from the Algiers gathering who subsequently recorded for BYG in Paris: Lester Bowie, Grachan Moncur III, Hank Mobley, Cal Massey, Oscar Peterson, Randy Weston, Jeanne Lee. pan-african-music.com

Approximately half of the Actuel series’ 52 albums originated from this concentrated August 1969 burst. Bandcamp Daily

The Actuel Festival (Amougies, Belgium, October 1969)

The Actuel Festival was a separate event from the Actuel LP series and from the Algiers festival. BYG organized it as a “60 hours of music for 60 francs” five-day concert event, originally planned for the Parc de Saint-Cloud, Paris, but banned by French authorities and relocated to Amougies, Belgium, where it ran 24–28 October 1969. The lineup mixed BYG’s free jazz roster with British progressive rock: Pink Floyd, Soft Machine, Captain Beefheart, Frank Zappa (MC), Yes, Ten Years After, The Nice. Attendance was approximately 20,000 over five nights; the festival was a financial failure for the label. Wikipedia — BYG Records; Zappa Wiki Jawaka; Bandcamp Daily

Karakos’s Specific Role at BYG and Departure

Karakos came to BYG as a record distributor and importer — this commercial/logistics role distinguished him from Young and Boruso who had label backgrounds. He is credited as producer (Jean Georgakarakos) on the Archie Shepp Live At The Panafrican Festival album alongside Jean-Luc Young, and supervised the core Actuel session output. Wikipedia — Live at the Pan-African Festival

BYG ran into severe financial difficulties by 1972 and filed for bankruptcy in 1975. Karakos sold his shares to Jean-Luc Young (who subsequently built the Charly Records reissue empire from the BYG masters); Karakos then moved to New York and founded Celluloid Records in 1976. Wikipedia — Jean Georgakarakos; patricksisson.com

Conflict note: The Patrick Sisson article identifies Jacques Bisceglia as a BYG co-founder alongside Young and Karakos; all other sources (Wikipedia, pan-african-music.com, Discogs) name the three founders as Boruso, Young, and Georgakarakos (BYG = initials). The Sisson article likely misidentifies Bisceglia, who was a collaborator/recruiter rather than a label founder. The Boruso/Young/Georgakarakos account should be treated as authoritative.
