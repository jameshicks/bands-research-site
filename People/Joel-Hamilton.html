Joel Hamilton

Joel Hamilton

Oct 17, 202611 min read

Joel Hamilton

Discogs ID: 306589
Base: Brooklyn, New York
Name variations: JH, Joe Hamilton, Joel, Joelt, Joelt Hamilton

American guitarist, drummer, producer, and engineer. One of the central nodes of the Brooklyn heavy underground (2000s–present), operating primarily out of Studio G Brooklyn. His credit range spans post-metal, jazz, hip-hop, avant-rock, and mainstream rock — with consistent technical and creative roles across all of them.

Overview

Joel Hamilton is credited on well over 100 releases spanning 1992–2025. He operates simultaneously as a performer, producer, recording engineer, and mixer — often all four on the same album. His production identity is rooted in the NYC and Brooklyn heavy underground of the mid-2000s (the Neurot / Relapse / ROIR axis), but he expanded steadily into jazz (Marc Ribot, Colin Stetson, Puss N Boots / Norah Jones), hip-hop (Blakroc / The Black Keys + Jay-Z circle), and indie rock (Highly Suspect, Sparklehorse, Bonobo). By the 2010s he was working across enough genres that no single scene claim captures his output.

His performing bands: Battle of Mice (drums, guitar, bass — all three studio outputs), The Book Of Knots (guitar, drums, synth), A Storm of Light (member; played instruments and co-produced), Glazed Baby, Chilmark, The J.J. Paradise Players Club, Iron Rite Mangle, and Shiner (5).

He is not to be confused with a Californian bassist of the same name (Discogs artist ID: 3451323).

Early Years (1992–1999)

Hamilton appears on guitar as early as 1992 (One Thousand Hopes, self-released) and 1993 (Driftwood, CMK Records). By 1996 he is credited as performer on Atomic Communists (Red Decibel). His engineering career begins in 1998 with two records on the Slowdime label: In Passing (engineer, producer) and The Farewell Bend (engineer, producer), plus a live sound credit on Men Are Monkeys. Robots Win. (Thick Records, 1998). He also recorded the split Breaker Morant / Amputee Set (self-released, 1998).

A 1999 self-released record (Enormous Black Power Gift) lists him as backing vocals, engineer, and producer — evidence that his multi-role approach was established before any major label work.

Early 2000s: DC/Hardcore Adjacency and Building a Studio Career

In 2000 Hamilton engineered basic tracks for Bluetip’s Polymer on Dischord Records — his earliest major-label engineering credit. The same year he played guitar, handled recording and engineering, and mixed Starless (Owned & Operated Recordings, 2000), and played guitar on Making Love E.P. (Anodyne Records).

In 2001 he served as engineer on two or three tracks of Sparklehorse’s It’s A Wonderful Life (Capitol Records), alongside Dave Fridmann and others — his first brush with a large commercial budget. He also played guitar and handled recording and mixing on Wine Cooler Blowout (Tee Pee Records, 2001, with The J.J. Paradise Players Club).

2002–2004 brings recording credits for Woven Hand (When The Roses Bloom Again, Diesel Only, 2002), Arab On Radar (The Great Old Ones, Reptilian Records, 2003, engineer and mix), a recording credit on Mogwai’s A Guess At The Riddle (FatCat Records, 2004), and engineer/mix on Exploring The Dangers Of (ROIR, 2004) — also crediting him on the New Heavy LP (ROIR, 2006) as mix/engineer/producer, suggesting a multi-year relationship with that artist.

2005–2008: The Neurot / Relapse / ROIR Core

This is the period where Hamilton’s production profile crystallizes around the Brooklyn heavy underground.

Made Out of Babies / Trophy (2005, Neurot Recordings): Producer, mixer, and recorder on this debut LP by Julie Christmas’s band. Hamilton co-produces tracks D and E on the Triad split EP the following year (see below).

Unsane / Blood Run (2005, Relapse Records): Engineer and producer on one of the defining albums from NYC noise-rock veterans. His credit here places him in the mid-2000s Relapse Records network alongside Vinnie Signorelli (drums), Dave Curran (bass), and Chris Spencer (guitar).

Battle of Mice / Triad (2006, Neurot Recordings NR-044): A three-way split EP with Red Sparowes and Made Out of Babies. Hamilton played drums and mixed the Battle of Mice tracks (B and C), co-produced those tracks, recorded additional tracks for Made Out of Babies (D and E), and mixed the Red Sparowes live tracks (A and F). One session, three bands, multiple overlapping roles. Other engineers: Andrew Schneider (track B).

Battle of Mice / A Day Of Nights (2006, Neurot Recordings NR-047): Hamilton served as drummer, producer, engineer, and mixer on the full Battle of Mice debut album — the band’s only proper LP. Josh Graham handled guitar, keyboards, design, and layout; Julie Christmas sang and wrote. Tony Maimone played bass. Mastered by Doug Henderson.

A Storm of Light / And We Wept The Black Ocean Within (2008, Neurot Recordings): Hamilton produced and recorded this debut LP from Josh Graham’s new project. The session brought in Vinnie Signorelli and Pete Angevine on drums, Domenic Seita on bass.

Dub Trio / Another Sound Is Dying (2008, Ipecac Recordings): Hamilton served as engineer, executive producer, producer, and co-mixer alongside the band. Mike Patton contributed lyrics to one track. Joe Tomino on drums.

Blakroc (2009, BlakRoc Records): The Black Keys and Damon Dash’s hip-hop project. Hamilton produced the full album and mixed several tracks (alongside Tchad Blake and Clay Holley). This credit marked a significant genre leap into a major hip-hop release.

A Storm of Light / Forgive Us Our Trespasses (2009, Neurot Recordings): Hamilton as guitarist, modular synth, Wurlitzer, percussion, recorder, engineer, and mixer. He is instrumentally embedded in this record, not just producing.

2009–2012: Expansion into Jazz and Indie

Marc Ribot’s Ceramic Dog / Party Intellectuals (2008, Pi Recordings): Hamilton recorded, mixed, and produced this avant-jazz/noise-rock record featuring Marc Ribot, Shahzad Ismaily, and Ches Smith. The session placed him in the downtown NYC jazz network.

Colin Stetson / New History Warfare, Volume 1 (2007, Aagoo Records): Producer, recorder, mixer on Stetson’s debut solo album, all solo saxophone. A very different aesthetic from the metal work — important early Stetson credit.

A Storm of Light / As The Valley Of Death Becomes Us Our Silver Memories Fade (2011, Burning World Records): Co-producer with Josh Graham, plus recording, mixing, modular synth, and Wurlitzer. Kim Thayil (Soundgarden) also contributed guitar on this album (tracked by Adam Kasper).

The Book Of Knots / Garden Of Fainting Stars (2011, Ipecac Recordings): Hamilton engineered, mixed, and produced the second Book of Knots album. Kihlstedt, Bossi, and Maimone co-produced.

Carla Kihlstedt & Matthias Bossi / Still You Lay Dreaming - Tales For The Stage, II (2011, self-released): Mixed by Hamilton.

Puss N Boots / No Fools, No Fun (2014, Blue Note): Hamilton engineered and mixed this debut record by the trio of Norah Jones, Sasha Dobson, and Catherine Popper — his highest-profile jazz label credit to date.

2014–Present: Broadened Scope

Highly Suspect / Mister Asylum (2015, 300 Entertainment): Hamilton produced, engineered, and mixed most of the album (Tom Dalgety handled one track). The record reached #1 on the Billboard Mainstream Rock chart in 2015.

Bonobo / Migration (2017, Ninja Tune): Additional recording engineer and vocal session engineer on two tracks; a marginal but notable credit on a high-profile electronic/jazz crossover album.

Thick / Happy Now (2022, Epitaph): Full producer, engineer, mixer, and percussion.

Lettuce (recurring): Hamilton has co-produced and recorded multiple Lettuce albums: Crush (2015), Mt. Crushmore (2017), Monorail 3000/Star Children (2024).

Season Of Mist (2020s recurring): Burning Shadows In The Southern Night (2023, Season Of Mist Underground Activists) and Where Gods Fear To Speak (2024, Season Of Mist) — producer, engineer, mixer.

Daptone Recording Co. / Hourglass (2025): Recording engineer — his most recent credit in the DB.
