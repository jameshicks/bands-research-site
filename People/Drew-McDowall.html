Drew McDowall

Discogs ID: 65583 Alias: Screwtape Role: Electronic musician, composer, modular synthesist Nationality: Scottish (born 28 January 1961, Paisley, Scotland; based in Brooklyn, NY) Active: c. 1979 – present Associated acts: Coil, Black Light District, Time Machines, Psychic TV, The Poems, Compound Eye, Captain Sons And Daughters

Biography

Origins and Early Work (late 1970s – early 1980s)

Drew McDowall was born on 28 January 1961 in Paisley, Scotland. In the late 1970s he co-founded The Poems with his then-wife Rose McDowall (later of Strawberry Switchblade and Sorrow). The band released a self-titled cassette in 1980 (Not On Label), with McDowall credited on vocals and piano. The Poems were a small fixture of the Scottish post-punk underground before the partnership dissolved alongside the marriage.

Psychic TV (1980s)

During the 1980s McDowall was a member of Psychic TV (Discogs ID: 17926), Genesis P-Orridge’s art-music collective. He also tour-managed The Shamen during this period. A 1985 Sub Rosa release, Je T’Aime…, shows him credited for drum programming and keyboards — likely the earliest session record of his synthesizer work.

Coil and Black Light District (1994 – early 2000s)

McDowall began working with Coil — the duo of John Balance and Peter Christopherson — as a regular collaborator from around 1994, becoming an official band member in 1995. He appears on the Eskaton 7” Nasa-Arab / First Dark Ride (1994) and is credited on several major Coil albums of the period.

In 1996 Coil and McDowall released A Thousand Lights In A Darkened Room (Eskaton) under the joint credit Coil Presents Black Light District. The record is credited to Black Light District, a Coil side-project whose lineup was McDowall, John Balance (as Jhon Balance), and Peter Christopherson — with additional contributions from Danny Hyde. McDowall’s credit on that record is as a member of “Those Who Shine Darkly,” the ensemble name used in the sleeve notes.

Black Light District is formally listed on Discogs as an alias of Coil, ELpH, Time Machines, and The Eskaton — these are overlapping Coil-family production names rather than distinct bands.

Time Machines (1998)

In 1998 the same trio — McDowall, Balance, and Christopherson — released Time Machines on Eskaton as Time Machines, a project explicitly conceived around drone-based psychedelic synthesis and the ritual use of trance frequencies. McDowall is credited as Performer alongside the others. A follow-up, Coil Presents Time Machines (2000, Eskaton), consolidated the project’s identity. Time Machines was reissued on Dais Records and has remained a canonical release in experimental drone/ambient circles.

Further Coil Appearances (1999 – 2008)

McDowall appears on several major Coil releases during the late 1990s and early 2000s:

  • Musick To Play In The Dark (1999, Chalice) — Performer
  • Astral Disaster (1999, Prescription) — Synthesizer (Moog), “Thought Forms” ensemble
  • Winter Solstice: North (1999, Eskaton) — Performer (uncredited)
  • Foxtrot (1998, Chalice) — Performer
  • Moon’s Milk (In Four Phases) (2002, Eskaton) — Performer
  • The Ape Of Naples / The New Backwards (2008, Important Records) — Performer
  • The New Backwards (2008, Threshold House) — Performer; also Photography By (New Orleans photos)

He relocated to the United States at some point in the early 2000s, and the move appears to have effectively ended his regular Coil membership. After John Balance’s death in 2004, Coil’s work appeared primarily in posthumous form.

Solo Career (2015 – present)

McDowall launched his solo career in earnest around 2015 with Collapse (Dais Records), a modular synthesis record mastered by Josh Bonati. The Dais Records relationship became his primary outlet, and he has remained on the label through the 2020s.

Subsequent solo albums show an evolving practice: from dense electronic composition to records that bring in chamber players and outside producers. Key releases:

  • Unnatural Channel (2017, Dais) — mixed by Joshua Eustis (Sons of Magdalene); vocalist Roxy Farman on two tracks
  • The Ghost Of Georges Bataille (2018, BANK Records NYC) — collaboration with Hiro Kone; artwork by Tauba Auerbach
  • The Third Helix (2018, Dais) — features vocalist and co-producer James K
  • Agalma (2020, Dais) — co-produced with Ben Greenberg; harp by Marilu Donovan, double bass by Justin Frye, violin by Adam Markiewicz; considered one of his most orchestrally developed records
  • A Thread, Silvered And Trembling (2023, Dais) — co-produced with Randall Dunn, mastered by Heba Kadry
  • Magnetism (2025, Ideologic Organ) — collaboration with composer Kali Malone; mixed by Randall Dunn, mastered by Heba Kadry; released on Stephen O’Malley’s Ideologic Organ imprint

A collection of Coil’s Astral Disaster sessions — Astral Disaster Sessions Un/Finished Musics (2018) and Vol. 2 (2020) — appeared on Prescription, returning his Moog/Thought Forms contributions from that era to public view.

He also worked in the early 2010s with Praxis and contributed to Raum’s Akashic Record compilation. A Live On Tape release appeared in 2021 on Under My Feet Records.

McDowall currently lives in Brooklyn and is active in the modular synthesis community.
