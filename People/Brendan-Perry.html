Brendan Perry

Discogs ID: 129361. Irish-New Zealand musician. Co-founded Dead Can Dance with Lisa Gerrard (1981, Melbourne, then London). Baritone vocalist, multi-instrumentalist.

Also known as Ronnie Perry (2) / Ronnie Recent.

Piano Magic collaboration

Perry was a guest vocalist on two tracks from Piano Magic’s album Ovations (October 2009, Make Mine Music UK / Darla North America & Asia):

  • “The Nightmare Goes On (feat. Brendan Perry)” — track 1
  • “You Never Loved This City (feat. Brendan Perry)” — track 8

Perry’s vocals were recorded at Quivvy Church, County Cavan, Ireland (his home studio base); the rest of the album was recorded at La Planque and The Fortress in London. Other guest contributors on the album included Peter Ulrich (also of Dead Can Dance), Gareth Parton, Kwesi Edman, and Ruth Elder. The album was reissued digitally via Bandcamp/Darla in February 2019.

How the collaboration came about

Dead Can Dance were a foundational influence on Piano Magic — Glen Johnson has stated he has been a DCD fan since 1984 and used to follow them around the country to see them live (“99% of modern music is disposable but DCD is not. It connects with my soul.” — terapija.net interview). The Darla Records press release for Ovations notes that Piano Magic’s fascination with Eastern European percussion stems partly from Dead Can Dance being “one of their biggest influences.”

Perry came to Piano Magic’s music as a relatively recent listener: the Darla press release describes him as “a very recent convert to Piano Magic’s music” who agreed to participate because he felt the group were making “the most beautiful music” he had heard for a long time. The press release characterises Perry as “reclusive,” framing his participation as an unusual event.

Vocals were tracked at Quivvy Church. Glen Johnson described the session in an interview (source: piano-magic.co.uk biography page):

“If we can get Brendan Perry to sing on one of our records, there’s nowhere else to go. The minute Jerome pressed ‘record,’ we just melted into a heap on the floor. It was a dream moment for both of us.”

Elsewhere Johnson is quoted adding: “I honestly had them on a pedestal, up there with the gods.” (Source appears to be the same biography page / press materials — the quote surfaces in multiple secondary sources citing piano-magic.co.uk.)

Note: No source found explaining the specific mechanism of first contact — whether Johnson wrote to Perry directly, went through a label intermediary, or met him some other way. The Darla press materials and the piano-magic.co.uk biography describe the mutual appreciation but not the logistics of how they got in touch.
