Glen E. Friedman

Discogs ID: 225397. Photographer; Los Angeles and New York. Active from 1981 to present.

Canonical photographer of the American punk, hardcore, and early hip-hop scenes. Career begins in the late 1970s as a teenager shooting the “Dogtown” skateboarders for Skateboarder magazine, before pivoting to music. His images defined the visual identity of Southern California hardcore (Black Flag, Circle Jerks, Suicidal Tendencies, Adolescents), the DC hardcore scene (Minor Threat, Fugazi, Faith), and the first wave of mainstream hip-hop (Run-DMC, Public Enemy, LL Cool J, Beastie Boys, Ice-T, Slick Rick). Introduced to hip-hop by Russell Simmons. Operated his own imprint / studio, Burning Flags, through which he published photobooks. Discogs lists 1,574 raw release credits across 146 unique masters.

Career by Era

Late 1970s–1981 — Dogtown skate photography

Begins shooting at the Del Mar Skate Ranch and Dogtown pools for Skateboarder magazine as a teenager. Transitions to music when the Southern California hardcore scene emerges from the same social world as the skateboarders.

1981–1985 — Southern California hardcore

First music credits from 1981: Adolescents debut LP (Frontier Records), Circle Jerks Wild In The Streets (Faulty Products), Suicidal Tendencies debut EP (Better Youth Organization), and early BYO compilations. Most significant work of this period: the Suicidal Tendencies self-titled debut (1983, Frontier Records), on which he is credited as producer, photographer, and designer — a full creative-control triple credit unusual for a photographer. Also photographs the Germs, DC’s Minor Threat and the Faith in this window.

1986–1990 — Hip-hop crossover; peak activity

Russell Simmons introduces him to the hip-hop world. Friedman shoots the Run-DMC / Aerosmith “Walk This Way” single cover (1986) — a crossover moment already iconic before the photo is published. Work accelerates through Def Jam and its orbit:

  • Public Enemy — Yo! Bum Rush The Show (1987, co-credited for cover concept) and It Takes A Nation Of Millions To Hold Us Back (1988)
  • LL Cool J — Bigger and Deffer (1987)
  • Slick Rick — The Great Adventures of Slick Rick (1988, cover)
  • Ice-T — Rhyme Pays, Power, O.G. Original Gangster, and the Rhyme Syndicate network of releases
  • Back From Hell (Run-DMC, 1990) — co-producer credit

Simultaneously continues DC hardcore coverage: Fugazi self-titled EP (1988), 13 Songs (1989), Repeater (1990).

1991–1995 — Continued hip-hop and transition

Beastie Boys Check Your Head (1992, Capitol / Grand Royal) — cover photograph. Ice-T O.G. Original Gangster (1991) cover. Art direction credits appear more frequently alongside photography (Ed O.G, King Tee releases). Co-production on Run-DMC Back From Hell.

1996–2000 — Retrospectives and reissues

Activity concentrates on reissue work and compilations. Covers Short Music For Short People (Fat Wreck Chords, 1999). Fugazi End Hits (1998) and live series begin.

2001–present — Retrospective and legacy

System Of A Down Toxicity (2001, American Recordings) — one of the highest-pressing albums in this dataset (74 pressings). Continuing reissue credits on Suicidal Tendencies, Minor Threat, Public Enemy, Run-DMC, and Beastie Boys releases. Directed and photographed A Look Back: Dogtown & Z-Boys documentary (2021, At The Dojo Records). Work continues to 2025 (Ice-T Power Rarities, Rhino Records; Fugazi and Minor Threat reissues).

Photobooks and Film (via Discogs)

Discogs documents a 2021 release on At The Dojo Records: A Look Back: Dogtown & Z-Boys — listed with Friedman as Directed By, Photography By, and Design [Front Cover]. This is the closest Discogs comes to documenting his photobook work directly; his major photobooks (Fuck You Heroes, Fuck You Too, Fuck You All, My Rules) are not catalogued as Discogs releases but are referenced indirectly through cover/design credits on his own Burning Flags label.
