Andrew Weatherall

DJ, producer, remixer, musician, visual artist. Born 6 April 1963, Windsor, Berkshire, England. Died 17 February 2020, London (pulmonary embolism, aged 56). Discogs artist ID: 2657. Real name: Andrew James Weatherall. Brother of Ian Weatherall.

Overview

One of the most consequential figures in post-acid-house British electronic music. Weatherall made his initial reputation as a DJ and remixer affiliated with the Boy’s Own fanzine collective (with Terry Farley, Pete Heller, and Cymon Eckel). The remix of Primal Scream’s “Loaded” in 1990 and his production of Screamadelica in 1991 established him as a major creative force bridging rave culture with rock and soul. He went on to form The Sabres Of Paradise (1992–1995) with Jagz Kooner and Gary Burns, then Two Lone Swordsmen (1995–2007) with Keith Tenniswood, before embarking on a solo career on his own Rotters Golf Club label. He also ran Emissions Audio Output, Sabres Of Paradise (label), Bird Scarer Records, Moine Dubh, and Byrd Out.

He worked across dub, techno, post-punk, industrial, acid house, folk, and psych — his later solo work (especially the albums Convenanza and Consolamentum, 2016) drew heavily on Krautrock and cosmic rock traditions. He was increasingly active as a visual artist in his final years.

Biography by Era

Early career and Boy’s Own (late 1980s–1990)

Weatherall came up through the late-1980s London club and rave scene, co-founding the Boy’s Own fanzine/collective alongside Terry Farley, Pete Heller, and Cymon Eckel. Boy’s Own ran club nights and raves, published an influential acid house fanzine, and eventually launched Boy’s Own Productions (ID 680176) as a label under London Records, with sublabels Junior Boy’s Own (ID 283) and Boy’s Own Recordings (ID 1659). Within Boy’s Own, Weatherall, Farley, and Heller also recorded together as Bocca Juniors (ID 6515) with Anna Haigh.

The 1990 Boy’s Own 12-inch “Raise (63 Steps To Heaven)” (as Bocca Juniors) and Boy’s Own label releases like “Bomba” (1990, remixed/mixed by Weatherall) document this formation period.

Primal Scream breakthrough (1990–1992)

Weatherall remixed Primal Scream’s “Loaded” (1990, Creation Records) — a decisive intervention that transformed a rock B-side into a landmark acid-rave record. He went on to produce the bulk of Screamadelica (1991, Sire/US; Creation/UK), credited on tracks 3, 5–7, and 9–11, with Hugo Nicolson as assistant producer on several of those. Screamadelica is widely regarded as one of the key albums of the era, synthesising gospel, soul, house, and psych rock.

Other notable early-90s productions and remixes: One Little Indian’s “Imperfect List” (1990), Hypnotone’s “Come Together” (1990, Sire), Flowered Up’s “Weekender” (as “Weatherall’s Weekender”, 1992, Heavenly — epic 12-inch double rework), New Order “Regret” Weatherall Mixes (1993, London Records), and One Dove’s Morning Dove White (1993, Boy’s Own) co-produced with Dot Allison, Jagz Kooner, and Gary Burns.

Sabres of Paradise (1992–1995)

Weatherall formed The Sabres Of Paradise (Discogs artist ID 950) with Jagz Kooner (ID 9660) and Gary Burns (ID 37783) in 1992. The trio recorded on Warp Records and their own imprint Sabres Of Paradise (label) (ID 4754, under Sabres Of Paradise Ltd., ID 378209). Their sound was built around dub basslines, industrial textures, and kosmische atmosphere.

Key releases:

  • Sabresonic (1993, Warp) — debut LP, also credited to Weatherall solo as writer
  • “Smokebelch II” (1993, Sabres Of Paradise label) — canonical 12-inch
  • Haunted Dancehall (1994, Warp) — second LP; Weatherall, Burns, and Kooner all co-wrote, produced, and mixed throughout
  • Sabresonic II (1995, Warp) — final LP
  • “Wilmot” (1994, Warp) and “Theme” (1994, Sabres Of Paradise label) — key singles

The band also issued a remixes collection, Septic Cuts (1994, Sabres Of Paradise label). They were known for adventurous remixes of other acts, including The Future Sound Of London’s “Papua New Guinea” and The Moody Boys’ “Centre of the World.” After disbanding in 1995, Kooner and Burns joined The Aloof.

Two Lone Swordsmen (1995–2007)

After the Sabres dissolved, Weatherall partnered with studio engineer Keith Tenniswood (Discogs ID 23899, also known as Radioactive Man, Big K) to form Two Lone Swordsmen (Discogs artist ID 958). Active from 1995, they operated primarily on Emissions Audio Output (ID 4086) and Warp Records (ID 2580) in the early period, and Rotters Golf Club (ID 4928) after 2002.

Two Lone Swordsmen developed a wide network of alias projects, including Aramchek, Basic Units, C-pij 04, Frisch Und Munter, Hidden Library, Klart, Klunk, Lino Squares, Rude Solo, The Black Balloons, The Hold, and The Major And Brother T — all listed as Discogs groups of both members.

Emissions Audio Output had five sublabels: Emissions Echoic, Emissions Lo-Fi, Emissions Static, New Emissions, and Special Emissions — all used to release Two Lone Swordsmen material in the 1996–1999 period.

Key Two Lone Swordsmen releases:

  • The Fifth Mission (Return To The Flightpath Estate) (1996, Emissions Audio Output) — debut EP
  • Swimming Not Skimming (1996, Emissions Audio Output)
  • A Bag Of Blue Sparks (1998, Warp)
  • Stay Down (1998, Warp)
  • Tiny Reminders (2000, Warp) — mastered by Noel Summerville
  • Further Reminders (2001, Warp)
  • From The Double Gone Chapel (2004, Warp) — Weatherall and Tenniswood co-wrote and co-produced throughout; mastered by Noel Summerville
  • Wrong Meeting (2007, Rotters Golf Club) — Weatherall vocals, keyboards
  • Wrong Meeting II (2007, Rotters Golf Club)

A remixes collection, Peppered With Spastic Magic (2003, Rotters Golf Club), collected their remix work.

Solo work and Rotters Golf Club (2006–2020)

Weatherall’s first true solo LP was The Bullet Catcher’s Apprentice (2006, Rotters Golf Club). A Pox On The Pioneers (2009, Rotters Golf Club) featured Bobby Gillespie on backing vocals alongside Tim Fairplay (guitar, bass), Steve Boardman (aka Blak Majik/Philtrator, programming/keys), and Chris Mackin (aka Chris Rotter, guitar/bass/backing vocals). Qualia (2017, Höga Nord Rekords, Sweden) was another landmark solo LP.

The paired albums Convenanza (2016) and Consolamentum (2016), both on Rotters Golf Club, represent his most fully realised late solo statement. Consolamentum was co-written with Nina Walsh (his most frequent late collaborator across 21 shared releases). Walsh (also credited as Obscura, c-pij 01) had Discogs ID 45807.

He also recorded as The Asphodells (ID 2733213) with Tim Fairplay — main release Ruled By Passion, Destroyed By Lust (2012, Rotters Golf Club).

His Byrd Out label (ID 2324854, under Byrd Out Limited, ID 1850762) released later EPs including Kiyadub EP (2017) and Blue Bullet EP (2018).

His final release, Pamela #1 (2020, Pamela Records), was issued shortly after his death.

Weatherall also ran the Moine Dubh label (ID 917612, 6 releases) and Bird Scarer Records (ID 389561, 7 releases).
