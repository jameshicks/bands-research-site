Biography

Lagos Origins and Early Career

Tony Oladipo Allen was born on 20 July 1940 in Lagos, Nigeria. He began his career working as a studio engineer before turning to the drums full-time. Self-taught on percussion, he absorbed the polyrhythmic traditions of West African music and the emerging influence of American funk and R&B that was reaching Nigeria in the late 1960s and early 1970s.

Joining Fela Kuti: Africa 70 (1969–1979)

Allen joined Fela Kuti’s band — initially the Koola Lobitos, credited as Fela Ransome Kuti & His Koola Lobitos — and became the drummer and musical director for Africa 70 from around 1969. The partnership was foundational to the development of Afrobeat: while Fela provided the compositional vision and political voice, Allen engineered the rhythmic engine. He combined the polyrhythmic layering of traditional Yoruba drumming with the groove of James Brown-era funk and jazz swing, constructing a beat that could sustain extended twenty-minute jams without losing tension.

The Africa 70 period produced some of the most celebrated recordings in African popular music: Live! (1972), Shakara (1973), Expensive Shit (1975), Yellow Fever (1976), Zombie (1977), No Agreement (1977). Allen is credited as drummer — often specifically “Drums [Leader]” or “Lead Drummer” — on all of them. He also took on producer and composer credits on his own solo projects during this period, including Jealousy (1975) and Progress (1977), both on Nigerian labels.

Africa 70 recorded prolifically from 1969 through to the late 1970s, primarily for Nigerian labels including His Master’s Voice, EMI, Jofabro, Coconut, Afrodisia, and Kalakuta Records. Allen departed from the organisation around 1979, reportedly over creative and financial disagreements with Fela.

Egypt 80 (1981 onward, without Allen)

After Allen’s departure, Fela formed Egypt 80, which continued recording into the 1990s — Original Suffer Head (1981), Army Arrangement (1985), Beasts Of No Nation (1989), Underground System (1992). Allen was not part of Egypt 80; his name does not appear in its credits. His absence from the later band underscores how central he had been to Africa 70’s sound.

Solo Career and Departure to France

Allen launched his solo career while still with Africa 70 and continued building it after leaving. His 1984 release N.E.P.A. (Never Expect Power Always) (Earthworks, UK) marked his international emergence. He relocated to France, where he became a permanent fixture of the Paris music scene. Working under the alias Ladi Alabi as well as his own name, he built a body of solo Afrobeat and psychedelic funk albums.

Key early solo works include:

  • Progress (1977, Coconut) — written, arranged, produced by Allen
  • No Accommodation For Lagos (1979, Polydor) — first full solo statement
  • N.E.P.A. (Never Expect Power Always) (1984, Earthworks)
  • Too Many Prisoners (1987, Barclay)
  • Afrobeat Express (1989, Cobalt)

Comet Records Era (1999–2004)

From the late 1990s, Allen released a run of albums on Comet Records (ID: 2160), a French label that became his primary home through the early 2000s. This period produced some of his most acclaimed solo work:

  • Black Voices (1999) — Comet Records
  • Other Voices (1999) — Comet Records
  • HomeCooking (2002) — Comet Records
  • Every Season (2002) — Comet Records
  • Live (2004) — Comet Records

Comet Records also released Psyco On Da Bus (2001), the project he shared with Cesar Anot, Jean-Phi Dary, Jeff Kellner, and Liam Farrell (aka Doctor L / Da Link).

Honest Jon’s Records Period (2005–2009)

Allen signed to Honest Jon’s Records (ID: 8895), the London label operated by Mark Ainley and Alan Scholefield, for several key releases:

  • Lagos No Shaking (2005) — produced by Allen
  • One Tree (2006) — with remixes by Moritz von Oswald
  • Kilode (2007)
  • Ole (2007)

During this period he was also recruited by Damon Albarn for The Good, The Bad & The Queen (2007, Honest Jon’s/Parlophone). The supergroup — also featuring Paul Simonon (bass, ex-The Clash) and Simon Tong (guitar, ex-The Verve) — recorded a self-titled debut album produced by Danger Mouse, characterised by melancholic post-punk, dub, and Afrobeat textures.

Strut / World Circuit Phase (2009–2014)

Secret Agent (2009, World Circuit) was a major critical re-entry, produced by Nick Gold with Allen’s Afrobeat band recorded live. The same year saw his Inspiration Information on Strut Records, alongside the compilation he produced, Nigeria 70 (The Definitive Story of 1970s Funky Lagos) (Strut, 2009).

Film Of Life (2014, Jazz Village) broadened his palette toward jazz-inflected arrangements, featuring an ensemble including Vincent Taurelle (keyboards), Vincent Taeger (bass), and Jean-Phi Dary.

Rocket Juice & The Moon (2012)

In 2012 Allen joined Rocket Juice & The Moon, a supergroup comprising Damon Albarn (keys/vocals), Flea (Michael Balzary, bass — Red Hot Chili Peppers), and Allen on drums. Their self-titled debut was released on Honest Jon’s Records. The project placed Allen in an explicit electro-funk and jazz-rock context alongside two of the most high-profile figures in their respective genres.

Blue Note and Late Career (2017–2020)

Allen signed to Blue Note (ID: 281) for two of his most acclaimed late-career albums:

  • A Tribute To Art Blakey And The Jazz Messengers (2017, Blue Note) — big-band jazz tribute, arranged by Allen
  • The Source (2017, Blue Note) — all-original material, Composed/Arranged By Allen

He also collaborated with Hugh Masekela on Rejoice (2020, World Circuit/BMG), a long-planned duo album that had been recorded years earlier but was released posthumously. The album features both musicians in an intimate setting, drawing on their shared roots in African music.

There Is No End (2020, released after his death) was a hip-hop and spoken-word project featuring a range of MCs rapping over Allen’s drumming; it included guest appearances by Damon Albarn and others.

The Good, The Bad & The Queen reunited for Merrie Land (2018).

Tony Allen died on 30 April 2020 in Paris at age 79.
