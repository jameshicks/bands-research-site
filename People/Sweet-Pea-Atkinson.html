Sweet Pea Atkinson

Discogs ID: 89978
Real name: Hillard Atkinson
Born: 20 September 1945, Oberlin, OH
Died: 5 May 2020 (aged 74)
Based: Los Angeles, CA

R&B, soul, and funk vocalist. Core lead singer of Was (Not Was) across the band’s entire run, one of three vocalists alongside Harry Bowens and Donald Ray Mitchell. Known for a deep, warm baritone suited to Was (Not Was)‘s blend of absurdist funk and Detroit soul. Born in Oberlin, OH, raised in Detroit; worked the Chrysler assembly line in the mid-1970s, where he formed a band called Hi Energy with fellow factory workers. Met Don Was at a studio around 1978–79 while wearing an all-orange outfit at 3am — Was later recalled he looked “on fire.” Recruited into Was (Not Was) as a founding vocalist. In parallel, worked extensively as a session vocalist in LA from the mid-1980s onward, appearing on records by Bob Dylan, Bonnie Raitt, Lyle Lovett, Cheb Khaled, and many others. Also sang in Lyle Lovett’s Large Band for a decade alongside Harry Bowens, Arnold McCuller, and Francine Reed. Released a solo album, Get What You Deserve (2017, Blue Note), late in life.

Solo Work

Get What You Deserve (2017, Blue Note, Discogs ID 10932776) — 10-track soul/funk album. Produced primarily by Keb’ Mo’, with Don Was producing and mixing tracks 1, 3, 5, and David Was producing track 2. Core band: Randy Jacobs (guitar), James Gadson (drums), with Harry Bowens returning on backing vocals for several tracks. Covers include Bert Russell’s “Are You Lonely For Me Baby”, a James Brown composition (“You Can Have Watergate”), and Bobby Womack’s “You’re Welcome, Stop On By”.

Hi Energy

Local Detroit band of Chrysler/United Auto Workers co-workers, active in the mid-to-late 1970s. Atkinson sang with the group by night while working the Chrysler assembly line by day. The band rehearsed at Sound Suite Studios in Detroit; it was at that facility — in a late-night hallway encounter, reportedly around 3am — that Don Was first saw Atkinson (dressed head-to-toe in orange) and later recruited him for Was (Not Was). (uDiscover Music; Post-Punk Monk; WBSS Media; Michigan Rock and Roll Legends — Don Was)
