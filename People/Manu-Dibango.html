Manu Dibango

Born: Emmanuel N’Djoké Dibango, 12 December 1933, Douala, Cameroon Died: 24 March 2020, France Discogs artist ID: 20560 Alias on Discogs: Junior Dibbs (ID: 1619521)

Cameroonian saxophonist, vibraphonist, marimbist, pianist, and vocalist. Best known internationally for the 1972 Afrobeat/soul single “Soul Makossa,” which became one of the most sampled and recycled tracks in pop history. Active from the early 1960s until his death in 2020, Dibango moved fluidly between Congolese rumba, makossa, funk, reggae, jazz, electronic music, and Afrobeat, recording for labels across Africa, Europe, the Caribbean, and North America.

Overview

Dibango trained initially in classical piano and began his professional career in Brussels and Paris in the 1950s. Around 1960 he joined the Congo-based orchestra Grand Kalle (Grand Kalle And African Jazz, Discogs ID: 1094585) directed by Joseph Kabasele, playing piano and saxophone alongside future Congolese music stars including Tabu Ley Rochereau and Dr. Nico. He formed his own band in Cameroon in 1963, then relocated to Paris in 1965.

His international breakthrough came in 1972 with “Soul Makossa,” initially a B-side on an African Football Cup anthem. The track was picked up by Atlantic Records and licensed widely, reaching audiences across the US, UK, and Europe. Its vocal hook — the “mama-ko, mama-ssa” refrain — was later sampled without permission by Michael Jackson (“Wanna Be Startin’ Somethin’,” 1983) and Rihanna (“Don’t Stop the Music,” 2007), both of which led to settlements.

Throughout the 1970s and 1980s, Dibango experimented extensively with reggae (recording with Jamaican session heavyweights), electronic production, and funk. His 1985 album Electric Africa (Celluloid/Polydor), produced by Bill Laswell, marked his most direct entry into the downtown NYC avant-funk orbit. The same year he appeared as co-writer and performer on Deadline’s Down By Law (Celluloid, 1985), one of Laswell’s key projects. He also appeared as a featured guest on Sly & Robbie’s Language Barrier (Island, 1985), alongside Afrika Bambaataa, Herbie Hancock, and Bob Dylan.

Later albums saw him collaborate with Cuban musicians (CubAfrica, 1998, with Cuarteto Patria and Eliades Ochoa), produce a New Orleans tribute (Hommage À La Nouvelle Orléans, 2007), and issue his final solo album Afro (Because Music, 2016). He continued performing and recording into his eighties.

Key Release Personnel

Makossa Man (1973, Fiesta (7), Discogs ID: 1438581)

Guitar: Jerry Malekani, Slim Pezin. Percussion: Freddy N’Kounkou. Produced by Rolande Le Couviour. Recorded by Fernando Braso. Mixed/edited by Brad Davis and Don Hahn. Dibango plays soprano sax, tenor sax, piano, organ, vibraphone, marimba, vocals.

Afrovision (1976, Decca, Discogs ID: 929245)

Bass: Vicky Edimo. Congas: Aiyb Dieng. Drums: Claude Vamur. Guitars: Jerry Malekani, Jo Tongo, Bernard Torelli. Engineered and mixed by Claude Achallé and Henri Arcens.

Gone Clear (1980, CRC Records, Discogs ID: 1160985)

Bass: Robbie Shakespeare, Clyde Bullard. Drums: Sly Dunbar. Guitar/keyboards: Geoffrey Chung, Mikey Chung, Ansel Collins, Clive Hunt, Robbie Lyn, Peter Ashbourne. Saxophone: Michael Brecker, Lou Marini. Trumpet: Randy Brecker, Jon Faddis. Trombone: Barry Rogers. Vocals: Gwen Guthrie, Ullanda McCullough, Brenda White, Frank Floyd. Produced and engineered by Geoffrey Chung.

Ambassador (1981, Mango, Discogs ID: 470171)

Identical Jamaican rhythm section as Gone Clear (Sly Dunbar, Robbie Shakespeare, Geoffrey Chung circle). Brass: Michael Brecker, Randy Brecker, Jon Faddis, Barry Rogers. Vocals: Gwen Guthrie, Ullanda McCullough, Brenda White, Frank Floyd, Yvonne Lewis.

Abele Dance (1984, Polydor, Discogs ID: 122401)

Produced and programmed by Martin Meissonnier. Bass: Francis Mbappe. Drums: Valery Lobe. Guitar: Jerry Malekani. Mixed by Godwin Logie. Electronic production.

Electric Africa (1985, Celluloid / Polydor, Discogs IDs: 41280, 102694)

Produced by Bill Laswell. Bass/vocals: Francis Mbappe. Drums/perc: Brice Wassy, Valery Lobe. Guitar: Jerry Malekani, Vincent Nguini. Electronic drums: Jojo Kuo. Fairlight programming: Frédérick Rousseau. Trumpet: Segona Peter Tholo. Vocals: Florence Titty Dimbeng, Sissy Dipoko. Engineered by Robert Musso.

Down By Law — Deadline (1985, Celluloid, Discogs ID: 1398332)

Dibango credited as co-writer on tracks 1, 2, 4. See Deadline for full personnel. Produced by Bill Laswell and Phillip Wilson. Engineered by Rob Stevens and Robert Musso.

Afrijazzy (1986, Polydor, Discogs ID: 40917)

Produced, arranged, written by Dibango. Bass: Francis Mbappe, Michel Alibo. Drums: Brice Wassy, Valery Lobe, Paco Sery. Guitars: Jerry Malekani, Vincent Nguini, Paul Personne. Keyboards: Ray Lema, Stéphane Joly, Justin Bowen. Bugle/trumpet: Hugh Masekela. Trombone: Yoro Gueye. Violin: Didier Lockwood. Percussion: Adama Dramé. Recorded/mixed by Gabriel Nahas.

Language Barrier — Sly & Robbie (1985, Island, Discogs ID: 41265)

Dibango appears as featured artist. Other guests: Afrika Bambaataa, Herbie Hancock, Bob Dylan, Bernie Worrell, Bernard Fowler, Wally Badarou, Robbie Lyn. Produced by Bill Laswell / Material. Engineered by Robert Musso, Steven Stanley.

CubAfrica (1998, Mélodie, Discogs ID: 1864712)

With Cuarteto Patria. Guitar/vocals: Eliades Ochoa, Umberto Ochoa. Guitar: Jerry Malekani. Bass: William Calderón. Percussion: Roberto Torres. Mixed by Jean-Loup Morette.

Alias: Junior Dibbs (Discogs ID: 1619521)

The Discogs alias Junior Dibbs (also credited as Jr. Dibbs) has only two known releases, both from 1971–1972 on Fiesta (7) and London Records/Minos — likely early French-market pressings or alternative credits. The alias is also linked to a group “Jr. Dibbs And Society” but no standalone discography beyond this. All creative output under Junior Dibbs overlaps entirely with Dibango’s main Discogs ID.
