John Congleton

Discogs ID: 265534
Real name: John Anthony Congleton
Born: 1977, Lubbock, Texas
Based: Dallas, Texas → Los Angeles, California
Roles: Recording engineer, mixer, producer, songwriter, performer
Groups: The Paper Chase, The Nighty Nite

Independent producer, engineer, and composer. Born 1977 in Lubbock, Texas; spent his formative years in Dallas. Principal songwriter of The Paper Chase and The Nighty Nite. Runs Elmwood Recording in Dallas and Animal Rites Recording Studio in Los Angeles. One of the most prolific producers in indie rock over the 2000s–2020s, with credits spanning Explosions In The Sky, St. Vincent, Baroness, Future Islands, The Decemberists, Mogwai, and dozens more. Won a Grammy Award (Best Alternative Music Album, 57th Annual Grammy Awards, February 2015) for producing St. Vincent’s self-titled album.

Biography

Early life. First concert memory: seeing Chuck Berry at age five or six. Became obsessed with Brutal Juice as a teenager — saw them approximately 75 times. At age 14–15, his own punk band Curvejob recorded a demo with Brutal Juice bassist Sam McCall. Congleton’s own words on the session: “Sam McCall recorded us, and he played bass in a band I absolutely worshipped called Brutal Juice.” Hearing multitrack playback in a recording studio for the first time was, in his words, a transformative experience: “I knew that was what I wanted to do with my life.” [Sources: Tape Op #81; Sound On Sound]

Note on Sam McCall: McCall (Discogs ID 347019) was bassist in Brutal Juice, an acid punk band from Denton, Texas (formed 1990, disbanded 1997; reunion shows 1999–2012). Other members: Craig Welch (vocals), Gordon Gibson (vocals/guitar), Ted Wood (guitar/vocals), Ben Burt (drums). McCall had recorded the band’s early demos before joining as a full member. He subsequently worked as a producer and recording engineer for other bands, though no specific post-Brutal Juice credits with Congleton have been documented. The Curvejob demo is the only known shared credit. Wikipedia: Brutal Juice, as of 2026-01-04

Conflict — band’s location: The vault previously described Brutal Juice as a “Dallas” band; Wikipedia and LocalWiki both place them in Denton, TX (Dallas–Fort Worth metro area). Denton is the correct origin. Wikipedia: Brutal Juice; LocalWiki: Brutal Juice

Education. Studied jazz composition at the University of North Texas, where he also worked as a staff recording engineer — recording world music and diverse ensembles, teaching him to get sounds quickly under pressure. Afterwards moved to Chicago and apprenticed at Electrical Audio under Steve Albini, absorbing analog technique, signal flow, and phase relationships.

Early professional work. Returned to Dallas and joined Dallas Sound Lab as a staff engineer. Left at age 22 to go freelance. First proper production credits around age 25. Early engineering base was Dallas; he insists on handling engineering himself rather than delegating it, viewing the physical act of moving microphones and working the room as inseparable from building trust with artists.

Studios. Co-founded Elmwood Recording with engineer Stuart Sikes in Dallas, in a former funeral home in the Oak Cliff neighbourhood. The room is built around a Neve 53-series console formerly used for Saturday Night Live. Congleton subsequently opened a second facility, Animal Rites Recording Studio, in Los Angeles. Roughly half his projects record at Elmwood; the rest at Animal Rites or on location.

Working method. Prefers projects outside his comfort zone: “I will almost always pick the thing that I’m less familiar with.” Does extensive pre-production conversations about influences and desired sound, then trusts instincts during sessions. Conducts his own engineering; considers closeness to the technical process essential to authentic capture. Core principle: “All my favourite records sound pure, spontaneous and hungry.”

The Paper Chase

Discogs ID: 289081
Active: 1998–c.2016
Origin: Dallas, Texas
Label progression: The Clandestine Project Recordings → Beatville Records → Divot → Southern Records → Trost Records → Kill Rock Stars → Magic Bullet Records / Southern Records → Fat Possum Records

Alternative rock band formed 1998 by Congleton in Dallas. Described (AllMusic) as “a jagged structure of avant-garde jazz, noise, indie, and punk.” Congleton played guitar and sang, and wrote the material. Occasionally spelled “the pAper chAse.” Members at various points: Aryn Dalton (ID 692639), Bobby Weaver (ID 692642), Sean Kirkpatrick (3) (ID 7642336). Final lineup included drummer Jason Garner.

First show was in a parking lot in 1999: “Super sunny, but sounding terrible. We sucked.” The band dissolved c.2010. A Someday This Could All Be Yours Vol. 2 was written and partially recorded — songs about natural disasters, like Vol. 1 — but Congleton chose not to finish it, describing it as “a very defeated and highly depressive record.” Vol. 2 has never been officially released.

The Nighty Nite

Discogs ID: 2315783
Origin: Dallas, Texas
URL: johncongletonandthenightynite.bandcamp.com

Experimental band from Dallas formed after The Paper Chase’s dissolution. Core members: Congleton and Jason Garner (Paper Chase’s final drummer). First Dallas show June 11, 2011 at The Double Wide. Full-length Until The Horror Goes (2016, Fat Possum Records). Also collaborated with Jeff Powell (Discogs ID 427871) on that record.

Production and Engineering Career

Congleton began recording other artists in the late 1990s alongside running The Paper Chase. His engineering base was Dallas; he later opened or affiliated with Elmwood Studios in Dallas and a second studio in Los Angeles. He is unusual in straddling full creative production (sometimes contributing drum programming, keys, piano, or string arrangements) and straight engineering/mixing work.

Early work (1997–2005)

First external credits from 1997 (Uncontrolled Records). Early work concentrated in the Texas indie and post-hardcore underground. Key early engineering client: Explosions In The Sky — The Earth Is Not A Cold Dead Place (2003, TRL), which established his name outside Texas. Also worked with Midwestern indie-rock bands on Flameshovel, Magic Bullet, and Suicide Squeeze.

Notable credits:

  • Explosions In The Sky — The Earth Is Not A Cold Dead Place (2003, TRL) — Recorded By; the album was captured essentially live in three days

Breakthrough period (2006–2010)

Increasingly prominent as producer for larger indie labels (4AD, Jagjaguwar, Kill Rock Stars, Relapse Records). Breakthrough came with St. Vincent / Annie Clark’s Actor (2009, 4AD), which established him as a go-to producer for art-pop. Simultaneously began work with Baroness’s sludge-metal catalog.

Peak productivity (2011–2016)

Became one of the most in-demand producers in indie rock and art-pop. Key run includes four St. Vincent albums (as sole or co-producer), the St. Vincent/David Byrne collaboration, and a broad range of stylistically diverse records. Also continued with Explosions In The Sky.
