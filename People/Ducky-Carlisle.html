Ducky Carlisle

Grammy award-winning mixing engineer, producer, recording engineer, and drummer. Discogs artist ID: 260419. Born and active in the US, primarily in Syracuse NY and Boston MA. Died October 2, 2023.

Name variations on Discogs: Duck Carlisle, Ducky, Ducky Carlise.

Career Overview

Carlisle began his career in Syracuse, NY, where he operated Ramjet Enterprises (Discogs label ID: 1733720), a music production company. He later relocated to the Boston area, where he owned and operated Room 9 From Outer Space (Discogs label ID: 323075), a recording studio in South Boston, MA. The studio appears under several name variations across release credits: Room 9 From Outerspace, Room Nine From Outer Space, Room Number Nine From Outer Space.

His career spans four distinct roles that often overlap on a single release: producer, recording engineer, mixing engineer, and session drummer/percussionist. His drumming roots surface throughout his discography — he played drums and produced The Ohms’ earliest recordings, and continued playing drums on sessions through at least 2020.

Syracuse / Early Career (1979–1990)

Carlisle’s earliest Discogs credit is The Ohms’ single “Teenage Alcoholic” (1979, Northside (2)), on which he played drums, sang backing vocals, and served as co-producer and engineer alongside Ohms vocalist/guitarist Zenny Caucasian. The Ohms — Carlisle’s own band, with bassist Rick Suburban and Caucasian — was a punk/new wave act out of Syracuse.

Ramjet Enterprises, his Syracuse-based production company, facilitated early independent production work in the region.

Boston Era and Room 9 From Outer Space (1991–2010s)

Based in South Boston, Carlisle built Room 9 From Outer Space into a center of the local rock and alternative scene. A consistent stream of indie-rock, power-pop, blues, and metal projects passed through the studio over three decades.

Early 1990s. His first Boston-area credits include recording Kid Crash (1991, self-released) and producing/engineering B’zrker’s Thats What You Get (1991, Nemesis Records). He engineered Growing Old / Dean I for Presto! Records (1992) and then began a recurring relationship with Leviathan Records (2), a US metal/hard-rock label established by David T. Chastain and Steve McClure. Carlisle engineered and mixed Guitar Dominance! (1993), Night Of The Living Shred (1994), Supersonic Shred Machine (1996), and Rapid Fire Rondo (1998) — all for guitarist Joe Stump on Leviathan. The Leviathan work extended to a Japanese second pressing (Second Coming, Brunette, 1997).

Mid-1990s. Carlisle produced, recorded, and mixed records for the Boston indie scene: Little Town (Eastern Front Records, 1995) and Rapture (Eastern Front Records, 1996), as well as This Is Boston-Not Austin (Black Wolf Records, 1995). He also engineered a live Aerosmith album, Last Call - Live At The Boston Teaparty - April 2nd, 1970 (Mr. Cat Music, 2000), suggesting his Blackbird/Boston engineering connections extended to legacy recordings.

Tone-Cool Records / Boston blues axis. A key recurring context for Carlisle in the late 1990s was Tone-Cool Records, a Boston-based blues label. He mixed Monster Mike Welch’s Catch Me (1998) and served as assistant producer on Susan Tedeschi’s Just Won’t Burn (1998) — her commercial breakthrough, produced by Tom Hambridge and issued on Tone-Cool, which reached the US charts.

Power-pop and singer-songwriter work. Carlisle was closely tied to the Boston power-pop circuit and its offshoots. He produced and recorded sessions for The Flashcubes — a Syracuse-based power-pop group active since the late 1970s — returning to that relationship across decades: producing most of Brilliant (Northside (2), 2003), producing tracks on Sportin’ Wood (Air Mail Recordings/Northside (2), 2012), and producing the bulk of Flashcubes Forever (Wasabi Records, 2018). The Brilliant sessions also featured Ed Stasium as a session player.

Other notable production work in this period:

  • Secret Society by The Sheila Divine (Arena Rock, 2002) — producer, engineer, mixer for the Boston indie-rock act
  • Nothing Personal by Mark McKay (Dren Records, 2001) — sole producer, engineer, mixer
  • They’re Everywhere by Jim’s Big Ego (self-released, 2003) — producer, mixer, recorder; and Free* (self-released, 2008) — producer

Wheat / Aware Records. Carlisle recorded the majority of tracks on Wheat’s Per Second, Per Second, Per Second… Every Second (Aware Records / Columbia, 2003), a key album in the Boston indie-rock scene, alongside engineers Dave Fridmann and John Fields.

Bleu. Carlisle co-produced and recorded the bulk of Four + Besides (2012) with Bleu (3), a Boston-based power-pop singer-songwriter; the album received a Japan deluxe edition on Powerpop Academy / The Major Label / Thistime Records.

Amanda Leigh / Mandy Moore. He mixed and recorded Amanda Leigh (Storefront Recordings, 2010) by Mandy Moore — a US release on the Lab 344 / Storefront label, also issued in Brazil.

Nashville / Major-Label Sessions (2010s)

By the 2010s, Carlisle’s mixing credit began appearing on high-profile blues and rock releases through Blackbird Studio (Nashville) and major-label output:

  • Buddy Guy / Silvertone Records. Carlisle engineered and mixed Born To Play Guitar (Silvertone / RCA / Sony Music, 2015) and handled recording on The Blues Is Alive And Well (Silvertone / RCA / Sony Music, 2018) and The Blues Don’t Lie (Silvertone / RCA / Sony Music, 2022) — all produced by Tom Hambridge. The Silvertone/RCA albums were Grammy-nominated / Grammy-winning releases, which grounds the Grammy credit listed in his Discogs profile.
  • Skin Deep / Buddy Guy (2008) — additional mixing work on the Silvertone / Sony set.
  • Living Proof (Jive / Silvertone, 2010) — co-mixer with Tom Hambridge.
  • I’m A Bluesman (Virgin America, 2004) — mixed and recorded.

The Hambridge-Carlisle working relationship is the through-line across these Nashville-era sessions.

Bands and Performance Credits

Beyond engineering, Carlisle maintained an active playing profile:

  • The Ohms (Discogs artist ID: 2227289) — drums, vocals, co-producer. Members: Ducky Carlisle, Rick Suburban, Zenny Caucasian.
  • The Major Labels (Discogs artist ID: 3230608) — a group he formed with Mike Viola (of Candy Butchers / The Wonders) and William James McAuley III.
  • Aquavia (2008, self-released) — drums and vocals.
  • Join The Crowd (Clang!, 2011) — percussion, guitar, harmony vocals, engineering, mixing.
  • The Rapid Shave (Stomper (2), 2014) — drums, producer, recording, mixing.
  • Pop Jewelry (Lovestruck Recordings / Thistime Records, 2015) — drums, percussion, producer, recording.
  • On The Slide (Stomper (2), 2016) — drums, recording, mixing.
  • Gold Plated by Minky Starshine (75 Or Less Records, 2020) — drums, producer, engineer.

Sun Ra Cosmos / Later Work

Carlisle engineered and mixed Songs From The Sun Ra Cosmos (Modern Harmonic, 2019) by The Barrence Whitfield Soul Savage Arkestra, a tribute to Sun Ra featuring Brother Cleve, Irwin Chusid, Rich Gilbert, and Waitiki. A late-career credit showing his range extending into avant-garde territory.

His last traceable Discogs credit is The Blues Don’t Lie (2022, Buddy Guy / Silvertone). He died October 2, 2023.

Studio

Room 9 From Outer Space — South Boston, MA. Owned and operated by Carlisle. Discogs label ID: 323075. Credits appear as “Room 9 From Outer Space,” “Room 9 From Outerspace,” “Room Nine From Outer Space,” and “Room Number Nine From Outer Space.”

Blackbird Studio — Nashville, TN. Carlisle’s Grammy-era Buddy Guy sessions are credited there (see The Blues Is Alive And Well, 2018: “Recorded By [@ Blackbird]”).
