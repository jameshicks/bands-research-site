Edward Simon

Venezuelan jazz pianist and composer, born 27 July 1969 in Punta Cardón, Venezuela. Based in New York from the late 1980s onward. Active across hard bop, post-bop, and Latin jazz contexts; also credited as “Eduardo Simon,” “Ed Simon,” and “E. Simon” on various releases. Discogs artist ID: 8574.

Career

Late 1980s — New York Arrival and Early Sideman Work

Simon surfaced on the New York scene in the late 1980s, quickly landing sideman slots on prominent sessions. His earliest Discogs credits date to 1988–1990: he appears on Wolfgang Puschnig’s Mindgames (JMT, 1988) and Season Of Renewal (JMT, 1989), Dave Samuels’s The Searcher (GRP, 1989), and Kurt Elling’s Promise Of Tomorrow (GRP, 1990). His positioning on these dates — European labels with an avant-leaning sensibility at JMT, mainstream post-bop at GRP — signals a pianist comfortable moving between idioms from the start.

The more decisive early association was with Bobby Watson and his group Horizon. Simon is credited on both The Inventor (Blue Note, 1990) and Post-Motown Bop (Blue Note, 1991), the latter co-credited to Victor Lewis; he contributed original compositions to both records. This placed him squarely in the Blue Note hard-bop lineage at a pivotal moment for that label’s 1990s revival.

Also from 1991: Man-Talk For Moderns Vol. X (Blue Note) and Claude Bolling’s Pan-Romanze (Herzklang), showing the range of his early bookings.

1992–1997 — Columbia/Atlantic Circuit and Debut as Leader

Through the mid-1990s Simon worked as a sideman on several high-profile Columbia and Atlantic releases. He appears on Bobby Watson’s Present Tense (Columbia, 1992) and Midwest Shuffle (Columbia, 1994) — both featuring Victor Lewis on drums — and on Jesse Davis’s The Pursuer (Atlantic Jazz, 1994). He also appears on the soundtrack to Spike Lee’s Clockers (Columbia, 1995) and Romantic Defiance (Columbia, 1995).

His debut as a leader came in 1995: the self-titled Edward Simon on Kokopelli Records, with a quartet of Mark Turner (2), Larry Grenadier, Adam Cruz, and Milton Cardona on percussion. Turner and Cruz would remain long-term collaborators.

1996–2000 — Latin Contexts and Broadening Scope

Simon appears on Chucho Valdés’s Bele Bele En La Habana (Candid, 1995) and on Andy Gonzalez’s The Latin Side Of John Coltrane (Astor Place, 1996). He also plays on Willie Colón’s Trombone Man (TropiJazz, 1995) and on recordings with Paquito D’Rivera.

La Bikina (Mythology Records, 1998) was his second leader date — a smaller-scale session with Mark Turner (2) on tenor and David Binney on alto, Binney also producing. Around the same time Simon is on the self-titled debut of Mark Turner (2) (Warner Bros./Criss Cross, 1998), alongside Brian Blade and Joshua Redman; this session crystallized a cluster of collaborators — Turner, Blade, Scott Colley — who would anchor Simon’s most important work over the next two decades.

Turbulent Flow (Blue Note, 2000) lists Simon as a sideman (electric and acoustic piano).

2001–2009 — Afinidad and the Criss Cross Years

The most prolific and creatively focused phase of Simon’s documented output. Two major threads run in parallel:

Afinidad — a co-led group with David Binney, featuring Scott Colley on bass, Brian Blade on drums, and Adam Rogers (2) on guitar. Afinidad (Red Record, 2001) launched the band; Fiestas De Agosto (Red Record, 2005) and Océanos (Criss Cross Jazz, 2007) followed, the latter adding Pernell Saturnino on percussion, Luciana Souza on vocals, and a brass section (Alan Ferber trombone, Shane Endsley and Jesse Newman trumpets).

Solo leader dates on Criss Cross Jazz — The Process (2002) is a piano trio with John Patitucci and Eric Harland, and Simplicitas (2005) and Océanos (2007) expand the lineup. These records are among Criss Cross’s most critically noted of the decade.

Simon’s sideman work continues: North And South (Sunnyside, 2003) with Adam Rogers; Horizon Reassembled (Palmetto, 2004); Codes (Blue Note, 2006) with Greg Osby.

The Unicity album (C.A.M. Jazz, 2006) is a trio record with John Patitucci and Brian Blade, produced by Ermanno Basso — a key Simon-as-leader statement, later reissued on Solid Records (2016).

New York Sessions (CAM/C.A.M. Jazz, 2006) and New York Encounter (Fresh Sound New Talent, 2009, co-led with Michael Simon, Marlon Simon, and Andy Gonzalez) round out the period.

2010–2018 — Venezuelan Suite, SFJazz Collective, Orchestral Work

Simon joined the SFJazz Collective, a rotating ensemble of eight musicians convened by the SFJazz organization. His Discogs credits list three live SFJAZZ Records releases: Live In New York 2011 (Music Of Stevie Wonder), Live At SFJazz Center 2014 (Music Of Joe Henderson), and Live: SFJazz Center 2017 (Music Of Thelonious Monk, Ornette Coleman, Stevie Wonder). Other Collective members across these releases include Miguel Zenon, Robin Eubanks, Avishai E. Cohen, Matt Penman, David Sanchez (3), Warren Wolf, and Obed Calvaire.

Venezuelan Suite (Sunnyside, 2013) is a landmark recording: a suite drawing directly on Venezuelan folk traditions (joropo, llanera) and featuring an all-Venezuelan cast — Jorge Glem on cuatro, Edmar Castaneda on harp, Marco Granados on flute, Luisito Quintero on percussion, Mark Turner (2) on tenor, Adam Cruz on drums — alongside bassist Roberto Koch and John Ellis on bass clarinet. Simon produced.

Steel House (ArtistShare, 2015) reunites the trio of Simon, Scott Colley, and Brian Blade.

Latin American Songbook (Sunnyside, 2016) is a trio date (with Joe Martin and Adam Cruz) built around Latin American standards; Simon arranged and wrote liner notes.

Sorrows & Triumphs (Sunnyside, 2018) is the most ambitious orchestral project in the Discogs record: Simon leads his Afinidad group (David Binney, Scott Colley, Brian Blade, Adam Rogers (2)) alongside Imani Winds (a classical wind quintet: Valerie Coleman, flute; Mark Dover, clarinet; Toyin Spellman-Diaz, oboe; Jeffrey Scott, French horn; Monica Ellis, bassoon), with Gretchen Parlato contributing lyrics and vocals on two tracks.

2019–Present

A 2019 Red Record release is credited as “Victor Lewis Feat. Ed Simon” alongside Eddie Henderson, Seamus Blake, Terell Stafford, Chris McBride, and Ed Howard.

25 Years (Ridgeway Records, 2020) marks a career retrospective. Solo Live (Ridgeway Records, 2021) captures Simon in solo piano format.

Three Visitors (GroundUP Music, 2024) is a trio with Scott Colley and Brian Blade — the core of several prior Simon projects distilled to its essentials. The album signals an ongoing creative partnership with those two musicians spanning more than twenty years.

Femeninas (Songs Of Latin American Women) (ArtistShare, undated in DB) remains a project without a confirmed release year in Discogs.
