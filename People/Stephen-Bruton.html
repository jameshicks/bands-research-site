Stephen Bruton

Born: November 7, 1948, Wilmington, Delaware, USA Died: May 9, 2009, Los Angeles, California, USA Real name: Turner Stephen Bruton Discogs ID: 724043

American guitarist, mandolin player, singer-songwriter, and record producer, raised in Fort Worth, Texas. Best known as the primary producer and guitarist on Alejandro Escovedo’s first three solo albums (Gravity, Thirteen Years, With These Hands) and as a longtime sideman and co-writer for Kris Kristofferson. A foundational figure in the Austin, Texas roots-music world across five decades, Bruton worked across country, folk, blues, and Americana with a remarkable breadth of collaborators. He died in 2009 of cancer, and his final production credit — the Crazy Heart film soundtrack — was co-produced with T-Bone Burnett and released posthumously.

Career

Early Work — Kristofferson Band (1969–1980s)

Bruton’s first Discogs-documented credit is as a guitarist on See Your Way Clear (Dot Records, 1969). By the early 1970s he was an established session and touring guitarist in the Nashville/Austin orbit. He appears on Kris Kristofferson’s Jesus Was A Capricorn (Monument, 1972) — on which he also received a co-writing credit for “Enough For You” (with Kristofferson and Donnie Fritts) — and Border Lord (Monument, 1972), where he is credited for acoustic guitar, electric guitar, backing vocals, and co-writing. He remained part of Kristofferson’s live and recording band through the decade, appearing on Shake Hands With The Devil (Monument, 1979) and the Natural Act album (A&M, 1978, with Rita Coolidge).

In 1973 Bruton took his first producer credit on Live At The New Blue Bird Nite Club (Blue Royal). He also appeared on the A Star Is Born soundtrack (Columbia, 1976) as a member of The Speedway, Kristofferson’s backing band in the film. The band also appeared on Full Moon (A&M, 1973) and Spooky Lady’s Sideshow (Monument, 1974) in this period.

Session and Touring Work (1974–1990)

Throughout the 1970s and 1980s Bruton appeared on a wide range of sessions. Notable credits include:

  • Gene Clark — No Other (Asylum, 1974): guitar
  • Bob Neuwirth — Bob Neuwirth (Asylum, 1974): performer
  • Lowell George — Thanks I’ll Eat It Here (Warner Bros., 1979): performer
  • T.K. Hulin — Truth Decay (Takoma, 1980) and Gospel Nights (Takoma, 1980)
  • T-Bone Burnett — Behind The Trap Door (Demon Records, 1984): musician, producer; Proof Through The Night (Warner Bros., 1983–2007 reissue): musician
  • The Songwriter film soundtrack (CBS, 1984): performer (musicians ensemble)
  • Kris Kristofferson — Repossessed (Mercury, 1986): guitar, vocals; A Moment Of Forever (Justice, 1995)
  • Nanci Griffith — Lone Star State Of Mind (not listed separately but within this orbit)
  • Rodney Crowell — Street Language (Columbia, 1986)
  • Jimmie Dale Gilmore — After Awhile (Elektra/Nonesuch, 1991): producer, acoustic guitar, electric guitar, slide guitar, mandolin, harmony vocals — this was Gilmore’s debut major-label album

The 1991 Jimmie Dale Gilmore record is Bruton’s first fully credited solo production of wide recognition: he served as sole producer and played most of the stringed instruments while Dave McNair engineered and mixed.

The Escovedo Albums (1992–1996)

Bruton’s most concentrated creative legacy is his three-album run producing Alejandro Escovedo:

Gravity (Watermelon Records, 1992) — Bruton produced, played slide guitar, acoustic guitar, electric guitar, mandolin, and sang harmony vocals on tracks 1–8, 10. The core rhythm section was bassist Terry Wilson (3) and two drummers: Dennis Kenmore (tracks 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10) and Barry “Frosty” Smith (tracks 2, 4, 6, 11). Dave McNair recorded and mixed individual tracks, and Bill Ginn played piano. String color came from cellist John Hagen (2) and bassist Spencer Starnes on upright. Additional harmony from Lou Ann Barton, JD Foster, Bruce Robison, and Charlie Robison. Mastered by Bernie Grundman.

Thirteen Years (Watermelon Records, 1993) — Bruton produced again; Dave McNair recorded and mixed. Bruton also received a co-writing credit on track B6. The album expanded the orchestral palette and featured a string ensemble including David Perales and others. Bruton played no instruments on this release (at least not credited individually); his role was purely production.

With These Hands (Rykodisc, 1996) — Third and final Escovedo production. Dan Bosworth and Jay Hudson recorded; Joe Gastwirt mastered. Stephen Barber provided string arrangements. Bruton credited as producer (as “T.S. Bruton” on this pressing).

The three albums established the template for Austin-rooted alternative country production in the 1990s: acoustic and electric guitar blended with pedal steel, strings, and chamber textures, all recorded with a warm but not over-polished sound.

Parallel Productions (1995–1999)

While producing the Escovedo trilogy, Bruton maintained an active parallel production career:

  • Sue Foley — Big City Blues (Antone’s Records, 1995): producer, tremolo guitar, mandolin, rhythm guitar, vocals; Walk In The Sun (Antone’s Records / Discovery, 1996): producer, acoustic guitar, baritone guitar, mandolin
  • Chris Smither — Small Revelations (Hightone, 1997): producer, guitar (track 6), rhythm guitar (tracks 1, 3, 10), banjo (track 10). Engineered by Jay Hudson. Mickey Raphael on harmonica.
  • Hal Ketchum — Awaiting Redemption (Curb, 1999): producer, slide guitar, mandolin, backing vocals. Dan Bosworth engineered.
  • Kelly Willis — Dog Years (Atlantic, 1998): producer
  • Hellbillies (Norwegian country band) — Urban Twang (WEA Norway, 2001): producer, electric guitar, 12-string acoustic guitar; Røta - Hellbillies’ Beste (WEA, 2006): producer — a Norwegian greatest hits collection he co-produced

He also appeared as a sideman on Bonnie Raitt’s Luck Of The Draw (Capitol, 1991): acoustic guitar and backing vocals; and on the Vince Bell album Phoenix (Watermelon, 1994): guitar and mandolin alongside T-Bone Burnett (liner notes), Geoff Muldaur, John Cale (piano), Lyle Lovett, and Victoria Williams.

Solo Recordings

Bruton released recordings under his own name throughout the 1990s and 2000s:

  • What It Is (Dos Records, 1993) — all songs written by Bruton; he played acoustic guitar, electric guitar, slide guitar, mandolin, and sang. Produced by Tom Canning. Featured Kris Kristofferson on backing vocals (track 10), Bonnie Raitt on liner notes and backing vocals (track 4), Lou Ann Barton (track 3), Reese Wynans (organ), Glen Clark (organ), Ricky Fataar (drums), Terry Wilson (3) (bass). Engineer: Dave McNair.
  • Right On Time (Dos Records, 1995) — primary-credited release, minimal additional credits in the DB
  • Spirit World (New West Records, 2002) — co-produced with Mark Goldenberg
  • From The Five (New West Records, 2005) — fully credited solo album; Bruton played acoustic guitar, baritone guitar, electric guitar, mandolin, and sang. Band included Steve Ferrone (drums), Bill Payne (organ/piano), Randy Jacobs (rhythm guitar), Yoggie Musgrove (bass), Stephen Barber (keyboards). Produced and engineered by Ross Hogarth; mastered by Gavin Lurssen.

He was also a primary member of The Resentments (Discogs ID linked to artist group 1505212) — an Austin collective that recorded The Resentments (Freedom Records, 2003) and Switcheroo (Freedom Records, 2005), and participated in the Geoff Muldaur project Geoff Muldaur And The Texas Sheiks (Texas Sheiks, Tradition & Moderne, 2009).

Late Career and the Crazy Heart Soundtrack

Bruton continued session work in the 2000s: Kris Kristofferson’s This Old Road (New West, 2006): guitar, mandolin, harmony vocals; Closer To The Bone (New West, 2009): guitar, mandolin, backing vocals. He appeared on the Band of Heathens’ The Band of Heathens (Blue Rose Records, 2008) as a guest musician (mandolin).

His final major production was the Crazy Heart film soundtrack (Fox Music / New West Records, 2010), co-produced with T-Bone Burnett. Bruton died on May 9, 2009 — the soundtrack was released in 2010, after his death. The album won multiple Grammy awards.

His songwriting extended beyond his own recordings: the Escovedo tribute album Por Vida (Or Music, 2004) included covers of Bruton co-compositions. Kris Kristofferson’s Essential (Columbia / Legacy, 2004) includes material Bruton co-wrote.

  • The Crazy Heart soundtrack relationship: Bruton co-produced it with T-Bone Burnett; how much of the music was tracked before his death in May 2009 vs. completed afterward is not determinable from Discogs data.
