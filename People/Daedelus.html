Overview

Alfred Weisberg-Roberts is an electronic producer from Santa Monica, California, born in 1977. Working under the name Daedelus, he built the bulk of his catalogue across the 2000s — 21 releases in that decade alone — moving from early homes at Plug Research and Mush to a substantial run on Ninja Tune (2005–2011) before shifting toward Brainfeeder and later Dome of Doom Records. His 266 total credits span production, composition, DJ mixing, and live performance across Experimental, Downtempo, IDM, and Hip Hop, with a collaborator network rooted in the LA beat scene: Daddy Kev (22 shared credits), Busdriver (11), and Carlos Niño (7) sit alongside jazz players including Ben Wendel, Vivek Menon, and the group Kneebody (9 each), pointing to an unusually wide reach across the city’s underground.

Notes (web)

Departure from Ninja Tune after Bespoke (2011)

No public statement from either Daedelus or Ninja Tune explicitly addresses whether he was dropped, chose to leave, or had his contract not renewed. The available record is one-sided: Daedelus framed the move to Anticon for Drown Out (2013) in terms of creative fit rather than a falling-out or business decision.

In an interview with The Skinny (October 2013) around the Drown Out release, Daedelus said: “Not to put anyone into a box, but Ninja Tune is known for a certain kind of dance music. They definitely like their guests, and they have certain interests.” He contrasted this with Anticon’s willingness to let artists “explore places which don’t get that much breath,” citing Baths as an example of an artist able to pursue emotional complexity on the label. He described Drown Out — a grief-influenced record made in the aftermath of his grandmother’s death and the death of close friend Austin Peralta — as “such an emotionally-driven record, it kind of transcends the current musical zeitgeist,” implying Ninja Tune’s aesthetic lane was not the right container for it. (theskinny.co.uk)

The XLR8R “Handy Dandy” interview (undated, appears pre-Drown Out) describes a positive working relationship with Ninja Tune on Love to Make Music To (2008) — the label gave direct feedback (“We’re down with it, we signed you for a reason, but how about this…?”) which Daedelus called constructive — with no indication of friction. (xlr8r.com)

Summary: The available evidence suggests Daedelus chose to leave (or chose not to pursue another record with Ninja Tune) for creative reasons specific to Drown Out’s emotional direction. No source claims he was dropped or that the split was acrimonious. Neither Ninja Tune nor Daedelus made any public statement about the end of the relationship. The 2012 release Looking Ocean appeared on Scion Audio/Visual (a one-off promotional imprint), consistent with being between label commitments.
