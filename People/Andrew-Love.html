Andrew Love

Discogs artist ID: 271652 Real name: Andrew Maurice Love Born: 21 November 1942, Memphis, Tennessee Died: 12 April 2012

Tenor saxophonist; one half of The Memphis Horns (co-founded 1969 with Wayne Jackson). One of the most-recorded horn section musicians in American popular music, with credits spanning Stax Records soul (1965–1968), the complete Hi Records catalog produced by Willie Mitchell (1969–1981), Atlantic soul and R&B sessions, and a wide range of rock and pop recordings from the 1970s through the 1990s. Name variations on record include A. Love, A. M. Love, Andrew Lowe, and “Memphis Horns Andrew Love Sax.”

Biography

Early Career and Stax Sessions (1963–1968)

Love began appearing on Memphis recordings in the early 1960s. His earliest Discogs-documented credit is a 1963 Savoy Records session (tenor saxophone, uncredited). By 1965 he was playing on Stax Records productions for Otis Redding, credited on Otis Blue / Otis Redding Sings Soul (Volt, 1965) — one of the most-pressed Stax albums in history (54+ pressings in that year’s US edition alone). He continued on Otis Redding sessions through The Soul Album (1966) and Love Man (1969, Atco Records).

At Stax he became part of the extended horn section working under arranger/producer direction. During this period he also appeared on:

  • The Sound of Wilson Pickett (Atlantic, 1967) — Wilson Pickett’s second Atlantic LP, recorded partly at FAME Studios
  • Aretha Now (Atlantic, 1968) — Aretha Franklin session with engineer Tom Dowd
  • This Is Clarence Carter (Atlantic, 1968)

He also co-wrote “Keep On Dancing” (Argo, 1963), receiving a written-by credit on that early career session.

Hi Records and Willie Mitchell (1968–1981)

The central period of Love’s career ran through Willie Mitchell’s productions at Hi Records and Royal Recording Studios in Memphis. Love first appears on a Willie Mitchell session on Willie Mitchell Live (Hi Records, 1968), and from 1969 onward was the tenor saxophone constant on virtually every Hi session Mitchell produced.

Alongside Wayne Jackson (trumpet), they formed the core of the horn section — the duo that became The Memphis Horns as a billing. The expanded Hi horn section also included James Mitchell (baritone saxophone), Ed Logan (tenor saxophone), and Jack Hale, Sr. (trombone). The Hi Rhythm Section — Leroy Hodges (bass), Mabon “Teenie” Hodges (guitar), Charles Hodges (organ/piano), Howard Grimes (drums) — was the invariant rhythm bed beneath them.

Memphis Horns as Recording Act (1969–2001)

The Memphis Horns issued recordings under their own name beginning with a single on Cotillion in 1969, followed by the LP Memphis Horns (Cotillion, 1970). The duo recorded for RCA in the mid-to-late 1970s — a run of five albums including Get Up And Dance (1975), High On Music (1976), Just For Your Love (1977), and Band II (1978) — during which they expanded into a larger ensemble. Later recordings include Midnight Stroll (Mercury, 1990), Flame Out (Lucky Seven Records, 1992), and Wayne Jackson & Andrew Love With Special Guests (Telarc, 1995).

Love also co-produced and contributed to Horns For Everything (Million Records Inc., 1972) as saxophone and producer, and received a production credit on Soul Bowl (Cotillion, 1970) and I Can’t Turn You Loose / Time (Cotillion, 1969).

Later Career (1993–2012)

Love continued session work through the 1990s and 2000s, including Al Green’s I Can’t Stop (Blue Note, 2003) — the reunion record with Willie Mitchell — and Don’t Look Back (RCA/BMG, 1993) for Al Green. He appeared on Toots Thielemans’ Toots In Memphis (Mango, 1988), playing alto and tenor saxophone solos. He also played on Luther Allison’s Two-Fisted Mama! (Alligator, 1989) and Soul Fixin’ Man (Alligator, 1994).

The Memphis Horns continued to work as a billed ensemble, appearing on Eddie Hinton’s Very Blue Highway (Bullseye Blues, 1993), and on records by Widespread Panic, You Am I, and others.

Love died on 12 April 2012 in Memphis.
