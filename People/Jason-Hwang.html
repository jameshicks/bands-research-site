Jason Hwang

Real name: Jason Kao Hwang. American violinist, composer, and educator. Discogs artist ID: 282229. Active in New York avant-garde and free improvisation from the late 1970s onward. One of a small number of violinists to carve out a sustained career in the downtown free jazz and creative music scenes alongside Billy Bang and Leroy Jenkins.

Known name variations: Hwang, J. Hwang, Jason K. Hwang, Jason Kao Hwang, Jason Wong.

Personal website: https://jasonkaohwang.com/

Career Overview

Hwang emerged from the New York loft jazz scene in the late 1970s, co-founding the ensemble Commitment around 1980 — an early outlet that he both played and produced for. His first documented recording credit is the group’s self-released LP on Flying Panda Records (1980), featuring William Parker on bass.

His early profile was built largely through session work with major free jazz figures. The landmark credit is Billy Bang’s Outline No. 12 (1982, Celluloid Records) — a large-ensemble conduction by Butch Morris that brought together David Murray, Frank Lowe, Khan Jamal, Sunny Murray, Charles Tyler, and Wilber Morris. Hwang contributed violin alongside Joseph Hailes in the string section. The session was recorded by Martin Bisi.

Through the 1980s and into the early 1990s Hwang became a regular in Butch Morris’s conduction projects: he appears on Homeing (1989, Sound Aspects Records) and Dust To Dust (1991, New World Records). The latter brought him into contact with an extended Morris ensemble including J.A. Deane, Zeena Parkins, Wayne Horvitz, Myra Melford, Andrew Cyrille, and Marty Ehrlich.

In 1991 Hwang co-led the international improvising ensemble X-Communication on the FMP album of the same name — one of his primary leadership credits. The group was a Butch Morris conduction project with a pan-European and American cast: Morris on cornet, Shelley Hirsch (vocals), J.A. Deane (trombone/electronics), Hans Koch (reeds), Paul Lovens (drums), Hans Reichel (guitar/daxophone/cello), and Martin Schütz (cello). The record was produced by Jost Gebers on FMP.

That same year he appeared on the Intakt Records album Approximations (1990, released 1990) — a Hans Koch / Martin Schütz date also featuring Shelley Hirsch, Tom Cora, Andrew Cyrille, Pippin Barnett, and Butch Morris, recorded by Martin Bisi.

The Far East Side Band (1990–2004)

Hwang’s most sustained ensemble project was The Far East Side Band, which he led from roughly 1990 to 2004. The group integrated free improvisation with traditional Asian instruments and sensibilities, and drew on the Asian American avant-garde community. Members:

  • Jason Kao Hwang — electric violin, electronic processing
  • Sang Won Park — gayageum (kayagum), ajang (zither), voice
  • Yukio Tsuji — taiko drums, shakuhachi, percussion
  • Joe Daley — tuba, electronics

The band released two albums:

ReleaseYearLabelNotes
Caverns1994New World RecordsHwang plays five-string electric violin with electronic processing

Caverns was produced by Hwang; Urban Archaeology was released on the Canadian Victo label (home of VICTO festival recordings). Both showcase the band’s blend of Korean, Japanese, and African American improvisational languages.

Henry Threadgill / Axiom Sessions (1993–1994)

Hwang played violin (on tracks 2 and 4) on Henry Threadgill’s Too Much Sugar For A Dime (1993, Axiom), a Very Very Circus record produced by Bill Laswell alongside Threadgill. The same ensemble — with Leroy Jenkins and Simon Shaheen also on violin — appeared on Blues In The East (1994, Axiom).

The Floating Box (2005)

Hwang’s chamber opera The Floating Box: A Story in Chinatown (2005, New World Records) is his largest compositional statement on record. He composed and produced; libretto by Catherine Filloux. The work was named one of the top ten opera recordings of 2005 by Opera News. The ensemble included:

  • Tomas Ulrich — cello
  • Min Xiao-Fen — pipa
  • Wang Guo-Wei — gaohu, erhu, zhonghu
  • Patti Monson — flute
  • William Schimmel — accordion
  • Michiyo Suzuki — clarinet/bass clarinet
  • Satoshi Takeishi — percussion
  • Diana Herold — vibraphone/percussion
  • Juan Carlos Rivas — conductor

Later Career / Continuing Work

  • Played violin on Anthony Braxton’s massive opera Trillium E (2011, New Braxton House) — Taylor Ho Bynum conducting/assistant conducting, ensemble of ~70 performers.
  • Appeared on Taylor Ho Bynum Plustet’s Enter The Plustet (2016, Firehouse 12 Records) alongside Mary Halvorson, Tomeka Reid, Tomas Fujiwara, Ingrid Laubrock, and others.
  • Guest on William Parker’s The Music of William Parker: Migration of Silence Into and Out of the Tone World (Volumes 1–10) (2021, Centering Records).
  • The Discogs profile (as of the DB snapshot) mentions his EDGE quartet on Asian Improv Records (Taylor Ho Bynum, Andrew Drury, Ken Filiano), and ongoing work with Pheeroan Aklaff’s Malcheck Muzikum.
  • Member of many ensembles including the Reggie Workman Ensemble, Sirone Sextet, Taylor Ho Bynum Plustet, and Steve Swell’s Nation of We.
