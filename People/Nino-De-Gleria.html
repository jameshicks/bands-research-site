Nino De Gleria

Slovenian bassist, mandolinist, arranger, producer, and actor. Discogs artist ID: 317107. Active from the early 1980s through at least the mid-2000s across Yugoslav and Slovenian new wave, jazz, folk-rock, theatre, and experimental contexts. Best known internationally as a member of Begnagrad and for his appearance on Nimal’s Dis Tanz (1992, Ayaa).

Known name variations on releases: De Gleria, N. De Gleria, NDG, Nino, Nino DeGleria, Nino Degleria.

Overview

Nino De Gleria emerged in Ljubljana’s early-1980s experimental music scene. He joined Begnagrad — the avant-folk/new wave ensemble led by Bratko Bibič — for the band’s 1982 self-titled LP on ZKP RTVL, playing electric bass, acoustic bass, mandolin, whistle, and contributing vocals. He co-composed two tracks (nos. 6 and 7) on the 1990 CD compilation Konzert For A Broken Dance (also released on Ayaa), alongside core Begnagrad members Bratko Bibič, Bogo Pečnikar, Boris Romih, and Aleš Rendla.

Through the mid-to-late 1980s he played with several other Yugoslav experimental and jazz ensembles: Srp (3) (1984), Quatebriga (1985, as bassist and co-writer), and contributed bass as a guest on the Videosex LP Lacrimae Christi (1985, ZKP RTVL). He also appeared on the Kladivo Konj In Voda LP Vidov Ples (1984, PGP RTB) and on Svetlana Makarovič’s Dajdamski Portreti (1985, Dokumentarna).

In 1992 he appeared on Nimal’s Dis Tanz (France, Ayaa) — credited for mixing alongside Bratko Bibič and Jean-Maurice Rossel, linking Begnagrad’s Yugoslav-era network to the Geneva-based RIO scene.

From the 1990s onward De Gleria worked heavily as a session player and arranger/producer in Slovenian pop and chanson contexts: children’s music (Mia Žnidarič’s Mia In Škrat Sanjavec, 1993, in which he served as composer, arranger, and producer), folk-pop (Hiša, 1994), and several albums for Slovenian singer-comedian Adi Smolar on Helidon (1995–1999). He also appeared on Jan Plestenjak’s Pogrešal Te Bom (1996, ZKP RTVS). In 2001 he contributed bass to Fake Orchestra’s Fake Life (Goga), and in 2014 he played bass on one track of Borut Kržišnik’s Currents Of Time (Claudio Contemporary, UK).

Discogs lists him as a member of Begnagrad, Srp (3), Quatebriga, Fake Orchestra, Boris Cavazza Kvartet, Bratko Bibič & The Madleys, Brina, Orkester Šviga Švaga, and Štefbet Rifi.
