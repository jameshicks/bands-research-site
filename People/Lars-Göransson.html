Lars Göransson

American recording engineer, producer, and occasional musician based in Austin, Texas. Discogs artist ID: 461121. Active from at least 1993 through 2025.

Note on identity: The brief that prompted this research assumed Lars Göransson was a Swedish guitarist and member of The Hives. That is incorrect. The Discogs entry for this Lars Göransson explicitly states he is an American engineer working in Austin, TX. There are multiple Swedish artists named Lars Göransson in the database (see Discogs IDs 5079458, 3469852, 4100103); none are connected to The Hives. The Hives’ six members are Per Almqvist (vocalist), Niklas Almqvist, Johan Gustafsson, Mattias Bernvall, Mikael Karlsson Åström, and Christian Grahn.

Overview

Lars Göransson is a career studio professional anchored to the Austin, TX independent and alternative-country scene. His primary base was Music Lane (Austin), with additional work at Sounds Outrageous (also Austin). He operated a production company called Hard Pawnshop Daddy (Discogs: 2504566). His discography spans punk/hardcore, alternative country, Americana, and one notable excursion into major-label Swedish pop.

Discogs name variations: Goransson, Lars, Lars Garansson, Lars Gorannson, Lars Goransen, Lars Goransson, Lars Gorranson, Lars Gorransson, Lars Göranson, Mr. McGoo.

Career by Era

Early Work — Austin Scene (1993–1999)

Göransson’s first credit is as co-engineer on Startime (1993, Unclean Records) by The Flying Saucers (3), a garage-rock act, alongside John Croslin of the Reivers. By the mid-1990s he was established enough at Music Lane to take producer credits.

The most prominent credit from this period is [[Releases/Jean Caffeine - Knocked Down 7 Times Got Up 8|Jean Caffeine’s Knocked Down 7 Times Got Up 8]] (1996, Joe Records), where he engineered tracks 1, 2, 5, 8, and 12, co-produced five tracks, mastered the album, and played bass and guitar on several tracks. The album drew on a wide pool of Austin musicians including Jon Dee Graham, Joey Shuffield (Fastball), Tony Scalzo (Fastball), Gurf Morlix, and Dennis Ku. This was a fully Austin-local project — the “transatlantic connection to country-punk” framing in the research brief is a misreading; Göransson is American.

In 1998–1999 he appears as “Assistant Hi-tech Engineer” on three major Cardigans releases recorded in Sweden with producer Tore Johansson: the Erase/Rewind single (Stockholm Records, 1998), the album Gran Turismo (Mercury/Stockholm, 1998), and the Gran Turismo Overdrive EP (1999), plus the Hanging Around single (1999). How he came to be attached to those Swedish sessions is not explained by Discogs alone — this is the genuine unexplained transatlantic connection in his resume. He may have traveled to Sweden to work at Tore Johansson’s Tambourine Studios, or the sessions may have had Austin-side overdubs.

Other late-1990s engineering credits: Power Songs For The Kidz (Havoc Records, 1999), Severed Head Of State (Lengua Armada Discos, 1999), Second Time Around (Dialtone Records, 1999).

Hardcore and Punk Production (2001–2004)

Göransson became a go-to engineer for Austin-area and national hardcore/punk/crust acts released on labels including Hardcore Holocaust Records, Ebullition Records, Prank Records, and No Idea Records.

Key credits:

  • An Invitation to a Beheading… (Ebullition, 2001) — recorded, mixed
  • No Love Lost (Ebullition, 2001) — recorded
  • Human Meat…Tossed To The Dogs Of War (Prank, 2002) — recorded, mixed
  • The Art Of Self-Destruction (Prank, 2002) — recorded
  • The Sucking Of The Missile Cock (Hardcore Holocaust, 2002) — recorded
  • World Burns To Death (Fight Records, 2002) — recorded, mixed
  • Anathema Device (Hardcore Holocaust, 2003) — producer, engineer
  • No Dawn Comes… Night Without End (Hardcore Holocaust, 2003) — recorded
  • Society Is A Carnivorous Flower — J Church (No Idea Records, 2004) — producer, engineer, mixed, mastered (full package)
  • Disclose / World Burns To Death split (Dan-Doh, 2004) — engineer

He also took on full production/musician roles on more eclectic projects: Too Soon To Say (Lilywhite Records, 2004) where he played percussion, vocals, keyboards and arranged strings/horns, and produced/programmed.

Americana, Blues, and Country Work (2004–present)

Göransson maintained a parallel track in Austin’s Americana and blues world throughout, working repeatedly with artists on Dialtone Records, Blue Rose Records (Germany), and San Jacinto Records.

Recurring clients include:

  • Releases on Dialtone Records (Austin): Second Time Around (1999), Blues & Soul Situation (2004), Houston’s Third Ward Blues (2006), I’m Alright (2011), Now Dig This! (2021)
  • Releases on Blue Rose Records (2) (Germany): Buried Treasures (2012), Tombstone (2014), My Way Or The Highway (2017, where he also played flute and synthesizer), Back To The Garden (2019, guitar/keyboards/handclaps plus full engineer/mix/master), Exiled On Mabel St. (2022, co-producer)
  • The Star Apple Kingdom (label): Future Clouds & Radar (2007), Peoria (2008), Kontiki (2012), Death Of The Cool (2016), Wild Kingdom (2017)
  • Enchanted Rock (San Jacinto Records, 2014) — mixed, mastered
  • Heart And Soul (San Jacinto Records, 2025) — musician, recorded, mixed, mastered

Other notable credits: Mother Spain & The Wayfaring Myth (1998, Graham Williams) — mastered, mixed, recorded; Constellation (Tapete Records, Germany, 2009) — mixed.

Connections

  • Jean Caffeine — The most documented creative collaboration. Göransson co-produced half of Knocked Down 7 Times Got Up 8, engineered those same tracks, mastered the album, and played bass and guitar on multiple tracks. This is a fully Austin-local connection, not a transatlantic one; both are Austin-based.

  • The Cardigans (Swedish connection) — The only genuinely unexplained transatlantic element. Göransson appears as assistant hi-tech engineer on Gran Turismo (1998) and associated singles. The Cardigans recorded that album in Malmö with producer Tore Johansson. How an Austin TX engineer came to be attached to a Swedish major-label pop record is not addressed by Discogs.

  • Austin scene nexus — His web of collaborators on the Jean Caffeine album (Jon Dee Graham, Gurf Morlix, Joey Shuffield/Tony Scalzo of Fastball, Dennis Ku) maps neatly onto the mid-1990s Austin Americana/alternative-country world.

  • Hardcore production cluster — Separate career strand, 2001–2004, producing/engineering for Hardcore Holocaust Records, Ebullition, Prank, and No Idea artists. J Church’s Society Is A Carnivorous Flower (2004) is the most substantial entry.

  • Hard Pawnshop Daddy — Production company entity (Discogs: 2504566) operated by Göransson.

  • NOT a member of The Hives — The brief’s premise was incorrect. The Hives are a Swedish garage-rock band from Fagersta with no documented connection to this Lars Göransson. The Hives member named in that context does not exist in Discogs.

Open Questions

  1. How did an Austin, TX engineer end up as assistant hi-tech engineer on Gran Turismo (The Cardigans, Malmö, 1998)? Tore Johansson connection? Austin recording sessions for overdubs? Not answerable from Discogs alone.
  2. The second studio affiliation, Sounds Outrageous (ID 559082), has minimal Discogs data — unclear what it was.
  3. Several Blue Rose Records (2) credits list only a genre/country but no primary artist name in the discography pull — full context of those Americana clients requires individual release lookups.
  4. The “Mr. McGoo” name variation is unexplained.
  5. The Discogs profile mentions he “operated a production company named Hard Pawnshop Daddy” but that entity has no release credits of its own in the DB.
