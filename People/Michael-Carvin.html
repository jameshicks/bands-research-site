Michael Carvin

Michael Carvin

Discogs ID: 280676 Real name: Michael W. Carvin Born: 12 December 1944, Houston, Texas, USA Alias: Thabo Vincar Name variations: Carvin, M. Carvin, Michael Thabo Carvin, Mike Carvin, Mike Carvin (Tabo), Thabo Michael Carvin

American jazz drummer and percussionist. A veteran of the New York post-bop and free jazz scenes from the early 1970s onward, Carvin worked as a sideman for an exceptionally wide range of leaders — from spiritual jazz (Lonnie Liston Smith, Cecil McBee) to hard bop and post-bop (Freddie Hubbard, Jackie McLean, Gerry Mulligan) to funk-adjacent sessions (Night Life on Gordy). He led his own records through the 1970s–90s, principally on SteepleChase and Muse Records. A Vietnam veteran, he was one of the musicians Billy Bang specifically recruited for his two Vietnam War suites, both recorded for Justin Time.

Biography

Early Career and the Black Jazz / Strata-East Scene (1971–1974)

Carvin’s earliest documented Discogs appearances place him in the Los Angeles spiritual jazz orbit associated with the Black Jazz Records roster. He played drums on Doug Carn’s Infant Eyes (1971, Black Jazz Records) — a devotional session with Jean Carn on vocals — and on Henry Franklin’s The Skipper (1972, Black Jazz Records), where he is credited as “Mike Carvin (Tabo)”, one of several Afrocentric name variations he used in this period. Both records were produced by Gene Russell.

By 1974 he was appearing in New York-connected contexts. He played gong and percussion (not drums) on Cecil McBee’s Mutima (1974, Strata-East) — a sprawling spiritual date that also featured Dee Dee Bridgewater on vocals, Onaje Allan Gumbs on piano, and Billy Hart on percussion. That same year he appeared on Voyage To Uranus (1974, Capitol Records) by Atmospheres (2) featuring Clive Stevens — a jazz-fusion date with John Abercrombie on guitar and Ralph Towner on electric piano.

SteepleChase and the Copenhagen Sessions (1975–1978)

The mid-1970s were Carvin’s most intensive period as both a leader and working sideman. He recorded two albums under his own name for the Danish label SteepleChase:

  • Antiquity (1975, SteepleChase) — co-billed as Jackie McLean & Michael Carvin, recorded in Copenhagen. On this session both McLean and Carvin contributed compositions and both played multiple instruments: Carvin on drums, temple block, bells, bamboo flute, voice, kalimba, and percussion. Produced by Nils Winther.
  • The Camel (1975/1976, SteepleChase / Inner City Records) — under the Michael Carvin Quintet name. Personnel: Sonny Fortune (alto and soprano sax), Cecil Bridgewater (trumpet, flugelhorn), Ron Burton (piano), Calvin Hill (bass). Again produced by Nils Winther in Copenhagen.

The Lonnie Liston Smith sessions are particularly notable: Carvin contributed a co-writing credit on Expansions (“B1”) and is also credited as mixer on Visions Of A New World, indicating a relationship that extended beyond pure sideman work.

The Freddie Hubbard Connection (1973, released later)

Carvin was part of Freddie Hubbard’s working quintet in 1973, appearing on several archival live releases documented much later: Live In France 1973 (released 2011, SteepleChase), Music Is Here (Live At Studio 104 Maison De La Radio ORTF Paris 1973) (released 2022, INA / Wewantsounds), and 1973: Live in Zonderschot (released 2017). The Hubbard quintet of this period featured Junior Cook (tenor sax), George Cables (electric piano), and Kent Brinkley (bass). These were prime-period post-bop documents of Hubbard at his most physically commanding.

The Muse Records Era (1988–1994)

After a gap in his leader discography through the 1980s, Carvin returned as a frontman with a sequence of records for Muse Records:

Cecil Bridgewater appears as producer on the first two Muse dates, and also plays trumpet and flugelhorn — a recurring collaborator across this period. The Between Me And You session was engineered by Rudy Van Gelder at his Englewood Cliffs studio.

During this same period Carvin also appeared on the Gerry Mulligan Quartet’s The Shadow Of Your Smile (1989, Moon Records), confirming his continued work as a hired sideman at the highest mainstream-jazz level.

The Billy Bang Vietnam Suites (2001, 2005)

Carvin is one of the core members of Billy Bang’s two Vietnam War suites for Justin Time, both of which drew specifically on veterans:

Both albums directly confront the Vietnam War from the perspective of veteran musicians. Butch Morris served as conductor on both sessions. Frank Lowe, James Spaulding, and Henry Threadgill appear as guest soloists. John Hicks anchors the piano chair on both dates. The two albums are among the most significant jazz documents to engage directly with Vietnam War experience from inside the veteran community.

Marsalis Music (2006)

Marsalis Music Honors Michael Carvin (2006, Marsalis Music / Rounder Records — release ID 13329268) was an explicit career tribute produced by Branford Marsalis, who also appears as tenor saxophonist on track 3. Personnel: Carlton Holmes (piano), Dezron Douglas (bass), Marcus Strickland (saxophone). The album’s existence as an “Honors” release in the Marsalis Music series signals institutional recognition of Carvin’s place in the jazz tradition.

Later Work

Carvin appeared as co-producer on Bobby Watson’s The Gates BBQ Suite (2010) and as producer on Tyshawn Sorey’s Mesmerism (2022, self-released) — a notably late credit connecting him to one of the most acclaimed younger jazz musicians of his generation.
