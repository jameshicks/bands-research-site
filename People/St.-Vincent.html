St. Vincent

Real name: Annie Erin Clark
Discogs ID: 366782
Origin: Tulsa, Oklahoma → Dallas, Texas → New York
Active: 2006–present
Labels: 4AD, Loma Vista

Singer, songwriter, guitarist, and producer. Studied at Berklee College of Music; later toured with Sufjan Stevens and The Polyphonic Spree before releasing solo work. Close long-term collaborator of John Congleton, who produced her breakthrough records.

Key Congleton-produced records:

  • Actor (2009, 4AD)
  • Strange Mercy (2011, 4AD)
  • Love This Giant (2012, 4AD) — with David Byrne
  • St. Vincent (2014, Loma Vista/Caroline) — won Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album

Notes (web)

Clark–Congleton collaboration origin

Clark and Congleton met while both working with The Polyphonic Spree — Congleton engineering, Clark playing guitar. Per SonicScoop: “The two met and became friends while working with The Polyphonic Spree, Congleton behind the board and Clark holding a guitar.” sonicscoop.com

The shared session was during the recording of The Fragile Army (2007), the last Polyphonic Spree album Clark appeared on as a member — reportedly recorded at Pachyderm Studio outside Minneapolis. (Source: search-result snippet from Dallas Observer, dallasobserver.com — full article 403-blocked; the snippet states Clark “got to know Congleton during that recording and thought he was great.“)

Before Actor, Congleton had also offered Clark candid feedback on her debut Marry Me (2007), telling her her vocals sounded “a little bit like a deer in headlights” due to her jazz-school focus on technique over emotion, and that “if we ever record together, I’m going to break you of that.” tingen.org/john-congleton-st-vincent/

Both Clark and Congleton are from Texas, a connection Clark acknowledged in a 2011 Consequence of Sound interview: “Congleton’s from Texas as well, and Polyphonic Spree…” consequence.net

The SonicScoop piece also notes that across their collaboration the working method evolved: on Actor Clark brought in fully formed orchestral arrangements; by Strange Mercy they were building songs from rough ideas in the studio; for St. Vincent (2014) Congleton took on more of a “classic producer kind of approach” with pre-production. sonicscoop.com

Sources checked (no additional detail found): Paul Tingen / tingen.org (covers St. Vincent 2014 only, not Actor origin); Consequence of Sound Behind the Boards 2022 interview with Congleton; Northern Transmissions Congleton interview; Tape Op issue 81 Congleton interview.
