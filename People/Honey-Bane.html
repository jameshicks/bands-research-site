Honey Bane

Stage name of Donna Tracy Boylan (born 4 November 1963, London). British singer and actress. Discogs artist ID: 261290. Name variations on record: Bane, Boylan, H. Bane, Honey Bane (Donna).

Biography

Fatal Microbes (1978–1979)

Honey Bane began her musical career at age 14 in 1978 when she co-founded the punk rock band Fatal Microbes in London alongside guitarist Pete Fender, bassist Scott Barker, and drummer Gem Stone (later also Dan Sansom and Dave Maltby). The band released a split 12” with anarcho-punk band Poison Girls in 1979 on Small Wonder Records (and the Xntrix imprint), with the Fatal Microbes contributing “Violence Grows” and “Beautiful Pictures” — both written by Bane, Fender, and Barker. The single received positive reviews from the British music press, notably Sounds.

The Fatal Microbes disbanded in 1979. Around this time Bane had a stint at the St. Charles Youth Treatment Centre in Essex, a juvenile detention facility, which drew further press attention.

Crass Records / Donna and The Kebabs (1979–1980)

After leaving the youth treatment facility and while on the run from social services, Bane began collaborating with the Essex anarcho-punk collective Crass. Under the name Donna & The Kebabs, with Crass members providing backing instrumentation (credited as “Kebabs” — Pete Wright on bass, Penny Rimbaud on drums, Phil Free (2) on lead guitar, Fingers Tarbuck on piano, N.A. Palmer on rhythm guitar), she recorded the EP You Can Be You. Engineered by John Loder, produced by Crass, it was released on Crass Records in 1979 (cat. 521984/1) — the first non-Crass release on that label. The record’s two matrix etchings are notably explicit and provocative, consistent with Crass’s anti-establishment aesthetic.

She also self-released the single “Guilty / Dub” (1980) on her own Honey Bane Records imprint (cat. hb 946), both sides written by herself, mastered by George Peckham (Porky). This was a dub-influenced post-punk 7”.

In February 1980, Bane sang vocals for Killing Joke on “What’s the Matter” at a gig at London’s Venue club. The recording later appeared on the unofficial live LP Live At The Venue (Square Records, 1982, credited as “Featuring [Encore]”).

Zonophone / EMI Pop Career (1981–1983)

In 1980, Jimmy Pursey (vocalist of Sham 69) began managing Bane’s career. She was signed to Zonophone (EMI subsidiary) for a five-year contract. Her debut Zonophone single, “Baby Love” (1981, Z 19) — a cover of the Holland-Dozier-Holland Motown classic — was produced by Alan Shacklock and designed by Assorted Images. It was released in Ireland on EMI and in Australia on EMI as well.

The follow-up, “Turn Me On Turn Me Off” (1981, Z 15), co-written by Bane, Pursey, and Peter Godwin, produced by Pursey with guitar by Godwin and engineering by Chris Stone, reached the UK Top 30. A third 1981 single, “Jimmy… (Listen To Me)” (Z 23), was a two-sided release with competing producers: the A-side produced by Colin Thurston, the B2 (“Negative Exposure”) co-written by Bane, Pursey and Godwin. Photography by Fin Costello.

The 1982 12” single “Wish I Could Be Me” (Z 32) shifted toward synth-pop, with the club version produced by Steve Levine and the B-side produced by Shacklock. The track was co-written by Bane and Jonathan Moore (6). Photography by Chris Dawes.

The final Zonophone release, “Dizzy Dreamers” (1983, Z 36), was produced by Nick Tauber and again co-written by Bane and Jonathan Moore. The B-sides “Io’s Burning” and “On Going Situation” were original tracks.

Later Work and Compilation (2006–2023)

Bane returned with the limited-edition 7” “Down Thing” / “Got Me All Wrong” (2006, Handsignal Recordings, Handy 4), produced and co-written with Mickey Howard.

In 2015 she released Acceptance of Existence (You Can Be You Records, YOUCANBEYOU01), a full album of original material produced and co-written with Mickey Howard, covering alternative rock and darkwave territory. The same year saw the retrospective compilation It’s A Baneful Life (The Anthology 1978–2015) (You Can Be You, UCBU01), executive produced by Bane and Anthony Mark Walton, who also handled digital restoration and artwork.

In 2023 a Record Store Day 12” reissue of Violence Grows (You Can Be You, YCYX002) was released under the credit “Honey Bane, Fatal Microbes,” featuring the original Fatal Microbes tracks alongside a new recording “Violence Grew.”

The You Can Be You EP has been reissued multiple times, including a 2022 reissue on Crass Records / One Little Independent Records.
