Irmin Schmidt

Keyboardist, composer, conductor. Born 29 May 1937, Berlin, Germany. Co-founder of Can. Discogs artist ID: 92183.

Background and Formation of Can

Irmin Schmidt was trained as a classical pianist and conductor in postwar Germany, studying under figures in the European contemporary classical tradition. By the mid-1960s he was active as a conductor and had encountered the New York avant-garde — meeting Holger Czukay and encountering the influence of composers such as Karlheinz Stockhausen was decisive for his pivot away from the concert hall. Around 1968, Schmidt and Czukay co-founded Can in Cologne together with drummer Jaki Liebezeit and guitarist Michael Karoli. The group operated communally and self-produced from the outset, building their own recording infrastructure — first at Schloss Nörvenich (a castle near Cologne) and later at the group’s own Inner Space Studio.

Schmidt’s role in Can was not purely instrumental. He was one of the primary organisational and conceptual forces in the group, and appears in Discogs credits with titles like “Adminaspace Co-Ordinator” on the debut album — a playful encoding of the band’s collective studio methodology. He co-authored the group’s history book All Gates Open – The Story Of Can (2018).

Can: The Studio Albums

Monster Movie (1969) — Can’s debut, released under the name “The Can.” Schmidt is credited as “Organ Laser” and “Adminaspace Co-Ordinator.” Vocalist on this record was Malcolm Mooney, a Black American artist the band encountered in Cologne. (Discogs release ID 2660567; master 7774.)

Soundtracks (1970) — Compilation of film music commissions, including work with early vocalist Damo Suzuki, who joined after Mooney’s departure. Schmidt: organ. (Discogs release ID 653389; master 11660.)

Tago Mago (1971) — Double album widely regarded as Can’s masterpiece and one of the foundational texts of krautrock and post-rock. Schmidt plays organ and electric piano throughout. Produced collectively by Can; no outside producer. Personnel: Czukay (bass), Liebezeit (drums), Karoli (guitar), Suzuki (vocals). (Discogs release ID 226132; master 11512; label United Artists Records.)

Ege Bamyasi (1972) — Follow-up; Schmidt extends his instrumental palette to organ, electric piano, violin, and steel guitar. Contains “Spoon,” which became a minor hit in Germany after placement on a TV crime series. (Discogs release ID 32139; master 11693.)

Future Days (1973) — Schmidt’s credit: “Keyboards.” Damo Suzuki’s final album with Can; the album moves toward a more ambient, flowing texture. Suzuki departed shortly after its completion. (Discogs release ID 330243; master 11765.)

Soon Over Babaluma (1974) — First album without a dedicated vocalist; Karoli and Schmidt shared vocal duties. Schmidt is credited with organ, electric piano, the Alpha 77 synthesizer, electronic percussion, piano, and vocals. (Discogs release ID 32140; master 11485.)

Landed (1975) — Keyboards, Alpha 77 synthesizer, vocals. (Master 11429; label Virgin.)

Flow Motion (1976) — Keyboards. (Master 11444; label Virgin.) The same year Can scored a UK chart entry with the single “I Want More” (co-written by Schmidt).

Saw Delight (1977) — Keyboards, Alpha 77, vocals. (Master 11460; label EMI Electrola / Harvest.) This record brought in new members Rosko Gee and Rebop Kwaku Baah from Traffic; the band’s cohesion began to fray.

Can (1978) — Keyboards, synthesizer; co-written by Schmidt. (Master 16897; label EMI Electrola / Harvest.)

Out Of Reach (1978) — Schmidt credited as writer; Liebezeit and Karoli largely led the recording. (Master 16257.)

Rite Time (1989) — The reunion album, recorded after a decade-long gap. Malcolm Mooney returned on vocals. Schmidt: keyboards and kalimba. Mixed by René Tinner. (Discogs release ID 264560; master 16239; label Mercury.)

Can: Archive and Live Releases

Schmidt was deeply involved in curation of the Can archive. He is credited as compiler, curator, and annotator on several posthumous releases:

  • Delay 1968 (1981, Spoon Records) — early recordings predating Monster Movie
  • Prehistoric Future (1984, Tago Mago label) — further early material
  • Cannibalism (1978) and Cannibalism 2 (1992) — compilations; Schmidt credited as re-editor and as writer/composer respectively
  • The Lost Tapes (2012, Spoon Records / Mute) — major three-disc excavation of unreleased studio material; Schmidt served as compilation producer and curator (Discogs release ID 3666496; master 447523)
  • Radio Waves (1997) — live/studio archival set; Schmidt credited with keyboards and Alpha 77 electronics
  • Live In Stuttgart 1975 (2021), Live In Brighton 1975 (2021), Live In Cuxhaven 1976 (2022), Live In Paris 1973 (2024), Live In Keele 1977 (2024) — ongoing series of official concert recordings issued via Mute / Spoon Records; Schmidt served as compiler and curator on all

Film Music

Schmidt began composing for film as early as 1969 — Kamasutra (1969, Metronome) features early soundtrack work. The Filmmusik series documents his sustained work in this area:

  • Filmmusik (1980, Base Record — Italy) — first solo release; Discogs ID 1628590
  • Filmmusik Vol. 2 (1981, Base Record) — composed, produced, arranged by Schmidt (ID 5406986)
  • Filmmusik Vol. 5 (1989, Virgin) — keyboards, composed by, arranged by, produced by Schmidt (ID 1291838)
  • Filmmusik Anthology Volume 4 & 5 (2009, Spoon Records) — retrospective of film scoring work

Rote Erde (1983, Teldec) was a title theme for a German television series.

Gormenghast (2000, Mute / Spoon Records) was his most ambitious orchestral work: a full score for the BBC television adaptation of Mervyn Peake’s novels. Schmidt composed, produced, and conducted the Brandenburg Symphonic Orchestra (Brandenburger Symphoniker); Jono Podmore (Kumo) served as sound engineer and producer; Michael Karoli contributed pre-recorded guitar; libretto by writer Duncan Fallowell. (Discogs release ID 179921; master 915576.)

Palermo Shooting (2008) — score for Wim Wenders’ film; released on City Slang / Mute. Schmidt: keyboards, composed by, produced. (Discogs release IDs 1559876, 1576694.)

Villa Wunderbar / A Selection (2013, Spoon Records) — career retrospective of his solo and film work with liner notes by Wim Wenders and Duncan Fallowell. (Discogs release ID 5066424.)

Solo Albums

Toy Planet (1981, Spoon Records) — Schmidt’s first collaborative solo album, made with Swiss saxophonist and synthesizer pioneer Bruno Spoerri. Schmidt: keyboards, synthesizer, composed by, arranged by, produced. Spoerri: Lyricon, saxophones, synthesizer. (Discogs release ID 552464; master 23310.)

Roll On, Euphrates (1986, WEA) — vocals, produced, arranged, music by Schmidt. (Discogs release ID 2816574; master 493017.)

Musk At Dusk (1987, WEA) — Schmidt’s most band-oriented solo album. Core players include Jaki Liebezeit (drums), Michael Karoli (guitar, also engineer), Trilok Gurtu (percussion), and bassist Franck Ema-Otu. Lyrics by Duncan Fallowell. Engineered and mixed by René Tinner and Ron Kurz. Schmidt: vocals, keyboards, synthesizer, producer, arranger. (Discogs release ID 1308885; master 255206.)

Impossible Holidays (1991, WEA) — another ensemble-style solo record. Liebezeit on drums, Karoli on guitar, with additional players including percussionist Steve Shehan, bandoneon player Juan José Mosalini, and flautist Geoff Warren. Schmidt: piano, vocals, synthesizer, composed by, arranged by, produced. Mixed by Gareth Jones. (Discogs release ID 1504006; master 23328.)

Masters Of Confusion (2001, Spoon Records) — first collaboration with Jono Podmore under his alias Kumo (real name Jono Podmore, Discogs ID 577). Schmidt: piano, keyboards, written-by, producer. Kumo: electronics, written-by, producer, recorded by, mixed by. (Discogs release ID 11393; master 530638.)

Axolotl Eyes (2008, Mute / Spoon Records) — second Schmidt & Kumo album; a multimedia project that included a sound installation commissioned by the Barbican Centre in London. Schmidt: piano, keyboards, slide guitar, sound designer, written-by, produced. Kumo: electronics, theremin, violin, film direction. (Discogs release ID 1503963; master 767689.)

5 Klavierstücke (2018, Spoon Records) — solo piano work; Schmidt plays prepared piano as well as conventional piano. Recorded by Gareth Jones; mixed by Schmidt and René Tinner. (Discogs release ID 12841274; master 1459022.)

Nocturne (2020, Mute / Spoon Records) — live recording at the Huddersfield Contemporary Music Festival; composed and produced by Schmidt. Recorded by Gareth Jones and René Tinner. (Discogs release ID 15383073; master 1746813.)

Cyclopean

In the 2010s Schmidt was part of Cyclopean (Discogs artist ID 3051769), an electronic group also comprising Jaki Liebezeit, Jono Podmore, and Bernd Friedmann. The project brought together Can veterans and contemporary electronic producers.

Other Collaborations and Credits

  • Lee Scratch Perry’s Vision Of Paradise (2016) — Schmidt appears as featured artist
  • The Killers’ Imploding The Mirage (2020) and A Tribe Called Quest’s We Got It From Here… (2016) both list Schmidt as a writer, attributing his Can compositions sampled therein
  • Jesus And Mary Chain’s April Skies (1987, Blanco Y Negro) credits Schmidt as written-by — a Can composition was sampled or licensed
  • Schmidt appeared as a voice actor (Popescu) in the German audio drama series Gabriel Burns (2003)

The recurring engineer and mixing collaborator across Schmidt’s solo work was René Tinner (Discogs ID 99808), who is credited on Musk At Dusk, Impossible Holidays, Rite Time, Axolotl Eyes, 5 Klavierstücke, Nocturne, and Villa Wunderbar. Hildegard Schmidt (Discogs ID 1162982) — his manager and partner — appears as management credit across virtually all Can and solo releases handled by Spoon Records.
