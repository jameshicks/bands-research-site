Ken Thomas

Full name: Kenneth Vaughan Thomas Born: 23 January 1952 Died: 8 July 2021 (aged 69) Based: Hampshire, England (High Bank Studio) Role: Recording engineer, producer, mixer

Overview

Ken Thomas was one of the most quietly significant producers of the post-punk era — a connective thread between the British industrial underground of the early 1980s and major Icelandic artists of the 1990s and 2000s. He engineered 23 Skidoo’s early Fetish Records work alongside Cabaret Voltaire and Throbbing Gristle, produced Clock DVA, Au Pairs, Modern English, Psychic TV, and the Cocteau Twins, then went on to produce The Sugarcubes and three Sigur Rós albums. He also released his own solo album in 1980.

He shared his Hampshire studio, High Bank, with his son Jolyon Vaughan Thomas (also a producer; worked with Slaves). His daughter Amy Odell is a singer-songwriter who sang on his Gavin Friday production.

MusicBrainz lists him as a member of Psychic TV — not just a hired engineer but embedded in that scene.

Confirmed by Sigur Rós (Twitter, 12 July 2021): “It is with much sadness we say goodbye to our friend, collaborator and producer Ken Thomas who passed away last week. Ken worked with us on AB, ( ), Takk and various other bits of music in our early years.”

Career Origins

Began as an assistant and engineer at Trident Studios and Advision Studios in London — two of the most prestigious 1970s studios (Trident: Queen, Bowie; Advision: similarly high-end). Early credit: assistant mixer on Rush’s A Farewell to Kings (1977). He also worked alongside Martin Hannett and Martin Rushent, which placed him at the centre of British post-punk production.

He released his own solo album, Beat the Light (1980): described as “post-punk sound combining traces of funk with reverb experiments in ways reminiscent of Cabaret Voltaire, along with sonic waterboarding that wouldn’t be out of place at a Throbbing Gristle show.”

Stephen Mallinder (Cabaret Voltaire) on the 23 Skidoo sessions:

“Cabaret Voltaire played quite a few shows with Skidoo… Ken was perfect for getting that across with structure but without diluting that live dynamic. I just remember they were always long sessions over a few days, much fun with brief breaks for a bit of sleep and breakfasts back at my old gothic house that seemed as anarchically organised as the studio sessions.”

The Iceland Thread

The pathway from post-punk London to Iceland:

  1. Cocteau Twins Aikea-Guinea EP (1985, 4AD) — Thomas produces their most experimental EP at Jacob’s Farm. The sound this produces is noted as “a template that an Icelandic act were soon to show an interest in.”

  2. The Sugarcubes Life’s Too Good (1988, One Little Indian) — Thomas engineers/mixes the debut. He develops a close relationship with guitarist Þór Eldon.

  3. Botnledja — an Icelandic band; Thomas produces their last two albums (mid-1990s). Deepening Iceland connections.

  4. Sigur Rós — Thor Eldon plays Thomas the band’s debut album Von (1997). Thomas pitches to work with them. Result: Ágætis byrjun (1999), ( ) (2002), Takk… (2005), Heima (2007), plus Valtari tracks (2012).
