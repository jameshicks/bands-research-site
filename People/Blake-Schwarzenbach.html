Blake Schwarzenbach

Discogs ID: 448146 Real name: Alexander Blake Schwarzenbach Born: May 20, 1967, Berkeley, California, USA Role: Vocalist, guitarist, keyboardist, songwriter Active: 1988–present

Vocalist and left-handed guitarist; frontman of Jawbreaker (1988–1996; 2017–present), Jets To Brazil (1997–2003), Thorns of Life (2008–2009), and Forgetters (2009–2014). Formed Jets To Brazil with Jeremy Chatelain after Jawbreaker’s dissolution. Also credited as a writer/illustrator on the comic book series What’s The Furthest Place From Here? (Image Comics / Read Leader, 2021–).

Biography

Early years: Rise and Jawbreaker (1986–1989)

Schwarzenbach was active in Los Angeles-area hardcore before the formation that would become Jawbreaker. An early project called Rise (Discogs ID 6837174) left a single self-released recording from 1988. Jawbreaker solidified with Schwarzenbach (vocals/guitar), Chris Bauermeister (bass), and Adam Pfahler (drums) — the three met at New York University in the mid-1980s. The band’s first release was the Whack & Blite 7” (1989) on their own Blackball Records.

Jawbreaker era (1989–1996)

Jawbreaker’s early output came through Shredder (Busy EP, 1989; Unfun LP, 1990). The band self-produced throughout their career, with Schwarzenbach and the band credited as producers on all records.

Bivouac (1992, Communion Label) — co-produced with Billy Anderson, Jonathan Burnside, and Mike Morasky. The band relocated from Los Angeles to San Francisco’s Mission District around 1991. UK/European distribution through Rough Trade Records GmbH and Tupelo Recording Company.

Chesterfield King EP (1992, Communion Label) — same co-production team.

24 Hour Revenge Therapy (1994, Communion Label) — Schwarzenbach credited for guitar, vocals, and all songwriting. Produced by the band; Billy Anderson engineered tracks 1, 3, 6; Fluss engineered tracks 2, 4, 5, 7–11. Mastered by John Golden.

Dear You (1995, DGC) — Jawbreaker’s major-label debut and final studio album. Produced by Jawbreaker and Rob Cavallo; mixed by Jerry Finn; engineered by Neill King; mastered by Bob Ludwig. Schwarzenbach’s credits list him playing “Guitar [Gutter], Vocals [Gusher]” — a characteristic credit style. The record drew “sellout” accusations from the punk fanbase; combined with internal tensions, it precipitated the band’s split in 1996.

A posthumous live recording, Live 4/30/96 (1999, Blackball Records), documents the band’s final period.

The band reunited in 2017 and has remained active.

Jets To Brazil (1997–2003)

Schwarzenbach formed Jets To Brazil in New York in 1997 with Jeremy Chatelain. The duo initially used a drum machine before adding Chris Daly (ex-Texas Is The Reason) on drums and Pete Martin (2) on guitar. Martin departed before recording began. Guitarist Brian Maryansky (ex-Van Pelt) joined just before the first album’s release, completing the lineup as a quartet.

All three Jets To Brazil studio albums were released on Jade Tree and produced by J. Robbins at Inner Ear Studio. Alan Douches mastered all three.

Orange Rhyming Dictionary (1998) — Produced and recorded by J. Robbins; engineered by Stuart Sikes. Schwarzenbach credited for “Written-By [Typing], Vocals [Recitation], Guitar [Strings], Keyboards [Keys].” This is a three-piece recording (Schwarzenbach, Chatelain, Daly); Maryansky joined after. 16 pressings on Jade Tree.

Four Cornered Night (2000) — Full quartet (Schwarzenbach, Chatelain, Daly, Maryansky). Recorded and mixed by J. Robbins at Inner Ear; additional engineering by Geoff Sanoff at Water Music. Cello by Amy Domingues on three tracks.

Perfecting Loneliness (2002) — Final studio album. Schwarzenbach plays an expanded range: vocals, piano, Juno, mellotron, lap steel, acoustic guitar, electric guitar. Recorded, mixed by J. Robbins; additional mix by Reuben Kaller. Cello again by Amy Domingues. The band quietly split in 2003.

Thorns of Life (2008–2009)

Short-lived project (Discogs ID 2446566) with Aaron Elliot and Dan-Yella Dyslexia. Minimal Discogs presence — no studio recordings catalogued.

Forgetters (2009–2014)

Band project (Discogs ID 2017440). Released two recordings on Too Small To Fail (2010, 2012). Schwarzenbach credited for “Guitar, Vocals [Language]; Text By [Lettering]” — the lettering credit reflects his simultaneous work as a comics writer.

Post-Forgetters and comics work (2014–present)

Schwarzenbach’s most recent Discogs primary credit is the ongoing Image Comics / Read Leader series What’s The Furthest Place From Here? (2021–), a comic book co-published with Kelly Thompson. Also appeared on a Joyce Manor split in that series’ orbit (12 shared Discogs credits). Jawbreaker’s 2017 reunion has continued through at least 2025 per Discogs activity data.

Connections

  • J. Robbins — produced all three Jets To Brazil albums at Inner Ear; also produced Jawbox and Burning Airlines; the key production bridge between Discharge-adjacent DC post-hardcore and Jade Tree’s emo world
  • Rob Cavallo — produced Dear You at DGC; later produced Green Day’s Dookie and American Idiot; signing Jawbreaker to DGC was part of a mid-90s major-label sweep of indie punk bands
  • Jerry Finn — mixed Dear You; career overlapped with many 90s/2000s punk and pop-punk records (Blink-182, Alkaline Trio)
  • Texas Is The Reason — Chris Daly (JTB drummer) was their drummer; the band broke up in 1997 and Daly joined JTB immediately after
  • Van Pelt — Brian Maryansky (JTB guitarist) came from here; Van Pelt were a New York post-hardcore band
  • Blackball Records — Adam Pfahler’s label; reissued Jawbreaker catalog and released new bands after the band’s split; reflects Jawbreaker’s insistence on self-ownership and punk infrastructure
  • The Discogs collaborator graph shows Blake connected to Joyce Manor (12 shared releases via What’s The Furthest Place From Here? comic) and mastering engineer John Golden (2 shared releases)
