Roberto Di Gioia

Discogs ID: 300875 Alias: Bobby Pleasure (Discogs: no separate ID) Born: 1965, Milan, Italy Based: Munich / Bavaria, Germany

Italian-born, Bavaria-based jazz pianist, Hammond organist, vibraphonist, and multi-instrumentalist. One of the most versatile sidemen in the Munich jazz and indie-crossover scene since the mid-1980s. Known outside jazz circles primarily for his guest appearances on Tied & Tickled Trio’s EA1 EA2 (1999) and Observing Systems (2003), and on The Notwist’s landmark Neon Golden (2002). Leader and sole member of the studio project Marsmobil, and co-founder of the jazz-funk group Web Web. Long-running collaborator with producers Christian Prommer and Peter Kruder.

Biography

Early Career — Munich Jazz Scene (1987–1994)

Di Gioia arrived in the Munich jazz ecosystem in the late 1980s, establishing himself quickly as a piano sideman on the European festival and club circuit. His earliest Discogs-traceable session is the 1987 Live In Vienna recording with the Roman Schwaller Jazzquartet (Bassic Sound), a quartet that also featured bassist Thomas Stabenow and drummer Paul Polansky. The same year he appeared on Message (Jeton).

He was active from the start in big-band contexts: the Al Porcino Big Band (Discogs ID 1771438) and the NDR Big Band (Discogs ID 503250) are among his listed group memberships, and he appeared on the 1996 NDR retrospective Bravissimo — 50 Years NDR Bigband (ACT). He is also a member of the Munich Jazz Orchestra (Discogs ID 2215550).

In 1993 he joined the Nils Landgren Funk Unit roster (Discogs ID 257427), cementing his foothold in the Scandinavian-Munich jazz crossover that ACT Records would later champion.

Mid-1990s — Mons Records and Film Scoring

In 1995 Di Gioia co-led what Discogs lists as the Paul Heller quintet album (Mons Records, cat. 874 764), appearing as a co-primary artist alongside tenor saxophonist Paul Heller, flugelhorn player Ack Van Rooyen, bassist Ingmar Heller, and drummer Wolfgang Haffner. This remains his most prominent leader / co-leader jazz credit of the decade.

In 1996 he provided piano and grand piano for the German film score Jenseits der Stille (Buena Vista International / Virgin, cat. no. 1283617), directed by Caroline Link — a notable mainstream-cinema placement.

Late 1990s — Marsmobil and the Payola / Weilheim Circle

In 1998 Di Gioia launched Marsmobil, a solo studio project, with an untitled 12” on Payola — the same Munich imprint that distributed early Tied & Tickled Trio material (the TTT’s label Community was distributed through Payola/Virgin). The project’s membership is listed as Roberto Di Gioia and vocalist Martine Rojina.

This Payola connection placed him squarely within the Weilheim/Munich indie-electronic circle centred on the Acher brothers. He was invited to guest on Tied & Tickled Trio’s second album EA1 EA2 (1999, Community/Payola/Virgin), playing Rhodes, Hammond organ, and vibraphone alongside core TTT members Markus Acher, Micha Acher, and Christoph Brandner, plus Andreas Gerth (electronics), Johannes Enders (tenor saxophone/piano), and Ullrich Wangenheim (bass clarinet/tenor saxophone). Producer: Mario Thaler.

2002–2003 — Neon Golden and Observing Systems

Di Gioia’s two highest-profile guest appearances landed in back-to-back years.

The Notwist — Neon Golden (2002, City Slang / Big Store)

Credited as “Guest, Piano, Keyboards” (listed as “Roberto Di Giola” on the sleeve). The Notwist core: Markus Acher, Martin Gretschmann (Console), Martin Messerschmid, Micha Acher. Produced by Galore (2). Mastered by Chris Blair.

2004–2010 — Marsmobil, Compost Records, and the Munich Funk Underground

Marsmobil released the Minx album in 2006 on Compost Records, and the full-length Black Album in 2011 on the same label. The project’s most ambitious release is the double album (Why Don’t You Take) The Other Side? (2010, Compost / G-Stone Recordings), on which Di Gioia performed all instruments and vocals, with production split between himself (tracks 2, 5, 8–11, 14) and the duo of Christian Prommer and Peter Kruder (tracks 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 12, 13, 15, 16).

This era also produced Jet Set Jazz (2008, Compost), a comedy-jazz album fronted by TV personality Wigald Boning on which Di Gioia co-wrote, co-produced, arranged, and played virtually every instrument (piano, keyboards, guitar, bass, drums, percussion) alongside Prommer and Boning.

In 2010 he contributed piano, organ, Rhodes, Minimoog synthesizer, Mellotron, and arrangement to Christian Prommer’s Drumlesson Zwei (!K7 Records), co-produced by Prommer and Peter Kruder:

2011 — KAHEDI and German Pop Crossover

Di Gioia is a core member of KAHEDI (Discogs ID 2221278) alongside Max Herre (Freundeskreis) and the Kahedi Radio Orchestra (Discogs ID 3625036). The ensemble released Hallo Welt! (2012, Nesola/Vertigo) and the live MTV Unplugged Kahedi Radio Show (2013, Nesola/Vertigo), on which Di Gioia is credited with piano, clavinet, harpsichord, sitar, and choir — a rare glimpse of his range beyond jazz and electronics.

He played piano and melodica on Herbert Grönemeyer’s MTV Unplugged — Live Aus Dem Hotel Atlantic (Starwatch, 2011).

2014 — Kruder & Di Gioia Duo

In 2023 Di Gioia and Peter Kruder released a collaborative duo album under the name Kruder, Di Gioia — catalogued on Discogs as „_ _ _ _ _ _ _ _„ (Peyo (3), 2023, master 3141240). The release carries no additional personnel credits in the database.

2015–present — Web Web and Compost Records Output

Di Gioia is a founding member and producer of Web Web (Discogs ID 5981923), a jazz-funk quartet: Di Gioia (keyboards, producer), Tony Lakatos (saxophones/flute), Christian Von Kaphengst (bass), and Peter Gall (drums). The group has released on Compost Records:

  • Worshippers (2020) — featuring Joy Denalane as special guest vocalist on four tracks; strings arranged by Di Gioia
  • Web Max (2021, Web Web x Max Herre) — co-produced by Di Gioia and Max Herre; Di Gioia mixed the album
  • Web Max II (2023) — produced by Di Gioia
