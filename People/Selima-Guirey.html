Selima Guirey

Born: 15 January 1960 Role: Flautist Instrument: Flute (on Occult Chemistry EP, 1980)

Family Background

Selima Guirey is one of three children of Prince Azamat Kadir Sultan Guirey and Princess Sylvia Guirey (née Obolensky, 1931–1997). Her mother was a White Russian heiress and art patron: after inheriting in 1958, Sylvia became a pop-art collector whose London home at Elm Place was a 1960s–70s salon for artists and dealers. She exhibited at the São Paulo Biennale (1973), the Betty Parsons Gallery New York (1976), and at the Hester van Royen and Benjamin Rhodes galleries in London. (Wikipedia — Sylvia Guirey)

Selima married Derek Goddard, a musician who worked with The Clash, The Raincoats, and Neneh Cherry.

The Occult Chemistry Connection

Selima played flute on the Occult Chemistry EP (1980, Dining Out Records, cat. Tux 4). The other musicians were Martha Tilson (vocals), Tom Dixon (bass, piano), Justin Langlands (drums), and Glenn Carmichael / Gordon Twilight (whistling, backing vocals).

Why This Is Significant

The Guirey family creates the strongest known circumstantial evidence that Tom Dixon (bass on the Occult Chemistry EP) is the same Tom Dixon who later played bass in Funkapolitan — and subsequently became the celebrated industrial designer.

The logic: Tom Dixon played with Selima Guirey (flute) in Occult Chemistry (1980), then went on to play bass in Funkapolitan — which was fronted by Kadir Guirey, Selima’s brother. The same family appears in both bands, with Tom Dixon as the common musical link. No other Tom Dixon active as a bassist in this scene has been identified.

Web Notes (added 2026-06-25)

Sagat Guirey — Clash connection clarified

The claim that Sagat Guirey “worked with The Clash” does not reflect a direct studio or session credit. His connection to The Clash is as a guitarist in Funkapolitan — the band earned “high-profile support slots with The Clash and Simple Minds” in their early period (c. 1980–82). Wikipedia’s Funkapolitan article notes “guitarists Kadir and Sagat Guirey were White Russian royalty” and lists Sagat as a band member alongside Kadir, Tom Dixon, Guy Pratt, and Simon Olivierre, among others. No direct session or recording credit for Sagat with The Clash has been found. (Wikipedia — Funkapolitan, as of 2026-06-25)

Sagat’s broader career credits (from his IMDB page and promotional bios) include: Nigel Kennedy, Belinda Carlisle, Roachford, and Robert Trujillo (Metallica bassist). Film/TV work includes About Time (2013, performed guitar on “When I Fall In Love” alongside vocalist Barbar Gough, bassist Andy Hamill, and saxophonist Tim Herniman), Shaun the Sheep Movie, and Wake of Death. He was classically trained in London, then studied at the Dick Grove School of Music in Los Angeles (jazz guitar, composition, arranging); further gypsy jazz studies with Lollo Meier in the Netherlands; private instruction from Ted Greene and Phil Upchurch. Currently based in Salisbury. (IMDB — Sagat Guirey) · (sagatguitar.co.uk/about) · (SoundCellar event, 2022)

Note on vault entry: The sibling table currently reads “worked with The Clash” — this should be read as “played support slots for The Clash as a member of Funkapolitan,” not a direct session/recording role.

Derek Goddard — specific credits

Derek Goddard is a drummer (also described as Afro-Caribbean British, based in London, later France). His connections to The Clash, Raincoats, and Neneh Cherry break down as follows:

The Clash (direct but non-recording):

  • In 1976 he was drum technician for Terry Chimes (then The Clash’s drummer). This is the earliest documented Clash connection. (The Baker blog)
  • In the early 1980s he played drums with Joe Strummer in a side group called the Soul Vendors, alongside Richard Dudanski and “Mole” (both from Strummer’s pre-Clash band the 101’ers). The Soul Vendors played “vintage ska, soul chestnuts, and old 101’ers tunes” at The Tabernacle, Powis Square, London “for three years running” on New Year’s Eve. (The Baker blog)
  • He appeared as an actor (barman at a shebeen) in Joe Strummer’s 1983 short film Hell W10, also contributing to production (transportation, set-up, a special effect). Paul Simonon (Clash bassist) played the lead. He also spent weeks co-editing the film in a studio off Shepherd’s Bush Green. (The Baker blog)

The Raincoats:

  • He toured the United States with The Raincoats. In February 1983, on returning from that US tour, he received a call from Joe Strummer leading to the Hell W10 project. The Raincoats’ Wikipedia article does not list him, but the Baker blog account is first-person and specific. (The Baker blog) — Conflict: Wikipedia’s Raincoats article does not mention Goddard; his role (session drummer, touring member, or crew) is unspecified by available sources.

Neneh Cherry:

  • His X/Twitter biography self-describes drumming work with Neneh Cherry. No independent source corroborating this credit has been found. Neneh Cherry’s Wikipedia article does not mention him. Treat as self-reported/unverified pending liner-note confirmation. (Derek Goddard @derkgodard / X — bio, not directly accessible)

Princess Sylvia Guirey (née Obolensky, 1931–1997) — art patronage

Her mother was Ava Alice Muriel Astor (of the Astor family) and her father was Raimund von Hofmannsthal. She married Prince Azamat Kadir Sultan Guirey, a descendant of the Tatar khans of Crimea.

After inheriting “a substantial sum” in 1958 (on her grandmother’s death), she became an active collector of pop-art and a practising painter (acrylic). Her London home at Elm Place became “popular with artists and dealers” through the 1960s and 1970s. She exhibited at:

  • São Paulo Biennale (1973)
  • Betty Parsons Gallery, New York (1976) — a major venue that showed Abstract Expressionists and contemporary artists
  • Hester van Royen Gallery, London
  • Benjamin Rhodes Gallery, London

In the 1950s she worked with stage designer Eugene Berman on a Metropolitan Opera production of Don Giovanni.

The Wikipedia article on Sylvia Guirey frames her Elm Place salon as overlapping with the music world only through her daughter Selima’s marriage to Derek Goddard — no direct evidence that Sylvia’s art circle and the post-punk/funk scene overlapped while the children were playing in those bands, though the timing (1960s–70s salon, 1980–82 Funkapolitan/Occult Chemistry) is adjacent. (Wikipedia — Sylvia Guirey, as of 2026-06-25)
