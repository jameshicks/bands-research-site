María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir

María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir

Oct 29, 202610 min read

María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir

Discogs ID: 366643 Born: 1980, Iceland Instruments: Violin, viola Roles: Performer, composer, arranger, mixer, producer

Icelandic violinist, composer, and multi-instrumentalist. Core founding member of Amiina and a member of Reykjavík Sinfonia (Discogs ID: 3492221). One of the most extensively credited Icelandic session players of her generation — appearing on at least eight Sigur Rós projects from 2002 to 2023 — and a primary artist on three Krúnk releases in 2020 alongside Sigur Rós, Steindór Andersen, and Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson.

Biography

Formation and Early Career (c. 1999–2004)

María Huld was one of four string players who came together in Reykjavík to form the ensemble that would become Amiina. Under their original name Amína, the group — completing the original lineup alongside Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir, Hildur Ársælsdóttir, and Sólrún Sumarliðadóttir, with Ólöf Júlía Kjartansdóttir on viola for early sessions — became the string quartet for Sigur Rós as that band’s audience was expanding internationally.

Her first Discogs-documented session credit came on Sigur Rós’s landmark ( ) (FatCat Records, 2002), where she performed violin in an uncredited capacity alongside the rest of the Amína quartet. That same year she played violin on the Robert Wyatt tribute compilation La Chanson Dada (Raft Recordings, UK) — a notably different context and an early indication of her range as a session musician.

In 2004 she appeared as a performer (credited “With The Help Of”) on The Album Leaf’s In A Safe Place (Sub Pop) — a Sigur Rós-circle album produced and engineered by Birgir Jón Birgisson at Sundlaugin studio, Iceland, alongside Jón Þór Birgisson, Kjartan Sveinsson, and Orri Páll Dýrason.

Amína / AnimaminA and Renaming (2005–2006)

The ensemble’s first formal release under their own name was AnimaminA (The Worker’s Institute, 2005), issued as “Amina.” In addition to performing, María Huld served as co-mixer and co-mastering engineer alongside Birgir Jón Birgisson — an early signal of her production involvement beyond playing. The group renamed to Amiina in March 2006 to avoid confusion with the Tunisian singer Amina.

The Seoul single (Amiina self-released, 2006) listed María Huld explicitly as a writer and performer alongside Edda, Sólrún, and Ólöf Júlía, with Birgir Jón Birgisson and Kjartan Sveinsson credited for mixing and mastering.

Sigur Rós String Arranger and Session Work (2005–2012)

From Takk… (Geffen/EMI, 2005) onward, María Huld and the Amína/Amiina quartet became the credited string section across the entire Sigur Rós studio run. Her Discogs-documented Sigur Rós string credits span:

  • Takk… (2005) — strings as part of Amiina/Amína
  • Heima (EMI, 2007) — violin
  • Með Suð Í Eyrum Við Spilum Endalaust (EMI/Krúnk, 2008) — strings on tracks 2–5, 8, 9; credited as “M. H. M. Sigfúsdóttir” with the full Amiina quartet
  • Valtari (Krúnk/Parlophone, 2012) — strings and string arrangements across the bulk of the album; one of three credited string arrangers alongside Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir and Hildur Ársælsdóttir (and separately Daníel Bjarnason on one track)

Outside Sigur Rós, the Amiina quartet toured and recorded as a discrete entity known as “Quartet Iceland,” appearing on:

  • Mark Lanegan’s Sweet Heart, Sweet Light (Double Six, 2012), alongside the Quartet London strings, and with Birgir Jón Birgisson recording
  • Julianna Barwick’s Nepenthe (Dead Oceans, 2013), produced by Alex Somers, where all four Amiina members are credited individually as “Strings [Amiina]“

Amiina’s Own Catalog (2007–2022)

While her session activity continued, María Huld remained an equal creative force in Amiina’s own releases. Kurr (Bláskjár Records, 2007), the debut full-length, was written, performed, produced, recorded, and mixed entirely by Amiina — she appears without individual production credit, as the band was credited collectively. Puzzle (Amínamúsík Ehf., 2010) continued that model. Fantômas (Mengi, 2016), the silent-film double LP, maintained the core lineup after Edda and Hildur departed in 2015 — leaving María Huld, Sólrún, Magnús Trygvason Eliassen, and Kippi Kaninus as the continuing four.

Recent self-released Amiina works — I’d Like To Teach the World To Sing (2020), Pharology (2021), YULE (2022) — continue without a label.

2019–2020: Krúnk Trilogy and Ágætis Byrjun

The Ágætis Byrjun 20th anniversary compilation (Krúnk, 2019) listed María Huld with both violin and written-by credits — a rare co-compositional credit on a Sigur Rós release.

In 2020 three releases appeared on Krúnk with her as a primary artist alongside Sigur Rós, Steindór Andersen (Icelandic rimur singer, Discogs ID: 104029), and Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson (composer, high priest of the Ásatrúarfélag, Discogs ID: 145298):

  • Odin’s Raven Magic (2020) — UK, Europe & US; six pressings
  • Dvergmál (2020) — two pressings
  • Stendur Æva (2020) — two pressings

All three are credited jointly to the four artists with no further production credits listed in Discogs. These are recordings of Nordic/Old Norse mythological/poetic texts; the combination of Steindór’s rimur practice, Hilmarsson’s compositional voice, and Sigur Rós’s instrumental backdrop contextualizes María Huld’s role as string performer and likely arranger.

Kom Vinur and Solo Work (2021)

Kom Vinur (Sono Luminus, 2021, cat# SLE-70019) is María Huld’s first clearly solo-credited Discogs release outside the Amiina/Sigur Rós circle. A two-track choral-and-strings single (CD), it features:

  • Hörður Áskelsson — conductor (Discogs ID: 996768)
  • Schola Cantorum Reykjavík — choir (Discogs ID: 996770)
  • Vilborg Dagbjartsdóttir — poems/text (Discogs ID: 2734046)

Track listing: “Kom Vinur” (5:40) and “Maríuljóð” (3:55). Genre: Classical / Choral / Contemporary. Label: Sono Luminus (US).

Átta and Continued Sigur Rós Work (2021–2023)

In 2021 she contributed violin to Alex Somers’s Siblings (Krúnk) alongside Edda Rún and Hildur Ársælsdóttir — a reunion of three of the original Amiina string players in a new context.

On Átta (Krúnk, 2023), Sigur Rós’s latest studio album, María Huld is credited as co-arranger of all string arrangements alongside Robert Ames (conductor of the London Contemporary Orchestra) and Sigur Rós. The orchestra performing the strings was the London Contemporary Orchestra.
