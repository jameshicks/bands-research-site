Alejandro Escovedo

Discogs ID: 179551

American alt-rock singer-songwriter and guitarist. Born January 10, 1951, San Antonio, Texas. Member of a large musical family: brothers Pete Escovedo, Coke Escovedo, Mario Escovedo, and Javier Escovedo are all musicians; niece is Sheila E. Escovedo moved through San Francisco punk, country-punk, and Austin alternative country before establishing himself as a solo artist of durable critical standing. Active from 1977 to the present, with a discography spanning punk singles, country-tinged LPs, orchestral-rock albums, concept records, and late-career work produced with Italian band Sacri Cuori.

Era 1 — San Francisco Punk: The Nuns (1977–1980)

Escovedo arrived in San Francisco in the mid-1970s and co-founded The Nuns (Discogs ID: 244089), one of the city’s first punk groups. The band played the Sex Pistols’ final concert at Winterland in January 1978 and released a single on 415 Records (“Decadent Jew / Savage,” 1978). Their self-titled LP appeared on Posh Boy / BOMP! in 1980. Other members included Jeff Olener, Jennifer Anderson, Richie Detrick, and Kevin Foley.

Era 2 — Country-Punk: Rank & File (1981–1983)

After The Nuns, Escovedo joined Rank & File (Discogs ID: 10386) — also known as Rank And File — a country-punk band co-founded with brothers Chip Kinman and Tony Kinman, former members of The Dils. The band’s debut Sundown (Slash, 1982) was produced by David Kahne and placed Escovedo on guitar and vocals alongside the Kinmans and drummer Slim Evans. The Kinmans wrote most of the material; Escovedo’s songwriting voice was not yet dominant. Rank & File is credited as a foundational act of the cowpunk/Americana genre.

Era 3 — Austin, The True Believers (1983–1990)

Escovedo relocated to Austin, Texas, in the early 1980s and formed The True Believers (Discogs ID: 600252) with his brother Javier Escovedo, guitarist Jon Dee Graham, bassist JD Foster, drummer Rey Washam, and Kevin Foley. The band released one self-titled LP (EMI America / Rounder Records, 1986), produced by Jim Dickinson. Escovedo sang lead and played guitar; the band mixed punk energy with Tex-Mex and country rock. Drummer Hector Munoz — who would become one of Escovedo’s longest-running collaborators — was also a member at various points.

Era 4 — Solo Career, Watermelon / Rykodisc (1992–1998)

Escovedo launched his solo career with Gravity (Watermelon Records, 1992), produced by Stephen Bruton — a partnership that defined his early solo sound. Bruton produced, played multiple guitars, and sang harmonies across the record; engineer Dave McNair also co-produced individual tracks. The album blended folk, country, and alternative rock and introduced the string arrangements and chamber touches that would recur throughout Escovedo’s work, anchored by cellist John Hagen (2) and violinist Susan Voelz.

The follow-up Thirteen Years (Watermelon, 1993) expanded the string palette further — violinists Susan Voelz, David Perales, and Danny Levin plus cellist Frank Kammerdiener. Bruton produced again; Dave McNair recorded and mixed. Guest vocalists included Malford Milligan and Bob Neuwirth. Charlie Sexton contributed electric guitar to “Losing Your Touch.”

With These Hands (Rykodisc, 1996) again featured Bruton as producer and a large cast: Charlie Sexton and Will Sexton on harmonies, Willie Nelson duetting on “Nickel and a Spoon,” Mickey Raphael on harmonica, Stephen Barber arranging strings and playing keyboards, and the whole Escovedo family — Pete, Juanita, Juan, Peter Michael, and Sheila (credited as Sheila Escovedo) — on percussion on the title track. Mastered by Joe Gastwirt.

A live album, More Miles Than Money: Live 1994–96 (Bloodshot Records, 1998), appeared on the Chicago roots label and documented the mid-decade touring band.

Era 5 — Bloodshot / Modern Recording: The Stamey Albums (1999–2001)

Bourbonitis Blues (Bloodshot Records, 1999) was produced and recorded at Modern Recording in Chapel Hill, North Carolina by Chris Stamey. Stamey produced three of the nine tracks (2, 5, 9) and played on them; the remaining tracks were co-produced by Escovedo in a more stripped acoustic style with Austin regulars Joe Eddy Hines (acoustic and electric guitar), Hector Munoz (drums), Brian Standefer (cello), and David Perales (violin). Guest vocalists included Kelly Hogan (“Pale Blue Eyes,” a Lou Reed cover) and Jon Langford (2) on a Jimmie Rodgers cover. Track 9 (“Sex Beat”) featured a small combo with Chris Phillips (2) on drums and Melissa Swingle on harmonica and backing vocals.

A Man Under The Influence (Blue Rose Records (2), 2001; US release Bloodshot) was fully produced by Stamey at Modern Recording and represents the most expansive of the two collaborations. The Chapel Hill scene converged here: Mitch Easter (guitar, multiple tracks), Jon Wurster (drums/percussion, tracks 2, 3, 5, 10), Caitlin Cary (violin, tracks 3, 4, 11), Mac McCaughan (Moog Rogue synthesizer, track 5), and Mike Daly as multi-instrumentalist throughout. Ryan Adams contributed backing vocals on tracks 6 and 10. Eric Heywood played pedal steel. Tres Chicas provided harmony vocals on tracks 5, 9, 11.

Era 6 — Texas Music Group / Back Porch (2002–2008)

By The Hand Of The Father (Texas Music Group, 2002) was a theatrical work about a Mexican-American family, featuring narration, Spanish-language passages, and a large Austin cast including brother Javier, Pete Escovedo, and traditional musicians Rumel Fuentes and Quetzal Flores. Steven Soles appeared as a collaborator here and again on Echo Dancing (2024).

The Boxing Mirror (Back Porch, 2006) marked a pivotal creative turn: produced by John Cale, with Cale playing keyboards and guitar throughout. The album’s band — drummer Hector Munoz, guitarist David Pulkingham, bassist Mark Andes, cellist Brian Standefer, and second cellist Matt Fish (2) — became Escovedo’s working group for the next several years. Co-writing credits include Stamey (“Looking For Love”), Standefer, and Pulkingham. Jon Dee Graham and Susan Voelz contributed.

Real Animal (Back Porch / Manhattan Records, 2008) was an autobiographical rock album produced by Tony Visconti, who also arranged strings, sang backing vocals, and mixed. The album featured Chuck Prophet on guitar, Pulkingham, Standefer, Voelz, and Munoz, with a cover photo by Mick Rock. Track 8 (“Nuns Song”) was an explicit callback to Escovedo’s punk origins; Visconti played organ on it.

Era 7 — Fantasy Records and Later Work (2010–present)

Escovedo moved to Fantasy Records for Street Songs Of Love (2010) and Big Station (2012), both co-produced with Tony Visconti. Burn Something Beautiful (Fantasy, 2016) featured a new cast of Northwest-rooted musicians: Peter Buck (R.E.M.), Scott McCaughey, Kurt Bloch, John Moen, Kelly Hogan, and Steve Berlin (Los Lobos). Produced by Adam Selzer and Jim Scott.

The Crossing (Yep Roc, 2018) was a concept album about immigration, recorded with Italian band Sacri Cuori — Antonio Gramentieri (guitar), Gianni Perinelli (guitar), Nicola Peruch (keyboards), Ivano Giovedì (bass), Matteo Monti (drums) — and the Prague Metropolitan Symphonic Orchestra. Wayne Kramer, James Williamson, Joe Ely, and Peter Perrett contributed.

Echo Dancing (Yep Roc, 2024) returned to Sacri Cuori and added orchestral arrangements; Escovedo’s wife Nancy Rankin Escovedo appeared as a credited collaborator.
