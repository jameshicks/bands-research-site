Jez Kerr

Discogs ID: 294636
Real name: Jeremy Kerr
Role: Bass, vocals, songwriter, producer
Active: 1979–present
From: Manchester, England

Bass player, vocalist, and founding member of A Certain Ratio. One of the longest-serving members of that group, shifting over time from bass anchor to lead vocalist. Also a member of Kalima and runs the solo/project vehicle Twenty Four Hours (2).

A Certain Ratio

Formation and Factory Years (1979–1991)

Jez Kerr joined A Certain Ratio (formed 1977 in Manchester) as bassist, operating at the core of the band’s rhythm section alongside drummer Donald Johnson. The band took their name from a lyric in Brian Eno’s “The True Wheel” (Taking Tiger Mountain By Strategy, 1974). Signed to Factory Records in 1979 — one of the label’s earliest and most enduring acts.

The debut release, The Graveyard And The Ballroom (Fact 16, 1980, cassette), captured two distinct sides: Side A produced by Martin Hannett, engineered Stuart Pickering; Side B mixed by Jeff Hooper and Tony Wilson. Sleeve design from Peter Saville’s workshop. Core lineup: Jez Kerr (bass), Donald Johnson (drums), Martin Moscrop (guitar), Peter Terrell (guitar), Simon Topping (vocals, trumpet).

Through the early 1980s the group developed a hybrid of post-punk, funk, and jazz influences — sometimes described alongside Quando Quango and other Factory acts as part of Manchester’s dance-inflected post-punk wave.

Sextet (Fact 55, 1982) is widely regarded as a peak Factory-era statement. Engineered by Phil Ault; sleeve design by Ben Kelly. Self-produced by ACR. Lineup added Martha Tilson on vocals; Kerr credited as Jeremy Kerr.

By Force (Fact 166, 1986), Kerr had taken on lead vocals alongside his bass duties — a significant shift in the band’s configuration. The album also brought in Andy Connell (piano, vocoder, synths, sampler) and Tony Quigley (soprano/tenor sax) as regular collaborators. Guest credits: Corinne Drewery (vocals on “Bootsy”), Tom Barnish (trombone). Produced by ACR + Stuart James; engineered Paul Harrison + Stuart James.

The band also signed briefly to A&M Records (1989–1990) for a major-label attempt before returning to independent releases. The A&M period yielded two releases: Good Together (September 1989) and acr:mcr (1990). Both underperformed commercially — the singles “The Big E,” “Backs To The Wall,” and “Your Blue Eyes” failed to chart significantly, and Good Together is described in contemporary accounts as an expensive production that generated no hits. A search snippet from a Quietus retrospective (exact article unconfirmed, see note below) references a band account in which their A&R contact at A&M was the only person at the label who understood what they were doing, but was unable to mobilise other departments behind them — framing the dropping as an ACR-specific commercial failure compounded by inadequate internal label support, rather than a broader roster cull. The PolyGram acquisition of A&M (announced October 1989, completed early 1990) coincided with this period and restructured A&M’s UK operations, but no source explicitly links ACR’s departure to that restructuring rather than to the albums’ commercial underperformance. The most consistently stated reason across Wikipedia, AllMusic-adjacent sources, and music press summaries is simply two albums that failed to generate hits.

Notes on sourcing: Wikipedia’s A Certain Ratio article (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Certain_Ratio) and the Good Together article (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Together_(A_Certain_Ratio_album)) attribute the label split to commercial underperformance of both albums — no named A&R person or internal politics detail. A Google search snippet from a Quietus “Strange World Of” feature (https://thequietus.com/interviews/strange-world-of/a-certain-ratio-interview/) surfaced language about the A&R man being the only supporter at A&M, but the full article was behind a 403 paywall — the snippet context is partial and should be verified by reading the full piece. Multiple band interviews (NARC Magazine, Louder Than War, Backseat Mafia, DJ Mag, LA Record 2008) were consulted; none contained material on the A&M period.

Post-Factory: Robs, Creation, Soul Jazz, Mute (1991–present)

After Factory’s collapse in 1992, ACR moved to Robs Records (1991–1997), then had a single on Creation Records (1994). A long quiet period followed before Soul Jazz Records reissued and documented their catalog from 2002. A further release appeared on Le Maquis (2008). In 2018 the band signed to Mute Records, resulting in ACR:MCR (2019) and continued activity.

Full ACR member roster over the years: Andy Connell, Denise Johnson, Donald Johnson, Ellen Beth Abdi, Jez Kerr, Liam Mullan, Martha Tilson, Martin Moscrop, Matt Steele, Peter Terrell, Simon Topping, Tony Quigley.

Kalima

Kerr appeared as guest bassist and vibraphonist on Kalima’s “Night Time Shadows” (Japan/Factory, 1987). Kalima was formed in 1983 from the ashes of Swamp Children and shared several members with ACR — notably Martin Moscrop, Tony Quigley, and Andy Connell. Kerr’s guest appearance reflects the close overlap between the two groups in the mid-1980s Manchester scene.
