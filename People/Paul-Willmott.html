Paul Willmott

Paul Willmott

Aug 09, 20265 min read

Paul Willmott

Bassist and co-songwriter. Discogs artist ID: 563206. Also credited as “Paul Wilmott” and “Paul Wilmot” across releases (Discogs notes the correctly spelled name may be “Paul Wilmot”). Core member of Disco Inferno (1989–1996); later member of Transformer (3) and Lisp.

Biography

Early years and Disco Inferno (1989–1996)

Willmott was one of the founding members of Disco Inferno, formed in Essex in January 1989. The band began as a four-piece with Ian Crause (guitar, vocals), Robert Whatley (drums), Daniel Gish (keyboards), and Willmott on bass. After Gish departed around 1991–92, the trio of Crause, Willmott, and Whatley became the definitive lineup.

As bassist, Willmott contributed to the band’s compositional identity — all Disco Inferno releases credit songwriting jointly to Crause, Willmott, and Whatley. His bass provided the harmonic and rhythmic anchor for the band’s distinctive sound: Crause routed guitar through a Roland S-750 sampler to produce both melodic texture and percussion triggers, while Whatley used drum pads to trigger samples. Willmott’s conventional bass grounded the arrangement.

The band moved through three labels: the small independent ché Records (1991–92), Cheree Records (1992), and finally Rough Trade (1993–95). The Rough Trade years produced their most celebrated work, including the D. I. Go Pop LP (1994, R3072) and four surrounding EPs.

The band split in 1995 amid what Willmott later described as an irreparable personal and creative rift with Crause. As Crause’s approach moved further toward sampler-dominated composition, the bass role was increasingly peripheral, and the working relationship became untenable. Willmott contributed an interview to the booklet of the 2017 reissue 91/92 (Rocket Girl) — the first documented public statement he gave about the band.

Post-Disco Inferno: Transformer (3), Lisp, and production (1996–2000)

After the split, Willmott and Whatley formed Transformer (3) together, joined by George Chesterton and Lee Walker. Transformer (3) has no primary-credited releases in the Discogs database.

Willmott contributed as co-producer to three tracks on Evacuate (1996, Chill Out Label), a compilation by The Mellowtrons, alongside Lee Walker (who was also a Transformer (3) member). Tracks co-produced: 3, 6, 8.

He then joined Lisp, a five-piece East London collective formed with Brad Rubinstein, Jonny Gordon, Jason Hall, and Adam Rich. Lisp released four records on Mind Horizon Recordings and London Records in 1999–2000: Cycles EP (1999), Flatspin (1999, the debut single), Long Way to Climb (1999, London Records — their widest-distributed release, which received XFM airplay), and Shrinking Violets (1999). A second pressing of Cycles appeared in 2000. Willmott is credited as Performer on Cycles; songwriting credits on Lisp releases go to Rubinstein and Gordon. Mixed by Adrian Bushby; mastered by Stuart Hawkes.

Discogs alias: Spaghetti (ID: 194559)

A Discogs entry for “Spaghetti” carries Willmott’s name as its real name. No releases are credited to this entry in the database — it may be an alias used informally or on releases not yet catalogued.
