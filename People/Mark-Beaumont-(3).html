Mark Beaumont (3)

UK musician and composer. Discogs artist ID: 4043810. Co-founder and member of Push-Button Pleasure alongside Terry Burrows. Also a member of The Spawn (2). Name variation: M. Beaumont.

Aliases (all Discogs-confirmed): Berbel Nobodius (ID: 752631), Enski Boski (ID: 294789), Melvyn Z Pad (ID: 2517875), Penal Colada (ID: 165194). The Melvyn Z Pad entry on Discogs carries the realname “Mark Beaumont”, directly linking all four pseudonyms to this identity. No further realname, biography, or web URLs appear in Discogs.

Overview

Mark Beaumont (3) operated exclusively within the orbit of Hamster Records And Tapes, the small UK independent imprint run by Terry Burrows. His output spans 1982–1989 across two bands and at least four solo pseudonyms, all of it released on Hamster. The work sits in the UK DIY experimental/electronic underground, characterised by absurdist humour in titles and an experimental approach to cassette and vinyl formats.

His primary vehicle was Push-Button Pleasure (1982–1987), which he co-founded with Burrows. He wrote, composed, and performed on the first three releases (1982), but no separate Beaumont credit appears on the final LP The Last Dissonance (HAM 19, 1987), which credits Push-Button Pleasure collectively as performer and writer. Whether this reflects a dissolution of the partnership before 1987 or an uncredited contribution is unknown.

By 1989 he had formed The Spawn (2), a five-piece group that released Harakiri For Beginners (HAM 27, 1989) on Hamster, where he played keyboards and saxophone.

His pseudonyms show continued activity into 1989: Berbel Nobodius released Wanton But Windblown (HAM24, 1989), produced and mixed by Melvyn Z Pad (another Beaumont alias) and Burrows. Enski Boski issued a single 7” in 1984 (I Found Jesus In A Gay Bar), with Melvyn Z Pad credited for lyrics and music. Penal Colada released the cassette A Hard Day At The Scout Hut in 1982, with Melvyn Z Pad listed as performer. “Penal Colada” also appears as a track title on the Push-Button Pleasure debut LP, suggesting the name predates or runs parallel to his work under his own name.
