Blondie Chaplin

Real name: Terence William Chaplin Born: 7 July 1951, Durban, KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa Discogs artist ID: 293913 Roles: Guitarist, vocalist, songwriter, occasional pianist and percussionist

South African-born rock guitarist and vocalist best known for his stints with The Beach Boys (1972–1973) and as a long-running backup guitarist and vocalist for The Rolling Stones (from the Bridges to Babylon Tour, 1997, through the 2020s). Three solo albums, the last in 2006.

Biography

Origins: South Africa and The Flames (1951–1969)

Terence “Blondie” Chaplin grew up in South Africa. His route to international recognition ran through a South African rock band called The Flames (Discogs: The Flames (6), ID: 1536122) — a group that also included brothers Ricky Fataar (drums), Brother Fataar (bass), and Steve Fataar (guitar). The Flames relocated to London in 1968, then to California in 1969, where they came to the attention of Carl Wilson of The Beach Boys. Wilson produced their self-titled debut as “The Flame” (Brother Records, 1970), with the band credited as personnel alongside Ricky Fataar on drums, Brother Fataar on bass, and Chaplin and Steve Fataar on guitar and vocals. The Flame was the only act other than The Beach Boys to record for Brother Records.

Two further South African releases — Burning Soul! (Rave, 1967, guitar/vocals) and Soulfire!! (Teal Records, 1989) — bookend his connection to the South African market.

The Beach Boys (1972–1973)

When Dennis Wilson sustained a hand injury that sidelined him from drumming, and as Bruce Johnston’s tenure ended, the Beach Boys absorbed Chaplin and Fataar as full members — not just sidemen. Chaplin co-produced alongside the existing members and is a credited co-writer on multiple tracks.

Solo Career: Asylum LP (1977)

The self-titled Blondie Chaplin (Asylum Records, 1977) is his debut solo record. Produced and mixed by Rob Fraboni, it features an ambitious session cast:

Chaplin played vocals, guitar, bass, percussion, and piano; wrote all songs. Mastered by Bernie Grundman. Two singles were released: “Can You Hear Me” (UK) and “Gimme More Rock ‘N’ Roll” (US).

A second solo album, Between Us (Big Karma Records, 2006), followed — the third in his discography per his Discogs profile (“three solo albums”), though the middle entry is not clearly documented in Discogs structured data.

Session Work and Collaborations (Late 1970s–Early 1990s)

Rick Danko — Chaplin sang background vocals on Rick Danko’s self-titled Arista debut (1977), produced by Rob Fraboni. Other contributors on that record included Band members.

Renée Geyer — backing vocals on the Australian singer’s US Portrait album Renee Geyer (1982). Ricky Fataar co-produced.

Paul Butterfield — guitar and vocals on Paul Butterfield’s The Legendary Paul Butterfield Rides Again (Amherst Records, 1986), alongside Anton Fig on drums and Paul Shaffer on keyboards.

The Band — During the late 1980s Chaplin toured with The Band, stepping in to cover some of Richard Manuel’s vocal parts after Manuel’s death in 1986.

The Byrds 20th Anniversary — Chaplin was a featured player in the Gene Clark / Michael Clarke “20th Anniversary Celebration of the Byrds” band.

Jennifer Warnes — appeared on The Hunter (Private Music, 1992) and presumably The Well (reputed but not yet confirmed in Discogs).

David Johansen — Chaplin was guitarist, co-songwriter, and production assistant on Johansen’s Here Comes The Night (Blue Sky, 1981), writing most of the album’s songs. Later appeared on the retrospective From Pumps To Pompadour: The David Johansen Story (Rhino/Sony, 1995) in a guitar and vocals capacity.

Wingless Angels — vocals and percussion on the Keith Richards-produced Rastafarian Burru drumming field recording project (Island Jamaica/Mindless Records, 1996); Rob Fraboni produced. A second volume appeared in 2010 (Wingless Angels Volumes I & II, Mindless Records).

Charlie Watts Jim Keltner Project — credited as Written-By (CyberOctave/Virgin, 2000).

Other scattered credits: Stranger In This Town (guitar, Germany, 1990); Picaresque (Written-By, Japan, 1987); Change Will Come (Backing Vocals, France, 1988).

His role on Bridges To Babylon goes beyond simple backing: he plays piano on three tracks, which were co-produced by Don Was and The Glimmer Twins, with Rob Fraboni recording/mixing three tracks. The regular Stones touring band on these recordings and live releases features Bernard Fowler, Lisa Fischer, Chuck Leavell, Darryl Jones, Bobby Keys, and the core band (Mick Jagger, Keith Richards, Ron Wood, Charlie Watts).
