Jeff And Jane Hudson

Husband/wife electronic duo. Boston / New York. Active 1981–1985; reformed 1995–c.1998; intermittently 2002–present. Discogs ID: 118494. Also credited as J & J Hudson, Jeff & Jane Hudson, Jane & Jeff Hudson.

Aliases on Discogs (all three are linked entries): Attack Under Attack (Discogs ID 1739859 — one single, 1982), Manhattan Project (7) (Discogs ID 5115446 — pre-duo NYC project, Jane Hudson with Nick Leyland, Rob Keay, Winston Robinson).

Jeff Hudson is also a member of Dresden Danse (Discogs ID 105852) — a separate 1989 project.

Formed out of the Boston art-punk band The Rentals (2) (1977–1980). When the Rentals broke up, Jeff and Jane relocated to Manhattan, opened for Suicide, and quickly signed with Charles Ball’s Lust-Unlust Music / Infidelity (2) operation. Their first two releases — all-synthesizer, lo-fi, confrontational — were Ball productions. From 1982 onward they ran their own J & J Records. Their 1983 LP Flesh is the high-water mark: self-produced, Roland synths and TR-808, alternating Jeff’s quivery “drug-addled” vocals with Jane’s detached and icy delivery. Heavily collected.

Both later co-founded the video studies program at the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.

Origins: The Rentals (2)

Jeff and Jane met and began living in a loft building in Boston. As teenagers they had both listened to psychedelic 1960s music and jazz; punk was a later conversion. In the summer of 1977 they traveled to Europe, saw the Sex Pistols on TV, and went to punk clubs — the trip catalyzed their creative direction. The Rentals formed when they returned that fall.

Lineup: Jeff Hudson, Jane Hudson, Pseudo Carol (Discogs ID 657739; name variations: “Pseudo Carol Angel”, “Pseudo Carol Meinke”). Pseudo Carol is a real person — a third member of the Rentals, not a pseudonym for Jeff or Jane. She took video classes with Jeff and Jane, made little steel guitars and printed etchings of them as art objects — already working in the intersection of sound and visual culture that would define the trio’s shared context. The Rentals opened for The Clash at Harvard Square Theater on February 16, 1979.

On the “I Got A Crush On You” UK single, Pseudo Carol is credited as “Pseudo Carol Angel” and wrote the A-side; Jane wrote the B-side “New York!“. Oedipus (WBCN program director, ID 657740) produced both Rentals singles.

“Gertrude Stein” (b-side: Low Rent) was re-recorded and appears as A4 on the Flesh LP.

Completely self-produced. Roland synths and TR-808. Jeff’s lyrics address technology and politics; Jane’s address cultural and psychological situations. Often cited as one of the most significant American synth records of the 1980s; original copies heavily collected.

Mark Morrisroe’s cover photography is apt: he was a central figure in the Boston queer-punk underground, studying at the same MFA Boston art school where Jeff and Jane would later teach, and is the closest visual analogue to the Flesh aesthetic — rawness and texture over polish.

Russ Smith (bass) and Erik Lindgren (engineer) are both embedded in the Cambridge/Boston post-punk scene: Lindgren runs Arf! Arf! Records and Sounds Interesting Studios, and is a member of Birdsongs Of The Mesozoic; Smith is Roger Miller’s regular bassist, placing this record directly adjacent to the Mission Of Burma world.

Special World / Mother Told Me (7”, 1983)

Label: J & J Records, H12223
Personnel: Jeff & Jane Hudson (synths, vocals), Russ Smith (bass), Wally Gagel (drums), Jeff Whitehead (engineer)

A | Special World (4:38) / B | Mother Told Me (3:31)

The final original-era release. Wally Gagel’s drums introduce a more physical rhythm element alongside the synths — a slight step toward the guitar-based sound of the 1995 reunion.

Edited and compiled by William Furlong. The Discogs alias linkage shows Manhattan Project (7) as an alternate identity for Jeff And Jane Hudson, but this release predates the duo and has a completely different lineup with Jane only.

Between the original run and 1995 reunion: Jeff directed music videos; Jane produced video art. Both co-founded the video studies program at the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston and taught there. Jeff’s 1998 solo project Gigantor was a trip-hop/techno/3D video multimedia show performed at The Bank (New York) and Middle East (Cambridge, MA).

Jeff Hudson also recorded as Dresden Danse (1989) alongside Chris Rossow, George Bolton (2), and Sean Keefe — released “Acid” (Harvard Square Records, cat. unknown). Separate from Jeff And Jane Hudson.

Jane Hudson — Visual Art / Video Art Practice

Jane Hudson is a multidisciplinary artist whose practice runs in parallel to (and well beyond) the Jeff And Jane Hudson music project. Sources include her own artist site (janehudsonpaintings.com/about, consulted July 2026) and contemporaneous press from Berkshire Fine Arts (berkshirefinearts.com).

Background

Hudson (b. New York City) holds a BA in English and Art History from the University of Pennsylvania (1962). Her parents were artists — her father Leonard Shure was a concert pianist; her mother Barbara Edwards attended Yale School of Drama. She describes entering video art in the 1970s “on the ground floor”: she and Jeff purchased a reel-to-reel black-and-white surveillance system (~$1,500) and began experimenting from there. She has consistently described herself as a “first generation video artist.” [source: berkshirefinearts.com, 2019]

Teaching

Hudson taught at the School of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston for 32 years, including graduate-level courses from 1987–2006. Undergraduate courses: Intro to Video (Final Cut Pro, iDVD, Dreamweaver), Fantasy Video (After Effects, Lightwave 3D, DVD Studio Pro), Advanced Media Arts Seminar. Graduate: Contemporary Art Practice Seminar. She and Jeff co-founded the video studies program there. [source: janehudsonpaintings.com/about]

NEA Grant

Hudson received a National Endowment for the Arts (NEA) grant for her video work. Year not specified in sources consulted. [source: saatchiart.com; berkshirefinearts.com, 2011]

Post-Video Painting Practice

From roughly the mid-2000s onward Hudson’s exhibited output shifted toward painting: geometric abstractions in gouache and acrylic, influenced by Kandinsky, Hilma af Klint, Klee, and Sonia Delaunay. Her most recent series is based on the 22 Major Arcana of the Tarot. She and Jeff ran Hudson Art gallery at 112 Water Street, Williamstown, MA (and previously Hudson’s Antiques on the Mass MoCA campus). [source: janehudsonpaintings.com/about]

Mass MoCA Performance (2012)

Jeff and Jane performed as a duo at Mass MoCA on January 14, 2012, in the Alt Cabaret series (Club B-10), celebrating the Dark Entries reissue of Flesh. The set was accompanied by video projections. Supporting artist: Aurora Halal. [source: berkshirefinearts.com, 2011]
