Girls At Our Best

Post-punk band, Leeds, UK. Active 1979–1982. Discogs artist ID: 327015. Also styled Girls At Our Best! on releases.

Overview

Girls At Our Best formed in Leeds in 1979, evolving directly out of a short-lived prior group called The Butterflies (14) (1978–79). The core of the band was vocalist Judy Evans (real name Jo Gaffney; Discogs ID: 315260) and guitarist James Alan (real name Jeremy Pritchatt; Discogs ID: 376762), with bassist Gerard Swift (Discogs ID: 376763) as the third constant member. Drumming was handled by a succession of players across the band’s lifespan.

They self-released their first singles on their own Record Records (2) label (cat nos RR1, RR2) before moving to Happy Birthday Records for the album and subsequent 45s. The “Politics!” single was licensed to Rough Trade for wider distribution. The band broke up in 1982. Cherry Red reissued the album in 2009 and released a full Deluxe Edition (3×CD) in 2022 that included singles, BBC sessions, live recordings, and a Butterflies demo.

Girls At Our Best sit firmly in the Leeds post-punk cluster of 1979–81, contemporaneous with Gang of Four, Delta 5, The Mekons, and the early Sisters of Mercy. Like Delta 5, they combined taut, angular guitar pop with politically charged, feminist-inflected lyrics. Their album Pleasure benefited from an unusually high-profile production circle: Flood (then early in his career) as engineer, Thomas Dolby on synthesizer, and Laurence Diana producing.

Origins: The Butterflies (14)

Before Girls At Our Best, Judy Evans (then Joanna Mary Gaffney), James Alan (then Jeremy Pritchatt), Gerard Swift, and Chris Oldroyd played together as The Butterflies (14) from 1978–79. A demo from 1978, “Attribute”, was included on the 2022 Deluxe Edition of Pleasure, providing the clearest documentation of their pre-GAOB sound.

Scene Context

Girls At Our Best are part of the Leeds post-punk class of 1978–81 documented extensively in the Where Were You- (Independent Music From Leeds 1978-1989) compilation. Their immediate peers in the city included:

  • Gang of Four — angular post-punk with political content; also on Rough Trade and Fast Product
  • Delta 5 — feminist post-punk with interlocking bass lines; similarly female-fronted
  • The Mekons — contemporaneous Leeds art-punk; Fast Product/Rough Trade orbit
  • Sisters of Mercy — formed in Leeds 1980; darker trajectory into goth

The Rough Trade connection on “Politics!” places them in the same distribution and aesthetic network as Scritti Politti, The Fall, Aztec Camera, and other early-80s UK indie acts. The band briefly toured the US in late 1981, playing the Peppermint Lounge in New York (6 November 1981).
