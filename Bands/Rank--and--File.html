Oct 10, 20267 min read

Rank & File

Discogs ID: 10386 Also known as: Rank And File Formed: 1981, Austin, Texas, USA Genres: Country Rock, Cowpunk Labels: Slash, Metronome, Rough Trade, Zensor, Rhino Handmade

Country-rock band founded by brothers Chip Kinman and Tony Kinman (ex-The Dils) alongside Alejandro Escovedo. One of the founding acts of the cowpunk movement, they bridged California punk and Bakersfield-style country. Their debut LP Sundown (1982) on Slash is the genre’s cornerstone record; Escovedo departed after it. The Kinman brothers carried on through a second LP (Long Gone Dead, 1984) and a self-titled third LP (1987) before dissolving.

Biography

Formation and the Cowpunk Moment (1981–1982)

Rank & File grew out of the wreckage of The Dils, the San Diego hardcore-punk duo that Chip Kinman and Tony Kinman had fronted from 1977 to 1980. After the Dils broke up, the Kinmans relocated to Austin, Texas and linked up with Alejandro Escovedo, who had been playing with The Nuns in San Francisco. The three formed Rank & File around 1981, recruiting drummer Slim Evans to complete the debut lineup.

The band signed to Slash, the Los Angeles independent that was simultaneously home to X, The Blasters, and Los Lobos — a cluster of artists renegotiating the boundary between American roots music and punk energy. Rank & File’s debut single, “Amanda Ruth” b/w “Lucky Day,” appeared in 1982 and set the template: tightly constructed country songs delivered with punk directness.

Sundown (1982)

The debut album Sundown was recorded with producer and engineer David Kahne and released in 1982 on Slash. All songs credited to Kinman and Kinman (Chip and Tony). The lineup on the record:

Sundown drew heavily on Merle Haggard-era Bakersfield country and the Everly Brothers’ close harmonies, while keeping the tempos and attack of punk. It is broadly cited as a founding document of cowpunk alongside records by The Blasters and Jason & The Scorchers. David Kahne had not yet become the mainstream pop producer he would later be; the session was lean and live-sounding.

Alejandro Escovedo departed the band after Sundown, going on to front The True Believers and later a long solo career.

Long Gone Dead and Lineup Consolidation (1984)

With Escovedo gone, the Kinmans rebuilt around guitarist Jeff Ross and brought in a new producer, Jeff Eyrich. The second LP, Long Gone Dead, was released in 1984 on Slash (and licensed to Metronome in Germany). The record expanded the sonic palette considerably — Stan Lynch (then also drumming for Tom Petty & The Heartbreakers) played drums and percussion; Richard Greene contributed fiddle; Pete Grant handled pedal steel guitar, slide guitar, autoharp, and banjo; Billy Meyers added woodwinds.

A single, “Sound Of The Rain,” also appeared on Slash in 1984, produced by Jeff Eyrich. A German-only single “I’m An Old Old Man / Sound Of The Rain” was issued on Metronome the same year.

The 1984 UK single “Rank And File” (Rough Trade / Slash) is notable for its tracklist: the A-side “Rank And File” was co-written by Escovedo and Tony Kinman, while the B-side “Amanda Ruth” used the Kinman brothers’ co-write from the debut. David Kahne produced and engineered both sides, suggesting a connection back to the original Sundown sessions or a later re-use.

Self-Titled LP and Dissolution (1987)

The third and final album, Rank And File (self-titled), appeared in 1987, released through Zensor in Germany. By this point the lineup had contracted to the Kinman brothers, Jeff Ross on guitar, and R. Kahr on drums. Producer Bill Pfordresher and engineer/mixer Brad Gilderman handled the recording. The album represents a stripped-back return to basics after the orchestrated textures of Long Gone Dead. The Kinmans wrote all material between them.

The band dissolved after this record. Chip and Tony Kinman went on to form Blackbird and later Chip Kinman has released solo material on In The Red Recordings (2022, 2025). Tony Kinman recorded under the alias Tony Nineteen.

Compilation / Legacy

In 2003, Rhino Handmade issued The Slash Years, a limited, numbered compilation collecting the Slash-era recordings. Wounded Bird Records reissued it in 2013 and again subsequently. This compilation is the primary point of access to the catalog for most listeners.

Scene Context

Rank & File occupy a specific and important node in the early-1980s American underground. The Kinmans arrived carrying hardcore credibility from The Dils, one of the more explicitly political first-wave US punk bands. Their pivot to country was not nostalgic but structural — a move toward space, melody, and American vernacular forms that many of their contemporaries were exploring simultaneously.

Slash served as the institutional home for this convergence. At the same time Rank & File were recording Sundown, Slash was releasing X’s Under The Big Black Sun and The Blasters’ Non Fiction. The label’s aesthetic — roots-inflected, Los Angeles-centred, punk in energy if not always in form — created a coherent milieu that journalists eventually tagged “cowpunk.” Jason & The Scorchers in Nashville and The Meat Puppets in Arizona were parallel nodes.

Alejandro Escovedo’s presence on Sundown connects Rank & File directly to San Francisco’s The Nuns (where Escovedo had previously played) and forward to The True Believers, his next Austin-based project. His departure after Sundown arguably narrowed Rank & File’s subsequent work — the Kinmans became a tighter, more traditionally country-oriented unit on Long Gone Dead, which (though a stronger country record) lacked the three-way guitar dynamic of the debut.
