Amiina

Amiina

Discogs ID: 779865 Origin: Reykjavík, Iceland Active: c. 1999–present (initially as Amína; renamed Amiina in March 2006) Genres: Modern Classical, Ambient, Post-Rock, Experimental Pop

Icelandic string quartet turned experimental pop group. Formed as Amína, they became the touring and studio string quartet for Sigur Rós before developing a substantial independent catalog. Originally a four-piece of female string players, they expanded to a six-piece from 2009 with the addition of drummer Magnús Trygvason Eliassen and electronic artist Kippi Kaninus (Guðmundur Vignir Karlsson). Two of the founding members, Edda and Hildur, departed in 2015.

Biography

Formation and Sigur Rós Association (c. 1999–2006)

Amiina formed in Reykjavík as a string quartet under the name Amína — María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir, Hildur Ársælsdóttir, Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir, and Sólrún Sumarliðadóttir (a fifth member, Ólöf Júlía Kjartansdóttir, appeared on early releases as viola). They came to wider notice as the uncredited string quartet on Sigur Rós’s ( ) (FatCat Records, 2002), where they also arranged the string parts. From there they became a regular part of the Sigur Rós touring band and appeared on Takk… (2005), Heima (live film, 2007), Með Suð Í Eyrum Við Spilum Endalaust (2008), Valtari (2012), and later Ágætis Byrjun anniversary pressings (2019).

Their first solo release appeared under the Amína name: AnimaminA (The Worker’s Institute, 2005), a four-track CD EP credited as “Amina” featuring all four core members as performers and produced by María Huld. In March 2006 they renamed to Amiina to avoid confusion with the Tunisian singer Amina.

Early Solo Records and Lee Hazlewood (2006–2008)

The single “Seoul” (Amiina self-released, 2006) was their first release under the new name, featuring Sólrún, Edda, María, and Ólöf Júlía. In 2007 they issued a split promo single, Hilli “At The Top Of The World”, pairing their track “Hilli” with the vocals of Lee Hazlewood on the Ever Records label — one of Hazlewood’s final recorded appearances before his death in August 2007.

Their debut full-length Kurr (Bláskjár Records, 2007) was entirely self-written, performed, recorded, mixed, and produced by Amiina, with Birgir Jón Birgisson (Biggi) co-recording and mixing, Kjartan Sveinsson (of Sigur Rós) also recording, and Graeme Durham mastering. The album includes guest spots from Orri Páll Dýrason (drums on “Lóri”) and a brass section of Icelandic players.

Puzzle and Expanded Lineup (2009–2013)

In 2009 drummer Magnús Trygvason Eliassen and electronic artist Kippi Kaninus (real name Guðmundur Vignir Karlsson) joined as permanent members, extending the group to six. The second album Puzzle (Amínamúsík Ehf., 2010) features additional strings from Kristín Lárusdóttir (cello), Borgar Magnason (double bass), and a chamber ensemble, with Kjartan Sveinsson on bass on three tracks. Mastered by Greg Calbi (Sterling Sound) and mixed by Birgir Jón Birgisson and Amiina.

The Lighthouse Project (Sound Of A Handshake, 2013) is a 12” EP partly recorded by Ben Frost. It includes a Lee Hazlewood cover (“Leather And Lace”), further consolidating the group’s connection to his catalog.

Post-Departure Work and Fantômas (2014–2022)

Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir and Hildur Ársælsdóttir left the band in 2015, leaving the quartet reduced to Sólrún, María Huld, Magnús, and Kippi Kaninus as the continuing lineup. Fantômas (Mengi, 2016) is a double LP conceived as a soundtrack to the 1913 silent film serial by Louis Feuillade. Credits include mixing and editing by Gunnar Örn Tynes, Silli Geirdal, and Ívar Ragnarsson alongside Amiina, with Birgir Jón Birgisson also recording. Edda and Hildur appear as featured guests on the opening track.

Pharology (self-released, 2021) is a limited 10” three-track EP, recorded by Albert Finnbogason, mixed by Ívar Ragnarsson, mastered by Friðfinnur Sigurðsson, and designed by Ragnar Helgi Ólafsson.

YULE (self-released, 2022) is their most recent release on Discogs.

Session and Side Work

Beyond Sigur Rós, members contributed strings to a broad range of projects: Alex Somers’s records, Jónsi & Alex (Riceboy Sleeps, Parlophone, 2009), Ben Frost (Rumspringa, Temporary Residence, 2011; Nepenthe, Dead Oceans, 2013), Ólöf Arnalds (Innundir Skinni, One Little Indian, 2010), Skálmöld (Ótta, Season of Mist, 2014 — per María Huld credits), and Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds (Push The Sky Away era — María Huld credited on Sweet Heart, Sweet Light by Mark Lanegan, Double Six, 2012). María Huld also arranged strings for Sigur Rós’s Ágætis Byrjun 20th anniversary (Krúnk, 2019) and the band’s Átta (Krúnk, 2023).
