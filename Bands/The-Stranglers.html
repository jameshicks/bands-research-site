The Stranglers

UK punk/new wave band formed as the Guildford Stranglers on 11 September 1974 in Guildford, Surrey. Discogs artist ID: 71891

Overview

One of the more musically experienced acts to emerge alongside the late-1970s British punk wave. The Stranglers’ distinctive sound was built on three pillars: J.J. Burnel’s melodic, aggressive bass guitar; Dave Greenfield’s prominent Hammond organ and synthesiser work; and the sardonic, sometimes misanthropic lyrics shared between Hugh Cornwell and Burnel. Their age — all members were in their late 20s or early 30s when punk broke — set them apart from their contemporaries; so did their musical range, which incorporated jazz, progressive, and psychedelic elements that punk orthodoxy rejected.

Signed to United Artists Records from their first single (1977) through the early 1980s, the band released six studio albums in five years, spanning punk aggression (Rattus Norvegicus, No More Heroes), experimental darkness (The Gospel According to the Meninblack), and romantic pop (La Folie, which yielded the UK top-5 hit “Golden Brown”). Following the UA era they moved to Liberty and then Epic, continuing to evolve through the 1980s. Cornwell left in 1990 to pursue a solo career; subsequent lineups maintained the band’s active recording and touring schedule. Greenfield died of COVID-19 on 3 May 2020; founding drummer Jet Black died on 6 December 2022. The band released their final studio album Dark Matters in 2021 and undertook a 50th anniversary tour in 2024.
