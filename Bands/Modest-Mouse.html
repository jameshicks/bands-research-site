Modest Mouse

Indie rock band. Issaquah, WA → Missoula, MT → Portland, OR. Discogs ID: 187919. Active 1992–present.

Founded by Isaac Brock (born July 9, 1975, Issaquah, WA). Began as a trio — Brock, Eric Judy (bass), Jeremiah Green (drums) — and evolved through four distinct eras marked by lineup changes, label shifts, and sonic expansion. Signed to Epic Records in 1999 after four years on indie Up Records. Best known for the crossover hits “Float On” (Good News, 2004) and “Dashboard” (We Were Dead, 2007). Brock’s side project Ugly Casanova released Sharpen Your Teeth (Sub Pop, 2002) during a gap in Modest Mouse recording.

Era 1 — Issaquah Trio (1992–1999)

Brock, Judy, and Green formed the band in Issaquah (suburban Seattle) as teenagers. Early releases on Up Records and K Records established the raw, literary aesthetic — Brock’s elliptical lyrics about American transience, driving, poverty, and mortality, over scrappy guitar rock with unconventional time signatures.

This Is A Long Drive For Someone With Nothing To Think About (1996, Up Records UP027) was produced by Steve Wold, who also played slide guitar and mandolin on the record. Calvin Johnson (K Records founder, Beat Happening) contributed extra vocals on “Head South.” The album’s sprawling, introspective quality — nine long tracks on loneliness and open roads — set the template for the band’s lyrical world.

The Lonesome Crowded West (1997, Up Records up44) was produced by Calvin Johnson. Dann Gallucci (guitar) contributed to “Trailer Trash” and “Bankrupt On Selling” — his first documented appearance. Tyler Riley played fiddle. Scott Swayze engineered and added guitar on two tracks. The album’s denser, more aggressive guitar work earned the band a cult following and a reputation as one of the defining indie acts of the Pacific Northwest.

Era 2 — Epic and The Moon & Antarctica (2000–2003)

Signed to Epic. The Moon & Antarctica (2000, Epic E2 63871) is widely regarded as the band’s peak achievement — a double LP of science, cosmology, and existential dread. Produced by Brian Deck (Califone, Red Red Meat), who also played keyboards on “A Different City.” Tim Rutili (Califone) contributed backing vocals on “Paper Thin Walls.” Tyler Riley returned for violin on multiple tracks. Ben Blankenship played lap steel, banjo, keyboards, and guitar throughout. Mastered by Bob Ludwig; cover photography by Simon Larbalestier (also known for 4AD covers).

Brock’s side project Ugly Casanova occupied 2002: Sharpen Your Teeth (Sub Pop), with Deck and Rutili as core collaborators.

Era 3 — Good News and the Lineup Shift (2004–2009)

Good News For People Who Love Bad News (2004, Epic EK 87125) marked a new lineup: Dann Gallucci joined full-time on guitar; Benjamin Weikel on keyboards; Jeremiah Green departed mid-sessions (returned later); Joe Plummer (drums) joined. Produced by Dennis Herring. The Dirty Dozen Brass Band contributed horns to two tracks (tr.1, tr.9). The Flaming Lips appeared on “The Good Times Are Killing Me” (mixed by Dave Fridmann). Tom Peloso contributed violin (tr.13) and double bass (tr.8, 11, 12). Clay Jones engineered. “Float On” became the band’s best-known single, reaching mainstream airplay.

Johnny Marr (The Smiths) joined the band in 2006. He appears as performer on We Were Dead Before The Ship Even Sank (2007, Epic). Jeremiah Green returned alongside Joe Plummer — both are credited as performers. Tom Peloso continues. Produced again by Dennis Herring; mixed by Joe Zook. Clay Jones engineered. Key outside contributions: James Mercer (The Shins) on backing vocals for “Florida,” “Missed The Boat,” and “We’ve Got Everything”; Kyle “Slick” Johnson (programming on several tracks). “Dashboard” became the era’s hit single.

No One’s First And You’re Next (EP, 2009, Epic) followed. Johnny Marr and Dann Gallucci both departed around 2008–2009.

Era 4 — Strangers To Ourselves and The Golden Casket (2015–2021)

After a six-year gap, Strangers To Ourselves (2015, Epic) arrived. Discogs credits for this release are sparse — no additional musicians credited beyond the band.

The Golden Casket (2021, Epic) brought a significantly expanded studio cast. Russell Higbee on bass; Tom Peloso (piano, lap steel, electric piano, horn, Nord electric piano, etc. across most tracks); Ben Massarella (percussion, coordinated across all tracks). Dave Sardy co-wrote and produced tracks 1–11, also performing guitar, drums, piano, organ, synth, and vocals extensively throughout. Jacknife Lee co-produced and performed on tracks 2, 4, 7, and 10. Jim Fairchild (Grandaddy) co-wrote “We Are Between,” “We’re Lucky,” and “Walking And Running” and played guitar. Lisa Molinaro contributed viola and vocals (tr.4, 5, 11, 12). Enrique Ugalde performed Tuvan throat singing on “Never Fuck A Spider On The Fly.” Track 12 (“Back To The Middle”) was produced by Andrew Weiss and engineered/mixed by John McEntire (Tortoise). Eric Judy received a writing credit on “Walking And Running” despite having left the band in 2012.

Key Connections

Brian Deck — produced The Moon & Antarctica (2000); also produced Holopaw’s debut (2003) and co-produced Ugly Casanova’s Sharpen Your Teeth (2002). The Deck connection anchors Modest Mouse to the Chicago Califone/Red Red Meat scene.

Tim Rutili — backing vocals on The Moon & Antarctica; core figure of Califone and Red Red Meat alongside Deck.

Calvin Johnson — extra vocals on Long Drive; produced Lonesome Crowded West. K Records / Olympia, WA connection.

Tyler Riley — fiddle on Lonesome Crowded West; violin on The Moon & Antarctica. Recurring session player.

Johnny Marr — joined 2006 for We Were Dead; one of the most prominent outside additions in the band’s history.

James Mercer — backing vocals on three tracks of We Were Dead; frontman of The Shins.

Jim Fairchild — guitarist of Grandaddy; co-writer and player on The Golden Casket.

John McEntire — Tortoise / post-rock Chicago figure; engineered and mixed “Back To The Middle” on The Golden Casket.

Ugly Casanova — Brock’s solo-alias side project with Deck and Rutili; two releases on Sub Pop (2002). Pall Jenkins (The Black Heart Procession) and John Orth (Holopaw) guested.
