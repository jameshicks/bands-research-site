No-Man

UK art-rock duo formed 1986 in Hemel Hempstead, Hertfordshire. Discogs artist ID: 40451.

Core members: Tim Bowness (vocals, lyrics) and Steven Wilson (instruments, production, writing). The group sits at the intersection of progressive rock, ambient, jazz, and art-pop — characterised by Bowness’s melancholic vocals over Wilson’s dense, layered instrumental arrangements.

Formation and Early Years (1986–1990)

No-Man began as a trio called No Man Is An Island (Except The Isle Of Man), with Bowness, Wilson, and violinist Ben Coleman, plus guitarist Stuart Blagden. Blagden departed early and the group shortened their name to No-Man.

Their first proper release was “The Girl From Missouri” (1989, Plastic Head Records Limited), followed by the EP Colours (1990, Probe Plus) — a trio effort with Coleman on violin, covering Donovan’s “Colours” alongside original material. Wilson handled electric guitar, keyboards, and drum programming throughout.

One Little Indian Period (1991–1994)

Signing to One Little Indian (label ID: 311) brought No-Man their highest public profile. The single “Days In The Trees” (1991) won Single of the Week in both Melody Maker and Sounds. The debut mini-album Lovesighs - An Entertainment (1991) established the trio sound, with Wilson producing and engineering (alongside engineers Graeme Holdaway, Eric Thompson, and Ingo Vauk), and Coleman contributing violin throughout.

Commercial success proved elusive, and the band began moving into denser art-rock territory, drawing in a remarkable roster of guest players from the post-punk and progressive worlds:

  • Robert Fripp — provided sample material on Wild Opera
  • Richard Barbieri — provided sample material on Wild Opera
  • Steve Jansen — drums on Returning Jesus
  • Mel Collins — sample material on Wild Opera
  • Mick Karn — associated with the One Little Indian sessions

Loveblows & Lovecries - A Confession (1993) was the key One Little Indian album: Coleman contributed violin across the record, and Wilson handled all production and instrumentation alongside Bowness’s vocals and lyrics. Ben Coleman and Wilson shared writing credits on “track 1 and 10”; Bowness and Wilson co-wrote the remainder.

Flowermouth (1994) was the final One Little Indian LP, produced and engineered entirely by Wilson (with Graeme Holdaway assisting). Bowness and Wilson split with both Coleman and One Little Indian at the end of 1994.

3rd Stone Period (1995–1998)

After departing One Little Indian, No-Man became a duo and signed to 3rd Stone (label ID: 10015). The label, associated with Steven Wilson’s broader network, released three studio records plus an anthology.

Wild Opera (1996) was a significant statement: Wilson and Bowness incorporated tapes and samples, with sample material provided by Fripp, Barbieri, and Mel Collins — drawing explicit connections to the King Crimson / Japan lineage. The record was entirely self-produced and performed by the duo.

Housewives Hooked On Heroin (1996) and Dry Cleaning Ray (1997) continued on 3rd Stone, with the duo handling all writing, performance, and production.

Carolina Skeletons Radio Edit (1998) was the final 3rd Stone single.

The archival Speak: 1988–89 (1993, Hidden Art Creations) and Radio Sessions 1992–96 (1998, Hidden Art) began documenting earlier material, as did All The Blue Changes - An Anthology 1988–2003 (2006, Hidden Art).

Hidden Art / Independent Period (1998–2007)

The duo’s own imprint Hidden Art (label ID: 13289) became the home for archival and side releases through this period. Key output:

  • Heaven Taste (1995) — the bridge EP between OLI and 3rd Stone eras
  • Flowermix (1995) — remix collection
  • Speak: 1988–89 (1993) / Radio Sessions 1992–96 (1998)
  • Lost Songs: Volume One (2001, Burning Shed) — rarities
  • All That You Are (2003, Hidden Art) — collaborative EP
  • All The Blue Changes (2006, Hidden Art) — double-disc anthology

Returning Jesus (2001, 3rd Stone) marked the duo’s first full LP in five years, and introduced a more refined personnel structure: Steve Jansen (drums), Colin Edwin (bass and double bass), with Wilson on all instruments and production, Bowness on vocals. Mastering by Chris Thorpe; Carl Glover (4) on design.

The Break-Up For Real (2007, Snapper Music) continued the partnership.

Kscope Period (2008–present)

Schoolyard Ghosts (2008, Kscope; label ID: 112611) was the most expansive production in the band’s history, featuring the London Session Orchestra (arranged by Dave Stewart), alongside a large cast of contributors:

Wilson recorded, mixed, and co-produced; Bowness supplied lyrics. Stephen Bennett contributed additional recording.

Love And Endings (2012, Burning Shed) brought in Michael Bearpark (guitar), Pete Morgan (bass), Andrew Booker (drums), and Stephen Bennett (keyboards), with Wilson on guitar and mixing. Carl Glover (4) handled artwork and photography.

Love You To Bits (2019, Caroline International) introduced Adam Holzman (electric piano), David Kollar (guitar), and Ash Soan (drums). Mixed by Bruno Ellingham; mastered by Matt Colton. Carl Glover (4) again handled design.

Swagger (Lost Not Lost Volume One: 1989/1990) (2024, Burning Shed) — archival release from the early trio period.
