Yeah Yeah Yeahs

New York art-rock trio formed 2000. Members: Karen O (vocals), Nick Zinner (guitar), Brian Chase (drums). Discogs artist ID: 95405.

Overview

Yeah Yeah Yeahs were one of the defining acts of the early-2000s New York indie rock scene — a raw, physically intense live band that emerged from the same Lower East Side milieu as The Strokes, Interpol, and LCD Soundsystem. They built their reputation through relentless touring and a run of self-released recordings before signing to Polydor/Dress Up and releasing their debut album Fever To Tell in 2003. From the start their primary producer was Dave Sitek of TV On The Radio, a relationship that would persist, with interruptions, through their 2022 comeback record Cool It Down.

The band are notable for their stylistic range: Fever To Tell is scrappy garage-punk; Show Your Bones pulls toward art rock; It’s Blitz! commits almost entirely to synthesizer-heavy new wave; Mosquito scatters across genre; Cool It Down is spare and orchestral. Across all of this Karen O remained one of the most distinctive front-persons of her generation — both in her vocal style and her elaborate, art-damaged stage presentation. Nick Zinner’s guitar work is similarly elastic, capable of both jagged no-wave noise and clean melodic lines. Brian Chase’s drumming anchors the trio with a physicality that often does the rhythmic work bass would handle in a more conventional band.

They went on extended hiatus after Mosquito (2013), with members pursuing solo projects, before returning with Cool It Down (2022) on Secretly Canadian.

Nick Launay and Dave Sitek both recorded the album, with Sitek back in a recording engineer role rather than lead producer. Mark “Spike” Stent mixed — a commercial mixer more commonly associated with major pop and rock records, representing a shift in production approach for the band. The album’s synth-heavy new wave sound (informed by New Order and LCD Soundsystem) was a deliberate stylistic pivot.

Tracklist: Zero / Heads Will Roll / Soft Shock / Skeletons / Dull Life / Shame And Fortune / Runaway / Dragon Queen / Hysteric / Little Shadow (+ 4 acoustic bonus tracks on some editions).

Three distinct production camps on Cool It Down: Sitek (4 tracks, the harder-edged material); Justin Raisen with “Money” Mark Nishita (2 tracks, stranger/experimental); Andrew Wyatt with orchestral strings (2 tracks, the most expansive). Shawn Everett mixed the full album. Greg Calbi and Steve Fallone co-mastered. The album marks the band’s signing to Secretly Canadian, their first release outside a major/major-distributed label.

NYC Scene Connections

The band formed in and around the Lower East Side in 2000, the same moment that produced The Strokes, Interpol, LCD Soundsystem, and TV On The Radio. Dave Sitek’s dual role as YYY producer and TV On The Radio co-founder is the single most important connection tying together the Brooklyn/downtown NYC art-rock network of the 2000s.

Other connections:

  • Perfume Genius (Mike Hadreas) — featured on Cool It Down track “Spitting Off The Edge Of The World”
  • James Murphy — engineered on Mosquito; LCD Soundsystem’s influence is audible on It’s Blitz!
  • Spike Jonze — director; appears as camera operator on Tell Me What Rockers To Swallow DVD; directed Karen O’s Where The Wild Things Are
  • Lance Bangs — documentary filmmaker; camera operator and director on the Tell Me What Rockers To Swallow DVD
  • Russell Simins (Jon Spencer Blues Explosion) — handclaps on Is Is EP
  • Head Wound City — Nick Zinner’s hardcore supergroup with The Locust / Blood Brothers members
  • Christian Joy — costume designer; credited for artwork on Is Is EP; Karen O’s longtime stage costume collaborator
  • Mark Ramos-Nishita (“Money” Mark) — synth on Cool It Down tracks “Fleez” and “Mars”; Beastie Boys affiliate
