Mr. Stress Blues Band

Blues / rock band. Cleveland, Ohio. Active 1966–2010. Discogs artist ID: 5064764.

Led by harmonica player and vocalist Bill “Mr. Stress” Miller, the MSBB was the longest-running blues institution in Cleveland, cycling through dozens of musicians over four decades. Its alumni list reads as a partial roster of Cleveland’s mid-1970s underground, including future members of Rocket From The Tombs, Pere Ubu, the Pretenders, and the New York downtown scene.

Biography

Origins (1966)

Bill Miller formed the Mr. Stress Blues Band in the summer of 1966, inspired by The Paul Butterfield Blues Band and Chicago electric blues performers. The band’s name was derived from a term used in psychiatric hospitals. Miller acknowledged other “white blues” acts of the era — The Blues Project, John P. Hammond, the Siegel-Schwall Band — but the MSBB reportedly outplayed all of them on a typical night. The Discogs profile quotes the Pretenders’ “Precious” (1979) as a tribute: “Now Howard The Duck and Mr. Stress both stayed / ‘Trapped in a world that they never made’” — written by Chrissie Hynde, a MSBB alumna.

Cleveland Residencies and the Mid-1970s Scene (1972–1979)

The band held a bi-weekly residency at the Brick Cottage (1972–1979), a legendary Cleveland club, and later the Euclid Tavern (1979–late 1990s). They rarely toured outside Ohio but opened for Bonnie Raitt, Joe Jackson, Little Steven, Steppenwolf, the MC5, Nazz, and Three Dog Night, among others.

The mid-1970s lineup period is the band’s most historically significant: the musicians who passed through during these years included several who would go on to shape post-punk and downtown avant-garde music. Anton Fier (later The Golden Palominos, The Feelies, Pere Ubu) drummed for the band before relocating to New York. Peter Laughner (later Rocket From The Tombs / Pere Ubu) was also an alumnus. Chrissie Hynde, before forming the Pretenders in London, passed through the Cleveland scene and the MSBB orbit.

The Live At The Brick Cottage 1972-1973 LP (Smog Veil Records, 2016) documents this earliest documented lineup on vinyl. The 1972–73 band comprised: Miller (vocals, blues harp), Chuck “Pontiac Slim” Drazdik (guitar), Mike “The Professor” Sands (electric piano), Tom Rinda (bass), and two drummers sharing duties — Kenny Ruscitto and Dr. Pete Sinks.

The Euclid Tavern Years (1979–1990s)

The band shifted home base to the Euclid Tavern, which became a Cleveland institution in its own right. The Tavern appeared in Paul Schrader’s 1987 film Light of Day (starring Michael J. Fox and Joan Jett), though the MSBB itself was not featured. The 1981 Mr. Stress Live at the Euclid Tavern LP captures this period with a different lineup from the Brick Cottage era: Mike Modlin and Timothy Matson on electric guitars, Mike Sands on electric piano (carried over from 1972–73), Ray DeForest on bass, and Nick Tranchito on drums. The record was self-released on ET Records (catalog ET 6969) and produced by Miller and Andrew “Saint” Baird.

Later Years and Death

Miller continued leading the band through the 1990s. A 1994 cassette/LP Killer Stress (Not On Label) captured a later lineup with Dave Buyers (guitar, vocals), James Gatewood (bass, vocals), Mike Sands (keyboards), Dan Mahoney (drums), and Anne M. Price (backing vocals on one track); produced by Buyers and co-produced by Mark Klein.

Miller ceased performing regularly in 2010 but made occasional guest appearances through 2013. He died on May 18, 2015, at his apartment in the Musicians Towers, Cleveland Heights, Ohio. A full band history was included in the liner notes of Smog Veil’s Live At The Brick Cottage 1972-73 (2016).

Cleveland Scene Context

The MSBB was a training ground for musicians who would define the Cleveland underground of the mid-1970s. The same milieu produced Rocket From The Tombs (proto-punk, 1973–1975), Electric Eels (proto-punk/no wave), Pere Ubu (post-punk, formed 1975), and The Styrenes (new wave). Peter Laughner, the most mythologized figure of that scene, moved between several of these groups and the MSBB. Anton Fier brought the blues-band discipline of the MSBB into more experimental territory after relocating to New York. Chrissie Hynde was part of the Cleveland scene before moving to London, where she assembled the Pretenders; her 1979 lyric referencing Miller documents that the band’s reputation extended beyond Ohio.

The 2016 Smog Veil archival LP was remastered by Paul Hamann of Suma Recording, a key figure in Cleveland recording. Package design by John Morton of Electric Eels.
