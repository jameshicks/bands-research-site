Formation and early history

Formed in 1975 in Lyon as Femme Fatale, assembled by students from Lycée Saint-Exupéry for a year-end concert. Original lineup: Marie Girard (vocals), her brother Philippe Girard (drums), Erik Fitoussi (guitar), Christian Faye (guitar), and Jean-Pierre Charriau (bass). Marc Zermati (founder of Skydog Records) spotted them performing and suggested the name change to Marie et les Garçons. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-05-27; rockmadeinfrance.com)

1976 — Philippe Girard departed to join Electric Callas. Marie Girard moved from vocals to drums, Patrick Vidal became lead singer, and Jean-Marc Vallod joined on bass. This is the reorganization that created the band’s classic post-punk lineup.

1977 — Performed at the Mont-de-Marsan Festival alongside The Jam, The Clash, The Police, and Shakin’ Street. This is where they met Michel Esteban, who signed them (first to Rebel Records, then to his new SPY Records). Late 1977: Jean-Marc Vallod also joined Electric Callas (the two bands were running in parallel, sharing members).

1978 — John Cale (Velvet Underground) heard their demos via Esteban and offered to produce them in New York. In March 1978 they recorded in New York. They also performed at CBGB. The band and Esteban performed at the “Le Rock d’Ici à l’Olympia” festival in Paris (July 8–10, 1978) alongside Electric Callas, Stinky Toys, Starshooter, and others.

1979 — During recording of their debut album in New York (which Patrick Vidal was steering in a disco direction), Marie Girard left due to “musical incompatibility” and returned to France. The remaining members continued as “Garçons” and released the album Divorce (March 1979) with the single “French Boy.” Neither was commercially successful. Marie Girard subsequently joined Electric Callas for the recording of Winner (1981). (rockmadeinfrance.com; Wikipedia)

Reunions and later activity

  • 1987: Reunited to re-record “Re-Bop”
  • 1988, 1990: Live performances
  • 2009, 2011, 2015: Further reunions (Wikipedia, as of 2025-05-27)

Marie Girard

Marie Girard (1956 – 6 August 1996) worked as a fashion designer after her music career. She died of a cerebral aneurysm rupture, aged 40. (Wikipedia, as of 2025-05-27)

Relationship to Electric Callas — detailed timeline

The two bands were not sequential but heavily overlapping and interconnected:

PeriodEvent
1975Femme Fatale formed (proto-Marie Et Les Garçons)
1976Philippe Girard leaves for Electric Callas; Marie Et Les Garçons lineup solidifies
late 1976Electric Callas forms with Philippe Girard, Jean-Marc Vallod, Frankie Fender, Jangil Callas
1977Jean-Marc Vallod joins Electric Callas while still (or recently) in Marie Et Les Garçons
July 1978Both bands perform at Olympia punk festival
early 1979Marie Girard leaves Marie Et Les Garçons / Garçons; joins Electric Callas
1979Electric Callas releases So Chic (with Jangil + the current lineup)
1981Electric Callas releases Winner (with Marie Girard on drums)

The bands shared Philippe Girard, Jean-Marc Vallod, and Marie Girard across their respective runs — but not simultaneously in all cases. Electric Callas was in effect Jangil’s project that absorbed ex-Marie Et Les Garçons members as that band evolved and fractured. (rockmadeinfrance.com; Wikipedia)
