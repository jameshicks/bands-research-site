Algebra Suicide

Chicago-based spoken-word / post-punk duo active 1982–1994, comprising poet Lydia Tomkiw (vocals, lyrics) and musician Don Hedeker (guitar, synthesizer, drum machine). Their sound placed Tomkiw’s deadpan, literary-inflected poetry over Hedeker’s minimal post-punk instrumentation. They released prolifically on their own Buzzerama Records imprint and later via international labels before dissolving in 1994. Both original recordings and archive compilations were reissued by Dark Entries in the 2010s.

Discogs artist ID: 118500

History

Formation and Early 7”s (1982–1985)

Algebra Suicide formed in Chicago in 1982. Their debut release, the 7” EP True Romance At The Worlds Fair (Buzzerama Records, AA-500, 1982), established the template immediately: four tracks of Tomkiw’s deadpan-delivered poetry over Hedeker’s skeletal guitar and electronics, engineered by Jerry Soto. A second 7” EP, An Explanation For That Flock Of Crows (Buzzerama, BB-1000, 1985), continued the same approach and the same engineer-producer pairing.

Sleeve photography on both early EPs was handled by John Tomkiw (Discogs ID: 2772758), likely a family member of Lydia’s.

Mid-period: Cassettes and First Album (1986–1988)

Big Skin (Cause And Effect, 1986) was released as a cassette-plus-text package — a combined audio and poetry volume, consistent with Lydia Tomkiw’s parallel activity as a published poet. The cassette was credited to Hedeker (guitar, synthesizer) and Tomkiw (voice, lyrics) without additional personnel.

The Secret Like Crazy (Dom Elchklang / RRRecords, 001 / RRR 022, 1987) was their first proper LP. It functioned partly as a retrospective, gathering tracks from the two 7”s alongside newer material — 21 tracks across two sides. Lydia Tomkiw is credited with cover design in addition to vocals and lyrics. Engineer again: Jerry Soto.

Real Numbers (Pursuit Of Market Share, POMS-001, 1988) was recorded live and released in Germany as a limited, numbered CD. Hedeker is credited with guitar and tape; Tomkiw with voice and text. Recording was by Ken Rasek. Cover design involved Algebra Suicide themselves alongside German designers Bernhard Assfalg and Franz Liebl.

The duo toured Europe twice during this period, presenting live shows with slide projections, and maintained an active network of underground tape labels and artists throughout the 1980s. They also ran Chicago’s Link Hall Club.

Belgian Years: Body Records (1990–1991)

Alpha Cue (Body Records, BR7008, 1990) marked a move to the Belgian label Body Records (a sublabel of Antler-Subway). Production expanded to a full studio setup with multiple engineers — Benj Kanters, David Appelt, and Scott Steinman as primary engineers, with Andy Herman, Kathy Yore, and Scott Grinder as assistants. Notably, Dave Riley (Big Black bassist) contributed bass, piano, and percussion on two tracks (“Summer Virus Night,” “What Rubs Up To You”).

Swoon (Body Records, BR-7011, 1991) was released as a CD collecting the Alpha Cue LP tracks plus bonus material from those sessions. Scott Steinman engineered; Dave Riley again contributed bass, piano, and percussion on the Alpha Cue bonus tracks. Hedeker is credited with guitar and synthesizer; this release carried co-writing credits for both members.

Dissolution and Final Appearances (1992–1994)

The duo contributed five tracks to the Chicago compilation Rehearsal For Retirement (Widely Distributed Records, WDRU023, 1992), alongside The Lilacs, Hip Deep Trilogy, and Fingerjays. Algebra Suicide produced their own five tracks; engineers for those tracks were Chuck Uchida and Mark Schwartz.

Lydia Tomkiw appeared (uncredited) on a 7” by Tag Team (3) — Evidence (Crosstalk, xtr001, 1993) — contributing voice and lyrics to the A-side, alongside co-producers Steve Fisk and Phillip Hertz.

The duo’s active period ended in 1994.

Post-Breakup

After the split, Don Hedeker formed The Polkaholics, a polka-punk outfit with a first release in 1998. Lydia Tomkiw released one solo album in 1995 with various collaborators, then concentrated on writing. She died September 4, 2007 in Phoenix, Arizona, at age 48.

Dark Entries Reissues (2013–2019)

San Francisco label Dark Entries reissued Algebra Suicide’s catalog across two compilations:

  • Feminine Squared (Dark Entries, DE-042, 2013): LP + DVD package compiling the two Buzzerama 7”s plus additional tracks, with live video from the “666 Show” at the Noise Factory (August 30, 1984). Remastered by George Horn; tape transfer and restoration by Alberto Hernandez. Engineer: Jerry Soto. Video directed by Mark Giangrande.
  • Still Life (Dark Entries, DE-252, 2019): LP compiling tracks from the Big Skin cassette and additional unreleased/rare material. Engineer: Jerry Soto.
