23 Skidoo

Active: 1979–1987; reformed 2000, 2012 Based: North London (later Hackney, then Highbury) Labels: Pineapple, Fetish, Operation Twilight/Crépuscule, Illuminated, Virgin, Ronin, LTM Genre: Post-punk / Industrial / Funk / Percussion / Avant-garde

Band Name Origin

The name was chosen by founding drummer Fritz Catlin, drawing on two literary sources simultaneously. In a 2021 Ransom Note interview, a band member explained:

“Its from the Illuminatus and also Alistair Crowley from the Book Of Lies. Chapter 23 is called Skidoo. What man is at ease in his inn—get out. We took it as a reference to perpetual change. We believed/believe very strongly that individuals should evolve constantly rather than stagnate.”

The two sources are:

  1. Robert Anton Wilson and Robert Shea’s Illuminatus! trilogy (1975) — which made heavy use of the number 23 as a cosmic/conspiratorial motif, itself drawing on Burroughs’ “23 enigma.”
  2. Aleister Crowley’s The Book of Lies (Liber CCCXXXIII) — Chapter 23 is titled “Skidoo.” The chapter’s central imperative is “get out,” describing the Great Work as progressively shedding all attachments. Crowley’s own commentary on the chapter notes that “23” and “Skidoo” are both American slang for “get out.”

The band explicitly rejected occult readings of the name: “There is a bit of a misconception with regards to Skidoo and Crowley. We are not occultists or anything like that.” The philosophical meaning — constant movement, refusal of stagnation, perpetual change — was the operative one. The Crépuscule biography summarizes this as “cut, run and confound” being “a guiding principle for this unique, unorthodox group.”

Tom Heslop separately acknowledged engagement with Burroughs’ literary techniques (“Certainly I read a lot of William Burroughs, and we’ve played with the cut-up thing”), consistent with the Burroughs/Wilson/Crowley triad all converging on the “23” motif, but the specific name derivation is from Crowley + Illuminatus rather than directly from Burroughs.

Sources: Ransom Note interview, August 2021; Crépuscule biography; LTM biography

Seven Songs (February 1982) captures this lineup. “Tearing Up The Plans” (May 1982) was recorded without the Turnbull brothers, who were in Indonesia at the time — leaving Catlin, Mills, and Heslop. It was the last recording with that trio.

June 1982 split: Mills and Heslop were dismissed when the Turnbull brothers returned from Indonesia carrying gamelan influence. The musical direction had diverged irreconcilably — Mills and Heslop wanted to stay in the punk/electronics lane; the Turnbulls were moving toward percussion, world music, and funk.

Lineup 2 — Trio (mid-1982 – 1983)

Fritz Catlin, Johnny Turnbull, Alex Turnbull. The Culling Is Coming (1983) was recorded and performed by this core three, with David Tibet (Current 93) guesting on Tibetan trumpet. The two Crépuscule/WOMAD performances documented on the LP were during this period.

Connection to Dining Out Records

The connection is deeper than previously documented. Alex Turnbull was a member of Club Tango (1981, Dining Out Records TUX7) — a band formed by Dave Henderson and Andy Ross (both ex-Disco Zombies) after the Disco Zombies split. This places Alex Turnbull as an active participant in the Dining Out inner circle, not merely someone who helped finish sleeves.

Additionally, 23 Skidoo members helped with sleeve finishing for Dining Out Records releases — a separate, hands-on social connection in the same orbit.

How They Got Signed (Fetish Records)

Tom Heslop shared a squat with Neville Brody, who was design director at Fetish Records. Brody connected the band to Fetish and designed their iconic early artwork, launching one of the most important creative partnerships in post-punk visual history.

Crépuscule — full catalog and context

The connection to Les Disques du Crépuscule originated with the Move Back/Bite Harder tour (October 1982) — a Crépuscule-organized Belgian package tour featuring Cabaret Voltaire, Tuxedomoon, Antena, Isolation Ward, and The Pale Fountains alongside 23 Skidoo.

The Culling Is Coming (TWI 123, 4 February 1983) — co-released with Operation Twilight, Crépuscule’s UK sublabel run by Patrick Moore (later the writer Philip Hoare). Documents the WOMAD festival set (July 1982, scrap metal and tape loops) and a Dartington College session with the Balinese Gamelan Orchestra (October 1982). Both original sides ran exactly 23 minutes.

James Nice and the dispute: Nice worked at Crépuscule from 1987, rising from the mailroom to A&R. He departed in 1988 specifically over a 23 Skidoo contract — he describes it as “an argument about a 23 SKIDOO contract, not that anyone will be interested.” The specific subject (royalties, rights, a new deal, accounting) has never been put on record by either party. Nice and Michel Duval reconciled “soon after.” Nice later became the primary custodian of the Crépuscule catalog and released more 23 Skidoo material than any other label since Illuminated. See also LTM Recordings.

Eric Random note: Eric Random’s single “23 Skidoo / Subliminal” (TWI 029, 1981) — A-side titled “23 Skidoo” — predates the band’s own Crépuscule releases. Random was embedded in the same orbit; the title is likely a tribute rather than a coincidence.

L.A.Y.L.A.H. 1988 reissue and the Nice dispute — research note (web, 2026-07-17; updated 2026-08-11):

The 1988 L.A.Y.L.A.H. reissue of The Culling Is Coming (LAY 23 vinyl + LAYCD 23 CD) and Nice’s departure in the same year are two distinct events involving the same band and the same Crépuscule orbit, but available sources do not confirm a direct causal link between them.

What the sources establish:

  • L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords was founded by Marc Monin in 1984, with Crépuscule providing initial funding; A&R was independent of Crépuscule (funding later passed to distributor Himalaya). Sources: Wikipedia — L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords; Crépuscule history page.
  • L.A.Y.L.A.H.’s roster (Coil, Current 93, Nurse With Wound, Laibach, The Hafler Trio) was in the same industrial/occult orbit as The Culling Is Coming — making the reissue a logical A&R fit for Monin’s label regardless of any internal Crépuscule politics. The 1988 reissue used different artwork from the 1983 original.
  • The reissue was authorized. The 1988 L.A.Y.L.A.H. vinyl sleeve explicitly states: “L.A.Y.L.A.H antirecords, in association with Operation Twilight, Les Disques du Crépuscule, and 23 Skidoo ℗ 1983, 1988 © 1988 Made in Belgium.” This credits the original label (Operation Twilight/Crépuscule) and the band directly, confirming it was not an unauthorized release. Source: Discogs release r120168.
  • Nice joined Crépuscule in 1987 after graduating from Glasgow University, starting in the mailroom/mail-order backlog role. He worked there for approximately one year before departing in 1988 — “finally I ended up walking out over a 23 Skidoo contract” (Louder Than War). He and Michel Duval reconciled soon after. After leaving Crépuscule he went to work at PIAS; the Futurism & Dada Reviewed compilation he had been working on moved with him to Sub Rosa. Sources: Louder Than War interview; Electricity Club interview.
  • Nice’s own description of his departure is deliberately self-deprecating — he says “not that anyone will be interested” of the 23 Skidoo contract that triggered his exit, then pivots immediately to describing a meeting with William S. Burroughs in Amsterdam. The nature of the disputed contract has never been put on public record by either party across any known interview (Louder Than War, Electricity Club, Ransom Note, Original Sin fanzine all checked). It could have concerned: royalties on the original TWI 123, the terms under which the L.A.Y.L.A.H. reissue was arranged, a proposed new recording deal, or accounting matters — all remain possible; none is confirmed.
  • The band’s own perspective on the Crépuscule/L.A.Y.L.A.H. period is also absent from interviews: a 2022 Alex Turnbull interview (Ban Ban Ton Ton) covers label history from Fetish through to Illuminated and Ronin but does not mention L.A.Y.L.A.H. or the 1988 reissue. Turnbull’s comment that Illuminated was taken on out of “desperation — they were the only people that would work with us after we released The Culling!” suggests the band’s active label relationship was with Illuminated, not Crépuscule, from 1983–84 onward. source: Ban Ban Ton Ton interview, July 2022

Assessment: The reissue was authorized — issued with full credit to and in association with Operation Twilight/Crépuscule and the band. The “dispute” was between James Nice and Michel Duval inside Crépuscule — Nice walked out over an unspecified “23 Skidoo contract” in 1988. Given the authorized nature of the reissue, the most likely scenario is that the dispute concerned the internal terms of that arrangement (royalty split, accounting, or how the deal was structured with Monin), but this is inference. No interview or contemporaneous account names the specific subject of the disagreement. A thorough search of all available Nice interviews and band interviews has not resolved the question. The specific subject of the contract dispute remains open pending a direct statement from Nice, Monin, or the Turnbull brothers.

Production Work (as Assassins With Soul / Ronin)

From their Highbury studio, produced and remixed for: Ice-T, Seal, Sade, Stevie Wonder, Bomb the Bass, Public Enemy. A remarkable second career running parallel to the band.

The Chemical Brothers Connection

“Block Rockin’ Beats” (Chemical Brothers, 1997 — #1 UK) uses a reimplemented (not directly sampled) version of the “Coup” bassline. Alex Turnbull confirmed: “Everybody was like, ‘Oh shit, they’ve ripped off 23 Skidoo.’ It’s not actually a straight sample, they replayed it.” Producer Simon Boswell’s own EMI biography explicitly cited the connection as a career highlight. The Chemical Brothers appear to have reconstructed the bassline from The Crusaders’ “The Well’s Gone Dry” to sound like “Coup.” No formal writing credit was given to 23 Skidoo; an intra-roster publishing arrangement (both were on EMI-affiliated labels) has been speculated but not confirmed.

The Final Academy (October 1982)

23 Skidoo performed at Final Academy on 29 September 1982 at the Ritzy Cinema, Brixton — describing themselves in the official programme (designed by Neville Brody) as “cultural assassins.” Also performing: Psychic TV (their live debut), Cabaret Voltaire, Brion Gysin, William S. Burroughs, Z’EV, Fritz Catlin’s Last Few Days, Marc Almond, and Derek Jarman. Organised by Genesis P-Orridge — who had already produced Seven Songs earlier that year.

Last Few Days — Fritz Catlin’s Parallel Collective

While still in 23 Skidoo, Fritz Catlin was a regular contributor to Last Few Days — a deliberately obscure experimental collective formed in 1979 by Daniel Landin (alias D. Styme / Stan Bingo), Si Joyce (alias S Gross), and Keir Wahid (alias K. Warhead). Wikipedia describes the group as a “highly conceptual” and “experimental militant classicist” collective. (Wikipedia — Daniel Landin, as of 2026-08-07) Catlin began performing with them from 1981; fellow ex-Skidoo member Sam Mills also contributed occasionally after his dismissal from 23 Skidoo in June 1982. Landin had been working with Super 8 and VHS video from 1978, documenting Throbbing Gristle performances before co-founding LFD. The group consciously avoided releasing material, kept live appearances rare and extreme — all-night concerts (sometimes running to 7am) in unconventional venues (chapels, cinemas, burger bars, silos, tunnels), using megaphones, guitars played with broken bottles, and relentless percussion. (LTM Recordings biography)

Full article: Last Few Days

April 1983 — Zagreb Music Biennial

Laibach invited both Last Few Days and 23 Skidoo to perform at the XII Music Biennale Zagreb on 23 April 1983, at a concert titled We Forge the Future (“Mi Kujemo Bodočnost”). Laibach prepared a special multimedia concert with 12 video monitors on stage and film projections. Police interrupted the performance (reportedly at 5am), and the authorities subsequently banned Laibach’s name and public appearances in Yugoslavia. The episode directly triggered Laibach’s decision to tour Western Europe. (Gorki.de concert listing; Laibach discography)

November–December 1983 — Occupied Europe Tour (with Laibach)

Laibach mounted the Occupied Europe Tour through Western Europe (November–December 1983; 16 cities, 8 countries), with Last Few Days as support. Catlin performed with Laibach on the tour — and played drums on recordings which later formed Laibach’s first single, “Panorama / Decree” (12 EWS 3, 1984). (LTM Recordings biography; Laibach official biography)

Last Few Days recorded extensively throughout the tour — over 100 hours of material in total. In June 1986, K Warhead, S Gross, and Dave Henderson sat with a pile of cassettes, an equalisation unit, and a Revox to compile this into Pure Spirit And Saliva Live (Dead Man’s Curve DMC 002, 1986). Landin was reportedly opposed to releasing the record and left the collective shortly after it appeared, going on to a career as a cinematographer. (Last.fm wiki) Eastern European shows on this tour drew approximately 3,000 per night — against the ~300 typical at UK concerts — a contrast the surviving members (Keir and Si) cited when they reconstituted as a pop duo in 1990. (Selfish Few blog, 2019)

1990 pop-duo phase (as Last Few Days! / LFD!)

After Landin’s departure (1986), the collective effectively dissolved. Si Joyce and Keir Wahid reconstituted in 1990 as a pop/house duo, citing exposure to mainstream radio while touring Eastern Europe as the impetus for the stylistic shift. They released two Fontana singles before the project ended.

Ronin Records / Assassins With Soul

After going on hiatus in the late 1980s, the Turnbull brothers built a recording studio in Highbury, North London and shifted into production under the aliases Ronin and Assassins With Soul. Ronin Records was founded c.1990, with one of its first releases being Jailbreak by Paradox — described as “one of the first breakbeat records.”

Production/remix clients at Highbury:

  • Ice-T, Seal, Sade, Stevie Wonder, Bomb the Bass, Public Enemy

Ronin Records also became a significant UK hip-hop imprint: releasing records by Paradox, Skitz, Deckwrecka, Roots Manuva, Rodney P, Mud Family, DJ Skitz. 23 Skidoo reformed and released Just Like Everybody Part Two on Ronin in 2002.

Turnbull Family Connection

Johnny and Alex Turnbull are the sons of sculptor/painter William Turnbull and sculptor Kim Lim. William Turnbull was a founding member of the Independent Group — the ICA collective (1952–54) credited with inventing British Pop Art, alongside Eduardo Paolozzi, Richard Hamilton, and Lawrence Alloway (who coined the term “pop art”). Kim Lim was a Singaporean-British sculptor — marginalized but significant, and the subject of major retrospectives at Hepworth Wakefield (2023) and National Gallery Singapore (2024).

Alex Turnbull on the family connection: “Johnny and I thought 23 Skidoo’s anti-commercial tendencies came from a punk sensibility. But it turns out we had a genetic predisposition to anti-establishment practices.”

The ICA thread is extraordinary: the same institution that hosted William Turnbull’s Independent Group work in 1952–54 hosted COUM Transmissions’ Prostitution exhibition in 1976 — which directly spawned Throbbing Gristle. TG then produced 23 Skidoo’s debut album. The loop closes over three decades at the same building.

Research Queue

  • What was the specific subject of the “23 Skidoo contract” that caused James Nice to walk out of Crépuscule in 1988? The reissue authorization is confirmed (the LAY 23 sleeve credits “in association with Operation Twilight, Les Disques du Crépuscule, and 23 Skidoo”), so the dispute was about internal Crépuscule terms rather than whether the reissue should happen. Nice has never specified the subject in any published interview (Louder Than War, Electricity Club, Ransom Note, Original Sin fanzine all checked). Resolvable only by a new interview with Nice, Marc Monin, or the Turnbull brothers directly addressing the 1988 period.

Web sources (added 2026-08-05 — band name origin)

  • Ransom Note — “23 Skidoo Talk” interview (August 2021) — primary source; band member states Fritz Catlin chose name from Crowley’s Book of Lies Chapter 23 (“Skidoo”) and Wilson/Shea’s Illuminatus!; exact quote: “Chapter 23 is called Skidoo. What man is at ease in his inn—get out. We took it as a reference to perpetual change.”; band clarifies they are not occultists.
  • Les Disques du Crépuscule — 23 Skidoo biography — attributes name to Illuminatus! trilogy (Wilson/Shea) as primary borrowing; notes phrase also appears in Burroughs and Crowley; describes guiding ethos as “cut, run and confound”; Tom Heslop quote on cut-up technique.
  • Everything2 — The Book of Lies: Chapter 23 — confirms Chapter 23 of Crowley’s Book of Lies is titled “Skidoo”; central text is the imperative “Get out”; commentary (Karl Gerner) describes it as “the Great Work under the figure of a man ridding himself of all his accidents.”
