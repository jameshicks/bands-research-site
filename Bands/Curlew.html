Curlew

Discogs ID: 366109
Origin: New York, USA
Active: 1979 – present (with gaps)
Labels: Red Records (2), Moers Music, Cuneiform Records, Open Minds, Sabiton Records, ARS 2, Roaratorio

American experimental free jazz group founded in New York in 1979 by saxophonist George Cartwright. Over four decades and numerous lineup shifts, Curlew has moved between downtown New York no-wave jazz, post-rock improvisation, and composed song structures, always with Cartwright as the fixed center. The earliest lineup placed them in the orbit of Bill Laswell and Nicky Skopelitis; the arrival of guitarist Davey Williams and cellist Tom Cora locked in the lineup most associated with their Cuneiform-era catalogue.

Formation and Early Lineup (1979–1983)

Founded by George Cartwright (b. 10 December 1950, Midnight, Mississippi) in New York in 1979. The early lineup brought together key figures from the downtown NYC scene: Bill Laswell on bass and Nicky Skopelitis on guitar, alongside Tom Cora on cello, Michael Lytle, and Bill Bacon. This configuration placed Curlew in the orbit of Material, Massacre, and the broader Laswell/Frith network. The self-titled debut LP appeared in 1980 on Red Records (2), with further pressings issued later (including at least one UK edition).

North America Era (1983–1987)

North America (1985, Moers Music) introduced a substantially different lineup. Fred Frith (of Henry Cow / Skeleton Crew) plays bass and serves as co-producer and mixer alongside engineer Martin Bisi. Pippin Barnett handles most drum duties with Rick Brown taking three tracks. Mark Howell plays guitar. Tom Cora continues on cello. Compositions are split between Cartwright, Cora, Frith, and Howell/Brown; the cover includes J.B. Lenoir’s “She Don’t Love Me This Way” (B6). Lacquer cut by Günter Pauler.

Live In Berlin Era (1985–1991)

Live In Berlin (1988, Cuneiform Records) — Curlew’s first release on their long-term home label — documents the band with Davey Williams (b. 1952, Alabama; d. 5 April 2019) on guitar, replacing Frith and Skopelitis, and Wayne Horvitz (b. 1 September 1955, New York) adding keyboards and synth bass. Recorded in Berlin by Amadeus Reineck (most tracks) and Judy Mareiniss (track B1). Mastered by Roger Seibel.

Bee (1991, Open Minds / Sabiton Records) sees Ann Rupel join on bass and the band record without Horvitz. Hahn Rowe produces, records, and mixes (with John Siket assisting). Mastered again by Roger Seibel. The Knitting Factory (1992, ARS 2) is a Polish live release from the same core lineup.

A Beautiful Western Saddle (1993)

Co-credited: Curlew with Amy Denio (Lyrics By) and Paul Haines. Amy Denio provides vocals and lyrics (and music on track 7). Paul Haines supplies additional lyrics. Bob Appel produces; Hugo Dwyer mixes (with Scott Gilfix assisting); recorded by Jon Rosenberg; mastered by Roger Seibel. Cover designed by Ruth Peyser.

Paradise (1996)

Notable lineup change: Samm Bennett replaces Pippin Barnett on drums and percussion. Chris Cochrane joins on guitar and vocals alongside Williams. Tom Cora is absent (he died in April 1998 — this may have been his last recording with the group, or he was already stepping back). Jim Spake guests on baritone and soprano saxophone. Produced by Cartwright; recorded and mixed by Rusty McFarland. Cover art by Anne Elias; design by Ruth Peyser. Executive-produced by Steven Feigenbaum (Cuneiform Records founder).

Tom Cora

Tom Cora (Thomas Henry Corra; b. 14 September 1953, Yancey Mills, Virginia; d. 9 April 1998, France) was the cellist present on every Curlew recording from the debut through the A Beautiful Western Saddle era. Outside Curlew he was a prolific figure in downtown NYC: co-founded Skeleton Crew (2) with Fred Frith; played in Pidgin Combo, Roof, Nimal, The Hat Shoes, Hansundtom, and many collaborative ensembles. Tzadik released the tribute compilation Hallelujah, Anyway – Remembering Tom Cora in 1999.
