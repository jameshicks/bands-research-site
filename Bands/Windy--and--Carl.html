Windy & Carl

Oct 27, 20269 min read

Windy & Carl

Discogs ID: 51797 Members: Carl Hultgren (guitar, keyboards, percussion) · Windy Weber (bass, vocals, artwork) From: Dearborn, Michigan, USA Active: 1993–present (productive hiatus c. 2001–2005) Primary labels: Blue Flea · Kranky · Darla Records · Enraptured Name variations: W&C · Windy + Carl · Windy And Carl

Husband-and-wife drone/ambient duo from Dearborn, Michigan. Carl Hultgren plays guitar (heavy use of delay, reverb, E-Bow); Windy Weber plays bass and contributes vocals, though most pieces are instrumental. The sound draws heavily on 1980s 4AD — specifically Robin Guthrie and the Cocteau Twins — and on earlier drone lineage: Cluster, Brian Eno, Popol Vuh. Distinctive for building drone from guitar rather than synthesizers, and for the consistent absence of drums. AllMusic tagged them “a leading light of the Michigan space-rock scene.”

Biography

Origins and early recordings (1992–1994)

Carl Hultgren and Windy Weber were already performing together in Detroit-area bands before Windy & Carl coalesced. Both were members of Five Way Mirror (with Greg Gasiorowski of Blue Flea’s parent operation Stormy Records) and of Once Dreamt (with Brenda Markovich and Randall Nieman). Carl had earlier played bass in a 1989 release on Plan 8 Records (Emotionally Mangled) and was also involved with Providence (2) and Moog Monday.

Windy & Carl’s first releases emerged in 1992–1993 on Blue Flea, the self-run label that would remain their most personal imprint throughout their career. The debut, Water Song (Blue Flea, 1993), established the template: slow-building guitar drones, bass undertow, and very sparse or absent percussion. Critic Dave Segal contributed liner notes. A collection of Unreleased Home Recordings 1992–1995 (Blue Flea, 2020) later surfaced, documenting this era in detail — Carl on guitars, keyboards, occasional bass, and samplers; Windy on bass guitar, percussion, and vocals.

The 1994 Instrumentals EP (Burnt Hair) and Portal (Blue Flea, 1994) solidified the sound. Portal featured a wider ensemble, including Jay Kuehn, Jacques Cohen, Ligia Bouton, Pedro Martinez, and Bernard Tubiana — an early sign of the Michigan scene network around the duo.

Mid-90s peak: UK connections and label diversification (1995–1998)

The mid-1990s saw Windy & Carl reach beyond Michigan. UK label Enraptured released Emerald (1995) — a lean two-credit recording (Carl: guitar; Windy: bass, percussion) that became one of their most cited early records. British label Enraptured was a key conduit for the US shoegaze/dream-pop underground reaching UK ears; its sublabels include Earworm and Endorphin Records.

Dreamlanding / Left Without Air (Blue Flea / Mind Expansion, 1995) was followed by Drawing Of Sound (Icon, 1996), recorded with Jay Kuehn and with Matt Gasper contributing artwork — a connection that would recur.

The year 1997 was especially busy. A Dream Of Blue appeared on UK label Ochre Records; Rocket Girl issued the double-sided 7” I Have Known Love / Crazy In The Sun (shared with Silver Apples and Daiske); Darla Records included Antarctica (The Bliss Out, Vol. 2) in their influential Bliss Out series (DRL 027) — one of Darla’s ambient/shoegaze compilation sub-series, with Seth Robson handling design. The same year brought Noises From The Darkroom (Earworm, UK) alongside Saddar Bazaar.

Depths (Kranky, 1998) marked the duo’s first proper Kranky full-length — a milestone given that Chicago label’s identity as the home of Labradford, Stars of the Lid, and other post-rock and drone artists. Photography by Jody Helme and Brenda Markovich. The same year the duo appeared on a split release with UK ambient act Amp (Blue Flea, 1998), with design by Amp member Richard Walker and layout by Peggy Finger.

Return and mature era on Kranky (1999–2001, 2008–)

Consciousness (Kranky, 2001) marked the duo at their most refined before the hiatus. Carl and Windy are credited as “Written-By, Recorded By” under their surnames; artwork by Mike Dereniewski; design by Robert & Barbara Flynn. The album is often considered their definitive statement.

After a several-year break, the pair returned with The Dream House / Dedications To Flea (Kranky, 2005) — an EP/compilation that also functioned as a tribute release (Flea: Discogs artist 260411; Joshua Pfeffer and Michael Babcock also credited on associated releases). Christy Romanick contributed photographs, beginning a long collaboration; Craig McCaffrey handled package assembly.

Songs For The Broken Hearted (Kranky, 2008) pulled in Warren Defever for mastering — a Detroit-scene connection, Defever being the driving force of His Name Is Alive and running his own mastering operation. Windy contributed vocals and photography; the record is credited to the duo for “Guitar, Bells, Keyboards.”

Solo work

Windy Weber issued the solo record I Hate People (Blue Flea, 2008) — all sounds, written-by, and mixed-by Windy; Carl engineered; Warren Defever mastered two tracks; Chris Koltay edited and mixed one track. Christy Romanick contributed photography.

Carl Hultgren issued the solo LP Tomorrow (Blue Flea, 2014) — guitars, keyboards, percussion, and home sounds; mastered by Rafael Anton Irisarri; photographed by Carl and Windy; graphic design by Joshua Pfeffer.

Later period and ongoing activity (2012–present)

We Will Always Be (Kranky, 2012) — Windy wrote lyrics, sang, and took photographs; Carl performed, written-by, and recorded. Mastered by Steve Swartz. The same year saw the self-titled Blue Flea archival retrospective and a 7” on Geographic North (You Can’t Hide Your Love Forever).

Calliope / Carnivale (The Great Pop Supplement, UK, 2013) and I Walked Alone / At Night (Blue Flea, 2014 — with all covers hand-painted by Windy) continued sustained activity through the mid-2010s.

Allegiance And Conviction (Kranky, 2020) — their most recent major label release — was produced and mixed by both Carl and Windy; mastered by Rafael Anton Irisarri; cover photograph by Dale Kelsey. Windy took sole “Lyrics By” credit and handled back-cover artwork.

In 2024 the duo released Heavy Early & The Creation Of Venus (Blue Flea) and two archival live sets on their own imprint.

Carl appeared as a guest performer on Rafael Anton Irisarri’s Sirimiri (2023).

Key Scene Connections

  • His Name Is Alive — Warren Defever (also of Detroit/Michigan) mastered several W&C and solo records. Both operate within the same Ann Arbor / Dearborn/Detroit underground.
  • Amp — UK drone/shoegaze act; split 7” on Blue Flea (1998). Amp member Richard Walker handled cover design.
  • Jessica Bailiff — appeared on a shared compilation; another Kranky-connected drone/ambient artist.
  • Rafael Anton Irisarri — mastered Allegiance And Conviction and Carl’s solo Tomorrow; Carl appeared on Irisarri’s Sirimiri (2023). Irisarri also records as The Sight Below.
  • Silver Apples — shared Rocket Girl 7” (1997).
  • Saddar Bazaar — shared Earworm release (2000).
  • The Lothars — Windy & Carl & The Lothars collaboration release, featuring Jon Bernhardt, Scott Sterbenz, Paul Hilcoff, Mike DiMilla, Ron Russell, Jeff Wilson, Kris Thompson, Jon Hindmarsh, Ramona Herboldsheimer, Alastair Galbraith, Jeff Lipton, and Grawer.
  • Five Way Mirror — pre-W&C group with both members and Greg Gasiorowski of Blue Flea’s parent.
  • Once Dreamt — pre-W&C group with both members, Brenda Markovich, and Randall Nieman.
  • Landing — shared compilation appearance.
  • Füxa — shared early Blue Flea / Mind Expansion release.
