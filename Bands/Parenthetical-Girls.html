From Discogs

Parenthetical Girls was an experimental pop band formed in Everett, Washington, and then based in Portland, Oregon.

Begun primarily as a recording project, the touring ensemble consisted of vocalist Zac Pennington, Rachael Jensen, Eddy Crichton and Matt Carlson, all sharing a variety of instrumental duties. Former touring members Jherek Bischoff and Sam Mickens (of the band Dead Science) joined them for recording sessions.

Their music was notable for its combination of saccharine pop and experimental elements and has been compared with such disparate acts as Brian Eno, Philip Glass, Xiu Xiu and Phil Spector.

Originally taking their name from the Brian Eno composition “Swastika Girls”, original members Zac Pennington and Jeremy Cooper self-released their limited run debut EP Christmas With Swastika Girls in 2002. After Cooper left the band, they changed their name and Pennington released (((GRRRLS))), the band’s vinyl-only debut album on his own Slender Means Society label in 2004. This got recorded with the help of Jherek Bischoff and Jamie Stewart (2) (of Xiu Xiu fame). A split 7-inch with the band Dead Science was released by Obsolete Vernacular in 2007. In 2008 they switched labels to Germany’s Indietronic source Tomlab.

In 2017, bandleader Zac Pennington declared that the project had ended. Announcing, “Parenthetical Girls is over.”

Overview

Parenthetical Girls was an experimental pop band formed in Everett, Washington and later based in Portland, Oregon, built around vocalist Zac Pennington. Their music fused saccharine pop melodicism with experimental and avant-garde textures, drawing comparisons to Brian Eno, Philip Glass, Xiu Xiu, and Phil Spector. Originally named Swastika Girls after a Brian Eno composition, the project renamed itself after founding member Jeremy Cooper departed; Pennington released the vinyl-only debut (((GRRRLS))) in 2004 on his own Slender Means Society imprint, recorded with Jherek Bischoff and Jamie Stewart of Xiu Xiu. The catalogue spans eight releases across two decades — five in the 2000s and three in the 2010s — with the band moving to the German label Tomlab in 2008 before Pennington formally announced the project’s end in 2017.
