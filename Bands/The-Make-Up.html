The Make-Up

Discogs ID: 281792 Location: Washington, D.C., USA Genres: Post-punk, Gospel Punk, “Gospel Yeh-Yeh” Active: 1995–2000 (reunions 2006, 2011–2012)

Overview

The Make-Up formed in 1995 from the ashes of The Nation Of Ulysses, retaining three-quarters of that band’s lineup — Ian Svenonius (vocals), James Canty (guitar, organ), and Steve Gamboa (drums) — and adding bassist Michelle Mae. Where The Nation Of Ulysses had trafficked in a confrontational art-punk, The Make-Up developed something more exuberant and harder to categorize: a fusion they called “gospel yeh-yeh,” yoking the ecstatic performance tradition of Black American church music to the raw energy of 60s garage and French pop (“yeh-yeh”). Svenonius became an overtly theatrical frontman — preaching, testifying, working the crowd — while the band generated a tight, organ-driven groove beneath him.

The band released four studio albums between 1996 and 1999 on Dischord Records and K Records, as well as a string of singles and split releases on smaller imprints. They disbanded around 2000, then reconvened for a live document in 2006 (In Film / On Video, Dischord) and again for reissue campaigns in the 2010s. The core of Svenonius, Mae, and Canty continued into Weird War (2002–2005) on Drag City.

Despite their relatively brief initial run, The Make-Up are regarded as a key transitional band in the DC underground — bridging the post-hardcore severity of the early Dischord era with a more expansive, pleasure-oriented aesthetic that incorporated soul, funk, and gospel references.

Post-Breakup Projects

Weird War (2002–2005)

Weird War — Discogs ID 327643 — was the immediate successor project, formed by Svenonius, Mae, and Canty along with Neil Hagerty (of Royal Trux) and Alex Minoff. Released three albums on Drag City: Weird War (2002), I Suck On That Emotion (2003), If You Can’t Beat ‘Em, Bite ‘Em (2004), and Illuminated By The Light (2005). Also recorded as Scene Creamers.

Chain And The Gang (2009–2021)

Chain And The Gang — Discogs ID 1447437 — Ian Svenonius’s long-running subsequent project, with James Canty as a core member. Rotating cast of collaborators. Released on K Records and In The Red. Albums include Down With Liberty… Up With Chains! (2009), Music’s Not For Everyone (2011), In Cool Blood (2012), Minimum Rock N Roll (2014).

Escape-Ism

Ian Svenonius solo project under the alias Escape-Ism. Introduction To Escape-Ism (Merge Records, 2017) and The Lost Record (Merge, 2018).

Ian Svenonius Solo

Hurtin’ Class (Ernest Jenning Record Co. / Khannibalism, 2015) — sole release under his own name as primary artist.

Michelle Mae — Frumpies

Michelle Mae was a member of Frumpies (Discogs ID 276323, on Kill Rock Stars) prior to The Make-Up, active from 1993.

Scene Connections

  • The Nation Of Ulysses (Discogs ID 267485) — direct predecessor, 1990–1993; Svenonius, Canty, and Gamboa all members. Two LPs on Dischord: 13-Point Program To Destroy America (1991) and Plays Pretty For Baby (1992).
  • Dischord Records — DC punk/post-punk label co-run by Ian MacKaye and Jeff Nelson; The Make-Up’s primary home for studio albums.
  • K Records — Olympia, WA indie founded by Calvin Johnson; released Sound Verite, I Want Some, and Save Yourself, plus several singles.
  • Guy Picciotto — Fugazi co-frontman; produced the debut LP Destination: Love.
  • Brendan Canty — Fugazi drummer; produced Save Yourself. James Canty (guitar) is Brendan Canty’s brother.
  • Slant 6 — DC punk band; appeared on a split with The Make-Up in Japan (1995); Christina Billotte connection to the DC scene.
  • Fugazi — Dischord cornerstone; Picciotto and Brendan Canty production links.
