Too Much Texas

Manchester indie rock band formed 1984, active until 1992. Discogs artist ID: 1106206. Best known as the band Tom Hingley fronted before joining Inspiral Carpets in 1989.

Overview

Too Much Texas formed in Manchester in 1984, founded by singer Tom Hingley (Thomas William Hingley) alongside fellow Abingdon natives Gordon MacKay and Raymond Breckon. All three worked part-time as glass collectors at The Haçienda nightclub. The band’s name gestured at excess — big sound, Manchester scene ambition, American-referencing indie posturing.

Their first release was a split flexi-disc with Debris magazine (1986), sharing a side with Soil (4). Their proper debut single, “Hurry On Down (When Will I See You Again)”, appeared on Ugly Man Records in 1988 and was produced by Tim Oliver, a Factory-adjacent engineer and producer who worked extensively at Manchester’s Strawberry Studios and built a significant career spanning Factory, Deconstruction, and Real World Records. The band toured supporting New Order, The House of Love, and The Beloved.

When Hingley left to join Inspiral Carpets in 1989, Too Much Texas continued with a new vocalist, releasing the Smart EP on Playtime Records (6) the same year. They split in 1992.

A posthumous compilation, Juvenilia (2006), was released on Tom Hingley’s own New Memorabilia label and accompanied a reformed UK tour. It gathers 21 tracks including the single, B-sides, and a larger body of previously unreleased or archival material spanning both Hingley-era and post-Hingley material.

Connections

  • Inspiral Carpets — Tom Hingley joined as lead vocalist in 1989, replacing Stephen Holt; most of Inspiral Carpets’ commercial run (1989–1995) features Hingley
  • Dub Sex — Guy Ainsworth was a member; Dub Sex also appeared on Ugly Man Records and Debris, same Manchester indie circuit
  • Soil (4) — shared the 1986 Debris flexi
  • Tim Oliver — producer of Hurry On Down; a central figure in Manchester/Factory-adjacent production
  • Lavolta Lakota — Guy Ainsworth was also a member
  • Manchester North Of England (A Story Of Independent Music Greater Manchester 1977 - 1993) — placed on Disc 5 (“24 Hour Party People”, 1987–1989) alongside Happy Mondays, Morrissey, Barry Adamson, The Jazz Defektors, A Guy Called Gerald
