Officer!

Discogs ID: 722910
London / Paris avant-rock ensemble centered on Mick Hobbs, with Felix Fiedorowicz and Bill Gilonis as the most consistent co-members. Active from 1982–83 through Hobbs’s death in January 2025, the group operated for over four decades — releasing records on small French, British, and Austrian labels and encompassing a wide international cast of contributors. The Discogs profile describes it as “the name most used for Mick Hobbs’s songs and instrumentals since 1984 — often a collaborative process, pooling a wide range of international talent.”

Officer! is not a fixed lineup but a project: sometimes Hobbs alone, sometimes a quartet, sometimes a twenty-person ensemble. What remains constant is Hobbs as primary composer and the recurring presence of Fiedorowicz (keyboards, bassoon, arrangements) across almost all phases of the catalog.

Formation

Sources conflict slightly on the founding date:

  • Café OTO states Officer! “formally surfaced in ‘82 with Eight New Songs By Mick Hobbs.”
  • The cassette’s Discogs release date is 1983; the Psychedelic Baby Magazine interview (2022) also treats the 1983 tape as the first release.
  • The Officer! Bandcamp page gives “inception in 1984.”

Best current reading: the project took shape in 1982–83, with 8 New Songs By Mick Hobbs as the first documented release. 1982 likely refers to when it began; 1983 to the tape appearing. Fiedorowicz described that first tape in the 2022 interview as “fully developed” and “the most pure release” of the catalog. Hobbs’s connections to This Heat’s Cold Storage studio in Brixton are noted in relation to the early period, and the production credits on 8 New Songs place Fiedorowicz as lead engineer and Dominique Diebold — the operator of the Reims-based AAA label — as producer.

Discography

8 New Songs By Mick Hobbs (1983, AAA, France)

Discogs ID: 2323642 · Cat no.: A.A.A C01 · Cassette, limited edition, numbered.

The debut. Eight songs credited to Officer! but explicitly foregrounding Hobbs as composer and sole writer. The lineup shifts track by track: core participants are Hobbs, Fiedorowicz, and Rick Wilson, with guests including Tim Hodgkinson (Hawaiian guitar and reverb voice on “Join The AA”), Alig (guitar on “Life At The Water’s Edge”), Richard Wolfson (keyboards), Terri Robson (violin), and Anna Piva (voice). Produced by Dominique Diebold; Fiedorowicz as lead engineer, with Bill Gilonis and Andy Bole as assistants.

Track-level credits reveal the eclectic range: “Life At The Water’s Edge” is the most developed ensemble piece (bass/voice Hobbs, drums Wilson, guitar Alig, keyboards Wolfson, violin Robson, voice Piva) and will be revisited repeatedly across the catalog. “Tunnels” is Hobbs solo with tape. “Way” brings in Hodgkinson, Fiedorowicz, and tap dancer Leslie. “All Reality Is Symbolic” is guitar/bass/vocals/keyboards Hobbs with Rick Wilson on percussion.

Ossification (1984, Ayaa, France)

Discogs ID: 1060752 · Cat no.: DT 1284 · LP.

The first vinyl release and the most densely populated Officer! record. Fourteen tracks using a rotating cast of twelve credited performers across two sides.

Side A (tracks A1–A7):

  • “Dynamite” — Rick Wilson (percussion, voice), Fiedorowicz (voice)
  • “Anagrams” — Gilonis (bass, clarinet), Fiedorowicz (bassoon), Hobbs (drums, piano, guitar, wind arrangement), Andy Bole (guitar), Zeena Parkins (harp), Judy Carter (vocals)
  • “Air” — Graham Painting (guitar, double bass, drums), Hobbs (keyboards, recorder), Fiedorowicz (vocals)
  • “Telepathy” — Gilonis (keyboards, bass, objects), Hobbs (keyboards, objects, string arrangement), Terri Robson (violin), Catherine Jauniaux (voice), Fiedorowicz (voice)
  • “Spitter’s Hooked (On Love)” — Gilonis (bass), Fiedorowicz (bassoon, recorder, strings composed/arranged), Georgie Born (cello), Hobbs (drums, guitar, percussion, recorder, vocals), Zeena Parkins (harp), string section: Anne Stokes, Christina Wild, Jussi Karel, Terri Robson; voices: Catherine Jauniaux, Judy Carter
  • “Dogface” — Fiedorowicz (bassoon, recorder, percussion), Hobbs (keyboards, percussion), Judy Carter (vocals)
  • “Some” — Tim Hodgkinson (alto sax, lap steel), Hobbs (bass, keyboards, percussion, recorder, voice), C.D. Greyt (featured), The Work (featured), Gilonis (piano, percussion, melodica), Catherine Jauniaux (voice)

Side B (tracks B1–B7):

  • “Boxers V Wrestlers” — Hobbs (guitar, organ, percussion, voice, recorder), Fiedorowicz (piano, recorder, voice, harmonica) — a rare Hobbs/Fiedorowicz duo
  • “Tunnels” — Hobbs (bass, tape), Fiedorowicz (bassoon, piano, voice), Hodgkinson (clarinet, alto sax, baritone sax), Rick Wilson (drums), Patrick Q. Paganini (violin)
  • “Apply” — Hobbs (guitar, percussion, voice), Fiedorowicz (keyboards, percussion, recorder, voice, strings composed/arranged), Terri Robson (violin)
  • “Office Occasion” — Fiedorowicz (bassoon, recorder, voice, strings composed/arranged), Patrick Grundy-White (French horn), Hobbs (guitar, bass, drums, percussion, voice), string section, Judy Carter (vocals), Catherine Jauniaux (voice)
  • “Origin” — Fiedorowicz (bassoon, voice), Tom Cora (cello), Zeena Parkins (harp), Hobbs (piano, recorder, voice), Jussi Karel (viola), Catherine Jauniaux (voice)
  • “Hello” — Gilonis (bass), Fiedorowicz (bassoon, recorder, percussion), Georgie Born (cello), Hobbs (keyboards, guitar, percussion, voice), Judy Carter (voice, whistle)
  • “Top. Top, Top” — Fiedorowicz (bassoon, recorder, handclaps), Georgie Born (cello), Hobbs (percussion, voice, handclaps)

Release-level credits: Engineering and production assistance by Gilonis, Charles Bullen, Peter Bullen, Stephen Rickard, and Tim Hodgkinson — placing much of the recording at or near This Heat’s Cold Storage complex in Brixton. Mastered by Tim Young (“tim tom”). Sleeve design: Nathalie Lucas. Written-by credits split among Hobbs (most tracks), Fiedorowicz (A4, B1–B3), and Gilonis (A4), plus The Lowest Note credited as songwriter on A2.

Genres/styles on Discogs: Jazz, Rock / Art Rock, Avantgarde, Post-Punk.

Cough (1985, Ayaa, France)

Discogs ID: 2281069 · Cat no.: none · Cassette.

Twelve tracks. Unlike Ossification, Discogs credits are assigned at release level only, with no track-by-track breakdown. Four credited performers: Fiedorowicz, Marie-Jeanne, Hobbs, Patrick Q-Wright. The tracklist includes revisited material (“Anagrams,” “Telepathy,” “Boxers Vs. Wrestlers,” “Dogface”) alongside new pieces (“Kalenda Maya,” “Instructions Not Included,” “Du Boxe,” “Nummus,” “Maria’s Song,” “We All Sat Watching The Same Film”). The reissue compilation Earlier Music (2018) later provided track-by-track personnel for the Cough material: the core quartet is Hobbs, Fiedorowicz, Marie-Jeanne (clarinet, vocals, percussion), and Patrick Q-Wright (bass, violin), with Antoine Gindt appearing on “Bone Connections” alongside Jérôme Bourdellon (flute) and Raoul (sax).

Yes Yes No No Yes No Yes (1988, self-released, UK & Europe)

Discogs ID: 1047974 · Cat no.: NO 001 · LP.

Self-released under the catalog number NO 001. Eleven tracks. Credits list seven performers collectively (“The Following Participated”): Hobbs, Fiedorowicz, Gilonis, Antoine Gindt, Claudia Schmid, Daniel Koskowitz, Keisuke Matsui — the most international Officer! lineup to appear on a release, drawing on both the London/Paris axis and the Reims scene (Gindt and Koskowitz both appear on Hellebore and NEO Museum releases for Ayaa). No track-by-track breakdown. Runout etchings: “BODMAS R” (side A), “HAPPY XMAS R” (side B).

A lengthy hiatus followed between this release and the 2014–2015 revival. Dead Unique (2014, Blackest Ever Black) partially explains this: it is a compiled retrospective of 1995 recordings that were shelved at the time.

Dead Unique (2014, Blackest Ever Black, UK)

Discogs ID: 5718255 · Cat no.: BEB LP 006 · 2xLP.

Eighteen tracks on four sides; recorded circa 1995 but released only in 2014 — almost two decades after the sessions. No performer credits listed in Discogs beyond the primary Officer! credit. The tracklist suggests a more abstract, less ensemble-oriented direction than the 1980s records: “Nest,” “Elephant Flowers,” “Go Back,” “Cows Hum In The Fields,” “Nardis,” “Bugs In Amber,” “The Pony Was Contented,” “Rachot,” “Lilac & Orange,” “Clint.” A parallel reissue campaign brought Ossification back into print in 2014 on the US labels Knock’em Dead Records and Megaphone Records (3).

Recording context (web-sourced): The sessions took place in early 1995 at The Warehouse, Baltimore, while Half Japanese were recording their Hot LP (Hobbs was a guest musician on that record). Engineered by Jason Willett and Mick Hobbs. Additional recording at Studio 99 (London E2) and Worldwide International (London W10). Mixed in summer 1995 by Julia Brightly at her 16-track home studio in Bethnal Green. The album remained shelved until Blackest Ever Black released it in 2014. [source: https://www.chaindlk.com/news/blackest-ever-black-officer-dead-unique-lost-album/, accessed 2026-06-20; https://officer-official.bandcamp.com/album/dead-unique, accessed 2026-06-20; https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/officer-dead-unique-cd/BLACKEST.006CD.html, accessed 2026-06-20]

Personnel (web-sourced, from press materials and Bandcamp liner notes): Tim Hodgkinson (bass clarinet), John Dierker (clarinet, saxophone), Jad Fair (vocals), Patrick Q (violin; also in Legendary Pink Dots), Joey Stack (vocals on selected tracks), Fred Collins (vocals; Pleasant Livers), Martha Colburn (vocals; filmmaker/animator), Jason Willett (bass, keyboards, trumpet), Gilles Rieder (drums), Amy Denio (uncredited instrumentalist), and all members of Strobe Talbot. One track (“Good”) co-written with Felix Fiedorowicz, who does not appear to have participated in the Baltimore sessions. Described by press materials as “ragged-raw rock ‘n roll, sumptuous chamber music, pastoral folk, blowsy prog-jazz and paranoid dub-space.” [source: https://www.chaindlk.com/news/blackest-ever-black-officer-dead-unique-lost-album/, accessed 2026-06-20; http://dieordiy2.blogspot.com/2018/03/officer-dead-unique-blackest-ever-black.html, accessed 2026-06-20]

Life At The Water’s Edge (2015, Blackest Ever Black, UK)

Discogs ID: 6505563 · Cat no.: BLACKEST030 · 7”, limited edition.

A two-track single. Side A: “Life At The Water’s Edge” — the song first recorded for 8 New Songs in 1983, here revisited with a band lineup: Hobbs (bass, voice), Rick Wilson (drums), Alig Pearce (guitar), Richard Wolfson (keyboards), Terri Robson (violin), Anna Piva (voice). Produced by Dominique Diebold. Mastered by Matt Colton. Side B: “Dogface” — Hobbs alone (keyboards, percussion, recorder) with Judy Carter (voice). The two tracks effectively reprise the 1983 recordings with the same or near-identical personnel, presenting a direct line between the debut cassette and the Blackest Ever Black period.

Never Mind The Bucket (2016, Blackest Ever Black / Life And Living Records, Germany)

Discogs ID: 8914909 · Cat no.: BEB-ICA002 · LP, limited edition.

Eleven tracks. Mixed by John Dieterich; recorded by Andrew Jacques. New lineup centered on Hobbs (voice, guitar) and Mary Currie (voice, harmonium/shruti, theremin, glockenspiel), with Joey Stack (voice, keyboards), Tilly Bean (bass, voice), Ed Bernez (drums), Lydia Fischer (flute, trumpet, baritone horn), Bill Gilonis (guitar on “Shrug”), and Jeroen Visser (baritone sax on “Coma”). Songs by Hobbs and, on “The Best Weapon” and “Beguiling The Hours,” by Gareth Williams and Mary Currie. Mastered by Noel Summerville.

Gareth Williams identification (web-sourced): The Gareth Williams credited here (Discogs ID 196210) is confirmed as Gareth Williams of This Heat (born Cardiff, 23 April 1953; died 24 December 2001, of cancer). Williams co-founded This Heat in 1976 and left before Deceit (1981). In 1985 he formed Flaming Tunes with Mary Currie, releasing a hand-coloured cassette of folk-inflected songs; this project is the direct source of “The Best Weapon” and “Beguiling The Hours.” Mary Currie subsequently became a recurring Officer! performer, bringing her Flaming Tunes connection into the Hobbs circle. Mick Hobbs was involved in the 2009 CD reissue of Flaming Tunes, overseeing the tape transfer project with Andrew Jacques and producing music for a Gareth Williams memorial concert. Similarly, Nothing On (2020) is a cover of a Williams song that had previously circulated via a part-finished tribute video by filmmaker Colin Harrison. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gareth_Williams_(British_musician), accessed 2026-06-20; https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/interviews/flaming-tunes-interviews, accessed 2026-06-20; web search results citing Blackest Ever Black press, accessed 2026-06-20]

Earlier Music (2018, Klanggalerie, Austria)

Discogs ID: 12426397 · Cat no.: gg265 · CD, Compilation / Reissue / Remastered.

A compilation drawing on both 8 New Songs By Mick Hobbs (1983) and Cough (1985) — the two cassette-only releases unavailable since the 1980s. Tracks 1–8 are the 8 New Songs material; tracks 9–20 are the Cough material. Crucially, Earlier Music provides the first track-by-track personnel breakdown for Cough, previously uncredited at that level. Words and music credited across Hobbs, Fiedorowicz, Patrick Q-Wright, and Alig Pearce. Digital transfers by Andrew Jacques. Mastered by Noel Summerville. Illustration by Benoît Proust; layout by Lisa Robotka.

A new recording (“All Reality Is Symbolic”) adds a Park Hill Choir credit: Andy Bole, Bill Gilonis, Charles Bullen, Helen East, Mary Currie, Rick Wilson, Trefor Goronwy.

Brexit Means Toxic (2019, Life And Living Records, UK)

Discogs ID: 14214753 · Cat no.: LALRCD 019 · CD single / enhanced.

Two tracks plus video. Track 1: Hobbs (guitar, producer), Pete Ringrose (contrabass), Mestre Mags (Afro-Brazilian percussion), Flora Tunesberg (voice, piano); produced and mixed/mastered by Ben Hobbs (5) — Mick Hobbs’s son, who becomes a recurring production figure in the late period. Track 2: Gilonis (alto clarinet), Fiedorowicz (keyboards, programmed), Rick Wilson (Tibetan bowl), Hobbs (voice, guitar, bass, percussion), Flora Tunesberg (voice, piano); mixed/mastered by Ben Hobbs (5). Design: Peter Hobbs (4).

Folke (2020, Life And Living Records, UK)

Discogs ID: 15711808 · Cat no.: LALR 022 · Digital mini-album.

Six tracks of British and Irish folk material (“O Mistress Mine,” “Boys of Bedlam,” “Oak and Ash and Thorn,” “Crazy Man Michael,” “Because I Love,” “While Gamekeepers Lie Sleeping”). Lead vocals throughout by Mary Currie. Hobbs plays acoustic guitar, ukulele, piano, and bells. Ensemble: Tilly Bean (bass), Ed Bernez (drums), Aimée Leonard (bodhrán, backing vocals), Lydia Fischer (flute/oboe), Joey Stack (keyboards/backing vocals), Will Morland (violin). Produced and engineered by Alison Craig; mastered and laid out by Andrew Jacques.

Nothing On (2020, Life And Living Records, UK)

Discogs ID: 15732160 · Cat no.: LALR 023 · Digital single.

A cover of a song by Gareth Williams (Music By, Words By). Arrangement credited to Officer!. Performers: Hobbs (acoustic guitar, percussion, producer), Mary Currie (lead vocals), Tilly Bean (bass, backing vocals), Joey Stack (piano, keyboards, backing vocals), Alison Craig (backing vocals), Lisa Lavery (synth), Celia Morgan (tuba). Mastered by Andrew Jacques. Recorded by Manu Laffeach.

Paragraphs and Principles (2021, Klanggalerie / Jelodanti Records, France)

Discogs ID: 20896849 (Klanggalerie) · Cat no.: gg366 · CD.

A Cornelius Cardew tribute album encompassing 14 tracks and approximately 30 performers across multiple countries, recorded over an extended period. Fiedorowicz serves as primary arranger and producer (tracks 1–10, 12–14); Hobbs handles track 11 independently. Bill Gilonis co-produces and co-engineers. Mixed by Ben Hobbs (5) (tracks 1, 2, 5, 8, 9, 11–14), Jeroen Visser (tracks 3, 4, 7, 10), and Martin Harrison (track 6). Mastered by Noel Summerville. Liner notes by Harry Gilonis (Bill Gilonis’s brother); front cover installation by Mick Hobbs; design and layout by Peter Hobbs (4).

The Cardew connection is thematic and explicit: several pieces adapt Cardew’s arrangements of political songs (“The Croppy Boy,” “The East Is Red,” “Moscow Nights,” “Red Flag Prelude”) alongside Fiedorowicz and Hobbs originals. Performers include: Rick Wilson, Catherine Jauniaux, Joey Stack, Andy Bole, Jeroen Visser, David Kerman, Daniel Studer, Flavio Rohner, various Swiss and French instrumentalists, and Ben Hobbs (5) on piano (tracks 9, 11).

Some Songs / Bandagen (2023, Klanggalerie, Austria)

Discogs ID: 26636348 · Cat no.: gg362 · CD.

A two-part release. “Some Songs” (tracks 1–6) are new recordings by a Swiss ensemble featuring Hobbs, Joey Stack (vocals), Grazia Pergoletti (vocals), Andre Schmid (clarinet), Rut Schwegler (performer), Chrigou (violin). Tim Hodgkinson and Andy Wake (2) guest on tracks 1, 4, and 5. “Bandagen” (tracks 7–16) is a reissued/remastered version of the 1989 Mick Hobbs solo cassette of the same name, previously released on No Records (9) in Switzerland. One track (“Plan For Peace”) credits Kalahari Surfers as written-by. Mastered by Martin Bowes; layout by Lisa Robotka.

Dead Right (2025, Klanggalerie / Jelodanti Records, France)

Discogs ID: 35097026 · Cat no.: gg521 · CD.

Posthumous. Mick Hobbs died on 3 January 2025; this is his final record. Thirty tracks. Hobbs is credited for composition, voice, instruments, mixing, concept, and field recording; Fiedorowicz for composition, arrangement, keyboards, mixing, programming, and mastering — the two effectively co-authored the complete work. Claudia Schmid: artwork. Mastered also by Noel Summerville, Patrick Muller, and Martin Bowes.

The tracklist is explicitly political: “Kick Them Out Now,” “The Jeremy Hunt Song,” “Unsuited For The Job,” “Let Them Eat Cake,” “Infinite Greed,” “Toffs,” “Those Evil Tories,” “Strong Butler,” “Toryism,” “The Internationale,” “The Red Flag,” “Les Chants Des Partisans,” “Strike!,” “Dans La Cave,” “Putin’s Table.” The record represents a direct continuation of the Cardew-inflected political songwriting on Paragraphs and Principles, but with the full production handled by just Hobbs and Fiedorowicz. One track features Alistair Strachan on cornet. Laid out by Clara Djian and Nicolas Leto of Jelodanti and Lisa Robotka.

Relationship to The Work

Bill Gilonis and Mick Hobbs were founding members of both Officer! and The Work, and both projects ran concurrently through the 1980s. Tim Hodgkinson (The Work’s other anchor) appears on Ossification (1984) in a production and performance role, and on Some Songs / Bandagen (2023) as a guest. Rick Wilson (The Work drummer) appears on 8 New Songs, Life At The Water’s Edge, Brexit Means Toxic, and Paragraphs and Principles. The two projects share the same This Heat / Cold Storage orbit: production assistance on Ossification credits Charles Bullen alongside Hodgkinson and Gilonis.

The Work’s Ayaa LP Rubber Cage (1989) and Officer!‘s Ayaa releases (1984–1985) confirm the same French distributor connecting both bands to Dominique Diebold’s Reims operation.

Relationship to the Reims Scene

The Ayaa label released both Ossification and Cough alongside records by Hellebore and NEO Museum (2), both Reims-based groups featuring Antoine Gindt and Daniel Koskowitz. Gindt appears in the Officer! Discogs member list and on Cough-era recordings (visible in Earlier Music: guitar on “Bone Connections”). Koskowitz appears on Yes Yes No No Yes No Yes. The Reims/Paris connection runs through Dominique Diebold who produced the debut cassette and the Life At The Water’s Edge 7”.

Open Questions

  • Exact formation date (1982 vs. 1983 vs. 1984) remains partially unresolved. Evidence to date: Café OTO (“formally surfaced in ‘82”), the Officer! WordPress site (“since 1983”), the Psychedelic Baby interview (first tape in 1983, described as fully developed), and the Bandcamp bio (“inception in 1984”). No single contemporaneous document settles this; 1982 is most likely the date the project began taking shape, 1983 the first cassette, and 1984 possibly when Hobbs began using the Officer! name as his primary ongoing umbrella. [Bandcamp source: https://officer-official.bandcamp.com/, accessed 2026-06-20; WordPress source: https://peterpaulandgordon.wordpress.com/about/, accessed 2026-06-20]
  • Gareth Williams identification: resolved — confirmed as Gareth Williams of This Heat / Flaming Tunes (see Never Mind The Bucket entry above).
  • Dead Unique (1995) personnel: substantially resolved — see Dead Unique entry above.
  • Full biography of Mick Hobbs prior to c. 1979 not in Discogs; see Mick Hobbs for what is known. Web sources add only that he was playing “in various jazz situations” and “rocking out” before answering The Work’s Melody Maker ad in 1978/79.
  • Life And Living Records (Discogs ID: 147391) appears as co-releaser from 2016 but has no vault page yet.
  • Jelodanti Records (Discogs ID: not confirmed from this trawl) co-released Paragraphs and Dead Right but has no vault page.
  • Whether the “Nothing On” digital releases (2020) were released to physical media is not confirmed.
  • Joey Stack’s legal name is given as “Jo Benjafield” on the Officer! WordPress site; she is described as having died (date not specified). This should be verified against her Discogs entry (913088).
