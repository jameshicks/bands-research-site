The New Tony Williams Lifetime

Discogs artist ID: 274675 Active: 1975–1976 Genre: Jazz-Rock Fusion

Reformed version of The Tony Williams Lifetime, assembled by Tony Williams after the original group dissolved circa 1974. Distinguished from the first Lifetime by the presence of guitarist Allan Holdsworth — a British player then little-known in the United States — in the slot previously occupied by John McLaughlin. Recorded two studio albums for Columbia, a promotional single, and performed live at the Village Gate (New York); the live recording surfaced on an unofficial release in 2017.

Formation

After the original Lifetime ran its course through four studio albums on Polydor (1969–1972), Williams regrouped with an almost entirely new set of personnel. The label changed from Polydor to Columbia — where Williams had extensive history as a sideman on the The Miles Davis Quintet records of the 1960s. The reason for the Columbia deal rather than a Polydor renewal is not documented in Discogs sources; plausibly it reflected Williams’s existing Columbia relationships and/or a change in his management situation (the original Lifetime’s Polydor run had been overseen by producers Jack Lewis and Monte Kay). The new lineup — Williams, Holdsworth, Alan Pasqua (keyboards), and Tony Newton (bass) — was a true quartet, adding a dedicated bassist to the essentially organ-bass-substituting format of the first Lifetime.

Holdsworth had recently played with Soft Machine and Tempest (6) in the UK, giving him a profile in progressive rock circles but limited US exposure. His selection by Williams reflected an interest in a technically demanding single-note stylist who could occupy a Holdsworth-specific space rather than attempt to replicate McLaughlin’s approach.

Production

Both studio albums were produced and engineered by Bruce Botnick — best known for his work with The Doors at Elektra and for co-producing The Old Bum’s Rush (Polydor, 1972) in a mixing capacity for the original Lifetime. His involvement with both Lifetime iterations was thus a point of continuity across the label change. On Million Dollar Legs, Botnick worked alongside recording engineer Mark Guercio and assistant engineers Al Burnam and Tom Lykes; string and horn sessions were separately recorded by Doug Botnick (credited as “Douglas Blotnick” — likely a relative of Bruce’s). The string/horn arrangements were written by Jack Nitzsche, a Los Angeles arranger with deep Spector-era roots.

The Columbia connection is likely explained by Williams’s existing relationship with the label: he had recorded on Columbia throughout the Miles Davis years (1963–1969) and the label signed him as a leader for The Joy of Flying (1979) after the New Lifetime run. The transition from Polydor may also reflect the dissolution of the Lewis/Kay production team that had administered the original Lifetime deal.

Connections

  • Tony Williams — drummer, leader; came from The Tony Williams Lifetime (1969–74) and The Miles Davis Quintet (1963–69)
  • Allan Holdsworth — guitar; previously Soft Machine, Tempest (6); later UK, Bruford; this band was his US breakthrough
  • Alan Pasqua — keyboards; later jazz session career; member of Van Stephenson group, Giant (4), Los Angeles Jazz Ensemble
  • Tony Newton — bass; also a member of Earl Van Dyke Sextet, The 8th Day, G-Force (19), Joachim Kühn Band
  • The Tony Williams Lifetime — predecessor band (Polydor, 1969–74); original lineup included John McLaughlin and Larry Young
  • John McLaughlin — New Lifetime’s indirect precedent; his departure from original Lifetime led to the eventual reformation
  • Bruce Botnick — producer/engineer; previously mixed The Old Bum’s Rush (1972) for original Lifetime
  • Jack Nitzsche — arranger on Million Dollar Legs; Spector-era LA session fixture
  • The Miles Davis Quintet — Williams’s pre-Lifetime home; established his Columbia relationship

Open Questions

  • Why Columbia? Discogs does not document the contractual/business reason for the switch from Polydor (original Lifetime’s label) to Columbia. Most likely explanations: (a) the Lewis/Kay production team that held the Polydor relationship disbanded, leaving Williams free to renegotiate; (b) Columbia’s existing relationship with Williams from the Miles Davis years made them a natural home; (c) Columbia may have been actively courting jazz-rock artists in 1975 (Weather Report was on Columbia; Miles Davis remained on Columbia). Confirming the specific deal would require non-Discogs sources.
  • Recording dates: Discogs does not carry session dates for either album. The recording locations for Believe It and Million Dollar Legs are not in the Discogs database.
  • Holdsworth’s accounts: Any interviews or retrospective commentary by Holdsworth on this band are not accessible via Discogs.
  • “Running Wild”: One shared release between the New Lifetime and Jack Bruce / Webster Lewis titled “Running Wild” appears in the collaborators graph. This is not listed in the main discography — likely a compilation track or a session cut not in the primary catalog. Needs investigation.
  • Al Cleveland co-writing credit: Al Cleveland (Discogs ID 391755) is credited as co-writer of “You Did It To Me” on Million Dollar Legs. This appears to be the same Al Cleveland who wrote for Motown (Marvin Gaye’s “I Want You Back”), but the connection is not confirmed by Discogs alone.
