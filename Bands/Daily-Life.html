Daily Life

Origin: New York City
Active: late 1977 – June 1978
Associated with: Glenn Branca, Barbara Ess

No wave band led by Barbara Ess (bass, vocals); performed at the “Bands at Artists Space” festival (early 1978) but was not included on the No New York compilation. Distinct from Branca’s concurrent project Theoretical Girls in both membership and repertoire — the two bands shared only Branca. Daily Life dissolved when Paul McMahon departed for Los Angeles in summer 1978; the remaining trio (Ess, Branca, Hahn) immediately reformed as The Static. [source: paulmcmahon.tv/bands; scaruffi.com/avant/branca.html]

Note on Branca’s role: Earlier vault entry described Branca as “leader”; web sources consistently frame Daily Life as Ess’s band — Ess recruited members and wrote the core material. Scaruffi describes Branca as having “played with Barbara Ess’s Daily Life.” The McMahon account on paulmcmahon.tv confirms Ess was the bandleader. The vault entry has been corrected accordingly.

Relationship to Theoretical Girls

Daily Life and Theoretical Girls were concurrent but entirely distinct projects sharing only one member: Glenn Branca.

Membership: Theoretical Girls comprised Branca, Jeff Lohn (vocals, guitar), Margaret De Wys (keyboards), and Wharton Tiers (drums). Daily Life comprised Barbara Ess (bass, vocals), Branca (guitar), Paul McMahon (guitar), and Christine Hahn (No Wave) (drums). No other member overlap.

Repertoire: The two bands played entirely different material:

  • Daily Life — Ess wrote the core songs, described as “screamed mantras” with repetitive lyrics (“No work, no job, no love and no money”; “Be my mother be my father be my sister be my brother”) delivered over steady, bass-driven rhythm. Branca contributed “train-themed songs emphasizing momentum.” McMahon provided chord-based guitar accompaniment and at least one cover (“Train Kept a Rollin’ All Night Long”). [source: paulmcmahon.tv/bands, as of 2026-07-31]

  • Theoretical Girls — Co-written by Branca and Jeff Lohn, the repertoire comprised skewed punk songs including “U.S. Millie” (Lohn composition, released as a single), “Chicita Bonita,” “Computer Dating,” and “Mom and Dad.” The approach blended contemporary classical sensibility with no wave noise. [source: tinymixtapes.com; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_Girls, as of 2026-07-31]

No repertoire overlap between the two bands is documented in any available source. The only organizational connection is Branca’s participation in both and the subsequent reformation of the three remaining Daily Life members as The Static.

Context at Artists Space: Both bands performed at the five-night “Bands at Artists Space” festival (early 1978, organized by Michael Zwack and Robert Longo) alongside DNA, the Contortions, Mars, Teenage Jesus and the Jerks, and others. Neither appeared on the resulting No New York LP. [source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_New_York, as of 2026-07-31]

Discogs

Research Queue

  • Barbara Ess (Discogs ID: 342784): Best known as a founding member of Y Pants (bass, ukulele, vocals; “Little Music” EP, 99 Records, 1980; retrospective on Periodic Document, 1998). Also performed with The Static (Glenn Branca’s early project; “Songs ‘77-‘79”, Atavistic, 1995), appeared on Branca’s “Theoretical Record” (1979), and played baritone guitar/bass/percussion on Glenn Branca’s Symphony No. 1 (ROIR, 1983), mallet guitar on Symphony No. 2 (Atavistic, 1992), and as musician on Symphony No. 3 (Les Disques Du Crépuscule, 1983). Released “Beat It Down” (Neutral Records, 1982) and co-edited the “Just Another Asshole” compilation (1981). Also credited for cover photography on various releases.
  • Paul McMahon (Discogs ID: 566764): Minimal footprint on Discogs — released “Are You Fearless?” (1978, self-released, vocals/guitar/written-by) and a self-titled LP (Neutral Records, 1986, vocals/guitar). No other credited appearances found.
  • Christine Hahn (No Wave) (Discogs ID: 375281): A primary member of Malaria- (the Berlin no wave/post-punk group), credited on their “How Do You Like My New Dog? / Pernod.” single (Les Disques Du Crépuscule, 1982), “Emotion” (1982), “…Revisited” (ROIR, 1983), “Compiled” (Moabit Musik, 1991), and “Compiled 2.0” (2019). Also performed with Luxus (18) (“The Person Who Can’t Dance Says The Band Can’t Play”, S.O. 36 Records, 1979), appeared on Branca’s “Theoretical Record” (1979) and “Songs ‘77-‘79” as The Static (1995), played synthesizer on Innovative Communication releases “One Is One” (1981) and “Zwischenmischung” (1982), and appeared on “New York Passage” (Cachalot Records, 1982) and “Versus” (Superstar Recordings, 2001).
