James White & The Blacks

Active: 1979–present (intermittently) Based: New York City Labels: ZE Records, Animal Records, Chrysalis, Unicorn, Munster, Modern Harmonic Also known as: James Chance & The Contortions (parallel/related project) Genre: No Wave, avant-funk, free jazz, post-punk

The DB treats James White & The Blacks and James Chance & The Contortions as one entry — both are vehicles for James Siegfried (born 1953, Milwaukee). The distinction: The Contortions was the rougher, more confrontational No Wave project; James White & The Blacks was the same scene repositioned toward funk and jazz. In practice, overlapping lineups and interchangeable personnel.

James Siegfried / James Chance / James White

Born 1953 in Milwaukee. Studied music; moved to New York in the mid-1970s. Became one of the central figures of the No Wave scene — confrontational performances, aggressive saxophone, a fusion of free jazz aggression with punk energy and disco/funk rhythm. Christened both personas from a single source: Anya Phillips, his manager and partner, named the Contortions project; the White/Blacks name carried a deliberate irony.

Anya Phillips

The most important figure adjacent to the band after Siegfried himself. Phillips was co-manager (with Michel Esteban) of the No Wave scene’s commercial face: she managed James Chance, co-organized the No New York compilation (Brian Eno, 1978), and was instrumental in negotiating the ZE Records deal. She died of cancer in April 1981, aged 26. Her presence accounts for the 23 shared releases — she’s credited across the full early catalog as manager, design, and organizing force.

ZE Records context

ZE Records was co-founded by Michel Esteban (French; also Anya Phillips’s partner) and Michael Zilkha (British; son of Marks & Spencer chairman). Operating from NYC from 1978, ZE was the label that most deliberately bridged the No Wave underground and commercial dance: its roster included James Chance/White, Kid Creole And The Coconuts, Was (Not Was), Material, ESG, Suicide (distribution), Cristina. Off White and “Contort Yourself” were among ZE’s first releases.

Esteban appears in the collaborators graph with 5 shared releases — he’s credited as producer/manager across the early catalog.

Notes (web)

Glenn O’Brien’s role in Downtown 81

Glenn O’Brien was the writer and producer of Downtown 81 (also released as New York Beat Movie). He did not direct — direction was by Edo Bertoglio. O’Brien conceived the film as a vehicle to showcase the downtown NYC bands he was covering in his “Glenn O’Brien’s Beat” column in Interview magazine, and cast Jean-Michel Basquiat (whom he knew from TV Party and the Mudd Club) as the lead. Co-producer on the original shoot (December 1980–January 1981) was Patrick Montgomery; the project was funded by Rizzoli but abandoned in the mid-1980s due to financial problems. O’Brien reacquired the rights in 1999 and completed post-production with Maripol; Michael Zilkha (ZE Records co-founder) served as executive producer for the 1999–2000 completion. The film premiered at Cannes in 2000. (Wikipedia — Downtown 81, as of 2026-08-11; Wikipedia — Glenn O’Brien, as of 2026-08-11)
