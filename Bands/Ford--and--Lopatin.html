Ford & Lopatin

Discogs ID: 2231054
Members: Joel Ford · Daniel Lopatin (aka Oneohtrix Point Never)
Active: 2011 (as Ford & Lopatin); preceded by Games (3) (2010)
Labels: Software (2) · Mexican Summer
Alias/Name variation: Ford And Lopatin

American electronic music duo based in New York, formed by producer/multi-instrumentalist Joel Ford and composer/artist Daniel Lopatin. Their output as Ford & Lopatin consists of one LP, a handful of singles and EPs, and a remix compilation, all released in 2011. The project grew directly out of their earlier collaboration as Games (3).

Biography

Origins: Games (3)

Joel Ford and Daniel Lopatin began working together under the name Games (3) around 2010. That project released material on Hippos In Tanks and established the duo’s shared aesthetic: dense, melodic synthesizer music drawing on ’80s pop, new wave, and early MIDI production — nostalgic in texture but not in irony. The Games recordings attracted attention in the emerging chillwave/hauntology adjacent scene while remaining harder to classify than most of it.

Channel Pressure (2011)

For the full-length LP they renamed the project Ford & Lopatin, both to distinguish the new work from Games and because the album represented a more deliberate compositional statement. Channel Pressure was released in June 2011 as SFT-001 — the inaugural release on Software (2), a label Lopatin co-founded with Oneohtrix Point Never manager/collaborator Gabe Meier (label details on the Software page). The LP was also distributed through Mexican Summer in the US.

The record was engineered by Al Carlson and mixed by Scott Herren (credited as Guillermo Scott Herren — his full name; also known as Prefuse 73). Mastering was by Joe LaPorta. Cover design was by Thunderhorse (2) (credited as Thunderhorse (2)); layout and type by B. Sisto.

The tracklist spans 14 tracks across two sides. Guest credits on individual tracks:

Himanshu Suri is the rapper also known as Heems, then a member of Das Racist. Autre Ne Veut is the project of Arthur Ashin, who was emerging simultaneously on the Brooklyn scene.

Channel Pressure was released in multiple pressings: a limited numbered vinyl LP (SFT-001-2I), a standard vinyl LP, a CD, a cassette (via Mexican Summer), and a digital download. A later pressing in 2014 appeared on Software (2) in the UK (release ID 11357625). An instrumental version was also issued digitally (release ID 8490611, SFT-001).

Genres on Discogs: Electronic, Pop. Styles: IDM, Leftfield, New Wave, Synth-pop, Synthwave.

Singles

“Emergency Room” (SFT 002, Software (2), 2011) — 12” limited edition picture disc. Three tracks:

  • A: Emergency Room (Album Version) (3:34)
  • B1: Emergency Dub — produced by The Bug (Kevin Martin)
  • B2: Emergency Room (Gavin Russom Remix) — remixed by Gavin R. Russom

“I Surrender” (COOPR482, Cooperative Music / Software (2), 2011) — CD single promo, UK. One track (3:14). Issued via Cooperative Music as a UK promotional CD.

“Too Much MIDI (Please Forgive Me)” (2011) — digital single issued via -adult swim- (the Adult Swim singles program). One track (4:14), MP3.

“World Of Regret” — UK CDr promo (Software (2), 2011) and later a proper single release (Software (2) / V2 Records, UK; master ID 3217234).

“Snakes / Flying Dream” (MEX100, Mexican Summer, 2011) — 12” 45 RPM split, limited edition. This is the release the Research Queue flagged. It is a proper four-track 12”:

  • A1: Snakes (3:57) — Shannon Funchess / Ford & Lopatin
  • A2: Snakes (A Capella) (3:57) — Shannon Funchess / Ford & Lopatin
  • B1: Flying Dream (3:17) — Tamaryn (2) / Ford & Lopatin
  • B2: Flying Dream (A Capella) (3:17) — Tamaryn (2) / Ford & Lopatin

Shannon Funchess (also known as Skata Blackberry) sang on the A-side; Tamaryn (2) — the singer-songwriter project of Tamaryn Brown — sang on the B-side. Both tracks also appear as instrumental a cappella versions.

FACT Mix 249 (2011)

Ford & Lopatin recorded FACT Mix 249 for FACT Magazine (UK), released as a digital file. The tracklist is a DJ mix of licensed material: Scritti Politti, Angela Bofill, Total Contrast, Stanley Clarke, Todd Terje, Tiger & Woods, Krystol, Carly Simon. It shows the duo’s reference points — smooth, melodic ’80s pop and funk, filtered through contemporary club aesthetics.

Production Credits (Non-F&L Releases)

Beyond their own releases, Ford & Lopatin produced for two artists on Software (2):

  • Harmonizer (3) — World Complete EP (SFT 005, Software (2), 2011). 12” 45 RPM, 4 tracks. Performers: Greg Davis and Toby Aronson. Engineered by Al Carlson. Mastered by Joe Lambert.
  • Megafortress — Megafortress EP (SFT 006, Software (2), 2012). 12” 45 RPM, 4 tracks. Music by Bill Gillim. Engineered and mixed by Al Carlson. Mastered by Paul Gold.

Both releases were part of the early Software (2) catalog and show the label’s extended roster.

Dissolution / Post-2011

Discogs year range for Ford & Lopatin shows activity through 2014 (a UK repress of Channel Pressure). No new original material is documented in the DB after 2012. Both members continued separately: Lopatin as Oneohtrix Point Never (prolific through the 2010s and into the 2020s) and Joel Ford under his own name and as Airbird, and in groups including Ejecta, Young Ejecta, and Tigercity.
