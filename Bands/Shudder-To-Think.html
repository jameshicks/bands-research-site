Oct 20, 202612 min read

Shudder To Think

Discogs ID: 276053
Origin: Washington, DC, USA
Formed: 1986
Active: 1986–1998 (reunions from 2009 onward, currently active)
Genres: Post-hardcore, art rock, glam-inflected indie rock
Labels: Not On Label (self-released), Sammich Records, Dischord Records, Epic

One of the most formally ambitious bands to emerge from the DC hardcore scene: Shudder To Think began as a self-released post-hardcore act in the mid-1980s and evolved, over a decade, into a theatrically expressive art-rock band whose major-label records sit largely outside DC convention. Craig Wedren’s falsetto-heavy vocals and Nathan Larson’s increasingly sophisticated guitar writing set the band apart from their Dischord peers even while they shared stages and engineering credits with Fugazi and Jawbox.

Lineup notes:

  • The 1987 self-titled demo and 1988 Curses, Spells, Voodoo, Mooses feature the original quartet: Wedren (vocals), Chris Matthews (guitar), Stuart Hill (bass), Mike Russell (drums).
  • Ten-Spot (1990) credits: same quartet of Wedren, Hill, Russell, Matthews — though Matthews is listed under “Band,” Hill handles bass.
  • Funeral At The Movies (1991) credits: Hill (bass), Matthews (guitar), Russell (drums), Wedren (voice). By this point Matthews has moved to guitar and Hill remains on bass.
  • Get Your Goat (1992): Wedren (vocals/guitar), Hill (bass), Russell (drums). The band credit and writing credits do not list Matthews, suggesting his departure between Funeral and Goat. Nathan Larson is not yet listed.
  • Pony Express Record (1994): Wedren (vocals/guitar), Nathan Larson (guitar), Stuart Hill (bass), Adam Wade (drums). Russell out, Wade in; Larson confirmed as full member.
  • 50,000 B.C. (1997): Wedren (vocals/guitar/keyboards), Nathan Larson (guitar/keyboards/backing vocals), Stuart Hill (bass/backing vocals), Kevin March (drums, tracks 1–10 and 12). Steve Calhoun appears on percussion.
  • First Love, Last Rites (1998 soundtrack): credits “Shudder To Think” as Wedren, Nathan Larson, and Stuart Hill — three-piece configuration.

Adam Wade (Discogs ID 276052) went on to Jawbox and Film School.

Biography

Beginnings (1986–1988)

Shudder To Think formed in Washington, DC in 1986. Their first release was a self-produced, self-released cassette (Discogs release ID 12218048, 1987), produced with help from Ed Wenn and Manolo Lagdameo. The core lineup — Wedren on vocals, Chris Matthews on guitar, Stuart Hill on bass, Mike Russell on drums — is documented here.

The first proper record, Curses, Spells, Voodoo, Mooses (1988, Sammich Records), was engineered by Don Zientara at Inner Ear Studios, placing the band early in the Inner Ear orbit that would define much of the DC scene’s sound. Producer on this record was Eric Lagdameo. The tracklist includes a cover of a John Lennon composition (credited on the label) alongside originals; Craig Wedren and Chris Matthews handled the artwork themselves.

Dischord Years (1990–1992)

Ten-Spot (1990, Dischord 46; release ID 1190054) was co-produced and mixed by the band with Kenny Inouye, engineered by Chris Biando — notably not at Inner Ear under Don Zientara, marking a departure from the standard Dischord production setup. J. Robbins (of Jawbox) did the cover artwork. The lineup remained the original four.

Funeral At The Movies (1991, Dischord 54; release ID 1060869) is a more complex production: basic tracking was done at Southern Studios (London) by Harvey Birrell and Paul Harding, then mixed in multiple passes — some at Black Pond Studios by Chris Biando, Wedren, and Hill; some at ENS by Wharton Tiers. Still the original four-piece lineup (Wedren, Matthews, Hill, Russell), with Wedren credited specifically for “Voice.”

Dischord also issued Funeral At The Movies & Ten Spot as a combined CD (release ID 20628880, 1991), which the band produced and mixed.

Get Your Goat (1992, Dischord 67; release ID 495734) brought a significant shift in production: Eli Janney produced and mixed, with Chris Biando recording. The lineup credits on this record list Wedren (vocals/guitar), Stuart Hill (bass), Mike Russell (drums) — Matthews is absent, and Nathan Larson is not yet listed. Janney, known for his work with Girls Against Boys, brought a different sonic sensibility to the record. Laura Costas did the design and illustration; Jim Saah handled photography.

The band also recorded a live session for the German series Your Choice Live Series (1992, Your Choice Records).

Transition and the Sub Pop Singles (1993–1994)

Between the Dischord period and the Epic deal, the band’s lineup solidified into the Wedren/Larson/Hill/Wade formation. Two Sub Pop singles bracket the Pony Express Record release:

  • X-French Tee Shirt / Shake Your Halo Down (1994, Sub Pop; release ID 1623807)
  • Shudder To Think / Sunny Day Real Estate split 7” (1994, Sub Pop; release ID 1689348)

The Sub Pop split with Sunny Day Real Estate is an interesting moment of two post-hardcore bands moving simultaneously toward more melodic and emotionally expressive territory.

Epic Records Period (1994–1997)

Shudder To Think signed to Epic (a Sony Music Entertainment imprint, label ID 1005), one of a small number of Dischord-adjacent bands to make the major-label move in the early 1990s alongside Jawbox (Atlantic) and Jawbreaker (DGC).

Production team: Both Epic albums were produced by Ted Niceley, who had produced Fugazi’s first three records and was by 1994 doing major-label work across the DC scene. The Niceley connection links the band to the broader Dischord production network even on a major label.

Pony Express Record (1994, Epic EK 66415; release ID 622538):

  • Produced by Ted Niceley (credited as “Ted Nicely”)
  • Mixed by Andy Wallace
  • Engineered by Steve Palmieri; mix engineer Steve Sisco; second engineer Michael Rippe
  • Mastered by Bob Ludwig
  • Lineup: Craig Wedren (vocals/guitar), Nathan Larson (guitar), Stuart Hill (bass), Adam Wade (drums)
  • A&R: Michael Goldstone

The same period produced the Hit Liquor EP (1994, Epic; release ID 622536), which contains the title track produced by Niceley/Palmieri/Wallace, two tracks (“Red House,” “Heaven Here”) produced by Nick Pellicciotto, and a home-recorded demo by Wedren and Larson. The EP’s tracklist shows Chris Matthews credited for writing “Red House” and “Heaven Here” (alongside Wedren, Russell, and Hill) — material apparently written in the Dischord era and carried forward.

50,000 B.C. (1997, Epic; release ID 401859):

  • Produced by Ted Niceley
  • Mixed by Carl Glanville; assistant mix engineer Jay Nicholas
  • Engineered by Andy Baker and Carl Glanville; assistant engineers Jamie Campbell, Juan Garcia, Vince Reynolds
  • Lineup: Craig Wedren (vocals/guitar/keyboards), Nathan Larson (guitar/keyboards/backing vocals), Stuart Hill (bass/backing vocals), Kevin March (drums on tracks 1–10 and 12), Steve Calhoun (percussion)
  • A&R: Michael Goldstone (retained from Pony Express)

The drum change from Adam Wade to Kevin March between albums is worth noting; March (Discogs ID 276055) replaced Wade for this record. The album was issued in Europe first; the US version followed. A single, Red House, was also issued in Germany/Austria/Switzerland (1997, Epic; release ID 2568533).

Neither Epic album achieved significant commercial success. The band’s theatricality — Wedren’s operatic falsetto, the density of the arrangements — positioned them awkwardly against both the alt-rock mainstream and the more stripped-down indie sounds of the period.

Film Soundtrack Work and Dissolution (1997–1998)

The band’s final phase was oriented around film scoring:

High Art soundtrack (1998, Velvel Records; release ID 2158254): Music written and produced by Shudder To Think for Lisa Cholodenko’s debut film. Wedren and Larson share individual writing credits across the 15-track score — Wedren credited on tracks 1–3, 5, 9, 11, 14, 15; Larson credited on tracks 3, 4, 7, 8, 12, 13. Joan Wasser (later known as Joan As Police Woman) is credited for writing track 15. Mastered by Greg Calbi.

First Love, Last Rites soundtrack (1998, Epic/Forensic Films/Sony Music Soundtrax/Strand Releasing; release ID 1513904): Performed and produced by Shudder To Think (Wedren, Larson, Hill in three-piece configuration). Includes a co-write on track 6 with Steven Bernstein. Jesse Peretz and Sarah Sofer handled cover photography.

These two soundtracks mark the end of Shudder To Think’s original run.

Reunion and Later Activity (2009–present)

The band reconvened for Live From Home (2009, Team Love Records; release ID 2487826). The lineup for this recording is documented in Nathan Larson’s credit note: “For this recording, Shudder To Think was” [Wedren, Larson, Hill, with additional members Jesse Krakow, Jherek Bischoff, Mark Watrous, Clint Walsh — all listed as Discogs members].

As of the Discogs profile note, the band has reunited several times and released a new 7” on Dischord Records.

Post-Band Careers

Nathan Larson

Nathan Larson (Discogs ID 345447; real name Nathan Peter Larson) transitioned into film scoring and record production after the band’s dissolution. Key post-STT work on Discogs:

  • Film scoring: Boys Don’t Cry soundtrack (1999, Koch Records) — producer credit. This is his first documented score work.
  • A Camp (2001, Stockholm Records/Universal) — Larson played bass, theremin, guitar, piano, Hammond organ, programmed synths, and harp, and co-produced vocals across the Cardigans’ Nina Persson side project’s debut album. He continued working with A Camp through the 2000s.
  • Jealous God (2001, Artemis Records) — Solo album under his own name; musician, vocals, bass, guitar, keyboards, songwriter.
  • Solo film score: Filmmusik (2005, Commotion Records) — Credited as primary artist; producer, writer, arranger, performer.
  • He also co-produced vocals and engineered on multiple A Camp / Cardigans releases (Stockholm Records): Long Gone Before Daylight (2003), Super Extra Gravity (2005), Colonia (2009, Reveal Records — extensive instrumental contributions).

Larson was also a member of Swiz before Shudder To Think (bass), and later of A Camp, Mind Science Of The Mind, and Leslie (29).

Craig Wedren

Craig Wedren (Discogs ID 92256; real name Craig Benjamin Wedren) has sustained an active post-STT career across film/TV composition and solo work:

  • Solo: Lapland (2005, Team Love Records) — solo album.
  • Wet Hot American Summer: Original Score & Music (2016, Rusted Wave) — Wedren composed the original score.
  • Blood Hive (Original Score From The Showtime Series Yellowjackets) (2022, Lakeshore Records) — Wedren’s profile as TV composer; credited as primary artist on the score release.
  • Other appearances: Guest vocalist on The Invitation soundtrack (2016, Mondo); backing vocals on Nite Creatures (2020, Decca).
  • Groups: Also a member of Baby (30), Shudder To Sink, and The Immoral Minority per Discogs.

Wedren has also contributed to Naïve Records releases (Miss You, 2002; Production, 2005 — both as vocalist/co-producer) and appeared on the Vic Chesnutt-associated Constellation Records release Villainaire (2008).

Stuart Hill

Stuart Hill (Discogs ID 580598; name variation “Dead End Stu”) played bass throughout the entire Dischord era and into the Epic period, remaining through at least the First Love, Last Rites soundtrack. Discogs lists him as a member of Frankfurter and Shudder To Sink in addition to Shudder To Think. No major solo career documented on Discogs.

Mike Russell

Mike Russell (3) (Discogs ID 580600) drummed through the Dischord period (Ten-Spot, Funeral At The Movies, Get Your Goat). He is also listed as a member of Shudder To Sink. No further release credits documented on Discogs outside STT.

Adam Wade

Adam Wade (Discogs ID 276052) joined for the Epic era and is documented on Pony Express Record (1994) and the Hit Liquor EP (1994). He subsequently joined Jawbox, then Film School, The Jealous Sound, and other bands.

Open Questions

  • The transition of Chris Matthews out of the band: Get Your Goat (1992) does not credit him, but no Discogs source explains exactly when or why he left. His writing credits appear on the Hit Liquor EP (1994) for tracks apparently composed earlier, suggesting the split was between Funeral At The Movies (1991) and Get Your Goat (1992).
  • Nathan Larson’s exact entry point: credited on Get Your Goat (1992) writing credits are band-collective; his first named guitar credit is Your Choice Live Series (1992) and Pony Express Record (1994). The precise joining date is not in Discogs.
  • The 2009 reunion lineup (Krakow, Bischoff, Watrous, Walsh) — which of these were touring members vs. recording members for Live From Home is unclear from Discogs alone.
  • Whether there are additional Dischord-era releases beyond the three LPs (29 Discogs entries noted for the label — the gap may be compilation appearances).
