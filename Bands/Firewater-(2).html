Overview

Firewater is an NYC indie rock band founded in 1995 by Tod Ashley (Tod A.), who has remained its sole constant presence as singer, songwriter, and bassist through extensive lineup turnover. The original ensemble was notably large, including Duane Denison (of The Jesus Lizard), Hahn Rowe, Dave Ouimet, Yuval Gabay, Kurt Hoffman, and Jim Kimball; of those, Denison, Rowe, Ouimet, Adam Scheflan, and George Javori appear among credited members. The catalogue runs across three decades—with the bulk concentrated in the late 1990s and 2000s—first on Jetset Records, then Nois-O-Lution and Bloodshot Records into the 2010s. The music blends indie and alternative rock with klezmer and folk strains, and drummer Tamir Muskat (Balkan Beat Box) is by far the most frequent collaborator with 15 shared credits.

Notes (web)

The Golden Hour label: Bloodshot Records (US) / Nois-O-Lution (Europe) — co-release

The release table above (Discogs data) lists The Golden Hour (2008) under Nois-O-Lution, while Wikipedia lists it under Bloodshot Records. Both are correct: the album was a co-release — Bloodshot Records (catalog BS 152) distributed it in the US and Australia; Nois-O-Lution (catalog 910832) distributed it in Germany/Europe from April 6, 2008 (a month ahead of the US date of May 6, 2008). Sources: Discogs master 58228; Wikipedia — The Golden Hour (as of 2026-11-10); MusicBrainz release 364c36d4.

Why Bloodshot rather than Jetset? — no source found

The specific reason Firewater signed with Bloodshot Records for The Golden Hour rather than returning to Jetset Records is not documented in any source located. The following were checked and contain no explanation:

  • Wikipedia articles for Firewater (band) and The Golden Hour (as of 2026-11-10) — record the fact of the label change without comment.
  • Wikipedia article for Jetset Records (as of 2026-11-10) — confirms the label is defunct, but gives no closure date or reason. The label was founded by Shelley Maple in 1996; it is described in past tense throughout, implying closure sometime after its last Firewater release (Songs We Should Have Written, 2004 / The Man On The Burning Tightrope, 2003).
  • 2008 Alarm Magazine interview with Tod A: Firewater: From A World Away — discusses the travels behind the album, does not address the label.
  • 2008 PopMatters 20 Questions interview with Tod A: popmatters.com — no mention of label change or Jetset Records.
  • 2012 Magnet Magazine Q&A with Tod A: magnetmagazine.com — discusses International Orange! on Bloodshot but does not address how the Bloodshot relationship began.
  • The Vinyl District interview with Tod A: thevinyldistrict.com — no label history discussed.

The most plausible working hypothesis — consistent with Jetset’s defunct status and Tod A’s four-year absence from recording (2004–2008) — is that Jetset Records had effectively ceased operations or become inactive by the time Tod A returned, leaving him to find a new US distributor. Bloodshot, despite its alt-country identity, had by 2008 expanded its roster beyond strict alt-country. However, this is inference, not a cited fact. No interview in which Tod A explains the choice has been located.
