Molasses (2)

Avant-folk ensemble led by singer/songwriter Scott Legrande Chernoff, active in Montreal 1998–2004. Discogs artist ID: 544686. Drew on a revolving cast from the Montreal experimental scene — including members of Godspeed You Black Emperor!, Set Fire To Flames, and Shalabi Effect — to make intimate, candlelit performances blending drunken slowcore folk, noise, free jazz, country ballads, and poetry. Released four albums between 1999 and 2004, primarily on Alien8 sublabel Fancy and then Alien8 Recordings proper.

Overview

Molasses began performing around Montreal in 1998, often giving free concerts at Bar Miami. The group’s instrumentation was deliberately unconventional — scrap metal, singing saws, and an ever-shifting lineup of string, wind, and electronics players from across the Montreal underground.

Their stripped-down debut, You’ll Never Be Well No More (1999, FANCYCD1), was recorded at Hotel2Tango with Thierry Amar (of Godspeed and A Silver Mt. Zion) handling recording and mixing, and Sam Shalabi also appearing. The album was mixed by both Molasses and Amar, and mastered by Harris Newman.

Second album Trilogie: Toil & Peaceful Life (2000, FANCYCD2) expanded the lineup with cellist Norsola Johnson, violinist Kate Lawrence, electronics from Alexandre St-Onge, hurdy gurdy from Martin Arnold, and bass/organ from Thierry Amar. Howard Bilerman joined as mixer alongside the band. It included a 14-minute adaptation of “Amazing Grace.”

Three years later came A Slow Messe (2003, FANCYCD5), a double CD and the group’s most ambitious work, featuring Thalia Zedek, Chris Brokaw, vocalist Jennifer Ménard, and numerous other musicians.

Trouble At Jinx Hotel (2004, ALIENCD46) appeared on Alien8 proper and brought in an even larger cast: Efrim Menuck (Godspeed / A Silver Mt. Zion) as both performer and co-recorder, Bruce Cawdron, cellist David Michael Curry, Jean-Sébastien Truchy (Fly Pan Am), Lisa Gamble (also known as Gambletron), and others. Menuck and Howard Bilerman handled recording. The group contributed a track to I Am a Cold Rock. I Am Dull Grass: A Tribute to the Music of Will Oldham later in 2004, then went inactive.
