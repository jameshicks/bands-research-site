The Tony Williams Lifetime

Jazz-rock fusion group, formed 1969, dissolved c. 1974. Discogs artist ID: 298013.

Founded by drummer Tony Williams immediately after he left The Miles Davis Quintet in 1969. One of the first and most influential jazz-rock fusion ensembles. The group went through two distinct phases: an exploratory power-trio / quartet era (1969–1971) featuring John McLaughlin and Larry Young, and a more R&B-oriented phase (1972–1974) with an almost entirely new lineup. All studio albums were released on Polydor.

Name variations on Discogs: Lifetime, Tony Williams Lifetime, Tony Williams’ Lifetime.

Formation and Background

Williams formed Lifetime around the time The Miles Davis Quintet’s second great quintet era wound down. McLaughlin was invited to join Miles Davis’s group but declined, preferring a context where he could play his own compositions — Lifetime provided that. The other anchor was Hammond organ innovator Larry Young (also known as Khalid Yasin Abdul Aziz), fresh off his landmark Blue Note recordings.

The original trio — Williams (drums/vocals), McLaughlin (guitar), Young (organ) — was a deliberately bass-less configuration, the organ’s pedals covering the low end. After recording Emergency!, ex-Cream (2) bassist Jack Bruce joined for live dates and the second album (Turn It Over). McLaughlin departed after that record to pursue his own career (most prominently the Mahavishnu Orchestra); Bruce also left for other projects.

Connections

  • Tony Williams — founder; previously The Miles Davis Quintet; later led The New Tony Williams Lifetime
  • John McLaughlin — went on to form Mahavishnu Orchestra (1971); also later Shakti (2)
  • Larry Young — parallel project Love Cry Want; also worked with Larry Young’s Fuel
  • Jack Bruce — came from Cream (2); later West, Bruce & Laing and solo work
  • Ron Carter — longtime Miles Davis sideman; appeared on Ego
  • Ted Dunbar — jazz guitarist; sole Lifetime album appearance
  • Miles Davis — context: Williams left Davis’s group to form Lifetime; McLaughlin declined Davis’s own invitation to join
  • The V.S.O.P. Quintet — later context: Williams reunited with former Davis colleagues
  • Allan Holdsworth — appears on later live recordings (Discogs: Live 1969 / 1975, The Lost Wildlife Sessions)
  • Bill Laswell — produced a Turn It Over redux reissue
  • Chick Corea — two compositions on (Turn It Over)
  • Bruce Botnick — mixed The Old Bum’s Rush; known for The Doors sessions

Open Questions

  • Exact recording dates for Emergency! (1969) — Discogs does not carry session dates
  • Whether “A. Hall (2)” on Emergency! writing credits is definitively a McLaughlin pseudonym or another contributor — Discogs does not resolve this
  • Play or Die referenced in some sources as a later Lifetime release — not found in Discogs under artist ID 298013; may be a Tom Grant (2) session or misattribution
  • Full personnel for the 1975 Lifetime compilation and 1979 Berkeley live recording — Discogs credits list Bunny Brunel, Tom Grant (2), Tod Carver, Bruce Harris (8) but the context is unclear (may be a distinct later group rather than a continuation of the 1969–1974 lineup)
  • McLaughlin’s stated reasons for leaving, and Williams’s response — not in Discogs data; would require web sources
