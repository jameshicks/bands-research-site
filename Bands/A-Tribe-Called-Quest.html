A Tribe Called Quest

Discogs ID: 3268 Location: Queens, New York City, NY Genre: Hip-hop / Rap; Jazz Rap; Conscious Hip Hop Active: 1988–1998 (original run); 2006 (reunion appearances); 2016 (final album) Labels: Jive Records (1989–1999); Epic (2016)

Critically acclaimed rap group formed in Queens, New York. One of the most influential hip-hop groups of the 1990s, central to the Native Tongues Posse and the jazz-rap fusion that defined conscious hip-hop. Inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2024.

Overview

A Tribe Called Quest formed in 1988 at high school in Queens, New York. The group’s name was given to them by Afrika Baby Bambaataa of the Jungle Brothers. Their core membership consisted of rapper/producer Q-Tip (Kamaal Fareed, born Jonathan Davis, 10 April 1970), DJ/producer Ali Shaheed Muhammad (born 11 August 1970, Brooklyn), rapper Phife Dawg (Malik Taylor, born 20 November 1970, Brooklyn; died 22 March 2016), and rapper/multi-instrumentalist Jarobi White. Jarobi departed early — The Low End Theory (1991) was recorded as a trio — though he returned for the 2016 reunion.

ATCQ debuted with “Description Of A Fool” (Jive, 1989), then broke through with “Bonita Applebum” and “Can I Kick It?” (both 1990). “Can I Kick It?” earned the first-ever 5-star review from Source Magazine. Their first four albums — People’s Instinctive Travels And The Paths Of Rhythm (1990), The Low End Theory (1991), Midnight Marauders (1993), and Beats, Rhymes And Life (1996) — established the template for jazz-inflected, sample-based hip-hop with literate, often playful lyricism. Beats, Rhymes And Life debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200. They were named Group of the Year at the inaugural Source Magazine Hip Hop Awards in 1994.

Their final two original-era albums — Beats, Rhymes And Life (1996) and The Love Movement (1998) — were produced by The Ummah, the in-house collective comprising Q-Tip, Ali Shaheed Muhammad, and J Dilla (James Yancey). The Love Movement debuted at No. 3 on the Billboard 200. After its release, the group disbanded.

The group reunited for live appearances in 2006 and 2008. In 2016, they announced We Got It From Here… Thank You 4 Your Service on Epic Records, their first album in 18 years. Phife Dawg died on 22 March 2016 of diabetes complications while the album was in sessions; the record was completed and released on 4 November 2016 to widespread acclaim. The reunion album features guest appearances from Kendrick Lamar, André 3000, Jack White, Elton John, and others, with all four original members contributing.

Honors include the Founders Award at the Billboard R&B/Hip-Hop Awards, the 4th VH1 Hip-Hop Honors, the 2017 Brit Award for International Group, and the 2024 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame induction.

Scene Connections

Native Tongues Posse

ATCQ were founding members of the Native Tongues collective, an informal network of Afrocentric, jazz-influenced hip-hop groups and artists active from the late 1980s. Core affiliated acts included:

  • Jungle Brothers (who named ATCQ; Afrika Baby Bambaataa credited as “supervised by” on the debut)
  • De La Soul (Tommy Boy; Discogs ID: 4091)
  • Queen Latifah (Dana Owens; listed on Discogs Native Tongues entry)
  • Monie Love (UK; listed on Discogs Native Tongues entry)
  • Chi-Ali (listed on Discogs Native Tongues entry)
  • Black Sheep (listed indirectly)

The Native Tongues aesthetic — Afrocentrism, positivity, jazz and soul samples, playful wordplay — defined ATCQ’s approach from the debut through Midnight Marauders.

The Soulquarians

Q-Tip is listed as a member of The Soulquarians (Discogs ID: 178849), the Electric Lady Studios–based neo-soul collective active around 1999–2002 that included D’Angelo, Erykah Badu, Common, Questlove, and J Dilla. This places ATCQ (via Q-Tip and J Dilla/Ummah overlap) at the center of the late-1990s progressive Black music network.

The Ummah Production Collective

The Ummah’s formation for Beats, Rhymes And Life brought J Dilla’s Detroit sensibility into ATCQ’s sonic environment. The Ummah also produced for Busta Rhymes and Mariah Carey during 1996–1998, positioning ATCQ’s production team at the heart of major-label hip-hop.

Jazz Connections

The Low End Theory made explicit jazz incorporation a signature — Ron Carter (Miles Davis/Coltrane bassist) played live bass on the album. Samples throughout the catalog draw from jazz, funk, and soul sources including Lou Reed, Stevie Wonder, and Earth, Wind & Fire.
