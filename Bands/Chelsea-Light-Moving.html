Chelsea Light Moving

Discogs artist ID: 2839023
Active: 2012–2015
Origin: New York City
Label: Matador Records
Genres: Alternative Rock, Noise Rock, Post-Punk, Experimental

American noise rock band formed in New York in 2012 by Thurston Moore following the indefinite breakup of Sonic Youth in 2011. The group released a run of singles on Matador Records throughout 2012 before issuing their only full-length, the self-titled debut LP, in early 2013. They dissolved shortly after.

Biography

Formation (2012)

Chelsea Light Moving came together in the immediate aftermath of Sonic Youth’s dissolution, which was triggered by the separation of Thurston Moore and Kim Gordon in 2011. Moore assembled a new group around himself, drawing on players with roots in the New York and New England free-improv and psychedelic underground.

The lineup comprised Moore (guitar, vocals), Keith Wood (guitar), Samara Lubelski (bass), and John Moloney (drums). Wood had been a long-time participant in the sprawling improv collective Sunburned Hand Of The Man and related projects. Lubelski — primarily a violinist and solo artist — brought connections to The Sonora Pine, Spectre Folk, and the Tower Recordings orbit. Moloney was likewise a Sunburned Hand veteran and had played in numerous free/psych collaborative contexts.

The band’s name is drawn from a line in a Frank O’Hara poem — an association reinforced by their 2012 single “Frank O’Hara Hit.”

Singles and early activity (2012)

Chelsea Light Moving debuted on Matador with a series of digital and 7” singles across 2012: “Groovy & Linda,” “Frank O’Hara Hit,” “Burroughs,” and “Empires Of Time.” All were recorded and mixed by Justin Pizzoferrato, who also handled the LP. The singles established the band’s template: clattering drums, dual-guitar noise, and Moore’s drawling vocals over long, stretched structures.

Self-titled LP (2013)

The band’s sole album, Chelsea Light Moving, appeared on Matador in March 2013. It was recorded and mixed by Justin Pizzoferrato. The vinyl edition (OLE 1010-1) was mastered by John Golden (credited as “GOLDEN”) and included a bonus 7” (OLE 1031-7) with “Heavenmetal” and a cover of the Germs’ “Communist Eyes” (written by Darby Crash and Pat Smear). The CD edition (OLE 1010-2) presented the ten tracks in a slightly different order.

The album received coverage within the noise/indie rock press but the band did not release further material. A split 7” with Merchandise (2) appeared the same year on a self-released basis.

Continued activity and unreleased recordings (2013–2014)

Despite the LP receiving only limited press coverage, Moore indicated in mid-2013 interviews that he considered Chelsea Light Moving his ongoing vehicle for rock music. In a Rolling Stone Q&A (May 2013) he described it as his “full-time band,” and in a late-2013 interview floated the idea of crediting a follow-up release to “Thurston Moore & Chelsea Light Moving.” No second album was announced by Matador and none materialised. A track called “Sunday Stage,” recorded in 2014 and performed live during the band’s touring period, remained unreleased until Moore shared it on Bandcamp in March 2020; an outtake, “No Go,” was shared at the same time. (source: stereogum.com, 30 March 2020)

Dissolution (2015)

Moore gave a specific explanation for ending Chelsea Light Moving in an interview with The Boston Globe (July 2015), reported by Stereogum on 3 August 2015: the band’s deliberately low profile — playing basement shows and small venues without Moore’s name in advertising — backfired because at those intimate settings “every crazy person could just come up to me and be crazy.” Obsessive fans from the Sonic Youth years who tracked him to small rooms with no security or physical barriers made the project untenable. Moore dissolved the band rather than scale up the venues and change its character. (source: stereogum.com, 3 August 2015)

There is no public record of Matador Records announcing or planning further CLM releases beyond the 2013 LP and its associated singles. Moore subsequently relocated to London and released solo material and work under the Thurston Moore Band name, also on Matador. The other members returned to their pre-existing projects.
