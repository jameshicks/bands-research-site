The Rascals

Also billed as: The Young Rascals (1965–1968) Discogs IDs: The Young Rascals: 418174 · The Rascals: 264481 Genre: Blue-eyed soul, R&B, rock, psychedelic rock Location: Garfield, NJ / New York City Active: 1965–1972 (original run); various reunions through 2013 Label run: Atlantic Records (1965–1971) → Columbia Records (1971–1972) Rock and Roll Hall of Fame: Inducted 1997 (Performer)

History

Formation and Early Career as The Young Rascals (1965–1967)

The group formed in 1965 in Garfield, New Jersey, drawing on the New York area club circuit. The core lineup was:

  • Felix Cavaliere — organ, piano, lead vocals
  • Eddie Brigati — vocals, percussion
  • Gene Cornish — guitar, bass, vocals
  • Dino Danelli — drums, artwork (contributed visual concepts throughout their career)

They signed to Atlantic Records and released their debut album The Young Rascals (1966). Early sides were produced by the band themselves, with Arif Mardin and Tom Dowd sharing a “Recording Supervisor” credit — the Atlantic house designation for staff personnel overseeing sessions. Mardin’s supervision was scoped to specific tracks (A1, B1–B4 on the debut). Tom Dowd co-supervised and served as chief engineer throughout most of the Atlantic run.

Their first chart hit, “Good Lovin’” (1966), reached #1 in the US. A string of Atlantic singles followed: “You Better Run,” “Come On Up,” “I’ve Been Lonely Too Long,” and “Groovin’” (1967, also #1). The group wrote the majority of their original material — Cavaliere and Brigati as principal songwriters — though early albums leaned on covers (Bob Dylan, Wilson Pickett, Motown material) alongside originals.

Peak Atlantic Period (1967–1969)

Between late 1967 and early 1968, the group dropped “Young” from their name, becoming The Rascals. Groovin’ (1967, Atlantic SD 8148) marked the beginning of Arif Mardin’s transition from supervisor to arranger: he provided orchestral arrangements for several tracks, and for the first time received an “Arranged By [Orchestra]” credit on the album, alongside Tom Dowd’s engineering. The band produced the album themselves.

Once Upon a Dream (1968, Atlantic SD 8169) is the most elaborate of the Atlantic albums — a 15-track double-LP concept with Mardin credited as Arranger and Conductor, an orchestral complement that included King Curtis (tenor sax), Hubert Laws (flute), Ron Carter (bass), Chuck Rainey (bass), Richard Davis (2) (bass), and Gene Orloff’s strings. The band produced it themselves; Mardin’s role was purely orchestral. Tom Dowd was chief engineer with Adrian Barber on sound effects. Dino Danelli designed the cover sculpture. The album featured David Brigati (Eddie’s brother) on backing vocals.

Freedom Suite (1969, Atlantic SD 2-901) was a double album — four vinyl sides — with the band again self-producing. No Arif Mardin production credit appears on this release; the session featured Chuck Rainey, Jerry Jemmott, and Richard Davis sharing bass duties, David “Fathead” Newman on solo tenor saxophone, and Charles Morrow arranging horns and effects. Adrian Barber and Tom Dowd handled engineering on individual sides.

See (1969, Atlantic SD 8246) explicitly credits Arif Mardin as “Co-producer” alongside the band. Engineers were Adrian Barber, Barry Goldberg, and Don Casale. Session players included Chuck Rainey (bass), Ron Carter (bass on B1–B2), Hubert Laws (flute), and Joe Bushkin (piano on B2). The cover used a René Magritte painting.

Late Atlantic Period and Columbia Move (1970–1972)

Search and Nearness (1971, Atlantic SD 8276) co-credits Arif Mardin as producer alongside the band — the first time Mardin held a full producer co-credit with them rather than “co-producer” or supervisory roles. Session players included Chuck Rainey, Harold Cowart, and Ron Blanco on bass; Joe Farrell and Seldon Powell on saxophones; Joe Newman on trumpet; and backing vocals from Cissy Houston, Tasha Thomas, The Sweet Inspirations, and David Brigati. Engineers: Chuck Kirkpatrick, Don Casale, Jack Adams, Jimmy Douglass, and Ron Albert.

The group then moved to Columbia Records. Eddie Brigati departed before or during the Columbia sessions; the remaining members — Cavaliere leading — produced the two Columbia albums:

  • Peaceful World (1971, Columbia G 30462) — a sprawling double LP heavily featuring Buzzy Feiten (guitar/bass) and Ralph MacDonald (percussion/congas), with Joe Farrell (flute/sax), Hubert Laws, Jerry Jemmott, Ron Carter, and guest Alice Coltrane (harp on “Little Dove”). Felix Cavaliere produced. Alice Coltrane’s appearance is a notable cameo.
  • The Island Of Real (1972, Columbia KC 31103) — another double LP; Cavaliere produced. Session players included Buzzy Feiten, Ralph MacDonald, Robert Popwell (bass), David Sanborn (alto sax), Hubert Laws, Joe Farrell, and the Woodstock Horns. Arthur Jenkins arranged tracks B2–B3.

The group disbanded in 1972 after The Island Of Real.

Reunions

Various touring and reunion configurations occurred in the 1990s–2000s, featuring different combinations of members. All four original members reunited in April 2010 for a benefit in New York, leading to a 2012–2013 theatrical concert production, The Rascals: Once Upon a Dream, produced and directed by Steven Van Zandt and Maureen Van Zandt. Dino Danelli died on December 15, 2022. A revival touring band featuring Cavaliere and Cornish continues as of the mid-2020s.

Scene Connections

  • Atlantic Records — parent label for the full Young Rascals / Rascals run; Tom Dowd and Arif Mardin were staff personnel.
  • Tom Dowd — Atlantic’s chief engineer through the 1960s, present on most of their sessions.
  • Arif Mardin — shifted from supervisor to arranger to co-producer across their Atlantic discography; one of the tightest Mardin relationships in the Atlantic catalog.
  • King Curtis — Atlantic house tenor saxophone player; appears on Once Upon a Dream.
  • Alice Coltrane — appeared on Peaceful World (Columbia, 1971) — harp.
  • David Sanborn — early session appearance on The Island Of Real (1972).
  • Buzzy Feiten — primary guitarist on Columbia period albums.
  • Jimmy Douglass — engineer on Search and Nearness; later a major Atlantic/NYC figure.
  • Cissy Houston — session vocalist, Search and Nearness.
  • The Sweet Inspirations — backing vocals, Search and Nearness.
  • Columbia Records — final two albums; label switch marked the departure from Atlantic sound.

Open Questions

  • Eddie Brigati’s exact departure date from the band — Discogs does not give a precise year; Search and Nearness (1971, Atlantic) credits him as vocalist on several tracks, but he appears absent from the Columbia albums. Web confirmation needed.
  • Exact production credits for Groovin’ (1967) — Discogs credits the band as producer with Mardin as orchestral arranger only; some sources attribute more production involvement to Mardin for this period. Discogs does not resolve this further.
  • Whether any formal production credit for Tom Dowd on the band’s Atlantic albums (beyond engineer) exists — his “Recording Supervisor” credit on the debut and Collections is the closest, but he is not credited as producer on any individual album.
  • Gene Cornish’s exact role evolution in the band — he is credited as “Guitar, Vocals” on the debut but “Vocals, Guitar, Bass” on Collections; whether he permanently shifted to primary bassist at any point needs confirmation.
  • Dino Danelli’s formal billing on the 2012–2013 theatrical reunion — Discogs has sparse detail on the reunion run.
  • Specific chart positions for key singles outside the US — Discogs lacks chart data.
