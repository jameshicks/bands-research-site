Paul Shaffer & The Party Boys Of Rock ‘N’ Roll

Concert and recording persona for Paul Shaffer’s band performing outside the Letterman show context. Discogs artist ID: 3948238

Overview

Paul Shaffer & The Party Boys Of Rock ‘N’ Roll was the name under which Paul Shaffer released his second and final solo album, The World’s Most Dangerous Party (SBK Records, 1993). The project drew on the same core rhythm section as The World’s Most Dangerous Band — the house band for Late Night with David Letterman / Late Show with David Letterman — supplemented by a remarkable roster of celebrity guests recorded in live-performance or live-in-studio conditions across both New York and Los Angeles.

The album is a double CD of classic-rock and R&B covers, bookended by original material, recorded live in concert and mixed and produced throughout by Todd Rundgren. The “Party Boys” concept is essentially a one-time vanity/event project: the band name never released any further recordings after 1993, and the ensemble had no separate touring identity from The World’s Most Dangerous Band.

Label: SBK Records

SBK Records (Discogs ID: 944) was a Capitol Records subsidiary. The World’s Most Dangerous Party was released with catalog number 0 777 7 89530 2 9. Charles Koppelman, an SBK Records executive, is notably among the credited “Featured Party Guests” — an in-joke about the label boss attending the party.

Relationship to The World’s Most Dangerous Band

The World’s Most Dangerous Band (Discogs ID: 877067) was the official name of the Letterman house band. Its Discogs-listed members overlap almost entirely with the Party Boys core: Paul Shaffer, Anton Fig, Sid McGinnis, Will Lee, plus Aaron Heick, David Sanborn, Felicia Collins, Frank Greene (2), Hiram Bullock, and Tom Malone. The World’s Most Dangerous Band later became The CBS Orchestra when the show moved to CBS. The “Party Boys” name was essentially a one-off recording persona for the 1993 album project.

Todd Rundgren’s Role

Todd Rundgren handled all production, engineering, and mixing duties on The World’s Most Dangerous Party, and also played additional guitar on the original track “Jamp.” The sampler promo credits him identically. The Discogs collaborator data shows him credited on 5 releases associated with this project (the album across multiple pressings + the sampler), indicating he was central to the entire release campaign. The album appears to have been recorded live/remote, with Rundgren mixing the results — a typical approach for his production work in the early 1990s.
