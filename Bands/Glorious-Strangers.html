Glorious Strangers

New Wave / Art Rock group, New York City, c. 1980. Discogs artist ID: 443014.

Overview

Glorious Strangers was a New York City group started by Wharton Tiers as a vehicle for his own compositions, principally co-led with vocalist Carol Tiers. Tiers was at the beginning of his production career when the band was active, having recently come off drumming with Theoretical Girls. The group performed live at Danceteria, CBGB’s, and the Mudd Club, and were joined by various NYC musicians for gigs. Billboard described them as “a new band that eschews pretention in favor of the snappy pop song” — a characterization that stood apart from the typical downtown NYC mode of the period. Source: Wharton Tiers Bandcamp, Glorious Strangers album page (accessed 2026-06-08).

Their 1980 7” single “Why Don’t You Join The Army?” / “Media, Media” (TR-03, Theoretical Records) connected them directly to the No Wave circuit. The single’s A-side later served as the title track of the influential 1982 compilation Why Don’t You Join The Army?, documenting the scene. Source: Last.fm, Glorious Strangers biography (accessed 2026-06-08).

In June 1981, Glorious Strangers performed at Noise Fest — the nine-night festival at White Columns (New York City) organized by Thurston Moore, June 16–24, 1981. Their performance was recorded and included on the festival’s companion cassette, Noise Fest (ZG Music, ZG Music 5, released December 15, 1981). The cassette cover was designed by Barbara Ess (also a performer on The Static’s Theoretical Records single). Other performers at Noise Fest included Glenn Branca, Sonic Youth, Lee Ranaldo, Y Pants, Rhys Chatham, Jeffrey Lohn, Ut, and Mofungo. Sources: Wikipedia, Noise Fest (as of 2026-06-08); no longer forgotten music blog, tracklist (accessed 2026-06-08).

The material that became the band’s LP was developed over several years on a four-track reel-to-reel setup, then recorded at Celebration Recording (a jingle house in midtown Manhattan) with engineer Mark Hood, and later at Fun City Studio (eight-track). Wharton contributed mellotron and various house instruments; Carol Tiers provided vocals. The LP was released in 1983/1984 on Fun City Records (Wharton Tiers’ own label). A 40th-anniversary reissue was released July 23, 2022, including most original LP tracks plus two previously unreleased songs from the 1980 single. Music and lyrics published under Whartontime Music (1982, ASCAP). Source: Wharton Tiers Bandcamp, Glorious Strangers album page (accessed 2026-06-08).

Members

  • Wharton Tiers — vocals, all instruments; wrote the A-side and music for B-side; primary composer
  • Carol Tiers — vocals; co-wrote lyrics on B-side “Media, Media”
  • Jan Ewing — keyboards (live and LP lineup)
  • Jean Seton Shaw — bass (live and LP lineup)

Additional NYC musicians joined for live performances.
