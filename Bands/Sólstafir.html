Sólstafir

Sólstafir

Sep 27, 20268 min read

Sólstafir

Discogs ID: 511792 Origin: Reykjavík, Iceland Active: 1995–present Genres: Black Metal (early), Post-Metal, Post-Rock, Stoner/Sludge Rock, Gothic Rock, Viking Metal

Icelandic rock band formed in Reykjavík. Started as a raw black/viking metal act before undergoing a prolonged sonic evolution through sludge, post-rock, and gothic rock, emerging by the late 2000s as one of the most distinctive heavy rock voices in Scandinavia. Fronted throughout by vocalist/guitarist Aðalbjörn Tryggvason (“Addi”), the band is defined by epic long-form songs sung entirely in Icelandic, blending atmospheric grandeur with metal weight.

Biography

Formation and Black Metal Era (1995–2004)

Sólstafir formed in 1995 in Reykjavík. Their earliest release was the self-produced demo Í Norðri (1995), recorded in Iceland and self-released. The following year they released the EP Til Valhallar (1996) on Czech label View Beyond Records — an early work in the viking/black metal vein, with track lyrics sourced in part from Old Norse poets. Core founding members at this stage were Aðalbjörn Tryggvason (guitar, vocals) and Guðmundur Óli Pálmason (drums), alongside early bassist Halldór Einarsson (credited as “Dóri Ölvaldi” / “King Evil (2)”).

In 2002, the band released two significant items: the studio album Í Blóði Og Anda (German label Ars Metalli) and the split release Black Death (Ketzer Records / Neodawn Productions). Í Blóði Og Anda featured the settled lineup of Aðalbjörn (guitar/vocals), Guðmundur Óli Pálmason (drums), and Svavar Austman (bass), engineered by Hörður Óttarsson and produced by the band itself. A promo cassette appeared in 2004 (Svalbard Music Group), marking the end of their early underground phase.

Transition and Spikefarm Era (2005–2010)

Masterpiece Of Bitterness (2005, Spikefarm Records) introduced Sæþór M. Sæþórsson as a second guitarist, establishing the four-piece lineup that would carry them through their genre pivot. The album was co-produced by Aðalbjörn and drummer-turned-coproducer Sigurgrímur Jónsson, who also recorded, engineered, and mixed the record. Mastering was handled by Mika Jussila (Finnvox Studios, Helsinki). The Spikefarm connection placed them within the Finnish metal orbit (Spikefarm was a Spinefarm Records sublabel).

Köld (2008/2009, Spikefarm Records) is widely regarded as the pivot album — the release where the band’s sound decisively broke from black metal into sprawling post-rock and gothic rock territory. Eight tracks, none shorter than four minutes, two over twelve. The Discogs entry lists member credits as “uncredited,” reflecting the band’s deliberate de-emphasis of individual roles. Styles on Discogs: Black Metal, Post Rock, Viking Metal.

Season of Mist and International Breakthrough (2011–2017)

The move to Season of Mist (ID: 33674) marked Sólstafir’s international breakthrough. Svartir Sandar (2011) was a double album with a significant orchestral and choral dimension: Gunnar Ben conducted and arranged a choir; Halldór A. Björnsson contributed electric piano; various guest vocalists including Heiða Eiríksdóttir provided female backing vocals; the Reykjavík choir collective Hljómeyki appeared; and Jón Björn Rikharðsson played gong. Producer was Aðalbjörn; co-producer and mixer was Fredrik Reinedahl (Swedish engineer/mixer). New drummer Hallgrímur Jón Hallgrímsson appeared here as a guest musician and backing vocalist, a transitional credit preceding his full membership. Birgir Jón Birgisson served as assistant engineer.

Ótta (2014) remains the band’s most celebrated record and is often cited as their creative peak. The album added full string arrangements by members of Amiina: Edda Rún Ólafsdóttir, Hildur Ársælsdóttir, María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir, and Sólrún Sumarliðadóttir. Additional textures came from Halldór A. Björnsson (piano) and Bjarni Sigurðarson (banjo). Birgir Jón Birgisson produced and mixed; Andy Jackson mastered; Silli Geirdal co-produced. The band used the phrase “Sólstafir Are” to credit each member’s instrument individually. Photographer Ragnar Axelsson provided imagery.

Berdreyminn (2017) saw the band continue with Season of Mist. Ted Jensen (Sterling Sound) handled mastering — a notable upgrade to one of the most sought-after mastering engineers in rock. Birgir Jón Birgisson remained in the producer/mix role. Jaime Gomez Arellano (Orgone Studios) appeared as a contributor on some pressings.

The 2017 single Silfur Refur and the live release Fjara / Runaway Train (2014, Artoffact Records, Canada) also date from this period. The cover of Soul Asylum’s “Runaway Train” featured Halldór A. Björnsson from the band Legend (32).

Lineup Consolidation and Century Media (2020–present)

By Endless Twilight Of Codependent Love (2020, Season of Mist), Hallgrímur Jón Hallgrímsson (credited as “Hallgrímur Bárðdal”) was fully established as drummer. Birgir Jón Birgisson produced, mixed, and mastered. Guest contributions included Viggi Daða (backing vocals, track 2), Jóhann Richardsson (“Jói Motorhead Richardson”, percussion, track 2), Davíð Þór Jónsson (piano, track 8), and Helena Dumell (violin, track 3). Cover art was an archival painting by Victorian illustrator Johann Baptist Zwecker.

Hin Helga Kvöl (2024) marked a label change to Century Media (ID: 10956). The album was engineered by Jóhann Rúnar Þorgeirsson, who also co-produced alongside Aðalbjörn. Mixing was by Fredrik Reinedahl (returning from Svartir Sandar). Mastering by Sigurdór Guðmundsson. Notable guests: Sigurjón Kjartansson (vocals, track 9); Erna Hrönn Ólafsdóttir (female vocals, track 5); Borgar Magnason (double bass soundscape, track 6); Jens Hansson (saxophone); Halldór A. Björnsson (piano). Additional engineering by Úlfur Hansson.

Connections

  • Amiina — Reykjavík chamber quartet; all four members contributed strings to Ótta
  • Birgir Jón Birgisson — producer/engineer across four albums; central to the Season of Mist sound
  • Guðmundur Óli Pálmason — founding drummer; also active as visual artist under “Kuggur” and in Katla.
  • Aðalbjörn Tryggvason — also active in Bastarður (Icelandic black metal supergroup)
  • Kukl — Reykjavík post-punk/experimental scene that predates Sólstafir; part of the same Icelandic underground lineage
  • múm — contemporary Icelandic post-rock/electronic act; overlapping Reykjavík scene
  • Sigur Rós — Reykjavík post-rock, shared Icelandic identity; several Amiina members crossed over
