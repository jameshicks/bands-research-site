From Discogs

Ike Yard was founded in 1979 in New York by Stuart Argabright (percussion/synthesizer/vocals), Kenny Compton (bass/vocals), Fred Szymanski (percussion/synthesizers) & Michael Diekmann (guitar). This lineup released one EP on Les Disques Du Crepuscule and a self-titled LP on Factory America before dissolving in 1983. The band reformed in 2007 as a three-piece consisting of Argabright, Compton and Diekmann, and started releasing new music beginning with the Öst EP in 2009.

History

Dissolution (1983)

The band dissolved at the beginning of 1983. The primary cause was Stuart Argabright’s relocation to West Berlin; according to the Bandcamp Daily retrospective, “The band split up soon after when Argabright moved to West Berlin.” Label indifference from Factory America is cited as a contributing factor: Argabright is quoted as saying “we never heard from Factory again” after the self-titled LP was released in 1982. There is no evidence in any source that Factory America’s closure directly triggered the split, nor any indication of an internal band conflict; the dissolution appears to have been driven by Argabright’s departure geographically rather than a label collapse or creative dispute. Factory America did cease operations around this period, but no source establishes a causal link between that and the band ending.

Overview

Ike Yard formed in New York in 1979, the original four-piece of Stuart Argabright, Kenny Compton, Fred Szymanski, and Michael Diekmann releasing an EP on Les Disques Du Crépuscule and a self-titled LP on Factory America before dissolving in 1983. The band reformed in 2007 as a trio — Argabright, Compton, and Diekmann — and sustained a prolific second run through the 2010s, issuing seven releases across Desire Records, Blackest Ever Black, and affiliated imprints. Their work spans experimental, darkwave, minimal, and electro territory, with close engineering and production ties to David Ruffo and Paul Geluso across the catalogue’s later phase.
