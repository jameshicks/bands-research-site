History

Formation (1978–1979)

The Waitresses grew out of the fertile Akron, Ohio new wave scene of the late 1970s, the same milieu that produced Tin Huey and Devo. The project was the vehicle of guitarist and songwriter Chris Butler (born Christopher Butler), who had been a member of Tin Huey. After that group’s major-label run with Chris Spedding and CBS ended inconclusively, Butler assembled The Waitresses as a looser collective built around his sardonic, character-driven songs.

Their earliest recorded appearance was the 1978 local compilation In “Short Stack” (Clone Records), the Akron new wave document that also featured Devo and others. The group’s defining move was placing a female vocalist — Patty Donahue — front and center on Butler’s material: narratives in first-person female voices, sharp social observation, often deadpan or aggressively self-aware.

”I Know What Boys Like” and ZE Records (1980)

The breakthrough single “I Know What Boys Like” (1980) appeared first on Polydor (US) and simultaneously/shortly after on ZE Records / Antilles (ZE’s Island distribution). Produced by Kurt Munkacsi — the engineer and co-producer most associated with Philip Glass and the Philip Glass Ensemble — the single established the band’s sound: minimal post-punk guitar, Patty Donahue’s nasal, know-it-all vocals, and lyrics that dissected gender games with equal parts menace and comedy. It became one of the more enduring singles of the early 1980s US new wave moment.

A second 1980 single, “No Guilt (It Wasn’t The End Of The World),” followed on Polydor.

Wasn’t Tomorrow Wonderful? (1982)

The debut album was released on Polydor in 1982 with 22 pressings across territories (US, UK, France, Germany, Netherlands, Japan, Canada, Australia). Co-produced by Butler and Munkacsi. The core band tracked: Billy Ficca (drums), Tracy Wormworth and David Hofstra (bass), Dan Klayman (keyboards), Mars Williams (reeds), Patty Donahue (vocals), with Ariel Warner on backing vocals. Two tracks feature different drummers: “No Guilt” and the album version of “I Know What Boys Like” used Don Christensen and Stuart Austin respectively, with Ralph Carney contributing reeds on both. Tape-loop contributions from Kramer (Mark Kramer) appear on “Jimmy Tomorrow.”

“Christmas Wrapping” — an a cappella-driven holiday track credited to The Waitresses — was issued as a single in 1981/1982 on ZE Records/Island (UK) and became a perennial holiday radio fixture far outpacing the band’s other releases in terms of longevity.

The album also produced the “Square Pegs” theme: the band contributed music to the CBS sitcom of the same name, and the EP I Could Rule The World If I Could Only Get The Parts (1982, Polydor) collected associated material.

Bruiseology (1983) and dissolution

The second album, Bruiseology (1983, Polydor), marked a shift in production — Hugh Padgham (producer of Police, Peter Gabriel, Genesis) took over alongside Butler, with Michael Frondelli and Ed Garcia handling remix engineering. Mars Williams remained on saxophone; Billy Ficca on drums; Tracy Wormworth on bass; Dan Klayman on organ. Management had shifted to Q Prime Inc. (Cliff Burnstein and Peter Mensch’s company, then also managing Metallica and Def Leppard). The album and its “Make The Weather” / “Bread And Butter” singles saw moderate release.

Patty Donahue was diagnosed with lung cancer shortly after Bruiseology. She died on December 2, 1996, aged 40. The band had effectively dissolved in 1984.

Legacy

The Best of the Waitresses was issued on Polydor in 1990 and reissued in Canada in 2003. “Christmas Wrapping” has remained in rotation every holiday season since 1982, ensuring the band’s ongoing discovery. ZE Records’ revival under Michel Esteban produced multiple reissue/compilation releases from 2003 onward, keeping the catalog in print. A complete career set, Just Desserts: The Complete Waitresses, has also been issued.
