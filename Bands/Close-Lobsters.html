Close Lobsters

Indie/jangle-pop band from Paisley, Scotland. Discogs artist ID: 294318. Name variations: The Close Lobsters, CloseLobsters. Active 1983–c.1990; reformed 2013; still recording as of 2024.

Signed to Fire Records from 1986. Associated with the C86 scene and the broader Scottish post-punk / indie landscape alongside The Pastels and Teenage Fanclub. Their US releases came out on Enigma (4); later reformation-era records appeared on Shelflife Records and Last Night From Glasgow.

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1983–1986)

Close Lobsters formed in Paisley, Scotland in 1983, though their first recorded output did not appear until 1986. The band’s sound drew on the post-punk and jangle traditions of the period — trebly, chiming guitars over propulsive rhythms, with melodic but somewhat literary vocal delivery from Andrew Burnett. They are most frequently cited in connection with the C86 cassette / NME indie movement of the mid-1980s, which grouped together a wave of UK independent guitar bands.

Their debut single, Going To Heaven To See If It Rains b/w Boys And Girls, appeared on Fire Records in 1986 (cat. BLAZE 15). The engineer on that session was Scott Rodger (Discogs ID: 261865).

Foxheads Stalk This Land and UK Singles (1987)

Their debut album Foxheads Stalk This Land followed in 1987, produced by John A. Rivers and engineered by Francisco Cabeza (Discogs: 475109), with John A. Rivers also contributing Hammond organ. The album was released in the US on Enigma (4) (D1-73333) and in the UK on Fire Records (FIRE LP9). Mastering credits on the US pressing went to Graeme Durham (Discogs: 399393).

Songwriting on Foxheads was shared across the band, with credits split between various pairs and subgroups of members: Andrew Burnett and Graeme Wilmington co-wrote several tracks; Burnett and Tom Donnelly collaborated on others; and a handful of tracks (“Foxheads”, “Mother Of God”, “Pathetique”) involved three or more members.

Two UK singles preceded or accompanied the album:

  • Never Seen Before (BLAZE 20T, 1987) — produced by Pascal Gabriel (Discogs: 40406); instrumented lineup listed as Wilmington (guitar), Donnelly (guitar), Bob Burnett (bass), McFayden (drums), Andrew Burnett (vocals), with additional vocals from Anne Hamilton (2) (Discogs: 1375428). The B-side “Wide Waterways” credits Peter Perrett (Discogs: 337518) as writer — a cover of the Only Ones songwriter. Photography by Grahame Bent (Discogs: 2387758).
  • Let’s Make Some Plans (BLAZE 22T, 1987) — a three-track 12”; the A-side and B1 produced by John A. Rivers, B2 (“Get What They Deserve”) self-produced by the band.

BBC Session and What Is There To Smile About? (1988)

In 1988 the band recorded The Janice Long Session for BBC Radio, released as a 12” EP on Strange Fruit / Nighttracks (SFNT 008). The session was produced by Barry Anthony Andrews (Discogs: 572170) and engineered by Martin Colley (Discogs: 267330). Tracks: “Nothing Really Matters”, “Heaven…Rains”, “Pathetic Trivia”, “Never Seen Before”.

Also in 1988 they issued What Is There To Smile About?, a six-track mini-album on CD (Enigma 7 73342-2 / Fire Records), produced by John A. Rivers. Members are all five core members; sleeve design by Rudy Tuesday (Discogs: 1931468).

Headache Rhetoric (1989)

The second full album Headache Rhetoric appeared in 1989 on Enigma (4) (7 73521-2). Production credit went to Phil Vinall (Discogs: 227231); engineering by Howard Turner (Discogs: 330518); mastered by Tony Cousins (Discogs: 112836) at Townhouse. All five band members are credited individually: Andrew Burnett (vocals, backing vocals), Bob Burnett (bass guitar), Graeme Wilmington (guitar, backing vocals), Tom Donnelly (guitar), Stewart McFayden (drums, percussion, backing vocals). Songwriting credits shared across all five. Front sleeve artwork by Ashley R. Cook (Discogs: 5472469).

The Nature Thing single (BLAZE 34CD / Fire Records, 1989) also came from this period, a four-track CD single produced by Phil Vinall and engineered by Howard Turner. It included covers of Neil Young (“Hey Hey, My My (Into The Black)”) and Leonard Cohen / Phil Spector (“Paper Thin Hotel”), plus a live version of “Never Seen Before”.

Hiatus and Compilations (1990–2008)

The band dissolved around 1990. Two compilations on Fire Records document the original run:

  • Forever, Until Victory! The Singles Collection (2009, Fire Records) — compiled the singles catalogue; released in both UK and Japan (the latter jointly with Diffuse Echo Records).
  • Foxheads Stalk This Land / Headache Rhetoric (2010, Fire Records) — reissue of both albums on one CD.

Reformation (2013–present)

Close Lobsters reformed around 2013. Their comeback releases came out on US label Shelflife Records:

  • Kunstwerk In Spacetime EP (LIFE112, 2014, Shelflife Records) — 7” EP, two tracks (“Now Time”, “New York City In Space”); recorded by Colin Cameron (2) (Discogs: 5156311); John A. Rivers contributed remix. Backing vocals by Sharon Bain and Summer Bain (Discogs: 8017903, 8017904). Wilmington and Andrew Burnett split lead guitar duties between the two tracks.
  • Desire And Signs EP (LIFE136, 2016, Shelflife Records) — 7” EP; produced and remixed by John A. Rivers; recorded, mixed, and engineered by Colin Cameron (2). Sleeve photography by Judi Milne (Discogs: 5156310). Presses by Pirates Press.

The 2015 box set Firestation Towers 1986–1989 (FIRELP397, Fire Records) collected all three original releases in a 3-LP set, with the singles collection as a third disc remastered/re-assembled by Andrew Burnett and Graeme Wilmington.

In 2019 the band released Post Neo Anti : Arte Povera In The Forest Of Symbols on Last Night From Glasgow (LNFG33), their first new full-length of the reunion era.

Their most recent release is the Stepping Across 12” (LNFG169, 2024, Last Night From Glasgow), engineered by John A. Rivers — now styled as shoegaze/indie rock on Discogs.
