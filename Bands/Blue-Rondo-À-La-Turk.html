Blue Rondo À La Turk

UK Latin-funk / jazz-pop group, London, 1981–1984. Discogs artist ID: 88575

Overview

Blue Rondo À La Turk formed out of the London Blitz club scene in 1981, taking their name from a Dave Brubeck Quartet composition. The group was built around frontman and style icon Chris Sullivan (2), and at peak numbered around twelve members — a deliberately multicultural assembly bringing together musicians with Brazilian, Afro-Cuban, South African, Greek Cypriot, and British backgrounds. Their sound fused Afro-Cuban rhythms, Brazilian samba, salsa, and hard-bop jazz with the energy and attitude of post-punk. Their theatrical presentation (outfits, make-up, theatrical staging) was as much a part of the identity as the music itself.

The group signed to Dindisc, a Virgin Records subsidiary then home to OMD and Martha and the Muffins. Their debut single “Me and Mr. Sanchez” (1981) reached the UK charts and announced a fresh direction in British dance music. Their sole studio LP, Chewing The Fat (1982), spread production duties across Mike Chapman, Clive Langer & Alan Winstanley, Godley & Creme, and Pete Wingfield. The group dissolved in 1984; a 1986 compilation on Virgin (Too Soon To Come) collected the catalog. Key members Mark Reilly and Kito Poncioni went directly into Matt Bianco, which had a lengthy international career on WEA. Segona Peter Tholo (trumpet) had roots in the South African exile jazz scene and continued with multiple jazz ensembles.
