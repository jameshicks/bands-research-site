Phoenix

Discogs ID: 2269 Members: Thomas Mars, Deck D’Arcy, Christian Mazzalai, Laurent Brancowitz Active: 1997–present (first release 1997; years on Discogs: 1991–2025) Origin: Versailles, France Genres: Indie Pop, Indie Rock, French Pop, Electronic Labels: Ghettoblaster (own); Source / Virgin (2000–2006); V2 / Loyauté (2009); Glassnote (2) / Loyauté (2013–present)

French rock band from Versailles who emerged from the same circle as AIR, blending crisp guitar pop with European electronic influences. After a cult early period on Source, they broke internationally with Wolfgang Amadeus Phoenix (2009), winning a Grammy for Best Alternative Music Album. They have released eight studio albums across a quarter-century and remain one of the most consistently successful French rock exports.

Biography

Formation and early releases (1995–1999)

The band traces to a Versailles social scene in the mid-1990s. Thomas Croquet (vocals; performs as Thomas Mars, Discogs ID 550176), Frédéric Moulin (bass; performs as Deck D’Arcy, Discogs ID 550174), and Christian Mazzalai (guitar, Discogs ID 550177) were an early trio. Christian’s older brother Laurent Mazzalai (guitar/keyboards; performs as Laurent Brancowitz, Discogs ID 550175) joined after his previous band Darlin’ dissolved around 1995. Darlin’ had included Thomas Bangalter and Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo — who would go on to form Daft Punk.

The four-piece adopted the name Phoenix around 1997 and self-released a debut 7” — Party Time / City Lights (1997) — on their own imprint Ghettoblaster, cat no. Ghetto01. A second self-released single, Heat Wave (1999), pushed toward French house territory and attracted attention from the Paris label Source.

Through Source they became acquainted with label-mates AIR (Jean-Benoît Dunckel and Nicolas Godin, also from Versailles). Thomas Mars sang on AIR’s “Playground Love” (from The Virgin Suicides OST, 2000) under the pseudonym Gordon Tracks.

United and the Source / Virgin period (2000–2008)

Phoenix signed to Source (distributed by Virgin) and released their debut album United in 2000 (Source/Virgin, cat no. CDVIR107). The album was produced by Phoenix with Alex Firla and Stéphane “Alf” Briat, and mixed in large part by Philippe Zdar. It featured an unusually wide cast of session players — clavinet from Rob (Robin Coudert), synthesizer on “Embuscade” from Thomas Bangalter, strings arranged by Hugo Ferran, and percussion from Cubain (Cub1). Lead single “If I Ever Feel Better” became a slow-burn hit.

The band took a lengthy hiatus before returning with Alphabetical (2004, Source/Virgin). Co-produced with Tony Hoffer, it shifted toward tighter indie-rock — straighter arrangements, less house influence — with guest bass from Pino Palladino on several tracks, and artwork/photography by fashion designer Hedi Slimane. It was the last album to use the Source imprint alongside Virgin.

It’s Never Been Like That (2006, Virgin) continued the guitar-pop direction and marked the arrival of Thomas Hedlund as the band’s primary session drummer, a role he would maintain through the next several albums. Engineered and mixed almost entirely by Julien Delfaud, with mastering by Brian Gardner. A live album, LIVE! ThirtyDaysAgo (2004, Source), documented the band’s touring circa Alphabetical.

Wolfgang Amadeus Phoenix and breakthrough (2009)

Wolfgang Amadeus Phoenix (2009) was released on V2 / Cooperative Music / Loyauté. Produced by Phoenix and Philippe Zdar, with drums sampled and played by Thomas Hedlund, flute by Cedric Plancy, marimba by Rob (Robin Coudert), and engineering by Julien Delfaud. Singles “1901” and “Lisztomania” broke through internationally, aided by heavy blog-era exposure. The album won the Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album at the 52nd Grammy Awards (2010). Remixes were released through Kitsuné Music and Glassnote Entertainment Group.

Bankrupt! and Glassnote era (2013–2017)

The band moved to Glassnote (2) for Bankrupt! (2013), again produced with Philippe Zdar. Thomas Hedlund continued on drums; engineer Laurent D’Herbécourt joined as a key crew member. Remixes commissioned from Sleigh Bells and Dinosaur Jr. were released as standalone singles.

Ti Amo (2017, Glassnote/Loyauté) was an Italian-inflected concept album recorded largely in analogue. Co-produced with Pierrick Devin, it sampled Chris & Cosey’s “Lovelife” and Fela Kuti on separate tracks. Philippe Zdar received a credit for “Special Guidance By.” Gesaffelstein contributed to “Fior Di Latte.” Mastering split between Alex Gopher and Joe LaPorta.

Alpha Zulu and later work (2022–present)

Alpha Zulu (2022, Glassnote/Loyauté) featured Ezra Koenig of Vampire Weekend on “Tonight” and brought in Manny Marroquin and Ariel Rechtshaid as additional mixing/production collaborators. Blood Orange (Dev Hynes) appeared on a bonus track (“Winter Solstice”). Angèle collaborated on the 2024 single “Nightcall” (Loyauté/Record Makers). An Odyssey EP (2023, Capitol Records) included a collaboration with Beck. Thomas Mars and Sofia Coppola (daughter of Francis Ford Coppola; director) have been a couple since the early 2000s; the personal connection runs through the Versailles/AIR/Source network, though Discogs records no direct Sofia Coppola film-credit release in Phoenix’s name beyond the general cultural association.
