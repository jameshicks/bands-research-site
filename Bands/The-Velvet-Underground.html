The Velvet Underground

Rock band from New York City, active 1964–1973 (with a 1993 reunion). Discogs artist ID: 39766.

Proto-punk and avant-rock pioneers; co-founded by Lou Reed and John Cale, with Sterling Morrison and Maureen Tucker. Often cited as one of the most influential groups in rock history — inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996.

Formation and Early Years (1964–1966)

The Velvet Underground grew out of the New York avant-garde scene. Lou Reed (born Lewis Allan Reed, 2 March 1942) and John Cale (born John Davies Cale, 9 March 1942) met through the experimental music milieu around La Monte Young and the Theatre Of Eternal Music, where Cale had been a member alongside Angus MacLise and Tony Conrad. Reed brought a background in pop songwriting — including work at Pickwick Records — and a lyrical sensibility focused on street life, drugs, sexuality, and alienation.

The band coalesced in 1964–65 under the name The Velvet Underground (after a Michael Leigh paperback), with Reed and Cale joined by guitarist Sterling Morrison (born Holmes Sterling Morrison Jr., 29 August 1942) and eventually drummer Maureen Tucker (born Maureen Ann Tucker, 26 August 1944). Their early drummer was Angus MacLise, a percussionist with ties to Theatre Of Eternal Music, who left before the band began paid performances. Aliases from this early period include The Falling Spikes and The Warlocks.

The Warhol Period (1966–1967)

In late 1965, Andy Warhol became the band’s manager and de facto producer, bringing them into his Factory scene and adding German model and chanteuse Nico (born Christa Päffgen) as a featured vocalist. Warhol’s involvement gave the band visibility and resources; he arranged recording sessions and designed the iconic banana sleeve for their debut.

Tom Wilson (2) (Thomas Blanchard Wilson Jr.) handled technical production duties. The album appeared on Verve under MGM’s distribution structure.

Nico appears only on four tracks; the Discogs profile notes she “was only a fleeting member, merely contributing vocals to 4 songs.”

Engineers: Val Valentin (director of engineering), Gary Kellgren (recording). Cover design by Acy Lehman with concept by Andy Warhol. Photography by Billy Name.

A deliberately abrasive, confrontational record — notably including “Sister Ray” (17+ minutes). Marked the full departure from Warhol’s management and a sharper turn toward noise and drone. It was the last VU album with John Cale.

The Cale Departure and Self-Titled Album (1968–1969)

John Cale left (or was pushed out) in 1968, replaced by Doug Yule (born Douglas Alan Yule), recommended by Boston-scene musician Jonathan Richman. Yule brought a more conventional rock musicianship and later sang lead on some tracks.

The Velvet Underground (1969)

Label: MGM Records (SE-4617) · Released: 1969 Producer: The Velvet Underground (self-produced)

Credits on the 1969 MGM pressing are minimal: the band self-arranged, self-produced, and self-conducted. Engineer: Val Valentin (director of engineering). Photography by Billy Name. The “closet mix” story — that the album was mixed in a closet at Reed’s request for an intimate, close sound — is not confirmed from Discogs metadata.

Tracks include “Candy Says,” “Pale Blue Eyes,” “Jesus,” “Beginning to See the Light,” “I’m Set Free,” “That’s the Story of My Life,” “The Murder Mystery,” “After Hours.”

A quieter, more introspective record; often called the band’s most accessible. Self-produced without Warhol or an outside producer.

Loaded and Lou Reed’s Departure (1970)

Tucker was absent for much of the Loaded sessions (maternity leave); Bill Yule (Doug’s brother) filled in on drums.

Contains “Sweet Jane” and “Rock and Roll.” Reed left the band before the album was released, reportedly unhappy with edits made without his consent. Loaded was designed for commercial radio, the most hook-driven VU record.

Post-Reed Years and Squeeze (1970–1973)

After Reed’s departure, Doug Yule effectively led the band through 1970–1973, adding Walter Powers on bass and Willie Alexander on keyboards. The band recorded Live at Max’s Kansas City (1972, Atlantic) — a cassette recording by Brigid Polk of the final Reed-era show.

Squeeze (1972)

Label: Polydor · Released: 1972 (Germany) Produced and arranged by: The Velvet Underground (Yule) Written by: Doug Yule

A Doug Yule solo effort in all but name — no other original members appear. Considered the nadir of the band’s identity. Discogs credits confirm Yule as sole songwriter and producer.

1993 Reunion

The classic lineup — Reed, Cale, Morrison, Tucker — reunited in 1993 for a European tour.

Live MCMXCIII (1993)

Labels: Sire / Warner Bros. Records · Released: 1993
