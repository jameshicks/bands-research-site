Soft Heap

Discogs ID: 299215 Active: 1978–1989 (with 1983–1989 as live-only; final European concerts culminating in 1989) Origin: UK Genre: Canterbury scene, jazz-rock, free improvisation

Overview

Soft Heap was a Canterbury scene supergroup formed in January 1978, drawing its initial membership from alumni of Soft Machine. The name is a double pun: “Soft” nods to Soft Machine, while “Heap” is an acronym of the four founding members’ first names — Hugh, Elton, Alan, and Pip. A further layer references Hatfield And The North, whose drummer Pip Pyle was one of those four, making the name a portmanteau of both parent bands.

The band’s direction was described as “very jazzy and improvisational,” rooted in the modal jazz-rock language the members had developed through Soft Machine and related projects. The original quartet released one studio LP in 1979. Personnel shifts — Hopper replaced by John Greaves, then Gowen’s death in 1981 bringing in Mark Hewins on guitar — produced a second, quite different unit that continued touring through the 1980s and recorded a live cassette release in 1989. Archival recordings from this later period have been released in small runs by Hewins himself from 2020 onward.

The Name Explained

The founding lineup — Hugh Hopper, Elton Dean, Alan Gowen, Pip Pyle — provided the initialism HEAP. Combined with “Soft” (from Soft Machine, the band three of the four came from), the name anchors the group firmly in Canterbury lineage. When John Greaves and Mark Hewins joined, Hewins noted that the name could have changed: “John Greaves and I were given the opportunity to change the name of the group when we joined, but we all decided that in respect of Alan Gowen’s memory we would keep the HEAP moniker… It could have been… Soft JEMP!” The acronym was thus retained as a memorial to Gowen after his death in 1981.

History

Formation and the Soft HeaD Detour (1978)

Soft Heap formed in January 1978 with Hopper, Dean, Gowen, and Pyle. For the first tour, Pyle was unavailable due to his commitments with National Health, and drummer Dave Sheen deputised. The band operated under the name Soft Head for this period — the name shift dropping the final “p” to reflect Pip’s absence. The Soft Head lineup (Hopper, Dean, Gowen, Sheen) recorded the album Rogue Element (1978, Ogun; released 1996), which documented that tour.

Soft Heap LP (1979)

With Pip Pyle back in the fold, the full HEAP lineup recorded the studio album Soft Heap (1979, Charly Records, CRL 5014). This is the definitive document of the original quartet: modal, spacious jazz-rock driven by Gowen’s electric piano and synthesizer, Dean’s saxophone, Hopper’s bass, and Pyle’s drums. All compositions are by band members, split across Gowen, Dean, and Hopper.

The album was also released in Japan in 1982 (Charly Records / Seven Seas) and has been reissued multiple times — by Spalax Music (France, 1995), Esoteric Recordings (UK, 2009), Belle Antique (Japan, 2017), and Charly Records again in 2025.

Personnel Changes and Alan Gowen’s Death (1979–1981)

After the studio album, Hugh Hopper departed the group. John Greaves (formerly of Henry Cow and also active in National Health) joined on bass. This gave the group a new character — Greaves brought a different harmonic sensibility and also contributed voice and organ on the later A Veritable Centaur recording.

Alan Gowen died of leukemia on 17 May 1981, aged 33. His death was a decisive rupture. Rather than dissolve, the remaining members — Dean, Pyle, and Greaves — chose to continue and invited Mark Hewins to join on guitar. The decision to retain the Soft Heap name was a deliberate act of memorial.

1980s Live Activity and A Veritable Centaur (1982, released 1995)

The post-Gowen lineup (Dean, Pyle, Greaves, Hewins) toured Europe throughout the 1980s — four tours with approximately 25 European concerts across the decade. No studio recordings were released during this period.

A 1982 recording was eventually released as A Veritable Centaur (1995, Impetus Records, IMP CD 18219). The set draws on Gowen compositions as well as Dean originals, and features a guest appearance on track 3 (“Space Funk”) by Alain Eckert on synthesizer guitar.

The live cassette Live (1989, Musart Services) captured a two-sided improvised performance and documented the final phase of the group’s activity. The climax was a concert at the Coutances Festival (France, 1989) in a circus tent, recorded and broadcast live by FR1 Radio.

Archive Releases (2008, 2020)

Al Dente (2008, Reel Recordings, RR 008) is an unusual release: it documents the original quartet (Hopper, Dean, Gowen, Pyle) performing material that became Soft Heap staples, apparently from live recordings. This gave the founding lineup its own separate release document for the first time since the 1979 LP.

In 2020, Mark Hewins self-released a series of archival recordings documenting the 1980s lineup: Soft Heap Lives: Dunois Paris 1982, Paris December 1982 (Dunois 1st Day, 1st Set), Soft Heap Lives: Ferme Nord 1982, and Soft Heap Lives: Dunois Paris 1988 — all on his own imprint as FLAC downloads.

Related Group: Soft Head

Soft Head (Discogs ID: 3348772) was the temporary variant name for Soft Heap during its first tour in 1978, when Pip Pyle was unavailable. Dave Sheen deputised on drums. The lineup — Hopper, Dean, Gowen, Sheen — recorded Rogue Element (1978, Ogun; released 1996 as OGCD 013). The name Soft HeaD reflected the missing “p” for Pip.

Member Backgrounds and Canterbury Context

Hugh Hopper (Discogs ID: 235031) — began in Wilde Flowers (the Canterbury scene progenitor also featuring Robert Wyatt and Kevin Ayers) and joined Soft Machine for their most celebrated run, including Third (1970). A distinctive bassist with a fuzz-heavy, modal sound. Left Soft Heap around 1979; continued in numerous other Canterbury-adjacent formations including Soft Works and Soft Machine Legacy.

Elton Dean (Discogs ID: 235033) — the longest-serving Soft Heap member, present throughout the band’s entire run. Came to prominence in Soft Machine (Third, 1970), where his alto saxophone playing on extended modal pieces defined the band’s jazz period. Also worked extensively with Keith Tippett, Chris McGregor’s Brotherhood Of Breath, and ran several of his own ensembles (Ninesense, Newsense). Died 2006.

Alan Gowen (Discogs ID: 299211, born 19 August 1947, died 17 May 1981) — keyboardist best known for Gilgamesh (2) and National Health. Gowen was the primary composer on the 1979 LP (three of six tracks sole-credited to him). His death from leukemia at 33 cut short a major Canterbury voice; the band’s decision to keep the HEAP name was a direct memorial.

Pip Pyle (Discogs ID: 277014, born 4 April 1950, died 28 August 2006) — one of the most prolific Canterbury scene drummers. Joined Gong in the early 1970s, then was central to Hatfield And The North (1973–1975) and National Health (1975 onward). His death during a Paris tour in 2006 — in the same city as his first major Canterbury engagements — was noted throughout the scene.

John Greaves (Discogs ID: 65788) — bassist and vocalist from Henry Cow, the British avant-rock collective led by Fred Frith and Tim Hodgkinson. Greaves’s Henry Cow background gave him a more compositional, formally rigorous approach than the Soft Machine veterans; he also participated in National Health. His solo work (notably Songs, 1995) has been championed by listeners across the Canterbury and post-RIO circuits.

Mark Hewins (Discogs ID: 249461) — electric guitarist who joined after Gowen’s death in 1981. A multi-project player associated with Gong, various Canterbury continuation ensembles, and his own work. Served as de facto archivist for the later Soft Heap recordings, releasing the 2020 live documents himself and contributing liner notes and photography to A Veritable Centaur.

Connections

  • Soft Machine — Hopper, Dean, and Gowen all passed through Soft Machine; the “Soft” in the name is a direct citation
  • Hatfield And The North — Pip Pyle was Hatfield’s drummer; the “HEAP” acronym nods here
  • National Health — Alan Gowen, Pip Pyle, and John Greaves all had National Health connections; their concurrent activity created scheduling conflicts during Soft Heap’s formation
  • Soft Head — temporary Pip-less variant, recorded Rogue Element (1978)
  • Gilgamesh (2) — Alan Gowen’s other main 1970s vehicle, alongside National Health
  • Henry Cow — John Greaves’s pre-Soft Heap band; different avant-garde tradition
  • Wilde Flowers — Hugh Hopper’s Canterbury origin point
  • Ogun — released the Soft Head archival material
  • Charly Records — released the 1979 LP and the National Health Of Queues and Cures the same year
  • Impetus Records — released A Veritable Centaur (1995)
