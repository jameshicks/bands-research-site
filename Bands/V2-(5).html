V2 (5)

Discogs ID: 721873

Manchester post-punk/glam-punk band, formed December 1976, disbanded 1980. Post-Panik offshoot: members included Ian Nance and Steve Brotherdale — both previously in The Panik (1977). The “(5)” disambiguator distinguishes them from other acts named V2 on Discogs.

Lineup

Full lineup across their active period (boredteenagers.co.uk, punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com):

Dec 1976 – Mar 1977 (founding core):

  • Mark Standley (aka Rev. P.P. Smythe) — guitar, vocals
  • David Wilks (aka Jonathan E / Jonathon E) — vocals

Mar 1977 – Oct 1978:

  • Mark Standley — guitar, vocals
  • David Wilks — vocals
  • Steve Brotherdale (aka B’Dale) — drums (ex-The Panik, ex-Warsaw)
  • Stan “The Man” Matthews — bass

Oct 1978 – 1980:

  • Ian Nance — lead guitar, vocals (ex-The Panik, joined late 1978 at Brotherdale’s urging)

Later reformations (not primary scope):

  • Phil Pope — vocals (1996 reformation)
  • Eddie Mooney — guitar/vocals (2001 onwards)

Scene Context & Origins

Standley and Wilks were regulars at the Pips nightclub Bowie/Roxy room before forming V2; Brotherdale joined after a chance encounter on a bus. The band emerged alongside The Fall, Buzzcocks, Warsaw, and Slaughter and the Dogs from the early Manchester punk circuit, notably the Electric Circus. They were considered for the Short Circuit: Live at the Electric Circus (1978) compilation but were dropped; sources differ — one says they were excluded for being “too inept,” another says they simply did not appear (punk77.co.uk, punkygibbon.co.uk). Paul Morley reviewed their debut EP, describing “stripped, effective, trebly punk” with a “garish musical homage” to glam influences and calling their stage presence “very flash, and very imposing” (boredteenagers.co.uk).

Manchester Scene Connections

Labels

  • Bent Records — released V2’s debut EP “Speed Freak / Nothing To Do / That’s It” (1977/early 1978). Reportedly pressed in 800 black vinyl and 7,000 red vinyl copies. Sold out immediately.
  • TJM Records — released “Man In The Box / When The World Isn’t There” (1978). Reportedly sold ~8,000 copies in three months and reached number one in the alternative chart. TJM also released material by other early Manchester acts.
  • Groove Records — released “Gee Whizz It’s You / Face In The Crowd” (1979/1980; reportedly withdrawn). Catalog number: ZOE 1 (per Discogs).

Venues

Confirmed performance venues (punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com, boredteenagers.co.uk, punk77.co.uk):

  • Electric Circus, Manchester — early scene hub; V2 performed here, including a documented show 1 October 1977
  • Russell Club, Manchester
  • Rafters, Manchester
  • Free Trade Hall (Lesser Free Trade Hall area), Manchester
  • Middleton Civic Hall / Middleton Hall
  • Wythenshawe Forum, Manchester — with Slaughter and the Dogs (1977)
  • The Marquee, London — with Slaughter and the Dogs (1978)
  • Eric’s, Liverpool
  • Boardwalk, Manchester (1985 reunion show)

Associated Bands & People

The Panik — direct predecessor band for both Brotherdale and Nance. The Panik formed June 1977 in Prestwich, managed by Rob Gretton (who later managed Joy Division and co-founded Factory Records). The Panik’s manager connection gives V2 an indirect link to the Factory axis. (punk77.co.uk/band/the-panik)

Warsaw (later Joy Division) — Steve Brotherdale drummed briefly for Warsaw between Tony Tabac and Stephen Morris. Warsaw fired Brotherdale — reportedly pulling over after a studio session and leaving him on the roadside — after he allegedly tried to recruit Ian Curtis into The Panik. (faroutmagazine.co.uk, sonicmoremusic.wordpress.com)

Slaughter and the Dogs — V2 performed frequent support slots with them; shared Wythenshawe Forum (1977) and Marquee (1978) dates. (boredteenagers.co.uk)

The Drones — Mark Standley joined the reformed Drones in 1997; the reformed V2/Drones also toured America together in 1998. (punkygibbon.co.uk)

The Nosebleeds — cited in contemporaneous sources as a V2 scene connection; at least one member shared history with V2 according to punk77.co.uk.

The Earwigs — post-V2 project formed ~1981 by Mark Standley, with ex-V2 drummer Steve Brotherdale switching to lead vocals, and Hugh O’Boyle (ex-Victim) on bass. Standley and Wilks also worked together in Earwigs. (punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com)

Fast Cars — Steve Brotherdale drummed for Fast Cars May–July 1979, between leaving V2 and forming The Earwigs. (punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com)

Rob Gretton — managed The Panik (the immediate precursor to V2’s post-Panik lineup). Gretton went on to manage Joy Division/New Order and co-found Factory Records. The Panik connection makes Gretton a scene associate of V2’s core members. (factoryrecords.org, punk77.co.uk/band/the-panik)

Paul Morley — reviewed V2’s debut EP in NME, providing one of the few contemporary critical assessments. (boredteenagers.co.uk)

Tony Parsons — NME reviewer who gave the Speed Freak EP a harsh review (described elsewhere as “aural-punch of damp fart in a rust corrugated baked bean can”). (punk77.co.uk, attribution to Parsons per punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com)

Ian Nance — Post-V2

One source (punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com) states Nance joined “Teardrops” after V2. This likely refers to The Teardrops (a Manchester-area band distinct from The Teardrop Explodes, who were Liverpool-based). This could not be confirmed from a second source — see Research Queue.
