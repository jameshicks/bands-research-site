Kahil El’Zabar’s Tri-Factor

Free jazz trio co-led by Kahil El’Zabar, Billy Bang, and Hamiet Bluiett. Active 2000–2002. Discogs artist ID: 933684. Also credited on releases as “Tri-Factor.”

Overview

Kahil El’Zabar’s Tri-Factor was a three-way co-leadership project pairing Chicago AACM-connected percussionist Kahil El’Zabar with two New York-rooted free jazz veterans: violinist Billy Bang and multi-reedist Hamiet Bluiett. The instrumentation was unusual — violin, flute/bass saxophone/contrabass saxophone, and percussion/thumb piano — with no bass or piano, producing an open, spacious sound built on sustained textures and cross-rhythmic interplay rather than conventional jazz harmony. Two albums were recorded and released within two years, after which the group does not appear to have continued as a documented working unit.

The group sits at the intersection of two distinct avant-garde lineages: El’Zabar’s long association with AACM-rooted Chicago experimentalism (the Ritual Trio, the Ethnic Heritage Ensemble) and the New York loft-jazz world that produced Bang and Bluiett. Bang and El’Zabar had collaborated going back to 1987 (Another Kind Of Groove, Sound Aspects Records) and a 1995 Delmark session (Big Cliff) before this trio project crystallized. Bluiett’s presence brought a connection to the World Saxophone Quartet and the broader BAM/loft scene.

Members

Kahil El’Zabar — percussion, thumb piano Chicago-based percussionist and composer, born 1953. Core figure of the AACM; founder and leader of the Ethnic Heritage Ensemble (from 1974) and Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio (from the mid-1980s). Discogs artist ID: 309759.

Billy Bang — violin William Vincent Walker, born 20 September 1947 in Mobile, Alabama; died 11 April 2011, The Bronx, New York. Free jazz violinist; co-founder of the String Trio of New York (1977). Known for a raw, blues-inflected approach to the instrument and two late-career Vietnam War suites (Vietnam: The Aftermath, 2001; Vietnam: Reflections, 2005). Discogs artist ID: 174749.

Hamiet Bluiett — flute, bass saxophone, contrabass saxophone (The Power); baritone saxophone (If You Believe) Born 16 September 1940, Lovejoy, Illinois; died 4 October 2018. Baritone saxophonist and bass woodwind specialist; founding member of the World Saxophone Quartet (1976). His command of the full low-end reed spectrum — from baritone through bass sax to contrabass sax — made him a distinctive voice in both large-group and intimate settings. Discogs artist ID: 296988.

If You Believe (2002)

Label: 8th Harmonic Breakdown (8THHB80004) Format: CD Year: 2002 Country: US Genre: Jazz Discogs release ID: 6440907

The second and final Tri-Factor album was released on 8th Harmonic Breakdown, a smaller independent imprint (Discogs label ID: 233853; five releases total in the DB). Unlike The Power, If You Believe has full per-track composition credits, establishing the session as composed rather than purely improvised — though the trio format and players strongly suggest substantial improvisation within the composed frameworks. El’Zabar contributed six of the ten compositions; Bang and Bluiett contributed two each. El’Zabar is also credited for vocals on this record (not present on The Power credits). The dedications embedded in several titles — “For Kaduwa” (track 5), “For Kasan” (track 7) — are personal dedications likely to El’Zabar’s family.

Context and Connections

The Tri-Factor project emerged from a well-established working relationship between El’Zabar and Bang. Their collaborations trace back at least to 1987 (Another Kind Of Groove, Sound Aspects Records, with Malachi Favors) and continued through Big Cliff (1995, Delmark Records), a Kahil El’Zabar’s Ritual Trio session that featured Bang as guest. By 2000 the duo-at-core added Bluiett, extending the ensemble into the low-register reed territory that Bluiett had made his signature in the World Saxophone Quartet and his own groups.

The CIMP context for The Power is significant. Bob Rusch’s label philosophy — no overdubs, no editing, direct stereo documentation — framed the music as pure process. CIMP’s catalog in this period documented a range of free improvisers across the New York–Chicago corridor; the Tri-Factor session fits naturally within that. The Power was issued as CIMP 205, placing it within the label’s numbered series.

The shift to 8th Harmonic Breakdown for If You Believe represents a move to an even smaller, more marginal imprint (five total releases in the Discogs DB, catalog number 8THHB80004). The label’s only other documented release is John Tchicai / Yusef Komunyakaa’s Love Notes From The Madhouse (1998, 8THHB80001) — suggesting an imprint specifically oriented toward free jazz and spoken-word poetry intersections.
