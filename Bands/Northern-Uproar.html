Overview

Northern Uproar were an English rock band from Stockport, active from 1995 with a sound grounded in Britpop, indie rock, and mod. The four-piece — Leon Meya on vocals and bass, Paul Kelly and Jeff Fletcher on guitars, and Keith Chadwick on drums — issued most of their catalogue in the late 1990s through Heavenly and Deconstruction before disbanding in 1999. They reunited in 2006 and returned to recording in the 2010s on their own Northern Uproar imprint. Producer Dave Eringa is their dominant collaborator across 27 shared releases, and James Dean Bradfield appears across 12, pointing to a notable connection to the wider Manic Street Preachers circle.

Notes (web)

1999 Disbandment

After being dropped by Heavenly Recordings in June 1998, the band sold their recording studio and attempted to record an EP that never materialised. By June 1999, they called it a day. Leon Meya gave the clearest account of the split in a 2015 interview with xsnoize.com: “There were a lot of issues around — two of the lads wanted a change of direction, they couldn’t see it carrying on, and financially we got into a lot of trouble with tax and all sorts of stuff. It was only me who really carried on… I always did music, and I played with different people.” xsnoize.com interview with Leon Meya

No contemporary press coverage (1999) of the disbandment was located. The NME, which had covered the band in their peak years, does not appear to have an archived interview or news item about the split that is accessible online.

2006 Reformation — Motivation

The reunion process was incremental rather than a single event. Fan demand grew through the early 2000s. In 2004, Leon Meya and Jeff Fletcher reunited for two Manchester shows, playing old material and some new songs. Nothing formalised in 2005. Meya gave the plainest account of how the 2006 reformation came about in a 2015 interview with essentiallypop.com: “Before that between our 5 year break, I had been playing in another band and playing a different style, I got back in touch with Jeff and said, ‘lets have a rehearsal for a laugh, and play some of the old tunes’.” He added: “Sometimes you need a break to realise how much you miss what you had.” essentiallypop.com, August 2015

The 2006 reformation lineup was Leon Meya, Jeff Fletcher, and Leon’s cousin Noel Meya on drums. Original members Paul Kelly (21) and Keith Chadwick did not return, reportedly because they were full-time parents by that point. A fourth member, Chris Gorman (who had played with the band briefly in 1996), later joined. The goal was to record a third studio album, which became Stand And Fight (2007, Tiny Rebel Records). rwffmusic.blogspot.com biography by Ben P Scott, October 2011

No contemporary press coverage from 2006 announcing or detailing the reformation was found. The earliest accessible press item is a May 2011 NME news piece about a one-off Vibe Bar show — that piece notes the band “previously reformed for tours in 2006 and 2007” but gives no detail about the original reunion impetus. NME, 6 May 2011

Conflict note

The May 2011 NME article states Northern Uproar “originally disbanded in 1997.” Both Discogs and Wikipedia give 1999 as the disbandment year, consistent with Meya’s own interview account. The NME figure appears to be an error, possibly confusing the label departure (Heavenly dropped them in 1997–1998) with the full band split.
