The Anomoanon

Louisville, Kentucky band active 1997–2004. Discogs artist ID: 267688. Also billed as Anomoanon and The Ano•moanon (the early 7” spelling). Led by Ned Oldham (Will and Paul’s brother); produced throughout by Paul Oldham.

Core lineup: Ned Oldham (lead vocals, guitar, harmonica), Aram Stith (lead guitar, layout), Willy MacLean (bass, backing vocals), Jack Carneal (drums, percussion). Jason Stith (keyboards) appears on the debut LP and early releases but is credited “There In Spirit” on the second LP onward. By the third LP, the lineup expands to include David Heumann (keyboards, harmony vocals) and Walker David Teret (upright bass, banjo) as full band members.

The band’s catalog runs from a Palace Records 7” in 1997 through three studio LPs — two for Palace Records / Temporary Residence Limited, one self-produced for the short-lived Box Tree Records — plus a mini-album, an EP, and a split. Their music sits in American folk-rock and acoustic country-rock territory, drawing on traditional nursery rhymes and ballads alongside original songwriting by Ned and Aram Stith.

Discography

Singles and EPs

“Hello My” / “The Free Web” (7”, 1997, Palace Records, cat. PR14) The band’s debut release, credited as The Ano•moanon on the label. Two tracks, personnel listed as Ned, Aram, Jack, Jason, Willy, and Paul Oldham (all as “Performers”). Written by Ned Oldham.

“Portland” / “Now Is The Season” (7”, 1999, LowFly Records, cat. LF004 — Portugal pressing) Split or standalone single. The Anomoanon credited as producers.

Summer Never Ends (12” mini-album, 1999, Palace Records, cat. PR21) Seven-track 12”. Produced by Paul Oldham, Bonnie “Prince” Billy (Will Oldham), and The Anomoanon. Bonnie “Prince” Billy contributes background vocals. Paul engineers and masters. Jason Stith on keyboards and half-scale acoustic bass. Photography by Jennie Oldham.

Tracks: “Her Little Kitty” / “First Date” / “Around The Road” / “Rock And Roll” / “Summer Never Ends” / “Night Is Most Uncertain” / “The Wanderer”

Songs From Robert Louis Stevenson’s “A Child’s Garden Of Verses” (CD, 1999, Palace Records, cat. PR23) 23-track song-cycle setting Robert Louis Stevenson poems to music. Produced by Paul Oldham, The Anomoanon, and Will Oldham. Special guest musicians: David Pajo (drums, guitar), Paul Oldham (guitar, bass, synth), Will Oldham (guitar, keyboards, harmony vocals), Peter Townsend (drums, percussion). Individual songs written by Ned Oldham and Aram Stith (credited “A. Stith” / “N. Oldham”). Illustrated by Charles Robinson (6). This is the fullest early collaboration between the Anomoanon and the broader Louisville / Palace circle.

Envoi Villon (12” EP, single-sided etched, 2002, Galaxia, cat. glx-15) Billed as “Anomoanon.” Four tracks setting poems by François Villon (translated by Fance Franck) to music by Ned Oldham. Engineered and co-produced by Paul Oldham. Features Jason Stith (synths/piano) and Tony Bailey on drums (replacing Jack Carneal on some tracks), plus Mike Portera on guitar. Design by Demetrie Tyler and T. Mocklynx.

Tracks: “Ballade Of Villon And Fat Margot” / “Ballade Of Good Doctrine To Those Of Ill Life” / “Ballade” / “John Of The Slum”

“Portrait Of John Entwistle” (10”, 2003, Western Vinyl, cat. West 019) Four-track 10” with no release-level credits; all credits are per-track. Features two different rhythm sections across the four tracks — Jack Carneal and Paul Oldham on tracks 1 and 3, Willy MacLean and Tony Bailey on tracks 2 and 4. David Heumann (credited “Dave Heumann”) plays organ and sings on tracks 1 and 3. Paul Oldham engineers tracks 1, 3, and 4; Craig Bowen engineers track 1. Track 2 credits C.M. Barker (Cicelia Mary Barker) for lyrics, set to Ned Oldham music; tracks 1 and 3 credit John Entwistle for lyrics and music (the Who bassist, who died in 2002). Jason Stith plays synth and sings on track 2. Mike Portera on guitar for track 2.

Tracks: “Heaven And Hell” / “Cherries” / “Two Eyes Wild” / “John Entwistle”

LP 1: The Anomoanon (2001)

Palace Records, cat. PR 25 CD Produced by Paul Oldham and The Anomoanon. Paul engineers. Format: CD (also released in Japan on P-Vine Records).

Core lineup: Ned Oldham (vocals, harmonica, guitar, bass on some tracks), Aram Stith (lead guitar, lap steel, 12-string, synths, layout), Willy MacLean (bass, backing vocals), Jack Carneal (drums, percussion), Jason Stith (keyboards). Layout jointly by Aram Stith and Ned Oldham. Photography by Tim Wright (inner photo).

Notable: track 6, “Baby, It’s You,” is credited to a different band entirely — Mike Portera (bass), Randall Shurbert (drums, vocals), Davey Williams (guitar lead), Ed Reynolds (organ) — with engineers John Barron (2) and Randall Shurbert. This appears to be a guest or embedded track by an outside group rather than The Anomoanon. Track-level credits on the other songs show members freely swapping instruments: Ned plays bass on tracks 2, 3, 7, 8, and 9 (where he also plays synth); Willy plays electric guitar labeled “Wandering Guitar” on tracks 2 and 3; Aram plays synths alongside guitar across several tracks.

Tracklist: “Going To The Sea” / “Flock” / “Camp” / “Mermaid’s Womb” / “Baby, Baby, Baby” / “Baby, It’s You” / “Expect Snakes” / “Wonder Better” / “Window (I Can’t See Past The…)” / “On The Beach”

LP 2: Asleep Many Years In The Wood (2002)

Temporary Residence Limited, cat. TRR54 Produced by Paul Oldham and The Anomoanon. Paul engineers. Format: CD.

Core lineup: Ned Oldham (guitar, lead vocals; written-by on tracks 2–11), Aram Stith (guitar; also organ, acoustic guitar, slide guitar, mellotron on specific tracks), Willy MacLean (electric bass, backing vocals), Jack Carneal (drums; also photography). Jason Stith is credited “There In Spirit” — no longer playing but acknowledged.

Key additional contributors:

  • David Heumann (credited “Dave Heumann”) — backing vocals on tracks 2, 3, 5, 7, 9
  • David Michael Curry — viola on tracks 3 (“Bluebird Of Happiness”) and 5 (“Complaint”)
  • Will Oldham — backing vocals on track 6 (“One That Got Away”)
  • Paul Oldham — backing vocals on track 4 (“Kick Back”)
  • Jennie Oldham — backing vocals on track 1; voice credited to Sam Oldham (child, crying) on same track
  • Ward Deters — backing vocals and high ooos on closing title track

Design and layout by Jeremy DeVine (Temporary Residence co-founder). Cover artworks credited separately (three paintings/drawings: “Bourbon Label” by John Fabrizio, “Desert Trees” by Brian Deran, “Tree Pan” by Peter Scott (11)). Pooh Johnston credited for engineering assistance.

Track 1 (“Sixteen Ways”): lyric by Ned, music by Aram Stith; track credits Jennie Oldham (backing vocals), Aram (mellotron/virtual, backing vocals), Sam Oldham (voice/crying). The only track where Aram takes sole music credit on this album.

Tracklist: “Sixteen Ways” / “Ain’t Skeert” / “Bluebird Of Happiness” / “Kick Back” / “Complaint” / “One That Got Away” / “Tongue And Heart” / “Sadie And Rudy” / “Y’Know” / “A Story” / “Asleep Many Years In The Wood”

LP 3: The Derby Ram (2004)

Box Tree Records, cat. BT 001 (also issued on Houston Party Records, Spain; September Gurls, Germany) Produced by Paul Oldham and The Anomoanon. Paul also mixes (with Ned), masters, and contributes slide guitar on “O Lady Wind” and choir/synth on “Bourbon Whiskey.” Recorded by Ned Oldham and Aram Stith; additional recording by Paul at Folly Beach / Robe Studios and by Ned via “Invasive Mobile.” Format: CD.

Expanded lineup: Ned Oldham (vocals, acoustic and electric guitar, bass on some tracks, harmonica, piano, toms), Aram Stith (acoustic and electric guitar, bass on some tracks, synth), Jack Carneal (drums, photography), Willy MacLean (absent from track credits — Aram handles bass on most tracks), David Heumann (credited “Dave” — harmony vocals on tracks 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 10, 12; keyboards on 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9), Walker David Teret (credited “Walker” — upright bass, banjo on tracks 4 and 8b). Jodie Jean Marston sings harmony vocals on track 10.

All lyrics by Traditional (folk/nursery rhyme sources); all music by Ned Oldham (tracks 2–12), with “Little Birdy” (track 1) by Aram Stith. Layout by John Fabrizio (J.F.). Artwork: colored dot arrangement by Asa Osborne (Lync / Unwound connection — same Asa Osborne). Cover sculpture by Michael Pegler, adapted front cover image by John Fabrizio.

Tracklist: “Little Birdy” / “A Man Of Words” / “As I Walked By Myself” / “Cambric Shirt” / “Mary Had A Pretty Bird / Bluebirdy Jam” / “Ding Dong Bell” / “For Every Evil” / “Bourbon Whiskey / The Derby Ram” / “O Lady Wind” / “One For Anger” / “I Would If I Could” / “The Girl In The Lane”

Joji (2004)

Temporary Residence Limited, cat. TRR69 (also Houston Party Records, Spain) Eight-track album. Recorded by Paul Oldham; additional recording by Aram Stith and Ned Oldham. No mixing or mastering credit listed — Paul credited only as “Recorded By.” Format: CD.

Lineup billed as “The Anomoanon With”: Ned Oldham (guitar, vocals), Aram Stith (guitar, backing vocals), Willy MacLean (bass, backing vocals), Jack Carneal (drums, backing vocals), plus David Heumann (keyboards, backing vocals), Walker David Teret (electric upright bass, banjo, backing vocals), Jodie Jean Marston (performer, backing vocals).

Songwriting: Ned Oldham writes words throughout; music split between Ned (tracks 1, 3, 5, 6, 7) and Aram Stith (tracks 2, 4, 8). Layout and design by Jeremy DeVine. Photography by Jack Carneal (all other photos), Joe Oldham (spider photo), Jennie Oldham (wood face photo). BMI registered.

Tracklist: “Down And Brown” (8:36) / “Leap Alone” / “Mr. Train” / “Green Sea” / “After Than Before” / “Wedding Song” (10:13) / “Nowhere” (7:11) / “Bird Child”

Note: Discogs lists this release separately from The Derby Ram in the same year (both 2004), and both credit the expanded lineup with Heumann and Teret. Joji appears to be recorded at different sessions from The Derby Ram given the distinct production credits.

Open Questions

  • The exact relationship between Joji and The Derby Ram (both 2004) is unclear — whether they were recorded in sequence or simultaneously, and why they appeared on different labels (TRL vs. Box Tree Records / Houston Party Records).
  • Track 6 (“Baby, It’s You”) on the debut LP has an entirely separate cast (Portera, Shurbert, Williams, Reynolds) — this may be an embedded recording by a Louisville friend-band rather than a proper Anomoanon track.
  • Box Tree Records (BT 001) appears to be a one-off imprint; whether it was Ned Oldham’s own label or an outside entity is not in the Discogs metadata.
  • “Mother Goose” (Palace Records, 1998, PR19) is a 23-track CD credited to The Anomoanon with Will Oldham as Executive Producer and Jennie Oldham contributing piano, synths, and vocals — this is a larger family/collaborative recording and likely sits between the two 1999 LPs in sequence.
