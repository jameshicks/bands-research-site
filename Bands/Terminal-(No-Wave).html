Terminal (No Wave)

NYC No Wave band. Formed and disbanded 1978. Discogs: Terminal (30), ID 12965475.

Biography

Formation and Context (1978)

Terminal was formed in New York City in 1978 by Charlie Nash and Anne Deon, the latter then the girlfriend of Alan Vega of Suicide. The band came together during the concentrated flowering of the No Wave scene, a period of roughly 1977–1980 when lower Manhattan’s art and music communities converged around deliberately anti-commercial, confrontational sound experiments.

The Artists Space Shows (May 1978)

Terminal played what became one of No Wave’s defining live events: the “Bands at Artists Space” series, May 2–6, 1978, a five-night run at the Artists Space gallery at 105 Hudson Street in Tribeca. The series was organised by artists Michael Zwack and Robert Longo and featured ten local bands across five nights. Terminal shared bills with Theoretical Girls, Tone Death, the Communists, Rhys Chatham’s The Gynecologists, Daily Life, DNA (4), The Contortions, Mars, and Teenage Jesus and the Jerks. Brian Eno, in New York to produce the second Talking Heads album, was in the audience and was sufficiently impressed to propose what became No New York. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_wave, as of 2026-06-08; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Space, as of 2026-06-08]

The Artists Space shows were subsequently documented on the canonical compilation No New York (1978, Antilles), produced by Brian Eno. Terminal did not appear on that compilation.

X Mag Benefit (March 1978)

On March 11, 1978, Terminal played the X Mag Benefit, a fundraiser for the No Wave magazine X (published by Collaborative Projects / Colab). The concert poster — designed by Seth Tillett — lists Terminal alongside the Contortions, Boris Policeband, the Erasers, DNA, and Theoretical Girls. The stated purpose on the poster was to raise money to print the third issue of X. [source: https://gallery98.org/2010/x-magazine-benefit-concert-poster-march-11-1978-2/ (date confirmed by URL slug and Gallery 98 metadata)]

A short film of the event was shot by Coleen Fitzgibbon and Alan W. Moore in black-and-white Super 8mm; the film was later edited and completed in 2009 under the title X Magazine Benefit. The film documents only the Contortions, Boris Policeband, and DNA sets; Terminal’s performance is not included in the surviving footage. [source: https://mubi.com/en/us/films/x-magazine-benefit; https://jameschanceofficial.blogspot.com/2013/06/x-magazine-1978-benefit-footage-with.html]

Note: if the Artists Space series ran May 2–6, 1978, the X Mag Benefit on March 11 predates it — meaning the X Mag Benefit was not Terminal’s final performance but an earlier one. The article’s existing framing (“among their final performances”) is contradicted by this date. See Conflicts section below.

Disbandment and the Demo

Terminal disbanded in 1978 without releasing any official record. They are counted among the No Wave bands — alongside others from the Artists Space week — that left almost no recorded trace. A cassette demo from 1978 (Discogs release ID 27040479, self-released) documents five tracks and represents the only known recorded output. It was not commercially released at the time.

Key Connections

Conflicts

  • X Mag Benefit timing: The existing article described the X Mag Benefit as “later in 1978” and “among their final performances.” The concert poster and Gallery 98 documentation date the event to March 11, 1978. If the Artists Space series was May 2–6, 1978, the X Mag Benefit preceded it by nearly two months. The chronological order is: X Mag Benefit (March 11) → Artists Space (May 2–6). The article’s framing of the X Mag Benefit as a “final” performance appears to be wrong.
