Cyclo.

Discogs ID: 6949
Members: Alva Noto (Carsten Nicolai, ID: 178718) and Ryoji Ikeda (ID: 11139)
Active: 2001–present (sporadic; three album-length statements over 16 years)
Labels: Raster-Noton (2001, 2011), Noton (2017)

Overview

Cyclo. is the collaborative project of Carsten Nicolai (recording as Alva Noto) and Japanese sound artist Ryoji Ikeda. Both are core figures of the Raster-Noton experimental-electronic ecosystem: Nicolai as the label’s co-founder and primary conceptual voice, Ikeda as one of its most internationally recognized artists. Cyclo. represents a formal fusion of their respective practices — Nicolai’s microsound/glitch abstraction and Ikeda’s data-driven, mathematically precise sine-tone compositions — into a single sustained audio-visual project.

The project’s name refers to cyclical structures: the first album’s tracks are titled C0 through C9, and the visual dimension of Id (2011) makes cyclic/oscilloscopic waveform geometry explicit. Id was released with a companion CD-ROM volume (Cyclo. Id Vol. 01, r-n 127-3) containing video for each of the 11 tracks — the .mov filenames mirror the audio track IDs exactly. This locked audio-visual pairing distinguishes Cyclo. from both members’ solo work and signals the project’s concern with perception, pattern, and feedback across sensory modalities.

The first album (2001) appeared simultaneously as a double-LP vinyl pressing (vyr041) and a standard CD (CDR 041) — an unusually elaborate debut for a Raster-Noton release of that period. Ten years passed before Id (2011), and the 2017 reissue of Id on the newly independent Noton imprint (as a digital WAV release, N-037) coincided with the dissolution of Raster-Noton. No compilations featuring Cyclo. as a credited artist appear in the Discogs database.

The “Xy Phase Scope Software” is a custom oscilloscopic visualization tool developed for the project, credited jointly to Cyclo. (the entity) and Tomonaga Tokuyama. The Japanese edition on p*dis (PDIP-6517) includes the full CD-ROM video component bundled with the audio disc.

Web research note (2026-07-04): No evidence found that the Xy Phase Scope Software was ever released publicly, open-sourced, or used in any documented project beyond Id. Exhaustive searches of GitHub, code repositories, Tokuyama’s own site (tomonagatokuyama.com), artist bios (ENCAC, Eufònic), Raster-Noton archives, and press coverage of Id (Fluid Radio 2011, The Wire unedited interview with Nicolai) turned up no distribution, download link, repository, or mention of the tool outside the Discogs credit. Nicolai references using “an oscilloscope as a creative tool” in general terms in interviews but names no software and no developer. Tomonaga Tokuyama’s subsequent work with Ikeda (supersymmetry, datamatics publication) and with architects Junya Ishigami and Kengo Kuma (architectural design tools) is documented in his bio pages but without any reference to Xy Phase Scope. Conclusion: the software appears to have been purpose-built for Id and kept as an internal production tool. (source: encac.eu/artistas/tomonaga-tokuyama/, eufonic.net, tomonagatokuyama.com, fluid-radio.co.uk, thewire.co.uk, as of 2026-07-04)
