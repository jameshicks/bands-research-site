Stone Foundation

British soul and R&B band formed in Coventry, UK, 1998 (first release 2001). Discogs artist ID: 2083407. Active through at least 2025.

Genre: Soul, R&B, Northern Soul, Jazz. Known for brass-heavy arrangements, socially conscious lyrics, and energetic live performances. Closely associated with Paul Weller from mid-2010s onward.

Website: http://www.stonefoundation.co.uk/

Overview

Stone Foundation were formed in Coventry in 1998 by Neil Jones and Neil Sheasby, rooted in a love of Northern Soul, Motown, and classic British R&B. Their self-run label The Turning Point carried them through the 2000s and early 2010s, building a grassroots fanbase around album releases and live work. The band’s tight rhythm section, multi-piece horn section, and rich vocal harmonies became their signature.

Their wider breakthrough came with the move to 100- Records (2) (100% Records) around 2016, coinciding with a deepening creative partnership with Paul Weller, who guested, co-wrote, and produced across several releases from Street Rituals (2016) onward. Weller appeared on two tracks on Street Rituals and co-wrote material on Everybody, Anyone (2018). The band recorded regularly at Black Barn Studios (Weller’s Surrey studio), which brought them into the orbit of a wider pool of Weller-associated session players.

A 25th anniversary compilation, Standing In The Light: 25 Years Of Stone Foundation, appeared in 2023, followed by The Revival Of Survival in 2025, indicating continued activity.

Neil Sheasby also has a background in Dance Stance, a pre-history on Discogs (artist ID: 4689625).

Scene Connections

Paul Weller / Black Barn orbit: From Street Rituals (2016), Stone Foundation became closely associated with Paul Weller’s world, recording at Black Barn Studios in Surrey. This brought in Weller regulars: Steve White (3) (drums), Mick Talbot (keys), Charles Rees (engineer), Jon Astley (engineer/mastering). Weller has appeared as a guest vocalist and co-writer rather than as a producer in the full sense, though the collaboration has been deep and sustained across at least 21 shared credits.

Coventry soul scene: The Horace Panter connection (of The Specials) on the To Find The Spirit cover art is emblematic of Stone Foundation’s Coventry roots — the city’s ska and soul heritage looms large. Neil Sheasby’s earlier group Dance Stance also situates the band in that lineage.

American soul guest credits: The long-running relationship with Nolan Porter (6 shared credits) — an original 1970s Los Angeles soul singer — signals the band’s reverence for classic soul sources. Similarly, Carleen Anderson and Andy Fairweather-Low on To Find The Spirit reflect a consistent strategy of bringing in established names from British soul and rock’s past.

Dr. Robert (of the Blow Monkeys) appears on Live Rituals, Everybody, Anyone, and The Coming Of Grace — another guest from the British blue-eyed soul tradition.
