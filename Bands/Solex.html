Overview

Solex is the solo project of Elisabeth Esselink, an Amsterdam-based record-shop owner and songwriter born in 1965, with the name borrowed from a French motor scooter manufactured from 1946 to 1988. Working in a leftfield blend of abstract downtempo, experimental electronics, and trip hop, she released steadily through the late 1990s and 2000s, with her peak commercial period on Matador Records spanning 1998 to 2001. After leaving Matador she moved through smaller labels including Discmeister, Konkurrent, and Bronzerat, with activity thinning to a single release in the 2010s. Her catalogue includes notable collaborations with Jon Spencer and Cristina Martinez of the Jon Spencer Blues Explosion and Boss Hog, alongside a consistent circle of Dutch collaborators including Robert Lagendijk and Geert de Groot.

Notes (web)

Jon Spencer / Cristina Martinez collaboration — how the connection formed

The collaboration that produced Amsterdam Throwdown King Street Showdown! (2009/2010, Bronzerat) grew out of touring encounters over several years. Jon Spencer’s account, given in a 2010 interview at The Quietus, is the most detailed primary source found:

“We were fans of Elizabeth a little bit and ran into her a few times on tour and around Europe. She DJed at a couple of Heavy Trash nights in Amsterdam. After a while we ended up talking about what a collaboration would sound like.”

Spencer’s side-project Heavy Trash (with Matt Verta-Ray) was therefore the specific context for at least some of the Amsterdam encounters. Cristina Martinez played an active part in getting the project started: Spencer noted that “Cristina encouraged her to get in touch with us as we were eager to find someone to collaborate with.”

The working process was largely remote. Esselink sent initial sketches across the Atlantic; Spencer described it as “she sent us a few ideas to check out at first and we ended up simply adding some ideas to the recordings.” Spencer and Martinez also travelled to Amsterdam to work in person — tracks were compiled at Esselink’s Amsterdam apartment/record store and passed back and forth to New York. A separate short piece at noearplugs.com (May 2010) describes “a few months of emailing and overnight shipping” before the trio completed the album.
