Maximum Joy

Post-punk / funk / dub band. Bristol, UK. Formed 1979. Discogs ID: 47968.

Overview

One of the key bands in the Bristol post-punk scene, Maximum Joy fused funk rhythms, dub sensibility, and no wave-adjacent tension into a sound that stands apart from their peers. Their membership drew from two major Bristol predecessors — Glaxo Babies and The Pop Group — and their alumni web includes Nellee Hooper, who went on to produce Soul II Soul, Massive Attack, Björk, Madonna, Janet Jackson, Sade, and Tracy Chapman.

Active 1979–1983; reformed 2015 for a Bristol live show; Janine Rainforth and Charlie Llewellin continued as MXMJoY from 2019.

Formation

Founded in 1979 by vocalist/violinist/clarinettist Janine Rainforth and saxophonist/trumpeter Tony Wrafter (ex-Glaxo Babies). Other founding members were:

  • Charlie Llewellin (drums) — ex-Glaxo Babies
  • John Waddington (guitar) — ex-The Pop Group (born 1 Jan 1960; died 20 June 2023)
  • Dan Catsis (bass) — ex-Glaxo Babies and The Pop Group

Later additions:

  • Kevin “Ebo” Evans (bass, piano, vocals) — replaced Dan Catsis
  • Jeremy Hirsch (keys, trumpet)
  • Nellee Hooper (percussion, backing vocals) — simultaneously active in The Wild Bunch, the Bristol collective that became Massive Attack

Three of the founding five were ex-Glaxo Babies; two had been in The Pop Group. The Pop Group connection brought them directly into the orbit of Y Records and its owner/manager Disc O’Dell.

Discography

”Stretch” (99 Records, US, 1981)

Their US debut, released on Ed Bahlman’s 99 Records in New York — the same label home as ESG, Liquid Liquid, and Bush Tetras. A direct transatlantic link between the Bristol post-punk scene and the New York no wave underground. The lacquer was cut by Howie Weinberg; artwork by Gina Franklyn.

”Station M.X.J.Y.” (Y Records, UK, 1982)

Their most fully realised record. Produced by Adrian Sherwood (On-U Sound), Dave Hunt, Peter Woodiscroft, and the band; eight UK pressings. Kev Evans joins on bass, piano, and vocals; Rainforth plays violin and clarinet alongside her singing; Wrafter adds flute and trumpet alongside sax.

”White And Green Place” (Y Records, UK, 1982)

Nine UK pressings — their best-selling single. Produced by Disc O’Dell and the band; engineered by Dave Hunt; lacquer cut by Porky (George Peckham). Artwork by Kronski.

”Do It Today / Touchdown” (Fontana, UK, 1982)

Split 7” with The Higsons, on Fontana — a surprising major label appearance for both acts.

”Why Can’t We Live Together” (Garage Records (6), France, 1983)

A cover of Timmy Thomas’s 1972 soul classic, produced by Dennis Bovell — the dub producer and Matumbi frontman who also worked with The Slits, Linton Kwesi Johnson, and many others. Released in France.
