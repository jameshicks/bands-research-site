Phantom Orchard

American experimental duo formed in New York City. Discogs artist ID: 484271.

Members: Zeena Parkins (harp, accordion, electronics) and Ikue Mori (electronics, laptop, drum machines).

Related formations: Phantom Orchard Ensemble (Discogs ID: 3903427) and Phantom Orchard Orchestra (Discogs ID: 2097212).

Overview

Phantom Orchard is the ongoing duo of harpist and electronics artist Zeena Parkins and electronic percussionist / laptop improviser Ikue Mori, both longtime fixtures of New York’s downtown experimental music scene. The two musicians had overlapping networks from at least the late 1980s — Mori appeared as a guest on Parkins’s debut LP Something Out There (1987) — and the duo project formalized sometime in the 1990s or early 2000s, releasing its sole studio album Orra in 2008 on Tzadik.

The Phantom Orchard name also extends to two larger formations: the Phantom Orchard Ensemble, which adds cellist Margaret Parkins, violinist Sara Parkins, pianist Sylvie Courvoisier, and vocalist/electronics artist Maja S. K. Ratkje to the core duo; and the Phantom Orchard Orchestra, which incorporates all Ensemble members plus French horn / electronics player Hild Sofie Tafjord and percussionist Shayna Dunkelman.

All three formations record for Tzadik, with John Zorn serving as executive producer on their releases.

History

Origins: overlapping downtown orbits (1987–2000s)

Ikue Mori and Zeena Parkins first intersected in the downtown New York experimental scene of the late 1980s. Mori is one of several guest performers on Parkins’s debut LP Something Out There (1987, No Man’s Land), contributing electronic drums and drum machines to multiple tracks alongside Tom Cora, Christian Marclay, and Samm Bennett.

Mori had arrived in New York from Tokyo in 1977 and quickly joined the no-wave band DNA (4) as drummer, appearing on the landmark No New York compilation (1978, Antilles, prod. Brian Eno). After DNA disbanded she moved away from the acoustic drum kit and into electronic percussion and drum machines, eventually settling into laptop-based electronics. Parkins had relocated to New York in 1984 following studies at Bard College, and by 1987 was already performing and recording harp, electric harp, accordion, and electronics with the full range of downtown improvisers.

Both musicians developed close associations with John Zorn and Tzadik through the 1990s. Mori is extensively credited as a cover and sleeve designer for dozens of Tzadik releases from 1995 onward, alongside her own recording credits. Parkins appeared on Zorn’s game-piece recordings and contributed to Tzadik-aligned projects. The Phantom Orchard name appears to have crystallized from this shared ecosystem — the duo is listed jointly on Parkins’s profile from at least the mid-1990s — though their first documented recording together under that name is Orra (2008).

Mori’s profile from this period also includes: drummer for John Zorn’s Locus Solus (Rift, 1983); co-founder of Death Ambient with Kato Hideki and Fred Frith (formed 1995); member of Zorn’s Hemophiliac trio (with Mike Patton); member of Electric Masada; collaborations with Kim Gordon, DJ Olive, Jim O’Rourke, and Julianna Barwick (on FRKWYS Vol. 6, Rvng Intl., 2011).

Orra and the duo format (2008)

Orra (2008, Tzadik, cat. TZ 7718) is the sole studio album credited to the core Phantom Orchard duo. Parkins plays acoustic harp, electric harp, and omnichord; Mori plays electronics. The album is produced and mixed by Mori (and co-mixed by Dougie Bowne and Kenji Shimoda), with Kenji Shimoda also handling recording. Scott Hull mastered.

Several additional performers are featured throughout: Brazilian percussionist Cyro Baptista (percussion), Norwegian vocalist and electronics artist Maja S. K. Ratkje (voice and real-time electronics processing), Japanese vocalist and jew’s harp player Makigami Koichi (voice, jew’s harp), and steel drum player Josh Quillen. John Zorn is executive producer; Kazunori Sugiyama is associate producer.

The album contains 11 tracks, total runtime approximately 50 minutes. Track titles include “Inquisitive Fingers,” “Cavalcade,” “Ship Of The Damned,” “Green Dome,” and “Kiss That Conquers.”

The Discogs genre entry reads: Classical; styles: Avantgarde, Contemporary.

Phantom Orchard Orchestra: Trouble In Paradise (2012)

Trouble In Paradise (2012, Tzadik, cat. TZ 7728) is the sole album credited to the Phantom Orchard Orchestra. The full lineup: Parkins (electric harp, acoustic harp, composed by), Mori (electronics, composed by, artwork/dolls/doll photos), Maja S. K. Ratkje (voice, electronics), Hild Sofie Tafjord (French horn, electronics), Margaret Parkins (cello), Sara Parkins (violin), Shayna Dunkelman (percussion).

John Zorn is producer and executive producer; Kazunori Sugiyama is associate producer. Co-produced by SWR2 Jazz Department, Baden-Baden and Tzadik (indicating this was likely recorded at or for the SWR2 Jazzfestival). Manfred Seiler mixed; Scott Hull mastered.

Eight tracks, running roughly 59 minutes. Titles include “Over The Gap” (9:28), “Face” (11:19), “Laughter In The Dark,” and “Trap.”

Discogs genres: Electronic, Jazz; styles: Abstract, Contemporary Jazz, Experimental, Free Improvisation.

Phantom Orchard Ensemble: Through The Looking-Glass (2014)

Through The Looking-Glass (2014, Tzadik, cat. TZ 4003) is the sole album credited to the Phantom Orchard Ensemble. The lineup drops Hild Sofie Tafjord and Shayna Dunkelman but adds Sylvie Courvoisier (piano) to the orchestra’s remaining core: Parkins (acoustic harp, electric harp, celesta), Mori (electronics, artwork/dolls), Maja S. K. Ratkje (voice, electronics), Margaret Parkins (cello), Sara Parkins (violin).

The album’s concept draws on fairy tales and folk stories: track titles reference “Luminous Fairies,” “Goblin Spider,” “The Little Mermaid,” “The Snow Queen,” “Alice In Wonderland,” “Kaguya,” “Snow White,” “Hedgehog In The Fog,” “The Beauty And The Beast,” “Psyche And Cupid,” “The Ugly Duckling,” and “Sleeping Beauty.” Mori provides the visual element — doll artwork and photography that accompanies the music.

Concept and composition credited jointly to Phantom Orchard. Marc Urselli recorded and mixed; Scott Hull mastered. John Zorn executive producer; Kazunori Sugiyama associate producer. Jeff Preiss contributed portrait photography.

Discogs genres: Electronic, Jazz; styles: Contemporary Jazz, Experimental, Free Improvisation.

The Members: Solo Careers

Zeena Parkins (b. 1956, Detroit)

See Zeena Parkins for full biography and discography.

Parkins studied at Bard College and relocated to New York in 1984. She quickly became a member of Skeleton Crew (2) (with Fred Frith and Tom Cora), one of the downtown scene’s central avant-rock units. Her debut LP Something Out There (1987, No Man’s Land) already featured Ikue Mori as a guest.

Beyond Phantom Orchard, her most prominent international credit is as harpist and harp arranger for Björk on Vespertine (2001, Elektra) and multiple subsequent Björk records through 2014 (Biophilia, Biophilia Live). She is also a member of Cosa Brava (chamber-improv ensemble with Fred Frith, Carla Kihlstedt, Matthias Bossi, Trevor Dunn) and of the M Z M piano trio with Miya Masaoka and Myra Melford.

Ikue Mori (b. 1953, Tokyo; real name 森 郁恵)

See Ikue Mori for full biography and discography.

Mori arrived in New York in 1977 and became the drummer for the no-wave band DNA (4) (with Arto Lindsay, Robin Crutchfield, and later Tim Wright). DNA appeared on the No New York compilation (1978, Antilles, prod. Brian Eno) and issued A Taste Of DNA (1981, Italian Records). After DNA, Mori abandoned the acoustic drum kit in favor of drum machines and eventually laptop electronics.

Her closest long-running collaborator is John Zorn, with whom she shares 18 credits in the Discogs database — among the highest of any Mori collaboration. She has been a member of Zorn’s Electric Masada, a member of the Hemophiliac trio (Zorn, Mori, Mike Patton), and has appeared across dozens of Tzadik releases in various capacities. She is also a graphic designer responsible for cover art on a large number of Tzadik albums from the mid-1990s through the 2000s.

Additional key formations: Death Ambient (trio with Kato Hideki and Fred Frith; formed 1995); Mephista (2) (trio with Sylvie Courvoisier, which also connects to the Phantom Orchard Ensemble lineup); various collaborations with Kim Gordon, DJ Olive, Jim O’Rourke, Satoko Fujii/Natsuki Tamura, Evan Parker, and Ken Vandermark.

Extended Members: Biographical Notes

Maja S. K. Ratkje (b. Maja Solveig Kjelstrup Ratkje)

Discogs ID: 65603. Norwegian vocalist, composer, and electronics artist. Active in noise, experimental vocal music, and improvisation. Member of the vocal duo Fe-mail and the black/experimental metal band Trinacria. Also a member of Spunk (Norwegian improv group). Appears on all three Phantom Orchard/Ensemble/Orchestra releases. Her presence on Orra (2008) as well as the Orchestra and Ensemble records makes her effectively an extended core member of the Phantom Orchard constellation. See Maja S. K. Ratkje.

Sylvie Courvoisier

Discogs ID: 369026. Swiss pianist based in New York, active in avant-jazz and free improvisation. Member of Mephista (2) (trio with Ikue Mori and Susie Ibarra), connecting directly to the Mori side of the Phantom Orchard network. Long collaboration with violinist Mark Feldman; also member of the Transatlantic Trance Map project. Appears on Through The Looking-Glass (2014) only.

Margaret Parkins

Discogs ID: 366110. Cellist; appears on both the Orchestra (Trouble In Paradise, 2012) and Ensemble (Through The Looking-Glass, 2014) recordings, credited as “Maggie Parkins.” Also a collaborator on Zeena Parkins’s solo work — 6 shared releases documented. Likely a family relation to Zeena Parkins (the name recurrence and shared credits suggest a sister), though this is not confirmed in Discogs data.

Sara Parkins

Discogs ID: 339644. Violinist; appears on both the Orchestra and Ensemble recordings. Also shares 7 releases with Zeena Parkins — more than any other collaborator except Ikue Mori and Elliott Sharp. Likely a family relation to Zeena (possibly another sister). Not confirmed in Discogs data.

Hild Sofie Tafjord

Discogs ID: 464839. Norwegian French horn player and electronics artist. Member of the Orchestra formation only (Trouble In Paradise, 2012). Active in Norwegian experimental music circles that overlap with Maja S. K. Ratkje’s networks.

Shayna Dunkelman

Discogs ID: 977576. Percussionist. Member of the Orchestra formation only (Trouble In Paradise, 2012).

Label Context: Tzadik

All three Phantom Orchard formations record for Tzadik, the label founded by John Zorn in New York in 1995. Zorn serves as executive producer on all three releases. The Tzadik connection is central to understanding the Phantom Orchard project: both Parkins and Mori are deeply embedded in the Tzadik ecosystem — Mori as both performer and cover designer, Parkins through multiple credits from the late 1990s. The label’s Composer Series and Radical Jewish Culture series are somewhat adjacent to the Phantom Orchard releases, which sit within Tzadik’s experimental/avant-garde cluster.

Kazunori Sugiyama, who appears on all three releases as associate producer, is a recurring Tzadik collaborator — 15 shared credits with Mori and 5 with Parkins in the database.

Scott Hull (2) (Masterdisk, NYC) mastered all three releases, sharing 10 credits with Mori and 2 with Parkins — his work on these records is consistent with his heavy presence across Tzadik’s catalog.
