Suburban Lawns

Post-punk/new wave band formed in Long Beach, California in 1978. Active c. 1978–1983, they released two singles, a debut LP, and a farewell EP before dissolving. Distinguished by a rotating cast of lead vocalists — most prominently Su Tissue (Susan McLane) — and an arch, often absurdist songwriting sensibility that stood apart from the LA punk mainstream. Their self-titled 1981 LP on I.R.S. Records remains their most documented work and has been reissued multiple times.

Discogs artist ID: 227257

History

Suburban Lawns coalesced in Long Beach in 1978. Their first release was the self-released 7” single “Gidget Goes To Hell” / “My Boyfriend” (1979) on their own imprint Suburban Industrial (cat no. 6379). The single was co-produced by the band and E.J. Emmons, and featured the core lineup of Su Tissue (vocals), Frankie Ennui / Richard Whitney (vocals, guitar), Vex Billingsgate / William Ranson (bass), John Gleur / John McBurney (guitar), and Chuck Roast / Charles Rodriguez (drums).

A second 7” single, “Janitor” / “Protection” (1980), followed on Suburban Industrial (cat no. 91680), again produced by the band and E.J. Emmons.

Their debut album, Suburban Lawns (1981), was released on I.R.S. Records (cat no. IRS SP70024) with a co-credit to Suburban Industrial Records. It was produced and recorded by E.J. Emmons and Troy Mathisen. The 14-track LP features the full band in a variety of lead vocal roles across different tracks — Su Tissue, Frankie Ennui, Vex Billingsgate, and John McBurney each take leads — with dense multi-part credits including keyboards, string ensemble (by Emmons), and synthesizer work. Art direction was by Carl Grasso.

Their final release was Baby (1983), a 12” EP on I.R.S. Records (cat no. SP70503), produced this time by Richard Mazda. The five-track EP represents a sonic shift — more synthesizer-heavy and produced, with Richard Mazda contributing guitar synthesizer on “Cowboy.” The band is credited with “Concept By.” This EP effectively marked the end of the band’s active run.

The 1981 LP was reissued by UK label Futurismo in 2015 and 2018. A 2021 digital reissue appeared on I.R.S. Records (FLAC). Superior Viaduct also issued a 2021 reissue of the LP.

Dissolution and Aftermath

Breakup (1983)

Guitarist John McBurney (John Gleur) departed during the recording of the Richard Mazda-produced Baby EP — the primary documented catalyst. Chuck Roast described the broader context in a 2015 interview: “Like any relationship, being in a band has its good days and its bad days. Internal strife, different ideas, (and) being together for so long.” Frankie Ennui elaborated that McBurney “ended up smack dab in the middle of a lot of business vs. art disputes, and other disputes among factions in the band.” After McBurney’s departure the remaining four-piece attempted to continue with a new guitarist, but “the band dynamic had changed; it ran its course.” The Baby EP was released with McBurney receiving a guitar credit on one track (“Baby”), suggesting he completed at least some recording before leaving. The band formally dissolved in 1983. [source: https://somethingelsereviews.com/2015/10/21/suburban-lawns-interview/]

Post-Dissolution Member Activities

The Lawns (c. 1983–mid-1980s) Chuck Roast, Frankie Ennui, and John McBurney regrouped as the Lawns, adding bassist Tom Corey (of The Fibonaccis). Ennui acknowledged it “wasn’t anywhere close to the same thing” without Su Tissue and Vex Billingsgate; the project lasted roughly a year. [source: https://somethingelsereviews.com/2015/10/21/suburban-lawns-interview/; https://jonbarmak.tumblr.com/post/86707479947/frankie-ennui]

Electric Sheep (mid-1980s) The Lawns dissolved into or morphed into Electric Sheep, a hybrid R&B/punk project. Electric Sheep featured David Kendrick (formerly of Sparks, later of Devo) on drums and vocalist Gloria Dawson. Ennui described it as “a hybrid thing, mixing R&B with punk.” The project did not achieve commercial traction. [source: https://jonbarmak.tumblr.com/post/86707479947/frankie-ennui]

Alfalfa (1985, William Ranson) Vex Billingsgate (William Ranson) formed a side project called Alfalfa in 1985. Members included Jamie Loveless (guitar), Steve Reed and Curtis DeHoff (bass), and Jeff Schweer and Lee Howell (drums). They released one 7” single, “Lucky Guy / Jewels,” on Urban Garden Hi Fi Disc. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban_Lawns (as of 2026-05-16); https://www.discogs.com/artist/1757972-William-Ranson]

Johnny Mark and the Ricks (Frankie Ennui, ongoing) After Electric Sheep, Frankie Ennui formed Johnny Mark and the Ricks with childhood friends John Bitterly, Mark Handley, and (later) Rick McDermott. The band played originals, performing regularly at venues including DiPiazza’s in Long Beach. Their philosophy, per Ennui: “if we keep writing songs, eventually we’ll come up with something good.” [source: https://jonbarmak.tumblr.com/post/86707479947/frankie-ennui]

The Modifiers (Chuck Roast) After the Lawns, Roast “rejoined Billy [Ranson] and these two cats from Memphis in a band called the Modifiers.” [source: https://somethingelsereviews.com/2015/10/21/suburban-lawns-interview/] No further documentation found.

Suburban John (William Ranson + John McBurney, early 2000s–present) In the early 2000s, Ranson and McBurney formed Suburban John, initially with drummer Jon Tomis. The band later added George Hurley (Minutemen / fIREHOSE) on drums. They released at least one album (Pulsator, 2002) and are active on Bandcamp (suburbanjohn.bandcamp.com). CalArts noted the project publicly, describing Ranson and McBurney as “founding members of Suburban Lawns.” [source: https://suburbanjohn.bandcamp.com/; https://www.facebook.com/calarts/posts/suburban-john-co-founded-by-alum-william-ranson-and-john-mcburney…]

Frankie Ennui / Richard Whitney — non-music career Whitney worked in computer technology, earned a teaching degree, briefly taught history and social studies, then became an attorney in Newport Beach — a career he maintained for approximately two decades while continuing to play music. [source: https://jonbarmak.tumblr.com/post/86707479947/frankie-ennui]

Su Tissue (Susan McLane) Attended Berklee College of Music (piano studies), released Salon De Musique (1984), and appeared in Jonathan Demme’s Something Wild (1986). Withdrew from public life thereafter. See Su Tissue for full detail.

Reunions

No full Suburban Lawns reunion has taken place. Chuck Roast stated in 2015: “I’d be lying if I said the thought didn’t cross my mind. Who knows what the future brings? Any kind of reunion would not involve Su, I’m sure.” Ranson and McBurney’s ongoing Suburban John project is the closest to a partial reunion of original members. [source: https://somethingelsereviews.com/2015/10/21/suburban-lawns-interview/]
