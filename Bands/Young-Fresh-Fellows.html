Overview

Young Fresh Fellows are an indie rock quintet from Seattle, Washington, formed in the early 1980s with a core lineup of Scott McCaughey, Kurt Bloch, Chuck Carroll, Jim Sangster, and John Perrin. Their catalogue spans four decades and 28 releases, weighted heavily toward the 1990s (11 releases) before a quieter stretch in the 2000s (3 releases) and a renewed run into the 2020s. Their early recordings appeared on the local Popllama label before the band moved through Frontier Records and eventually signed with Yep Roc Records in 2009; producer Conrad Uno has been a near-constant presence across their career with 58 shared credits. Their Seattle scene connections are extensive, with compilation ties to Fastbacks, Mudhoney, Love Battery, 7 Year Bitch, and a shared-credits footprint that reaches Nirvana, Pearl Jam, and Soundgarden.

Notes (web) — 2009 return and Yep Roc signing

Was there a formal hiatus? No. McCaughey told Magnet Magazine in July 2009 that the band never officially broke up: “My usual stance is that officially breaking up would be too much work.” The 1997–2009 stretch was simply inactivity while members pursued parallel projects (McCaughey with the Minus 5; Kurt Bloch and Jim Sangster with Thee Sgt. Major III; Tad Hutchison with visual art and the Chris & Tad project). There was a brief reactivation in 2001 for the split Let The War Against Music Begin / Because We Hate You on Mammoth/Malt, but no sustained activity followed until 2008–09. Magnet Magazine, 2009-07-06

What prompted the 2009 return? Robyn Hitchcock drove the reunion by simply scheduling the session himself. McCaughey (Magnet, 2009): “he was the one who basically said, ‘I’m coming to Seattle on November 20, and we can go into the studio and do the Fellows record then’ — thus, again, taking decision-making pretty much out of the equation for us.” The band recorded I Think This Is November 20–24, 2008 at a Seattle studio, with Hitchcock producing. Magnet Magazine, 2009-07-06; confirmed by the I Think This Is Wikipedia article (as of 2026-10-22): “The album was recorded at the behest of former Soft Boys member and friend Robyn Hitchcock, who offered to produce it.” Wikipedia — I Think This Is

Why Hitchcock? McCaughey had wanted Hitchcock to produce a Fellows record since the late 1980s — approximately 20 years before the session finally happened. The two stayed connected through collaborative Crocodile Club shows in Seattle in the late 1990s, and several Fellows members contributed to Hitchcock’s Jewels for Sophia album. That deepened relationship eventually made the 2008 session possible. Wikipedia — I Think This Is; Magnet Magazine, 2009-07-06

How did the Yep Roc deal come about? No source found explicitly explains how the label deal was brokered. Both I Think This Is (Fellows) and Killingsworth (Minus 5) were released on Yep Roc in 2009, on the same day (July 7, 2009). The Magnet interview, the Scene Point Blank review, and all other sources checked do not address the label arrangement.

The two most plausible routes, both of which had active Yep Roc relationships by 2009:

  • McCaughey / Minus 5 route: The Minus 5 had been on Yep Roc since at least 2003, when Down with Wilco (with Jeff Tweedy) appeared on the label. Wikipedia — Down with Wilco That relationship continued through Killingsworth (2009), giving McCaughey a six-year Yep Roc history before the Fellows signed.
  • Hitchcock route: Robyn Hitchcock has been on Yep Roc since Spooked (2004), with further albums Olé! Tarantula (2006) and Goodnight Oslo (also released 2009). Wikipedia — Olé! Tarantula Hitchcock was simultaneously an active Yep Roc artist and the producer of I Think This Is, so a label recommendation from him is plausible.

Both routes remain inference. No press release, McCaughey interview (Magnet 2009, OndaRock, Magnet 2026), or Yep Roc statement found to confirm which — or whether both — led to the signing.
