Kissing The Pink

Also known as: KTP, K.T.P., The Pink
Formed: London, 1980
Active: 1980–1988; occasional activity 2015, 2024
Genre: Synth-pop, New Wave, Post-Punk
Discogs artist ID: 31963

Overview

Kissing The Pink were a large-ensemble London group who emerged from the post-punk ferment of 1980–81 and evolved into a sleek synth-pop act by the mid-1980s. At their peak the band numbered seven or eight members — a rare configuration for the era — combining analogue synthesizers, live drums, bass, guitar, violin, saxophone, and multiple vocalists. The ensemble approach gave their records a layered density distinct from the leaner sound of contemporaries.

Their debut single “Don’t Hide In The Shadows” (1981, The Martyrwell Music Co.) was produced by Martin Hannett, then the presiding genius of Manchester’s post-punk scene, with Chris Nagle engineering. The Hannett credit is remarkable — one of his very few productions outside his Northern England orbit. The band then signed to Magnet (2) (a subsidiary of Magnet Records Ltd.), where they worked primarily with producer Colin Thurston for their debut LP Naked (1983) and singles, and with Peter Walsh on key tracks.

Naked (Magnet, 1983) contained “The Last Film,” their highest-charting UK single at #19. The album was produced by a split team: Thurston handled the pop tracks; Walsh contributed “Love Lasts Forever” and “Big Man Restless”; the band self-produced with engineer Neil Richmond on others. A second LP, What Noise (Magnet, 1984), followed, produced by Philip Bagenal and Ken Thomas on different tracks. They moved to Polydor and then Mercury/WEA for the third album Certain Things Are Likely (1986, released in the US and Europe as K.T.P.), produced by Walsh, with contributions from Stock Aitken Waterman-associated mixer Phil Harding and Ian Curnow.

Their final release under the original run was Stand Up (WEA, 1988), a mini-CD produced with Harding and Curnow. After dissolution, members Jon Kingsley Hall, Nicholas Whitecross, and George Stewart formed Ascendant Masters (1992) and Hall and Stewart later recorded as Luxia (2004). The band’s catalog was compiled in Anthology 1982–2024 (Cherry Red / Warner, 2024).

Scene Context

The band appeared on the 2021 Cherry Red box set Shake The Foundations (Militant Funk & The Post-Punk Dancefloor 1978-1984) with “Big Man Restless” (Disc 2, track 8), contextualising them within the militant funk and post-punk dancefloor movement of 1978–1984.

Concert appearances include the 1983 US radio broadcast The Penthouse/Omni Magazine College Rock Concert (London Wavelength) and BBC In Concert sessions alongside JoBoxers.

Research Queue

  • Was the Hannett production of “Don’t Hide In The Shadows” booked through a Manchester connection, or was there a London-based introduction? A contemporary press piece or interview would identify how Kissing The Pink came to Hannett’s attention in 1981.
  • Did the band chart in any other territories besides the UK with “The Last Film”? The US Atlantic deal (1983) suggests US push — was there a Billboard Hot 100 placement?
  • What was the precise UK chart position of “Mr. Blunt” or “Watching Their Eyes”? Only “The Last Film” (#19) is documented in the brief; a chart reference source would resolve the others.
  • Josephine Wells: was she related to or the same person as Jo Wells (Discogs ID 296506), who shares two releases with KTP? Discogs lists them separately — a contemporary band bio would clarify.
  • Simon Aldridge’s entry into the lineup: he does not appear on Naked credits but is fully credited on What Noise — did he join between albums or mid-session? A band interview from 1984 would confirm.
  • Was Philip Bagenal (ID 103486) the sole producer credited on the A-side of What Noise, or did he co-produce with another? The release credits list him without Ken Thomas on A-side tracks; Ken Thomas appears only in the collaborators list — a liner note check would resolve the split.
