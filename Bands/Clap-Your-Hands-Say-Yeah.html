From Discogs

Clap Your Hands Say Yeah (often abbreviated CYHSY) is the musical project of American indie rock musician Alec Ounsworth. The group was founded in 2004 and is based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, and Brooklyn, New York. Until 2014, the project operated as a band with drummer Sean Greenhalgh being a member for its entirety. Keyboardist/guitarist Robbie Guertin and brothers Lee (guitar/vocals) and Tyler (bass) Sargent were also a part of the band from its inception until their collective departure in 2012. Their debut album, Clap Your Hands Say Yeah, was self-released in 2005.

Overview

Clap Your Hands Say Yeah is an indie rock project helmed by Alec Ounsworth, formed in Philadelphia in 2004. The original lineup — Ounsworth, drummer Sean Greenhalgh, keyboardist/guitarist Robbie Guertin, and brothers Lee and Tyler Sargent on guitar/vocals and bass — held together through the self-released 2005 debut that broke the band via blog-era word-of-mouth, before Guertin and the Sargents departed collectively in 2012, leaving CYHSY as effectively Ounsworth’s solo vehicle. The catalogue is weighted toward the 2000s and 2010s, moving from early distribution through Wichita and V2 to a self-run imprint from 2011 onward; Dave Fridmann and Adam Lasus are the most prominent recurring production collaborators, connecting the project to a broader indie-rock network that also includes Quentin Stoltzfus of Philadelphia’s Mazarin.

Notes (web) — 2012 lineup departures

Short answer: no reported falling-out. Robbie Guertin and Tyler Sargent posted voluntary resignation letters on the band’s own website in July 2012, citing personal decisions to move on after eight years — not any creative conflict. Lee Sargent stayed on; the exits were Guertin and Tyler Sargent only.

Sequence of events. On 3 July 2012, Guertin publicly announced he would leave after a final New York show on 7 July. Tyler Sargent followed with his own statement shortly after. Both letters were posted to the band’s website. The final show (a CBGB Festival date) was followed by a benefit performance at Manhattan venue Cake Shop as a “proper send off.” diffuser.fm, July 2012 | stereogum.com, July 2012

Guertin’s stated reason. “Being a member of Clap has been an incredible experience, but I have decided to move on and see what’s next.” He said he would continue with his other band Radical Dads, “pursue miscellaneous artistic endeavors, and also grow some vegetables.” No criticism of Ounsworth or the band. stereogum.com | musicfeeds.com.au

Tyler Sargent’s stated reason. “I am very proud of what this band has accomplished over the past eight years, and I will miss playing with such talented musicians.” He jokingly noted he was “currently in the process of forming a presidential exploratory committee for 2016” and promised “an epic bass solo” at the final show. stereogum.com

Ounsworth’s response (Spin, 17 July 2012). “I utterly respect Robbie and Tyler for the decision that they’ve made. The idea of holding on to this sort of concept is one that is difficult. It’s tough for people to continue doing this after doing it for eight years.” He described the final show as “all very sappy and maudlin” and said “We’re not depressed, we’re not upset about anything, we’re all on very good terms.” He immediately drafted Brigham Brough and Wyndham Garnett (from Elvis Perkins’ band) for upcoming Spanish festival dates, and said he had “the next record pretty well discovered — probably 15 to 20 tracks. It’s very reminiscent of the first record.” He mentioned working with producer Dan the Automator on that material. spin.com, 17 July 2012 (snippet/agent-browser — spin.com blocks direct fetch)

Lee Sargent (Tyler’s twin) remained with the band after his brother’s departure. No statement from Lee regarding his brother’s exit was found in any of these sources.

Gradual dissolution vs. clean break. The 2012 exits were a clean break by two members, not a gradual dissolution. However the subsequent evolution into a solo project was gradual: Lee Sargent and Sean Greenhalgh continued performing with the band through at least the Only Run (2014) cycle, but by The Tourist (2017) onward the project was credited purely to Ounsworth. A 2013 highway81revisited.com interview found Ounsworth describing “new band members” named only as “Zack and Matt” but declining to discuss the previous departures. highway81revisited.com, August 2013
