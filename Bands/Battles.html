Battles

Experimental / math rock band, New York City. Formed 2002. Discogs artist ID: 238372

Overview

Post-hardcore / art rock quartet formed in New York in 2002, drawing members from Don Caballero, Helmet, and Tomahawk. All four members brought prior instrumental projects and a shared interest in layered, machine-like rhythmic structures. The group’s signature approach — guitar parts looped and routed through effects, cross-cutting with John Stanier’s enormous, metronomic drumming — was codified across two early EPs before crystallising on the 2007 debut album Mirrored.

After frontman/vocalist Tyondai Braxton departed in 2010, the remaining trio (Williams, Konopka, Stanier) continued without a permanent replacement vocalist, instead recruiting guest singers for Gloss Drop (2011) and subsequent albums.

Biography

Formation (2002–2006)

Ian Williams formed Battles in New York City in 2002 following the breakup of Don Caballero in 2001. Williams recruited John Stanier — then known as drummer of Helmet and Tomahawk — along with Dave Konopka and Tyondai Braxton, son of avant-garde composer Anthony Braxton. The lineup was a full quartet from the start.

The band’s first recordings circulated as a self-titled mixtape sampler in 2004 (Battles Productions). Three early releases followed the same year on small US indie labels: EP C on Monitor Records (cat no. mon 019), B EP on Dim Mak Records (DM 070), and the single Tras on Cold Sweat Records (CLD 002). All four members appeared as performers throughout. Recording was handled by Emery Dobyns, with mastering by Tom Hutten.

In 2005 Warp Records issued a UK compilation of EP C and B EP together (EP C / B EP, WAP—see Warp cat below), consolidating the early EPs for a European audience.

Mirrored era (2007)

The debut album Mirrored was released in 2007 on Warp Records (WARPCD156). Tracklist: “Race:In,” “Atlas,” “Ddiamondd,” “Tonto,” “Leyendecker,” “Rainbow,” “Bad Trails,” “Prismism,” “Snare Hanger,” “Tij,” “Race:Out.” Warp also issued the single “Atlas” / “Atlas (DJ Koze Remix)” as a 12” (WAP219). Mirrored was credited to Battles alone with no additional production credits in the Discogs master — the band self-produced. Genres tagged: Electronic, Rock / Experimental, Indie Rock, Math Rock.

Mirrored brought the band significant critical attention and established their sound internationally — heavily looped guitars, Braxton’s processed falsetto vocals, and Stanier’s wide-interval drum patterns.

Tyondai Braxton’s departure (2010)

Tyondai Braxton left Battles in 2010, prior to the recording of the second album, to pursue a solo career. He released Central Market on Warp in 2009 before his departure was public, and subsequently Hive1 (Nonesuch, 2015) and Telekinesis (New Amsterdam/Nonesuch, 2022). His Discogs discography shows continued composer/performer activity in contemporary classical contexts.

The remaining trio — Williams, Konopka, Stanier — chose to continue as Battles, replacing Braxton’s vocal role with a rotating cast of guest vocalists on the second album.

Gloss Drop (2011)

Gloss Drop (2011, Warp, WARP LP212) was the first album by the trio lineup. Rather than recruiting a permanent vocalist, the band invited guest performers for individual tracks:

Production on Gloss Drop was handled by Keith Souza and Seth Manchester alongside Battles. Art direction and design by Dave Konopka. Mastered by Ryan Smith; lacquer cut by Ray Janos (RJ) at Sterling.

The lead single “Ice Cream” / “Sundome” was also released as a standalone 12” (Rise Record Club, 2011), and “My Machines” got a separate single (WARP Records).

Dross Glop (2012, WARPCD226) collected remixes of Gloss Drop tracks. Remixers included Gui Boratto, The Field, Alchemist, Shabazz Palaces, Kode9, Silent Servant, Kangding Ray, Qluster, Brian Degraw (Gang Gang Dance), and Hudson Mohawke. The Dross Glop series also appeared as four separate EPs (Dross Glop 1–4).

La Di Da Di (2015)

La Di Da Di (2015, Warp, WARP CD263) was the second trio album and the first without any guest vocalists — entirely instrumental. Produced by Battles with Keith Souza and Seth Manchester. Mastered by Greg Calbi with additional mastering by Steve Fallone. Photography by Lesley Unruh. Art direction/design by Dave Konopka. Tracklist: “The Yabba,” “Dot Net,” “FF Bada,” “Summer Simmer,” “Cacio E Pepe,” “Non-Violence,” “Dot Com,” “Tyne Wear,” “Tricentennial,” “Megatouch,” “Flora>Fauna,” “Luu Le.”

Juice B Crypts (2019)

Juice B Crypts (2019, Warp, WARP301) returned to the guest vocalist model. Released first as a digital file album (MP3). Discogs credits note Williams (guitar/keyboards) and Stanier (drums/percussion) as “uncredited” instrumentalists — suggesting those credits were added editorially.
