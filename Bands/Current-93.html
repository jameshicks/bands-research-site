The early lineup made Current 93 a direct overlap point between the 23 Skidoo circle and the Psychic TV/Coil underground.

Connection to This Network

Fritz Catlin is the direct personnel bridge between 23 Skidoo and Current 93. He was a co-founder of both bands simultaneously in the early 1980s.

David Tibet had also appeared at WOMAD 1982 playing thigh bone trumpet alongside 23 Skidoo — this performance (recorded) became Side 1 of 23 Skidoo’s The Culling Is Coming LP (1983).

The broader scene overlap: Genesis P-Orridge and Peter Christopherson of Throbbing Gristle produced 23 Skidoo’s Seven Songs; Tibet came from the Psychic TV/TOPY circle that P-Orridge created post-TG.

David Tibet and TOPY

Tibet was a co-founder of TOPY in 1981, alongside Genesis P-Orridge, Peter Christopherson, Geoff Rushton (later John Balance), Stan Bingo, and others. source: TOPY FAQ, ain23.com

In 1982 Tibet appeared in the TOPY promotional film First Transmission, which depicted ritual performances by Tibet, P-Orridge, Balance, Peter Christopherson, and Derek Jarman (narrated by Alan Oversby). source: WRSP, wrldrels.org This film later became controversial when Channel 4’s Beyond Belief (1992) misrepresented its footage.

Tibet departed TOPY in the early-to-mid 1980s — no single source gives an exact year. His departure coincided with his leaving Psychic TV in 1983 and founding Current 93. The stated reason across secondary sources is concern over TOPY’s tendency toward hierarchy and charismatic leadership: Tibet and Balance are consistently cited together as the members who left over this objection, with Christopherson following later (c. 1984). source: WRSP

Note: Wikipedia’s David Tibet article (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Tibet, as of 2026-08-04) does not mention TOPY at all — it addresses only his Psychic TV credits and 1983 departure from PTV. The Wikipedia TOPY article references “later members of Coil and Current 93” without naming Tibet specifically. The TOPY FAQ (ain23.com) is the clearest primary-sourced confirmation of his co-founder status.

Conflict / gap: The precise year Tibet disengaged from TOPY is not confirmed by any source consulted. Secondary sources say “a couple of years in the Temple” before Current 93 (implying c. 1983–84) and “fairly early” relative to the broader membership exodus of the late 1980s. No interview or primary document gives a specific departure date.
