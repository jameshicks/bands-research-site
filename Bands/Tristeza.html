Tristeza

Post-rock / instrumental group. San Diego, California. Discogs ID: 68259. Active 1997–present (with extended quiet periods).

Formed spring 1997 in San Diego. Named with the Spanish/Portuguese word for “sadness” or “melancholy.” Ran through two distinct eras divided by Jimmy LaValle’s departure in early 2003 to focus on The Album Leaf full-time. Known for layered, largely wordless guitar-driven post-rock with melodic complexity and a warm San Diego aesthetic; Spanish-titled songs appear across the catalog from the start.

Foreshadow / Smoke Through Glass (1998, Caffeine vs. Nicotine)

Debut 7”, recorded December 1997 by Gar Wood at his garage. Two tracks: “Foreshadow” and “Smoke Through Glass.” Released spring 1998 on Caffeine vs. Nicotine (cat. c vs. n 002) in a limited pressing. The five-piece lineup — Sprague, LaValle, Hermosillo, Lehner, Swesey — was already assembled.

Spine And Sensory (1999, Makoto Recordings)

Recorded autumn 1998 by Tim Green (2) at Louder Studios, San Francisco. Mastered and edited by Rafter Roberts — a San Diego-scene figure who also appears across Black Heart Procession and other local records. Ten tracks; mood ranges from delicate (“Memphis Emphasis”) to sprawling (“When We Glow,” 8:18). Released April 1999.

Dream Signals In Full Circles (2000, Tiger Style)

Recorded April 2000 at King Size Sound Labs, Chicago (two weeks, mid-tour) by producer Dave Trumfio. Nine tracks; mastered by Mike Hagler. Two notable guests: Bobby Conn (guitar on “I Am A Cheetah”) and Julie Pomerleau (violin and viola on “Opiate Slopes”). Released September 2000; toured Europe and Japan in early 2001 — the band’s first international headline tours. Reissued by Numero Group as a limited LP in 2025.

The Transition (2001–2003)

Stephen Swesey left; Eric Hinojosa joined as touring keyboardist. The band recorded experimental material with Matt Anderson for his Gravity label, released as Mania Phase 12” (Gravity #41, 2002). Pall Jenkins (The Black Heart Procession) co-mixed alongside Tristeza — another San Diego scene overlap. Gabriel Sundy contributed baritone saxophone; Soirac played trumpet. Lacquer cut by JJ Golden.

In early 2003, LaValle played his last gig with the band and left to focus on The Album Leaf. Two things converged: the Sigur Rós tours (two US runs and a European leg) had elevated Album Leaf to a level where managing both bands was no longer viable, and LaValle had developed ideas for where he wanted to take Tristeza musically that the other members didn’t want to pursue. In a 2004 interview he put it directly: “I got too busy with The Album Leaf to stay committed to Tristeza. Also I started wanting to do different things with Tristeza musically and some members didn’t want to add those elements.” On the departure itself: “Tristeza is still together, but I won’t be a part of it anymore. It’s kinda weird, but more power to them. I wish them well.” — measured, not bitter, but not entirely clean either.

The band went on hiatus; Jimmy Lehner moved back to Michigan for a period. The Espuma 12” (Gravity #42, 2003) bridged the gap — mostly unreleased and live material, plus “This Trap,” the first track recorded without LaValle, described in the Discogs profile as “the first song recorded without LaValle.” Also notable: late 2003 demo sessions at Pall Jenkins’s home studio, with Mitch Wilson (guitar) assisting.

A Colores (2005, Better Looking Records)

The first proper LP with the reconstituted lineup: Sprague, Lehner, Hermosillo, new guitarist Alison Ables, new keyboardist Sean Ogilvie. Recorded by Bill Skibbe and Jessica Ruffins; additional recording and mixing by Alan Sanderson; mastered by Mark Chalecki. Gabriel Sundy returns on saxophone (“Wand”). Twelve tracks; the Spanish naming convention continues throughout. All five members credited as co-writers.

Paisajes (2010, Darla / Sanity Muffin / Better Looking)

The final LP to date. An expanded lineup with Cameron Stephens, Chris Hash, Joshua Lindenfelzer, and Marty Dowers joining the core three. Produced, engineered, mixed, and mastered by Tim Green (2) — the same engineer from Spine And Sensory (1999), returning a decade later. Tim Green also played guitar and keyboards on the record. Nine tracks; genres drift toward psychedelic rock and krautrock alongside the post-rock base.

Key Connections

Jimmy LaValle — co-founding guitarist; left early 2003 for The Album Leaf. LaValle also co-founded or was part of the Crimson Curse, Swing Kids, Guyver-One, and other San Diego acts before Tristeza.

Luis Hermosillo — bassist throughout Tristeza’s entire run; also appears as bassist in The Album Leaf’s live band (documented on A Chorus Of Storytellers, 2010).

Rafter Roberts — mastered/edited Spine And Sensory; San Diego multi-instrumentalist also credited on Black Heart Procession’s 2 (Touch And Go, 1999). See Rafter Roberts.

Pall Jenkins — co-mixed Mania Phase (2002) and hosted late-2003 demo sessions at his home studio. San Diego / Touch And Go scene nexus.

Dave Trumfio (King Size Sound Labs, Chicago) — produced Dream Signals In Full Circles. Trumfio’s studio is also associated with Wilco and other Chicago-adjacent acts.

Tim Green (2) (Louder Studios, San Francisco) — recorded Spine And Sensory (1999) and returned as full producer for Paisajes (2010).

Bobby Conn — Chicago art-rock eccentric; guitar on “I Am A Cheetah” (Dream Signals).

Numero Group — reissued Dream Signals In Full Circles as a limited LP in 2025, alongside their reissue of The Album Leaf’s Lines In A Leaf (also 2025). Both Tristeza and Album Leaf entering the Numero catalog simultaneously.
