Connection to This Network

Genesis P-Orridge and Peter Christopherson provided rehearsal space at 10 Martello Street, Hackney to 23 Skidoo and co-produced their debut album Seven Songs (1982, Fetish Records). The sessions were credited pseudonymously as “Tony, Terry and David.”

TG’s soundman Daniel Landin (Stan Bingo) handled live mixing for 23 Skidoo. Landin was also a key member of the experimental collective Last Few Days — which featured Fritz Catlin and toured Europe with Laibach.

10 Martello Street — “The Death Factory”

COUM Transmissions (P-Orridge and Cosey Fanni Tutti) established a squat at 10 Martello Street near London Fields from 1973, after relocating from Hull following police harassment. They named the basement studio “The Death Factory.” Industrial Records used it as its postal address/HQ.

Confirmed other users of the space:

  • 23 Skidoo — rehearsal space until they were evicted; this eviction prompted them to shift focus toward hip-hop and build their own studio, Precinct 23
  • Current 93 (indirectly) — Fritz Catlin bridged both bands; Tibet came from the Psychic TV circle based around P-Orridge
  • Psychic TV — P-Orridge’s post-TG project continued at the same address

The definitive source for 10 Martello Street’s full history is Simon Ford’s Wreckers of Civilisation (Creation Books, 1999) and Cosey Fanni Tutti’s memoir Art Sex Music (2017).
