Smart Went Crazy

Washington, D.C. post-hardcore band, active 1993–1998. Discogs artist ID: 309583. Name variation: SWC. Direct predecessor to Beauty Pill.

Biography

Formation (1993)

Smart Went Crazy formed in Washington, D.C. in 1993. The core of the band was Chad Clark (guitar, vocals), who would go on to become the central figure in the DC underground through his later work with Beauty Pill and his role as an engineer and mastering engineer at Silver Sonya studio (Arlington, VA). Alongside Clark: Abram Goodrich (bass), Jeff Boswell (guitar), Hilary Soldati (cello, vocals), and Devin Ocampo (drums).

The cello as a core instrument rather than a guest ornament gave the band an unusual texture in the DC post-hardcore orbit — closer in some respects to Slint or the more arrangement-conscious end of the Dischord catalog than to the label’s hardcore roots.

Cubbyhole EP (1994)

The debut release, the four-track CD EP Cubbyhole (Cozy Disc, CZD001, 1994), appeared before the band signed to Dischord. Chad Clark self-produced; Don Zientara and Ken Schubert recorded. The lineup credited is Goodrich (bass), Soldati (cello), Tony Dennison (drums — notably listed here, though the vault context places Ocampo as the 1993–1996 drummer; see Open Questions), Boswell (guitar), and Clark (producer, vocals).

Tracks: “A Halo And A Nosebleed” / “Fossils In Ink” / “Pitch Black Postcard” / “Well, You Needn’t”

The EP is tagged Indie Rock on Discogs; format is CD EP only, Cozy Disc being a one-release label in the DB (release count: 1).

Now We’re Even (1996)

The first Dischord release — Now We’re Even (Dischord Records, DIS96V, 1996) — is a 12-track LP (vinyl). Produced jointly by the band (as “SWC”) with Don Zientara handling the bulk of the tracking and producing, and Andy Charneco co-producing tracks A3, B3, and B5.

The lineup expands the sonic palette considerably: Goodrich plays bass, guitar, and voice; Soldati plays cello, organ, voice, and trumpet. Clark’s performance credit is voice, guitar, organ, and piano — no longer just guitar/vocals. Dennison plays drums throughout. Ocampo is not credited on the LP, consistent with the 1993–1996 drum tenure framing (though the Cubbyhole EP credits are anomalous — see Open Questions).

Mastered by David Glasser and John Loder (“Rendered To Spinning Disc”). Photography by Pat Graham (band) and Will Weems (cover). Design by ION Design with assistance from Steve Raskin and Rob Myers.

Tracks: “Best And Final Offer” / “Spy Vs. Spy” / “Null Set” / “I Liked You Better When You Were Sick” / “Sugar In Your Gas Tank” / “That Which Is In The Way” // “Gentleman Caller” / “Garden Variety Hate Song” / “Pallbearer’s Blues” / “Domestic Tension” / “Love Goes Blank” / “Gold Star”

Con Art (1997)

Con Art (Dischord Records, DIS112CD, 1997) is the band’s final and most fully realized statement — an 18-track CD. By this point Ocampo is back in the lineup as drummer; the Dennison/Ocampo drum transition is reflected in the fact that Ocampo is credited here but not on Now We’re Even. This makes the precise years of each drummer’s tenure slightly ambiguous in Discogs (see Open Questions).

Clark’s role expanded dramatically: he is credited with lyrics (tracks 1–4, 6–18), mixed by and mastered by (tracks 1–10, 12–18), and on performance: vocals/crooning, guitar, piano, organ (Farfisa), sampler, and percussion. The additional recording sessions pulled in Don Zientara and Tom Carrol (recorded by). Track-level credits include:

  • Track 5 (“Con Art”): lyrics by Hilary Soldati
  • Track 11 (“Bullfighter”): mixed by Ocampo and Boswell; co-written with Will Eastman
  • Tracks 14 (“Tight Frame Loose Frame”) and 17 (“Tijuana 3/28/96”): “found-sound sculpture” noises by Clark and Michael Spiessbach
  • Track 18 (“Now We’re Even” — 21:46 long track): mixed by J. Robbins (credited as “J. ‘Modest Spice’ Robbins”)

Mastered by David Glasser (credited as “Dave Glasser”). Photography by Pat Graham (band) and Andrea Haffner (flora). The Farfisa organ is shared across Goodrich, Soldati (on Now We’re Even she plays organ), Boswell, and Clark.

The inclusion of a 21-minute closing track — a glacial piece mixed by J. Robbins — signals an expansive and experimental orientation that points toward what Beauty Pill would develop.

The band dissolved in 1998.

Aftermath

After the dissolution, the DC network redistributed:

  • Chad Clark founded Beauty Pill (c. 2000), which has remained his primary vehicle, on Dischord then Butterscotch Records then Northern Spy. He simultaneously became one of the DC scene’s key mastering engineers.
  • Devin Ocampo joined Faraquet (with Jeff Boswell) and Beauty Pill, and later Medications, Deathfix, The Mary Timony Band, and others.
  • Jeff Boswell went into Faraquet (Discogs ID: 309596; active 1998–present; Dischord, Mis En Scene, DeSoto Records). Faraquet’s members are Boswell, Ocampo, and Chad Molter.
  • Abram Goodrich continued as a recurring collaborator on Beauty Pill recordings.
  • Tony Dennison went on to The Caribbean.
  • Hilary Soldati went on to Rouge (3).
