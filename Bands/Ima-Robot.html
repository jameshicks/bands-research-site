Ima Robot

Los Angeles art-rock / new wave band. Discogs ID: 281358. Formed in the late 1990s. Two albums on Virgin Records (Ima Robot, 2003; Monument to the Masses, 2006), plus a third self-released on Werewolf Heart Records (Another Man’s Treasure, 2010). Also known as Imarobot.

History

Origins and Demo Period (late 1990s)

Ima Robot formed in Los Angeles in the late 1990s. A two-track cassette Demo Tape (1998, Not On Label) circulated — labeled on Discogs as Hip Hop/Trip Hop, which likely reflects an early sound or an erroneous genre tag rather than their eventual style. Tracks: “Weirdest Girl” and “Sink or Swim.”

Virgin Records Debut (2003)

The self-titled debut Ima Robot was released on Virgin Records in 2003 (cat. 7243 5 43798 0 2). It was produced entirely by Josh Abraham, with mixing split between Chris Lord-Alge (tracks 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12) and Ryan Williams (tracks 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11). The record was released in the US as a CD (Enhanced format with bonus content).

The album contains 13 tracks including a three-part suite at the end: “What Are We Made From” / “silence” / “Black Jettas.” Styles tagged in Discogs: Indie Rock, New Wave.

Singles released from the album:

  • “Dynomite” (UK, Virgin, 2003) — one of the album’s best-known tracks; lyrics by Ebert, music by Ima Robot
  • “Song #1” (UK, Virgin Records America, Inc., 2003)
  • “Public Access” (UK, Virgin, 2003) — the clearest document of the classic lineup: credits list Meldal-Johnsen (bass, backing vocals, programming), Waronker (drums, percussion, programming), Tim Anderson (guitar, programming, backing vocals), Oliver Goldstein (keyboards, guitar, programming, percussion), Alex Ebert (vocals); produced by Josh Abraham
  • “Alive EP” (US, Virgin, 2003) — Josh Abraham produced the title track; tracks 2–4 self-produced and mixed by the band (as “Mickey P” mixed by Mickey Petralia)

Monument to the Masses (2006)

The second album Monument to the Masses (Virgin, cat. 7243 4 73815 2 2) saw a significant lineup shift and production change. Waronker and Goldstein are absent from the album-level credits; the classic rhythm section was replaced by Scott Devours on drums. Meldal-Johnsen remained credited for bass and programming.

Produced primarily by David Bendeth (tracks 1–5, 7–12), with Bendeth also handling vocals on track 6. Vocal production for additional tracks handled by John Bender (2). Individual tracks also brought in Junior Sanchez and Ray Martin as additional producers/mixers (tracks 1, 6). Mastered by Ted Jensen. Dan Korneff served as engineer.

Ebert is credited throughout as “Edward Sharpe” — the alter-ego that would become the name of his next band.

Track-by-track lyric credits are almost exclusively Ebert, with a co-write on “Creeps Me Out” with Billy Mann and on “The Beat” with Kevin Kadish. Music is credited to Ima Robot throughout, with “Dangerous Life” crediting both Ima Robot and David Bendeth on music.

Roger Joseph Manning Jr. (the Jellyfish / Beck-band keyboardist) plays piano on the album.

Search and Destroy EP (2006)

A self-produced and self-mixed EP released on PrinceHouse Records (2006, US) with Ima Robot credited on mastering, mixing, production, and writing.

Another Man’s Treasure (2010)

The third album Another Man’s Treasure was released on Werewolf Heart Records (2010, US) — the label name listed on Ima Robot’s current Discogs profile, suggesting it may be a band-run imprint. Producers and songwriters credited: Filip Nikolic, Tim Anderson, and Alex Ebert (as co-producer). This is the stripped-down later-era lineup: no Meldal-Johnsen, no Waronker, no Goldstein; Nikolic and Anderson are the core.

The Discogs profile as of the database notes Werewolf Heart Records as their label and lists only Ebert, Nikolic, and Tim Anderson as current members.

Producers

  • Josh Abraham (Discogs ID: 161174, real name Joshua Abraham) — produced Ima Robot (2003) and co-produced the “Public Access” / “Alive EP” singles. A well-established LA-based rock producer (also listed with a Mike Shinoda name variation — likely a Discogs error).
  • David Bendeth — produced and mixed the bulk of Monument to the Masses (2006); also arranged strings. An established rock producer (Paramore, Breaking Benjamin, The Used, among others).
  • Band self-production — tracks 2–4 of the Alive EP, the Search and Destroy EP (2006), and Another Man’s Treasure (2010) were all self-produced, with Nikolic and Anderson taking the lead from 2010 onward.

Connections

  • Alex Ebert — vocalist; credited as “Edward Sharpe” on Monument to the Masses; formed Edward Sharpe And The Magnetic Zeros post-Ima Robot; continued solo under Alex Ebert (solo albums 2011–2023, including film scores)
  • Justin Meldal-Johnsen — bass player on debut era; member of Beck’s core band alongside Joey Waronker; left before the stripped-down 2010 lineup
  • Joey Waronker — drums on debut era; member of Atoms for Peace, Beck’s band, Oasis (2) live touring; departed before Monument to the Masses (Scott Devours replaced him)
  • Roger Joseph Manning Jr. — piano on Monument to the Masses; core member of Beck’s band alongside Meldal-Johnsen and Waronker; formerly of Jellyfish

Open Questions

  • Lars Vognstrup (Discogs ID: 612831) is listed as a band member but has no release credits in the Discogs database — his instrument, tenure, and role are unknown.
  • Oliver Goldstein (Discogs ID: 704991) is listed as a member and plays on the debut-era singles but is absent from Monument to the Masses credits — the circumstances of his departure are undocumented.
  • The 1998 Demo Tape is tagged “Hip Hop / Trip Hop” in Discogs — this is almost certainly a genre mislabel, but the actual sound of those early recordings is not documented.
  • The Discogs profile says the band is managed by “Alexis Rivera” — no other context on this person or their connection.
  • Whether Another Man’s Treasure (2010) was their final release, or whether Ima Robot has continued to record, is not established from Discogs alone.
