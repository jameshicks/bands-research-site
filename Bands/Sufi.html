Overview

Sufi is a duo formed by Maggie Tambala and Rudy Tambala, working in downtempo, ambient, and drum n bass across the mid-to-late 1990s. Their catalogue of five releases ran from 1995 to 1998, with the majority appearing on Secret Agent Records and earlier work issued through Caroline Records and Virgin. Regular studio collaborators across those sessions include Benny Dimassa, Lenny Franchi, and Andy Jenks, with the collective Environmental Science also appearing on multiple credits.

Notes (web)

Why Sufi? The direction shift from A.R. Kane

The proximate cause of Sufi’s formation was the breakdown of A.R. Kane during and after New Clear Child (1994). Rudy Tambala explains in a Tone Glow interview (date: as of 2026-09-28, https://toneglow.substack.com/p/tone-glow-101-ar-kane):

“Alex had moved to America, I was still living in London, and I had to go to America, which was disruptive for me. It was a difficult time for him and me and our spouses and it really had to work the next time around—it had to be better. But it got worse.”

The duo began writing in isolation from each other rather than collaboratively, which Tambala considered a fundamental deterioration: “They stopped writing together and started writing alone and asking the other to ‘lay down some guitar’ or whatever. They became a fucking rock band!” (paraphrase/reported speech from the same interview). A planned fourth album for Luaka Bop (David Byrne’s label) collapsed over artistic disagreement — Tambala could not buy into Alex Ayuli’s proposed direction: “We were supposed to make the next album for David Byrne’s label, and Alex had written what he wanted to do but I couldn’t buy into it. So I was the one who stopped us from working together at that point. I said, ‘No, I’ve got more freedom than that.‘”

His response was to redirect the creative energy toward a project with his sister Maggie Tambala: “It wasn’t working for me, so I just went back and made the Sufi album with my sister to exorcise that.” (Tone Glow interview, https://toneglow.substack.com/p/tone-glow-101-ar-kane)

The word “exorcise” is Tambala’s own framing. Sufi was explicitly an act of catharsis following the creative conflict with Ayuli — not a pre-planned stylistic pivot.

No interview found to date contains Tambala explaining why Sufi landed on downtempo/ambient/drum n bass specifically, as opposed to some other genre break from A.R. Kane. The most plausible contextual reading is that the mid-1990s London electronic scene (drum n bass, trip-hop, ambient) was the sonic environment Tambala was absorbing post-dissolution; Life’s Rising (1995) was released as part of Virgin’s Ambient series and critically placed in that continuum. A Melody Maker “new band” feature ran on 15 July 1995, but the full text of that interview has not been recovered (noted by Facebook page Abstract Analogue, https://m.facebook.com/abstractanalogue/photos/a.210340752851777/1034314110454433/?type=3 — text of the piece not reproduced there).

Tambala has also noted that music-making was a family activity from childhood: “Me, Alex, Maggie, my other brothers, his brothers and sisters — we played together, it was just something we did… music was just another version of play.” (source: via search result paraphrase; original source unclear — treat as weak until confirmed.)
