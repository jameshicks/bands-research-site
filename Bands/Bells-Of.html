Bells Of

Discogs ID: 447089 Origin: Washington, DC, USA Active: 1985–ongoing Also credited as: Bells Of.., Bells Of… Primary label: Teen Beat Records (Teenbeat, label ID: 25701)

Experimental DC project founded in 1985 by guitarist/vocalist Lawrence McDonald (2) (Discogs ID: 1851978). Lawrence McDonald is the sole constant member throughout the band’s history. The project is described on Discogs as “something like song-based free jazz indie-pop” — lightning-quick guitar work, unusual melodies, and a spoken/sung scratchy vocal delivery combining into experimental-improv pop.

Biography

Origins (1985)

Lawrence McDonald — self-described former semi-pro skateboarder and current organic farmer, ceramicist, guitarist, singer, composer, historian, and poet — formed Bells Of in Washington, DC in 1985. The band emerged in the Revolution Summer moment, starting as a punk-slanted outfit with members drawn from DC hardcore bands including Faith (3) and Capital Punishment. The connection to Alec MacKaye (vocalist for Faith) placed the project squarely within the DC hardcore diaspora that was collectively pivoting toward more melodic and experimental territory in that period.

Early-to-mid period: the Teenbeat albums (1992–2000)

Bells Of recorded primarily for Teen Beat Records (the Teenbeat label run by Mark Robinson of Unrest (2), founded in 1984 in Arlington, Virginia). The first album, 11:11 (1992, Teenbeat 93), showcases the band’s expanded, instrumentation-heavy approach: the recording session drew on shifting personnel including two bassists (Ethan Brown, Ian Michaels), two drummers (Fernando Carr, Tom Allnutt), harp (Jason Meggs), multiple keyboard players (Geoff Turner, John Airs, McDonald, Sam Jannotta), saxophone (Charles Bennington), and violin (Dave Van Fiddlbender). McDonald co-produced with Geoff Turner (a different person from the DC engineer of that name); Mark McDonald (4) co-engineered.

The second album, Two Dos Or Not 2? (1994, Teenbeat 143), continued on Teenbeat with a similar rotating-cast approach. McDonald and Geoff Turner co-produced tracks 1–5, 7, 8, and 10–13. Jason Farrell contributed layout; Peter Quinn (8) did cover art. A 13-track album with an untitled closing track running over thirteen minutes.

Four Your Listening Pleasure (1998, Miguel (2) cat# 3, Switzerland) is a Swiss-released collection that assembles recordings featuring the same core players from the early albums — Ethan Brown and Ian Michaels on bass, Fernando Carr and Tom Allnutt on drums, Geoff Turner on keyboards and engineering, Jason Meggs on harp, Charles Bennington on saxophone, Dave Van Fiddlbender on violin, Sam Jannotta on keyboards — alongside McDonald as guitarist, keyboardist, and vocalist. The release on a Swiss label (Miguel (2)) suggests European distribution interest in the project’s accumulated recordings.

3’s Company (2000, Teenbeat 283) marks a shift: McDonald is listed as solely responsible for all arranging, writing, and recording, with Fernando “Nate” Carr on drums and Ian “Bass” Michaels on bass. Two producers appear alongside McDonald — Geoff Turner and Alex Hall (4). Tom Allnutt contributed photography.

Hiatus and return: 2010s

After a decade-long gap in releases, Bells Of returned with Young McDonald & The 5 Season Farmers (2010, Teen-Beat 493), credited to the Bells Of Discogs entry. The lineup stripped down to two: McDonald (vocals, guitar, bass, producer, recorded by) and Tom Allnutt (drums, drum programming, producer, recorded by). Mark Robinson handled design; Peter Quinn (8) continued as visual contributor.

The most recent Discogs-documented release is 00/85 (2015, The Move Sounds TMS003), a 12” vinyl EP released at 45 RPM. This recording introduced a stable three-piece lineup — McDonald (guitar, vocals), Bleu Kopperl (bass), and Peter Wilborn (drums) — and was credited as “Bells Of..” (the two-dot name variation). Mixed by Don Zientara and the band; mastered by Dan Coutant. Jason Farrell contributed graphics and liner notes; Alec MacKaye wrote liner notes; Peter Wilborn and Rich Jacobs (2) also contributed liner notes. Alec MacKaye additionally sang backing vocals on the opening track “Live As You Please.” The Discogs genre/style tags list it as Emo and Punk, reflecting a return to the band’s hardcore-adjacent roots while retaining the experimental core.
