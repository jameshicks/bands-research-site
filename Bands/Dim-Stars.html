Dim Stars

Discogs ID: 273182 Active: 1991–1992 Origin: New York City

NYC supergroup. Members: Don Fleming, Richard Hell, Robert Quine, Steve Shelley, Thurston Moore. Released an EP (1991, Paperhouse Records, 5p) and a self-titled LP (1992, Au Go Go, 14p). Quine is credited on specific tracks of the LP (A2, A4–A6, B3), not the full album.

Notes

From the LP credits (City Slang, 1992): Quine is credited on guitar specifically for tracks A2, A4, A5, A6, and B3 — not the full album. Jad Fair (saxophone) guested on “Rip Off” (B6). Production was collective (credited to “Dim Stars”). Recorded by Jim Waters, assisted by Greg Talenfeld. Mastered by Howie Weinberg.

Formation (web sources)

The project originated as a fiction. Thurston Moore invented “Dim Stars” as a fake band name — alongside another invented act, Mirror/Dash — to make his Ecstatic Peace! label appear more active when placing ads in the fanzine Forced Exposure. After the ads ran, people started enquiring about those records, so Moore felt obliged to bring the band into existence. (Source: Stereogum, “We’ve Got A File On You: Thurston Moore,” stereogum.com)

Moore assembled the core lineup with Steve Shelley and Don Fleming, then recruited Richard Hell as vocalist. Hell had recently written a review (of Goo for SPIN) and visited Sonic Youth in the studio around that time. Moore’s reasoning, in his own words: “Why don’t we get the guy who invented punk rock to be the lead singer?” Hell agreed. Hell recalled Moore telling him he had been a fan of Hell’s earlier band CUZ, which surprised him. (Source: Stereogum interview; Richard Hell quote from furious.com/perfect/richardhell.html)

The project eventually included Robert Quine (Hell’s former Voidoids guitarist) on selected tracks.

The one live show

Dim Stars played a single public performance — a benefit at The Ritz in New York City, on 26 October 1991, at which Sonic Youth also appeared. The Stereogum interview identifies it as a Village Voice benefit; an earlier secondary source (Past Prime) described it as a WFMU benefit. The Stereogum account (Moore speaking directly) is the more authoritative of the two. (Sources: stereogum.com; pastpri.me)

Musical direction

Moore proposed covering a song he considered “one of the greatest songs of all time” — the Dim Stars version of “You Gotta Lose” ran as an eight-to-ten-minute jam, which the triple-7” EP spread across four sides. Moore later described Hell as “very charming, argumentative, and impossible,” and noted that Hell welcomed the sessions partly because he was not actively recording at the time. (Source: Stereogum interview)
