The Fearless Four

Old-school hip-hop group from the South Bronx, New York. Active 1982–c.1987, reunited 1994. Discogs artist ID: 13868.

One of the foundational acts of early hip-hop, recording for Enjoy Records under producer Bobby Robinson before moving to Elektra and Tuff City. Their debut single “Rockin’ It” (1982) became one of the earliest hip-hop releases on a UK independent label when it appeared on Y Records (cat. YT105).

Overview

The Fearless Four formed in the South Bronx in the early hip-hop era, operating in the same scene as Grandmaster Flash & The Furious Five and the Treacherous Three — both fellow Enjoy Records acts under Bobby Robinson. The group comprised four MCs (DLB, The Great Peso, Devastating Tito, Mighty Mike C) and two DJs (Master O.C. and Krazy Eddie).

Their 1982 debut “Rockin’ It” on Enjoy (ER-6028) sampled Kraftwerk — specifically the rhythmic pulse from “Numbers” — making it one of the earliest electro-hip-hop records. The track was written by Bobby Robinson and four members of the group (D. Barksdale, M. Clee, P. Graham, T. Dones) and produced by Robinson. It quickly crossed the Atlantic: Y Records, the London independent run by Disc O’Dell and distributed by Rough Trade, licensed or pressed the track as YT105, placing it in UK record shops alongside post-punk and avant-garde releases by The Pop Group, Pigbag, Diamanda Galás, and Sun Ra.

After two Enjoy singles the group moved to Elektra (1983), where production shifted to Kurtis Blow and the team of M² (3) on the “Problems Of The World” EP. Their Elektra single “Something New” (1983) featured “Just Rock” — built on Gary Numan source material — mixed by Larry Levan at New York’s Paradise Garage, and “Got To Turn Out,” which sampled Gamble & Huff and Grace Jones. DLB and Master O.C. handled arrangements throughout.

A 1984 single on Tuff City (“Dedication”, TUF 120001) was produced by Aaron Fuchs with a “S.u.r.e. Shot Mix” by Master O.C. himself. By 1987 the group appeared on Mercury with “After Tonight” (888 384-1), produced by J.B. Moore, Keith Jackson, and Robert Ford Jr., with a remix by the Latin Rascals.

In 1994 the group reunited for Creepin’ Up On Ya, an LP released on Easylee Records / Wrap Records (WRA 8139-1), produced largely by DLB, Master O.C., and collaborators including Scratch God and Forty Duce. The album included a new recording of “Rockin’ It” and guest vocals from L.A. Sunshine.

Enjoy Records Context

Enjoy Records (Discogs ID: 10149) was founded in 1962 by Bobby Robinson (real name Morgan Clyde Robinson), operating out of Harlem (P.O. Box 1192, NYC 10027). The label had been home to King Curtis and in 1979 began releasing hip-hop, starting with Funky 4 + 1’s “Rappin’ and Rockin’ in the House.” The Fearless Four were among its busiest hip-hop acts (9 releases, 1982). Label contemporaries included:

  • Treacherous Three (14 releases, 1980–81)
  • Grandmaster Flash & The Furious Five (7 releases, 1979–80)
  • Disco Four (6 releases, 1980–82)
  • Doug E. Fresh (3 releases, 1984)
  • Spoonie Gee (3 releases, 1980–85)

Enjoy Records went out of business in 1993.

Y Records Context

Y Records (Discogs ID: 5338) was a London independent run by Disc O’Dell, distributed by Rough Trade. It was primarily known for Bristol post-punk (The Pop Group, The Slits, Pigbag, Maximum Joy) and avant-garde releases (Diamanda Galás, Sun Ra, Shriekback). The Fearless Four’s “Rockin’ It” (YT105) stands as one of the label’s most incongruous-seeming but historically significant releases — hip-hop from the South Bronx reaching UK independent shops through the same network that carried free-jazz and post-punk. The connection likely ran through Bobby Robinson’s US-to-UK licensing relationships rather than a direct Disc O’Dell-to-group relationship. The label also released R.A.P.P.’s “Wicked City” (Y 30, 1982), suggesting O’Dell had some interest in New York funk and rap beyond a single one-off.
