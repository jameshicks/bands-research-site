The Bomb Party

Active: 1984–1989 Based: Leicester, UK Label: Abstract Records, Workers Playtime, Normal Records, Artlos Records Genre: Post-punk, gothic rock, grebo-adjacent Predecessor: Farm Life Discogs artist ID: (see Discogs — search “The Bomb Party”)

Overview

The Bomb Party were a Leicester rock band who formed in 1984 from the ashes of Farm Life. Three Farm Life members reconstituted as The Bomb Party — Andy “Jesus” Mosquera (switching from guitar to vocals), Steve Gerrard (2) (guitar), and Mark Thompson (drums) — with the addition of new bassist Sarah Corina. The name came from a Graham Greene novel (Doctor Fischer of Geneva or the Bomb Party). They debuted in 1985 on Abstract Records and have been described as “The Godfathers of Grebo”, a tag the band themselves rejected. They split in 1989/1990.

History

Formation from Farm Life (1984)

Farm Life disbanded in 1984 after releasing a single (“Susie’s Party”, 1982, Dining Out Records) and having a second single (“Big Country”) withdrawn from Dan Treacy’s Whaam! label. Three members reformed under a new name: Mosquera moved from guitar to vocals, Gerrard remained on guitar, Thompson on drums. New bassist Sarah Corina completed the lineup. The name The Bomb Party was taken from Graham Greene’s novella Doctor Fischer of Geneva or the Bomb Party. The reconstitution with a different lineup and Mosquera’s role change meant this was effectively a new band rather than a simple rebrand. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bomb_Party, as of 2026-08-12]

1985–1986: Abstract Records era

Debuted in 1985 with the Ray Gun EP on Abstract Records, described by press as “a Molotov cocktail of hardcore grebo gothability lying somewhere between The Cramps and Bauhaus.” The debut album Drugs followed in 1986. Despite the “grebo” tag, Mosquera stated: “We’re not grebos and we’re not bikers or any of that shit. We’re experimental.” [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bomb_Party, as of 2026-08-12]

1987: Guitarist change

By 1987, Gerrard had departed and was replaced by Leszek Rataj (guitar and keyboards), who appeared on Liberace Rising (Workers Playtime). The exact date of Gerrard’s departure is unconfirmed; it occurred between the 1986 Drugs LP and the 1987 Liberace Rising LP. Gerrard subsequently became a UK club DJ. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bomb_Party, as of 2026-08-12; search results via Last.fm/Wikipedia]

1988–1990: Final years

Signed to Normal Records for 1988’s “Sugar Sugar” (a cover featuring Voice Of The Beehive) and the 1989 LP Fish. Final album Nativity #3 (Artlos Records, 1990) was recorded before the band’s last live show at The Powerhaus, London, on 17 December 1989.
