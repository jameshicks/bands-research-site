Codeine

Discogs ID: 30585
Origin: New York City
Active: 1989–1994; reunion shows 2012, 2023–2024
Genres: Slowcore, indie rock
Labels: Sub Pop; Glitterhouse Records; Simple Machines; Numero Group (reissues)

New York slowcore band formed 1989. Widely credited as one of the originators of the slowcore/sadcore aesthetic — extremely slow tempos, heavily downtuned guitars, sparse arrangements, and Stephen Immerwahr’s understated vocals over thick, crushing low-end. Active five years, released two LPs and a handful of EPs before disbanding in 1994. Comprehensively reissued by Numero Group in 2012. Reunion shows in 2012 and again 2023–2024.

Frigid Stars LP (1990)

Debut LP. Co-produced with Mike McMackin, who also recorded it. Lacquer cut by Günter Pauler at Glitterhouse Records’ facility. Released simultaneously on Sub Pop (US) and Glitterhouse (Germany/Europe). Chris Brokaw plays both drums and guitar.

Barely Real (1992)

Six-track 12” EP. Guest appearances expand the sonic palette: Jon Fine (of Bitch Magnet) plays noisy guitar on “Jr”; David Grubbs (of Bastro) contributes piano arrangement on “W.” — an early Grubbs–Codeine connection that would deepen on the next record.

Tom 7” (1993)

First recording with Doug Scharin on drums, replacing Brokaw. David Grubbs plays guitar on the A-side, “Tom.” B-side: “Something New.” Produced by Codeine and Mike McMackin; DMM by Günter Pauler.

The White Birch (1994)

Final studio LP. Nine tracks. David Grubbs plays guitar on “Tom” (reprised from the single) and “Wird.” Mastered by Scott Hull (2) at Masterdisk; layout by Jeff Kleinsmith; mixed by Mike McMackin.

When I See The Sun (2012, Numero Group)

Comprehensive retrospective box set. Lacquer cut by Josh Bonati; reissue produced by Ken Shipley and Rob Sevier. Includes remastered versions of all three original LP-era releases plus unreleased material.

Atmosphere B/W Disorder (2024)

Two Joy Division covers released as a 7” on Numero Group, recorded for the reunion.

Member Trajectories

Chris Brokaw

After Codeine, Brokaw went on to Come (2) (Boston noise rock, with Thalia Zedek), The New Year (with the Kadane brothers), Pullman (post-rock supergroup with Casey Rice and James McNew), and Consonant. Extensive solo work and collaborations throughout.

Doug Scharin

Scharin became a connective node in the post-Codeine experimental scene: June Of 44, HiM, Rex, Mice Parade, Enablers.

David Grubbs (guest)

Grubbs came to the Codeine sessions from Bastro (with John McEntire and Bundy K. Brown) and Squirrel Bait; immediately after The White Birch he moved into Gastr Del Sol with Jim O’Rourke.

Breakup and Aftermath

Broke up in 1994 shortly after The White Birch. Brokaw described the band as burned out from touring with no new songs; Immerwahr felt Codeine was “very specific and very finite” — a complete statement. Immerwahr and Engle both considered it done.

Post-breakup: Immerwahr worked briefly as assistant engineer at Sound on Sound Studios (NYC), then moved to a non-music career at the City of New York. Engle went to work at a market-research company. Both largely stepped away from music. The two remained close friends. Brokaw continued in music (Come (2), The New Year, etc.).

The 2012 reunion was prompted by Numero Group’s When I See The Sun box set project.

Dessau (2022) — Lost Second Album

Dessau was recorded in June 1992 at Harold Dessau Recording studio, New York City — intended as the second album, with Chris Brokaw still on drums. Scrapped due to production conflicts. Consists largely of early versions of songs that were later re-recorded for The White Birch with Doug Scharin. Much noisier and more raw than the final LP. Unearthed and released by Numero Group on the recordings’ 30th anniversary, restored with Mike McMackin.

Lexi Mitchell

Lexi Mitchell is not a Codeine member. She co-founded an earlier band (the Lilies, a Jesus and Mary Chain-influenced four-piece) with Stephen Immerwahr at Oberlin College. She and Sooyoung Park co-wrote “New Year’s,” which became a track on Frigid Stars LP. Mitchell subsequently played in Seam (Park’s band). The Oberlin connection — Mitchell, Immerwahr, Park — is a key node for understanding Codeine’s immediate origins.
