Kollaa Kestää

Punk and new wave band from Tampere, Finland (1977–early 1980s). Discogs artist ID: 358688

Biography

Name and Context

The band name is a deliberate echo of one of Finland’s most resonant wartime phrases. During the Winter War (1939–40), Finnish forces held the Kollaa River line against Soviet assault for the entire duration of the war. The phrase Kollaa kestää (“Kollaa holds”) became a national symbol of defiance and resilience. The band adopted it as an irreverent punk statement — the phrase’s weight was culturally inescapable for any Finnish audience.

The debut LP title Jäähyväiset Aseille (“Farewell to Arms”) is a separate reference, evoking Hemingway while also gesturing at the disillusionment running through the post-punk era.

Formation (1977–1978)

Kollaa Kestää was founded in Tampere in 1977 by guitarist/vocalist Kai Kivi, drummer Jyrki Siukonen, and guitarist/vocalist Juha Helminen. Bassist Mikko Nevalainen (alias Vaari) joined after their first single in spring 1978.

The band released their debut single “Musti, Sotakoira” b/w “Tähtien Rauha” in 1978 on Poko Rekords (cat. PIS 004), an early Finnish punk document with no external production credits — recorded by Pekka Nurmikallio.

Later in 1978 came the four-track EP Kollaa Kostaa (Poko Rekords, cat. PIMP 014), produced by Mikko Syrjä (also known as Pantse Syrjä) and recorded by Mika Sundqvist. Notable for its track-by-track scope: A1 is a Finnish-language cover of The Adverts’ “One Chord Wonders” (written by TV Smith), and there are self-referential tracks like “Johnny Rotten Go-Go” and “Ramones & Stravinsky” — the band’s eclectic range audible from the start.

The Debut LP (1979)

Jäähyväiset Aseille appeared on Johanna in 1979 (cat. JHN 2004). Self-produced (credited to “Kollaa”), recorded and mixed by Reino Iso-Aho (credited as Reiska Isoaho). The album expands significantly on the early singles: 14 tracks across two sides, with the band adding keyboards (Kai Kivi), and drawing in outside contributors for colour.

The most striking of these is Lora Logic (real name Susan Carena Whitby), UK saxophonist from X-Ray Spex and Essential Logic, who contributed tenor saxophone and backing vocals across the album. Finnish multi-instrumentalist Jukka Mikkola added tenor saxophone on tracks A3 and A5, melodica and harp on B2. Miika Söderholm also contributed additional backing vocals.

The album includes covers: A1 “Syksy” is a Finnish version of Buzzcocks’ “Ever Fallen in Love” (written by Howard Devoto and Pete Shelley); A3 “Kaupunkisissi” is drawn from Hawkwind material (written by Dave Brock and Robert Calvert). Most other tracks are originals.

Cover artwork was handled by Helminen and Siukonen.

The Discogs profile notes that the band’s interest in musique concrète and jazz attracted Logic and Finnish associate Mikko Saarela to their orbit. By 1979 Logic was extremely active as a session player in the British DIY scene (The Raincoats LP, Red Crayola’s Soldier-Talk, and Swell Maps all fall in this period), and the Rough Trade network is the likeliest vector — but the specific mechanism of how a Finnish band recruited a London-based UK saxophonist has not been confirmed in any English-language source.

A separate single “Kirjoituksia Kellarista” b/w “Kuumat Raiteet” (Poko Rekords, PIS 029) was released the same year, recorded and mixed by Mikko Syrjä.

After the LP (1980–1984)

Individual members remained active on the Johanna label. Jyrki Siukonen produced and performed on releases by other Finnish acts through 1980–1982. Kai Kivi appeared as an additional performer on the Vaaralliset Lelut LP Ystävämme Hyönteiset (Johanna, JHN 2043, 1981), an avant-garde/new wave collective that also featured Jukka Mikkola.

In 1984, Poko Rekords issued the cassette-only compilation Neljän Pennun Ooppera (cat. KEKE 8), bringing together the band’s early single and EP material in re-recorded or compiled form. Production was shared between Pantse Syrjä (overall), Epe Helenius, and Kari Heimonen (both on track A1). Guest performers Jussi Niemi and Seppo Pietikäinen appear on B1. Esko The Bassman (Jouni Hirvonen) plays bass on A1 alongside Nevalainen.

The band split in the early 1980s.

Reissues (1995, 2012, 2020–2021)

In 1995, Johanna released Kokonaan: Jäähyväiset Aseille (a full expanded retrospective), with liner notes by Jukka Junttila. Svart Records issued remastered vinyl reissues of Jäähyväiset Aseille, Kirjoituksia Kellarista, and Kollaa Kostaa in 2012, with further pressings of the debut LP in 2020.

Neljän Pennun Ooppera was reissued by Svart Records in 2021.

In 2010, Combat Rock Industry issued a retrospective pressing of “Musti, Sotakoira.”

Scene Context

Kollaa Kestää were part of a short-lived but internationally significant Finnish punk and new wave moment centred on Poko Rekords and Johanna in Tampere and Helsinki. Poko Rekords (parent: Universal Music Oy) was the main hub for Finnish punk circa 1977–1981 with over 2,900 releases; Johanna (parent: Love Kustannus Oy) leaned toward adventurous new wave and post-punk.

The Discogs profile credits them as influencing later Finnish bands including Liikkuvat Lapset and Sielun Veljet, and positions them as “the minimalist answer” to the more expansive jazz-punk of contemporary act Se (led by a musician identified as Jari).

Jyrki Siukonen went on to work as producer and performer on numerous Johanna-label releases through the early 1980s, and all four core members appear on later Finnish rock projects. Kai Kivi and Juha Helminen both joined Cenci post-Kollaa; Mikko Nevalainen became a member of Eppu Normaali, one of Finland’s most commercially successful rock acts.

Open Questions

  • Lora Logic connection mechanism — Discogs cannot establish how Logic came to record in Finland in 1979. The Rough Trade/DIY network is the likely vector (she was actively sessioning across British post-punk in 1979), but no English-language source confirms who initiated contact, whether she travelled to Helsinki, or when the session took place. Needs Finnish-language archival sources or an interview.
  • Miika Söderholm — credited for backing vocals on Jäähyväiset Aseille (Discogs ID: 531180) but not listed among the band’s formal members. Role unclear beyond this session credit.
  • Mikko Saarela — named in the Discogs profile as a jazz collaborator, listed as a group member (Discogs ID: 519623). No releases visible under this credit in the current dataset.
  • Erkki Huhtamo — listed as fringe member (Discogs ID: 4108887); no release credits found. Possibly the Finnish media scholar of the same name.
  • Live activity and touring — no live recordings or documented concerts in the Discogs dataset.
  • “Jäähyväiset Aseille” LP studio — the Lora Logic page (sourced from a Finnish blog) states the album was recorded at Finnvox studio in Helsinki. Not confirmed directly from the Discogs record, which credits only Reino Iso-Aho as recorder/mixer with no studio named.
