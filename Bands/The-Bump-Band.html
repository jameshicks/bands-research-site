The Bump Band

Ian McLagan’s backing band, based in Austin, Texas. Active from c. 1981 (first credited on Bump In The Night) through McLagan’s death on December 3, 2014. Discogs artist ID: 1406645. Also credited as “Bump Band” and “Ian McLagan’s Bump Band.”

Overview

The Bump Band was Ian McLagan’s primary touring and recording vehicle across three decades. The name first appeared on Bump In The Night (Mercury, 1981), McLagan’s second solo album, with a Los Angeles-assembled lineup. After McLagan relocated to Austin, Texas, around 1994, the band reconstituted around a rotating cast of Austin-scene musicians and became a fixture of the city’s roots-rock and Americana circuit. From 2000 onward all McLagan studio albums were co-credited to the Bump Band.

Membership was never fixed; McLagan functioned as the constant center (keyboards, vocals) around which different configurations of guitarists, bassists, and drummers rotated across tours and recording sessions. Two names appear most consistently in the Discogs record across the 2000s and 2010s: guitarist Jud Newcomb (“Scrappy Jud Newcomb”) and drummer Conrad Choucroun.

The band’s profile beyond Austin was bolstered by McLagan’s pre-existing connections to the Faces and Rolling Stones orbits — guest appearances by Ron Wood and the recurring presence of veterans like Ricky Fataar bridged the Bump Band’s records to a broader rock-heritage audience.

History

Formation — Los Angeles, 1981

The Bump Band unit was assembled for McLagan’s second solo LP Bump In The Night (Mercury, 1981), produced by Rob Fraboni. The core lineup on that record was:

  • Ian McLagan — guitar, keyboards, lead vocals
  • Johnny Lee Schell — lead guitar, vocals
  • Ricky Fataar — drums, percussion, vocals, piano (also bass on track B4)
  • Ray Ohara — bass

Guest appearances on Bump In The Night included Ron Wood (duelling lead guitar and bass on track A1) and Bobby Keys (horns, track B4), drawing directly on McLagan’s Faces and Rolling Stones touring circles. Renée Geyer contributed guest vocals on track B5. Fraboni, who had produced McLagan’s debut Troublemaker (1979), maintained continuity across the first two solo albums.

The Bump Band unit was then engaged to back Bonnie Raitt on her 1982 album Green Light (Warner Bros.) — the same core lineup of Schell, Fataar, and Ohara — and Renée Geyer’s self-titled US album (Portrait, 1982), where Fataar co-produced and McLagan played keyboards. These early 1980s credits show the Bump Band functioning as a flexible Los Angeles session unit beyond McLagan’s own projects.

1985 gap

McLagan’s third solo album Last Chance To Dance (Barking Dog, 1985) carries no Bump Band credit on Discogs. Whether the band was inactive or simply uncredited is unclear from the DB record.

Turn Faces and the Austin era (2000)

By 2000, when McLagan released Turn Faces (Hypertension, Germany) — the first album since 1985 to credit him as a primary artist with the Bump Band — the lineup had shifted entirely to Austin-based musicians:

  • Jud Newcomb — acoustic guitar, guitar, lead guitar, slide guitar, vocals
  • Gurf Morlix — acoustic guitar, guitar, producer, mixed by, vocals
  • Don Harvey — drums, percussion
  • Sarah Brown (7) — bass, vocals

Guest appearances on Turn Faces included Ron Wood (acoustic guitar, electric guitar, vocals, executive producer on tracks 3 and 12) and Billy Bragg (vocals, track 1). The album was engineered by Stuart Sullivan, who became the recurring engineer for McLagan’s Austin recordings. Morlix produced and co-mixed the record alongside McLagan and Sullivan.

Rise & Shine! (2004) and live releases

Rise & Shine! (Gaff Music, 2004) was McLagan’s first self-produced co-credit with the Bump Band. He handled production, engineering, photography, and all vocals and keyboards himself. No individual Bump Band members are separately listed in Discogs additional credits for this release beyond McLagan’s own multi-role credit — the band is credited as a unit.

Two live albums appeared in 2004–2005:

  • Bump Live (Maniac Records, 2004) — lineup: Jud Newcomb (guitar, vocals), Mark Andes (bass, vocals), Don Harvey (drums, vocals), McLagan (organ, piano, vocals). Produced and engineered by Walter Morgan. Mastered by Stuart Sullivan.
  • Extra Live (Evangeline Records, 2005) — same core lineup (Newcomb, Andes, Harvey, McLagan). Tracks produced and engineered by Walter Morgan (most tracks) and Bill Johnson (8) (tracks 5 and 13).

Maniac Records (10) (Discogs label ID: 841683) also issued a US pressing of Never Say Never (2009) and a compilation Best Of British (undated) credited to McLagan and the Bump Band, alongside reissuing Bump In The Night (2005).

Never Say Never (2008)

Never Say Never (Proper Records (2), 2008) is credited to “Ian McLagan & The Bump Band” but carries no additional personnel credits in the Discogs record beyond the primary artists. The European release came out via Proper Records; a US pressing appeared on Maniac Records (10) in 2009.

United States (2014) — final studio album

United States (Yep Roc Records, 2014) is McLagan’s last studio album, released the year of his death. It is also the most thoroughly credited Bump Band record in Discogs:

  • Ian McLagan — acoustic guitar, electric guitar, organ, piano, vocals
  • Jud Newcomb (“Scrappy Jud Newcomb”) — acoustic guitar, electric guitar, slide guitar, twelve-string guitar, vocals
  • Conrad Choucroun — drums, percussion, vocals
  • Jon Notarthomas — bass, vocals
  • Leigh Mahoney — violin

Newcomb’s credited role here as co-vocalist and guitarist on acoustic, electric, slide, and twelve-string guitar indicates a role substantially beyond conventional sideman. The late-period lineup was a stripped-down trio-plus of McLagan, Newcomb, and Choucroun, with Notarthomas holding the bass chair previously occupied by multiple different players.

McLagan died in Austin on December 3, 2014, following a stroke.

Live Baden-Baden 2000

Live Baden-Baden 2000 (Not On Label, date unspecified) is a release credited jointly to “Not On Label (Ian McLagan)” and “Not On Label (The Bump Band).” It captures a performance from 2000, coinciding with the Turn Faces era. No individual personnel credits are listed in Discogs.

Registered Discogs members (various periods)

The Bump Band’s Discogs entry lists the following as members: Conrad Choucroun, Don Harvey, George Reiff, Gurf Morlix, Ian McLagan, Johnny Lee Schell, Jud Newcomb, Mark Andes, Ray Ohara, Ricky Fataar, Sarah Brown (7). George Reiff (Discogs ID: 916972) appears on the membership list but is not individually credited on any Bump Band release in the Discogs DB; he was an active Austin bassist (Cotton Mather, Charlie Sexton Sextet) so the connection is plausible but undocumented in the release record.
