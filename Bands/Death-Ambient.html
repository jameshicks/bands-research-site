Death Ambient

New York experimental trio active 1995–2007. Discogs artist ID: 381015.

Overview

Death Ambient was formed in New York City in 1995 by Japanese bassist Kato Hideki and Japanese-born electronic musician Ikue Mori, who then invited English guitarist Fred Frith to join. All three were already well-established figures in the downtown experimental and free improvisation scene.

Kato Hideki (born 1962, Nagoya) had been a founding member of the Tokyo noise collective Ground Zero (3) and relocated to New York in 1992, where he began working with John Zorn, Marc Ribot, and Christian Marclay. Ikue Mori had come to New York from Tokyo in 1977 and rose to prominence as the drummer for the no wave band DNA (4); after DNA disbanded she abandoned acoustic drums entirely and began modifying drum machines to play samples, aiming (in her words) to make them “sound broken.” Fred Frith, a founding member of the British avant-rock group Henry Cow, had lived in New York for fourteen years before the group formed and had a broad catalogue of solo and collaborative work on labels including Ralph Records, RecRec, and Tzadik.

According to Mori, the aim of Death Ambient was “to create sounds and texture extravaganza.” Their debut album was recorded at Green Point Studio in Brooklyn and released the same year on Tzadik’s New Japan series, produced by John Zorn. The music consisted of freely improvised pieces blending Mori’s drum-machine samples with Frith’s guitar and Kato’s bass.

In 1997 American guitarist James Plotkin temporarily replaced Frith for a UK tour including concerts in London (broadcast on BBC Radio 3’s Mixing It) and Stirling. Frith rejoined in 1998, and the trio performed together outside the studio for the first time in Halle, Germany. Material from that concert became the basis of the second album, Synæsthesia (1999), recorded in New York in June 1999 with engineer/mixer Martin Bisi at BC Studio.

The third album, Drunken Forest (2007), came after six years, with percussionist Jim Pugliese appearing as a guest throughout. Kato produced and mixed the record; it was recorded at Guerrilla Recording (Oakland) by Myles Boisen and “Shadow World” by Kato himself. The trio performed the album at John Zorn’s venue The Stone on May 4–5, 2007. Sources (Wikipedia, Discogs) do not indicate any further activity after 2007.
