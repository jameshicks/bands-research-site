Overview

Gladys Knight And The Pips were an Atlanta-born R&B and soul group whose recording career stretched from the early 1960s through the 1980s, with their catalogue heaviest in the 1970s. The group comprised Gladys Knight alongside family members Merald Knight and Brenda Knight, plus longtime members Edward Patten and Langston George, and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996 as Performers. Their label trajectory ran from Motown-family imprints — Soul and Tamla Motown — through Buddah Records (their most prolific period) and later Columbia and MCA. Principal production collaborators included Norman Whitfield, Tony Camillo, Johnny Bristol, and Curtis Mayfield, across a sound that encompassed soul, R&B, funk, disco, and ballads.
