The Sisterhood

The Sisterhood

Aug 11, 20267 min read

The Sisterhood

Discogs ID: 115235
Origin: UK
Active: 1986 (one release only)
Label: Merciful Release
Style: Electronic / Goth Rock / Industrial / Synth-pop

A one-off project assembled by Andrew Eldritch (Sisters of Mercy) in early 1986, existing solely to deny former bandmates Wayne Hussey and Craig Adams the right to trade under the name “the Sisterhood” as they formed what would become The Mission. Eldritch described the project explicitly as “a weapon in this corporate war.” Its sole output — the single “Giving Ground” and the LP Gift — were released on Eldritch’s own Merciful Release imprint. Neither was intended as a serious creative statement; both were subsequently killed off. Eldritch later recycled two of the compositions for the Sisters of Mercy’s 1987 comeback.

Origin and Context

Sisters of Mercy Mk II collapse (mid–late 1985)

Sisters of Mercy released their debut LP First and Last and Always in March 1985 through Merciful Release / WEA, followed by a headlining UK tour culminating at the Royal Albert Hall on 18 June 1985. In the aftermath, Eldritch relocated to Hamburg. That August and September he took Wayne Hussey to Hamburg-Bramfeld to compose material for a second album. The sessions were unproductive: Eldritch rejected Hussey’s song ideas and had him work on single chords. Hussey later recalled: “I went to Hamburg for a month with Andrew to try and write songs for the second Sisters album, and we came back with all my ideas rejected and Andrew’s very skeletal.” (Wikipedia, “First and Last and Always”, as of 2024-06-27)

Back in England in October 1985, rehearsals broke down further. When Eldritch proposed unconventional chord progressions, Craig Adams walked out, with Hussey following. The band effectively dissolved.

Hussey and Adams plan “the Sisterhood”

By the end of 1985, Hussey and Adams had assembled a new four-piece: drummer Mick Brown (ex-Red Lorry Yellow Lorry) and guitarist/keyboardist Simon Hinkler (ex-Artery (2)). The group chose the name “the Sisterhood” as a deliberate nod to their Sisters of Mercy roots. They announced their live debut: 20 January 1986, Alice in Wonderland venue, London.

The Name Dispute

Eldritch’s counter-move

When Eldritch learned of Hussey and Adams’s plans to trade as the Sisterhood, he moved immediately. His own account: “They began to claim rights to [the Sisters name], which patently had to be stopped. And when they wanted to be called the Sisterhood, there was nothing I could do but be the Sisterhood before them — the only way to kill that name was to use it, then kill it.” (Wikipedia, “The Sisterhood”, as of 2024-06-27)

Eldritch registered a company under the Sisterhood name. There was also a commercial dimension: RCA Records reportedly offered £25,000 — whichever party released material under the name first would secure the advance (and by extension, a claim on the name). Eldritch accepted the challenge. (post-punk.com)

“Giving Ground” single — 20 January 1986

In approximately five days, Eldritch recorded the single “Giving Ground,” co-written with Merciful Release manager Boyd Steemson and co-produced with Lucas Fox. Because Eldritch remained under contract to WEA as a vocalist, he could not sing on the record without triggering WEA’s options. James Ray (of Gangwar) provided lead vocals instead. The single was pressed and released on 20 January 1986 — the same day Hussey and Adams played their debut gig as the Sisterhood. It entered the UK Indie Chart at No. 1 on 15 February 1986. James Ray later said of the track: “It’s a terrible track.” (Wikipedia, “The Sisterhood”, as of 2024-06-27)

Outcome: Hussey/Adams become The Mission

The pre-emptive release effectively blocked Hussey and Adams from the name. They announced the rebrand to The Mission in late February 1986. Hussey attributed the name to a “proselytising mission” from his Mormon background; drummer Mick Brown attributed it to his preferred speaker brand. (Wikipedia, “The Mission”, as of 2024-06-27)

Legal dimension

No formal legal action appears in the public record. The dispute was resolved strategically rather than through litigation: Eldritch’s company registration and the prior-release of the single established his claim, and Hussey/Adams accepted the outcome and rebranded. The £25,000 RCA advance went to Eldritch by virtue of being first to release an album under the name.

Gift LP (July 1986)

Eldritch recorded the album in approximately one week to claim the RCA advance. He later described it as “a techno record” by 1986 standards. The title carries a deliberate double meaning: Gift in German means “poison.” His summary: “The Sisterhood album was a weapon in this corporate war. That’s why I called it Gift (in German: poison).” (post-punk.com; louderthanwar.com)

Reception and aftermath

The album received universally negative reviews and failed commercially. RCA subsequently dissolved their publishing contract with Eldritch and retained The Mission instead. The album went out of print and remained so for over 25 years. A reissue on Cadiz Music (in conjunction with Merciful Release) was released 26 May 2023, remastered from original tapes, on limited coloured vinyl, digipack CD, cassette, and digital (its first digital release). (slicingupeyeballs.com; side-line.com)

The official Sisters of Mercy website later described Gift as “a classic” that “parallelled the New Beat of the Continental avant-garde.” (Wikipedia, “The Sisterhood”, as of 2024-06-27)
