The Moog Cookbook

Los Angeles comedy/novelty synthesizer duo. Discogs artist ID: 45679. Active 1996–1997 (releases); disbanded 1998. An independent release of previously unreleased material appeared in 2005 under The Moog Cookbook name.

Overview

The Moog Cookbook were Roger Joseph Manning Jr. (credited as Uli Nomi; Discogs alias ID: 784479) and Brian Kehew (credited as Meco Eno; Discogs alias ID: 784480), two Los Angeles keyboardists who met and immediately recognized a shared obsession with the novelty Moog records of the late 1960s and early 1970s — the genre popularized by Moog-cover acts like Meco and Walter Carlos. Their concept: recreate contemporary rock and pop radio hits entirely on vintage synthesizers, in the spirit (and with the deadpan commitment) of those earlier records.

Manning recalled: “When Brian and I finally met, we knew we had to do this, because we knew we could do it right, and we knew we had the resources — before someone else did it, and did it wrong.” (Wikipedia, Roger Joseph Manning Jr. article)

The duo performed and promoted in space-suit disguises — a presentation coincidentally similar to that adopted around the same time by Daft Punk, reportedly without either group being aware of the other. Manning subsequently noted: “As creatively fulfilling as it was, Moog Cookbook was not financially viable. We weren’t coming out of rave culture and house music like Daft Punk.” (Wikipedia)

Music journalist Brian Chidester described the band as having “yielded solid overseas sales amidst the retro-obsessed landscape of Pulp Fiction, the Swing revival and thrift shop mania. The duo even… found a small domestic audience attuned to similar electronic psych-pop coming out of Europe by bands like Stereolab, Mouse on Mars and the High Llamas.” (Wikipedia)

The project was conceived directly after the dissolution of Manning’s previous band Jellyfish (2) in 1994. Kehew — already an active sound engineer and vintage synthesizer collector — brought deep technical knowledge of period-correct instruments, and both are credited on the releases as Arranger, Performer, and Recorded By (via their Uli Nomi / Meco Eno aliases), with the duo jointly supervising as their real names. All releases appeared on Restless Records (Discogs ID: 653), an LA-based independent then distributed under parent Rykodisc.

The second album features guest synthesizer and voice credits from several notable musicians, as listed above. Mark Mothersbaugh (of Devo) contributes a Davoli Synt solo on “Ain’t Talkin’ ‘Bout Love”; Michael Penn handles computer vocals on the same track; Wayne Kramer (MC5) plays guitar synthesizer on “Whole Lotta Love”; Charlotte Caffey (The Go-Go’s) plays synthesizer on “25 Or 6 To 4”; Lyle Workman plays guitar synthesizer on “Rock And Roll All Night”. E (4) (Eels) provides the astronaut voice intro on “Whole Lotta Love”.

Production credits (vol. 2): Art Direction and Digital Art by Lisa Sutton (2); Mastered By Dean Phelps; Photography by Vicki Berndt.

Connections

  • Roger Joseph Manning Jr. — co-founder; alias Uli Nomi; post-Jellyfish project; continued to solo, Beck band, session work, The Lickerish Quartet
  • Brian Kehew — co-founder; alias Meco Eno; keyboardist, producer, engineer; extensive studio credits including co-producing Fiona Apple’s Extraordinary Machine; wrote Recording The Beatles (2006) with Kevin Ryan; live keyboardist for The Who (2006–07 tours)
  • Jellyfish (2) — Manning’s previous band; Moog Cookbook formed directly after Jellyfish disbanded (1994)
  • Imperial Drag — Manning’s other immediate post-Jellyfish project (1995–1997), running concurrently
  • Restless Records — releasing label; an LA-based independent (parent: Rykodisc); also home to They Might Be Giants, Flaming Lips, Devo, and Diamanda Galás, among others
  • AIR — French electronic duo whose 10,000 Hz Legend (2001) features both Manning (keyboards) and Kehew (engineer) independently; Kehew also did earlier live work with AIR

Research Queue

  • Wikipedia’s Roger Joseph Manning Jr. article names the second Moog Cookbook record as Ye Olde Space Bande (1997), while Discogs credits the primary release as Plays The Classic Rock Hits (1997, Restless, cat 01877-72941-2). Confirm whether “Ye Olde Space Bande” was an alternate subtitle or a distinct version — one Discogs page or sleeve photo would resolve this.
  • The 2005 Bartell release (Cookbook Kitschen Records) has only one pressing in Discogs with no tracklist or personnel credits beyond the duo. Was this a CD-R / limited run, and what material does it contain?
  • Mark Mothersbaugh’s Davoli Synt credit on “Ain’t Talkin’ ‘Bout Love” is an unusual cameo — was this a planned collaboration or a coincidence of studio access? An interview with Manning or Kehew from the 1996–1997 period might confirm.
