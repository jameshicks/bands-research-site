The Shamen

Oct 18, 20269 min read

The Shamen

Scottish psychedelic-turned-electronic band, formed Aberdeen 1985 (initially as Alone Again Or). Discogs artist ID: 7297. One of the UK’s earliest and most commercially successful acts to bridge indie-rock and rave culture, the band evolved from jangling C86-adjacent guitar pop into a fully electronic outfit by 1989–1990. Core members across all eras: Colin Angus (vocals, electronics, songwriter) and bassist/vocalist Will Sin (William Sinnott, joined 1987; drowned May 1991) and rapper/vocalist Mr. C (Richard West, from 1989). Signed to One Little Indian from 1989 and Epic for US releases from 1992. Fifteen UK singles chart entries, five top-ten, including the UK #1 “Ebeneezer Goode” (1992).

History

1985–1986: Alone Again Or

Colin Angus, Derek McKenzie, and Keith McKenzie formed in Aberdeen under the name Alone Again Or (Discogs ID: 109087), with Pete Stephenson on guitar. The early sound was psychedelic indie rock. First release: a cassette demo Wayward Wednesday In May Affair (Skipping Kitten, 1986, distributed within the Aberdeen scene). The band changed its name to The Shamen in late 1985, shortly before its first formal vinyl release.

1986–1988: Early Moksha era

First vinyl as The Shamen: “Young Till Yesterday / World Theatre” (Moksha Recordings, 1986, SOMA 1T). Two further early singles (“Christopher Mayhew Says,” “Something About You,” “Knature Of A Girl”) and the debut LP Drop (1987) followed on Moksha Recordings. “Christopher Mayhew Says” (1987) used sampled dialogue from the politician and LSD experimenter Christopher Mayhew — an early marker of the band’s drug-culture interests.

Drop (1987) was produced with Wilf Smarties and Mike Hedges (Discogs: 94149). Derek McKenzie, unimpressed with Colin Angus’s growing interest in electronic music and sampling, left in 1987 to return to university. Pete Stephenson departed around the same time.

Will Sin (William Sinnott) joined as bass player in October 1987, and the duo — Angus and Sin — became the core creative unit. The Moksha single “Jesus Loves Amerika” (1988, on Ediesta Records) introduced the band’s electronic-rock hybrid publicly. The Italian release Strange Day Dreams (Materiali Sonori, 1988) collected early material for the European market.

1989: Transition — Phorward and One Little Indian

The mini-album Phorward (Moksha Recordings, 1989) was the hinge point: produced by Colin and Will, with Eddie Richards (aka Evil Eddie Richards) contributing additional beats and remix work. Phorward discarded almost all guitar and embraced sampling, sequencing, and dance beats. The Shamen fell in with the developing London rave scene — connecting with Paul Oakenfold, Orbital, Mixmaster Morris, and Eddie Richards via the Synergy tour.

First One Little Indian releases: the single “Omega Amigo” (OLI, 1989) and “Pro>gen” (OLI, 1989), which would become the band’s biggest hit after remixing in 1991.

In Gorbachev We Trust (Demon Records, 1989) was the second full LP and the last before the definitive stylistic shift. Production assistance came from Brian Pugsley (who would become the band’s house engineer), Derek Birkett (OLI founder, credited as production assistant), Eddie Richards, and others.

1990–1991: En-Tact and indie-dance peak

En-Tact (One Little Indian, 1990) — the breakthrough. Self-produced by The Shamen, engineered by Brian Pugsley, Ian Tregoning, Mark Angelo Lusardi, and Steve Osborne (later of Paul Oakenfold & Steve Osborne / Perfecto). Design by Me Company (Paul White, Discogs: 547352). Vocalist Plavka (Plavka Coleridge, Discogs: 29921) contributed notably — her lead on “Hyperreal” became the single’s signature. She stayed in the lineup only until 1991. Mr. C (Richard West, rapper/MC) and Plavka were brought in to increase live vocal presence.

En-Tact singles: “Make It Mine” (1990), “Hyperreal” (1991), and — most significantly — “Pro>gen” / “Move Any Mountain,” which was remixed as “Move Any Mountain – Progen 91” and reached UK #4.

Will Sin drowned on 23 May 1991 in Tenerife, immediately after filming the “Move Any Mountain” promo video. His death cast a long shadow over the band’s subsequent success.

1992–1993: Boss Drum — commercial zenith

After a necessary break, Angus and Mr. C reconvened. Boss Drum (One Little Indian / Epic, 1992) was a transatlantic hit, going platinum in the UK. Personnel on the US pressing (Discogs: 49459): Mr. C (vocals, electronics), Angus (vocals, guitar, programmed by), Gavin Knight (live drums), Bob Breeks (live keyboards), Jonathan Richard Sharpe (occasional analogue electronics). Mixed by Pugsley and the band; produced by The Shamen.

Singles from Boss Drum: “LSI (Love Sex Intelligence)” (feat. Jhelisa Anderson), “Ebeneezer Goode” (UK #1, September 1992 — “E’s are good” drug reference acknowledged in the profile), “Phorever People,” “Boss Drum,” “Different Drum,” “Comin’ On.”

“Re: Evolution” (One Little Indian, 1993) was a spoken-word / live mini-release co-credited to The Shamen with Terence McKenna (ethnobotanist and psychedelic thinker), who provided vocal extracts. Produced and mixed by the band and Brian Pugsley.

1995–1998: Axis Mutatis, Hempton Manor, UV

Axis Mutatis (Epic / One Little Indian, 1995) moved toward ambient techno and global sounds. Associated live album Arbor Bona Arbor Mala accompanied it. Collaborators on mid-period work included Nüw Idol (Michel Spiegel, Discogs: 2902), Jono Podmore (Discogs: 37274, aka Kumo), Miquette Giraudy (Discogs: 39787), Steve Hillage (Discogs: 4452), and William Latham (computer graphics artist, Discogs: 1545037) who provided visual work.

Hempton Manor (One Little Indian, 1996) and UV (Moksha Recordings, 1998) followed — the band had returned to their own Moksha label for the final period. Singles continued on OLI through 1996 (“Move Any Mountain ‘96”). “Universal” and a final single “U Nations” on Moksha bookended the catalog.

The Shamen ceased activity around 1998 after UV.

Post-dissolution

Colin Angus continued under the Nemeton project (Discogs: 403427) with Mr. C, and through Moship (Discogs: 12862925) and Pablo Sandoz (Discogs: 1333168). His F Troop group (Discogs: 47685) also appears in his Discogs group credits.

Mr. C (Richard West) developed a substantial solo DJ/producer career, founding End Recordings (from 1998) and Superfreq Records (from 2004), releasing techno and deep house under his own name. He also recorded as Mantrac and Unity (4). Active through at least 2017 on Superfreq.

Victoria Wilson-James (alias Glamazon, Discogs: 31934), who provided vocals on later releases including “Destination Eschaton” and “Transamazonia,” was also a member of Soul II Soul and the Avitas group.
