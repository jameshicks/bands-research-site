Camouflage

German synth-pop trio from Bietigheim-Bissingen, Baden-Württemberg. Formed 1983 (initially as “Lizenenced Technology”), active 1985–present. Discogs artist ID: 36581. Known internationally for the 1987 breakout single “The Great Commandment” and five albums on Metronome through the early 1990s. Core members: Marcus Meyn, Heiko Maile, and Oliver Kreyssig.

Overview

Formation and Early Years (1983–1987)

Heiko Maile, Oliver Kreyssig, Marcus Meyn, and a fourth member, Martin Kähling, founded the band under the name “Lizenenced Technology” in 1983. Kähling departed in 1984, and the remaining trio renamed themselves Camouflage — drawing on the song “Camouflage” (composed by Yukihiro Takahashi) from Yellow Magic Orchestra’s 1981 album BGM (Alfa Records). Yellow Magic Orchestra (YMO) is the pioneering Japanese electronic trio of Haruomi Hosono, Ryuichi Sakamoto, and Yukihiro Takahashi; Discogs artist ID a22061. (Wikipedia, Camouflage band article as of 2026-05-26; BGM album tracklist confirmed via Wikipedia as of 2026-05-26) Their first local live performance was in 1984. The following year they recorded two demo cassettes in their own studio and circulated them to record labels.

In 1986 one of those tapes was entered into a talent competition run by Hessischer Rundfunk (Hessen Radio). Camouflage won first place, attracting the attention of the small Frankfurt label Westside. “The Great Commandment” was reworked, re-recorded, and shopped to major labels, triggering a bidding situation. The band signed with Metronome (label ID: 5870, a Warner Music Group imprint).

Metronome Era — Debut and International Breakthrough (1987–1989)

“The Great Commandment” was released as a single in 1987 (Mega Records in Scandinavia) and became a significant hit across Europe and in the US, where it was issued on Atlantic Records. The debut album Voices & Images (1988, Metronome 835 437-2) was produced by Camouflage and Alexander “Axel” Henninger, who also recorded and mixed. Heiko Maile served as co-producer on most tracks.

The second album Methods of Silence (1989, Metronome 839 613-2) introduced Belgian electronic producer Dan Lacksman (of Telex) as a co-producer alongside Camouflage and Axel Henninger on select tracks. This collaboration continued through the early 1990s.

Meanwhile and Peak Commercial Period (1991–1993)

The third album Meanwhile (1991, Metronome 849 140-2) marked a production shift: Colin Thurston (known for his work with Duran Duran and Magazine) co-produced and mixed alongside the band, with Tom Colley serving as creative engineer and mixer. Recording sessions drew on additional studio players including Ingo Ito (previous recordings), with mix assistance from Ron Aslan and Eugene Ellis. Meanwhile was the band’s commercial high-water mark in Germany.

The fourth album Bodega Bohemia (1993, Metronome 517 703-2) brought back Dan Lacksman as co-producer with Heiko Maile. The album featured backing vocals from Julie Ocean (aka Petra Fenn), electric violin from Julian Boyd, guitar and loops from Ingo Ito, and drum programming by Thomas Dörr.

RCA and Label Transitions (1995–1999)

Spice Crackers (1995, RCA 74321 29261 2) saw the band move to RCA. Heiko Maile produced, with Ingo Ito contributing additional production and arrangements, and Stephan Fischer providing additional production. The 1999 album Thief (Virgin 7243 8 96041 2 2) was a self-produced record; the band had moved to Virgin by this point.

Sensor and the Polydor / Island Phase (2001–2006)

“The Great Commandment 2.0” appeared in 2001 on Virgin as a rework of the signature single. The album Sensor (2003, Polydor / Island Records 06024 9806878 6) was co-produced by electronic act Humate (who also mixed most tracks), with Heiko Maile producing the remaining tracks and recording handled by Ronda Ray (alias of Heiko Maile). Volker Hinkel and Jörn Heilbutt contributed guitars to select tracks; André Winter provided additional production and programming.

Relocated (2006, Synthetic Symphony SPV 63952 CD) appeared on the SPV sublabel Synthetic Symphony. Heiko Maile produced; Jochen Schmalbach mixed; Ronda Ray engineered; additional recordings by Volker Hinkel and Christian Cyfus; Bo Kondren mastered.

Bureau B Period and Later Work (2009–2024)

A live album, Live in Dresden (2009), appeared on Synthetic Symphony. Archival and compilation releases followed. In 2015 the band released three records on Bureau B (a sublabel of Tapete Records): the album Greyscale (BB192 — actually listed as no catalog in DB; the EP Shine (BB191); and the single Count On Me (BB216), which featured Peter Heppner as a collaborator.

The 2021 EP Winter was issued on Krautclub Music Publishing. The 2024 set Rewind To The Future And Goodbye (Polydor / Universal Music Group) closed the active phase of releases, with the Discogs listing suggesting it as a final project.
