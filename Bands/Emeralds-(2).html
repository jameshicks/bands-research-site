Emeralds (2)

Discogs ID: 704599 Active: 2006–2013 (disbanded January 2013; live archival release 2020) Origin: Cleveland, Ohio, USA Members: Mark McGuire (2) · John Elliott (4) · Steve Hauschildt Genres: Electronic, Ambient, Drone, Berlin-School, Krautrock / Kosmische

Overview

Emeralds were an American electronic trio founded in 2006 in Cleveland, Ohio. The core instrumentation was Mark McGuire on guitar and electronics, John Elliott and Steve Hauschildt on synthesizers. Their music was improvisational in nature, drawing on synthesizer-based drone, kosmische, ambient, and noise-adjacent styles. Discogs references touchstones including Tangerine Dream, Ashra, Coil, Terry Riley, and Popol Vuh.

They began issuing cassette tapes on small US imprints (notably Wagon and American Tapes) almost immediately after forming, releasing prolifically through the cassette underground before breaking through to wider audiences with the 2010 LP Does It Look Like I’m Here? on Editions Mego — their most commercially successful and critically recognised record. The band dissolved in January 2013 when first McGuire and then Hauschildt departed. A live archival release, Live II, appeared in 2020.

Biography

2006–2007: Formation and Cassette Underground

Emeralds formed in Cleveland in 2006. Within the first year they released multiple cassettes on small US imprints: Bullshit Boring Drone Band (2006, American Tapes), Laying Under Leaves and No More Spirits Over The Lake (both 2006, Wagon), Dirt Weed Diaries Vol. 1 (2006, Maim & Disfigure). The 2007 output expanded further — multiple Wagon cassettes (Smoke Signals, Servant, Hallucinations, Golden Swirl), a release on Fag Tapes (Grass Ceiling), and Allegory Of Allergies (Gods Of Tundra), a sprawling double-cassette that marked an early peak of the improvised drone approach. Also in 2007: Ledges on Gneiss Things, a label closely associated with the Cleveland scene.

The three members were simultaneously active in numerous solo and side projects. McGuire released solo guitar work on Wagon (e.g. Glass Bowls, Tidings). Elliott recorded as Imaginary Softwoods, among other aliases. Hauschildt recorded as The Celestial Sprinkler and would later build a substantial solo career.

2008–2009: Hanson Records and First CD Albums

The first full CD albums arrived in 2008 on Hanson Records (a Massachusetts imprint run by noise/underground figures): Solar Bridge (HN183) and a split with Aaron Dilloway, Under Pressure (HN186, cassette). Solar Bridge — two side-long pieces, “Magic” (12:34) and “The Quaking Mess” (14:14) — is widely considered the definitive early statement of their sound, crediting McGuire on guitar, Elliott and Hauschildt each on synthesizer.

2009 brought What Happened on No Fun Productions (NFP-35), a five-track CD running nearly 60 minutes with mastering by James Plotkin, and Fresh Air (A Soundesign Recording). Both pushed further into extended, immersive drone-ambient territory.

2010: Does It Look Like I’m Here? and Editions Mego

The breakthrough came with Does It Look Like I’m Here? (2010, Editions Mego; cat. Editions Mego 101P), a 12-track album recorded in Austria. Mastered again by James Plotkin; the trio credited individually as Performers. Stylistically tagged as Ambient, Berlin-School, and Krautrock on Discogs. The album received significant press attention and introduced the band to a European avant-electronic audience. Editions Mego — Peter Rehberg’s Vienna-based label, a revival of the original Mego imprint — was an ideal home, placing Emeralds alongside artists such as Fennesz and Pita.

The same year saw several 7” singles on Wagon (Candy Shoppe / The Cycle Of Abuse, Lake Effect Snow / Science Center, In Love / Summerdata, Shade / August), extending the album’s material.

2011–2012: Just To Feel Anything and Dissolution

A remix 12” of Does It Look Like I’m Here? by Daphni (Caribou alias) appeared in 2012 on Jiaolong (UK).

The final studio album, Just To Feel Anything (2012, Editions Mego; EDITIONS mego 150), was mixed by Andrew Veres (assisted by the band) and mastered by Plotkin. Cover art and typography by Mark Fell. Seven tracks including the near-nine-minute title track. Stylistically listed under Krautrock and Synth-pop.

In January 2013, Mark McGuire left the band. Two weeks later Steve Hauschildt also departed, ending the group.

2020: Live II

A self-released archival live recording, Live II (2020), appeared on Not On Label, credited to the original trio with Ryan Kuehn also listed in credits.

Members — Solo / Side Projects

Mark McGuire (Discogs ID: 45733)

Born December 31, 1986, Cleveland, Ohio. Real name Mark Daniel McGuire Jr. Guitar, vocals, tapes, keyboards. Left Emeralds January 2013. Solo discography on Wagon, Editions Mego (Living With Yourself, 2010), Dead Oceans (Noctilucence 2014, Beyond Belief 2015), and numerous self-releases. Also a member of The Afghan Whigs (later). Aliases: Cat Nap, People’s Parties, The Bad Dreamers (2), The Bad Habit, The Magi Of Eire, The Road Chief. Active in groups: Above Or Below, Amazing Births, Fancelions, Free Time, Inner Tube (3), Mental Prism, Silver Futures, Skyramps, Sparkling Water, Sun Watcher, Telecat Prowlers.

John Elliott (Discogs ID: 914336)

Real name John Elliott. Synthesizer. Remains active through 2025. Solo alias Imaginary Softwoods (drone/synthesizer). Other aliases: Lilypad, Quiet Light Water Gap, The Accidents (4). Name variations include “Blue O”, “Blue O’Randa”. Associated groups: Colored Mushroom And The Medicine Rocks, Fancelions, Hot Air Balloon Ride, Inner Spaced, Medicine Rocks, Mental Prism, Mist (12), Neon Depth, Organic Dial, Outer Space (3), Puffy Areolas, Silver Pearl. Top labels: Distant Duos, American Tapes.

Steve Hauschildt (Discogs ID: 1121448)

Synthesizer. Left Emeralds two weeks after McGuire in January 2013. Launched a significant solo career: Tragedy & Geometry (2011, Kranky), Sequitur (2012, Kranky), S/H (2013, Editions Mego), Where All Is Fled (2015, Kranky), Strands (2016, Kranky), Dissolvi (2018, Ghostly International), Nonlin (2019, Ghostly International), Aeropsia (2025, Simul Records). Alias: The Celestial Sprinkler (earlier recordings). Groups: Fancelions, Masqves, Mental Prism.
