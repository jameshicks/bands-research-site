Malcolm McLaren’s Management and the Bow Wow Wow Split (1979–1980)

Background

After their debut album Dirk Wears White Sox (Do It Records, November 1979) achieved only cult rather than commercial success, Adam Ant sought out Malcolm McLaren — former manager of the Sex Pistols — for guidance. The two met at McLaren’s shop (Seditionaries/Worlds End on the King’s Road). Adam hired McLaren as manager for a flat fee.

McLaren’s approach was characteristically idea-dense. Adam recalled: “He told me about the construction of pop music…he’d throw five thousand ideas at you, and you’d catch three of them; but those three could change history.” And also: “He’d talk to you for an hour on something, and if you were lucky you would understand a minute of it.” (Classic Pop Magazine, as of 2026-07-13)

The Burundi Beat Handoff

McLaren gave the band a 17-track mixtape of world music recordings to inspire their next direction. The key track was “Burundi Black” — a 1971 remix by Mike Steiphenson that layered keyboards and guitars over a 1967 field recording of the Royal Drummers of Burundi. McLaren believed the tribal percussion could be the basis of a commercially viable new sound. (Medium / Loop & Replay, as of 2026-07-13)

The Lineup Theft (January 1980)

The 1979–80 lineup of the Ants backing band comprised:

MemberInstrument
Matthew AshmanGuitar
Leigh GormanBass (joined November 1979, replacing Andy Warren)
Dave BarbarossaDrums

The band played a sell-out New Year’s Day 1980 gig at the Electric Ballroom. Then, at the end of January 1980 (date cited specifically as 26 January 1980 in some sources), McLaren persuaded all three to leave Adam and the Ants and form a new group under his management. Adam described the maneuver: “He organized a mutiny, basically. He said the right things to them.” (Medium / Loop & Replay, as of 2026-07-13)

McLaren’s own framing was candid about treating musicians instrumentally: “I decided to use people, just the way a sculptor uses clay.” (Medium / Loop & Replay, as of 2026-07-13)

Notes (web): Split location and timing

Researched 2026-10-09. The specific location of McLaren’s meeting with the Ants members remains unconfirmed by any named primary source. Findings per source:

  • Kill Your Pet Puppy (music blog, secondary): “at the end of 1979 he gave Adam the bad news at a rehearsal” — this is the only source that mentions a rehearsal and the only one giving “end of 1979” as the timing. No rehearsal studio name is given. The phrasing suggests McLaren informed Adam Ant at a rehearsal, rather than specifying that this was the meeting where the members were recruited. (killyourpetpuppy.co.uk)
  • Wikipedia / most secondary sources: dates the split as 26 January 1980, no location specified. (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_and_the_Ants, as of 2026-10-09)
  • BrandNewRetro.ie: states McLaren and Adam Ant first met at a wedding on 27 October 1979, after which they agreed a management deal — but does not describe the split meeting. (brandnewretro.ie)
  • Dave Barbarossa (primary — Louder Than War interview): describes McLaren as having “lured us away” after seeing him, Ashman, and Gorman at some point, but gives no location, venue, or date. (louderthanwar.com/interview-dave-barbarossa/)
  • Far Out Magazine: quotes Adam Ant — “He organized a mutiny, basically. He said the right things to them.” No location. (faroutmagazine.co.uk)

Conflict: Kill Your Pet Puppy says “end of 1979” and “at a rehearsal”; Wikipedia and most sources say 26 January 1980. A plausible reconciliation is that there were two separate events — McLaren may have told Adam at a rehearsal in late 1979 that he was going to take the band, with the formal departure happening on or around 26 January 1980 — but this is inference, not confirmed by any source. The autobiography Stand and Deliver (Pan Macmillan, 2006) is the most likely primary source for the specific account, but no accessible excerpt was found online.

Formation of Bow Wow Wow

McLaren assembled the three ex-Ants as the instrumental core of Bow Wow Wow. To front the group he needed a singer, and ran an audition process of roughly six months duration.

Talent scout Dave Fishel discovered the vocalist while she was working a Saturday job at a dry cleaner in West Hampstead: 13-year-old Annabella Lwin (born Myint Myint Aye, of Burmese and English heritage) was singing along to ABBA on the radio when Fishel overheard her and convinced McLaren to audition her. Lwin recalled thinking Fishel “wanted to pick us up” when he approached her; she auditioned for McLaren by singing “I Will Survive.” (Classic Pop Magazine / Annabella Lwin interview, as of 2026-07-13; Wikipedia / Annabella Lwin, as of 2026-07-13)

McLaren on why he wanted a young, untrained singer: “Any kid of 18 I met was so self-conscious…That was what was so great about Annabella.” (Medium / Loop & Replay, as of 2026-07-13)

Lwin described McLaren as “a genius” but “very dysfunctional in how he worked.” His parting advice to her: “You have to learn to sink or swim.” (Classic Pop Magazine / Annabella Lwin interview, as of 2026-07-13)

McLaren also briefly added George O’Dowd (later Boy George) as a second vocalist under the name “Lieutenant Lush,” but O’Dowd departed quickly to form Culture Club. (Wikipedia / Bow Wow Wow, as of 2026-07-13)

Bow Wow Wow signed to EMI Records in July 1980 and released their debut single “C·30 C·60 C·90 Go!” — a pro-home-taping provocation entirely in keeping with McLaren’s Situationist-adjacent promotional style.

Adam Ant’s Reaction and Comeback

When the three left, Adam retained the band name, the Burundi beat concept from McLaren’s mixtape, and — crucially — his own ideas about image and spectacle. He described the immediate aftermath: “At the time it was devastating. There’s one thing a band splitting up, but we were buddies and there was a camaraderie. That all came into question and I think Malcolm saw a situation where he could conveniently get a really good band to back up the ideas that he had.” (HuffPost interview, as of 2026-07-13)

Kevin Mooney, who joined the new Ants lineup as bassist, put it more bluntly: “the greatest genius of all was Malcolm McLaren…but when Malcolm stole Adam’s band he picked the wrong pirate to fuck with because in return Adam took all of Malcolm’s ideas and did them better.” (Louder Than War, as of 2026-07-13)

Between February and April 1980, Adam rebuilt the Ants:

New MemberInstrumentNotes
Marco PirroniGuitarBecame core creative partner
Kevin MooneyBass
Chris Hughes (“Merrick”)DrumsAlso producer
Terry Lee MiallDrumsEx-The Models; auditioned in Waterloo

The dual-drummer setup drew directly on the Burundi percussion concept from McLaren’s mixtape — Adam took the idea with him. The new lineup signed to CBS Records, recorded Kings of the Wild Frontier over approximately 12 weeks in summer 1980, and released it in November 1980. It reached No. 1 on the UK Albums Chart in January 1981.

Meanwhile Bow Wow Wow, despite critical attention, achieved far less commercial success than the reconstituted Ants.

Long-Term Perspective

After McLaren’s death in April 2010, Adam revised his public position considerably: “He now stands as one of the great rock ‘n’ roll managers. Tom Parker, [Brian] Epstein, and Malcolm… I think the world is a sadder place without him.” He also acknowledged: “A lot of water has gone under the bridge since then, we made up and everything…they did Bow Wow Wow, which I thought was a really good project and sounded great, and I did Kings which was my view of things.” (HuffPost interview; Classic Pop Magazine, as of 2026-07-13)

Notes (web): Two-event sequence — end-of-1979 rehearsal vs. January 1980 departure

Researched 2026-10-10. The specific question: was there a distinct earlier conversation at a rehearsal in late 1979 where McLaren first told Adam Ant his plan, with the formal departure of Barbarossa/Ashman/Gorman coming weeks later in January 1980?

Sources checked:

  • Kill Your Pet Puppy (killyourpetpuppy.co.uk/news/bow-wow-wow-e-m-i-records-1980/): The only source that explicitly mentions both the rehearsal and the “end of 1979” timing. Exact passage as fetched: “at the end of 1979 he gave Adam the bad news at a rehearsal”. Phrasing clearly refers to McLaren informing Adam (not recruiting the members), which is consistent with a two-event reading — i.e. McLaren told Adam at a rehearsal late 1979, and the members formally departed in January 1980. No rehearsal studio named. No corroborating source found.
  • Dave Barbarossa (Louder Than War interview, louderthanwar.com/interview-dave-barbarossa/): States only that McLaren “came down and saw Adam Ant… he saw me, Matthew and Lee and decided ‘he could do something with these boys’ and he lured us away with all promises of richness and nonsense.” No date, no location, no mention of a late-1979 preliminary conversation with Adam. Does not confirm or deny two-event sequence.
  • Leigh Gorman (Thrasher Magazine, 2007, snippet via search — fetch returned 402): Quote: “Malcolm told us to get rid of Adam, but I felt bad, ‘cause he hired me, not the band. I always was bummed on that one.” Confirms McLaren’s instruction to ditch Adam, but gives no timeline or location. (thrashermagazine.com/articles/music-interviews/bow-wow-wow/)
  • Leigh Gorman (Anne Carlini interview, annecarlini.com/ex_interviews.php?id=633): States he was in the Ants “about four months” before forming Bow Wow Wow; gives no specific dates for departure or any McLaren approach meeting.
  • Washington Times (Gorman interview, 2016): Returned 403 — no content retrieved.
  • Matthew Ashman: No post-split interview found in any accessible source. Ashman died in November 1995; published interviews from the Bow Wow Wow period may exist in print only.
  • 80s blog (80sblog.all80s.co.uk/adam-and-the-ants/, search snippet): Gives date as “24th January 1980” (cf. Wikipedia’s 26 January) and adds a detail not found elsewhere: “drummer Dave Barbarossa, bassist Leigh Gorman and guitarist Matthew Ashman telling him [McLaren] they were fed up having no say in group decisions” — framing it as the members approaching McLaren rather than McLaren recruiting them cold. Source is secondary/uncredited; no citation given.
  • Adam Ant autobiography Stand and Deliver (Pan Macmillan, 2006): Blog review (theartofexmouth.blogspot.com/2016/08/stand-and-deliver-autobiography-adam-ant.html) does not mention the rehearsal claim or the specific timeline. The book itself remains the most likely primary source for Adam’s account; no accessible excerpt was found online.

Summary of findings:

No named interview with Barbarossa, Ashman, or Gorman confirms or denies a separate late-1979 rehearsal conversation with Adam. The Kill Your Pet Puppy passage remains the only source for that claim, and the phrasing (“gave Adam the bad news at a rehearsal”) is consistent with a two-event reading but is a single secondary source with no rehearsal venue named. The date discrepancy (24 vs. 26 January) across secondary sources suggests even the formal departure date is not nailed down from primary sources.

Conflict note: 80s blog frames the departure as the members approaching McLaren because they were “fed up having no say,” whereas Barbarossa’s own interview implies McLaren spotted and “lured” them. These are not mutually exclusive — discontent may have made them receptive to McLaren’s pitch — but neither account is a verbatim primary source for the sequence.

Remaining gap: The autobiography Stand and Deliver (2006) and any contemporary 1980 press interviews with the Ants members are the only plausible primary sources that could confirm the two-event sequence. No online excerpt covers this passage.

  • Confirm the exact date the members left: secondary sources give both 24 January and 26 January 1980; a contemporary press source or a named interview would resolve the one-day discrepancy.

Notes (web): Stand and Deliver and the rehearsal claim — source tracing

Researched 2026-10-11. The specific question: does Adam Ant’s 2006 autobiography Stand and Deliver (Pan Macmillan) contain a specific scene set at a late-1979 rehearsal where McLaren told Adam he was taking the band, which would be the primary source for the Kill Your Pet Puppy “at a rehearsal” claim?

Sources checked:

  • “Scenes from Rip It Up and Start Again” blog (scenesfromripitupandstartagain.blogspot.com/2012/03/malcolm-mclaren-bow-wow-wow-and-adam.html): This blog is explicitly drawn from Simon Reynolds’s Rip It Up and Start Again (2005). The blog post — which is the most likely upstream source for the KYPP “at a rehearsal” passage — attributes “at the end of 1979 he gave Adam the bad news at a rehearsal” to Reynolds’s account. This means the KYPP claim most likely traces back to Reynolds, not to Adam Ant’s own autobiography. Reynolds’s book is a secondary history based on contemporary accounts and interviews conducted for the book; it is not stated whether Reynolds cites a specific interview or a primary source for the rehearsal claim.

  • The Art of Exmouth blog review of Stand and Deliver (theartofexmouth.blogspot.com/2016/08/stand-and-deliver-autobiography-adam-ant.html): Reviews the 2006 autobiography directly. The reviewer’s paraphrase is “McLaren simply stole Adam’s band” — no specific rehearsal scene, no late-1979 timing detail, no mention of a moment when McLaren told Adam his intention. The book is described as covering the McLaren episode but no detailed scene matching the KYPP description is mentioned.

  • Postpunkprof review of Stand and Deliver (postpunkprof.com/2018/06/03/stand-and-deliver/): No mention of a rehearsal scene or specific McLaren conversation.

  • Goodreads reviews (goodreads.com/en/book/show/115014.Stand_Deliver): General reader reviews confirm McLaren is discussed in the book, one noting Adam discusses “characters as large-as-life as… Malcolm McLaren,” but no reviewer cites a specific rehearsal scene.

  • Pan Macmillan publisher page (panmacmillan.com/authors/adam-ant/stand-and-deliver/9780330440127): Synopsis only; no excerpt.

  • prince.org excerpt thread (prince.org/msg/8/248093): Returned 403 — no content retrieved.

Assessment:

The Kill Your Pet Puppy “at a rehearsal” claim (phrased as “at the end of 1979 he gave Adam the bad news at a rehearsal”) appears to originate from Simon Reynolds’s Rip It Up and Start Again (Faber & Faber, 2005), not from Adam Ant’s 2006 autobiography Stand and Deliver. The “Scenes from Rip It Up” blog explicitly draws from Reynolds’s book for that passage.

Stand and Deliver almost certainly covers the McLaren episode — multiple reviews confirm it does — but no accessible online excerpt, blog review, or reader summary describes a specific late-1979 rehearsal scene. It is possible the book corroborates Reynolds’s account, or provides the source Reynolds used; but this cannot be confirmed without access to the physical text.

Conflict note: If Reynolds is the origin, the KYPP passage is second-hand: KYPP cites or paraphrases Reynolds (2005), who is himself a secondary historian. The actual primary source for the rehearsal claim — the named interviewee or firsthand account Reynolds drew on — remains unidentified in any accessible online source.

Remaining gap: Physical access to Stand and Deliver (Adam Ant, Pan Macmillan, 2006) would confirm whether Adam’s own account describes a rehearsal setting. Separately, the Simon Reynolds Rip It Up and Start Again notes/bibliography would identify Reynolds’s source for the rehearsal claim.

Notes (web): “Fed up” vs. “McLaren persuaded” — primary source review

Researched 2026-10-11. This section addresses the specific conflict between the uncredited 80s blog’s claim that Barbarossa, Gorman and Ashman approached McLaren because they were “fed up having no say in group decisions”, versus Wikipedia’s framing that McLaren persuaded/approached them.

Primary and named-interview sources checked:

  • Dave Barbarossa (Louder Than War interview, louderthanwar.com/interview-dave-barbarossa/): “he lured us away with all promises of richness and nonsense”; “Malcolm promised me my own band.” Agency is McLaren’s throughout — he “lured” them. Barbarossa also says McLaren “came down and saw Adam Ant… he saw me, Matthew and Lee and decided ‘he could do something with these boys’.” This is a McLaren-initiates framing, not a members-approach framing.

  • Dave Barbarossa (Repeat fanzine, 2014, repeatfanzine.co.uk/interviews/DAVE%20BARBAROSSA%202014.htm): “Malcolm stole us away… and Adam went on to be fabulously successful.” Again positions McLaren as the actor, not the members.

  • Dave Barbarossa (Fear and Loathing fanzine, fearandloathingfanzine.com/dave-barbarossa.html): Describes McLaren’s pitch to him as “You could be the star, you could run your own band and front your own thing. You don’t need to be somebody’s side-man.” McLaren approached him with an offer; Barbarossa’s own ambitions made him receptive. Barbarossa: “I’m glad I made that decision because if I hadn’t, I’d probably still just be one of Adam’s boys, getting a pat on the head when things went well.” This is the closest any source comes to suggesting member-side motivation, but the initiating agent is still McLaren.

  • Adam Ant (Classic Pop Magazine, 2017, classicpopmag.com/features/adam-ant-kings-of-the-wild-frontier/, as of 2026-10-11): “someone else came in and started casting doubts among us. That created a kind of mutiny if you like.” And: “I think Malcolm saw a situation where he could conveniently get a really good band to back up the ideas that he had.” Adam frames this as external manipulation by McLaren, not members acting on their own grievances.

  • Leigh Gorman (Thrasher Magazine, 2007 — fetch returned 402; snippet via search): “Malcolm told us to get rid of Adam, but I felt bad, ‘cause he hired me, not the band.” This confirms McLaren gave the instruction; it does not describe the members going to McLaren first. The phrase “I felt bad” implies reluctance, not proactive dissatisfaction.

  • Leigh Gorman (Washington Times, 2016 — returned 403): Search snippet via prior session: McLaren took Gorman to the pub and said “You can be an Ant, an employee, or you can have your own band. I’ll be your manager.” McLaren made the pitch; Gorman did not seek him out with grievances.

  • Stand and Deliver (Adam Ant, 2006): The blog review at theartofexmouth.blogspot.com was fetched; the blogger’s paraphrase reads “McLaren simply stole Adam’s band” — consistent with the McLaren-initiates framing, but this is the blogger’s own summary, not a direct quote from the book. No passage from the book directly quoting Adam’s account of the members’ motivations was found in any accessible online excerpt.

Assessment:

All named primary-source interviews — three separate Barbarossa interviews across different publications and years, two Gorman snippets, and Adam Ant’s own 2017 interview — consistently frame the departure as McLaren-initiated: he spotted the members, made them an offer, and “lured” them. None describe the members approaching McLaren with grievances.

The “fed up having no say in group decisions” claim appears only in the uncredited secondary 80s blog. It may be a reasonable inference about the members’ receptiveness — the Fear and Loathing Barbarossa interview does suggest he wanted more creative ownership — but no named source uses that phrasing or describes members approaching McLaren. The framing in the 80s blog is not corroborated by any primary source found.

Conflict resolution: Wikipedia’s framing (“McLaren persuaded/approached them”) is better supported by available primary sources than the 80s blog’s framing (“members approached McLaren because they were fed up”). The two accounts are not entirely irreconcilable — discontentment may have made the members receptive — but the agency in every primary account rests with McLaren.

Remaining gap: No accessible online excerpt from Stand and Deliver (2006) was found that quotes Adam’s account of the members’ motivations or uses the “fed up” phrasing. The book remains unverified as a source for or against either framing.
