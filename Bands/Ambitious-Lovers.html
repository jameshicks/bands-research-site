Ambitious Lovers

Aug 02, 20269 min read

Ambitious Lovers

Discogs ID: 46688
Active: 1984–1991
Origin: New York City, USA
Genre: Synth-funk, New Wave, Latin, Art Rock
Labels: Editions EG (1984), Virgin (1988), Elektra (1990–1991)

New York duo formed 1984 by Arto Lindsay (guitar, vocals) and Peter Scherer (keyboards, production). The group fused No Wave/downtown-NYC aesthetics with synth-funk, Brazilian MPB, and tropicália — the defining tension being Lindsay’s deliberately atonal, off-key vocals set against Scherer’s polished, electronics-driven arrangements. Active for seven years across four studio releases before dissolving in 1991.

Biography

Formation (1984)

Lindsay and Scherer met in New York’s downtown music scene, where Lindsay had been active since 1978 — first with No Wave group DNA, then with The Lounge Lizards and broader avant-garde circles. Scherer brought a command of then-current synthesizer technology (Yamaha DX-7, Synclavier) and production sensibility. Their shared interest in Brazilian popular music — rooted in Lindsay’s childhood in Brazil — gave the project its hybrid character from the outset.

The name Ambitious Lovers first appeared on Discogs-credited releases in 1984, co-billed with Arto Lindsay on the UK pressing of Envy. The Brazilian percussionists from the Envy sessions — Claudio Silva, Jorge Silva, Reinaldo Fernandes, and Toni Nogueira — became recurring collaborators throughout the project’s run.

Envy and the Editions EG Period (1984)

Envy (Editions EG, EGED 39 / 823 688-1) was released in 1984 across multiple territories through Editions EG, a subsidiary of E.G. Records Ltd. that housed Brian Eno, Penguin Cafe Orchestra, and The Lounge Lizards. The album was co-produced by Lindsay, Peter Scherer, and Mark E. Miller. Martin Bisi (BC Studio) mixed most of the album; Robert Musso mixed “Let’s Be Adult” (B1) and “Locus Coruleus” (B7). Anton Fier contributed DMX drum programming to “Locus Coruleus.”

The tracklist moves between synth-funk originals, Brazilian percussion instrumentals credited to the Silva/Fernandes/Nogueira contingent, and a cover of Dorival Caymmi’s “Dora.” The album introduced the group’s characteristic mix: DMX and DX-7 programming beneath Brazilian samba bateria, with Lindsay’s atonal guitar and vocals as the destabilizing element. David Moss appears on sampled vocals and backing vocals. Duncan Lindsay (Arto’s brother) sings on “Beberibe.”

A 12” promo single, Let’s Be Adult (Editions EG, Egox19), was issued the same year with “Let’s Be Adult” (A-side, 4:18) and “Locus Coruleus” (B-side), both also on the LP.

Greed and the Virgin Period (1988)

The group signed to Virgin Records for their second full-length, Greed (Virgin, 7 90903-1 / 1-90903, 1988). By this point, Lindsay and Scherer were the duo at the center, with session musicians assembled track-by-track. Naná Vasconcelos played percussion throughout. The record’s downtown New York guest roster reads as a who’s-who of the era: John Zorn on saxophone (“Privacy,” “Admit It”), John Lurie on saxophone (“Omotesando”), Vernon Reid on rhythm guitar (“Love Overlap,” and others), Bill Frisell and Joey Baron as a unit on “Steel Wool” and “Para Não Contrariar Voce.” Naná Vasconcelos co-wrote and sang on “It Only Has To Happen Once.” The album was recorded and mixed by Roger Moutenot (with Alan Meyerson mixing “Copy Me”). Mastered by Bob Ludwig. Scherer produced alone, credited as: keyboards, synthesizer bass, drum programming, sampler.

A promotional 12” single for “Love Overlap” (Virgin) was issued in multiple territories in 1988, with remixes.

Greed was reissued in the UK in 1996 (Virgin) and in Japan in 2011 (Tower Records/Virgin).

Umbabarauma Single (1990) and Lust (1991)

Preceding the third album, a 12” single for “Umbabarauma” (Elektra, 0-66583, 1990) appeared — a Jorge Ben cover in five remixed versions (World Beat Club Mix, Jorge Meets Junior House Dub, Mix Da Verdade, Cassanova’s Kickin’ House Mix, Lemon Tree Basement Dub), remixed by Charley Casanova and Goh Hotoda. This was a dance-floor pivot that anticipated the Lust sound.

Lust (Elektra, 9 60981-2 / 60981-2, 1991) was the final album. Scherer produced alone again. The rhythm section for most tracks was Melvin Gibbs (bass) and Tony Lewis (drums), with Marc Ribot on guitar throughout and Naná Vasconcelos on percussion on multiple tracks. The album was recorded by Francis Manzella, Roger Moutenot, and Jason Baker; mixed by Moutenot; mastered by Bob Ludwig. The most notable guest credit is Caetano Veloso, who co-wrote and sings on “Villain.” Nile Rodgers plays guitar and takes the guitar solo on “Slippery” and “Make It Easy.” The album closes with a solo piano setting of “É Preciso Perdoar” (written by Alcivando Luz and Carlos Coqueijo), with Scherer on piano and Lindsay’s unaccompanied vocals.

No further releases appeared after Lust. The group dissolved in 1991, the same year the album was released. Lindsay pursued an extensive solo career and Brazilian production work through the 1990s and 2000s; Scherer maintained a lower profile, with credits appearing on compilation projects (Arch Ensemble, Island (4), Seigen Ono Ensemble).

Post-AL Careers

Arto Lindsay continued as a solo artist through the 1990s and 2000s, releasing on Crammed Discs (Pretty Ugly, 1990, co-credited with Scherer), For Life Records/Güt (Japan), and Righteous Babe Records (Prize 1999, Invoke 2002, Salt 2004). He maintained an active production career in Brazil throughout. Most recent solo release: Charivari (Corbett vs. Dempsey, 2022). Full page: Arto Lindsay.

Peter Scherer is credited on the 1990 Pretty Ugly EP (Crammed Discs, co-credited with Lindsay). Later Discogs-traceable projects include Very Neon Pet (1994, Musikszene Schweiz — with Arch Ensemble) and Nerve Type No (1995, Metro Blue). He is also listed as a member of Island (4) and Seigen Ono Ensemble. Currently teaching at Bern University of the Arts (Music and Media Art program). Full page: Peter Scherer.

Both Lindsay and Scherer are credited on the joint project Pretty Ugly (Crammed Discs, 1990), released between Greed and Lust — a transitional record bridging the duo’s two label phases.
