Rip Rig & Panic

Post-punk / free jazz / funk band. Sweden / UK. Discogs ID: 27501. Active 1981–1984/85.

Emerged directly from the wreckage of The Pop Group (Bristol, 1980) and the orbit of The Slits. Named after the 1965 Roland Kirk album Rip, Rig and Panic. Self-produced throughout; released on Virgin Records. One of the most sonically restless and rhythmically complex bands of the early post-punk moment — drawing on free jazz, funk, dub, and avant-garde composition in equal measure. After dissolving, most members reconvened in Float Up CP (1984–1986) and then God Mother & Country; Neneh Cherry went on to a major solo career.

The band is biographically linked to the Y Records orbit — Gareth Sager and Bruce Smith were founding members of The Pop Group, the label’s inaugural act; Neneh Cherry had been in The Slits, another early Y Records band — but Rip Rig & Panic itself released on Virgin, not Y Records.

History

Formation (1981)

Formed in Sweden after Bruce Smith and Neneh Cherry had married and relocated. Gareth Sager joined them there. Sean Oliver and Mark Springer (3) completed the initial lineup. The name references Roland Kirk’s 1965 Blue Note LP. The band self-produced all their recordings from the start — an unusual move for a debut act, enabled partly by Sager’s prior studio experience with The Pop Group.

Early Releases and WOMAD (1981–1982)

Signed to Virgin Records. Debut LP Bob Hope Takes Risks (Virgin, 1981) was produced entirely by the band, as were all subsequent releases. The group performed at the first WOMAD festival in 1982, shortly before Cherry returned to Sweden to give birth to her first child. Andrea Oliver (Sean Oliver’s sister) stepped in as vocalist during this period; South African free jazz drummer Louis Moholo also joined temporarily.

Peak Activity (1982–1983)

The band’s most prolific period. Three LPs in two years — God (1981), I Am Cold (1982), Storm The Reality Asylum (1982) — plus several singles. I Am Cold featured Don Cherry (Neneh’s stepfather) on trumpet across six tracks, and a large expanded ensemble including cello, baritone sax, trombone, viola, and two additional trumpets. The cover carried a lithograph by Pablo Picasso.

Dissolution and Float Up CP (1983–1985)

The final LP Attitude (Virgin, 1983) showed a further shift toward a larger ensemble sound. The band split in 1984/85 and immediately realigned as Float Up CP — same core of Sager, Smith, Cherry, Sean Oliver, and with Ollie Moore — releasing a single (Joy’s Address, Rough Trade, 1984) and an album (Kill Me In The Morning, Rough Trade, 1985) before that project also dissolved. A further short-lived project, God Mother & Country, followed.

Note: All members used pseudonyms on Attitude. Mark Stewart (of The Pop Group / Mark Stewart And The Maffia) is credited as engineer — a direct connection back to the Pop Group circle.

On Mark Stewart’s engineer credit: The credit reads “M. Stewart” in the same abbreviated style as the other two engineers (K. Thomas, T. Hunt), not in the playful pseudonym style used for performers. No published source — interview, review, or liner-notes discussion — describes it as a joke or honorary credit. Ken Thomas and Tim Hunt are documented professional engineers with track records on other releases; Stewart’s credit sits alongside theirs without any caveat on the sleeve or in secondary literature. Given the post-punk/DIY milieu — Gareth Sager himself noted that in the Pop Group era “me and Gareth are plugging it all in backwards” (i.e., hands-on, unorthodox studio participation was the norm for this circle) — the most defensible reading is that Stewart participated in a real, if informal, capacity during the Sussex recording sessions, rather than receiving a pure joke credit. However, no source explicitly confirms he operated recording equipment. The nature of the contribution (technical participation vs. honorary/friendship credit) remains unconfirmed by any accessible primary source. A further short-lived group, God Mother & Country (Discogs: 996598), also featured Smith, Cherry, and Sean Oliver; released one single “Foot On The Rock” (Kaz Records, 1985).

Connections

  • The Pop Group — Sager and Bruce Smith were founding members; the Pop Group’s collapse in 1980 directly precipitated RRP’s formation
  • The Slits — Neneh Cherry had been a member; Bruce Smith also played with The Slits
  • Y Records — biographically connected (Pop Group/Slits orbit) but Rip Rig & Panic released on Virgin; Disc O’Dell had managed The Pop Group
  • Don Cherry — Neneh’s stepfather; guested on I Am Cold
  • Andrea Oliver — Sean Oliver’s sister; stepped in as vocalist during Neneh Cherry’s absence
  • New Age Steppers — Adrian Sherwood’s collective; Bruce Smith and Sean Oliver were both members
  • Mark Stewart — engineer on Attitude; co-founder of The Pop Group and Mark Stewart And The Maffia
  • Adam Kidron — recurring engineer across God, I Am Cold, Attitude
  • Nellee Hooper — not in RRP but in Mouth and Maximum Joy; part of the same Y Records / Bristol circle
  • Float Up CP — immediate successor group (1984–1986); Rough Trade releases
  • Sean Oliver — died 1990 of sickle-cell anaemia; co-wrote “Wishing Well” (music) for Terence Trent D’Arby’s debut LP Introducing The Hardline (1987)
