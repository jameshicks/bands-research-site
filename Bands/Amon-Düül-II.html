Amon Düül II

Formed: Munich, Bavaria, Germany, 1968
Discogs ID: 192177
Genres: Krautrock, progressive rock, psychedelic rock, space rock, experimental rock
Labels: Liberty Records, United Artists Records, Nova (6) (ID: 271403), Strand, ATCO Records, Telefunken, Schneeball
Years active: 1969–1981, 1995, 2010–present (with various hiatuses)

Amon Düül II are a German rock band formed in Munich in 1968 out of the radical West German commune scene. They are considered among the pioneers of krautrock alongside Can, Faust, Tangerine Dream, and Neu!. Their 1970 double LP Yeti was described by The Wire as “one of the cornerstones of… the entire Krautrock movement.” The band grew from a split within the original Amon Düül commune, with the more musically proficient members breaking off to form Amon Düül II.

History

Origins in the Amon Düül Commune (1967–1969)

The band emerged from the radical West German commune scene of the late 1960s. The Amon Düül art commune in Munich was a gathering of mainly university students who formed a music group partly to fund the commune — everyone living there participated in music regardless of ability. The commune included some future founders of the Red Army Faction among its members.

When the group was offered a recording opportunity, the more musically skilled members boycotted the resulting sessions and split off to form Amon Düül II. The sessions recorded by the remaining commune members were later released under the Amon Düül name, primarily to capitalize on the success of Amon Düül II’s albums. (The original commune recordings were poor quality and only found release later.)

The founding core of Amon Düül II was: Chris Karrer (violin, guitars, saxophone, vocals), John Weinzierl (guitar, bass, piano), Falk-Ulrich Rogner (organ, keyboards), Renate Knaup (vocals, tambourine), Peter Leopold (drums), Dieter Serfas (drums), and Christian “Shrat” Thiele (bongos, violin, vocals). Early bassist Dave Anderson (3) was also in place for the debut recordings.

According to Weinzierl: “The band played almost every day. We played universities, academies, underground clubs, and every hall with a power socket and an audience.” (Source: Wikipedia)

The Classic Period (1969–1973)

Phallus Dei (1969, Liberty) — The debut album consisted of pieces drawn from the band’s live set. Guests included Holger Trülzsch on Turkish drums (then a member of Popol Vuh) and Christian Burchard (vibraphone; future co-founder of Embryo (3)). Producer was Olaf Kübler. The album established the band’s signature approach: long psychedelic improvisations anchored by Karrer’s violin and electric guitar, Rogner’s organ, and the increasingly prominent vocals of Renate Knaup.

Yeti (1970, Liberty) — The second album, a double LP, introduced more arranged compositions alongside extended jams. Still produced by Olaf Kübler. Personnel from Phallus Dei largely intact. Dave Anderson left shortly after for England, where he joined Hawkwind; he was replaced by Lothar Meid. Yeti launched the band outside Germany and remains the most celebrated album in their catalog.

Tanz Der Lemminge (1971, United Artists) — A double LP based on four extended progressive rock suites. Lothar Meid now fully on bass. New additions included keyboardist Karl-Heinz Hausmann and guest organist/pianist Jimmy Jackson (2); also featuring sitar guest Al Gromer Khan and vocal guest Rolf Zacher. Engineer credit went to both Hausmann and Kalle Hausmann (same person, different crediting). Renate Knaup’s vocal alias “Henriette Kroetenschwanz” appears as a guest vocals credit.

Carnival in Babylon (1972, United Artists) — Marked a shift toward shorter, more song-based structures. Vocalist Renate Knaup’s presence became more central. Daniel Fichelscher joined as drummer/multi-instrumentalist (his first appearance with the band), with Peter Leopold co-drumming. Also introduced Joy Alaska (guest backing vocals). Produced by Amon Düül II and Olaf Kübler. Engineer: Peter Kramper.

Wolf City (1972, United Artists) — Continues the more concise direction of Carnival in Babylon. Produced by Amon Düül II and Olaf Kübler.

Live in London (1973, United Artists) — Recorded 16 December 1972 at The Greyhound, Croydon. Remixed at Studio 70, Munich, January 1973.

Vive La Trance (1973, United Artists) — Guest drummer Keith Forsey appears on this album — his only credit with the band in the classic period. Robby Heibl joined as additional performer. Still produced by Amon Düül II and Olaf Kübler; engineered and recorded by Peter Kramper.

Transitional Period and Disbandment (1974–1981)

Renate Knaup departed in 1974 to join Popol Vuh, where she remained a member for several years. Following her departure, the band released:

  • Hijack (1974, Nova (6)) — first release on the Nova label; added several outside session players
  • Made in Germany (1975, ATCO Records) — US release on Atlantic’s ATCO imprint, a double LP in Germany
  • Pyragony X (1976, Nova (6))
  • Almost Alive… (1977, Nova (6)) — sessions in England with a partly UK lineup including Ian Gomm, Chris Nagle, and others
  • Only Human (1978/1979, Strand)
  • Vortex (1981, Telefunken) — a reformation attempt by Chris Karrer with Renate Knaup returning; had limited success

The band initially disbanded after Vortex (1981).

Reunions (1995, 2010)

The band reformed in 1995, releasing Nada Moonshine # on Schneeball label, and performed across Europe and Japan. Live in Tokio (Captain Trip Records, Japan, 1996) documented this period.

A further comeback came around 2010 with the release of Bee as Such (later released on CD/vinyl as Düülirium in 2014). The band performed again through approximately 2018.

Deaths of Core Members

  • Peter Leopold (drummer) died 8 November 2006. A memorial service was held in Munich; remaining members sang for him. He was replaced for later live shows by Daniel Fichelscher, who had long affiliations with both Amon Düül II and Popol Vuh. (Source: Wikipedia)
  • Lothar Meid (bass) died 3 November 2015. (Source: Wikipedia)
  • Chris Karrer died 2 January 2024, aged 76. He had been the primary driver of the band’s reunions. (Source: Wikipedia)

Scene Context

Amon Düül II were part of the first wave of krautrock alongside Can, Faust, Tangerine Dream, Neu!, and Cluster. They shared Munich ties with Popol Vuh (both Holger Trülzsch and Daniel Fichelscher moved between the two bands). Chris Karrer had also been briefly active with proto-commune outfit Amon Düül O alongside future Embryo (3) members Lothar Meid and Christian Burchard around 1966. The band also toured alongside Tangerine Dream and visited other communes including Kommuné 1 in Berlin during their early touring years. (Source: Wikipedia)

The band won a Deutscher Filmpreis (German Film Award) for their musical contribution to the 1970 film San Domingo.

  • Daniel Fichelscher — multi-instrumentalist bridging Amon Düül II and Popol Vuh
