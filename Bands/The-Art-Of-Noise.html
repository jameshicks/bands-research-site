The Art Of Noise

Discogs ID: 7142

British art-pop/electronic group established 1983. Core members: Anne Dudley (keyboards, arrangements), J.J. Jeczalik (Fairlight CMI), Gary Langan (engineering), Paul Morley (concept/writing), Trevor Horn (production/writing). Name taken from Luigi Russolo’s 1913 Futurist manifesto The Art of Noises.

First act on ZTT Records; debut was Into Battle With The Art Of Noise (ZTIS 100, 1983). Debut LP Who’s Afraid Of The Art Of Noise (ZTTIQ 2, 1984). Dudley, Jeczalik, and Langan split acrimoniously from Horn, Morley, and ZTT in 1985; Gary Langan left amicably in 1987. Dudley and Jeczalik carried on until 1990. Notable collaborations: Tom Jones (“Kiss,” 1988); Duane Eddy (“Peter Gunn,” 1986). Reformed in 1998–2000 with Lol Creme added; released The Seduction of Claude Debussy (1999). 279 Discogs releases.

Departure of Horn and Morley (1985)

The split was a departure by Dudley, Jeczalik, and Langan from both Horn/Morley and the ZTT label — not an expulsion of Horn and Morley. The three core creators walked out on the project and the label; Horn and Morley remained at ZTT.

The precipitating event was the “Value of Entertainment” ZTT showcase at the Ambassadors Theatre, London (running from approximately 20 May to 1 June 1985). Dudley, Jeczalik, and Langan withdrew from the showcase, reportedly citing insufficient preparation time. According to fan-site accounts, Morley announced from the stage: “Last Monday when Anne, Gary and J.J. turned up for rehearsals we shot them, and now we’re back with no group, no names, no faces.” Morley improvised a performance consisting of him reading an essay over Art of Noise recordings with three dancers. [source: https://theartofnoiseonline.com/4.php; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_Noise as of 2026-06-27]

Underlying tensions — creative control and attribution: No party has given a fully on-record account of the root cause, but the consistent picture from available sources is resentment over creative attribution and Horn/Morley’s plans for the group’s direction. As media coverage of the early singles credited Horn and Morley most prominently, Jeczalik and Langan — who initiated the project — felt the work had been taken from them. In an October 1985 Melody Maker interview, Jeczalik stated that Horn and Morley’s creative contribution to Who’s Afraid of the Art of Noise? was “approximately 1.73 percent, but it could even be as high as two percent,” and that they “took steps to get it back.” He also described Morley’s writing as pretentious. Dudley indicated in the same period that she felt parts of Who’s Afraid were of dubious quality. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_Noise as of 2026-06-27]

Contract factor: Sources note that ZTT had failed to notice the group’s recording contract had expired, which contributed to the crisis point. [source: https://theartofnoiseonline.com/4.php; https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/120646]

Planned projects Horn/Morley had in mind (per Oct 1984 Smash Hits): a cover of “Video Killed the Radio Star”; Raiding the 20th Century (a collage album drawing on 20th-century recorded sound); The Living End (film score, written by Morley, directed by Godley & Creme); and a ballet soundtrack — none of which materialised under that banner. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_Noise as of 2026-06-27]

Aftermath: Dudley, Jeczalik, and Langan signed to Derek Green’s China Records, retaining the Art of Noise name. Horn and Morley stayed at ZTT. ZTT released the compilation Daft (1986) shortly after, with pseudonymous liner notes by Morley (credited as “Otto Flake”) critical of the group’s new direction. Morley later wrote in the Observer (September 2002) that without him and Horn the group “became a novelty group who had hits with Tom Jones.” [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_Noise as of 2026-06-27]

Note on framing: Common phrasing that “Horn and Morley departed” (including the intro of this page) inverts the direction of the split. The three instrumentalists departed; Horn and Morley were the label-affiliated parties who remained.
