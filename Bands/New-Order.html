New Order

Discogs ID: 3909
Genre: Post-punk / synth-pop / electronic / dance
Origin: Manchester, UK
Active: 1980–present (hiatuses 1993–1998, 2007–2011)
Labels: Factory Records → Factory Benelux (European) → London Records → Reprise / Warner → Rhino → Mute

Formed by the three surviving members of Joy Division after vocalist Ian Curtis died by suicide on 18 May 1980. They added keyboardist/guitarist Gillian Gilbert within months and renamed themselves New Order — proceeding under the agreement that if any member left, the band’s name would change. Over the following four decades they moved from post-punk austerity through synth-pop and became central figures in the merger of rock and electronic/dance music. “Blue Monday” (1983) is the bestselling 12” single of all time.

Biography by Era

1980–1981: Formation and Movement

Ian Curtis died the eve of Joy Division’s first North American tour. The remaining members — Bernard Sumner, Peter Hook, and Stephen Morris — had pre-agreed that any member leaving would trigger a name change. They chose “New Order” and recruited Gillian Gilbert (Stephen Morris’s partner) on keyboards and guitar by late 1980, officially from 1981.

The debut LP Movement (FACT 50, Factory, 1981) was produced by Martin Hannett — the only time Hannett produced for New Order. It is the most sonically continuous with Joy Division: bleak, cavernous, dub-influenced. Sumner took over lead vocals with no prior experience. The album was largely written before Curtis’s death and is in some ways a transitional document rather than a fully autonomous statement.

First single: “Ceremony” (FAC 33, 1981) — the last song written by all four Joy Division members together, recorded after Curtis’s death. B-side: “In A Lonely Place.” Both sides produced by Hannett.

Key 1981 releases:

  • “Ceremony” / “In A Lonely Place” (FAC 33)
  • “Procession” / “Everything’s Gone Green” (FAC 53, 1981) — first truly self-produced New Order single; the pivot toward electronics
  • Movement (FACT 50, Factory, 1981)

1982–1987: Factory’s Commercial Engine

From 1982 New Order self-produced and moved decisively into synthesizer-driven territory, drawing on Kraftwerk, Giorgio Moroder, and the burgeoning New York hip-hop and electro scenes.

“Blue Monday” (FAC 73, March 1983): 12” single; 7:29 running time; almost entirely sequencer-driven; the most recognizable drum-machine pattern in post-punk. Peter Saville and Brett Wickens designed the die-cut sleeve to resemble a floppy disk — it cost more to manufacture than the £1.99 retail price, so Factory lost money on every copy sold. Still the bestselling 12” single in UK history. Engineer: Michael Johnson. No producer credit other than the band.

Power, Corruption & Lies (FACT 75, Factory, 1983) — co-produced by the band, engineered by Michael Johnson (Discogs ID 29010), with assistant engineers Barry Sage and Mark Boyne. Cover art: a Fantin-Latour flower painting from the National Gallery, scanned and used without fee — Peter Saville’s idea. The Peter Saville / Trevor Key color-coding system for the sleeve connects visually to their earlier work and to Section 25’s Factory albums produced under the Be Music alias.

Low-Life (FACT 100, Factory, 1985) — self-produced. First album with Gillian Gilbert established as full member. “The Perfect Kiss” 12” is 8 minutes long; the music video directed by Jonathan Demme. “Sub-Culture” featured an early collaboration with Arthur Baker.

Brotherhood (FACT 150, Factory, 1986) — two distinct sides: post-punk guitar on side A, electronics on side B. Design: Peter Saville (2) Associates; photography: Trevor Key.

Technique (FACT 275, Factory, 1989) — recorded partly in Ibiza during the height of the house music scene there, with influence on the album’s tone evident. Self-produced, recorded by Michael Johnson. Design: Peter Saville Associates / Trevor Key.

Substance (Factory, 1987) — two-LP compilation of 12” single versions, the definitive singles collection; one of the label’s bestselling releases.

Blue Monday 1988 — re-recorded version; UK chart #3.

“True Faith” (1987): co-produced with Stephen Hague; biggest UK chart hit to that point (#4). Stephen Hague also contributed writing to Republic.

“World In Motion” (1990): England World Cup single; featuring actor/comedian Keith Allen and various England squad members (Paul Gascoigne, Chris Waddle, John Barnes, Peter Beardsley, Des Walker, Steve McMahon). Reached #1 in the UK. Produced by the band; mixed by New Order and Graeme Park.

Factory, The Haçienda, and the Dance Music Explosion

New Order co-owned The Haçienda (FAC 51) — opened 21 May 1982, 11–13 Whitworth Street West, Manchester — with Factory Records. The club was designed by Ben Kelly. It ran at a loss for years before becoming the epicenter of UK acid house and rave from 1988–90. New Order’s earnings effectively subsidized both the label and the club; this was a constant source of internal tension. The Haçienda’s DJs — Mike Pickering, Graeme Park — played a pivotal role in the UK Madchester scene.

Factory collapsed in November 1992 under approximately £2.5 million in debt. The immediate cause was the expensive and under-performing Yes Please! by Happy Mondays. London Records acquired the Factory name and some back catalog.

1993–1998: London Records Era / Republic

Republic (London Records, 1993) — the last album before the mid-1990s hiatus. Produced jointly by New Order and Stephen Hague. Mix of Hague-driven pop production and band self-production. Additional musicians: Dee Lewis (vocals), Audrey Riley (cello), David Rhodes (guitar), Andy Duncan (drums/percussion). The band had burned out; tensions — particularly between Hook and the others — were severe. The Haçienda finally closed in 1997.

“Regret” (1993) — first single on London Records; produced with Pascal Gabriel. “World (The Price Of Love)” and “Ruined In A Day” (with Sly and Robbie on rhythm section) were other singles.

2001–2006: Reunions and Get Ready / Waiting for the Sirens’ Call

New Order reformed. Phil Cunningham (ex-Marion (3)) joined permanently in 2001 on guitar/keyboards.

Get Ready (London Records / Reprise, 2001) — produced entirely by Steve Osborne (who had worked with Happy Mondays and Primal Scream). The most guitar-forward album since Brotherhood. Singles: “Crystal” (directed by Jonathan Glazer), “60 Miles An Hour,” “Someone Like You.”

Lost Sirens (Rhino, 2013) — leftover recordings from the Waiting For The Sirens’ Call sessions, released after Peter Hook’s departure. Written by all four members (Sumner, Hook, Morris, Cunningham credited).

2007–2011: Hiatus and Reunion (Without Hook)

Peter Hook announced his departure in 2010 after a prolonged falling-out with the other members. The dispute became acrimonious and public; Hook subsequently formed Peter Hook and The Light to perform Joy Division and New Order material, leading to an ongoing legal dispute over the band name.

Gillian Gilbert returned in 2011. Tom Chapman (2) (Thomas Louis Chapman, ID 719377) joined as bassist. New lineup: Sumner, Morris, Gilbert, Cunningham, Chapman.

2015–present: Mute Era

Music Complete (Mute, 2015) — the most expansive New Order album sonically. Self-produced with contributions from Tom Rowlands (Chemical Brothers), Craig Silvey (mixing), Richard X. Featured guest vocals: Brandon Flowers (The Killers) on “Superheated,” La Roux on “Tutti Frutti,” Iggy Pop on “Stray Dog,” Denise Johnson on “Unlearn This Hatred.” String arrangements by Joe Duddell; strings performed by Manchester Camerata. Daniel Miller (Mute founder) served as executive producer.

Be Music — Production Alias

The “Be Music” credit on Discogs (artist ID 156001) was a shared production alias for New Order members doing outside work. Principal uses:

  • Bernard Sumner produced Section 25’s From The Hip (FACT 90) as Be Music
  • The Be Music credit also appears on Haçienda-related Factory releases

The alias was also used by Peter Hook, Stephen Morris, and Gillian Gilbert independently. The Discogs “Be Music” page lists New Order itself as an alias, and vice versa.

The Haçienda Connection

The Haçienda (FAC 51) — 11–13 Whitworth Street West, Manchester — opened 21 May 1982. Co-owned by Factory and New Order. Designed by Ben Kelly. The band’s earnings funded the club through years of losses. Its DJs (Mike Pickering, Graeme Park, Sasha) became catalysts for UK acid house and rave, 1988–90. The club’s success in the rave era ironically came too late to save Factory; it closed in 1997 and was demolished in 2002. The financial drain of the Haçienda was a defining source of tension within New Order and a contributing factor in Factory’s 1992 collapse.
