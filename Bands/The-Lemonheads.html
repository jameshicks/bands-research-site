The Lemonheads

American alternative rock band formed in Boston, Massachusetts in 1986 by Evan Dando, Ben Deily, and Jesse Peretz. Dando has been the only constant member throughout the band’s history. After a run of indie releases on Taang! Records in the late 1980s, they signed to Atlantic Records and broke through with It’s A Shame About Ray (1992), followed by Come On Feel The Lemonheads (1993). The band went on hiatus in 1997 and reformed in 2005 with an almost entirely new lineup; they remain active through the 2020s on Fire Records. Genres: alternative rock, jangle pop, power pop, pop-punk.

Discogs artist ID: 294469

History

Early Years (1986–1989) — Taang! Records

Dando, Deily, and Peretz formed the band while students at the Commonwealth School in Boston. The original name was the Whelps; they self-released a debut 7-inch EP, Laughing All The Way To The Cleaners, in 1986 on their own Amory Arms / Huh-Bag Records imprint. After signing to Boston label Taang!, they released three LPs in quick succession: Hate Your Friends (1987), Creator (1988), and Lick (1989). The early lineup was fluid — Dando and Deily shared lead guitar, drums, and songwriting across studio sessions and live shows, with Doug Trachten, John P. Strohm, and others cycling through on drums. Tom Hamilton produced or co-produced the first two LPs.

Ben Deily departed in 1989, ending the shared-songwriter era.

Atlantic Years and Breakthrough (1990–1997)

With Deily gone, Dando recruited Corey Loog Brennan (guitar) and David Ryan (drums) for Lovey (1990, Atlantic). The album was produced and mixed by Paul Q. Kolderie with Lou Giordano engineering. Though it gained college radio attention, it failed to chart.

For the breakthrough album It’s A Shame About Ray (1992), Dando flew to Australia to write with Sydney musicians Tom Morgan (2) and Nic Dalton. The album was recorded with Juliana Hatfield on bass, David Ryan on drums, and produced, engineered, and mixed by the Robb Brothers (Bruce, Dee, and Joe Robb). A cover of Simon & Garfunkel’s “Mrs. Robinson” — tacked onto the reissued Ray — became one of their most-played singles (later used in Wayne’s World 2 and Scorsese’s The Wolf of Wall Street). Dando was in People’s 50 Most Beautiful People in 1993.

Hatfield left after Ray to focus on the Juliana Hatfield Three; Dalton stepped in as bassist. Come On Feel The Lemonheads (1993, Atlantic) — again co-produced with the Robb Brothers — featured Hatfield returning as backing vocalist on most tracks, alongside guest appearances from Belinda Carlisle, Rick James (uncredited), and steel guitarist Sneaky Pete Kleinow. Tom Morgan co-wrote several songs including “Into Your Arms” (their highest UK charting hit) and key album tracks.

The band officially changed their name from “Lemonheads” to “The Lemonheads” prior to this album’s release.

Drug problems and public instability marked 1994–1995. Dando went into rehab.

Car Button Cloth (1996, Atlantic / Tag Recordings) was recorded with a mostly new lineup: Murph (Patrick Murphy of Dinosaur Jr.) on drums, Bill Gibson on bass, and John P. Strohm on guitar. Produced by Bryce Goggin. If I Could Talk I’d Tell You (1996 single/B-side) was also produced and engineered by Goggin. The band toured in 1997 and played a final pre-hiatus show at Reading Festival. Atlantic compiled The Best Of The Lemonheads: The Atlantic Years (1998) on their departure.

Hiatus, Solo Years (1998–2004)

Dando released solo material during the hiatus, including Baby I’m Bored (2003, Setanta / Clearspot), produced and mixed by Bryce Goggin and Daniel Rey.

Reunion and Later Period (2005–present)

The band reformed in 2005 with recording help from Descendents members Bill Stevenson (drums, production) and Karl Alvarez (bass). The self-titled The Lemonheads (2006, Vagrant Records) featured guest spots from J Mascis, Garth Hudson (The Band), and Gibby Haynes (Butthole Surfers). Produced by Dando and Stevenson; engineered by Stevenson, Andrew Berlin, and Jason Livermore at the Blasting Room.

Varshons (2009, Cooking Vinyl) was a covers album produced by Gibby Haynes, featuring an unexpected selection of Wire, Gram Parsons, GG Allin, Christina Aguilera, and others; actress Liv Tyler dueted on a Leonard Cohen track.

Atlantic divested the Lemonheads’ back catalog to Fire Records in 2017. Fire has since issued Varshons II (2019), archival releases, and the new album Love Chant (2025).
