Overview

Daisy Chainsaw were a British alternative/noise rock band formed in London in 1989 when guitarist Crispin Gray placed a newspaper ad and vocalist Katie Jane Garside responded. Bassist Richard Adams and Canadian drummer Vince Johnson rounded out the original lineup. The group became notorious for ferociously theatrical live performances — Garside drilling doll heads onstage, drinking from baby bottles, and occasionally losing consciousness — before releasing their debut EP Love Sick Pleasure on the One Little Indian sublabel Deva Records in 1991.

Their debut album Eleventeen (1992) was produced jointly by Garside, Daisy Chainsaw as a collective, and engineer Ken Thomas, with track-by-track production credits split between those parties. “Love Your Money” from the album reached No. 26 on the UK Singles Chart in February 1992; the album itself peaked at No. 62 on the UK Albums Chart. Songs were almost entirely written by Crispin Gray (the exception: “Room Eleven” credited to the full band, and “Lovely Ugly Brutal World” co-credited to Garside). A&M released a US version, Selections From Eleventeen, in 1992.

Garside left the band in 1993, reportedly suffering a nervous breakdown. The remaining trio — Gray, Adams, and Johnson — recruited vocalist Belinda Leith for the second album For They Know Not What They Do (1994, FFRR / London Records), produced by the band and engineer Nick Addison with mixes by Mark Stent (Spike Stent). A third lineup, with Gray moving to vocals, recorded the EP Your Gruesome (1995) before the band renamed itself Dizzy Q Viper (1996–1997) for two further EPs. A recording under the name Vapid Dolly with Japanese artist Hanayo on vocals also dates to 1997.

Gray and Garside reunited in 1998/1999 to form Queenadreena, which released four studio albums between 2000 and 2008.

Sources: Discogs; Wikipedia (“KatieJane Garside”, “Eleventeen (album)”, retrieved from offline snapshot).
