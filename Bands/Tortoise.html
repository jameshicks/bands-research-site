Tortoise

Post-rock / experimental / electronic band. Chicago, Illinois. Formed 1990 (as Mosquito); renamed Tortoise 1993. Active to present. Discogs ID: 4727. Early alias: Mosquito (10).

Foundational figures in the Chicago post-rock scene alongside Gastr Del Sol and The Sea And Cake. Primarily instrumental; synthesized post-rock, jazz, dub, minimalism, and electronic music into a fluid style that defined a strand of 1990s American experimentalism. Signed to Thrill Jockey for most of their catalog; released Standards (2001) on Warp Records; most recent LP Touch (2025) on International Anthem Recording Company.

Herndon and McCombs co-founded the band in 1990 as Mosquito (confirmed in both their profiles); McEntire and Bitney joined when the name changed in 1993. Bundy K. Brown was a founding bass player and left in 1995. David Pajo brought the guitar voice that defined Millions Now Living Will Never Die and TNT; Jeff Parker — a jazz guitarist and AACM member who studied at Berklee and moved to Chicago in 1991 — joined around the same time, and became the permanent guitarist after Pajo’s departure.

The defining early statement. “Djed” is a 21-minute suite that opens Side A; it draws on marimba loops, dub basslines, and processed textures in a way that synthesized krautrock, minimalism, and electronic music. Became a touchstone of post-rock. Bundy K. Brown had left by this point but contributed compositions. David Pajo’s guitar is present; the full lineup is now the quintet of McCombs, Herndon, Bitney, Pajo, McEntire.

Thrill Jockey retained the US rights; Warp Records handled UK/European release under an exclusive territory licence. Physical pressings carry the credit line ”℗ & © 2000 Thrill Jockey under exclusive license to Warp Records Limited”, confirming the arrangement was a territorial licensing deal rather than a full label switch. No public interview from band members or either label has been found that explains the business or creative reasoning behind choosing Warp for the UK/Europe deal specifically; it was likely a standard indie distribution arrangement of the era. From It’s All Around You (2004) onward, Thrill Jockey handled all territories directly — no Warp licensing visible on subsequent releases. [source: Discogs release 30799 (vinyl) and 7220459 (CD), physical pressing credits] US Thrill Jockey pressing (ID 78481) credits: Douglas McCombs (Bass 6, Bass, Guitar, Lap Steel Guitar), Dan Bitney (Bass, Guitar, Percussion, Vibraphone, Marimba, Keyboards, Baritone Saxophone), John McEntire (Drums, Synthesizer, Guitar, Electric Harpsichord, Keyboards), John Herndon (Drums, Vibraphone, Keyboards, Sequencer), Jeff Parker (Guitar, Bass). Pajo not credited — confirmed departed before recording.

Personnel: Dan Bitney, Douglas McCombs, Jeff Parker, John Herndon, John McEntire (band). Recorded and mixed: John McEntire. Mastered: Dave Cooley. Design: Jeremiah Chiu. First album on International Anthem Recording Company (Chicago jazz label), departing from longtime home Thrill Jockey.

Label move — why International Anthem: Jeff Parker explained the switch in an interview with Crack Magazine (2025): “[Label founder] Scottie is a generation younger than us, and he grew up listening to Tortoise and having a crazy reverence for the band. We thought it was time to try something different, and even though it was a really hard decision, International Anthem put everything into their projects. I knew there would be so much energy behind us and a younger perspective happening.” [source: https://crackmagazine.net/article/profiles/tortoise-chicago-interview/]

The move was facilitated in part by Parker’s pre-existing relationship with International Anthem: he had released solo work on the label since The New Breed (2016) and had collaborated with label artists including Makaya McCraven and Joshua Abrams. International Anthem co-founder Scottie McNiece (a drummer and Chicago scene figure who co-founded the label with David Allen in 2012, launched 2014) grew up listening to Tortoise; the label’s ethos of genre-crossing Chicago experimentalism mapped closely onto Tortoise’s own identity. [source: https://crackmagazine.net/article/profiles/tortoise-chicago-interview/; label background: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Anthem_Recording_Company as of 2026-08-02]

Critical reception framed the switch as complementary rather than a break: reviewers noted that International Anthem is “a label led by musicians who grew up on their records” and that the move represented “a natural continuation rather than a restart.” [source: https://www.carrythezero.co/tortoise-touch/] No public statement from Thrill Jockey about the departure has been found. The Kreative Kontrol podcast (ep. 1026, Oct 2025) featured Dan Bitney and Douglas McCombs discussing the label change directly, but a transcript is not available online. [source: http://vishkhanna.com/2025/10/14/ep-1026-tortoise/]
