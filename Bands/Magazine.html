Magazine

Discogs ID: 26054 Origin: Manchester, England Active: 1977–1981; reunion 2009–2011 Labels: Virgin Records (original run); Wire-Sound (reunion)

English post-punk band, one of the most musically sophisticated of the late 1970s. Formed by Howard Devoto after he left Buzzcocks in early 1977. Over four studio albums they developed a sound that blended Devoto’s literary, unsettling lyrics with keyboards-forward arrangements and jazz-inflected guitar work — distinct from the raw minimalism of their punk contemporaries.

Formation and Early Career

Howard Devoto (born Howard Andrew Trafford, 1952, Scunthorpe) met Pete Shelley at Bolton Institute of Technology. They co-founded Buzzcocks in 1976, but Devoto left before the Spiral Scratch EP release. He formed Magazine in 1977, explicitly aiming for something more progressive and less conventionally rock-oriented than punk. The band signed to Virgin Records and released their debut single “Shot By Both Sides” / “My Mind Ain’t So Open” in January 1978.

Album Credits in Detail

Real Life (1978)

Produced and engineered by John Leckie at Abbey Road. Lineup: Barry Adamson (bass), Martin Jackson (drums), John McGeoch (guitar, saxophone), Dave Formula (keyboards), Howard Devoto (vocals, lyrics). Artwork: Linder Sterling (monoprint and design — her first prominent collaboration with the Manchester post-punk scene). Photography by Adrian Boot.

Secondhand Daylight (1979)

Produced by Colin Thurston (also the engineer on early Human League and Duran Duran). Recorded with Manor Mobile. John Doyle replaced Martin Jackson on drums. McGeoch played keyboards on B1 in addition to guitar. Cover illustration by Ian Pollock. Sleeve typography/artwork: Malcolm Garrett.

The Correct Use of Soap (1980)

Produced by Martin Hannett — his most significant production credit outside Factory Records, and notably cleaner/more direct than his Factory work. Backing vocals by Laura Teresa. Sleeve design by Malcolm Garrett. Hannett’s spatial clarity is present but the record has more punch than his Joy Division productions. Pete Shelley is not credited on this album but his influence on Devoto’s trajectory is context.

Notes (web) — Hannett production context

Hannett’s own assessment: Hannett considered The Correct Use of Soap his best technical production. (sonicscoop.com; corroborated by furious.com/Perfect Sound Forever)

Studio logistics: Sessions were spread across several London studios and the band’s own rehearsal space, recorded with a Virgin 24-track mobile unit; final mixing was done at Britannia Row Studios. (furious.com/Perfect Sound Forever, as of 2026-10-23)

Devoto on working with Hannett: In a September 1981 interview with Dutch music magazine Oor, Devoto said: “Martin moves in his own mysterious way. A lot of musicians find it hard to work with him, because he doesn’t communicate very well. He sits like Buddha behind the mixing desk, untouchable.” Devoto also told the NME: “We lent on him a bit more with Soap” — indicating the band exerted more creative control on this album than on Magic, Murder And The Weather. (Both quotes sourced from secondary web sources referencing the original interviews; original interview text not directly verified — rqsretrouniverse.blogspot.com and factorybenelux.com — the latter returned a 500 error during this research pass.)

Tracks most distinctively shaped:

  • “Because You’re Frightened” (opening track, A1): The most cited example of Hannett’s hand on this record. The locomotive rhythm is built from the interplay between John Doyle’s snare and the lower strings of John McGeoch’s guitar, with the guitar signal almost certainly processed through an Eventide Harmonizer — the first commercially available digital effects processor, capable of applying delay and feedback while reshaping timbre to produce a pliable, elastic texture. (furious.com/Perfect Sound Forever, as of 2026-10-23)
  • “A Song From Under The Floorboards” (lead single): Consistently cited alongside “Because You’re Frightened” as a standout where Hannett’s production is most audible; the track’s propulsive clarity is attributed to his mix work. (furious.com; thenewvinylvillain.com)
  • “Stuck”: Also named as a standout with Hannett’s production contributing to its sonic impact. (search-result snippet from furious.com, not independently verified in page body)

Signature techniques used: Beyond the Harmonizer on guitar, a forum post by an engineer (SSLtech on groupdiy.com) who worked with Hannett describes his use of the Marshall Time Modulator (MTM) for keyboard pads and sustained drone notes — creating a “not-quite-a-phase-but-not-quite-a-flange” character with fast-slap repeats and deliberately random modulation. This technique kept potentially monotonous passages sonically interesting while maintaining mix clarity. The Helios EQs at Strawberry Studios are also documented in Hannett’s workflow. (groupdiy.com, as of 2026-10-23)

Overall character vs. Factory work: Multiple contemporary and retrospective sources describe the production as “unusually crisp and clear without too much gimmickry” — more direct than Hannett’s Joy Division recordings, with strong spatial separation (each instrument occupying distinct space in the mix) while retaining the claustrophobic, unsettling atmosphere of the band’s earlier work. (thenewperfectcollection.com; thenewvinylvillain.com)

Play (1980)

Live album, produced by Magazine and John Brand. Captured the lineup with Robin Simon on guitar (McGeoch had left for Siouxsie & The Banshees in late 1980). Sleeve by Malcolm Garrett.

Magic, Murder And The Weather (1981)

Final original-run album. Produced by Martin Hannett; mixed by Hannett with assistant Chris Nagle (Hannett’s regular Factory assistant). Ben Mandelson (guitar, violin) replaced McGeoch. Backing vocals from Laura Teresa and Ray Shell. Hannett produced but John Brand handled recording/engineering. Devoto disbanded Magazine later in 1981.

No Thyself (2011)

Reunion album with Noko on guitar in place of McGeoch (who died in 2004). Pete Shelley co-wrote and co-credited lyrics/music on track 1. Self-produced by Magazine with Dave Formula and Noko recording. John Doyle and Dave Formula the only original members besides Devoto.

After Magazine

  • John McGeoch joined Siouxsie & The Banshees, then Public Image Limited, then The Armoury Show, then Visage. Died 4 March 2004, aged 48.
  • Barry Adamson joined Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds and Visage, then had a long solo career as a film composer and recording artist.
  • Howard Devoto worked at the Network photographic agency in London for 16 years. Reunited with Pete Shelley in 2002 (project Discogs ID 580338). Magazine reformed for live shows in 2009 and recorded No Thyself (2011).
  • Dave Formula and John Doyle participated in the 2009–2011 reunion.
