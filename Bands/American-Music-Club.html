American Music Club

Discogs ID: 92581
ANV: AMC
Origin: San Francisco, California, USA
Active: 1983–1995 (first run); 2003–2009 (reunion); occasional activity 2021
Labels: Grifter Records, Zippo Records, Demon Records, Alias Records, Virgin, Reprise Records, Merge Records, Cooking Vinyl

Alternative rock band from San Francisco. Centered on singer-songwriter Mark Eitzel and guitarist Vudi, with a rotating cast of collaborators over the years. Known for Eitzel’s confessional, frequently devastating lyrics set against spare arrangements that evolved from post-punk garage to lush, pedal-steel-inflected chamber-folk. One of the defining voices of the American indie underground in the late 1980s and early 1990s, critically championed but commercially elusive throughout their run.

Formation and Early Years (1983–1990)

Founded in San Francisco in 1983 (Discogs years_active from 1985). The earliest lineup — Eitzel, Vudi, Daniel Pearson, Matt Norelli on drums, and Brad Johnson (2) on keyboards — recorded The Restless Stranger (Grifter Records, 1985), produced by Tom Mallon. Mallon is both a co-musician (guitar, bass) and the consistent sonic architect of the first three records.

Engine (1987) expanded the lineup with Carla Fabrizio on cello and brought in multiple drummers (Dave Scheff, Tim Vaughan alongside Norelli). Still co-produced by Mallon, it moved closer to the bleak, country-inflected sound the band would develop.

California (1988, Demon Records) marked the arrival of Bruce Kaphan, whose pedal steel guitar became central to the band’s texture. Mallon produced and recorded. The UK-only United Kingdom (1989) added Mike Simms on drums and a guest piano spot from Charles Gillingham.

Major Label Era (1991–1995)

Everclear — Alias Records (1991)

The breakthrough. Eitzel, Vudi, Pearson, Simms, and Kaphan. Produced by AMC collectively with Kaphan and Norman Kerner (tracks 2 and 8 produced/recorded at Brilliant Studios by Kerner). Kaphan also mixed tracks 6 and 10; Joe Chiccarelli mixed the remaining nine. Bob Ludwig mastered. Cover painting by Jean Lowe. 14 pressings.

San Francisco — Reprise Records (1994)

Co-produced by AMC and Joe Chiccarelli. Jim Scott mixed most tracks (1, 3, 6–13). Tracy Chisholm recorded. Tom Recchion art direction; Stine Schyberg design. Jean Lowe cover painting. Bob Ludwig mastered. 16 pressings — most-pressed AMC album.

Reunion Era (2004–2009)

Love Songs For Patriots — Merge Records (2004)

First album in nine years. Produced by Eitzel and Tim Mooney; Mooney also engineered. Matt Pence mixed. Marc Capelle plays an extraordinary range of keyboards and brass (Hammond BC, Mellotron, Moog Voyager, Clavioline, Optigan, Orchestron, Freeman String Symphonizer, flugelhorn, melodica). JJ Golden mastered. Management by Bob Andrews (3), Amy Pojman, and Undertow Music Collective.

The Golden Age — Cooking Vinyl (2008)

Produced, engineered, and mixed by Dave Trumfio. Lineup shift: Sean Hoffman on bass, Steve Didelot on drums, Jason Borger on keyboards, Danny T. Levin on horns. Vudi and Eitzel both play guitar and keyboards. Guest vocal by Leyla Akdogan on track 2. Mastered by Mark Chalecki.

Mark Eitzel — Solo Career

Extensive parallel solo career from 1991 onward. Key albums:

  • 60 Watt Silver Lining (1996, Warner Bros.)
  • West (1997, Warner Bros.) — produced by Peter Buck of R.E.M.
  • Caught In A Trap And I Can’t Back Out ‘Cause I Love You Too Much, Baby (1998, Matador)
  • The Invisible Man (2001, Matador)
  • Candy Ass (2005, Cooking Vinyl USA)
  • Klamath (2009, Decor Records)
  • Don’t Be A Stranger (2012, Decor Records)
  • Hey Mr Ferryman (2017, Merge Records)

Also a member of Toiling Midgets, Naked Skinnies, The Cowboys (2), The Eitzel Ordeal, The Undertow Orchestra.
