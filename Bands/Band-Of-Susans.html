Band Of Susans

NYC noise rock / guitar-orchestra band. Discogs artist ID: 55969.

Formed in New York City in 1986 by guitarist and composer Robert Poss; disbanded 1996. The band’s signature sound was a massed wall of electric guitars — typically three or four guitars playing interlocking drone-heavy lines — over a driving rhythm section with vocals. Rooted in the guitar-orchestra tradition of Glenn Branca and Rhys Chatham, but with a more song-structured, post-punk sensibility.

Biography

Formation and Trace Elements era (1986–1988)

Robert Poss had prior history in experimental New York music: uncredited guitar work as early as 1980 (Whiplash Records), and a 1981 band release (Security Risk, on his own Trace Elements imprint). He released a solo single as “Sometimes” in 1986 on Trace Elements Records.

Band Of Susans formed around 1986–87 with the defining concept of multiple Susans — the founding lineup included Susan Stenger (bass, vocals), Susan Lyall (guitar, vocals), Susan Tallman (guitar), and Alva Rogers (vocals), with Poss on guitar/vocals and Ron Spitzer on drums.

The debut LP Blessing And Curse (1987, Trace Elements Records) was produced by Poss and engineered by Bob Power (among others). Poss wrote all words and music.

Blast First / Mute era (1988–1990)

Hope Against Hope (1988) was picked up by Blast First (Paul Smith’s Mute sublabel, home to Sonic Youth’s UK releases), bringing the band to a wider international audience. The LP was engineered by Jim Klein. The lineup remained the founding six: Poss, Stenger, Lyall, Tallman, Rogers, Spitzer.

By Love Agenda (1989), a significant lineup change had occurred: Susan Lyall and Susan Tallman were replaced by Karen Haglof (guitar, vocals) and Page Hamilton (guitar, vocals). This was the lineup for the band’s Blast First breakthrough. Hamilton would leave Band Of Susans shortly after to form Helmet. Karen Haglof continued; also active with The Crackers (3) and The Wad (2).

The single Child Of The Moon / Fall Down (1989) came out on 4AD and Blast First.

Rough Trade / Restless era (1991–1994)

After Love Agenda, the lineup shifted again: Hamilton and Haglof were replaced by Anne Husick (guitar) and Mark Lonergan (guitar), who would remain the core guitar pair through the band’s end. This quartet of Poss, Stenger, Husick, and Lonergan (with Spitzer on drums) is the lineup most associated with the Restless Records period.

The Word And The Flesh (1991) was released on Rough Trade in Europe / World Service. This was produced by Poss.

For the US market, Restless Records took over: Now (1992), Veil (1993), and Here Comes Success (1994) all appeared on Restless, with the UK/European versions on Blast First / World Service. Songs were written jointly by Poss and Stenger throughout this period.

The recurring producer and engineer for the Restless years was Bryce Goggin, who engineered Now, Veil, and Here Comes Success.

The John Peel Sessions (recorded for BBC Radio) were issued in 1992 on Dutch East India Trading.

A cassette-only release, Mood Swing (1993), came out on the small Sing Fat label.

Final period and dissolution (1994–1996)

Here Comes Success (1994/UK, Blast First / World Service) was the last studio album. The retrospective / compilation Wired For Sound (1995, Blast First) gathered earlier material. The band disbanded in 1996.

After the band: Robert Poss continued as a solo artist and collaborator (appearing on Rhys Chatham’s A Crimson Grail in 2010 for Nonesuch, and in other avant-guitar contexts); Susan Stenger went on to form Big Bottom and remains active in experimental music. Both have remained in touch with the Coh Da Guitar Quartet project (Discogs group ID: 3496252).
