Family Fodder

Discogs ID: 132796 Active: c. 1975 – present (ongoing) Origin: UK Genres: Post-Punk, Experimental, Art Pop, New Wave Labels: Fresh Records (14), Jungle Records, Crammed Discs, Staubgold, Dark Beloved Cloud, Psych.KG

Family Fodder are a UK experimental group formed around 1975, led by songwriter and multi-instrumentalist Alig (John Pearce). Their music draws on an unusually wide palette — acoustic folk, punk, new wave, reggae, dub, classical, chanson, and avantgarde — making them one of the more genuinely eclectic acts in the post-punk period. They were among the earliest signings to Jungle Records and have maintained a continuous if intermittent recording career into the 2020s.

Biography

Formation and Early Period (c. 1975–1979)

Family Fodder’s origins lie in North London around 1975. Alig (John Pearce), Mick Hobbs, and Felix Fiedorowicz — all Dada enthusiasts — formed a student group called The Ideologically Pure Nude, playing Farfisa organ, guitar, bass, and electric bassoon. Mark Doffman joined shortly after on drums. The group’s name was an irreverent suggestion for what the Worker’s Revolutionary Party might include in their newspaper, reflecting the ensemble’s avant-garde and countercultural sensibility. Their early influences ranged from “Syd’s Floyd, the Velvets and Beatles to Partch, Cage and Stockhausen.” (source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk)

The Ideologically Pure Nude evolved into a larger collective known as The Sheep’s Pig, which added Ian Hill (keyboards, vocals, tape-loops) and Martin Harrison (bass, guitar). Harrison’s influence introduced the collective to dub reggae, significantly shaping their sonic direction. When punk exploded in 1976, the group already had experimental credentials; rather than identifying as punks, they absorbed punk’s energy while maintaining their avant-garde orientation, viewing This Heat and Eno as heroes while dismissing more commercial new wave. (source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk)

At some point in this period, Ian Hill and Alig established Studio San Carlo in a basement — the group’s attempt to “own the means of production” — which became the creative hub for overdub work and early experimentation. The earliest Family Fodder gigs were being played at this time, featuring Mark or Rick Wilson on drums, Mick Hobbs on bass, Alig on guitar/keyboards/vocals, Dominique Levillain on lead vocals, and Felix on organ and bassoon. (source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk)

Alig has described what became Family Fodder as “initially my solo project, but came out of a sprawling London collective.” The Fact Mag overview (2016) characterises the first formal incarnation as a duo of Alig and Rick Wilson which then expanded when joined by Dominique Levillain and the drifting multi-instrumentalists. (source: factmag.com, 2016)

The group formally coalesced around the name Family Fodder, with a rotating cast that at various points included vocalist Dominique Levillain, keyboardist/vocalist Ian Hill, multi-instrumentalist Felix Fiedorowicz, cellist/bassist Graham Painting, drummer Rick Wilson, bassist Mick Hobbs, percussionist Mark Doffman, drummer Bazz Smith, vocalist Judy Carter, vocalist/keyboardist Lynn Alice, and bassist/guitarist Martin Harrison (also billed as Martin Frederix).

Their debut single arrived in 1979 through Parole Records, whose founder Alan Hauser had merged the imprint with Alex Howe’s Fresh Records (14) that year, bringing Family Fodder, Cuddly Toys, The Wall, and others onto the combined roster. Hauser had originally founded Parole to release material by Raped when other companies refused to touch the band. The Family Fodder single, “Playing Golf (With My Flesh Crawling) / My Baby Takes Valium” (FRESH 1 / PURL 4), was produced by Charles Bullen (of This Heat) and Alig under the shared pseudonym “Billy Bag.” Bullen at this period was working at Southern Studios alongside Phil Free (Crass), Ian Lowry (The Wall), and Jowe Head (Swell Maps), placing Family Fodder squarely within the London experimental underground circuit. (source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk) (source: punkrocker.org.uk)

That same year they released Sunday Girls (FRESH 9 / KNOT 1), a twelve-track mini-album credited to “Family Fodder And Friends,” which opened with a cover of Blondie’s “Sunday Girl” (written by Chris Stein) and ranged across short, multi-styled pieces, a Syd Barrett cover (“No Man’s Land”), and French-language tracks — a self-produced document of their eclecticism at that stage. The fan site Vivonzeureux confirms that the Sunday Girls mini-album “contains the earliest recordings of the band (some go back to 1975).” (source: vivonzeureux.fr)

Fresh Records Peak (1980–1981)

The early 1980s were the group’s most productive period. The single “Warm / Desire” (FRESH 8, 1980) was self-produced; “Savoir Faire” (FRESH 22 / CRAM 2457) was released simultaneously on Crammed Discs in Belgium, marking early European ties. “Savoir Faire” was produced by Charles Bullen (This Heat) and David Cunningham (The Flying Lizards) under the joint production alias “Executives for Executives.” Cunningham’s co-production credit brings his parallel post-punk circle — he was at the time producing This Heat’s studio albums and running his own Piano label — into direct contact with Family Fodder’s Fresh Records milieu. (source: factmag.com, 2016) The debut album Monkey Banana Kitchen (FRESH LP 3, 1980) is the most fully documented record of the classic lineup: produced by The People In Control (a collective credit covering Charles Bullen and associates), recorded at multiple London studios (Berry St., Elephant, Mobile, Vineyard), and featuring contributions from Alig, Felix Fiedorowicz, Ian Hill, Graham Painting (cello), Charles Bullen (drums, guitar, viola), Rick Wilson (drums), Mick Hobbs (bass), Martin Harrison (bass, guitar), Mark Doffman (percussion), Dominique Levillain (vocals), Judy Carter (vocals), and Naresh Singh Kaul (vocals). The tracklist juxtaposed original songs with a Schubert arrangement (“Der Leiermann / Organ Grinder”) and “Cerf Volant,” one of several bilingual French pieces.

Also in 1980, Greatest Hits — despite the title, an actual album of original material (not a compilation) — was issued by Crammed Discs as CRAM 016, featuring the broader ensemble and compiled from recordings that straddled both the Fresh and Crammed catalogs. Crammed Discs had been founded in 1980 by Marc Hollander (of Aksak Maboul) in Brussels, and in its earliest phase its roster spanned London post-punk (Family Fodder), Israeli rock (Minimal Contact), and Afro-European music — the connection to Family Fodder being one of the label’s very earliest international ties. (source: daily.bandcamp.com)

ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy ! (FRESH 37/12, 1981) was a 12” mini-album featuring a stripped-down four-piece: Alig (keyboards), Ian Hill (keyboards), Graham Painting (bass, guitar, percussion), and Bazz Smith (drums). Produced by Chris Mansbridge and the group, it included the nine-minute “Dinosaur Sex” — a track that would become one of their most-reissued pieces.

“Film Music” / “Room” (FRESH 32, 1981) was a self-produced 7” single.

Jungle Records Period (1982–1983)

Family Fodder moved to Jungle Records as one of the label’s very early signings. “Coral” (JUNG 4, 1982) — one of Jungle’s first catalog entries — was a 7” single featuring Lynn Alice on lead vocals, Alig (piano, synthesizer, alto saxophone), Graham Painting (cello, tambourine), and Bazz Smith (drums). Songwriting credit went to John Pearce; sleeve design was by Blodgett Waxwing.

All Styles (FREUD 2, 1983) was the group’s second full LP, a double album on Jungle Records. It was a conceptual tour-de-force: each track explicitly named a genre in its title (Progressive, M.O.R., Classical, Folk, Europop, Funk, Goodtime, Badtime, Soul, Jazz, Cabaret, Easy Listening, Punk, Country and Western, Disco), with the group — reduced to a core of Alig, Felix Fiedorowicz, Bazz Smith, and Lynn Alice — performing across those styles. Covers included “Mack The Knife” (Brecht/Weill) and “Falling In Love Again” (Hollaender). It remains one of the more ambitious UK post-punk LPs of its period.

Hiatus and Best-Of Era (1984–1999)

Activity slowed through the mid-to-late 1980s and into the 1990s. Savoir Faire: The Best Of (FREUD CD 062 / DBC 215, 1998) compiled material from the Fresh Records and early Jungle period; it was co-released by Jungle Records and the US label Dark Beloved Cloud. Liner notes were written by Dominique Levillain; design by Mark Robinson. Track-level credits confirm that Charles Hayward (This Heat) had contributed percussion to the original “Savoir Faire” recording. The credited executive producer for that track was David Cunningham (see note on his production alias above).

Water Shed and Dark Beloved Cloud (2000)

Water Shed (DBC 226, 2000) appeared on Dark Beloved Cloud, co-produced by Andy Le Vien and Martin Harrison. The album marked a shift towards a more spacious, ambient-tinged sound. Dominique Levillain sang on several tracks; the ensemble included Martin Harrison (bass, sampler), Rick Wilson (drums), Cécile Rabhi (percussion), and Ricky Edwards (bass clarinet, alto saxophone).

Staubgold Reissues (2014)

In 2014, German experimental label Staubgold reissued two key early records. Monkey Banana Kitchen (staubgold 130) appeared as a CD with seven additional tracks drawn from the ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy ! period. Schizophrenia Party (Director’s Cut) (staubgold 131) appeared as a limited LP with a revised and extended tracklist drawn from the ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy ! recording sessions, adding “Film Music,” “Room,” “The Big Dig,” and “Plant Life.” Markus Detmer served as executive producer of the reissues; mastering for the Monkey Banana Kitchen CD was handled by John Pearce.

Later Activity and Collaborations (2008–present)

More Great Hits! (Jungle Records, 2008) was a second retrospective compilation, gathering further pre-1984 material.

Idol Fodder / Bäbytalk (2008)

In 2008, Alig released an EP under the alias Idol Fodder (Discogs ID 1940809) in collaboration with vocalist Darlini Singh-Kaul, one of the daughters of original Family Fodder vocalist Dominique Levillain. The EP, titled Bäbytalk, was released as Volume VI of the Pregnancy Series co-produced by Slender Means Society (catalog SMS008) and States Rights Records (US). Slender Means Society is an independent US label founded in 2004 by Zac Pennington (vocalist of Parenthetical Girls). The EP explores themes of pregnancy and parenthood across seven tracks, combining post-punk with classical chamber music, jazz, funk, and ambient passages. Key tracks include “The Onliest Thing” (a simple duet over sawing cello), “Infamy,” “Death and the Maiden,” and “Analyse my life?” The instrumental “Earlimusix” draws comparisons to traditional Indian music. Alig plays oud prominently on the EP, reflecting his interest in Eastern instrumentation. (sources: vivonzeureux.blogspot.com; slendermeanssociety.com; wikipedia.org)

Vox Humana (8) is a further alias used by Family Fodder on Discogs alongside Idol Fodder.

Foreverandever (2016)

Foreverandever (Jungle Records, FREUDCD119, 2016) is a collection of recordings made in the late 1990s / early 2000s — “a couple of decades” before their eventual release — documenting a collaborative project between Alig and avant-vocalist Viv Dogan Corringham. The recordings were originally made for an album that remained unreleased. Some tracks were subsequently appropriated by Alig under his alter-ego Johnny Human for an obscure Italian release (label not confirmed in available sources); others were donated to a cooperative project. Jungle Records eventually assembled and renovated the material for official release in November 2016 under the Family Fodder name. Personnel: Alig Fodder (keyboards, guitar, vocals), Viv Corringham (vocals), Rick Wilson (drums and percussion), Dick Jude (drums). The album thus serves as the primary documented output of the V-Human project (the Alig / Viv Corringham duo registered on Discogs under ID 69181), though released under the Family Fodder name rather than V-Human. (sources: jungle-records.net; familyfodder.bandcamp.com)

The state51 Conspiracy / Singularity Series (2010–2013)

Family Fodder released material on The state51 Conspiracy, a London-based independent music company and distributor founded in 1991 by Paul Sanders. State51 was an early digital-era innovator (developed the Virgin Records website The Raft in the mid-1990s; co-founded Consolidated Independent distribution); by the 2010s it had restructured as The state51 Music Group with offices across Europe and Asia, and operated a boutique imprint alongside its distribution services. Its roster included Wire, Tom Robinson, Steve Jansen, and Guillemots alongside smaller acts. (source: everybodywiki.com)

In 2011 Family Fodder released six digital EPs — the Singularity series — through The state51 Conspiracy. Each release contains three tracks (approximately 11–13 minutes). The six volumes are:

Psych.KG / Kommissar Hjuler Collaborations (2010s–2020s)

From the 2010s onwards, Family Fodder became prolific participants in the international mail-art and cassette-culture network centered around Danish/German artist Kommissar Hjuler (Detlev Hjuler) and his project Kommissar Hjuler Und Frau. These releases appear on Psych.KG and associated imprints — a German micro-label operating in the Fluxus and sound-art tradition, dealing in limited vinyl editions. Confirmed collaborative releases include Fluxus in the Bathroom (2017), Widerstand & gute Polizeiarbeit (2018), and The Inevitable Krystal “Belle” Boyd Record (2020, split LP also featuring Faust (7)). The collaboration extends to the FLUXUS +/- series. (sources: discogs.com releases 10204375, 12920936; discogs.com master 1477746) These works place Family Fodder within a stream of European sound-art and Fluxus-adjacent activity quite removed from their original post-punk context.

Sex Works (JUNG 079, 2016) was a return to Jungle Records — a limited 7” EP in the All Styles vein.

In 2020, The Inevitable Krystal “Belle” Boyd Record (Psych.KG 545) appeared as a collaborative LP with Faust (7) and Kommissar Hjuler Und Frau.

Savoir Faire: The Best Of (Director’s Cut) (Jungle Records, 2020) was a vinyl reissue and expanded version of the 1998 best-of.

Press / Reception

Wire magazine included Monkey Banana Kitchen in their “100 Records That Set The World On Fire (While No One Was Listening)” list, describing it as: “A loose collection of friends and, more often than not, wanderers, Family Fodder reached their apex (or at least one of them) with Monkey Banana Kitchen. The music took the ferocity of contemporaneous British punk and scaled it way back, eschewing the giant pop hook and replacing it with the hoop jumping of songs in three languages, instruments played for only four seconds, harmonic call-and-response motifs and opaque but symbolic political lyrics. Multiple reprises of phrases and fragments result in a much more subtle and effective memory-tickle.” (source: staubgold.bandcamp.com / Wire citation)

Trouser Press (Ira Robbins) called Monkey Banana Kitchen “bizarre works of multifacial genius from a strange musical collective” and “Remarkable and great fun.” On All Styles, Robbins wrote it was “consistently enjoyable and infused with invention, cleverness, talent and a totally open outlook that discards nothing without first having an enthusiastic go.” (source: trouserpress.com)

NME described “Dinosaur Sex” (from ScHiZoPhReNiA pArTy !) as a “forgotten post-punk classic.” (source: Wikipedia, Family Fodder article, as of 2026-02-11)

AllMusic described Water Shed as “one of the better reunion albums of the post-punk generation.” (source: Wikipedia, Family Fodder article, as of 2026-02-11)

Jonny Trunk (Trunk Records founder) was quoted: “Musically I’ve got no idea what they are up to, but what they manage to create is a strange, engaging sort of sound.” (source: factmag.com, 2016)

Family Fodder are mentioned in Simon Reynolds’ Rip It Up and Start Again: Postpunk 1978–84. They have also been championed by Andrew Weatherall (crate-digger, DJ) and referenced by LCD Soundsystem. They appeared on Optimo’s Cease & Desist DIY! compilation. (source: factmag.com, 2016)

Live activity: The Fact Mag overview notes the group toured three times between 1981 and 1982. One documented early 1980s London gig is at The Lo Yo Yo at the Hope and Anchor (1984). In 2014, following the Staubgold reissues, original members reformed with new singer Bee Ororo and played live dates in Germany, France, and Switzerland. (source: vivonzeureux.fr; Wikipedia, Family Fodder article, as of 2026-02-11)

Open Questions

  • Pre-1979 precursor band names now confirmed (The Ideologically Pure Nude → The Sheep’s Pig), but specific gig dates and venue records from 1975–1978 remain undocumented.
  • The “Henry Fodder” alias appears on Discogs credits for both Alig and Graham Painting. No web source (interviews, liner notes, press) has confirmed whether this was an intentional shared alias, an informal collective tradition, or a cataloguing confusion. Treat as unresolved.
  • The name of the Italian label that released the “Johnny Human - Foreverandever” tracks has not been identified in available sources.
  • Full track listings for Singularity 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6 not individually verified.
  • Full Psych.KG / Kommissar Hjuler catalog with Family Fodder only partially surveyed.
  • David Cunningham’s “Executive Producer” credit on the Savoir Faire: The Best Of compilation may be distinct from his production role on the 1980 single (which is as co-producer under “Executives for Executives”). The two credits may refer to the same person’s involvement in different capacities, but this is not confirmed.
  • The “Coral” single (JUNG 4) lacks a master_id on Discogs, suggesting it may be under-catalogued.
  • Full track listings for Singularity 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6 not individually verified — only Singularity 1 and 3 have confirmed full track listings from web sources.
  • Psych.KG / Hjuler collaboration now more fully documented: 24 confirmed shared releases between Family Fodder and Kommissar Hjuler; Family Fodder has 42 primary release credits on Psych.KG (2014–2020). See Kommissar Hjuler for full list of co-releases.
  • No formal published interview with Alig/John Pearce located online; the Fact Mag article (2016) quotes him but is not itself framed as an interview.
