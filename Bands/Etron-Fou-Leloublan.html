Also known as: E.F.L., EFL, Etron Fou Le Loublan, Etron Fou-Leloublan
Country: France
Active: 1973–1986 (reunion recordings 2009–2010)
Genre: Avant-rock, Rock In Opposition, Art Rock

Overview

Etron Fou Leloublan were a French avant-rock group founded in 1973, active through 1986, and one of the key French representatives in the international Rock In Opposition network. Their name is untranslatable slang — roughly “the Crazy Pale Thug’s Turd” — and the music matched the provocation: jagged angular rock with French cabaret melodics, saxophone, organ, deadpan and absurdist vocals, and a formal rigour that placed them squarely in the post-Henry Cow/RIO tradition.

They released six original studio albums: the first two self-produced on their own labels (Gratte-Ciel 1976; 9H 17 Productions 1978), then on Celluloid (live 1980), then on the Swiss Turbo Music S.A. (1982, 1984), and finally two more on Ayaa / RecRec Music (1985), the latter two produced by Fred Frith. A live compilation À Prague (Gazul, 2010) documented a 2009 reunion tour; the retrospective 43 Songs (Musea, 1991) collected the three Turbo/Ayaa studio albums on 3 CDs. The 1991 compilation also confirmed all seven members who passed through the group. They dissolved in 1986 after Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés.

Biography

Formation and Early Period (1973–1978)

The band formed in France in 1973. The Discogs profile gives no further detail on the founding context, but the lineup on Batelages (1976) — self-released on the Gratte-Ciel label (cat: CIEL 2001) — shows Ferdinand Richard and Guigou Chenevier alongside Chris Chanet (brass, lyrics on the A-side). The record is credited to the full group as composer and arranger. The side-long opening track “L’Amulette Et Le Petit Rabbin” (18 minutes) establishes the group’s approach: extended forms, free-improvisation-adjacent structures, and theatrical Francophone vocality. The album was recorded in 1976 and sits at the intersection of jazz and avant-garde rock.

The second album Les Trois Fou’s Perdégagnent (Au Pays Des…) (1978) was self-released on 9H 17 Productions and brought a new member, Francis Grand, who played six instruments. The album was recorded and mixed by Jean-Pierre Grasset (“Verto” on guitar for the A-side). An Italian pressing appeared in 1979 on L’Orchestra, suggesting early international distribution through underground networks.

In 1978 EFL performed at the founding Rock In Opposition festival at the New London Theatre — footage of which was eventually released by Replica Records in 2015.

Rock In Opposition and International Connections (1979–1982)

The RIO connection put EFL in orbit with Henry Cow, Art Zoyd, Univers Zero, and other groups working at the edges of rock and contemporary composition. This relationship extended to recordings: Guigou Chenevier appeared as drummer on Fred Frith’s Speechless (Ralph Records, 1981) — an important LP in the Fred Frith catalog that brought together EFL members with NYC musicians including Bill Laswell, Fred Maher, and members of Massacre (2). Ferdinand Richard played bass and Jew’s harp on the same record, and Jo Thirion played organ and harmonium on the A-side.

In 1981, Chenevier also played drums and percussion on Vidéo-Aventures’ Musiques Pour Garçons Et Filles (Recommended Records, RR 8.75) — a 10” released through Chris Cutler’s Recommended Records UK, reinforcing the EFL/Henry Cow axis.

En Public Aux États-Unis D’Amérique (Celluloid, 1980) was their first major-label release, documenting a live US tour. Celluloid — Jean Karakos’s Paris label with NYC reach — was the right home for a band at this intersection of avant-garde and European underground.

Switzerland and the Turbo Music Years (1982–1984)

The core trio of Richard, Chenevier, and Jo Thirion consolidated on Les Poumons Gonflés (Turbo Music S.A., TMSA 3301, 1982), recorded in Switzerland with engineer Michel Engeli. This was the first album to feature Fred Frith as producer and guest musician — he plays guitar on “Christine” and “Pas L’sou,” and violin throughout, and is credited for “Production Artistique.” The collaboration established a template that would carry through to the Ayaa records.

Bernard Mathieu (soprano and tenor saxophone) appeared on Les Poumons Gonflés alongside the core trio. On Les Sillons De La Terre (Turbo Music S.A., TMSA 3302, 1984), Mathieu was replaced by Bruno Meillier (alto, tenor, and baritone saxophones), who also co-wrote three tracks. Chenevier produced this fourth album himself (“Production Artistique”). Engineer: Michel Engeli again.

Sophie Jausserand contributed artwork to both Turbo Music albums and would continue through the Ayaa period.

The Ayaa Years and Dissolution (1985–1986)

In 1985, Turbo Music reissued Les Poumons Gonflés via the French label Ayaa (Reims) — now with Fred Frith explicitly credited as producer on the sleeve. The same year brought Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés (AYAA dt 1085 / RecRec Music RecRec 08), the final studio album. This was co-released between Ayaa (France) and RecRec (Switzerland), with additional pressings on Germany’s Review Records.

Frith produced Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés and contributed guitar (three tracks) and violin. RecRec Music is co-credited as producer. Mastering by Rico Sonderegger. The album stands as EFL’s most widely distributed original release. The band dissolved in 1986.

The same year (1986), Ferdinand Richard produced Noisy Champs (Ayaa, DT 0486) by Les Batteries — a drum-group featuring Chenevier alongside Charles Hayward (This Heat) and Rick Brown. This was the last EFL-adjacent release on Ayaa before the label’s catalog diversified further.

Post-Dissolution and Reunion (1987–present)

After EFL dissolved, members went separate ways:

Ferdinand Richard became a producer and cultural organizer. He founded In-Poly-Sons and was president of Aide aux Musiques Novatrices (AMN). His groups after EFL include Bruniferd, Ferdinand Et Les Diplomates, Ferdinand Et Les Philosophes, and Gestalt Et Jive. He played 6-string bass on Skeleton Crew (2)‘s adjacent productions and appeared on the Dropera LP (RecRec, 1991) and the …Enclume release (RecRec, 1991).

Guigou Chenevier remained extremely prolific. His post-EFL groups include Octavo (Ayaa, 1992), Volapük, Encore Plus Grande, Les Batteries, Buga Up, Le Miroir Et Le Marteau, and many others. He released a solo percussion LP Le Batteur Est Le Meilleur Ami Du Musicien (In-Poly-Sons, 2003) and continued into the 2010s with groups including Howl (AltrOck, 2015) and appearances with Balungan, Les Mutants Maha, Piles (6), and Rêve Général.

Jo Thirion (real name Joséphine Thirion) participated in Art Moulu Tréfin, Inouï Productions, and ZAR (6) after EFL.

Bruno Meillier was also a member of Les I and Zero Pop. He shares the Bruniferd project with Ferdinand Richard.

In 2009, the core trio of Richard, Chenevier, and Thirion reunited for a European tour, documented on À Prague (Gazul Records, 2010). The album was recorded live in Prague and draws from the full studio catalog. It was well received within the progressive/avant-rock community.

The 3-CD retrospective 43 Songs (Musea, 1991) presented the complete studio output from all three original LPs, confirming all member credits.

The 2012 documentary Romantic Warriors II: A Progressive Music Saga — About Rock In Opposition (Zeitgeist Media) featured EFL.

RIO Network Context

Etron Fou Leloublan were one of the founding participants in Rock In Opposition. The movement was initiated by Henry Cow (specifically Chris Cutler) and held its first festival at the New London Theatre in 1978. The other founding bands were Art Zoyd (Belgium/France), Univers Zero (Belgium), Stormy Six (Italy), and Samla Mammas Manna (Sweden). EFL represented France.

Their broader network connections include:

  • Celluloid Records (Celluloid Records) — the Paris/NYC label that released their 1980 live LP, connecting them to the wider underground that included Material, Was (Not Was), Massacre (2), and Rosa Yemen.
  • RecRec Music (RecRec Music) — Swiss label founded 1979; co-released Face Aux Éléments Déchaînés and distributed EFL into the European avant-rock circuit. RecRec was also closely tied to Recommended Records (UK/Switzerland).
  • Recommended Records (ReR Megacorp) — UK distribution network; Chenevier appeared on Recommended product (Vidéo-Aventures, 1981) and EFL’s broader catalog circulated through this network.
  • Turbo Music S.A. — the Swiss label/management entity (Discogs ID: 464947) that managed and released the two mid-period studio albums; it was distinct from RecRec but part of the same Swiss avant-garde infrastructure.
  • Ralph Records — the US eccentric rock label (The Residents, et al.) that released Frith’s Speechless (1981), the most prominent joint document with EFL.

Key Personnel Details

Ferdinand Richard (Discogs ID: 509991)

Bass guitar (often tuned in G, described as “Guitare Bass En Sol”), vocals, lyrics, co-composer. Credited as “H. Richard” or “F.H.R” on several releases. Co-founder of In-Poly-Sons label; president of AMN (Aide aux Musiques Novatrices). Post-EFL groups: Bruniferd (with Bruno Meillier), Ferdinand Et Les Diplomates, Ferdinand Et Les Philosophes, Gestalt Et Jive.

Guigou Chenevier (Discogs ID: 488540)

Drums, tenor saxophone, vocals; prolific composer within EFL. Played with an extraordinary range of bands before and after EFL. Notably appeared on Vibrato (Fléau, 1979) as a composer; on Vidéo-Aventures (Recommended Records, 1981); on Arthur Et Les Robots (SAPEM, 1982) as primary artist. Post-EFL activity extends well into the 2010s. Name variations include “Guigou ‘Samba Scout’ Chenevier.”

Jo Thirion (Discogs ID: 509987)

Real name Joséphine Thirion. Organ (Farfisa), piano, trumpet, backing vocals. Also appeared on ZAR (6), Art Moulu Tréfin, and Inouï Productions after EFL.

Bruno Meillier (Discogs ID: 101310)

Member 1984 (Les Sillons De La Terre) and Hannover 1983 cassette. Also in Les I, Zero Pop, and Bruniferd (with Ferdinand Richard).

Chris Chanet (Discogs ID: 463637)

Original member on Batelages (1976); also credited as “Eulalie Ruynat” (alias). After EFL: Camizole, Urban Sax, Le Grotorkèstre.
