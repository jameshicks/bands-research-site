Martha And The Muffins

Canadian art-pop / new-wave band formed 1977 in Toronto, ON. Discogs ID: 30735. Also recorded as M+M (Discogs ID: 79790).

Biography

Formation and Toronto scene (1977–1979)

Martha And The Muffins emerged from the Ontario College of Art (OCAD) and the clubs along Queen Street West, Toronto. Several founding members — notably Martha Johnson, Mark Gane, Martha Ladly, and Andy Haas — were students or connected to the OCAD milieu. The band self-released their debut 7” Insect Love / Suburban Dream (1978) on their own Muffin Music (2) imprint (cat: MM 001), self-produced. The single’s lineup was: Martha Johnson (vocals, keyboards), Mark Gane (guitar), Martha Ladly (keyboards, backing vocals), Andy Haas (saxophone), Carl Finkle (bass), Tim Gane (2) (drums). All tracks written by Gane.

The band caught attention from Virgin Records UK through its Dindisc subsidiary. They signed to Dindisc and in 1979 traveled to The Manor residential studio near Oxford, England, to record their debut album with producer Mike Howlett.

Dindisc era / Echo Beach breakthrough (1980–1981)

Metro Music (1980, Dindisc) was the debut LP. Produced by Mike Howlett and engineered by Richard Manwaring; sleeve design by Peter Saville (2). The band-as-listed lineup: Martha Johnson (vocals, keyboards), Mark Gane (guitar, Synthi A synthesizer), Martha Ladly (vocals, keyboards, trombone), Andy Haas (saxophone), Carl Finkle (bass), Tim Gane (2) (drums). David Millar contributed to writing on one track. The album artwork concept was credited to the band themselves.

The album produced “Echo Beach” (Dindisc DIN 9, 1980), a Mark Gane–written single that became a top-ten hit in the UK, Canada, and several other markets. B-side “Teddy The Dink” was co-written by Gane and Brian Harvey (4). The single reached the top ten in the UK and was a significant chart hit internationally, establishing the band’s name.

Also in 1980: the second LP Trance And Dance (Dindisc) was released in the same year — produced again by Mike Howlett, same core lineup. Singles “Saigon,” “Suburban Dream,” “About Insomnia,” “Was Ezo,” and “Paint By Number Heart” were released from the Dindisc catalog in 1980–81.

This Is The Ice Age (1981, Dindisc) was the third studio album and a significant artistic shift. The band co-produced with Daniel Lanois, marking the beginning of the Lanois collaboration. Lanois also served as engineer and contributed backing vocals, percussion, and effects treatments. Jocelyne Lanois (Daniel’s sister) joined on bass, backing vocals, and percussion. Andy Haas contributed alto saxophone and trombone. Sleeve/cover design was by D/R Institute (credited as “15% D/R Institut (Saville-Wickens)”) — connecting to the Peter Saville design circle. The album produced the single “Women Around The World At Work” (Dindisc, 1981) and “One Day In Paris” (1981, Europe).

Martha Ladly departed from the band around this period; by the Danseparc (1983) sessions she was no longer in the lineup. She subsequently became a member of The Associates (UK post-punk group).

Transition to M+M / RCA era (1983–1986)

By 1983 the band had contracted to a core duo of Martha Johnson and Mark Gane, who rebranded as M+M (also written M + M, M&M). The Danseparc sessions included Jocelyne Lanois (bass, percussion), Nick Kent (drum machine, percussion), and co-production again by Daniel Lanois.

Danseparc (1983, RCA / RCA Victor) — released under both “Martha And The Muffins” and “M+M” as joint primary credits. Produced by Daniel Lanois with Mark Gane and Martha Johnson. The single “Danseparc (Every Day It’s Tomorrow)” was released on RCA Victor.

Mystery Walk (1984, Current) — M+M o/k/a Martha And The Muffins. Co-produced by Daniel Lanois with Johnson and Gane. The album drew on a larger session pool: Yogi Horton (drums on most tracks), Fred Maher (drums on one track), David Piltch and Tinker Barfield (bass), Randy and Michael Brecker (horns on one track), Dick Smith (4) (percussion), Quammie Williams (percussion), various backing vocalists. David Bottrill (later a major producer in his own right) was one of the assistant engineers. The album reached #2 on the US Billboard Dance Chart with “Black Stations / White Stations” (1984, RCA).

Cooling The Medium (1984, Current) — M+M, co-produced by Daniel Lanois. This was the third consecutive Lanois co-production.

The World Is A Ball (1986, RCA) — the final release under the M+M designation. A single “Song In My Head (Dance Re-Mix)” (1986, RCA) was also released. “Someone Else’s Shoes” appeared in 1986 on Current. These releases effectively closed the M+M chapter.

Post-M+M / Far Away In Time (1987)

Far Away In Time (1987, Virgin) — the band returned under the Martha And The Muffins name. Discogs credits are minimal for this release; no production credits are listed in the database.

1990s: Scoring, children’s music, hiatus

Martha Johnson and Mark Gane spent the 1990s primarily writing and recording music for television and film projects. In 1992, Modern Lullaby was released (Intrepid Records / Rebel Rec., Germany) — produced by Johnson and Gane, with David Piltch (bass), Michael Sloski (percussion), Tim Gane (2) (percussion), Stuart Gordon (violin), Earl Torno (mixing), and Peter Moore (mastered). Johnson and Gane shared all writing, performing, and production credits.

The birth of their daughter in 1992 inspired Martha Johnson to record Songs From The Tree House, a children’s album that won the 1996 Juno Award for Best Children’s Album.

A retrospective, Then Again: A Retrospective (1998, EMI Music Canada / Muffin Music (2)), was released and credited jointly to Martha And The Muffins and M+M.

2000s–2020s: Revival and archival work

Select Cuts From Echo Beach (Remix Versions) (2002, Select Cuts, Germany) — repackaging of catalog material.

Echo Beach (30th Anniversary Version) (2010, Muffin Music (2)) — 30th anniversary single.

The World Is A Ball (30th Anniversary Edition) (2017, Muffin Music (2)) — credited to both M+M and Martha And The Muffins. Produced by Johnson and Gane with David Lord as engineer and co-producer. Additional players: Tony Levin (bass/stick), David Piltch (bass), Tinker Barfield (bass), Michael Sloski (drums), Yogi Horton (drums), Dick Smith (4) (percussion), Rob Gusevs (keyboards), David Lord (keyboards). Peter Moore handled remastering. Hugh Syme did design.

Do You Ever Wonder? (This Sound Will Save You Remix) (2019, The Confidence Emperors).

Marthology: In And Outtakes (2021, popguru) — archival/rarities compilation produced and compiled by Johnson and Gane, with restoration and remastering by Peter Moore.

Where Blue Meets Green (Balearic Edits) (2017, Echo Beach, Germany) — another catalog re-edit package.

Scene Context

Martha And The Muffins were part of the Toronto new-wave scene that coalesced around Queen Street West and the Ontario College of Art in the late 1970s. The OCA connection supplied several members and shaped the art-school aesthetic of the early records. The Queen Street West corridor was the equivalent of New York’s no-wave or London’s post-punk scenes — small venues, art-practice cross-pollination, cheap rents.

Their signing to Dindisc placed them alongside OMD (Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark) on the Virgin subsidiary roster, and the Mike Howlett production team linked them to the wider British new-wave industrial. The Peter Saville (2) sleeve for Metro Music explicitly connected the band to the post-Factory design aesthetic (Saville’s Factory Records work is handled under the separate Discogs entry Peter Saville).

The Lanois relationship is the other defining thread: Daniel Lanois co-produced three consecutive albums (1981–1984) from his Hamilton, Ontario base at Grant Avenue Studio and Lanois’s own operations. This predates Lanois’s international breakthrough (Eno, U2, Peter Gabriel) by several years — This Is The Ice Age (1981) is among the earliest substantial documentation of Lanois as a record producer.

Martha Ladly’s departure and subsequent involvement with The Associates — a UK post-punk band — is the most notable member cross-connection to another documented vault band.

Andy Haas’s career after departing the band — documented extensively in his own vault page — traces a path from Toronto art-pop into the NYC experimental/no-wave underground (God Is My Co-Pilot) and later Toronto experimental jazz (The Cosmic Range).

Open Questions

  • Martha Ladly’s precise departure date from the band — the DB shows her credits only through Trance And Dance (1980); she does not appear on This Is The Ice Age (1981). The exact timeline and circumstances of her exit are not determinable from Discogs data alone.
  • Jocelyne Lanois’s connection to the band beyond these recordings — whether she toured with them or was a session player only.
  • “Far Away In Time” (1987, Virgin) has no additional credits in the database — producer, personnel unknown from Discogs.
  • The “Songs From The Tree House” children’s album (Juno 1996 winner) has no Discogs entry visible in the discography trawl — may be listed under Martha Johnson solo rather than Martha And The Muffins.
  • “Modern Lullaby” (1992) is listed under the band name on Discogs, though its character (Johnson/Gane duo production, small ensemble) suggests it may be a transitional/duo record — the Discogs label info (Intrepid / Rebel Rec.) is unusual for their catalog.
  • Nick Kent (Discogs ID: 910718) — listed as a MatM member on Discogs and credited on Danseparc; not the same as UK music journalist Nick Kent. Full background unknown from Discogs data.
  • Exact pressing/chart history for “Echo Beach” in non-UK markets (Canada, Australia) would require label discography research or web sources.
