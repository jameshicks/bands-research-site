Yeah Yeah Noh

Discogs ID: 261771
Origin: Leicester, UK
Active: c.1983–1987; reformed 2012
Labels: In Tape (1984–1985), Vuggum (1986), Strange Fruit (1987), Cherry Red (2001, 2014–2015)
Genres: Indie Rock, Post-Punk, New Wave

Leicester “unpop” group. Self-described as an “unpop” band (per Discogs profile), Yeah Yeah Noh released 2 albums and 5 singles between 1984 and 1987 before dissolving, then reformed in 2012. They are among the more obliquely witty acts of the mid-1980s British indie underground — song titles like “Prick Up Your Ears”, “Home-Ownersexual”, “Crimplene Seed Lifestyle” and “Mr Hammond Is Out To Lunch” signal the band’s sensibility. The core creative partnership was between vocalist/lyricist Derek Hammond and guitarist John Grayland, who share most of the writing credits.

The group shares significant membership with The Deep Freeze Mice: Alan Jenkins and Graham Summers were in both bands. Jenkins’ role in Yeah Yeah Noh is as an editor (credited on Fun On The Lawn Lawn Lawn) rather than as a performer, suggesting his participation was organizational/curatorial rather than as a regular player.

Biography

Formation and Early Singles (1984)

Yeah Yeah Noh formed in Leicester around 1983–1984 and signed to In Tape, the Manchester-based label run by Marc Riley (formerly of The Fall). Their debut release was The Cottage Industry EP (IT 008, 1984), produced by Rick Willson. The core lineup on the earliest records comprised Derek Hammond (vocals), John Grayland (guitar, euphonium), Adrian Crossan (bass), and Sue Dorey on drums or percussion — with Graham Summers also providing drums on some tracks.

Three singles appeared in 1984. The Beware The Weakling Lines E.P. (IT 010) had Crossan on violin and Dorey on drums (A-sides), with Summers filling in on the B-side, produced again by Rick Willson and mastered by Porky (5) (George Peckham). The Prick Up Your Ears EP (IT 012) was produced and engineered by Wayne George and introduced a fuller lineup: Crossan (bass), Grayland (guitar, euphonium), Dorey (percussion). The B2 track “Bias Binding” was credited to a separate act, Terry And Gerry.

Writing credits on all early releases are shared across the Hammond/Grayland axis, with Crossan and Dorey co-credited on several tracks — confirming a genuinely collaborative songwriting process within the rhythm section.

When I Am A Big Girl and Another Side To Mrs. Quill (1985)

1985 was the band’s most prolific year on In Tape. The mini-LP When I Am A Big Girl (IT 016) compiled early single tracks with Grayland (guitar, euphonium), Crossan (bass), Dorey and Summers on drums across different tracks, and Hammond on vocals.

The 12” single Another Side To Mrs. Quill (ITTI 020) is the most elaborate production from the In Tape period: its four tracks use different drummer configurations (Anthony Woolley on A1 and B4, Dorey on B1 and B2), Wayne George and Rick Willson as engineers/producers, and Rob Grant as arranger and Wasp synth player on “Penetration”. John Grayland took on mixed/editing roles for overdubs on several tracks. The etched runout inscriptions — “SUNDAY’S ON THE PHONE TO MONDAY, TUESDAY’S ON THE PHONE TO ME” — are characteristic of the band’s sardonic wit.

Cutting The Heavenly Lawn Of Greatness…Last Rites For The God Of Love (LP, IT 021, 1985)

The first full-length album, released on In Tape (catalog IT 021). Fifteen tracks across two sides. The recording adds Andy Nicholls on piano, organ (Vox), and vibraphone — the first appearance of keyboards in the lineup — and Tom Slater on rhythm and lead guitar alongside Grayland, expanding the guitar palette considerably. Hammond plays acoustic guitar in addition to lead vocals. Dorey provides drums and vocals; Grayland, Slater, and George provide backing vocals. Wayne George produced and engineered most of the album; a BBC session-style track (“See Through Nature”) was produced by André Jacquemin with engineer Mike ‘Megawatts’ Robinson. Carol Leeming contributed backing vocals to the full album.

This is the document of the band’s classic lineup: Hammond, Grayland, Slater, Nicholls, Dorey, Crossan — all six central members present. The album’s title and tracklist (“Home-Ownersexual”, “Panacea’s Pictures”, “Stealing In The Name Of The Lord”, “Blood Soup”) consolidate the band’s identity as wry, literate post-punk with a strong sense of the absurd.

Temple Of Convenience E.P. (ITTI 023, 1985)

A 12” EP collecting four tracks (with a bonus Beatles cover “Rain” credited to The Psychodelic Filberts) and the multi-part “Jigsaw” suite. “Jigsaw” is the band’s most adventurous recording from the period: Grayland overdubs twelve-string guitar, synthesizer, and sampler; the track samples incidental music from The Beatles and a “Sampled Talkover” by John Peel; Andrew Grayland (John’s relative) is credited for samples. Hammond plays piano on “Jigsaw” as well as singing. Wayne George produced and engineered.

Fun On The Lawn Lawn Lawn (LP, Vuggum, BAAD 002, 1986)

A retrospective/live-tracks LP released on the obscure Vuggum imprint (not In Tape). Alan Jenkins is credited as “Edited By”. Multiple producers appear across different track groupings: Dale Griffin (ex-Mott The Hoople) produced the majority of tracks; André Jacquemin and Mike Robinson produced others; and Mike Engles co-produced several. Sleeve notes by music journalist Mat Snow. This compilation/archival release consolidates the band’s singles-era catalog with live and unreleased material.

The Peel Sessions (Strange Fruit SFPS026, 1987)

Four tracks recorded for John Peel’s BBC Radio 1 programme, released on Strange Fruit (the specialist Peel Sessions imprint). Produced by Dale Griffin, mixed by Mike Engles. The lineup is the core post-Cutting The Heavenly Lawn group: Hammond (vocals), Grayland (guitar), Slater (guitar), Nicholls (piano, credited as “Andrew Nicholls”), Dorey (drums), Crossan (bass). Additional vocals by Julie Dennis. The four tracks — “The Superimposed Man”, “Stealing In The Name Of The Lord”, “Blood Soup”, “(It’s) Easier To Suck Than Sing” — present the band in a live/session context with a relatively punchy sound compared to the studio records.

Dissolution and Compilations (1987–2001)

The band dissolved after the Peel Sessions release. Cherry Red issued Leicester Square: The Best Of Yeah Yeah Noh (CDM RED 183) in 2001 — a 20-track CD compiled by Derek Hammond and Tim Madgwick, with the original Fun On The Lawn sleeve notes by Mat Snow reprinted.

Reformation and New Material (2012–2015)

The band reformed around 2012. Cherry Red released a promo CDr She Pulls The Petal From The Flower (2014) with new and remixed tracks (including “Score Mix”, “Juneau Mix”, “Swoomptheeng Mix”, and “Arc Vel Mix” variants), then issued singles Automatically Saturday and In A Loop In Time (Cherry Red, 2015) and the full album Automatically Saturday (Vuggum BAAD 003, 2015) — a 12-track CD album, the first proper new studio LP since the 1980s.

Labels

In Tape (Discogs ID: 27977) — Manchester label run by Marc Riley (ex-The Fall). 136 releases in DB. Based in Sale, Cheshire. Yeah Yeah Noh released their first seven records here (1984–1985), including both LPs. Sublabel: Sidetrack Music.

Vuggum (Discogs ID: 68078) — Small UK imprint; released Fun On The Lawn Lawn Lawn (1986) and the 2015 reformation album Automatically Saturday. Catalog prefix BAAD.

Strange Fruit (Discogs ID: 1567) — BBC Peel Sessions specialist label; part of Strange Fruit Records Ltd. Over 1,000 releases in the DB. The Peel Sessions (SFPS026, 1987) was their only release here.

Cherry Red (Discogs ID: 5174) — London independent. Released the 2001 best-of Leicester Square and the 2014–2015 reformation material.
