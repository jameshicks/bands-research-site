Massacre (2)

New York experimental rock / free improvisation trio. Discogs artist ID: 99558. Genres: Jazz, Rock. Styles: Avantgarde, Free Improvisation.

Founded 1980 by guitarist Fred Frith, bassist Bill Laswell, and drummer Fred Maher. Performed and recorded as an improvising unit for just over a year, producing one studio album. Reconvened in 1998 with Charles Hayward replacing Maher on drums; the reunion configuration released four further albums on Tzadik through 2013.

The BBC described them as “an unholy union of The Shadows, Captain Beefheart, Derek Bailey and Funkadelic.”

Biography

Formation and initial run (1980–1981)

Massacre formed in New York City in 1980. Frith had recently relocated from the UK, where he had been a founding member of Henry Cow; Laswell and Maher were core members of Material, the New York avant-funk collective that had released records on Celluloid Records. The three worked as an improvising unit — all material is credited “Composed Or Improvised By Massacre” — with Frith playing electric guitar, voice, and Cassiotone keyboards, Laswell on bass and pocket trumpet, and Maher on drums and percussion.

They performed live for just over a year and recorded Killing Time, released in 1981 on Celluloid (cat. CEL 6597). Recording took place across two sessions: one at OAO Studio in New York (engineered by Martin Bisi) and one in Paris at 24 Rue Dunois (engineered by Jean-Marc Foussat). Additional tape work was handled by Greg Curry at Sorcerer Sound, New York. The album was released as a French pressing; 25 pressing variants are documented on Discogs.

The band dissolved after the initial run. Maher went on to become the drummer for Scritti Politti (1984–1988) and a staff producer at Elektra Records, working with Lou Reed, Matthew Sweet, Lloyd Cole, and Information Society. Laswell remained prolific in the Celluloid orbit — producing, performing, and building the Material extended network.

Reunion with Charles Hayward (1998–2013)

Frith and Laswell reformed Massacre in 1998 with Charles Hayward (founding drummer of This Heat, also a member of Gong, Quiet Sun, The Camberwell Now, and Keep The Dog) replacing Maher. The reunion Massacre operated as a live improvisation project; all four reunion albums were recorded in live performance.

Funny Valentine (Tzadik, 1998, TZ 7601) was recorded by Robert Musso and is the only reunion album credited as produced by the band. John Zorn served as executive producer on this and all subsequent Tzadik releases, with Kazunori Sugiyama as associate producer throughout.

Meltdown (Tzadik, 2001, TZ 7606) was recorded live by Oz Fritz (DRT Recording), with Fritz also handling concert sound. Track titles were credited jointly to Frith, Laswell, and Hayward, except “Song For Che” which carries a Charlie Haden songwriter credit.

Lonely Heart (Tzadik, 2007, TZ 7619) was produced by Frith, recorded live by Oz Fritz, with Peter Hardt assembling the final mix from live recordings. Pre-mastering by Myles Boisen; mastered by Scott Hull (2).

Love Me Tender (Tzadik, 2013, TZ 7642) compiled and edited by Frith and Myles Boisen from European festival recordings made between 1999 and 2008. Hayward plays drums, melodica, and voice throughout the reunion period; on Love Me Tender he also carries vocal credits. Lyrics on tracks 4 and 5 are credited to Merlin Hayward (Charles’s son, also known as Merlin Nova).

Context and connections

Massacre sits at the intersection of two distinct lineages. Frith came from the UK RIO / avant-rock tradition — Henry Cow, Art Bears — and was exploring solo guitar work and New York’s downtown improvisation scene. Laswell and Maher brought funk-rooted rhythm grammar from Material, whose Memory Serves (Celluloid, 1981) was recorded in the same year as Killing Time with the same basic rhythm section and the same engineer (Martin Bisi at OAO Studio). The band was part of the broader Celluloid cluster of early 1980s New York downtown artists — overlapping with Material, Defunkt, James White, and the label’s jazz-punk crossover roster.

The reunion lineage connects to the UK post-punk experimental tradition through Hayward. His This Heat — a founding landmark of the British avant-rock and industrial-influenced scene — was active 1976–1982; Hayward then played in The Camberwell Now and various collaborative projects including Keep The Dog (a Fred Frith composition sextet also featuring Hayward) before joining the reunion Massacre. The Tzadik home for the reunion aligns Massacre with John Zorn’s downtown NYC experimental label, which documented much of the 1990s–2000s New York improv and avant-jazz scene.

Frith and Laswell had crossed paths well before Massacre: both appeared on Memory Serves (Material, Celluloid, 1981), on Speechless (Fred Frith solo, Ralph Records, 1981), and together with Maher on the original Massacre. The trio’s tight web in the Celluloid orbit — the same rhythm section playing in both Material and Massacre simultaneously — is one of the more compact illustrations of how fluid the downtown scene was.

Hayward’s prior relationship with Frith also runs deep: both played in Keep The Dog (Frith’s composition sextet) and on Meridiem (Materiali Sonori, 1998), a quartet session with Percy Howard recorded just as the Massacre reunion was being formed.

Cover art note

The cover of Funny Valentine uses artwork by Adolf Wölfli (1864–1930), the Swiss outsider artist whose compulsive pictographic work became one of the defining examples of art brut. The Wölfli estate / Adolf Wölfli Foundation in Bern manages his archive; his work appeared on several experimental/avant-garde records in the 1990s–2000s (the Tzadik label used it on multiple releases). Design for Funny Valentine was handled by Ikue Mori, drummer of DNA and long-standing downtown improv figure.

The Meltdown “Song For Che” title

The track title “Song For Che” on Meltdown carries a Charlie Haden songwriter credit — not because Haden performed on the record, but because the musicians took the title from Haden’s composition (the same piece Haden recorded with Ornette Coleman’s quartet). The title-by credit is the Discogs-documented credit.
