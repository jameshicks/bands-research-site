Overview

Swan Lake is a three-piece group formed in 2006 by Carey Mercer, Daniel Bejar, and Spencer Krug — each already established in separate Canadian indie acts. The catalogue spans two releases across the 2000s, issued primarily through Jagjaguwar with earlier appearances on Scratch Records and Houston Party Records. Their sound draws on indie rock, art rock, and leftfield aesthetics. Recording and mixing work is shared between Colin Stewart and John Congleton, both of whom appear across multiple releases.

Notes (web)

Formation — why they came together in 2006

The collaboration grew out of existing personal and working relationships rather than any deliberate plan. Daniel Bejar and Carey Mercer first performed together on the opening night of SXSW 2004, when Mercer’s Frog Eyes backed Bejar’s Destroyer — a pairing that went on to tour the US and Europe in 2005. Spencer Krug was drawn in when Mercer’s keyboard player dropped out before the European leg; Krug was an old friend and former Frog Eyes member, and he and Bejar had also lived together for a year in the late 1990s before Krug moved to Victoria and co-founded Frog Eyes with Carey and Mel Mercer. (Source: search-result snippet citing Billboard / PopMatters, billboard.com; popmatters.com)

After touring together as Destroyer with Frog Eyes, the three found that playing each other’s songs in those configurations was creatively pressured — attacking one of Bejar’s songs in a Destroyer context came with preconceptions. A dedicated project with no pre-set identity felt freeing. Mercer told Radio Free Canuckistan (November 2006): “It felt really good to me, and we felt like it would be awesome if we could provide ourselves with a more formal atmosphere.” (radiofreecanuckistan.blogspot.com)

Krug framed it similarly in a contemporaneous interview on the same blog: “There were no preconceived notions of what any of it would sound like. Whatever came out was going to be the record… This was really pure, for lack of a better word. We just wanted to work with each other and see what happened.” Jagjaguwar backed the idea; each member contributed songs that the trio developed together in the studio. (radiofreecanuckistan.blogspot.com)

The album title Beast Moans was reportedly Mercer’s description of Krug’s sonic influence. (Source: search-result snippet citing Billboard)

Dissolution or dormancy after Enemy Mine (2009)

No member has issued a formal dissolution statement. The band simply stopped releasing material and making live appearances after Enemy Mine. The 2009 Exclaim! article announcing Enemy Mine noted “there are no tour dates currently planned for Swan Lake” and carried no comment from the members about the project’s future. (exclaim.ca)

The clearest on-record statement comes from Daniel Bejar in an interview with Asheville Stages (date unclear from page; piece covers the Labyrinthitis era, c. 2022–2024). Asked about plans to revisit Swan Lake, Bejar replied: “Zero plans.” He did not describe the project as formally over, only that nothing was in motion. (ashevillestages.com)

A 2012 A.V. Club profile of Spencer Krug (as Moonface) noted Swan Lake was inactive alongside his other bands, but used no language of permanent disbandment. (avclub.com)

Assessment: The available record supports dormancy / indefinite hiatus rather than a formal breakup. No member has announced a split; Bejar’s “zero plans” is the strongest signal, and it reads as indifference rather than a closed door. The pattern matches how Krug handled Wolf Parade and Sunset Rubdown — extended silences that eventually ended in reunion — though no reunion has been announced for Swan Lake as of October 2026.
