Overview

Strange Fruit (9) was a post-punk group from Kalamazoo, Michigan, active 1982–1984. Their small catalogue — two releases on Babel Records — blended post-punk with art rock, noise, and experimental textures. The band’s lasting significance lies in its membership: Steve Shelley went on to become Sonic Youth’s drummer from 1985, and Tim Foljahn became a prolific collaborator in the New York indie and experimental underground. A 2020 issue on Vampire Blues suggests archival recordings from the Kalamazoo era resurfaced decades later.

Notes (web)

2020 Vampire Blues release — what the web says

The 2020 archival release on Vampire Blues is the self-titled Strange Fruit Abiku digital release, which compiles the original 1983 7” (three tracks) with three previously unreleased bonus tracks. It is issued through Steve Shelley’s own label; Vampire Blues is described in its own press materials as dedicated to “rare, archival, and previously unreleased recordings” with Shelley acting as “curator, searcher, and label founder.” (shorefire.com press release)

The Bandcamp page for the release (strangefruitabiku.bandcamp.com) describes it as “the very first vinyl appearance made by Steve Shelley of Sonic Youth” and contextualises the music within Kalamazoo regional despair. No statement on the page explains what specifically prompted the 2020 release.

What the web could not establish: No interview, press release, or liner note accessible via web search states what specifically triggered the release in 2020 — e.g. whether a tape discovery, a band member’s initiative, a Bandcamp Friday, or the Sonic Youth dissolution (2011) prompted Shelley to finally digitise the recordings. The Straight.com two-part Shelley interview (part one, part two), the Talkhouse piece (talkhouse.com), the Brian Turner archive-dive episode (Oct 2021, brianturnershow.com), and the Daydream Nation legacy interview (mookidmusic.com) were all checked; none discuss the Strange Fruit Abiku release specifically. Boomkat, Allmusic, and Stereophile were attempted but returned 403 errors.

The most plausible framing from available evidence: Shelley has used Vampire Blues as a personal archival imprint since at least 2010–2012, releasing his own pre-Sonic Youth material (including Strange Fruit, the Nozones, and other mid-Michigan projects) alongside new recordings. The Strange Fruit Abiku digital release follows this established pattern; the specific 2020 timing is undocumented in any public source found.
