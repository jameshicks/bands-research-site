Pere Ubu

Avant-rock / post-punk band. Cleveland, Ohio. Formed late 1975. Discogs artist ID: 83071.

Overview

One of the most important American avant-rock groups, the defining band of Cleveland’s post-punk underground and a long-running experiment in subverting rock form through art-damaged sound design. Formed by vocalist David Thomas (2) (b. 1953; also known as Crocus Behemoth) and guitarist Peter Laughner (1952–1977), the band has operated in various configurations across five decades with Thomas as the consistent anchor. Their sound combined garage-rock intensity with synthesizer noise, tape manipulation, and Thomas’s distinctive warbling baritone, anticipating post-punk and industrial music well before those terms existed.

Name variations on Discogs: P. Ubu, Pere Ube, Proto Ubu, Ubu, Ubu Projex. Discogs also notes an alias entry for “The U-Men” (unclear context).

Biography by Era

Proto-Ubu / Cleveland Underground (1975–1977)

Pere Ubu emerged in the wake of Rocket From The Tombs, a proto-punk band that had also included Peter Laughner, David Thomas (2), and several others who would go on to Dead Boys and related acts. When Rocket dissolved in 1975, Thomas and Laughner convened a new group oriented toward more experimental, noise-inflected rock. The first lineup included Tom Herman (guitar), Tim Wright (2) (bass), Scott Krauss (drums), and Allen Ravenstine (synthesizer).

Their first recordings were self-released on Hearthan (later catalogued under the parent label Hearpen Records): the debut single “30 Seconds Over Tokyo” b/w “Heart Of Darkness” (1975), followed by “Final Solution” (1976) and “Street Waves / My Dark Ages” (1976). These singles — raw, abrasive, and tonally strange for their time — are now regarded as foundational texts of American proto-punk and post-punk.

Peter Laughner’s health deteriorated rapidly due to drug and alcohol abuse; he died of acute pancreatitis in June 1977 at age 24. He was replaced by Mayo Thompson (Red Krayola) for subsequent recordings.

Classic Period: Blank / Chrysalis / Rough Trade (1978–1982)

The Modern Dance (1978, Blank Records) — the first full album — captured the core quintet of Thomas, Herman, Ravenstine, Maimone, and Krauss at their most aggressive. Produced by Ken Hamann with the band, and engineered by Hamann and Paul Hamann at Cleveland Recording Company. The album’s mix of brutal riffing and Ravenstine’s EML synthesizer noise defined a template for post-punk noise rock. A UK licensing deal through Radar Records (EP Datapanik In The Year Zero, 1978) and then Chrysalis brought them European exposure.

Dub Housing (1978, Chrysalis) — a more abstract and rhythmically dislocated follow-up, co-produced by Ken Hamann. Same core lineup. Widely regarded as one of the era’s most challenging records.

New Picnic Time (1979, Chrysalis) — third album, still the core five-piece, produced with Ken Hamann and Paul Hamann. Growing experimentation with texture and space.

After Chrysalis, the band signed to Rough Trade. The Art of Walking (1980) — produced by Paul Hamann and the band — saw Tom Herman depart; the album featured contributions from Mayo Thompson (guitar) and introduced a more skeletal, anti-rock aesthetic.

Song of the Bailing Man (1982, Rough Trade) — the last record of this phase — was produced by Adam Kidron and marked a significant lineup change: Anton Fier (drums), Mayo Thompson (guitar), Tony Maimone (bass), Allen Ravenstine (EML synthesizer). Guest trumpet by Eddie “Tan Tan” Thornton. Thomas was sole vocalist and lyricist.

The band dissolved after Song of the Bailing Man. A compilation of the Hearthan singles, Terminal Tower — An Archival Collection, was released by Rough Trade in 1985.

Hiatus, Solo Work, and Reformation (1982–1987)

During the hiatus, David Thomas (2) recorded a series of experimental solo and group albums under names including David Thomas And The Pedestrians (two albums), David Thomas And The Wooden Birds, and David Thomas And Two Pale Boys. Chris Cutler (Henry Cow, Art Bears) was a recurring collaborator across these projects.

Several former members found themselves on stage together under the Wooden Birds project, which provided the nucleus for a reunion.

Reformation and Pop Experiment (1988–1994)

Pere Ubu reformed in 1988 on Fontana (a PolyGram imprint). The new lineup brought in Jim Jones (3) on guitar and Chris Cutler (drums/percussion) alongside returning members Tony Maimone, Scott Krauss, and Allen Ravenstine.

The Tenement Year (1988) — reformed debut, produced by Paul Hamann and the band, mixed by Hamann, Thomas, and Cutler. John Kirkpatrick (melodeon) appeared as a guest.

Cloudland (1989) — the most commercially ambitious Pere Ubu record, pursuing pop songcraft through multiple outside producers: Stephen Hague (tracks A1, A2, A5, A7), Paul Hamann (multiple tracks), Daniel Miller (B1), and Dave Meegan (B3, plus several mixes). Rico Conning also produced and mixed one track. David Bates served as executive producer. The sprawling multi-producer approach yielded a genuinely strange major-label pop record.

Worlds in Collision (1991) — produced by Gil Norton. Brought in Eric Drew Feldman (computer, keyboards, synthesizer) and Garo Yellin (violin). John Kirkpatrick again on melodeon; Stephen Hague had executive-producer credit.

The Story of My Life (1992) — Fontana. Personnel credits sparse in Discogs entry.

Tim/Kerr Period (1995–2000)

The band relocated to the Portland-based Kerr Records and produced some of their most vigorous work of the 1990s.

Ray Gun Suitcase (1995) — produced by David Thomas (2). Lineup: Thomas, Jim Jones (3), Michele Temple (bass), Scott Benedict (drums), Scott Krauss (drums on selected tracks), Garo Yellin (cello), Robert Wheeler (synthesizer, theremin). This album introduced Robert Wheeler and Michele Temple as key long-term members.

Pennsylvania (1998) — produced by Thomas. Lineup included Temple, Jones, Tom Herman (returning), Steve Mehlman (drums), and Wheeler. All instruments uncredited in the original sleeve.

Apocalypse Now (1999, Thirsty Ear) — live album.

A reissued/expanded Datapanik in the Year Zero (DGC, 1996) collected the Hearthan singles with bonus material; the Hearpen Singles compilation (Hearthan/Tim/Kerr, 1995) similarly archived the early 45s.

St. Arkansas and Glitterhouse Period (2002–2008)

St. Arkansas (2002, Glitterhouse Records) — returned the band to studio work after several years. Germany-based Glitterhouse was the primary outlet for this stretch.

Why I Hate Women (2006, Glitterhouse) — further expanded the band’s sonic palette with heavy electronic processing.

Why I Remix Women (2006, Glitterhouse / Hearpen Records) — remix companion.

Late Work and “Long Goodbye” (2009–2023)

“Long Live Père Ubu!” (2009, Cooking Vinyl) — reactivation on a UK label, back to London-facing indie.

Lady from Shanghai (2013, Fire Records) — produced by David Thomas. Hamann as engineer. Marked the start of the Fire Records period.

Carnival of Souls (2014, Fire Records).

20 Years in a Montana Missile Silo (2017, Cherry Red) — lineup: Thomas, Michele Temple (bass), Keith Moliné (guitar), Robert Wheeler (synthesizer, theremin), Steve Mehlman (drums), Gary Siperko (guitar), Darryl Boon (clarinet), Kristof Hahn (steel guitar). Gagarin (digital synths). Produced by Thomas, engineered by Paul Hamann.

The Long Goodbye (2019, Cherry Red).

By Order of Mayor Pawlicki (Live in Jarocin) (2020, Cherry Red) — live document from Poland.

Trouble on Big Beat Street (2023, Cherry Red) — most recent studio album. Lineup: Thomas, Temple, Keith Moliné, Andy Diagram (trumpet, electronics), Gagarin (synthesizer, drums), Alex Ward (guitar, clarinet), Jack Jones (theremin). Thomas produced, mixed, and engineered. David Thomas died in 2025 (per Discogs profile), making this the final album.

Archive Releases

Fire Records issued several definitive archive box sets in the mid-2010s:

  • Elitism for the People 1975–1978 (2015, Fire Records)
  • Architecture of Language 1979–1982 (2016, Fire Records)
  • Les Haricots Sont Pas Salés 1987–1991 (2018, Fire Records)

Connections

  • Rocket From The Tombs — immediate predecessor; shared Thomas, Laughner, and later Gary Siperko
  • David Thomas (2) — leader; extensive solo discography as David Thomas And The Pedestrians, David Thomas And The Wooden Birds, David Thomas And Two Pale Boys, David Thomas And His Legs, etc.
  • Red Crayola — Mayo Thompson’s band; shared multiple members and a mutual avant-rock sensibility
  • Henry Cow / Art Bears — Chris Cutler’s bands; intersection of British prog-improv and post-punk
  • The Golden Palominos — Anton Fier’s main band after leaving Ubu
  • DNA — Tim Wright (2) departed Pere Ubu for the No Wave trio DNA
  • Cassiber — Chris Cutler, also connected through avant-improv networks
  • Fire Records — UK label, archive and new releases from 2013
  • Rough Trade — key label, 1980–1985
  • Fontana — reformation period, 1988–1993
  • Cooking Vinyl — UK indie, 2009

Open Questions

  • Tom Herman’s departure is confirmed post-New Picnic Time (1979); Wikipedia’s Pere Ubu article states he “subsequently quit” after that record, with Mayo Thompson replacing him for The Art of Walking (1980). His return in 1995 was as touring substitute for Jim Jones on the Ray Gun Suitcase cycle; he then went on to record Pennsylvania (1998) and St. Arkansas (2002), departing again after the 2002 Mighty Road Tour.
  • Dave Taylor (4) and Graham Dowdall are listed as Discogs members but appear in no major album credits found — their exact tenures unclear.
  • “The U-Men” alias listed on Discogs requires clarification (may be an error or early name usage).
  • The Story of My Life (1992) Discogs entry has no additional personnel credits — lineup for that album not confirmed from DB alone.
  • David Thomas died in 2025 per the Discogs profile; exact date and circumstances not available from Discogs.
  • Peter Ole Jørgensen’s role and tenure in the band not clear from available credits.
  • Jim Jones (3) — Discogs notes he died in 2008; his tenure after Pennsylvania (1998) needs clarification.
