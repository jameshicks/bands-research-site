Oct 22, 20266 min read

Bloodless Pharaohs

Discogs ID: 1886045
Origin: Long Island / New York City
Active: 1978–1979
Genres: New Wave, Garage Rock, Psychedelic Rock, Psychobilly

Pre-Stray Cats band led by Brian Setzer, active 1978–1979. One of the earliest documented groups to feature the Setzer brothers (Brian and Gary) together. Songs were co-written by Brian Setzer and Ken Kinnally, with a small number of tracks credited to Setzer and “Pell” (identity unclear). The band recorded a body of studio and live material that remained largely unreleased until the mid-1990s.

George Ellert played bass on the studio recordings (tracks 1–9 of the Distortions LP / tracks 1–9 of the Collectables CD). Bob Beecher replaced him for later material, including the live recordings at Max’s Kansas City and a further studio session. Lee Tobia appears in the Discogs member list but is not individually credited on any tracked release; his role is undocumented in the database.

Songwriting

The two tracks the band contributed to Marty Thau Presents 2x5 (“Bloodless Pharaoh” and “Nowhere Fast”) are credited to Setzer and “Pell” — an unidentified co-writer who does not appear in any other Discogs release credit. The bulk of the archive material is credited to Setzer and Kinnally jointly (Music By and Lyrics By split between them or shared). Kinnally also wrote words and music for the 1983 bonus track “Here In The Night.”

History

1978–1979: NYC New Wave Scene

Bloodless Pharaohs operated in the lower Manhattan/New York new wave orbit centered around venues such as Max’s Kansas City. The band’s known recorded output consists of studio sessions from 1978 and a live set at Max’s Kansas City in 1979. Alongside the Pharaohs, Brian and Gary Setzer were also active in The Tomcats (3), a parallel or successor group whose lineup extended to include Lee Rocker (Lee Rocker, born Leon Drucker) and Slim Jim Phantom (Slim Jim Phantom, born James McDonnell) — the two musicians who would complete the Stray Cats.

The Pharaohs came to wider attention through their association with Marty Thau, former New York Dolls manager and founder of Red Star Records. Thau included the band on his 1980 compilation Marty Thau Presents 2x5 (RED 100), which grouped two tracks each from five New York new wave acts. The album was produced by Jimmy Destri (Blondie’s keyboard player); cover design was by Peter Zaremba (The Fleshtones). Masteredby Howie Weinberg at Masterdisk.

1980–1981: Transition to Stray Cats

By 1980–1981, Brian Setzer, Gary Setzer, Lee Rocker, and Slim Jim Phantom relocated to the UK and formed Stray Cats, achieving international success with the rockabilly revival. The Bloodless Pharaohs material was mothballed.

1996: Archive Releases

Two separate archive releases appeared in 1996:

  1. Bloodless Pharaohs 1978–1979 Recordings! (Distortions Records, DR 1030) — 12-track LP, vinyl only, cut at Europadisk (DMM). Produced by Brian Setzer and Ken Kinnally. Photography on front cover by Jay Schwartz.

  2. Brian Setzer And The Bloodless Pharaohs (Collectables, COL-0687) — 17-track CD (plus one bonus track). Expanded presentation: studio recordings (tracks 1–9) and live recordings from Max’s Kansas City, 1979 (tracks 10–16), plus a 1983 unreleased single “Here In The Night” (track 17, words and music by Ken Kinnally). Liner notes by David L. Brown. Cover photo courtesy of Bobby Startup; tray photo courtesy of D.H. Stewart.

The two releases share core repertoire but differ in track listing and format. The Collectables CD is the more comprehensive document, adding the live Max’s Kansas City set and the 1983 bonus track.

2001: Split Single

In 2001, the German label Musical Tragedies (a sublabel of Empty Records) issued a 6-inch shaped limited edition split single pairing Blondie’s “X Offender” (Side A) with the Bloodless Pharaohs’ “Bloodless Pharaoh” (Side B, featuring Brian Setzer). Cat no: MT-508. Multiple pressings/variants exist (at least five in Discogs). The pairing underscores the bands’ shared 1970s–80s New York orbit; both had connections to Jimmy Destri and the 2x5 compilation world.

Open Questions

  • Identity of “Pell” — co-writer with Setzer on “Bloodless Pharaoh” and “Nowhere Fast” (2x5 credits); no other Discogs credit. Could be a pseudonym, a non-musician collaborator, or a person who didn’t appear on recordings.
  • Lee Tobia’s role — listed as a member in Discogs but has no track-level or release-level credit in any release. Discogs member ID: 5589703.
  • Exact venue and dates of the Max’s Kansas City recording (Discogs notes only “1979”).
  • Whether any original Bloodless Pharaohs singles or demos were pressed at the time (1978–1979) and went entirely undocumented on Discogs.
  • Brian Setzer’s age/background at time of band formation — web sources would be needed.
