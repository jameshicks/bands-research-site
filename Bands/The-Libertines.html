Overview

The Libertines are a London rock and roll band whose output spans garage and indie rock, with their catalogue anchored to Rough Trade across two decades from 2002 to 2022. The great majority of their releases fall in the 2000s — a first run that ended in acrimony — followed by a cluster of work after a 2010s reunion and a single 2020s release. The most prominent credit relationship in their discography is with Mick Jones, the Clash guitarist, who accumulates 102 shared credits, dwarfing engineers Bill Price and Nick Terry at 45 each — an unusually deep production partnership for a band of their generation. Paul Dufour rounds out the credited membership alongside the core four of Pete Doherty, Carl Barat, John Hassall, and Gary Powell.

Production — Mick Jones connection

How the relationship began

Mick Jones came to produce Up the Bracket (2002) through a two-step introduction. By Jones’s own account, a friend told him The Libertines were playing near him (Jones lived in Notting Hill); Rough Trade then independently got in touch with Jones, asked whether he wanted to meet the band, and sent him a demo CD of their material. Jones said he thought it was “really good — otherwise I wouldn’t have done it.” The band’s own telling is characteristically oblique: when asked how they got Jones involved, they said “He just turned up at a rehearsal with loads of alcohol. He heard a cry in the darkness and that was us.” (repeatfanzine.co.uk interview, October 2002)

The sequence therefore appears to be: informal word-of-mouth to Jones (a friend, identity unknown) → Rough Trade’s label approach and demo CD → Jones attending a rehearsal → agreement to produce. The album was recorded at RAK Studios, London, in August 2002 and released 14 October 2002.

According to Alan McGee’s memoir Creation Stories, Jones had already agreed to produce before McGee became involved; Jones invited McGee to the sessions because he knew McGee liked the band. McGee’s role at the sessions was described as logistical — suggesting songs, keeping Doherty and Barât from fighting, managing hangers-on. (publicism.info excerpt from Creation Stories)

Jones produced the second album (The Libertines, 2004) as well, reportedly stepping in after Pete Doherty’s difficulties derailed a planned collaboration with Bernard Butler.

Sources: Jones’s own account via NME snippet (“LIBERTINES TAKE THE MICK”, nme.com — page 403’d; cited from search-engine snippet); band quote from repeatfanzine.co.uk (Cambridge Boat Race interview, October 2002); McGee account from publicism.info (Creation Stories excerpt).

What remains unknown: the identity of the friend who first told Jones about the band; the exact month the initial contact was made (before or after the band signed to Rough Trade in late 2001/early 2002); and whether the approach originated with Rough Trade’s A&R or with the band/management suggesting Jones’s name.
