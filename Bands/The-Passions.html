The Passions

English post-punk band. Formed June 1978 in Shepherd’s Bush, London; disbanded August 1983. Discogs artist ID: 74187

Overview

The Passions emerged from the remnants of an earlier London group called The Derelicts. They signed to Fiction Records (a Polydor imprint) and built a following through angular, guitar-driven post-punk with melodic hooks and cinematic atmosphere — the voice of Barbara Gogan at the centre throughout. Their chart breakthrough came with “I’m In Love With A German Film Star” (1981), which reached #25 in the UK. After two studio albums on Polydor, the band dissolved in August 1983. A posthumous compilation, Passion Plays, appeared in 1985.

The core lineup for most of their career was Gogan (vocals, guitar), Clive Timperley (guitar), David Agar (bass), and Richard Williams (drums). Timperley had previously been lead guitarist in The 101’ers alongside Joe Strummer. Guitarist Kevin Armstrong joined in the Sanctuary period, replacing earlier members; Armstrong went on to a prolific session and production career working with David Bowie, Thomas Dolby, and Morrissey.

Key Release Details

Michael & Miranda (1980, Fiction Records)

Produced by Chris Parry. Engineered by Mike Hedges (also remix engineer) and Mike Dutton, with Mark Freegard as assistant. The LP showcases the full lineup of Gogan, Timperley, Agar, and Claire Bidwell on bass. Timperley plays guitar, vocals, keyboards, violin, and marimba; Gogan plays guitar, vocals, and piano.

”I’m In Love With A German Film Star” (1981, Polydor)

Produced by Peter Wilson. Performers credited individually: Gogan, Timperley, Agar, and Richard Williams. Reached #25 UK. The track has had a long afterlife — later covered and sampled; Kompakt Pop released a cover version (2008), and Sonic Cathedral released a version in 2025.

Thirty Thousand Feet Over China (1981, Polydor)

Produced largely by Nigel Gray, with Buhlmann and Moss engineering. Agar credited on bass/vocals, Timperley on guitar/vocals, Gogan on vocals/guitar. Art direction by Rob O’Connor.

Sanctuary (1982, Polydor)

Produced and engineered by Mick Glossop (tracks A2–B5). Kevin Armstrong on guitar/vocals. Jeff Smith (9) on synthesizer. Colin Leggett and Corinne Simcock as assistant engineers.
