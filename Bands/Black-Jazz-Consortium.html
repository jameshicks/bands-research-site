Black Jazz Consortium

Alias of: Fred P. (Fred Peterkin) Discogs artist ID: 651194 Real name: Fred Peterkin Name variations: Black Jazz Constitution Active: 2006 – 2019 (on record; alias may be dormant post-2019) Origin: New York City, USA Genres: Deep House, Broken Beat, Future Jazz, Ambient

Overview

Black Jazz Consortium is the primary alias under which Fred Peterkin launched his house music career and issued the bulk of his studio-album output. The name appeared first on Italian label Gotta Keep Faith Records in 2006, migrated immediately to Peterkin’s own Soul People Music imprint in 2007, and remained its principal vehicle through a 2013 full-length before giving way to the Fred P. alias for most of the 2010s. A late return to the name produced the Evolutions EP (2018) and the double-LP Evolution Of Light (2019) on Perpetual Sound (2), both marking a clear summary and closing statement for the alias.

The distinction between Black Jazz Consortium and the Fred P. alias is curatorial rather than sonically absolute. In broad terms, the BJ Consortium name has been attached to longer-form, more introspective material — the album-format releases and deeper-reaching EPs — while the Fred P. name carries the more varied or club-targeted output. Peterkin himself has described both as expressions of the same practice. The names coexist across roughly the same period and frequently share catalog space on Soul People Music.

Peterkin’s sonic signature under either alias: unhurried tempos, jazz-chord progressions absorbed into house structures, minimal but swinging percussion, long track durations (7–9 minutes typical), and an explicit spiritual sensibility in track and album titles (God’s Promise, Blessings, RE:Actions Of Light, Evolution Of Light). The 2013 album Codes And Metaphors is the most elaborated expression of this: eleven tracks with five featured vocalists, structured as a meditative arc rather than a DJ tool set.

RE:Actions Of Light (2007) — SPMCD 01

CDr album, 11 tracks. Produced, mixed, and arranged by Fred Peterkin (credited as “Fred Peterkin”). This is the first extended-format statement under the BJ Consortium name — tracks titled “Blessings”, “More Love Please Pt.1 & Pt.2”, “Seeing The Way Through”, “Message”, “Look To The Future”, “Deepness”, “There Is Still Hope”, “The Source”, “RE:Birth Groove”, “GOD’s Promise”. Entirely solo production.

God’s Promise (2007) — SPM014

12” single with four versions. The original mix is mixed by Fred P.; the “Salvation Mix” is mixed by Jenifa Mayanja; the “Lost Soul Mix” is mixed by Jus-Ed (credited as DJ Jus-Ed); the “Redemption Mix” is mixed by Fred P. This is one of the earliest examples of the NYC deep house network (Jus-Ed, Jenifa Mayanja) being drawn into BJ Consortium releases.

Scene Context

Black Jazz Consortium sits within the mid-2000s New York underground deep house network whose key nodes include Jus-Ed’s Underground Quality, DJ Qu’s Quintessentials imprint, and Terrence Dixon’s cross-Detroit/NYC practice. The BJ Consortium alias was the launching point for Fred Peterkin’s entry into this network — the God’s Promise 12” in 2007 brought in Jus-Ed and Jenifa Mayanja for remix versions, establishing early connections.

The Codes And Metaphors LP (2013) marks the most outward-reaching moment: five vocalists, an ASCAP registration, and a CD format suggesting an audience beyond the vinyl-only underground. The mastering relationship with Mark Richardson (3) (MJR) across the 2007–2013 period is a recurring thread — Richardson mastered or cut lacquers on at least four BJ Consortium 12”s.

The Evolutions EP (2018) Simbad connection (he remixed “Free Your Mind” back in 2012 on SPMCM001) prefigures the Fred P. & SMBD collaboration (Mantras For The Travelling Souls Vol. 1, Modern Dialect, 2018) that overlaps in the same period.

The Evolution Of Light 3xLP (2019) reads as a career-summary statement — the title echoes RE:Actions Of Light (2007), and the move to Perpetual Sound (2) marks a new label home after Soul People Music’s activity wound down.
