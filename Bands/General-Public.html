General Public

British new wave / ska-pop band formed in London in 1984 by Dave Wakeling and Ranking Roger following the dissolution of The Beat (2). Discogs artist ID: 93737. Active 1984–1987, reformed 1994–1995.

Overview

General Public grew directly out of the ashes of The Beat (2), the Birmingham ska act that dissolved in 1983. Vocalists Dave Wakeling and Ranking Roger (Roger Charlery) carried the core of that band’s sound — melodic pop hooks, reggae and ska rhythms, socially conscious lyrics — into a new unit that recruited members from The Specials (Horace Panter on bass, John Bradbury on drums), Dexys Midnight Runners (Micky Billingham on keyboards), and The Clash’s Mick Jones on guitar for the debut sessions.

Their debut album …All The Rage (1984, I.R.S. / Virgin) reached the UK and US charts on the strength of the single “Tenderness,” which became their signature hit. Producers Colin Fairley and Gavin MacKillop helmed the record, with engineering contributions from J.J. Jeczalik (of Art of Noise fame) among others. A second album, Hand To Mouth (1986, Virgin / I.R.S.), was self-produced by Wakeling, Roger, and David Leonard; it featured Pato Banton among the guest contributors, though it was less commercially successful.

The band split in 1987. The breakup stemmed from compounding creative and commercial pressures: Hand To Mouth performed significantly worse than the debut (reaching only No. 83 on the US Billboard 200, vs. No. 26 for …All The Rage, and failing to chart in the UK top 100), and the recording sessions had exposed a fundamental songwriting divergence between the two frontmen. Wakeling is a lyric-first writer (lyrics → melody → rhythm), while Roger is rhythm-first and became enthused by drum machines and sequencers during the Hand To Mouth sessions — an approach Wakeling resisted. Wakeling described the endpoint in a 1999 interview: “we were either going to have a Paul side or a John side, or we should just go off and follow our own muse, which is what we decided to do.” Roger, interviewed separately circa 1995, said he was not certain of the specific reason for the original breakup. After the split, Roger pursued reggae as a solo act (Radical Departure, 1988) and Wakeling released a solo album (No Warning, 1991). Wakeling and Roger reunited for a second phase in 1994–1995, recording the single “I’ll Take You There” (1994, Epic/Epic Soundtrax — a cover of the Staple Singers classic, on which Andy Cox and David Steele of Fine Young Cannibals are also credited) and the album Rub It Better (1995, Epic), the latter produced by Jerry Harrison of Talking Heads. The reunion lineup was distinct from the 1984 lineup and included Michael Railton, Norman Jones, Wayne Lothian, and Thomas White.

Connections

  • The Beat (2) — direct precursor band: Wakeling and Roger were its two lead vocalists; the band’s dissolution in 1983 triggered General Public’s formation.
  • The Specials — Horace Panter (bass) and John Bradbury (drums) joined for the debut; both were founding Specials members, core figures of the Two-Tone ska revival.
  • Dexys Midnight Runners — Micky Billingham (keyboards) came from Dexys.
  • Fine Young Cannibals — Andy Cox and David Steele, the other two founding members of The Beat (2), formed FYC rather than joining General Public. Cox and Steele are nonetheless credited on the 1994 “I’ll Take You There” single.
  • The Clash — Mick Jones (guitarist of The Clash) played guitar on …All The Rage.
  • Talking Heads — Jerry Harrison produced Rub It Better (1995).
  • Two-Tone scene — General Public is a direct personnel outgrowth of the Two-Tone ska revival, connected via The Beat (2), The Specials (Panter, Bradbury), and Saxa.
  • Pato Banton — Birmingham reggae vocalist who contributed to both the original and reunion eras; embedded in the same Midlands ska/reggae community.
  • John “Jellybean” Benitez and Arthur Baker remixed 1984 singles, connecting the band to the mid-80s New York dance/pop production scene.
