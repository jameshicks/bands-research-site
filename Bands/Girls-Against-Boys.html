Girls Against Boys

Discogs artist ID: 56262
Also known as: GVSB, Girls Vs Boys
Origin: Washington, DC, USA (formed); relocated to New York City shortly after formation
Active: 1989–2009; brief reunion 2013–2017; extended reunion 2021–present
Labels: Adult Swim Records · Touch And Go · DGC · Hut Recordings · Jade Tree · Koch Records · Epitonic

Post-hardcore band formed in Washington, DC in 1989, later based in New York City. Known for a heavy, riff-driven sound built around dual bass guitars — bassist Johnny Temple and keyboardist/bassist Eli Janney playing simultaneously — anchored by Scott McCloud’s baritone guitar riffs and Alexis Fleisig’s driving drums. One of the defining acts of the DC-to-NYC post-hardcore pipeline, with a long production relationship with Ted Niceley and a core run of records on Touch And Go from 1993–1996. Their 1998 major-label debut Freak*on*ica (DGC) represented an electronic-influenced departure and proved commercially disappointing, leaving the band in label limbo before their 2002 return on Jade Tree. (Sources: Discogs ID 56262; Wikipedia)

History

Formation and Early Recordings (1989–1992)

Girls Against Boys began as a studio side-project for keyboardist/bassist Eli Janney and drummer Brendan Canty (of Fugazi), who initially called themselves Skind and wanted to experiment with sounds outside their main projects. They recruited Soulside guitarist Scott McCloud for vocals. By 1989 the trio had recorded three industrial-disco tracks; by early 1990, with Soulside dissolving, McCloud brought in Soulside bandmates Johnny Temple (bass) and Alexis Fleisig (drums), and the expanded quintet recorded three more post-hardcore-leaning songs. Both sessions were released in 1990 as the debut Nineties vs. Eighties on Janney’s novelty label Slate Records (later reissued on Adult Swim Records). Canty departed shortly afterward to focus on Fugazi; the remaining four persuaded Janney to move from Florida to New York, where the band continued as a full-time quartet. Guitarist Luke Wood joined briefly before departing for a major-label business career. The band recorded Tropic of Scorpio (1992, Adult Swim Records), self-produced by Janney, with guest contributions from Nathan Larson (Shudder To Think), Luke Wood, and others. Eli Janney’s performing credits on this era appear under the alias Silas Greene (Discogs ID: 971311), while his engineering/production work appears under his real name. (Source: Wikipedia)

Touch And Go Years (1993–1998)

After signing with Chicago’s Touch And Go, Girls Against Boys released three critically praised albums produced by Ted Niceley:

  • Venus Luxure No.1 Baby (1993) — Niceley produced; Janney engineered. Reviewed favorably; later included in 1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die. The band toured on the side stage of the 1993 Lollapalooza and supported The Jesus Lizard, Helmet, and Jawbox.
  • Cruise Yourself (1994) — Again Niceley/Janney; reached No. 9 on the UK Rock & Metal Albums Chart. “(I) Don’t Got a Place” charted at No. 91 on the UK Singles Chart. “Kill the Sexplayer” appeared on the Clerks soundtrack. The band made their US television debut on The Jon Stewart Show in January 1995.
  • House of GVSB (1996) — Reached No. 76 on the UK Albums Chart; Spin ranked it No. 5 on their Best Albums of 1996 list. “Super-Fire” peaked at No. 82 on the UK Singles Chart.

Between Cruise Yourself and House of GVSB the band covered Joy Division’s “She’s Lost Control” for the compilation A Means to an End: The Music of Joy Division (1995, Hut Recordings); the track was released as a standalone single.

Despite interest from multiple major labels, the band fulfilled their Touch And Go deal before signing. Former member Luke Wood — by then an executive at Geffen Records — brokered the deal.

Geffen, Freak*on*ica, and Label Limbo (1997–2001)

The band signed a three-album deal with DGC/Geffen and recorded Freak*on*ica (1998) with producer Nick Launay over approximately six months at multiple studios. The album incorporated electronic and industrial influences reflecting Janney’s DJ work and the band’s exposure to electronica during touring. It peaked at No. 11 on the Billboard Heatseekers Chart and No. 182 on the UK Albums Chart. “Park Avenue” reached No. 83 on the UK Singles Chart and No. 28 on the US Hot Mainstream Rock Tracks chart.

Shortly after the album’s release, Seagram acquired Geffen, and McCloud reports being informed during the Garbage tour that “Geffen Records would cease to exist as of January 1, 1999.” The band was subsequently moved to Interscope but parted ways without releasing further material. They spent 2001 scoring the film Series 7: The Contenders (Koch Records, 2001). (Source: Wikipedia)

Return and Intermittent Activity (2002–present)

Girls Against Boys signed with Jade Tree for You Can’t Fight What You Can’t See (2002), again produced by Niceley and engineered/mixed by Janney. They toured with Yeah Yeah Yeahs as support. Sohrab Habibion (Edsel frontman) filled in for Janney on a 2002 European leg. In late 2003 the band (without Janney) served as backing band for actress Gina Gershon’s tour supporting Prey for Rock & Roll.

The band entered extended semi-retirement from 2004 onward, with sporadic tours and festival appearances. Guitarist John Schmersal (Enon, Brainiac) filled in for Janney on multiple European tours (2005, 2013, 2026). A brief reunion in 2013 produced the self-released EP The Ghost List (Epitonic). After another hiatus, they reunited for an extended run in 2021, reissuing House of GVSB with bonus material (Touch And Go, 2022). A remastered reissue of Cruise Yourself followed in 2026. (Source: Wikipedia)
