Biography

Origins (2006)

Beirut began as the solo project of Zachary Francis Condon, a self-taught multi-instrumentalist who grew up in Santa Fe, New Mexico. As a teenager Condon became obsessed with Eastern European brass-band music and French chanson, teaching himself trumpet, ukulele, mandolin, accordion, and a battery of keyboards. The debut album Gulag Orkestar was recorded at home and released in 2006 on Brooklyn indie label Ba Da Bing! (cat: BING048). For the recording Condon was joined by Jeremy Barnes (accordion, percussion on selected tracks), Heather Trost (violin), Perrin Cloutier (cello), and Hari Ziznewski (clarinet). Josh Clark (8) handled additional recording and mixing. The album placed itself squarely in the Balkan / Romani brass tradition filtered through lo-fi indie aesthetics and immediately attracted wide critical attention.

The debut was followed in late 2006 by the Lon Gisland EP (BING052), a five-track release that introduced a fuller band lineup: Kelly Pratt (trumpet, euphonium, flugelhorn), Jon Natchez (baritone saxophone, clarinet, glockenspiel), Paul Collins (2) (ukulele, percussion, organ), Nick Petree (percussion), and Kristin Ferebee (violin). This became the core touring and recording band. Design throughout this period was handled by Rob Carmichael (2).

4AD and French Turn: Elephant Gun / The Flying Club Cup (2007)

The single Elephant Gun (2007, 4AD, CAD2720) was Beirut’s first 4AD release and drew broader attention in the UK. The Flying Club Cup (2007, 4AD / Ba Da Bing!, CAD 2732CD), the second album, was largely recorded in Albuquerque and Quebec. The album turned from the Balkans toward French musette and chanson — titles like “Nantes,” “Cliquot,” “Un Dernier Verre (Pour La Route),” and “St. Apollonia.” Owen Pallett (Final Fantasy) was brought in to arrange strings and contribute violin, harpsichord, piano, steel drums, and vocals, making him the album’s most significant outside collaborator. Griffin Rodriguez co-produced, co-recorded, and mastered. The album was recorded in two cities; Albuquerque sessions were handled by Condon and Rodriguez; Montreal sessions by Mark Lawson (2). Ryan Condon (Zach’s brother) contributed lyrics on the title track and other songs throughout this period.

March Of The Zapotec / Holland Double EP (2009)

The 2009 double EP (Pompeii Records (2), POMP 001) marked two distinct statements. The “March Of The Zapotec” side was recorded in Teotitlán del Valle, Oaxaca, Mexico, where Condon collaborated with Band Jimenez — a real Zapotec brass band, the Jiménez family of musicians — recording tracks like “La Llorona” and “The Shrew” with the full ensemble. Griffin Rodriguez produced and arranged those sessions alongside Condon and Perrin Cloutier. The “Holland” side appeared under the alias Realpeople (Condon’s electronic alias, Discogs ID 1376961), presenting five tracks of bedroom synth-pop.

This double release inaugurated Pompeii Records (2), Condon’s own label. The label has been the primary home for all subsequent Beirut output.

The Rip Tide (2011)

The third full-length album (Pompeii Records (2), POMP-03LP) was the most conventionally song-structured Beirut record to that point. The core lineup solidified: Condon, Perrin Cloutier, Nick Petree, Paul Collins (2), Kelly Pratt, and Jon Natchez; Ben Lanz joined on trombone and tuba, becoming a regular brass contributor. Sharon Van Etten appeared as a vocalist on “A Candle’s Fire” and “Payne’s Bay.” Heather Trost contributed violin to two tracks. Griffin Rodriguez co-produced and mixed. The album’s sound was warmer and more American-leaning than earlier records, though the brass arrangements remained central.

Two singles preceded the album: East Harlem / Goshen (POMP 002, 2011) and Santa Fe (2011).

No No No (2015)

After a relative hiatus, Beirut returned with No No No (4AD, CAD 3525), a shorter, more direct album recorded in a live-to-tape manner with Gabe Wax producing and engineering. The album was recorded largely in New York. Per Discogs, the release has no additional instrument credits listed separately — it represents the most stripped-back Beirut studio album up to that point.

Gallipoli (2019)

Gallipoli (4AD, 4AD0121LPE) was recorded during a residency in the town of Gallipoli, southern Italy, using a Farfisa organ borrowed from the town. The album is dominated by Farfisa organ, Moog Voyager synthesizer, and modular synth alongside Condon’s trumpet. The lineup contracted to a trio: Condon, Nick Petree, and Paul Collins (2). Gabe Wax produced and engineered; Francesco Donadello and Norman Nitzsche mastered. The album’s artwork was created by Brody Condon (Zach’s brother, a visual artist).

Artifacts Compilation (2022) and Hadsel (2023)

Artifacts (Pompeii Records (2), POMP009, 2022) is a double-LP career retrospective covering material from the full catalog, including rare and previously unavailable tracks such as “Elephant Gun,” “Transatlantique,” “O Leaozinho,” and “Fisher Island Sound.”

Hadsel (Pompeii Records (2), POMP011, 2023) is a near-solo album, named after the island of Hadsel in Norway where it was largely conceived. Condon plays all instruments — pump organ, modular synthesizers, Moog Voyager, baritone ukulele, drum machine (Rhythm Ace), French horn, trumpet, glockenspiel, and accordion — except where Craig Silvey (Mixed By) and Francesco Donadello (Mastered By) were involved in post-production. Design and photography by Lina Gaisser.

A Study Of Losses (2025)

The most recent album (Pompeii Records (2), POMP012, 2025) incorporated string arrangements by Clarice Jensen — cello, with an ensemble including Kyle Miller (10) (viola), Ben Russell (4) (violin), and Laura Lutzke (violin) — recorded by John Valesio. Griffin Rodriguez and Nick Petree each appear on select tracks. Francesco Donadello mixed and mastered.

Scene Connections

  • Neutral Milk Hotel — Jeremy Barnes was NMH’s drummer; shared Albuquerque / Elephant Six adjacency
  • Bablicon — Griffin Rodriguez’s other band; co-founder with Barnes; Rodriguez became Beirut’s long-term engineer
  • Owen Pallett — string arranger on The Flying Club Cup; Canadian chamber-pop connection
  • Sharon Van Etten — vocal guest on The Rip Tide
  • Clarice Jensen — cellist and string arranger on A Study Of Losses; New York new-music scene

Open Questions

  • Condon’s connection to the wider Elephant Six / Athens scene via Barnes and Rodriguez is circumstantial; whether Condon had direct contact with that scene (beyond personnel overlap) is unclear from Discogs alone.
  • The Realpeople alias (Discogs ID 1376961) has its own discography entries — the Holland side of the double EP is the main crossover; other Realpeople releases may exist.
  • Condon’s exact role (trumpet) on A Hawk And A Hacksaw’s The Way The Wind Blows (2006) is confirmed in the AHAAH article; Discogs confirms this via the AHAAH discography entry (release 816828) but the personnel detail is in the AHAAH article rather than the Beirut discography.
  • So Allowed (2015 single, 4AD) does not have detailed personnel in Discogs; it may be drawn from No No No sessions.
  • The City Slang single (2006) co-credits Calexico on one side — whether this is a proper split single or a compilation single is not fully resolved in the data.
