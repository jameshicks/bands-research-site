DNA

No Wave trio. New York City. Active 1978–1982. Discogs artist ID: 125107 (listed as “DNA (4)”).

Also documented in vault as DNA (4) (same entity — Discogs disambiguation number).

Overview

DNA was one of the four bands documented on No New York (Antilles AN 7067, 1978), produced by Brian Eno. Alongside The Contortions, Teenage Jesus And The Jerks, and Mars, DNA represented the extreme, anti-musical pole of the No Wave scene: atonal guitar, non-idiomatic drumming, deliberately wrong-headed playing, and Arto Lindsay’s signature shrieked, Portuguese-inflected vocals over pure noise.

The band formed in 1978 and dissolved in 1982. Their recorded output was sparse in their lifetime — a debut 7”, four tracks on No New York, and one EP — but was compiled comprehensively in 2004 on DNA On DNA (No More Records), a 32-track retrospective.

Formation and Context (1978)

DNA’s formation is inseparable from the No Wave moment in lower Manhattan. The scene coalesced around spaces like Artists Space, CBGB, Max’s Kansas City, and Tier 3 in 1977–1978. Lindsay had arrived in New York in 1974 after growing up in Brazil; Mori had moved from Japan. Crutchfield had relocated from Ohio.

The original trio — Lindsay (guitar, vocals), Crutchfield (organ, vocals), Mori (drums) — was assembled in time to be selected by Brian Eno for inclusion on his No Wave document. DNA’s approach was the most extreme of the four Eno-selected bands: Lindsay’s guitar was played without reference to conventional tonality or technique, Mori’s drumming followed its own logic rather than a backbeat, and Crutchfield’s organ added another layer of dissonance.

Members’ Later Careers

Arto Lindsay

After DNA, Lindsay formed Ambitious Lovers (1984–1991) with Peter Scherer, fusing No Wave vocals with Brazilian percussion and synth-funk production. He then built a second career as a major producer of Brazilian MPB — Caetano Veloso, Marisa Monte, Tom Zé among his production credits. Solo records continued through the 2020s. See Arto Lindsay for full detail.

Ikue Mori

Mori moved from acoustic drumming to electronic percussion and laptop performance. She became a key figure in the downtown New York avant-garde/improv scene — long association with John Zorn (multiple Tzadik releases, including Zorn’s Locus Solus in 1983), and co-founded Phantom Orchard with Zeena Parkins. She also did extensive sleeve design work for Tzadik in the 1990s. See Ikue Mori.

Robin Crutchfield

Left DNA in 1979 — shortly after the No New York sessions — to form Dark Day, a cold wave/minimal electronics project that continued in various forms through at least 2024. Per Wikipedia and the Walk on the Wild Side interview, he had been with DNA for nearly a year and left when the band’s direction diverged from his own interests. (Wikipedia: Robin Crutchfield as of 2026-05-31; Walk on the Wild Side NYC interview — full text paywalled.) Dark Day’s members included Nina Canal (also of Ut), Phil Kline, and Nancy Arlen (also of Mars). Crutchfield’s debut single on Strike It From The Records (1979) was produced by Charles Ball. From c.2000 he shifted to harps and lyres. See Robin Crutchfield and Dark Day.

Tim Wright (2)

Tim Wright (b. 1952, d. 4 August 2013) was a founding member of Pere Ubu (1975–1978) before joining DNA. He left DNA with the band’s dissolution in 1982 and continued as a songwriter, session musician, studio engineer, and tour technician. He is credited on Pere Ubu releases into the early 1990s (as a songwriter), and also appeared on Brian Eno & David Byrne’s My Life In The Bush Of Ghosts (1981, bass on one track). See Tim Wright (2).
