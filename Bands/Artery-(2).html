Artery (2)

Sheffield, UK post-punk / art-rock group. Discogs artist ID: 275154. Active 1978–1985 (first run), 2007–c.2011 (reformed). Evolved from earlier punk band named ‘The’. Received John Peel support; appeared on Red Flame, Aardvark, Armageddon, Golden Dawn, and Pleasantly Surprised labels. Key member Simon Hinkler later joined The Mission; Wilson, Hinkler (David), and Hinkler (Simon) all played briefly in Pulp around 1983.

Biography

Origins: ‘The’ → Artery, 1978–1980

Artery evolved from a Sheffield punk band called ‘The’, formed around 1978. The initial lineup was Mark Gouldthorpe (guitar), Toyce Ashley (vocals, guitar), Neil McKenzie (bass), and Garry Wilson (drums). They self-financed a debut single, Mother Moon (Limited Edition Records, cat no. TAKE 1, 1979) — a three-track 7” EP produced by the band and Marcus Featherby.

In 1980 they released Unbalanced / The Slide on the Sheffield-based Aardvark label (STEEL 3), co-produced with Steve Hopkins. Shortly after, Mick Fidler (vocals, guitar, saxophone) was added as a fifth member.

Early singles and Peel attention, 1981

Two further Aardvark singles followed in 1981: Afterwards / Into The Garden (Armageddon Records AS 026) and Cars In Motion (Aardvark AARD 5). Afterwards / Into The Garden was produced in split fashion — side A by Dale Griffin and Overend Watts (both ex-Mott the Hoople), side B by Tony Wilson (Artery’s producer credit on the Red Flame releases, not Factory Records’ Tony Wilson). Cars In Motion was again produced by Steve Hopkins.

During this period Toyce Ashley departed; Mark Gouldthorpe took over on lead vocals, and Simon Hinkler (formerly of TV Product) joined on keyboards and guitar. The band began recording sessions for John Peel’s BBC Radio 1 show — the first in July 1981, a second early 1982. “Into The Garden” would reach number nine in the Festive Fifty at year-end 1982.

Red Flame era, 1982–1983

The band signed to Red Flame in 1982. Two releases that year:

  • The Clown (7”, RF 704, 1982) — limited credits on Discogs, New Wave styling.
  • Oceans (mini-LP, RFM 4, 1982) — the defining release of this period. Produced across tracks by Dale Griffin, Tony Wilson, and Tony Cook (2), with Gouldthorpe and Hinkler co-producing tracks A1/A2/B2–B4. Simon Hinkler played organ, piano, strings, and violin; Fidler added saxophone and guitar; McKenzie on bass; Wilson on drums. “Into The Garden” reprised here as track A2.

The band were often compared to Joy Division, a comparison Gouldthorpe explicitly rejected.

In 1983 further lineup changes came: John White replaced Fidler (who was sacked for missing rehearsals), Christopher Hendrick replaced McKenzie on bass/keys/guitar, and David Hinkler (Simon’s brother) joined on keyboards. Two 1983 Red Flame releases:

  • One Afternoon In A Hot Air Balloon (LP, RF 18, 1983) — self-produced and arranged by the band. By this point the functional trio was Gouldthorpe, Wilson, and Hendrick; the album is credited accordingly, with Hendrick handling piano, keyboards, bass guitar, flamenco guitar, and xylophone alongside Gouldthorpe’s vocals and guitars. Engineer: Simaen Skolfield.
  • The Alabama Song (7”, RFB 25 / also 10 Records, 1983) — cover of Brecht/Weill; produced and arranged by Simon Hinkler; David Hinkler on trombone/organ; David Hendrick on bass; engineered by Simaen Skolfield.

By the time of One Afternoon…, White had left to form UV Pop, and Simon Hinkler moved into production work.

Golden Dawn era and split, 1984–1985

In 1984 Hendrick left; McKenzie returned. Murray Fenton was added, and the band moved to Golden Dawn.

  • A Big Machine (12”, GD 1203, 1984) — Gouldthorpe/Wilson/McKenzie/Fenton lineup; engineered by Simaen Skolfield and Barry Sage.
  • Diamonds In The Mine (12”, GD 1204, 1984) — includes a cover of Leonard Cohen’s “Diamonds In The Mine”; no additional credits on Discogs.
  • The Second Coming (mini-LP, GDLP01, 1984) — produced by Tim Parry; engineered by Kenny Jones (3) and Martin Rex; Murray Fenton on guitar/organ; backing vocals by Elizabeth Jean Murray; Neil McKenzie returns on bass. Also included a Gouldthorpe/Wilson original alongside a further Leonard Cohen cover.

A live album, Number Four (Live In Amsterdam) (LP, GDLP 02, 1985), and a cassette-only document, Afterwards (Pleasantly Surprised PS011, 1985), documented the band’s final phase; the cassette collected demos, live material, and interviews spanning 1979–1983 lineups. The band split in 1985; Fenton joined The Batfish Boys.

Post-Artery: key trajectories

  • Simon Hinkler joined The Mission from their formation (appearing on Gods Own Medicine, 1986, and throughout their Mercury catalog) and remained a core member into the 2000s–2020s.
  • Mark Gouldthorpe and Simon Hinkler collaborated as The Flight Commander on two albums: Flight Commander Solitude & the Snake (1986) and A Room Full of This (Cosmic Dice Recordings, 1993).
  • Garry Wilson, Simon Hinkler, and David Hinkler all played in Pulp around 1983.

Compilations and reunion, 2006–2011

Two 2006 compilations surveyed the catalog: Afterwards (Recordings From 1979 To 1983) (Spinney Records) and Into The Garden – An Artery Collection (Cherry Red).

The band reformed in 2007 after an invitation from Jarvis Cocker to perform at the Meltdown Festival, and recorded a session for Marc Riley’s BBC 6 Music show. The Standing Still EP (Phantom Power Records, 2009) was their first new recording in over two decades. A further album, Civilisation (Twinspeed Records, 2011), followed. David Hinkler’s departure led to a near-split, but James Bacon was recruited and the band carried on briefly before folding again.

In 2009, Sheffield Vision released 3 Days In June – The Reformation Of Artery, a documentary.

Italian label Spittle Records issued vinyl reissues of Oceans (2020) and One Afternoon In A Hot Air Balloon (2021).

Connections

  • Red Flame — primary label 1982–83; also home to The Decorators, Carmel (2), Blurt, Normil Hawaiians, The Room (3)
  • Simon Hinkler — keyboards/guitar 1981–83; moved into production (produced The Alabama Song), then joined The Mission at formation
  • Pulp — Garry Wilson, Simon Hinkler, and David Hinkler all played in Pulp around 1983
  • Dale Griffin — produced Afterwards / Into The Garden (1981) and tracks on Oceans (1982); also associated with Mott the Hoople
  • The Batfish Boys — Murray Fenton joined after Artery split
  • UV Pop — John White’s subsequent band after leaving Artery
  • Jarvis Cocker — invited Artery to perform at Meltdown 2007, triggering reunion
