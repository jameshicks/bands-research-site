New Age

Active: 1980–1982 Based: Groby, Leicestershire (village near Leicester) Labels: Dining Out Records, Marilou (for LP), S+T Records (compilation) Genre: Post-punk → New Romantic / Synth-pop Members: Unknown (not documented in any accessible source)

History

New Age were from Groby, a village on the western edge of Leicester — making them part of the same Leicester scene that produced Disco Zombies (originally Leicester university students) and Farm Life / The Bomb Party.

Their earliest known recording was “Can’t Get In”, recorded at Street Music (a Leicester record shop with a studio space) in early 1981. The recording was reportedly made in the early morning hours after the original track was damaged during preparation. The engineer cut part of the intro off. It appeared on the Leicester compilation Where The Hell Is… Leicester (S+T Records, STAB 1, 1981) — but the band had already committed to S+T for a further single before signing with Dining Out Records instead.

Their sound started as post-punk comparable to The Sound (described by one critic as “a low-budget version of The Sound”), then evolved toward the heavier production and synth-driven textures of new romantic — earning the label “Leicester’s version of Classix Nouveaux.” One reviewer noted: “As much as I detested all that noo romantic style over-production at the time, I now find it bizarrely attractive.”

All subsequent releases were on Dining Out Records (Dave Henderson’s label), with the LP issued on its own Marilou subsidiary imprint.

Notes on the Name

Searching for this band is extremely difficult because “New Age” as a term is swamped by the 1980s/90s spiritual music genre. They predate that cultural meaning of the phrase — the name likely had a more straightforwardly forward-looking connotation in 1980 post-punk terms.

Production Notes (Foel Studio / Dave Anderson connection)

The LP All the Monkeys Aren’t in the Zoo, Marylou (Marilou / TUX 25, 1982) was produced by Dave Anderson at his own Foel Studio, Llanfair Caereinion, mid-Wales — roughly 100 miles from Groby, Leicestershire. The route from a Leicester post-punk band to a rural Welsh studio associated with ex-Amon Düül II / Hawkwind personnel is unexplained in any surviving interview, but the available evidence points strongly to Dave Henderson’s working relationship with Illuminated Records as the most plausible mechanism.

About Foel Studio

Foel Studio was established in 1973 (some sources say 1974) by Dave Anderson after his stints as bassist with Amon Düül II (1967–71, recording Phallus Dei and Yeti) and Hawkwind (1971–72). It is located in Llanfair Caereinion, Powys, described as “the second oldest residential recording studio in the world (after Rockfield).” The studio offered residential accommodation in an adjacent 17th-century cottage, making it attractive for bands needing an immersive, away-from-home recording environment. (foelstudio.co.uk, as of 2026-07-13; recordproduction.com)

Foel’s role in early-1980s independent UK music

By 1980–82, Foel was already a well-established node in the UK independent and post-punk recording network — not an obscure choice for a left-field band. Confirmed sessions at the studio during this period include:

  • Essential Logic — debut LP Beat Rhythm News (Rough Trade, 1981), engineered by Anderson; described as the recording that preceded the Young Marble Giants session (reynoldsretro.blogspot.com)
  • Young Marble Giants — Colossal Youth (Rough Trade, 1980), engineered by Anderson in five days (Wikipedia, Colossal Youth, as of 2026-07-13)
  • The Pop Group — For How Much Longer Do We Tolerate Mass Murder? (Rough Trade, 1980), engineered/mixed by Anderson (jazzrocksoul.com)
  • The Fall, Au Pairs, Mo-dettes, The Gist — all recorded sessions at Foel in the same 1979–81 window (reynoldsretro.blogspot.com)
  • Normil Hawaiians — More Wealth Than Money (Illuminated Records, recorded June 1982), with Anderson engineering and playing bass on several tracks (last.fm/music/Normil+Hawaiians; upsettherhythm.bandcamp.com)
  • Scritti Politti, New Model Army, Hawkwind, Gong, Peter Hammill — all listed among Foel clients

The Illuminated Records connection

The clearest documented link between Dave Henderson and the Foel/Anderson network runs through Illuminated Records (formed 1980, UK):

  • Dave Henderson was doing graphic design work for Illuminated Records during the Dining Out Records period (1979–82), including sleeve artwork and the Elephant Table compilation. (coldwarnightlife.com)
  • Illuminated Records was actively routing its artists to Foel Studio: the Normil Hawaiians session (June 1982) was facilitated by Illuminated producer Keith Bagley and Anderson; Inner City Unit’s Newanatomy was also on Demi Monde (Anderson’s own label, sister to Illuminated). (last.fm)
  • Anderson is described in multiple sources as being personally associated with Illuminated Records, with Demi Monde Records (his own imprint, based at Foel) operating in parallel with Illuminated during the same years.

The most economical hypothesis: Henderson’s working relationship with Illuminated gave him direct knowledge of Foel’s facilities, rates, and Anderson’s sympathies with independent music. When Dining Out needed a residential studio with a sympathetic producer for the New Age LP, Foel was a known and tested option within Henderson’s immediate professional network. The New Age session would have sat alongside other 1982 Foel bookings (Normil Hawaiians recorded there the same year).

What cannot be confirmed from sources consulted

No surviving interview with Henderson, with the New Age band members, or with Anderson directly addresses why New Age specifically went to Foel. The Henderson–Illuminated–Anderson chain is a reasonable inference from documented facts; it is not stated explicitly in any source found.
