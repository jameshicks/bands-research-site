My Dad Is Dead

Discogs ID: 311148 Active: 1985–2011 (farewell performance); 2023 (comeback release) Location: Cleveland, Ohio, US Genres: Lo-fi, post-punk, indie rock Primary Member: Mark Edwards (9) Name Variations: MDID

History

My Dad Is Dead was formed in Cleveland, Ohio in 1985 by Mark Edwards (Discogs alias entry: Mark Edwards (9), ID 918333) after a stint drumming in the Cleveland band Riot Architecture (ID 954188). From the start the project was effectively a solo vehicle for Edwards — the 1985 self-titled cassette debut credits him alone for instruments, vocals, and effects.

Early years / St. Valentine / Birth Records (1985–1987). The debut cassette was self-released (1985). The second record, …And He’s Not Gonna Take It Anymore (1986), came out on St. Valentine Records and was co-produced and engineered by Chris Burgess (ID 311149), who would remain a key collaborator for most of the next decade. The third album, Peace, Love & Murder (1987), appeared on Birth Records (2).

Homestead Records era (1988–1989). Two albums landed on the influential New York indie Homestead Records: Let’s Skip The Details (1988), co-produced by Chris Burgess, and The Taller You Are, The Shorter You Get (1989), which introduced bassist Jeff Curtis (2) (ID 1758383) and also featured accordion work from James Wilson. Edwards is credited as sole performer on the former; Burgess co-produced the latter, with Jeff Curtis writing and playing bass on several tracks.

Scat Records era (1990–1993). Three records with the Cleveland-based Scat Records (2):

  • Shine (1990) — core trio of Edwards / Burgess (bass, producer, engineer) / Scott Pickering (drums)
  • Chopping Down The Family Tree (1991) — notable as the first record to feature a pre-Tortoise John McEntire (ID 3545) among the performers; also Doug Gillard (ID 367510), Tim Gilbride (ID 771169), and Jeff Curtis alongside Burgess and Pickering; also issued on the German label Houses In Motion
  • Out Of Sight, Out Of Mind (1993) — added bassist Matt Swanson (ID 287716) to the rotating cast; Burgess recorded; Mike Griffith (ID 507146) mixed; Paul Hamann did additional tracking

By around 1993, the working relationship with Burgess wound down; Burgess is not credited on the subsequent Emperor Jones albums.

Emperor Jones era (1995–1997). Three albums on the Austin, Texas independent Emperor Jones — the label’s first three releases were by My Dad Is Dead:

  • For Richer, For Poorer (1995, EJ-01) — Edwards on guitar and vocals; Matt Swanson on bass; Scott Pickering on drums; Mike Griffith engineering
  • Shine(r) (1996, EJ-05) — reissue/re-recording of 1990’s Shine (no additional credits in DB)
  • Everyone Wants The Honey But Not The Sting (1997, EJ-16LP) — Edwards / Swanson / new drummer Shayne Ivy (ID 1280543); produced by Don Depew (ID 504006); mastered by Jim DeMain (ID 379981)

The three Emperor Jones albums represent the project’s most prominent profile period, benefiting from the label’s distribution reach alongside labelmates The Mountain Goats and The American Analog Set.

Later activity (2002–2011). The Engine Of Commerce appeared in 2002 on Vital Cog Records. Edwards wound down the My Dad Is Dead project after the release of what appears to be a final album in 2009 (not separately detailed in DB credits), with a farewell concert in 2011.

Post-MDID / Secular Joy. Following the farewell performance, Edwards formed Secular Joy (ID 4087753), whose debut was released in 2012. Members include Billy Buckley, Rob Koegler, and Zeno Gill.

2023 return. Pow! was released in 2023 on Scat Records (2), marking a return to that Cleveland label after three decades.

Scene Connections

  • Emperor Jones — primary label 1995–1997; My Dad Is Dead was the label’s first artist (EJ-01)
  • Homestead Records — three albums 1988–1989; key US indie of the era
  • Scat Records (2) — three Scat albums 1990–1993, plus Pow! in 2023
  • Prisonshake — Chris Burgess and Scott Pickering are both members; strong Cleveland post-punk overlap
  • John McEntire — pre-Tortoise appearance on Chopping Down The Family Tree (1991); McEntire would go on to anchor Tortoise, Bastro, and The Sea And Cake
  • Doug Gillard — also appeared on Chopping Down The Family Tree; later played in Guided By Voices and Cobra Verde
  • Secular Joy — Mark Edwards’s post-MDID band, formed c. 2012
  • Riot Architecture — Cleveland band Edwards drummed in before forming MDID
  • The Mountain Goats — Emperor Jones labelmates; both among the label’s earliest signings
  • The American Analog Set — Emperor Jones labelmates; the label’s flagship act
