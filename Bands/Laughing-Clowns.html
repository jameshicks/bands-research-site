Laughing Clowns

Discogs artist ID: 381699

Australian jazz-inflected post-punk group formed in Sydney in 1979 by Ed Kuepper after his departure from The Saints (2) the previous year. Active 1979–1984; briefly reformed for live performances 2007–2010. Self-produced throughout. Internationally notable enough for UK licensing through Red Flame (1982–1983); Australian releases on Prince Melon Records and Hot Records (2).

Biography

Origins and Formation (1979–1980)

Ed Kuepper left The Saints (2) in 1978 after that band’s move to the UK, returning to Australia and assembling a new group with a radically different palette. Where The Saints were a raw three-chord outfit, Laughing Clowns incorporated saxophone and trumpet from the outset — giving the group an angular, off-kilter character that sat between post-punk, free jazz, and avant-cabaret. The founding lineup coalesced around Kuepper on guitar and vocals, Jeffrey Wegener on drums (also an ex-Saint), the Wallace-Crabbe brothers on bass (Ben Wallace-Crabbe) and piano (Dan Wallace-Crabbe), and Bob Farrell on saxophone. Peter Doyle (2) contributed trumpet from very early on.

The first recordings appeared in 1980. The seven-inch Sometimes, The Fire Dance… (Prince Melon Records PM-01 EP) captured the initial lineup: just Kuepper, Wegener, the Wallace-Crabbes, and Farrell — no trumpet yet on record. The debut LP The Laughing Clowns (Missing Link, MLB 001, 1980) followed, adding no trumpet but delivering six tracks that combined Kuepper’s terse guitar and oblique songwriting with Farrell’s saxophone and Dan Wallace-Crabbe’s acoustic piano. Engineer Tony Cohen mixed the record. Cover art was by Robin Wallace-Crabbe.

Also in 1980 appeared the compilation LP Reign Of Terror, Throne Of Blood (Prince Melon PM-2000), which collected eight tracks — including the debut single sides — and brought Peter Doyle (2) on trumpet into the documented lineup. That same year, the group also released 3 (Prince Melon, 1981), a three-track EP.

The Red Flame Period (1982–1983)

By 1982 the band had crystallised into a slightly different configuration. Louise Elliott joined on saxophone (overlapping with and eventually replacing Farrell on many recordings), and Leslie Millar replaced Ben Wallace-Crabbe on bass for some sessions. The band self-produced throughout this period, with multiple engineers across different sessions (Tony Cohen, John Bee, Peter Doyle (2), Ralph Smith (2), Peter Walker).

The UK label Red Flame licensed three releases, which served as the international face of the band:

  • Laughing Clowns (RF 14, 1982) — Ten-track compilation LP drawing on sessions from 1980–1982. Credits show both Ben Wallace-Crabbe and Leslie Millar on bass (on different tracks), and both Bob Farrell and Louise Elliott on saxophone. Peter Doyle (2) plays trumpet on multiple tracks. Mixed and pressed at Utopia/MPO (France).
  • Laughter Around The Table (RF 23, 1983) — Seven-track LP. Credited solely to Kuepper as writer. Tracks include “Everything That Flies…”, “Theme From Mad Flies, Mad Flies”, and the extended “Laughter Around The Table”. Minimal Discogs credits — likely re-drawn from Australian sessions.
  • Everything That Flies (RF 1223, 1983) — 12-inch single. Three tracks: the title track (A-side), “The Year Of The Bloated Goat”, “A Knife In The Head”. Kuepper only songwriter credit.

In Australia during the same period, the group released Mr Uddich-Schmuddich Goes To Town (Prince Melon PM5000, 1982), a full eight-track LP. This was the most jazz-oriented release: Louise Elliott plays tenor saxophone, alto saxophone, and flute; Peter Doyle (2) plays trumpet; Leslie Millar handles bass. Production was shared by Kuepper, Wegener, and Peter Doyle (2). Engineers: Peter Doyle (2) and Peter Walker. Cover artwork by Judi Dransfield (later Judi Dransfield Kuepper — Ed’s partner).

Late Period and Dissolution (1983–1985)

By the Eternally Yours 12-inch (Hot Records HOT 12001, 1984) and the LP Law Of Nature (Hot Records HOT 1004, 1984), the lineup had shifted substantially. Peter Milton Walsh (of The Apartments) took over bass. Chris Abrahams (later of The Necks) began appearing on piano. Engineer Ian Davies took over from the rotating earlier crew. Louise Elliott remained on saxophone. Kuepper consolidated as sole producer.

Law Of Nature (1984) is the first full LP on Hot Records (2). Nine tracks, Kuepper on guitar, vocals; Walsh on bass; Wegener on drums; Elliott on saxophone; Abrahams on piano. Mastered by Don Bartley.

The final LP Ghosts Of An Ideal Wife (Hot Records HOT 1013, 1985) represented the fullest expansion of the band’s sound: Louis Tillett replaced Abrahams on piano; Diane Spence joined on saxophone alongside Elliott; Glad Reed contributed trombone. Kuepper also plays banjo on this record. Engineer: Steve Crane. The lineup is the most harmonically dense of the band’s career, adding trombone to the brass section for the first time.

The separate History Of Rock N’ Roll — Volume One (Hot Records HOT 1010, 1984) was a ten-track retrospective LP drawing from earlier recordings, with credits showing both lineup eras.

Kuepper dissolved the band in 1984/85 and moved into a solo career, later forming The Aints (a Saints tribute project) and various other configurations.

Posthumous

Golden Days — When Giants Walked The Earth (Hot Records, 1995) gathered key recordings as a compilation. The definitive retrospective is Cruel, But Fair (The Complete Clowns Recordings) (Hot Records HOT 1088, 2005), a 3-CD set of 48 tracks covering the full career and cross-indexing all lineup permutations. It was reissued via Prince Melon Records in 2021.

The band reformed briefly 2007–2010 for live shows; this reunion included Alister Spence on piano.
