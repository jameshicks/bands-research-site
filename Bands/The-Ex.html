The Ex

Discogs ID: 65740 Origin: Amsterdam, Netherlands Active: 1979–present Genres: Anarcho-Punk, Post-Punk, Free Improvisation, Noise Rock, Experimental, Ethio-jazz Labels: Ex Records, Touch And Go, RecRec Music, Konkurrent, Terp Records, Mordam Records, Fist Puppet Name variations: Ex; The Ex & Guests

Biography

Formation and Early Punk Era (1979–1984)

The Ex formed in Amsterdam in 1979, initially as a raw anarcho-punk outfit in the spirit of the British post-punk explosion. Founding members were GW Sok (vocals), Dolf Planteijdt (guitar), René De Groot (bass), and Geurt van Gisteren (drums). Early releases appeared on a succession of their own one-off DIY imprints — Eh Records, Hé Records, Ping Pong Records, More D.P.M. — with the catalog code “EX” appearing across them all before Ex Records consolidated as the primary label around 1986.

Their 1980 debut single “Disturbing Domestic Peace” / “All Corpses Smell The Same” set the template: short, fast, politically charged tracks with angular guitars. The 1982 LP History Is What’s Happening and the 1983 double LP Tumult (with guest appearances from Jon Langford (2) of the Mekons) demonstrated a growing range — longer, more complex song structures alongside the punk core. A changing lineup through this period saw Katherina Bornefeld (Katherina Bornefeld, also credited as Katrin Ex) join on drums in 1984, replacing Geurt van Gisteren.

Finding Their Sound — Spanish Civil War and Tom Cora (1985–1994)

The 1986 double LP 1936, The Spanish Revolution (EX 028/029; co-released with Ron Johnson Records) was a turning point — a sprawling political concept record about the Spanish Civil War and anarchist movements, produced by Dolf Planteijdt and The Ex. Lineup at this stage: Luc Ex (bass/guitar), Terrie Hessels (guitar), Katherina Bornefeld (drums), Dolf Planteijdt (guitar), GW Sok (voice).

Guitarist Andy Moor (2) (Andy Ex) joined in 1990, strengthening the group’s dual-guitar attack. That same year they began a transformative collaboration with New York cellist Tom Cora, resulting in two critically acclaimed albums: Scrabbling At The Lock (1991, Ex Records / RecRec Music) and And The Weathermen Shrug Their Shoulders (1993, Fist Puppet). Both records blended hardcore post-punk with free improvisation, with the full band credited as multi-instrumentalists across guitar, drums, bass, accordion, and voice; Tom Cora’s cello was the melodic counterpoint. Both were co-produced by Dolf Planteijdt, the band, and Tom Cora. Guest vocalist Catherine Jauniaux appeared on vocal improvisations on Scrabbling At The Lock. Tom Cora died in 1998; the collaboration stands as one of the defining works of the post-punk/improv overlap.

The 1988 double LP Joggers & Smoggers (EX 040/041) featured an enormous array of guests, and 1988’s Aural Guerrilla (EX 036, produced by Jon Langford (2)) included a lyric by Peter Hammill of Van der Graaf Generator. Hands Up! You’re Free (1988, EX 035) functions as a live document with a shifting cast including Jon Langford (2), Susie Honeyman (the Mekons), and Tommy Greene.

Free Improvisation Experiments and Fishtank Sessions (1995–2003)

The mid-1990s saw The Ex lean further into improvisation. Mudbird Shivers (1995, EX 060D; with Han Buhrs) and Instant (1995; credited as Guests/The Ex, with Tristan Honsinger among others) marked the period. Starters Alternators (1998) was recorded by Steve Albini, beginning a long working relationship with him — he also recorded Turn (2004) and Dizzy Spells (2001) for the band.

The “In The Fishtank” series — a Dutch residency/recording project run by Konkurrent — produced two key entries: In The Fishtank (1999, with Tortoise) and In The Fishtank 9 (2002, with members of Sonic Youth including Thurston Moore, Lee Ranaldo, Steve Shelley, plus Jim O’Rourke). These marathon sessions brought together post-rock (Tortoise: Rob Mazurek, Dan Bitney, Doug McCombs, John McEntire, Jeff Parker) and No Wave streams with the Ex’s approach.

During this period the Ex also played prolifically with the Dutch free jazz world — the “6 Series” (EX 6 Series sublabel) documented live performances with Han Bennink, Ab Baars, Wolter Wierbos, Colin McLean (Dog Faced Hermans), and others at the Bimhuis in Amsterdam (notably the Bimhuis 29/06/91 recordings and the 6 Series: 6.1–6.6 box).

Luc Ex departed around the mid-2000s (his last primary-credited releases are around 2004); bassist Rozemarie Heggen appeared on Turn (2004).

Ethiopian Music Collaborations (2006–2015)

A pivotal new direction emerged with the introduction of Ethiopian music. The 2006 LP Moa Anbessa (Terp Records), credited to Getatchew Mekuria, The Ex & Guests, brought together veteran Ethiopian saxophonist Getachew Mekuria with the Ex and an array of Dutch and Canadian guests: Brodie West (alto sax), Xavier Charles (clarinet), Joost Buis (trombone), Cor Fuhler (organ), Colin McLean (bass). Melodies were by Mekuria, English lyrics by The Ex, arranged collectively; Dolf Planteijdt and Jeroen Visser recorded it. The follow-up Y’Anbessaw Tezeta (2012, Terp Records) continued this partnership.

The Ethiopian connection extended further: Catch My Shoe (2010) features a photo of Ethiopian dancer Melaku Belay on the sleeve; Lale Guma / Addis Hum (2015, EX 140, with Fendika) and ongoing collaborations with the Addis Ababa scene cemented this as a sustained rather than one-off engagement.

The 2013 LP Enormous Door (EX 138LP), credited to The Ex & Brass Unbound, paired the guitar band with a full brass section: Ken Vandermark (tenor/baritone saxophone/clarinet), Mats Gustafsson (baritone saxophone), Roy Paci (trumpet), Wolter Wierbos (trombone), Joost Buis (trombone). The record was self-produced by The Ex.

Vocalist Transition and Continuation (2009–present)

In 2009, founding vocalist GW Sok (Jos Kleij) departed after roughly 30 years. He was replaced by Arnold de Boer, guitarist and vocalist from the Dutch band Zea. De Boer’s debut with the band on Catch My Shoe (2010) signaled continuity rather than rupture — the political and sonic commitments remained intact. De Boer plays guitar and sampler in addition to vocals.

The 30th anniversary LP 30 (2009) and subsequent releases Catch My Shoe (2010), Enormous Door (2013), 27 Passports (2018), and If Your Mirror Breaks (2025) all document a stable four-piece lineup: Andy Moor (guitar/baritone guitar), Terrie Hessels (guitar/baritone guitar), Katherina Bornefeld (drums/vocals), Arnold de Boer (vocals/guitar/sampler). If Your Mirror Breaks was mastered by Bob Weston and recorded by Jan Schenk.

Steve Albini recorded the band on multiple late-period albums; his Electrical Audio imprint is a recurrent production home from Starters Alternators (1998) through 27 Passports (2018).

Ex Records Label

Ex Records (Discogs ID: 33157) is the band’s own imprint, based at P.O. Box 635, Amsterdam. Contact: mailorder@theex.nl. Active from approximately 1986 onwards (catalog code “EX” was used earlier across one-off labels).

Ex Records has issued almost all of the band’s releases from 1936, The Spanish Revolution onward, sometimes co-releasing with international partners (Touch And Go, RecRec Music, Mordam Records, Fist Puppet, Konkurrent).

The label also issued at least one release by a non-Ex artist: Goodbye Berlin (2017, EXVEP01) by Pat Dam Smyth.
