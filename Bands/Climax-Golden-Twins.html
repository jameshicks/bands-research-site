Climax Golden Twins

Climax Golden Twins

Oct 23, 20269 min read

Climax Golden Twins

Discogs ID: 84032 Also known as: CGT Based: Seattle, Washington, US Active: 1994–present (documented releases) Genre: Experimental, Noise, Ambient, Electronic Styles: Experimental, Leftfield, Minimal, Ambient Label home: Fire Breathing Turtle (primary); also Anomalous Records, Meme, Since 1972, Abduction

Overview

Climax Golden Twins is a Seattle-based experimental music duo — primarily Robert Millis and Jeffery Taylor — with a rotating cast of collaborators. Their work spans noise, drone, collage-based electronics, field recordings, live improvisation, soundtracks, gallery installations, scores for choreography, and tape-circuit experimentation. Core members include Scott Colburn, John Vallier, Jesse Paul Miller, and Dave Abramson in various configurations.

The group is as much a production collective as a fixed band: credits across their catalog show different subsets of the membership on each release, with the core duo of Millis and Taylor appearing most consistently. Scott Colburn functions as both performer and producer/engineer across the majority of releases.

Biography

Formation and Early Releases (1994–1997)

Climax Golden Twins emerged from the Seattle experimental underground, releasing their debut self-titled double-7” on their own Fire Breathing Turtle label in 1994 (cat# FBT 001). The debut showcased the group’s collage-heavy approach: Jeffery Taylor and Robert Millis as performers, John Vallier on occasional drums, Barbara Ierulli and Russell Johnson providing location recordings, and minimal production notes — no producer credited.

The group immediately established an aggressive release schedule. In 1995, a second self-titled release appeared on Fire Breathing Turtle (FBT 002), alongside a cassette Eyeless Fabrication on E.F. Tapes (EF#140) and a release on Union Pole (Climaxgoldenhiss, UP#32). Their collaboration with the Seattle-based experimental network was well-established by this point, with Jesse Paul Miller joining as a recurring performer.

The Road Cone label release (1996, RoCo 016) and the Imperial Household Orchestra CD (1996, Scratch Records, Scratch # 23) further expanded the lineup. Imperial Household Orchestra listed an unusually large performer roster — all “uncredited” on the record — alongside Scott Colburn producing alongside CGT. This ensemble approach, with performers listed anonymously or in peripheral roles, would become characteristic.

Three live volume releases appeared in 1997 on Fire Breathing Turtle (FBT005–FBT007), documenting the duo’s performances with extended lineups. Live Vol. 2: New York Radio featured Jeffery Taylor, Jesse Paul Miller, Robert Millis, Scott Colburn, and Spencer Sundell. Live Volume 3 expanded further to include Eyvind Kang, Jeph Jerman, Michael Northam, and Patrick Barber.

Late 1990s: Drone and Label Expansion (1998–1999)

Lovely Drone (1998, Gravelvoice Music) marked a tighter production credit: CGT and Scott Colburn as joint producers — a template that would persist through the next decade. A fourth self-titled Fire Breathing Turtle release appeared in 1998 (FBT 004).

The group signed with Anomalous Records for a reissue/catalog release program beginning in 1999: Live Vol. 1, Live Vol. 2, Live Vol. 3, and Live Vol. 4 appeared on Anomalous as FBT005A–D, making the Fire Breathing Turtle live series more widely available.

Japanese Release and International Reach (2000)

Dream Cut Short In The Mysterious Clouds (2000, Meme, meme018CD) was their Japanese market release on the Meme label — 14 untitled tracks of minimal/ambient/experimental electronic music, produced by CGT and Scott Colburn, with additional sound contributions from Beatrice Harrison and Simon Poole (2). The album appeared simultaneously on Anomalous Records for the US market (FBT006).

Soundtrack Work and Self-Titled LP (2001)

2001 brought two significant releases. Session 9 (Original Motion Picture Score) (Milan, 73138 35957-2) was the score to Brad Anderson’s horror film, performed, recorded, and edited by Jeffery Taylor, Robert Millis, and Scott Colburn. The trio functioned as both performers and engineers on this project. The soundtrack was released on Milan Records with Carson Daly among the executive producers for the music side.

A self-titled LP on Fire Breathing Turtle (FBT 007) and a Since 1972 release (MRS 001) that same year featured the largest documented CGT performer roster: Jeffery Taylor, Jeph Jerman, Jesse Paul Miller, John Vallier, Linda Peschong, Robert Millis, Scott Colburn, and Spencer Sundell — all credited as “Players,” alongside CGT and Scott Colburn jointly as Producer and Engineer. The 2001 FBT release is the clearest crystallization of the CGT collective approach.

Continued Activity and Compilations (2002–2009)

The group released Lovely (2002, Anomalous Records, NOM 15), Fuck Ups (2003, Since 1972), a Japan release Live In The 21st Century (2003, Kabukikore, kkcd45), and Highly Bred And Sweetly Tempered (2004, North East Indie, NEI34).

Smaller imprints including Soccer Mom Ebonics (Secret Recordings Vol. 2, Soothing Sounds For Broken Bones, both 2004; Live 7.28.06, 2006) document a parallel low-key release stream alongside the more distributed label releases. 5 Cents A Piece (2007, Abduction, ABDT038) featured a streamlined three-piece lineup: Dave Abramson (drums, electronics, percussion, vocals), Jeffery Taylor (guitar, slide guitar, electronics, piano, vocals), and Robert Millis (guitar, vocals, electronics, toy, percussion).

Insect Agents (2006, Fire Breathing Turtle) brought in Alan Bishop (of Sun City Girls) and Erin Sullivan alongside the core members.

The Journal Of Popular Noise, Vol. 1 Issues 10-12 Fall / Winter 2008 (The Journal of Popular Noise, JPN110–112) collected contributions alongside PWRFL Power, Paul Gold, Josh Bonati, and others.

Eerie Fragrance (2009, Etude Records, Etude020) is the final entry in the primary discography through that period.

2022 Return

A 2022 self-titled release appeared on Fire Breathing Turtle (no catalog number assigned in Discogs), suggesting the group remains sporadically active.

Open Questions

  • The exact lineup for each early release is often unclear — Discogs credits on the 1994–1996 releases are sparse; the “core duo” framing (Millis + Taylor) is inferred from recurring credits but may be contested
  • No producer is credited on the 1994 debut or several other early releases; whether Colburn was involved before Lovely Drone (1998) is unclear from the DB
  • The Slowly self-released single has no year in Discogs; possible 1990s bootleg/promo
  • The Oro (1998, Scratch Records) release credits CGT under a “Design” role, not as primary artists — context unclear; possible compilation or film credit
  • Post-2009 activity beyond Eerie Fragrance is thin in the Discogs data; the 2022 Fire Breathing Turtle release is undocumented further
  • No Discogs biography text for individual members Jeffery Taylor or Jesse Paul Miller — their instrument roles beyond “Performer” are undocumented in DB
