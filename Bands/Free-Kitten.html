Free Kitten

American noise/indie rock band formed in 1992 by Kim Gordon (Sonic Youth) and Julie Cafritz (Pussy Galore (2)). Active 1992–1997; briefly reactivated 2007–2008. Discogs artist ID: 75354.

Biography

Free Kitten formed in 1992 as a side project pairing two of the most prominent women in the New York noise-rock underground. Kim Gordon, bassist and vocalist for Sonic Youth, joined forces with Julie Cafritz (real name Julia Cafritz), guitarist of Pussy Galore (2) — the Washington D.C.–turned–NYC confrontational noise-rock band associated with Jon Spencer and Neil Hagerty. The pairing brought together the art-rock sensibility of the Sonic Youth orbit with the rawer, more antagonistic energy of the Pussy Galore / Boss Hog scene.

The band’s first release was the Call Now 12” EP (1992, Ecstatic Peace!), recorded with Don Fleming and Jim Waters (four tracks) and Lee Ranaldo (two tracks). At this early stage the lineup was fluid, with no fixed drummer.

In 1993, Yoshimi (Yoshimi P-We, born Yokota Yoshimi) — drummer and trumpeter of the Osaka noise group Boredoms — joined as the band’s drummer. This gave Free Kitten a more defined identity: two guitars and vocals over an international rhythm section with an improvisational, anything-goes ethic. A 7” on In The Red Recordings (Lick!, 1993) and a Japan tour 7” split with Mosquito (6) (Time Bomb Records, 1993) documented this trio configuration.

Mark Ibold — bassist of Pavement — came aboard in 1994 and appeared on all subsequent studio records through the 1990s. The quartet’s first full-length, Nice Ass (1994/1995, Kill Rock Stars, KRS-240), was released on KRS in the US and Wiiija Records in the UK, and in Japan through Time Bomb Records. The record captured a shambolic, lo-fi approach: short, spiky songs alongside looser jams, covers of X-Ray Spex (“Oh Bondage Up Yours!”) and the Minutemen, and deliberately anti-commercial production.

Sentimental Education (1997, Kill Rock Stars, KRS-287) was produced by Kim Gordon and Wharton Tiers, mixed by Tiers. It opened with a cover of Serge Gainsbourg’s “Teenie Weenie Boppie.” The record marked a slight refinement — sparser arrangements, more space — before the band went on extended hiatus later that year.

The 1997 12” Free Kitten With DJ Spooky That Subliminal Kid / Free Kitten With We™ (KRS-286) documented Free Kitten’s excursion into downtown electronic collaboration: Side A featured a remix by DJ Spooky (Paul D. Miller); Side B featured mixes by We™.

The band was reactivated in 2007–2008, without Mark Ibold, and released Inherit (2008, Ecstatic Peace!), produced and engineered by Justin Pizzoferrato. J Mascis (Dinosaur Jr.) contributed additional guitar on two tracks (“Surf’s Up” and “Bananas”). Inherit was recorded in Summer 2007 at Bisquiteen in Amherst, MA and Bank Row Recording. The Exclaim! announcement of the reunion offered only the tongue-in-cheek rationale that the band wished “to remind everyone they were one of the first real ‘animal bands’” — no substantive explanation or band-member quotes about the reunion’s origins have surfaced in press. (Exclaim!, 2008)

Post-Inherit activity is sparse. One documented live performance occurred on 10 April 2012 at Littlefield, Brooklyn, NY — the only confirmed show from the reunion era or after. (Songkick, archived listing) No further releases, tours, or official statements of disbandment have been found as of July 2026.

The name “Don Kotler” which appeared in early press/promotional context does not appear in Discogs personnel credits for any release; this may reflect a rehearsal-era participant not credited on record.

Steve West drum credit on Punks Suing Punks EP — why not Yoshimi? (web research 2026-08-25)

Short answer: No contemporaneous documentation has been found explaining why Steve West drummed on the Punks Suing Punks session instead of Yoshimi. The substitution is a bare credit fact: the EP liner notes credit “Mr. Steve West” on drums. No interview, press piece, or liner note annotation states a reason.

What the timeline suggests (circumstantial, not documented):

  • The session took place on 19 April 1995 at a space called “Lotto.”
  • Pavement’s Wowee Zowee finished recording by February 1995 and was released 11 April 1995 — Steve West had no documented Pavement commitments on 19 April 1995. (Wikipedia: Wowee Zowee, as of 2026-08-25)
  • Setlist.fm documents no Boredoms shows in April 1995. Their earliest documented 1995 performances are Glastonbury (23 June) and Roskilde (29 June), followed by a North American run in July. (setlist.fm: Boredoms, checked 2026-08-25) This does not confirm Yoshimi was unavailable — the Boredoms’ April 1995 activity in Japan is not captured on setlist.fm, and no touring or recording schedule for Yoshimi in that month has been located.
  • Mark Ibold (Pavement bassist) was already a Free Kitten member by April 1995; West was his bandmate. The simplest logistical explanation — West was available and already in the Pavement/KRS orbit via Ibold — is consistent with the facts but remains unsourced conjecture.

”Erica (7)” engineer credit — Punks Suing Punks EP (1995)

Web research conducted 2026-06-28. Sources checked: Discogs release page (via Google cache/search snippet), AllMusic, MusicBrainz API, Wikipedia, Trouser Press, Grokipedia, KEXP, Jezebel, scaruffi.com, Bandcamp, and general web searches.

What is confirmed:

  • The liner notes on the Punks Suing Punks EP credit only the first name “Erica” (no surname) as recording and mixing engineer. (Discogs r1022122, as reported in multiple search snippets referencing liner note text)
  • The session took place at a space referred to as “Lotto” on April 19, 1995. This studio name does not appear in any known NYC studio history source; it may be an informal name for a loft or private space rather than a registered commercial facility. (same source)
  • Discogs disambiguates this person as “Erica (7),” indicating six other people named “Erica” already exist in the database.

What is not confirmed:

  • Full surname of “Erica.” No contemporary 1990s press, liner scan, interview, AllMusic credits page, or MusicBrainz relation record names her beyond the first name.
  • “Erica Noise” (Discogs artist 1578467) is a different person — different disambiguation number; their 13 technical credits do not appear to overlap with the KRS/Free Kitten orbit based on search evidence.
  • No other recordings in the KRS or NYC noise-rock scene from 1994–1997 have been found crediting a first-name-only “Erica” in an engineering role, which would help cross-identify her.

Next step: Physical inspection of the Punks Suing Punks 7” sleeve (KRS-257) is the only remaining avenue. The sleeve may contain a surname or additional studio detail not captured in the Discogs data entry.
