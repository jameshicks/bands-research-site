Swans

Post-punk / noise rock / avant-garde rock. New York City. Active 1982–1997, reformed 2010–present. Founded and led throughout by Michael Gira. Discogs artist ID: 154588.

Marginally associated with the no wave scene at the outset; original sound was slow, extremely heavy, and confrontational. Evolved through multiple phases: brutal minimalism (1982–85), speed-up with drum machines and Jarboe’s vocal presence (1986–89), a major-label orchestral detour (1989–90), a return to Young God Records with a more expansive sound (1991–96), dissolution (1997), and a reformed line-up from 2010 to at least 2025.

Biography

Early brutal period (1982–1985)

Swans formed in New York City in 1982, founded by Michael Gira — who had previously played with Glenn Branca’s ensemble (Circus Mort). The earliest lineup included Jonathan Kane and Roli Mosimann on drums/percussion, Norman Westberg on guitar, and Harry Crosby on bass. The band self-released a debut 7” in 1982 on Labor Records before signing to Neutral Records for the debut LP.

Filth (1983, Neutral Records, Discogs release ID 371539) — the debut album — was engineered by Mark Berry in New York. Personnel: Gira (bass, vocals, tape), Mosimann (drums, percussion, tape), Kane (drums, percussion), Westberg (guitar), Crosby (bass). The sound was brutalist, grinding, and glacially slow — bass-heavy riffs repeated until the listener’s resistance collapsed.

Cop (1984, K.422, Discogs release ID 253756) — followed on the German label K.422. Recorded partly in New York (with engineer Mark Berry and Jonathan Thayer), partly in Zürich (engineers Harry Lombardi and Voco Fauxpas). Co-produced by Gira and Mosimann. JG Thirlwell also assisted engineering. The lineup shed Jonathan Kane; Mosimann was the sole drummer.

The EP Young God (1984, K.422) followed. Throughout 1984–85, the band also appeared on Homestead Records releases.

Jarboe era — transitional phase (1986–1989)

By 1986 Jarboe had joined as vocalist and keyboardist (she is also credited on background vocals on Greed). Ivan Nahem had taken over on drums, with Algis A. Kizys becoming the regular bassist.

Greed (1986, K.422, Discogs release ID 213528) — produced by Gira, engineered by Jorge Esteban. Personnel: Gira (vocals, tape, sampler, bass), Westberg (guitar), Crosby (bass), Nahem (drums), Jarboe (backing vocals).

Holy Money (1986, K.422) followed same year. Ted Parsons also appears in this period. The twin release of Greed/Holy Money marked a tempo uptick — drum machines and samples became more prominent — alongside the remarkable live album Public Castration Is A Good Idea (1986, self-released), recorded live and capturing the punishing live experience.

Children Of God (1987/US 1988, Caroline Records, Discogs release ID 305775) — released as the band crossed over to a wider audience. Discogs credits on the primary pressing are sparse, but the personnel expanded significantly: Jarboe’s voice is central; orchestral elements appear. This album is commonly dated 1988 in most discographies. Jarboe became co-equal presence vocally, and the sound broadened toward apocalyptic rock.

The New Mind single (1987, Product Inc.) and related releases appeared via European distribution.

Major-label detour (1989–1990)

The Burning World (1989, UNI Records, Discogs release ID 391232) — produced by Bill Laswell and Gira, recorded at Material’s studio. A deliberately expanded palette: string arrangements by Karl Berger, electric violin by Shankar, baglama and bouzouki by Nicky Skopelitis, tabla by Trilok Gurtu, violin by Fred Frith and others. Bass provided by Bill Laswell and Jason Asnes. Drums by Virgil Moorefield. A major departure into world-influenced production; widely considered an anomaly in the catalog. Jason Corsaro mixed.

Return to Young God — expansive phase (1991–1993)

Swans returned to Young God Records (Gira’s own imprint, founded c.1990) for a run of ambitious albums.

White Light from the Mouth of Infinity (1991, Young God Records, Discogs release ID 404236) — produced by Gira. Personnel: Gira (voice, acoustic guitar, keyboards, effects, sampler, arrangements), Jarboe (voice, backing vocals, keyboards, choral/orchestral arrangements), Westberg (electric guitar), Kristof Hahn (acoustic and electric guitar), Clinton Steele (acoustic and electric guitar), Nicky Skopelitis (acoustic guitar, baglama, bouzouki, banjo), Jenny Wade (bass), Anton Fier (drums, drum programming), Vinnie Signorelli (percussion), Steve Burgh (mandolin, 12-string guitar), Hahn Rowe (violin). Engineered by Bryce Goggin and Steve McAllister. JG Thirlwell contributed additional policing of remixes.

Love of Life (1992, Young God Records) followed. By this period the lineup settled around Gira, Jarboe, Westberg, Hahn, Steele, Kizys, and Signorelli, with Lary Seven and others.

Mid-period consolidation (1994–1996)

The Great Annihilator (1994, Young God Records via Vital, Discogs release ID 882130) — another lineup shift; William Rieflin becomes prominent. Lary Seven, Joe Goldring, Vudi, and Larry Mullins all appear in this era.

Soundtracks for the Blind (1996, Young God Records / Atavistic, Discogs release ID 329797) — the final studio album of the first period, a double-disc/box set. Produced almost entirely by Gira (Jarboe produced one track). Personnel: Gira (acoustic and electric guitar, voice, sounds, sampler, loops), Jarboe (voice, sounds, loops), Westberg (performer), Hahn (performer), Kizys (performer), Steele (performer), Rieflin (performer), Mullins (drums, vibraphone), Vudi (acoustic guitar), Joe Goldring (bass, guitar), Larry Lamé/Lary Seven. Mixed by Billy Anderson, Chris Griffin (2), Larry Lamé, and Martin Bisi. Kris Force (viola) guests. A sprawling, often terrifying work — widely regarded as the high-water mark of first-period Swans.

Dissolution and interim (1997–2009)

Swans performed final concerts in 1997 and released the farewell live document Swans Are Dead (1998, Young God Records). After dissolution, Gira continued with Angels of Light (1999–2010, also on Young God Records — 7 albums). Jarboe pursued an extensive solo and collaborative career under her own name and as The Living Jarboe. Norman Westberg did session and solo work. The catalog was reissued repeatedly via Young God.

Reformation (2010–present)

Gira reformed Swans in 2010 with an entirely new lineup aside from Norman Westberg and Kristof Hahn. No Jarboe in the reformed band.

My Father Will Guide Me Up a Rope to the Sky (2010, Young God Records, Discogs release ID 2449680) — first reformed album. Core lineup: Gira (guitar, vocals), Westberg (electric guitar), Hahn (lap steel, electric guitar), William Rieflin (multi-instrumental), Chris Pravdica (bass), Thor Harris (drums, percussion, dulcimer, vibraphone), Phil Puleo (drums, percussion). Additional: Kenny Siegal (electric piano, guitar), Grasshopper/Sean Mackowiak (mandolin), Siobhan Duffy Gira (vocals). Recorded by Kenny Siegal and Jason LaFarge. Mixed by Bryce Goggin, Adam Sachs, and Kevin McMahon (2).

The Seer (2012, Young God Records, Discogs release ID 3832321) — a massive double album, widely considered the centrepiece of the reformed era. Core Swans: Gira, Westberg, Hahn, Pravdica, Harris, Puleo; William Rieflin as “Honorary Swan.” Special guests: Jane Scarpantoni (cello), Eszter Balint (violin), Sean Mackowiak (mandolin, clarinet), Kevin McMahon (guitar). Recorded and mixed by Kevin McMahon. Mastered by Doug Henderson. Karen O (of Yeah Yeah Yeahs), Alan Sparhawk (Low), and Mimi Parker (Low) all appear.

To Be Kind (2014, Young God Records, Discogs release ID 5649159) — recorded and mixed by John Congleton. Core lineup: Gira, Westberg, Hahn, Pravdica, Harris, Puleo; Rieflin (synth, keys, bass). Backing vocals: St. Vincent, Cold Specks, Jennifer Church. Julia Kent arranged strings on one track. Daniel Hart on violin. Thor Harris left after this album.

The Glowing Man (2016, Young God Records, Discogs release ID 8634393) — recorded by John Congleton (additional recording by Ingo Krauss, Doug Henderson, Martin Bisi, and others). Core Swans: Gira, Westberg, Hahn, Pravdica, Puleo, Rieflin. Kaela Sinclair featured on backing vocals. Jennifer Gira (Gira’s daughter) sings lead on one track. Okkyung Lee on cello. Stated as the final album of the reformed second era; Pravdica and Harris did not continue.

Leaving Meaning. (2019, Young God Records) — new lineup; Dana Schechter on bass. Extensive guest contributors including Anna von Hausswolff, A Hawk and a Hacksaw, Baby Dee, The Necks.

The Beggar (2023, Young God Records / Mute) and Birthing (2025, Young God Records) continue Swans’ activity.

  • Jarboe — core member 1985–1997; extensive solo career as The Living Jarboe
