From Discogs

New Wave group started by Mike Score and his brother Ali in Liverpool, UK. Initially active between 1979 and 1986, they reunited in 2003 for a one-off performance on the “VH1 Series, Bands Reunited”, and reformed again in September 2004 for a small number of live shows in the United States, but broke up immediately afterward.

In 2018, the members of the original lineup came together to record an album with the Prague Philharmonic Orchestra entitled Ascension. In 2021, the original lineup once again reunited temporarily to record another album with the Prague Philharmonic Orchestra entitled String Theory.

Overview

A Flock of Seagulls was a New Wave and synth-pop group from Liverpool, founded by brothers Mike Score and Ali Score around 1979, with the core lineup also including Frank Maudsley on bass and Paul Reynolds on guitar. They built the bulk of their catalogue in the 1980s (24 releases) through Jive and Arista before disbanding in 1986; Paul Reynolds departed in 1984. The group made brief returns in 2003 and 2004 and reunited the original lineup in 2018 and 2021 to record orchestral albums with the Prague Philharmonic Orchestra. Producer Mike Howlett was the dominant creative partner across their output, sharing credits on an extraordinary 337 releases, with mixer Mike Shipley (136) and engineer Steve Lovell (111) also recurring throughout their catalogue.

Notes (web)

2004 reunion split — reason not publicly documented

No web source provides a direct, on-record explanation for why the original lineup (Mike Score, Ali Score, Paul Reynolds, Frank Maudsley) disbanded again immediately after the September 2004 US shows. Sources searched: the band’s official biography (aflockofseagulls.org), Classic Pop Magazine interview with Mike Score, Electricity Club interview with Mike Score, Periodic Table of Synthpop, Wikipedia (already exhausted per brief). None state a reason.

The closest available context comes from two Mike Score interviews that address the surrounding period:

  • In Classic Pop Magazine, Score described the November 2003 VH1 Bands Reunited reunion falling apart rapidly due to recurring internal tensions: “the same problems that had taken five or six years to arise first time round, they came up in two weeks.” [https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/a-flock-of-seagulls-interview-iconic-hair-and-the-folly-of-youth/]

  • In an Electricity Club interview (date unlisted, context: 2018 Ascension album era), Score attributed the original band’s 1986 downfall to volatile sibling dynamics and substance issues: “Brothers will be brothers, that part was quite volatile. But really, the old drugs ‘n’ booze tale was the downfall of the band.” [https://www.electricityclub.co.uk/a-flock-of-seagulls-interview/] — this quote addresses 1986, not 2004, but the same underlying tensions recurred.

The practical outcome of the 2004 split: Ali Score, Paul Reynolds, and Frank Maudsley did not continue with the band; Mike Score carried on touring under the name with replacement personnel (Joe Rodriguez on guitar, and later Michael Brahm and Pando). The original four did not appear together again until the 2018 Ascension orchestral recording project.
