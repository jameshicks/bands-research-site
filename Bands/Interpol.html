American rock band from Manhattan. Along with The Strokes, one of the defining acts of the early-2000s NYC post-punk revival, drawing explicitly from the lineage of Joy Division, New Order, and Echo & The Bunnymen. Turn On The Bright Lights (2002) is among the most influential debut albums of its decade.

History

Formation and Self-Released Beginnings (1997–2001)

Formed at New York University in 1997. Original lineup: Paul Banks (born Paul Julian Banks), Daniel Kessler (born Daniel Alexander Kessler), Carlos Dengler (credited as Carlos D.), and drummer Greg Drudy. The band self-released a debut CDr (1998) and the Precipitate EP (1999) before Drudy’s departure in 2000. Samuel Fogarino (born Samuel Joseph Fogarino) replaced him.

The band’s first release outside New York came via Fukd I.D. #3 (2000, Chemikal Underground). Fukd ID was a Chemikal Underground imprint launched specifically to release limited-edition one-off singles and EPs by acts not signed to the main label — giving the Glasgow-based label flexibility to work outside its Scottish roster. Each issue in the series was pressed to 1,000 copies, released in both 12” vinyl (45 RPM) and CD formats. [source: https://www.discogs.com/release/791011-Interpol-Fukd-ID-3, https://www.discogs.com/Interpol-Fukd-ID-3/release/432216] Chemikal Underground co-founder Alun Woodward later confirmed the label’s role in a Drowned in Sound profile: “We were also the first label to release Interpol in the UK, on our Fukd ID series.” [source: drownedinsound.com/in_depth/3150519-label-profile-16—chemikal-underground] The precise mechanism — whether the band submitted demos directly to the label or a third party made the introduction — is not documented in any available interview or press source. The EP contained four tracks: “PDA”, “Precipitate”, “Roland”, and “5”. “PDA”, “Roland”, and “5” were recorded in 1998; “Precipitate” was recorded in 1999. The versions of “PDA” and “Roland” on this EP differ from the re-recorded versions on Turn On The Bright Lights (2002). [source: https://eil.com/shop/moreinfo.asp?catalogid=556520] [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukd_ID, https://www.getintothis.co.uk/2020/06/chemikal-underground-records-mogwai-delgados-arab-strap-and-the-enduring-influence-of-the-scottish-indie-label/]

Matador and the Peak Era (2002–2006)

Signed to Matador in 2002. The Interpol EP appeared first, followed in August by Turn On The Bright Lights — eleven tracks recorded and mixed by Peter Katis (Tarquin Studios, Bridgeport CT), mastered by Greg Calbi. Katis mixed seven tracks; Gareth Jones mixed three. Immediate critical recognition; 67 pressings over the following two decades. The album’s debt to Joy Division — Banks’s baritone, Kessler’s angular post-punk guitar, Dengler’s melodic bass — was widely noted, though the band consistently positioned themselves as influenced by the broader Wire/Gang of Four lineage.

Antics (2004, also Matador, also Katis) was the commercial follow-through: “Slow Hands” and “Evil” received substantial radio and MTV play in Europe; 70 pressings catalogued. Greg Giorgio assisted Katis on engineering.

Capitol Records (2007)

Our Love To Admire (July 2007) marked a major-label transition to Capitol Records. Produced by Interpol and Rich Costey (who also engineered and mixed), recorded at Electric Ladyland Studios and The Magic Shop, NYC. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Singles “The Heinrich Maneuver” and “Mammoth” received European chart placement via Parlophone. Engineer Claudius Mittendorfer assisted.

Self-Titled and the Dengler Departure (2010)

Carlos Dengler performed on the self-titled fourth album (Interpol, September 2010, Soft Limit / Cooperative Music) but departed during or shortly after recording. Mixed by Alan Moulder. Banks took on bass duties himself; Truax and Curtis joined the touring band. Dengler has since pursued writing and other fields; no return to music.

Trio Era: El Pintor Onward (2014–present)

El Pintor (September 2014, Matador / Soft Limit) — title is an anagram of “Interpol” — was the first studio album as a confirmed trio. Self-produced; mixed by Alan Moulder with John Catlin and Caesar Edmunds. Brandon Curtis played keyboards on nine of ten tracks. Roger Joseph Manning Jr. contributed on this and subsequent albums. Recorded at Electric Lady Studios, NYC.

Marauder (August 2018, Matador) was produced by Dave Fridmann — a harder-edged, more distorted texture than the Katis or Costey records.

The Other Side Of Make-Believe (July 2022, Matador) was produced by Flood (Mark Ellis), recorded largely in London. Alan Moulder contributed additional production. Flood, engineer Richie Kennedy, and vocalist Juliet Seger appeared as guest performers on specific tracks. Steve Fallone joined Greg Calbi on mastering.

Live At Third Man Records (2024, Third Man Records) captured a 2022 performance.

Scene Context

Interpol arrived at the precise moment New York’s rock underground was achieving its broadest reach since the 1970s. Their Joy Division-adjacent aesthetic placed them adjacent to The Strokes and LCD Soundsystem while pointing in a different direction — darker, more melodic, less ironic. The Matador relationship also placed them in the company of Yo La Tengo and Pavement-era indie seriousness rather than the DFA dance-rock axis.

The Capitol signing (2007) was an anomaly for a Matador-era band; they returned to Matador for El Pintor (2014). Turn On The Bright Lights has been reissued multiple times and its 67-pressing count makes it among the most widely distributed rock debuts of the decade.
