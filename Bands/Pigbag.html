Pigbag

Post-punk / funk / brass band. Cheltenham / Bristol, UK. Active 1980–1983. Discogs ID: 55839.

Known for the infectious instrumental “Papa’s Got A Brand New Pigbag” (Y Records, 1981), which hit UK #3 on re-release in 1982 with two Top of the Pops appearances. Part of the Bristol post-punk ecosystem — Simon Underwood came straight from The Pop Group, and their manager Disc O’Dell ran Y Records. Described by James as “the UK counterpart to Konk.”

Formation

Founded in late 1980 by Chris Hamlin in Cheltenham. He recruited multi-instrumentalist Roger Freeman (an old friend from Birmingham), Chris Lee (trumpet), and James Johnstone (guitar/alto sax). Chip Carpenter (drums) and Mark Smith (7) (bass) — both school friends of Johnstone and ex-Hardware — joined for informal rehearsals. It was in these jam sessions that “Papa’s Got A Brand New Pigbag” developed from an idea Hamlin had before the band formed.

Simon Underwood (ex-The Pop Group) was then invited to join, bringing with him tenor saxophonist Ollie Moore. Underwood’s connection with manager Disc O’Dell — who also ran Y Records — landed the band their first gig supporting The Pop Group at Bristol’s Romeo and Juliets. The crowd’s response to a 20-minute performance of their single song was so strong that O’Dell invited them to record it for Y Records the next day.

Discography

”Papa’s Got A Brand New Pigbag” (1981 / 1982)

Label: Y Records, UK
Discogs master ID: 66284 (39 pressings original; 18 pressings 1985 reissue on Kaz Records)
Discogs release ID: 76841 (original 1981 pressing)

Lacquer cut by Porky (5) (George Peckham). Plated by PAG. The title takes its name from James Brown’s “Papa’s Got A Brand New Bag” — the music is entirely original. Became an underground dance hit in 1981, selling thousands and charting high in the indie charts. Re-released 1982 (master 67597) — UK #3; two Top of the Pops appearances, April 8 & 22, 1982.

After Pigbag

The band split June 1983. In 1983 Angela Jaeger and Simon Underwood were married at Hammersmith Registry Office; notable attendees included John Lydon. Angela, James Johnstone, and Simon formed Instinct (7) — signed to Sire but the recording of a single was never released. One track, “Swamp Out,” appeared on a 1985 compilation.

Ollie Moore later formed Leonard and the Cats with Janine Rainforth (Maximum Joy) and Nick Sheppard (The Cortinas / The Clash).

Angela Jaeger relocated to Barcelona for nine years; returned to NYC in the late 1990s. Collaboration with David Cunningham: Artificial Homeland (1998). Roger Freeman resurfaced in Dr. Calculus.
