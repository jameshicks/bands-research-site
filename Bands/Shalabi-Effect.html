Shalabi Effect

Discogs ID: 138827
Active: 1996–present (core period 1998–2005; periodic reunions/releases from 2012)
Also Known As: The Shalabi Effect
Origin: Montreal, Quebec, Canada
Genre: Psychedelic, experimental, free jazz, free improvisation, drone, electronics

Montreal-based experimental quartet centred on Sam Shalabi (oud, guitar, electronics). Emerged from the same Alien8/Constellation milieu as Set Fire To Flames, GY!BE-adjacent collaborators, and the broader Montreal post-rock underground. Core lineup active 1998–2005 produced four Alien8 releases; the band has regrouped for recordings in 2012, 2013, 2015, and 2021.

Biography

Origins (1996–1998)

Shalabi Effect began in 1996 as a duo: Anthony Seck (electric guitar, lapsteel, Moog, keyboards) and Sam Shalabi (oud, electronics). The two played around Montreal and self-released a cassette recording. The 1998 debut, The Shalabi Effect, was a self-released cassette/LP documenting this early duo configuration, with Anthony Seck also taking a production role alongside François Miron.

Core Quartet (1998–2005)

In 1998, Alexandre St-Onge (double bass, electronics) and Will Eizlini (percussion, tabla, electronics) joined, completing the four-piece lineup that would define the band’s recorded output. Performances during this era were described as increasingly dense — live electronics, unusual instruments, percussion and drone-makers, and occasional screenings of archival NFB (National Film Board of Canada) nature films.

The self-titled 2000 LP on Alien8 Recordings introduced the full quartet. The Trial of St-Orange (2002, Alien8) followed, engineered and mixed by Tracey Holmes with recording by John Dooher. Pink Abyss (2004, Alien8) was the most expansive release of the era, bringing in a raft of Montreal guests including Thierry Amar (acoustic bass — also of Godspeed You! Black Emperor and Esmerine), Sophie Trudeau (violin — also of Godspeed), Charles Spearin (trumpet — Do Make Say Think / Broken Social Scene), Geneviève Heistek (violin/viola), Elizabeth Anka Vajagic (vocals), Gabriel Levine (clarinet, vocals), Craig Dionne (flute), and Will Glass (drums). The band self-produced it; Tracey Holmes recorded.

Unfortunately (2005, Alien8) marked a stripped-back return to the core quartet (St-Onge, Seck, Shalabi, Eizlini), with Radwan Moumneh (of Jerusalem In My Heart) producing and mixing.

After the Initial Run (2012–2021)

The band reconvened for Feign to Delight Gaiety of Gods (2012, Annihaya Records), produced by the full quartet minus Will Eizlini on drums — replaced in part by John Heward and a raft of guest musicians including Jason Sharp (baritone/bass sax), Joshua Zubot (violin), Philémon Girouard (alto sax), Patrick Conan (drums), Elizabeth Anka Vajagic (vocals), and others. Radwan Moumneh returned as engineer and mixer.

A 2013 contribution to the 3X3 - Series Two - Volume Three compilation on -walnut + locust- brought in collaborators Thisquietarmy and .cut featuring Gibet. A self-released Floating Garden appeared in 2015. In 2021, Friends of the Prophet 6 (Unrock) returned to the live/improv format, and The Shalabi Effect — a 2021 reissue of the debut — appeared on Radio Khiyaban.
