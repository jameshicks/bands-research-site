Red Transistor

Discogs ID: 443012
Origin: New York City
Active: September 1977–August 1978
Labels: Red Star Records (planned, unreleased); Ecstatic Peace! (1990 reissue)

New York No Wave band. Formed around the core of Rudolph Grey and Von LMO in late 1977. Performed at the “Bands at Artists Space” festival (1978) alongside the four acts that appeared on No New York — but were not included on the record. Their sole release, Not Bite / We’re Not Crazy, was recorded for Red Star Records and sat in the vault until Thurston Moore issued it on Ecstatic Peace! in 1990.

Biography

Red Transistor coalesced in September 1977 around guitarist/vocalist Rudolph Grey and multi-instrumentalist Von LMO (Frank Cavallo, born March 10, 1951, Brooklyn). The band’s lineup was fluid — multiple drummers cycled through across the eleven-month run — but the Grey/LMO core remained constant.

The band played the SoHo underground loft and gallery circuit during the 1977–78 season. Their signal moment came at the “Bands at Artists Space” festival in 1978, a five-night event co-organized by Michael Zwack and Robert Longo that also featured the four acts later documented on No New York. Brian Eno attended to document the scene and selected The Contortions, DNA, Mars, and Teenage Jesus And The Jerks for his compilation; Red Transistor, along with Theoretical Girls and others, were passed over.

In December 1977 — before the Artists Space festival — Red Transistor recorded two tracks at Sorceror Sound, NYC: “Not Bite” and “We’re Not Crazy.” The band were formally on the Red Star Records roster (Marty Thau, manager of the New York Dolls), but the release was shelved before it came out. The recordings stayed unreleased for over a decade until Thurston Moore issued them as a 7” on Ecstatic Peace! in 1990, with artwork and liner notes by Grey himself.

By August 1978, Red Transistor had dissolved. Jim Sclavunos moved to Teenage Jesus And The Jerks and subsequently 8 Eyed Spy. Rudolph Grey continued as a guitarist and noise improviser, forming Blue Humans — a long-running project drawing in players like Thurston Moore, Arthur Doyle, Alan Licht, and Beaver Harris. Von LMO went on to record the influential cosmic-punk LP Future Language (1981) and continued as a solo artist into the 2000s.

Web Notes

Added from web research; sources cited inline. Facts here supplement or clarify Discogs-sourced data above.

Recording date of “Not Bite / We’re Not Crazy”

The single was recorded in December 1977 at Sorceror Sound, NYC — prior to the Artists Space festival (1978). The drummer on the session was Mark Edmands. (popsike auction listing) This resolves the “c. 1978” uncertainty in the Discography section above; the sessions predated the festival.

Red Star Records — band was formally on the roster

Multiple sources confirm Red Transistor were formally part of Marty Thau’s Red Star Records roster — not merely in talks. The Billboard obituary for Thau (2014) lists the label’s artists as “Suicide, The Real Kids, The Fleshtones, violin player Walter Steding, noise duo Red Transistor and, later on, Richard Hell.” The release was shelved before it could come out, but the band was signed. (Billboard)

Whether Red Transistor performed at Artists Space

Conflict flag: The existing biography section states Red Transistor “Performed at the ‘Bands at Artists Space’ festival (1978) alongside the four acts that appeared on No New York.” This is not confirmed by either the Wikipedia article on No New York or the Wikipedia article on Artists Space. Both articles list the festival’s ten acts, and Red Transistor is absent from both lists. The confirmed ten are: the Gynecologists (Rhys Chatham), Communists, Theoretical Girls (Glenn Branca), Terminal, Tone Death (Rhys Chatham), Daily Life (Branca), DNA, The Contortions, Mars, and Teenage Jesus and the Jerks. (Wikipedia — No New York, Wikipedia — Artists Space)

A “ten bands Eno considered” list circulating in fan wikis and blogs (Beat Patrol, Fandom wikis) includes Red Transistor alongside the festival bands plus Boris Policeband — but neither Boris Policeband nor Red Transistor appear in the Wikipedia festival lists, and the primary source for this “considered” list has not been identified. It may derive from Marc Masters’ No Wave book (2008) or another secondary account not yet checked. The Head Heritage review of Not Bite / We’re Not Crazy states RT played CBGB and Max’s Kansas City — not Artists Space. Until a primary source (the Masters book, a 1978 press clip, or a contemporary programme) confirms RT’s Artists Space appearance, that claim in the biography should be treated as unverified.

Why Red Transistor was excluded from No New York

No primary-source Eno statement about Red Transistor’s exclusion exists in the public record. The conclusion is well-evidenced, not merely tentative:

  • Marc Masters, author of the No Wave book (Black Dog Publishing, 2008), attempted to interview Eno for the project. Eno’s management declined: he was not giving interviews in 2007. Masters confirmed this in his Washington City Paper interview. (Washington City Paper — Marc Masters interview). This is the most definitive explanation for why no Eno primary statement exists — he has not spoken on the selection publicly in any documented source found to date.

The best available secondary accounts, in descending order of evidential weight:

Glenn Branca (interviewed by Marc Masters, The Wire): “Eno had personally met the people on the East Village side, and they convinced him that the idea of four bands was better.” On Red Transistor: “they used to play at Max’s all the time, and they were great.” Branca speculated about his own exclusion: “he actually might have seen us as more direct competition with him personally than any of the other bands.” He criticised the compilation’s homogenising effect: “Eno took all four bands and made them sound like the same fucking thing. How in the world could Mars ever sound like The Contortions? Those were four very different sounds.” (The Wire / Marc Masters interview)

Adele Bertei (Contortions member, interviewed Walk on the Wild Side NYC substack): “He was there every night. I don’t know why he chose the ones he chose. My speculation is the things I said. We were the most exciting to him for some reason.” On potential reasons for other exclusions: she suggested Eno may have been “turned off” by some downtown figures’ “anti-capitalist, anti-commercial” hostility, naming specifically Jeffrey Lohn and Glenn Branca. She drew a musical parallel: “when you listen to Theoretical Girls or Y Pants or some of those bands, they were very similar to what DNA or Mars were doing.” Red Transistor not mentioned specifically. (Walk on the Wild Side NYC — Adele Bertei interview)

Brian Eno, Creem interview, late 1978 (indirect): Eno described the no wave bands as “research bands” — “They take deliberately extreme stances that are very interesting because they define the edges of a piece of territory.” He said in a contemporaneous New York Times conversation with critic John Rockwell: “The New York bands proceed from a ‘what would happen if’ orientation.” These statements characterise his interest in the scene but do not address the selection of four bands specifically or name any excluded act. (Creem, 1978; NYT, July 1978 — cited in multiple secondary sources including RBMA Daily)

Rock and Roll Globe, “No New York at 45” (2023): States Eno excluded Theoretical Girls and the Gynecologists because they were “too artsy” / too associated with the SoHo art scene. This rationale is not applied to Red Transistor in that piece. (Rock and Roll Globe)

Note on Theoretical Girls: Multiple secondary sources treat Theoretical Girls as simply “excluded” alongside Red Transistor, but the situation is more complex and is now resolved. Eno was aware of Theoretical Girls: he attended an evening of short pieces at Jeff Lohn’s SoHo loft, per Glenn Branca in The Wire / Marc Masters — “Eno showed up. So he was clearly aware of us.” (The Wire / Marc Masters interview)

Formal offer made; Lohn declined. Jeff Lohn stated: “When I say I don’t like pretentious art rock or pop music, Eno’s music is a case in point…. I wasn’t interested in participating with him on any project at all.” (Thurston Moore & Byron Coley, No Wave: Post-Punk. Underground. New York. 1976–1980, Abrams Image, 2008, p. unspecified — oral history from personal interviews; cited at Driving Me Backwards, footnote 4.) The blog’s framing — “apparently asked and refused to participate” — corroborates the offer; the Lohn quote confirms the refusal. Adele Bertei’s implication that Lohn’s anti-commercial hostility “pushed Eno away” is consistent with this: Lohn’s hostility preceded any offer and the offer may have come despite it.

Red Transistor’s situation is categorically different: no statement from Rudolph Grey or Von LMO about their exclusion has been found, and they did not appear at the Artists Space festival at all (see conflict flag above).

Alan Vega on Red Transistor: Alan Vega (Suicide) described attending RT shows: “Red Transistor… Von LMO sang and played guitar and Rudolph Grey was on guitar or bass and it was total insanity. Von LMO was a nut, a great nut. I was afraid to be in the same room as him.” (Source: Future Language Documents, cited in Wikipedia / Von LMO article — likely liner notes for the Future Language reissue.) This confirms RT’s reputation as a potent live act but says nothing about the compilation.

Summary: No primary-source Eno statement about Red Transistor’s exclusion has been found, and the likelihood of one existing is low: Eno declined all interviews on the subject when Marc Masters researched the topic circa 2007, and no 1978–1980 contemporaneous interview has surfaced in which Eno named excluded bands or gave selection rationale beyond general enthusiasm for the “research band” concept. The best available account (Branca/Masters/The Wire) points to the four included acts — specifically the East Village contingent — lobbying for a four-band-only format. No statement from Von LMO or Rudolph Grey about the exclusion has been located.

Kip Kupa

One source (search aggregate from nowave.pair.com) describes Kip Kupa as having played bass in Von LMO’s post-Red Transistor live band and as a fixture of the South Brooklyn scene, performing at CBGB and Max’s Kansas City. This is a weak secondary source and could not be independently verified; treat as provisional. No Discogs record found.

Von LMO — pre-Red Transistor history

Before Red Transistor, Von LMO (Frank Cavallo) played in two earlier groups: Funeral of Art (featuring bassist Sal Maida, who later played with Roxy Music and Sparks) and Kongress (described as “somewhat Velvet Underground-ish”). He launched his solo career in 1979 after Red Transistor dissolved. (Bandcamp Daily)

Rudolph Grey — Mars association confirmed

Wikipedia’s Mars (band) article states: “They were joined briefly by guitarist Rudolph Grey of Red Transistor.” This confirms Grey had a brief stint with Mars — not merely a Discogs data error. Duration and exact timing not specified. (Wikipedia — Mars (band), as of 2025-06-06)
