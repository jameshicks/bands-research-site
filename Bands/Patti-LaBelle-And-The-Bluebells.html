Patti LaBelle And The Bluebells

Discogs ID: 403181 Also known as: The Blue Belles (early name, Discogs 3122859), The Bluebelles Origin: Philadelphia / Trenton, NJ, USA Active: c.1961–1970 (as Bluebells/Blue Belles); continued as LaBelle 1971–1976

Philadelphia doo-wop and soul vocal quartet. One of the major R&B vocal groups of the 1960s. Recorded for Newtown Records, Parkway Records, and Atlantic Records before evolving into the glam-soul trio LaBelle.

Formation

Patti LaBelle (Patricia Holt) and Cindy Birdsong came out of a Philadelphia group called The Ordettes; Nona Hendryx and Sarah Dash came from The Del Capris in Trenton, NJ. The four coalesced c.1961 and began recording for Newtown Records, a subsidiary of the Cameo-Parkway operation, the following year. Early sleeve credits vary widely — “Patti La Belle And The Blue Belles,” “Patty La Belle & The Blue Belles,” “Patty La Belle & Her Belles,” and “Patti LaBelle & The Bluebelles” all appear on original pressings.

Bobby Martin (b. 4 May 1930, Lockland OH; d. 6 Sep 2013, San Diego CA) — arranger and songwriter credited on Newtown-era recordings, including the B-side of Down The Aisle. Went on to become a foundational figure of the Philadelphia soul sound and was later referred to as “The Granddaddy of R&B.”

Tom Dowd (b. 20 Oct 1925 NYC; d. 27 Oct 2002 Aventura FL) — one of the great Atlantic house engineers; credited on Dreamer (1967) alongside Bob Gallo and Phil Iehle.

Bert Berns (b. 8 Nov 1929 The Bronx; d. 30 Dec 1967) — songwriter (also credited as Bert Russell, Russell Byrd); co-founded Bang Records in 1965 with Jerry Wexler and others. Active at Atlantic during the group’s peak years there.

Artie Butler (real name Arthur Joel Butler) — arranger across 16 releases with the group during the Atlantic period.

Evolution

When Cindy Birdsong departed c.1967 to join The Supremes (replacing Florence Ballard), the group continued as a trio with Patti, Nona, and Sarah. They kept recording for Atlantic through 1970, then relaunched as LaBelle in 1971 — shedding the doo-wop presentation for a harder, glam-inflected sound produced by Allen Toussaint on their landmark Nightbirds album.
