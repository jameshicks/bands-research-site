Knots (2)

New York City punk band, active c. 1979–1980. Also known as The Knots. Discogs artist ID: 2005281. Name variations: The Knots.

Overview

The Knots were a New York City punk/power-pop band whose sole original release was the 1980 single “Heartbreaker” b/w “Action,” issued on their own Ideal Records at 55 Mercer St., New York, NY — placing them in the SoHo/TriBeCa orbit of the NYC punk scene. Their label address and membership overlaps with The Heartbreakers (2) associates (two members went on to The Waldos, Walter Lure’s post-Heartbreakers band) suggests deep roots in the Max’s Kansas City / CBGB milieu.

“Action” was later anthologized on Punk 45 Vol. 1 - Kill The Hippies! Kill Yourself! (Soul Jazz Records, 2013), track 15, placing the band alongside Theoretical Girls, Pere Ubu, Electric Eels, the Flamin’ Groovies, and Johnny Thunders & The Heartbreakers. “Heartbreaker” appeared on the Max’s Kansas City 1976 & Beyond (Jungle Records, 2017) LP compilation (credited as “The Knots”), confirming their connection to that venue and scene.

Scene Context

The Knots’ sole original single was self-released on Ideal Records at 55 Mercer St, New York — an address in the SoHo/TriBeCa neighborhood, the same zone that hosted No Wave lofts, CBGB, and Max’s Kansas City. The B-side “Action” was recorded and tight enough to be licensed for the Soul Jazz Punk 45 American punk survey three decades later.

The connection to The Waldos is the clearest post-Knots thread: both Joey Pinter and Tony Coiro joined Walter Lure’s Waldos, the continuation of Johnny Thunders’ Heartbreakers energy into the 1990s and 2000s. Pinter also played guitar on Walter Steding’s 1980 LP (Red Star Records) and played in Pleasure Pirates with Ricki Kirk.

Tony Coiro’s other credits include The New Too Much Junkie Business (ROIR, 1983 — bass), and Sympathy For The Record Industry’s Rent Party (1994 — bass, vocals) with The Waldos. He died in 1995.
