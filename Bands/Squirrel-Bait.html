Squirrel Bait

Louisville, Kentucky post-hardcore / hardcore punk band. Active c.1982–1988. Discogs ID: 323834.

The central band of the first wave of Louisville underground punk, Squirrel Bait connected the city’s early hardcore scene to the post-hardcore experimentalism that defined Slint, Bastro, and Gastr Del Sol. Formed by guitarist David Grubbs and bassist Clark Johnson while still in high school, the band rotated drummers across its short life and never settled on a single “classic” lineup. Both major released lineups — the 1985 EP and the 1987 LP — were produced by the band themselves with engineer Howie Gano at Sound on Sound studio in Louisville.

History

Origins and early tapes (1982–1984)

The band began in 1982 under the name Squirrelbait Youth (also a Discogs name variation), with David Grubbs on guitar and vocals, Clark Johnson on bass, and Richard Schuler on drums. A first cassette demo on the self-run label Watergate Tapes (2) (cat# WT-001) included ten tracks of driving hardcore punk — “Tylenol Scare,” “Ghetto Blaster,” “Insurrection,” and others — with a fourth, uncredited member on guitar/vocals listed only as “Evan (8)” and Johnson credited under the alias “Spooge (2).” A second self-released tape followed in 1983.

After Richard Schuler moved away, Britt Walford took over on drums and Peter Searcy joined as vocalist. In July 1984, this lineup recorded ten songs at Sound on Sound with engineer Howie Gano, auditioning for a possible release on Homestead Records. The recordings demonstrated a more melodic, driving sound departing from straight hardcore thrash. Two songs from that session appeared on the 1985 EP; “Tense/Earth Shattering” was held back and eventually released in 1987 under a one-off alias.

Debut EP (1985)

By May 1985 the lineup had shifted again: Britt Walford was out, replaced by Ben Daughtrey on drums, and Brian McMahan had joined as second guitarist. This five-piece — Grubbs, Johnson, McMahan, Daughtrey, Searcy — recorded six more songs at Sound on Sound with Howie Gano engineering and co-producing. The self-titled EP came out in fall 1985 on Homestead Records (HMS028), receiving significant critical notice in the indie underground press.

Walford appears on the EP on two tracks (A4 “When I Fall,” B3 “Disguise”), credited alongside Daughtrey, making the record a document of two different lineups. The eight-song 12” EP ran just over 17 minutes and established the band’s signature — melodic hardcore with emotionally raw vocals and interlocking guitars.

Kid Dynamite 7” (1986)

A two-song 7” single on Homestead (HMS061), “Kid Dynamite” b/w “Slake Train Coming.” Both songs were also recorded for the LP. No additional personnel credits beyond the Porky lacquer cut. The single appeared simultaneously in US and European editions.

Skag Heaven LP (1987)

The band’s only full-length, recorded at Sound on Sound with Howie Gano engineering and the band self-producing. Released on Homestead Records (HMS072), ten tracks. Final track “Tape From California” is a cover of a Phil Ochs song, with Ochs credited for lyrics and music on the Discogs entry. Ben Daughtrey drew the cover artwork (credited with characteristic humor as “Ben ‘I Am In This Band, Too’ Daughtrey”). David Grubbs is credited with “Guitar, Voice [Yell]” in addition to primary guitar duties.

After Skag Heaven, the band dissolved. Grubbs and Johnson left Louisville for college (they later reunited in Bastro). McMahan and Walford — who had played together since middle school and overlapped in Squirrel Bait in different periods — went on to form Slint.

Reissues (1997)

In the mid-1990s, Grubbs reissued both the self-titled EP and Skag Heaven on his short-lived imprint Dexter’s Cigar (a Drag City sublabel), catalog numbers DEX10 and DEX11 respectively, both in 1997. The Discogs compilation entry “Skag Heaven Plus 8 Bonus Tracks” on Homestead Records documents a later expanded reissue.

Connections

  • Slint — McMahan and Walford formed Slint directly after Squirrel Bait’s dissolution; Slint’s debut Tweez (1989) and Spiderland (1991) built on the sonic vocabulary Squirrel Bait had begun developing
  • David Grubbs — guitar and early vocals; left Louisville for college, co-founded Bastro with Johnson, then Gastr del Sol
  • Bastro — formed by Grubbs and Johnson after leaving Louisville; continued Squirrel Bait’s trajectory toward more experimental post-hardcore
  • Gastr Del Sol — evolved from Bastro in Chicago; Grubbs’s central project through the 1990s
  • The For Carnation — McMahan’s post-Slint project
  • King Kong (3) — Louisville collective featuring Richard Schuler after his time in Squirrel Bait
  • Peter Searcy — vocalist 1984–1988; later fronted Big Wheel (2), Starbilly, The Glasspack as a solo/band artist
  • Homestead Records — released all official Squirrel Bait records (HMS028, HMS061, HMS072); sublabel of Dutch East India Trading
  • Drag City — parent of Dexter’s Cigar, through which Grubbs reissued the catalog in 1997
  • Howie Gano — Sound on Sound engineer and co-producer; present on both the EP and LP
