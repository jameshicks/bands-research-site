Aural Exciters

Discogs ID: 27661 Also credited as: The Aural Exciters; Sue Who & The Aural Exciters; Ariel

New York City. Active 1979–1982 (original run); a 2003 ZE reissue revived the name. Not a fixed band — a rotating aggregation of downtown NYC musicians assembled around Bob Blank’s Blank Tape Studios and the ZE Records orbit. The core conceptual architects were Blank (producer/engineer/guitarist) and August Darnell (composer/arranger), who brought in a shifting cast of No Wave and mutant-disco figures for each release.

The Discogs entry lists eight official members: Bob Blank, Lizzy Mercier Descloux, Coati Mundi (Andy Hernandez), Taana Gardner, Adele Bertei, Ron Rogers, Christine Wiltshire, and James Siegfried (a.k.a. James Chance). In practice, the credit lists shift per release and extend well beyond this core.

Origins and Context (1979)

Aural Exciters emerged at the intersection of two currents that defined late-1970s downtown New York: the No Wave noise scene (James Chance, Pat Place, Adele Bertei) and the Caribbean-inflected dance music being cooked up around ZE Records by August Darnell and the Kid Creole And The Coconuts extended family. Darnell — who also fronted Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band — wrote or co-wrote the majority of material on the debut LP, which he conceived as a vehicle for what Discogs’s own profile calls “No Wave and Mutant disco.”

Bob Blank’s role was pivotal: he produced, engineered, and played guitar on all major sessions, functioning as the project’s sonic anchor across every lineup change. His studio, Blank Tape Studios, was the production home for much of ZE’s early catalog.

The name “Aural Exciter” itself references a real piece of studio hardware — a psychoacoustic audio-enhancing device (the Aphex Aural Exciter) widely used in recording studios from the mid-1970s onward.

My Boy Lollipop (1979)

Single issued on ZE Records / Buddah Records the same year as the LP. Produced by Bob Blank. Credits in the DB are sparse (Blank as sole additional credit), suggesting session overlap with the LP band. Six pressings in the DB.

Chinese Rap (1982)

Single on Top Flight Records. A distinct shift — the ZE/Darnell axis gives way to a more commercial rap-inflected sound. Credits:

NameRole

Randy Muller (aka Brooklyn Groove Machine) was affiliated with the funk/disco outfit Brass Construction; his involvement here signals a brief pivot toward the emerging hip-hop mainstream. Lola Blank co-produced with Bob.

Emile (Night Rate) (2003 / France)

A ZE Records reissue single that reassembled the original cast — or at least their names — in the credits. Issued in France, it revived the buried LP track “Emile (Night Rate)” (originally credited to Darnell on side B of Spooks In Space).

Open Questions

  • Pat Place’s guitar credit and Lizzy Mercier Descloux’s vocal credit appear in the 2003 single’s liner credits and the Discogs “members” field, but the 1979 LP pressing (ZEA 33-001) does not explicitly list them in the DB credits. Possible: they are on specific tracks, not the full LP. Web sources (AllMusic, original sleeve scans) might clarify.
  • Adele Bertei is listed as a Discogs member but does not appear in any of the DB release credits for this project. She may have appeared on 12” pressings or alternate versions not in this DB extract.
  • “Spooks In Space” year: DB shows 1979 for both masters; the brief described it as “ZE, 1981.” The catalog number ZEA 33-001 and the 1979 copyright are consistent with a 1979 release; the “1981” may reflect a later repress or European issue date.
  • The 22-release count on ZE cited in the brief is higher than the 5 unique masters in the DB discography. Likely includes multiple pressing variants (the DB shows up to 9 per master) — but it’s worth confirming whether any non-LP/non-single releases (promo versions, DJ copies, alternate formats) exist in the full dataset.
  • “Chinese Rap” (1982) is on Top Flight Records, not ZE — a departure. Context for that label relationship (licensing? distribution?) not available in DB.
