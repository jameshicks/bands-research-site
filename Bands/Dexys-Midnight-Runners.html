Dexys Midnight Runners

British soul/pop band from Birmingham, formed 1978 by Kevin Rowland and Kevin Archer (Al Archer). Discogs artist ID: 192557. Active 1978–2003, revived 2012–present as Dexys.

Overview

Dexys Midnight Runners were founded in Birmingham by Kevin Rowland and Kevin Archer, both previously of punk outfit The Killjoys. The band’s career divides into three distinct eras defined by dramatic lineup changes, stylistic reinvention, and Rowland’s uncompromising creative control.

Era 1 (1978–1981): The soul rebels — The original lineup drew from white Birmingham soul and American R&B, built around a tight brass section with trombone, saxophone, and Hammond organ. The debut album Searching For The Young Soul Rebels (1980) was produced by Pete Wingfield on Parlophone/EMI and established the band’s intense, confrontational style. Kevin Archer departed before the album’s release; several other members quit en masse in 1981 following a dispute with the label.

Era 2 (1982): Celtic soul — A rebuilt lineup incorporated Irish folk elements via The Emerald Express fiddle trio. Too-Rye-Ay (1982) was produced by Clive Langer and Alan Winstanley (jointly credited as Clive Langer & Alan Winstanley) alongside Rowland, and spawned “Come On Eileen” — a UK and US number one single in 1982. Violinist Helen O’Hara and her group The Emerald Express were central to this sound.

Era 3 (1985): Soul as theatre — Don’t Stand Me Down (1985) was a wilfully uncommercial record, featuring long track runtimes, spoken-word passages, and a jazz-influenced orchestration. Produced solely by Rowland (with Alan Winstanley recording), it was critically dismissed on release but is now regarded as a cult classic.

Revival (2012–present) — Rowland revived the band as Dexys, releasing One Day I’m Going To Soar (2012) and The Feminine Divine (2023), with Mick Talbot (ex-Style Council) on keyboards.

Don’t Stand Me Down — Reissue History

1997 Creation Records reissue

Licensed to Creation Records in 1997. Two track titles were changed on this edition:

  • “Knowledge of Beauty” → “My National Pride”
  • “Listen to This” → “I Love You (Listen to This)”

Rowland later said the Creation reissue was mastered with a stereo enhancer that “ruined the dynamics.” No additional tracks. Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-27)

2002 Director’s Cut (EMI)

Released by EMI in 2002. Remastered without the stereo enhancer from the 1997 edition — Rowland’s stated intent was to restore the sound “as it was intended to sound.” A limited edition CD/DVD set was also issued (2-disc: CD + DVD with three music videos directed by Jack Hazan).

Era 1 Walkout — Timeline (1980–81)

The original Dexys lineup fractured in two stages following the failure of the single “Keep It Part Two (Inferiority Part One),” which was released in November 1980 (November 14 per jazzrocksoul.com — source).

Stage 1: The five who formed The Bureau

Five members quit collectively as a result of the single’s failure and ongoing frustration with Rowland’s autocratic leadership and refusal to engage the music press. Wikipedia and Trouser Press both describe this as happening “at the end of 1980.” The Bureau’s own Wikipedia article states the band was “formed in November 1980 in Birmingham, England, when the original line-up of Dexys Midnight Runners split up” — placing the collective departure in November 1980. (The Bureau Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-16; Trouser Press)

The five who left collectively to form The Bureau were:

Pete WilliamsBass
Geoff BlytheTenor saxophone

Mick TalbotKeyboardsEx-Merton Parkas; had only recently joined Dexys

In December 1980, The Bureau added Rob Jones, Tony Bischoff, and Archie Brown (from a group called The Upset). (The Bureau Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-16)

Caveat on “November 7, 1980”: One search result and a fan wiki cite this specific date for the split. This date has not been verified against a primary source (interview, contemporary press report, or book). Treat with caution until confirmed — it may be propagated from a single unverified wiki edit.

Stage 2: Kevin Archer’s separate departure

Wikipedia is explicit that Archer and Jim Paterson both remained with Rowland briefly after the five quit, and that Archer’s departure was a second, separate event: “Archer and Paterson both remained with Rowland at first, but then Archer also decided to leave.” (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-16)

Most sources describe Archer’s departure as “early 1981.” The specific month is not confirmed in any reliable source found. A Kevin Archer Wikipedia article says he left “in early 1981.” (Kevin Archer Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-16)

After leaving, Archer formed The Blue Ox Babes (with Andy Leek, a former Dexys keyboard player who had left even earlier, reportedly because he “didn’t want to be famous” following “Geno“‘s success in 1980).

Summary of sequence

  1. “Keep It Part Two” released ~November 1980, flops.
  2. Blythe, Spooner, Williams, Growcott, Talbot quit collectively — November 1980 per The Bureau’s formation date.
  3. Archer stays on briefly, then departs separately — early 1981, exact month unknown.
  4. Rowland and Jim Paterson alone remain as founding members; Rowland refers to them as “the Celtic soul brothers.”
  5. Rowland’s March 1981 NME/press ad states previous members “hatched a plot to throw Kevin out and still carry on under the same name.”

Individual departure dates for each of the five (Blythe, Spooner, Williams, Growcott, Talbot separately) do not exist in the public record — they departed as a single collective act in November 1980, not on individual dates.
