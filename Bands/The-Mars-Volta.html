The Mars Volta

Discogs ID: 96218
Also known as: Burnt The Dipthongs
Origin: El Paso, Texas → Los Angeles, California
Formed: 2001
Broke up: January 2013
Reunited: June 2022
Core: Omar Rodríguez-López (guitar, producer) + Cedric Bixler-Zavala (vocals, lyrics)
Labels: Gold Standard Laboratories (2002–2007) → Universal Records (2007–2010) → Rodriguez Lopez Productions / Warner Bros. (2012) → Clouds Hill (2022–present)

Post-hardcore/progressive rock band formed from the dissolution of At The Drive-In. Omar Rodríguez-López and Cedric Bixler-Zavala were the core duo throughout; the band also co-owned Gold Standard Laboratories from 2001, making it effectively their vehicle. Musically ranged from angular post-hardcore (Tremulant EP) to prog-rock maximalism with Latin, jazz, and electronic elements (De-Loused, Frances The Mute) to a stripped-down acoustic approach (Octahedron) to a pop-accessible reunion album (The Mars Volta, 2022).

Biography

Origins: At The Drive-In and El Paso

Omar Rodríguez-López was born September 1, 1975 in Bayamón, Puerto Rico; Cedric Bixler-Zavala was born November 4, 1974 in Redwood City, California. Both grew up in El Paso, Texas, where they met in the early 1990s through mutual friend Paul Hinojos. At The Drive-In (ATDI) formed in El Paso in 1993, with the canonical lineup — Cedric (vocals), Omar (guitar), Jim Ward (guitar), Paul Hinojos (bass), Tony Hajjar (drums) — solidifying by 1996.

Jeremy Michael Ward (born May 5, 1976, Fort Worth TX) was a cousin of Jim Ward’s; he contributed to ATDI and in 1998 joined the experimental dub reggae project De Facto (2) with Cedric and Omar. When ATDI disbanded in 2001, Cedric and Omar formed The Mars Volta; Jim Ward, Hinojos, and Hajjar continued as Sparta.

Pre-TMV, De Facto released Megaton Shotblast (GSL45, 2001) on Gold Standard Laboratories — De Facto was the first ATDI-adjacent act to sign to GSL, before The Mars Volta presented their debut.

2002–2003: GSL Debut, Jeremy Ward’s Death

Jeremy Michael Ward (sound manipulation, electronics, vocals) was a founding member of The Mars Volta. He performed with a tabletop rig of guitar pedals, effects, and small electronic instruments, creating abstract soundscapes and processing live vocal sources. His contributions defined the band’s early textural identity.

The Tremulant EP (GSL54, 2002) — three tracks, produced by Alex Newport — was the debut release. Lineup: Cedric (vocals), Omar (guitar/producer), Jeremy Ward (electronics), Jon Theodore (drums), Isaiah “Ikey” Owens (keyboards), Eva Gardner (bass).

Ward died May 25, 2003 of a heroin overdose in Los Angeles, age 27 — weeks before De-Loused In The Comatorium was released. His death prompted Cedric and Omar to quit opioids. Ward left a lasting posthumous imprint: a journal he had found in a repossessed car later formed the lyrical basis for Frances The Mute; he had invented the word “Amputechture” (TMV’s third album title); Omar collected their archival collaborations and released them in 2008.

2003: De-Loused In The Comatorium

De-Loused In The Comatorium (GSL75, 2003) was the band’s debut LP. Concept album about “Cerpin Taxt” — a character drawn from the life and death of Julio Venegas (1972–1996), a close El Paso friend who committed suicide at 24. Jeremy Ward had co-written a Cerpin Taxt short story with Cedric; GSL published the illustrated text.

Produced by Rick Rubin. Art direction and cover design: Storm Thorgerson (Pink Floyd designer). Graphics: Peter Curzon. Illustration: Dan Abbott (2). Mixed by Rich Costey. Recorded by David Schiffman and Andrew Scheps.

Key credits: John Frusciante (guitar + A100 effects on “Cicatriz ESP”), Flea (trumpet), Justin Meldal-Johnsen (double bass on “Televators”), Lenny Castro (additional percussion). Jeremy Ward appears on the album: it was largely completed before his death.

10 tracks, 2LP. Singles: “Inertiatic ESP” (GSL/Universal, 2003), “Televators” (Erika Records, 2003).

2004–2005: Frances The Mute

Frances The Mute (GSL95, 2005) — concept album based on Jeremy Ward’s repossessed-car journal. Sprawling double-LP; four long movements plus “The Widow.” Produced by Omar. Art direction: Storm Thorgerson, Peter Curzon. Mixed: Rich Costey. New members: Juan Alderete de la Pena (bass), Adrian Terrazas (woodwinds); Marcel Rodriguez-Lopez (Omar’s brother) joins in a peripheral role. John Frusciante contributes. Massive orchestral and Latin band augmentations on several tracks. Singles: “The Widow” (Universal, 2004/2005), “L’Via L’Viaquez” (Universal Europe, 2005).

Scabdates (GSL118, 2005) — live album, documenting the De-Loused / Frances The Mute touring era.

2006: Amputechture

Amputechture (GSL126, 2006) — the title was Jeremy Ward’s invention. 8 tracks, double-LP. Produced by Omar; engineered by Jon Debaun, Paul Fig, Robert Carranza; mixed by Rich Costey. John Frusciante is a full credited performer; Marcel Rodriguez-Lopez and Paul Hinojos join the performing lineup. Sonny Kay layout. Last release on Gold Standard Laboratories (GSL closed October 29, 2007 as TMV moved to Universal).

2007–2010: Universal Era

The Bedlam In Goliath (Universal, 2008) — Thomas Pridgen on drums. Omar produced; Lars Stalfors and Robert Carranza engineered. Rich Costey mixed. Concept album allegedly inspired by a Ouija board Omar found in Jerusalem. Includes bonus DVD with covers (Siouxsie & The Banshees, Sugarcubes, Soft Machine, Nick Drake, Circle Jerks). Singles: “Wax Simulacra,” “Goliath.”

Octahedron (Mercury Records, 2009) — stripped down; Thomas Pridgen drums; John Frusciante performs. Lars Stalfors engineered; Omar produced and mixed; Howie Weinberg mastered. 8 tracks. Notable acoustic approach relative to earlier work.

2012: Noctourniquet and Dissolution

Noctourniquet (Rodriguez Lopez Productions / Warner Bros., 2012) — Deantoni Parks on drums; Lars Stalfors recorded/mixed; Heba Kadry mastered. Sonny Kay returned for artwork and design. 13 tracks.

January 23, 2013: Cedric announced he was no longer part of the band and that it had broken up. Cedric and Omar subsequently formed Antemasque (with Flea and Travis Barker, 2014–15).

Landscape Tantrums (Rodriguez Lopez Productions, 2021) — posthumous release of the unfinished original recordings of De-Loused In The Comatorium.

2022–present: Reunion

TMV reunited June 2022. The Mars Volta (Clouds Hill CH300, 2022) — self-titled reunion LP. 14 tracks. Lineup: Omar, Cedric, Marcel Rodriguez-Lopez, Eva Gardner (bass), Willy Rodriguez (3); additional musicians Leo Genovese, Daniel Diaz Rivera. Mixed by Marcel; lacquer cut by Heino Leja. New pop-accessible direction relative to the prog era.

Que Dios Te Maldiga Mi Corazon (Clouds Hill, 2023) — follow-up release. Lucro Sucio; Los Ojos Del Vacio (Clouds Hill, 2025) — most recent release.
