The Flamin' Groovies

The Flamin’ Groovies

Discogs ID: 284145
Origin: San Francisco, California, USA
Active: 1965–1991; reformed 2013–present
Genres: Garage Rock, Rock & Roll, Power Pop, Punk (proto)

Overview

The Flamin’ Groovies are a San Francisco rock band whose career spans six decades across two distinct eras and several reformations. Active from 1965, they developed a raw, roots-inflected rock & roll style that predated and then ran parallel to the punk explosion — arriving too early for the first wave, then being rediscovered mid-career by the UK punk generation who recognised them as forebears.

The band exists in two broadly distinct phases:

  1. The Roy Loney era (1965–1971): a rough, high-energy quintet drawing on Chuck Berry, Stones, and blues; documents the transition from garage rock to something rawer on Teenage Head (1971).
  2. The Cyril Jordan/Chris Wilson era (1972–1991): a more melodic, British Invasion-influenced outfit, culminating in the Sire Records albums produced by Dave Edmunds; the song “Shake Some Action” (1976) became their signature track and was cited by the Ramones as a direct influence.

The 2013 reunion brought back the 1970s nucleus of Cyril Jordan, George Alexander (2), and Chris Wilson (3) for a further active phase, including the album Fantastic Plastic (2017).

Eras

1965–1971: Origins and the Roy Loney Lineup

Formed in San Francisco around 1965 as the Chosen Few, the band settled on The Flamin’ Groovies by the late 1960s. The core lineup in this period: Roy Loney (vocals), Cyril Jordan (guitar), Tim Lynch (guitar, harmonica), George Alexander (2) (bass, harmonica), and Danny Mihm (drums).

The self-released Sneakers EP (1968, A Snazz Recording) is the label debut — reportedly produced by the band themselves, limited pressing. Epic Records signed them for Supersnazz (1969), a high-energy debut LP that went nowhere commercially. Flamingo followed in 1970 on Kama Sutra, and Teenage Head (1971) is the peak of this era: a blues-and-Stones-inflected LP produced by rock critic Richard Robinson, with Jim Dickinson on piano for the sessions. The title track became their most enduring composition from this period.

Roy Loney departed around 1971, ending the first chapter.

1972–1975: Rockfield and the Skydog Years

Post-Loney, Cyril Jordan and Chris Wilson (3) took over as the dual-guitar, dual-vocal core. Mike Wilhelm (an early member) played on some sessions. This transitional phase produced the United Artists singles “Slow Death” / “Married Woman” (1972), recorded at Rockfield Studios in Wales — sessions engineered by Dave Edmunds, beginning a key working relationship. The French label Skydog issued the Grease compilation (1973) and “Alive Forever! (More Grease)” (1974), building a cult following in France and Europe. “You Tore Me Down” (1974) was released on Greg Shaw (2)‘s BOMP! label, foreshadowing the later Sire deal.

1976–1981: Sire Records and the Shake Some Action Peak

Signing to Sire Records brought the band’s most commercially visible and critically lauded records. Dave Edmunds produced both Shake Some Action (1976) and Now (1978) at Rockfield, stamping the records with his thick, compressed guitar sound. The title track “Shake Some Action” — co-written by Jordan and Wilson — became a power pop standard; the Ramones covered it and cited the Groovies publicly as an influence. A Sire promotional EP in 1977 paired them with the Ramones, Talking Heads, and the Dead Boys — an unlikely pairing that confirmed their status as proto-punk touchstones in New York circles.

Jumpin’ In The Night (1979) was produced by Roger Bechirian, with “Absolutely Sweet Marie” (1979 single) featuring sleeve design by Barney Bubbles. The lineup for the Sire years: Jordan, Wilson, George Alexander (bass), David Wright (drums), with Mike Wilhelm returning for Now.

Activity declined through 1980–81; the band played its last gig of this phase around 1981.

1983–1991: Sporadic Activity and Final Phase

Various low-key releases: Bucketful Of Brains (1983, Voxx Records), live recordings on Australian labels, and occasional singles. One Night Stand (1986, ABC (9)) and Step Up (1991) represent the tail end of the original run. Jack Johnson (3) and Paul Zahl joined in later configurations.

2013–Present: Reunion

The 2013 reunion centred on Jordan, Alexander, and Wilson. 2016 brought “Crazy Macy” on Burger Records. Fantastic Plastic (2017) was the first full studio album since the early 1980s, released on Sonic Kicks Records / Premium Otis Recordings (Italy).
