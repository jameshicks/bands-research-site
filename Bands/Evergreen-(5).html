Evergreen (5)

Early 1990s punk/hardcore band from Louisville, Kentucky. Discogs ID: 1025688. Also recorded and released music as Cinderblock (3) (Discogs ID: 4260823). Active c. 1992–1996.

Released two records as Cinderblock (3) on Self-Destruct Records (1992–1993), a 7” EP and an LP as Evergreen (5) on Hi-Ball Records (1994, 1996), and a 2009 CD retrospective compiling the early material on Noise Pollution.

Overview

Evergreen (5) / Cinderblock (3) operated on the fringes of the Louisville underground scene at a moment when the post-Slint, post-Squirrel Bait diaspora was dispersing into new configurations. The band’s primary recording identity before 1994 was Cinderblock (3); they appear to have renamed themselves Evergreen (5) for the Hi-Ball years. The lineup shifted slightly between the two phases — Cinderblock (3) credits show Pollard (vocals), Tucker (drums), Ruth (guitar), and Cox (bass), while the 1994 Evergreen 7” adds Sean McLoughlin (vocals) and brings Britt Walford in on drums. The 1996 LP is the fullest statement: all six members credited, Walford playing drums and keyboards but listed without credit on the pressing.

Stylistically the records move from indie rock (the 1992 EP) through punk (the 1993 cassette) to garage rock / lo-fi (the Hi-Ball 7” and LP). The LP was recorded by James Murphy (later of LCD Soundsystem) and Nicolas Vernhes (later the engineer associated with Animal Collective, Deerhunter, and others) — an early session for both.

Hi-Ball Records Context

Hi-Ball Records (ID: 61813) was a 1990s indie label from Chicago, Illinois. Its catalog of 11 releases (in the Discogs database) is dominated by The Coctails (the Chicago pop-jazz group featuring Archer Prewitt) and bookended by Evergreen (5) and solo Prewitt records. Archer Prewitt’s cover illustration on the Evergreen 7” is the link: Prewitt was a Coctails member and had an ongoing relationship with Hi-Ball through the mid-1990s. The label never released a King Kong (3) 7” — that connection was incorrect in the initial brief. King Kong (3)‘s records are predominantly on Drag City and self-releases.

Hi-Ball catalog highlights:

  • The Coctails — Hip Hip Hooray! (1990), Here Now Today (1991), Songs For Children (1991), Long Sound (1993), Peel (1994)
  • Evergreen (5) — Pants Off / The Queen Song 7” (1994), Evergreen LP (1996)
  • Archer Prewitt — In The Sun (1997), White Sky (1999)

Notable Production Connections

  • James Murphy (ID: 153534) — recorded the 1996 LP. This is an early recording credit for Murphy, who went on to found LCD Soundsystem and The DFA. Also in Pony (2) and Falling Man (3) during this period.
  • Nicolas Vernhes (ID: 266862) — co-recorded the 1996 LP. Vernhes later built Rare Book Room studio in Brooklyn and became the go-to engineer for Animal Collective, Deerhunter, War on Drugs, and many others. An early session for him as well.
  • Archer Prewitt (ID: 239475) — cover illustration on the 1994 7”. Prewitt was a member of The Coctails (Chicago) and later The Sea And Cake. His involvement links Evergreen (5) to Hi-Ball’s primary roster.
  • Mike Bucayu (ID: 1413980) — produced both Self-Destruct Records releases (1992 EP and 1993 cassette). Also later associated with Maurice (51) alongside Britt Walford and Brian McMahan.
  • Howie Gano (ID: 323888) — engineered both Self-Destruct releases.

Open Questions

  • The Discogs description says “Early ’90s punk/hardcore” but the Hi-Ball records lean garage rock and lo-fi — unclear whether the name change to Evergreen (5) reflected a stylistic shift or was driven by other factors (existing band named Cinderblock elsewhere?).
  • Dave Pollard’s absence from the 1996 LP credits: whether he left before the LP was recorded, or simply went uncredited, is not documented in Discogs.
  • Matt Tucker (4)‘s absence from the Hi-Ball releases: he is in the Evergreen (5) Discogs member list but not credited on either Hi-Ball record. Britt Walford drummed on both — Tucker may have departed before the Hi-Ball phase.
  • The 2009 Wholeness Of The Soul CD on Noise Pollution has no personnel credits in Discogs. Unclear who compiled it or authorized the release, and whether it includes any previously unreleased material beyond the 21 known tracks.
  • Whether the 1993 cassette (Go Cart Ride) was commercially distributed or purely a self-release; Self-Destruct Records (ID: 129123) context not fully explored here.
  • James Murphy’s connection to Louisville or how he came to record the Evergreen LP in 1996 is not documented in Discogs.
