Imperial Drag

Los Angeles glam-pop band, active 1994–1997. Discogs artist ID: 352438.

Formed by Roger Joseph Manning Jr. and Eric Dover in the immediate aftermath of Jellyfish (2)‘s breakup. The group positioned itself within a glam/power-pop mode that ran counter to the post-grunge mainstream of the mid-1990s. They released one album on The Work Group / Columbia Records (1996), scored a minor modern rock chart hit with “Boy or a Girl” (#30 US Modern Rock), and disbanded in 1997. The band’s image and sound — fluorescent glam iconography, layered vocal harmonies, and candy-coated arena-pop production — was out of step with the grunge-defined market and did not convert critical curiosity into sales. According to Wikipedia, Rolling Stone gave the album a poor review.

History

Imperial Drag formed in 1994 as Doverman, a duo project between Eric Dover and Roger Joseph Manning Jr. following Jellyfish’s dissolution (the name “Doverman” is referenced in Wikipedia’s Eric Dover article as the transitional project name before they added members and renamed). Dover had briefly left to record vocals for Slash’s Snakepit’s debut album It’s Five O’Clock Somewhere (1995, Geffen) before returning to rejoin Manning. The group was completed by drummer Eric Skodis and bassist Joseph Karnes.

Notably, Dover and Manning appeared together as part of Davy Jones’ band in the 1995 film The Brady Bunch Movie (per Wikipedia/Eric Dover) — a detail not captured in Discogs.

The band recorded a pre-album demo tape, Demolotion (1994, Beaten Bitch label, cat# Jelly-004), which circulated on CDr. This unofficial release contains early versions of several tracks that would appear on the album, including “Spyder,” “Dandelion,” “Scaredy Cats & Egomaniacs,” and “Breakfast By Tiger” — suggesting the band’s core repertoire was in place well before their Columbia signing.

After signing to The Work Group (a Columbia imprint), they recorded their self-titled album with producer Brad Jones (2), who also engineered and mixed the record alongside Manning. The album was released in 1996 and issued across multiple territories (UK, Europe, US) with slightly different catalog numbers on both Columbia and Work labels.

The single “Boy or a Girl” charted at #30 US Modern Rock Tracks and #54 in the UK. The band disbanded in 1997, the same year several post-Jellyfish projects (The Moog Cookbook, the Umajets recording) were also winding down.

Manning and Dover later reunited as The Lickerish Quartet (2017–2022), alongside Tim Smith (5) (the Jellyfish bassist).
