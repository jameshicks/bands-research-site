Overview

The Van Pelt were a New York emo/indie rock band active from 1993 to 1997, distinguished by abstract lyrics and a sound rooted more in art rock than in the hardcore scenes their members came from. After disbanding, Chris Leo and a bandmate named Toko founded The Lapse, while Brian Maryansky went on to join Jets to Brazil in 1998. The band staged reunion shows in subsequent years and eventually released a fourth studio album, Artisans & Merchants, in March 2023. Their catalogue spans the 1990s through the 2020s, with primary label relationships at Gern Blandsten in the early years and La Castanya and Ernest Jenning Record Co. in their later period; recurring collaborators include Alap Momin and Keith DeBlasi, alongside engineer Nicolas Vernhes.

Reunion and Artisans & Merchants (2023)

The path back to recording began with a 2009 SXSW performance on a 1990s nostalgia bill organised by a friend. Chris Leo described the experience as “a blast, but an awkward blast” and said replaying old material with fresh perspective made something click: “It was clear to all of us that we were still a band and that no one would ever be allowed to quit again.” (Psychedelic Baby Magazine, Feb 2023)

A second catalyst came in 2014 when All Tomorrow’s Parties’ Jabberwocky festival in London was cancelled at short notice; bands already in the country scrambled to arrange replacement shows, and The Van Pelt performed two sold-out intimate concerts that, according to Read Dork, “reignited the group’s passion for the band.” Around the same time, Spanish label La Castanya reissued their out-of-print vinyl, introducing the records to a new generation of listeners. (Read Dork, Apr 2023)

The specific impetus for new songwriting came during the pandemic. Leo described an unusual convergence of circumstances — a one-year-old son, a newly opened wine shop that remained open, and approaching his fiftieth birthday — that created space for reflection he had not previously had: “there could have been no better time to reflect on it all in silence away from all the clutter and noise in non-pandemic America.” The band also unearthed and digitised a cache of VHS tapes from their mid-1990s US tours, which fed the album’s thematic interest in nostalgia, longing and retrospection. (Psychedelic Baby Magazine, Feb 2023; thoughtswordsaction.com, Jan 2023)

The band framed the album explicitly as not a reunion record: Leo told Read Dork, “If we moved at a more rapid pace, that fear would probably have been there…But right now, we’re all so defeated as human beings…that I don’t think anything could get in the way.” Press coverage at the time characterised the release as vindication of their original refusal to chase commercial success, rather than nostalgia-driven cash-in. (Read Dork, Apr 2023)

The album was announced in December 2022 via Instagram and released in March 2023 on Ernest Jenning Record Co. / La Castanya / Gringo Records (2).
