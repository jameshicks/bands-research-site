At The Drive-In

Discogs ID: 81742
Also known as: ATDI, ATDI
Origin: El Paso, Texas
Formed: 1993
Hiatus: 2001–2012
Labels: Western Breed Records (1994–1995), Flipside Records (1997), Onefoot Records (1997), Fearless Records (1998–1999), Grand Royal / Virgin / Interscope (2000–2001), Rise Records (2017–present)

El Paso, Texas post-hardcore band formed in 1993. One of the most critically regarded US post-hardcore acts of the 1990s, known for aggressive, kinetic live performances and albums that fused punk energy with art-rock complexity. Dissolved in 2001 after Relationship Of Command, splitting into The Mars Volta (Omar Rodríguez-López + Cedric Bixler-Zavala) and Sparta (Jim Ward + Tony Hajjar + Paul Hinojos). Reunited in 2012; resumed recording in 2016 with a revised lineup.

History

1993–1995: Formation and Early Recordings

At The Drive-In formed in El Paso, Texas in 1993. The original lineup included Cedric Bixler-Zavala (vocals), Jim Ward (guitar/vocals), Jarrett Wrenn (guitar), Kenny Hopper (bass), and Bernie Rincon (drums). The band recorded their first release, Hell Paso (WSTB 004, 1994), on the local Western Breed Records — a cassette-only compilation/demo document. A second Western Breed release followed in 1995, the 7” ¡Alfaro Vive, Carajo! (WSTB-006).

The original lineup was unstable. Multiple members cycled through guitar, bass, and drums positions between 1993 and 1997 (see Lineup History below) before the classic five-piece solidified.

1996–1997: Lineup Consolidation and Debut Album

By 1996, Omar Rodríguez-López had joined on bass (initially), stabilizing the lineup. The five-piece that recorded Acrobatic Tenement (FLIP94, 1997) — Cedric Bixler-Zavala, Omar Rodríguez-López, Jim Ward, Adam Amparan (guitar), and Ryan Sawyer (drums) — was not yet the classic lineup: Paul Hinojos and Tony Hajjar were still to come. The Discogs credits for Acrobatic Tenement list those five as the performing lineup, with Omar presumably on bass as he was the most recent addition and the classic rhythm section had not yet formed. The album was released by Flipside Records, self-produced with Blaze James and Doug Green, who also engineered. Jim Ward designed the jacket. Emberly Modine contributed the front cover painting.

The same year, the band released El Gran Orgo (OFR 20040 / OF-40, 1997) on Onefoot Records, self-produced with Bryan Jones; Mike Major engineered.

1998–1999: In/Casino/Out and Vaya

In/Casino/Out (F034, 1998) on Fearless Records is regarded as the breakthrough that brought the band wider underground attention. Alex Newport recorded and mixed the album. The lineup on In/Casino/Out added Marcelo Rodche (Angel Marcelo Rodriguez-Cheverez) on percussion. The album was mastered by John Golden.

The band also released a split 7” with The Aasee Lake (Ghetto Defendant / Nerd Rock Records, 1999; cat. no. 3) and contributed two tracks to the split At.The.Drive-In & Sunshine (BWR0223, 2000, Big Wheel Recreation).

Vaya (F040, 1999) — an EP on Fearless Records — confirmed the band’s trajectory. The performing lineup was the classic five-piece (Cedric, Jim, Omar, Pall, Tony). Mastered by John Matousek; layout by Joe D. Foster; cover photo by Rick Robot; additional photos by Paul Drake. No producer or engineer is credited on any original Vaya pressing in Discogs; Alex Newport’s occasional association with this EP is only confirmed on the 2004 V2 reissue, where he is credited as mixer. (Note: the stub erroneously listed Vaya as a GSL release; Discogs confirms Fearless Records.)

2000: Relationship Of Command and Major-Label Breakthrough

Relationship Of Command (Grand Royal / Interscope, 2000; US cat. 7243 8 49999 1 9; single “One Armed Scissor” GR091) was produced by Ross Robinson and mixed by Andy Wallace. Ross Robinson was the producer behind Korn, Slipknot, and Glassjaw, and brought a dense, compressed studio approach. The album was released on the Beastie Boys’ Grand Royal imprint distributed through Interscope, giving the band major-label reach.

Other 2000 output: the split 7” Rascuache / Press Gang (Buddyhead #3 / BH-003) with Buddyhead label; the split At The Drive-In / Burning Airlines (THK-066, Thick Records).

Singles from Relationship Of Command:

  • “One Armed Scissor” (GR091, Grand Royal, 2000)
  • “Rolodex Propaganda” (Grand Royal / Virgin, Europe, 2000)
  • “Invalid Litter Dept.” (Grand Royal / Virgin, 2001)

The UK/European releases were co-issued on Virgin.

Illustration throughout the Relationship Of Command era was by Damon Locks; layout by Jason Farrell.

2001: Split

The band dissolved in early 2001 following tension between members over musical direction. Cedric Bixler-Zavala and Omar Rodríguez-López formed The Mars Volta; Jim Ward, Tony Hajjar, and Paul Hinojos formed Sparta. A live recording from the final Japan tour — Live In Tokyo, Japan, January 18, 2001 (LH-2010-LP, Lost Horse Records, 2001) — documented the band’s final performances.

A posthumous compilation, This Station Is Non-Operational (FRL 30074-2, Fearless Records, 2005), collected rarities and B-sides.

2012: Reunion

On January 9, 2012, At The Drive-In announced a reunion. The reformed band performed at Coachella 2012 and toured extensively. The original five-piece (Bixler-Zavala, Rodríguez-López, Ward, Hinojos, Hajjar) reunited.

A 2013 remix single — “One Armed Scissor (The Field Remix)” (TRANS 161, Transgressive Records) — was released during this period.

2016–2017: Jim Ward’s Departure and in•ter a•li•a

In 2016, Jim Ward left the band, replaced by Keeley Davis (Discogs ID: 915307), guitarist from Sparta. The reformed lineup (Bixler-Zavala, Rodríguez-López, Hinojos, Hajjar, Davis) recorded two releases in 2017 on Rise Records:

  • Diamanté (2017) — produced by Omar Rodríguez-López; engineered and mixed by Johann Scheerer; mastered by Chris von Rautenkranz. Illustration by Damon Locks.
  • in•ter a•li•a (2017) — produced by Omar Rodríguez-López and Rich Costey; mixed by Rich Costey; mastered by Gentry Studer. Illustration by Damon Locks. Photography by Jùnn.
