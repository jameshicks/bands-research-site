Theoretical Girls

NYC No Wave band, formed 1977. Discogs artist ID: 125108.

Overview

Theoretical Girls were one of the most discussed bands of the late-1970s New York No Wave scene despite releasing only a single 7” during their existence. Formed in 1977 by guitarist Glenn Branca and guitarist/vocalist Jeffrey Lohn — a classically trained composer who, like Branca, had not worked with popular musical forms until inspired by the punk explosion — the quartet also included keyboardist Margaret DeWys and drummer Wharton Tiers.

Their sound drew from American minimalist composition and art-punk: sparse, clattering rhythm pieces with an abrasive, confrontational character. Discogs describes them as comparable to The Contortions and DNA — part of the same downtown Manhattan cluster — and as a direct precedent for early Sonic Youth. The songwriting was split: “U.S. Millie” was written by Lohn, “You Got Me” by Branca; on the 2002 retrospective compilation all music is credited to Lohn, with Branca, Lohn, DeWys, and Tiers listed as performers.

After dissolution, Branca formed The Static (with Barbara Ess and Christine Hahn, 1979) and then the Glenn Branca Ensemble, building toward his guitar symphony work on 99 Records and Neutral Records. Wharton Tiers became one of the central producer/engineers of the NYC underground, founding Fun City Studios and working with Sonic Youth, Dinosaur Jr., Helmet, and many others.

History

Formation and early shows (1977–1978). The band formed in 1977 in New York City. In May 1978 they played the “Bands at Artists Space” showcase alongside groups that ended up on Brian Eno’s No New York compilation — including Terminal (No Wave), Tone Death, The Contortions, DNA, Mars, and Teenage Jesus And The Jerks — but were not selected for the record. The omission has historically inflated the mystique around the band’s live reputation.

Original single (1978). The band self-released a 7” on their own imprint Theoretical Records (catalog number listed as “none” on Discogs) — one of the few self-financed No Wave singles of the period. Side A: “U.S. Millie” (written by Lohn). Side B: “You Got Me” (written by Branca). The A-side matrix etching crosses out “You Got Me” and substitutes “US MILLIE,” suggesting a late-stage track swap. Styles listed on Discogs: Art Rock, Avantgarde, New Wave, No Wave, Punk.

Dissolution and aftermath. The band broke up in 1979. One source (Amoeba Music bio) states they played their final show at Max’s Kansas City in early 1979; Branca himself says that once he heard the multiple-guitar direction “very quickly that was the end of Theoretical Girls” — consistent with his forming The Static the same year. Wikipedia and Tiny Mix Tapes date the band 1977–1981, but the active performing period ended by 1979. The 2014 Die or DIY? expanded edition and AllMusic describe the recorded material as spanning 1978–1981, suggesting some recordings may postdate the live band. [sources: Amoeba Music bio — amoeba.com; Branca/Marc Masters interview — thewire.co.uk; Wikipedia — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_Girls, as of 2026-05-22]

Branca moved immediately to The Static (1979), then launched his guitar ensemble work; Wharton Tiers became a sought-after engineer and producer. Jeffrey Lohn remained active as a solo artist (solo LP on Daisy Records, 1984; see Jeffrey Lohn). Margaret DeWys continued as a composer and sound installation artist; she joined the faculty of Bard College’s Milton Avery Graduate School of the Arts and had works performed at MoMA, the Whitney, The Kitchen, and other venues (see Margaret DeWys).

Posthumous documentation (2002/2004). In 2002 Acute Records (ACT001) released a 19-track CD retrospective, released October 15, 2002. The collection draws on studio recordings, rehearsal tapes, and live recordings spanning 1978–1981; Acute proprietor Dan Selzer spent several years working with Lohn to compile material “from poorly recorded old rehearsal tapes and live reels.” Track 1 is explicitly labelled “(Live)” and track 15 is “(Studio)”; the remaining tracks lack explicit venue attribution in available sources — specific recording dates and locations beyond “1978–1981” are not documented in accessible liner notes. The comp was produced and designed by Dan Selzer and Todd Hyman, remastered by Joel Hamburger and Sean McFaul. A Japanese edition was issued in 2004 on the same catalog number with liner notes in Japanese (liner notes by 鈴木丈). The 2002 compilation accounts for both of Theoretical Girls’ Discogs entries (years_active span: 1978–2004). [sources: Bandcamp page — theoreticalgirls.bandcamp.com; Acute Records blog — acuterecords.com/blog/?page_id=230; AllMusic]

No New York — Exclusion Accounts

Theoretical Girls played the same May 1978 “Bands at Artists Space” showcases as the four bands selected for Brian Eno’s No New York compilation (Mars, DNA, Teenage Jesus and the Jerks, The Contortions) but were not included. Two divergent first-person accounts exist:

Glenn Branca (interview with Marc Masters, The Wire): “The problem was it seems that Eno had personally met the people on the East Village side, and they convinced him that the idea of four bands was better.” Branca notes Eno attended a Theoretical Girls show at The Kitchen before finalising the record, concluding: “I have to assume that he just didn’t like us.” He also speculates that Eno “might have seen us as more direct competition with him personally than any of the other bands” — partly because the band had circulated a rumour they were writing a song about Eno, which may have been what brought him to the Kitchen show. [source: thewire.co.uk/in-writing/interviews/glenn-branca-interview-with-marc-masters]

Jeffrey Lohn: Lohn claimed direct responsibility, stating that Eno “had some interest in putting us on the No New York compilation” but that word reached Eno that Lohn “was absolutely not interested in working with him” — Lohn objected to what he characterised as Eno’s pretentious art-rock aesthetic. The Lohn quote is reported in Thurston Moore & Byron Coley, No Wave: Post-Punk. Underground. New York. 1976–1980 (Abrams Image, 2008), p. [page unconfirmed]; also circulated via drivingmebackwards.wordpress.com (2022-02-15).

Geographic/aesthetic factor noted in criticism (Adele Bertei; Marc Masters, No Wave, 2007): Theoretical Girls were SoHo-based, while the four selected bands were East Village/Lower East Side. The two camps were socially distinct and there was “almost a war between the two sides” in Branca’s words.

The two first-person accounts are not mutually exclusive — Lohn’s disinterest and Eno’s preference for the East Village bands may both have been operative. No documentation of Eno’s own account has been located.

Open Questions

  • Conflict: Branca and most retrospectives give active period as ending 1979; Wikipedia/Tiny Mix Tapes say 1977–1981; the Acute comp material spans 1978–1981. Whether recordings after 1979 were made by the full band or represent solo/reduced-lineup sessions is unconfirmed.
  • Who is “Mikey (13)” credited on “Keyboard Etude”? Not identified in any source found.
  • Specific recording dates, venues, and studio vs. live breakdown for individual tracks on the 2002 comp — liner notes are not accessible online; only track 1 (Live) and track 15 (Studio) are explicitly labelled.
  • Jeffrey Lohn’s Discogs entry lists activity 1978–2002 but only one solo LP (Music From Paradise, 1984) is confirmed documented; any releases between 1984 and 2002 are unverified.
