Deltron 3030

Discogs ID: 27507
Active: 1999–present (intermittent)
Labels: 75 Ark (debut), Bulk Recordings (sequel)
Genre: Hip-hop, concept

Hip-hop supergroup built around a sci-fi dystopian concept narrative set in the year 3030. Formed in San Francisco circa 1999 by three members: Del Tha Funkee Homosapien (MC, portraying the character Deltron Zero), Dan The Automator (producer, composer, arranger), and Kid Koala (turntables). The project is distinct from all three members’ solo careers — it operates as a single cohesive fictional universe across both albums.

Deltron 3030 (2000)

Label: 75 Ark (cat. 75033)
Format: CD

The debut and defining record. The concept frames Del’s rapping as the adventures of Deltron Zero, a futuristic superhero in a corporate-dystopian world. Dan the Automator produced, arranged, and mixed the entire album; Kid Koala’s turntable work is woven throughout. Mark Bell (of LFO) is credited “Music By” on six interlude/skit tracks (3, 6, 9, 11, 13, 17 — “The Fantabulous Rap Extravaganza,” “St. Catherine St.,” “New Coke,” “National Movie Review,” “Meet Cleofis Randolph the Patriarch,” “The Fantabulous Rap Extravaganza Part II”); he also remixed “Turbulence” (track 16). Mastered by Howie Weinberg.

The Gap (2000–2013)

Work on a sequel began as early as 2004. By November 2006 Del reported four songs written and the lyrical theme mapped out; Dan the Automator predicted a December 2006 finish and 2007 release. The project then stalled through a series of delays — statements about imminent completion came in 2008, 2010, 2011, and 2012. Del was candid about the hold-up: “He was waiting on me, basically” (nodding toward Automator). He said the concept wasn’t where his interest was for a decade-plus, and that when they tried to push in the mid-2000s he didn’t feel lyrically ready. Dan the Automator framed it as timing: “We had always planned to do it. Ultimately it was a matter of being in the right place at the right time.” The album’s political/topical nature added to the complexity — addressing the present was necessary before projecting the future. In 2012 the trio performed ten Event 2 tracks at the Luminato Festival with a full horn, strings, and rhythm section; the album finally appeared in September 2013.

Event 2 (2013)

Label: Bulk Recordings (cat. 0602537520411)
Format: 2xLP

The sequel album, arriving 13 years after the debut. Dan the Automator programmed, produced, and mixed; Kid Koala on turntables. Damon Albarn played keyboards and appeared on “What Is This Loneliness”; Mike Patton contributed choir/backing vocals throughout and appeared on “City Rising From The Ashes.” A much larger cast of celebrity guests — actors, musicians, chefs — populate the skit/cameo structure.
