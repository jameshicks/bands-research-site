Chalk Circle (2)

American punk/post-punk band formed 1981 in Washington DC. Disbanded 1983. Discogs ID: 2188798.

Overview

Chalk Circle formed in Washington DC in 1981 around co-founders Sharon Cheslow (guitar, vocals) and Anne Bonafede (drums), soon joined by Mary Green (guitar, vocals) and a rotating bass slot filled at various points by Jan Pumphrey, Tamera Lyndsay, and Chris Niblack. Active for roughly two years, they dissolved in 1983 without releasing a record during their lifetime.

Their sound was raw, rhythmic, and minimal — closer to post-punk or art punk than to the emerging DC hardcore scene they rubbed up against (the early Dischord circle, Minor Threat, Teen Idles). The absence of a contemporary release meant they remained largely unheard outside DC until Cheslow assembled a retrospective nearly three decades later.

Context

Chalk Circle occupied an unusual position in early DC punk: they were part of the initial wave that coalesced around the Dischord circle circa 1981, but their stripped-down, rhythmically focused art punk distinguished them from the faster, more aggressive hardcore sound that came to define the scene’s reputation. The fact that they recorded but never released during their active years left them outside the documented history for decades.

The 2011 retrospective positioned them within the DC post-punk narrative, co-released by Mississippi Records (known for archival and reissue work) and Post Present Medium (a DC-connected label with ties to the scene’s ongoing oral history). The liner notes by Don Fleming — himself a DC-to-New York crossover figure active in the indie underground since the early 1980s — provide period context.
