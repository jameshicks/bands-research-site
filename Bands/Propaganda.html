Propaganda

Oct 17, 20266 min read

Propaganda

Discogs ID: — (search needed)

West German synth-pop group formed in Düsseldorf 1982; signed to ZTT Records as the label’s very first signing in June 1983. Members included Claudia Brücken (vocals). Debut LP A Secret Wish (ZTTIQ 3, 1985) produced by Stephen Lipson. 270 Discogs releases.

Covers on A Secret Wish: “Sorry For Laughing” (Paul Haig / Malcolm Ross of Josef K); “Dream Within A Dream” (lyrics by Edgar Allan Poe). Guest performers: David Sylvian, Glenn Gregory, Ian Mosley, Steve Howe, Andy Richards. Photography and sleeve design by Anton Corbijn.

Background and Scene Context

Düsseldorf origins

Propaganda emerged from the same Düsseldorf post-punk ecosystem that had produced DAF, Die Krupps, Der Plan, and Fehlfarben — acts associated with the punk club Ratinger Hof and the Ata Tak label. Founding member Ralf Dörper came directly from Die Krupps (which he left in 1982), a pioneering EBM/industrial act. Before Die Krupps, Dörper had played in avant-garde punk band S.Y.P.H. His 1980 solo single “Eraserhead/Assault” was named single of the week in the NME, establishing his UK credibility and, per his own account, “opened many doors in UK — also for Die Krupps and later for Propaganda.” [source: mkultramagazine.com/propaganda-die-krupps-interview, as of 2026-09-27]

Michael Mertens joined from the Düsseldorf Symphony Orchestra; Claudia Brücken and Susanne Freytag had previously performed together in synth-pop duo The Topolinos. [source: https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/xpropaganda-interview/]

Relationship to Neue Deutsche Welle (NDW)

Propaganda formed at the tail end of the NDW wave (1982) and are occasionally listed among NDW acts alongside Trio, Palais Schaumburg, Der Plan, and X-Mal Deutschland. However, their sonic ambitions and international orientation placed them outside the NDW mainstream. One source describes them explicitly as “the sophisticated end of German electronic music rather than NDW proper.” [source: https://www.cianciodj.it/eg/propaganda.html] The Düsseldorf scene from which they came — rooted in Ata Tak and the Ratinger Hof — was the more art-oriented, industrial-leaning wing of what would become NDW, as opposed to the commercial Nena/Falco end of the movement. Ralf Dörper, in a later interview, positioned Düsseldorf alongside Sheffield and Detroit as a city that “created its own electronic signature,” naming DAF, Die Krupps, La Düsseldorf, Pyrolator, and Der Plan as genuine innovators rather than acts merely copying English trends. [source: https://www.ox-fanzine.de/interview/propaganda-3936, as of 2026-09-27]

There is no evidence in available sources that band members explicitly identified with or rejected the NDW label; the question of their NDW positioning is largely framed by critics and historians rather than the band themselves.

The ZTT signing: how it happened

The path to ZTT was a specific chain of events rather than a deliberate choice of UK over German labels. The initial three-piece (Dörper, Susanne Freytag, artist Andreas Thein) recorded a German-language cover of Throbbing Gristle’s “Discipline” (titled “Disziplin”) as a demo in early 1983. Andreas Thein knew a journalist who knew Paul Morley; the cassette was passed to Morley, who passed it to Trevor Horn. Morley told Horn: “I’d like that to be my first signing.” [source: https://www.classicpopmag.com/2021/03/propaganda-do-you-want-to-know-a-secret/, Claudia Brücken quote, as of 2026-09-27]

Horn has explained his own motivation: “Paul came up with this idea. I was such a fan of Kraftwerk and Germany was kind of like the home of techno, so Paul thought it would be an interesting idea to sign a German band.” [source: https://www.classicpopmag.com/2021/03/propaganda-do-you-want-to-know-a-secret/, Trevor Horn quote, as of 2026-09-27] The initiative came from the label side, not from the band choosing ZTT over German alternatives. Claudia Brücken was recruited afterwards: “They needed a musician because at the time it was just heavy beats and dark, heavy synth sounds…for some musicality they needed a singer.” [source: same]

Propaganda were ZTT’s first ever signing, June 1983 — predating Frankie Goes to Hollywood and Art of Noise. [source: https://www.universalmusic.ca/press-releases/propaganda-a-secret-sense-of-rhythm-a-secret-sense-of-sin-the-definitive-ztt-collection/]

Germany vs. UK reception

Propaganda’s provocative imagery — described by one source as “fascist aesthetics” (stark black-and-white visuals, monumental references, the band name itself) — was more readily absorbed in the UK art-pop context than in Germany. One source states this was “a key reason why Great Britain quickly became Propaganda’s point of reference.” [source: https://laut.de/Propaganda, as of 2026-09-27] The tension was concrete: when the 12-inch of “p:Machinery” included a JG Ballard quote praising the German Red Army Faction (inserted by Paul Morley), Ariola — ZTT’s German distributor — refused to carry the release. The German pressing substituted a different Ballard quote (on the aesthetic perfection of German suburbs). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_(band), as of 2026-09-27]

Ralf Dörper has noted that the British music weeklies (NME, Melody Maker) wielded extraordinary power in the 1980s, and that British audiences were “fascinated by German language melody and speech patterns,” while also noting that visual presentation was equally important — these acts “definitely didn’t look English.” [source: https://www.ox-fanzine.de/interview/propaganda-3936, as of 2026-09-27]

  • What specific venue or context did Dörper’s 1980 solo single “Eraserhead/Assault” appear on — was it on a German independent label (Rondo?) and if so, which?
