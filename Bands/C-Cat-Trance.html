C Cat Trance

Sep 28, 20266 min read

C Cat Trance

From: Nottingham, England Active: 1983–1988 (original run); 2017 (remix releases) Labels: Red Flame (1983), Ink Records (1984–1988) Discogs artist ID: 25477 Name variations: C-Cat Trance, C.Cat Trance

Overview

C Cat Trance were a Nottingham post-punk / experimental band formed in the early 1980s by John Rees Lewis following his departure from Medium Medium. Where Medium Medium were a tightly wound funk-punk outfit, C Cat Trance pushed further into world music territory, grafting Middle Eastern and Turkish melodic influences, African percussion, and ethnographic instrumentation onto a post-punk funk chassis. The band’s name variations appear in the Discogs database as “C-Cat Trance” and “C.Cat Trance.”

Their debut was a self-titled cassette EP (1983, self-released), followed immediately by the mini-LP also titled C Cat Trance on Red Flame (cat. RFM 19, 1983), which includes their most-licensed track “Hypnotised” — a mid-tempo trance-funk piece co-written with Brill Building writers Boris Midney and Sandy Linzer. From 1984 onward all releases appeared on the Red Flame sublabel Ink Records. The core discography runs to two studio LPs (Khamu (She Sleep Walks), 1984; Zouave, 1986), one mini-LP (Play Masenko Combo, 1987), and a string of 12” singles. The band went quiet after the Jinniyya 12” (1988).

Reissue interest surfaced in 2005 when Cherry Red compiled Karadara (The Cream Of C Cat Trance), a sixteen-track CD retrospective. In 2017, Emotional Rescue (with Israeli label Malka Tuti) issued two 12” remix EPs, Screaming Remixes Vol. 1 and Vol. 2, recruiting artists including JD Twitch and Autarkic. “Hypnotised” was licensed for the 2021 Cherry Red box set Shake The Foundations (Militant Funk & The Post-Punk Dancefloor 1978-1984).

John Rees Lewis died in June 2024.

The K Kittens

A closely related vocal group who appear on multiple C Cat Trance releases from Zouave (1986) onward. Their Discogs member list (ID: 1005387) overlaps substantially with C Cat Trance: John Rees Lewis, Kev ‘Kif’ Sanderson, The Curiously Named Dave Walker, and Trev ‘Fury Sofia’ Naylor. Credited as backing vocalists and voice/chants.

Connections

  • Medium Medium — predecessor band; John Rees Lewis was frontman. Both bands from Nottingham. Nigel Kingston Stone also appears in Medium Medium’s Discogs member list.
  • John Rees Lewis — founder, primary songwriter, multi-instrumentalist; died June 2024
  • Red Flame — issued the debut mini-LP (1983)
  • Ink Records — Red Flame sublabel; home of all subsequent original releases (1984–1988)
  • Cherry Red — issued the 2005 retrospective Karadara; also home of Medium Medium’s Hungry, So Angry single (1981) and The Glitterhouse LP (1981)
  • Tony Harris — remix/mix engineer on Shake The Mind (1986) and Ishta Bil Habul! (1987); Tony Harris page documents his broader work for Red Flame / Ink Records
  • Shake The Foundations (Militant Funk & The Post-Punk Dancefloor 1978-1984) — 2021 Cherry Red box set that includes “Hypnotised”
  • JD Twitch — remixed “Dalbouka” for Screaming Remixes Vol. 1 (2017)

Research Queue

  • Was “Hypnotised” originally credited to C Cat Trance as writers, or was it an arrangement of the Midney/Linzer composition “Dangling On A String”? The Discogs tracklist credits Midney and Linzer as Written-By on “Hypnotised” — confirm whether this is a cover/rework or a coincidence of credits.
  • Confirm the recording studio for Khamu (She Sleep Walks) (1984): matrix runout reads “TOWNHOUSE AS ABOVE / SO BELOW” — does this refer to Townhouse Studios in London, and is that confirmed in liner notes?
  • What was Nigel Kingston Stone’s role in Medium Medium specifically — the Medium Medium Discogs page lists him as a member but his primary instruments (percussion, illustration) suggest a different function than the core band. Confirm via a contemporary press source or interview.
  • Did the band tour or perform live during the 1984–1988 period? No live releases appear in Discogs for C Cat Trance (unlike Medium Medium, who have a Live In Holland release on Trance Records).
  • What is the connection between C Cat Trance and Hassan Erraji — was he brought in specifically for Play Masenko Combo or did he collaborate with Lewis/Stone on other projects? Erraji has his own Discogs entry (841602) suggesting broader activity.
