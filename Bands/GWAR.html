GWAR

Richmond, Virginia shock-metal / heavy metal collective, formed 1984 (preceded by Death Piggy, 1982–1984). Discogs ID: 251932. Known for elaborate alien-barbarian stage personas, theatrical gore-soaked live shows, satirical extreme metal, and in-house costume/prop fabrication (“Slave Pit Inc.”). Primary label home from 1992 onward: Metal Blade Records.

Biography

Origins: Death Piggy and Formation (1982–1987)

GWAR grew out of Richmond’s art-school underground. The precursor band Death Piggy was active 1982–1984; by 1984 the name GWAR and the mythology of frozen Antarctic alien monsters were established. The group developed a cosmology of stage characters — Oderus Urungus (vocals), Flattus Maximus (lead guitar), Balsac The Jaws Of Death (rhythm guitar), Beefcake The Mighty (bass), and Jizmak Da Gusha (drums) — with an extended cast of slaves, managers, and antagonists. Costumes and props were built in-house at what became known as Slave Pit Inc., the band’s workshop and production arm.

Dave Brockie (real name: David Murray Brockie, Discogs ID: 754033) was the founding vocalist as Oderus Urungus, and remained the central creative voice until his death on March 23, 2014. Don Drakulich (Discogs ID: 557639), credited as Sleazy P. Martini in character, served as a founding member involved in art, film, management, and stage design from 1985 onward.

Debut and Shimmy Disc Era (1988–1989)

The debut album Hell-O! (1988) was released on Shimmy Disc (label ID: 23561) and produced by Kramer (2) (Mark Kramer, Discogs ID: 239431), with the band assisting as co-producers and engineers. The lineup for Hell-O! included Oderus Urungus on vocals (Dave Brockie), with Beefcake The Mighty (2) on bass, Flattus Maximus (4) / Dewey Rowell on guitar, Balsac The Jaws Of Death (2) on guitar, and Nippleus Erecticus on drums, alongside a large ensemble of performers including Hunter Jackson (as Techno Destructo), David Musel (keyboards/samples), Scott Krahl, Steve Douglas (2), Rob Mosby, Lisa Harrelson, Michael Bishop (2), and Chuck Varga. RAWGWAR (1989) was a self-released cassette on the band’s own Slavepit imprint.

Metal Blade Era Begins: Scumdogs and America Must Be Destroyed (1990–1992)

Scumdogs Of The Universe (1990) marked a significant lineup consolidation and heavier sonic direction. Produced by Ron Goudie (Discogs ID: 304962), the album featured: Oderus Urungus (vocals), Beefcake The Mighty (2) (bass), Flattus Maximus (4) (lead guitar), Balsac The Jaws Of Death (rhythm guitar, now Michael Derks, Discogs ID: 828481 as BalSac The Jaws of Death), and Jizmak Da Gusha (drums, Brad Roberts (3), Discogs ID: 1268608). Joe Horner engineered.

America Must Be Destroyed (1992, Metal Blade Records) was produced and engineered by Glen Robinson (Discogs ID: 115901), who became a recurring collaborator. The full character ensemble was credited as featured performers: Oderus Urungus, Flattus Maximus, Balsac The Jaws Of Death, Beefcake The Mighty (2), Jizmak Da Gusha, and Slymenstra Hymen (Rebecca Danyell Stampe, Discogs ID: 3444814). Hunter Jackson appeared as Techno Destructo; Sleazy P. Martini (Don Drakulich) as management.

Mid-Period: Ragnarök, Carnival Of Chaos, We Kill Everything (1993–1999)

This Toilet Earth (1993), Ragnarök (1995), Carnival Of Chaos (1997), and We Kill Everything (1999) were all released on Metal Blade. Ron Goudie returned as producer on Carnival Of Chaos. This period saw ongoing lineup shifts in the Flattus Maximus and Beefcake roles:

  • Tim Harriss (Discogs ID: 557605) served as Flattus Maximus (2): 1986 and 1998–1999. Also a member of Kepone.
  • Casey Orr (Discogs ID: 402563) as Beefcake The Mighty: 1994–1997, 1999–2002, 2008–2011, 2019–present.
  • Michael Bishop (2) (Discogs ID: 772680) as Beefcake The Mighty (2): 1987–1993, 1998–1999, later returned as Blothar The Berserker (vocals) from 2014 onward. Also a member of Kepone.
  • Slymenstra Hymen (Rebecca Danyell Stampe) as vocalist/dancer: 1988–2000.
  • Man’s Ruin Records released an untitled 7” in 1998.

2001–2008: DRT Entertainment Period

Violence Has Arrived (2001) and War Party (2004) / Beyond Hell (2006) moved the band to DRT Entertainment (Discogs ID: 42534). The lineup for Lust In Space (2009, back on Metal Blade) featured Cory Smoot (Discogs ID: 757082) as Flattus Maximus (3) on guitar, engineering, mixing, and production — representing the full creative integration of the Smoot era.

Loss of Key Members and Regrouping (2011–2014)

Cory Smoot (Flattus Maximus (3)) died November 3, 2011. Dave Brockie (Oderus Urungus) died March 23, 2014. These losses prompted major lineup restructuring. Brent Purgason (Discogs ID: 3962597) joined as Pustulus Maximus (guitar) in 2012. Battle Maximus (2013) was the last album with Brockie; Glen Robinson (Snake) returned as mixer, Dave Gibson (10) and John Angelo co-produced and engineered, Howie Weinberg mastered.

After Brockie’s death, Michael Bishop (2) returned to GWAR in a new vocal role as Blothar The Berserker, debuting 2014.

Post-Brockie Era (2017–present)

The Blood Of Gods (2017) was released on Metal Blade. The New Dark Ages (2022) and The Disc With No Name (2021) appeared on Pit Records (11) (Discogs ID: 2263639), the band’s own more recent imprint. The Return Of Gor-Gor (2025) continues on Pit Records (11).

Current lineup (per Discogs): Blothar The Berserker / Michael Bishop (2) (vocals), BalSac The Jaws of Death / Michael Derks (guitar), Jizmak Da Gusha / Brad Roberts (3) (drums), Pustulus Maximus / Brent Purgason (guitar), Beefcake The Mighty / Casey Orr (bass).

Notes

  • The “Slave Pit” is GWAR’s Richmond workshop and production collective, responsible for costumes, props, and sets. Multiple Slave Pit imprints appear in Discogs: Slave Pit Inc. (ID: 398613), Slave Pit Records (ID: 528560).
  • The fictional origin story (frozen in Antarctic ice, freed by ozone-hole hairspray, rescued by Sleazy P. Martini) is integral to the band’s persona.
  • Discogs lists the alias “Death Piggy” under GWAR; Death Piggy was the pre-GWAR band name.
  • Phallus In Wonderland (1992) was a long-form VHS/film release, nominated for a Grammy for Best Music Video Long Form.
