Origin — The B-Sides

The Monochrome Set emerged from a college group called The B-Sides, which also included Stuart Goddard — who went on to become Adam Ant of Adam and the Ants. This is a founding-level connection between two very different post-punk trajectories.

Additional Connections

  • John Haney’s sister is Joy de Vivre of Crass — connecting to the anarcho-punk world
  • Bid later recorded with Scarlet’s Well during the 1998–2008 hiatus
  • The band backed Jessica Griffin’s Would-Be-Goods project (see section below)
  • Tony Potts (experimental filmmaker) — collaborator from 1979; designed stage lighting and film projections; described as “fifth member”

Jessica Griffin and Would-Be-Goods

Would-Be-Goods is a British indie pop project fronted by singer-songwriter Jessica Griffin (b. c. 1965, London), active 1987–present. The connection to The Monochrome Set is foundational at every level.

How the connection began

Griffin met Mike Alway — then at Cherry Red Records — backstage at a Monochrome Set show around 1983, when she was approximately 18 years old. AllMusic biography describes this as a “chance meeting.” They kept in touch; when Alway launched El Records (originally a Cherry Red subsidiary, later briefly under the Warner/Blanco Y Negro umbrella before returning to Cherry Red), he asked Griffin to write liner notes for él releases, then invited her to record an album of her own. Wikipedia — él Records, as of 2026-07-01, confirms Alway recruited her as part of “the best English pop writers of their generation.”

Before Would-Be-Goods

No documented music career predates Would-Be-Goods. Griffin’s own statements indicate she was not embedded in any specific scene in the early 1980s — she described herself as “out on a limb” artistically during that period, crediting her parents’ record collection (notably Françoise Hardy) rather than any contemporaneous movement for her musical formation. indiepopit.net interview. She has received pronunciation classical singing training, though she rarely employs full soprano range in Would-Be-Goods material. chickfactor interview, 2026. No prior band, group, or credited work before 1987 has been found.

Debut album — Monochrome Set as backing band

The Camera Loves Me (él Records, 1988) was recorded with members of The Monochrome Set providing the backing band, as Griffin had no band of her own at that point. Produced mainly by Keith West (tracks 1–11), with Richard Preston producing tracks 12–13. Her sister Miranda Griffin contributed songwriting and backing vocals at this stage. The album was critically acclaimed in the UK and Japan. Last.fm biography; Wikipedia — Would-Be-Goods.

The first Would-Be-Goods single (1987) was written by Simon Turner and Colin Lloyd Tucker, not Griffin. Last.fm biography.

Career break — City of London

After The Camera Loves Me, Griffin left the music world to work in the City of London (financial sector). She returned to record Mondo (Polystar/Japan, 1993), produced by Bid of The Monochrome Set and again recorded with Monochrome Set members at Moat Studios, London. After Mondo she returned to the City before coming back to music permanently in the late 1990s. Last.fm biography; indiepopit.net.

Later lineup — further Monochrome Set thread

From the mid-2000s onward, Andy Warren (bass; Monochrome Set, Adam and the Ants) joined Would-Be-Goods as a permanent member, strengthening the personnel overlap. Peter Momtchiloff (ex-Talulah Gosh, Heavenly, Marine Research, Scarlet’s Well) joined as lead guitarist in 1999 — he had previously interviewed Griffin for chickfactor 13 (2000) and later became her partner. Wikipedia — Would-Be-Goods.

The 2004 album The Morning After featured drummer Debbie Green (ex-Thee Headcoatees) and bassist Lupe Nuñez-Fernández (Pipas). Wikipedia — Would-Be-Goods.

Recent output

Griffin began writing a song a day from October 2020 (during COVID-19 lockdown), producing 173 songs total, released as EPs on Bandcamp in 2021 and compiled as The Night Life (CD, 2023). The sixth studio album Tears Before Bedtime was released on Skep Wax Records in February 2026. chickfactor interviews; [web search result, 2026].
