múm

Discogs ID: 8298
Origin: Reykjavík, Iceland
Founded: 1997
Active: 1997–present
Labels: TMT Entertainment (1998–2001) → FatCat Records (2002–2007) → Morr Music (2009–present)
Name variations: mum, MUM, Mum

Experimental and electronic pop group from Reykjavík. One of Iceland’s most internationally visible indie-electronic acts since the late 1990s. Longest-running artist on Morr Music with 51 releases spanning 2001–2025. Broadly characterised by layered electronics, treated field recordings, delicate vocal harmonies (the Valtýsdóttir twins), and a rotating cast of Reykjavík collaborators. Stylistically catalogued across IDM, ambient, glitch, leftfield, downtempo, and folk.

Name pronunciation and origin: Pronounced “moom.” Per Gunnar Örn Tynes: “It’s just a sound, that’s all.” An early interview with Icelandic paper Fókus offered a visual reading — the letters “múm” resemble two elephants nodding their trunks. No deeper meaning is claimed by the band.

Formation and early years (1997–2001)

múm formed in 1997 in Reykjavík. The four founding members — twin sisters Gyða Valtýsdóttir and Kristín Anna Valtýsdóttir (born 5 January 1982), Örvar Þóreyjarson Smárason (born 1977, also a poet/author), and Gunnar Örn Tynes (born 5 March 1979) — began releasing on the small Icelandic label TMT Entertainment from 1998.

Their 1999 debut Yesterday Was Dramatic - Today Is OK (TMT02CD) established the core aesthetic: long-form ambient-electronic pieces built around processed sounds, with orchestral guests drawn from the Reykjavík scene. Hildur Guðnadóttir (then a teenager) contributed strings to “Awake On A Train,” alongside Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson on trumpet — both would become recurring collaborators.

The debut was recorded and mixed by Finnur Björnsson and Finnur Hákonarson.

In 2001, Morr Music (Berlin) issued Please Smile My Noise Bleed (MM020), múm’s first international release — a vinyl LP mini-album comprising two original múm tracks (“On The Old Mountain Radio” and “Please Sing My Spring Reverb”) plus six remixes by fellow Morr roster artists: Styrofoam (Arne Van Petegem), B. Fleischmann, Phonem (Elliot Perkins), Christian Kleine, Arovane (Uwe Zahn), and ISAN (Antony Ryan & Robin Saville). A remix album (Remixed) also appeared on TMT Entertainment that year.

FatCat era (2002–2007)

Gyða Valtýsdóttir departed after Finally We Are No One (~2002) to return to her studies in Reykjavík. Kristín Anna Valtýsdóttir stopped working with the band from June 2005; her departure was officially announced on 23 November 2006. After both twins left, the band continued as a larger rotating collective including Ólöf Arnalds, Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson, Hildur Guðnadóttir, Samuli Kosminen, and Sigurlaug Gísladóttir (Mr. Silla).

múm signed to FatCat Records (Brighton/Glasgow) for Finally We Are No One (FATCD18, 2002), their major international breakthrough. The album introduced Samuli Kosminen — a Finnish drummer (born 1974, formerly credited as Samuli Koskinen) — who contributed “noises [banged things, scraped things on the floor and built rhythms].” Production and mixing was handled by Valgeir Sigurðsson (founder of Bedroom Community), who became a key figure in the Icelandic underground. Orri Jónsson contributed recording and “organ crackles.”

Summer Make Good (FATCD26, 2004) expanded the palette further: Ólöf Arnalds played guitar, violin, viola, xylophone and backing vocals; Adam Pierce (of Mice Parade) contributed harp; Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson played trumpet, piano [pianette], synthesizer and whistling. A choir of six Icelandic women sang on “Weeping Rock, Rock.” Mastered by Mandy Parnell.

Go Go Smear The Poison Ivy (FATCD46, 2007) closed out the FatCat period. Discogs credits list no additional personnel beyond the band — a more self-contained production than its predecessors.

During this period múm also contributed to the Icelandic music documentary Screaming Masterpiece (2005) and released The Peel Session (FatCat, 2006).

Return to Morr Music (2009–present)

múm returned to Morr Music in 2009 with two releases: Prophecies And Reversed Memories (co-released with Euphono Records) and Sing Along To Songs You Don’t Know. The 2012 compilation Early Birds (Morr) collected early material.

Smilewound (2013) appeared on Morr Music (MM124) and the A Number Of Small Things sublabel (Anost 043), with an additional limited 7” white label. The same year, múm collaborated with Kylie Minogue on the single Whistle (released on Parlophone), an unusual step into mainstream pop territory.

Sigurlaug Gísladóttir (alias Mr. Silla, also a visual artist) joined múm as a member during this period, appearing in the Discogs member list alongside the original founders.

History Of Silence (MM206, 2025) is the most recent album, continuing the Morr relationship.

Label: Morr Music | Cat#: MM206 | Released: 19 September 2025

Scene connections

múm sit at the centre of the Reykjavík experimental/indie scene of the late 1990s–2000s. Several collaborators appear across multiple Icelandic acts on Morr Music:

  • Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson also plays in Seabear and Benni Hemm Hemm
  • Örvar Þóreyjarson Smárason also in FM Belfast, Seabear, Benni Hemm Hemm
  • Hildur Guðnadóttir collaborates with Pan Sonic, Throbbing Gristle, The Knife, Jóhann Jóhannsson, Animal Collective, Sunn O))); solo as Lost In Hildurness
  • Valgeir Sigurðsson engineered/produced across the Icelandic scene; his Bedroom Community label is a sister hub
  • Kristín Anna Valtýsdóttir was previously married to Avey Tare (Animal Collective)
  • Gyða Valtýsdóttir won the 2019 Nordic Council Music Prize; solo/project as Imago
