X-Ray Spex

UK punk band formed in London in 1976. Discogs artist ID: 263825

Overview

One of the defining bands of British punk, led by vocalist Poly Styrene (Mari Elliott). Distinctive for Poly Styrene’s confrontational, anti-consumerist lyrics and the prominent saxophone of their early recordings. Lora Logic was a founding member but was fired in 1977; she was replaced by Rudi Thomson. The band’s debut LP Germfree Adolescents (EMI, 1978) is their landmark record. Disbanded after Poly Styrene left in 1979. Reformed 1991 and again 1995. Poly Styrene died 2011.

Lora Logic dismissal — circumstances (1977)

Lora Logic was dismissed from X-Ray Spex in late 1977, shortly after the band’s debut single “Oh Bondage Up Yours!” (September 1977). Multiple accounts exist, with the public explanation differing from Logic’s own account.

Official band explanation at the time: Logic needed to return to school. Poly Styrene gave this reason publicly, including in a 1997 interview: Logic “couldn’t really [tour], she had to finish her education.” Styrene also stated that during live performances Logic would “play the saxophone over all my lyrics” and she “couldn’t actually hear myself sing.” (Please Kill Me interview)

Lora Logic’s account: Logic states she was away on a family holiday to Russia when she was replaced, discovering on her return that a new saxophonist had been hired who had learned all her parts. She said she was “quite shocked when I was asked to leave” and felt “heartbroken,” stressing she wasn’t “trying to usurp” Poly Styrene’s position. In a separate interview she stated: “Poly saw that I was getting a little too much of the spotlight and I was just replaced without any notice after a year.” (punk77.co.uk; furious.com Essential Logic interview)

The Jane Suck / Sounds factor: A review by journalist Jane Suck in Sounds magazine had declared “the saxophone sound is X-Ray Spex,” foregrounding Logic’s contribution in a way that reportedly irritated Styrene. Logic had arranged all the saxophone parts for Germfree Adolescents; her arrangements were used uncredited by her replacement Rudi Thomson on the finished LP. (essential-logic.co.uk/bio)

Manager’s perspective: Logic recalled that manager Falcon Stuart had initially seen commercial potential in two women fronting the band: “Two girls together, it’s gonna be lucrative.” (Please Kill Me)

Summary: The dismissal appears to have stemmed from Poly Styrene feeling threatened by Logic’s growing prominence — the Sounds review crystallising it — combined with practical tensions on stage (Logic’s sax volume drowning Styrene’s vocals). The schooling explanation was the public face. Logic was not informed directly; she returned from holiday to find herself replaced.
