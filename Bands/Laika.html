Laika

Discogs ID: 11173
Origin: UK
Active: 1994–2003 (primary run); archival/compilation activity to 2025
Labels: Too Pure (all primary releases); American Recordings (US tour cassette, 1995)
Name variations: .Laika, Laïka

Overview

Laika was a UK post-rock and experimental band built around the songwriting and production partnership of guitarist/vocalist Margaret Fiedler and multi-instrumentalist/engineer Guy Fixsen. The duo wrote all material jointly (credited on releases as “Fiedler/Fixsen”) and shared production, engineering, and mixing duties throughout the band’s catalog. Around them rotated a consistent cast of collaborators — drummer/percussionist Lou Ciccotelli, bassist John Frenett, and woodwind player Louise Elliott appear across most of the studio albums — while the sound drew on post-rock textural layering, sampler-heavy electronics, Moog and Minimoog synthesis, vibraphone, marimba, and prepared piano.

All of Laika’s studio releases and both their major compilations appeared on Too Pure (Discogs ID: 1002), the London indie label that by the mid-1990s had become a subsidiary of Beggars Group. Their catalog spans six studio albums/EPs, one US tour release, one remix single, and two compilations. The 2003 double-CD retrospective Lost In Space - Volume One (1993–2002) is their definitive archival statement.

Biography by Era

Formation and Debut (1994)

Laika formed in the UK around 1993–1994. Their debut 7” 44 Robbers / Coming Down Glass (Too Pure, 1994) introduced the Fiedler/Fixsen writing partnership with a spare lineup — only writing credits are documented on Discogs for this release. The debut album Silver Apples Of The Moon (Too Pure, 1994) followed rapidly and established the band’s full working lineup: Margaret Fiedler and Guy Fixsen shared vocals, guitar, melodica, Moog synthesizer, vibraphone, and marimba; John Frenett played bass; Lou Ciccotelli played drums and percussion; and Louise Elliott contributed flute and saxophone. Fiedler and Fixsen co-wrote and co-engineered throughout.

The album appeared in the UK on Too Pure and in Germany via Rough Trade distribution. It generated fifteen known pressings across territories, marking it as their most widely distributed debut-era release. The concurrent Antenna EP (Too Pure, 1994) features essentially the same lineup with Fixsen additionally credited on sampler, adding the electronic palette that would define later records.

US Tour and Consolidation (1995)

Laika U.S. Tour ‘95 (American Recordings, 1995) was a US-only cassette tour release. American Recordings (Rick Rubin’s label) briefly distributed Laika in North America; this release documents that arrangement. Seven known pressings/variants exist, indicating meaningful US circulation.

Sounds Of The Satellites and the Mature Sound (1997)

Sounds Of The Satellites (Too Pure, 1997) is widely considered the band’s creative peak. The lineup expanded significantly: Rob Ellis (Discogs ID: 521) contributed drums, prepared piano, and percussion; Alonso Mendoza added vibraphone; Louise Elliott remained on flute; and the Fiedler/Fixsen core took on a wider instrument range — sampler, guitar, bass, Minimoog, and a Dolphin drum machine, plus trumpet. The two also engineered and mixed the majority of the album themselves, with Jai Williams and Liam Molloy assisting on a subset of mixes. Tony Cousins mastered at Metropolis. This release generated twenty known pressings, making it Laika’s most-pressed record.

The companion single Almost Sleeping (Too Pure, Pure 71 CDS, 1997) featured an edit of the album track plus three remixes: Maxwell House (Max Brennan), Cabbage Boy (Si Begg of Begg Recordings, restructured remix), and Luke Vibert. The Rob Ellis/Louise Elliott/Fiedler/Fixsen lineup and Alonso Mendoza on vibraphone are credited on the single’s A-side; the remix side represents the band’s intersection with the UK electronic scene.

The Breather EP (Too Pure, 1997) is a shorter release from the same period; credits are sparse in the DB (only writing credits documented).

Good Looking Blues and the Expanded Ensemble (2000)

Good Looking Blues (Too Pure, 2000) — often cited alongside Sounds Of The Satellites as the band’s key record — saw the lineup deepen further. John Frenett returned on bass (tracks 1, 2, 4–10); Lou Ciccotelli expanded his drumming palette to include djembe, conga, and sogo; Guy Fixsen added Rhodes electric piano, Minimoog, guitar, and sampler; Margaret Fiedler (credited on this release as “Margaret Murphy Fiedler”) added vocals, guitar, and sampler across alternating tracks. Pete Whyman contributed bass clarinet (tracks 1, 5, 6, 9); Louise Elliott played flute (tracks 1, 5, 6, 9); Matt Barge added trumpet (tracks 1, 6); Danny Doyle played turntables (tracks 1, 2, 8–10). Fixsen produced, mixed, and engineered; Mike Senior recorded drums and bass; Andrew Hernandez (4) recorded percussion and piano. Tony Cousins again mastered.

The companion EP Badtimes (Too Pure, 2000) is a companion release to Good Looking Blues with a nearly identical lineup — Rob Ellis returning on triangle alongside the core players; Steve Cox (3) adding Hammond organ; Pete Whyman on bass clarinet; and Danny Doyle on turntables again.

Uneasy (Too Pure, 2000) is a third release from this productive period: Rob Ellis on piano and shaker; Pete Whyman on clarinet; Louise Elliott on flute; the full core lineup present.

Final Album and Retrospective (2003)

Wherever I Am I Am What Is Missing (Too Pure, 2003) marks the band’s last studio album. By this point the lineup contracted: only Lou Ciccotelli is credited as an additional performer alongside Fiedler and Fixsen (who are listed as “Laika are” in the credits). Fixsen produced, engineered, mixed, and recorded; Richard Morris assisted on mixing; Tony Cousins mastered. Alison Fielding provided artwork and illustrations.

Lost In Space - Volume One (1993–2002) (Too Pure, 2003) is the double-CD retrospective, formally titled as a “Volume One” though no volume two was released. The credits note that the Laika core is Fiedler and Fixsen (“Laika Is”), with Frenett and Ciccotelli as consistent “with” collaborators, and a longer list of periodic contributors including Alonso Mendoza, Colin Cairns, Danny Doyle, Kevin Bass, Louise Elliott, Matt Barge, Pete Whyman, Philippe d’Amonville, Rob Ellis, and Steve Cox (3). Photography by Duncan Brown, Guy Fixsen, Joe Dilworth, and Margaret Fiedler; design by Chris Bigg (2) of Beggars Group. Fixsen and Fiedler produced and engineered most of the content (tracks 1-1 through 2-1 and 2-6 through 2-12 documented with specific producer credits).

Too Pure Relationship

Too Pure (ID: 1002) is Laika’s exclusive label throughout their career. All twelve primary-credit releases are on Too Pure, which became a sublabel of Beggars Group (ID: 114971). The label relationship mirrors that of other Too Pure acts of the period — Stereolab, PJ Harvey, Th’ Faith Healers — where creative autonomy was retained while distribution operated through the Beggars infrastructure. Laika were one of the label’s most consistent long-term artists, releasing material from 1994 through 2003.
