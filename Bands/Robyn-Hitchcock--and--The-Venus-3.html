Robyn Hitchcock & The Venus 3

Discogs ID: 1438371
Members: Robyn Hitchcock (vocals/guitar), Peter Buck (R.E.M., guitar), Scott McCaughey (bass/keyboards), William Rieflin (drums)

Collaborative band formed around Hitchcock’s Yep Roc Records period. Released Olé! Tarantula (2006, Yep Roc) and Goodnight Oslo (2009, Yep Roc). Buck, McCaughey and Rieflin were all associated with R.E.M.’s later touring and recording circle.

Formation

The Venus 3 grew out of a long-running set of connections between Hitchcock and the Seattle rock community, reaching back at least to the late 1980s.

Hitchcock and Peter Buck — since 1987. Buck first played with Hitchcock on Globe of Frogs (1988), contributing guitar to four tracks. The two worked together again on Queen Elvis (1989) and Perspex Island (1991). In 1989 they also performed a pair of cover-set gigs as Nigel and the Crosses, alongside Peter Holsapple of the dB’s. By the time of a 1998 interview Buck estimated they had been playing together for thirteen years. Buck later described the appeal simply: “Robyn is a classic songwriter, up there with John Lennon or Syd Barrett. It’s a pleasure to be able to contribute to the records.” He also noted that Hitchcock “likes the fact that I work really quickly, and it kind of adds energy to the sessions.” (source: remhq.com)

The Seattle scene — Young Fresh Fellows and the Crocodile Café. Scott McCaughey, founder of the Young Fresh Fellows and The Minus 5, developed a parallel friendship with Hitchcock through the Seattle live circuit. McCaughey later cited “great beginnings in the Hitchcock/Fellows collaborative surprise shows that reared up regularly at the Crocodile [Café] in Seattle in the late ’90s” as the foundation of their bond. (source: magnetmagazine.com) The Young Fresh Fellows also toured with Hitchcock’s reformed Soft Boys in 2001.

Jewels for Sophia (1999) — the first full Seattle session. The album Jewels for Sophia was recorded across four separate sessions. The Seattle session united Hitchcock with Buck, McCaughey, drummer Tad Hutchinson and guitarist Kurt Bloch (three-quarters of the Young Fresh Fellows), producing tracks including “Viva! Sea-Tac”. This was effectively the first studio encounter of the future Venus 3 core lineup. (source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewels_for_Sophia)

Spooked (2004) and the Nashville connection. Before the Venus 3 fully crystallised, Hitchcock recorded Spooked (2004) in Nashville with Gillian Welch and David Rawlings at Woodland Studio — his first significant US studio project outside the Seattle circle. (source: thealicecode.substack.com) This Nashville visit prefigured his later permanent relocation there.

Bill Rieflin’s route in. Rieflin (Seattle-born, drums: Ministry, Revolting Cocks, KMFDM) entered this network via McCaughey’s Minus 5, which also featured Buck. His work with McCaughey and Buck in that project — and his subsequent appointment as R.E.M.’s live drummer (2003–2011) — put him squarely inside the same circle when the Venus 3 took shape for Olé! Tarantula in 2006. (source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Rieflin)

Olé! Tarantula (2006) — the Venus 3 as a named band. The album was recorded in Seattle, engineered by Kurt Bloch. Hitchcock described the resulting lineup as “3/4s of the Minus 5 and half of R.E.M.” — framing it as a reunion of long-standing friends rather than a new assembly. McCaughey noted that when Venus 3 came together, he was working with “someone whose music I’ve admired pretty obsessively for close to 30 years.” (source: magnetmagazine.com)

US relocation — Nashville, August 2015. Hitchcock’s permanent move to the US postdates the Venus 3 albums by nearly a decade. He relocated to Nashville in August 2015 to live with his partner, Australian singer-songwriter Emma Swift. He had been a regular visitor and touring presence in the US throughout the Venus 3 years but was still UK-based when Olé! Tarantula and Goodnight Oslo were made. (source: nashvillescene.com; source: musiccitymike.net)
