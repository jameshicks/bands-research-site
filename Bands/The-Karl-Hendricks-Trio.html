The Karl Hendricks Trio

Pittsburgh indie rock band. Discogs artist ID: 506894. Also released records as The Karl Hendricks Rock Band (Discogs ID: 888818).

Overview

The Karl Hendricks Trio was an American rock band from Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, active 1991–2016. The band was the primary vehicle of guitarist, vocalist, and songwriter Karl Hendricks (born July 17, 1970 in Port Vue, near Pittsburgh; died January 21, 2017, Bloomfield, Pittsburgh). Hendricks had previously fronted Sludgehammer, and formed the Trio after that band broke up. He first began writing songs and releasing cassettes in his teens, with the earliest Discogs credit being the 1989 cassette Jolly Doom on his own Peas Kor imprint.

The band’s debut lineup — Hendricks on guitar and vocals, Tim Parker (3) on bass, and Tom Hoffman on drums — formed in late 1991, played their first show on New Year’s Eve 1991, and recorded Buick Electra a few weeks later. The band’s lineup shifted significantly across its run; Hendricks was the only constant member. The Trio’s sound was described as moody, muscular indie rock with confessional lyrics in the early years, becoming more expansive in later albums (Wikipedia).

Early releases appeared on Hendricks’s own Peas Kor label (often co-released with Fiasco) and on small Pittsburgh-area imprints. Merge Records picked the band up for For A While, It Was Funny (1996), releasing three albums through 2003. The final album, The Adult Section (2012), came out on Pittsburgh’s Comedy Minus One. A UK-only compilation-format release appeared on Fire Records in 1994.

The band’s releases were produced by Brian Paulson from 1995 onward through the Merge era. Paulson — whose credits include Slint’s Spiderland, Uncle Tupelo’s Anodyne, and Wilco’s A.M. — recorded and mixed A Gesture Of Kindness, For A While, It Was Funny, and Declare Your Weapons. Artwork across the early catalog was consistently supplied by Pittsburgh cartoonist Wayno (Discogs ID: 1540300), and the recording engineer on early records was Lee Hollihan (Discogs ID: 596648). Cover art for The Jerks Win Again (2003) was by Pittsburgh cartoonist Frank Santoro (credited as Frankie Santoro, Discogs ID: 3708299). A Gesture Of Kindness (1995) carried artwork by Chris Ware (Discogs ID: 803929).

Outside the Trio, Hendricks was a member of The Speaking Canaries alongside Don Caballero drummer Damon Che and drummer Noah Leger, and briefly appeared as a bassist on a Speaking Canaries record. He also owned a Pittsburgh record store, taught English at the University of Pittsburgh, and wrote fiction (Wikipedia).

Hendricks died on January 21, 2017, at age 46. His death effectively ended the Karl Hendricks Trio project.

Member Profiles and Outside Connections

Tim Parker (3) (Discogs ID: 596647) — bassist on the original lineup through A Gesture Of Kindness. Outside the Trio he played bass on a release for Vehicle Flips (Discogs group: 1011055) and on the For You I Pine album for Magic Marker Records (2000). His Discogs credits show activity only in the early-to-mid 1990s Pittsburgh scene.

Tom Hoffman (Discogs ID: 596646) — original drummer. Outside the Trio, Hoffman’s Discogs credits include drums on a release credited to “Train Song” (Harriet Records, 1991) and drums on an Ankle Deep release (Little Teddy Recordings, 1993, Germany). He also has an art direction/design credit on a 2003 Capitol Records release (Casualties) and drums on releases for an act called Bushfire (self-released, Germany, 2010 and 2013). His Discogs footprint is primarily the Trio.

Caulen Kress (Discogs ID: 506895) — bassist on Declare Your Weapons and The Jerks Win Again. Outside the Trio he is a member of Centipede Eest (Discogs group: 1372096). His Discogs credits are almost entirely Trio-related.

Jake Leger (Discogs ID: 506893) — drummer on The Jerks Win Again and The Adult Section. Outside the Trio, Jake is a member of 53rd State (Discogs: 1611456), Carousel (5) (Discogs: 3176286), Magic Wolf (Discogs: 1777518), and Pikadori (Discogs: 3613807). He played drums on a Jeweler’s Daughter record (Tee Pee Records, 2013) and appeared in a Carousel (5) release (Tee Pee Records, 2015), and also did road crew for a Relapse Records band. He has the broadest footprint outside the Trio among the members.

Corey Layman (Discogs ID: 2831536) — bassist on The Adult Section and The World Says (as Rock Band). Layman is described as a bassist and songwriter from Pittsburgh. His Discogs credits are limited to the Trio/Rock Band context. He recorded, mixed, and played bass on The World Says (2007).

Noah Leger (Discogs ID: 418073) — drummer on Declare Your Weapons (1998). He also appears in The Speaking Canaries (with Hendricks and Damon Che). Discogs credits beyond the Trio and Speaking Canaries are not documented.

Len Jarabeck (Discogs ID: 1336725) — bassist on For A While, It Was Funny (1996) and reportedly returned c. 2004–2006 (Wikipedia). Outside the Trio he has credits in early Pittsburgh bands (c. 1989–1990) on guitar. He is listed in groups Slag (11) (Discogs: 4171625) and T4 (4) (Discogs: 2203188). His Trio-era credits appear to be limited to the Merge period.
