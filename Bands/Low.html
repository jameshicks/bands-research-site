Low

Discogs ID: 10824. Duluth, Minnesota. Formed 1993. Slowcore / indie rock duo (later trio) built around the married couple Alan Sparhawk and Mimi Parker, whose interlocking guitar and drums-and-vocals created one of the most distinctive sounds in American indie: spare, unhurried, reverb-soaked, hymnal. Active 1993–2022; disbanded following Mimi Parker’s death.

Full name: George Alan Sparhawk (guitar, vocals). Mimi J. Parker, born 1967, died 6 November 2022, Duluth MN — ovarian cancer. Sparhawk’s wife. Their daughter Hollis Mae Sparhawk appears as a credited performer on multiple albums.

Formation and Vernon Yard era (1993–1997)

Formed 1993 in Duluth, Minnesota. Sparhawk and Parker had been a couple; the band grew from their domestic musical space. Initial bassist John Nichols appeared only on the debut before Zak Sally took the position in 1994.

Signed to Vernon Yard Recordings, a Virgin Records imprint. Debut I Could Live In Hope (1994, Vernon Yard YARD CD6) produced by Kramer (2) — a key early patron of the American lo-fi/slowcore scene — and engineered by Steve Watson. Established the template: ten originals plus a cover of “Sunshine” (Charles Mitchell / Jimmie Davis), minimal percussion, close harmonies, long resonant chords.

Long Division (1995, Vernon Yard) followed. The Curtain Hits The Cast (1996, Vernon Yard VYD 18) produced by Steve Fisk (keyboards on three tracks), engineered by John Goodmanson, mastered by Greg Calbi. A 2xLP. Their most critically noted Vernon Yard release; consolidated the aesthetic.

Kranky era (1997–2003)

Moved to Kranky (Chicago). Songs For A Dead Pilot EP (1997) and One More Reason To Forget (1998) preceded the landmark Secret Name (1999, Kranky KRANK 035), recorded by Steve Albini at Electrical Audio, Chicago. Zak Sally plays bass and Optigan. String section (Triple A Strings) appears. Albini’s flat, room-capturing recording approach gave the album unusual dryness for Low.

Things We Lost In The Fire (2001, Tugboat TUGCD027) — recorded and co-mixed by Steve Albini. Most densely populated of the early albums: Mark De Gli Antoni (Soul Coughing) on piano, keys, and sampler; Bob Weston (Shellac) on trumpet; Ida Pearle violin; Tresa Ellickson viola; Jaron Childs cello; Daniel Huffman guitar and loops. Hollis Mae Sparhawk credited for “squeaks, yells.”

Trust (2002, Kranky) continued the Albini collaboration.

Sub Pop era — louder (2004–2011)

Signed to Sub Pop. The Great Destroyer (2004, Cycle (2) CYCLE 023) — produced by Dave Fridmann, their first step toward a fuller, louder sound. Gerry Beckley of America (2) provides guest vocals on “Everybody’s Song.” Hollis Mae Sparhawk on vocals for “Step.” Zak Sally contributed artwork; this was his final album with the band.

Drums And Guns (2007, Sub Pop SP 736) — again produced by Dave Fridmann. Matt Livingston on bass. Anti-war themes throughout; electronic textures enter more prominently.

C’mon (2011, Sub Pop). Steve Garrington had joined on bass (2008).

Middle Sub Pop era (2011–2015)

The Invisible Way (2013, Sub Pop SP1030) produced by Jeff Tweedy (Wilco). More acoustic and intimate in texture.

Ones And Sixes (2015, Sub Pop) — recorded by BJ Burton at April Base, Wisconsin.

Electronic turn: BJ Burton era (2018–2021)

Double Negative (2018, Sub Pop SP 1250) — produced, recorded, mixed, and mastered by BJ Burton (also known for Bon Iver, Perfume Genius). A radical departure: electronic noise, distortion, and ambient density replaced the spare acoustic framework. Genres shifted to electronic rock, ambient, leftfield, shoegaze. Widely cited as their most adventurous and critically successful late record.

Hey What (2021, Sub Pop) — continued in the electronic/experimental direction. Final studio album released during Mimi Parker’s lifetime.

Mimi Parker’s death

Mimi Parker died 6 November 2022, in Duluth, Minnesota, of ovarian cancer. She had been diagnosed in 2020. Low effectively ended with her death.

Collaborations and side projects

  • In The Fishtank 7 (1999, Konkurrent) — collaboration with Dirty Three. Recorded in two days in Amsterdam.
  • Bombscare EP — collaboration with Spring Heel Jack.
  • Zak Sally ran La Mano (comics/music label) and released work as Kid Dakota.
  • Alan Sparhawk in The Black-Eyed Snakes (garage/blues side project) and Retribution Gospel Choir.
