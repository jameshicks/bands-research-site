Manicured Noise

Discogs ID: 702990 Active: 1978–c.1981 Origin: Manchester, England Genres: New Wave, post-punk, punk-funk

Manchester new wave/post-punk band formed 1978. Released two singles on PRE (5) (a Charisma sublabel) in 1980, and a BBC session. Retrospective Northern Stories 1978/80 (2006, Caroline True Records) collects nearly all their recorded output including the singles, BBC session, and unreleased material.

Note: The pre-existing stub described them as releasing on “Object Music” — this is incorrect per Discogs. Their label was PRE (5), a sublabel of Charisma Records.

Retrospective

80 (2006, Caroline True Records, CTRUE3)

  • CD compilation, 18 tracks
  • Compiled and produced by Steve Walsh (4) and John Kertland
  • Collects both singles, a BBC session, and unreleased material
  • Production credits per track:
    • Tracks 1–12, 15, 16, 18: produced by Manicured Noise (self-produced)
    • Tracks 6–9: also produced by Mark Stewart (The Pop Group)
    • Tracks 13, 14, 17: produced by David Cunningham (The Flying Lizards)
  • Owen Gavin credited as lead vocals on tracks 13–14; Steve Walsh on all other vocal tracks
  • Lalo Schifrin credited written-by on tracks 12 (“The Human Fly”) and 17 (“Great White Whale (Dub)”). “The Human Fly” is confirmed from the Enter the Dragon (1973) soundtrack (track 13 on the extended edition; schifrin.com, whosampled.com). The source work for “Great White Whale (Dub)” could not be identified — no Schifrin composition of that title appears in his documented discography or filmography.
  • Reissued on LP in 2015 (Caroline True Records, limited edition and test pressing variants)

BBC Session

Tracks 6–9 of Northern Stories 1978/80 (“Mystery Sound”, “Survival Time”, “Dreams Money Can Buy”, “Payday”) were recorded at BBC Studios Maida Vale for a session hosted by Radio 1 DJ David “Kid” Jensen. Mark Stewart (The Pop Group) was present at the recording and contributed backing vocals. The session pre-dates Jensen’s departure from BBC Radio 1 on 29 May 1980, placing it sometime in late 1979 or early 1980. No broadcast date has been confirmed in available sources.

Sources: lostbands.blogspot.com; search results corroborating Maida Vale recording and Jensen credit.

Label: PRE (5)

PRE (5) was a short-lived sublabel of Charisma, based at 90 Wardour Street, London W1. Marketed by Charisma, distributed by Phonogram UK. Concentrated on new wave and reggae acts. Per postcard included with PRE 003 (the Manicured Noise single).

Biography Notes

Band name given by Manchester scenester and artist Linder Sterling (Linder). carolinetruerecords.com

The band were based on the Hulme estate and shared a rehearsal room with Joy Division. lostbands.blogspot.com; search result corroborating Hulme and Joy Division rehearsal room.

Influences cited by Caroline True Records: Television, Ornette Coleman, Talking Heads, August Darnell’s Machine, French soundtracks, Disco Chic, Northern Soul, Giorgio Moroder, and 1970s cop/kung-fu film scores. carolinetruerecords.com

Support slots: Wire, Siouxsie & the Banshees, Big in Japan, Human League. lostbands.blogspot.com

Paul Morley / Factory interest: The cerysmatic.factoryrecords.org review notes Paul Morley mentioned them in NME (December 1978) with “Factory have expressed an interest in…Manicured Noise.” This clarifies the “Factory Records-adjacent circuit” claim in the earlier stub — the Factory interest was real but no release resulted. cerysmatic.factoryrecords.org

Dissolution: Steve Walsh’s own comment (“Had we stayed together that little bit longer, who knows what might have been?”) implies the split was not clean or planned. No confirmed dissolution date was found. Stephanie Nuttall’s move to Argentina with SUMO places that departure in 1981; Jodie Taylor’s presence on the 2019 remix (“Moscow 1980 (Wedderkopp + Taylor’s Lost In Georgia Mix)”) and Steve Walsh’s solo career continuing from 1982 suggest the band was effectively over by 1981.

Stephanie Nuttall / SUMO: After Manicured Noise, Steve Walsh moved from Manchester to London; he encouraged Nuttall to follow. In London she met Luca Prodan (SUMO), who brought her to Argentina in 1981. She was the founding drummer of SUMO and can be heard on their debut album Corpiños en la Madrugada and on the posthumous Luca Prodan demo compilation Time Fate Love. She left Argentina after the Falklands War broke out in April 1982. SUMO’s song “Percussion Baby” was dedicated to her by Prodan. Sources: lostbands.blogspot.com; mtv.com; cellardoorproject.com.

Member Career Notes

Steve Walsh (4)

After Manicured Noise, Walsh continued releasing on PRE (5) under his own name and the alias The Weatherman (5):

  • “Life!” (1982, as The Weatherman (5), PRE (5), PRE 21-12) — 12”; Boogie/Disco/Funk; produced and co-written with Emrys Baird
  • “Edge Of Night” (1982, PRE (5), PRE 25-12) — 12”; produced by Steve Brown, recorded at Townhouse Studios
  • “Letters Of Love” (1983, Innervision Records (2), TA 3635) — 12” Funk/Disco; produced with Duncan Bridgeman; backing vocals by The Sapphires (2); on the Innervision/CBS label

Walsh was also briefly a member of Flowers of Romance, the short-lived project of Sid Vicious and Viv Albertine. He had previously written for NME and Zig-Zag magazines.

Caroline True Records

Reissue label run by John Kertland (Discogs artist ID 2247723), who co-compiled Northern Stories 1978/80. Exists since 2005. Has a sublabel Infamous.
