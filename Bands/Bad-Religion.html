Bad Religion

Bad Religion

Oct 11, 20269 min read

Bad Religion

Discogs artist ID: 17479 Formed: 1980, San Fernando Valley / Los Angeles, California, US Genres: Melodic hardcore, punk rock, skate punk, hardcore punk Labels: Epitaph Records (1981–1993, 2001–present), Atlantic (1993–2001), Dragnet (European releases, 1994–2002)

Bad Religion is one of the defining bands of US melodic hardcore. Formed in 1980 by high school students Greg Graffin (vocals), Brett Gurewitz (guitar), Jay Bentley (bass), and Jay Ziskrout (drums), they released their debut EP on Gurewitz’s newly formed Epitaph Records in 1981. The band is known for rapid-fire songs with a melodic edge, strong three-part vocal harmonies, and unusually sophisticated lyrics dealing with religion, politics, and science. Only Graffin has remained through every phase of the band’s history, though Bentley and Gurewitz have each been present for substantial stretches. Brian Baker (ex-Minor Threat, Dag Nasty) joined as second guitarist in 1994 when Gurewitz briefly departed, and has remained a full member through the 2020s. By various accounts the band has sold over five million albums worldwide and is considered one of the best-selling punk acts of all time (Wikipedia).

The band pioneered the 1990s punk rock revival that also produced Green Day, The Offspring, NOFX, and Rancid — several of them signed to or closely associated with Epitaph Records. Stranger Than Fiction (1994), their Atlantic debut proper, was certified gold in the US and Canada.

History

Formation and first records (1980–1985)

Formed 1980 in the San Fernando Valley. First public performance reportedly opened for Social Distortion in a Fullerton warehouse. The 1981 self-titled EP (Epitaph EPI 001) launched both the band and Gurewitz’s label, which he started with money borrowed from his father. Their debut LP How Could Hell Be Any Worse? (1982) followed; drummer Jay Ziskrout quit mid-recording and was replaced by Pete Finestone. Engineered by Jim Mankey. The album sold roughly 12,000 copies.

In 1983, the band released Into the Unknown, a keyboard-heavy prog rock departure that was widely disowned, went out of print rapidly, and led to the band’s temporary dissolution. Gurewitz entered rehab; Graffin moved out of state for college.

Graffin returned and reformed the band in 1984 with Greg Hetson (ex-Circle Jerks) on guitar, Pete Finestone on drums, and Tim Gallegos on bass. Gurewitz produced but did not play on the Back to the Known 12” EP (1985, Epitaph).

Reunion and Epitaph golden era (1986–1993)

The band solidified around 1986–87 with Gurewitz returning to the fold, now sober. The classic lineup — Graffin, Gurewitz (as “Mr. Brett”), Hetson, Bentley, Finestone — recorded Suffer (1988), self-produced with engineering by Donnell Cameron and Gurewitz (credited as The Legendary Starbolt). The album was voted Best Album of the Year by Maximum Rocknroll, Flipside, and Trust.

No Control (1989) followed rapidly, eventually selling over 80,000 copies. Against the Grain (1990) passed 100,000 and contained “21st Century (Digital Boy)”, one of the band’s signature songs.

Pete Finestone departed in April 1991 to focus on another band; Bobby Schayer replaced him. Generator (1992) was recorded largely live in the studio at Westbeach Recorders (now relocated to larger premises). The compilation 80-85 (1991, Epitaph) collected early material.

Recipe for Hate (1993, Epitaph) featured a notable guest harmony section (“Oozin’ Aahs”) including Eddie Vedder and Johnette Napolitano alongside Graffin, Gurewitz, and Bentley. Mastered by Doug Sax. The album broke the band into mainstream audiences; Atlantic Records signed them and re-released it.

Atlantic years (1993–2001)

Bad Religion signed to Atlantic in 1993. Stranger Than Fiction (1994, Atlantic) — produced by the band and Andy Wallace — became their most successful album, containing “Infected”, “Stranger Than Fiction”, and a re-recorded “21st Century (Digital Boy)“. It debuted at #87 on the Billboard 200 and was later certified gold. Shortly before its release, Gurewitz left the band to focus on Epitaph, which was then experiencing unexpected commercial success from The Offspring’s Smash. He was replaced by Brian Baker.

The Gray Race (1996, Atlantic) was co-produced by former Cars frontman Ric Ocasek and the band, engineered by Bruce Calder. Mastered by George Marino. Baker received co-writing credits on tracks 1, 7, 9, and 10. Atticus Ross programmed electronics on The Empire Strikes First (2004).

No Substance (1998, Atlantic) was produced by Alex Perialas, Ronnie Kimball, and the band. The New America (2000, Atlantic) — the last Atlantic album — was produced by Todd Rundgren, mixed by Bob Clearmountain, mastered by Gavin Lurssen. Gurewitz made a guest cameo, prompting reunion speculation. Bobby Schayer left with a shoulder injury; Brooks Wackerman (ex-Suicidal Tendencies) replaced him.

Return to Epitaph (2001–present)

In 2001, Bad Religion returned to Epitaph and Gurewitz formally rejoined — primarily as songwriter and producer rather than full touring member. The Process of Belief (2002) was produced by Graffin and Gurewitz, mixed mostly by Gurewitz with one track by Jerry Finn; mastered by Bob Ludwig. The six-piece lineup now comprised Graffin, Gurewitz, Baker, Hetson, Bentley, Wackerman.

The Empire Strikes First (2004) was recorded and mixed by Joe Barresi, with Atticus Ross providing programming. New Maps of Hell (2007) was also produced by Joe Barresi. True North (2013, Epitaph) became the first Bad Religion album to crack the Billboard 200 top 20, peaking at #18.

Greg Hetson stopped touring in mid-2013 due to personal issues (later revealed to involve addiction to painkillers). Mike Dimkich filled in, becoming a permanent member in January 2014. Brooks Wackerman left in October 2015 to join Avenged Sevenfold; Jamie Miller (ex-…And You Will Know Us by the Trail of Dead) replaced him.

Age of Unreason (2019, Epitaph) was the seventeenth studio album. As of early 2025 the band has been recording a follow-up.
