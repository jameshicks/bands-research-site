Royal Family And The Poor

Post-punk / experimental / industrial. Formed Liverpool, 1978. Core identity: Michael Anthony Keane as principal creative force throughout every phase. Discogs artist ID: 23694.

Note: the band are from Liverpool (Keane and McDonald met in Toxteth). Factory Records was Manchester-based. Multiple web sources confirm Liverpool. “Art On 45” appearing on Revolutionary Spirit - The Sound Of Liverpool 1976-1988 (3-12) is consistent with this. [ltmrecordings.com, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-05-31]

Name variations used in print: RF&TP, RFATP, Royal Family & The Poor, The Royal Family & The Poor, The Royal Family And The Poor, The Poor. Alias entry on Discogs: The Project (25).

History

Origins (1978–1979)

Formed in Liverpool (not Manchester — the band are from Liverpool; Factory Records was Manchester-based). Keane moved into a shared house in Toxteth with Arthur McDonald, an art graduate with a deep interest in the avant-garde and Situationism. McDonald introduced Keane to The Revolution of Everyday Life by Raoul Vaneigem, which shaped the band’s Situationist lyrical stance. Their crude demo tape from late 1979 — Keane’s synthesizer noise overlaid with McDonald’s Situationist recitations — was sent to Factory Records and caught Tony Wilson’s attention. Tony Wilson did not name the band: the name was chosen by Arthur McDonald, taken from Fred and Judy Vermorel’s book Sex Pistols: The Inside Story. [ltmrecordings.com; contradicts the earlier stub — see Connections note]

By 1979 the lineup expanded: Nathan McGough joined on bass and Phil Hurst on drums, while Keane took over vocals, guitar, and keyboards. The band played the Moonlight Club in London supporting Joy Division. In his book Shadowplayers, James Nice describes the band as “less of a group than a pseudo-Situationist provocation.” [ltmrecordings.com]

Factory Records period, first phase (1980–1982)

A Factory Quartet (1980, Fact 24): Their first release was six tracks — three proper tracks and three short dirges — on this compilation double album shared with the Durutti Column, Kevin Hewick, and Blurt. The tracks were produced by Martin Hannett and engineered by Chris Nagle, recorded live. Tracks: “Vaneigem Mix,” “Death Factory,” “Rackets” (plus three dirges). Critics were harsh: Adrian Thrills dismissed them as a joke; Dave McCullough called them “stupendously redundant.” [ltmrecordings.com]

In 1980 Phil Hurst departed to join Hollycaust and was replaced by Donald Johnson (of A Certain Ratio). The classic early lineup was now Keane, McDonald, McGough, and Johnson.

In late 1981 the band reverted to the original Keane/McDonald duo. Their last gig was at Plato’s Ballroom (set up as a venue by Arthur McDonald and Nathan McGough). McDonald and McGough opened Plato’s Ballroom in Liverpool, hosting the original band’s final live show. McDonald then moved to Newcastle; Keane pursued solo projects and formed the Legend Agency. [ltmrecordings.com]

The sole release from this phase: “Art - Dream - Dominion” (FAC 43, 1982) — a 12” single with three tracks produced across two separate sessions. Side A (“Art On 45”) was co-produced by Donald Johnson and Anthony H. Wilson (uncredited); the B-side (“Dream,” “Dominion”) was produced by Be Music (Bernard Sumner and Peter Hook, uncredited). Runout inscriptions: “FROM AUSCHWITZ TO THE RITZ TOWN HOUSE” / “TO HEAVEN FROM THE BLITZ 666 TOWN HOUSE.”

Some Bizzare interlude (1982–1983): After Keane began touring with China Crisis (approx. 20 UK dates, May–June 1982), China Crisis guitarist Eddie Lundon passed a tape to manager Stevo (of Some Bizzare). Stevo signed Keane and planned to release “I Love You” as a mainstream single. In January 1983 Keane recorded “Moonfish Is Here,” “Motherland,” and other tracks at Trident Studio in London with Matt Johnson (The The) providing backing vocals. Keane ultimately rejected Stevo’s major-label strategy — a decision he later acknowledged as self-destructive. These Trident recordings were not released at the time. [ltmrecordings.com]

Spiritual crisis and pilgrimage (1983): Following the Some Bizzare collapse and a severe heroin addiction, Keane walked the pilgrim route from Winchester to Canterbury (late June – late August 1983), living rough on “nettle soup, fruit and a small amount of rice.” This transformative experience preceded his return to Factory. [ltmrecordings.com]

Factory Records period, second phase (1984–1986)

In 1984 Keane revived the name with a new lineup: John Neesham (violin), Peter Hook (drums, vocals, keyboards), Lita Hira (keyboards), and Chris Jones (vocals). At the same time Keane was touring the UK with the Stockholm Monsters.

By 1984/85 yet another complete lineup shift: Keane (vocals, keyboards, guitar, percussion), Karen Halewood (keyboards), John Walsh (bass, guitar), Ambrose Reynolds (bass, percussion), Jeff Turner (saxophone, guitar, violin), Kif Cole/Higgins (percussion). This version of the band toured with New Order.

Factory Records, at Rob Gretton’s instigation, offered Keane an album deal conditional on Peter Hook’s Manchester production. Recording for Temple Of The 13th Tribe began at Strawberry Studios in Stockport in February 1984, with engineers Mike Johnson and Chris ‘CJ’ Jones. [ltmrecordings.com]

The full-length debut The Project Phase 1 - The Temple Of The 13th Tribe (FACT 95, November 1984) was produced by Be Music and engineered by Michael Johnson. Peter Hook contributed uncredited backing vocals; Lita Hira played piano; John Neesham violin; Chris Jones backing vocals. All lyrics and compositions by Keane. The album’s occult/magick themes alienated the core Factory audience; Keane noted: “It has alienated me. Most people in Liverpool seem to think I’m some sort of black magician or fascist.” [ltmrecordings.com]

The band also contributed the track “The Kremlin In Flames” to The Zulu Compilation (1984) under the alias S.T.F.O.T.P.A. — this was McDonald, Teresa (unidentified), and an unnamed third person, with Ambrose Reynolds on guitar. The Zulu Compilation was produced by Jayne Casey and Ambrose Reynolds (also of Pink Industry). [qualityofmercy.com, bimble blogspot 2008]

The single “We Love The Moon” (FAC 139, 1986) and LP The Project Phase 2 - We Love The Moon (FACT 140, 1986) completed the Factory relationship. The LP was engineered and production-edited largely by Ambrose Reynolds, Mark Phythian, and Keith Hartley. Jeff Turner played saxophone, clarinet, guitar, and violin; Karen Halewood synthesizer and backing vocals; John Walsh bass and guitar on individual tracks.

We Love the Moon was recorded at Amazon Studios in summer 1985. Factory’s refusal to provide detailed sales figures or finance new equipment caused friction. By May 1986 Tony Wilson noted in the FacFacts booklet: “The records are wonderful. Our relationship with the artist isn’t.” [ltmrecordings.com]

In 1985–86 Royal Family & The Poor left Factory Records. Before establishing Gaia Communications, Keane worked with Bourbonese Qualk’s Recloose Organisation, releasing Live 1983–85 (Loose 13) in October 1986 with 1,000 copies pressed. Keane then established Gaia Communications with Red Rhino as distributor, releasing In The Sea Of E in July 1987 (cost: approximately £1,000, per Keane’s interview with Cut magazine at the time). Keane planned ambitious Gaia releases including a Robert Anton Wilson album and a chaos magic compilation featuring Coil and Sleep Chamber, but these fell through. Red Rhino collapsed at the end of 1988, leading to Keane’s personal bankruptcy and seizure of equipment by bailiffs. [ltmrecordings.com]

Between labels: live cassettes (1985–1986)

A series of self-released and small-label live recordings appeared on Project Tapes (Keane’s own tape label) and Recloose Organisation: Live (Project Tapes 001, 1985), In The Studio (Project Tapes 004, 1985 — demo/rehearsal cassette), The Genetic Terrorists (Project Tapes 002, 1986), Live 1983–1985 (Recloose Organisation LOOSE 13, 1986), and additional live cassettes on Project Tapes 006, 007, 008 (1986). These are among the rarest items in the catalog.

1986: lineup changes and Holland tour

In 1986, Jeff Turner, Karen Halewood, and Kif departed. They were replaced by Paula Simms (vocals), John Metcalfe (violin), and Judith Laity (cello). The new lineup toured Holland.

Gaia Communications (1987–1988)

In 1987, Simon Crab (Bourbonese Qualk) joined on vocals, keyboards, and co-production; Merlin Shepherd on saxophone; Andy Frizell on bass and flute. The band toured Germany and Belgium.

The LP In The Sea Of E (Gaia Communications Phase 3, 1987) was co-produced by Keane and Simon Crab. Side A was engineered by Keith Hartley; Side B by Frank Sparks. Simon Crab brought Mirage sampler and keyboard programming; Merlin Shepherd played alto and tenor saxophone and strings; Andy Frizell bass and flute.

The 12” single The Project Phase 4 (Gaia Communications PHASE 004, 1988) saw Gary Daly (China Crisis) produce and co-write “Restrained In A Moment” (Side A); Pete Coleman produced the B-sides; Adrian Jackson co-wrote the B-side material.

Hiatus and Gaia relaunch (1989–2001)

By 1995, Keane invested in a PC and created an extensive Gaia Communications website. Songs For The Children Of Baphomet appeared in a limited 1999 edition (1,000 numbered copies, per the label), billed as “a celebration on the theme of magick and a tribute to the life and works of Aleister Crowley.” It received a five-star review in Melody Maker; Uncut called it “fascinatingly bizarre” and The Wire “stylish and poignant.” [ltmrecordings.com] The Anthology 1978–2001 (GAIA 005, 2001) was compiled and digitally remastered by Keane.

Boutique label reissues and new material (2003–2004)

In 2003, Boutique (2) label reissued the three Gaia LPs on CD, plus a Portuguese radio show from October 1988 (The Project Phase 5, 2003). In 2004 North-West Soul (Boutique BOUCD 6607) was released — an all-Keane solo effort with Korg Triton synthesizers, Korg EM-1 electronic drums, and Fender Venus 12-string guitar.

Post-Keane activity and later releases (2010–2014)

In 2010, Arthur McDonald self-released The Pope’s Daughter under the RFATP name through New Art Editions (PO Box 1111, Newcastle upon Tyne), priced £10.00. Not involving Keane. Track listing: “Art on 45 (Liverpool 1982)” (live version), “New Babylon,” “War Film,” “Classicism,” “Beneath A Gothic Moon.” [cerysmatic.factoryrecords.org, 2010-02] Note: Discogs lists the label as Gothic Moon Records; the original release was via New Art Editions (same imprint or distribution arrangement). The 2012 digital-only Haunted (Gothic Moon Records) was credited jointly to “RF&TP” and Teresa Kelly (2).

In 2014, Mannequin (Rome) reissued The Temple Of The 13th Tribe (MNQ 052) on vinyl.

Open Questions

  • Red Flame / Cherry Red: No evidence found via web research of any RF&TP release on either label. Red Flame is confirmed as a separate label (Blurt’s debut 1982). Cherry Red appears only in connection with the Revolutionary Spirit box set compilation (a Cherry Red release that includes “Art On 45”), not as a label RF&TP recorded for. The connection appears to be a myth or a conflation with the Revolutionary Spirit compilation. Ruled out as a label relationship.
  • Martin Hannett: RESOLVED. Hannett produced the six FACT 24 (A Factory Quartet, 1980) tracks, engineered by Chris Nagle. He has no role on Temple Of The 13th Tribe (FACT 95). The Discogs collaborator graph link is to FACT 24. The confusion likely arises because the BOUCD 6608 CD reissue bundles FACT 24 tracks as bonuses with Temple, and some online listings (e.g. Amazon.ca) grouped the credits together.
  • “Pete Fuck” (aka Pic Meter, Peter Jones, Cosmic): No web source confirms the real name. The alias appears only on the Discogs artist profile text. The name “Peter Jones” is common enough that no disambiguation is possible without primary-source confirmation. Remains unknown.
  • Windsor pseudonym collective: No web source provides an explanation of the Keane/Windsor, Hurst/Windsor, McGough/Windsor, McDonald/Windsor aliases. The LTM biography and Wikipedia make no mention of them. Likely a shared collective alias used on early pressings, but the rationale is undocumented in any accessible source.
  • Paula Simms, Judith Laity, and John Metcalfe (1986 Holland tour lineup) have no studio credits on Discogs or in any web source found.
  • “Songs For The Children Of Baphomet” (GAIA 004) — tracklist and full personnel remain sparse.
  • Haunted (2012) — the “Teresa” mentioned in the S.T.F.O.T.P.A. alias (Zulu Compilation) may be the same person as “Teresa Kelly (2)” on Haunted, but this is unconfirmed.
  • The Some Bizzare / Trident 1983 recordings (with Matt Johnson backing vocals) — these remain unreleased as of last available information. Their current status is unknown.
