Comateens

Discogs ID: 141078
Origin: New York City
Active: 1978–1985; reunited later (recordings resurface 2019, 2023)
Genres: New Wave, Synth-pop, Electro
Name variations: The Comateens, Commateens

NYC new wave / synth-pop group formed in 1978. Core members for most of the active run were Nicholas Dembling (bass, vocals), Oliver Dembling (guitar, vocals), and Lyn Billman (synthesizers, vocals). Early lineup included founder Ramona Jan (guitar, vocals) and Harry Viderci (drums). Achieved stronger commercial footing in Europe — particularly France and Germany — than in the US. Three studio LPs on Jupiter Records Germany, with concurrent US releases through Mercury and Virgin France.

Formation and Early History (1978–1980)

Formed in New York in 1978 by Ramona Jan (guitar) and Nicholas Dembling (bass, vocals), initially with a drum machine. Lyn Billman (synth, vocals), Oliver Dembling (guitar, vocals), and Harry Viderci (drums) joined to flesh out the group.

The band’s first recording session produced three songs: two Ramona Jan compositions — “Danger Zone” and “Elizabeth’s Lover” — plus Diana Dominicci’s “Cool Chick.” The first 7” single, Cool Chick / Danger Zone (Teenmaster Records TM111, 1979), was released independently on their own Teenmaster imprint. Engineers were Don Wershba and Harvey Goldberg; the band self-produced.

In 1980 Comateens contributed two tracks — “Overseas” and “Late Night City” — to Marty Thau’s Marty Thau Presents 2x5 (Red Star Records RED 100), alongside The Fleshtones, Revelons, Bloodless Pharaohs, and Student Teachers. The compilation was produced by Jimmy Destri (Blondie) and helped establish the band in the NYC new wave underground.

Ramona Jan departed in 1980. The trio of Nicholas Dembling, Oliver Dembling, and Lyn Billman continued as the core lineup.

Tracklist includes originals (“Ghosts,” “Late Night City,” “One By One,” “Nightmare,” “Strangler,” “Overseas,” “Raceway” — drums on this track by Jeff Zilm) alongside notable covers: David Bowie’s “TVC 15,” the Lovin’ Spoonful’s “Summer In The City,” and a version of “The Munsters Theme” (written by Bob Mosher / Jack Marshall) that became a cult favorite. “Cool Chick” (by Diana Dominicci) reprises from the debut single.

Most originals are credited to combinations of Nicholas Dembling, Oliver Dembling, and Lyn Billman. “Raceway” features external drummer Jeff Zilm.

A shift from the debut — producer Norman Mighell (who used various nicknames including “Crocodile” and “Dingo Blue”) brought a tighter, more polished sound. Songwriting split across Dembling/Dembling/Billman combinations; “Get Off My Case” is solely by Nic North; “Garbanzo” solely by Lyn Byrd. Cover features an “Uptown” (Barry Mann / Cynthia Weil) cover.

Unreleased First Session (released 2023)

Danger Zone / Elizabeth’s Lover (2023, Left For Dead Records)

Release ID: 28982869 | Cat no: LFD-1007-1 | USA & Canada
Format: 12”; Limited Edition; Numbered; Stereo

The original 1979 recording session that produced the debut single also captured two further tracks: Ramona Jan’s “Danger Zone” (also on the original 7”) and “Elizabeth’s Lover” (the B-side withheld at the time). This release finally issued both tracks, now identified as the original four-piece lineup: Ramona Jan (guitar, vocals), Nicholas Dembling (bass, vocals), Lyn Billman (synth, vocals), Harry Viderci (drums).

Produced by Comateens; original engineers Don Wershba and Harvey Goldberg. Remastered by Jack Control (2); graphic design by Lyn Byrd and Jim Reynolds. Released on Left For Dead Records (ID: 1173768).

Release ID: 2524714 | France

Credits Lyn Byrd (vocals) and Don Wershba (engineer), suggesting this is a new recording rather than a catalog reissue. Issued as a lead-up to the best-of compilation, also on Virgin France.

One By One - Best Of Comateens (1991, Virgin France)

Release IDs: 209852, 6929019, 14631826
French-market compilation, compiled and produced by Comateens. Covers the Virgin/Mercury catalog.

1980 - 1985 (2019, Tricatel)

Release ID: 14333784 | Cat no: TRILPFR055 | France
Format: 3×LP; Deluxe Edition; Limited Edition; Remastered

34-track retrospective on Tricatel spanning the full active period. Draws from both LP and single releases; includes “Keep the Pace” (debut LP track) not on the US pressings. The Tricatel release is the first time the full catalog has been collected in one package. Styled “Coldwave, Pop Rock” on Discogs — a retroactive scene descriptor applied by the label.
