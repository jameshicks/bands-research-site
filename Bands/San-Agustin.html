San Agustin

Experimental guitar trio from Atlanta, Georgia, active since 1996. Discogs artist ID: 378085. Members: David Daniell and Andrew Burnes on guitars, Bryan Fielden on drums. They emerged from the Atlanta post-hardcore scene — Daniell and Burnes had both played in the earlier band Barrel (3) — and moved toward drone, minimalism, and free improvisation as San Agustin. Their music draws on what the Discogs profile calls “minimalism, free jazz, metal, folk, blues and beyond,” with spontaneous compositions that the profile describes as “a flowing, dynamic stream-of-consciousness song form.”

Their Discogs presence is sparse relative to their actual output. The DB indexes only two core releases: the 1997 debut 7” on Old Gold / Samizdat, and the 2000 LP Amokhali on Family Vineyard. However, the 2002 three-CD box The Expanding Sea on Table Of The Elements is also indexed (as a European tour document), plus the 2002 EP Passing Song in collaboration with vocalist Suzanne Langille, also on Family Vineyard. Wikipedia (via the David Daniell article) additionally lists Songs for Lakes (1999) and Aquifer (2008) — neither indexed in Discogs.

Note on Andrew Burnes’s instrument: Burnes is credited as “Electric Bass” in Barrel (3)‘s Pine And Cotton, Sand (1995), but on all San Agustin and related releases he is credited as “Guitar.” He also released a solo lap steel guitar album (Telescope, 2008, Table Of The Elements). The transition from bass to guitar appears to have coincided with — or preceded — the formation of San Agustin.

Research Queue

  • Songs for Lakes (1999): what label released this LP? Wikipedia lists it as the first San Agustin full-length but it is not indexed in Discogs; a label credit would allow a proper stub.
  • Aquifer (2008): same question — not indexed in Discogs; a press review, Bandcamp page, or AllMusic entry would name the label and format.
  • The Passing Song promo compilation (release ID 6584626) pairs San Agustin / Suzanne Langille tracks with Rope (2)‘s Fever EP — was this ever released commercially, or was it purely a promo CDr? The Family Vineyard catalog number is “none,” suggesting it was not a standard retail release.
  • Andrew Burnes released Telescope (2008) on Table Of The Elements as a solo lap steel record — was there any press or interview around this release that addresses his transition from bass (in Barrel) to guitar/lap steel?
  • The Wikipedia article on David Daniell places The Expanding Sea in 2001, but the Discogs release year is 2002 — was the European tour in March 2001 but the box set released in 2002? A label press release or review from 2001–2002 would confirm.
  • Jon Philpot is co-engineer on The Expanding Sea and a member of Presocratics and Bear In Heaven — was he based in Atlanta throughout this period, or had he already moved? A Bear In Heaven bio would clarify.
