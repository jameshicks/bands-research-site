Overview

Bongwater was a New York City experimental art-pop duo formed in 1987 by vocalist and performance artist Ann Magnuson and multi-instrumentalist/producer Kramer (2) (Mark Kramer). Rooted in the downtown NYC post-No Wave milieu — the world of the Mudd Club, Pulsallama, and the early Shimmy Disc scene — the band fused Magnuson’s trained theatrical wit and literary lyrical sensibility with Kramer’s eclectic production approach, which drew freely from psych rock, country, noise, and tape manipulation. The results were records that could be genuinely funny and genuinely unsettling within the same track, often built around absurdist cultural monologues (“David Bowie Wants Ideas,” “Decadent Iranian Country Club”), irreverent covers (Gary Glitter, Johnny Cash, George Harrison, Kevin Ayers, Led Zeppelin), and occasionally very direct pop songwriting.

The duo recorded and released five studio albums on Shimmy Disc between 1987 and 1992. The core performing lineup on most records was Magnuson and Kramer with guitarist Dave Rick (also of King Missile and B.A.L.L.) and drummer David Licht (formerly of Shockabilly and later a co-founder of The Klezmatics). Guest appearances over the course of the discography included avant-jazz trumpet legend Don Cherry, free improviser Fred Frith, and Fugs member Tuli Kupferberg.

All five studio albums were produced, engineered, and largely arranged by Kramer at his Noise New York and Noise New Jersey studios. Bongwater broke up in 1993 amid a well-publicized legal dispute between Magnuson and Kramer over unpaid royalties and label debts. The catalog was anthologized on the 1998 box set Box Of Bongwater and various CD reissues through the late 1990s (Knitting Factory Records).

Breakup and Legal Dispute

Bongwater’s dissolution in 1992–1993 was precipitated by both a romantic and professional rupture between Magnuson and Kramer. According to Kramer (speaking to Magnet Magazine in 1995, cited in a 2007 retrospective), the two entered a romantic relationship in 1991 following the breakdown of their respective relationships. During the final recording sessions for The Big Sell-Out — a two-month residence at Magnuson’s Los Angeles home — the relationship collapsed and Bongwater imploded with it. (magnetmagazine.com)

The lawsuit: In 1994 Magnuson filed a federal lawsuit against Kramer and Shimmy-Disc seeking $4.5 million in damages. The complaint contained 15 counts including fraud, breach of contract, copyright infringement, and invasion of privacy. The case was assigned to federal judge Kimba Wood. (deseret.com, 1994-10-27)

Kramer denied all charges and indicated plans to countersue for breach of contract, libel, and slander. He stated: “I tried to give her rights to the Bongwater albums to settle the lawsuit, but she wouldn’t accept it. So I finally just borrowed money from a good friend and decided to fight it.” A benefit concert was organized at CBGB on December 5, 1994 to offset Kramer’s legal costs, with performers including Too Much Joy, Dogbowl, and Penn Jillette. (deseret.com, 1994-10-27)

Resolution: The suit was settled out of court in 1997, with a confidentiality clause preventing either party from discussing the terms publicly. Shimmy-Disc subsequently went bankrupt. (magnetmagazine.com)

Magnuson’s public account was more oblique: “It was time to move on. Several of us were miffed over the business aspects…the only issue was that of accounting. Most bands have an expiration date, and I think Bongwater simply reached it.” (magnetmagazine.com)

Kramer told the Deseret News (1994): “After this I’m going to run down the label…I just don’t have time for it on top of producing.”
