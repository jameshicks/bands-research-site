Overview

The Membranes are an angular post-punk band from Blackpool, England, formed in 1978 and anchored by frontman John Robb. Their initial run across the 1980s produced fifteen releases — moving from UK indie labels Criminal Damage Records and Constrictor through to the American Homestead Records — and placed them alongside the US underground scene, with Steve Albini contributing to nine shared credits. After disbanding around 1990, the band reunited in 2010 at the invitation of My Bloody Valentine to play the All Tomorrow’s Parties festival, and have since maintained a sustained second career: seven releases in the 2010s and four more from 2020 onward, primarily through Louder Than War and Cherry Red.

Break-up (c. 1990)

John Robb does not frame the end of the band as a formal split. Speaking to The Quietus on the 2010 reunion, he said: “We had no ending. This is just the Membranes re-explosion! We just took a rest and the rest lasted for 19 years!” (The Quietus, 2010)

The primary cause he names is the pull of the emerging rave/acid house scene: “I was getting sidetracked by the underground rave scene and the madness of those times and playing in back rooms of pubs seemed a bit boring in comparison to acid house. Then there was more stuff going on after that and the gaps got bigger between the gigs ‘till the gap got to 19 years.” (The Quietus, 2010)

He also positions this as consistent with the band’s philosophy: “The band was never about a conventional career — it was about momentary celebration of skewed noise and the feral wildness of discords and a 19 year break seem natural.” (The Quietus, 2010)

A secondary factor noted in press coverage: the band “never really got the kind of record label support they deserved, meaning some short lived popularity, a hell of a lot of promise and notoriety in the mid to late 80s was never capitalised on and they kind of fizzled out of existence by 1990.” (Isolation Records interview) No specific label dispute or acrimonious parting is documented in available sources — the dissolution appears to have been gradual rather than event-driven.

No source found attributing the break-up to creative differences, member departures, or a specific incident. The Isolation Records phrasing about label support is from a 2015-era interview and is not attributed to a direct Robb quote in the fetched text.
