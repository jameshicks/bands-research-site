Overview

The Young Knives are an English indie rock trio from Ashby-de-la-Zouch, Leicestershire, founded in 1998, consisting of Henry Dartnall (guitar, vocals), his younger brother Thomas “The House of Lords” Dartnall (bass, vocals), and Oliver Askew (drums). Their output is heaviest in the 2000s — 12 releases in that decade — tapering through the 2010s and continuing into the 2020s, with a sound that moves across indie rock, art rock, post-punk, and industrial. Transgressive Records has been their principal label home from 2005 through 2023, with early releases on Shifty Disco and later output on their own GADZOOK imprint; their most prominent production collaborators are Guy Davie and Andy Gill, each carrying over a dozen shared credits.

Notes (web) — Andy Gill collaboration backstory

The band sent Andy Gill demos, and he called them back expressing interest: “We sent him some demos last year and he called us and said, ‘I’d like to work with you’” (Henry Dartnall, Drowned in Sound interview, 2006 — archived). The House of Lords (Thomas Dartnall) emphasised that it was not a label-brokered arrangement: “It was nice. It wasn’t our label calling him and offering him squillions of pounds, he wanted to do it.” The band secured a loan to fund the recording and Gill offered them a discounted rate on studio time.

Gill’s production approach on The Decision and the subsequent debut album Voices of Animals and Men was markedly spare: he stripped a complex guitar part down to two sustained notes to create tension (a characteristically Gang of Four move), and reoriented their song structures so that the strongest material — the chorus — came first rather than being buried late. Gill himself reflected on the sessions in a Tape Op interview (issue 71): “I loved working with them. Very strong lyrics — you don’t need to ask what they’re about.” (andygillmusic.com; Tape Op interview via harmonicbuzz.com)

Summary: the connection appears to have been initiated by the band sending unsolicited demos directly to Gill; Transgressive Records is not reported as having brokered it. The Gang of Four influence on the band (noted by Wikipedia) plausibly explains why they targeted Gill specifically.
