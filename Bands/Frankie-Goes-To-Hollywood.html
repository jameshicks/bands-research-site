Frankie Goes To Hollywood

Discogs ID: 6273

British pop group formed in Liverpool, 1980. Members: Holly Johnson (vocals), Paul Rutherford (vocals), Brian Nash (guitar), Peter Gill (drums), Mark O’Toole (bass).

Signed to ZTT Records in 1983; flagship act of the label’s Island era. Three UK #1 singles in 1984 — “Relax” (ZTAS 1), “Two Tribes,” “The Power Of Love” — all produced by Trevor Horn. Debut double LP Welcome to the Pleasuredome (1984) also entered at #1. Second album Liverpool (1986) underperformed commercially. Holly Johnson left in 1987, filing suit against ZTT Records and sister publisher Perfect Songs over the 1983 recording and publishing contracts; the High Court ruled in his favour on 10 February 1988 (restraint of trade); the band dissolved shortly after. ZTT subsequently released most of its remaining artists from their original contracts. 855 Discogs releases.

The original lineup reunited briefly in May 2023 for a National Lottery event.

Research Queue

ZTT Litigation

Background

When Holly Johnson left FGTH in 1987, ZTT Records and its sister publishing company Perfect Songs sought an injunction against him, asserting he remained bound by the 1983 recording and publishing contracts he had signed as a member of FGTH. Those contracts prohibited departing members from signing with any other label without ZTT’s consent. Johnson filed suit in the UK High Court, challenging the contracts as an unreasonable restraint of trade.

The 1983 contracts

The ZTT contracts were found to be substantially more restrictive than industry norms:

  • Nominally for five albums but with open-ended options that could extend indefinitely — as the judge noted, “Mr. Johnson could be 70 years old and still be bound to this contract” (wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Johnson, as of 2026-06-27)
  • Royalty rates below comparable UK labels
  • ZTT retained final say over producer choice and studio location
  • Recording costs incurred by ZTT exceeded what the agreement specified as “reasonable costs” — an independent breach the court identified (zttaat.com article 544, via search summary)

Ruling — 10 February 1988

UK High Court ruled in Johnson’s favour. The judge held that the 1983 recording and publishing agreements constituted an unreasonable restraint of trade and were therefore unenforceable against Johnson. (wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Johnson, as of 2026-06-27); (zttaat.com article 544)

Appeal — 26 July 1989

ZTT appealed. The Court of Appeal dismissed the appeal, concluding that the original recording and publishing contracts were “not a fair bargain.” (wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Johnson, as of 2026-06-27)

Industry impact

Contemporary press described the outcome as having “set a legal precedent which rocked the music business.” (zttaat.com article 544, via search summary) Following the ruling, ZTT released most of its remaining artists from their original contracts, including the other FGTH members. (wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Johnson, as of 2026-06-27)

The case became a reference point in subsequent UK recording-contract disputes. When George Michael sued Sony Music in 1994 (Panayiotou v Sony Music Entertainment (UK) Ltd), his legal team cited the Johnson precedent as the basis for a restraint-of-trade argument. The High Court distinguished the two cases: ZTT’s contracts had non-standard terms and Johnson had no real bargaining power in 1983 (FGTH had been passed over by most labels partly because of the open homosexuality of Johnson and Rutherford); Michael, by contrast, was a superstar with sophisticated legal counsel when he renegotiated with Sony in 1988. Sony prevailed in 1994. (victor-li.com/george-michael-lawsuit/); (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panayiotou_v_Sony_Music_Entertainment_(UK)_Ltd.)

Aftermath for Johnson

Freed from the ZTT contracts, Johnson signed with MCA Records. His debut solo album Blast (1989) reached UK #1. Singles “Love Train” and “Americanos” both reached UK top 5. (wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Johnson, as of 2026-06-27)
