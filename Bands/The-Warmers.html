The Warmers

Discogs ID: 309587 Active: July 1994 – December 1997 Origin: Washington, DC, USA Label: Dischord Records Genre: Post-hardcore / indie rock

Washington DC trio formed in July 1994 and active through December 1997. Consisted of Alec MacKaye (guitar, vocals), Amy Farina (drums), and Juan Carrera (bass, vocals). All studio recordings produced by Ian MacKaye and engineered by Don Zientara at Inner Ear Studios.

History

The Warmers formed in July 1994, five years after Alec MacKaye’s previous band Ignition (5) had dissolved. The new trio occupied a different sonic territory from MacKaye’s earlier hardcore work — slower, more spacious, closer to indie rock — but maintained the Dischord house approach: recorded at Inner Ear Studios in Arlington, Virginia, with Ian MacKaye producing and Don Zientara engineering.

The band’s first release was a two-sided 7” in 1995. The A-side, “Thin Air,” would reappear on the LP; the B-side, “Occupation: Fish,” would also be included there. The 7” appeared under a split catalog arrangement between Dischord (DIS 100.5) and a Not On Label release (W-001). In 1996 the band released their only full-length, the self-titled LP, on Dischord in both CD and vinyl formats. All fourteen tracks were co-written by all three members.

The band broke up in December 1997. After the split, Amy Farina became a founding member of The Evens (with Ian MacKaye) and later Coriky. Juan Carrera moved into recording engineering, working extensively with Slowdime Records artists before building a career in artist management via his firm Ravenhouse Ltd. Alec MacKaye remained largely inactive as a band member until Hammered Hulls in 2019.

In 2004, Dischord released Wanted: More, a posthumous six-track CD EP collecting material the band had recorded during their active years. It was mixed by Ian MacKaye and Don Zientara and released under catalog number DIS144CD.

Context and Connections

Alec MacKaye’s trajectory

MacKaye had been central to DC hardcore’s founding generation: the Untouchables (1979–1981), then Faith (3) (1982–1983) — whose split LP with Void on Dischord 8 is a key document of early DC hardcore — then Ignition (5) (1986–1989), whose Machination LP represented a more developed post-hardcore approach. There was a five-year gap between Ignition’s dissolution and The Warmers’ formation. The Warmers were quieter and more melodic than either predecessor band.

The MacKaye family production apparatus

Every Warmers release involves the same network that runs through nearly all of Alec MacKaye’s career: his brother Ian MacKaye as producer, Don Zientara as engineer, and Dischord Records as label. The Thin Air 7” is the partial exception — no producer or engineer is credited on Discogs, though the Dischord catalog number (DIS 100.5) connects it to the same infrastructure. The LP’s mastering came from Mayking (matrix reference “MASTERED BY MAYKING B”), while the 7” was mastered by Greg Vaughn (credited as “GSV”).

Amy Farina

Amy Farina later became a founding member of The Evens, the duo she formed with Ian MacKaye in 2001, and subsequently Coriky (2020), also with Ian MacKaye and Joe Lally. Her drumming in The Warmers was her most prominent role before The Evens.

Juan Carrera

After The Warmers, Juan Carrera (real name Juan Luis Carrera) built a significant career in independent music outside performance. He worked as a recording engineer and producer for numerous Slowdime Records and DC-area artists through the late 1990s and early 2000s — credits include recording work on releases by Hoover, The Lurid Traversal of Route 7 (Lungfish), and others in the Dischord/Slowdime orbit. He also did additional recording work on Modest Mouse’s Moon & Antarctica (Sub Pop, 1999 adjacent — Arches And Aisles). By the mid-2000s he transitioned to artist management through his company Ravenhouse Ltd., managing Modest Mouse (multiple releases on Epic through the Good News for People Who Love Bad News era), Bright Eyes / Conor Oberst (Merge Records), and The Hold Steady (Rough Trade / Saddle Creek). He also recorded releases by artists including Wilderness, Faraquet, and others in the Dischord adjacency zone during the late 1990s.
