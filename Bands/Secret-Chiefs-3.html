Secret Chiefs 3

Discogs ID: 134681
Active: c. 1993–present
Origin: San Francisco, California

Avant-garde experimental ensemble led by Trey Spruance (composer, multi-instrumentalist; also Mr. Bungle, Faith No More). Draws on surf, spy film scores, Middle Eastern music, metal, drone, and Sufi/Islamic cosmology. The project operates under several alias sub-groups, each corresponding to a different genre register: Ishraqiyun, Holy Vehm, FORMS, Traditionalists, The Electromagnetic Azoth, UR, Noddingturd Fan.

Core and frequent members: Eyvind Kang (violin, erhu), William Winant (percussion), Danny Heifetz (drums), Ches Smith (drums), Bär McKinnon (flute, saxophone), Trevor Dunn (bass), Shahzad Ismaily (bass), Jason Schimmel (guitar), Timb Harris (violin, trumpet), Toby Driver (bass).

Conceptual Framework

Ishraqiyyun / Ishraq Philosophy

SC3 is structured as a “band of bands” — seven satellite ensembles, each embodying a distinct genre and philosophical register, announced formally in 2007: Ishraqiyun, Holy Vehm, FORMS, Traditionalists, The Electromagnetic Azoth, UR, and NT Fan (Noddingturd Fan). Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-13

The name Ishraqiyun (also spelled Ishraqiyyun) derives from Ishraq — the “philosophy of illumination” associated with Shahab al-Din Suhrawardi (1154–1191), a Persian mystic-philosopher whom Spruance encountered through the scholarship of French phenomenologist Henry Corbin. Corbin termed Suhrawardi the “Hellenite magi” for synthesizing Neoplatonism with Zoroastrian mysticism. Spruance described the draw: “I was just reading Suhrawardi… a very practical outlook comes together in him between the rational intellect and the intuitive intellect.” Westword interview

Spruance frames the philosophy as directly generative of the music, not decorative: “The music itself comes from those ideas. The different tuning and rhythmic stuff we do is very literally based on those philosophies and are the real sub-stratum or the hermetic basis of the Secret Chiefs 3.” James Stafford interview The scales and tuning systems draw on quasi-Pythagorean and Eastern tonalities rather than equal temperament — Ishraqiyun specifically represents “quasi-Pythagorean tonalities, referring to the more Eastern tonal system.” [Westword]

Sub-group cosmology: Electromagnetic Azoth functions as the structural center (sound collage/ambient). Spruance has described Holy Vehm, NT Fan, and Electromagnetic Azoth as sharing one hermetic axis (Sun/Moon/Saturn), while Ishraqiyun, UR, and Traditionalists occupy another. Medium interview, 2017 UR is characterized as “suprasensory surf with roots in Anatolian psyche appropriations of Chaldean/Semitic themes, often running through the post-colonial conceptual filters of Krautrock.” Trebuchet Magazine Spruance explicitly rejects eclecticism as a reading: there is “a definite and quite rigidly-adhered-to superstructure to every element of SC3 music.” [Trebuchet]

Book of Angels / Masada Songbook Connection

Xaphan: Book of Angels Volume 9 (Tzadik, April 2008) arose from John Zorn’s “Masada Book Two” project, in which Zorn composed 300+ short pieces (typically ~16 bars of melody) drawn from Jewish liturgical and angelology sources and distributed them as lead sheets to various ensembles for independent arrangement and recording. SC3 was one such ensemble; Spruance arranged all eleven tracks and served as producer. The album title references Xaphan, a fallen angel figure from Jewish/Christian mythology — consistent with the series’ angelological naming convention. Wikipedia / Tzadik press

Tzadik’s label copy describes Spruance’s arrangements as drawing on “exotica, surf, Ethiopian funk, and gypsy swing” and calls it “one of the most compelling installments in the entire Masada series.” Personnel overlapped with Estradasphere and Mr. Bungle orbits: Ches Smith (drums), Shahzad Ismaily (bass), Timb Harris (violin/trumpet), Tim Smolens (cello/upright bass). Tzadik

SC3 subsequently contributed to Zorn’s third Masada book: Malkhut (2018) contains arrangements from Masada Book Three — “Book Beri’ah.” [Wikipedia] Spruance in 2017 referred to the third book as “a pretty big labour” he was prioritizing. [Medium, 2017]
