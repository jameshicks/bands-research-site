Big Bottom

Experimental electric bass ensemble. Formed New York City, 1997. Discogs artist ID: 275768.

Overview

Big Bottom was formed by Susan Stenger in New York City in 1997 as a strict conceptual project: five electric bass guitars and a drum machine, nothing else. The group exists at the intersection of experimental music and contemporary visual art — the core lineup beside Stenger comprises three figures better known in the art world than the music press: Canadian-born visual artist and Turner Prize nominee Angela Bulloch, Welsh conceptual artist and sculptor Cerith Wyn Evans, and Tom Gidley. Completed by UK bassist J. Mitch Flacko (also of Hagar The Womb), the quintet’s sound is built entirely from the low end, with no upper-register melodic instruments and no conventional drummer.

Part-time members have included Gina Birch of The Raincoats and Alexander Hacke of Einstürzende Neubauten, both of whom play or have played bass. The project reflects Stenger’s sustained interest in ensemble drone and texture: she developed similar ideas through her decade in Band Of Susans (the guitar-orchestra band she co-led with Robert Poss from 1986 to 1996) and continued them here by pushing the concept toward a mono-timbral extreme.

Big Bottom is referenced in the Wikipedia article on Band of Susans as one of Stenger’s post-BOS projects alongside collaborations with choreographer Michael Clark, author Iain Sinclair, and Cerith Wyn Evans directly (Wikipedia: Band of Susans).

No releases by Big Bottom are documented in the Discogs database as of this research. The project appears to operate primarily in live and installation contexts.
