Smegma

Discogs ID: 67352 Name variation: The Smegma Group Formed: 1973, Pasadena, California Based: Pasadena / Los Angeles (1973–), Portland, Oregon (later base) Genre: Experimental, Noise, Free Improvisation, Avant-Garde Associated label: Soleilmoon Recordings, Los Angeles Free Music Society, Pigface Records, Tim-Kerr Records, Important Records (2)

Biography

Origins: Pasadena and the LAFMS, 1973–1979

Smegma formed in Pasadena, California in 1973, coalescing around a loose collective approach to music-making that rejected conventional rock structures in favor of free improvisation, noise, found-sound manipulation, and home-recorded tape collage. They were early participants in—and contributors to—the Los Angeles Free Music Society (LAFMS), the cooperative network of Pasadena-area experimentalists that also included Tom Recchion, Doo-Dooettes, and others. The LAFMS aesthetic—self-released cassettes and LPs, group improvisation, a DIY ethic before the term was widespread—was central to Smegma’s formation.

The core early lineup crystallized around Ju Suk Reet Meate (real name Eric Stewart), who became the group’s most consistent long-term member and primary organizer. Other early participants included Dr. Id (Mike Lastra / Michael Lastra), Amazon Bambi (Amy DeWolfe), D.K. (Brad Hostetler), Harry Cess Poole, Cheez-it-Ritz, and Danton Dodge.

Their debut LP, Glamour Girl 1941 (1979), released on the LAFMS label (cat. LAFMS-008), documents the early sound: side-long improvisations built from saxophone, bass, organ, drums, tape manipulation, and voices. Track-level personnel vary throughout, reflecting the open-ensemble logic the group would maintain across their entire career. Istvan Kantor (later known as Monty Cantsin) appears on one track.

Early LPs and the Pigface Era, 1980–1989

In 1980 Smegma appeared on a split 7” with NON (Boyd Rice) released on Mute (cat. MUTE 007), exposing them to the UK industrial/experimental audience. The single contains two Smegma tracks (“Can’t Look Straight,” “Flash Cards”) alongside NON material featuring Boyd Rice and Robert Turman.

Pigs For Lepers (1982, Pigface Records, cat. Pigface Records 007) is the group’s second LP, produced and engineered by Dr. Id / Michael Lastra. The record shows the collective at full range: free-jazz improvisation, noise guitar, tape manipulation, and extended vocals. Personnel on that album include Amazon Bambi (bass, vocals, violin), Harry Cess Poole (guitar, vocals), Ju Suk Reet Meate (guitar, trumpet, effects), D.K. (guitar, percussion), Dr. Id (tape effects), Dr. Odd (vocals), Mike King, Craig Magyorody, and Jackie Stewart.

In 1987 Smegma contributed tape material to the Selektion compilation The Impossible Humane (Germany), appearing alongside other experimental figures. Then in 1988 came the pivotal release: Morass (Soleilmoon Recordings, cat. SOL-01), a cassette-only document of live performances from 1985–87 plus studio material. This was the first numbered release on Soleilmoon Recordings, making Smegma foundational to that label’s catalog. The cassette lists performers as Amazon Bambi, Big Dirty, Dr. Id, Jackie Stewart, Jerry A., Ju Suk Reet Meate, Marla, Paul Rioux, and V.D. Production and engineering credited to Michael Lastra (Mike Lastra); photography and design by Ju Suk.

Also in 1988: Nattering Naybobs Of Negativity (Dead Man’s Curve, UK), with UK label contact David Uden and Steve Underwood involved.

Idiodyssey (1989, Rockport Records) and Smell The Remains (1989, Dom, Germany — a compilation) round out the decade.

Portland Years and Tim/Kerr Period, 1991–1997

By the early 1990s the group had a Portland, Oregon base. The 7” Swampdick (1991, Sympathy For The Record Industry, cat. SFTRI 65) features the quartet of Ju Suk Reet Meate, Amazon Bambi, Burned Mind (Bernd Minde), Dr. Id, Samek Cosmano, Rock ‘N’ Roll Jackie (Jackie Stewart), and Big Dirty; produced by Michael Lastra.

Ism (1993, Tim-Kerr Records, cat. TK92CD027) is their most extensively personnel-documented studio album. The full ensemble on that CD:

Live 1991 Thru 1993 (1994, Pigface Records) followed, with guest appearances by Pig Champion (Tom Roberts / Tom Pig) of Poison Idea.

Smegma Plays Merzbow Plays Smegma (1996, Tim/Kerr Records, cat. TK96CD140) is a two-part exchange collaboration with Japanese noise artist Merzbow / Masami Akita: Merzbow processes Smegma tapes (“Electro Smegmacoustic Music,” 19:37), Smegma processes Merzbow tapes (“Smegma Plays Merzbow,” 19:27).

The Mad Excitement, The Barbaric Pulsations, The Incomparable Rhythms Of… (1996, Tim/Kerr) features recording credits to Steven Birch and design by Bruce Licher.

In 1997 Smegma released a split 7” with Japanese label G.R.O.S.S. (Clitoridis / Praeputil), with Stan Wood (alias Conroy (3)) appearing in the lineup.

2000s: De Stijl, Wolf Eyes, Writer-Vocalist Period

Author and rock critic Richard Meltzer joined as vocalist in the late 1990s, documented on the live release Live At The New Paris, Portland, Org. 1/25/98 (1998). He also appears on The Tropic Of Nipples EP (2002, Off Records) and The Completed Soundtrack For The Tropic Of Nipples (2002), the latter also featuring Robert Pollard (Guided by Voices), Chris Slusarenko, and Jeff Stuart Saltzman.

The Beast (2003, De Stijl, cat. IND 036) is a Smegma & Wolf Eyes split LP—a landmark in the noise underground. The full performance roster:

2010s Onward: Continued Activity

Tom Recchion (LAFMS, Doo-Dooettes) appears here, connecting back to the collective’s origins. Dutch lutenist Jozef Van Wissem is an unexpected collaborator.

Wild Blue Yonders (2012, iDEAL Recordings, Sweden) features Madelyn Villano, William Cyrus Ford, and Rogue Iiniki alongside the core. Villano is also a member of Giggles (4) (with Alieta Herrera-Train, also a Smegma member) and records as solo project Guzo.

40 Years Of Smegma (2015, Otoroku, UK) documents the anniversary tour set; the credited composers and performers are Ace Farren Ford, Dennis Duck, Ju Suk Reet Meate, Madelyn Villano, and Rock And Roll Jackie.

Smegma Vs. Black Leather Jesus (2017, Axis Mundi) pairs Smegma with Texas noise project Black Leather Jesus (Richard Ramirez). Further releases include Ballast (2019, Helicopter), Sub-Underground (2022, E-Klageto, Germany), and Folklore & Concepts (2025, Helicopter).

Smegma were listed on the Nurse With Wound list (the influential 1979 Steven Stapleton reference list of obscure experimental records), and were featured on the cover of The Wire in August 2006.

Connections

  • Los Angeles Free Music Society — Smegma were participants in the LAFMS network from the beginning; Glamour Girl 1941 appeared on that cooperative label
  • Soleilmoon Recordings — Morass (SOL-01, 1988) was the label’s inaugural numbered release
  • Tom Recchion — LAFMS figure (Doo-Dooettes), collaborator on Mirage
  • Merzbow — 1996 exchange-collaboration LP on Tim/Kerr
  • Wolf Eyes — split LP The Beast (2003, De Stijl); both groups were central to the early 2000s US noise underground
  • Richard Meltzer — author/rock critic, became Smegma vocalist from ~1998; also credited as Borneo Jimmy and Mr. Vom
  • Boyd Rice / NON — split 7” on Mute (1980), early UK industrial connection
  • Nurse With Wound — included Smegma on the influential 1979 NWW list
  • Jozef Van Wissem — Dutch lutenist, collaborated on Mirage (2010)
  • John Wiese — LA noise/experimental; collaborated on multiple releases
  • Pig Champion / Tom Roberts — Poison Idea guitarist, appeared on Live 1991 Thru 1993
