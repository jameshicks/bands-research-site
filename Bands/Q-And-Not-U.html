Q And Not U

American post-hardcore / dance-punk band formed in Washington, D.C., 1998; disbanded 2005. Discogs artist ID: 309597.

Released three albums on Dischord Records, moving from angular post-hardcore on their debut toward increasingly synth-driven dance-punk and electronic textures. Widely regarded as one of Dischord’s most adventurous late-period acts.

Biography

Formation (1998–1999)

Q And Not U formed in Washington, D.C. in 1998. The founding core was guitarist/vocalist Christopher Richards, guitarist/vocalist Harris Klahr, drummer/vocalist John Davis, and bassist Matt Borlik. All four came out of the DC underground scene orbiting Dischord Records and Ian MacKaye. The band’s name is a play on formal logic notation (Q ∧ ¬U), signalling the nervy, intellectual edge that would mark their records.

No Kill No Beep Beep era (2000–2001)

The debut full-length No Kill No Beep Beep (Dischord, 2000) was recorded and produced by Don Zientara and Ian MacKaye at Inner Ear Studios. It’s a sharply angular post-hardcore record — interlocking guitar lines, fractured time signatures, all four members trading vocal duties. The sleeve was shot by photographer Shawn Brackbill, who would continue with the band throughout their career. Design was credited to 17+ (-21).

Also in 2000, the band released the split EP Hot And Informed on DeSoto Records / Dischord, again recorded at Inner Ear by Zientara and MacKaye (MacKaye listed as uncredited producer). Graphics on that release were handled by J. Robbins and the band.

At this stage the lineup was: Richards, Klahr, Davis, Borlik.

Different Damage era (2002–2003)

By 2002, Matt Borlik had left; the band continued as a trio (Richards, Klahr, Davis). Different Damage (Dischord, 2002) was again produced and engineered by Zientara and MacKaye, with mastering by Chad Clark (of Beauty Pill and Smart Went Crazy). Assembly / packaging was credited to Jeff Nelson and the band; design by Q And Not U themselves; photography by Shawn Brackbill.

The record marks the pivot point: the post-hardcore architecture is still present but synths, more open rhythmic space, and danceable grooves begin entering the mix. It was followed by the EP On Play Patterns (Dischord, 2002), same trio with Zientara / MacKaye at the recording desk, and the double-A-side 7″ X-Polynation / Book Of Flags (Dischord, 2003), with layout by Jennifer Krako.

Power era and dissolution (2004–2005)

Power (Dischord, 2004) is the full electronic turn — the trio leaning heavily into synthesizers, drum machines, and dance-floor energy. For the first time the record was not produced by MacKaye or Zientara; recording was handled by Pete Cafarella and Rafael Cohen, with Don Zientara mixing and Chad Clark mastering. Photography again by Shawn Brackbill; layout by Nick Pimentel.

The band played their final show in 2005 and quietly issued the one-sided 12″ / digital release Wonderful People (self-released, 2005) as a coda. They disbanded that same year.

After Q And Not U

  • Chris Richards (also known as Ris Paul Ric) went on to The Elusive, Paint Branch, and solo work as Chris Richards (4).
  • John Davis formed Georgie James (with Laura Burhenn), later Title Tracks, and also played in The Elusive and Paint Branch.
  • Harris Klahr was later associated with Clear Band.
  • Matt Borlik does not appear to have had further prominent recording credits after Q And Not U.

DC Scene Connections

Q And Not U were firmly embedded in the Dischord / DC hardcore continuum:

  • Dischord Records — their label throughout, the central institution of DC post-hardcore.
  • Inner Ear Studios — recording home for the first two LPs and most EPs, operated by Don Zientara.
  • Ian MacKaye — co-produced and recorded the first two LPs; MacKaye is the founder of Dischord and played in Minor Threat, Fugazi, and later The Evens.
  • Jeff Nelson — co-founder of Dischord Records alongside MacKaye; credited on Different Damage packaging.
  • J. Robbins — DC post-hardcore veteran (Jawbox, Burning Airlines); did graphics for Hot And Informed.
  • Chad Clark — mastered both Different Damage and Power; Clark is best known as the frontman of Beauty Pill and formerly Smart Went Crazy, two other DC outfits.
  • Member overlaps: Richards and Davis both subsequently played in The Elusive and Paint Branch; Davis formed Georgie James with Laura Burhenn.

DeSoto Records / Dischord co-release of Hot And Informed (2000)

The DeSoto / Dischord co-release of Hot And Informed is rooted in a long-standing scene relationship, not a one-off arrangement. DeSoto Records was nominally founded in 1989 by members of the DC band Edsel; Kim Coletta (bassist of Jawbox) later took over the name, and DeSoto became Jawbox’s own imprint for small releases. Jawbox’s very first 7” — Tools & Chrome (1990) — was itself co-released on DeSoto / Dischord, establishing a direct precedent for the same dual-label format used on Hot And Informed a decade later. Jawbox then recorded two full albums on Dischord (Grippe, 1992; Novelty, 1992) before moving to Atlantic, retaining an amicable relationship with MacKaye/Dischord throughout. source: dischord.com/band/jawbox, as of 2026-07-27

By the late 1990s, Dischord was actively distributing several DC-area indie labels; Dischord’s own history page names DeSoto (alongside Slowdime, Simple Machines, Teenbeat, Superbad, Resin, and others) as labels it felt “connected to” and distributed. source: dischord.com/history/page03, as of 2026-07-27

DeSoto was run by Kim Coletta and Bill Barbot (both ex-Jawbox); Barbot had also been a member of Edsel, the band that originally founded the DeSoto name. DeSoto’s operational model was explicitly modelled on Dischord’s contract-free, DIY ethics. source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeSoto_Records, as of 2026-07-27

In sum: the Hot And Informed co-release follows the exact same pattern as Jawbox’s 1990 debut — DeSoto and Dischord had an established, friendly dual-label relationship rooted in shared DC-scene membership, mutual distribution, and a decade of working history before Q and Not U’s single. No press source located explains why specifically Q and Not U’s first single (and only that release) used the co-badge rather than going straight to Dischord alone; the most likely explanation is that DeSoto, as an already-active DC label distributed by Dischord, simply took a co-release slot on the first single — consistent with how both labels had operated since 1990.

No press source found that states explicitly why Hot And Informed specifically was co-released while all subsequent Q and Not U releases were Dischord-only. This remains an open narrow question: was it a deliberate DeSoto initiative, or Dischord’s standard practice for debut singles of new acts at that moment?
