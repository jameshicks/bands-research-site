Sub Sub

Discogs ID: 26531
Name variation: SubSub
Active: 1990–1997
Alias/successor: Doves (same lineup, post-1997)

Manchester dance and house group. Three members — Jimi Goodwin, Jez Williams, and Andy Williams (4) — formed the core; vocalist Melanie Williams was a prominent featured collaborator. All three met at school and became regulars at The Hacienda (Discogs label ID: 309717). Signed first to Ten Records (debut single “Space Face”, 1991), then to Robs Records (Discogs ID: 4225), the Manchester independent founded by Rob Gretton. After a studio fire destroyed most of their equipment in the mid-1990s, the trio reformed as Doves.

Sub Sub appear on Disc 7 of the 2017 Cherry Red compilation Manchester North Of England (A Story Of Independent Music Greater Manchester 1977 - 1993) (CRCDBOX38), contributing “Space Face” — the track that first brought them to wider attention in 1991.

Origins and Formation

Jimi Goodwin (born Jamie Francis Alexander Goodwin), Jez Williams (Jeremy Francis Williams), and Andy Williams (Andrew Sebastion Williams) grew up in the Manchester area and met as schoolmates. Their immersion in the Haçienda’s acid house and rave scene directly shaped their approach: they resolved to make a record that would earn them a place on the guest list. The debut single “Space Face” was recorded and pressed up using money from their mothers.

The three members’ Discogs aliases reflect their later solo directions: Goodwin records as Coup Diablo; Jez Williams uses Deviation; Andy Williams and Jez Williams together formed Black Rivers during Doves’ hiatus.

Discography

Ten Records period (1991)

“Space Face” / “Space Face (Todd Terry Remixes)” (Ten Records, 1991)
Debut single. Self-produced; engineered by Nick Garside. Todd Terry (Discogs ID: 4116) provided the remixes on a companion 12”. The original release entered Discogs master 118665 with nine pressing variants. Featured on the Manchester North Of England Cherry Red compilation (Disc 7, position 7-6).

Ten Records dropped the band shortly after the single. They moved to Robs Records.

Note on “Ain’t No Love (Ain’t No Use)”: The 1993 release is the group’s commercial peak, reaching the UK Top 10. It was co-written equally by all four names on the label (Goodwin, J. Williams, A. Williams, and Melanie Williams) and Melanie Williams is credited as “Featuring, Vocals [Uncredited]” on the Discogs additional credits.

“This Time I’m Not Wrong” (1997): Co-headline credit with Bernard Sumner of New Order / Electronic. The connection is notable: Goodwin, Jez Williams, and Andy Williams all appear on Sumner’s solo/collaborative activity on Robs Records, and Sumner’s association with the label (via his manager Rob Gretton) makes this a natural convergence. See also the Bernard Sumner vault page for production notes on Twisted Tenderness (1999), where Jimi Goodwin appears as a session player.

Australia / Late-period (1998)

Delta Tapes (Cortex, Australia, 1998) — Compilation/anthology of Sub Sub material; Discogs master 118661, release ID 151892. Portuguese pressings also exist (Robs Records, Megamusica).

Studio Fire and the Transition to Doves

In the mid-to-late 1990s, a fire destroyed most of the band’s studio equipment. This event is cited by Discogs (and by the band in interviews) as the catalyst for a clean break and a fresh start under the name Doves. The transition occurred around 1997–1998: the final Sub Sub release is “This Time I’m Not Wrong” (1997), and the first Doves release is the Cedar EP (Casino label, a Robs Records sublabel, 1998).

The three core members — Goodwin, Jez Williams, Andy Williams — are identical across both incarnations.
