Deadline (5)

Washington DC hardcore band. Discogs artist ID: 287298. Active summer 1981 – September 1982.

Early DC scene band that included Brendan Canty before his tenure in Rites Of Spring. Their sole release is a live recording of a show from August 2, 1982, released in 1989 on Peterbilt Records (cat. Peterbilt 2). Guy Picciotto operated Peterbilt, and he was a close personal associate of Deadline from the band’s earliest days — see “Picciotto connection” below.

Picciotto connection and why Peterbilt released them

Peterbilt Records was founded by Guy Picciotto in 1988, initially to release a memorial 12” for Happy Go Licky, the short-lived band he formed after Rites Of Spring with Brendan Canty, Mike Fellows, and Eddie Janney. (dischord.com/band/happy-go-licky)

Picciotto’s connection to Deadline predates Peterbilt by years. According to the Dischord bio for Deadline, Picciotto and Fellows were “barnacled to Brendan’s first band, Deadline” from the beginning — they would rush the stage at the tail end of Deadline shows, grab the instruments, and improvise until the plug was pulled. (dischord.com/band/deadline) Separately, Canty has described first meeting Picciotto at age 16, with Picciotto becoming “a lifelong artistic brother.” (15questions.net)

Peterbilt therefore released 8/2/82 as an archival project rooted in personal loyalty: Picciotto was an eyewitness participant in Deadline’s scene, Canty was his closest long-term collaborator, and Peterbilt’s broader catalog (Rain, Deadline, One Last Wish, Black Light Panthers) is explicitly made up of DC bands from within that same circle. The label’s stated purpose for the Deadline release was to rescue “one of the great lost gems of DC punk” from the archive.

Mark Prindle interview — Picciotto on Deadline (web research, 2026-10-27)

The Mark Prindle interview with Guy Picciotto at markprindle.com/picciotto-i.htm is accessible and does contain quotes about Deadline, but does not give a specific explanation of why the tapes were released in 1989. The relevant passages:

On Peterbilt’s general approach: “It’s kind of a weird label. I was doing it even before Fugazi started, when I was in a band called Happy Go Licky. I put out a live EP by that band.” Then on Deadline specifically: “Sometimes I put out some old archival tapes like this great band Brendan was in called Deadline.”

On Deadline’s quality: “Flex Your Head, yeah. This stuff beats any of their stuff on Flex Your Head. That was the floor I came in on — Deadline was the band I was really down with.”

On the release being out of print: “Oh yeah! It came out a long time ago. It’s actually out of print now. I’m thinking about reissuing it though.” (This aligns with the 2007 CD reissue.)

On pressing quantities: “I usually do 1000 copies of each pressing, and they come in these manila envelopes that I handcut. It’s actually a fuckin pain in the ass, but they look cool!”

The interview confirms Picciotto’s personal enthusiasm for Deadline and his framing of the release as “archival,” but offers no direct statement about the timing decision (why 1989 specifically). No other interview with Picciotto or Canty found in this pass contains a more specific explanation of the release timing.
