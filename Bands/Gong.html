Gong

British/French psychedelic and progressive rock band, founded by Daevid Allen and Gilli Smyth in Paris, France, in 1969. Discogs artist ID: 10346. Active 1969–present (with hiatuses and many lineup changes). Pioneers of space rock and the psychedelic underground; associated with the Canterbury scene.

Overview

Gong emerged from the Paris bohemian underground in 1969, drawing on Australian-born guitarist Daevid Allen’s earlier connections to Soft Machine and the pre-Canterbury collective Wilde Flowers. Allen had been denied re-entry to the UK in 1967 (his passport was seized at the French border) and chose to remain in France, forming Gong as a loose commune-band. The group developed an elaborate mythology — the “Radio Gnome Invisible” cosmology of Zero the Hero, the Pot Head Pixies, and the Planet Gong — that would run through their most celebrated work.

The band’s long history divides roughly into three distinct eras: the Allen-led space-rock period (1969–1975), the jazz-fusion transition under Pierre Moerlen (1975–1980), and the post-1990 reunion and continuation period under various configurations, culminating in the Kavus Torabi-led band that has recorded and toured since 2014.

History

Formation and early years in Paris (1969–1972)

Daevid Allen had been a founding member of Soft Machine alongside Robert Wyatt and Kevin Ayers, but was barred from returning to Britain after a 1967 European tour. He and his partner Gilli Smyth settled in France and formed Gong as a free-spirited commune-collective in the Paris underground.

The debut album Magick Brother (1969, BYG Records) established the core of the early band: Allen on guitar and vocals, Smyth on “space whisper” vocals, Didier Malherbe on flute and saxophone, with shifting supporting casts including drummer Rachid Houari and bassist Christian Tritsch (“The Submarine Captain”). The BYG label — a Paris-based free-jazz imprint — also released the follow-up Camembert Electrique (1971), which was recorded with Pip Pyle on drums. These early records were produced by Jean Georgakarakos and Jean-Luc Young for BYG.

In 1971 Gong recorded the soundtrack to Jerome Laperrousaz’s motorcycle documentary Continental Circus for Philips — an early instance of the band’s cinematic reach.

The Radio Gnome Invisible trilogy on Virgin (1973–1974)

The creative peak of the Allen-era band is the three-LP Radio Gnome Invisible trilogy, recorded for Virgin Records with producer Giorgio Gomelsky:

  1. Flying Teapot (Radio Gnome Invisible Part 1) (1973, Virgin V 2002) — Introduced Steve Hillage (“Stevie Hillside”) on guitar alongside the core of Allen (vocals/glissando guitar), Smyth (space whisper), Malherbe (flute/sax), Tim Blake (synthesizer/Crystal Machine), and Laurie Allan (drums). Key tracks: “Radio Gnome Invisible,” “Flying Teapot,” “Zero The Hero And The Witch’s Spell.”

  2. Angel’s Egg (Radio Gnome Invisible Part 2) (1973, Virgin V 2007) — Pierre Moerlen joined on drums and percussion; Hillage continued on guitar. The album deepened the “Zero the Hero” mythological arc. Malherbe credited under his alias “Bloomdido Bad de Grass.”

  3. You (1974, Virgin) — Completed the trilogy with a lineup of Allen, Hillage, Malherbe, Blake, Moerlen (with siblings Benoit Moerlen and Mireille Bauer on additional percussion), Mike Howlett on bass, and Smyth on vocals. The album was self-produced, with Simon Heyworth engineering. Tracks include “Master Builder,” “A Sprinkling Of Clouds,” “The Isle Of Everywhere,” and the epic “You Never Blow Yr Trip Forever.”

The trilogy is considered the definitive statement of the Allen-era band: an immersive, free-associative cosmic mythology expressed through extended psychedelic jams, Hillage’s fluid guitar, Blake’s electronic textures, and Smyth’s poetry.

Steve Hillage had come to Gong via Khan (3) and was associated with the Canterbury scene through the Uriel / Arzachel lineage. His presence from 1972 onwards was central to the trilogy sound. Tim Blake brought the synthesizer dimension, drawing on his Crystal Machine apparatus. Both Hillage and Blake were also involved in the concurrent Paragong offshoot.

Giorgio Gomelsky, the veteran promoter who had worked with the early Rolling Stones and Yardbirds, produced Flying Teapot and Angel’s Egg, helping crystallize the band’s approach for the label context.

Transition and the Pierre Moerlen era (1975–1980)

Daevid Allen left Gong in 1975, departing during the recording of Shamal (1975, Virgin). Gilli Smyth had already departed, and Steve Hillage left shortly after. The band continued under the effective leadership of drummer Pierre Moerlen, moving in a jazz-fusion direction.

Shamal (1975, Virgin) — first album without Allen; Moerlen, Malherbe, Howlett, and new members.

Gazeuse! (1976, Virgin V 2074) — the high-water mark of the Moerlen era; a tightly constructed jazz-fusion record featuring Allan Holdsworth on electric and acoustic guitar, Francis Moze on bass, Mireille Bauer and Benoit Moerlen on marimba/vibraphone/glockenspiel, Mino Cinelu on percussion, and Didier Malherbe on flute and saxophone. Produced by Dennis Mackay. The album moves far from the psychedelic mythology toward percussive, Mahavishnu-influenced writing.

Expresso II (1978, Virgin) — continuation of the fusion direction; Holdsworth was gone, replaced by Bon Lozaga.

The live document Gong Est Mort (1977, Tapioca) captured the transitional period on stage. By this point the band was increasingly performing and recording as Pierre Moerlen’s Gong, which continued into the early 1980s under Moerlen’s name on Arista and other labels.

Allan Holdsworth’s participation on Gazeuse! represents one of the clearest crystallizations of his style in a group setting.

Post-Allen spinoffs and offshoots (1975–1990)

Following Allen’s departure, several parallel projects emerged from the Gong world:

  • Planet Gong (2) — Allen’s immediate post-Gong project, incorporating the Here & Now collective, active late 1970s.
  • New York Gong — Allen’s New York-based group, which included members of the no wave underground including Bill Laswell. Released About Time (1979/1980, Charly CRL 5021).
  • Mother Gong — Gilli Smyth’s own group, formed late 1970s. Debut Fairy Tales (1979, Charly CRL 5018). Continued sporadically under Smyth’s leadership.
  • Pierre Moerlen’s Gong — the fusion continuation under Moerlen’s leadership, officially separate from Gong but drawing on the same pool of musicians. Active through the early 1980s.

Daevid Allen continued as a solo artist (the N’Existe Pas! LP, 1979, Charly CRL 5015) and formed groups including Brainville and Gongmaison throughout the 1980s.

Reunions and the 1990s–2000s phase

Gong reconvened in various configurations beginning in the early 1990s, often around anniversary tours and festivals. Key releases from this period:

  • Shapeshifter (1992, Celluloid) — Allen-era reunion recording
  • The Birthday Party (1995, GAS Records / Voiceprint) — live document of the 25th anniversary
  • Zero To Infinity (2000, One Eyed Salmon Records) — studio album with a lineup including Allen, Dave Sturt (bass), Cheb Nettles (drums), and Theo Travis (wind instruments)
  • 2032 (2009, Wakyo Records) — further studio work
  • High Above The Subterania Club 2000 (2002, Snapper Music) — live document

The late 1990s–2000s lineup centered on Allen, bassist Dave Sturt, and drummer Cheb Nettles, with varying guests.

The Kavus Torabi era and continuation (2014–present)

Daevid Allen returned to active work with Gong in 2014 for the album I See You (Madfish SMACD1023), a comeback LP featuring Allen on vocals and guitar, Kavus Torabi on guitar, Fabio Golfetti on guitar, Dave Sturt on bass, Orlando Allen on drums, Ian East on winds, and Gilli Smyth as a guest vocalist (returning for the track “Shakti Yoni & Dingo Virgin”). The album was co-produced by Orlando Allen.

Daevid Allen died on 13 March 2015, at the age of 77, in Byron Bay, Australia. He had been diagnosed with cancer.

Following Allen’s death, the surviving members decided to continue Gong with Kavus Torabi taking over as vocalist and co-frontman. Torabi — already well established through Knifeworld and Cardiacs — had been a member of the 2014 lineup.

  • Rejoice! I’m Dead! (2016, Madfish) — first album without Allen; Torabi-led lineup of himself, Golfetti, Sturt, Orlando Allen, and East
  • The Universe Also Collapses (2019, Kscope KSCOPE1039) — LP pressing; lineup: Torabi (vocals, guitar), Golfetti (guitar), Sturt (bass), Cheb Nettles (drums), Ian East (flute, saxophones, clarinet). Produced by the band with Frank Byng engineering.
  • Pulsing Signals (Live) (2022, Kscope) — live document
  • Unending Ascending (2023, self-released) — studio album
  • Bright Spirit (2026, Kscope) — most recent studio release

Canterbury scene context

Gong’s relationship to the Canterbury scene is partially by membership overlap, partially by shared geography (France rather than Canterbury, but with the same UK musicians rotating through), and partially by mythology. Daevid Allen was present at the formation of Soft Machine; Pip Pyle brought his Canterbury drumming to the early records before moving to Hatfield and the North; Steve Hillage connected through Khan (3) and Uriel. The “Radio Gnome Invisible” trilogy is often cited alongside Soft Machine’s Third and Fourth, Hatfield and the North’s debut, and National Health’s records as defining documents of the Canterbury/prog-adjacent underground.

The Discogs profile confirms the description: “They are considered pioneers of psychedelic rock, and were associated with the Canterbury scene.”
