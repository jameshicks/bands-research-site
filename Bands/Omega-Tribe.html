From Discogs

Melodic anarcho-punk band. Originally formed in New Barnet, London, England, UK in summer 1981 as Deadly Game. They changed their name to Omega Tribe before they started gigging, playing their first show at the Monkfrith Boy’s Club that year, with the following lineup:

Hugh Vivian (ex-Comatose): guitar and vocals Daryl Hardcastle (ex-Woosh!): bass Pete Shepherd: drums

For their second show, they played in a support slot for The Meteors at the 100 Club in London.

Their first demo was recorded in March 1982. It found its way to the member of Crass, who selected the song “Nature Wonder” for the compilation. They recruited Pete Fender (son of Poison Girls frontwoman Vi Subversa) on guitar later that year, having met him through Daryl’s fanzine “The Realities of Society”. With this lineup they promptly recorded their first EP, “Angry Songs”, which was produced by Penny Rimbaud and Fender for Crass Records. When it was released in April 1983 the single did well on the Indie Chart. This success was eclipsed by the follow-up LP “No Love Lost”, released on Corpus Christi and recorded for a mere £300 at a 24-track studio called Heart and Soul.

Lineup and direction changes dogged the band over the next year or so: Fender quit in early 1984, Sonny Flint joined on drums while Shepherd switched to percussion, and a woman called Jane joined on sax and flute. Overview

Omega Tribe were a melodic anarcho-punk band from New Barnet, London, formed in summer 1981 originally under the name Deadly Game. They broke through via Crass Records, with their debut EP “Angry Songs” (1983) produced by Penny Rimbaud — a connection that came through Pete Fender, son of Poison Girls frontwoman Vi Subversa — followed by the LP “No Love Lost” on Corpus Christi. Lineup changes through 1983–84 curtailed their initial run, with the final single “It’s A Hard Life” (1984) marking the close of their first era. After a long dormancy, the band returned with four releases on Grow Your Own Records between 2020 and 2025, with original members Hugh Vivian and Daryl Hardcastle joined by newer collaborators including Dan Sansom, Emma Page, and Keith Gilles.

Notes (web)

2016 reformation — why it happened

The reformation was spontaneous rather than driven by any anniversary or external event. According to the September 2016 interview at Mill Hill Music Complex (The Hippies Now Wear Black, 2016-09-15):

  • Sonny Flint had first floated the idea of reforming as early as 2012, but Daryl Hardcastle did not act on it until 2016.
  • When the moment came, Daryl’s reply to Sonny was simply: “OK, let’s have a jam then.” He then invited Hugh Vivian to a Sunday rehearsal and Hugh agreed.
  • Hugh himself described the timing as “pretty random”; Daryl confirmed: “Yep.”
  • Daryl’s stated philosophy: “We may as well have a laugh. We will all be dead one day.”
  • A Facebook page was set up almost immediately after the first rehearsal, suggesting no prior planning.

The August 2016 announcement (The Hippies Now Wear Black, 2016-08-17) gave no reason for the reunion either — it simply stated that “Band members Hugh, Daryl and Sonny have reunited in August 2016 and are currently in rehearsals.”

Summary: No specific catalyst, anniversary, or external event prompted the return. The seeds were planted by Sonny Flint in 2012; Daryl finally said yes in 2016, and momentum took over from there.
