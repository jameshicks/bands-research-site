Japan

Discogs artist ID: 23036 Genre: Art rock / New wave / Post-punk / Synth-pop Origin: Catford, South London, England Years active: 1974–1982; reunited as Rain Tree Crow 1991

New wave and art rock band formed in Catford, South London in early 1974. Prominent figures in the New Romantic movement by the time of their break-up, despite predating it. Known for a dramatic stylistic evolution from glam-inflected punk-era rock through sophisticated art-pop and electronic minimalism.

History

Formation and early years (1974–1977)

Japan formed in Catford, South London around 1974, though they did not record until 1977. The lineup crystallized as Sylvian, his younger brother Jansen, Karn, Barbieri, and guitarist Rob Dean. In the mid-1970s the band developed a glam-rock and proto-punk influenced sound, with Sylvian’s androgynous image modeled partly on David Bowie and Roxy Music.

They recorded a set of demos in 1977 — released much later as Demos 77 (Relax & Swing, 2021) — and entered a label-sponsored talent contest run by Hansa Records in 1977. Wikipedia and the fan biography lifeintokyo.net both state that Japan came runner-up (losing to The Cure, not winners), but the deal with Hansa/Ariola-Hansa followed regardless. The exact mechanism — whether runners-up were also offered deals, or whether Hansa signed Japan separately — is not detailed in available sources. Conflict: the existing Discogs-era text (from the trawler run) stated they “won” the contest; Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-05, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_(band)) and lifeintokyo.net (https://www.lifeintokyo.net/biography.html) both say they came runner-up to The Cure. The deal came through in 1977 and the band signed with Ariola-Hansa.

Hansa period (1978–1979): Adolescent Sex, Obscure Alternatives, Quiet Life

Adolescent Sex (1978, Ariola Hansa AHAL 8004) was produced by Ray Singer, who went uncredited on the UK pressing. A glam and punk-influenced debut, it found little traction in the UK but performed modestly in Japan and parts of Europe. Songwriting credited to Sylvian (tracks A1–B4).

Obscure Alternatives (1978, Ariola Hansa ZCAHA 8007) was also produced by Ray Singer, engineered by Chris Tsangarides. All tracks composed by Sylvian. The album continued the band’s glam-rock orientation while incorporating more experimental textures.

Life In Tokyo (1979, Ariola Hansa single) marked a pivot: produced by Giorgio Moroder and co-written by Sylvian and Moroder, the single introduced a disco-influenced electronic sheen. It was the band’s first serious commercial entry point and pointed to the direction the band would develop.

Quiet Life (Ariola Hansa AHAL 8011) saw the band working with producer John Punter and engineer Colin Fairley. It remains the pivotal transitional record — the glam influences were almost entirely shed in favor of a cooler, more textured sound incorporating synthesizers (Barbieri), saxophone (Karn), and orchestration (string arrangements by Ann Odell, conducted by Martyn Ford). Rob Dean contributed guitar throughout. David Sylvian played occasional guitar and lead vocals. The album found its audience in Japan more than the UK, though it was later reissued repeatedly as recognition of the band grew in Britain.

Release dates: First released 7 December 1979 in Canada, Japan (the country), and the Netherlands on Hansa; UK release followed on 18 January 1980. The Discogs catalog number AHAL 8011 is the UK pressing, which dates to 1980. (Source: Wikipedia, Quiet Life article, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiet_Life, as of 2026-06-05; Orkus article https://orkus1.com/en/japan-quiet-life-was-released-45-years-ago-today-do-you-know-these-5-facts/)

The band spent significant time touring Japan during the Hansa years, finding there the success that eluded them at home in the UK punk/post-punk era.

Virgin period (1980–1982): Gentlemen Take Polaroids, Tin Drum

After three Hansa albums, Japan signed to Virgin Records in 1980. Rob Dean played on the first Virgin album and toured in support of it, but then departed permanently.

Gentlemen Take Polaroids (1980, Virgin V2180) was again produced by John Punter and engineered by Colin Fairley and Nigel Walker. The cover featured photography by Stuart McLeod and Nicola Tyson. Songwriting predominantly by Sylvian (tracks A1–B1, B3); the title track incorporates elements from Smokey Robinson’s writing (credited on the Smokey Robinson cover on the album). The album reached the UK charts; the band’s androgynous image made them suddenly fashionable in the emerging New Romantic scene.

Rob Dean’s departure after this album left Japan as a quartet. Masami Tsuchiya (guitar/keyboards) was brought in as a live touring member.

Tin Drum (1981, Virgin V2209) is generally considered the band’s masterpiece. Self-produced by Japan and Steve Nye (engineer and co-producer), the album stripped the sound to a sparse, almost Chinese-influenced minimalism. Karn played fretless bass, African flute, and a Chinese double-reed instrument (suona, credited as “Dida”). Barbieri programmed synthesizers. Jansen contributed drums, electronic percussion, and keyboard percussion, with writing credits on several tracks. Simon House played violin; Yuka Fujii contributed vocals. Sylvian’s cover concept was designed by Steve Joule. The singles “Visions of China” and “Ghosts” were major UK hits.

Key singles from the Virgin era:

  • “Visions of China” (1981, Virgin) — UK Top 40
  • “Ghosts” (1982, Virgin) — UK Top 5, self-produced by Japan
  • “Cantonese Boy” (1982, Virgin)
  • “Nightporter” (1982, Virgin)

By 1982, the creative and personal tensions between Sylvian and Karn had become irreconcilable. The central personal conflict: Yuka Fujii had been Mick Karn’s girlfriend; she subsequently left Karn and moved in with David Sylvian. Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_(band), as of 2026-06-05) states: “Long-simmering differences among the band members came to a head when Karn’s girlfriend, photographer Yuka Fujii, moved in with Sylvian.” Steve Jansen later told the Guardian: “The tension back stage on tour was unbelievable.” (Sourced via movingtheriver.com: https://movingtheriver.com/2022/12/18/japan-the-final-concert-40-years-on/ and classicpopmag.com: https://www.classicpopmag.com/features/the-lowdown-japan-and-david-sylvian/.) Sylvian told Smash Hits after the split was announced in November 1982 that financial and songwriting-credit disputes also contributed: “As Japan became more successful, the music became more of a business… Suddenly who wrote what songs became relevant.” The band performed farewell shows at London’s Hammersmith Odeon in December 1982 and disbanded.

Oil On Canvas (1983) and compilations

Oil On Canvas (1983, Virgin VD 2513) is a double live album compiled from the 1982 tour. It features the post-Dean quartet plus touring guitarist Masami Tsuchiya. Produced by Japan and John Punter (live performances) and Japan/Punter/Walker/Nye (studio tapes). Cover uses a painting by Frank Auerbach (“Head of J.Y.M. II, 1980”), tinted by Yvette Anna; photography by Anton Corbijn. The live album was released posthumously as a commercial and critical marker of the band’s peak live period.

Assemblage (1981, Hansa) — UK compilation drawing from the Hansa back catalogue. Exorcising Ghosts (1984, Virgin) — double compilation covering both the Hansa and Virgin eras, the definitive hits package of the era.

Post-dissolution solo careers

After Japan’s break-up:

  • David Sylvian launched an acclaimed solo career on Virgin with Brilliant Trees (1984), Gone to Earth (1986), and many subsequent records exploring ambient, experimental, and jazz-influenced territory. Collaborated extensively with Ryuichi Sakamoto, Robert Fripp, Holger Czukay, and others.
  • Mick Karn released solo records and collaborated widely, including Dalis Car (with Peter Murphy of Bauhaus, 1984).
  • Steve Jansen and Richard Barbieri worked together as Barbieri and in the trio Karn; Jansen later joined Mark Isham and The Dolphin Brothers (with Barbieri). Barbieri subsequently joined Porcupine Tree.
  • Rob Dean worked with Illustrated Man and later Vivabeat among others.

Rain Tree Crow (1991)

The four core members (Sylvian, Karn, Jansen, Barbieri — without Dean) reunited in 1991 under the name Rain Tree Crow and released one album:

Rain Tree Crow (1991, Virgin) — produced by Rain Tree Crow themselves; mixed by Sylvian/Steve Nye (most tracks) and Pat McCarthy/RTC (others). Engineers: Pat McCarthy; additional engineering by Tim Martin. The record is credited as Japan’s alias on Discogs (and Rain Tree Crow lists Japan as its alias). More experimental and atmospheric than Japan’s Virgin work, it was well received critically but the reunion did not extend further. The band did not tour as Rain Tree Crow. See Rain Tree Crow for full notes.
