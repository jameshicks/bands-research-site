The Dictators

Discogs ID: 263833
Origin: New York City, USA
Active: 1973–1978; 2001–present

New York proto-punk band. Issued Fuck ‘Em If They Can’t Take A Joke (ROIR A102, 1981) — a live cassette that was one of the label’s earliest releases. Known for their wisecracking, high-energy style bridging hard rock and punk. Members include Handsome Dick Manitoba, Ross the Boss (Ross Friedman), Andy Shernoff.

Manitoba’s Wild Kingdom (1986–1991)

Manitoba’s Wild Kingdom was not a side project or a clean band rename — it was an intermediate formation that gradually reconstituted The Dictators from the inside. The Dictators had dissolved in late 1978; when Richard Manitoba (Richard Blum) and Andy Shernoff began working together again in late 1986, they did so under a new name with a partly different lineup, and The Dictators name remained dormant.

Formation (1986): Richard Manitoba, Andy Shernoff, and guitarist Daniel Rey (a Ramones producer, not a Dictators alumnus) formed the group, initially calling it simply Wild Kingdom.

1989 lineup shift: Rey departed and was replaced by Ross the Boss (Ross Friedman), who was a founding Dictators member. Drummer J.P. “Thunderbolt” Patterson and guitarist Ron Hanley also joined at this point. The band also renamed itself Manitoba’s Wild Kingdom in 1989 — the change was forced by a threatened lawsuit from the TV program Wild Kingdom. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-03)

Return to The Dictators (1991): When rhythm guitarist Scott Kempner — another Dictators original — rejoined in 1991, the reconstituted group reverted to The Dictators name. The Wild Kingdom period thus functioned as a transitional phase: it began with two ex-Dictators and a non-member, then progressively reabsorbed former members until the classic lineup was complete enough to reclaim the original name. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-03)

Dictators reformation: The Dictators resumed activity under their own name from approximately 1991 (Kempner rejoining), with documented reunion performances through the mid-2000s–2009 era, including the 2008 Joey Ramone Birthday Bash (Manitoba, Shernoff, Friedman, Patterson). (Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-03)

Summary of relationship: Manitoba’s Wild Kingdom was neither a pure side project nor a straight rename. It was a transitional band: formed by two Dictators alumni (Manitoba, Shernoff) plus a non-member (Rey), which over 1989–1991 reabsorbed a third (Ross the Boss) and then a fourth (Kempner) original member, at which point it reverted to The Dictators. The Dictators name was effectively dormant 1978–1991.
