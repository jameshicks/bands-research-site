This Heat

Experimental/post-punk trio. UK. Active 1976–1982. Discogs artist ID: 33931.

Members: Charles Bullen (guitar, clarinet, viola, voice, tape), Gareth Williams (keyboards, guitar, bass, voice, tape), Charles Hayward (percussion, keyboards, voice, tape). Late-period touring member: Trefor Goronwy (bass), who replaced Williams in 1981–82.

Name variations: ディス・ヒート (Japanese), “9” (Discogs alternate entry)

Biography

Formation and Cold Storage (1976–1978)

This Heat was formed in 1976 when Charles Hayward — who had been drumming for Phil Manzanera’s art-rock group Quiet Sun and briefly for Gong — joined with Charles Bullen and Gareth Williams. The three musicians set up a self-funded rehearsal and recording space at a disused cold-storage facility in Brixton, south London: a former meat-locker with thick insulation walls that made for unusual acoustic properties. This space, which they named Cold Storage, became central to their working method — they had continuous access for recording, allowing them to build up tracks across long sessions, work with tape loops and multi-track layering without commercial studio time-pressures, and document work in progress.

Cold Storage was simultaneously studio, rehearsal room, and social hub for London’s post-punk experimental community. It was also used by other musicians in David Cunningham’s orbit (see the Health And Efficiency engineer credits, which list multiple collaborators recording there). The 2006 retrospective box set was named Out Of Cold Storage in acknowledgement of the space’s centrality.

The trio came from art-rock and British jazz-fusion roots but rapidly developed a sound that had no direct precedent: tape loops, found recordings, overlapping time signatures, processed vocals, free improvisation, and songs with explicitly political themes, all made with a DIY sensibility that rejected both pop convention and academic experimentalism.

Self-Titled Album and Piano Label (1979)

The debut LP This Heat (1979, Piano THIS-1) was released on David Cunningham’s Piano label — the label’s most significant release beyond Cunningham’s own work. The production was collective: Cunningham co-produced and co-engineered alongside an unusual ensemble of engineers (Chris Blake, Frank Bryan, Kevin Harrison, Rik Walton, and Anthony Moore), with the band itself also credited as producer and engineer. The album was issued as a special cut, with both stereo and mono sides.

Health And Efficiency EP (1980)

The 12-inch EP Health And Efficiency (Piano, “this 12 01” / “this 1201”) followed in 1980. Two tracks, produced by David Cunningham:

  • A: “Health And Efficiency” (8+ minutes, grinding post-punk)
  • B: “Graphic / Varispeed” (at 45rpm on the original pressing)

Engineers on this session: Chris Blake, Chris Gray (2), Geoffrey Zipper, Jack Balchin, Laurie-Rae Chamberlain, Peter Bullen, Phil Clarke. The expanded engineering roster relative to the debut reflects the complexity of the Cold Storage recording setup. Peter Cobb is credited for the painting on the sleeve.

Deceit (1981)

Deceit (1981, Rough Trade ROUGH 26) is the band’s masterpiece — a full LP on Rough Trade that fuses post-punk directness with experimental process and political urgency. Produced by David Cunningham, engineered by a large Cold Storage team.

The album’s lyrical content is explicitly anti-militarist and anti-imperialist: “Cenotaph” addresses war commemoration and trauma; “S.P.Q.R.” invokes Roman imperial decay as a metaphor; “Hi Baku Shyo” addresses the atomic bombing of Japan. The album also features dense layering and production experimentation — “Shrinkwrap” is nearly abstract.

Live Work and Gareth Williams’s Departure (1981–1982)

This Heat toured through 1980–81. Their live approach was highly physical and confrontational — the Out Of Cold Storage box set’s sixth disc (Live 80/81) captures concert recordings from this period, showing the band playing tighter, more rock-driven versions of the studio material. Live sound engineers Jack Balchin and Martin Harrison are credited on these recordings; Peter Bullen handled tape operations. Gareth Williams operated organ, bass, and guitar live.

At some point in late 1981 / early 1982, Gareth Williams departed the band. Trefor Goronwy — who would later form The Camberwell Now with Hayward — came in as his replacement for the final European tour in 1982. This Heat dissolved following that tour.

The 1982 live recording S.P.Q.R. The Last Live 1982 (Canterbury Dream) and Live In Koln Germany 1982 (2005, Japan, Footstomp) document the final Goronwy-era lineup.

The Repeat / Loops Material

The 1993 This Is / These Records release Repeat (THESE 6 CD) is distinct from the studio albums: it collects tape-loop and studio improvisation material in three long tracks — “Repeat” (20:21), “Metal” (23:16), and “Graphic / Varispeed” (15:26). These were recorded at Cold Storage with assistance from Anthony Moore, Chris Blake, David Cunningham, and Rik Walton; “Metal” was mixed with Rob Mekon (a.k.a. Rob Mekon from Pigbag). This material is a separate stream from the two studio albums — more ambient and loop-driven, closer to industrial-era tape music.

John Peel Sessions

The Made Available (John Peel Sessions) release (first issued 1996, These Records THESE 10 CD; reissued 2006 on This Is and 2018 on Modern Classics) documents two BBC Peel sessions. Tracks 1–3 were engineered by Dave Dade and produced by Tony Wilson (BBC); tracks 4–8 by Nick Gomm and produced by Malcolm Brown (BBC). The eight tracks are:

  1. Horizontal Hold (8:26)
  2. Not Waving (8:11)
  3. The Fall Of Saigon (6:04)
  4. Rimp Romp Ramp (6:42)
  5. Makeshift (6:18)
  6. Sitting (2:22)
  7. Basement Boy (2:13)
  8. Slither (2:14)

Cover design credited to a team: Andrew Jacques, Charles Hayward, James Mannox, Stephen Thrower, Sue Lawes.

Label Context

Piano (debut label)

The Piano label was founded by David Cunningham in 1976. The This Heat debut (THIS-1, 1979) and the Health And Efficiency 12” were the only This Heat releases on Piano. The label’s profile text notes the This Heat album as one of Piano’s founding successes. Cunningham describes the label’s focus as “hybrids and minority music” with a relationship to the visual arts and performance.

Rough Trade (Deceit)

Deceit (1981) was released on Rough Trade (ROUGH 26), placing This Heat firmly in the distribution network of the UK post-punk independent scene. Rough Trade was simultaneously releasing records by The Fall, Young Marble Giants, The Raincoats, and Scritti Politti at this time.

These Records and the Archival Phase

The label These Records (affiliated with ReR Megacorp’s distribution arm) handled the first wave of CD reissues and archival releases from 1988 onward — This Heat CD (1988), Deceit CD (1988), Repeat (1993), Made Available (1996/1999), and the combined Health & Efficiency / Repeat (1998).

This Is (ReR Megacorp sublabel)

The This Is sublabel (Discogs ID: 58644) is a dedicated sub-imprint of ReR Megacorp, established in 2006 specifically to handle the comprehensive reissue program. The 2006 Out Of Cold Storage box set (cat: “this heat box”) was issued on This Is and contained six CDs: This Heat (this is 1), Deceit (this is 2), Health And Efficiency (this is 3), Made Available (this is 4), Repeat (this is 5), and Live 80/81 (this is 6). The Nivelles single (this is 7) was issued on the parent ReR Megacorp label. Individual titles from the box were also issued separately on This Is, and US editions appeared simultaneously on Morphius Records.

Members

Charles Bullen

Discogs ID: 283932. Alias: Billy Bagg.

Guitar, clarinet, viola, voice, tape on all This Heat recordings. Co-founder of the band. Credited as engineer and producer (along with the rest of the band and Cunningham) on the self-titled debut. Post-This Heat, Bullen set up his own studio in the late 1980s and released solo and collaborative work. Groups include Circadian Rhythms (ID: 27041), Lifetones (ID: 304113), Ning (2) (ID: 1193639), People In Control (ID: 205581). He also worked extensively as engineer/producer for other artists through the 1980s — key credits include Ut’s Ut EP (1984, Out Records), Conviction (1985, Out Records), and engineering/production assistance on releases by Monochrome Set–adjacent bands on Woof Records. On the Gareth Williams / Mary Currie Flaming Tunes cassette (1985), Bullen plays guitar, bass, and drums and engineers one track (“B To A”). He also co-produced a Family Fodder single with David Cunningham under the alias “Executives for Executives.”

Full page: Charles Bullen

Gareth Williams

Born: Gareth John Williams. Discogs artist ID: 196210.

Keyboards, guitar, bass, voice, tape on both studio albums; also credited with “mask” on Deceit (suggesting theatrical performance elements in live presentation). Williams appears to have been a key conceptual force in the band’s visual and political dimensions — he is credited with the cover rebake and mask artwork on Out Of Cold Storage, and wrote the liner notes for that box set alongside Lesley Evans. He also contributed photography to multiple releases, including the cover of Out Of Cold Storage.

Williams departed This Heat in late 1981, before the final 1982 European tour. He subsequently made the cassette-only album Flaming Tunes (1985, Contagious Tapes) as Flaming Tunes with Mary Currie — a lo-fi art-folk record with contributors including Charles Bullen (on two tracks), Martin Frederick, Mick Hobbs, and others. He continued to work as a DJ. Williams died in 2001.

Full page: Gareth Williams

Charles Hayward

Born: Charles John Hayward. Discogs artist ID: 90225.

Percussion, keyboards, voice, tape on the self-titled debut; voice, drums, keyboards, guitar, bass, tape on Deceit. Before This Heat, Hayward had played in Quiet Sun (the Phil Manzanera–led art-rock group, with Bill MacCormick, Dave Jarrett, and Manzanera) and briefly in Gong. After This Heat dissolved, he remained active as a performer — forming The Camberwell Now with Trefor Goronwy and Stephen Rickard, and maintaining a sustained solo and collaborative career.

Trefor Goronwy

Discogs artist ID: 311590. Name variations: Trefor Goronwi, Trevor Goronwy.

Bass; joined This Heat as touring replacement for Gareth Williams for the 1981–82 European tour. Subsequently co-founded The Camberwell Now with Hayward and Stephen Rickard, and was also a member of Truth Club (ID: 215687).

Full page: Trefor Goronwy

Producer: David Cunningham

David Cunningham produced both studio albums and the Health And Efficiency EP. He was not a band member but ran the Piano label on which the debut appeared. His working relationship with This Heat was central to the formation of his production identity — the Deceit engineering credits list his name alongside nine other engineers, reflecting the collective, multi-session recording approach used at Cold Storage.

Cunningham also produced Michael Nyman’s film score records for Peter Greenaway through the 1980s–90s, and fronted The Flying Lizards.
