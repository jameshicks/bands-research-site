DJ Spen & Thommy Davis

DJ Spen & Thommy Davis

May 19, 20269 min read

DJ Spen & Thommy Davis

Discogs ID: 423703 Members: DJ Spen (Sean Stafford Spencer) and Thommy Davis (Walter Thomas Davis / Thommy Gypsy) Based: Baltimore, Maryland Active as duo: 2005–present Labels: Code Red Recordings, Quantize Recordings, unquantize, Defected, BPM King Street Sounds

Baltimore soulful/gospel house duo composed of two former core members of The Basement Boys production collective. Both DJ Spen and Thommy Davis bring deep Basement Boys roots to their partnership, and their work as a duo spans the entire arc of Spen’s post-Basement Boys career — from his independent Code Red Recordings label through the Quantize era. The configuration also operates collectively under Spen’s broader production network including Gary Hudgins, Gary Deane, and Irvin Madden.

Biography

Individual Backgrounds

Sean Stafford Spencer (DJ Spen) and Walter Thomas Davis (Thommy Davis / Thommy Gypsy) were both founding or early members of The Basement Boys, the Baltimore production collective active from the late 1980s. The Basement Boys also included Teddy Douglas, Jay Steinhour, Karizma (Kris Klayton), Maurice Fulton, Neal Conway, Victor Williams, and Wayne Cooper. The group ran Basement Boys Records and produced a foundational body of soulful/gospel house in the 1990s.

Thomas Davis’s pre-Basement Boys career extends to the early 1980s. Under the name Thommy Gypsy, he is credited on at least one 1983 gospel LP (Come Together, Myrrh Records). His production credits from the mid-1980s include work on Thommix Records compilations (1987), and he served as co-producer on RCA/Popular Records releases for acts including Black Rock And Ron (Stop The World, 1989; True Feelings, 1989; You Can’t Do Me None, 1990). He is also listed as writer on Crystal Waters’ “100% Pure Love” (A&M, 1994), one of the major US house crossover tracks of that decade.

Sean Spencer (DJ Spen) became the most prominent solo face of the Basement Boys, building his name through a long-running series of “DJ Spen Presents Jasper Street Co.” releases on Basement Boys Records from 1995 onward, and later through the DJ Spen & Karizma duo with fellow Basement Boy Karizma.

Coming Together — The Duo (2005)

The duo’s first release as a credited unit appeared in 2005 on Code Red Recordings (Spen’s own imprint): “Nightrain” (cat# CR12-510), credited on the label as “DJ Spen Presents Thommy Davis.” The track was produced and arranged by the duo, with keyboards from Gary Hudgins and additional keyboards from Irvin Madden and KW Griff; DJ Spen played turntables and vocoder; Gary Hudgins also contributed vocoder. All writing credits went to Gary Deane (Ahrens), Gary Hudgins, Irvin Madden, Sean Spencer, and Thomas Davis. Gary Deane (Ahrens) also served as mastering engineer.

This release established the full creative circle that would recur throughout the duo’s discography: Spen and Davis as producers and co-writers, with Gary Hudgins and Gary Deane (The MuthaFunkaz) completing the production team.

Also in 2005, they were presenters on the Code Red EP (Defected, DEF 003-12), alongside KW Griff, giving the duo exposure on the UK’s leading soulful house imprint.

Code Red Era (2005–2008)

All early duo releases appeared on Code Red Recordings:

  • “Nightrain” (2005, CR12-510) — debut; produced and arranged by the duo; Electro/House
  • “Shine” (2006, CODE07) — presenting vocalists Terry Thompson (3) and Lisa Mack; the release also carried two remixes credited to the duo; co-produced with Terry Thompson (3); Gary Hudgins on keyboards; Gary Deane mastered
  • “Gotta Have House (The Code Red Remixes)” (2008, CODE20) — remix commission on the Knee Deep & DJ Spen track from KneeDeep USA; the duo contributed the “Spen & Thommy 12” Remix” and “Thommy & Spen Klassic House Mix” alongside Gary Hudgins and Irvin Madden on remix duties; additional vocal features included Kelly Tribble Spencer (Spen’s wife), Biblical Jones, and Kyle Tribble

The 2006 UK release of “Shine” was issued on Code Red’s CODE prefix (CODE07), distinct from the earlier US-market CR12 catalog series, reflecting Code Red’s shift to UK distribution through Defected.

Quantize Era (2011–present)

When Spen and Davis co-founded Quantize Recordings with Kelly Tribble Spencer in 2011, the duo became embedded in the label’s executive production structure. Their shared credits across the Quantize catalog are extensive — appearing as executive producers on the work of other Quantize artists as well as direct producing credits. Thomas Davis’s own solo discography on Quantize includes primary releases as “Thommy Davis” (the Hot EP on unquantize, 2018; Isn’t It A Shame, 2019; Darlin’ Darlin’ Baby, 2021) alongside his broader co-writer and executive producer credits.

Production Circle / The MuthaFunkaz Connection

The duo’s core production team overlaps directly with DJ Spen & The MuthaFunkaz. The MuthaFunkaz unit — Gary Hudgins, Gary Deane (Gary Deane Ahrens), and Irvin Madden — appear on nearly every Code Red Recordings release connected to the Spen/Davis partnership:

  • Gary Hudgins — keyboards, vocoder, written-by; recurring across Nightrain, Shine, and the Gotta Have House remixes
  • Gary Deane (Ahrens) — mastering, design/layout, executive producer, written-by
  • Irvin Madden — additional keyboards, remix credits

This effectively means the “DJ Spen & Thommy Davis” credit on any given release denotes Spen and Davis as the principal architects, with the MuthaFunkaz providing the live-instrument and post-production layer.

  • Thomas Davis’s 1983 gospel credit (Myrrh Records) and his 1980s co-production work on RCA deserve deeper investigation if context for the pre-house era is wanted
