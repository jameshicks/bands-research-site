Dirty Projectors

Discogs ID: 400992 Primary member: David Longstreth Active: 2002–present Origin: New Haven, CT (founded) / Brooklyn, New York (based) Genres: Art Rock, Experimental Pop, Indie Rock, Chamber Pop Labels: Western Vinyl (2003–2006), Dead Oceans (2007), Domino Records (2009–present), Nonesuch Records (2025)

New York art-rock project founded and led by David Longstreth as a vehicle for his compositional ideas, ranging from deconstructed rock covers (Rise Above, 2007) and interlocking guitar-and-voice counterpoint (Bitte Orca, 2009) to solo R&B-inflected song cycles (Dirty Projectors, 2017) and orchestral collaboration (Song Of The Earth, 2025). At its peak the group comprised a five-person ensemble with an unusually prominent role for its three female vocalists; since Longstreth’s core lineup dissolved around 2014 the project has operated with shifting membership.

Biography

Origins: New Haven and Western Vinyl (2002–2006)

Longstreth began releasing music as Dirty Projectors in 2002 while still at Yale University in New Haven, CT. The debut LP The Glad Fact (Western Vinyl, 2003) established the project’s idiosyncratic folk-rock approach: Longstreth played nearly everything, with only occasional outside contributions (Sam Bernstein on French horn on one track). Jona Bechtolt contributed layout. The album was released as West 026 on Western Vinyl.

Slaves’ Graves & Ballads (Western Vinyl, 2004) expanded the ensemble — early collaborators included Ezra Seltzer, Emily Rostetter, Jason Nesmith, Hank Miller, Jon Zalben, Yasemin Schatz, Adam Forkner, Elana Arian, Adam Bloniarz, and Sam Bernstein, most of whom appear only on this release.

The Getty Address (Western Vinyl, 2005) was the project’s most ambitious early statement: a loose concept album about Don Henley and the Eagles, scored for choir (the Brown Finch Choir), the Orchestral Society for the Preservation of the Orchestra (also a Longstreth vehicle), and additional vocalists including Lucy Greene. Longstreth handled vocals, guitar, acoustic guitar, drums, arrangement, production, and engineering. John Golden mastered.

The New Attitude EP appeared on Marriage Records in 2006.

Rise Above and the Emerging Lineup (2007)

Rise Above (Dead Oceans, 2007) was the pivotal transitional record. The conceit — Longstreth reconceiving Black Flag’s Damaged from memory, without re-listening to the original — attracted wide critical attention and introduced the lineup that would define the classic era: Amber Coffman and Susanna Waiche on vocals and guitar, Nat Baldwin on upright bass, Brian McOmber on drums, Chris Bear on drums and electronics, Charlie Looker on guitar, Sam Hillmer on flute, and a string section including Jessica Pavone, Sharon Nakhimovsky, Yuki Aizawa, and Ezra Seltzer. Greg Ginn is credited as written-by (for the Black Flag material). Produced and mixed by Longstreth and Chris Taylor (of Grizzly Bear). Paul Gold mastered.

Bitte Orca and the Domino Era (2009–2010)

The move to Domino Records coincided with Dirty Projectors’ breakthrough. Bitte Orca (Domino, 2009) tightened the lineup to Longstreth, Amber Coffman, Angel Deradoorian, and Brian McOmber as core Dirty Projectors, with Haley Dekle contributing additional harmonies across several tracks, Nat Baldwin on bass on two tracks, and a string quartet — Jordan Dykstra (viola, string quartet director), Andrew Todd and Caleb Russell (violins), and Anna Fritz (cello). Produced by Longstreth with Nicolas Vernhes as additional producer; mixed by Longstreth and Vernhes; mastered by Joe Lambert. Recorded by Robby Moncrieff and McOmber. The album’s complex, syncopated guitar parts and the three-way vocal interplay of Longstreth, Coffman, and Deradoorian became its signature.

Singles from the Bitte Orca cycle included “Ascending Melody” (2009) and “Stillness Is The Move” / “Temecula Sunrise / Cannibal Resource.” The latter demonstrated the pop/R&B sensibility that would characterize Coffman’s contributions and later inform Longstreth’s solo direction.

“I Dreamed I Saw St. Augustine” (Levi’s Revival Recordings, 2010) was a Bob Dylan cover issued as a promotional single.

Mount Wittenberg Orca (2010, self-released on Not On Label) was a full collaboration with Björk — jointly credited Dirty Projectors + Björk, with both serving as primary artists. The classic lineup (Longstreth, Coffman, Deradoorian, McOmber, Dekle, Baldwin) performed alongside Björk; Longstreth, Björk, and Nicolas Vernhes co-mixed. Mastered by Joe Lambert.

The “No Intention / As I Went Out One Morning” double A-side (Domino, 2010) continued the Dylan connection.

Classic Lineup: Swing Lo Magellan (2012)

Swing Lo Magellan (Domino, 2012) is widely regarded as the peak of the classic five-piece lineup. Personnel: Longstreth, Coffman, McOmber, Dekle, and Baldwin as Dirty Projectors; chamber ensemble yMusic — Rob Moose (violin), Hideaki Aomori (clarinets/saxophone), Alex Sopp (flute), C.J. Camerieri (trumpet/horn), Nadia Sirota (viola), Clarice Jensen (cello) — on four tracks. Additional mixing by Carl Barc and Donato Paternostro. Mastered by Bob Ludwig. Amber Coffman co-wrote track 10 (“Gun Has No Trigger” and the title track were concurrent singles). Longstreth produced, arranged, and mixed throughout; brother Jake Longstreth shot the cover photo.

The About To Die EP (Domino, 2012) preceded the album. Offspring Are Blank and The Socialites (Remixes) followed in 2013.

Post-Lineup Albums: 2016–2020

After Coffman, Deradoorian, and other members departed, the project contracted toward Longstreth as sole constant. Keep Your Name (Domino, 2016) appeared as a standalone single.

Dirty Projectors (Domino, 2017) was a solo statement in all but name — Longstreth wrote and produced throughout, with Tyondai Braxton providing additional production and co-writing on several tracks, and guest vocals from Solange Knowles (who co-wrote her feature). Jimmy Douglass mixed most tracks; Sonny DiPerri mixed one; David Tolomei mixed additional tracks. Samples include Bernard Herrmann/Royal Scottish National Orchestra (“Scene D’Amour”), and Dan Deacon (“Sheathed Wings”). Teresa Eggers co-wrote one lyric; Chris Athens mastered. Co-design by Longstreth and Rob Carmichael.

The “Cool Your Heart” single (2017) featured Solange as a full co-lead; remixes appeared on Cool Your Heart (Remixes).

Lamp Lit Prose (Domino, 2018) featured guest appearances from Robin Pecknold (Fleet Foxes) and Rostam Batmanglij on track 9 (each with separate vocal engineers). Produced entirely by Longstreth; Greg Calbi mastered; Manny Marroquin mixed track 2. Nat Baldwin and Mike Johnson co-wrote the closing track. Engineering distributed across Sonny DiPerri, David Tolomei, Ryan Tuttle, Robby Moncrieff, and Miro Mackie.

Sing The Melody (Domino Documents, 2019) was an EP. The 5 EPs compilation (Domino, 2020) collected the 2020 EP run — Windows Open, Lose Your Love, Earth Crisis — released across the year during the pandemic. The Earth Crisis EP appeared as a standalone release.

On And Ever Onward (Live From Housing Works 2009) (Domino, 2023) documented a 2009 charity concert.

Song Of The Earth (2025)

Song Of The Earth (Nonesuch, 2025) marked both a label shift and a new formal collaboration — credited to David Longstreth, Dirty Projectors, and Stargaze (4) (the Amsterdam-based chamber ensemble, credited as “s t a r g a z e”). André de Ridder conducted; Danny Reisch mixed; Max Lorenzen mastered. Longstreth handled words, music, production, and co-mixing.

Personnel: Key Releases

Rise Above (2007)

Produced and mixed by Longstreth and Chris Taylor (of Grizzly Bear). Nat Baldwin (bass), Brian McOmber and Chris Bear (drums), Charlie Looker (guitar), Sam Hillmer (flute), Amber Coffman and Susanna Waiche (vocals/guitar), string section (Jessica Pavone, Sharon Nakhimovsky, Yuki Aizawa, Ezra Seltzer). Written-by: Greg Ginn (Black Flag source material). Mastered: Paul Gold.

Bitte Orca (2009)

Core lineup: Longstreth, Amber Coffman, Angel Deradoorian, Brian McOmber. Additional: Haley Dekle (harmonies), Nat Baldwin (bass, two tracks), string quartet (Jordan Dykstra, Andrew Todd, Caleb Russell, Anna Fritz). Produced by Longstreth; Nicolas Vernhes additional producer and co-mixer. Mastered: Joe Lambert. Recorded by Robby Moncrieff.

Mount Wittenberg Orca (2010)

Primary: Dirty Projectors + Björk. Full Bitte Orca lineup plus Björk performing throughout. Mixed by Longstreth, Björk, and Nicolas Vernhes. Mastered: Joe Lambert.

Swing Lo Magellan (2012)

Core lineup: Longstreth, Coffman, McOmber, Dekle, Baldwin. Chamber ensemble yMusic (Rob Moose, Hideaki Aomori, Alex Sopp, C.J. Camerieri, Nadia Sirota, Clarice Jensen) on four tracks. Produced, arranged, and mixed by Longstreth; additional mixing by Carl Barc and Donato Paternostro. Mastered: Bob Ludwig.

Dirty Projectors (2017)

Longstreth as sole core performer; Tyondai Braxton additional production and music; Solange Knowles guest co-lead on one track; Teresa Eggers lyrics. Mixed by Jimmy Douglass (most tracks), Sonny DiPerri, David Tolomei. Mastered: Chris Athens.

Lamp Lit Prose (2018)

Produced by Longstreth. Guest: Robin Pecknold (Fleet Foxes), Rostam Batmanglij (track 9). Nat Baldwin and Mike Johnson co-wrote track 10. Mastered: Greg Calbi.

Song Of The Earth (2025)

Co-credited to David Longstreth, Dirty Projectors, and Stargaze (s t a r g a z e). Conductor: André de Ridder. Mixed: Danny Reisch. Mastered: Max Lorenzen.
