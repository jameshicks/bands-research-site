Last Exit

Discogs ID: 265435 Origin: USA Active: 1986–1994

American free jazz supergroup formed in 1986 by bassist/producer Bill Laswell, saxophonist Peter Brötzmann, guitarist Sonny Sharrock, and drummer Ronald Shannon Jackson. The group fused free jazz’s collective improvisation with rock volume and physicality — Brötzmann’s scorched-earth tenor over Sharrock’s feedback sheets, anchored by Laswell’s six-string bass and Jackson’s polyrhythmic thunder. Critical consensus places them at the intersection of free jazz, noise rock, and heavy metal; Discogs credits tag releases with styles ranging from Free Jazz and Jazz-Rock to Grindcore and Hardcore. Active primarily 1986–1990 with sporadic live recordings and posthumous releases to 2021.

History

Formation (1986)

Last Exit coalesced in New York’s downtown music scene in 1986, bringing together four musicians with distinct but overlapping orbits. Bill Laswell had spent the early decade at the center of the Celluloid/Enemy axis — producing across funk, world music, and avant-garde through his Material collective. He had already reconnected Sonny Sharrock to the music world after a mid-1970s hiatus: Sharrock appeared on Material’s Memory Serves (1981), beginning a collaboration that ran through the decade. Peter Brötzmann was by 1986 the defining voice of European free improvisation, known since his 1968 Machine Gun LP. Ronald Shannon Jackson had played with Albert Ayler and Cecil Taylor before leading his own Decoding Society; by the mid-1980s he was a known quantity in both jazz and the New York avant-garde. The combination was deliberate and extreme: no acoustic instruments, maximal volume throughout.

Debut LP and Tokyo Live (1986)

The self-titled debut Last Exit (1986, Enemy Records, Cat: 88561-8176-1) was recorded in the US and mixed by Robert Musso, who became the group’s primary engineer. Nine tracks, all composed collectively, ranging from dense free improvisation to blues-inflected blowouts. Brötzmann handles saxes; Laswell plays six-string bass; Jackson adds voice alongside drums; Sharrock plays guitar throughout.

The same year, the group toured Japan and recorded the live double-album The Noise Of Trouble (Live In Tokyo) (1986, Enemy Records, Cat: 88561-8178-1). The Tokyo session is notable for two guest appearances: Akira Sakata (alto saxophone and clarinet) and — on the closing track “Help Me Mo, I’m Blind” — Herbie Hancock on piano. Recording by Seigen Ono.

Cassette Recordings ‘87 / From The Board (1987–1988)

Cassette Recordings ‘87 (issued first in Germany on Enemy Records, then on Celluloid Records in the US as Cassette Recordings 87, Cat: CELL 6140) draws from 1987 live recordings. The same session material was issued as From The Board (1987, Enemy Records, Cat: EMY 105) — a reissue/repackaging that circulated Brötzmann’s expanded saxophone arsenal on tape (bass saxophone, alto, tenor, tárogató). Mixed by Robert Musso; Howie Weinberg mastered.

Iron Path (1988)

Iron Path (1988, Virgin, Cat: 7 91015-1) was the group’s only major-label release. Produced by Laswell with the full group credited as co-producers, it was engineered by Martin Bisi at his BC Studio in Brooklyn. Ten tracks with individual track lengths — the most composed-feeling of their studio albums. Brötzmann provides calligraphy for the sleeve. Liner notes on the inner sleeve by Steve Lake (2). All four members credited as writers.

European Live Period and Dissolution (1990–1993)

Köln (1990, ITM Records (2), Cat: ITM 1446) is a CD recording of a Cologne concert. Five tracks including the 19-minute “Hard School”; all compositions credited to the full group. Mixed by Robert Musso. Brötzmann designed the cover.

Best Of Live (1990, Enemy Records, Cat: EMCD 110) is a retrospective compilation compiled by Gerd Filtgen, drawing tracks from the studio album and both live sets. Liner notes by Steve Lake (2).

Headfirst Into The Flames – Live In Europe (1993, MuWorks Records, Cat: MUW 1013) documents European touring and is credited as produced by Robert Musso (“Final Production”). Track titles draw from Kenneth Patchen poetry. The final release in the active period.

Posthumous Releases (2021)

Two archival releases appeared in 2021 via Bill Laswell’s Bandcamp: Moers (live recording, digital FLAC) and Allentown (digital MP3). The Moers session notably includes Billy Bang (violin) and Diamanda Galás (voice) as guests — an expanded lineup not documented in the core discography. Artwork by Yoko Yamabe; mastered by James Dellatacoma.
