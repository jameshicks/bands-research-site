The Rolling Stones

Discogs ID: 20991 Formed: London, England, May 1962 Genre: Rock and roll, blues rock, R&B, hard rock, pop rock

English rock band, one of the longest-lived and most commercially successful groups in rock history. Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1989. The band built its sound on Chicago blues and R&B before evolving through psychedelia, hard rock, country, disco, and back to straight-ahead rock across six decades of recording.

Biography

Early Years (1962–1963)

The Rolling Stones coalesced around Brian Jones (5) (born Lewis Brian Hopkin-Jones), who advertised for musicians in a London music paper. Mick Jagger and Keith Richards — childhood friends who had lost touch — reconnected at Dartford station in 1961 and joined Jones’s group. The original lineup included Ian Stewart on piano, Dick Taylor on bass (later replaced by Bill Wyman), and Tony Chapman on drums (replaced by Charlie Watts in January 1963).

Manager Andrew Loog Oldham (Discogs ID: 252539) signed the band in 1963, also bringing in co-manager Eric Easton (Discogs ID: 362176). Oldham pushed Brian Jones out of the frontman role and positioned Jagger-Richards as the songwriting duo, actively encouraging them to write originals by locking them in a kitchen until they produced a song. He also controversially removed Ian Stewart from the official lineup for not looking rock-and-roll enough, though Stewart continued as road manager and occasional session player until his death in 1985.

Decca Era and Brian Jones (1963–1969)

The band’s debut UK single was “Come On” (Decca, 1963), followed by a cover of Lennon-McCartney’s “I Wanna Be Your Man.” Their self-titled debut album (UK, 1964) was produced by Andrew Loog Oldham with engineer Dave Hassinger (Discogs ID: 256155) in the US. Early albums were recorded at Regent Sound in London and Chess Studios in Chicago.

A key figure during this period was Jack Nitzsche (Discogs ID: 256156), who contributed keyboards and arrangements, and Phil Spector (Discogs ID: 190767), who collaborated occasionally with Oldham. Nicky Hopkins (Discogs ID: 255177) emerged as the preeminent session pianist, playing on records from Their Satanic Majesties Request onward.

Aftermath (1966) was the first album composed entirely of Jagger-Richards originals. It showcased Brian Jones (5)‘s instrumental eclecticism — sitar, dulcimer, marimba, bells — and featured arrangements by the band with Jack Nitzsche on additional keyboards.

Their Satanic Majesties Request (1967) was the band’s psychedelic experiment, self-produced (credited to the band as a whole) and engineered by Glyn Johns (Discogs ID: 52230). Nicky Hopkins contributed piano. The album is generally regarded as the Stones at their least characteristic.

Beggars Banquet (1968) and Let It Bleed (1969) marked a decisive return to blues-rooted rock, produced by Jimmy Miller (Discogs ID: 17207) and engineered by Glyn Johns, Eddie Kramer (Discogs ID: 253216), and Andy Johns (Discogs ID: 249830).

Brian Jones, increasingly incapacitated by drug addiction and mental deterioration, was fired by the band in June 1969. He drowned in his swimming pool on 2 July 1969. His replacement was Mick Taylor (Discogs ID: 300117), a 20-year-old guitarist who had been playing with John Mayall’s Bluesbreakers.

Jimmy Miller Era and Mick Taylor (1969–1974)

With Jimmy Miller producing and Mick Taylor on lead guitar, the Stones entered their most critically acclaimed period. Let It Bleed (1969), Sticky Fingers (1971), Exile on Main St. (1972), and Goats Head Soup (1973) form the core of this period.

Sticky Fingers (1971) was the first release on their own Rolling Stones Records (distributed by Atlantic in the US), with cover art commissioned from Andy Warhol (Discogs ID: 240120). Jimmy Miller produced; Glyn Johns and Andy Johns engineered.

Exile on Main St. (1972) was recorded largely in the basement of Keith Richards’s rented villa in the south of France (Villa Nellcôte), with additional sessions in Los Angeles. The double album featured an extraordinary cast of session players: Nicky Hopkins on piano, Bobby Keys (Discogs ID: 255176) on saxophone, Jim Price (Discogs ID: 114736) on trumpet and trombone, and Billy Preston (Discogs ID: 46767). Cover photography by Robert Frank (3) (Discogs ID: 719005). Engineers: Glyn Johns, Andy Johns, Jeremy Gee (Discogs ID: 265803), and Joe Zagarino.

Goats Head Soup (1973), also produced by Jimmy Miller with Andy Johns engineering, brought in additional session players including Nicky Harrison (Discogs ID: 321737) for string arrangements and percussion from Rebop Kwaku Baah (Discogs ID: 414036) and Nik Pascal (Discogs ID: 706123).

Mick Taylor, frustrated by lack of songwriting credit, departed in December 1974.

Ron Wood Era and the Glimmer Twins Producing (1975–1982)

Ron Wood (Discogs ID: 271311), then a member of the Faces, joined for the 1975 tour before becoming an official member in 1976. Black and Blue (1976) was partly an audition record for Taylor’s replacement, featuring guest guitarists Harvey Mandel and Wayne Perkins alongside Wood.

From Some Girls (1978) onward, production shifted to Jagger and Richards under the alias The Glimmer Twins (Discogs ID: 228853), sometimes with Chris Kimsey as associate producer. Engineer Chris Kimsey (Discogs ID: 85730) was a central figure through this period. Some Girls (1978) also introduced Sugar Blue (Discogs ID: 306715) on harmonica, and featured disco-inflected tracks alongside pure rock. Ted Jensen (Discogs ID: 259758) mastered the album.

Tattoo You (1981), a patchwork of outtakes from various sessions, was assembled and produced by the Glimmer Twins with Chris Kimsey. Bob Clearmountain (Discogs ID: 11415) handled remixing.

Chuck Leavell (Discogs ID: 299429), former member of the Allman Brothers Band, became the band’s regular keyboardist from 1982 onward, appearing on all subsequent recordings.

The CBS/Virgin Years (1983–1998)

The label journey in this era: Rolling Stones Records shifted distribution from Atlantic to CBS (1983–1993), then to Virgin (1994–2002).

Undercover (1983) — Glimmer Twins producing, Kimsey engineering. Dirty Work (1986) — credited to the Glimmer Twins and Steve Lillywhite (Discogs ID: 50809) as co-producer. The mid-1980s saw Jagger and Richards in public feuding over solo careers. Steel Wheels (1989) — produced by the Glimmer Twins and Chris Kimsey. Key session players included Matt Clifford (Discogs ID: 261457) on keyboards and orchestrations, Bernard Fowler (Discogs ID: 14400) and Lisa Fischer (Discogs ID: 61250) on backing vocals, and The Kick Horns (Discogs ID: 260479) on brass. The Farafina ensemble and Master Musicians of Jajouka (Bachir Attar, Discogs ID: 143423) contributed to “Continental Drift.”

Bill Wyman retired in January 1993. Darryl Jones (Discogs ID: 271000) was brought in as touring bassist.

Voodoo Lounge (1994) — first record with Darryl Jones on bass; produced by Don Was (Discogs ID: 62920) and the Glimmer Twins. Marked the Virgin era.

Bridges to Babylon (1997) — Glimmer Twins as executive producers; co-produced by Don Was and other producers including The Dust Brothers (Discogs ID: 36647) on individual tracks.

The Interscope/Polydor Era and Post-Watts (2005–present)

A Bigger Bang (2005) — produced by Don Was and the Glimmer Twins; Chuck Leavell on piano/organ, Darryl Jones on bass. Stephen Marcussen (Discogs ID: 254942) mastered. Recorded with the core four-piece plus Leavell and Jones; the leanest-sounding Stones album since the 1970s.

Charlie Watts died in August 2021. For Hackney Diamonds (2023), Steve Jordan (Discogs ID: 173865) replaced him, playing on ten of twelve tracks; Watts plays drums on two tracks recorded before his death. The album was produced by Andrew Watt (3) (Discogs ID: 3821409) alongside Don Was and the Glimmer Twins. Darryl Jones plays bass throughout, Matt Clifford contributes keyboards.

ABKCO Records—Allen Klein’s label; controls pre-1971 catalog

Note on ABKCO: Manager Allen Klein (Discogs ID: 452833, listed under alias “Lenne Allik”) negotiated the Stones’ US deal in 1965 and later took control of the pre-1971 master recordings. The relationship ended acrimoniously; ABKCO still controls the Decca-era catalog.

Aliases and Side Projects

The band recorded under several pseudonyms:

  • Nanker Phelge — early songwriting alias for group compositions (vs. Jagger-Richards for the main duo)
  • The Glimmer Twins — Jagger and Richards as production duo
  • The Cockroaches (4), The Little Wonders (2), The Moaning Groans, The Rockers (21) — tour pseudonyms used for soundchecks / incognito gigs
