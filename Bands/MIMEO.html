MIMEO

Music In Movement Electronic Orchestra. Discogs artist ID: 215649. Large-scale laptop/electronics ensemble active in European free improvisation and electroacoustic contexts, 1997–early 2000s.

Overview

MIMEO was a collective of eleven European electronic and improvising musicians, drawn from the intersection of free improvisation, computer music, and electroacoustic traditions. The ensemble was geographically dispersed — members based in London, Vienna, Amsterdam, Cologne, Grenoble, Nantes, Lisbon, and Sydney — and met primarily for live performances at major European festivals, recordings of which became the basis for its two album releases.

The ensemble operated at the intersection of two worlds: the British free improvisation tradition (represented most directly by Keith Rowe via AMM) and the Vienna/Cologne laptop/computer music scene (represented by Fennesz, Peter Rehberg, and Marcus Schmickler). Its practice was collective and non-hierarchical — no conductor, no fixed score, everyone contributing simultaneously via their own instruments and electronics.

Both MIMEO releases appeared on GROB, a Berlin-based label documenting European free improvisation and electroacoustic work.

Label: GROB

Both MIMEO releases appeared on GROB, a German label (website: churchofgrob.com, Discogs label ID: 17185) focused on European free improvisation and electroacoustic work. Other artists on GROB include Franz Hautzinger, Derek Bailey, John Butcher, Phil Minton, Peter Brötzmann, Radu Malfatti, and Mattin. The label’s catalog spans roughly 1998–2004.

Context and Scene

MIMEO operated at the intersection of several overlapping scenes:

British free improvisation: Keith Rowe brought the ethos of AMM and the British improv tradition directly into the ensemble. Phil Durrant and Richard Barrett were also rooted in that tradition. The non-hierarchical, non-idiomatic approach to group playing — the hallmark of British free improvisation since the 1960s — shaped MIMEO’s working method.

Vienna computer music / Mego: Fennesz, Peter Rehberg, and Marcus Schmickler were all central to the Viennese laptop scene around Mego and its successors. MIMEO brought their laptop practices into a free improvisation ensemble context at a moment (1997–1999) when this kind of crossover was new.

European electroacoustic improvisation: Thomas Lehn, Jérôme Noetinger, Gert-Jan Prins, and Cor Fuhler each worked within distinct European electroacoustic traditions — Lehn with analogue synthesis, Noetinger with the electroacoustic tradition, Prins with radios and amplified electronics, Fuhler with keyboard electronics.

Free improv and jazz margins: Kaffe Matthews brought a computer music and experimental approach; Rafael Toral came from a background in guitar/electronics in Portugal.

The period 1997–1999 saw overlapping large-ensemble electronics projects across Europe; MIMEO was among the most rigorously constituted, with a stable roster and documented recordings rather than a purely festival-oriented existence.
