Loose Joints

NYC dance/disco project. Active 1980–1983. Discogs artist ID: 6054.

Core members: Arthur Russell (vocals, cello, production) and Steve D’Aquisto (DJ, production, co-writer). Emerged from the Paradise Garage scene. A blend of early house precursors, post-disco, and Russell’s avant-garde sensibility — one of the cleaner examples of the downtown/club crossover that defined Russell’s creative life.

Overview

Loose Joints was not a band in any conventional sense but a project name — a vehicle for Russell and D’Aquisto to operate in the club world. Russell was simultaneously active across cello composition, avant-garde work (with Peter Gordon, in the Philip Glass orbit), and the downtown loft scene. Loose Joints was his deepest sustained venture into dance music proper, producing records that circulated almost entirely through DJ culture before gaining wider recognition decades later.

Steve D’Aquisto brought deep roots in NYC disco infrastructure: he had co-DJed at Nicky Siano’s The Gallery — one of the foundational spaces of NYC disco — and was embedded in the same world as Larry Levan and the Paradise Garage. The Russell/D’Aquisto pairing connected avant-garde compositional sensibility to street-level club distribution.

The Paradise Garage, resident DJ Larry Levan, and West End Records (founded 1976 by Mel Cheren and Ed Kushins) provided the infrastructure through which these records circulated.

Discography

Two-sided 12”. The primary Loose Joints release. 11 pressings documented in Discogs.

ProducerArthur Russell, Steve D’Aquisto

Larry Levan played “Is It All Over My Face?” extensively at the Paradise Garage. François Kevorkian edited the track (uncredited on original release). Became a foundational NYC dance record; widely sampled in subsequent decades.

Note on “Can’t You Feel It”: The 1986 repress of “Is It All Over My Face?” was paired with “Can’t You Feel It” rather than “Pop Your Funk.” “Can’t You Feel It” is credited to “Michele” — later identified as Chantal Sitruk, a Tunisian disco singer who recorded it for producer Pierre Jaubert. That recording was released on West End Records and dated from 1977, predating “Is It All Over My Face?” by three years. Sitruk was murdered in Israel in 1985, making the 1986 pairing a posthumous juxtaposition.

”Tell You (Today)” (4th & Broadway / Sleeping Bag Records, 1983)

12”. The second Loose Joints release, three years after the first.

“Killer Whale” is an Arthur Russell alias; Discogs profile ID 190198 has realname listed as Arthur Russell. The B-side mixer credit “In The Corn Belt” (Discogs ID 72844) is likely another Russell alias — no profile, and the only other release under that name is a 2025 Dimitri From Paris remix.

Arthur Russell had co-founded Sleeping Bag Records in 1981 with William Socolov and Juggy Gales; the label releasing “Tell You (Today)” in 1983 connects directly to his own infrastructure.

Production Context

Arthur Russell was operating across multiple scenes simultaneously. The Loose Joints releases sat at the intersection of:

  • The Paradise Garage / Larry Levan DJ axis — the records were built for Levan’s room and played there regularly
  • The West End Records infrastructure — Mel Cheren and Ed Kushins’s label was the primary NYC dance/disco independent of the period
  • Sleeping Bag Records — Russell’s own label, co-founded 1981, which released “Tell You (Today)” through the 4th & Broadway distribution arrangement
  • The downtown avant-garde — Russell’s cello compositions and work with Peter Gordon, Julius Eastman, and others ran parallel and occasionally intersected

Steve D’Aquisto’s background at The Gallery (with Nicky Siano) gave him a direct line into the DJ network that these records needed to reach. D’Aquisto and Larry Levan moved in overlapping circles; the mixing credits on “Tell You (Today)” reflect those connections made concrete.

The production alias “Killer Whale” (used on “Tell You (Today)”) appears in Russell’s Discogs profile alongside other aliases: Dinosaur, Dinosaur L, Indian Ocean, Loose Joints itself. The proliferation of aliases reflects the practical reality of Russell maintaining separate identities across scenes.

Open Questions

  • Full lineup of musicians on the original 1980 sessions — Discogs credits are sparse; only primary artist/producer credits listed, no session musicians named. Who played on the original tracks?
  • Exact recording timeline of “Can’t You Feel It” relative to “Is It All Over My Face?” — the 1977 date on the Michele recording predates the 1980 Loose Joints release, but the relationship between the tracks is unclear.
  • Full story of the Michele / Chantal Sitruk / “Can’t You Feel It” connection — who licensed it to West End Records? What was the Pierre Jaubert relationship to the label?
  • Elizabeth McElroy — who is she and what was her involvement in the Russell sessions?
  • Were there other unreleased Loose Joints recordings? The gap between 1980 and 1983 is unexplained by released material alone.
