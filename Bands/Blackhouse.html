Blackhouse

Discogs ID: 15561 Active: 1984–present (years active on Discogs: 1984–2025) Origin: United States Genre: Christian industrial, power electronics, noise Primary member: Brian Ladd

History

Blackhouse is a US Christian industrial and power electronics project founded in 1984 by Brian Ladd (real name Brian Ladd). The project is one of the earliest and most sustained attempts to bring explicitly Christian theological content into the aesthetic territory of industrial and power electronics music — genres that had up to that point been closely associated with nihilism, transgression, and anti-Christian provocation.

The Discogs profile notes that the choice of project name itself was a deliberate provocation against the nihilistic ideology prevalent in early 1980s industrial circles. The “blackhouse” metaphor operates on multiple levels: as a polemical inversion of the nihilism embraced by many industrial contemporaries, and as a symbol of man’s spiritual weakness, original sin, and fear of death and divine judgment. The music is described as expressing the horror and grief of human sinfulness alongside a conditional hope of salvation — but delivered through the same maniacal, confrontational sonic vocabulary as secular industrial and power electronics.

Early Period (1984–1988)

The debut release Pro-Life (1984, Ladd-Frith) introduced the project immediately with its title signaling the project’s Christian perspective. The release credited Ivo Cutler, Sterling Cross, and Roger Farrell as performers alongside Ladd — indicating a collaborative multi-performer setup in the earliest recordings. These three names are all aliases of Ladd himself (Ivo Cutler and Sterling Cross appear in Discogs as alternate names for the same person/entity), suggesting that the credited performers may represent different conceptual “voices” or personae within the project, or are tape collaborations with himself under assumed names.

Hope (1985, RRRecords) followed as a compilation/LP, released on Ron Lessard’s noise label RRRecords (catalog number RRR 777). This was a significant placement — RRRecords was one of the key institutional nodes of the American noise underground. Hope Like A Candle (1985, Ladd-Frith) was a related release in the same year.

The acknowledged magnum opus of the early period — and the album most cited in reference to Blackhouse — is Five Minutes After I Die (1986). This was released in two editions: one on Ladd-Frith (cassette) and one on Cause And Effect (cassette, limited edition). The Cause And Effect edition is the one typically cited in historical references to the project. The album is a cassette-format work with a side-long title track on the A-side, and six further pieces on the B-side: “His Zion,” “Halo Hearth,” “Answers For You,” “A Thousand Tongues,” “Numerology,” and “Five Minutes After I Die (Reprise).” Credits list Ivo Cutler as both Producer and Performer, and Sterling Cross as Performer, with all music credited to Blackhouse. The Discogs profile characterizes the album as showing resemblances to early E.N. (Einstürzende Neubauten) and the power electronics movement — a coherent, well-constructed work rather than a raw document.

Holy War (1987, RRRecords) extended the collaboration with Ivo Cutler and Sterling Cross as performers, with Brian Ladd and Ivo Cutler co-producing and handling the megamix. Julie Frith handled graphics — she is credited on several subsequent Ladd-Frith releases and may be connected to the label’s operational side (the label name “Ladd-Frith” suggests a partnership between Ladd and a Frith).

We Will Fight Back! (1988, Staalplaat) marked a significant international connection: release on the Amsterdam-based experimental/industrial label Staalplaat, which was a major distribution and curatorial node for European and international industrial, noise, and experimental music. The release credits Ivo Cutler and Sterling Cross for samples, percussion, rhythms, noises, vocals, and music, with Brian Ladd also playing guest guitar on three tracks. The cover was photographed by Erwin Olaf.

Later Output (1989–2000)

Stairway To Heaven (1989, Ladd-Frith) and the pair of 1990 albums — Material World (Ladd-Frith) and The Father, The Son And The Holy Ghost (Ladd-Frith) — continued the project’s run on the home label. Material World credits Julie Frith for artwork and Brian Ladd for guitar and programming alongside Ivo Cutler and Sterling Cross as performers. The Father, The Son And The Holy Ghost credits Brian Ladd as technical assistant and tape operator alongside Ivo Cutler (producer, performer) and Sterling Cross (performer).

Hidden Beneath The Metal (1991, Ladd-Frith) has Sterling Cross credited for voice, sound module, and percussion, and Ivo Cutler for voice, sampler, radio, chair, and effects — the “chair” credit is of interest, suggesting physical found-sound percussion.

The split release Ice Skin / The Gospel According To The Men In Black (1991, Minus Habens Records, Italy) with Nightmare Lodge brought Blackhouse into the Italian industrial scene. Nightmare Lodge is an Italian industrial project with members B. Mazzilli, Ivan Iusco, and Russolo (2).

After a gap, Dreams Like These (2000, Blacklight Records) appeared — a CD album on a different US label, with no additional credited personnel in the Discogs entry.

Return and Later Activity (2011–present)

The 2011 split/collaborative release Comin’ On Strong with Hypnoskull (French label Divine Comedy Records) marked a return to activity, connecting Blackhouse with Patrick Stevens (Hypnoskull), a Belgian harsh electronic artist.

Live in Leipzig 05.25.15 (2016, Dark Vinyl Records, Germany) documents a live performance, indicating continued touring or live appearances at industrial/noise events in Europe. The same year, Sacrificium Messiae (2016, Steinklang Industries, Austria) was a collaboration with Italian electronics pioneer Maurizio Bianchi (also known as M.B.) — one of the foundational figures in power electronics and industrial tape music, who had converted to Christianity himself in the mid-1980s. This collaboration is a natural pairing of two figures whose Christian faith is embedded in their industrial practice.

Robots (2018, ATSLA, Canada) and the appearance as producer/arranger on Soli Deo Gloria (2018, Tatra, Europe) round out the known recent activity.

The project maintains active online presence: Bandcamp (blackhouse.bandcamp.com), Facebook, YouTube, and is listed in Wikipedia.

Members

Blackhouse is a solo project of Brian Ladd (Discogs artist ID: 15628). Ladd operates under multiple aliases: Brian Ladd (credited under his real name on engineering/production credits), and the project-internal personas Ivo Cutler, Sterling Cross, Roger Farrell, Lokomotiv S.S., and X COMMUNICATOR. The Discogs page lists all of these as aliases with no separate artist IDs assigned to them — they appear to function as credited co-performers/collaborators on releases but are aspects of the same creative entity. Ladd’s own Discogs artist entry (ID 15628) is credited separately on several Blackhouse releases in roles such as Guitar, Producer, Mixed By, and Recorded By / Engineer, treating “Brian Ladd” and “Blackhouse” as distinct entries on the same records.

Julie Frith — credited for Graphics (Holy War, 1987) and Artwork (Material World, 1990) — appears to be associated with the Ladd-Frith label infrastructure. Her surname’s presence in the label name “Ladd-Frith” suggests a founding or operational partnership.

Anna Ladd — credited on Holy War (1987), likely a family member.

Winston Stern — credited on two releases (Stairway To The Gospel Word, Shock The Nation!); a recurrent collaborator in the later catalog.

Open Questions

  • The exact nature of the Ivo Cutler / Sterling Cross / Roger Farrell credits: Discogs lists all as aliases of the same person (Brian Ladd / Blackhouse), but whether they represent actual collaborative sessions with other individuals using pseudonyms, or are entirely self-performed under assumed names, is not resolvable from Discogs data alone.
  • Julie Frith’s relationship to the Ladd-Frith label: her surname appears in the label name and she is credited on at least two releases. Whether she was a co-founder, a business partner, or a family member who contributed artwork is not documented in Discogs.
  • The original brief states Five Minutes After I Die (1986) was on Cause And Effect as a limited edition — confirmed. The Ladd-Frith release of the same album appears to be the primary master (Discogs master ID 46779); the Cause And Effect pressing is a second edition of the same cassette.
  • Soli Deo Gloria (2018, Tatra) — Blackhouse is credited as Producer/Arranger but not as primary artist. The primary artist on this release is not captured in the discography data above; this may be a production credit for another artist’s release.
  • Discogs lists Stairway To The Gospel Word and Shock The Nation! as releases featuring Winston Stern, but these do not appear in the unique-masters discography above — they may be compilation appearances or releases not captured in the main discography pull.
