Kitchens Of Distinction

UK indie rock trio. Formed Tooting, South London, 1986. Discogs artist ID: 81474.

Overview

Kitchens of Distinction were formed in 1986 when Dan Goodwin (drums) and Julian Swales (guitar) — who had met at college in 1980 — linked up with Patrick Fitzgerald (vocals/bass), whom Swales met at a party in 1985. The band took their name from an advertisement Swales spotted on the side of a bus for a company specialising in home decor and kitchen fixtures. (Wikipedia)

Their debut single “The Last Gasp Death Shuffle” was recorded in a single day on eight-track in a Kennington basement and released in December 1987 on the band’s own Gold Rush Records (3). It was named Single of the Week in NME and led to a deal with One Little Indian. Around this time, Fitzgerald — a medical doctor — put his career on hold to devote himself to the band.

The group are characterised by Julian Swales’s effects-heavy guitar work, which drew comparisons to the Chameleons, Cocteau Twins, and A.R. Kane, and by Fitzgerald’s densely personal, often bluntly political lyrics. Fitzgerald was openly gay — having come out in 1984 — and his sexuality was unusually visible in indie rock of the era: tracks like “Prize,” “Within the Daze of Passion,” and “Breathing Fear” (which addressed gay bashing) placed queer themes at the centre of their work rather than at its margins. A&M Records in the US allowed Fitzgerald to express himself freely, never asking him to change lyrics or closet himself in interviews. Despite this, the band were frequently pigeonholed by the press, which Fitzgerald felt reduced their reach.

The band were retrospectively aligned with the shoegaze subgenre, though their sound predates the peak shoegaze period and has also been described as dream pop, neo-psychedelia, and post-punk. KOD occasionally performed “secret” gigs under the alter ego Toilets of Destruction, including a drag show at the Bull & Gate in Kentish Town in August 1990 where they covered ABBA, David Bowie, and Bauhaus.

By the end of 1995, both A&M and One Little Indian had dropped the band. They regrouped briefly as Kitchens O.D. for a final single on Fierce Panda (1996) before dissolving that summer after a farewell gig in Kings Cross. A reunion was announced in 2012; the original trio released the fifth studio album Folly in 2013 on 3 Loop Music, their first new material in 19 years. (Wikipedia)

Post-Band Solo & Side Projects

Patrick Fitzgerald

After the band’s dissolution Fitzgerald recorded and released music as Fruit (2) (Discogs: 269055) — a project releasing on One Little Indian in 1996 (What Is Fruit?) and Virgin in 1997 (Hark At Her). All three KOD members played on the Hark At Her sessions (Swales: guitar on selected tracks; Goodwin: percussion/drums/vocals throughout). Fitzgerald also produced a track on the Heidi Berry anthology Pomegranate (4AD, 2001).

Since 2000, Fitzgerald has recorded as Stephen Hero (Discogs alias: 264394). He also co-founded Lost Girls (Discogs: 281149) with Heidi Berry — a duo that released one single (“Needle’s Eye”) before eventually putting out a full album in 2014 on 3 Loop Music. He has also worked with Oskar’s Drum (5364660) and The April Seven (5574506).

Julian Swales

After KOD’s dissolution, Swales moved into composing scores for film, theatre, and dance (per Wikipedia). His Discogs footprint outside KOD is limited to a guest guitar credit on Heidi Berry’s 1993 self-titled album (4AD) and on Fruit (2)‘s Hark At Her (1997). Despite a reported collaboration with Terry Bickers (The House of Love / Levitation), no release materialised.

Dan Goodwin

Goodwin’s post-KOD credits on Discogs include percussion on Lush’s Lovelife (4AD, 1996) and drums/percussion on Fruit (2)‘s Hark At Her (1997). He also plays on the Legendary House Cats release Ocean Step b/w Sea Com (2018) and contributed percussion to Lush’s Chorus (4AD, 2016).

Research Queue

  • “Prize” (1988): Fitzgerald states in the Capsule liner notes that he could not learn the bassline in time to record the song, so Swales played bass and sang lead vocals. Confirm whether this applied only to the original single recording or also to subsequent live performances.
  • Cowboys And Aliens (1994): OLI reportedly rejected the album twice in its original form before Pascal Gabriel was brought in to co-produce “Come on Now” and remix “Sand on Fire” and “Now It’s Time to Say Goodbye.” Confirm from press contemporaneous with the release how much of the final album represents Gabriel’s input vs. the Bartlett-produced sessions.
  • Discogs notes Patrick Fitzgerald’s role on Quick As Rainbows as “Lyrics By” (not producer), while the band self-produced Love Is Hell and Elephantine E.P. Confirm who produced or co-produced the Drive That Fast and Quick As Rainbows sessions — Hugh Jones is listed as producer on Drive That Fast but not on Quick As Rainbows.
  • Julian Swales reportedly planned a collaboration with Terry Bickers (The House of Love / Levitation) following KOD’s breakup. Confirm whether any recordings were made and why the project did not result in a release.
  • Dan Goodwin is credited with percussion on Lush’s Lovelife (4AD, 1996) — confirm whether this was a session engagement or a deeper production relationship with the band.
  • The Hark At Her (Fruit (2), 1997) album featured all three KOD members. Confirm whether this constituted an informal KOD reunion or was purely Fitzgerald’s solo project with hired musicians.
