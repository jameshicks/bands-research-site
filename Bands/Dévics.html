Overview

Dévics is a duo comprising Dustin O’Halloran and Sara Lov, whose catalogue runs from 1993 through the mid-2000s — two releases in the 1990s and five in the 2000s, working across indie rock, dream pop, and folk rock with an ethereal edge. The bulk of their output appeared on Bella Union (six releases, 2001–2006), with earlier work issued through Splinter Records. Their core production circle was tight: Tracy Chisholm and Mark Chalecki appear across the majority of their discography, shaping a consistent sonic identity throughout the active years.

Notes (web) — Bella Union signing

How the Bella Union deal came about

Dustin O’Halloran has directly addressed this in interview. The Cocteau Twins were among his formative musical influences, so signing with Simon Raymonde’s label was, in his words, “a full circle of musical inspiration.” He elaborated: “many years later when I had my band Devics, with Sara Lov, we ended up signing with their label, Bella Union. I’ll never forget, Simon Raymonde from the Cocteau Twins played bass with us on a couple shows in Italy and we played some of their tracks.” (Source: Dustin O’Halloran — “A Different Dynamic”, Native Instruments blog)

This establishes that:

  1. The Bella Union connection was rooted in O’Halloran’s long-standing admiration for the Cocteau Twins, making it a natural fit rather than a purely commercial arrangement.
  2. Simon Raymonde had a personal, hands-on relationship with the band — performing live with them in Italy — which suggests the signing involved genuine mutual affinity rather than a remote licensing deal.
  3. The duo had relocated to or spent significant time in Italy around this period (corroborated by Bella Union’s SXSW-focused scouting for other US acts, which does not appear to apply here; Dévics may have been brought to Raymonde’s attention through the Italy connection rather than through SXSW).

What could not be confirmed

The specific mechanism of the signing — who initiated contact, whether a demo or EP triggered the deal, and whether there was a US licensing partner or straight UK-only deal — was not found in any source checked. Searched: Wikipedia (Devics article, My Beautiful Sinking Ship article, Dustin O’Halloran article, Sara Lov article), Native Instruments blog, Line of Best Fit (Bella Union 20-year retrospective), Consequence of Sound (Simon Raymonde interview, 2010), whatsonthehifi.com (Sara Lov interview). None of the Bella Union label retrospectives name Dévics as a pivotal signing.

Note on Bella Union’s US activity in this era

Raymonde has described discovering most early US signings (Lift to Experience, Midlake, Jetscreamer, Robert Gomez) through SXSW. Dévics were based in Los Angeles and, by the time of the Bella Union deal, spending time in Italy — consistent with Raymonde meeting them in a European context rather than at SXSW. (Line of Best Fit; Consequence of Sound)
