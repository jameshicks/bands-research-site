The Bizarros

Punk / proto-punk / new wave band. Akron, Ohio, USA. Also catalogued as Bizarros. Active c. 1976–1980. Discogs artist ID: 147913. Name variations: The Bizarros.

Overview

The Bizarros formed in 1976 in Akron, Ohio — the same mid-Rust-Belt city that produced Devo, Rubber City Rebels, Tin Huey, and The Waitresses. The city’s declining rubber industry, shuttered factories, and economic displacement fed a concentrated art-punk / new-wave scene centred around the Clone Records label and a tight network of shared personnel.

The band’s sound moved across the spectrum of what Akron was doing in that window: blunt three-chord punk on the early material, mutating into something more textured and pop-inflected by the 1979 Mercury LP — with Terry Walker’s viola appearing as a recurring odd-instrument accent. Nick Nicholis was the primary songwriting voice (with Walker and Donald Parkins as the other key writers), and served as producer on the group’s own sessions. The band self-produced on Laser Boys (1978) with Nicholis co-crediting production, and co-produced the Mercury LP alongside engineer Michael Bishop (3).

Their highest-profile compilation appearance remains “Ice Age” (5:02) on Punk 45 Vol. 1 - Kill The Hippies! Kill Yourself! (Soul Jazz, 2013), the longest track on that double-LP. Three further tracks — “I Bizarro,” “Lady Doubonette,” and “Nova” — appear on Punk 45 - Burn Rubber City Burn! (Akron Ohio) (Soul Jazz, 2015), where they are the most represented single act.

Rick Garberson was the original drummer; he was later replaced by Martyn Flunoy (not found in Discogs DB with credits). The Complete Collection 1976–1980 (Windian, 2012) documents additional lineup variants including Marty Zelei and Dave Ashley (2) on drums for different sessions, and Paul Hively on bass for some tracks.

Scene Connections

The Bizarros were embedded in the Akron Ohio art-punk ecosystem of 1976–1980, which produced an unusually high concentration of experimental-leaning acts from a single post-industrial city:

  • Rubber City Rebels — closest Akron contemporaries; shared the From Akron split 7” (1977); co-appear on both Soul Jazz Punk 45 compilations. The Rebels went on to sign with Capitol Records.
  • Devo — also from the Akron area; also on Clone Records in the early period. The Bizarros and Devo both appear on the Burn Rubber City Burn! compilation.
  • Tin Huey — Akron art-punk/new-wave; Clone Records / Warner Bros. Two tracks on Burn Rubber City Burn! Shared scene.
  • The Waitresses — Akron new-wave; one track on Burn Rubber City Burn!
  • Pere Ubu — Cleveland (not Akron, but adjacent Ohio proto-punk); appears on Punk 45 Vol. 1 alongside The Bizarros.
  • Electric Eels — Cleveland proto-punk; also on Punk 45 Vol. 1.
  • The Pagans — Cleveland punk; also on Punk 45 Vol. 1.
  • Chi-Pig — Akron duo; two tracks on Burn Rubber City Burn!
  • 15-60-75 (Numbers Band) — Kent, Ohio blues-punk; one track on Burn Rubber City Burn!

Clone Records (label ID: 43697) served as the primary axis for the Akron scene, with the Bizarros, Rubber City Rebels, and Devo all releasing material through or adjacent to it in 1977–1978.

Open Questions

  • Martyn Flunoy (replacement drummer) has no Discogs entry — no credits, no dates, no confirmation of when the changeover happened.
  • The Sean Bonniwell writing credit on the Complete Collection (track D3) is unexplained: Bonniwell was the leader of The Music Machine (1960s LA psych-punk), not an Akron figure. Whether this indicates a cover song or a licensing anomaly is not determinable from Discogs alone.
  • “Ice Age” — the source recording for the Punk 45 Vol. 1 appearance — does not appear as a standalone single in the Discogs DB. Origin (LP outtake, unreleased recording, radio session) is unclear from DB alone.
  • The band’s website (http://www.thebizarros.com/) is listed in their Discogs profile but not archived in the DB.
  • No information on when the band dissolved or whether any members pursued solo projects under their own names — the Discogs member pages for all five members show no solo releases.
