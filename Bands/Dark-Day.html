Dark Day

Post-No Wave band. New York City. Founded late 1978. Also credited as R.L. Crutchfield’s Dark Day. Discogs ID: 118769. Active 1979–present (intermittently).

Founded by Robin Crutchfield immediately after leaving DNA (4), the No Wave trio he had co-founded with Arto Lindsay and Ikue Mori. Where DNA was an extreme, abrasive live outfit, Dark Day was a more song-structured project drawing on cold wave, minimal electronics, and medieval/pagan imagery — Crutchfield’s voice and keyboard textures at the center, surrounded by a shifting cast of collaborators.

Robin Crutchfield Beyond Music

Per his Discogs profile: performed at CBGB, Max’s Kansas City, Tier 3, Artist’s Space, The Kitchen, 8BC, The Pyramid; toured US and Netherlands. Contributed visual artwork at 112 Greene St., Charlotte Moorman’s Avant Garde Festival, the Whitney Museum, and MoMA. Readings at Club 57 and Joseph Papp’s Public Theater. Short films with Lydia Lunch, Kathy Acker, and others. Acted in Amos Poe’s The Foreigner and Cable’s Inner Tube. Music used in Errol Morris’s First Person (TV series).

From c.2000: shifted focus to harps and lyres, exploring acoustic glissando and drone work. Released on Hand/Eye and Important Records labels.

Open Questions

Notes (web)

“Mystery Woman” on Exterminating Angel A6 — web research, 2026-07-04

The credit on the original 1980 sleeve (Infidelity JMB-229) reads: “Second Vocal on ‘No, Nothing, Never’ by Mystery Woman.” The 2017 Bandcamp reissue repeats the credit without elaboration. The 2022 CD reissue (Mark Records) includes an 8-page booklet with lyrics but the booklet’s content is not publicly indexed; no online source reports that the booklet names her.

Sources checked and result:

  • Simon Reynolds’s 2009 interview with Crutchfield (totallywiredbysimonreynolds.blogspot.com) — Crutchfield discusses Exterminating Angel personnel (Steven Brown, Jimmy De Sana, Jack Zaloga) but does not mention the Mystery Woman at all.
  • Dark Entries “reviewed around the globe” roundup (darkentriesrecords.com) — three reviews (Other Music, Igloo Magazine, Aquarius Records); none mention the Mystery Woman.
  • Wikipedia, Robin Crutchfield article (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Crutchfield, as of 2026-06-03) — no personnel detail for Exterminating Angel beyond the album title.
  • Bandcamp track page for “No, Nothing, Never” (darkdayrobincrutchfield.bandcamp.com) — credits field empty.
  • Lydia Lunch detailed discography (fromthearchives.com) — no Dark Day entry or credit on any Crutchfield release.
  • Kathy Acker discography searches — no documented musical appearance on any Dark Day release.
  • Scaruffi, Die Or DIY, The Cold Tears blog, C86 Show interview with Crutchfield — none name the Mystery Woman.
  • AllMusic, Rate Your Music, Soundohm (2022 CD) — access blocked or no additional credit information.

Conclusion: The identity of the Mystery Woman is not documented in any publicly accessible online source as of 2026-07-04. The credit was intentionally withheld on release and has not been retrospectively disclosed in any interview, reissue liner note, or review found during this search. Kathy Acker and Lydia Lunch remain unconfirmed speculations; neither appears in Lydia Lunch’s detailed discography for 1980, and Acker has no documented musical appearance on a Dark Day release in any source checked. The 2022 CD booklet is the one unverified source that might contain an answer.

Note on catalog number: The brief describes this as “ROIR A111” but the release is on Infidelity (JMB-229), not ROIR. ROIR A111 is a different release entirely.

  • Jesse Rifkin interview with Crutchfield, Walk on the Wild Side NYC / Substack, November 2023 (walkonthewildsidenyc.substack.com/p/robin-crutchfield-dna-dark-day) — paywalled; content not accessible. This interview has not previously been checked. It is a post-2022-reissue conversation and is the most likely public source to have elicited new biographical detail from Crutchfield.

Updated conclusion (2026-07-25): No change to prior conclusion. Mystery Woman remains unidentified in all publicly accessible sources. The Rifkin/Substack interview (paywalled) is the one new lead that could not be evaluated.

Crutchfield film work — web research, 2026-08-07

The Discogs profile bio (artist 118769) states Crutchfield “made short films with Lydia Lunch, Kathy Acker and others.” This appears to be the only public source that makes this claim, and no subsequent source names the titles.

Sources searched:

  • Robin Crutchfield personal blog (robincrutchfield.blogspot.com) — bio text repeats the same claim verbatim (“made short films with Lydia Lunch, Kathy Acker and others”) without naming any titles.
  • Simon Reynolds 2009 interview (totallywiredbysimonreynolds.blogspot.com) — discusses Exterminating Angel personnel and his New York scene encounters (mentions meeting Kathy Acker on a European tour; mentions filmmaker James Nares sharing rehearsal space with Lydia Lunch), but no short film titles mentioned and Crutchfield is not described as a filmmaker.
  • Dark Entries Records bio (darkentriesrecords.com/bands/dark-day/) — no film titles; notes Errol Morris used Dark Day music in his First Person TV series (Bravo/2000).
  • Wikipedia, Robin Crutchfield (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Crutchfield, as of 2026-06-03 per offline snapshot) — no film titles; sole film mention is his acting role in Amos Poe’s The Foreigner (1978).
  • IMDb profile (imdb.com/name/nm0190187/) — credits: Actor (The Foreigner, 1978; X Magazine Benefit, 2009). No directing or writing credits.
  • MoMA Club 57 exhibition (2017–18) rotation schedule PDFs — now parsed (2026-08-19). Both the October–March rotation PDF (moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/calendar/club57_fvrotations_updated_march.pdf) and the updated version (club57_fvrotations_updated_march_next.pdf) have been read in full. Both end identically: “Forthcoming programs throughout April 2018, which will be announced as they become available, include the work of Clayton Patterson, Lisa Baumgardner, Steve Doughton, Robin Crutchfield, and more.” Crutchfield’s program was scheduled for April 2018 but the specific titles were never listed in these documents — they were to be announced separately. The initial MoMA press release (October 2017) lists the primary filmmakers featured in the in-gallery rotation as: John Ahearn, Anney Bonney, Tessie Chua, Courtney Harmel, Michael Holman, Julius Klein, Jennifer T. Ley, Eric Marciano, Kestutis Nakas, Kenny Scharf, Jonathan Quinn, Esther Regelson, Tom Rubnitz, Robin Schanzenbach, Barry Shils, and Nelson Sullivan — Crutchfield is not in this list. (artviewer.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/club57_expandedpressrelease_final58.pdf). MoMA artist page (moma.org/artists/69607) remains inaccessible (403).
  • Nowave.pair.com Dark Day page (nowave.pair.com/no_wave/darkday.html) — connection timed out; not readable.
  • Scaruffi (scaruffi.com/vol4/darkday.html) — no film titles; discography only.
  • LuxuriaMusic bio, C86 Show episode page, uzine.wordpress.com, Wikidata — all repeat the generic bio without film titles.
  • Lydia Lunch filmography checked separately — no Crutchfield-directed short film appears in her documented film appearances.
  • Kathy Acker filmography and collaborative works searched — no Crutchfield-directed work documented.

What is confirmed:

  • X Magazine Benefit (filmed 1978, completed/released 2009) — dir. Coleen Fitzgibbon and Alan W. Moore. 12-minute documentary of the March 11, 1978 NYC benefit for X Motion Picture Magazine (Collaborative Projects Inc.). Crutchfield appears as a performer with DNA, not as filmmaker. Screened at Viennale; listed on MUBI (not currently streaming) and IMDb. (imdb.com/title/tt4462002/, collaborativeprojects.wordpress.com/x-magazine-benefit-show/) — Crutchfield is a subject, not the director.
  • Crutchfield acted in Amos Poe’s The Foreigner (1978) — confirmed by IMDb, Wikipedia, and his own blog.
  • Dark Day music was licensed by Errol Morris for his Bravo TV series First Person (c.2000) — confirmed by Dark Entries bio. Crutchfield had no creative role.
  • His visual artwork (painting, not film) was exhibited at MoMA, Whitney, and other venues — confirmed by multiple sources.

Conclusion (updated 2026-08-19): No short film titles directed or produced by Robin Crutchfield are documented in any publicly accessible source. The claim originates from the Discogs profile bio and is echoed verbatim across secondary sources without elaboration. Crutchfield was active in the same No Wave film milieu as Eric Mitchell, Amos Poe, Vivienne Dick, James Nares, and Beth B, and it is plausible he made Super 8 short films in that context — but no titles, subjects, or viewing links have been located.

The MoMA Club 57 rotation PDFs are now confirmed readable and exhausted: Crutchfield’s work was programmed for April 2018 but the specific titles were deferred to a separate announcement that does not appear to have been published online. The MoMA press release (October 2017) does not name him among the primary filmmakers. No online record of the April 2018 MoMA Crutchfield program — whether a press notice, a review, or a supplementary schedule — has been found. If such a record exists, it was not indexed by search engines. The MoMA film department’s internal records or the exhibition catalogue (Club 57: Film, Performance, and Art in the East Village, 1978–1983, MoMA, 2017, ISBN 978-1-63345-030-1) remain the most likely sources for specific titles.
