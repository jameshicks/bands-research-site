Overview

King Missile is an American avant-garde art-rock band founded in 1986 by poet and vocalist John S. Hall (born John Charles Hall, September 2, 1960, Brooklyn, NY). The band is best known for the 1992 single “Detachable Penis,” which reached No. 25 on the Billboard Modern Rock Tracks chart and No. 17 in Australia. Hall’s signature approach — deadpan spoken-word delivery, absurdist and satirical lyrics, alternating with melodic singing — defines the band across its several incarnations. Hall has led disparate lineups under the King Missile name since the beginning, dropping or retaining the “Dog Fly Religion” subtitle and numbering suffixes to mark distinct eras. (Wikipedia)

Hall grew up in Manhattan’s West Village, graduated from Stuyvesant High School (1978), and participated in the Lower East Side poetry scene in the early 1980s, reading at venues including ABC No Rio. He had two earlier bands — Purple Sunshine and You Suck — before co-founding King Missile. After the collapse of the second major incarnation in 1994 he attended law school, graduating cum laude from the Benjamin N. Cardozo School of Law, and co-founded Heraty Hall, an entertainment law firm. (Wikipedia: John S. Hall)

Incarnations

King Missile (Dog Fly Religion) — 1986–1989

The original lineup formed in 1986 when Hall, feeling that twenty minutes of solo poetry readings “would be totally boring,” invited guitarist friend Dogbowl (Stephen Tunney) to augment his performances with original music. Bassist Alex DeLaszlo, drummer R. B. Korbet, and xylophonist George O’Malley completed the initial lineup. In 1987 the band recorded and mixed the debut album Fluting on the Hump in ten hours at the Noise New York studio, produced/engineered by Kramer (2), and released it on Kramer’s then-fledgling Shimmy Disc (cat. SHIMMY-00003). The album was distributed to college radio and performed well on CMJ charts. The second album, They (1988), was recorded with cellist Charles Curtis and new drummer Steve Dansiger; Dogbowl wrote all the music; it was less well received. Dogbowl departed to pursue a solo career; the Dog Fly Religion period ended. (Wikipedia; Discogs)

The original lineup — Hall, Dogbowl, Korbet (on bass), DeLaszlo — reunited in 2023 for live shows with percussionists Susan Hwang and Marlon Cherry. A new King Missile (Dog Fly Religion) album, Quest For Fire, was announced for 2026. (Wikipedia)

King Missile — 1989–1994

After Dogbowl’s departure, Hall asked Dave Rick — then the guitarist in Bongwater — to help rebuild the band. Rick recruited multi-instrumentalist Chris Xefos, and Dansiger continued on drums. Hall dropped the “Dog Fly Religion” subtitle “since that was [Dogbowl’s] idea.” In late 1989 and early 1990 they recorded Mystical Shit; the single “Jesus Was Way Cool” hit No. 1 on CMJ, earning the band a major-label deal with Atlantic Records. Hall joked for years: “‘Jesus’ got me signed to Atlantic Records.” Before beginning the Atlantic run, Hall released a side-project record, Real Men (1991, Shimmy Disc), recorded with Kramer.

Dansiger left before the Atlantic debut; David Ramirez (of Hypnolovewheel) replaced him for The Way to Salvation (1991). Ramirez in turn was replaced by Roger Murdock for the commercial peak: Happy Hour (1992). “Detachable Penis” was the breakthrough single; Atlantic also released videos for follow-up singles “(Why Are We) Trapped?” and “Martin Scorsese” with less success. The band recorded a cover of R.E.M.’s “Get Up” for the compilation Surprise Your Pig: A Tribute to R.E.M. (Staple Gun Records, 1992) and contributed “Our Jungle” to the Surf Ninjas soundtrack (Atlantic, 1993).

The self-titled third Atlantic LP King Missile (1994), produced by Daniel Rey, was a commercial disappointment. Atlantic dropped the band; they broke up shortly after. (Wikipedia)

King Missile III — 1996–2004

Hall attended law school through the mid-1990s. A 1996 “solo album,” The Body Has a Head (Manifatture Criminali, Germany), credited John S. Hall but featured multi-instrumentalists Sasha Forte, Bradford Reed, and Jane Scarpantoni, and was effectively the foundation of the next band. With those three plus Charles Curtis, Hall formed King Missile III. Failure was released September 15, 1998 on Shimmy Disc; producer Bradford Reed also recorded and mixed it. Curtis and Scarpantoni left after Failure; the band continued as a trio (Hall, Reed, Forte) releasing The Psychopathology of Everyday Life (Instinct Records / Knitting Factory, 2003) and Royal Lunch (Important Records, 2004). (Wikipedia; Discogs)

King Missile IV / You, Me and This Fuckin’ Guy — 2014–2020

In September 2014 Hall performed four shows with the Los Angeles band LoveyDove; this was later deemed a new King Missile incarnation. They toured New Zealand (February 2015) and released the EP This Fuckin’ Guy (Powertool Records, 2015). In 2019 the group renamed to You, Me and This Fuckin’ Guy; their LP Garden Variety Fuckers was released by Dromedary Records on April 17, 2020. (Wikipedia; Discogs)

Reunions

The classic King Missile lineup (Hall, Rick, Murdock) reunited June 2015 at Shea Stadium, Bushwick, for the first time in over twenty years. Various combinations of Hall, Rick, Murdock, Cordero, and Korbet performed through 2015–2019. Dogbowl and Hall reunited as King Missile (Dog-Fly Religion) for a 2010 one-off at Le Poisson Rouge and subsequently for the 2023–2026 revival. (Wikipedia)
