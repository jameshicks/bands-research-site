Primal Scream

Discogs ID: 3852 Formed: 1982, Glasgow, Scotland Active: 1982–present Labels: Creation Records; Elevation/WEA; Columbia; Epic; First International; BMG; Silvertone (Bobby Gillespie & Jehnny Beth solo) Genre arc: indie/jangle pop → acid house crossover → Memphis soul/rock → dub/krautrock → noise-rock/post-punk → chamber pop

Overview

Primal Scream is a Glasgow-originated rock band built around vocalist and lyricist Bobby Gillespie (Discogs ID 8979). Over four decades the band has made radical genre pivots — from Byrds-influenced jangle pop to acid-house crossover (Screamadelica, 1991), Memphis roots-rock (Give Out But Don’t Give Up, 1994), dub/krautrock abstraction (Vanishing Point, 1997), noise-rock (XTRMNTR, 2000), and electronic-inflected hard rock thereafter. Screamadelica won the inaugural Mercury Music Prize (1992) and is widely regarded as a landmark of British music.

The core creative nucleus across most of the band’s history is Gillespie (vocals, lyrics), Andrew Innes (guitar, production), and the late Robert Young (2) (bass/guitar; died 2014); Martin Duffy (keyboards; died 2022) was a key member from 1989 to 2022.

Discography: Studio Albums

Sonic Flower Groove (1987)

Label: Elevation/WEA Discogs release ID: 908231 (Europe pressing) Producer: Mayo Thompson (most tracks); Clive Langer & Colin Fairley (one track) Engineer: Pat Collier

The debut album. Lineup: Gillespie (vocals), Innes (rhythm guitar), Jim Navajo (12-string lead guitar), Robert Young (bass), Dave Morgan (drums, as helper), Martin Duffy (piano, as helper), Frank Sweeney (viola). Written by Gillespie and Beattie. Produced by Mayo Thompson of Red Krayola — an unusual choice that gave the record a slightly arch, jangling atmosphere. The Byrds and 13th Floor Elevators were audible touchstones. Released on Elevation (a WEA sublabel).

Primal Scream (1989)

Label: Creation Records Discogs release ID: 545438 Role of Gillespie: Composed By; Lead Vocals

Second LP, still on Creation. The band’s sound was tightening toward the rave crossover that would arrive with Loaded the following year.

Screamadelica (1991)

Label: Creation Records (UK) / Sire / Warner Bros. (US) Discogs release ID: 6295 (US pressing) Producer: Andrew Weatherall (tracks 3, 5–7, 9–11); Hugo Nicolson (Assistant, tracks 3, 5, 6, 9–11) Writers: Gillespie/Innes/Young (tracks 1, 3–11); Roky Erickson/Tommy Hall (track 2, “Slip Inside This House” — 13th Floor Elevators cover) Cover image: Paul Cannell Photography: Grant Fleming Mercury Music Prize winner, 1992

The defining album. Weatherall’s transformation of an outtake into Loaded (1990) was the pivot; Screamadelica extended that synthesis of gospel, soul, house, and psych across a full LP. Denise Johnson sang lead on Don’t Fight It, Feel It (co-billed as primary artist on the single) and contributed vocals throughout. Key singles: Movin’ On Up, Higher Than The Sun, Don’t Fight It, Feel It. Followed by Dixie-Narco EP (1992, Creation) and a long-form VHS/video release Screamadelica (1992, M.C.E.G. Virgin). A 20th-anniversary live performance was filmed as Screamadelica Live (2011, Eagle Vision).

Give Out But Don’t Give Up (1994)

Label: Creation Records Discogs release ID: 339112 Producer: Tom Dowd (tracks 1–6, 8–12); uncredited on track 7 Engineer: Jeff Powell; Jeffrey S. Reed (second) Writers: Gillespie/Innes/Young (tracks 1–11); George Clinton (co-write, track 10) Front cover photography: William Eggleston

A deliberate swing toward American roots: Memphis recording sessions, Southern soul/funk textures, Tom Dowd producing. Denise Johnson sings throughout. Singles: Rocks, Jailbird, (I’m Gonna) Cry Myself Blind. Also released: The Original Memphis Recordings (2018, Sony Music) — a compilation of session material from these sessions (Discogs release ID 12646547), credited with Gillespie on design and vocals.

Vanishing Point (1997)

Label: Creation Records (UK) / Reprise (US) Discogs release ID: 12568 (US pressing) Producers: Primal Scream; Brendan Lynch Engineer: Martin “Max” Heyes (tracks 1–9, 11); George Shilling (mix, tracks 2, 3, 11) Design: Julian House; Matthew Rudd (2)

A post-Memphis pivot toward dub, kosmische, and soundtrack atmospheres. Brendan Lynch co-produced throughout; Marco Nelson on bass for several tracks. Singles: Burning Wheel, Kowalski, Star, If They Move Kill ‘Em (the latter a Peel session-derived track, 1998). The companion Echo Dek (1997, Creation) was a full dub reworking by Weatherall.

Echo Dek (1997)

Label: Creation Records Discogs release ID: 29294 Role: Producer [Original]; Recorded By (Primal Scream/Innes listed)

Andrew Weatherall produced the dub remix album from Vanishing Point sessions — essentially a Weatherall solo production credited to Primal Scream. One of the band’s most abstract releases.

Exterminator (XTRMNTR) (2000)

Label: Creation Records / Epic Discogs release ID: 12567 (Canada pressing) Producer/Recorded By: Primal Scream; Brendan Lynch (tracks 2, 6, 11) Core lineup: Gillespie, Innes, Young, Duffy, Mooney, Mounfield, Jim Hunt, Duncan McKay Guest musicians: Bernard Sumner, Kevin Shields, Phil Mossman, Marco Nelson, Gay-yee Westerhoff, Greg Knowles, Zach Danziger

The most politically confrontational Primal Scream album — anti-government, anti-war, anti-authority. Combines noise-rock, industrial, and rave textures. Bernard Sumner (New Order) appears as musician. Kevin Shields joins as de facto member from this album forward. Singles: Kill All Hippies, Accelerator, Swastika Eyes.

Evil Heat (2002)

Label: Columbia / Epic Discogs release ID: 75836 (US pressing) Producers: Kevin Shields (tracks 1–01, 1–04 to 1–07, 1–09); Two Lone Swordsmen (tracks 1–03, 1–08, 1–10, 1–11) Recorded by: Andrew Innes; Brendan Lynch; Jagz Kooner Engineer: Mads Bjerke (tracks 1–01, 1–04 to 1–06, 1–09) Design: Julian House Core lineup: Gillespie, Innes, Young, Duffy, Mooney, Mounfield

Split production between Kevin Shields (My Bloody Valentine) and Two Lone Swordsmen (Weatherall/Tenniswood) gave the album two distinct textures: noise-shoegaze on the Shields side, motorik-industrial on the TLS side. Singles: Miss Lucifer, Autobahn 66.

Riot City Blues (2006)

Label: Columbia Discogs release ID: 709401 (Japan pressing) Producer: Youth (tracks 1–6, 8, 10); Andrew Innes (tracks 7, 9) Mixed by: Dave Sardy Mastered by: Stephen Marcussen Lineup: Gillespie (vocals), Innes (guitar, mandolin, banjo, Moog), Young (guitar, harmonica), Mounfield (bass), Mooney (drums), Duffy (piano, organ, harmonium) Programmed by: Tim Bran

A straighter rock record. Produced by Youth (Killing Joke bassist, prolific producer). Singles: Country Girl, Dolls. Robert Young departs after this album cycle; Barrie Cadogan joins on guitar 2006.

More Light (2013)

Label: First International Discogs release ID: 4566032 Producer: David Holmes Mixed by: Rich Costey Artwork: Jim Lambie Gillespie instruments: Lead Vocals, Backing Vocals, Mellotron, Handclaps, Tambourine, Electric Piano [RMI], Drums, Percussion Innes instruments: Lead Guitar, Rhythm Guitar, Bass, Electric Guitar, Twelve-String Guitar, Bass [6], Acoustic Guitar, Synthesizer, Mellotron, Sitar [Electric Sitar], Piano, Electric Piano [RMI], Electronics [Sleepdrone], Dulcimer, Autoharp, Electric Harpsichord, Matrix Synth

Produced by Belfast-based David Holmes, with a vast range of instrumentation from both Gillespie and Innes. Musically expansive, drawing on psych and space-rock traditions.

Chaosmosis (2016)

Label: First International Discogs release ID: 8256120 Producers: Bobby Gillespie; Andrew Innes; Björn Yttling (co-producer throughout, additional on tracks 2, 5, 8, 10) Design: Bobby Gillespie; Matthew Cooper (2) Artwork: Jim Lambie

Co-production with Björn Yttling (of Peter Bjorn and John) brought a Scandinavian pop sensibility. Yttling also co-wrote several tracks. Singles: Where The Light Gets In, 100% Or Nothing, Mantra For A State Of Mind.

Come Ahead (2024)

Label: BMG Discogs release ID: 32230896 Engineer: Michael Harris (10) Singles: Love Insurrection (Black Science Orchestra Remix), Ready To Go Home (Terry Farley & Wade Teo Dub Mix), Innocent Money (Pet Shop Boys Remix)

The most recent studio album. Pet Shop Boys remixed a single from this campaign.
