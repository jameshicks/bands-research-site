Overview

Suicide Romeo were a French New Wave group active in 1980, with five members: Daniel Brunetti, Frédéric Cousseau, Jean-Louis Winsberg, Michel Bellocq, and Pierre Cousseau. They operated at the crossroads of the Paris and New York post-punk underground, releasing on both Celluloid and ZE Records — labels that formed the backbone of the Franco-American no-wave/disco-punk circuit. Producer-engineer Alex Sadkin is credited on essentially their entire known output, placing the band in the same recording orbit as his better-known clients including Grace Jones and Tom Tom Club. ZE Records co-founder Michel Esteban also appears among their collaborators, suggesting the label relationship went beyond distribution.

Notes (web)

Recording location and connection to Alex Sadkin

Suicide Romeo recorded their album (Images / Pictures) at Compass Point Studio, Nassau, Bahamas, as stated in the full liner credits published on the ZE Records official release page (zerecords.com/release/89/images.html). The session dates given there are 1979–1981 (original ZE copyright 1979; studio credit reads 1981). Alex Sadkin is credited as producer and mixer; assistant engineers are Benjamin Armbrister and Horace Pierre, both regular Compass Point personnel.

The connection to Sadkin runs through the studio infrastructure, not through a direct personal invitation. Compass Point was an Island Records-owned facility in Nassau; Sadkin was its resident head engineer and had been a full member of the Compass Point All Stars from the studio’s early days. Artists came to him by booking Compass Point — he was part of the room. Michel Esteban, Suicide Romeo’s executive producer and ZE Records co-founder, was taking multiple ZE acts to the same studio in the same period: Lizzy Mercier Descloux recorded Mambo Nassau at Compass Point in 1980 under Esteban’s production (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mambo_Nassau, as of 2026-11-27). The pattern — Esteban booking Compass Point, Sadkin serving as the in-house engineer/producer — explains the shared credits across Suicide Romeo’s entire output without requiring a prior personal relationship between the band and Sadkin.

No web source currently found names any other studio as involved in Suicide Romeo’s recordings. No interview or contemporaneous press piece has been located describing how Esteban initially chose Compass Point for Suicide Romeo specifically.

Michel Esteban, ZE Records, and Suicide Romeo’s place on the roster

Michel Esteban is credited as Executive Producer on Suicide Romeo’s album Images (France: Celluloid/ZE Records, 1980; UK title Pictures, Island Records/ZE Records), with Alex Sadkin as producer and mixer. This is confirmed by the ZE Records official release page at zerecords.com/release/89/images.html.

No web source states explicitly that Esteban was personally responsible for signing Suicide Romeo in a formal A&R sense, as opposed to Michael Zilkha. However, the executive producer credit — the only co-founder credit on the release — is the clearest available evidence of his direct hands-on involvement. Wikipedia’s ZE Records article (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZE_Records, as of 2026-11-26) lists Suicide Romeo in the roster without attributing the signing to either founder specifically.

Esteban’s broader role at ZE was as the European-facing co-founder: before ZE he ran a Paris concept store specialising in imported records, ran Rebel Records (France), and is documented bringing French acts to the label — most clearly Marie et les Garçons, whose demos he carried to John Cale and subsequently to New York (superweirdsubstance.com/ze-records/). Suicide Romeo, as a Parisian band, fit this same pattern: a French new wave act channelled into the Franco-American ZE/Celluloid circuit and recorded at Compass Point Studios in Nassau with Alex Sadkin. The executive producer credit strongly implies Esteban was the internal champion, but no interview or contemporaneous source yet found makes this explicit.

ZE roster context. Wikipedia’s ZE Records article lists Suicide Romeo alongside Cristina, Kid Creole and the Coconuts, Was (Not Was), Lydia Lunch, Material, Suicide, The Waitresses, Lizzy Mercier Descloux, and others. The label’s identity — New York no-wave/disco-punk with a Franco-American axis — directly frames Suicide Romeo’s place: they are one of several French-rooted acts (alongside Lizzy Mercier Descloux, Lio, Marie et les Garçons) that Esteban’s European network fed into ZE’s otherwise New York-centric roster.
