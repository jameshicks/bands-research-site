Ut

NYC No Wave / post-punk trio. Founded December 1978 by Nina Canal, Jacqui Ham, and Sally Young. Discogs artist ID: 55964

Biography

Origins — New York, 1978

Ut formed in December 1978, emerging from the downtown Manhattan No Wave scene that had crystallised around the “Bands at Artists Space” festival (May 1978) — the same moment that produced the artists on No New York. Ut were not on that compilation, but the three founders were active in that milieu. Nina Canal had already performed in Rhys Chatham’s Tone Death and Guitar Trio ensembles and was a member of Dark Day (Robin Crutchfield’s post-DNA project).

The band’s founding premise was explicitly anti-hierarchical: each member — Canal, Jacqui Ham, and Sally Young — played all instruments and rotated the role of vocalist and director on any given song. Songs were constructed through collective improvisation. The rotation policy meant the lineup as it appeared on stage or record was fluid by design.

An early member Karen Achenbach (Discogs: 1176021) played in the band before the stable trio of Canal, Ham, and Young was established; she is credited as a “Former Member” on the Early Live Life retrospective.

Early recordings — Out Tapes / Out Records, 1981–1985

The band’s first documented recording was a 1981 London live cassette, released in 1982 on their own Out Tapes imprint (OUT 001). The set captures the three-woman lineup at full intensity. Scott Piering (Discogs: 384533), who also recorded the session, co-produced the debut EP.

The band launched their own label, Out Records, and released three formal records through it:

  1. Ut EP (1984, OUT R 01) — self-produced with Piering; engineered by Charles Bullen (This Heat), John Loder, and Phil Legg.
  2. Confidential 12” (1985, OUT R 02) — three tracks; recorded by Tim Hodgkinson (Henry Cow); mixed by Scott Piering; features Hodgkinson on horn.
  3. Conviction LP (1985, OUT R 03) — recorded by Hodgkinson with Bill Gilonis and Charles Bullen assisting; Tim Hodgkinson also plays saxophone on “Stain”; guest drummer Allison Phillips on “Sick.”

Move to Blast First, 1987–1989

By 1987 the band had moved to Blast First (sub-label of Mute), Paul Smith’s label that was simultaneously home to Sonic Youth, Big Black, and Head of David.

Early Live Life (1987, BFFP 12) — a retrospective of early material, produced and engineered by Paul Kendall. The tracklist reveals the early rotating membership: Karen Achenbach plays guitar on two tracks; Allison Phillips appears as a visitor-drummer on “Fire in Philadelphia.”

In Gut’s House (1987, BFFP 17) — the band’s definitive studio statement. A double-LP produced by Ut with Paul Kendall co-producing and engineering; Kendall also plays percussion on “Homebleed” and programs vocal sequences on “Mosquitto Botticelli.” The album made the NME’s end-of-year Top 50.

Griller (1989, Torso CD 154) — final studio album. Recorded and engineered by Steve Albini. Percussionist Charlie D. (Discogs: 390826) plays drums on tracks 2–7, 10, and 11, marking a shift toward a fuller rhythmic arrangement on those cuts. The CD release came out on the Dutch label Torso; vinyl pressings exist on Blast First.

Hiatus and return

The band went on hiatus in 1991. They returned for live dates from 2010 onward. A 2017 reissue, UT & Confidential (Out Records), bundled the debut EP and Confidential 12”.

The Discogs discography entry also includes a member Graham Bonnar (Discogs: 1367240), whose specific role or period in the band is not documented in the DB.
