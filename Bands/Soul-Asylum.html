Soul Asylum

Alternative rock band. Minneapolis, Minnesota. Formed 1981. Discogs artist ID: 251941 (listed as “Soul Asylum (2)”).

Biography

Formation and Early Years as Loud Fast Rules (1981–1983)

Soul Asylum formed in Minneapolis in 1981 under the name Loud Fast Rules (Discogs artist ID: 1422017), featuring Dave Pirner (vocals, guitar), Dan Murphy (guitar), and Karl Mueller (bass). Pat Morley played drums in the original lineup. They changed the name to Soul Asylum in 1983.

Twin/Tone Records Era (1983–1988)

Pat Morley was replaced by Grant Young on drums in 1984. The band signed to Twin-Tone Records, the Minneapolis independent label also home to The Replacements and The Jayhawks.

Say What You Will… Clarence… Karl Sold the Truck (1984) was their debut, co-produced by Bob Mould of Hüsker Dü and co-produced with Blake Gumprecht. Engineered by Steve Fjelstad. Pirner played guitar, vocals, and saxophone; Murphy on guitar and vocals; Mueller on bass; Morley on drums.

Made To Be Broken (1986) followed, again produced by Bob Mould and engineered by Steve Fjelstad. By this point Grant Young had replaced Morley on drums.

While You Were Out (1986) continued the Twin/Tone run, listed alongside the 7” EP Time’s Incinerator (1986).

Clam Dip & Other Delights (1988) was a covers EP co-produced by the band and Tom Herbers, featuring backing vocals from Gary Louris of The Jayhawks and Heather Mackereth.

The band also appeared on the live bootleg compilation Just Plain Evil (1988, What Goes On Records).

A&M Records Era (1988–1991)

The band signed to A&M Records while still releasing material on Twin/Tone. Their A&M debut Hang Time (1988) was produced by Ed Stasium and Lenny Kaye, with engineering by Paul Hamingson and Stasium. Pirner played vocals, piano, and saxophone in addition to guitar.

Note: Discogs credits do not list Butch Vig on Hang Time or any other Twin/Tone-era release. The stub note about possible Vig involvement on earlier records is not supported by the database.

Sometime To Return (1988) was another A&M release, again produced by Ed Stasium and Lenny Kaye.

And The Horse They Rode In On (1990) was produced by Steve Jordan, with Joe Blaney as associate producer and recording/mixing engineer. Mueller played both electric bass and upright bass on this album. The lineup remained Pirner, Murphy, Mueller, and Grant Young.

Easy Street (1990) and Something Out Of Nothing (1990) were A&M singles from the same period.

Breakthrough: Grave Dancers Union (1992)

In 1992 the band signed to Columbia and released Grave Dancers Union, produced by Michael Beinhorn. The album became a commercial breakthrough, going triple platinum on the strength of “Runaway Train” — which won the Grammy for Best Rock Song in 1993. Sterling Campbell (later of David Bowie’s band) played percussion on the album; Gary Louris and Kraig Johnson of The Jayhawks contributed backing vocals on tracks 4 and 8. Booker T. Jones played Hammond B3 organ. Most of the album was mixed by Andy Wallace; the closing track was mixed by David Leonard. The band performed at President Bill Clinton’s inauguration in January 1993.

The album’s Discogs lineup: Pirner (vocals, guitar), Murphy (guitar, vocals), Mueller (bass), Grant Young (drums).

Key tracks: “Somebody to Shove,” “Black Gold,” “Runaway Train,” “Without a Trace.”

Let Your Dim Light Shine and Columbia Peak (1995)

Let Your Dim Light Shine (1995, Columbia) was produced by Butch Vig and co-produced with the band, with Andy Wallace mixing. It went platinum, driven by the single “Misery.” Additional Columbia singles from this album: “Just Like Anyone,” “Promises Broken.” This is Vig’s sole Discogs production credit with Soul Asylum.

The album also generated the single Can’t Even Tell (1994, Columbia) from the Clerks soundtrack, which the band co-produced.

Candy From A Stranger and Columbia Departure (1998)

Candy From A Stranger (1998, Columbia) was produced by Chris Kimsey, with David Nicholas engineering. Sterling Campbell returned on drums, replacing Grant Young. Jon Carin (known for work with Pink Floyd) played piano, Wurlitzer, Mellotron, harmonium, Farfisa organ, and lap steel. This was the band’s last album for Columbia.

Hiatus, Karl Mueller’s Death, and Return

Mueller died of cancer in June 2005. The band reconvened with Tommy Stinson (ex-The Replacements) on bass. They released The Silver Lining (2006, Legacy/Sony BMG).

The compilation Closer To The Stars - Best Of The Twin Tone Years (2006, Rykodisc) collected their early catalog with production and writing credits acknowledged.

Later Albums: 429 Records and Blue Élan (2012–2024)

Delayed Reaction (2012, 429 Records) was produced by John Fields and the band, with Tommy Stinson and John Fields both contributing bass. Michael Bland (drums), Tommy Barbarella (keyboards), Pete Donnelly, and Tommy Stinson all appeared as contributors. Tommy Stinson contributed vocals and guitar on some tracks.

Hurry Up And Wait (2020, Blue Élan Records) was produced by John Fields and the band, mixed by Fields and Paul David Hager, mastered by Emily Lazar. The performing lineup by this point: Pirner, Michael Bland (drums), Ryan Smith (guitar), Winston Roye (bass).

Dead Letter (2019, Blue Élan Records) and Slowly But Shirley (2024, Blue Élan Records) continued the band’s activity into their fourth decade.
