Madness

Ska / 2 Tone / pop band from Camden Town, North London. Formed 1976. Discogs artist ID: 10388. Aliases (performance names): Secret 7, The Hip Young Producers.

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1976–1979)

Madness coalesced in the North London borough of Camden in 1976 out of the ska and skinhead scene, initially performing as The North London Invaders. The classic seven-piece lineup settled around vocalist Graham McPherson (“Suggs”), keyboardist Mike Barson, guitarist Chris Foreman, saxophonist Lee Thompson, bassist Mark Bedford (“Bedders”), drummer Dan Woodgate (“Woody”), and vocalist/trumpet player Carl Smyth (“Chas Smash”). An earlier member, John Hasler, also appears in Discogs member credits but predates the classic lineup. The band took their name from the 1963 Prince Buster ska song.

Their debut single “The Prince” — a direct tribute to Prince Buster — was released in August 1979 on Two-Tone Records (catalog CHS TT 3, produced by Clive Langer solo under the credit “A Clanger Production”). It was only the third release on the Two-Tone Records label, following The Specials’ “Gangsters” and The Selecter’s “On My Radio.” The single reached #16 in the UK charts and announced the band as part of the emerging 2 Tone ska revival movement centred on The Specials and Jerry Dammers’ label.

Stiff Records and the Classic Period (1979–1984)

After the Two-Tone debut, Madness signed to Stiff Records and released the bulk of their classic catalogue there. The full-length debut One Step Beyond… appeared in October 1979, produced by Clive Langer & Alan Winstanley (their most important ongoing production relationship). The album reached #2 in the UK, establishing the template: compressed ska-pop, comedic and sharp lyrical observations on North London life, and the “nutty” visual identity. The band became one of the dominant forces in the UK singles charts across 1980–1985, eventually spending 214 weeks on those charts in a single decade — tied with UB40 for the record, but achieved in a shorter window (1980–1986).

The second album Absolutely (1980) expanded the palette while retaining the ska/pop core. Credits show Mike Barson playing vibraphone, marimba, harmonica, piano, and organ alongside standard keyboards; Lee Thompson on tenor and baritone saxophone; Chris Foreman on guitar, sitar, and slide guitar; Chas Smash on trumpet and vocals. Artwork for Absolutely was by Humphrey Ocean.

7 (1981) continued the run with Langer & Winstanley again producing and engineering. Key singles from 1980–81 include “Baggy Trousers,” “Embarrassment,” “The Return Of The Los Palmas 7,” “Grey Day,” “It Must Be Love,” and “Shut Up.”

The Rise & Fall (1982) is generally regarded as the artistic peak. The album expanded sonically into more melancholic territory, with string and brass arrangements provided by David Bedford (no relation to band member Mark Bedford — David Bedford was a classical/avant-garde composer who had worked with Kevin Ayers). Engineers on The Rise & Fall were Jeremy Allom and David Woolley. That same year, “House of Fun” — a song about a teenager trying to buy condoms at a chemist — became the band’s first UK number one single. “Our House” followed as a major international hit, particularly in the United States.

The American market was served by releases on Geffen Records. The US-only Madness compilation (Geffen, 1983) repackaged tracks for that market.

Keep Moving (1984) was the last album with Mike Barson, who had relocated to Amsterdam and left the band after its release. The album used string arrangements again by David Bedford. Engineers included Steve Churchyard, Matt Butler, Gavin Greenaway, and Carb. Mastering was by Denis Blackham. Percussion contributions from Luis Jardim appear here (he would continue to contribute to later Madness records and related work).

Zarjazz and The Madness Spinoff (1984–1988)

With Barson’s departure, Madness established their own label Zarjazz (Discogs ID: 25176), a Virgin sublabel. Mad Not Mad (1985, Zarjazz) was the first album without Barson and showed them reaching toward a more polished pop-soul direction. The album brought in guest musicians including Steve Nieve (Elvis Costello’s pianist) on keyboards, Afrodiziak on female backing vocals, Jimmy Chambers, Jimmy Helms, and Jimmy Thomas on male backing vocals, Gary Barnacle on horns, and Judd Lander on harmonica. The cover photograph was by Anton Corbijn. Artwork was by Ian Wright. Tom Morley (formerly of Scritti Politti) appears as a computer supervisor credit. Langer & Winstanley again produced, assisted by Mark Saunders, Matt Howe, and Richard Sullivan.

“(Waiting For) The Ghost-Train” (1986) was their final single before an initial split. The band effectively disbanded in late 1986.

A splinter group — four of the seven — continued as The Madness from 1988, comprising Suggs (Graham McPherson), Chas Smash (Cathal J. Smyth), Chris Foreman, and Lee Thompson. They released one album before dissolving.

Reunion and Later Work (1992–present)

The classic seven-piece reunited for the Madstock! festival at Finsbury Park, London, in August 1992 — documented on the live album Madstock! (Go! Discs, 1992). The reunion proved durable. The band returned to Virgin for the studio album Wonderful (1999), again with Langer & Winstanley producing. That album featured string arrangements by Simon Hale, conducted by Hale, with The London Session Orchestra.

Further studio albums followed: The Dangermen Sessions Volume One (2005, V2/Live & Intensified Records) — a covers album — and The Liberty Of Norton Folgate (2009, Lucky Seven), a concept piece about their North London heritage. Oui Oui Si Si Ja Ja Da Da (2012, Lucky Seven/Cooking Vinyl) was followed by Can’t Touch Us Now (2016, Lucky Seven/UMC) and most recently Theatre Of The Absurd Presents C’est La Vie (2023, BMG).

The band continues to perform with most of the classic lineup, making them one of the longest-running 2 Tone-era groups.
