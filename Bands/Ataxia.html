Ataxia

Discogs artist ID: 535432. Experimental rock trio formed in Los Angeles, February 2004. Members: John Frusciante (guitar, synthesizer, voice), Joe Lally (bass, voice), Josh Klinghoffer (drums, synthesizer, voice). Both albums released on Record Collection.

Overview

Ataxia existed for approximately two weeks in February 2004, forming and recording during a burst of sessions that produced enough material for two albums. The group played two shows at the Knitting Factory in Los Angeles before disbanding. Despite the brevity of their active existence, the recordings were issued three years apart: Automatic Writing in 2004 and AW II in 2007.

The lineup draws from distinct orbits of the Los Angeles and Washington DC rock scenes. John Frusciante was then mid-way through his second stint as guitarist of Red Hot Chili Peppers; he also produced both Ataxia records. Joe Lally was the founding bassist of Fugazi, which had gone on indefinite hiatus in 2003. Josh Klinghoffer was a Los Angeles multi-instrumentalist who had been collaborating closely with Frusciante (co-credited on A Sphere In The Heart Of Silence, also 2004, Record Collection). All three members share songwriting credit on both albums.

The music is guitar-led and largely instrumental, slow and expansive in structure, often described as post-rock with prog leanings. Frusciante’s role extended to synthesizer and occasional voice as well as production. Klinghoffer contributed synthesizer alongside drums, foreshadowing his later electronic work. Lally’s bass is prominent and melodic, consistent with his approach in Fugazi.

Ryan Hewitt engineered and mixed both albums. Bernie Grundman mastered Automatic Writing; Dave Collins mastered the CD edition of AW II, while Kevin Gray (credited as KPG) co-mastered the vinyl LP edition. Design on both releases was shared between Frusciante and Mike Piscitelli. Photography on both albums was by Lola Montes (Discogs ID: 2237966). Ryan Castle served as assistant engineer and assistant mixer on Automatic Writing.

Notes (web)

AW II delay and release decision

Studio: Both albums were recorded at Sunset Sound, Los Angeles. Engineer Ryan Hewitt confirmed the sessions used a Neve 8088 console, a Studer 800 16-track at 15ips, and John Frusciante’s guitar went through a Vox AC-15. (invisible-movement.net, Ryan Hewitt Q&A, November 2008)

Early plan: As of October 2004, Frusciante was already advertising a second Ataxia disc as “planned for next year” in Magnet Magazine, alongside a solo boxed set. This means the album was expected in 2005 — the delay ran even longer than the band initially intended. (Magnet Magazine, October 2004)

2006 leak: The album circulated online in March 2006, a year before the official release. The leaked version differed from the final release in track order, track titles, and sound quality. No public statement from any band member directly addresses whether the leak accelerated or prompted the official release decision. (Wikipedia, AW II article, as of 2026-07-14)

Official release: May 29, 2007 (CD) / June 19, 2007 (vinyl), on Record Collection. No interview has been located in which Frusciante, Lally, or Klinghoffer names the person or circumstance that finally triggered the 2007 release.

Lally on the delay (March 2008): In an interview transcribed by Invisible Movement, Joe Lally commented: “It’s strange when people start paying attention to it. I think we all agree that if we were to ever do it again, we’d do more work on it; that’s why the second release was kinda’ downgraded. If we thought people were that interested, it’d have been marketed more.” This is the only on-record explanation from a band member: the album was given a low-key release because the band underestimated the audience’s interest, not because of a specific dispute or legal obstacle. (invisible-movement.net, Joe Lally interview transcript)

Completeness of recordings: Ryan Hewitt confirmed in his 2008 Q&A that “everything that we recorded was released between those 2 records” — there is no unreleased third body of material from the sessions. (invisible-movement.net, Ryan Hewitt Q&A, November 2008)

Conflict note: The Overview section above states the sessions took place in “February 2004.” The Wikipedia article on AW II gives “January 2004” as the recording date. Discogs does not specify a month; the Klinghoffer fansite and Invisible Movement are not precise on month either. This should be resolved against liner notes or a primary-source interview. (Wikipedia, AW II)

Live Shows

Knitting Factory, Los Angeles — February 2–3, 2004

Both shows were at the Knitting Factory, Los Angeles. Support on both nights: Moris Tepper. No official live release exists. No soundboard recording has been located or reported in any source. All circulating recordings are fan-made audience recordings or amateur video. The shows were documented almost entirely by the bootleg/trading community, not by the band or label.

The label appears to be setlist.fm’s own categorisation of the improvised “Jam” tracks performed at these shows, not a real release.
