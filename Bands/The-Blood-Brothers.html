The Blood Brothers

Discogs ID: 253650
Location: Seattle, Washington
Active: 1997–2007
Genres: Post-hardcore, art punk, noise rock

Seattle post-hardcore band known for dual-vocalist attack, jagged rhythms, and a sound that escalated from fast-hardcore toward theatrically chaotic art-punk over five studio albums. Broke up in 2007.

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1997–1999)

The Blood Brothers formed in Seattle in 1997 and released a self-titled debut 7” EP on Hopscotch Records (cat# 11, 1997) — four tracks of fast hardcore with the dual-vocal interplay that would define the band. The following year they contributed a track to the Home Alive Benefit 98 compilation on Crash Rawk Records, and in 1999 released a split 7” with Milemarker on Boards Don’t Hit Back.

The founding lineup included six members, with Devin Welch (later of Chromatics and Past Lives) departing before the recording of the first full-length.

This Adultery Is Ripe (2000)

The band’s debut full-length, This Adultery Is Ripe (2000, Hopscotch Records), was produced by Matt Bayles, the Seattle engineer-producer who would also work the following record. Credits list the five-piece lineup: Whitney and Blilie on vocals, Votolato on guitar, Gajadhar on drums, Henderson on “bass, sounds.” The album expanded the hardcore template with more dissonance and dynamic range.

March On Electric Children / Three One G (2002)

The mini-LP March On Electric Children (2002) was released on Three One G, the San Diego label run by Justin Pearson of The Locust — reflecting Blood Brothers’ position in the overlapping network of American art-hardcore. It was again produced by Matt Bayles (credited “Matt B.”) and engineered with assistance from Troy Tietjen, then mastered by Ed Brooks. Henderson is credited with bass and sampler; the band handled its own artwork and photography.

Burn Piano Island, Burn (2002/2003)

The full-length Burn Piano Island, Burn moved the band to a higher profile producer: Ross Robinson, whose client list included Korn, Slipknot, At the Drive-In, and Glassjaw. The album was co-released between Artist Direct/BMG (2002) and later re-released on Second Nature Recordings (2003, with expanded credits). It marked a stylistic inflection point — the band moved toward more theatrical arrangements while retaining the aggression of the earlier records. The Second Nature pressing reveals additional musicians: Lenny Castro on percussion, and engineer/mixer Mike Fraser.

Ambulance vs. Ambulance EP (2003)

A 2003 EP on Second Nature Recordings, again produced by Ross Robinson and engineered by Mike Fraser. All five band members share songwriting credits. This was the last Robinson-produced release.

Crimes (2004)

Crimes (2004, V2 / Radar Records) was a significant moment: the band landed a major-label deal and produced what many consider their finest record — dense, chaotic, and melodically sophisticated. Production moved from Robinson to John Goodmanson, who produced, recorded, and mixed it, with the band co-producing. Goodmanson also brought on Kip Beelman as second engineer.

The Discogs credits for Crimes reveal the full scope of Henderson’s instrumental range: bass, guitar, double bass (upright), synthesizer, accordion, piano, electronics (laptop), and backing vocals. Whitney is credited on vocals, piano, electric piano (Wurlitzer), electric organ (Farfisa), and percussion. Votolato: guitar, trumpet, baritone guitar, percussion, whistle, e-bow, and backing vocals. The album was mastered by Brian Gardner.

Love Rhymes with Hideous Car Wreck (2004, V2 UK) was a companion EP.

Rumors Laid Waste (2004, Sound Virus / Luckyhorse Industries) — a rarities/b-sides compilation also released this year.

Young Machetes (2006)

The final studio album, Young Machetes (2006, V2), was co-produced by Guy Picciotto (of Fugazi) and John Goodmanson, with the band credited as co-producers. Goodmanson also handled engineering and mixing. Picciotto’s involvement brought the record a certain rawness and directness — his production style favors ensemble feel over polish. Mastered by Brian Gardner.

Preceding it were two UK singles on Wichita Recordings: Laser Life and Set Fire to the Face on Fire (both 2006).

Henderson contributed photography alongside Blilie and Zoë Verkuylen; Blilie and Andy Dixon (2) handled layout.

Breakup (2007)

The Blood Brothers broke up in 2007. No formal breakup statement or final tour is documented in Discogs. The band reunited for select live appearances in subsequent years (not documented in the DB).

Post-Blood Brothers

Past Lives

Four members — Jordan Blilie, Morgan Henderson, Mark Gajadhar, and Devin Welch — formed Past Lives after the breakup. (Discogs artist ID: 1246858.)

Morgan Henderson and Fleet Foxes

Morgan Henderson became one of the most widely active post-breakup members. He is a credited member of Fleet Foxes (Discogs ID: 1105843) and appeared on their Helplessness Blues (2011, Bella Union — double bass, woodwind) and Crack-Up (2017, Nonesuch). His instrumental range in the DB is unusual: bass, upright double bass, clarinet, bass clarinet, piano, keyboards, mandolin, synthesizer, accordion, flute, tenor saxophone, guitar — applied across projects ranging from post-hardcore to folk-chamber music. He also appeared on releases by Robin Pecknold side projects and on recordings for Constellation Records and Jagjaguwar.

Morgan Henderson was also a member of Nineironspitfire and Sharks Keep Moving pre-Blood Brothers, and formed Past Lives alongside Blilie, Gajadhar, and Welch.

Johnny Whitney and Devin Welch

Devin Welch (the early member who left before This Adultery Is Ripe) later appeared in Chromatics, Past Lives, Shoplifting, and several other Seattle-adjacent acts.

Johnny Whitney post-Blood Brothers projects are not documented in Discogs in a primary capacity.

Notes (web) — Devin Welch departure

Web trawl conducted 2026-08-26. The following sources were consulted: Wikipedia (The Blood Brothers), Wikipedia (Cody Votolato), thebloodbrothers.com/members-profile, en-academic.com mirror of the Wikipedia article, Exclaim! (ex-Blood Brothers/Past Lives), Ryan’s Rock Show (Past Lives interview with Blilie and Welch), Punknews.org interviews, Vice (2014 Blilie reunion interview). None contain any statement — from Welch, Blilie, or any other band member — about the reasons or circumstances of the departure.

Confirmed facts from web sources:

  • Welch is described as having “left the band prior to the recording of This Adultery Is Ripe” — consistent with the Discogs/Wikipedia timeline of 1997–1999. (en-academic.com, mirroring Wikipedia)
  • The official band site (thebloodbrothers.com) states Votolato joined at age 17 and replaced Welch “before the release of the first album” — Votolato is described as “a year younger than the rest of the band.” (thebloodbrothers.com)
  • Wikipedia (Cody Votolato article) notes he “attended Redmond High School with his bandmates in the late 1990s when the band originally formed, graduating Spring of 2000.” This aligns with the Wikipedia (band article) statement that the band “left on their first tour immediately after Votolato graduated from high school.” (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Votolato, as of 2026-08-26)
  • Exclaim! (2008) refers to Welch only as “the Blood Brothers original guitarist who exited the group at the beginning of the decade before hooking up with cult acts Soiled Doves, Chromatics and Shoplifting, among others.” No reason given. (exclaim.ca)
  • Welch “had stayed in contact” with the core members over the years between his exit and Past Lives forming in 2007 — suggesting the split was not acrimonious, though this is an inference only. (exclaim.ca)

Conclusion: No source — interview, press, or fan documentation — addresses whether the departure was voluntary, a mutual decision, or a lineup change imposed by the band. The circumstantial evidence (Welch remaining in friendly contact with Blilie/Henderson/Gajadhar for nearly a decade before Past Lives; his appearance on the debut EP cover per Rumors Laid Waste credits) is consistent with an amicable exit, but no source confirms this. The question remains open.
