Telstar Ponies

Glasgow rock band formed in 1994 by David Keenan on his departure from 18 Wheeler. Discogs artist ID: 51612.

Overview

Telstar Ponies were formed in Glasgow in 1994. Their sound ranges across Krautrock, experimental noise, folk, and post-punk, shifting markedly across the band’s three line-ups. The original quartet (Keenan, Neil Grant, Sushil K. Dade, John Hogarty) barely made it to record before Neil Grant was replaced by Brendan O’Hare (ex-Teenage Fanclub); Dade and Hogarty then departed before the debut single was even released, replaced by Gavin Laird and Rachel Devine. That second line-up recorded the two Fire Records albums. After splitting in 1997 — when O’Hare and Laird left to form Macrocosmica — the band reformed in 1999 around Keenan, Devine, Laird and a new rhythm section (Raymond Prior, Kenneth Broom), but a planned third album mixed by Christoph Heemann and Andrew Chalk was never released. The band was described at various points as being on “long term sabbatical.” (Wikipedia)

David Keenan went on to write extensively on music (liner notes, essays) and run the Volcanic Tongue shop and label in Glasgow. Brendan O’Hare went on to play drums briefly in Mogwai and later in Macrocosmica and Lightships.

Connections

  • Stevie Jackson — songwriting co-write on 1994 debut EP (track 3, “S.Jackson”); member of Belle & Sebastian
  • Tony Doogan — engineer/recording throughout 1994–1996 catalog; primary engineer on all Fire Records output; also longtime engineer for Belle & Sebastian and Mogwai
  • Brendan O’Hare — second-line-up drummer; ex-Teenage Fanclub (1989–1994); early Mogwai member; went on to Macrocosmica and Lightships
  • Sushil K. Dade — original line-up bassist; also Soup Dragons, BMX Bandits, Future Pilot A.K.A.; sleeve design on early singles
  • John Hogarty — original guitarist; also BMX Bandits, The Pastels, National Park, Phantom Engineer (2)
  • Richard Youngs — joined for Voices From The New Music (1996); Glasgow-based experimental musician with extensive solo career
  • Bill Wells — piano on 2001 Farewell, Farewell; Glasgow jazz improviser; member of Bill Wells Trio and collaborator with Aidan Moffat
  • Mick Cooke — trumpet on Farewell, Farewell; Belle & Sebastian brass player
  • David Keenan — founder; ran Volcanic Tongue (Glasgow shop/label); wrote liner notes for Editions Mego, Dancing Wayang, and other experimental labels; associated with Keenan/Russell Duo (with Richard Youngs)
  • Fire Records — UK indie label; all main-period releases 1994–1996
  • Geographic — 2001 single release label (also home to International Airport)
