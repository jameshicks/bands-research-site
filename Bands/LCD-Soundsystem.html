LCD Soundsystem

Origin: New York City, USA
Active: 2001–2011; reformed 2015–present
Discogs ID: 38948
Name variations: LCD, LCD Sound System
Labels: DFA Records, EMI, Parlophone, Columbia

Dance-punk and electronic project founded and led by James Murphy as the flagship act of DFA Records. Conceived as a studio and live project with a fluid lineup, LCD Soundsystem became the defining band of the early 2000s NYC dance-punk revival and one of the most critically acclaimed acts of its decade. Explicitly indebted to 99 Records-era NYC — Liquid Liquid, ESG, and Glenn Branca are acknowledged reference points — as well as post-punk, Krautrock, house, and disco.

Biography

Formation and Early Singles (2001–2004)

Murphy had been working as a sound engineer and studio hand in New York, logging an early recording credit on the Evergreen (5) self-titled LP (Hi-Ball Records, 1996) alongside Nicolas Vernhes. He worked as live sound engineer for Six Finger Satellite (Providence RI), an experience that would eventually lead him to co-found DFA Records with Tim Goldsworthy and Jonathan Galkin in 2001.

LCD Soundsystem’s debut release was Losing My Edge (DFA, 2002), a seven-minute tirade of name-dropping self-deprecation set over a motorik groove. It attracted immediate attention on the NYC scene and in the UK press. Give It Up followed the same year on Output Recordings (UK). The project operated during these years as a studio entity — Murphy and Goldsworthy recording as The DFA production alias — with Nancy Whang featured on additional vocals.

Yeah (2003) and Daft Punk Is Playing At My House (2004) cemented the band’s profile internationally, with the latter becoming an indie-dance crossover hit in both the US and UK.

Debut Album (2005)

LCD Soundsystem (DFA, 2005) was assembled from the early singles plus new material and released as a double album. Produced by The DFA; mixed by Murphy and Andy Wallace. The album established the sound: Murphy’s sardonic, referential lyrics over groove-oriented arrangements drawing from disco, house, and post-punk. Stand-outs included “Tribulations” and “Movement.”

A touring live band began to form around this period, with Nancy Whang (keys/vocals), Patrick Mahoney (drums), Tyler Pope (bass), Phil Mossman (guitar), and others rotating through.

Sound of Silver (2007)

Widely considered the band’s masterpiece. Produced by The DFA; mixed by Murphy and Dave Sardy; engineering assistance from Eric Broucek, Ian Neill, and Matt Thornley; mastered by Geoff Pesche. Co-writing credits on tracks 2, 5, 6, and 9 went to Patrick Mahoney and Tyler Pope.

Key tracks: “Get Innocuous!”, “Someone Great,” “All My Friends,” “Us v Them,” “New York, I Love You But You’re Bringing Me Down.” The album is structurally adventurous — “All My Friends” runs eight minutes on a single repeated piano figure — and emotionally direct despite Murphy’s usual ironic remove.

The single All My Friends (Harvey Remix) was released separately on DFA/EMI; the Harvey remix became a dance-floor staple. Someone Great was also issued as a standalone 12”.

This Is Happening (2010)

Third studio album. Murphy took sole producer credit (no longer billing The DFA). Mixed by Murphy and Dave Sardy; mastered by Robert Weston (Bob Weston). Engineering assistance from Alec Gomez, Gunnar Bjerk, and Matt Thornley. Photography by Ruvan Wijesooriya.

Announced as the band’s final album. Stand-outs: “Dance Yrself Clean,” “Drunk Girls,” “I Can Change,” “Home.”

Dissolution and MSG Farewell (2011)

LCD Soundsystem played their farewell concert at Madison Square Garden on April 2, 2011. Liquid Liquid opened — a public acknowledgment of the historical debt Murphy had always cited. The show was documented in the film Shut Up And Play The Hits (Oscilloscope Laboratories, 2012) and the live album The Long Goodbye: LCD Soundsystem Live At Madison Square Garden (DFA/Parlophone/Warner Bros., 2014).

Reformation and American Dream (2015–2017)

Murphy announced a reformation in 2015 with a one-off Christmas single (“Christmas Will Break Your Heart,” self-released on the LCD Soundsystem label). The full band reconvened and released American Dream (Columbia/DFA, 2017), co-produced by Murphy and mixed by Murphy (tracks 1–6, 8, 10) and Dave Sardy (tracks 7, 9). Mastered by Bob Weston. The album debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 — the band’s commercial peak.

The lineup for American Dream and touring drew on returning members and the Electric Lady Sessions (2019) documented further live and studio work from this period.

Ongoing Activity (2022–present)

Murphy contributed the track “New Body Rhumba” to Noah Baumbach’s White Noise (2022, Columbia/DFA). New singles have continued: X-Ray Eyes (DFA, 2024), Home (Tom Sharkett Edit) (DFA, 2025).
