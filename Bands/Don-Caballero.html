Don Caballero

Instrumental math rock band, Pittsburgh PA, 1991–2001 (original run), 2003–2009 (Che-led revival). Discogs artist ID: 254549

Overview

Don Caballero are considered one of the originating forces in math rock, though the members reportedly disliked the label. Their sound centred on the “lead drumming” of Damon Che — complex, polyrhythmic, and compositionally dominant — combined with interlocking guitar parts from Mike Banfield and, later, Ian Williams, whose finger-tapping approach and heavy delay-pedal use became a defining texture. Despite the improvised feel, every piece was a structured composition. They had no guitar solos.

Formed in Pittsburgh in 1991 by Damon Che, Pat Morris (bass), and Mike Banfield; Ian Williams joined as second guitarist in 1992. Signed to Touch And Go in 1993.

Biography by Era

Formation and Early Singles (1991–1992)

The trio of Damon Che, Pat Morris, and Mike Banfield launched with the The Lucky Father Brown 7” EP (1992, Pop Bus Records), a three-track self-release recorded by Lee Hollihan. The same year they put out a second 7” single — Unresolved Karma / Puddin’ In My Eye — on Broken Giraffe (BG 002). Ian Williams came aboard that year, expanding the lineup to a quartet.

A split 7” with And And And… / First Hits appeared on Third Gear Records (1993), recorded by Steve Albini.

Touch And Go Years: Original Lineup (1993–2000)

For Respect (1993, Touch And Go TG120) — debut LP, recorded while the band was still a four-piece with Pat Morris on bass. Mastered by John Golden (credited as “JG”). Morris departed after this record. Multiple bassists rotated through before Don Caballero 2.

Our Caballero / My Ten Year Old Lady Is Giving It Away (7”, 1993, Touch And Go) — single bridging the first two albums.

Don Caballero 2 (1995, Touch And Go TG143) — Matt Jencik played bass. Produced by Al Sutton, who would remain the band’s primary engineer and producer through American Don. Widely regarded as a high-water mark of the genre.

Waltor / Shuman Center 91 (7”, 1996, Chunklet Magazine) — credited to Damon Che, Mike Banfield, and Pat Morris, indicating Morris returned for some sessions.

What Burns Never Returns (1998, Touch And Go tg185cd) — Pat Morris returned on bass; full four-piece with Banfield and Williams. Produced by Al Sutton, mixed by Bob Ebeling. Morris left again after this release. Mike Banfield also departed after subsequent touring, replaced by Jon Fine (of Bitch Magnet). Eric Emm (bassist in Ian Williams’s side project Storm And Stress) joined on bass.

Singles Breaking Up (Vol. I) (1999, Touch And Go TG184cd) — compilation of the early 7” sides. Documents the Pat Morris and Matt Jencik bass eras across different tracks; recording credits split among Lee Hollihan, Bob Weston, and Al Sutton.

American Don (2000, Touch And Go tg218) — recorded by Steve Albini (“The Proprietor”), lacquer cut by John Golden. By this point a trio: Che, Williams, and Emm. The last album for Touch And Go and the last of the original run. Released on double LP.

Breakup (2001)

A van accident on tour in 2001 effectively ended the band. Underlying tensions — particularly between Che and Williams — had already been building. Ian Williams went on to form Battles (2002); Jon Fine returned to Bitch Magnet work and other projects; Eric Emm joined Tanlines among others.

Revival under Damon Che (2003–2009)

Che revived the Don Caballero name in 2003. He invited Banfield and Morris back; both declined. Che stated he would not work with Ian Williams again. The new lineup drew from Pittsburgh’s Creta Bourzia scene: Eugene Doyle and Jason Jouver alongside Che, with Jeffery Ellsworth also appearing.

The revived band signed to Relapse Records.

World Class Listening Problem (2006, Relapse RR 6693-1) — LP.

Punkgasm (2008, Relapse RR 6124-2) — recorded by Al Sutton (tracks 1–13) and Jason Jouver; mixed by Al Sutton; mastered by Alan Douches. Performers credited: Damon Che, Eugene Doyle, Jason Jouver.

The band ended in 2009.

Archival Releases (2012–2016)
