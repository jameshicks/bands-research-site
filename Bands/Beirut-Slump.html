Beirut Slump

New York No Wave band. Active July 1978 – June 1979. Originally called Belfast Ghetto. Discogs ID: 285757.

First band formed by Lydia Lunch, concurrent with Teenage Jesus And The Jerks. Originated as participation in a benefit for Vivienne Dick’s film Guerrillère Talks (July 14, 1978, Millennium, East 4th Street — first show, as Belfast Ghetto). Four concerts total. Disbanded June 1979 when Lydia Lunch left for a European tour.

Sound: “a cross between early Swans, electric eels and Black Sabbath — loping, dirged-out, organ-fed, stumbling sound punctuated by Lydia Lunch’s serrated guitar slashings.” Sclavunos and Liz Swope’s sluggish tempos anticipated the early Swans.

Recorded at Hothouse Studio, New York, December 8, 1978.

Web Notes

Belfast Ghetto → Beirut Slump name change

What is documented: First show (July 14, 1978, Millennium) was billed as “Belfast Ghetto” (From the Archives chronology). By April 29, 1979 the billing was “Beirut Slump.” No source found that pins the changeover to a specific show or date between those two.

August 17, 1978 Max’s Kansas City show: The concert entry describes “a ‘No Wave jam band’” including members of The Contortions and The Mumps alongside Lydia Lunch — no band name billed for this slot. Whether they performed as Belfast Ghetto, Beirut Slump, or nameless at this show is unresolved. (From the Archives chronology)

Why “Beirut”: No interview or published source found explaining the name change. In 1978, Beirut was the byword for urban warfare and civil collapse — consistent with the band’s sonic aesthetic — but this is inference, not a documented statement from Lunch or any member.

Conflict note: The punk music catalogue (punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com) dates the band “Aug 78 – Jun 79,” implying the Beirut Slump name was already in use by August 1978. This is plausible if the August Max’s show was the first under the new name, but the August show’s billing was not Beirut Slump per the concert listing — treat the “Aug 78” start date as the approximate period of adoption, not confirmed debut of the name.

Information (supporting act, April 29, 1979, Max’s Kansas City)

No Wave band closely tied to NO Magazine (which Sclavunos co-founded). The NO Magazine #4 flexidisc (1979) featured Information tracks “Translator” and “Alphabet” (lineup: Phil Dray, Chris Nelson, Rick Brown) plus “I Like Boys Who Say No” (credited to Sclavunos and “Delolio”). Information later evolved into The Scene Is Now (Nelson, Dray, Jeff McGovern). Sources: Bandcamp Daily; Wikipedia — Jim Sclavunos (as of 2026-05-06).

Boris Policeband (final show, May 17, 1979, Mudd Club)

Solo No Wave performer. Real name Mark Perelman (also credited as Boris Pearlman). Classically trained violist; studied and taught at CalArts. Joined early No Wave band Jack Ruby in 1973. Adopted the “Boris Policeband” name after a 1976 performance at which he recited police scanner chatter while playing electric viola. Instrument: electrified viola run through a personal FM transmitter and police walkie-talkies worn around his waist. Performed at CBGBs, Max’s Kansas City, the Mudd Club, The Kitchen, Artists Space. Main release: Policeband: Stereo / Mono (7”, 1979, prod. Dike Blair). Also on New York Noise Vol. 3 (2006). Ended the Policeband project mid-1980s to return to classical viola; died between 1986 and 2011. Sources: Wikipedia — Boris Policeband (as of 2026-06-26); Discogs — Mark Perelman.
