Overview

The Dismemberment Plan were a Washington, DC indie rock and post-hardcore band formed in 1993 and active through 2003. Their sound fused angular guitar work with dance-inflected rhythms, processed electronics, and singer Travis Morrison’s conversational, emotionally direct vocal style — a synthesis that set them apart from both the straight-ahead DC hardcore tradition and the mainstream alternative rock of the mid-1990s. Their album Emergency & I (1999, DeSoto Records), produced and mixed by Chad Clark and J. Robbins and engineered at Don Zientara’s Inner Ear Studios, became one of the defining indie rock records of its era.

The classic lineup — Travis Morrison (vocals/guitar), Jason Caddell (guitar), Eric Axelson (bass), Joe Easley (drums) — stabilized by the time of their first DeSoto album ! (1995). Their earlier debut EP Can We Be Mature? (1994, Alcove! Recordings) featured Steve Cummings (3) on drums instead of Easley. The band broke up in 2003 following A People’s History Of The Dismemberment Plan (2003, DeSoto), a Japanese-market best-of. They reunited in 2011 and released Uncanney Valley in 2013 on Partisan Records.
