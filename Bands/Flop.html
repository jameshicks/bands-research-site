Overview

Flop were a Seattle power pop/punk outfit of the early-to-mid 1990s, built around Rusty Willoughby (vocals, guitar), Bill Campbell (guitar), Paul Schurr (bass), and Nate Johnson (drums). Their eight releases fall entirely within the decade, moving from Dashboard Hula Girl Records to Frontier Records and then 550 Music/Epic — a brief flirtation with major-label infrastructure. Compilation appearances alongside Mudhoney, Nirvana, Pearl Jam, and the Melvins placed them inside the Seattle grunge ecosystem, though their own sound ran toward power pop and indie rock rather than heavy guitar noise. An unexpected transatlantic thread runs through the credits: British synth-pop architect Martin Rushent, best known for producing Human League’s Dare, emerges as their top collaborator.

Martin Rushent / Flop connection — web research (2026-11-17)

Question: What drew Martin Rushent to produce Whenever You’re Ready (1993)?

Short answer: No surviving public source explains the connection. The specific question — how Rushent came to work with a Seattle power pop band in 1993, well outside his known wheelhouse — remains unanswered.

Sources checked (all drew blanks on this specific question):

  • Wikipedia article on Whenever You’re Ready (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whenever_You%27re_Ready_(album)) — confirms Rushent produced the record; no background on the connection.
  • Sound On Sound Rushent profile (https://www.soundonsound.com/people/martin-rushent-producer) — covers his 1970s–1980s career; no mention of Flop or 1990s Seattle work.
  • The Arts Desk, “Autonomy: The Productions of Martin Rushent” reissue review (https://theartsdesk.com/new-music/music-reissues-weekly-autonomy-productions-martin-rushent) — compilation spans 1977–1985 only; no 1990s work discussed.
  • Dan Epstein, Substack piece on Whenever You’re Ready (https://danepstein.substack.com/p/flop-whenever-youre-ready) — calls Rushent a “legendary Buzzcocks/Stranglers/Human League producer” and says he “brought out the sparkle in Willoughby’s melodies without sapping the band of its power”; no explanation of how they connected.
  • Trouser Press, Flop entry (https://trouserpress.com/entry.php?a=flop) — credits Rushent only parenthetically for “the mix”; no background.
  • Trouser Press, Whenever You’re Ready review (https://trouserpress.com/reviews/flop/) — same: Rushent credited with the mix and the “Big Rock sound”; no origin detail.
  • sorock.nl encyclopaedia entry on Rushent (https://sorock.nl/en/encyclopedie/artiest/martin-rushent.html) — notes that “in the 1990s he distanced himself from the studio and opened a dance club at Greenham Common”; makes the Flop credit more anomalous but gives no explanation of it.
  • Washington Post, “Flop Sans Flotsam Is Powerful Pop” (1993-11-05) — 403 blocked; could not retrieve.

Contextual note: Rushent’s 1990s production work was sparse — the sorock.nl entry confirms he largely stepped away from studios in that decade. The Flop credit is therefore an outlier in his own career as well as an odd fit geographically. The Whenever You’re Ready sessions were recorded at Hanzsek Audio in Seattle (confirmed by the Wikipedia album article), so Rushent travelled to Seattle for the project rather than bringing the band to a UK studio.
