Glenn Branca Ensemble

Guitar ensemble led by Glenn Branca, New York City. Discogs artist IDs: 3374382 (Ensemble), 55972 (Glenn Branca solo). Active in various forms from the late 1970s through Branca’s death in May 2018; final release under the Ensemble name issued 2019 (posthumous). The Ensemble is the live and recording vehicle for Branca’s large-scale guitar symphony compositions — typically four or more heavily amplified electric guitars in unusual tunings, augmented by a rock rhythm section.

Overview

Glenn Branca (October 6, 1948, Harrisburg, PA – May 13, 2018) was an American composer who studied performing arts at Emerson College. He formed Theoretical Girls with Jeffrey Lohn in 1977, and shortly after The Static, before pivoting to orchestral guitar work that would occupy him for four decades. The Ensemble is simultaneously a compositional project and a repertory group: the same core musicians often appear across many recordings spanning years, and rosters shifted in distinct generational waves.

Branca’s compositional language centered on massed electric guitars — soprano, tenor, alto, baritone — augmented by mallet guitars, bass, drums, and occasional keyboards. Works were numbered symphonies; by his death he had completed at least 13. Symphony No. 9 (L’Eve Future, 1995) extended the format to include a full orchestra (the Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra) and choir. Symphony No. 13 (Hallucination City, 2016) used 100 guitars.

Early champions included 99 Records (Lesson No. 1, The Ascension) and Les Disques Du Crépuscule (Symphony No. 3). From the 1990s onward Atavistic became the primary home for archival and new releases; the final 2019 album appeared on Systems Neutralizers. Cover art on several key releases was by artist Robert Longo.

History by Era

Late 1970s – 1981: Origins and The Ascension

Branca’s early ensemble work grew out of the No Wave milieu. The debut standalone release under his name, Lesson No. 1 (99 Records, 1980), is a single-piece work for guitar ensemble produced by Mark Bingham with Ed Bahlman (of 99 Records) as assistant producer. The landmark The Ascension (99 Records, 1981) established the ensemble’s classic early sound: guitars by Branca, Lee Ranaldo, David Rosenbloom, and Ned Sublette; drums by Stephan Wischerth; bass by Jeffrey Glenn; recorded and mixed by James Farber; produced by Ed Bahlman. The opening track’s composition was credited jointly to Branca, Glenn, and Wischerth.

1983: First Symphonies — ROIR and Les Disques Du Crépuscule

Two symphonies appeared in 1983. Symphony No. 1 (Tonal Plexus) (ROIR cassette) documented a large ensemble: Thurston Moore and Craig Bromberg on tenor guitar, Lee Ranaldo on soprano guitar, Wharton Tiers on keyboards/guitar/percussion (co-producer), Barbara Ess on baritone guitar and bass, David Rosenbloom, Ned Sublette, Robert Harrison, Stephan Wischerth (drums), Anne DeMarinis (keyboards, co-producer), and wind players including Richard Edson (trumpet) and Fritz van Orden (tenor saxophone). Wharton Tiers co-produced alongside Branca and DeMarinis.

Symphony No. 3 (Gloria) (Les Disques Du Crépuscule, 1983) was recorded live and featured a large musicians section including Lee Ranaldo, Thurston Moore, Michael Gira, Barbara Ess, Craig Bromberg, Jeffrey Glenn, Margaret DeWys, and others. Josh Baer served as executive producer; James Farber produced.

1989: Symphony No. 6 — Blast First

Symphony No. 6 (Devil Choirs At The Gates Of Heaven) (Blast First, 1989) introduced what became a stable mid-career ensemble: Page Hamilton (later of Helmet), Jonathan Bepler, Evans Wohlforth, Eric Hubel, John Myers, Carolyn Master, Ellen Watkins, Phil Kline, and Algis Kizys on guitar/bass; Stephan Wischerth drums; Wharton Tiers produced.

Early 1990s: Symphony Nos. 2 and 8/10 — Atavistic

Symphony No. 2 (The Peak Of The Sacred) (Atavistic, 1992) featured a guest percussion section with Z’EV. Mallet guitar ensemble included Lee Ranaldo, Thurston Moore, Barbara Ess, Craig Bromberg, Jeffrey Glenn, Robert Harrison, Al Arthur, David Linton, Sue Hanel; Wharton Tiers and Patrick Derivaz mixed.

Symphony Nos. 8 & 10 (The Mysteries) (Atavistic, 1994) — studio recording with the late-period core lineup: David Reid (bass/octave guitar), Miriam McDonough (bass/keyboards/assistant conductor), Virgil Moorefield (drums), Carolyn Master, Steven Antonelli (alto guitar), Brannon Hungness, Ellen Watkins (soprano/octave guitar), John Myers (soprano guitar), Eric Hubel, Phil Kline (tenor guitar). Wharton Tiers engineered live sound.

The 2003 release Symphony Nos. 8 & 10 – Live At The Kitchen (Atavistic / Music Video Distributors, catalog: see Discogs 265604) is credited to “Glenn Branca Ensemble” (as “Branca Ensemble”) — this is the release most directly associated with Discogs entry 3374382. Personnel identical to the 1994 studio recording; Atavistic recorded live.

Mid-1990s: Further Symphonies

  • Symphony No. 5 (Describing Planes Of An Expanding Hypersphere) (Atavistic, 1995): ensemble includes Evans Wohlforth, Jonathan Bepler, Dan Braun, Matt Munisteri, Mark Roule, Emily XYZ, Miriam McDonough, Virgil Moorefield. Branca conducted and played harmonics guitar.
  • Symphony No. 9 (L’Eve Future) (Point Music, 1995): for orchestra and choir — Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra, Camerata Silesia choir, conducted by Christian Von Borries. A departure from the guitar ensemble format.

2016–2019: Late Work and Final Recording

Symphony No. 13 (Hallucination City) For 100 Guitars (Atavistic, 2016): Reg Bloor as concertmaster, John Myers conducting, Virgil Moorefield on drums; recorded live with 100 guitarists.

The Third Ascension (Systems Neutralizers, 2019) — the final release, issued posthumously. Personnel: Reg Bloor (guitar, mixed by, mastered by, co-producer), Arad Evans, Brendon Randall-Myers, Luke Schwartz — guitars; Greg McMullen — bass; Owen Weaver — drums. Branca conducted and produced; recorded by Eben Hoffer and Stephen Cooper. Cover art by Robert Longo.

Symphony Gaps — Web Research Notes

Symphony No. 4 (Physics)

No commercial audio release existed for most of Branca’s life. The symphony was performed in 1983 (including a European tour). The only surviving document was a VHS tape titled Glenn Branca: Symphony No. 4/Physics (1984, Monday/Wednesday/Friday Video Club), an 11-minute docu-collage directed by Arleen Schloss, filmed in Berlin 1983; sound engineer Connie Kieltyka assembled the four-track audio. Personnel documented in the VHS: Thurston Moore, Lee Ranaldo, Barbara Ess, Margaret DeWys, Stephan Wischerth, Amanda Linn. (WorldCat OCLC 78376260; Kickstarter campaign)

A Kickstarter campaign by Reg Bloor (launched November 2025, closed January 2026) raised $4,449 against a $4,000 goal (90 backers) for an audio release of Symphony No. 4 on Systems Neutralizers. As of May 2026 the release has not yet appeared on Discogs. (Kicktraq)

Symphony No. 7 (For Orchestra): Live in Graz

Recorded live at the world premiere in Graz, Austria, 1989; the recording was not released until 2010. Label: Systems Neutralizers (catalog SN03CD). Performed by the Graz Festival Orchestra, conducted by Günter Meinhart. (Discogs release 2557060)

Symphonies No. 11 and No. 12

Neither symphony had been commercially released as of Branca’s death in 2018 or as of the time of this research. Symphony No. 11 is written for traditional orchestra (not guitar ensemble). Symphony No. 12 (Tonal Sexus, c.1998) also remained unissued; in a 2016 interview Branca mentioned Red Bull Music Academy might record and release it, but that did not occur. Both remain in the “performed but unreleased” category alongside Symphonies 14 and 15. (Red Bull Music Academy Daily, 2016)

Neutral Records vs. Les Disques Du Crépuscule — Symphony No. 3

Symphony No. 3 (Gloria) was originally Branca’s own release on Neutral Records (catalog NEUTRAL 4 / N-4), recorded live at Brooklyn Academy of Music, January 16, 1983; mixed at Power Station, NYC. Les Disques Du Crépuscule issued a simultaneous or near-simultaneous Belgian/European pressing (catalog TWI 151, August 1983). The two releases are separate pressings of the same recording rather than a licensor/licensee relationship — both appeared in 1983. Josh Baer is executive producer on both. Discogs lists the Crépuscule pressing (ID 442311) as the primary entry; the Neutral Records original (ID 24676421) is a separate red-vinyl pressing. (eBay listing for Neutral original; RYM Neutral pressing)

Thurston Moore and Lee Ranaldo — Ensemble Exit

Both appeared on Symphony No. 3 (recorded January 16, 1983). The Branca/Sonic Youth split happened during or immediately after the European tour for Symphony No. 4 in spring/summer 1983 — Sonic Youth piggybacked their own first European tour onto Branca’s symphony tour, which he was reportedly unhappy about. Sonic Youth released Confusion Is Sex in February 1983, marking the de facto end of their Branca ensemble participation. A falling-out over disputed royalty payments for Neutral Records releases also contributed to the break. Lee Ranaldo is credited on Symphony No. 2 (recorded May 14, 1982; released 1992), meaning his last studio recording with the ensemble predates even Symphony No. 3. Thurston Moore appears on Symphonies 1, 2, and 3 only. Neither appears on any Branca release after 1983. (nirvana-legacy.com; Wikipedia Sonic Youth as of 2026-05-22)
