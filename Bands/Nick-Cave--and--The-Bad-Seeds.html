Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds

Origin: Melbourne, Australia (relocated to Berlin, then London)
Active: 1983–present

Post-punk / art rock band led by Nick Cave, formed after the dissolution of The Birthday Party. Kid Congo Powers was a member from the early period; Blixa Bargeld of Einstürzende Neubauten was also a longtime member (1983–2003). The band and The Gun Club operated in closely overlapping milieux — Pierce and Cave were contemporaries in the European post-punk underground.

Discogs artist ID: 36665.

Founding Lineup (1984, From Her To Eternity)

  • Nick Cave — vocals
  • Barry Adamson — bass (founding member; departed c.1986–1988; returned for Push The Sky Away 2013)
  • Blixa Bargeld — guitar
  • Mick Harvey — guitar, drums
  • Hugo Race — guitar (early period only)
  • Anita Lane — vocals/lyrics

Also in lineup across various eras: Kid Congo Powers, JG Thirlwell, Jim Sclavunos, Conway Savage, Martyn P. Casey, Warren Ellis, James Johnston.

Key Discography Notes

  • From Her To Eternity (Mute, 1984) — debut; engineered by Flood
  • The Firstborn Is Dead (Mute, 1985) — Adamson played bass, guitar, organ, drums
  • Kicking Against The Pricks (Mute, 1986) — covers album
  • Push The Sky Away (Bad Seed Ltd., 2013) — Adamson returns on bass

Blixa Bargeld’s Departure (2003)

Blixa Bargeld announced his departure on 3 March 2003, after 20 years as guitarist. Nocturama (February 2003) was his final studio album with the band; it had been recorded in one week during an Australian tour in March 2002. (RTÉ, 3 March 2003)

Official statements at the time

Bargeld’s public statement: “My leaving has nothing to do with artistic or personal differences with the band, I just feel it is time to concentrate on other creative areas in my life.” Cave responded: “We have parted as friends and we will miss him dearly.” Scheduled European summer 2003 dates were confirmed to proceed without him. (RTÉ, 3 March 2003)

Stated reasons (later interviews)

In a 2012 interview Bargeld was more specific: “There was no quarrel between Nick and I. I left only because of my personal life, I mean I had gotten married and playing in two bands was no longer an option for me.” He also cited dissatisfaction with the band’s management as a factor: “I left, basically, because of our management, and I felt I couldn’t keep up a marriage and two bands.” When Cave offered to replace the manager, Bargeld said he would consider it but never followed up, and Cave chose not to pursue it further. (Digging A Hole, Guestlisted blog, November 2012; Waivio summary of Bargeld/Cave interview)

How Cave learned of the departure

Bargeld sent Cave an email out of the blue saying “I’ve decided to leave the Bad Seeds.” Cave later described it in his book Faith, Hope and Carnage (2022, with Seán O’Hagan) as “a weird ‘Dear John’ letter of the most rudimentary and unsatisfying kind, almost as if it had been written by a robot.” (Rolling Stone / Yahoo News excerpt, Faith, Hope and Carnage, 2022)

The final studio incident (Wim Wenders session)

The last time Cave and Bargeld were in the studio together before the email — during a recording session for a Wim Wenders documentary about blues music — ended in a dispute. Cave had proposed an uptempo arrangement of a J.B. Lenoir song, deliberately modelling the performance on the Muppets to avoid a “slow, lugubrious” blues cover. After multiple takes with the band playing energetically, Bargeld became frustrated and threw down his guitar, saying “I didn’t get into rock & roll to play rock & roll.” When Cave jokingly responded “What about the Muppets?”, Bargeld replied: “Fuck the Muppets! You be a fucking Muppet!” and stormed out. Wenders did not capture the incident on film. (Rolling Stone / Yahoo News excerpt, Faith, Hope and Carnage, 2022)

Cave’s retrospective assessment

In Faith, Hope and Carnage, Cave acknowledged Bargeld’s guitar contributions had become “inconsequential” in later recordings, and praised his earlier distinctive approach — conceptualising overdubs methodically after the song was already recorded rather than playing along in the room. Cave stated: “I miss Blixa enormously.” (Rolling Stone / Yahoo News excerpt, Faith, Hope and Carnage, 2022)
