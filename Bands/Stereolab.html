Stereolab

UK/French indie-pop and experimental group formed in London, 1990. Core duo: Tim Gane (guitar) and Lætitia Sadier (vocals). Active 1990–2009, on hiatus 2009–2019, reunited 2019. First new studio material since 2009 released in 2025.

Discogs artist ID: 388

Biography

Formation and early years (1990–1992)

Stereolab was founded in London by Tim Gane — a record collector and guitarist previously in McCarthy (the left-wing indie group he co-founded with Malcolm Eden) — and French singer Lætitia Sadier, whom he had met while she was visiting London. The original lineup also included bassist Martin Kean (ex-The Chills) and drummer Joe Dilworth.

The band launched their own imprint, Duophonic Super 45s (later expanded to Duophonic Ultra High Frequency Disks), a parent label called Duophonic (Discogs label ID: 1734). Their earliest recordings — the Stunning Debut Album and Super 45 EPs (1991, Duophonic Super 45s) — established the template: motorik rhythms borrowed from German kosmische music, Farfisa organ drones, and Sadier’s cool, near-monotone French and English vocals. The band signed to Too Pure (a Beggars Group imprint) for their debut LP Peng! (1992), which was produced with engineer Robbs and recorded with Kean on bass. The same year brought Switched On (1992, Too Pure), the first of several compilation/odds-and-ends volumes gathering early singles.

Vocalist/multi-instrumentalist Mary Hansen joined in 1992 and became a defining presence for the next decade, adding a second vocal layer and instrumental colour across nearly all subsequent records.

Expansion and breakthrough (1992–1996)

Andy Ramsay took over drums from Joe Dilworth in 1992 and remained the drummer through the 2009 hiatus and into the reunion. Sean O’Hagan — frontman of The High Llamas and formerly of Microdisney — joined briefly on keyboards 1993–1994, and his orchestral arranging influence carried forward even after he left the full-time lineup. Katharine Gifford (also of Moonshake and later Snowpony) was also keyboardist 1993–1994. Bass duties passed from Martin Kean (departed 1993) to Duncan Brown (1993–1996).

The 1993 double album Transient Random-Noise Bursts With Announcements (Elektra/Duophonic) was the US breakthrough, co-produced and engineered by Phil Wright (2) alongside the band. Elektra became their primary US outlet through the 1990s. Mars Audiac Quintet (1994) brought expanded brass arrangements courtesy of O’Hagan and featured engineer/producer Paul Tipler, who became a recurring collaborator. Emperor Tomato Ketchup (1996) brought in John McEntire of Tortoise as co-producer and engineer alongside Tipler, deepening the Chicago post-rock connection; new bassist Morgane Lhote joined that year and the band’s keyboard palette widened further.

Singles on Duophonic through this period (including “Ping Pong,” “Wow And Flutter,” “Cybele’s Reverie,” “French Disko”) built a dense catalogue of 7” and 12” releases, many with elaborate sleeve designs and multiple B-sides unavailable on the albums.

Orchestral peak (1997–2001)

Dots And Loops (1997, Elektra/Duophonic) is arguably the high point of the orchestral-pop strand: McEntire produced half the album while Mouse On Mars (Andi Toma and Jan St. Werner) produced three tracks, contributing unusual electronic textures. O’Hagan arranged brass and strings; Julian House (of The Focus Group) contributed sleeve artwork — beginning a long design association. Richard Harrison replaced Duncan Brown on bass 1996–1998; Simon Johns took over from 1998 to the 2009 hiatus.

Cobra And Phases Group Play Voltage In The Milky Night (1999) added Jim O’Rourke as co-producer and occasional performer (bass, guitar, keyboards), alternating tracks with McEntire. Rob Mazurek contributed cornet. Refried Ectoplasm (Switched On Volume 2) (1995, Drag City) collected more rarities; Aluminum Tunes (1998, Warp Records) was a European rarities compilation.

Sound-Dust (2001, Duophonic) was the last album with Mary Hansen. It again split production between McEntire and O’Rourke, with O’Hagan on guitar and arrangements and Mikael Jorgensen (later of Wilco) on keyboard assistance.

Mary Hansen was killed in a cycling accident in London in December 2002.

Later period and hiatus (2002–2009)

Instant 0 In The Universe (2003, Elektra) was recorded and mixed by Fulton Dingley; Margarine Eclipse (2003, Duophonic) was released the same year — the two LPs together functioning as a double release (marketed separately but timed together). Dominic Jeffery joined on keyboards 2001–2006, replaced by Joe Watson from 2006 to the 2009 hiatus; Joe Walters contributed French horn 2006–2009.

Fab Four Suture (2006, Too Pure) and Chemical Chords (2008, 4AD) saw a return to more melodically concise writing. Chemical Chords was engineered and mixed by Joe Watson; O’Hagan again arranged strings and brass. The anthology Serene Velocity (2006, Elektra/Rhino) gathered the definitive Elektra-era highlights. Not Music (2010, Drag City), recorded around the same sessions as Chemical Chords, was released posthumously after the band went on hiatus in 2009.

Reissues, reunion, and new material (2019–present)

In 2019 Stereolab reunited for live performance and launched a campaign of seven-album reissues under the Duophonic/Warp banner. The Switched On archive series continued: Electrically Possessed (Switched On Vol. 4) (2021, Duophonic/Warp), Pulse Of The Early Brain (Switched On Volume 5) (2022, Duophonic/Warp), and Little Pieces Of Stereolab (A Switched On Sampler) (2024). The reunion lineup brought in new bassist Xavi Muñoz (2) and retained Joe Watson on keys and Andy Ramsay on drums. Julien Gasc is also listed as a band member on Discogs.

In 2025 the band released their first newly recorded music since 2009/10: the LP Instant Holograms On Metal Film and accompanying singles including Melodie Is A Wound, Cloud Land / Flashes In The Afternoon, Standard - Unsolicited Stereolab Material, Transmuted Matter, and Fed Up With Your Job? / Constant And Uniform Movement Unknown.
