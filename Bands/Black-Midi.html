Black Midi

Experimental rock band from London, UK. Discogs artist ID: 6447017. Formed 2017 after the core members graduated from The BRIT School for Performing Arts & Technology, Croydon. On indefinite hiatus as of 2024.

Members:

  • Geordie Greep — vocals, guitar, piano, multi-instrumentalist (Discogs ID: 6967892)
  • Morgan Simpson — drums (Discogs ID: 6967891; real name Morgan George Simpson)
  • Cameron Picton — bass, guitar, flute, multi-instrumentalist (Discogs ID: 6967893; real name Cameron Overeynder)
  • Matt Kelvin — guitar (Discogs ID: 6967890; real name Matthew Anthony Kwasniewski-Kelvin; departed before Hellfire)

All four members also appear together under the side project listing Black Midi, New Road (Discogs ID: 12823787; Cameron Picton also in My New Band Believe, ID: 17379355).

Formation and Early Career (2017–2018)

Black Midi formed “properly” in 2017 when the four founding members graduated from The BRIT School. They quickly became a fixture of the London underground scene, building a reputation for chaotic, high-energy live shows before releasing any formal studio material.

Their first official release was the self-titled bmbmbm single (Speedy Wunderground, 2018), produced by Dan Carey — their first and only release on that label. The single captured the band’s early approach: abrasive guitar noise, stop-start dynamics, and collectively written compositions (all four members credited as writers on that record). Dan Carey’s production gave it a raw, immediate character consistent with his Speedy Wunderground house style.

The same year they appeared live with Damo Suzuki: Damo Suzuki Live At The Windmill Brixton With ‘Sound Carriers’ Black Midi (2018, self-released, Not On Label) documented that collaboration.

Schlagenheim (2019)

Label: Rough Trade (cat. no. RT0212LP) | Producer: Dan Carey | Released: 2019

By 2019 Black Midi had signed to Rough Trade and released their debut album Schlagenheim, produced and mixed by Dan Carey. The album is a concentrated dose of the band’s live-honed approach — interlocking guitars, technical drumming from Morgan Simpson, surging and lurching song structures, and Geordie Greep’s distinctive vocal style.

Cavalcade (2021)

Label: Rough Trade (cat. no. RT0212LP / RT0212CD) | Producers: Black Midi, John ‘Spud’ Murphy, Marta Salogni (track A1) | Released: 2021

The second album marked a significant expansion of the band’s palette. Matt Kelvin had departed; Geordie Greep, Morgan Simpson, and Cameron Picton continued as a trio, supplemented by a recurring cast of session contributors — most prominently keyboardist Seth Evans (Discogs ID: 6850108), saxophonist Kaidi Akinnibi (Discogs ID: 5360760), cellist Blossom Caldarone (Discogs ID: 9423442), trombonist Joe Bristow (Discogs ID: 6975325), and violinist Joscelin Dent-Pooley (Discogs ID: 7549290).

The bulk of the album was produced and mixed by John ‘Spud’ Murphy (Discogs: John Murphy (32), ID: 5494106) — credited on the UK LP pressing as “Producer, Mixed By” alongside a “Producer” credit for Black Midi as a whole. Marta Salogni produced, programmed, and mixed track A1 (“John L”) on her own. Harvey Birrell handled mastering on the UK pressing.

Cavalcade was also released with an accompanying Cavalcovers EP (Rough Trade, 2022), and a John L / Despair double single (Rough Trade, 2021) preceded the album.

Geordie Greep’s playing expanded dramatically on Cavalcade — the track-level credits show him simultaneously handling guitar (in multiple electric and acoustic varieties), lap steel, accordion, piano, organ, synthesizer, and vocals, often across five or six different instruments on a single track. Cameron Picton similarly ranged across bass, guitar, flute, marxophone, bouzouki, synthesizer, and programming. The album’s genre register incorporates elements of chamber music, jazz, folk, and progressive rock alongside the noise-rock core.

Hellfire (2022)

Label: Rough Trade (cat. no. RT0321LPE / RT0344SE) | Producer: Marta Salogni | Co-producer: Max Goulding (“Max ‘Sizzle’ Goulding”, Discogs ID: 6726753) | Released: 2022

Hellfire was the first album Marta Salogni produced in full. The core trio — Greep, Simpson, Picton — returned without Seth Evans as a recurring presence across the full record, though Evans does appear on “Eat Men Eat” (A3). The expanded live ensemble from Cavalcade continued: Kaidi Akinnibi, Joe Bristow, Joscelin Dent-Pooley, Blossom Caldarone, and Ife Ogunjobi (trumpet, Discogs ID: 7958340) all appear across multiple tracks. Demi Garcia Sabat (Discogs ID: 6882649) contributes flamenco percussion (palmas, cajón) on several tracks. Pedal steel guitarist BJ Cole (Discogs ID: 61104) appears on “Still,” “The Defence,” and “27 Questions.” Marta Salogni herself plays Roland Jupiter 8 synthesizer on “Still.”

A notable track is “953 (Live At Electrical Audio),” credited with Steve Albini (Discogs ID: 59373) as Recorded By — the only track across the three albums with a live-in-studio recording at Albini’s Chicago facility.

Hellfire is musically the most theatrically ambitious of the three albums. Geordie Greep’s guitar and piano parts on “Hellfire” (title track) are dense with orchestral whips, whistles, classical guitar, mandolin, and lap steel alongside his vocals and string arrangements. Cameron Picton takes lead vocals on several tracks (“Eat Men Eat,” “Still,” “Half Time”), establishing him as a second distinct voice in the band. The album also included a special edition flexi-disc (7”, Side C only) with the Albini-recorded live track.

Solo Work and Post-Hiatus Activity

Geordie Greep released solo material on Rough Trade. Discogs notes an indefinite hiatus as of 2024.

Cameron Picton records as Camera Picture (Bandcamp). Morgan Simpson uses alias DJ Orient (2). Geordie Greep uses aliases DJ Greep and MC Spritz.
