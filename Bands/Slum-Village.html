Slum Village

Sep 27, 20266 min read

Slum Village

Discogs ID: 10586
Also known as: J-88
American hip-hop group from Conant Gardens, Detroit, Michigan. Founded mid-1990s (Discogs profile says 1996).

Biography

Origins and the Dilla Era (1996–2001)

Slum Village formed in Detroit’s Conant Gardens neighbourhood, coalescing around three MCs/producers who grew up together: T3 (T3 (2), real name RL Altman III), Baatin (Baatin, real name Titus Glover), and the producer-rapper J Dilla (James Yancey, 1974–2006). In their early years they recorded under the alias J-88.

Their debut, Fan-tas-tic Vol. 1 (1997), was a self-pressed cassette on Donut Boy Recordings (Discogs ID 51440), produced in full by Dilla under his Jay Dee alias. It circulated among Detroit heads and tastemakers but received no commercial release. A Japanese 12” pairing two tracks from it appeared on Sudden Impact the same year.

Fantastic Vol. 2 was recorded around 1997–98 and circulated widely as a promo and bootleg before official release. A&M Records (via Good Vibe Recordings/Wordplay Records) cleared it for commercial release in 1998–2000. The commercial pressings credit Jay Dee / James Yancey as primary producer on almost all tracks, with one track produced by D’Angelo (track B2 on the vinyl) and one by Pete Rock (track E1 on vinyl). D’Angelo also contributed a written-by credit. Engineers Kirk Yano, Frank Amato, and Todd Fairall worked the sessions; Dilla and Fairall handled mixing. The album is widely regarded as one of the defining records of the neo-soul / alternative hip-hop moment.

By 2001 Dilla left the group to pursue a solo deal with MCA Records. His departure was not acrimonious; he continued producing for the group sporadically.

Transition and Tainted (2001–2002)

With Dilla gone, Elzhi (Jason Powers, Discogs ID 1746431) joined as the third MC. The Tainted EP (2001, Barak Records / JCOR Entertainment, cat. INTR-10631-1) split production: Karriem Riggins produced the A-side (tracks A1–A3), and Scott Storch produced the B-side (B1–B3). Young RJ (Ralph J. Rice Jr.) appears in mixing and recording credits, an early indication of his growing role. Baatin continued as a member through this period.

Trinity (Past, Present And Future) (2002, Capitol Records, Discogs release ID 257762) was the group’s major-label full-length. Discogs has no additional production credits listed on the canonical pressing — the credits data for Trinity is sparse in the DB.

Post-Dilla Era and Detroit Deli (2003–2006)

After Dilla’s death in February 2006, the group continued under T3’s direction. Detroit Deli (A Taste Of Detroit) (2004, Capitol Records, Discogs ID 577437) was produced predominantly by B.R. Gunna (credited on 9 of 13 tracks). Black Milk contributed additional vocals on tracks 1, 10, and 13 — one of his first major-label appearances. Baatin’s health complications led to his departure around this period.

The self-titled Slum Village (2005, Barak Records, Discogs ID 557551) continued with the Elzhi-era lineup. Production was shared between Young RJ (tracks 1, 5, 8, 10), B.R. Gunna (3, 4, 7, 12), and Black Milk (2, 9, 11). Carl Broaden contributed co-production and keyboards.

Later Years and Continuing Lineups (2007–present)

Following Baatin’s death in July 2009, T3 restructured Slum Village as an ongoing project. The archival J88 (2010, Barak Records / Donut Boy Records) compiled early J-88-era material. Villa Manifesto (2010, E1 Entertainment / Ne’Astra Music Group) introduced the T3 + Young RJ duo configuration that has persisted since. T3 and Young RJ are credited as executive producers on Yes! (2015, Ne’Astra Music Group / Yancey Media Group). The group released F.U.N in 2024 (self-released).
