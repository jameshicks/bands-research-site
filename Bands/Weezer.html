Weezer

American alternative rock / power-pop band formed in Los Angeles, California, 1992. Discogs artist ID: 105730.

Overview

Weezer coalesced around singer-guitarist Rivers Cuomo after he relocated to Los Angeles from Connecticut in 1989. The classic founding lineup — Cuomo, bassist Matt Sharp, drummer Pat Wilson, and guitarist Jason Cropper — formed on February 15, 1992 (Wikipedia). Cropper was replaced by Brian Bell during recording of the debut. The band signed to Geffen Records (via the DGC sublabel) in June 1993 after their demo tape reached A&R rep Todd Sullivan.

Their 1994 self-titled debut, known as the Blue Album, was produced by Ric Ocasek at Electric Lady Studios in New York City. Backed by Spike Jonze-directed videos for “Undone – The Sweater Song” and “Buddy Holly,” it achieved multi-platinum status and defined a post-grunge power-pop moment. The follow-up Pinkerton (1996), self-produced by the band, took a rawer, more confessional direction inspired by Cuomo’s experiences at Harvard University; initially panned commercially, it became a cult touchstone and an acknowledged influence on 2000s emo (Wikipedia).

After Pinkerton touring ended in mid-1997, the band went on hiatus. Matt Sharp left in 1998 to focus on The Rentals. The band briefly performed under the alias Goat Punishment during 1998–2000 before returning in 2001 with the Green Album, again produced by Ric Ocasek, with new bassist Mikey Welsh. Welsh left for health reasons mid-tour and was replaced by Scott Shriner in 2001, cementing the lineup that has persisted to the present.

Subsequent albums ranged from hard-rocking (Maladroit, 2002; Van Weezer, 2021) to orchestral pop (OK Human, 2021) to covers (Teal Album, 2019). The band has sold over 35 million records worldwide and has one Grammy win (Best Short Form Music Video for “Pork and Beans,” 2009) from five nominations (Wikipedia).

Research Queue

  • Maladroit production: Discogs credits Weezer as self-produced, but Wikipedia says they self-funded sessions against Geffen’s wishes. Confirm whether any outside producer had any formal role, or if Chad Bamford’s “additional producer” credit was the only non-band production involvement.
  • Pinkerton recording timeline: multiple engineers are listed (Fridmann, Kasper, Puig, Barresi, Norrell, Jacobs, Rondinelli). Were these engineers for different recording sessions or different tracks? A contemporaneous press piece or studio session log would clarify.
  • Spike Jonze’s Pinkerton photography: Discogs credits him for photography on Pinkerton (1996). Was this a formal commissioned shoot or repurposed tour/session photography? A single source (e.g. the album’s liner notes or an interview with Jonze) should confirm.
  • Karl Koch’s precise credit history: Discogs lists him variously as “Photography By,” “Other [Regulator],” “Other [Quintus Socius Hominum],” “Other [Metallurgical Compliance Officer]” across releases. What is his actual credited role on the Blue Album specifically?
  • Ric Ocasek’s posthumous Van Weezer vocals: Discogs credits him for “Vocals [Additional Vocals]” on track A4 of Van Weezer (2021). Which song is A4, and was this recorded before or during his last sessions with the band in 2019 before his September 2019 death? An interview with Rivers Cuomo or the band would confirm the recording date.
  • Goat Punishment Discogs entry (ID 11537777): the alias is shared by Rivers Cuomo and Pat Wilson in the Discogs member list. Is it formally registered as a separate artist entity, and does it have any officially logged releases beyond the 1998/2000 live performances?
  • Homie (3) — Discogs ID 2322192: Wikipedia says Rivers Cuomo formed Homie during the hiatus with Greg Brown (Cake/Deathray), Matt Sharp, Yuval Gabay (Soul Coughing), Adam Orth, and Mikey Welsh. Confirm whether Discogs has any catalog entries for this project beyond the single “American Girls” (1998 Meet the Deedles soundtrack).
  • Jason Cropper’s departure: Wikipedia says he “was fired during recording” of the Blue Album. Discogs still credits him as Written-By on “My Name Is Jonas” (A1). Confirm the specific circumstances of his departure and whether he contributed any other recordings to the Blue Album sessions.
