SPK

Australian/UK experimental industrial group. Discogs artist ID: 22501. Founded 1978 in Australia by Graeme Revell and Neil Hill; one of the founding acts of the industrial genre alongside Throbbing Gristle and Einstürzende Neubauten.

Overview

SPK (the initials stood for several things across their career — Sozialistisches Patienten Kollektiv, Surgical Penis Klinik, System Planning Korporation, SepPuKu) began in Sydney, Australia in 1978–1979 as a noise/industrial collective rooted in extreme sonic assault, political provocateurism, and confrontational performance. They were early adopters of the post-industrial framework pioneered by Industrial Records / Throbbing Gristle, releasing their debut EP on their own Side Effects label in 1979.

Over roughly a decade, SPK underwent a dramatic stylistic evolution: from harsh noise and power electronics (1979–1982), through a transitional period of rhythmic metal-percussion industrial (1982–1984), into synth-pop and electronic body music (1984–1985), and finally into a solo orchestral/ambient phase under Graeme Revell (1985–1988). By the late 1980s SPK had effectively dissolved as a collective; Revell went on to a major Hollywood film-scoring career.

A notable tragedy: in 2025, the entire SPK archive — musical multitracks, masters, and photographs — was destroyed in the Los Angeles wildfires. Revell subsequently reactivated the SPK name with his son Robert Revell.

Era-by-Era Narrative

Phase 1: Noise and Industrial Assault (1978–1982)

SPK formed in Sydney in 1978, co-founded by Graeme Revell (then using the alias EMS AKS or Operator) and Neil Hill (aka NE/H/IL). Their context was the psychiatric hospital — both founders had connections to institutional settings, and the group took conceptual inspiration from the German Sozialistisches Patienten Kollektiv, a radical patient-run organisation.

The 1979 debut EP No More (Side Effects PRS-2617) was an Australian-only 7” with punk velocity and drum-machine industrialism; it credits David Virgin (bass), Dan Rumour (guitar), Neil Hill (voice/rhythm machine), and Graeme Revell (voice/synthesizer). The track “Germanik” would be reworked repeatedly.

The 1981 LP Information Overload Unit (Side Effects SER01) — credited on the sleeve as “System Planning Korporation” — was recorded in the UK and defined SPK’s noise period: synthesizers, drum programming, tape manipulation, and electronics by Operator (Revell) and Tone Generator (Dominic Guerin), with Mike Wilkins on guitar/bass/tape. The tracklist deploys provocative German-language titles and clinical/psychiatric language (Stammheim Torturkammer, Epilept: Convulse). The lacquer was cut by Steve Angel.

The 1982 LP Leichenschrei (Thermidor T-9, released in the US on the Berkeley label affiliated with Subterranean Records) was the peak of SPK’s noise-industrial phase. It collected a rotating cast: Neil Hill (synthesizer, electronics, vocals), Graeme Revell (as Oblivion), Karel Van Bergen (violin, vocals), James Pinker (drums, synkussion, metal percussion), Paul Charlier (bass), Peter Kennard (guitar/bass), Sinan Leong (vocals/photography). Crucially, Lustmord — then Brian Williams, who had been assisting SPK during their UK residency — appears as “Lust Mord” credited for “Additional Vocals”; the same credit also names him for “Text” in the sleeve notes. This is one of the earliest Lustmord credits. The record was mixed by Phil Punch and SPK jointly. The two sides are titled Seite ((Lysso)) and Seite ((Klono)) — lysergic/pharmaceutical references.

Phase 2: Metal Percussion and Transition (1982–1984)

By 1983 SPK had relocated substantially to London and was slimming to a smaller core. The Auto-Da-Fé compilation (Walter Ulbricht Schallfolien WULP 002, Germany) gathered earlier material; the label was founded by Uli Rehberg (Ditterich von Euler-Donnersperg), who also produced the release.

The 12” Dekompositiones (Side Effects SER 03, 1983) — credited as “SepPuKu” — was a three-tracker emphasising mid-period rhythmic industrial: “Another Dark Age,” “Twilight Of The Idols,” “Culturcide.” Mixed by Utopia/MPO.

The 12” Metal Dance (Desire Records WANT X 1, 1983) is the pivotal transitional release. Produced by Michael Johnson and SPK, it features Graeme Revell (synthesizer, electronics, metal percussion), Sinan Leong (vocals), and Derek Thompson (trumpet, keyboards, metal percussion). The B-side “Will To Power” (a Nietzsche reference in keeping with SPK’s intellectual provocations) already sounds like synth-pop. Graven Images did the sleeve art; Peter Ashworth photographed. The Desire Records connection suggests UK major-label interest was already forming.

Phase 3: Synth-Pop and Major Label (1984–1985)

Machine Age Voodoo (WEA, 1984) was SPK’s fullest pivot to mainstream electronic pop. By this stage the unit was essentially Revell and Sinan Leong (whom Revell had married), with a large session cast: Phil Scorgie (bass), James Kelly (guitar), Jeff Bartolomei and Sam McNally (additional keyboards), Graham Jesse (saxophone), Mary Bradfield-Taylor (additional vocals). Revell arranged, produced, and played all primary instruments. Fashion house Comme Des Garçons provided costume design; Judy Blame contributed jewellery. The album reached Elektra for US release, and the single “Junk Funk” got remixed as Junk Funk (The Special Crash Mix) for a UK 12” on Elektra. The album was co-credited as composed by both Revell and SPK; Sinan Leong has co-writing credits.

Flesh And Steel (Elektra, 1985 promo 12”) continued in this vein, engineered by Christo Curtis and produced/written by Revell and Leong.

Phase 4: Orchestral and Ambient (1985–1988)

From 1985–1986 onward, SPK became effectively a solo vehicle for Revell, with Sinan Leong providing vocal contributions. The music shifted toward dense, instrument-layered orchestral ambient:

Zamia Lehmanni (Songs Of Byzantine Flowers) (Side Effects SER 09, 1986) is the masterwork of this era. Revell played all instruments across both sides — marimba, flutes, bells, piano, cello, violin, synthesizer, congas, Tibetan drums, steel bowls, Balinese bell trees — alongside recordings of orchestras, factory ambiences, railway yards, chains, and the Russian Old Orthodox Church Of The Holy Annunciation choir of Sydney. Sinan Leong provides voice on several tracks; Jan Thornton voices the two-part “In Flagrante Delicto.” Sleeve design by Graven Image (4); photography by Jurgen Ryck. The Gaudi detail of the Sagrada Familia graces the cover.

Digitalis Ambigua, Gold And Poison (Nettwerk, 1987) and Breathless (Nettwerk, 1988) continued the arc with Karina Hayes providing additional voice alongside Sinan Leong.

Oceania — In Performance 1987 (Side Effects, 1988) documented a live performance, with PAG, Brian Williams, and Mark Cage credited as contributors.

Dissolution and Legacy

After Breathless, Revell moved into professional film scoring, transitioning seamlessly from industrial-turned-orchestral to Hollywood. His film credits include The Hand That Rocks The Cradle (1992), Body Of Evidence (1993), Hard Target (1993), The Crow (1994), Street Fighter (1994), The Craft (1996), The Crow: City of Angels (1996), The Saint (1997), Daredevil (2003), Sin City (2005), and many others — all through Varèse Sarabande, Milan, Hollywood Records, and related soundtrack labels.

The Side Effects label, started in 1976 by SPK members, was gradually handed to Brian Williams (Lustmord) around 1985. Williams expanded it to other artists, distributing through Rough Trade until that label’s bankruptcy forced a pause; it restarted in the early 1990s with World Serpent, then Soleilmoon / Dark Vinyl, until ceasing in April 1999, absorbed by Soleilmoon. Its sublabel Musique Brut was Revell’s personal imprint for his ambient/experimental solo work.

The Grey Area (a Mute sublabel) issued CD reissues of the early SPK catalog in the early 1990s (Auto Da Fe, Box), done in cooperation with Side Effects.

Connections

  • Lustmord — Brian Williams (Lustmord, ID: 8265) was an early SPK associate, credited on Leichenschrei (1982) for additional vocals and text. He later inherited the Side Effects label from SPK. His own dark ambient project Lustmord is listed as a distinct Discogs entry (ID: 8265), separate from his “Brian Williams” artist page (ID: 226979).
  • Throbbing Gristle — The principal UK peer and fellow Industrial Records-adjacent act; SPK credited alongside TG in histories of industrial music’s founding generation.
  • Einstürzende Neubauten — German contemporary; shared use of metal percussion and found-sound industrialism.
  • Industrial Records — Not a direct release label for SPK, but the defining label of the movement they emerged from.
  • Uli Rehberg (Ditterich von Euler-Donnersperg, ID: 478830) — German experimental figure; produced Auto-Da-Fé on his own Walter Ulbricht Schallfolien label.
  • Graeme Revell — After SPK dissolved, became a major Hollywood film composer (The Crow, Daredevil, Sin City, etc.). Also ran Musique Brut for personal experimental releases.
  • Nettwerk — Canadian label that signed SPK for their final two studio albums; later major label for Sarah McLachlan and other artists.
  • Sub Rosa (Belgium) — Released System Of Flux & Energies (1985), a Belgian compilation documenting the transitional era.

Open Questions

  • The precise timeline of Neil Hill’s departure from the group is unresolved from Discogs data alone — he appears on No More (1979) and Leichenschrei (1982) but not on subsequent releases. Web sources would clarify.
  • The nature of Brian Williams’s early role (roadie/technician vs. collaborative musician) before his Leichenschrei vocal credit is unclear from Discogs.
  • Annie Stubbs (ID: 380934) is listed as a member on Discogs but has no credited releases in the discography — her exact role and period unknown.
  • The precise circumstances of the 2025 Los Angeles wildfire archive loss and the nature of the Revell/Robert Revell SPKtR reactivation are not documented in Discogs.
  • Sinan Leong’s aliases (Sinan Hayes, Sinan Revell) suggest name changes around marriage/divorce — timeline unclear from Discogs.
  • Despair (1982) appears in the discography credited to Twin Vision (Australia) — may be an alternate name or Australian release under a different guise; needs clarification.
