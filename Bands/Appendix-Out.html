Appendix Out

Discogs ID: 105126 Active: 1996–2001 Origin: Scotland, UK Genres: Folk Rock, Lo-Fi, Post-Rock, Experimental Folk Members: Alasdair Roberts, Gareth Eggie, Tom Crossley, Dave Elcock (early), Louise Dowding (early), Eva Peck (early) Labels: Palace Records, Drag City, Creeping Bent, Temporary Residence Limited, Bad Jazz, Liquefaction Empire, Galvani Records, Western Vinyl, Shingle Street

Appendix Out was a Scottish folk-rock group built around songwriter Alasdair Roberts (born in Swabia, Germany; raised in Scotland), active from 1996 to 2001. The group released four albums and a cluster of singles and split 7”s, all on small US and UK independents, before Roberts moved into a solo career under his own name. Their sound blended acoustic British and Scottish folk traditions with lo-fi recording aesthetics, string arrangements, and occasional post-rock density.

Biography

Formation and Early Singles (1996–1997)

Appendix Out’s debut was a two-track 7” on Palace Records — the Louisville, Kentucky label run by the Palace Brothers / Will Oldham circle — titled Ice Age (1996, PR10). The single’s two tracks (“Ice Age” / “Pissed With You”) were credited as written by Alasdair Roberts and recorded in the lo-fi/indie-rock vein associated with the early-mid 1990s Palace Records roster.

The debut album The Rye Bears A Poison appeared in 1997 on Drag City (DC126CD), the Chicago independent that had by then absorbed Palace Records artists and distributed Oldham-adjacent folk material. The lineup for the album included Alasdair Roberts (guitar, vocals, keyboards, mandolin, sampler), Dave Elcock (bass guitar, violin, harmony vocals), Louise Dowding (cello, twelve-string guitar, keyboards), and Eva Peck (drums, guitar, slide guitar, autoharp), with engineering by Johnny Cameron (3). Eva Peck contributed additional lead vocals on “Lassie, Lie Near Me.” The album is ten tracks; genres listed on Discogs as Lo-Fi and Post Rock.

That same year Appendix Out contributed a track (“Well Lit Tonight”) to a split 7” with The Leopards on Creeping Bent (bent 027). The track was written by Roberts.

Split 7”s and UK Scene Activity (1998)

1998 saw Appendix Out appear on two further split releases. A split 7” with The Mongers appeared on Galvani Records (milk 001), with Appendix Out contributing two tracks — “Wild Living” and “Boyhood” — featuring Roberts on guitar, voice, and violin; Dave Elcock on bass; and Louise Dowding on percussion and mouth organ. Written by Roberts; produced by The Mongers for their side.

The more prominent split of that year was the Appendix Out / Songs: Ohia 7” (1998), co-released on Bad Jazz (bebop3) and Liquefaction Empire (duske3). Appendix Out contributed four tracks (Side A): “Second Perthshire House Song,” “Round Reel Of Eight,” “Twelve Of Them,” and “Hay Bale Blues.” Songs Ohia contributed one track (Side B): “Nay Tis Not Death.” The split connected two geographically distant but aesthetically adjacent acts — Roberts’s Scottish folk/lo-fi and Jason Molina’s Ohio-based americana. Cover design and illustration were by L. Gladwell and P. Kuzemczak.

The Creeping Bent Singles Club also released Appendix Out’s “Lassie, Lie Near Me” backed with Gordon Kilgour’s project Policecat on “Dark Holiday” (bent 034, Singles Club #8, 1998).

Daylight Saving and the Expanding Lineup (1999)

The second Drag City album, Daylight Saving (1999, dc152cd), marked a shift in the band’s texture. The lineup expanded to a quintet — Roberts, Dave Elcock, Gareth Eggie, Kate Wright, and Tom Crossley — and the sound moved toward folk-rock with more layered arrangements. Recorded by Scott Walker (not the singer — the engineer credited as “Scott W.” on the sleeve). Tracklist: “Foundling,” “The Grey Havens,” “Tangled Hair,” “The Scything,” “Little Owl,” “Row Upstream,” “Merchant City,” “Exile,” “Arcane Lore.” Genres: Folk Rock.

The same year produced a solo 7” credited to Appendix Out, Lieder Fur Kaspar Hauser (Western Vinyl, West 007, 1999), which features only Alasdair Roberts performing a wide range of instruments: acoustic guitar, organ (Emenee Audion Chord Organ), voice, tin flute, synthesizer, electric piano (Wurlitzer), semi-acoustic guitar, piano, sampler (Yamaha Portasound VSS-30). The two tracks are titled in German: “Ein Grauerstar In Der Kavallerie” / “An Den Nachthimmel Gewöhnt.”

Travels In Constants Vol. 13 (2001)

In 2001, Appendix Out contributed Volume 13 to Temporary Residence Limited’s Travels In Constants series (TRR 13). The five-track limited-edition CD EP is the most fully documented single Appendix Out recording. Lineup: Alasdair Roberts (voice, guitars), Gareth Eggie (extra singing), Tom Crossley (sleeve design), Donald WG Lindsay (bagpipes — described as “Hungarian bagpipes”), and Mark Harvey reading from The Golden Bough by Sir James Frazer (track 2, “Speech”). Roland Sabatier (2) contributed drawings for the sleeve; sleeve design was by Roberts and Tom Crossley.

Tracks: “Daylight Saving, Gibson Street (Part One)” (6:59), “Speech” (2:27), “Ritual Ingestion Of A Yellow Rhizome” (7:08), “Invocation Of The Corn Mother” (10:35), “Daylight Saving, Gibson Street (Part Two)” (8:26). The conceptual framing — spoken anthropology text, agrarian ritual titles, extended instrumental passages — marks a departure from the earlier folk-rock sound toward something more ceremonial and drone-adjacent.

The Night Is Advancing and Dissolution (2001)

Appendix Out’s final full-length, The Night Is Advancing (Drag City, DC189), was also released in 2001, in a vinyl LP pressing. It is the most fully credited recording in the discography. The expanded lineup included:

  • Alasdair Roberts — acoustic guitar, electric guitar, vocals, violin, bulbul tarang, piano, dulcimer
  • Gareth Eggie — acoustic guitar, electric guitar, percussion, keyboards, backing vocals
  • Tom Crossley — drums, percussion, electric guitar, backing vocals
  • Donald WG Lindsay — Hungarian bagpipes
  • Mark Harvey — bass guitar
  • Sean O’Hagan — bass guitar, piano, electronics
  • Sheryl Norquay — clarinet, recorder
  • Rian Murphy — percussion
  • Annabel Wright (also credited as Aggi (4)) — chorus
  • Dave Elcock — chorus
  • Rikke Iversholt — chorus
  • Konrad Strauss — mastered by

Produced by Rian Murphy and Sean O’Hagan. Recorded by Geoff Allan and Robin Rankin. Cover art: Eugène Gabritschevsky’s painting “Coral Formations Rising From The Sea” (1939). All songs written by Alasdair Roberts.

Twelve tracks across two sides: “A Path To Our Beds,” “The Seven Windows (The Sprigs Of Night),” “The Groves Of Lebanon,” “Golden Tablets Of The Sun,” “Year Waxing, Year Waning,” “Fortified Jackdaw Grove” (Side A); “The Night Is Advancing,” “Cyclone’s Vernal Retreat,” “(Bringing The Yearlings) Home,” “Hexen In The Anticyclone,” “Compfire’s Burning (Round),” “Organise A March” (Side B).

Also in 2001: the A Warm And Yeasty Corner CD EP (Shingle Street, Shing001) is credited to Appendix Out but represents Roberts performing with a different circle — Bill Lowman (double bass), Brad Gallagher (guitar/charango), and Lindsay Anderson (piano). Recorded by Jeremy Lemos. The EP covers Vashti Bunyan / Robert Lewis, Cyril Tawney, Ewan MacColl, and a Stephin Merritt song (“Josephine”), plus “A Very Cellular Song (Coda)” written by Incredible String Band’s Mike Heron. This EP sits at an interesting intersection of Scottish folk revivalism and American singer-songwriter traditions.

Following The Night Is Advancing, Appendix Out ceased activity as a going concern. Roberts moved into a substantial solo career under his own name, beginning with The Crook Of My Arm (Secretly Canadian, 2001).

Connections

  • Alasdair Roberts — founding member and sole consistent presence; all Appendix Out releases are effectively his compositions. After dissolution, continued as a solo artist through Drag City and Stone Tape Recordings into the 2020s.
  • Songs Ohia / Jason Molina — split 7” partner 1998; sonic contemporaries in the US/UK lo-fi folk intersection.
  • Drag City — primary label for all three studio albums (The Rye Bears A Poison, Daylight Saving, The Night Is Advancing).
  • Temporary Residence Limited — released Travels In Constants Vol. 13 (2001); part of the broader TRL post-rock/experimental folk ecosystem.
  • Travels In Constants — Vol. 13 in the series; neighbors include Mogwai (Vol. 12) and Songs Ohia (Vol. 14).
  • Creeping Bent — Glasgow independent; two releases (bent 027, bent 034); Appendix Out were part of the Glasgow/Scottish indie underground around this label.
  • Sean O’Hagan — High Llamas leader; co-produced and performed on The Night Is Advancing; his involvement marks that album’s most orchestrated production.
  • International Airport — Roberts was also a member; Discogs artist ID 105123.
  • Amalgamated Sons Of Rest — Roberts was a member; Discogs artist ID 528984.
  • Gareth Eggie and Tom Crossley — core 1999–2001 lineup; no other major Discogs presence beyond Appendix Out.

Open Questions

  • Exact dissolution date/circumstances — Discogs gives no profile text; the group appears to have wound down naturally after The Night Is Advancing (2001) with Roberts moving to solo releases. No formal announcement documented.
  • The name variation “Appendix Caledonia” listed in Discogs — it is unclear whether this was used for any specific release or is a variant spelling.
  • Personnel on Ice Age 7” (1996) — only Roberts is credited (as songwriter); other performers not documented.
  • Lieder Fur Kaspar Hauser (Western Vinyl, 1999) — credited to Appendix Out but only Roberts is listed as performing; may function as a solo single released under the band name.
  • A Warm And Yeasty Corner (2001) — also credited to Appendix Out despite featuring a distinct set of collaborators (Lowman, Gallagher, Lindsay Anderson) rather than the established lineup.
  • Sean O’Hagan’s connection to Roberts — whether it was a one-off collaboration or part of a longer relationship is not documented in Discogs.
