Blue Humans

Discogs ID: 119581
Also known as: The Blue Humans
Led by: Rudolph Grey
Active: c. 1980–1994 (documented); intermittent across subsequent decades
Genres: Free improvisation, noise rock, no wave, free jazz

Biography

Blue Humans is the name Rudolph Grey uses for the improvised performances he leads with a rotating cast of musicians. Grey, a noise/free jazz guitarist who first surfaced in the late-1970s NYC post-punk and art scene — playing in no wave band Mars (4) and co-founding Red Transistor — assembled the Blue Humans as a vehicle for long-form, aggressively atonal improvisation drawing on both free jazz and noise-rock traditions.

Lineups are typically trios: two guitars and drums, or guitar, saxophone, and drums. The music is fast-moving, noisy, and atonal, though on extended pieces like the 20-minute title track of Clear To Higher Time it builds controlled tension across the duration. The project has no fixed membership; Grey is the sole constant.

The earliest documented performance is a 1980 New York session (captured on Live - N.Y. 1980, released 1995 on Audible Hiss), which features Arthur Doyle on tenor saxophone and flute alongside Beaver Harris on percussion — players from the free jazz continuum rather than the punk/noise world. By the early 1990s the lineup shifted toward the downtown rock underground, bringing in Alan Licht (guitar) and Tom Surgal (drums) on the New Alliance releases, with Thurston Moore producing Clear To Higher Time (1992). The 1994 London performance captured on Live In London 1994 (Blast First) documented a lineup with Charles Gayle and Tom Surgal both credited on drums.

Jim Sauter (of Borbetomagus) is listed in Discogs’ Blue Humans member roster but does not appear in the release credits covered by available records — his participation was likely in live or unreleased contexts.

Open Questions

  • Jim Sauter’s specific Blue Humans appearances: Discogs lists him as a member but he has no release credits in the available catalog. Live or unreleased sessions presumably account for this.
  • Charles Gayle is credited on Live In London 1994 as “Drums” — unusual for a saxophonist. Whether he also played saxophone on that date is not documented in Discogs credits.
  • The 1980 recording predates the New Alliance-era lineups by over a decade — whether there were intermediate lineups and recordings between 1980 and 1992 is not documented in Discogs.
  • Grey’s membership in Mars (4) is flagged as brief; the temporal relationship to Red Transistor (concurrent or adjacent) is unclear from Discogs alone.
