Overview

Original Rockers is the founding name of the duo formed by Glyn “Bigga” Bush (Discogs ID: 485336) and Richard “DJ Dick” Whittingham (Discogs ID: 619433) in 1991 in Birmingham, UK. The pair built their practice at the intersection of dub, hip-hop, breakbeat, and acid house — a synthesis that would become the hallmark of the mid-1990s Bristol bass-music scene. In 1992 they co-founded Different Drummer, their own imprint (co-run with parent company Different Drummer Ltd., Birmingham), through which they released the majority of their early material.

The name Original Rockers was in use from the collective’s formation through 1993–94. In 1994 they formally adopted the name Rockers Hi-Fi, under which they signed to 4th & Broadway (and later Downbeat (3)) and built their most commercially visible run of releases. As Rockers Hi-Fi they added rapper/vocalist Farda P. (Patrick Plummer, Discogs ID: 8622) to the lineup. The DJ-Kicks mix-CD for -K7 Records (1997) stands as their most-documented document of that era.

Bush also operates as a solo/project entity under the names Bigga Bush, Lightning Head, Moody White, and Wavelad. Both Bush and Whittingham are members of Airgoose (ID: 17981) and Switchblade Sisters (ID: 1387), further project-names used on Different Drummer. Bush additionally co-formed Magic Drum Orchestra (ID: 3858509) with percussionist Ralph Cree in 2005 — a 20-piece live percussion ensemble.

The Discogs artist page (ID: 933) and profile note that the group is Birmingham-formed; the Bristol association is strong from the Heavenly/Saint Etienne connection and the broader UK bass scene they operated in, but their label’s address was Birmingham B30.

Key Release Detail

Tiger Bay — Saint Etienne (1994)

Discogs release ID: 85566 | Label: Heavenly | Cat#: HVNLP8

Original Rockers are credited as “Featuring [Rhythms, Bass And Mixes By]” on tracks A5, B4–B6:

  • A5 “On The Shore” (also features Kate St. John on oboe, Shara Nelson on vocals)
  • B4 “Western Wind”
  • B5 “Tankerville”
  • B6 “Western Wind” (reprise, featuring Stephen Duffy on vocals)

Other credits: Ian Catt (engineer, bass, guitar, keyboards), Bob Stanley & Pete Wiggs (written-by, arranged trad.), Sarah Cracknell (vocals), Rick Smith (mixed, programmed). This collaboration placed Original Rockers at the centre of the Heavenly/Britpop-meets-dub crossover moment.

Rockers To Rockers — Original Rockers (1993)

Discogs release ID: 43968 | Label: Different Drummer | Cat#: CD G001

Debut album. 10 tracks. Styles: Ambient, Dub, Techno. Guest Robert McKenzie (aka “The Whispering Rapper”) on “What A Life.” Written by Bush/Whittingham throughout. “Stoned (Manali Cream Mix)” included. Mastered by Miles Showell.

DJ-Kicks: The Black Album — Rockers Hi-Fi (1997)

Discogs release ID: 73988 | Label: !K7 Records | Cat#: !K7056cd

Rockers Hi-Fi’s contribution to the DJ-Kicks series. Mixed and compiled by the group. Farda P. provides intro/outro vocals and contributes “The Black Single (DJ-Kicks Rockers Hi-Fi Rmx).” Tracklist draws from Rhythm - Sound, DJ Cam, More Rockers, Deep Space Network, Snooze, and others in the international dub/downtempo network.

Connections

  • Saint Etienne — featured on Tiger Bay; connected via Heavenly and the early-90s Heavenly/indie-dance overlap
  • Groove Corporation — fellow Different Drummer artist; co-produced “Stoned”; Groove Corp later produced their own “Remixes From The Elephant House” series on Different Drummer
  • Peter Kruder / Kruder - Dorfmeister — remixed “Going Under” in 1996, one of K&D’s formative sessions
  • Neotropic (Riz Maslen) — Different Drummer labelmate; credited on Bastard Tracks compilation alongside Airgoose
  • Ewan Pearson — early credit as producer/writer on Airgoose material via Bastard Tracks
  • More Rockers (Rob Smith, Smith - Mighty associate) — included on DJ-Kicks; part of the same Bristol/UK dub web
  • Farda P. — Patrick Plummer; vocalist member of Rockers Hi-Fi; realname also listed as Patrick Plummer (same as the Rockers Hi-Fi member entry)

Open Questions

  • Birmingham vs. Bristol: Discogs (and the Different Drummer label address) place the collective in Birmingham; the Bristol association comes from the broader UK bass/dub scene they’re grouped with. Needs clarification — possibly they relocated or simply operated in both cities.
  • Rockers Hi-Fi full album details: The 1995 Rockers To Rockers (4th & Broadway) tracklist is confirmed; a separate full studio album beyond that is not clearly documented in DB — the Downbeat-era Overproof (1998) and Times Up (1999) are the later LPs.
  • Switchblade Sisters name conflict: The name “Switchblade Sisters” is also the title of a 1975 film; unclear if intentionally referential.
  • Lightning Head solo releases: Not found under that name in the DB as a standalone artist page with its own releases — may be a production alias only.
  • Moody White / Wavelad: No separate Discogs artist pages found; likely production/remix aliases rather than release-bearing names.
  • Magic Drum Orchestra live documentation: Only one Discogs release found (In The Studio, 2009); the group is described as a 20-piece live ensemble — likely has more activity outside Discogs.
