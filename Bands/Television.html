Television

Origin: New York City, USA
Genres: Rock — New Wave, Post-Punk
Active: 1973–1978, 1991–1993, 2001–2023
Labels: Ork Records, Elektra, Capitol Records

American rock band, founded in New York City. Central figures of the mid-1970s CBGB scene alongside Patti Smith, Blondie, and the Ramones. Known above all for Marquee Moon (1977), one of the defining guitar albums of the era. Tom Verlaine’s death on January 28, 2023 effectively ended the band.

History

The Neon Boys / Formation (1972–1973)

Tom Verlaine (born Thomas Joseph Miller, December 13, 1949, Morristown, New Jersey) and Richard Hell (born Richard Lester Meyers, October 2, 1949, Lexington, Kentucky) had been friends since their teens in Delaware. Moving to New York, they formed The Neon Boys around 1972 with drummer Billy Ficca. The Neon Boys never officially released music during their brief existence. When guitarist Richard Lloyd joined in 1973, the group renamed themselves Television.

CBGB Era and the Classic Lineup (1973–1975)

Television became resident figures at CBGB almost from its opening. Richard Hell’s torn-clothing, safety-pin look and confrontational energy influenced British punk — Malcolm McLaren encountered him in New York before managing the Sex Pistols.

Richard Hell’s departure (spring 1975). Hell left Television in spring 1975 — most sources point to March or April 1975, with The Heartbreakers (2) forming in May 1975. The circumstances are contested: the Perfect Sound Forever account of the early Television years describes the split as ambiguous — Hell “split the band or was thrown out by Verlaine or both” — and Wikipedia’s Television article characterises it as Hell having “parted ways” after a dispute over creative control. Wikipedia — Television (band), as of 2025-06-29; Perfect Sound Forever — Television’s early years

Several interlocking frictions drove the break:

  • Songwriting credit. Hell later stated (including in his 2013 memoir I Dreamed I Was a Very Clean Tramp) that he and Verlaine had initially agreed to divide songwriting equally, but that Verlaine increasingly refused to perform Hell’s compositions in concert — notably “Blank Generation.” Wikipedia — Richard Hell; I Dreamed I Was a Very Clean Tramp Wikipedia entry
  • Musical direction. Verlaine’s vision shifted toward highly arranged, long-form guitar suites — the direction that would produce Marquee Moon — while Hell remained deliberately untrained and raw in approach. Hell described this divergence in the memoir: “That’s not what Tom was interested in anymore. He heard these crystal-clear crisp sweet-guitar suites of highly arranged series of time and dynamics in his head, and they were about specific parts constructed for effect where everything was subordinate to what his guitar would be doing.” The Rumpus review of I Dreamed I Was a Very Clean Tramp, 2013
  • Stage presence and image. Verlaine reportedly told Hell to stop moving so energetically onstage, feeling Hell’s presence overshadowed the songs. Verlaine also resented Hell’s torn-clothes, safety-pin aesthetic as incompatible with the more sophisticated image he wanted for the band. Wikipedia — Television (band), as of 2025-06-29
  • Technical gap. As Verlaine, Lloyd, and Ficca grew more technically proficient, Hell’s bass playing remained basic; the Perfect Sound Forever account suggests Brian Eno’s demo sessions with the band (around this period) may have exposed this gap. Fred Smith — described as “a real bassist and non-showman” — replaced Hell, representing the band trading a “show” for pure music. Perfect Sound Forever — Television’s early years
  • Island Records interest. At least one account notes that Island Records showing interest in one of their compositions around this time accelerated Hell’s decision to leave and take his songs with him. Wikipedia — Television (band), as of 2025-06-29

No single precipitating incident is documented in available sources. Hell left the same week that Jerry Nolan and Johnny Thunders quit the New York Dolls; the three formed The Heartbreakers (2) in May 1975. Hell subsequently formed Richard Hell & The Voidoids. Fred Smith, who had been playing with early Blondie, joined Television as bassist. The classic four-piece was now set: Verlaine, Lloyd, Fred Smith, Ficca.

Marquee Moon and Adventure (1975–1978)

Television’s debut single, “Little Johnny Jewel” Parts 1 & 2 (1975), came out on Ork Records — Terry Ork’s small label, an early hub of the CBGB scene. Elektra signed the band; Marquee Moon appeared in February 1977. Co-produced by Verlaine and Andy Johns, with cover photography by Robert Mapplethorpe, it was neither punk nor conventional rock — twin-guitar interplay between Verlaine and Lloyd, long instrumental passages, and Verlaine’s elliptical lyrics. The title track runs nearly ten minutes. Second album Adventure followed in 1978, co-produced with engineer John Jansen. Television broke up later that year.

Hiatus, Live Document, and Reunion (1978–1993)

The Blow Up (ROIR, 1982) compiled live performances on cassette. The band reunited in 1991, recording the self-titled Television (Capitol, 1992) — the original four-piece, self-produced, with engineer Mario Salvati. They toured briefly before disbanding again in 1993.

Second Reunion and Final Years (2001–2023)

Television reformed again around 2001 and played sporadically for the next two decades. In 2007, Richard Lloyd amicably left; Jimmy Rip — a guitarist who had been working with Verlaine since the early 1980s — took his place. Tom Verlaine died on January 28, 2023, in Manhattan. Fred Smith died February 5, 2026, at age 77. The band is no longer active.
