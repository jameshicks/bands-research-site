T.V. Eye

Birmingham post-punk group, active c.1976–1980. Named after the Iggy & the Stooges song. Discogs artist ID: 1693428. Name variation: TV Eye.

Overview

T.V. Eye were a post-punk band from Birmingham, formed around 1976 from musicians connected to the Moseley area of the city. Their lineup brought together Andy Wickett (vocals), Dave Kusworth (guitar), Paul Adams (13) (guitar), Eamonn Duffy (bass), and drums duties shared between Goff (3) and David Twist. The band took their name from the Iggy & the Stooges song and performed regularly at Birmingham’s Barbarella’s club.

Their principal significance lies in the web of connections they generated. Nick Rhodes (later of Duran Duran) was a regular at the band’s rehearsals and gigs — often recording them on a tape recorder — and this is the direct link through which Andy Wickett came to be recruited as Duran Duran’s first lead vocalist in 1979. Wickett went on to co-write early demos of “Girls on Film” and “Rio” before being replaced by Simon Le Bon in 1980. Guitarist Dave Kusworth went on to form Jacobites with Nikki Sudden, one of the defining acts of the UK indie/romantique underground. Drummer David Twist and guitarist Paul Adams (13) continued into The Hawks (4) (also known as The Subterranean Hawks), alongside Stephen Duffy — himself another early Duran Duran member. Eamonn Duffy later played bass for The Nightingales and The Prefects.

The band recorded a set of demos in 1977–1978, eventually issued posthumously on the 1977–1978 LP (Seventeen Records, 2017). One track — “Stevie’s Radio Station” — was selected for the archival compilation Un-Scene! Post Punk Birmingham 1978-1982 (Easy Action, 2022), compiled and annotated by David Twist himself.

Formation and Context

Dave Kusworth’s Discogs profile states that he formed his first punk rock band in 1976 at age 16, calling it “TV Eye,” with Prefects drummer Dave Twist — placing the founding at 1976 even though the recorded material dates from 1977–1978. The name “c.1976/1978–1980” reflects the uncertainty between founding and active gigging/recording periods. Their stomping ground was the Birmingham punk/post-punk circuit centred on Barbarella’s club, the same venue that nurtured the early Duran Duran. The sound drew on the New York Dolls and The Stooges rather than the British punk mainstream.

David Twist’s biography on Discogs places his time in T.V. Eye as “1977–1978,” after which he moved briefly to The Prefects (late 1978–January 1979) before moving into the Hawks/Subterranean Hawks configuration from 1979–1981.

The overlap of two drummers (Goff and David Twist) suggests lineup changes across the band’s lifespan. The 1977–1978 LP credits Eamonn Duffy, David Twist, and Dave Kusworth by name (as well as Andy Wickett on vocals). The Un-Scene! compilation tracklist credits Andy Wickett (vocals), Dave Kusworth (guitar), Paul Adams (guitar), Eamonn Duffy (bass), and Goff (drums) for “Stevie’s Radio Station” — suggesting Goff preceded or ran parallel to Twist on drums.

Andy Wickett and Duran Duran

Nick Rhodes’s attendance at T.V. Eye rehearsals and gigs at Barbarella’s is the documented link between the band and Duran Duran. Rhodes, who was forming Duran Duran with John Taylor and Stephen Duffy in 1979, recruited Wickett as lead vocalist after seeing him front T.V. Eye.

Wickett recorded the band’s first professional demos at Bob Lamb’s studio in Birmingham. He co-wrote the early versions of “Girls on Film” — reportedly writing the melody on a night shift at Cadbury’s — and “Rio.” When he departed (reportedly preferring to pursue dub reggae), he received £600 in exchange for signing away his songwriting royalty claims. Simon Le Bon replaced him in 1980.

Wickett’s co-writing contribution to “Girls on Film” and “Rio” is formally documented in his Discogs credits: he is listed as “Vocals, Piano; Written-By” on the 2023 LP Girls On Film (Complete 1979 Demos) (Duran Duran featuring Andy Wickett, Cleopatra Records, CLO2707), which collects eight tracks from those 1979 sessions. Personnel on those sessions: Andy Wickett (vocals, piano, written-by), John Taylor (guitar), Nick Rhodes (keyboards), Roger Taylor (drums), produced by Bob Lamb.

A second archival release, Dreaming Of Your Cars (1979 Demos Part 2) (Cleopatra, 2020), also credits Wickett as “Vocals, Piano, Written-By.” Wickett later wrote liner notes for the 2004 compilation Duran:Duran (released on his own Andywickett Production imprint).

Andy Wickett also appeared as keyboard and harmonica player on the Jacobites’ Robespierre’s Velvet Basement (Glass Records, 1985) — a reunion of sorts with Dave Kusworth after their T.V. Eye days.

Scene Context

T.V. Eye sit at the dense centre of the Birmingham post-punk web documented by Un-Scene! Post Punk Birmingham 1978-1982. Their track appears alongside The Prefects, The Nightingales, The Hawks (4), and The Bible Belt — all bands that shared personnel with T.V. Eye. The same venue circuit (Barbarella’s), the same studio (Bob Lamb’s), and the same handful of musicians recur across all these projects.

The band’s indirect contribution to Duran Duran’s early catalogue gives them an outsized cultural footnote relative to their tiny documented output: one posthumous LP and one compilation track are all that survives on record.
