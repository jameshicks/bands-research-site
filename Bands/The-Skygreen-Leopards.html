The Skygreen Leopards

US psych-folk duo/band, formed San Francisco Bay Area, 2001–2014. Discogs artist ID: 473739.

Also known as Sky Green Leopards and Skygreen Leopards (name variations appear across releases).

  • Bandcamp: https://skygreenleopards.bandcamp.com/
  • MySpace archive: http://www.myspace.com/skygreenleopards
  • Genres: Folk Rock, Psychedelic Folk, Lo-Fi
  • Location: San Francisco, California, US
  • Years active: 2001–2014 (releases span); 2018 retrospective

History

Formation and the Jewelled Antler Era (2001–2003)

The Skygreen Leopards were founded by Donovan Quinn (Discogs ID: 473740) and Glenn Donaldson (Discogs ID: 450383), two San Francisco-area musicians deeply embedded in the Bay Area freak-folk and DIY cassette/CDR underground. The band’s debut, I Dreamt She Rode On A Pink Gazelle & Other Dreams (2001), was released on Jewelled Antler as a limited CDr. It was a lo-fi home recording featuring Quinn and Donaldson credited simply as “Performer.” The album ran 11 short tracks including “The Jingling World (The Skygreen Leopards Love You),” which later lent its title to the 2018 retrospective.

Jewelled Antler was a loose collective/label based around Glenn Donaldson and others; the band’s early releases fit squarely within its CDR-era aesthetic of homemade folk recordings.

Soft Abuse / Jagjaguwar Period (2004–2009)

One Thousand Bird Ceremony (2004, Soft Abuse) expanded the duo’s approach and was notable for both Quinn and Donaldson being credited with an identical 17-instrument list: 6-string guitar, 12-string guitar, 5-string banjo, bouzouki, Jew’s harp, accordion, drums, Hammond organ, chord organ, tambourine, maracas, mandolin, penny whistle/tin whistle, harmonica, recorder, dulcimer, and Echoplex effects — plus turntables (portable) and field recordings. This signalled a true co-production model with no functional division of roles. Donaldson also contributed the collage artwork. Layout credited to both “dq” (Quinn) and “gd” (Donaldson).

In 2005 the band moved to Jagjaguwar for Life & Love In Sparrow’s Meadow. Credits on that album are minimal on Discogs — Quinn and Donaldson credited as “Written-By [Songs], Performer [Sounds],” with Quinn also credited for a liner notes poem and Donaldson for collage artwork.

Disciples Of California (2006, Jagjaguwar) saw the band expand to a proper lineup: Shayde Sartin (Discogs ID: 599604) on bass, Jasmyn Wong (Discogs ID: 708403) on drums. Quinn played guitar, vocals, piano, and electric piano; Donaldson played guitar, vocals, organ, and bowed banjo. Jason Quever (Discogs ID: 486189) contributed organ on track B4 and piano on track A2. Wymond Miles (Discogs ID: 708402) played pedal steel on tracks A4, B1, and B3.

Also in 2006, a separate release The Skygreen Leopards Sing The Songs Of The Lindner Brothers appeared on Not On Label, with songs credited to fictional or pseudonymous writers “Golden Hotel, The” and “Hotel Alexis” (tracks split between the two).

Gorgeous Johnny (2009, Jagjaguwar, cat. Jag147) was a CD-only album. Discogs carries no additional credits beyond the band as primary artist, suggesting Quinn and Donaldson again handled production and performance internally.

Woodsist Period and Final Album (2014)

Family Crimes (2014, Woodsist, cat. 073) was released as a vinyl LP. It marked the band’s move to Woodsist (Jeremy Earl’s label, also home to Woods (2)). The album’s tracklist revisits the theme of family and contains a re-recording of “Mascara Priscilla” (a title that appeared on the 2001 debut). Again no additional credits beyond the band on Discogs.

Retrospective (2018)

The Jingling World Of The Skygreen Leopards (2018, Soft Abuse) is a career retrospective/compilation LP released on the same label as the 2004 album. Credits confirm Quinn (Performer) and Donaldson (Performer, Artwork By); the compilation was remastered by Sean McCann (4) (Discogs ID: 1083646).

Formation and Approach (Web Sources)

How They Met

Donaldson and Quinn connected ca. 2000–2001 through a Craigslist want-ad. Donaldson has described his motivation: “I wanted to find a musician that was outside of my circle and was interested in making songs” — he posted an ad citing Television Personalities, the Byrds, and the Monkees as touchstones. Quinn, then living in Walnut Creek, responded. Donaldson recalled his first impression: “He gave me maybe a CD-R of songs and it just struck me that they were different and interesting.” Quinn has acknowledged his own starting point with characteristic self-deprecation: “My songs at the time were mostly terrible. Glenn did a good job turning me onto new music and ripening up my aesthetic.” (BOMB Magazine, 2014; Bandcamp Lifetime Achievement, date unknown)

The Tiny Mix Tapes feature dates their meeting to 2000 and describes their bond over shared admiration for “the Byrds, The Fall, and Television Personalities.” (Tiny Mix Tapes, date unknown)

The Band Name

The name derives from the poetry of Kenneth Patchen. Donaldson appreciated how Patchen combined words in ways that felt simultaneously familiar and unresolved: “sounds like something but it’s not — it sounds familiar but it never settles in as something familiar.” (BOMB Magazine, 2014)

Aesthetic Shift and Intent

For Donaldson, Skygreen Leopards represented a deliberate pivot away from the avant-garde work he was doing with Thuja and related projects. The Bandcamp piece quotes him: “Okay, I’m ready to put the performance art aside and just play songs, because I love melody.” He credited Quinn with deepening his knowledge of traditional song structure: “he knew a little more about traditional song structures than me, so I definitely learned from him the ways chords could go together.” (Bandcamp Lifetime Achievement)

Donaldson has also described the project’s scope in plainly poetic terms: “two people strumming 200 year-old guitar chords and trying to discover a new mythology of love, hope and dreams.” (Terrascope, date unknown)

Songwriting Method

The duo worked spontaneously and improvisationally from the start. Donaldson described a key technique: “We would lay down the chords and then say, ‘Okay, it’s your turn to sing, make something up.‘” Early songs were constrained to under two minutes. (Bandcamp Lifetime Achievement)

Donaldson has characterised their approach as “Pre-cognitive Songcraft,” also called “Backwards-shadowing”: “Each song is a remembrance of a song not yet written.” Both members acknowledged drawing on the unconscious — with Quinn working from a Freudian orientation and Donaldson from a Jungian one — which Donaldson says gives the songs “a mythological quality to them, but at the same time they are very personal stories about our lives and experiences.” (Terrascope)

Quinn described the appeal of their combined lyric approach: “We’re really into that ambiguity and mixed messages, mixed metaphors, and putting things together like, my lyrics and his lyrics and seeming to contradict each other.” (Tiny Mix Tapes)

Both members framed their ambition in pop terms, even as critics emphasised the folk/psych tag. Quinn explained: “I think because we started off, we were such shitty musicians, we couldn’t do anything approaching pop music” — but the stated goal was concise, hook-driven songs in the tradition of 1960s AM radio. Donaldson: “all the great ’60s records, all the classic songs are under three minutes.” Quinn: “All these fucking bridges, intros, outros and so on… It’s too much!” (BOMB Magazine, 2014; Tiny Mix Tapes)

The duo also wrote songs that deliberately included themselves and their social circle: “we put ourselves in the songs a lot, like little tales of the band and our friends.” (BOMB Magazine, 2014)

Touchstone Records

In interviews, the duo cited Village Green-era Kinks and the Incredible String Band’s Changing Horses as models for an artistic arc toward straightforward pop from experimental origins. Beat Happening and The Go-Betweens have also been mentioned alongside the Byrds and Monkees. (Tiny Mix Tapes)

The Collaboration Dynamic

The pair acknowledged a creative rhythm that cycled between intense productivity and necessary distance. Quinn put it directly: “We usually have about a year of harmonious beautiful music making togetherness before we start screaming at each other.” Donaldson characterised their long-term aim with dry humour: their objective is simply “to outlast every other band and trend.” (Aquarium Drunkard, 2014)

Recording Notes

Donaldson has mentioned that some material was recorded in a trailer on the back of a horse ranch, with ambient environmental sounds captured naturally during sessions. (Terrascope)

The Tiny Mix Tapes piece notes that for Disciples of California the aim was “a kind of live-sounding record, with as few overdubs as possible,” while Gorgeous Johnny involved working with Jason Quever to incorporate “a lot of piano, and organ, percussion parts” — a more layered approach. (Tiny Mix Tapes)

Conflict note: The Aquarium Drunkard interview (2014) mentions Jason Quever was involved in Family Crimes production, which the existing Discogs entry does not credit. Discogs carries no producer credits for Family Crimes; the web source attributes production collaboration with Quever to this album. (Aquarium Drunkard, 2014)

Producer Credits Per Album

Discogs carries no explicit “Produced By” credits on any Skygreen Leopards release. The band consistently self-produced (or co-produced) with no external producer credited. Jason Quever is a session contributor on Disciples Of California, not a producer. The duo’s identical instrument lists on One Thousand Bird Ceremony suggest truly collaborative home recording with no designated producer.

Quever’s main project is Papercuts (2) and he is credited as a member of Donovan Quinn & The 13th Month alongside Quinn.

Scene Connections

  • Glenn Donaldson is among the most prolific figures in Bay Area freak-folk/psych, maintaining ~30 active projects simultaneously. His other groups include Thuja (improvised drone-folk collective), The Blithe Sons (experimental folk), Badgerlore (psych-folk), Flying Canyon, Art Museums, The Reds, Pinks And Purples, and many others.
  • Donovan Quinn records solo as Verdure and co-founded New Bums with Ben Chasny (Six Organs of Admittance). He is also part of Donovan Quinn & The 13th Month.
  • Shayde Sartin and Wymond Miles are both members of The Fresh & Onlys (SF garage-psych band).
  • Jason Quever (Papercuts (2)) is a recurring SF scene figure; he also appears in Donovan Quinn’s 13th Month project.
  • Jewelled Antler connects the band to the broader Bay Area CDR underground of the early 2000s (also home to Ben Chasny and others in the Jewelled Antler circle).
  • Jagjaguwar (part of Secretly Group) placed the band alongside Bon Iver, Okkervil River, and other indie-folk acts of the mid-2000s.
  • Woodsist connects to Woods (2), Real Estate, and the Brooklyn/upstate NY indie scene.
  • The 2006 “Lindner Brothers” release gestures at the duo’s interest in fictional personae and collaborative song-writing games.
