Overview

A Popular History Of Signs was a British electro pop group founded in 1979 by bassist and vocalist Andrew Jarman, with all of their recorded output concentrated in the 1980s. Their sound moved across synth-pop, new wave, and post-punk with EBM inflections, and the band saw multiple lineup changes before disbanding in 1988 — Jarman the consistent core alongside rotating members including Christine Isherwood, John Luccibello, Lindsey Smith, and Paul Clarke. Their first three singles came out on Melodia Records before Jungle Records became their main home for two albums and the bulk of their catalogue. The breadth of their label affiliations — including Spain’s Producciones Twins and Chicago’s Wax Trax! Records — suggests a wider international reach than their modest profile might imply.

Notes (web)

Wax Trax! Records connection (1985)

The 1985 Wax Trax! release is a four-track US 12” (catalogue WAX 005) titled Ladder Jack / House, compiling tracks from the band’s pre-Comrades singles. Two of the four tracks — “Ladder Jack” and “House” — are US remixes credited to Al Jourgensen (Ministry) and Thom Moore; the other two (“If She Was A Car”, “Stigma”) are original mixes. Both remixes later appeared on the 1988 singles compilation Taste. Trouser Press review (snippet only; site blocked to fetch)

Multiple secondary sources describe the connection in identical terms — “the band caught the attention of Ministry’s Al Jourgensen, who remixed two of the band’s tracks for a fledgling Wax Trax label in 1985” — but none explains the mechanism: how Jourgensen heard the band, whether there was an intermediary, or whether this was initiated by the band, their UK label Jungle Records, or Wax Trax! directly. systemsofromance.com, Jan 2025

What is established: Jourgensen’s involvement as remixer is the direct link to Wax Trax! — at that point (1984-85) a nascent Chicago industrial label whose core roster included Ministry, Front 242, and Revolting Cocks. The release is catalogued as a US pressing of a UK act’s tracks, which is consistent with Wax Trax!‘s documented practice of licensing and distributing European acts for the US market (confirmed by their parallel licensing of Belgian acts such as Front 242). Wikipedia — Wax Trax! Records (as of 2026-10-14)

What remains unknown: No named interview with Andrew Jarman, Jim Nash, Dannie Flesher, or Al Jourgensen addresses who initiated the deal, whether money changed hands as a licensing fee, or whether any prior acquaintance existed between APHOS and the Chicago circuit. No primary source has surfaced in searches across Trouser Press, Systems of Romance, ministryband.com, and general news searches.
