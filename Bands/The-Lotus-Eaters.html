Overview

The Lotus Eaters were a New Wave band from Liverpool whose recorded output was concentrated in the 1980s — seven releases across Arista and their own Sylvan Records imprint — before a pair of later releases in the 2000s on Vinyl Japan and Cherry Red. The five-piece (Peter Coyle, Michael Dempsey, Jeremy Kelly, Gerrard Quinn, and Alan Wills) worked primarily in New Wave and Synth-pop with touches of Indie Rock and Pop Rock, and their productions leaned heavily on Nigel Gray (34 shared credits) and Bob Sargeant (23), with Alan Tarney and Dale Griffin also contributing. Sylvan Records remained active as a catalogue home well into the 2000s, suggesting the band retained control of its own masters.

Notes (web)

Michael Dempsey joining — what the web establishes

The Lotus Eaters went through a significant lineup change between their October 1982 John Peel session and their commercial debut in 1983. The original rhythm section — bassist Phil Lucking and drummer Alan Wills — were both replaced, along with keyboard player Gerrard Quinn. Into these slots came Michael Dempsey on bass, Steve Crease on drums, and Stephen Emmer on keyboards. Sources: Wikipedia — The Lotus Eaters (band) (as of 2026-10-06); electricityclub.co.uk Peter Coyle interview; c86show.org Peter Coyle special.

No interview or contemporary press source found explains the specific mechanism of Dempsey’s recruitment. Peter Coyle’s interview at electricityclub.co.uk states only that “the rhythm section eventually settled with drummer Steve Crease and bassist Michael Dempsey who had been in THE CURE and ASSOCIATES” — credentials are named but no account is given of how or why Dempsey was approached. The electricityclub.co.uk Michael Dempsey interview (focused on the Associates reissue campaign) does not mention the Lotus Eaters at all. Sources checked and found to contain no first-hand account of the recruitment: electricityclub.co.uk Dempsey interview; Pennyblack interviews (Parts 1 and 2) — returned 403; saltyka.blogspot.com Peter Coyle interview; essentiallypop.com Stephen Emmer interview.

The Associates-to-Lotus-Eaters thread is the strongest documented link. Both Dempsey and Stephen Emmer (Minny Pops, Associates) joined the Lotus Eaters at the same juncture. Emmer’s own biography at stephenemmer.com lists both the Associates and the Lotus Eaters among his 1980s bands without further elaboration. This parallel recruitment suggests a shared social or professional network rooted in their Associates-era overlap, but no source confirms this directly.

Stylistic influence from the Cure period — what the web establishes

No interview with Dempsey, Coyle, or Kelly addresses whether his Cure-era bass approach influenced the Lotus Eaters’ sound. The only bass-specific critical observation located is from an Italian-language review of No Sense of Sin at debaser.it, which notes that Dempsey’s bass on “Put Your Touch On Love” “imposes a fast pace” and “almost catches the listener off guard.” The review identifies him as “former bassist of the Cure” but draws no explicit line between his Cure style and his playing on the album. Source: debaser.it review of No Sense of Sin.

A Trouser Press/AllMusic characterisation (surfaced in search snippets) describes the album as “a gorgeously crafted collection of melancholic guitar pop” — notably different in texture from the post-punk austerity of Dempsey’s Cure period (1978–1979) — but no critic or band member draws this contrast explicitly. The question of stylistic inheritance from his Cure work remains unaddressed in any source found.
