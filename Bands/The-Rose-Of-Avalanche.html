The Rose Of Avalanche

Leeds goth/psychedelic rock band, active 1984–1993 (reformed 2019). Discogs artist ID: 279061. Associated with the Leeds goth scene alongside Sisters of Mercy and The March Violets, though their primary stylistic reference point — particularly on early material — was American psychedelic rock, especially The Doors. Signed initially to the local Leeds Independent Label, then to Fire Records, before establishing their own Avalantic Records imprint for later releases. The band self-produced throughout their career, but suffered from poor label management and contractual disputes that impacted their trajectory. They split in 1992–93 and reformed in 2019.

Wikipedia: The Rose of Avalanche

History

Formation and LiL Years (1984–1985)

The band formed in 1984 in Leeds, founded by vocalist Phillip Morris, guitarist Paul James Berry, and drummer Alan Davis. The name was constructed to convey beauty (the rose) and power (the avalanche). Before playing a live gig, they signed to the local Leeds Independent Label (LiL) and released two 12-inch singles: “L.A. Rain” (1985) and “Goddess” (1985). Both reached the UK Independent Chart Top 20 — “L.A. Rain” at No. 10, “Goddess” at No. 16 — and “L.A. Rain” appeared in John Peel’s Festive Fifty for 1985. The debut compilation LP First Avalanche (LIL/LP3, 1985) gathered early studio material; it later peaked at No. 3 on the UK Indie Chart. Engineers on these early sessions were Neil Ferguson and Stephen Allan (2).

An Italian Contempo Records release (The Rose Of Avalanche, 1985) appears to be a separate compilation issued for the European market.

Fire Records and the Classic Lineup (1986–1988)

After the LiL singles generated attention, Fire Records signed the band. The lineup stabilised around Phil Morris (vocals), Paul James Berry (guitar), Glenn Schultz (guitar), Nicol McKay (bass), and Mark Thomson (drums) — this five-piece recorded the Fire-period singles and the first proper studio albums.

Singles on Fire were all self-produced by the band:

  • “Too Many Castles In The Sky” (FIRE, 1986) — UK Indie No. 8
  • “Velveteen” (BLAZE 18T, 1986) — UK Indie No. 9
  • “Always There” (BLAZE 18T, 1986/87) — UK Indie No. 3
  • Too Many Castles In The Sky / Velveteen EP (double 7-inch reissue, 1987)

A key career moment came with a support slot on The Mission’s 1986–87 World Crusade Tour, documented on the bootleg World Crusade Tour 1987 (shared with The Mission). Mass exposure from this tour raised the band’s profile considerably.

The first official full studio album, Always There (Fire, 1986/87), peaked at No. 3 on the UK Indie Chart. It was released in the Netherlands via Emergo and in the US via Restless Records. Engineer credits across the Fire single sessions were split between Neil Ferguson (engineer on “Always There” A-side) and Colin Richardson (engineer on B-sides). Engineer Jih Seymour worked the “Too Many Castles In The Sky” and “Velveteen” sessions.

In Rock (Fire LP 12, 1988) was the second album on Fire, shifting further toward psychedelic rock influences — Discogs lists its styles as Alternative Rock and Indie Rock rather than Goth Rock. The album reached UK Indie No. 10. It was self-written and self-produced by the band.

Anthology (First Avalanche / Always There) (Fire, 1988) compiled LiL and Fire material.

Avalantic Period (1988–1991)

The band set up their own Avalantic Records imprint for subsequent releases, starting with the single “The World Is Ours” (AVE 1T, 1988) which reached UK Indie No. 8.

Schultz and McKay departed in 1989. Darren Horner joined as bass player, and Andy Porter replaced Mark Thomson on drums. This transitional lineup recorded the EP “A Peace Inside” (AVE CD4, 1989) and the album Never Another Sunset (AVE LP1, 1989), the latter peaking at UK Indie No. 10. Never Another Sunset was self-produced; illustration by Andrea White.

The final lineup — Morris, Berry, Horner, and Porter — delivered String ‘A’ Beads (AVE LP002, 1990). Personnel credits are fully documented on this release: Phillip Morris (vocals), Paul James Berry (guitar/synthesizer), Darren Horner (bass), Andy Porter (drums/synthesizer). Engineers were Dave Creffield and Steve Harris (Stephen Harris, Discogs ID 115914).

The final album, I.C.E. (AVE LP003, 1991), was engineered by Pat Grogansacant. The band officially split in 1992, though a brief reunion performance took place in 1993 — Morris, Horner, Schultz, and a drum machine.

Later Releases and Reformation

L.A. Rain - The Singles Album (Nectar Masters, 1997) collected the early singles. At The BBC (Chapter 22 Records, 2021; reissued on Dakota Records (7), 2024) presented BBC session recordings. The band announced a reformation in 2019.

The brief mentions Bedlington Records and Situation Two; however, Discogs has no releases by The Rose Of Avalanche on either label. Situation Two (Discogs ID: 5116, sub-label of Beggars Banquet) is not in the band’s catalog — this association appears to be an error in the research brief.

Connections

  • The Mission — shared the 1986–87 World Crusade Tour; documented on World Crusade Tour 1987 bootleg
  • Sisters of Mercy — contemporaries in the Leeds goth scene; both part of the F Club / early-80s Leeds post-punk milieu
  • The March Violets — contemporaries in the Leeds goth scene; formed 1981 at Leeds University, released via Merciful Release
  • Red Lorry Yellow Lorry — another Leeds goth/post-punk act on Situation Two; David Wolfenden (an early Rose Of Avalanche member) was also a member
  • Expelaires — early Leeds band; David Wolfenden member; cited in sources as the first band of Craig Adams and Dave Wolfenden
  • Fire Records — primary label 1986–88; roster also included Fields Of The Nephilim, Gene Loves Jezebel, Death Cult, The Bolshoi, Tones On Tail
  • Leeds Independent Label — founding label; also released Flowers For Agatha and Hang The Dance
  • Situation Two — not a label the band released on (contrary to the research brief); included here to flag the discrepancy

The Leeds goth scene was anchored in the F Club (1977–1982), a club night founded by John Keenan at Leeds Polytechnic and later venues, where Sisters of Mercy and March Violets first performed and Andrew Eldritch met Gary Marx. By the time The Rose Of Avalanche formed in 1984, the F Club had closed; they came up in the scene it had established. (Source: Wikipedia, F Club article)
