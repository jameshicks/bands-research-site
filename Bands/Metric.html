Metric

Canadian indie rock / synth-pop band. Discogs artist ID: 251798. Formed Toronto, Ontario; based between Toronto and Los Angeles.

Overview

Metric is a four-piece guitar-driven band with synth-pop and new wave textures, built around the songwriting partnership of vocalist/keyboardist Emily Haines and guitarist/producer James Shaw. They self-released their debut LP in 2001, broke through in Canada on Last Gang Records through the mid-2000s, then became fully self-managed under their own labels (Metric Music International, Crystal Math Music) from 2009 onward. They have been one of the most consistently active Canadian indie rock acts, releasing nine studio albums through 2023.

Discogs also lists an alias: Mainstream (12) — appears to be an early name variant; no separate release catalog.

Notes on lineup:

  • The 2001 self-released Grow Up and Blow Away (recorded before the band became a proper four-piece) credits only Haines (vocals, keyboards) and Shaw (guitar).
  • Full four-piece lineup (Winstead + Scott Key added) is credited from Old World Underground, Where Are You Now? (2003) onward.
  • Winstead and Scott Key also perform together in Bang Lime (Discogs ID: 1208654), which released one album on Last Gang Records in 2007.

Member Solo & Side Projects

Emily Haines

  • Emily Haines & The Soft Skeleton (Discogs ID: 624547) — solo project; albums: Knives Don’t Have Your Back (2006, Last Gang Records) and Choir Of The Mind (2017, Last Gang Records). Shaw produced/engineered Choir Of The Mind.
  • Broken Social Scene — member of the Toronto collective; appears on You Forgot It In People (2002), Broken Social Scene (2004), Forgiveness Rock Record (2010), Hug Of Thunder (2017).
  • Earlier solo release: (Cut In Half And Also Double) (1996, self-released, as Emily Haines), which also featured Shaw on trumpet.
  • Guest/session: Feist Let’s Stay Friends (2007, piano), Charlotte Gainsbourg Stage Whisper (Knock You Out, 2009, featuring credit), Jarvis Cocker (2009).

James Shaw

  • Broken Social Scene — member; appears across multiple BSS records.
  • Outside production credits include: Stars Set Yourself On Fire (2004, additional guitar/production), Klaxons Golden Skans (2007, mix/production), Emily Haines Choir Of The Mind (2017, co-producer/mix/performer), Lisa LeBlanc Late Show (2017, producer).
  • Also credited as “Jimmy Shaw” throughout his production work.

Joshua Winstead & Joules Scott Key

  • Bang Lime (Discogs ID: 1208654) — duo formed by Winstead and Scott Key; released Best Friends In Love (2007, Last Gang Records). No other releases in Discogs.

Open Questions

  • The self-released 2001 Grow Up and Blow Away shows minimal credits (no producer listed) — exact recording circumstances are unclear from Discogs alone.
  • Restless Records appears to have issued a 2001 promo release in the US (“From the forthcoming Restless release ‘Grow Up and Blow Away’”) — unclear why the commercial release was ultimately self-released rather than on Restless. Would need web research to confirm.
  • Art of Doubt (2018) vinyl pressing (Canada) carries no additional credits in Discogs; the CD/digital pressing (USA, Canada & Europe) has the full production credits. Credit availability varies by format in this DB entry.
  • Liam O’Neil (Discogs ID: 451718), co-producer on Synthetica, Cosmopolis, Formentera, and Formentera II, has an unclear background outside Metric credits — Discogs does not clarify whether he is a band associate or independent producer.
  • The “Mainstream (12)” alias on Discogs has no associated releases — unclear if this reflects an early band name or a data error.
