Broken Social Scene

Canadian indie rock collective, Toronto. Discogs artist ID: 138844. Active 1999–present. Name variations: BSS, Boken Social Scene.

Biography

Origins: K.C. Accidental and Feel Good Lost (1999–2001)

Broken Social Scene grew out of an instrumental duo formed by Kevin Drew and Brendan Canning — both already embedded in the Toronto indie underground — who had previously recorded together as K.C. Accidental alongside Charles Spearin. The collective proper debuted with Feel Good Lost (2001, Noise Factory Records), a largely instrumental ambient record produced entirely by Drew and Canning: Drew on drums, guitar, keyboards, samplers; Canning on bass, guitar, keyboards, samplers. Mixing was handled by Charles Spearin and Ohad Benchetrit. Mastering by Noah Mintz.

Breakthrough: You Forgot It In People (2002)

You Forgot It In People (2002) is the record that made BSS’s reputation. Co-released by Paper Bag Records (Canada) and later licensed to Mercury/Vertigo (UK/Europe), it introduced the full rotating collective format — up to 14 musicians on a given track. Producer David Newfeld shaped the sound. The full ensemble for that record included Drew, Canning, Spearin, Amy Millan, Andrew Whiteman, Emily Haines, Evan Cranley, Feist (Leslie Feist), James Shaw, Jason Collett, Jo-Ann Goldsmith, John Crossingham, Justin Peroff, Marty Kinack, Ohad Benchetrit, Torquil Campbell, and David Newfeld himself. Guest performers included Julie Penner, Jason Tait, K-OS, and Murray Lightburn. The album has 32 known pressings across territories — one of the most widely distributed Canadian indie records of its era.

The release also marks the birth of Arts & Crafts Productions: it was the label’s first catalog item (PAPER001, co-badged with Paper Bag).

Self-Titled and Global Reach (2004–2006)

Broken Social Scene (2004) was self-produced and brought John McEntire of Tortoise into the fold as a notable addition to the collective’s orbit. The same year, the EP Bee Hives (Arts & Crafts) appeared as a companion. The self-titled LP was distributed widely, reaching Australia and New Zealand via Shiny. Singles Ibi Dreams Of Pavement (A Better Day) (2005) and 7/4 (Shoreline) (2006, City Slang) and Fire Eye’d Boy (2006) extended the band’s European profile. Guest performers on the self-titled included K-OS and Murray Lightburn.

BSS Presents and Hiatus (2007–2009)

Rather than releasing another collective BSS album immediately, the Arts & Crafts “Broken Social Scene Presents” series spotlighted individual members as solo artists:

  • Spirit If… (2007) — Kevin Drew
  • Something For All Of Us… (2008) — Brendan Canning

The band also released live recordings Backed Out On The… and Tbtf (both 2007, Arts & Crafts International) and the live Live At Radio Aligre FM In Paris, June 2004 (2005).

Forgiveness Rock Record (2010)

Forgiveness Rock Record (2010) marked a full-band return. Produced by the band itself (credited as “BSS”) and co-produced, recorded, and mixed by John McEntire, with additional recording by Sebastien Grainger and Ohad Benchetrit. Mastered by Roger Seibel. Released in Germany via City Slang and globally via Arts & Crafts. It is the band’s most-pressed record: 21 known pressings. A single, Forced To Love (2010, City Slang/Cooperative Music), preceded the album.

Second Hiatus and Hug Of Thunder (2011–2017)

The band went largely quiet for several years, with members pursuing solo projects and side bands. Lisa Lobsinger joined the collective during this era. The return came with Hug Of Thunder (2017), produced by Joe Chiccarelli and Nyles Spencer, mixed by Shawn Everett, mastered by Emily Lazar. Released on Arts & Crafts (Canada) and City Slang (Europe), with accompanying singles Halfway Home, Skyline, and Stay Happy (all City Slang). 14 pressings documented on Discogs for the Arts & Crafts edition.

Recent Activity (2018–2022)

Let’s Try The After Vol. 1 (2019) and the combined Let’s Try The After Vol 1&2 followed on Arts & Crafts. Singles Old Dead Young (2018) and Can’t Find My Heart (2019) preceded these releases. Live At Third Man Records (2020, Third Man Records) captured a live set in Nashville. Live At The Phoenix Concert Theatre, 2003 (2022, Arts & Crafts) and Old Dead Young (B-sides & Rarities) (2022) extended the archival catalog.
