Beatnik Beatch

Los Angeles pop-rock band, active c. 1987–1989. Discogs artist ID: 2134791.

A short-lived but historically significant group, remembered chiefly as the incubator for Andy Sturmer and Roger Joseph Manning Jr.’s songwriting partnership — a partnership that went on to define Jellyfish (2). According to Wikipedia, the band formed in San Francisco in the mid-1980s. The core lineup was Chris Witt Ketner (bass, vocals, viola), Andy Sturmer (drums, vocals), and George Cole (guitar), with keyboardist Se Padilla in an early configuration, later replaced by Manning after the Atlantic Records deal was struck.

Manning joined as a keyboardist after relocating to Los Angeles and enrolling at USC to study musical composition; according to Wikipedia, he and Sturmer — who had known each other from Amador Valley High School in Pleasanton, California — began writing together in a direction stylistically distinct from Beatnik Beatch’s existing catalog. In August 1989, approximately a year after the Atlantic album’s release, Sturmer and Manning departed to form Jellyfish. (Source: Wikipedia, “Roger Joseph Manning Jr.“)

Labels

Industrial Records (2) (Discogs ID: 249332) — A small San Francisco-based indie label with only one release in Discogs: At The Zula Pool. Not related to the UK Industrial Records (home to Throbbing Gristle et al.).

Atlantic Records (Discogs ID: 681) — Major label. Parent: Atlantic Recording Corporation. Signed Beatnik Beatch c. 1988 for the self-titled LP (catalog 81854-1) and promo single (PR 2340).
