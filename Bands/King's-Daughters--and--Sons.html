Bringing together alumni from bands including Shipping News, Rachel’s and The For Carnation, King’s Daughters & Sons make a music we have come to recognise as very Louisville: atmospheric, complex post-rock music, trudging as though weighed down by some heavy emotional burden. If Then Not When, though, crafts something of stark, sinister beauty. The intertwined vocals of Rachel Grimes, Joe Manning and Michael Heineman recall something of Low’s sombre chorus, but gothic folk tales like “Open Sky” walk a tangled path, starting hushed but building towards blazing, dramatic denouments

Overview

A five-piece post-rock group from Louisville, Kentucky, King’s Daughters & Sons brought together alumni of Shipping News, Rachel’s, and The For Carnation — a cluster of bands central to Louisville’s atmospheric rock scene. Their sound is slow, weighty, and emotionally dense, built around Art Rock and Post Rock structures with three-part intertwined vocals shared by Rachel Grimes, Joe Manning, and Michael Heineman. The catalogue is compact — one release in the late 2000s and one in the early 2010s — with their album landing on Glasgow’s Chemikal Underground in 2011, an unusual transatlantic label home for a Louisville act. Kevin Ratterman and Robert Weston both appear across their recordings as key collaborators.

Chemikal Underground signing — how it came about

Searched across: Chemikal Underground artist page, Rachel Grimes’ official site, The List (Edinburgh), The Skinny, Drowned in Sound review, a 2011 blog capture (captainoflies.blogspot.com), Popdose, and multiple targeted searches (2026-10-16). No source on the open web documents the mechanism of the signing — who initiated contact, whether a festival appearance, UK tour, mutual contact, or demo submission was involved.

What the sources do confirm:

  • The signing was announced via a Chemikal Underground newsletter, discovered by at least one reviewer that way. (captainoflies.blogspot.com snippet, 2011)
  • The band had a US show history concentrated in Louisville and Chicago through 2012, with a Forecastle Festival (Louisville) appearance in 2012. No UK festival appearances documented. (rachelgrimespiano.com)
  • Rachel Grimes’ prior band Rachel’s had some European profile; Kevin Ratterman mastered via Chicago Mastering Service and Robert Weston (Bob Weston, Chicago Mastering Service) is credited across both KD&S releases — but no source connects either to facilitating the Chemikal introduction. (chemikal.co.uk)
  • The band remains on Chemikal Underground as of 2023 (EP The Next First Thing, released May 12 2023). (chemikal.co.uk)
