The Cold (2)

New Orleans power-pop / new wave band. Discogs artist ID: 1786927. Also listed as “Cold.”

Overview

The Cold formed in New Orleans, Louisiana in the late 1970s (emerging from an earlier band called Totally Cold) and released a run of 7” singles on their own imprint Top Pop Records between 1980 and 1984, followed by two studio LPs and several CD compilations on the same label through the 1990s and 2000s. The band’s five-piece lineup — Kevin Radecker and Bert Smith on guitars, Vance DeGeneres on bass, Barbara Menendez on keyboards/organ, and Chris Luckette on drums — remained stable across virtually their entire active period. Songwriting was primarily shared among Radecker, DeGeneres, and Smith.

The band’s original run lasted roughly three years, from late 1979 to mid-1982, during which they became one of New Orleans’ most popular live acts before disbanding near the height of their local success. A 1987 Times-Picayune retrospective described their trajectory as “brief and frantic.” The proximate cause of the dissolution was Barbara Menendez’s departure: a May 28, 1982 Times-Picayune piece by Bunny Matthews (“Barbara’s Exit,” threechordcity.com/barbaras-exit.html) reported that Menendez announced her “permanent retirement” from the band, citing burnout — “I’ve just had enough” — along with the incompatibility of the rock lifestyle (“late hours, smoky bars, drinking all night and partying”) with her desire for a more stable domestic life. She planned a fall 1982 marriage to Ray Ganucheau (former RZA bassist) and intended to pursue visual art. At the time, manager Bruce Spizer stated the band would continue and audition replacements; the band apparently did not find a lasting substitute and dissolved. After the breakup, members scattered: Radecker and DeGeneres relocated to Los Angeles, Menendez also moved west (later returning to New Orleans), Smith left music entirely, and Luckette remained in New Orleans and continued playing with other acts.

The band reunited briefly in 1984 — approximately two years after the original split — to release an LP and a new single. The LP title 16 Songs…Off A Dead Band’s Chest is a self-conscious acknowledgement that the band had already broken up: it anthologised their 1980–1982 singles-era material rather than presenting new recordings. A press run of 3,000 copies was confirmed in contemporary press; the LP was out of print by the time of the 1987 reunion show. A second LP, Major Minor (1985), followed the 1984 reunion before the band again went dormant. Further reunion shows took place in 1987, 1999, and 2001. The band was inducted into the Louisiana Music Hall of Fame in 2018.

Their debut 7” “You” / “Three Chord City” (1980, Top Pop Records, cat. none / KRS-611) was produced by Traci Borges and engineered by Terry Bickle. The follow-up “Mesmerized” / “Wake Up” (1981, TP-002) was co-produced by DeGeneres and Cliff Derbins, with Camile Baudoin engineering. The 1982 single “Do The Dance” / “Missing Hit Man” (TP-007) brought in noted producer Craig Leon — best known for producing the Ramones and Blondie — as producer and engineer. A 1984 single “Take All The Time” / “I Go To Pieces” (TP-009, a cover of Del Shannon) was produced by David Farrell alongside DeGeneres.

The debut LP 16 Songs…Off A Dead Band’s Chest (1984, TP008) was a 16-track mono vinyl collection. The second LP Major Minor (1985, TP-010) was a 10-track stereo album. Beginning in 1997, Top Pop Records issued a series of archival CD compilations — Three Chord City (1997, TP-1011; 26 tracks), Cold Sweat (1999, TP-1012; 26 tracks, produced by Cliff Derbins and remastered by Guy Mouledoux), and Limbo’s Getting Crowded (2005, TP-1013; 22 tracks). A DVD release of a live performance — Live At Jimmy’s July 31, 1981 (2012, TP-1014) — documented the band at a New Orleans club near the height of their original run.

Drummer Chris Luckette was also a member of The Normals (2), the New Orleans punk band whose 1978 Lectric Eye single “Almost Ready” / “Hardcore” is a key document of Southern punk. Bassist Vance DeGeneres (brother of comedian Ellen DeGeneres) later played in House Of Schock (Capitol Records, 1988) alongside Go-Go’s drummer Gina Schock.
