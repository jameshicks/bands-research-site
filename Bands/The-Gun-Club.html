The Gun Club

The Gun Club

Oct 17, 20265 min read

The Gun Club

Discogs ID: 270779
Origin: Los Angeles, California
Active: 1979–1995 (Jeffrey Lee Pierce died March 31, 1996)
Labels: Ruby Records, Animal Records, Red Rhino Records, Fire Records, New Rose Records, Sympathy For The Record Industry

Cowpunk / punk-blues / psychobilly. Founded in Los Angeles in 1979 by vocalist-guitarist Jeffrey Lee Pierce (June 27, 1958 – March 31, 1996) and guitarist Kid Congo Powers (Brian Tristan, b. March 27, 1959, La Puente, CA), initially under the name The Creeping Ritual. Pierce merged Delta blues, Hank Williams country, and confrontational punk energy into something critics reached for “tribal psychobilly blues” and “cowpunk” to describe, neither quite fitting. The band was foundational for post-punk’s absorption of American roots music and had a profound influence on the Birthday Party / Bad Seeds milieu and the entire Americana underground.

History

Founding and Fire Of Love (1979–1982)

Pierce and Tristan (soon renamed Kid Congo Powers) formed The Creeping Ritual in LA in 1979, becoming The Gun Club before recording. The founding rhythm section came from LA punk band The Bags: Rob Ritter on bass and Terry Graham on drums. Ward Dotson filled the second guitar slot. Pierce had previously been briefly in The Red Lights (2) with Texacala Jones.

Fire Of Love (1981, Ruby Records JRR 102 — 53 pressings) was produced in two separate sessions: A1–A2 and A6–B2 by Chris D. (of Flesh Eaters), A3–A5 and B3–B5 by Tito Larriva (of The Plugz). Pat Burnette engineered throughout and cut the lacquer. The record leaned heavily on delta blues: “Preaching The Blues” arranged from Robert Johnson, “Cool Drink Of Water” from Tommy Johnson. “For The Love Of Ivy” was a Pierce / Kid Congo co-write. Tito Larriva played violin on “Promise Me”; Chris D. co-designed the cover and appeared on backing vocals on “Jack On Fire.”

Miami (1982, Animal Records 204 977 — 38 pressings) was recorded with the same core lineup and produced by Chris Stein of Blondie. Session engineer Joe Arlotta, mix engineer Butch Jones; mastered by José Rodriguez. Stein played bongos on “Watermelon Man”; Walter Steding added fiddle.

Death Party and The Las Vegas Story (1982–1984)

The Death Party EP (1983, Animal Records 600 804 — 18 pressings) introduced a reshuffled lineup: Dee Pop of Bush Tetras on drums, Jim Duckworth on lead guitar, Jimmy Joe Uliana on bass (Pierce played bass on “The Lie”). Texacala Jones added backing vocals and screams. Chris Stein co-produced with Pierce; Joe Arlotta engineered.

The Las Vegas Story (1984, Animal Records / Chrysalis 206 445 — 32 pressings) brought back Kid Congo Powers and introduced Patricia Morrison (Patricia Anne Rainone, b. January 14, 1962, LA) on bass — Morrison was also a Bags alumna (stage name “Pat Bag”) and later married Dave Vanian of The Damned. Terry Graham returned on drums. Produced by Jeff Eyrich; mastered by Bernie Grundman. Dave Alvin of The Blasters guested on lead guitar for “Eternally Is Here” and “The Stranger In Our Town.” Covers include Gershwin/Heyward’s “My Man’s Gone Now” and Leon Thomas / Pharoah Sanders’ “The Creator Was A Master Plan.”

European exile and Mother Juno (1985–1989)

Pierce relocated to Europe (London, later Geneva) from the mid-1980s. The late-period lineup consolidated around Pierce, Kid Congo Powers, Romi Mori (real name Hiromi Otani) on guitar and bass, and Nick Sanderson on drums.

Mother Juno (1987, Red Rhino Records RED LP 84 — 22 pressings) was produced by Robin Guthrie of Cocteau Twins — an unlikely pairing that yielded dense, reverb-saturated sound. Blixa Bargeld of Einstürzende Neubauten (and Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds) appears on “Yellow Eyes” as the “Inexplicable Guitarist.” Romi Mori plays lead guitar on “The Breaking Hands.” Engineered by Lincoln Fong; sleeve paintings by Claus Castenskiold.

Breaking Hands EP (Fire Records, 1987) also produced by Robin Guthrie.

Final albums and Pierce’s death (1990–1996)

Pastoral Hide & Seek (1990, Fire Records FIRE LP 28 — 16 pressings) and Lucky Jim (1993, What’s So Funny About… — 14 pressings) continued the European period. A 1995 live lineup added Wayne Kramer and Mike Martt. Pierce died March 31, 1996 in Oxford, England, of a stroke (cirrhosis complications), aged 37.

Kid Congo Powers went on to work extensively with Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds and The Cramps, and later formed Congo Norvell and Kid Congo & The Pink Monkey Birds.
