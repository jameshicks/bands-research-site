Pink Peg Slax

Leeds rockabilly/indie band, active 1980–late 1980s. Discogs artist ID: 1006500. Also credited as “The Slax.”

Overview

Pink Peg Slax formed in Leeds in 1980, occupying an awkward and productive position in the UK music landscape: too rockabilly for the indie scene, too indie for the neo-rockabilly circuit. Where the contemporaneous UK rockabilly revival (centred in London and the South-East) was focused on faithful genre recreation, Pink Peg Slax approached the form from a more anarchic, indie-aligned angle — writing original material, self-producing records, and releasing everything through Leeds-area independent labels.

Their output drew on rockabilly, Cajun, and old-time fiddle music, filtered through a post-punk DIY sensibility. John Peel — who championed the band at BBC Radio One and recorded multiple sessions with them — described the music as “Cajun-tinged jive-a-billy.” NME’s James Brown offered: “If Elvis had been a Marx Brother, he would have invented Pink Peg Slax.” Despite Peel sessions and daytime BBC Radio One airplay from 1984–1988, the band never broke through commercially, a situation the Discogs profile attributes to mutual alienation of both scenes they touched. No major label signing followed.

The band released two singles, one 12” EP, one LP, and one further LP on Ediesta Records (a sublabel of Red Rhino Records) before a 2012 Raucous Records compilation assembled the bulk of their catalog on CD. A track was licensed to the 2023 Cherry Red box set Where Were You- (Independent Music From Leeds 1978-1989), placing them in the documented canon of the Leeds independent scene.

Vince Berkeley is the primary songwriter — writing credits on “Self-Pitying Stan,” “Pay Dough,” and (co-written with Ant Hanlon) “Mud On My Clothes.” Martin Lefou’s credits shift from fiddle alone on the debut single to fiddle and acoustic guitar on the LP, reflecting the Cajun/country element deepening over time. A fifth member, Ant Hanlon (Discogs: 1006502), appears as co-writer on “Mud On My Clothes” but is not credited as a performer on either official release.

Guest musicians on Belting Out A Tuna (1986): Mike Hirst (Cajun accordion), Colin Modérne (pumping piano).

Context & Scene

Pink Peg Slax emerged from Leeds in 1980, the same city that had produced Gang of Four, Delta 5, and The Mekons in the immediate post-punk wave. By 1984, when they debuted on vinyl, Leeds was in its goth phase — Sisters of Mercy, The March Violets, Red Lorry Yellow Lorry — and the band’s Cajun-flavoured rockabilly stood apart from both the gothic gloom and the jangly C86 indie that followed. The release of 12 Songs Never Recorded By Frank Sinatra on Ediesta/Red Rhino in 1987 connects them loosely to the broader Northern indie infrastructure, even if they remained marginal to it.

The title Belting Out A Tuna and the matrix etching humour (“ABNER’S WOBBLING HULK,” “MARTIN IS TOO POSH”) signal the band’s self-conscious silliness — the anarchic quality that Peel and James Brown responded to, and that probably did limit mainstream crossover.
