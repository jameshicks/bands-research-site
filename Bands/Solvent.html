Solvent

May 14, 20266 min read

Solvent

Discogs ID: 603
Real name: Jason Amm (Discogs ID: 601271)
Origin: Born Zimbabwe; based in Toronto, Canada
Active: 1996 – present
Primary labels: Suction Records, Ghostly International, Morr Music

Electronic music producer and co-founder of Suction Records. Operates under the Solvent alias for solo electronic work; also records as Black Turtleneck (with Thomas Sinclair) and Tinfoil Teakettle (with Gregory de Rocher / Lowfish). A founding-era Morr Music artist — Solvent City (MM013, 2001) — Amm later relocated his primary releases to Ghostly International before returning to Suction for later work.

Biography

Early years and Suction Records (1996–1999)

Solvent’s first documented release is a self-titled cassette/CDr from 1996, issued on Not On Label (Canada). In 1997, alongside fellow Toronto producer Lowfish (Gregory de Rocher), he co-founded Suction Records and inaugurated the label with the Lowfish / Solvent split 12” (suction001, 1997) — each side featuring the two artists. The same split was revisited in a second configuration, Solvent / Lowfish (Suction Records, 1998). A third Suction release, Solvently One Listens (suction006, 1999), was his first solo full-length: 13 tracks of propulsive electro and vintage synth-pop, mastered by Stefan Figiel (Half Moon Dragster).

In 1999, Solvent and Lowfish jointly released the Duckie / Glued Smile 7” (BLOCK 003) on Berlin’s City Centre Offices — one of Solvent’s only non-Suction / non-Morr / non-Ghostly releases of the period.

Morr Music and Solvent City (2000–2001)

Solvent appeared on the flagship Morr Music compilation Putting The Morr Back In Morrissey (MM012CD, 2000) with two contributions: the original track “Tonka Truck” (disc 1, track 13) and a remix of Kevin & Paul’s “Die Wunderbaren Jahre” (disc 2, track 7). The compilation also featured The Notwist, Lali Puna, Christian Kleine, Styrofoam, ISAN, Arovane, and others — situating Solvent squarely in the Morr aesthetic of melodic IDM and electronic pop.

Solvent City (MM013CD, 2001) followed as Solvent’s second full-length and his sole album on Morr Music. Eight original tracks plus two remixes — a Skanfrom reinterpretation of “Frozen Food” and a Lowfish reinterpretation of “A Panel Of Experts” — gave the record a collaborative texture while remaining a Solvent statement. Styles span electro, IDM, and synth-pop. Cover artwork and illustration by Jan Kruse (2) and o8 Design; mastering by Rashad Becker.

Ghostly International era (2004–2010)

Apples & Synthesizers (GI-35, 2004) marked the move to Ghostly International and is widely considered Amm’s most fully realised record — 13 tracks anchored in classic electro and vintage synthesis aesthetics, mastered by Loop-o (credited as “Lupo”). Singles spun off the album included Radio Ga Ga Pt. 1 & 2 and the Think Like Us 12” (GI-38, 2005), which collected remixes by Lowfish, Ectomorph, and Alter Ego.

Elevators And Oscillators (2005) compiled earlier tracks. A retrospective, Demonstration Tape (1997–2007), appeared in 2007. The Loss For Words digital single (2010) was his last Ghostly release — a synth-pop track backed by Solvent’s own “CompuRhythm Mix” and a Vector Lovers remix.

Return to Suction and later work (2011–present)

The RDJCS5 EP (SUCTION022, 2011) — title referencing Roland/Drumtraks/Juno/Casio/Sequential Circuits/etc. — returned Solvent to his own label with five tracks of stripped electro. C For Comfort (2014) followed. Amm has remained active as a label operator, curator, and occasional designer for Suction releases (design concept credits on several EPs through 2017–2019). He produced New Ways: Music From The Documentary “I Dream Of Wires” (2014/2015) — a Suction compilation tied to Robert Fantinatto and Jason Amm’s documentary on modular synthesis — and New Ways: Addendum (2016), a companion release.
