Xylouris White

Greek-Australian duo. Discogs artist ID: 3614452

Members: George Xylouris (Cretan lute, lyra, vocals) and Jim White (2) (drums) Formed: c.2012, Crete, Greece Active: 2014–present Genres: Folk Rock, Free Improvisation, World / Experimental Website: xylouriswhite.com Name variations: George Xylouris And Jim White

Biography

Origins

Xylouris White grew out of a long-standing friendship and musical kinship between two very different musicians. George Xylouris (b. 1965, Anogeia, Crete) — also known by his nickname Ψαρογιώργης (Psarogiorgis) — is a Cretan master of the laouto (lute) and lyra; he is the son of Psarantonis (Antonis Xylouris, b. 1937), himself a legendary Cretan lyre player and singer, and the nephew of Nikos Xylouris (1936–1980), the towering “Archangel of Crete” who was instrumental in reviving traditional Cretan music in the modern era. George grew up in Anogeia in a family of hereditary musicians and became a highly regarded international exponent of Cretan music through the Xylouris Ensemble (active 1995–2014) and extensive solo work.

Jim White (2) (b. 1962, Melbourne) is the Australian drummer best known as a founding member of Dirty Three (with Mick Turner and Warren Ellis), a band formed in Melbourne in 1992 whose long catalog of post-rock/chamber improvisation runs from Torn & Frayed to Touch And Go to Drag City. White has also played across a vast range of sessions and collaborative projects — with Chan Marshall (Cat Power’s Moon Pix), PJ Harvey (White Chalk), Nick Cave, Bill Callahan (Smog), Nina Nastasia, Will Oldham / Bonnie Prince Billy, and dozens more.

The two met through the Melbourne-based Cretan diaspora community and began playing together informally. The name for their debut record came from a telling detail: as George drove Jim from home to the studio across Crete’s rocky hillsides, they passed goat herds clinging to the crags. Both men agreed that the goat’s calm risk-taking — going right to the edge — was a metaphor for what the music would do: fuse traditional Cretan idioms with rock improvisation, each tradition genuinely adapting.

Goats (2014) — Other Music Recording Co.

Their debut was produced by Guy Picciotto — singer/guitarist of Fugazi and Rites Of Spring, who had built a parallel reputation as a producer of adventurous and difficult-to-pigeonhole records. The album was recorded partly in Crete with local engineers and partly with Picciotto at the board. Engineering credits were split: Picciotto handled the majority of tracks (A1–A5, B2–B4), with Nikos Kefaloyannis (Nikos Kefalogiannis) engineering B1–B2 in Greece, and Vangelis Apostolou engineering B1 locally. Mastering was by Dan Millice.

The nine tracks range from tightly structured traditional forms (a sousta, a syrto attributed to Psarantonis) to more expansive original compositions. Songwriting credits show most tracks as co-written by White and George Xylouris, with one track (“Psarandonis Syrto”) credited to Psarantonis and two traditional pieces (“Old School Sousta,” “Fandomas”) credited to Traditional. The luthier credited for the instruments is Manny Boumakis.

Released on Other Music Recording Co. (catalog OM-015-1) — the label arm of the New York record shop Other Music. The vinyl was pressed by Pirates Press (matrix: PIRATES PRESS – OMO15-1A/B). APRA rights society (Australian). Cover art by Andi Magenheimer.

Move to Bella Union

For their second album the duo signed to Bella Union, the London-based independent label founded by Simon Raymonde and Robin Guthrie of Cocteau Twins. Bella Union had by the mid-2010s built a diverse roster spanning folk, post-rock, and experimental music. Picciotto remained producer across the next two albums.

Black Peak (2016) — Bella Union

Released in the US (Drag City distributed) and internationally on Bella Union. Catalog BELLA570V (vinyl). The album shows a more expansive approach, with longer tracks and the first appearance of significant guests.

“Erotokritos (Opening)” features additional vocals from Bonnie Prince Billy (Will Oldham), singing from the 17th-century Cretan epic poem Erotokritos by Vitsentzos Kornaros. “The Feast” features Psarantonis — George’s father — on vocals and Cretan lyra. George himself plays lute, lyra, and lead vocals across the record.

Engineering was distributed across several rooms: Guy Picciotto (A2, B2–B4), Yosimar Gomez (A1, B1), Birgir Jón Birgisson — Biggi — (A3, recorded at a different facility), with additional engineering from Bonnie Prince Billy (B1), Ben Knox Miller (A1, A3), Jeff Miller (A1), Nikos Kefaloyannis (B1), and Aristotelis Telis (B4). Mixing: Eli Crews and Guy Picciotto (jointly across the album), with Aristotelis Telis mixing B4. Mastered by Harris Newman at Grey Market Mastering, Montreal. Lyrics for the original tracks by Δημήτρης Σταυρακάκης (Mitsos Stavrakakis).

The Sisypheans (2019) — Drag City

The duo’s fourth album moved to Drag City (catalog DC758), the Chicago independent that had also been home to Jim White via Dirty Three and numerous associated artists. George plays lute, lyra, whistling, and vocals. Production still by Guy Picciotto, with Picciotto credited jointly as Mixed By and Producer — for the first time without multiple mix engineers, suggesting a more consolidated production process. Mastering credits not listed on the primary Discogs entry.

Tracklist: A1 Tree Song · A2 Goat Hair Bow · A3 Heart’s Eyes · A4 Telephone Song · B1 Black Sea · B2 Inland · B3 Wedding Song · B4 Ascension

The Forest In Me (2023) — Remote Control

A fifth studio album, released on Remote Control (catalog RCON165LP), an Australian independent. This represents a further label shift — back toward Australia after the US-focused Drag City years. Notably, production is listed differently: the album credits Picciotto, White, Kefaloyannis, and George Xylouris all jointly as engineers, with Picciotto still as Producer. Mastered by Harris Newman. George is also credited with Photography. Cover and layout by Shelagh Hannan. Writing credited jointly to Xylouris White.

The album has 12 tracks — the longest record of their catalog.

Family Tree

George Xylouris belongs to the most celebrated musical dynasty in Crete:

  • Nikos Xylouris (1936–1980) — George’s uncle; singer, lyra player, “Archangel of Crete.” Won San Remo folk prize 1966; recorded with Yannis Markopoulos; died of a brain tumor. Discogs ID: 1792424.
  • Psarantonis (Antonis Xylouris, b. 1937) — George’s father; Cretan lyra player, singer, composer. Brother of Nikos Xylouris. Collaborated with Nick Cave, Vinicio Capossela, Dirty Three’s Warren Ellis. Discogs ID: 3116537.
  • George Xylouris (Γιώργος Ξυλούρης / Psarogiorgis, b. 1965) — lute, lyra, vocals. Discogs ID: 1463707.
  • Apollonia Xylouris — George’s daughter; provided artwork for Mother (2018). Discogs ID: 3991363.

Note: the stub originally stated George is son of “Nikos Xylouris (legendary Cretan musician)” — this requires correction. The legendary Nikos Xylouris (1936–1980) is George’s uncle, not father. George’s father is Psarantonis (Antonis Xylouris). Psarantonis and Nikos Xylouris were brothers.

Connections

  • Dirty Three — Jim White’s primary band since 1992. Shares label (Drag City) with Xylouris White for The Sisypheans.
  • Guy Picciotto — producer on all five Xylouris White albums; Fugazi / Rites of Spring guitarist.
  • Fugazi — Picciotto’s main band; influential Washington DC post-hardcore group.
  • Rites Of Spring — Picciotto’s earlier band; forerunner of post-hardcore / emocore.
  • Bonnie Prince Billy — guest on Black Peak; also credited as additional engineer; longtime collaborator of Jim White.
  • Harris Newman — mastering engineer (Grey Market Mastering, Montreal) on three albums.
  • Bella Union — label for Black Peak and Mother; founded by Simon Raymonde and Robin Guthrie of Cocteau Twins.
  • Drag City — home of The Sisypheans; longtime home of Jim White via Dirty Three.
  • Psarantonis — George’s father; appears as guest on Black Peak.
  • Nikos Xylouris — George’s uncle; the defining figure of modern Cretan music.
  • Xylouris Ensemble — George’s long-running Australian-based traditional Cretan music group (1995–2014). Discogs ID: 2615889.
