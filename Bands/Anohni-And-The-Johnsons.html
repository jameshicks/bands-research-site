Overview

Anohni And The Johnsons is the 2023 reformation of Anohni’s ensemble, which had operated as Antony And The Johnsons before disbanding in 2015. When Anohni announced the reunion in May 2023, the group retained core members Chris Vatalaro, Jimmy Hogarth, Leo Abrahams, and Rob Moose. The two releases in that first year appeared on Rebis Music alongside Rough Trade and Secretly Canadian, landing in an art rock and avant-garde register consistent with the group’s earlier output. Regular contributors William Basinski, Martin Slattery, and Gabriel Cabezas each share credits across ten releases in the broader Anohni catalogue.

Notes (web)

Catalyst for the 2023 reformation

No single dramatic catalyst has been identified across the press around My Back Was a Bridge for You to Cross. The picture from multiple interviews is that the reunion happened organically through studio work and was bound up with the album’s artistic purpose rather than a formal decision to “reform” the band.

The clearest account comes from the Dazed Digital interview (July 2023): Anohni had been uncertain whether she would make another record at all. After entering studio sessions with producer Jimmy Hogarth, she said, “After the first couple of sessions, I knew I was going to make a record” — suggesting the return to the Johnsons format emerged from those sessions rather than preceding them. She also acknowledged some personal reluctance about re-entering public life: “As I’ve gotten older I don’t necessarily feel like a duck to water with…being centred in this way, publicly.” (Dazed Digital, July 2023)

The decision to credit the work to “Anohni and the Johnsons” rather than releasing it under her solo name carries a specific meaning: the name functions as an homage to Marsha P. Johnson, the gay liberation activist whose photograph appears on the album cover. Anohni stated: “For me, it is an honor to represent Marsha here. This record cover, the most important of my career, commemorates the restoration of connections and neural pathways between generations, past and future.” (Grammy.com, 2023)

Anohni also stated her intent for the songs to “be useful, to help others move with dignity and resilience through these conversations we are now facing” — framing the album as a purposeful intervention on themes of trans rights, ecology, and intergenerational queer lineage rather than a nostalgic reunion. The album was noted in multiple reviews as drawing inspiration from Marvin Gaye’s What’s Going On and Stevie Wonder’s Innervisions as models of socially engaged soul music. (Bandcamp Daily, July 28, 2023)

The Quietus review notes the gap since Swanlights (2010) and the intervening solo period, but Anohni gave no public account of why she set the Johnsons aside in the first place or what specifically ended that hiatus. The return appears to reflect a convergence of: a productive set of studio sessions with Hogarth; the thematic pull of reclaiming the Marsha P. Johnson name at a moment of heightened anti-trans hostility; and an intent to work in a warmer, more soul-rooted register than the electronic-focused Hopelessness (2016). (The Quietus, 2023)
