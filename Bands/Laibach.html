Laibach

Slovenian avant-garde/industrial group founded in Trbovlje, Yugoslavia (now Slovenia), 1980. Discogs artist ID: 25404. Primary label home since 1987: Mute Records. Also known as “Kraftbach” (alias).

Overview

Laibach are one of the defining acts of European industrial and post-industrial music, operating since 1980 as the music project of the Neue Slowenische Kunst (NSK) collective. NSK — “New Slovenian Art” — is a gesamtkunstwerk (total artwork) entity encompassing Laibach (music), the IRWIN group (visual art), Scipion Nasice Sisters Theatre, and other divisions. The collective’s strategy of ironic but deadpan appropriation of totalitarian aesthetics — simultaneously referencing Nazism, Stalinism, Yugoslav socialism, and corporate power — was a political provocation in the SFRY context and remained one internationally.

Musically, Laibach began in a harsh industrial mould: confrontational electronics, martial rhythms, heavily processed voice (associated with vocalist Milan Fras), and a sonic palette indebted to early Throbbing Gristle and the European post-punk underground. By the late 1980s they incorporated lush orchestral arrangements and covers of Western pop songs (“Life Is Life” by Opus, the Beatles’ Let It Be album reworked wholesale, “Sympathy For The Devil”) reimagined through totalitarian rhetoric and overblown grandeur. The cover-album approach continued with Jesus Christ Superstars (1996, Andrew Lloyd Webber material), Volk (2006, national anthems), and The Sound Of Music (2018, Rodgers & Hammerstein). Later albums (Spectre, 2014; The Future, 2022; Alamut, 2025) maintain the project’s conceptual seriousness on Mute Records.

The official membership is listed on Discogs as four anonymous figures: Dachauer, Eber, Keller (2), and Saliger — pseudonyms maintained throughout the band’s history. Identified actual contributors and collaborators span several decades (see Members below).

“Laibach” is the German name for Ljubljana, the Slovenian capital.

Origins and Early Years (1980–1984)

Founded in Trbovlje — an industrial coal-mining town — in 1980 by, among others, Tomaž Hostnik (vocals), Dejan Knez (keyboards/electronics/voice/drums), Srečko Bajda (electronics), and Bine Zerko. Andrej Lupinc (bass guitar) was also a founding member. The Yugoslav context was crucial: under Tito and then in the uncertain post-Tito 1980s, the band’s deliberate ambiguity about totalitarianism was simultaneously protected by (and in tension with) the socialist state’s cultural apparatus.

Tomaž Hostnik, the original vocalist and a key conceptual figure, died in December 1982 — a pivotal early event for the group. After Hostnik, Milan Fras became the primary voice.

Their early recordings circulated on cassette and small-run Yugoslav pressings. The self-titled debut LP (Laibach, ŠKUC, 1985 — recorded earlier, released on the Ljubljana student cultural centre label) was produced by Igor Vidmar and co-produced uncredited by Laibach, with Laibach Kunst handling design uncredited.

The Zagreb Music Biennial (1983)

In April 1983, Laibach invited Last Few Days and 23 Skidoo to perform with them at the Zagreb Music Biennial. The concert was interrupted by police at 5am. A split cassette documenting the Laibach/Last Few Days encounter — Laibach / Last Few Days (Galerija ŠKUC Izdaja, 1983) — was issued in an edition of 400, with the Laibach side produced by Igor Vidmar and Laibach, and Laibach Kunst credited for sleeve. Stephen Mallinder of Cabaret Voltaire engineered the Last Few Days side.

Fritz Catlin of Last Few Days / 23 Skidoo performed with Laibach on a subsequent tour. This connection led to Catlin playing on Laibach’s debut single.

Debut Single (1984)

Boji / Sila / Brat Moj (L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords, 1984) — Laibach’s first single, released on the Belgian label affiliated with Les Disques du Crépuscule. Producer credited as Jurij Toni alongside Laibach. Laibach Kunst did cover design. L.A.Y.L.A.H. Antirecords (Discogs label ID: 10713) was a sublabel of Les Disques Du Crépuscule.

Panorama / Decree (East West Trading Company, 1984) — another early UK single, produced with D-moniker.

European Breakthrough (1985–1987)

Cherry Red years

Die Liebe (Cherry Red, 1985) — produced and engineered by Laibach. Cherry Red label (Discogs Cherry Red).

Nova Akropola (Cherry Red, 1986) — first full-length album of note for the UK market. Produced by Jurij Toni, Ken Thomas, Laibach, and Rico Conning. Design by Laibach Kunst and David Black (6).

Krst Pod Triglavom / Baptism (1987)

Krst Pod Triglavom - Baptism (Sub Rosa / Walter Ulbricht Schallfolien, 1987) — a major early work documenting a large-scale theatrical event. Released on Sub Rosa (SUB 33006-7/9) and on the German imprint Walter Ulbricht Schallfolien. Produced by J.T. (13) and Laibach; design by Neue Kunsthandlung. This was among Laibach’s first non-Yugoslav European album releases.

Mute Records Era (1987–present)

From 1987 onward, Laibach’s principal label became Mute Records, which released the bulk of their studio discography. The Mute relationship continued through the label’s sale to EMI (2003) and Miller’s re-independence (2010) and continues to the present day.

Beatles Album Covers (1988)

Let It Be (Mute, 1988) — entire Beatles album rearranged by Laibach; recorded and mixed by Iztok Turk and Janez Križaj; design by New Collectivism Studio; artwork by Irwin (2) (the NSK visual artists’ group). The New Collectivism Studio (Discogs ID: 1842585) is the design entity closely linked to NSK.

Across The Universe (Mute, 1988) — further Beatles material.

Sympathy For The Devil (Mute / NSK Recordings, 1988) — Rolling Stones cover.

The Sound Of Music (North Korea, 2015)

In 2015, Laibach performed two concerts in North Korea — the first Western rock/pop act to perform there (a claim later disputed). This was connected to the The Sound Of Music album project, produced in collaboration with Norwegian director Morten Traavik and the Slovenian group Silence (2).

NSK and Design

Neue Slowenische Kunst (New Slovenian Art) is the gesamtkunstwerk collective of which Laibach is the music arm. Visual design for Laibach releases has been closely tied to NSK’s visual divisions:

  • Laibach Kunst (Discogs ID: 1885572) — the in-house design entity credited on early Laibach sleeves (Boji / Sila / Brat Moj, Laibach LP, the 1983 split cassette)
  • New Collectivism Studio (Discogs ID: 1842585) — NSK-affiliated design studio; credited on Let It Be (1988), NATO (1994)
  • Irwin (2) (Discogs ID: 2259093) — the NSK visual artists’ group; provided paintings for Let It Be sleeve (1988)
  • Neue Kunsthandlung (Discogs ID: 2259444) — credited on Krst Pod Triglavom (1987) and Kapital (1992)

NSK also declared itself a “state in time” in 1992, issuing passports and stamps — another dimension of the collective’s conceptual practice that extends beyond the music.
