The Nation Of Ulysses

Discogs ID: 267485. Washington DC art-hardcore band. Active ~1988–1993.

Ian Svenonius (vocals), Tim Green (guitar), Steve Kroner (guitar), James Canty (bass), Steve Hodges (drums). Released three records on Dischord Records: 13-Point Program To Destroy America (Dischord 57, 1991), The Birth Of The Ulysses Aesthetic (Dischord 62, 1991), Plays Pretty For Baby (Dischord 71, 1992). Confrontational, agitprop HC with a performative political persona. The Embassy Tapes (Dischord 124, 2000) was released posthumously.

Svenonius then formed The Make-Up (Dischord 99+, 1995–2000).

Notes (web)

NOU, the Embassy, and riot grrrl

NOU’s household, “the Embassy” (a punk house in Mount Pleasant, DC), was a direct node connecting the band to the emerging riot grrrl scene. Kathleen Hanna and other members of Bikini Kill spent time at the Embassy during summer 1991 when they relocated from Olympia to DC. Tim Green (2) (NOU guitarist) recorded Bikini Kill material on a four-track at the Embassy that summer, and Hanna also recorded a side project, Wondertwins, with Green during this period. The term “riot grrrl” itself was coined at the Embassy in 1991, according to Christina Billotte (later of Slant 6), who was present when Bratmobile members exclaimed the phrase during the Mount Pleasant riots. [Source: Suture (band) Wikipedia article, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suture_(band); Bikini Kill Bandcamp search snippet; Kathleen Hanna Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Hanna; Boundary Stones/WETA article on riot grrrl in DC, https://boundarystones.weta.org/2024/08/19/riot-grrrl-feminist-revolution-dcs-punk-scene]

NOU’s radical left/Situationist political ideology is documented as providing an ideological backdrop that the nascent riot grrrl movement drew on — riot grrrl extended NOU’s critique of capitalism and hierarchy into an explicit feminist frame. The Tropic of Meta piece “When She Talks, I Hear the Revolution” (2012) notes that riot grrrl members who came to DC “fell in with a collection of punks connected to the legendary Nation of Ulysses, Fugazi, and Positive Force,” and that “Svenonius, NOU, and other D.C. punks gathered at the Embassy to discuss music, politics, and agit prop, serving as an ally to the Riot Grrrl movement.” [Source: Tropic of Meta, https://tropicsofmeta.com/2012/05/02/when-she-talks-i-hear-the-revolution-looking-back-at-the-riot-grrrl-revolt/]

NOU and Bikini Kill played together at St. Stephens in Washington DC on 16 November 1991. [Source: search snippet from UMD DC Punk exhibition, https://exhibitions.lib.umd.edu/dc-punk/bands/nation-of-ulysses — page returned 400, cited from snippet only]

No contemporaneous statements from NOU members explicitly endorsing riot grrrl or feminist politics were found in this search. A 1991 Sonic Archives interview with Svenonius (https://sonicarchives.com/taste/interviews/nou/) focuses on youth culture and revolutionary aesthetics without addressing gender or feminism.

Ian Svenonius — 2020 misconduct admission and open letter

In July 2020, Svenonius posted and then deleted an Instagram statement admitting to “abusive predatory modes,” writing: “I have acted the creep. That I have made people I cared about and respected feel terrible, have been completely inappropriate to women.” Merge Records severed ties with him following the post. [Source: Exclaim!, https://exclaim.ca/music/article/ian_svenonius_admits_to_abusive_predatory_behaviour_towards_women; Stereogum, https://stereogum.com/2093176/ian-svenonious-dropped-by-merge-records/news]

An anonymous open letter specifically addressed to “the Dischord & Riot Grrrl scenes & members” was also circulated (URL: https://static1.squarespace.com/static/64efea71ba63473425e1aacd/t/661095daabe0732b387db5c7/1712362970778/IanSvenonius_Anonymous.pdf.pdf — PDF returned 404 at time of research). Per search snippets, the letter described incidents including Svenonius spending an evening flirting with 15-year-old girls, and contained descriptions of statutory rape, sexual assault, and coercion spanning years. The letter’s explicit framing as addressed to the Dischord and riot grrrl scenes indicates the alleged conduct occurred within those overlapping communities. [Source: search snippet only — PDF inaccessible; Consequence of Sound article, https://consequence.net/2020/07/merge-records-ian-svenonius-allegations/]

Note: The evidence above documents proximity and informal alliance between NOU and riot grrrl (shared space, personal relationships, ideological overlap) rather than NOU formally or publicly engaging with feminist politics as a band. The band’s own political output (pamphlets, lyrics, interviews) addressed class, youth, and anti-capitalism rather than gender. The 2020 disclosures present a conflict between NOU’s scene reputation as a radical ally and Svenonius’s admitted personal conduct.
