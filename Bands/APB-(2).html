APB (2)

Post-punk/funk band formed in Ellon, Aberdeenshire, Scotland, c. 1979. Active in their first run from 1979 to 1989, reconvening briefly around 2006–2007 and again in 2011. Name allegedly stands for “All Points Bulletin.” Also credited as A.P.B. Discogs artist ID: 87344.

History

Formation and Early Singles (1979–1983)

APB formed in Ellon, a small town north of Aberdeen, around 1979. Their founding core was Iain Slater (bass, vocals), Glenn Roberts (guitar), and George Cheyne (drums), with Neil Innes (2) on keyboards and Mike Craighead on percussion joining as the lineup solidified. Nick Jones (4) played percussion with the band in 1982–1983.

Their first recordings were produced by Tony Pilley and released on Oily Records, an Aberdeen-based independent label run out of 59 Orchard Street. The debut single “Chain Reaction” / “Power Crisis” (1981, Oily SLICK 6) established the template: choppy post-punk guitar over a funk-influenced rhythmic base. “Shoot You Down” (1981, SLICK 7) followed; the second single added funk and new wave styles to their Discogs genre credits. “Rainy Day” (1982, SLICK 9) was their third 7”.

“Palace Filled With Love” (1982, Oily SLICK 8) marked the arrival of producer Wilf Smarties, who would become the band’s most consistent studio collaborator through the mid-1980s. The 1983 twelve-inch “One Day” (Oily SLICK 12/10) was their most ambitious early release — a 12” recorded with co-producer Ivan Ivan (Ivan Baker) and mixed in New York by Scott Noll, with Nick Jones (4) on uncredited percussion and guest backing vocals from Lisa McCaillis.

Albion Records and US Breakthrough (1984)

In 1984 APB signed to Albion Records (a UK indie distributed nationally), releasing two twelve-inch singles. “Danceability” (12 ION 160) was an EP produced by APB and Wilf Smarties; the B-side track “Crazy Grey” was produced separately by BBC Radio 1 producer Dale Griffin (Mott the Hoople), with engineering by Mike Engles. Scottish engineer Calum Malcolm recorded the A-side tracks. The second Albion single, “What Kind Of Girl?” (12 ION 170, 1984), was produced by APB and Steve Thompson, and recorded by New York engineers Frank Heller and Michael Barbiero, with session keyboardists Craig Peyton and Robbie Kilgore overdubbing in New York. The same track was licensed to Sleeping Bag Records in the US.

Red River Records and US Releases (1985–1989)

From 1985 the band operated through two main channels: Red River Records for UK releases and Link (a US indie) for North American distribution. Both labels had a close working relationship — most Red River titles also appeared on Link, and vice versa.

“Something To Believe In” / “So Many Broken Hearts” (Red River YTHAN T2, 1985) was produced by APB, Wilf Smarties, and Chic Medley (who also engineered), mastered by John Dent (“Jonz”). The US compilation LP Something To Believe In (Link LINK 002, 1985) collected early Oily singles and newer material into a ten-track overview of their career to that point; Wilf Smarties produced most tracks, with Ivan Ivan producing “One Day” and Steve Thompson & Michael Barbiero producing “What Kind Of Girl.”

“Summer Love” (Red River YTHAN T1, 1985) was a double-A-side twelve-inch with the B-side “Is The Music Loud Enough.” “Open Your Eyes” (Red River YTHAN T3, 1986) was mixed by Chic Medley.

The second album, Cure For The Blues (Link LINK 004 / Red River YTHAN LP4, 1986), marked a more polished pop-funk sound. Wilf Smarties and APB co-produced; Chic Medley engineered and mixed. Backing vocalists Carol Laula and Jackie Quinn appear on the record.

“Missing You Already” (Link LINK 006, 1986) was the next single, released in the US and then the UK (Red River, 1987). Bruce Mills co-wrote the title track.

The final release of the first era was Invective (Red River APB-1, 1989), an EP whose catalog number used the band’s own initials — Red River pressing it under APB’s own imprint. “Funk Invective” was produced by Andy Wallace; the other tracks were engineered by Wilf Smarties.

Reconvening (2006–2011)

In 2006–2007 APB reconvened, releasing two archival/new collections: The Radio 1 Sessions (Young American Recordings YAM-104) compiled BBC recordings; Three (Red River YTHANCD1) was a new studio album issued on CD, the first original LP since Cure For The Blues. Something to Believe In was reissued by Young American Recordings in a 20th Anniversary Edition.

In 2011 a new mini-album, Jaguar (Oatcake Records EAT4, 7 tracks), appeared with Jim Shepherd (2) contributing compositions and lead vocals on two tracks.

The Something To Believe In album was reissued again in 2022 on Liberation Hall, and Cure For The Blues followed in 2023.

Before and After / Scene Context

Pre-APB Origins

Iain Slater and Glenn Roberts began playing together as teenagers at school in Ellon around 1977, initially with a different drummer. When George Cheyne joined on drums the classic three-piece solidified, and the band formally became APB around 1979. No named predecessor band has been identified in any source — the 1977 pairing appears to have been an informal high-school outfit rather than a gigging group. (source: Bandcamp Daily interview, 2024)

Their early sound was heavy rock before exposure to punk shifted the template; a French holiday around age 19 introduced Slater and Roberts to Parliament/Funkadelic records and the funk direction became central. (source: Bandcamp Daily interview, 2024)

Post-APB: Loveless (c.1989–1993)

After drummer George Cheyne departed around 1989, the remaining members — Slater and Roberts — evolved APB into a new project called Loveless. APB played a final run of shows partly to fund this new ensemble. Loveless released an EP called Nappy in 1993. Beyond the name and EP title, no further details on Loveless’s lineup or label have been confirmed via web research. (source: Wikipedia — APB (band), as of 2025-05-16; source: Lost Bands of the New Wave Era blog, 2005)

Post-APB: Iain Slater

After APB and Loveless, Iain Slater joined Pablo, a band on the Poptones label (the UK indie run by Alan McGee post-Creation). He simultaneously worked as a live sound engineer for touring acts including Space, Keane, Regular Fries, and Pete Doherty. (source: Wikipedia — APB (band), as of 2025-05-16)

Post-APB: Glenn Roberts and George Cheyne

No post-1989 band activity confirmed for Glenn Roberts or George Cheyne beyond the 2006 and 2011 APB reunions. Cheyne described the 2006 reunion as “a bit like pinching yourself that it really happened after 17 years.” (source: Bandcamp Daily interview, 2024)

Aberdeen/Scottish Scene Context

APB occupied an unusual position in the 1980s Scottish indie scene: they were geographically remote (Ellon is a small town north of Aberdeen, far from the Glasgow/Edinburgh axis of Postcard Records and Fire Engines) and found their largest audience not in Scotland but in the New York Tri-State area, through heavy rotation on WLIR 92.7 FM (Long Island) and college radio. Contemporaneous comparisons cited by sources include Gang of Four, Au Pairs, Delta 5, and A Certain Ratio — they were aligned with the “militaristic dance” post-punk faction but described as “more celebratory and inclusive.” (source: AllMusic biography via Last.fm)

Tony Pilley, their early producer, was based at Barclay Towers studio in Edinburgh and also worked with The Electric Personalities — placing APB within a loose network of Scottish post-punk producers working outside Glasgow. (source: 7tt77.co.uk)

Calum Malcolm, who engineered the “Danceability” A-side (1984), is another cross-node: he was a member of The Headboys (Aberdeen new-wave band, late 1970s) and went on to become a prominent Scottish recording engineer (The Blue Nile, Prefab Sprout), providing a direct link between APB and the broader Scottish indie production world.

Ad-Rock (Adam Horovitz) of the Beastie Boys cited APB’s “Shoot You Down” as “a punk dance-floor classic” in his memoir — evidence of their reach in the New York scene that championed them. (source: Wikipedia — APB (band), as of 2025-05-16)

”Abstract Records” — Likely Misattribution

All sources confirm the label sequence as: Oily Records (1981–1983) → Albion Records (1984) → Red River / Link (1985–1989). “Abstract Records” is a known label name used by other acts (e.g., an Abstract label in the Chicago house scene); the mention in the brief may be a confusion with a different band or a misread of a catalog number.
