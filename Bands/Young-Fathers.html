Young Fathers

Discogs ID: 1526704
Formed: 2008, Edinburgh, Scotland
Members: Alloysius Massaquoi, Graham Hastings, Kayus Bankole
Earlier alias: 3Style

Edinburgh trio. Described on Discogs as “psychedelic hip hop” but Discogs’ own profile notes the label is inadequate — their work draws from hip hop, soul, folk, indie, trip hop, and African music, and all three members sing as well as rap. Each album has moved significantly in sound; Dead (2014) is raw and lo-fi, Cocoa Sugar (2018) leans into atmospheric melody, and Heavy Heavy (2023) incorporates South African choral traditions. They write, produce, and engineer almost all of their own material, with Graham Hastings serving as the primary in-house producer.

Career

Formation and 3Style era (2008–2011)

The group formed in Edinburgh in 2008 under the name 3Style — a project name they maintained on early releases. Their debut Discogs credit is “Straight Back On It” (2008, Blacksugar Records), a promo CDr single. The 2010 release “Automatic / Dancing Mantaray” (This Is Music label) credits Timothy London as co-producer alongside the trio, and features a b-side produced by Baobinga and James Ginzburg.

An “Automatic” EP also appeared in 2010 on Eurostar Records. “Tape One” (2011) was self-released under the Young Fathers name on Not On Label.

Tape era and Anticon (2012–2013)

Leader (2012) appeared on the Stanton Warriors’ Punks label. Tape Two (2013) was released through Anticon (cat: ABR0134) — the Oakland experimental hip-hop label that also put out their early cassettes. Timothy London has a co-writing credit across Tape Two’s nine tracks alongside all three members.

Dead and the Mercury Prize (2014)

Dead (2014, Big Dada Recordings, cat: BD239) won the Mercury Prize for Album of the Year in 2014, making Young Fathers one of the few hip-hop-adjacent acts to take the prize. The album is stark and confrontational, running to eleven tracks with minimal credits: the only named contributor is Simon Davey, who cut the lacquer at The Exchange (runout: “BD 239 A SIMON-THE EXCHANGE MPO”). Three singles followed on Big Dada: Get Up, Low, and Soon Come Soon.

The Anticon/Big Dada co-release arrangement from Dead consolidated their transition from the US indie underground to European major-adjacent distribution.

White Men Are Black Men Too (2015)

Second album on Big Dada Recordings (cat: BD264). Twelve tracks; mastered by Beau Thomas. The runout inscriptions are cryptic (“here’s anoTher TicKET”; “don’t run away I don’t hesitate for a second”). The album extended their critical profile without changing the self-produced core methodology.

Cocoa Sugar (2018)

Third studio album, first on Ninja Tune (cat: ZENCD248). Produced in collaboration with Dave Sitek (TV on the Radio); mastered by Vlado Meller. The twelve tracks lean more melodic than Dead or WMABT, with layered vocal harmonics and more pronounced synthesizer presence. Discogs genre: Rock (atypical for them). Engineer Ben Baptie mixed the album.

Heavy Heavy (2023)

Fourth studio album (Ninja Tune, cat: ZEN285). The most expansive production to date. Graham Hastings is credited as producer and multi-instrumentalist (electric organ/Vox Continental, Transcendent synthesizer, Boss PC-2, Mellotron, Syntrx, Korg Minipops, ER-1 drum machine, piano, jasper, percussion). Engineer and additional producer Iain Berryman plays guitar, bouzouki, piano, and percussion; he also co-mixes with Ben Baptie. Liam Hutton plays drums, percussion, talking drum, and timpani throughout. Lacquer and mastering by Matt Colton at Metropolis (runout: “Matt @ Metropolis”).

A notable production credit: “Sink Or Swim” (B3) is partly built from a sample, with Hal Ritson and Richard Adlam credited as producers of the sampled source material.

South African guest vocalists (all making Discogs debuts on this release): Nomasonto Mthembu, Toyiz Mchunu, Nonhlanhla Mhlongo, Tapiwa Mambo (also Written-By on “Ululation”), Anele Mthembu. Design by Tom Hingston Studio. Strings by Raven Bush.

Ceasefire with Massive Attack and Fontaines D.C. (2024)

A four-track 12” EP (Battle Box label, cat: BB008) released jointly with Massive Attack and Fontaines D.C.. Structure: A1 “Give” is a Massive Attack × Young Fathers track; A2 “In Ár gCroithe Go Deo” is a Fontaines D.C. track remixed by 3D (Massive Attack); B1 is a Conor Curley (Fontaines D.C. guitarist) remix of “Give”; B2 is an Orbital remix of “In Ár gCroithe Go Deo.” Lacquer cut by Rhys Crouch. Label: Battle Box (BB008) — appears to be a dedicated charity or one-off imprint.

28 Years Later soundtrack (2025)

Young Fathers scored the Danny Boyle film 28 Years Later (2025), the long-delayed sequel in the 28 Days Later franchise. Released on Milan Records as a 24-track digital album. Engineer: Jake Jackson.
