The Casual Dots

Washington DC / Olympia punk trio. Discogs artist ID: 1153135

Biography

The Casual Dots formed from the overlap of two scenes — DC post-hardcore and Olympia riot grrrl — brought together by personal and musical bonds. Christina Billotte (vocals, guitar) had previously fronted Slant 6 (Dischord Records) and Autoclave (2) before founding Quix-o-tic. Kathi Wilcox (guitar, backing vocals) was a founding member of Bikini Kill and later joined The Julie Ruin. Steve Dore (drums) came from Deep Lust and also played in Freakapuss and Snoozers.

The trio released their self-titled debut LP in 2004 on Kill Rock Stars (cat# KRS 404). It was produced and mixed by Guy Picciotto (Fugazi), engineered by Don Zientara at Inner Ear Studios in Arlington, Virginia. Picciotto’s involvement connected the record directly to the Dischord/Inner Ear nexus: he was already the most trusted outside producer in that studio’s orbit, and Kathi Wilcox is married to Picciotto — making this a project with deep personal as well as creative ties. The album was credited on Discogs under the name variation “Dots.”

The debut LP contained ten tracks including eight originals plus two covers: “I’ll Dry My Tears” (written by Etta James) and “Bumblebee” (written by LaVern Baker). Running time is approximately 30 minutes. The album was released on CD and later reissued on vinyl in 2022 by the band’s own imprint Ixor Stix (cat# IX06).

2022 Return: Sanguine Truth

In 2022 the band released a second album, Sanguine Truth, also on Ixor Stix (cat# IX07), their own label. This vinyl-only LP contains ten tracks (five per side). The production team was the same as the debut: Picciotto as producer and mixer, Zientara as engineer. Mastering was by John Golden. Billotte handled photography and co-wrote lyrics on “Live For Yourself” (based on a song by Bobby Moore & The Fourmost). The album is credited to all three members for songwriting (songs A1–A3, A5–B5 written collectively by The Casual Dots). “Live For Yourself” revises lyrics originally by Bobby Moore And The Fourmost, with revised lyrics credited to Billotte.

The 2022 activity package — vinyl reissue of the debut plus a new full-length — suggests the band had remained intact and that the 18-year gap between LP1 and LP2 was dormancy rather than dissolution.
