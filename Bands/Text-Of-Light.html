Text Of Light

NYC film-improvisation ensemble. Discogs artist ID: 288448. Formed 1999; active as a recording and performing group through at least 2006. Performed live improvised music to early American avant-garde cinema, most prominently the films of Stan Brakhage and others from the 1950s–60s avant-garde film movement. The group’s name comes from Brakhage’s 1974 film Text of Light, which served as the premiere performance and namesake of the project.

Overview

Text Of Light was conceived with the explicit premise of improvising to — rather than illustrating — films from the American avant-garde cinema tradition, a deliberately under-known body of work at the time. The lineup was fluid: members attended performances based on individual availability, so the group appeared in various permutations.

The founding members drew primarily from the NYC downtown experimental scene. Lee Ranaldo and Alan Licht (both guitarists with Sonic Youth-adjacent roots) formed the guitar core. Christian Marclay brought turntable manipulation. The percussion chair was shared between William Hooker (free jazz drummer) and Tim Barnes (appearing on different releases). Ulrich Krieger handled saxophone and electronics. Gregor Asch (performing as DJ Olive) played turntables alongside Marclay — the two were part of a longer-running turntable trio with Marclay that had performed at venues including the Whitney Museum (New York), Centre Pompidou (Paris), and Philharmonie (Cologne).

A related live recording, Koncertas Stan Brakhage Prisiminimui (2005, Sonic Youth Records), documents a concert with Sonic Youth and Tim Barnes as a memorial to Brakhage (who died in 2003). This release was separate from the main Text Of Light discography but featured overlapping personnel and context.

Harry Smith — avant-garde filmmaker, archivist, and folk music anthologist (born 1923, died 1991 at the Chelsea Hotel) — is credited on the group’s debut release for artwork (film stills). Smith’s work sat adjacent to Brakhage’s in the American experimental film tradition, and Discogs confirms his imagery was used on the debut’s sleeve, with image restoration credited to Susan Archie.
