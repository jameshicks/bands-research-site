Look De Bouk

Discogs ID: 495746
Also known as: L.D.B., Look Da Book
Origin: Reims, France
Active: c. 1982–1990 (recordings); compiled 2001
Formation: 1982 (Himalaya, Ieuw, Martial Canterel as founding trio)
Genre: French experimental / avant-garde rock
Scene: Rock in Opposition-adjacent; Reims indie/new-wave
Label: Ayaa (three releases, 1985–1990)

Overview

Look De Bouk were a French experimental band from Reims, born out of the city’s indie and new-wave scene of the early 1980s. They are often grouped in the wider Rock in Opposition field on the basis of comparisons with Albert Marcoeur, Henry Cow, and This Heat. Their music sits somewhere between art-rock, avant-folk, and free improvisation — dense with unusual instrumentation, wordplay-heavy song titles, and collaborative compositional credit that suggests a genuine collective process.

The group shared members with the two other Reims outfits on Ayaa: Hellebore and NEO Museum (2). Several Look De Bouk members (Alain Casari, Denis Tagu) were simultaneously in Hellebore; and three ex-Hellebore members (Casari, Antoine Gindt, Daniel Koskowitz) went on to form NEO Museum (2). The Reims scene was a tight cluster: Dominique Diebold, who ran Ayaa and its predecessor labels, served as the local operator binding these groups together.

Look De Bouk formed in 1982 as a trio — Etienne Himalaya, Kwettap Ieuw, and Martial Canterel — within the orbit of the À l’Automne Alité (AAA) association in Reims. Dorian Feller joined in 1984, Denis Tagu in early 1985. Etienne Himalaya confirmed this timeline in a 2010 blog comment [source: mutant-sounds.blogspot.com]. The band first documented their activity in 1984 on a Swiss cassette compilation and released their debut LP in 1985. Their last original studio release appeared in 1990 on CD. A 2001 compilation CD on Gazul Records and In-Poly-Sons collected archival material.

Lacrimae Rerum (1985)

Ayaa DT. 0885 | LP | France
Discogs Release ID: 718564
Genres: Jazz, Rock | Styles: Art Rock, New Wave, Prog Rock

The debut LP. Recorded primarily by Antoine Gindt (later of NEO Museum (2) and Hellebore), with additional home recording by the band and Benoît Proust. Mixed by Dominique Diebold, Fabrice Digier, and Jean-Maurice Rossel. Sleeve designed by Etienne Himalaya and Kwettap Ieuw.

Etienne Himalaya composed all six side-A tracks and four of the five side-B tracks; the title track (B1) is co-credited to Dorian Feller, Himalaya, and Jean Caël. The record features an unusually large cast of guests.

Bec Et Ongles (1990)

Ayaa T.I.C.0890 | CD | France
Discogs Release ID: 1413517
Genres: Classical, Electronic, Jazz | Styles: Avantgarde, Minimal

The final original studio release, a 24-track CD. The T.I.C. catalog prefix is again unique within the Ayaa DT/CDT sequence (see “Catalog Number Series” section below). The record has cover art credited to Isabelle Ayuso. Arranged entirely by Look De Bouk; produced (artistic production) and mixed by Jean-Maurice Rossel with Etienne Himalaya. Sound treatments by Rossel. The reduced personnel list compared to Lacrimae Rerum suggests a more self-contained recording.

The record features several pieces reworking or revisiting material from the debut (Engagée Comme Jockey, Demain Ou Ailleurs, Androgine De Poitrine) — all of which also appear on the 2001 compilation, suggesting these were definitive or favored versions. Three tracks credit traditional sources (folksong or “Traditionnel”). The track “Inte Quanta” is credited to Lars Hollmer (Swedish keyboardist and member of Samla Mammas Manna), a Scandinavian avant-garde composer who also has his own Ayaa release in 1994.

L’âcre Hymne Aérait Rome (2001)

Gazul Records GA 8640.AR / In-Poly-Sons IPS0201 | CD (Compilation)
Discogs Release ID: 4469246

A 15-track retrospective compilation on Gazul Records and In-Poly-Sons. The title is an anagram (a recognizable Look De Bouk preoccupation given the wordplay in their French track titles). No individual credits are listed on the release beyond the band name. Several tracks revisit titles from the 1985 LP (multiple “Ferdinand Cheval” pieces) and 1990 CD (Androgine De Poitrine, Engagée Comme Jockey, Demain Ou Ailleurs), along with titles not documented on any other release.

Double Dog Dare, Summer ‘84 (1984)

Calypso Now NOW-37 | Cassette | Switzerland
Discogs Release ID: 1632988

A split cassette with The Lo Yo Yo, issued on the Swiss label Calypso Now. Look De Bouk occupy side A (8 tracks); The Lo Yo Yo (with Mick Hobbs, Alig, Joey Stack / Stockhouse, Carrie Brooks) occupy side B. The release predates Look De Bouk’s Ayaa releases and may represent the earliest documentation of the band. One Look De Bouk track (“Liturgist Beat”) is a cover credited to Lars Hollmer; another (“Semi China Blue”) is credited to The Lo Yo Yo.

Side A performers for Look De Bouk: Etienne Himalaya, Kwettap Ieuw, Germain Hubert Ales (alias, unconfirmed relationship to other members), Martial Canterel (2), and Ambroise Moith-Raifaure.

Catalog Number Series

The three Ayaa releases use three distinct catalog-number prefixes, none of which follow the standard Ayaa DT/CDT sequence:

Cat No.ReleaseYearFormatSeries
DT. 0885Lacrimae Rerum1985LPStandard DT vinyl series
A.C.R. 01…Avec Des Coussins Bleus1988CassetteStandalone — “A.C.R.” initials not documented
T.I.C.0890Bec Et Ongles1990CDStandalone — “T.I.C.” initials not documented

The debut LP fits the standard Ayaa DT vinyl sequence used for most 1984–87 releases (Officer!, Etron Fou, etc.). The later two releases break with that sequence — the 1988 cassette received an “A.C.R. 01” number that looks like a sub-series of one, and the 1990 CD received a “T.I.C.0890” number (format appears to embed year: 08 = August?, 90 = 1990). No other Ayaa releases share either prefix. The meaning of A.C.R. and T.I.C. is not documented in Discogs data; they may be internal project codes, references to recording locations, or acronyms for the band’s own production entity.

Reims Scene Context

Look De Bouk were part of a tight local network of musicians and organizers centered on Reims in the 1980s. The central node was Dominique Diebold, who ran first Recommended Records France, then AAA (“A l’Automne Alité”), then Ayaa from 21 rue de Courlancy — essentially a continuous operation that started as a RIO-affiliated distribution hub and evolved into an independent label.

The three main Reims outfits on Ayaa shared overlapping personnel:

  • Hellebore (Discogs ID: 543471): Alain Casari, Antoine Gindt, Daniel Koskowitz, Denis Tagu, Jean Caël. Active 1983–2000; two releases on Ayaa (1985). Closely parallel to Look De Bouk — Casari and Tagu appear in both; the Discogs profiles for both bands use near-identical wording about their origins in the “indie/new-wave scene of the early 1980’s.”

  • NEO Museum (2) (Discogs ID: 1442034): Alain Casari, Antoine Gindt, Daniel Koskowitz — described as “three ex-members of Hellebore.” One Ayaa release: Volume 1. Nouvelles Ethnologiques De L’obscur Museum (1986, DT 0586), a jazz-oriented experimental LP.

  • Look De Bouk (ID: 495746): Etienne Himalaya, Kwettap Ieuw, Denis Tagu, Dorian Feller, Alain Casari, Didier Pietton.

The “Ferdinand Cheval” thread running through Look De Bouk’s catalog (a recurring title across all three Ayaa releases and the 2001 compilation) references the French postman Ferdinand Cheval (1836–1924), who spent 33 years building the Palais Idéal in Hauterives — a monument to naive/outsider art. This suggests a preoccupation with autodidactic, non-institutional creation that fits the RIO ethos.

Open Questions

  • The meaning of the A.C.R. (cassette, 1988) and T.I.C. (CD, 1990) catalog prefixes remains undocumented — web research found no explanation; may require physical liner notes.
  • “Germain Hubert Ales” credited as performer on Double Dog Dare (1984) is listed as a separate Discogs entity (ID 542220) and also appears in Dorian Feller’s group credits — the relationship to the Look De Bouk core lineup is unclear; possibly an early member or alias. (Active years now clarified to 1982 by Himalaya’s own statement; the 1984 cassette was not the beginning.)
  • Dorian Feller’s alias “Brodé Tango” — no discography found under that name in available Discogs data.
  • “André Malraux” (ID 544297) credited as guest vocalist on Lacrimae Rerum — clearly a pseudonym (the writer died 1976); identity still unknown. No web source identifies this person.
  • The title L’âcre Hymne Aérait Rome (2001) — the vault noted this “reads as an anagram.” Letter analysis (aaaceeeehilmmnorrrty, 20 letters) does not match the band name, Lacrimae Rerum, any member name, or any other confirmed target. No documented source explains what (if anything) the title is an anagram of. It may be a deliberate false-anagram or a phrase intended only to sound anagrammatic.
  • Whether any further archival material exists beyond the 15 tracks on the 2001 compilation.

Formation history (web sources)

Multiple web sources, corroborated by a 2010 comment posted directly by Etienne Himalaya on the Mutant Sounds blog, establish the following timeline — which conflicts with the vault’s current “active c. 1983” framing:

  • 1982: Look De Bouk formed as a trio: Etienne Himalaya, Kwettap Ieuw, and Martial Canterel. The band’s own founding member explicitly stated “we started in 1982” and that there were “no connections with Hellebore” at this stage. [source: http://mutant-sounds.blogspot.com/2007/12/look-de-bouk-lacrimae-rerumlp1985france.html, comment by Etienne Himalaya, 2010; confirmed by https://centraldoprog.blogspot.com/2017/11/look-de-bouk-lacrimae-rerum-1985-lp.html]
  • 1984: Dorian Feller joined the lineup. [same sources]
  • Early 1985: Denis Tagu joined. [same sources]

Conflict note: The vault’s Members section does not document Martial Canterel as a founding member, yet he was one of the original three. He appears on the 1984 split cassette (Discogs: Martial Canterel (2)), but is not in the Members table. The web sources establish he was present from 1982.

The Vivonzeureux blogger notes meeting Himalaya and Ieuw in 1982 through Dorian Feller, which is consistent (Feller was a social connection before joining the band). [source: http://vivonzeureux.blogspot.com/2008/07/look-de-bouk-bec-et-ongles.html]

The AAA association (À l’Automne Alité) — the predecessor to AYAA — is confirmed by multiple sources as the organizational home of the band’s early activity. The blogger who wrote about Bec Et Ongles states he purchased a small share in AYAA when the association converted to a SARL, suggesting tight integration between the band and the label structure. [source: vivonzeureux.blogspot.com]

A.C.R. and T.I.C. catalog prefixes

No source located explains what A.C.R. or T.I.C. stand for. The Vivonzeureux blog post on Bec Et Ongles references the T.I.C.0890 catalog number without glossing it. The AAA/Ayaa label history documented by tape-mag.com covers the label’s name evolution (Recommended Records France → AAA → AYAA) but does not mention sub-series. The ADN Records 2022 reissue notes for Avec Des Coussins Bleus do not explain A.C.R. The prefixes remain unexplained by any web source found.

Observation: the T.I.C.0890 format — if “0890” encodes August 1990 (08 = month, 90 = year) — would align with the 1990 release date. This is speculative. A.C.R. 01 being numbered “01” suggests it was intended as the first in a potential series that never continued.

Lars Hollmer connection

The Vivonzeureux blogger (who was personally connected to the Reims scene) states directly: “AAA avait notamment organisé une tournée de Von Zamla dans la Marne et les Ardennes au début des années 80” — the À l’Automne Alité association (precursor to Ayaa) organized a Von Zamla tour in the Marne and Ardennes departments in the early 1980s. [source: http://vivonzeureux.blogspot.com/2008/07/look-de-bouk-bec-et-ongles.html]

Von Zamla was the later group led by Lars Hollmer after Samla Mammas Manna. This tour — organized by the same association that became Ayaa and that hosted Look De Bouk — is the most plausible origin of the Hollmer connection: the Reims scene encountered Hollmer’s music through AAA’s concert programming, leading to Look De Bouk covering his work (“Liturgist Beat” on the 1984 cassette, and “Inte Quanta” on Bec Et Ongles).

The Vivonzeureux blogger further confirms that “Inte Quanta” is a cover of a Lars Hollmer solo composition from 1983, arranged by Look De Bouk with kazoos and processed vocals. [source: vivonzeureux.blogspot.com]

Ayaa subsequently released a Lars Hollmer solo album, Vandelmassa, in 1993/1994 — confirming that the Hollmer–Ayaa relationship was durable and extended beyond Look De Bouk’s own catalog. [source: Web search result citing Cuneiform Records biography data]

Additional context from web sources: Von Zamla played a notable concert at the Maison de la Culture in Reims, France, during their touring period (early-to-mid 1980s). This directly places Hollmer in Reims under AAA/Ayaa auspices. [source: web search result drawing on Von Zamla biography]

Jean-François Pauvros — role on …Avec Des Coussins Bleus

Multiple web sources confirm: Jean-François Pauvros played electric guitar on a track titled “Dunoiserie” (track B4 on the alternate pressing). This track was recorded separately from the live recordings: the main cassette captures live performances from Saint-Étienne and Besançon in 1987, while “Dunoiserie” was recorded in 1988 at Théâtre Dunois in Paris. The track title is a reference to the venue. [sources: https://adnrecords.com/album/look-de-bouk-avec-des-coussins-bleus/, https://www.soundohm.com/product/avec-des-coussins-bleus-l]

Pauvros (born 1947, Hautmont, France) is an established figure in French experimental and improvised music — associated with the NATO label and collaborators including Jac Berrocal, Fred Frith, George Lewis, and Rhys Chatham. His appearance on one track of the alternate pressing represents a single-session guest contribution, not an ongoing collaboration with the band.

The rythmes-croises.org review of a later Look De Bouk release (Le Monde entier moins le monde entier sans vous) also lists Pauvros as a guest there (playing “mandoline électrique”), confirming the connection extended beyond the 1988 session. [source: https://rythmes-croises.org/look-de-bouk-le-monde-entier-moins-le-monde-entier-sans-vous/]

“André Malraux” pseudonym

No web source identifies who performed under this name on Lacrimae Rerum. The credit is noted in passing by fan blog posts but none speculate on or document the identity. The name is clearly a joke/homage to the French writer and politician André Malraux (1901–1976), who was also notable for his writings on art and silence (Les Voix du Silence). The use of a deceased cultural figure’s name as a vocal credit is consistent with Look De Bouk’s broader playfulness with titles and wordplay. Identity remains unknown.

L’âcre Hymne Aérait Rome — anagram question

Letter analysis of the title (stripping the article “L’” and all accents) yields 20 letters: a, a, a, c, e, e, e, e, h, i, l, m, m, n, o, r, r, r, t, y. This does not match:

  • “Look De Bouk” (10 letters, entirely different set)
  • Lacrimae Rerum (13 letters)
  • Any member name tested

No web source confirms or documents what (if anything) the title is an anagram of. The vault’s existing note that “the title is an anagram” appears to be an assumption rather than a documented fact. The title may be designed to evoke “lachrymae” (tears, echoing Lacrimae Rerum), “hymne” (hymn), and “Rome” as a set of resonant words rather than a clean anagram of a specific phrase.
