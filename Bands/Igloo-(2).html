Overview

Igloo (2) is an experimental duo comprising Adam Pierce (of Mice Parade) and Doro Tachler (German composer and violinist based in New York). The project produced a single self-titled album, released in 2003 on After Hours in Japan (catalog AH-046) and co-credited to Bubble Core Records — Pierce’s own Port Chester, NY label. Both members share sole credit as Written-By and Recorded By, indicating the album is a true co-composition and co-production with no producer or engineer listed separately.

Discogs classifies the record under the genres Electronic and Rock, with styles Experimental, Indie Rock, and Post Rock — the same general space as Mice Parade’s output at the time. No live activity, singles, EPs, or further releases under the Igloo (2) name appear in the Discogs database.

Why “Igloo (2)“?

The Discogs disambiguation “(2)” simply means there was already an entry named “Igloo” when this project was filed. “Igloo” (Discogs ID: 206437) is an entirely unrelated UK electronic artist — Jools Butterfield, who also records as 2Darc and Alan La Porte, and released on Perfect Havoc and Ragbull between 2004 and 2018. There is no musical or biographical connection to Pierce or Tachler.

Context and Connections

Adam Pierce

Adam Pierce (Discogs ID: 14704) is the founder of Bubble Core Records and the sole creative entity behind Mice Parade — which is an anagram of his name. He plays drums, guitar, vibraphone, marimba, mbira, harp, synthesizer, and various other instruments on his records, and typically engineers and mixes his own output. He had been a drummer in Swirlies and co-founder (with Dylan Cristy) of The Dylan Group before launching Mice Parade in 1998.

The Igloo album appeared in 2003, the same year as two major Mice Parade records: Focus On The Roller Coaster (FatCat) and Obrigado Saudade (Bubble Core). It follows Mokoondi (Mice Parade, FatCat, 2001), on which Tachler had played violin (tracks 7, 11, 12). The Igloo project can be read as a direct extension of that working relationship.

Doro Tachler

Doro Tachler (Discogs ID: 173673; real name Dorothea Tachler) is a German composer and violinist based in New York City. Her Discogs credits span violin session work in Germany from 1997 — notably on releases by Alles Wie Gross (on Hausmusik and the Belgian label (K-RAA-K)³) alongside Markus Acher of The Notwist — through the early 2010s. Beyond Igloo (2), her collaborative credits include additional vocals on Mice Parade’s Obrigado Saudade (2003), featured vocals on HiM’s Peoples (2005), violin on Dis-ka’s We Only Have Music (Echokammer, 2005), and a 2011 7” split with Japanese group Chimp Beams on Concent Productions. She also leads or participates in Hunki Dori (2) (one release, Rêverie, 2021) and My Favourite Things (2).

Discogs credits Tachler as Written-By and Recorded By on the Igloo LP, with no separate instrument credit — unlike her session appearances on Mice Parade records where she is credited specifically as a violinist. Whether violin appears on the Igloo album, and what other instruments or approaches Tachler brought to the co-recordings, cannot be determined from Discogs metadata alone.

Label Context

After Hours (Discogs ID: 5842) is a Tokyo-based Japanese label run by the “After Hours” magazine, based in Setagaya. It had a long relationship with Pierce’s circle: Collaborations (Mice Parade, 2000, AH-008LP) was the first After Hours/Bubble Core co-release, and Igloo followed in 2003 as the label’s 46th catalog entry (AH-046). After Hours also released HiM (5/6 In Dub, AH-018; Peoples, AH-068; 1110, AH-084; ん, AH-104) and Kammerflimmer Kollektief (Hysteria, AH-017), as well as releases by The Notwist, Bill Callahan, and Ariel Pink’s Haunted Graffiti in later years. The Japan-only commercial release pattern for Igloo mirrors how several other Bubble Core-affiliated records reached Japanese audiences.

Bubble Core Records (Discogs ID: 1809) lists the Igloo LP as one of approximately 33 unique Discogs releases, with catalog years running from 1992 to 2022. The Igloo release appears to have no US commercial pressing — it was published via Bubble Core in name (as co-label), but only distributed as a Japan CD. This mirrors the Collaborations (2000) pattern.

Sonic Character (Discogs Metadata)

The Discogs genre/style tagging (Experimental, Indie Rock, Post Rock) places the album in the same general zone as Mice Parade’s mid-period records. Mice Parade’s Discogs profile describes that project as “electronic music without electronics” — instruments played live and recorded in one take — and that aesthetic plausibly extends to Igloo. Tachler’s background is in composed and improvised violin, while Pierce’s toolkit spans drums, guitar, vibraphone, marimba, synthesizers, and more. The track durations (several short pieces under 3 minutes, one long piece at 13:25) suggest a mix of formal and open-ended compositional approaches. Beyond genre tags and Discogs data, the album’s sonic character cannot be described without web sources or a listen.
