The Higsons

The Higsons

Aug 07, 20265 min read

The Higsons

Discogs ID: 308020
From: Norwich, England
Active: 1981–1987; reunion releases 2023
Labels: Romans In Britain Records (1981); Waap (1981–1987); Two-Tone Records (1982–1983); Fontana (1982); Upright Records (1984); R4 Records (1985); Sartorial Records (2023)

Norwich post-punk and funk band. Formed around vocalist/keyboardist Charlie Higson — later famous as TV comedian/writer (The Fast Show) and author of the Young James Bond novels. Multi-instrumentalist Terry Edwards (later of Gallon Drunk, The Blockheads) was a core member. Known for an energetic, brass-inflected funk-punk hybrid. Signed briefly to Two-Tone Records, where their second single was produced by Jerry Dammers.

History

Origins and Waap/Romans In Britain (1981)

Formed in Norwich, 1981. Debut single: “I Don’t Want To Live With Monkeys!” b/w “Insect Love” (Romans In Britain Records HIG 2, 1981). Lineup: Switch (21) (Charlie Higson) on vocals, Bilko (Colin Williams) on bass, Stuart McGeachin guitar, Simon Charterton drums, Terry Edwards handling “everything else.” Sleeve design by René Parapap (another Higson alias). Early recordings circulated in Europe via a Netherlands bootleg/promo pressing.

The band ran their own imprint, Waap, which became the primary vehicle for early and late-career material. “Conspiracy” and “It Goes Waap” appeared on Waap in 1982.

Two-Tone Records (1982–1983)

Signed to Two-Tone Records for two releases. “Tear The Whole Thing Down” b/w “Ylang Ylang” (CHS TT 21, 1982) was produced by Jerry Dammers (Two-Tone founder, The Specials); engineer John A. Rivers; lacquer cut Graeme Durham. A classic lineup: Colin Williams (bass/vox), Simon Charterton (drums/vox), Stuart McGeachin (guitar/vox), Terry Edwards (guitar, brass, vox), Switch/Higson (vocals). An unusually funky signing for the label.

Second Two-Tone release: “Run Me Down” 12” (CHS 12TT 24, 1983). Co-produced by the band and Warne Livesey. Three tracks: “Run Me Down (Long Version)” (7:22), “Run Me Down (Instrumental),” “Put The Punk Back Into Funk (Parts I & II).”

Alongside the Fontana single “Do It Today / Touchdown” (1982), which was co-released with Maximum Joy.

The Curse Of The Higsons and Upright Records (1984)

Debut and only studio LP: The Curse Of The Higsons (Upright Records UPLP 6, 1984). Produced by Warne Livesey; mixed by Alex Burak; lacquer cut by Porky (5) (George Peckham). Eleven tracks; notable guest contributions: Susie Honeyman (The Mekons) on strings, Tessa Niles on guest vocals, Danny White / Pete Saunders / Steve Borowski on guest keyboards.

Tracklist: “Where Have All The Club-A-Go-Gos Went Went?,” “The Junk Keeps Piling Up,” “Ice-Age,” “Heat,” “Push Out The Boat,” “One World,” “Music To Watch Girls By” (cover — written by Sid Ramin and Tony Velona), “Gangway,” “Born Blind,” “Annie And Billy,” “Run Me Down.”

A single from the sessions, “Music To Watch Boys By” (Upright Records, 1984), appeared separately and charted. “Push Out The Boat” was also issued as a Waap single in 1983, produced by Warne Livesey, with PAG credits.

Final Years and Attack Of The Cannibal Zombie Businessmen (1985–1987)

“Take It” appeared on R4 Records in 1985. The band’s final release was the retrospective/compilation LP Attack Of The Cannibal Zombie Businessmen (Waap LP 1, 1987) — collecting early Waap-era material (Side A: Joe Bull engineer, Stuart McGeachin lineup) plus later recordings (Side B: Paul Gadd engineer, Dave Cummings back on guitar, Vic Maile producing). Guest sax on Side B from Phil Veacock and Tommy Barlow; Ian Watson on trumpet. “Them Changes” credited to Buddy Miles.

2023 Revival

June 1, 1984 (Sartorial Records, 2023) — a digital FLAC album of previously unissued live recordings from June 1984. Ten tracks including reworked versions of “Born Blind,” “Run Me Down,” “Heat,” “Gangway,” and “I Don’t Want To Live With Monkeys.” Also in 2023, Sartorial released Run Me Down (The Complete 2-Tone Recordings) on vinyl for Record Store Day, compiling the Two-Tone singles.
