Wilde Flowers

Discogs ID: 340389 Also known as: The Wilde Flowers Active: 1964–1969 Origin: Canterbury, Kent, UK Genres: Beat, R&B, Psychedelic Rock

Psychedelic beat group from Canterbury, Kent, UK, formed in 1964. The Wilde Flowers never released any material during their active years, but they are widely regarded as the originating cell of the Canterbury scene: when the group dissolved in 1969, its members went on to found Soft Machine and Caravan, the two central bands of that tradition, from which nearly the entire Canterbury family tree descends.

History

Formation (1964)

The Wilde Flowers coalesced in Canterbury around 1964, initially drawing on a local beat-group circuit. The core founding lineup brought together Brian Hopper (lead guitar, saxophone) and his younger brother Hugh Hopper (bass), alongside Robert Wyatt (drums, vocals) and Kevin Ayers (vocals). Richard Sinclair (rhythm guitar, vocals) and vocalist Graham Flight also featured in the early rotating lineup.

The group took their name partly as an ironic gesture — “Wilde” nodding toward Oscar Wilde — and performed covers of American R&B and blues alongside originals written mainly by Hugh Hopper and Brian Hopper.

Shifting Lineups (1965–1967)

The Wilde Flowers were defined by an unusually fluid personnel. No single classic lineup persisted for long; members came and went as parallel projects and personal trajectories pulled them in different directions. Key additions in the mid-period include:

  • Pye Hastings (rhythm guitar, vocals; later to found Caravan)
  • Richard Coughlan (drums; later Caravan)
  • David Sinclair (keyboards; listed on Discogs as a member, Richard Sinclair’s cousin, later Caravan)

Daevid Allen, the Australian avant-garde figure later central to Gong, was also loosely associated with the Canterbury circle during this period, though his Discogs entry for the Wilde Flowers does not appear in the confirmed release credits.

By 1966–67, Mike Ratledge — later the keyboard anchor of Soft Machine — was orbiting the same Canterbury milieu and appears on the archival compilations playing flute and organ on two tracks (“The Big Show” and “Memories”), documenting his early connection to this circle.

Break-Up and Aftermath (1967–1969)

The group effectively dissolved by around 1967–68, with the final formal end around 1969. The split ran along clear lines:

  • Soft Machine stream: Robert Wyatt, Kevin Ayers, Hugh Hopper, and Mike Ratledge formed Soft Machine, which became one of the defining psychedelic and jazz-rock bands of the late 1960s and early 1970s.
  • Caravan stream: Pye Hastings, Richard Sinclair, David Sinclair, and Richard Coughlan formed Caravan, the more melodic, pastoral Canterbury strand.
  • Brian Hopper stayed closer to Canterbury, later appearing in Zobe (tracks 17–19 on the archival CD) and continuing to write; his solo arc was quieter than his former bandmates’.

Canterbury Scene Lineage

The Wilde Flowers are the single source node for the Canterbury scene’s two main branches. The lineage from a single mid-1960s beat group to a family of internationally recognized progressive and jazz-rock bands is unusually direct:

Wilde Flowers (1964–1969)
│
├── Soft Machine (1966–) ← Wyatt, Kevin Ayers, Hugh Hopper, Ratledge
│   ├── Matching Mole ← Wyatt
│   ├── Kevin Ayers And The Whole World ← Ayers
│   └── (later members branched into Hatfield And The North, National Health, etc.)
│
└── Caravan (1968–) ← Pye Hastings, Richard Sinclair, David Sinclair, Richard Coughlan
    └── Hatfield And The North ← Richard Sinclair
        └── National Health

Daevid Allen, also from the Canterbury milieu though not documented on the Wilde Flowers release itself, founded Gong after being refused re-entry to the UK in 1967.

Discography

The Wilde Flowers released no material during their active years. All recordings are archival, captured on home tape or demo equipment in the mid-1960s.

Tales Of Canterbury — Track-Level Personnel

The 1994 Voiceprint CD is the definitive document. It compiles 22 tracks: 16 Wilde Flowers recordings (various lineups, 1964–67) and three tracks by Zobe, a later Brian Hopper group that had no Wilde Flowers members besides him.

Key Collaborators / Connections

  • Soft Machine — direct successor band, founded by Wyatt, Ayers, Hugh Hopper, Ratledge
  • Caravan — direct successor band, founded by Pye Hastings, Richard Sinclair, David Sinclair, Coughlan
  • Matching Mole — Robert Wyatt’s post-Soft Machine group
  • Gong — Daevid Allen’s French-based group; parallel Canterbury diaspora
  • Hatfield And The North — second-generation Canterbury, featuring Richard Sinclair
  • National Health — third-generation Canterbury
  • Gilgamesh (2) — Hugh Hopper connection
  • Soft Heap — Hugh Hopper / Alan Gowen group
  • Voiceprint — UK archival label that issued the sole Wilde Flowers release (1994)
