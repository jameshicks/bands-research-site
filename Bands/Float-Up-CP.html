Float Up CP

Post-punk / funk band. UK. Discogs ID: 309466. Active 1984–1986. Also written as “Float Up C.P.” and “Float Up C·P”.

Direct successor to Rip Rig & Panic, formed by the same core personnel after Rip Rig & Panic’s dissolution in 1983/84. Released on Rough Trade Records. Released one single and one album before amicably dissolving around 1986; members then briefly formed God Mother & Country (Discogs: 996598) before Neneh Cherry pursued a major solo career with her debut Raw Like Sushi (1989).

According to a Neneh Cherry interview quoted in Wikipedia (via Spin, 1986), the band’s end was gradual: “Everyone needed to go and do their own thing. I don’t remember us splitting up, but there was an overspill into another overspill.”

Context and History

Float Up CP formed in 1984 from the core of Rip Rig & Panic following that band’s dissolution. The key personnel change from Rip Rig & Panic was the departure of pianist/keyboardist Mark Springer (3) and the addition of saxophonist Ollie Moore (from Pigbag). The band retained the same songwriting credit (all songs by Gareth Sager) and same self-production approach (Oliver and Sager as producers).

The Kill Me in the Morning album was recorded at Terminal 24 Studios, Almeida Street, London. Discogs classifies both releases as Experimental within Rock and Electronic. Wikipedia categorizes the band as post-punk.

After Float Up CP dissolved, several members reunited briefly as God Mother & Country (1985, Kaz Records), releasing the single “Foot On The Rock” (Kaz Records KAZ 60, 1985). God Mother & Country members per Discogs included Bruce Smith, Neneh Cherry, Dave Wright (2), and a larger ensemble including Sean Oliver, David Defries, Dave Flash Wright, Ashley Slater, and the vocal group Afrodiziak.

Connections

  • Rip Rig & Panic — direct predecessor; same core lineup (Sager, Smith, Cherry, Oliver); only change was Ollie Moore replacing Mark Springer (3)
  • Gareth Sager — primary songwriter and co-producer; continued to The Nectarine No. 9, Head (6), and solo projects
  • Bruce Smith — drummer; ex-The Pop Group, The Slits, New Age Steppers; later Public Image Limited
  • Neneh Cherry — vocalist; went on to major solo career (Raw Like Sushi, 1989); later collaborations with The Thing (2) and Four Tet
  • Sean Oliver — bassist and co-producer; died March 1990 of sickle-cell anaemia, aged 27; co-wrote Terence Trent D’Arby’s “Wishing Well”
  • Ollie Moore — saxophonist; ex-Pigbag (Y Records)
  • God Mother & Country — immediate successor project, 1985; Bruce Smith, Neneh Cherry, Sean Oliver, Dave Wright (2)
  • Rough Trade — label for both releases; large catalog (10,000+ releases), parent label Beggars Group
  • Dennis Bovell — co-produced one track on Kill Me In The Morning (A2); dub/reggae producer and Matumbi leader
  • Neville Brody — designed Joy’s Address sleeve; notable graphic designer
