Dianogah

Dianogah

Oct 07, 20263 min read

Dianogah

Discogs ID: 72116
Origin: Chicago, Illinois
Active: c.1995–2008
Genres: Post-rock, math rock

Chicago post-rock duo (two bassists + drummer). 13 releases on Southern Records 2000–2008.

Two-Bass Sonic Approach

Formation of the lineup

The dual-bass setup was not premeditated. Jay Ryan and Jason Harvey simply played bass — it was the only instrument either knew seriously when they began playing together in early 1995. They initially had a guitarist, but he left after drummer Kip McCabe’s first practice, leaving the bass-bass-drums trio intact. (ALARM Magazine, 2011)

How the two basses interacted

Critics consistently described the two bass lines as interwoven rather than split into a strict melodic-vs.-low-end division. AllMusic wrote of their debut As Seen from Above (1997): “Jason Harvey and Jay Ryan’s dexterous bass work interweaves perfectly with Kip McCabe’s precision drumming almost as if the three instruments were one.” The AV Club described their approach as a “graceful knack for winding together the highs and lows of two four-strings,” suggesting the two players did occupy different registers — one working higher melodic territory, one anchoring the low end — but that the effect was of a unified voice rather than counterpoint. (AllMusic and AV Club quotes via Wikipedia: As Seen from Above and AV Club interview)

The Chicago Reader compared their intricate arrangements to guitar-driven bands: the Minutemen, Slovenly, Slint, and Silkworm — framing one bass as effectively filling the role a lead guitar would occupy in a conventional band. (Wikipedia: As Seen from Above, as of 2026-10-07)

The tonal gap problem and how they addressed it

McCabe acknowledged the fundamental challenge directly: “We’re a bit challenged tonally, in that we have two basses and drums, and there’s a lot of room.” The band addressed this in two ways: (1) by bringing in guests to fill upper frequencies — Andrew Bird contributed violin on some recordings, and Stephanie Morris added vocals and keyboards; (2) over time, through increasing use of effects. (ALARM Magazine, 2011)

Effects and sonic evolution

For their 2008 album Qhnnnl (produced by John McEntire), Dianogah used distortion pedals for the first time. Ryan: “We bought distortion pedals and used them. We had never used distortion pedals on Dianogah songs until this record.” The shift was influenced by Meshuggah’s “really unusual, sharp bass sounds” and a growing interest in heavier material including the Melvins. Ryan stated they “got into Meshuggah a lot” and the band’s “musical tastes have continued to develop and have tended towards some heavier stuff.” (AV Club interview; ALARM Magazine, 2011)

Their debut As Seen from Above was recorded by Steve Albini.

Philosophy

The band operated under a motto of “nothing is too stupid” and consciously aimed to bring levity to a genre often considered self-serious — trying to make things feel “a little lighter and more whimsical.” McCabe summarized the duo’s achievement from the drummer’s perspective: “It seemed impossible that there’d be enough high-end, low-end to make it work, but you guys did.” (ALARM Magazine, 2011)
