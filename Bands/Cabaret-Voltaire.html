Named after the Cabaret Voltaire, the Zurich nightclub that was the centre of the Dada movement in 1916. The name was a statement of intent.

History

1973–1978: Pre-release experiments

Formed Sheffield 1973. Early work was Dada-influenced performance art and tape experimentation — predating punk entirely. First live performance: 13 May 1975. Mallinder was hospitalised with a chipped backbone after the audience threw objects at them.

Championed early by Sheffield punk fanzine Gunrubber (edited by Paul Bower of local band 2.3) and by Adi Newton of The Future / Clock DVA. The Sheffield electronic underground was a tight ecosystem.

Self-released cassette (Cabvolt Publications Ltd., 1978) documented early work before any label deal. Triple album Methodology (Mute, 2002) eventually compiled these experiments.

1978–1982: Rough Trade years

Signed to Rough Trade 1978. First release: Extended Play EP (RT 003, November 1978). Followed by the “Nag Nag Nag” single (RT 018, 1979) — their breakthrough, co-released via Celluloid in France.

Key Rough Trade LPs:

  • Mix-Up (1979)
  • The Voice of America (1980)
  • Red Mecca (1981) — widely considered their Rough Trade peak
  • 2x45 (1982)

Also released 1974–1976 on Industrial Records (1980) — cassette documenting early experiments, situating them in the Throbbing Gristle orbit.

Chris Watson left late 1981 for a job in television. He subsequently co-founded The Hafler Trio with Andrew M. McKenzie, then built a career as a BBC field recordist (Natural History Unit, Attenborough documentaries) with an extensive solo catalogue on Touch.

Toured Europe, Japan and America without major label support. Hai! (Rough Trade, 1982) captured a live Japan recording.

1983–1994: Virgin / Some Bizzare commercial turn

Conscious decision in 1983 to pursue accessibility. The Crackdown (Some Bizzare / Virgin TCV 1, 1983) reached UK #31 — over 60 places above any previous chart position. Industrial music with pop ambition.

Micro Phonies (Virgin, 1984): singles “Sensoria” and “James Brown” charted on the UK Indie Chart and crossed into the underground dance scene. The bridge between post-punk and what became UK dance music.

Subsequent albums: The Covenant, The Sword and the Arm of the Lord (Some Bizzare / Virgin, 1985), Code (EMI/Parlophone, 1987), Groovy, Laidback & Nasty (Parlophone, 1990), Body and Soul (Les Disques du Crépuscule, 1991 — last Mallinder vocal album).

1992–1994: fully instrumental releases on Plastex / Instinct / Apollo as the CV name gradually became a Kirk solo vehicle.

2014–2021: Kirk solo revival

Kirk revived the Cabaret Voltaire name for a solo performance at the Atonal Festival, Berlin, 2014. Played new material only. Extensive European live activity followed. Shadow of Fear LP (Mute, November 2020) was the last release.

Richard H. Kirk died 21 September 2021.

2025–: Mallinder + Watson

25 October 2025: Stephen Mallinder and Chris Watson reactivated Cabaret Voltaire for an exclusive show at the Forge Warehouse, Sheffield. A four-date UK tour followed in November 2025. A final tour for 2026 announced, ending at The Octagon Centre, Sheffield on 25 October 2026.

Solo / Side Projects

Richard H. Kirk

Solo work as himself, plus aliases Sandoz, Electronic Eye, Sweet Exorcist (with DJ Parrot — bleep techno, early Warp Records), XON, Acid Horse:

  • Disposable Half-Truths (Industrial Records IRC 34, 1980)
  • Time High Fiction (Doublevision DVR 2, 1983)
  • Black Jesus Voice (Rough Trade ROUGH 99, 1986)
  • Virtual State (Warp WARP CD19, 1994)
  • The Number of Magic (Warp WARP CD32, 1995)

Sweet Exorcist was a key early Warp release, bridging Sheffield industrialism and bleep-and-bass.

Stephen Mallinder

  • “Temperature Drop / Cool Down” 7” (Fetish Records, 1981)
  • Pow-Wow (Fetish Records, 1982)
  • Ku-Ling Bros. (electronic project)
  • Sassi & Loco (Perth, Australia)
  • Wrangler (with Benge and Phil Winter)
  • Creep Show (with John Grant + Wrangler)
  • Programming / synths: Shaun Ryder’s Amateur Night in the Big Top

Chris Watson

Left 1981 → The Hafler Trio (with Andrew M. McKenzie) → BBC Natural History Unit → prolific solo catalogue on Touch Records (field recording, wildlife sound). Releases include Stepping Into The Dark (Touch, 1996), Weather Report (Touch, 2003), El Tren Fantasma (Touch, 2011).

Western Works

Cabaret Voltaire built and operated their own studio — The Western Works, Sheffield. Used for their own recordings and extended to other artists:

  • 23 Skidoo — Mallinder and Kirk co-produced “Last Words” / “Gospel Comes to New Guinea” 12” here; one of the most direct Sheffield↔London connections in this network

Compilation Appearances

  • Punk 45 Vol. 3 - Sick On You! (Soul Jazz Records, 2014) — “Makes Your Mouth Go Funny” (4:40); early recording featuring all three original members (Kirk, Mallinder, Watson).

Connections to This Network

  • 23 Skidoo — Western Works production connection; both on Illuminated Records
  • Illuminated Records — released CV material alongside 23 Skidoo and Normil Hawaiians; three-way label overlap
  • Stephen Mallinder — solo work on Fetish Records (same label as Throbbing Gristle adjacent acts)
  • Sheffield scene — Clock DVA (Adi Newton was early champion), 2.3 (Paul Bower / Gunrubber)
  • Rough Trade — shared label with Scritti Politti, Young Marble Giants, The Pop Group, The Fall in the same period
  • Warp Records — Kirk’s Sweet Exorcist was a founding release; connects to the Sheffield electronic music continuum
