Kid Creole And The Coconuts

Theatrical Latin/Caribbean/funk revue led by August Darnell (Thomas August Darnell Browder), active from 1980. Co-performer and creative anchor: Coati Mundi (Andy Hernandez, Discogs ID 73414). Darnell’s persona as “Kid Creole” — a Latin bon vivant invented for the stage — drew consciously on Cab Calloway. The group’s elaborate costumes, narrative songwriting, and multi-vocalist stage revue format distinguished them from the ZE Records punk-dance axis they otherwise inhabited. The Coconuts were a shifting cast of female vocalists and instrumentalists.

Overview

Kid Creole And The Coconuts formed in New York City in 1980, growing directly out of Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band (1976–1979), in which August Darnell (bass, lyrics) and Coati Mundi (vibraphone, percussion) had both participated alongside Darnell’s brother Stony Browder. Where Dr. Buzzard’s mined retro big-band disco on RCA Victor, Kid Creole repositioned the same sensibility toward Latin pop, Caribbean rhythms, and New York funk, with a stronger theatrical and narrative dimension.

The debut album Off The Coast Of Me (Antilles/ZE, 1980) established the template: Latin orchestrations, deadpan storytelling vocals, elaborate Coconuts chorus-girl backing. It was critically admired but not commercially successful in the US. The second LP, Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places (Island/ZE, 1981), was conceived as a theatrical concept album tied to a New York Public Theater stage production. International breakthrough came with Tropical Gangsters (ZE, 1982; US title Wise Guy), which reached #3 in the UK and generated three UK Top 10 singles: “Stool Pigeon,” “Annie, I’m Not Your Daddy,” and “I’m A Wonderful Thing, Baby.” The album also featured special guest Cory Daye, the former lead vocalist of Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band, nodding to the group’s genealogy.

After moving from Island/ZE to Sire for Doppelganger (1983) and In Praise Of Older Women… And Other Crimes (1985), the band shifted again to Sire/CBS for the late-1980s albums, then Columbia for the 1991–1993 period. A 2021 release (Nothin’ Left But The Rest, 2C2C) shows the project continuing under Darnell. Julian McBrowne was the primary engineer after the Blank-Frondelli era.

The group is the largest single catalog on ZE Records with 269 releases in the Discogs database.

Yogi Horton note

Yogi Horton (Discogs ID 238357) had one of the most extensive independent session careers of any Coconuts-era musician. Credits include: Luther Vandross (Never Too Much, Don’t Want To Be A Fool), Aretha Franklin (Get It Right, Jumpin’ Jack Flash), the B-52s (Mesopotamia, Bouncing Off The Satellites), Chic-adjacent sessions, John Lennon (Milk And Honey, 1984), Diana Ross (Silk Electric), Cyndi Lauper, Jerry Harrison, and the original cast recording of The Catherine Wheel (David Byrne, Sire, 1981). Appears on Fresh Fruit In Foreign Places and Tropical Gangsters as a featured cast member.

Winston Grennan note

Winston Grennan (Discogs ID 376630) had a substantial reggae session career predating his Kid Creole work: credits include Tommy McCook, Paul Simon (Columbia, 1977), and dozens of early reggae and ska compilations. He also produced and arranged his own LP, Winston Grennan’s Big Apple Reggae (D.J. Nap Production Inc., 1982). On Kid Creole: drums on Off The Coast Of Me (1980), Fresh Fruit (1981), and Tropical Gangsters (1982).

Roni Griffith note

Roni Griffith (Discogs ID 30979) had an independent solo career on Vanguard: Mondo Man (1980), Desire (1981), self-titled LP Roni Griffith (1982), and singles including “(The Best Part Of) Breakin’ Up” (UK pressing 1982). All produced by Bob Blank. Her connection to the Coconuts was through the ZE/Vanguard downtown network.

Fonda Rae note

Fonda Rae (Discogs ID 13811) had a parallel career on Vanguard/Posse/Streetwave: “Over Like A Fat Rat” (Vanguard, 1982), Heobah (1983), Tuch Me (Streetwave, 1984), Alternations (Nuphonic, 2001). Active as a solo dance artist into the 2000s.

Web sources

  • Darnell/Coati Mundi cousin claim — web research note (2026-07-06): Discogs lists Coati Mundi as Darnell’s cousin.
