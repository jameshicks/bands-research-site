Disappears

Discogs ID: 1323033
Origin: Chicago, Illinois
Active: 2008–2015
Genres: Post-punk, noise rock, krautrock
Labels: Kranky (2010–2015), Maple Death Records, Sonic Cathedral

American indie rock / noise rock band formed in Chicago in 2008. Led by vocalist and guitarist Brian Case, Disappears pursued a motorik, repetition-heavy sound rooted in Neu!, Sonic Youth, and German kosmische musik. The band cycled through several lineups but maintained a core quartet across most of its catalog. Steve Shelley (Sonic Youth) drummed on the first two Kranky albums. Producer John Congleton shaped the latter half of their studio output.

Biography

Formation and Self-Released Debut (2008)

Disappears formed in Chicago in 2008 around vocalist/guitarist Brian Case (previously of 90 Day Men and The Ponys). The founding lineup consisted of Case on guitar and vocals, Jonathan van Herik on guitar, Damon Carruesco on bass, and Graeme Gibson on drums. That year the band self-released a debut EP titled 2008 on their own imprint, with Gibson engineering and mixing. The record established their template: locked grooves, motorik pulse, and sheets of guitar noise.

Kranky Debut and Steve Shelley Era (2010–2011)

For the move to Kranky, Disappears enlisted Steve Shelley (drummer of Sonic Youth) to replace Gibson on drums. Gibson shifted to engineering duties. This lineup — Case, Van Herik, Carruesco, and Shelley — recorded Lux (2010) and Guider (2011), both on Kranky. Lux was engineered by Gibson and mastered by Jason Ward. Guider was produced by Gibson and mastered by Ward. The Shelley association drew obvious comparisons to Sonic Youth’s more abrasive work and to Neu!‘s krautrock rhythms.

Shelley’s participation gave the band a degree of visibility in the post-punk / noise rock press. He was joined in the Disappears orbit through the group LSS, which also involved Case, Van Herik, Carruesco, and Gibson.

Pre Language and John Congleton (2012)

Pre Language (2012, Kranky) marked a creative shift toward tighter song structures and brought in producer/mixer John Congleton (known for his work with The Paper Chase and later a wide range of indie rock artists). Congleton co-produced the album with the band. Engineer Aaron Mullan and assistant engineer Bentley Anderson also contributed. Noah Leger replaced Shelley on drums for this record and became the band’s permanent drummer.

The Pre Language lineup — Case, Van Herik, Carruesco, Leger — was the most stable version of the band and recorded the next two studio albums.

Era (2013)

Era (2013, Kranky) was produced solely by John Congleton and featured the same core four-piece. The album continued in the motorik vein while incorporating more spacious arrangements.

Irreal and Final Period (2015)

Irreal (2015, Kranky) was again produced by John Congleton. It was the band’s final full-length studio release on Kranky and appeared after a period of reduced activity.

Also in 2015, two live documents were released: Low: Live In Chicago on Maple Death Records and Low: Live In London on Sonic Cathedral. A 2016 tribute compilation Kraftwerk? Ja Bitte! on Sonic Cathedral included a Disappears contribution. The band effectively wound down around this period, with Case, Van Herik, Carruesco, and Leger later forming Facs (2) (without Shelley).

Connections

  • Sonic Youth — Steve Shelley brought Sonic Youth’s drumming aesthetic directly into Disappears’ rhythm section
  • Neu! — primary stylistic touchstone; motorik pulse runs throughout the catalog
  • 90 Day Men — Brian Case’s previous band, also Chicago-based post-rock/experimental rock
  • The Ponys — Brian Case was also a member
  • Facs (2) — successor project formed by Case, Van Herik, Carruesco, and Leger after Disappears wound down
  • Kranky — primary label for studio output 2010–2015; Kranky’s Chicago base made it a natural home
  • John Congleton — key production figure from Pre Language onward; also worked extensively with The Paper Chase, Swans, and many others
  • LSS — related project involving Case, Van Herik, Carruesco, Gibson, and Shelley

Open Questions
