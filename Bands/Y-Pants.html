Y Pants

All-female No Wave / minimalist trio, New York City, active 1979–1982. Discogs artist ID: 258499.

Biography

Y Pants formed in downtown Manhattan around 1979, bringing together three women from the city’s overlapping art, film, and photography communities: Barbara Ess (photographer/musician), Verge Piersol (visual artist, born Virginia Piersol), and Gail Vachon (filmmaker). The trio developed a deliberately low-tech aesthetic — toy piano, ukulele, baritone ukulele, Mickey Mouse drum kit (paper-headed), supplemented by electric bass, Casio keyboards, and low-fi effects — in deliberate rejection of the guitar-hero norms of the downtown rock scene.

Their sound sat at the intersection of No Wave minimalism, feminist poetics, and art-world conceptualism. Lyrically they favoured the domestic and the off-kilter: the perils of doing laundry (“Favorite Sweater”), materialism (“We Have Everything”), patriarchy (“That’s The Way Boys Are”), and a reworking of Bertolt Brecht’s “Barbara’s Song” from The Threepenny Opera. The novelty timbre of the toy instruments was never treated as ironic — it was load-bearing to the group’s compositional logic.

They played both gallery spaces and punk rock venues including CBGB, navigating the dual contexts of Lower Manhattan’s art world and its No Wave music scene without fully belonging to either. British contemporaries The Raincoats have been cited as a reference point for their overlapping vocal choruses and kitchen-sink approach to rhythm and composition.

Debut EP (1980): Glenn Branca produced their debut four-song 7” EP, Little Music, for 99 Records (cat: 99-03EP7). This was the third release on 99 Records, following Branca’s own “Lesson No. 1” and the Bush Tetras’ “Too Many Creeps.” The EP was issued in multiple pressings, indicating sustained collector demand.

LP (1982): Beat It Down was recorded for Neutral Records (cat: N-Two) — the label Glenn Branca had founded after departing 99 Records. Wharton Tiers produced. The album expanded the group’s palette and range, with tracks spanning Brecht adaptations, an Emily Dickinson poem set to music (“The Fly”), and a cover of the girl-group standard “That’s The Way Boys Are.” Novelist and critic Lynne Tillman wrote the lyrics for “Obvious.” Engineer on the album was Naux (Juan Maciel); mix engineer was Tom Gartland.

Dissolution: Y Pants disbanded shortly after the LP’s release in 1982. According to Discogs profile lore, the members reportedly reunited informally on each other’s birthdays.

Reissues: A 1998 CD compilation on Periodic Document (PD001) collected the complete EP and LP plus one track from Tellus Audio Cassette Magazine and an unreleased piece. In 2017, Water Wing Records issued a 12” EP (WW17) compiling the six EP-era tracks, remastered by Robert Weston (Bob Weston). This reissue used the original engineering credits — Paavo Rowe (engineer), Alan Fierstein (mixing engineer) — indicating access to first-generation materials.
