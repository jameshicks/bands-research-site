David Thomas And The Pedestrians

Discogs ID: 241754 Alias: David Thomas And His Legs (1145120) Active: 1981–1985 (three albums) Labels: Rough Trade (1981, 1985); Plurex Records (1983, Benelux only)

Rotating ensemble organized by David Thomas (2), vocalist of Pere Ubu, across Pere Ubu’s various hiatuses in the early 1980s. No fixed lineup — each album assembled a different set of players, drawing from the Cleveland post-punk circle, British folk rock, and the Henry Cow / RIO avant-garde.

Variations On A Theme (1983)

Label: Rough Trade ROUGH 60 (UK) / Rough Trade Deutschland RTD 9 (Germany) / Plurex PLUREX 3301 (Benelux) / Sixth International Silo2 (US) · Pressings: 6 · Master: 58800 Co-credited as: David Thomas And The Pedestrians with Richard Thompson

Label note: The Plurex pressing is the Benelux regional license, not a departure from Rough Trade. The album was released simultaneously in June 1983 across four regional labels — Rough Trade UK, Rough Trade Deutschland, Plurex Records (Benelux), and Sixth International (US, August 1983). The phonographic copyright reads “1983 David Thomas/Sixth International,” indicating Thomas’s own US-based Sixth International label held the master and licensed it to regional partners in the standard independent-label practice of the era. There is no evidence of a falling-out with Rough Trade; Rough Trade UK released both this album and the 1985 follow-up. crocusbehemoth.com review · Wikipedia, as of 2026-07-12

Richard Thompson is a full co-primary credit on this release. Chris Cutler and Lindsay Cooper (Henry Cow) appear for the first time. Self-produced by Thomas; Kidron’s involvement reduced to engineer on two tracks only.

Context

The project runs 1981–85, overlapping with and between Pere Ubu periods:

  • Pere Ubu’s Song Of The Bailing Man (Rough Trade, 1982) was produced by Adam Kidron at the same moment, with Fier and Ravenstine also present — those two LPs were effectively recorded in the same orbit.
  • Richard Thompson’s involvement is the most unexpected element: his folk-blues guitar and dulcimer sit against Thomas’s post-punk howl and Ravenstine’s electronics on the debut. How it came about is documented in David Thomas’s own words in a 2021 interview: Geoff Travis at Rough Trade suggested Thomas make a solo record with Adam Kidron producing, then asked Thomas which guitarist he would choose. Thomas named Richard Thompson. Travis arranged a meeting; Thomas recalls: “I went over between Pere Ubu shows and Richard said, ‘I don’t know what you want me to do, but I’ll be happy to try it.‘” Thomas also recruited Philip Moxham (Young Marble Giants) — Geoff Travis asked Moxham on his behalf — and asked Anton Fier to drum. Psychedelic Baby Magazine interview with David Thomas, 2021 — Note: this is David Thomas’s account; no interview has surfaced in which Richard Thompson discusses the collaboration from his own side.
  • By More Places Forever, the band sounds closer to Henry Cow’s compositional collectivism: Cooper and Cutler’s RIO aesthetic has fully absorbed the project.

Research Queue

  • What is the precise founding date and history of Sixth International? Thomas appears to have run it as his own US imprint — confirm whether it predates Variations On A Theme or was created for it.
  • Did Plurex Records (Amsterdam, 1977–1983) have an ongoing licensing relationship with Rough Trade UK, or was the Benelux license for Variations On A Theme a one-off? Plurex’s other international releases (e.g. Minny Pops) may clarify.
  • Does Richard Thompson discuss the David Thomas collaboration from his own perspective in any published interview? His 2021 memoir Beeswing covers only 1967–1975 and does not reach this period. Check long-form RT interviews from 1981–1985 press cycle or any later retrospective.
