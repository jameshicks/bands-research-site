Biography

Konono Nº1 is a Kinshasa-based ensemble from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, led by founder Mawangu Mingiedi (Discogs: Mawangu Mingiedi, ID 860921). The group plays likembé — a traditional Congolese thumb piano — through a distinctive self-built amplification system: contact microphones fashioned from magnets salvaged from car parts, feeding into rough cone speakers assembled from scrap metal and old car horns. The result is a saturated, distortion-heavy electric sound that overlays dense interlocking polyrhythms layered across multiple likembé players, tam-tam percussion, and vocals. The music draws on the traditional trance music of the Bazombo people of the DRC’s Kasai region, but the electric system transforms it into something with no direct precedent.

Mawangu Mingiedi developed the amplification technique over many years of playing in Kinshasa’s working-class neighbourhoods, where the makeshift sound system was a practical necessity — a way to be heard in outdoor markets and street gatherings without access to conventional equipment. By the time Belgian producer and ethnomusicologist Vincent Kenis encountered the group in Kinshasa in the early 2000s, Konono Nº1 had been performing in this format for decades.

Kenis — already the resident producer at Crammed Discs in Brussels, and a central figure in the Crammed world-music strand through productions for Zap Mama and Taraf de Haïdouks — recorded the group’s existing repertoire on location in Kinshasa. The recordings were released in 2004 as Congotronics (Craw 27, Crammed Discs), under the sublabel imprint Congotronics (2) (Discogs ID 397588), which Crammed created specifically for this series. The album circulated quickly through the European and US music press and among musicians interested in African traditional and experimental music, generating unusually wide attention for a field recording project. The name “Congotronics” — used for the album title, the sublabel, and the broader series — became a genre term applied to the cluster of Kinshasa electric-roots artists Kenis documented and released over the following decade.

In 2005 Kenis produced Congotronics 2 (Craw 29), a Various Artists compilation presenting several other Kinshasa ensembles alongside Konono Nº1, establishing the Congotronics series as a broader survey of the scene. The two most prominent other acts on the series became Kasai Allstars (a large collective pulling musicians from multiple DRC traditions, documented across In The 7th Moon, The Chief Turned Into A Swimming Fish And Ate The Head Of His Enemy By Magic in 2008 and Beware The Fetish in 2014) and Staff Benda Bilili (a Kinshasa group of disabled street musicians, released on Crammed 2009–2012).

The international profile of Congotronics attracted collaborators from outside the DRC. The group contributed electrified likembé to Björk’s “Earth Intruders” (2007, One Little Indian), produced by Timbaland — an unusual placement in mainstream pop production. The group also appeared on a FatCat Records split promo in 2004 and continued releasing through Crammed with Assume Crash Position (Craw 60, 2010), recorded and produced again by Kenis, and including a wider range of the group’s established personnel.

In 2016 Konono Nº1 released Konono N°1 Meets Batida (CRAM 261, Crammed Discs), a co-production with Pedro Coquenão operating as Batida (4) — an Angolan-born, Lisbon-based DJ and producer working in Angolan kuduro, batida, and electronic music. Kenis and Coquenão co-produced; the collaboration involved extensive rearrangement of Konono material around Coquenão’s productions, with guest vocalists including Selma Uamusse, Sacerdote, and AF Diaphra.

A further archival project appeared in 2019: Kinshasa 1978 (Originals and Reconstructions) (Crammed Discs), a compilation credited jointly to Konono Nº1, Orchestre Sankaï, Orchestre Bambala, and Orchestre Bana Luya, presenting field recordings from 1978 Kinshasa.
