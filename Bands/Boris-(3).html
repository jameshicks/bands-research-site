Boris (3)

Discogs ID: 267726 Active: 1992–present Origin: Tokyo, Japan Also known as: ボリス Name variations: Sirob, b Name origin: Taken from the Melvins song “Boris”

Japanese experimental rock band operating across drone, sludge, doom metal, shoegaze, noise, and ambient — often simultaneously across different releases. One of the most prolific heavy music acts in Japan, with several hundred releases. Formed 1992; self-produce nearly everything through Atsuo’s label Fangs Anal Satan.

Biography by Era

1992–1995 — Formation and Early Demos

Formed in Tokyo during the early 1990s. The name is taken from the Melvins track. Nagata drummed on the first demos. Initial recordings appeared on cassette and small-run releases through Fangs Anal Satan. Demo Vol. 1 (1993, YU-WA LABEL) and Demo Vol. 3 (1994) document this primitive sludge-and-noise phase.

1996–2001 — Drone Doom Foundation

With Nagata gone and Atsuo behind the kit, Boris settled into their classic three-piece configuration. Absolutego (1996, Fangs Anal Satan) — reportedly over 60 minutes across two tracks — established their drone/doom approach, drawing direct lineage from Melvins and Earth. Amplifier Worship (1998, Mangrove) extended the formula into more textural territory, with Wata’s E-bow and feedback-heavy guitar becoming central.

Flood (2000) pushed further into minimalist drone, a single continuous piece expanding across the full album.

2002–2005 — Breadth and the Pink Breakthrough

This era saw Boris explode in stylistic range. Heavy Rocks (2002, first version) introduced a stoner/noise rock mode; the split Megatone (2002) launched the Merzbow collaboration thread that would continue for two decades.

Akuma No Uta (2003, Diwphalanx) and Boris At Last -Feedbacker- (2003, Diwphalanx) — both produced with engineer Souichirou Nakamura — marked a maturation of their studio approach and became among the most-pressed domestic releases.

Pink (2005, Diwphalanx) became their most widely discussed album internationally: a genre-dissolving record traversing shoegaze, pop, and feedback noise. 29 pressings across multiple international editions. Its US release opened Boris to a Western rock audience.

2006–2009 — Southern Lord and the Altar Collab

Signing to Southern Lord brought Boris into the US heavy music mainstream. Altar (2006) — a full Boris/[[Bands/Sunn O)))] joint album — remains their highest-profile collab release (33 pressings). It featured an extraordinary guest roster: Kim Thayil (Soundgarden), Dylan Carlson (Earth), Jesse Sykes, Joe Preston, Bill Herzog, Phil Wandscher, Tos Nieuwenhuizen, Troy Swanson, Rex Ritter, and Steve Moore (5); recorded and mixed by Randall Dunn and Mell Dettmer.

Rainbow (2006, Pedal Records) introduced guitarist Michio Kurihara — White Heaven / Ghost — as a recurring collaborator. Smile (2008, Southern Lord) was co-produced with Kurihara; the live version Smile -Live At Wolf Creek- extends his featured role.

Statement (2008, Southern Lord) and a run of Japanese Heavy Rock Hits compilations appeared before Boris ended their Southern Lord period.

2011–2014 — Sargent House Period

Boris moved to LA-based Sargent House for a productive run: Attention Please (2011) and Heavy Rocks (2011) were released simultaneously — the former a more pop-oriented set, the latter a return to grinding noise rock. New Album (2011) continued the prolific pace. Noise (2014) — co-produced by Shinobu Narita — was among the heaviest of the Sargent House-era records.

A split with Torche (Chapter Ahead Being Fake, 2009, Daymare Recordings) and further splits with Asobi Seksu and others extended Boris’s profile across genre audiences.

2015–2019 — Third Man, Prolific Releases

EROS (2015, Fangs Anal Satan), Gensho (2016, Relapse Records) — a second Boris/Merzbow split with Kevin Shields appearing on the expanded edition — and Dear (2017, Sargent House) continued the multi-directional output. Live At Third Man Records (2017) and LΦVE & EVΦL (2019, Third Man Records) cemented a relationship with Third Man Records.

2020–present — Relapse, Sacred Bones, Return to Noise

No (2020, Fangs Anal Satan; 11 pressings) and 2R0I2P0 (2020, Relapse) — another Boris/Merzbow album — marked a pandemic-era burst of activity. W (2022, Sacred Bones Records; 14 pressings), Heavy Rocks (2022, Relapse; 14 pressings), and Bright New Disease (2023, Sacred Bones) continue into the present. Hello There (2024, Fangs Anal Satan/U-desper Records) and Twins Of Evil (2024, Amphetamine Reptile) — split with Melvins — are among the most recent catalog entries.

Open Questions

Web sources (added 2026-06-26)

Question researched: Did Wata formally leave Boris?

Answer: No. As of June 2026, Wata remains an active member of Boris.

Evidence:

  • Boris official news page (borisheavyrocks.com/news/, accessed 2026-06-26) lists 2026 European/UK tour dates and Southeast Asia dates with no mention of lineup changes or member departures.
  • March 2024 Cyclic Defrost interview with Atsuo (https://www.cyclicdefrost.com/2024/03/boris-the-mystery-of-boris-only-deepens/) describes Wata as actively performing vocals, guitar, and keyboards, with no indication of departure.
  • Guitar World feature on Wata (December 2023, https://www.guitarworld.com/features/boris-wata-bright-new-disease) focuses on her continued role in Bright New Disease (2023); headline “three decades on, her playing is still impossible to pigeonhole” signals ongoing membership.
  • Boris 2025 Pink 20th anniversary tour announcements list the band as the standard three-piece with Wata. (Songkick)
  • Wata quoted (via search results, sourced from multiple outlets): “Now that the pandemic is over, we’ve officially resumed tours and live performances… I want to perform in cities worldwide for as long as my physical strength lasts.”
  • Metal Archives entry for Wata (https://www.metal-archives.com/artists/Wata/7356) lists her as current member of Boris with no departure date.

Conclusion: The speculation that Wata had departed appears to have been unfounded. No formal announcement of departure has been made. The open question in the Research Queue is now resolved.
