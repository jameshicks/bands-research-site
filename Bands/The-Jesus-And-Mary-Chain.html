The Jesus And Mary Chain

Discogs ID: 243280 Members: Jim Reid, William Reid (core); see full lineup below Active: 1983–1999; reformed 2007–present Origin: East Kilbride, Scotland Genres: Noise Pop, Alternative Rock, Shoegaze, Dream Pop, Indie Rock Labels: Creation Records (debut single); Blanco Y Negro (1985–1998); WEA; Warner Bros. Records; Def American Recordings / American Recordings; Sub Pop; Artificial Plastic Records; Fuzz Club Records

The Jesus and Mary Chain are a Scottish noise-pop band built around the songwriting partnership of brothers Jim Reid (vocals, guitar) and William Reid (guitar, vocals). Formed in East Kilbride in 1983, they became one of the defining acts of UK alternative rock, fusing Spector-esque melody with extreme guitar feedback and drone. Bobby Gillespie drummed for the band during their debut period (1984–85), appearing on all three debut singles and the landmark debut album Psychocandy. After a decade-long run from 1984 to 1999, they dissolved following an onstage altercation between the brothers, then reformed in 2007 and have remained active into the mid-2020s.

Biography

East Kilbride Origins and the Move to London (1983–1984)

Jim Reid (born James McLeish Reid) and William Reid (born William Adam Reid) grew up in East Kilbride, a post-war new town on the outskirts of Glasgow. The brothers began playing together in the early 1980s, drawing on a range of influences — the Velvet Underground, Phil Spector, the Stooges, surf rock — and arrived at a sound that buried catchy, even pretty pop melodies under walls of feedback and tape hiss.

They moved to London around 1984, where early bassist Douglas Hart completed the core group along with drummer Murray Dalglish. Their first live shows were notorious for brevity (reportedly under 20 minutes) and chaos, with the band performing with their backs to audiences who responded with bottles and abuse. The confrontational attitude and the deliberately short, abrasive sets became part of the band’s early mythology.

Creation Records and “Upside Down” (1984)

The band were picked up by Alan McGee, who ran Creation Records out of a pub in Tottenham Court Road. Their debut single “Upside Down” / “Vegetable Man” (Creation CREATION 012, 1984) was co-produced by McGee with Joe Foster producing the B-side. The lineup on the single — Jim Reid (vocals), William Reid (guitar), Douglas Hart (bass), Murray Dalglish (drums) — established the basic sound: three-chord melodies submerged in hiss and feedback. The single sold out its pressing and became an immediate word-of-mouth phenomenon in the UK indie underground.

Blanco Y Negro and the Psychocandy Period (1985)

McGee and Creation helped broker a deal with Blanco Y Negro, a WEA sublabel run by Mike Alway. By the time the band signed, Murray Dalglish had been replaced by Bobby Gillespie, then drumming while also fronting the embryonic Primal Scream.

The Blanco Y Negro debut singles — “Never Understand” (NEG 8, 1985; Gillespie on drums), “You Trip Me Up” (NEG 13, 1985; Gillespie on drums, John Loder engineering), and “Just Like Honey” (1985; Gillespie drums, Loder engineering) — arrived in quick succession, each generating controversy and media attention disproportionate to their chart positions. The NME placed them at the center of a “shambling” or “noise pop” moment in UK indie.

The debut album Psychocandy (Blanco Y Negro BYN 7, November 1985) was self-produced by the band with John Loder engineering at Southern Studios. The lineup: Jim Reid (vocals, guitar), William Reid (guitar, vocals), Douglas Hart (bass), Bobby Gillespie (drums), with Karen Parker and Laurence Verfaillie providing backing vocals. The record distills fourteen short songs through a production method of maximum guitar noise — feedback, tremolo, tape saturation — over drum-machine-spare arrangements, while the underlying melodies remained accessible. It is widely cited as a founding text of shoegaze and a direct influence on My Bloody Valentine, Lush, Ride, and subsequent generations of alternative rock.

Gillespie departed after Psychocandy to concentrate on Primal Scream. He remained credited on the band’s membership list and the two groups maintained a long relationship within the Glasgow/London indie scene.

Some Candy Talking and the Post-Gillespie Years (1986–1987)

The “Some Candy Talking” EP (Blanco Y Negro, 1986), self-produced with John Loder engineering, was the first release without Gillespie and the band’s first UK Top 40 hit. The EP prompted controversy when BBC Radio 1 banned it, claiming (incorrectly, the band maintained) the lyric referenced heroin. The resulting press coverage accelerated the band’s mainstream profile.

Second album Darklands (Blanco Y Negro, 1987) marked a quieter, more introspective direction. Drum machines replaced a live drummer; the feedback was pulled back to reveal cleaner guitar lines and more overtly melodic songwriting. Production was split between Bill Price (tracks A1, A3, A4, B1, B3, B4) and John Loder (tracks A5, B2, B5), with Jim and William Reid credited as writers throughout. Singles “April Skies” and “Happy When It Rains” both charted, bringing the band to a wider mainstream audience. Douglas Hart remained as bassist.

Barbed Wire Kisses and Automatic (1988–1989)

Barbed Wire Kisses (1988, Blanco Y Negro/WEA) collected B-sides, rarities, and out-takes from the 1985–87 period, establishing the secondary catalog as rich enough to sustain a full album.

Automatic (Blanco Y Negro, 1989) leaned further into drum machines and electronic textures. The album was engineered and mixed by Alan Moulder (with Dick Meaney assisting mixes), who became a central production collaborator across the next decade. Jim and William Reid wrote and produced, both credited as vocalists — William taking leads on “Blues from a Gun” and “Head On,” Jim on the remainder. The drum machine dominance reflected a deliberate choice to strip the rhythm section of organic feel, pushing the electronic pulse against the guitar noise.

Honey’s Dead and Commercial Peak (1992)

Honey’s Dead (Blanco Y Negro / Def American, 1992) is often cited as the band’s most commercially successful and fully realized studio record. Steve Monti played drums on nine of the twelve tracks; Alan Moulder engineered and mixed. Flood engineered one track. The album contains “Reverence” (its opening salvo of noise and lyrical provocation), “Far Gone and Out,” and “Almost Gold.” The band toured extensively behind it and appeared on the US Lollapalooza festival circuit in 1992, reaching their largest American audiences.

The companion release The Sound of Speed (1993) compiled further B-sides and outtakes.

Stoned & Dethroned (1994)

Stoned & Dethroned (Blanco Y Negro / American Recordings, 1994) was a deliberate course change: acoustic-led, intimate, with guest vocalists throughout, most significantly Hope Sandoval of Mazzy Star (dueting on “Sometimes Always,” a UK Top 30 hit). Ben Lurie joined as a guitarist. Alan Moulder and Dick Meaney shared engineering and mixing duties; Sophie Muller directed the associated videos. The album was divisive among the band’s noise-oriented fanbase but expanded their audience among fans of quieter, more acoustic-leaning alternative rock.

Late Period: Munki and Dissolution (1995–1999)

Hate Rock ‘N’ Roll (1995, B-sides compilation) and the single “I Hate Rock ‘N’ Roll” maintained the band’s profile. Final studio album Munki (Creation Records / Sub Pop, 1998) returned to heavier arrangements, with Nick Sanderson on drums, Ben Lurie performing, and Terry Edwards providing horns. Dick Meaney recorded and mixed the bulk of the album with Alan Moulder mixing three tracks. Songs were divided between Jim and William as individual writers throughout the record. The brothers’ personal and creative relationship had deteriorated severely; they disbanded in 1999 following an onstage argument in Los Angeles.

Solo and Side Projects (1999–2006)

William Reid pursued solo work under the alias Lazycame (also spelled as Ravenscraeg and CNYK on some releases). Jim Reid formed Freeheat (Discogs ID 879123), a looser, guitar-oriented project. Linda Reid (Jim and William’s sister, also performing as Sister Vanilla) recorded and released music; she appeared on Munki.

Reunion and Damage and Joy (2007–2019)

The band reformed publicly in 2007, initially for festival appearances and the Coachella headliner slot. The reunion was driven by reconciliation between Jim and William and by sustained demand from their fanbase across multiple generations. Touring continued intermittently through the late 2000s and 2010s.

Damage and Joy (Artificial Plastic Records, 2017) was the reunion-era studio album — the first in nineteen years. Produced largely by Youth (Martin Glover), with pre-production by Dave Trumfio on one track. Lineup: Brian Young (drums), Philip King (guitar on one track). Guest vocalists included Isobel Campbell (tracks 5, 6), Sky Ferreira (track 13), Bernadette Denning (track 4), and Linda Fox (4) — the latter being Linda Reid — on tracks 7 and 14. Songs co-written by both Reid brothers throughout.

The Barrowlands Live (Demon Records, 2015) documented a celebrated hometown show, featuring the live lineup of Jim Reid, William Reid, Mark Crozer (guitar), Brian Young (drums), and others.

Glasgow Eyes and Ongoing Activity (2024–present)

Glasgow Eyes (Fuzz Club Records, 2024) is the most recent studio album. Produced by Jim Reid, William Reid, and the band, with Tony Doogan (electronics, piano, mixing) and additional recording by Duncan Chave, Dollar Bill McLeish, and George Arnold. Mastered by Pete Maher. William Reid is credited with artwork. The album is their second post-reunion studio LP.

A 2025 compilation > 45s > 84 > 85 < (Warner Music, Record Store Day) collected early singles material.
