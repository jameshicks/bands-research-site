Overview

Chic was co-founded in New York in 1977 by guitarist Nile Rodgers and bassist Bernard Edwards, and became one of the defining acts of the disco era. Their catalogue is concentrated in the late 1970s and early 1980s — 28 releases across those two decades — built almost entirely on Atlantic Records with a tight circle of recurring collaborators: Rob Sabino, Bob Clearmountain, Sammy Figueroa, and Raymond Jones appear across the bulk of their output. The production partnership of Bernard Edwards & Nile Rodgers accumulated over 1,000 shared credits, reflecting how central the duo were not just to Chic but to the wider soul and disco production landscape of the era. After a quieter stretch through the 1990s and 2000s, the group continued under Rodgers’ leadership with an expanded rotating lineup including Audrey Martells, Bill Holloman, and Curt Ramm.
