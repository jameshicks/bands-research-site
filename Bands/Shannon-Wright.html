Shannon Wright is an American singer-songwriter. She was born in Jacksonville, Florida, United States, where she spent her childhood. Former member of the band Crowsdell, Shannon Wright moved from New York to Chapel Hill, North Carolina in 1998, and while in Chapel Hill, Wright started writing songs and playing them for friends. With their encouragement she sent a 4-track cassette tape to a friend of a friend who started Overcoat Records. They released her first 7” A Tin Crown for the Social Bash. Later that year, Wright signed with Touch and Go Records in 1999 from Chicago, releasing six albums. Wright signed with Vicious Circle (France) in 2003 and continues to release records with them. In 2012, Wright signed with Ernest Jennings and released Secret Blood and In Film Sound in the States.

Overview

Shannon Wright is an American singer-songwriter and guitarist, born in Jacksonville, Florida, who rose to prominence in the mid-1990s indie rock underground as a member of Crowsdell before relocating from New York to Chapel Hill, North Carolina and launching a solo career. She signed with Touch and Go Records in 1999, releasing six albums and building a catalogue that spans indie rock, alternative rock, and acoustic songwriting. From 2003 she shifted her primary label relationship to the French imprint Vicious Circle, where the bulk of her subsequent work appeared and where she has remained active into the 2020s. Her most extensive collaboration is with guitarist and producer David Chalmin (17 shared credits), and her work in the French experimental sphere also brought her into repeated contact with Yann Tiersen, Aidan Baker, and Christine Ott.

Yann Tiersen collaboration — origin

The 2004 album Yann Tiersen & Shannon Wright was co-released on Ici D’Ailleurs (Tiersen’s home label, catalog IDA030) and Vicious Circle (2) (Wright’s home label since 2003). The dual-label release is the clearest structural evidence of how the collaboration was brokered: Tiersen was already a cornerstone Ici D’Ailleurs artist (his Le Phare, 1998, was the label’s international breakthrough), and Wright had signed with Vicious Circle the previous year. The two French independent labels appear to have facilitated the pairing. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ici,_d%27ailleurs… (as of 2026-11-20)

Wright herself, in a French-language interview, described the meeting as unplanned rather than label-engineered: “Je n’y avais pas réfléchi avant, on n’est pas allés l’un vers l’autre avec cette idée, on s’est simplement rencontrés et on a réalisé qu’on avait pas mal de points communs musicalement” (“I hadn’t thought about it beforehand, we didn’t approach each other with that idea, we simply met and realised we had quite a bit in common musically”). The interview does not specify where or when they first met. https://exitmusik.fr/interview-shannon-wright/

A separate interview (indierockmag.com) confirms the recording session was approximately 10 days long and that Wright composed vocal melodies overnight between sessions. https://www.indierockmag.com/article12692.html

Sources checked with no result on the specific meeting circumstances: Wikipedia (Shannon Wright article, Yann Tiersen & Shannon Wright album article), OndaRock monograph, She Shreds interview (fetch failed), alter1fo interviews, exitmusik second interview (Tiersen not mentioned). No source names a specific tour, festival, or third-party introduction.

Working conclusion: The collaboration arose from Wright’s 2003 integration into the French independent music circuit via Vicious Circle, which brought her into proximity with Ici D’Ailleurs and its roster. The dual-label co-release structure is consistent with an introduction brokered between the two labels, though Wright’s own account frames the collaboration as a natural personal encounter rather than a business arrangement. The specific first meeting remains unconfirmed by any source found.
