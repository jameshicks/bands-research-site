Baby (7)

American rock band. Discogs artist ID: 1246900. Active c. 1974–1977.

Overview

Baby was an American southern rock band active in the mid-1970s, based in the orbit of Clovis, New Mexico (home to Norman Petty’s famed recording studio) before moving into a larger commercial context. The band released one album on the regional Lone Starr label in 1974, produced by Norman Petty — himself a legendary figure as the producer of Buddy Holly and early rock and roll recordings at his Clovis studios — before signing to Chelsea Records for their 1976 album Where Did All The Money Go?, produced by pop-industry veteran Wes Farrell. The Chelsea period also yielded two singles. The band’s most enduring legacy is as the launching point for guitarist and vocalist Johnny Lee Schell, who would go on to become one of Los Angeles’s busiest session players and a core member of The Bump Band and The Phantom Blues Band.

The Discogs profile describes them as “American southern rock group.” Their styles are catalogued as Classic Rock, Hard Rock, and Southern Rock (debut) and Classic Rock, Southern Rock (second album).

Lone Starr (Discogs ID: 465805) is a small independent label with only 5 releases in the Discogs database. Its geographic connection to the Clovis/Texas belt and Norman Petty’s involvement place Baby firmly in that regional Southern/Southwest rock ecosystem.

Chelsea Records (Discogs ID: 39811) was a New York–based label with 870 releases in the Discogs database, a subsidiary of Chelsea Records Corp. The move from Lone Starr to Chelsea represents a step toward national distribution and a more commercial production style under Wes Farrell.
