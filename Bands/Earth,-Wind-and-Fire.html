Earth, Wind & Fire

Discogs artist ID: 22164

American soul, funk, and R&B band founded in Chicago, Illinois, in 1970 by Maurice White. One of the best-selling music acts of all time, with over 90 million units sold worldwide. Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2000. Active from 1970 to the present, with extensive touring and recording continuing under the core trio of Maurice White (until his death in 2016), Philip Bailey, and Verdine White.

Overview

Maurice White formed the group after leaving his position as a session drummer and member of the Ramsey Lewis Trio. The initial lineup — built around an Afrocentric philosophy, Egyptian mysticism, and a blend of funk, jazz, soul, and R&B — debuted on Warner Bros. Records in 1971. After two albums on Warner Bros. that were commercially modest but established the group’s spiritual-cosmic aesthetic, White re-formed the band, signed to Columbia Records, and built the most commercially successful phase of EWF’s career through the mid- to late 1970s.

The Columbia years (1972–1983) produced a string of platinum albums — Head To The Sky, Open Our Eyes, That’s The Way Of The World, Gratitude, Spirit, All ‘N All, I Am, Faces, Raise!, Powerlight — and hit singles including “Shining Star,” “September,” “Boogie Wonderland,” “After the Love Has Gone,” and “Let’s Groove.” The band’s signature sound wove together Maurice White’s Earth philosophy, Philip Bailey’s high-register falsetto, Verdine White’s melodic bass, Larry Dunn’s keyboards, Al McKay and Johnny Graham on guitars, and a large horn section. Co-producer and arranger Charles Stepney shaped the orchestral sound of the middle period albums (1974–1976) before his death in 1976.

A hiatus (1983–1987) was followed by the return album Touch the World (1987, Columbia). The band moved to Reprise Records for Millennium (1993) before returning to their own Kalimba Records imprint and later Sanctuary and Legacy. Their most recent studio album was Now, Then & Forever (2013, Legacy).

Connections

  • Maurice White — founder, principal producer, vocalist, kalimba; formerly of Ramsey Lewis Trio and The Salty Peppers
  • Philip Bailey — co-lead vocalist; also has a substantial solo discography
  • Verdine White — bass; brother of Maurice White
  • Larry Dunn — keyboards throughout the classic Columbia era
  • Charles Stepney — co-producer and arranger, 1974–1976; died June 1976 during Spirit sessions; previously defined the Cadet / Chess Records sound with Rotary Connection and Minnie Riperton
  • David Foster — keyboards and string arranger on I Am (1979); went on to major pop production career
  • Rotary Connection — Charles Stepney’s other major project; Minnie Riperton’s group
  • Paulinho Da Costa — percussionist; appeared on EWF sessions from All ‘N All (1977) through The Promise (2003)
  • Jerry Hey — recurring horn arranger and trumpeter; appeared from I Am (1979) onward
  • Dorothy Ashby — harpist on All ‘N All, Spirit, I Am; notable jazz harpist
  • Eumir Deodato — horn/string arranger on All ‘N All; Brazilian arranger/producer
  • Steve Lukather — session guitarist on I Am; member of Toto
  • Zakir Hussain — tabla on Powerlight (1983)
