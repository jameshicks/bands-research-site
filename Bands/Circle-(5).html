Circle (5)

Free improvisation quartet, active 1970–1972. Discogs artist ID: 342265.

Overview

Circle was a short-lived but influential free improvisation group formed in late 1970, built around two alumni of Miles Davis’s electric band: pianist Chick Corea and bassist Dave Holland. They were joined by reedist Anthony Braxton and drummer/percussionist Barry Altschul. The group operated in the radical avant-garde, committed to pure collective improvisation and the dissolution of conventional jazz structure.

The quartet recorded prolifically during 1971, producing two double-LP live documents for CBS/Sony Japan (the only label to release Circle material while the group was active) and one ECM studio album under the Corea/Holland/Altschul trio name. Circle dissolved in early 1972 when Corea departed to form Return To Forever, turning toward a more accessible fusion-inflected direction. The remaining three members went on to other collaborative work — notably Holland’s Conference of the Birds (ECM, 1973) featured Braxton and Altschul alongside Sam Rivers.

The Paris - Concert double LP was recorded live in Paris in 1971 and released on ECM Records in 1972 — it became the definitive Circle document in the West, widely reissued across territories through the 2000s.

Connections

  • Chick Corea — piano; left to found Return To Forever (1972)
  • Dave Holland — bass; continued avant-garde work; led Conference of the Birds (ECM, 1973)
  • Anthony Braxton — reeds; continued collaborations with Holland post-Circle
  • Barry Altschul — drums/percussion; appeared on Holland’s Conference of the Birds (1973)
  • Miles Davis — both Corea and Holland came out of Davis’s electric band (1968–1970)
  • Manfred Eicher — produced Paris - Concert and A.R.C.; ECM label head
  • ECM Records — released Paris - Concert (1972) and A.R.C. (1971); reissued Paris - Concert extensively through the 2000s
  • Return To Forever — Corea’s next project after leaving Circle (1972)
