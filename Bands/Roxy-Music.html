Roxy Music

English art-rock band formed 1971 in London. Discogs artist ID: 56621. Inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame 2019. Broke up 2011.

History

Formation and the Eno Era (1971–1973)

Roxy Music formed around Bryan Ferry, who had studied fine art under Richard Hamilton at Newcastle University. The band assembled in London with Brian Eno handling synthesizers and tape treatments in a distinctly non-musician role — a key part of the early sonic identity. Debut album Roxy Music (1972, Island Records) was produced by Peter Sinfield (lyricist from King Crimson), with engineer Andy Hendriksen. The opening bass slot was taken by founding member Graham Simpson, who left the band shortly after.

The debut single “Virginia Plain” (1972) reached UK #4. Second album For Your Pleasure (1973, Island) was produced by Chris Thomas — the beginning of a recurring Roxy/Thomas partnership. Cover model: Amanda Lear. John Porter guested on bass. Eno departed after this record over creative tensions with Ferry.

The Island Albums Without Eno (1973–1975)

With Eno gone, Eddie Jobson (ex-Curved Air) joined on keyboards, violin, and synthesizer. Stranded (1973) was the first UK #1 album for the band, produced by Chris Thomas with John Punter engineering. John Gustafson handled bass. The covers art direction from this point drew heavily on designer Nicholas De Ville and fashion stylist Antony Price.

Country Life (1974, Island): self-produced (Ferry and Roxy Music) with John Punter producing and engineering. Jobson credited as “Edwin Jobson.”

Siren (1975, Island/ATCO): produced by Chris Thomas, engineered by Steve Nye. Featured Jerry Hall on cover. The single “Love Is the Drug” was a major hit.

After Siren, the band went on hiatus (1976–1978). Phil Manzanera pursued 801 and solo work; Brian Eno had by this point become one of the most influential producers in rock.

Comeback and the Polydor/EG Era (1979–1982)

Roxy returned with Manifesto (1979, EG/Polydor), self-produced. New members included Gary Tibbs on bass and Paul Carrack on keyboards. Engineers included Jimmy Douglass, Rhett Davies, Phill Brown, and Randy Mason.

Flesh + Blood (1980, EG/Polydor): produced with Rhett Davies, mixed by Bob Clearmountain, mastered by Bob Ludwig. Session players included Simon Phillips (drums), Alan Spenner (bass), Neil Hubbard (guitar), and Andy Newmark (drums).

Avalon (1982, EG): the final studio album and their commercial peak — UK #1. Produced by Roxy Music and Rhett Davies, mixed and engineered by Bob Clearmountain, mastered by Bob Ludwig. Cover design by Peter Saville (2). Key session players: Neil Hubbard (guitar), Alan Spenner and Neil Jason (bass), Andy Newmark (drums), Fonzi Thornton (backing vocals), Jimmy Maelen (percussion). “More Than This” and “Avalon” became signature late-period tracks.

The band played their last show in 2011. Bryan Ferry was awarded a CBE in November 2011.

Reunion Tours (2001–2011)

After the 1983 hiatus, the band reunited for touring in 2001 and played intermittently through to 2011. The 1983 mini-album The High Road documented the final original-era touring before the hiatus.
