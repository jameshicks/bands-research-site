From Discogs

Fly Ashtray is an American rock band, formed in 1983 in the Bronx, New York, by Chris Thomas, James Kavoussi, Eric Thomas, John Beekman and Mike Anzalone. At some point, Thomas and Anzalone left the band which led to Beekman moving from lead vocalist to bassist/guitarist, and then Kavoussi switched from keyboard to guitar. Glen Luttman joined the band at this juncture to play the drums.

Spoog, a side project, resulted with impromptu gigs when one or another member of the band was out of town. Anzalone and Kavoussi also played in Uncle Wiggly. Kavoussi has made solo records billed as pHoaming Edison. The line-up of Thomas, Kavoussi, Beekman and Luttman was stable for a lengthy period until Beekman’s duties were taken over in 1998 by Dave Abel.

In 2003, Luttman requested a leave of absence from Fly Ashtray, and in the same year Eric Marc Cohen took over drum duties.

The band released two records on Shimmy Disc and worked with Kramer.

Most recently, the band’s music was featured in “Responsible Ballet and What We Need Is a Bench to Put Books On”, a dance piece choreographed by Jon Kinzel, and performed at The Kitchen in New York City.

— Discogs artist profile, ID 336849

Overview

Fly Ashtray is a Bronx-bred American rock band formed in 1983, mixing indie rock, experimental, and psychedelic lo-fi sounds across a catalogue that stretches from the late 1980s into the 2020s. The original lineup underwent significant reshuffling early on — founders Chris Thomas and Mike Anzalone departed, leading John Beekman to shift to bass/guitar, James Kavoussi to move from keyboards to guitar, and Glen Luttman to join on drums; further changes brought Dave Abel in as bassist in 1998 and Eric Cohen on drums in 2003. The band released two records on Shimmy Disc in the early 1990s in collaboration with Kramer, and has seen renewed activity through Fuzzy Warbles Cassettes in the 2020s. Side projects include Spoog (an impromptu live outfit for when members were away) and Uncle Wiggly, while Kavoussi also recorded solo material as pHoaming Edison.

Notes (web)

Shimmy Disc connection and Kramer’s production role

The pathway to Shimmy Disc ran through Uncle Wiggly. Uncle Wiggly — a side project featuring Fly Ashtray guitarist James Kavoussi and former Fly Ashtray bassist Mike Anzalone — had already become a Shimmy-Disc act before Fly Ashtray landed there. According to the Big Takeover two-part profile of the band, that prior relationship made Shimmy-Disc a natural fit: the label co-released Fly Ashtray’s debut full-length Clumps Takes a Ride (1991) jointly with See Eye Records. (Note: Discogs credits this release to See Eye Records; Big Takeover describes it as a See Eye / Shimmy-Disc co-release — see Conflict note below.)

Tone Sensations of the Wondermen (1994): Recorded in late 1993 at Kramer’s Noise New Jersey studio. Kramer produced the album. The sessions felt rushed to the band — Chris Thomas recalled joking about a mock cover titled Kramer Prevents Fly Ashtray, “a cartoon of Kramer holding up this big clock, because we felt like it was such a hurried affair.” Big Takeover Part 1

John Beekman reflected critically on Kramer’s signature production style: “The thing with Kramer is he had a sort of sound: big reverb compression and we didn’t necessarily want to do that.” He reconsidered after hearing Low’s I Could Live in Hope, recorded at the same Noise New Jersey studio that summer, acknowledging the production quality was strong but noting “We were a very different sort of band than Low.” Big Takeover Part 1

Chris Thomas also reflected that Tone Sensations was “way too long. Way too many songs on that thing.” Big Takeover Part 1

No source found stating what specifically drew Kramer to Fly Ashtray beyond the Uncle Wiggly connection. The Big Takeover articles are a two-part profile by an author who witnessed a late-1993 CMJ Music Marathon performance. No Kramer interview reviewed (The Quietus, Silent Radio, Magnet Magazine, Psychedelic Baby Magazine) mentions Fly Ashtray.

Conflict

Clumps Takes a Ride label credit: Discogs attributes this release solely to See Eye Records (consistent with the Releases table above). Big Takeover Part 2 describes it as a co-release between See Eye Records and Shimmy-Disc. The Labels table above also shows Shimmy-Disc with 2 works spanning 1992–1994 — if the 1991 Clumps was a co-release, the Shimmy-Disc relationship may have begun a year earlier than the table implies. Discogs data should be considered authoritative for label credit; the Big Takeover claim warrants checking against the physical release.
