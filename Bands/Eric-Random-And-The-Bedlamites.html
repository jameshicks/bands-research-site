Eric Random And The Bedlamites

Discogs ID: 36945
Also credited as: Eric Random & The Bedlamites · Eric Random And His Bedlamites · Eric Random Meets The Bedlamites · The Bedlamites
Active: 1982–1986 (as recording unit; Klanggalerie reissue activity 2013)
Origin: Manchester, UK
Labels: New Hormones · Doublevision · Fon Records
Genres: Industrial · Dub · Electro · Post-Punk · World / Leftfield

Collaborative project centred on Eric Random (Eric Ramsden, b. 1961, Manchester). Functioned as a loose collective with a stable nucleus and a rotating cast of session contributors. Released three records — one LP and two further LPs, all on different labels — spanning 1982–1986, each marking a shift in style and production context.

Biography

Formation and Earthbound Ghost Need (1982)

Eric Random had already released two solo records on New Hormones (ORG 6, 1980; ORG 11, 1981) and played in The Tiller Boys with Pete Shelley (Buzzcocks) and Francis Cookson. By 1982 he had assembled The Bedlamites as a performing and recording unit for his third New Hormones release.

Earthbound Ghost Need (ORG 18, 1982) — credited to “Eric Random Meets The Bedlamites” on the sleeve — is the most densely connected record in the New Hormones catalog. The session drew in virtually the entire Manchester post-punk extended family, plus two members of Cabaret Voltaire from Sheffield. See the full personnel table below.

The record’s six tracks blend dub textures, industrial electronics, and world-music instrumentation (shakuhachi, violin, tabla), pointing toward the eclecticism that would run through all three Bedlamites records. Style credits: Electronic / Dub / Industrial.

Mad As Mankind and Time-Splice (1984)

The group moved to Doublevision — the Cabaret Voltaire-affiliated Sheffield label — for their next releases. Both appeared in 1984, with the Richard H. Kirk and Stephen Mallinder of Cabaret Voltaire producing the Mad As Mankind 12” (DVR 7). Time-Splice (DVR 11) was a full LP, co-produced by The Bedlamites themselves alongside Phil Bush (side A) and Stuart Pickering (engineer, side B).

Mad As Mankind is a three-track 12”: the title track (6:40), a radio edit, and “Dream Web Of Maya”. Genres: Electronic / Dub / Electro / Industrial. Lyn Walton is credited under the alias “Stagger Lynne.”

Time-Splice (8 tracks) retains the core of Eric Random, Wayne Worm (bass), Dids (percussion), and Lyn Walton (vocals), with Cliff Saffer on saxophone (track B1). The runout etchings include: “DVR 11 B1 PURE BEDLAM. PORKY. MPO” — a nod to the project name.

Ishmael (1986)

The third and final LP, released on Fon Records (BED 7, 1986), is the most produced and musically diverse entry. Produced by Robert Gordon and The Bedlamites; Andy Diagram guested on flugelhorn and trumpet, Tony Quigley (see Tony Quigley) on soprano saxophone and percussion, Henry Laycock on lute (Berber), guitar, synth, and backing vocals. The Designers Republic handled the sleeve design — an early credit for the Sheffield design collective.

Track “Hey Da Da Boom” (A3) credits a co-write with Collin Walcott (misspelled “Colin Walcott” on sleeve), the American percussionist associated with Oregon. Styles: Electronic / Dub / Electro / Leftfield.

Context and Connections

The Bedlamites occupied an unusual position in the early-1980s UK independent underground: rooted in the Manchester post-punk scene (New Hormones, the Manchester Musicians Collective) but with a strong Sheffield axis running through Cabaret Voltaire’s production and label infrastructure. This dual geography — Manchester personnel, Sheffield production — gives the 1984 records their particular sound.

Eric Random’s sustained involvement with Cabaret Voltaire as a session player (tabla, percussion, synthesizer) on multiple CV records (1984–86) blurred the line further: he was both a client of their production setup and a participant in their music. The Ishmael catalog number “BED 7” — on Fon Records — sits directly in the FON Studios Sheffield world (Mark Brydon’s studio, later the seedbed for Chakk, Moloko, etc.).

The Ishmael sleeve by The Designers Republic is one of their earliest documented credits; by the late 1980s TDR became one of the most prominent graphic design studios in British electronic music (Warp Records art direction, etc.).

Graham Dowdall (Dids) is the most consistent musical presence across all three records. His career bridged Ludus → Bedlamites → Nico touring band → Suns Of Arqa → Sonexuno → Gagarin project, and he remained one of the most widely connected Manchester scene figures until his death on 16 June 2024.

Web sources

Robert Gordon (producer, Ishmael, 1986)

Robert Gordon was the in-house engineer at FON Studios, Sheffield — not merely associated with the studio but employed there, building production credits through the mid-1980s before co-founding Warp Records in 1989. His pre-Warp work at FON included engineering Chakk’s Ten Days In An Elevator (the first major FON Studios LP sessions) and Top 40 mixes for Erasure, Ten City, and Joyce Sims. His production of Ishmael (1986, recorded at FON Studios) is consistent with this timeline and represents one of his earlier full LP production credits.

Gordon later co-founded Warp with Steve Beckett and Rob Mitchell, releasing Forgemasters’ “Track With No Name” as Warp’s debut single (1989), and left the label in 1991. The full account of his FON career is documented in Matt Anniss’s book Join The Future.

Sources: testpressing.org · Fact Magazine, 2012-04-17 · Wikipedia, Warp Records, as of 2026-05-31 · mattanniss.com

Mad As Mankind & Time-Splice (Klanggalerie gg180, 2013) — bonus material

The CD reissue contains all three tracks from the original Mad As Mankind 12” and all eight tracks from Time-Splice, plus one bonus track: “Mad As Mankind 2012” (5:05). This is a fully new recording — not an archival version — made by Eric Random in 2012 and premiered at the Doppelvision festival in Vienna prior to the CD’s release. It was produced and recorded by Eric Random himself.

Full tracklist (11 tracks): Mad As Mankind / Dream Web Of Maya / Himalaya Sun / Hardcore / Destination / Subliminal Seduction / Second Sight / No-Man-Trash / Father Can’t Yell / Himalaya Sun (Reprise) / Mad As Mankind 2012. Limited edition of 300 copies.

Sources: klanggalerie.bandcamp.com · rateyourmusic.com

Ishmael Klanggalerie reissue (GG226)

The Klanggalerie CD reissue of Ishmael adds six bonus tracks — two recorded in 1999 at Cachumba Studios and four recorded between 2000–2005 at The Agency — all previously unreleased. The original mini-album comprises the first six tracks (Cherish / Heaven Only Knows / Hey Da Da Boom / Nile Song / Qawali / The Knowledge Of Sorrow). The reissue was remastered by Martin Bowes.

Note: Discogs and some sources refer to Ishmael as a “mini-album” or LP; the original 1986 release (BED 7) has six tracks. The Klanggalerie CD totals twelve.

Source: klanggalerie.bandcamp.com/album/ishmael

Live activity

The Bedlamites were a performing live band during their active period, not solely a studio project. Documented performances include:

  • 1981: Band On The Wall, Manchester — experimental night organised by New Hormones Records (programme in Manchester Digital Music Archive); billed alongside The Beach Surgeon (Graham Massey and Colin Seddon). mdmarchive.co.uk
  • March 1985: ICA Rock Week showcase, London (with A Certain Ratio); short UK tour supporting Cabaret Voltaire around the same time. ltmrecordings.com
  • 13 March 1987: Manchester Town Hall — supporting Nico (flyer in Manchester Digital Music Archive). mdmarchive.co.uk
  • 1982–1988: The core of the Bedlamites (including Eric Random and Graham Dowdall) served as Nico’s backing band “The Faction” — extensive touring and several live releases under that configuration. ltmrecordings.com

The Manchester Digital Music Archive holds 23 artefacts related to the group (programmes, flyers, fanzine pages, press, a 1984 photograph, and a 2016 video tribute), confirming sustained live presence 1981–1987. mdmarchive.co.uk
