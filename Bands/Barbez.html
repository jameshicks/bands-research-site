Barbez

New York-based avant-garde ensemble. Discogs artist ID: 569703. Website: http://www.barbez.com/

Biography

Barbez is a New York ensemble led by guitarist and songwriter Dan Kaufman (Discogs ID: 1464507), drawing together players from the city’s experimental, klezmer, jazz, and post-punk worlds. The group’s sound combines guitar-driven songwriting with marimba/vibraphone, theremin, clarinet, and drums, setting original compositions alongside covers of Brecht/Weill, The Residents, and political folk songs in a style that cuts across cabaret, chamber pop, and downtown avant-garde.

The ensemble formed around 2000. Their debut self-titled release appeared that year on Not On Label, featuring a small core lineup including Danny Tunick on marimba, David Kannenstine on bass, Josh Matthews on drums, Dan Kaufman on guitar, Carl Maguire on accordion, and Matteo de Cosmo on vocals. By the 2004 CD reissue/new edition (Important Records, imprec036), the group had grown and refined its approach, bringing in guest players including Meredith Yayanos on theremin, Rebecca Moore (backing vocals, toy piano), and Sarah Bernstein on violin, while retaining Tunick, Kaufman, and Dan Coates on electronics. The tracklist on the 2004 release reveals Barbez’s literary and musical range: Brecht/Weill material (“Tango Ballade,” “Pirate Jenny”), a cover of The Residents, Russian traditional song, and lyrics by the Surrealist poet Charles Henri Ford.

Their 2005 album Insignificance (Important Records, imprec) marked a fuller ensemble statement, co-produced by Kaufman, Dan Coates, and Martin Bisi, who also recorded and mixed at BC Studio in Brooklyn. Pamelia Kurstin joined on theremin, with John Bollinger and Shahzad Ismaily sharing drum duties across different tracks, and Ksenia Vidyaykina contributing vocals. The Lonesome Organist (Jeremy Jacobsen) appeared on one track. This album anchored Barbez’s relationship with Important Records, where they remained for subsequent work.

Mexico City Impressions / Someone Get Rid Of The King (2007, Important Records) stripped back to a tighter core — Kaufman on guitar/vocals, Tunick on marimba/vibraphone, Kurstin on vocals, and John Bollinger on drums — again recorded and mixed by Martin Bisi at BC Studio and mastered by Fred Kevorkian.

Bella Ciao (2013) expanded the ensemble with clarinet specialist Peter Hess (clarinet, bass clarinet, contra-alto clarinet, alto clarinet), violinist Catherine McRae, bassist Peter Lettre, Dan Coates on electronics, and guest vocalists Dawn McCarthy (tracks 6, 9) and Fiona Templeton (tracks 4, 5), alongside the core of Kaufman, Tunick, Kurstin, and Bollinger. The album title — Bella Ciao is an Italian anti-fascist partisan song — signals the political folk dimension that would intensify in later work.

For Those Who Came After: Songs Of Resistance From The Spanish Civil War (2017, Important Records) is a co-credit release with vocalist Velina Brown, explicitly conceived as a political-historical document: a collection of songs from the Spanish Civil War. The album reflects a long-standing thread in Barbez’s work — the intersection of experimental music and historical political song traditions.

Elegy For Gaza (2025) is the most recent Discogs-listed release, featuring the by-then-settled core lineup of Kaufman, Tunick, Kurstin, Bollinger, Hess, McRae, and Lettre.

Genre and Context

Barbez operates in the tradition of NYC downtown eclecticism — a lineage that includes John Zorn’s Masada/Tzadik world, the chamber cabaret of The Dresden Dolls-adjacent spaces, and the Weimar-influenced avant-garde that runs through Brecht/Weill revivals in New York since the 1980s. The use of marimba, vibraphone, theremin, and clarinet as primary melodic voices (rather than keyboards or strings as the norm) gives the group a distinctive timbre. Their lyrical/thematic engagement with antifascist and resistance song traditions sets them apart from purely formalist experimentalism.
