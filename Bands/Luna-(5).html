Luna (5)

American indie rock / dream-pop band. Discogs artist ID: 260774. Origin: New York City. Active: 1991–2005, 2014–present. Genres: Indie Rock, Dream Pop, Shoegaze-adjacent. Name variation: “Luna²” (debut album sleeve credit). Website: lunamusic.com

Overview

Luna was formed in New York City in 1991 by Dean Wareham after the dissolution of Galaxie 500. The band drew on the slow-burn, reverb-heavy guitar approach of Galaxie 500 but developed a more ornate, melodic sound — close harmonies, jazz-inflected rhythms, and an increasingly lush production palette across seven studio albums for Elektra, Beggars Banquet, Jetset Records, and their own Double Feature Records.

The band broke up in 2005 following their farewell tour, documented in the film Tell Me Do You Miss Me (2006). Wareham and bassist Britta Phillips — who joined in 2001 and later married Wareham — continued as Dean & Britta. Luna reunited in 2014 with the classic Wareham/Phillips/Eden/Wall lineup and have released new studio material and live albums since.

History

Origins: Galaxie 500 (1987–1991)

Galaxie 500 — Wareham on guitar/vocals, Damon Krukowski on drums, Naomi Yang on bass — formed at Harvard University, Boston and released three albums on Rough Trade (Today, 1988; On Fire, 1989; This Is Our Music, 1990) before dissolving in 1991. The breakup was acrimonious; Wareham formed Luna shortly after. Krukowski and Yang continued as Damon & Naomi. Discogs ID for Galaxie 500: 254970.

Formation and Lunapark (1991–1992)

Luna launched with Wareham (guitar/vocals), Justin Harwood (bass — ex-The Chills), and Stanley Demeski (drums — ex-The Feelies). The debut Lunapark (Elektra, 1992, cat# 9 61360-2) was produced by Fred Maher and mastered by Bob Ludwig. The band was credited as “Luna²” on the sleeve. Grasshopper (2) (Sean Mackowiak of Mercury Rev) plays electric guitar on three Lunapark tracks (“Slide”, “Hey Sister”, “I Want Everything”); Kramer (2) contributes piano and organ on two tracks. Sean Eden (guitar) joined in time for single/EP sessions around 1992–1993.

Bewitched and Penthouse (1993–1995)

Bewitched (Elektra, 1993, cat# 61617-4) solidified the twin-guitar lineup with Eden as full member. Engineer on Bewitched was Victor Van Vugt. Penthouse (Elektra, 1995, cat# 61807-2) is widely considered the band’s artistic peak — co-produced by the band with Mario Salvati (recording) and Pat McCarthy (additional recording and mixing), mastered by Greg Calbi, with Jane Scarpantoni providing cello arrangements and Justin Harwood co-arranging strings. Penthouse also features photography by Nitin Vadukul and city photography by Ted Croner. Design throughout this era by Frank Olinsky.

Note: Penthouse credits still list Stanley Demeski on drums despite Demeski having been replaced; Lee Wall joined as drummer for Penthouse sessions.

Pup Tent and The Days Of Our Nights (1997–1999)

Pup Tent (Elektra, 1997, cat# 62055-2) maintained the Wareham/Harwood/Eden/Wall lineup; Pat McCarthy again co-produced alongside the band. The Days Of Our Nights (Beggars Banquet / Everlasting, 1999, cat# 90003-2) brought in Paul Kimble (of Grant Lee Buffalo) as producer, mixer, and multi-instrumentalist (piano, Fender Rhodes, Mellotron, backing vocals). Bryan Zee engineered. Jane Scarpantoni returned for cello. Greg Calbi mastered. This was the final album with Justin Harwood on bass.

Transition to Jetset; Britta Phillips joins (2001–2004)

Justin Harwood departed after The Days Of Our Nights. Britta Phillips — known as the singing voice of Jem from the 1985 animated series Jem, under her alias “Jem (9)” (born June 11, 1963, Boyne City, Michigan; also member of The Belltower and Ultra Baby Fat in the 1990s) — joined on bass and vocals for the Live album (Arena Rock, 2001), which documents the transition: Harwood plays on roughly half the set, Phillips on the other half.

Romantica (Jetset Records, 2002, cat# TWA44CD) was co-produced with Gene Holder (ex-The dB’s). Dave Fridmann provided additional production and mixing on most tracks; Gene Holder mixed the remainder. Scott Hull (2) mastered. Michael Ivins (of The Flaming Lips) served as assistant engineer. Rendezvous (Jetset Records, 2004, cat# TWA69CD) was produced and engineered by Bryce Goggin; Ray Ketchem did additional mixing on one track.

Breakup and Tell Me Do You Miss Me (2005–2006)

Luna announced their breakup in 2004 and completed a farewell tour in early 2005. The documentary Tell Me Do You Miss Me (dir. Matthew Buzzell, 2006) followed the final tour. Beggars Banquet issued Best Of Luna that year, with artwork by Adrian Tomine. Lunafied (Rhino Records, 2006) is a covers album of Luna songs by other artists.

Dean & Britta (2003–ongoing)

Wareham and Phillips began recording as Dean & Britta alongside the final Luna years. Key releases include L’Avventura (Jetset, 2003), Back Numbers (Zoë Records, 2007), 13 Most Beautiful… Songs For Andy Warhol’s Screen Tests (Double Feature, 2010 — a score commissioned for the Warhol Foundation), Mistress America OST (Milan, 2015), and A Peace Of Us (Carpark Records, 2024). They run their own label, Double Feature Records (Discogs ID: 133173). Also a member of The Million Dollar Bashers.

Reunion (2014–present)

Luna reunited in 2014 with Wareham, Phillips, Eden, and Wall. Reunion-era releases include the double album A Sentimental Education (Double Feature Records, 2017, cat# LP-DBL-0013), produced by Jason Quever (alias: Papercuts), with guest guitar from Grasshopper (2) and vocals from Jonathan Donahue (Mercury Rev). A companion EP, A Place Of Greater Safety (Double Feature, 2017, cat# LP-DBL-0014), followed. Also issued Play The Velvet Underground’s “Lonesome Cowboy Bill” (Feral Child Records, 2017) with Cheval Sombre. During 2022 they self-released a series of live album-length recordings: Live Bewitched, Live Penthouse, and Live Rendezvous. An earlier Tell Me Do You Miss Me reissue came out on Double Feature in 2019.

Scene Connections

Luna sit at the junction of the NYC indie rock lineage flowing from Galaxie 500 through the early 1990s East Coast scene. Key scene adjacencies:

  • Galaxie 500 lineage: Wareham’s primary band before Luna. Members Damon Krukowski and Naomi Yang continued as Damon & Naomi.
  • The Feelies connection: Stanley Demeski (Luna’s first drummer) was a Feelies member.
  • Mercury Rev: Grasshopper (2) (Sean Mackowiak, ID 436339) plays electric guitar on Lunapark tracks and on A Sentimental Education; Jonathan Donahue contributes vocals to A Sentimental Education.
  • The Flaming Lips: Michael Ivins served as assistant engineer on Romantica.
  • The Chills: Justin Harwood came from this New Zealand group.
  • Dave Fridmann: mixed bulk of Romantica; his engineering work also connects to Mercury Rev and Mogwai.
  • Beat Happening / K Records orbit: Beat Happening appear on Slide (1993 single).
  • Velvet Underground reverence: Luna covered “Season of the Witch” (Donovan, 1996) and recorded Play The Velvet Underground’s “Lonesome Cowboy Bill” (Feral Child, 2017) with Cheval Sombre. Lou Reed and John Cale (VU veterans) appear on the Slide single. Steve Wynn (The Dream Syndicate) also on Slide.
  • Adrian Tomine: sleeve artist for Best Of Luna; notable comics artist.
  • Kramer (2): Kramer (2) contributes piano/organ to Lunapark, connecting Luna to the Shimmy-Disc/indie-noise axis.
  • Grant Lee Buffalo: Paul Kimble (bassist/producer of Grant Lee Buffalo) produced The Days Of Our Nights.
  • The dB’s: Gene Holder (ex-The dB’s) produced/engineered Romantica.
