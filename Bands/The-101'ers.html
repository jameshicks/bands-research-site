The 101’ers

British pub rock / proto-punk band. London, UK, active 1974–1976. Discogs artist ID: 266429

Biography

Formation and the Squat Scene (1974–1975)

The 101’ers formed in London in 1974, taking their name from the collective squat at 101 Walterton Road, Maida Vale (also described as Maida Hill in some sources). Strummer had been living there since early 1973. The squat was part of a wartime-era row of terraced housing that the council was demolishing house by house: conditions were severe — no interior toilet, no hot water, no legitimate electricity (power was illegally tapped from the public grid), and the basement where the band rehearsed had a dirt floor. 101 Walterton Road was the last house on the block to be demolished, in 1975. (source: punkarchaeology.com)

After the demolition the band/community relocated to 36 St Luke’s Road, near a West Indian community in Notting Hill. A further squat connected to the scene was at Orsett Terrace — Dudanski mentions it in a later interview as a location where he knew Keith Levene and John Lydon from the 1976 era. (source: fodderstompf.com, Dudanski interview)

The squat at 101 was part of a broader mid-70s West London squatting movement. By the end of the 1970s, approximately 30,000 Londoners were occupying reclaimed empty properties city-wide. For musicians like Strummer the arrangement was practical as well as political: rent-free living gave them the time and financial freedom to focus on music. (source: London Museum) The band’s identity was inseparable from this scene: they played benefit gigs, pub backrooms, and drew on the communal life around them. The band debuted on 7 September 1974 at the Telegraph Inn in Brixton under the name “El Huaso and the 101 All Stars”, later shortened to “101 All Stars” and then “101’ers”. (source: Wikipedia, The 101ers, as of 2025-06-10)

The lineup crystallised around Joe Strummer — then billed as “John ‘Woody’ Mellor” (real name John Graham Mellor, Discogs ID 782160) — on rhythm guitar and lead vocals. Other early members included Chilean-born guitarist Alvaro Peña-Rojas (Discogs ID 998226), whose Latin American roots gave the group some of its broader eclecticism, and Antonio Narvàez (real name Antonio Narváez Dupuy, Discogs ID 1004979). Marwood Chesterton — stage-named Mole — handled bass in the early configuration; a separate Discogs entry for Mole (ID 206599) confirms his alias. Patrick Nother (Discogs ID 6733187) also passed through the lineup.

The core gigging lineup that would carry the band through to their demise settled as: Strummer (rhythm guitar, lead vocals), Clive Timperley (lead guitar, vocals; nicknamed “Evil Clive”), Dan Kelleher (“Desperate Dan”, bass/keyboards/vocals), and Richard Dudanski (“Snakehips” / “Snakes”, drums, vocals). Mole remained active on bass for a portion of the recorded material.

Pub Rock and the Road to Punk (1975–1976)

The 101’ers were a working pub band, playing residencies at venues across London. Their sound was built on R&B, rock and roll (Chuck Berry, Van Morrison’s “Gloria”, “Shake Your Hips”), and originals by Strummer and Kelleher with a raw, urgent delivery. By 1975–1976, they were among the most active live acts on the London pub circuit, and their energetic sets were drawing attention.

In early 1976, the band recorded their debut single at Pathway Studios, produced by Roger Armstrong of Chiswick Records. The A-side — “Keys To Your Heart”, written by Strummer — was released on Chiswick (catalogue number S 3) on 27 June 1976. The B-side, “5 Star Rock ‘N’ Roll Petrol”, was co-written by Strummer and Kelleher and produced by Simon Jeffes (of Penguin Cafe Orchestra) and the band themselves. It was the band’s only official release during their active lifetime.

Breakup (1976)

The band’s end came quickly and dramatically. On 3 April 1976, the 101’ers played at the Nashville Room, West Kensington, with the Sex Pistols supporting them. Strummer was struck by the Pistols’ performance, later saying that after five seconds into their first song “we were like yesterday’s paper, we were over”. Jones and Simonon were also in the audience that night. (source: Wikipedia, The 101ers, as of 2025-06-10)

On 30 May 1976, Bernie Rhodes and Keith Levene approached Strummer after a 101’ers gig. They invited him to meet the band at the Davis Road squat rehearsal space, where Levene played “Keys to Your Heart” — one of Strummer’s own tunes — back to him. Rhodes gave Strummer 48 hours to decide whether to join the new group, which Rhodes framed as something that would rival the Pistols. Within 24 hours Strummer agreed. (source: Joe Strummer Official Facebook) Jones later confirmed the 48-hour deadline in Westway to the World; Strummer’s own account was that he made the call after just 24 hours.

Strummer joined Jones, Paul Simonon, Terry Chimes, and Keith Levene to form what Simonon named The Clash. They debuted on 4 July 1976 in Sheffield, opening for the Sex Pistols at the Black Swan. (source: Wikipedia, The Clash, as of 2025-06-10) The 101’ers dissolved; “Keys To Your Heart” was barely in shops before its main protagonist had moved on. It is now understood as both a document of the pub rock scene it grew from and a point of origin for the punk moment that came next: the single’s urgency and Strummer’s voice already pointed toward what The Clash would accelerate.

Discography

Original Releases

”Keys To Your Heart” / “5 Star Rock ‘N’ Roll Petrol” (1976)

  • 7” single, Chiswick Records, catalogue S 3; released 27 June 1976
  • Chiswick — co-founded by Ted Carroll and Roger Armstrong in December 1975, emerging from the Rock On record stall at Camden Market — had just released the Count Bishops’ EP before approaching the 101’ers
  • Recording sessions:
    • Session 1, 4 March 1976 — Pathway Studios, Canonbury, London; produced by Roger Armstrong. “Keys To Your Heart” (A-side) taken from this session.
    • Session 2, 10 March 1976 — Pathway Studios; phased guitar effects applied
    • Session 3, 28 March 1976 — BBC Maida Vale Studios; produced by Simon Jeffes. “Five Star Rock’n’Roll Petrol” (B-side) taken from this session
    • Session 4, 10–13 April 1976 — BBC Maida Vale Studios; additional recordings
  • Armstrong and Carroll approached the band after a gig in early March 1976; Strummer’s recalled account: “Do you want to make a record?”
  • (source: Chiswick Records blog)
  • The band’s only official release in their active lifetime
  • Later reissued in Japan (SMS Records, 1985, catalogue 12” EP) and recompiled widely

”Sweet Revenge” / “Rabies (From The Dogs Of Love)” (1981)

  • 7” single, Big Beat Records, catalogue NS 63
  • Mastered by Porky (5) — etched runout reads “PORKY PRIME CUT / CLASS OF 76”
  • Posthumous release of 1976 recordings; credited to the original lineup (Strummer, Timperley, Kelleher, Dudanski)

Posthumous Compilations

Elgin Avenue Breakdown (1981)

  • LP compilation, Andalucia Records, catalogue AND 101
  • The first substantial retrospective. Twelve tracks drawn from multiple sessions:
    • Tracks A1, A2, B1, B2 — produced by Vic Maile at Jacksons Studio; Mole on bass
    • Tracks A3–A6, B6 — recorded and mixed by Mickey Foote
    • Tracks B3, B4 — produced by Simon Jeffes at BBC Studios Maida Vale; engineered by Mike Robinson
    • Track B5 (“Sweet Revenge”) — produced by Roger Armstrong at Pathway Studios
  • Covers: Chuck Berry’s “Monkey Business”, Slim Harpo’s “Shake Your Hips” (written by James Moore), traditional “Junco Partner” (arranged by James Wayne), Bo Diddley’s “Don’t Let Go” (Ellas McDaniel), Van Morrison’s “Gloria”
  • Full lineup on the release: Joe Strummer (rhythm guitar, lead vocals), Clive Timperley (lead guitar, vocals), Dan Kelleher (bass, keyboards, vocals; lead vocal on B3), Richard Dudanski (drums, vocals), Mole (bass on A1, A2, B1, B2)
  • Sleeve notes by Neal Brown
  • Released internationally (UK, France, Australia, New Zealand pressings documented)

Five Star Rock’N’Roll (1993)

  • CD compilation, Made In Heaven (2), France, catalogue 362020
  • 13 tracks including three bonus “alternative takes” of key songs
  • Produced by: Simon Jeffes (tracks 1–3, 11–13), Vic Maile (tracks 4–9), Roger Armstrong (track 10)
  • Engineer on tracks 1–3, 11–13: Mike Robinson
  • Liner notes and photography: John Tiberi
  • Dan Kelleher sings lead on “Surf City”

Beat Music Dynamite (2003)

  • LP compilation, Andalucia Records, catalogue AND BMD202
  • Unofficial release; UK pressing
  • 11 tracks drawn from existing recordings (includes multiple versions of “Keys To Your Heart”, “Sweet Revenge”, “Surf City”)
  • No production credits listed on Discogs entry

Elgin Avenue Breakdown — Revisited (2005)

  • CD, Astralwerks (US), catalogue ASW74458; also issued on EMI (Europe/UK/Canada/Australia)
  • Expanded 20-track remaster of the Elgin Avenue Breakdown material plus additional unreleased recordings
  • Credited to “The 101ers Featuring Joe Strummer”
  • Producers: Vic Maile (tracks 1, 2, 6, 7, 11, 12), Roger Armstrong (tracks 3, 4, 5), Simon Jeffes (tracks 8, 9, 10)
  • Full lineup credits identical to the 1981 Elgin Avenue Breakdown: Strummer (lead vocals, rhythm guitar), Timperley (lead guitar, backing vocals), Kelleher (bass guitar, backing vocals; lead vocals on tracks 9, 16), Dudanski (drums, backing vocals), Mole / Marwood Chesterton (bass guitar on tracks 1, 2, 6, 7, 11, 12)
  • Liner notes by Allan Jones
  • Includes additional tracks not on the 1981 LP: “Rabies (From The Dogs Of Love)”, “Steamgauge 99”, “Hideaway”, “Lonely Mother’s Son”, “Keep Taking The Tablets”, “Out Of Time” (Jagger/Richards), “Maybelline” (Chuck Berry)
  • Reissued on vinyl for Record Store Day 2015 (Andalucia Records / Parlophone, UK and US)

Member Fates After the Breakup

Joe Strummer joined The Clash immediately upon leaving the 101’ers in mid-1976. He remained the primary vocalist and co-songwriter throughout The Clash’s existence (1976–1986), then had a long post-Clash career including Joe Strummer & The Mescaleros. He died December 22, 2002.

Richard Dudanski went on to one of the more varied post-punk careers among the ex-members. After the 101’ers dissolved, Strummer invited Dudanski to join an early incarnation of The Clash, though Dudanski declined. Keith Levene (whom he knew from the era, including overlapping at the Orsett Terrace squat) later called him in late April/early May 1979 to join Public Image Limited. Dudanski played drums on the majority of Metal Box (Virgin, 1979), including “No Birds Do Sing”, “Swan Lake”, “Chant”, “Memories”, and “Socialist”, recorded at Townhouse Studios. He also played at a Manchester gig (June 1979) and a Top of the Pops taping. He left PiL in summer 1979 after a Leeds gig. (source: Fodderstompf, Dudanski interview) He was also a member of Basement 5 (joining mid-1980, leaving after a long tour supporting The Blockheads; Martin Hannett produced), and played with The Raincoats for approximately a year recording Moving (1984, Rough Trade) and an associated US tour. He published a memoir, Squat City Rocks: protopunk and beyond. a musical memoir from the margins, in 2014. Later work included Nancy Sesay And The Melodaires (Fairytales, Tim/Kerr, 1995), Al-Jaima, and continued recording into the 2020s (A Very British Coup, Cadiz Music, 2019; The Granada Sessions, Tiny Global Productions, 2024). (source: Wikipedia, Richard Dudanski, as of 2025-06-10)

Clive Timperley joined The Passions (Fiction Records), appearing on their debut LP Michael & Miranda (1980) and singles including “I’m In Love With A German Film Star” (Polydor, 1981) — a UK chart hit. He is credited as writer on “I’m In Love With A German Film Star” in later reissues.

Dan Kelleher formed The Martian Schoolgirls after the 101’ers — a short-lived post-punk group. Kelleher’s “Life In The 1980s” was self-released on the band’s own Red Planet label in November 1979 and received several plays on the John Peel show in late 1979, including being the first record played on Peel’s first show of 1980 (1 January 1980). A follow-up single “Motion” was issued on Albion Records in 1981; the band appears to have disbanded that year. Kelleher also went on to The Derelicts. (source: Martian Schoolgirls entry, punkmusiccatalogue.wordpress.com) (source: John Peel Wiki)

Antonio Narvàez (first drummer) was born in Valparaíso, Chile, 1943. After the 101’ers he played drums on two albums by fellow Chilean Álvaro Peña-Rojas (released 1977 and 1978). He went on to host a jazz radio programme — “No sólo jazz” — on Radio Contrabanda FM in Barcelona for over 20 years, and worked as a freelance photographer. He returned to Chile around 2019. (source: rockaxis.com)

Alvaro Peña-Rojas continued performing in Europe; he is credited on releases by Die Fünf Sorglosen and maintains a presence as a musician and filmmaker.

Scene Context

The 101’ers were at the centre of the London pub rock scene in 1975–1976, sharing bills with acts like The Count Bishops and others on the Chiswick roster. Roger Armstrong, who produced their single, was one of the founders of Chiswick Records — a label that emerged directly from the Rock On record stall at Camden Market and was among the first UK independents to sign and release punk and proto-punk acts. Chiswick’s early catalog (alongside the 101’ers) included the Count Bishops, the Damned, and Motörhead.

The squat culture context is important: many key figures in British post-punk passed through similar communal living arrangements in West London in this period. Richard Dudanski’s subsequent work with PiL and Basement 5 traces a direct line from the 101’ers’ squat-rock origins into post-punk’s more politically and sonically radical strands.
