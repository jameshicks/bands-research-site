Alternative TV

UK post-punk band founded in March 1977 by Mark Perry (vocals/guitar) and Alex Fergusson (guitar), with roots in the Sniffin’ Glue fanzine scene. Also known as ATV. The band played their first gig at the Nottingham Punk Festival in May 1977 and released their debut album in May 1978. By late 1978, Perry had grown disenchanted with punk orthodoxy and steered ATV toward free improvisation and experimental territory — a pivot crystallised on the second LP Vibing Up The Senile Man (Part One) (1979). The band broke up the first time late 1979 (briefly operating as The Good Missionaries), reformed in 1981, split again, restarted in 1984, and has remained intermittently active into the 2020s with a large and fluid lineup. Discogs artist ID: 252690.

Overview

Alternative TV are a rare case of a UK first-wave punk act that successfully mutated rather than stalled: the debut LP The Image Has Cracked (1978) is a tightly structured punk/new wave record featuring Jools Holland on piano and Moog, while the follow-up Vibing Up The Senile Man (Part One) (1979) draws heavily on free improvisation, with Genesis P-Orridge of Throbbing Gristle contributing percussion on several tracks. The band recorded primarily for Deptford Fun City Records in their original run, then I.R.S. Records for the 1981 reunion material, and have released more recent work through Chapter 22, Anagram Records, Fourth Dimension Records, and their own Public Domain Records.

Key Release Credits in Detail

How Much Longer / You Bastard (1977, Deptford Fun City Records) Performers: Mark Perry, Alex Fergusson, Chris Bennett (3), Tyrone Thomas (2). Self-produced. Sleeve by ATV, Jill Furmanovsky, Harry T. Murlowski.

Life After Life (1977, Deptford Fun City Records) Perry, Dennis Burns (bass), Chris Bennett (3) (drums), Kim Turner (guitar), Jools Holland (piano). Produced and arranged by Joe Sinclair.

The Image Has Cracked (1978, Deptford Fun City Records, DLP01) Performers: Mark Perry, Dennis Burns, Chris Bennett (3). Guest: Jools Holland (Moog on “Alternatives”; piano on “Viva La Rock N’ Roll”), Kim Turner (piano on “Nasty Little Lonely”). Produced by ATV and Chris Gray (2). Engineered by Chris Gray (2) and Nigel Gray.

Vibing Up The Senile Man (Part One) (1979, Deptford Fun City Records, DLP03) Performers: Mark Perry, Dennis Burns (instruments and voice). Guests: Genesis P-Orridge (percussion on “Release The Natives”, “Serpentine Gallery”, “Graves Of Deluxe Green”), Mick Linehan (guitar on “Release The Natives”, “The Good Missionary”), Steve Jameson (voice on “Smile In The Day”). Produced by Perry and Burns. Engineering and assistant production by Wally Brill. Cover photo and layout: Jill Furmanovsky.

Strange Kicks (1981, I.R.S. Records) Lineup: Mark Perry (vocals), Alex Fergusson (guitar), Dennis Burns (bass), Ray Weston (drums), Alan Gruner (keyboards). Guest: Brian James (guitar, B3). Produced and arranged by Richard Mazda (who also plays guitar, percussion, backing vocals). Additional vocals: Dee Dee Thorne (B3).

Peep Show (1987, Anagram Records) Lineup: Mark Perry (guitar, percussion, vocals), Protag (guitar), Steve Cannell (bass, keyboards, percussion), Allison Phillips (drums, percussion). Produced by Grant Showbiz; additional mixing by Grant Showbiz and ATV. Brass: Terry Edwards (trumpet, saxophone), Dave Jago (trombone). Violin/viola: Frank Sweeney.

Dragon Love (1990, Chapter 22) Lineup: Mark Perry, James Kyllo. Dave Morgan (drums). Produced by ATV. Engineered by Ian McKay.

Dark Places (2018, Fourth Dimension Records) Perry (vocals). Produced by Dave Morgan.

Direct Action (2023, Fourth Dimension Records) Core: Mark Perry, Dave Morgan. Featuring: Cos Chapman, Gareth Matthews (4), Ruth Tidmarsh. Mastered by Siôn Orgon.
