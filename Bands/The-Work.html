The Work

The Work

Oct 19, 202611 min read

The Work

Discogs ID: 163768
Active: 1980–1993 (intermittent)
Origin: London, UK
Genres: Avant-rock, post-punk, art rock, experimental

British experimental post-punk group formed in 1980. Founded by Tim Hodgkinson (Henry Cow co-founder) and Bill Gilonis, The Work occupied the overlap between British post-punk noise-art and the RIO / Recommended Records international underground. Their home label Woof Records was co-founded by Hodgkinson and Gilonis specifically for the group’s releases. The band released five studio albums and two live records over thirteen years, all self-produced and consistently iconoclastic.

Formation and Context

The Work emerged directly from the dissolution of Henry Cow. Tim Hodgkinson had been a co-founding member of Henry Cow (formed 1968), playing organ, clarinet, alto saxophone, and piano through the band’s entire run on Virgin Records (1973–1978). When Henry Cow ended, Hodgkinson and guitarist Bill Gilonis co-founded Woof Records in 1980, with their first release being the Gilonis / Hodgkinson duo EP I Do – I Do – I Don’t – I Don’t (Woof 001 / Recommended Records France RRF 002, 1980).

The full band took shape around the same time, drawing in Mick Hobbs (bass, guitar, multi-instruments), Rick Wilson (drums, percussion, vocals), and occasionally Catherine Jauniaux (vocals). Jim Welton (a.k.a. L. Voag, Xentos, and many other aliases) also appears as a member in the Discogs credits, bringing the prolific London punk/squat-tape scene into the band’s orbit.

The group self-identified with no particular genre, but their sound — repetitive riff structures, Hawaiian guitar, saxophone, shouted vocals, abrupt tempo shifts — had clear affinities with the post-punk art-rock being made simultaneously by This Heat, The Pop Group, and the broader Recommended Records world that included Cassiber, The Camberwell Now, and Art Bears.

Release Notes

I Hate America (1981, Woof 002)

Debut single. Three tracks: “I Hate America” (A-side, 5:50), “Fingers & Toes” (B1), “Duty” (B2, 1:00). Lineup: Mick Hobbs (bass, guitar), Rick Wilson (drums, vocals), Bill Gilonis (guitar), Tim Hodgkinson (vocals, organ, Hawaiian guitar). Mixed by Maggie Thomas. The group self-engineered; Maggie Thomas mixed. Genres tagged as Art Rock / Avantgarde / Lo-Fi on Discogs.

Slow Crimes (1982, Woof 003)

Debut LP, twelve tracks. The band spread their multi-instrumental approach across all four members — every player credited on bass, drums, and vocals. Hodgkinson plays Hawaiian guitar, saxophone, and keyboards; Gilonis adds euphonium and jew’s harp; Hobbs contributes tape and ukulele. Guest vocalists on specific tracks: Catherine Jauniaux (tracks A3 “Pop”, B1 “Cain & Abel”, B5 “Le Travail”, B6 “Maggot Song”), Chris Cutler (featuring), Bob Van De Breuve (featuring), Lepke Buchvalter (featuring). Engineered across multiple studios by Chris Gray (2), Etienne Conod, and Peter Bullen, with additional engineering by Charles Bullen and Maggie Thomas. Styles: Art Rock, Post-Punk.

The Worst Of Everywhere (1982, Woof 005)

Cassette release. An expanded live/performative document: the performer credits include Gilonis, Chris Cutler, Chris Cutler’s associate Bob Ostertag, Bob Van De Breuve, Catherine Jauniaux, Mick Hobbs, Rick Wilson, and Tim Hodgkinson, plus Jacques Jaminon. Compiled by Tim Hodgkinson; live mix by Maggie Thomas and Peter Bullen. The two cassette sides are listed as “Untitled.”

Live In Japan (1982, Recommended Records Japan RRJ 05)

Live LP recorded in Japan. Notable lineup shift: Chris Cutler plays drums and electric drums (taking Wilson’s usual role), and Amos (3) [Jim Welton] plays bass guitar and voice. Gilonis, Hobbs, and Hodgkinson remain. Tracklist draws heavily on Slow Crimes material plus the earlier EP. Bill Gilonis plays tenor saxophone on “Night By The Sea” (B7). Artwork by Chris Cutler. The Cutler connection is the clearest overlap with the Henry Cow / Recommended Records institutional network — Cutler co-ran ReR and his involvement here signals the band’s standing within that community.

Live (1983, Stille Tanten, Germany)

Live LP, Germany-only. No additional performer credits survive in the Discogs entry.

Rubber Cage (1989, Woof 012 / DT 1090 / IPS 1289)

Second studio LP (first since 1982). Self-produced and self-engineered by The Work. Core four-piece: Hobbs (bass, midi-horn), Wilson (drums, percussion, vocals), Hodgkinson (flat guitar, keyboards, alto sax, baritone sax, bass clarinet, vocals), Gilonis (guitar, sampler — marking his adoption of sampling technology). Twelve tracks. Wilson plays cello on “Dangerfish” (A5) and takes a vocal lead on “Trauma” (B3); Hobbs and Gilonis share guitar/vocals on “Quack” (B6). Co-released on Ayaa (France) and In-Poly-Sons (French distribution) alongside the Woof pressing.

See (1992, Megaphone 005 / Woof 015)

Final studio album, CD format. Twelve tracks. The group credits are unusually compressed — all four members listed as “Performer,” with the collective credited for “All Sounds, Recorded By, Producer.” Co-released with Megaphone Records (3), an American label also associated with Hodgkinson’s solo work (he released Live Improvisations with Fred Frith on Megaphone/Woof in 1991 and Each In Our Own Thoughts solo in 1994).

Woof Records Context

Woof Records was co-founded by Hodgkinson and Gilonis in 1980. The label’s stated policy was that Tim and/or Bill would play on, engineer, or produce every release. Beyond The Work’s own records (Woof 002, 003, 005, 012, 015), Woof issued:

  • Woof 001: Bill Gilonis / Tim Hodgkinson — I Do – I Do – I Don’t – I Don’t (1980, co-released with Recommended Records France RRF 002)
  • Woof 006: The Lowest Note — The Lowest Note On The Organ (1983; group including Hobbs, Wilson, Hodgkinson, and Gilonis)
  • Woof 007: Catherine Jauniaux / Tim Hodgkinson — Fluvial (1983; Hodgkinson engineered and produced)
  • Woof 009: Het (3) — Let’s Het (1984; Hodgkinson produced and engineered)
  • Woof 011: The Momes — Spiralling (1989; Hobbs, Hodgkinson, Andy Wake (2))
  • Woof 013: Fred Frith / Tim Hodgkinson — Live Improvisations (1991)
  • Woof 016: Tim Hodgkinson — Each In Our Own Thoughts (1994)

The label ran 15 titles over approximately 15 years, functioning as a personal imprint for the extended network around Hodgkinson and Gilonis.

Recommended Records / RIO Network

The Work operated at the intersection of two overlapping post-Henry Cow networks. Recommended Records (UK) and its associated labels (ReR Megacorp, Recommended Records Japan, Recommended Records France) distributed and championed the band. Live In Japan appeared on Recommended Records Japan (RRJ 05); the Hodgkinson/Gilonis debut EP co-appeared on Recommended Records France (RRF 002).

Chris Cutler — co-founder of Recommended Records alongside the Henry Cow network — appears as a performer and artwork contributor on Live In Japan and as a featured guest on Slow Crimes, cementing the institutional link. This placed The Work firmly within the Rock In Opposition (RIO) international avant-rock orbit alongside groups like Cassiber, Univers Zero, Art Zoyd, and The Camberwell Now.

Member Biographies (summary)

Tim Hodgkinson (b. 1949, Salisbury)

Organ, clarinet, alto saxophone, and piano player; primary composer and conceptualist. Co-founded Henry Cow in October 1968 with Fred Frith; took up organ in 1969. Henry Cow recorded for Virgin Records (1973–1979) and helped define the RIO aesthetic. After Henry Cow, Hodgkinson co-founded Woof Records and The Work. His solo and collaborative discography extends far beyond The Work: he engineered and produced Catherine Jauniaux’s Fluvial (1983), played on Mick Hobbs’s Bandagen (1989), engineered and produced Fred Frith’s Step Across The Border (1990) and The Country Of Blinds (1986). Later projects include K-Space (with Mongolian and Russian musicians), Konk Pack (with Tom Rainey and Alfred Harth), and extensive solo recording work continuing into the 2020s. See Tim Hodgkinson.

Bill Gilonis (b. 1958)

Guitarist, producer, engineer. Co-founded The Work and Woof Records with Hodgkinson. His profile notes extensive work as a producer and engineer for Robert Wyatt, News from Babel (Cutler, Lindsay Cooper, Zeena Parkins, Dagmar Krause), David Thomas, Peter Blegvad, Ut, Lindsay Cooper Film Music Group, Hail, and The Hat Shoes (which included Catherine Jauniaux, Tom Cora, Charles Hayward). Also in Officer!, Murphy Working Stiffs, Grrr (5), Stepmother (4), The Miners of Banal, and The Lowest Note. Relocated to Zurich in 1993. See Bill Gilonis.

Mick Hobbs (London, d. 3 January 2025)

Multi-instrumentalist and songwriter. In The Work 1981–1992; led Officer! from 1983 onward. Also in Family Fodder, 1-2 Japanese, The Momes, The Lowest Note, The Lo Yo Yo, Strobe Talbot, The Paddock. Died January 2025; his final Officer! album Dead Right (Klanggalerie, 2025) appeared posthumously. See Mick Hobbs.

Rick Wilson

Drummer and multi-instrumentalist from London. In The Work throughout its run; also in Family Fodder, People In Control, and The Lowest Note. Name variations include “Rob Harbron” on Discogs (likely an error). Discogs ID: 576.

Catherine Jauniaux (b. 1955, Brussels)

Belgian avant-garde vocalist. Guest on Slow Crimes and The Worst Of Everywhere. In 1983, collaborated with Tim Hodgkinson on her debut solo LP Fluvial (Woof 007). Also in Aksak Maboul, The Hat Shoes, and other experimental ensembles. Moved to New York in the early 1990s and became part of the Downtown music scene with Fred Frith, Tom Cora (whom she later married), Marc Ribot, and Ikue Mori (Vibraslaps duo). See Catherine Jauniaux.

Jim Welton

London-based musician and tape artist from the punk squat scene; prolific under numerous aliases including L. Voag, Xentos, Amos (3), and others. Associated with The Homosexuals, Die Trip Computer Die, The Tesco Bombers. Subject of a 2003 documentary by Luke Fowler. His role in The Work is documented via the Live In Japan credits (bass guitar, voice as “Amos (3)”) and his Discogs group membership listing.

Open Questions

  • Exact active year range: Discogs profile says “formed 1980” and Gilonis’s bio says the group was “active intermittently until 1993,” but no release after See (1992) is documented. Whether any 1993 activity was recorded or released is unknown.
  • Jim Welton’s membership scope: Discogs lists him as a member but his credits only appear on the Live In Japan lineup (as Amos (3)). His role in the studio recordings is unclear.
  • The Live LP (Stille Tanten, 1983): no performer credits in the Discogs entry; lineup unknown.
  • Whether Catherine Jauniaux was ever a full member or only a recurring guest.
