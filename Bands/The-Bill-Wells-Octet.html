The Bill Wells Octet

Glasgow-based jazz and improvisation ensemble led by pianist/composer/bassist Bill Wells. Discogs artist ID: 1439. Active c.1993–2003. Top labels: Loathsome Reels, Domino, Geographic.

Overview

The Bill Wells Octet is an eight-piece ensemble built around Bill Wells (born Falkirk, c.1963), a Scottish multi-instrumentalist active across jazz, improvised music, pop song, and experimental electronics. The Octet was the most formally structured of Wells’s ensemble projects, drawing on a stable lineup of Glasgow jazz and classical players while situating their work within the post-jazz / experimental pop crossover that characterised the Glasgow indie-jazz scene of the 1990s.

Their two official releases span 1995 and 1998: a limited live CD on the self-run label Loathsome Reels, and a joint studio LP with Future Pilot A.K.A. (Sushil Kumar Dade) on Domino/Geographic. Wells also contributed solo tracks to Future Pilot A.K.A.’s 1999 double-CD compilation Vs — A Galaxy Of Sound (Sulfur Records), appearing as a duo partner rather than under the Octet name.

The Octet belongs to a specific Glasgow milieu: it shared personnel with Belle & Sebastian (Stevie Jackson), the broader Scottish improvised music community, and the post-indie world of Geographic and Domino Records. The group name variations in use were “Bill Wells Octet” and “The Bill Wells Octect” (as misspelled on some releases).

Connections

  • Bill Wells — leader and primary composer; Falkirk-born pianist/bassist/guitarist
  • Future Pilot A.K.A. — joint LP 1998; duo collaboration across Vs — A Galaxy Of Sound; Sushil K. Dade (Glasgow Indian musician; also billed as Sushil K. Dade)

  • Domino Records — parent label for Geographic; issued the joint LP
  • Stevie Jackson — Belle & Sebastian guitarist; Bill Wells Trio member; session player on 1998 LP
  • Isobel Campbell — duo collaborator for Ghost Of Yesterday (Creeping Bent, 2002)
  • Aidan Moffat — Arab Strap vocalist; Bill Wells & Aidan Moffat project from 2011
  • Karaoke Kalk — German home for National Jazz Trio of Scotland (2012–2023)

  • The Pastels — via Katrina Mitchell and Robert Henderson; Future Pilot A.K.A. also deeply linked

Open Questions

  • Specific instrument for John Burgess (2) — not credited at release level in Discogs; he appears in Scottish jazz contexts (Glasgow Improvisers Orchestra, Tom Bancroft Orchestra, Harry Beckett Quintet) but his primary instrument is not confirmed from Discogs data alone.
  • Whether the Octet performed beyond the documented recording periods (1993–94 live, 1998 studio) — no further primary releases in Discogs after 1998.
  • Lindsay Cooper (2) (Discogs ID 440953) is confirmed as a different person from Henry Cow’s Lindsay Cooper — the Discogs entry for this Lindsay Cooper shows bass/cello credits going back to 1973, including the original Tubular Bells recording (Mike Oldfield, Virgin 1973) and Scottish folk/contemporary releases; he is a Scottish bassist, not the English vocalist/oboist.
  • Bill Wells’s wider collaborations with the Japanese avant-scene (Maher Shalal Hash Baz, Kama Aina, Tenniscoats) post-2005 — Osaka Bridge (2006), GOK (2009), Lemondale (2011) are the key records; detail not researched here.
