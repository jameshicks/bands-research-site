Groove Corporation

Bristol-based dub/electronic production collective, later operating as a duo. Discogs artist ID: 39008. Active as a band name from circa 1988; currently operating as G. Corp. Grew directly out of Electribe 101 after that group dissolved, with vocalist Billie Ray Martin going solo while the production core carried on. Co-founders (with Rockers Hi-Fi) of The Cake Label.

Also known as: G. Corp · G. Corp Crew · “Remixes From The Elephant House” Original Cast. Name variations include G-Corp, G-Corporation, Groove Corp, The Groove Corp., The Groove Corporation.

Biography

Origins in Electribe 101 (1988–1991)

The name “Groove Corporation” first appeared as a production moniker on Electribe 101 records and on remixes done for other artists. Electribe 101 comprised five members: Brian Nordhoff, Roberto Cimarosti, Joe Stevens, Les Fleming, and vocalist Birgit Dieckmann. When Electribe 101 dissolved, the four instrumentalists — Nordhoff, Cimarosti, Stevens, and Fleming — continued as Groove Corporation, while Birgit Dieckmann departed. The group’s earliest credited work in the DB is a 1988 remix (Mixed By) on Club Records and a 1989 remix for Fontana.

Early Years: Network Records / Six6 (1992–1994)

The debut release proper under the Groove Corporation name was the Summer Of Dub EP (1992, Network Records, cat. NWKT 53) — four tracks of deep house and dub. Written, recorded, produced, and mixed entirely by the group; the matrix runout carries the phrase “THE SOUND OF THE ELEPHANT HOUSE,” which would become the recurring tagline for their studio.

Together with Original Rockers and Glyn Bush / Richard Whittingham, they co-founded The Cake Label, a Bristol imprint. Their first Cake release, “Need More Love (In The Ghetto)” (1992), was followed by the co-production “Stoned” (1993) — credited to “The Groove Corporation Meet Original Rockers.” Songwriting on “Stoned” is split: the Gold Seal and Kashmir Twist mixes credited to Nordhoff, Stevens, Fleming, and Cimarosti; the Manali Cream mix credited to Glyn Bush and Richard Whittingham of Original Rockers.

The debut album Co-Operation (1994) was produced for Six6 / Network Records (cat. GROOVCD1). The album is a 12-track downtempo/dub/trip-hop statement, recorded when Network was bought out by Sony — the resulting lack of major-label support meant the album sank commercially despite being, in the group’s own self-assessment, “brilliant.” Styles on record: Downtempo, Dub, Trip Hop.

East 17 Production Credit (1993)

Groove Corporation produced one track on East 17’s debut album Walthamstow (1993, FFRR): “West End Girls — Faces On Posters Mix” (side B6). This is credited as Producer on the album, alongside a wider production team including Mykaell Riley, Robin Goodfellow, and Phil Harding & Ian Curnow. The track is a version of the Pet Shop Boys song, written by Tennant/Lowe.

Mid-1990s: Six6 Singles (1995–1996)

Following Co-Operation, the group released several six6 singles: Ghetto Prayer (1995), Twist And Change (1995), and Wash Wash (1995, on Century Records). Twist And Change featured MC Nick Plummer (alias MC 90% Water). The Good Intentions EP followed in 1996 on Medicine Records, featuring singer Shara Nelson (formerly of Massive Attack).

New Direction: Echo Beach / Germany (1998)

Co-Operation Dub (1998, Echo Beach, Germany, cat. EB019) is a full rework of the debut album in dub form — 13 tracks, self-produced, engineered, mixed, and arranged by the group. One track, “Ghettoprayer (Deep Blue Dub),” received an additional remix/reproduction from Rob Playford and Sean O’Keeffe. Liner notes by Neil Rushton.

The Elephant House Dub Series (1999–2002)

The group’s most sustained creative run under their own identity came through Different Drummer (a sublabel of Different Drummer Ltd., with sublabel Leftfoot Recordings):

  • Dub Plates From The Elephant House: Volume One (1999, DIFCD09) — 15 tracks, produced/engineered/performed/mixed by G. Corp; mastered by Miles Showell.
  • Dub Plates From The Elephant House Volume Two (2002, DIFLP23) — double vinyl LP, 12 tracks.

Both volumes feature collaborations with vocalist Bim Sherman and others from the Bristol reggae/dub community.

US Breakthrough: Guidance Recordings (2002)

The Groove Corporation Presents Remixes From The Elephant House (2002, Guidance Recordings, cat. GDRC-606 / GDRC606) is the group’s US-facing remix compilation, licensed to the Chicago house label. Tracklist covers reworks of Dillinger, Bob Marley & The Wailers, Rockers Hi-Fi (with Ella Fitzgerald), Rae & Christian feat. The Congos, UB40, Luciano, Yabby You, Bobby Womack, and Ennio Morricone, plus two original Groove Corporation tracks. Illustration / design by Ninety (artist ID 946271).

The same year a liner-notes credit appeared on the Morricone Rmx compilation (WEA / Reprise / Cinesoundz), linking them further to the lounge/exotica remix world.

Later Work and G. Corp (2004–present)

Post-2002 releases appeared primarily on Different Drummer (the 2004 Nothing To Proove EP) and, from around 2013, under the G. Corp name on self-release. The Sky Clears E.P. appeared in 2013. The companion album New Roots — originally recorded as a follow-up to Co-Operation but unreleased for almost two decades — finally appeared in 2013 as release ID 5410583.

Current lineup is a duo: Brian Nordhoff and Roberto Cimarosti. Both are also members of Overproof Sound System (where they produce as G. Corp).

A 2024 credit — Mixed By on Electribal Memories (Edsel Records), a reissue release connected to Electribe 101’s back catalog — marks the most recent DB entry.

Connections

  • Original Rockers (Discogs ID 933) — co-production “Stoned”; shared The Cake Label founders
  • Rockers Hi-Fi — alias of Original Rockers; co-founders of The Cake Label
  • Different Drummer — primary home for dub plate series

  • The “Elephant House” studio moniker recurs across all phases of their work (matrix text, release titles, alias “Remixes From The Elephant House” Original Cast)

Open Questions

  • Full member history: when exactly did Joe Stevens and Les Fleming leave the active lineup? The DB credits them across early 1990s releases but not the late 1990s dub series — likely departed before Co-Operation Dub (1998), where only Nordhoff/Cimarosti/Stevens/Fleming are listed as writers on most tracks, but the current duo is confirmed as Nordhoff + Cimarosti.
  • New Roots album (release ID 5410583) — recorded as a follow-up to Co-Operation but unreleased until 2013; details on that release not fully pulled.
  • Dub Plates Vol. 3 is referenced in collaborator credits (The Mighty Tree, John Dent, Jeffrey Wright, Robert Mullins appearing on it) but not in the primary discography pull — may exist as a release not yet fully indexed or under a different title.
  • Web sources may clarify the Elephant House studio location within Bristol, exact formation date, and Billie Ray Martin’s account of the Electribe 101 split.
