Gichy Dan’s Beachwood # 9

Discogs ID: 1141864 Discogs name: Gichy Dan’s Beachwood # 9 (also rendered: Gichy Dan’s Beachwood #9, Beachwood No. 9, Beachwood N° 9) Active: 1979 Origin: New York City Label: RCA Victor / RCA (France)

Band/project vehicle for vocalist Gichy Dan (Frank Passalacqua), produced and written entirely by August Darnell and Ron Rogers. The group released one LP in 1979, tracked at Bob Blank’s Blank Tape Studios in New York City and pressed in France, in a Latin-tinged disco style that directly prefigures the sound of Kid Creole And The Coconuts.

Overview

The band name comes from a 1950s rock and roll song (“Beachwood 9” or “Beachwood #9”) — the origin of Gichy Dan’s stage name itself. The project was essentially a Darnell/Rogers production showcase built around Gichy Dan’s voice, with Coati Mundi providing horn and string arrangements alongside Darnell.

The members listed on Discogs include Gichy Dan (as Frank ‘Pago Pago’ Passalacqua and Pago Pago (Passalacqua)), Lourdes Cotto, and Ron Rogers. Additional personnel on the LP credits include a larger ensemble of session players (see Release section below).

The band appeared as cameo performers on Cristina’s 2004 ZE Records reissue Doll In The Box (ZE Records ZEREC.CD11), confirming the name remained in use as a collective identity into the 2000s.

Context and Significance

This LP is a direct precursor to the Kid Creole sound. August Darnell wrote all the material and co-produced with Ron Rogers, and the horn/string arrangements by Darnell and Coati Mundi establish the same Latin-inflected, orchestrated disco framework that would define Off The Coast Of Me (1980) and the subsequent Kid Creole albums. The album was pressed in France on RCA Victor, consistent with the strong French interest in the ZE Records scene.

Darnell himself described the project as the template: “Gichy Dan was the precursor to Kid Creole and the Coconuts; had I known it, he would have been Kid Creole.”

The LP was tracked at Blank Tape Studios (opened 1976, run by Bob Blank), the same studio at the center of ZE Records’ production activity from 1979 onward. Darnell was functioning as ZE’s in-house producer at the time, and the Beachwood #9 sessions were part of a series of all-night recording sessions at the studio. (source; source)

After tracking, the album was mixed at RCA Recording Studios in New York by Warren Schatz, Howard Lindeman, and Al Garrison — a move that went against Darnell’s wishes. Darnell later stated that Schatz “had raped my Gichy Dan album by mixing it himself, which was a very painful experience,” and cited this as a reason he was “fed up with the big companies.” (source)

Robert Christgau reviewed the LP in his Consumer Guide, awarding a B+ and writing: “Gichy Dan mixes nostalgic fun and urban realism into something exotic. And though the melodies aren’t always there, the life and imagination that were wrung out of Dr. Buzzard’s second album have returned.” (source)

The LP reached listeners primarily through college radio in New York City and through play by DJs at the Mudd Club. (source)

Live performance

No evidence has been found of Beachwood #9 performing as a touring or gigging live band. Gichy Dan appeared as a special guest at “The August Darnell Revue” at the ’80s club in New York (likely early 1980s), but this was a Darnell revue event, not a Beachwood #9 concert. (source) All available evidence points to Beachwood #9 being a studio project assembled by Darnell and Rogers around Passalacqua’s voice, with no live touring identity of its own.
