Bark Psychosis

Post-rock / experimental / ambient. East London, UK. Discogs artist ID: 47870. Formed 1986; dissolved c. 1995; reformed 2004.

Core figure: Graham Sutton. Their 1994 album Hex is one of the canonical early post-rock records, alongside Talk Talk’s Spirit of Eden and Slint’s Spiderland. After a ten-year silence Sutton returned with Codename: Dustsucker (2004, Fire Records), bringing in a partly new cast while retaining the atmospheric density of the original material.

Biography

Formation and early singles (1986–1991)

Bark Psychosis formed in 1986 in Snaresbrook, East London. Graham Sutton and John Ling were schoolfriends, both 14 at the time, who began experimenting on a four-track and drum machine — initially as a Napalm Death cover project. They recruited drummer Mark Simnett through his connection to St John’s Church in Stratford, and the band rehearsed in the church crypt, a location that would leave its mark on their sound. Steven Page also joined the founding lineup. The core recording lineup was the quartet of Graham Sutton (guitar, vocals, songwriting), Daniel Gish (keyboards), John Ling (bass), and Mark Simnett (drums). This early incarnation released two 12” singles on the small Cheree label:

  • All Different Things / By-Blow (1990, Cheree CHEREE 6T) — guitar-pop with shoegaze textures; early song credits split among Sutton, Ling, Simnett
  • Nothing Feels / I Know (1990, Cheree CHEREE 10T) — bigger and more stylistically resolved; adds Steven Page to co-writing

Both singles were engineered by Scott Rushbury, with lacquer cuts by George Peckham (“Porky”).

3rd Stone period and the Manman / Scum EPs (1992)

The band moved to 3rd Stone, a Corby-based independent label specialising in exploratory music. Two EPs in 1992 marked a dramatic stylistic leap:

Manman EP (3rd Stone, stone 004 cd) — three tracks: “Manman”, “Blood Rush”, “Tooled Up”. Self-produced, self-arranged. Mixed by Kevin Harris and Tarquin Boyesen at separate sessions; recording by Steve Stewart and Tobey Horsnail. Genre credits: Ambient / Minimal / Experimental. Daniel Gish joins on keyboards; the sound is heavily textured and loop-based.

Scum (3rd Stone, STONE 006CD) — a single 21-minute track. Programmed and engineered by Neil Aldridge; mixed jointly by the band and producer Phill Brown. Photography by Toby McFarlan Pond. This piece established the band’s identity internationally and attracted press comparisons to ambient and post-rock avant-garde.

Circa Records deal and the run-up to Hex (1993–1994)

Bark Psychosis signed to Circa Records (a Virgin subsidiary), co-releasing material with 3rd Stone. Two pre-album singles:

A Street Scene (1994, Circa YRA 109 / 3rd Stone, 10” vinyl single) — four-way co-production by Sutton, Ling, Simnett, and Gish. Engineers: Mike Long, Nick Wollage; assistant engineers Henry Binns, Mog, Pete Molyneux. Mixed by Sutton et al. and Roy Spong. Trumpet: Del Crabtree. Triangle / programming: Neil Aldridge. Design / illustration / photography: Dave McKean.

Blue (1994, Circa YRCD 117 / 3rd Stone) — three-track CD single. Tracks produced by the band. Track 3, “Big Shot (Alice’s Cheshire Cat Mix)”, remixed by Rudy Tambala of A.R. Kane.

Hex (1994)

Hex (Circa, CIRCD 29 / 7243 8 39442 2 4) is the band’s debut album and their definitive statement. The full Circa / 3rd Stone co-release was also issued in North America on Caroline Records (CAROL 1753-2). 15+ pressings have appeared since.

Tracklist: The Loom / A Street Scene / Absent Friend / Big Shot / Fingerspit / Eyes & Smiles / Pendulum Man

Independency compilation and dissolution (1994–1995)

Independency (3rd Stone, STONE 010CD, 1994) was a career-spanning compilation bringing together the Cheree singles, the Manman EP, and Scum under one release. Liner personnel adds clarinet (Noel), French horn (Daniel Crawley), and additional programming (Neil Aldridge) to the Scum track. Steven Page and Daniel Gish are credited with writing, arranging, production, and mixing for various tracks.

The band dissolved shortly after Hex was completed. Graham Sutton and Daniel Gish moved into drum & bass, forming Boymerang. Sutton also began a career as an in-demand record producer (see Graham Sutton).

Hexcerpt promo (1994)

Circa issued Hexcerpt (Circa, BARK 94) as a promotional CD containing “A Street Scene”, “Big Shot”, and “Eyes & Smiles” — three Hex tracks for radio and press use.

Game Over compilation (1997)

Game Over (3rd Stone, STONE 031CD, 1997) assembled a retrospective compilation including “Blue”, “A Street Scene”, “Murder City”, “Scum”, and a live version of “Pendulum Man”. Notably included “Three Girl Rhumba” — a cover of Wire’s song (credits: Gilbert / Gotobed / Lewis / Newman), produced and recorded by Graham Sutton. Mastered by Chris Thorpe.

Return: Codename: Dustsucker and 400 Winters EP (2004–2005)

Bark Psychosis was revived in 2004 by Graham Sutton with a new lineup built around him and guitarist Colin Bradley (also of Splintered, aka Dual (2)).

Codename: Dustsucker (Fire Records, FIRECD90, 2004). Tracks co-written variously with Colin Bradley, Rachel Dreyer, and Lee Harris; solo Sutton compositions round out the set.

Replay compilation (2004)

Simultaneous with the comeback, 3rd Stone issued Replay (STONE 041CD), a 13-track remastered anthology of pre-Hex material (Cheree singles, early unreleased tracks, and remixes). Includes “Big Shot (Alice’s Cheshire Cat Mix)” (remix by Rudy Tambala). Mastered by Iain Wetherell, partially by Voda (Jonathan Moore). Su Page returns on vocals for tracks 12–13.

Later reissues (2017–present)

Fire Records reissued Hex in 2017 on vinyl (FIRELP084) and CD (FIRECD084) and Codename: Dustsucker on vinyl in 2018 (FIRELP090). Further vinyl reissues continue through 2024. Scum was reissued on vinyl by Rolling Heads Ltd. in 2023 (RH001LP). Independency was reissued by Hidden Art on LP in 2019 (HI-ART23LP) and again by 3rd Stone in 2025.

Boymerang (Graham Sutton alias)

After Bark Psychosis dissolved, Graham Sutton and Daniel Gish formed Boymerang, a drum & bass / jungle project. Discogs artist ID: 280.

Associated Labels

3rd Stone

Discogs label ID: 10015. Corby-based independent specialising in ambient, space music, and experimental soundscapes. Primary home of Bark Psychosis’s 1992–1997 output. Run out of PO Box 8, Corby, Northants. Also home to No-Man (Steve Wilson / Tim Bowness), Bass Communion, A.R. Kane, and others. Sublabels include Space Age Recordings, Good Records / Them’s Good Records, and The Halloween Society.

Fire Records

Discogs label ID: 2051. UK independent (parent: Fire Records Of London Ltd.). Released Codename: Dustsucker (FIRECD90, 2004) and subsequent Bark Psychosis output. See Fire Records for full label profile.

Circa / Circa Records Ltd.

Discogs label ID: 39413 (parent: Circa Records Ltd., ID 1642). Virgin subsidiary. Co-released A Street Scene, Blue, and Hex with 3rd Stone in 1994. Sublabels include Wild Bunch Records and Union City Recordings.

Cheree

Discogs label ID: 53327. Released the two 1990 debut singles.
