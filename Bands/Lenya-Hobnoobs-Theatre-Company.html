From Discogs

The Lenya Hobnoobs Theatre Company was a theatre co-operative founded in London in 1987 by radical gay artist David Bunnett. The group was originally created to workshop and produce the satirical show AIDS the Musical, but went on to stage two other productions with a left-wing slant.

David Bunnett named the company after a fictional lesbian writer and artist. Bunnett chose the name “Lenya” to suggest a persecuted Russian radical, and “Hobnoobs” after a well-known UK brand of biscuits, which he thought sounded vaguely exotic. He concocted a complete life history for the character.

Recordings

Music from the shows was distributed on cassette under the Poison Girls’ XNTRIX label. Recordings were AIDS The Musical in 1989 (a live recording at the Melkweg in Amsterdam), Desert Storm in 1991 (a studio recording), and Mother Russia (music recorded live at Chat’s Palace 1991, vocals recorded in Xntrix studios 1992).

Overview

Lenya Hobnoobs Theatre Company was a London theatre co-operative founded in 1987 by David Bunnett, initially to workshop and stage AIDS the Musical, a satirical production tackling a politically charged subject at the height of the AIDS crisis in Britain. The company went on to mount two further left-wing productions. Its name was Bunnett’s invention: “Lenya” suggested a persecuted Russian radical, “Hobnoobs” was borrowed from a British biscuit brand for its vaguely exotic ring, and together they formed the identity of a wholly fictional lesbian writer and artist whose complete life history Bunnett devised. The company’s three recordings — a live set at Amsterdam’s Melkweg (1989), and two further cassettes from 1991–92 — were distributed through Xntrix, the label operated by anarcho-punk group Poison Girls, placing Lenya Hobnoobs in a distinctly radical-left UK underground network.
