The Passage

Manchester post-punk band. Active 1978–1983. Discogs artist ID: 95927.

Overview

The Passage were formed in Manchester in March 1978 by Richard “Dick” Witts, Tony Friel, and Lorraine Hilton. The band operated at the intersection of post-punk, art-rock, and angular electronics — dense, literary, and politically alert. Dick Witts was the group’s central figure throughout: classically trained percussionist, co-presenter on Granada TV’s What’s On, music and dance officer for Merseyside Arts Association, and a founding member of the Manchester Musicians’ Collective.

Tony Friel co-founded the group while still playing bass in The Fall (he played on their debut Bingo-Master’s Break-Out! in 1978). The Passage’s debut 7” New Love Songs (December 1978, Object Music) featured two tracks each by Witts and Friel; the lead track “Love Song” attracted controversy but sold a reported 3,000 copies. The second EP About Time (1979) was produced by David Cunningham of The Flying Lizards and again split writing duties between Witts and Friel.

Friel left in early 1980 after a London show with Cabaret Voltaire. For the debut album Pindrop (1980, Object Music), self-produced by Witts alone on eight-track equipment at Graveyard Studios in 70 hours, the group was essentially a solo vehicle. The band subsequently expanded into a trio. A brief personnel transition period included Lorraine Hilton’s sister Martine joining on bass before a car crash sidelined activity.

By the time of For All And None (1981, Night & Day), the core trio was Witts, guitarist Andy Wilson, and multi-instrumentalist Joe McKechnie — releasing on their own Night & Day imprint. The band moved to Cherry Red for the next two albums, Degenerates (1982) and Enflame (1983), working with engineer Chris Nagle (who had engineered Joy Division’s Unknown Pleasures) and mixer Stephen W. Tayler. The compilation Through The Passage and the “best of” Seedy (1997) gathered key material after dissolution. Dick Witts later became an academic, publishing a history of the Arts Council in 1998 and lecturing on modern music.
