Lounge Lizards

Discogs ID: 79333 Origin: New York City, USA Active: 1978–1998 (sporadic activity to c. 2003) Also known as: The Lounge Lizards

Biography

Origins and No Wave Context (1978–1981)

The Lounge Lizards were formed in New York City in 1978 by saxophonist John Lurie and his brother, pianist Evan Lurie. The founding lineup placed them squarely in the No Wave downtown scene — Arto Lindsay (guitar, fresh from DNA (4)) and Anton Fier (drums, later of The Golden Palominos) were both in from the start. Bassist Steve Piccolo and guitarist Dana Vlcek rounded out the early live band.

Their 1981 debut on Editions EG — produced by veteran Columbia engineer Teo Macero and recorded by Frank Laico — was a startling statement: ironic, post-modern jazz that nodded to film noir and Ornette Coleman simultaneously. It was co-released in Spain under the title Harlem Nocturne = Harlem Nocturno. Live recordings from this period were later compiled on Live 79-81 (ROIR, 1985), which carries liner notes by Jim Jarmusch — an early marker of the Lurie/Jarmusch connection that would run through the decade.

Transition and Touring Lineup (1983–1985)

By 1983 the band had shed the No Wave alumni and settled into a working touring unit. The live album Live From The Drunken Boat (Europa Records, France, 1983) captures this version: John and Evan Lurie, Dougie Bowne (drums), Tony Garnier (bass), and Peter Zummo (trombone). Teo Macero co-produced. A compilation EP, Fusion (Europa Records, 1984), drew from similar sessions.

The Classic Sextet (1986–1988)

The mid-to-late 1980s lineup is considered the band’s mature peak. John Lurie consolidated writing and production duties and assembled a core ensemble:

  • Marc Ribot — guitar, banjo, cornet, trumpet
  • Dougie Bowne — drums
  • Erik Sanko — bass
  • Curtis Fowlkes — trombone
  • Roy Nathanson — reeds (alto/tenor/soprano/baritone saxophone)
  • E.J. Rodriguez — percussion
  • Evan Lurie — piano (continuing from the start)

Big Heart (Island Records, 1986) introduced this lineup, produced by John Lurie and Japanese engineer/producer Seigen Ono, with Jill Jaffe contributing violin on select tracks. No Pain For Cakes (Antilles, 1987) and Voice Of Chunk (Milan, 1988) continued in this vein, with John Lurie producing all three. His cover paintings began appearing on sleeves in this era.

Ono’s involvement as both engineer and co-producer on Big Heart and engineer/mixer on No Pain For Cakes reflects the band’s international currency: Seigen Ono was simultaneously working with major Japanese artists.

Berlin and the 1990s Lineup (1991–1998)

The early 1990s brought a significant personnel change. Out went Ribot, Nathanson, Fowlkes, Bowne, and Sanko; in came a new ensemble centered on:

  • Steven Bernstein — trumpet, cornet
  • Calvin Weston — drums
  • Oren Bloedow — bass
  • Michael Blake — soprano/tenor saxophone, bass clarinet
  • Jane Scarpantoni — cello
  • Billy Martin — percussion
  • Bryan Carrott — vibraphone, marimba, timpani

Berlin 1991 Part I (veraBra Records) and Live In Berlin 1991 Vol. II (veraBra, 1992) document this lineup live in Germany. John Lurie self-produced; Tom Lazarus mixed.

Queen Of All Ears (Strange & Beautiful Music, 1998) is the final proper studio album and marks the end of the band’s active recording run. Produced by John Lurie and Patrick Dillett, mixed by Dillett at Powerstation (NYC). The lineup retains Bernstein, Weston, Scarpantoni, Blake, and adds Erik Sanko (returning on bass) and Ben Perowsky (percussion); David Tronzo contributes slide guitar. John Lurie again painted the cover.

A limited pressing was also issued as Two Tracks From ‘Queen Of All Ears’ (New Note, UK, 1998) featuring John Lurie/Lounge Lizards.

Connections

  • John Lurie — co-founder, principal composer/saxophonist; also alias Marvin Pontiac; member of John Lurie National Orchestra
  • Evan Lurie — co-founder, pianist; John’s brother
  • Arto Lindsay — early guitarist; also DNA (4), Ambitious Lovers
  • Anton Fier — early drummer; also The Golden Palominos, Pere Ubu, Swans
  • Marc Ribot — guitarist 1986–1988; extensive solo career, Marc Ribot Y Los Cubanos Postizos, Marc Ribot’s Ceramic Dog
  • Jim Jarmusch — wrote liner notes for Live 79-81; directed films starring John Lurie (Stranger Than Paradise, Down By Law)
  • Teo Macero — produced debut; legendary Columbia Records producer (Miles Davis)
  • Seigen Ono — Japanese engineer/producer; co-produced Big Heart
  • Roy Nathanson — reeds 1986–1988; later co-founded The Jazz Passengers
  • Curtis Fowlkes — trombonist; also The Jazz Passengers
  • Steven Bernstein — trumpeter; also The Jazz Passengers, Sex Mob
  • Erik Sanko — bassist across two eras
  • Patrick Dillett — mixer/co-producer of Queen Of All Ears
  • Jane Scarpantoni — cellist, 1991–1998; downtown session mainstay
