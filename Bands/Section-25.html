Section 25

Post-punk / electronic. Formed Poulton-le-Fylde (near Blackpool), Lancashire, 1977/78. Factory Records throughout their original run. Still active.

Name taken from Section 25 of the Mental Health Act — the provision allowing compulsory detention.

Formation date conflict: November 1977 (Wikipedia) vs April 1978 (Discogs/Factory Benelux). Likely: brothers decided to form the band late 1977; rehearsals began April 1978. First gig: 1 June 1978, Lancaster City Football Club.

History

Formed as a duo by brothers Larry and Vin Cassidy in Poulton-le-Fylde, late 1977/April 1978. Larry had studied sculpture at Maidstone College of Art before returning to Blackpool. Phil Denton played guitar briefly from June 1978; Paul Wiggin replaced him November 1978.

Factory connection came through a June 1979 charity benefit at Blackpool Imperial Hotel, which the Cassidys organised and which featured Joy Division and Orchestral Manoeuvres In The Dark. Ian Curtis and Rob Gretton were impressed enough to bring the band to Cargo Studios, Rochdale, in September 1979 — recording “Girls Don’t Count,” “Knew Noise,” and “Up to You.” Both were uncredited on the release; Curtis brought a Syndrum to the session.

Debut single “Girls Don’t Count” (FAC 18, July 1980) was released nearly a year after recording, and after Curtis’s death in May 1980. His production credit is therefore posthumous. The sleeve was assembled by workers at a workshop for the deaf; the runout reads: “A FACTORY OUTING TO THE SEASIDE. PART TWO.”

Always Now (FACT 45, September 1981) produced by Martin Hannett at Britannia Row Studios (Pink Floyd’s recording facility), Islington, London. Recorded in 10 days, February 1981. Jon Caffery (Lord Of Knobs) engineered. Hannett also played uncredited synthesizer on “C.P.” Peter Saville designed the sleeve — waxed card matchbook-style pochette with French-sourced marble-paper interior, red seal, fold-out poster with Tibetan Buddhist text. First pressing of 10,000; packaging costs made the initial run slow to recoup.

The Key of Dreams (FBN 14, June 1982) was self-produced at SSRU, Blackpool — the band’s own rehearsal facility. Nine extended tracks; longest (“Sutra”) runs ~15 minutes.

Paul Wiggin departed September 1981 due to fear of flying. John Grice then toured with the band, followed by Lee Shallcross on percussion.

In 1982/83 Jenny Ross joined on keyboards/vocals; Angela Cassidy followed in 1984. This expanded lineup made From The Hip (FACT 90, March 1984) — recorded at Rockfield Studios, Monmouthshire, Wales, 1983 — with Bernard Sumner producing, credited as “Be Music” (New Order’s production alias). Engineer: Stewart Pickering. Peter Saville/Trevor Key designed the sleeve using the same color-coding system as Blue Monday and Power, Corruption & Lies.

“Looking From A Hilltop” became a club hit in New York and crossed over to black music radio in Chicago. No UK chart presence. A Warner Brothers deal was reportedly close but fell through.

In early 1983, Vin Cassidy identified a TB303 “squiggle effect” on a Roland TB303 bass synthesiser. The “Back To Wonder / Beating Heart” 12” (FAC 68, 1983) features this sound. The band then toured 16 North American cities in January–February 1985 with this material — including Chicago, then developing house music — approximately two years before acid house became a recognised European genre. Multiple Factory/band-aligned sources make this claim; no independent corroboration from the Chicago scene.

Note (web) — TB303/Chicago claim: research outcome

Claim reviewed June 2025. The TB303/acid house origin claim is made in the following sources, all of which are Factory- or band-aligned:

  • Band biography at section25.com: “a full year before these sounds reached Europe, an underdog group from Blackpool toured it across most of the major cities in the United States” including Chicago. The same page notes: “While it might seem unlikely that audience members in Chicago or Detroit were inspired by Section 25 to further refine house and techno styles, the fact remains…” — i.e. the band’s own bio hedges. (section25.com)
  • LTM Recordings biography (written by the label that holds the catalog): same wording. (ltmrecordings.com)
  • Factory Benelux expanded edition of From The Hip (FBN 90): liner notes by Vin Cassidy, Angie Cassidy, Bernard Sumner, and Jon Savage. Disc 4 (live North American tour 1985) is described as “showcasing the hard sequencer patterns and TB-303 ‘bass squiggle’ later adopted as a signature sound of acid house.” (factorybenelux.com)
  • Jon Savage contributed to those same liner notes — making him a Factory-aligned source in this context, not an independent historian.

“Looking From A Hilltop” and Chicago radio: Band bio and LTM bio both state the single “crossed over to black music stations in Chicago” and “consequently fed into the early house scene.” No Chicago DJ, station, or music historian is named as the source; this appears to be a claim originating with the band/label.

Independent corroboration: none found. Searches across Chicago house history sources, Ron Hardy / Frankie Knuckles / Larry Heard interviews and Wikipedia pages, Chicago house music Wikipedia, MusicRadar’s Chicago house history, and Red Bull Music Academy archives returned no mention of Section 25. No Chicago venue appearances are documented (no Muzic Box, Warehouse, or comparable venue named). Wikipedia’s Section 25 article does not mention Chicago, TB303, or acid house. Simon Reynolds’ Energy Flash is not indexed to Section 25 in connection with Chicago.

Conclusion: The claim is temporal (Section 25 played Chicago with TB303 sounds before acid house emerged in Europe) rather than documented influence. It is made consistently but exclusively by Factory/band-aligned sources. No independent corroboration from the Chicago side has been located.

Original band split 1985. Love & Hate (FACT 160) was recorded around 1985/86 but delayed by the Jonathan King publishing dispute (see below). Released March 1988.

Jonathan King / “Bad News Week” dispute: Factory publicists described the single as a cover of Jonathan King’s 1965 “It’s Good News Week.” King’s publishing company (Jonjo Music) claimed infringement. Section 25 were forced to forfeit 100% of publishing rights to settle. The “Bad News Week” single was recalled; Love & Hate was delayed approximately 18 months. A test pressing exists dated 1986; commercial release was March 1988. The album’s “Bad News Week” credit assigns publishing to “J King (Jonjo Music).”

Jenny Cassidy died 20 November 2004 of cancer, age 42. Cassidy brothers revived the band 2006 with a new lineup. Larry Cassidy died 27 February 2010, heart failure, age 56. Bethany Cassidy (Larry’s daughter) fronted the reformed band before joining Sea Fever. Retrofit (September 2010) had been completed before Larry’s death. Current lineup is Vin Cassidy and Stephen Stringer.

Kanye West: sampled “Hit” from Always Now on “FML” (feat. The Weeknd), The Life of Pablo (2016) — sample enters at ~2:55. Vin and Bethany Cassidy responded positively. Section 25 covered the Kanye track on Elektra (2018). Move On (2024) includes four tracks with Larry Cassidy involvement and guest guitar from Paul Wiggin (fear of flying apparently no longer an obstacle).

Factory Records Context

Signed to Factory 1980–1988. Tony Wilson: “Those clever bastards from Blackpool.” Peter Hook (New Order): “There were only three bands that ever made money on Factory and S25 was one of them.” Jon Savage: “Section 25 have evolved from obdurate nihilists into post-punk pioneers.”

Peter Saville designed sleeves for FAC 18, FACT 45, FACT 90 (with Trevor Key), and Dark Light. The From The Hip cover uses the same color-coding system as Blue Monday (FAC 73) and Power, Corruption & Lies (FACT 75). The Be Music alias (26 shared releases) is New Order’s production guise.

Factory collapsed 1992; LTM acquired the catalog and began reissuing from the mid-1990s. Factory Benelux revived from c.2013, issuing expanded editions.

Sampled by: The Shamen (“Make It Mine / Progress 1 Vox”), Orbital, Code 6 (“Off the Hilltop”), Kanye West (“FML” feat. The Weeknd, The Life of Pablo, 2016).

The Guardian (2014): “a revelation” / “the best new/old band in Britain.” The Wire on Always Now: “his [Hannett’s] crowning achievement.” The Wire on From The Hip: “the group’s masterpiece.” The Guardian included From The Hip in its “1000 Albums to Hear Before You Die” list.
