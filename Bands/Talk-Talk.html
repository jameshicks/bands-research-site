Talk Talk

Synth-pop / art rock / post-rock. London, UK. Active 1981–1991. Discogs ID: 60480.

Formed in London in 1981 around vocalist and songwriter Mark Hollis. Began as a commercial synth-pop act on EMI and scored a run of UK and European hits; gradually abandoned pop entirely across three albums, arriving at two of the most radical and influential records of the late 1980s and early 1990s. Spirit of Eden (1988) and Laughing Stock (1991) are canonical reference points for post-rock, ambient, and experimental music. The band dissolved in 1991–92. Mark Hollis died 25 February 2019.

Biography

Formation and synth-pop era (1981–1983)

Talk Talk formed in London in 1981. The core lineup was Mark Hollis (vocals, keyboards, guitar), Lee Harris (drums), Paul Webb (bass), and Simon Brenner (keyboards). Hollis had previously been in a band called The Reaction; his brother Ed Hollis co-wrote the debut single “Talk Talk.” Harris and Webb had played together in Eskalator, a Southend reggae band, before joining Talk Talk.

The band signed to EMI and released their debut album The Party’s Over in 1982, produced by Colin Thurston (who had worked on early Duran Duran and David Bowie records). Synth-pop with slick production; singles “Talk Talk” and “Today” charted in the UK.

It’s My Life and the Friese-Greene partnership (1984)

For the second album, Simon Brenner departed. Tim Friese-Greene — at that point primarily a producer and engineer, with credits including Thomas Dolby’s “She Blinded Me With Science” — was brought in to assist. He and Hollis quickly became a songwriting partnership that would define the rest of Talk Talk’s existence. Friese-Greene is credited as producer and keyboard player on It’s My Life (1984, EMI, cat. TALK 1 / EMC 2400021).

It’s My Life is a transitional record: still synth-pop in form, but more muscular than the debut. The singles “It’s My Life” and “Such a Shame” were the band’s biggest commercial hits. Contributing musicians included Robbie McIntosh (guitar), Ian Curnow (keyboards), Morris Pert (percussion), Henry Lowther (trumpet), and Phil Ramacon (piano). Friese-Greene did not tour with the band; he remained studio-only throughout his time with Talk Talk.

The Colour of Spring and the turn (1986)

With all songs now co-written by Hollis and Friese-Greene, The Colour of Spring (1986, EMI, cat. CDP 7462282) marked the pivot. Still melodic and accessible enough to yield the hit single “Life’s What You Make It,” but the production opened up: live musicians, acoustic textures, space. A large ensemble appeared across the album’s tracks.

Spirit of Eden (1988)

The break with pop convention. Spirit of Eden (Parlophone, cat. 064 7 46977 1) was recorded in sessions where large groups of musicians improvised in a darkened studio; Hollis and Friese-Greene then edited and assembled the results over months. EMI attempted to sue Talk Talk when the album proved uncommercial — they had expected another Colour of Spring. The suit was dropped.

The record consists of six pieces: “The Rainbow,” “Eden,” “Desire” (Side A); “Inheritance,” “I Believe in You,” “Wealth” (Side B).

Laughing Stock and dissolution (1991)

The final album. Laughing Stock (1991) was released on Verve Records / Polydor (LP cat. 847 717-1; CD cat. 847 717-2) — Verve being the jazz imprint of Polydor, chosen deliberately. Recorded at Wessex Sound Studios, September 1990–April 1991. The method matched Spirit of Eden — extended improvised sessions, heavy post-production editing — but the results were sparser, more openly jazz-inflected. Six tracks: “Myrrhman,” “Ascension Day,” “After the Flood,” “Taphead,” “New Grass,” “Runeii.”

Paul Webb had left the band after Spirit of Eden (1988); the Laughing Stock sessions were in practice a Hollis / Friese-Greene / Harris creation with session musicians filling the bass chair.

Legacy & Influence

Spirit of Eden and Laughing Stock are canonical reference points for post-rock. The term “post-rock” itself was coined by Simon Reynolds in a March 1994 Mojo review of Bark Psychosis’s Hex, which he described as following in the footsteps of Spirit of Eden. AllMusic: “the musical foundation for post-rock crystallized in 1991, with the release of two very different landmarks: Talk Talk’s Laughing Stock and Slint’s Spiderland.”

Documented influence connections:

  • Bark Psychosis — cited Talk Talk as an early influence; Graham Sutton later collaborated with Lee Harris and Paul Webb in .O.Rang. The coinage of “post-rock” was made explicitly in a Talk Talk ambient context.
  • Elbow — Guy Garvey: “Volume dynamics are an essential part of classical music, but a lost art with guitar music” — credited Talk Talk with shaping Elbow’s dynamic approach.
  • Graham Coxon — cited Spirit of Eden as an influence.
  • Doves — cited Spirit of Eden as an influence (Wikipedia; no specific quote documented).
  • Bedhead — cited Spirit of Eden as an influence (Wikipedia; no specific quote documented).
  • Jenny Hval — called Laughing Stock “an incredibly intuitive and bare recording.”
  • King Creosote — called Laughing Stock “the wayward, grubby wee brother” of Spirit of Eden.
  • Wild Beasts (Tom Fleming) — praised Laughing Stock’s achievement in minimalism.
  • Critics group Sigur Rós, Godspeed You! Black Emperor, Low, Spiritualized, Tortoise, and latter-period Radiohead as heirs to the Talk Talk template; no direct quotes from those acts confirmed in sources consulted.

Post-dissolution

  • Lee Harris + Paul Webb → .O.Rang (1994–1997, Echo label); two albums
  • Mark Hollis → self-titled solo album (1998, Polydor 537 688-2); retired from music thereafter; died 25 February 2019
  • Paul Webb → Rustin Man; Out of Season with Beth Gibbons (2002, Domino)
  • Lee Harris → Bark Psychosis Codename: Dustsucker (2004, Fire); Beth Gibbons Lives Outgrown (2024, Domino)
