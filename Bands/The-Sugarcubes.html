The Sugarcubes

Icelandic alternative rock / indie pop group. Reykjavík, Iceland. Discogs ID: 9452. Active 1986–1992.

Formed from the remnants of Kukl and the wider Reykjavík underground art-punk scene, The Sugarcubes became the first Icelandic band to achieve significant international commercial and critical success. They are best known for bringing Björk (Björk Guðmundsdóttir) to international attention. Signed to One Little Indian in the UK and Elektra in the US, the group released three studio albums before disbanding in 1992. Their debut single “Birthday” (1987) remains a landmark of UK indie.

Icelandic name variants: Sykurmolarnir, Sykurmolar. Also credited as Sugar Cubes and Sugarcubes on various releases.

Formation

By the band’s own legend, The Sugarcubes formed on 8 June 1986 — the same day vocalist Björk gave birth to her son Sindri. The core came directly out of Kukl, the noisy post-punk group active from 1983–1986 that had recorded two albums for the UK anarchist label Crass (the Crass label). Kukl’s membership included Björk, Einar Örn Benediktsson (vocals / trumpet), and Siggi Baldursson (drums). For the Sugarcubes, they added Björk’s then-husband Þór Eldon on guitar, Bragi Ólafsson on bass, and Margrét Örnólfsdóttir (known as Magga) on keyboards. Also listed in Discogs membership: Friðrik Erlingsson (Discogs artist ID: 621374), whose role has not been fully established.

The group were also co-founders of Smekkleysa (Bad Taste Ltd.), the Reykjavík collective / record label that released their 1986 Icelandic debut.

Life’s Too Good attracted strong reviews on both sides of the Atlantic and became a college radio staple in the US. The tension it created between Björk and Einar Örn — as critics lavished disproportionate praise on Björk — would become a defining dynamic of the band’s career.

Margrét Örnólfsdóttir (keyboards) is not individually credited on the Discogs UK LP entry for this album; she appears in the band’s membership list but not the release credits for this pressing.

Stick Around for Joy received stronger reviews than the second album, representing a tighter and more polished sound under Fox’s production. However it did not generate a breakout single, and following the album’s release the band disbanded.

Derek Birkett was the head of One Little Indian and served as producer on the first two albums. Ray Shulman (of prog-rock group Gentle Giant) co-produced the debut and the “Birthday” single. Paul Fox (US-based producer/musician) helmed the final album.

Dissolution and Aftermath

After the Stick Around for Joy tour concluded, the Sugarcubes formally disbanded in 1992. Björk launched a solo career in 1993, beginning with Debut (One Little Indian), which was critically acclaimed and commercially successful in the UK and beyond — establishing her as one of the most significant artists of the 1990s.

Einar Örn Benediktsson continued working in Icelandic music and media; Þór Eldon remained active in the Reykjavík music scene. Bragi Ólafsson pursued a writing career. Siggi Baldursson continued session work and production.
