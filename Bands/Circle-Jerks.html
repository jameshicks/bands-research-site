Circle Jerks

Southern California hardcore punk band. Discogs artist ID: 253257.

Formed in Los Angeles in 1979 by vocalist Keith Morris (ex-Black Flag) and guitarist Greg Hetson. One of the founding acts of the American hardcore punk movement, alongside Black Flag, the Germs, and the Adolescents. The band is distinguished by a short, explosive debut record (Group Sex, 1980 — 14 tracks in under 15 minutes) and a visual identity closely tied to illustrator Shawn Kerri and photographers Ed Colver and Glen E. Friedman. The band has broken up and reformed multiple times; the core of Morris and Hetson has persisted across all eras.

Name variations: “The Circle Jerks”, “C. Jerks”

History by Era

Formation (1979)

Keith Morris had been the original vocalist of Black Flag from the band’s formation through early 1979, when he left or was asked to leave (accounts differ). He formed Circle Jerks almost immediately, recruiting guitarist Greg Hetson, bassist Roger Rogerson (real name Roger Thomas Dowding, also credited as Roger Dowding), and drummer Lucky Lehrer (real name Keith Lehrer). The band played its first shows in late 1979.

Morris brought the confrontational, high-velocity approach he had developed in Black Flag but pivoted toward shorter, more sardonic songs — the Circle Jerks would become a primary influence on the “fast hardcore” style that defined 1980–1982 West Coast punk. Hetson simultaneously played in Red Kross (Redd Kross) during this period before that became a full commitment.

Group Sex Era (1980–1981)

The debut LP Group Sex was recorded and released in 1980 on Frontier Records (catalog FLP-1001). At 14 tracks in approximately 15 minutes it set a template for hardcore brevity. Producer: Cary Markoff (co-produced with the band). Engineers: Ben Lesko, Cliff Zellman, Ken Lauber. Mastered by Frank DeLuna. Photography: Ed Colver. Design and artwork: Diane Zincavage; logo artwork by Doug Drug. Illustrator Shawn Kerri contributed insert cartoon art — her skanking figure (“Skank Man”) would become an enduring emblem of the band.

Lisa Fancher of Frontier Records is credited with an unusual “Caused By” credit on the original pressing, acknowledging her role in getting the record made.

Wild In The Streets Era (1982)

The second LP, Wild In The Streets, appeared in 1982. The US pressing was on Faulty Products (a sublabel of I.R.S. Records); a UK pressing appeared on Step-Forward Records. Producers: David Anderle and Gary Hirstius. Engineers: Paul McKenna, Steve Katz. Photography: Ed Colver (front cover), Glen E. Friedman (back and inner). Artwork: Shawn Kerri (drawings), Carl Grasso (additional artwork). Mastered by Frank DeLuna.

The album represented a slight broadening of sound from the debut — slightly longer tracks, a wider range of tempos — while maintaining the sardonic lyrical approach.

Lucky Lehrer and Roger Rogerson remained the rhythm section through this record. Rogerson would play on one further album (Golden Shower of Hits) before departing; he died in 1996.

Golden Shower of Hits and Lineup Flux (1983)

The third LP, Golden Shower of Hits (1983, LAX Records), introduced a new drummer, John Ingram, while Rogerson contributed guitar and bass. Producer: Jerry Goldstein. Photography: Glen E. Friedman. Design: Mike Doud (Doud of Larchmont). The record mixed original material with parodic medleys of pop songs (the Jerks On 45 side), including covers built around songs by Burt Bacharach, Hal David, Paul Anka, and others.

Lineup continued to shift in 1983: Chuck Biscuits (real name Charles Montgomery) replaced Ingram on drums for a period, followed by the addition of bassist Earl Liberty (real name Mark Vidal, also credited as Mark Vidal in Discogs member list) in 1983–1984, who replaced Rogerson.

Wönderful and VI Era (1984–1990)

By Wönderful (1985, Combat Core), the stable lineup was Morris/Hetson/Zander Schloss (bass)/Keith Clark (drums). Schloss (credited as “Zander ‘Snake’ Schloss”) was also an actor and musician with connections to Joe Strummer and The Weirdos. Producer: Circle Jerks, co-produced by Karat Faye. Engineer: Karat Faye, with Jim McMahon as assistant engineer. Photography: Peter Oliver (2) (front), Gary Leonard (back). Mastered by Ken Perry.

VI (1987, Relativity) retained the same quartet. Producers: Circle Jerks and Karat Faye. Engineer: Karat Faye. Mastered by Eddy Schreyer. Cover art: Ed Repka (front), Shawn Kerri’s “Skank Man” reused on the back. Photography: Gary Leonard.

The band broke up in 1990 following a period of diminishing returns commercially.

Gig and Oddities (1992–1995)

Gig (1992, Relativity) was a live album recorded during the VI-era touring lineup (Morris/Hetson/Schloss/Clark). Produced by Paul du Gré. Mixed by Paul du Gré and Carlo Nuccio (live sound). Recorded by Studio On Wheels. Mastered by Eddy Schreyer. The written-by credits across the live tracklist document the full range of Circle Jerks songwriting credits, attributing compositions primarily to Morris and Hetson with contributions from Rogerson, Lehrer, Schloss, Clark, and Harlan Hollander.

Oddities, Abnormalities & Curiosities (1995, Mercury) was produced by Niko Bolas and represented the band’s major-label moment — a late entry in the ’90s era when hardcore-adjacent bands received major-label signings. The album featured a guest turn from Debbie Gibson.

The band again broke up following the Mercury period.

Reformations (2001–2011 and 2019–present)

Circle Jerks reformed in 2001 with Morris, Hetson, Schloss, and drummer Kevin Fitzgerald. A live album, Live At The House Of Blues (2004, Kung Fu Records), documents this era.

They broke up again in 2011 after Morris founded OFF! (formed with Dimitri Coats, Mario Rubalcaba, and others). OFF! released several records and became a prominent vehicle for Morris’s hardcore return.

A further reunion in 2019 brought the classic lineup back (Morris, Hetson, Schloss). Joey Castillo replaced Kevin Fitzgerald on drums by 2021. The band released a split 7” with Descendents in 2024 (Trust Records).

Connections

  • Black Flag — Keith Morris was founding vocalist of Black Flag (Discogs ID 253278); left 1979 to form Circle Jerks. Greg Ginn’s SST label orbit overlapped with the same LA scene
  • OFF! — Keith Morris’s post-Circle Jerks hardcore band (formed ~2009); members include Dimitri Coats, Mario Rubalcaba, Steven McDonald
  • Bad Religion — Greg Hetson was a full member of Bad Religion (Discogs ID 17479) from the mid-1980s onward, running both bands simultaneously
  • Redd Kross — Greg Hetson played guitar in early Redd Kross (Discogs ID 187786)
  • Descendents — Split 7” (2024, Trust Records); shared SoCal hardcore lineage; Bill Stevenson (Descendents) also played in Black Flag
  • Germs — same LA hardcore scene; Ed Colver shot both bands
  • Suicidal Tendencies — same Frontier Records roster; Glen E. Friedman produced/photographed the ST debut
  • Penelope Spheeris — directed The Decline of Western Civilization (1981), which documents Circle Jerks as a performing band
  • Shawn Kerri — illustrator whose “Skank Man” figure became the band’s visual signature
  • Ed Colver — shot Group Sex and Wild In The Streets (front cover); core SoCal hardcore photographer
  • Glen E. Friedman — shot Wild In The Streets back/inner and Golden Shower of Hits; peer of Colver in SoCal hardcore photography
  • Frontier Records — home label for debut; also home of Adolescents, Suicidal Tendencies, and much SoCal hardcore
  • Faulty Products — I.R.S. Records sublabel that issued Wild In The Streets in the US
  • I.R.S. Records — parent of Faulty Products
