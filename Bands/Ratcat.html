Ratcat

Australian indie pop / alternative rock band. Formed 1985 in Sydney. Active late 1980s–early 1990s; reunited and self-released in 2023. Discogs artist ID: 439070

Biography

Formation and Early Recordings (1985–1989)

Ratcat formed in Sydney in 1985, anchored from the outset by vocalist and guitarist Simon Day. The earliest documented lineup on the 1987 self-titled EP (Waterfront Records) was a trio: Day on guitar and vocals, Victor Levi on bass and vocals, and Trevor Wintle on drums. The band produced itself in partnership with Mark Roxburgh, Phil Kerney, and Steve Clarke (2), with Clarke also engineering. Mastering fell to Leon Zervos.

The 1987 EP appeared on Waterfront Records, the Sydney-based independent that also housed the Saints and the Hummingbirds. The band remained self-produced through their next two Waterfront releases: the single This Nightmare (1989) and the single Saying Goodbye (1990). Engineer John Hresc is credited on both of these later Waterfront recordings. This Nightmare introduced guest vocalist Robyn St. Clare (also of The Hummingbirds and Godstar), who would return on multiple subsequent records.

rooArt Signing and Commercial Breakthrough (1990–1991)

By 1990 Ratcat had moved to rooArt, the Sydney-based independent distributed by Phonogram. The debut rooArt album, Tingles (1990), was also issued in the US on Mercury Records — unusual reach for an Australian indie act at this moment. Nick Mainsbridge produced Tingles and became the band’s primary producer going forward. The lineup had shifted: Andrew Polin was now on drums and vocals; bass duties passed through several hands over this period (see Members table). The album benefited from Mainsbridge’s clean, radio-friendly production and made Ratcat one of the more prominent acts in the early-1990s Australian alternative mainstream.

Single releases from rooArt in 1991 — Blind Love, Don’t Go Now, and Baby Baby — continued to credit Mainsbridge as producer. Blind Love and Don’t Go Now feature guest backing vocals from both Robyn St. Clare and Margaret Urlich (NZ pop/R&B singer, also a member of Peking Man and When The Cat’s Away). Blind Love credits Robbie Rowlands and Scott Christie as assistant producers alongside Mainsbridge.

Nic Dalton — prolific Australian indie figure, later a member of The Lemonheads (1993–96) and central to the Half a Cow Records ecosystem — served a stint as bassist. He appears in the Discogs member list and on the 2001 compilation credits.

The live album Alive (1991, rooArt/Phonogram) was recorded by Phillip McKellar (credited as Phil McKellar), with live sound by Matt Crosbie. Nick Mainsbridge remixed it for release. Cover design was shared by Paul McNeil and Simon Day. Alive documents the band at their commercial peak.

Insideout and Decline (1992–1993)

The second rooArt studio album, Insideout (1992), marked a slight shift in personnel and sound. Simon Day is credited solo for vocals, guitar, bass, and artwork/photography — suggesting a more project-like configuration. Andrew Polin returned on drums and percussion and co-wrote several tracks. John Fryer mixed the album (Fryer also worked extensively with 4AD acts and Nine Inch Nails); engineer was Colin Simkins; Daniel Denholm provided programming. Nick Mainsbridge again produced.

The singles Candyman (1992) and Holiday (1992) followed. Holiday introduced a new bassist, Steve Garvey, and backing vocals from Suzie Higgie; Daniel Denholm again provided programming.

The band appears to have wound down after Insideout without a formal announcement on the Discogs record.

Compilation and Legacy (2001)

Twisted Tails (A Ratcat Best Of…) (2001, rooArt/BMG Australia) compiled the career retrospectively. The credits page in that release is the most complete document of Ratcat’s full bass-chair history, listing Amr Zaid, Marc Scully, Nic Dalton, Steve Garvey, Victor Levi, and John McAteer. The full recording-personnel list runs to fifteen names and confirms the range of studios and engineers the band worked with across their career: Tony Cohen, William Bowden, David Lumsdaine, Paul McKercher, and Chris Thompson (2) among them.

Reunion: All Stripped Back (2023)

In 2023 Ratcat self-released All Stripped Back on Not On Label, with a stripped acoustic lineup: Simon Day (vocals, lead guitar, ukulele, mandolin), Nic Dalton (bass guitar), Reuben Alexander (2) (drums and percussion), Michaela Davies (autoharp), and Jasmine Chew (backing vocals, glockenspiel).

Connections

  • Nic Dalton went on to bass duties in The Lemonheads (1993–96) and ran the Half a Cow Records indie label
  • Robyn St. Clare also sang on releases by The Hummingbirds and Godstar
  • Margaret Urlich was a prominent NZ-born pop artist in Australia in this era; also in Peking Man and When The Cat’s Away
  • Nick Mainsbridge later performed in Deprogram
  • Phillip McKellar went on to record Dirty Three (1994) and produce Grinspoon’s debut
  • John Fryer worked extensively with 4AD acts and Nine Inch Nails; his mixing of Insideout is an unusual international credit for an Australian indie record
  • Simon Day also played in 51 Monday and The Charismatics (4)
  • rooArt was part of the same early-90s Australian indie wave as acts like Died Pretty, The Hummingbirds, and Kim Salmon And The Surrealists

Open Questions
