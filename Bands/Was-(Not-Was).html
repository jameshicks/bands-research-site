Was (Not Was)

Discogs artist ID: 30093

Detroit avant-funk / art-pop ensemble founded 1979 by Don Was (born Donald Edward Fagenson) and David Was (born David Jay Weiss). Operated as a rotating collective rather than a conventional band — the two founders wrote and produced everything, marshalling a large cast of Detroit session players and celebrity guest vocalists through successive major-label homes. Active 1980–1992, reformed 2004, reunion album Boo! in 2008. Don Was went on to become one of the most prominent record producers of the 1990s–2000s (Bonnie Raitt, Rolling Stones, Bob Dylan).

History

Formation and Detroit Roots (1979–1981)

Don Fagenson (bass, keyboards, production) and David Weiss (winds, keyboards, lyrics) grew up in suburban Detroit, met at Oak Park High School, and formed the project around 1979. The name itself is a sardonic non-sequitur — a hallmark of David Was’s absurdist lyrical mode.

Their debut release was the 12” single “Wheel Me Out” (1980, Celluloid — France), which caught the attention of ZE Records in New York. Michael Zilkha, ZE’s co-owner, signed them and executive-produced the debut LP.

The debut album Was (Not Was) (1981, ZE Records / Island Records) established the template: lead vocals by Sweet Pea Atkinson and Harry Bowens, backed by a large Detroit ensemble including Wayne Kramer (MC5) on guitar, Marcus Belgrave on trumpet and flugelhorn, David McMurray on saxophone, and Luis Resto on synthesizers and piano. Don Was engineered and co-produced with David Was and Jack Tann.

The album produced the first charting single — a remix of “Out Come The Freaks” — and established the band’s reputation for outlandish casting. Michael Zilkha remained executive producer.

Breakthrough: What Up, Dog? (1988)

After a mid-decade gap (one single, “Robot Girl,” on Mercury in 1986), the duo re-emerged with pre-singles on Fontana — “Spy In The House Of Love” and “Walk The Dinosaur” (both 1987) — which made the UK charts. “Walk The Dinosaur” became their signature hit.

What Up, Dog? (Discogs 321571; US: Chrysalis FV 41664; Europe: Fontana) is the most commercially successful album, reaching the US and UK charts.

Are You Okay? (1990) and Dissolution

The fourth album (Discogs 126390, Fontana) continued the celebrity guest approach, with Leonard Cohen delivering the lead vocal on “Elvis’ Rolls Royce” (with Iggy Pop on backing vocals) and The Roches appearing on “Maria Novarro.” Syd Straw sang lead on “You! You! You!” Doug Fieger returned as a backing vocalist on two tracks.

“Papa Was A Rollin’ Stone” was released as a single (US: Chrysalis, 1990) — a Motown cover with a rap update.

The group toured to support the album, then dissolved by 1992. A second compilation, Hello, Dad…I’m In Jail (Fontana, 1992), gathered hits and rarities.

Hiatus and Reunion

The duo were largely absent from recording through the 1990s, though Don Was built a parallel career as a sought-after producer. David Was wrote, worked in journalism, and continued in music on a smaller scale.

In 2004, a Spectrum Music compilation (The Collection) renewed interest. The duo reformed and recorded Boo! (2008, Rykodisc RCD 10943), the first new album in eighteen years.

Boo! (Discogs 1301265) returned to the core-vocalists format — Sweet Pea Atkinson, Harry Bowens, and Donald Ray Mitchell all contributed lead vocals — with a broader palette of guests: Kris Kristofferson (vocals on “Green Pills In The Dresser”), Booker T. Jones (Hammond B3), Wayne Kramer (guitar), Marcus Miller (bass), Tim Drummond (bass), David Campbell (string arrangements). One track covers a Bob Dylan composition; David Was drew the cover illustration himself.

Don Was: Production Career

Following the success of What Up, Dog? (1988), Don Was became one of the most in-demand producers in mainstream American music through the 1990s and 2000s. Key clients and records:

  • Bonnie Raitt — Nick Of Time (Capitol, 1989; Grammy AOTY 1990), Luck Of The Draw (1991), Longing In Their Hearts (1994), Road Tested (1995), Fundamental (1998)
  • The B-52’s — Cosmic Thing (Reprise, 1989) — “Love Shack,” “Roam”
  • Bob Dylan — Under The Red Sky (CBS, 1990)
  • Iggy Pop — Brick By Brick (Virgin, 1990); Avenue B (Virgin, 1999)
  • The Rolling Stones — Voodoo Lounge (Virgin, 1994, with The Glimmer Twins), Stripped (1995), Bridges to Babylon (1997, executive producer), A Bigger Bang (2005), Exile On Main St. (2010 reissue)
  • Cheb Khaled — Khaled (Barclay, 1992) — breakthrough album of Algerian raï in the West; also on Chebba (Mango, 1993) and Ya-Rayi (Universal, 2004)
  • Randy Newman — Randy Newman’s Faust (Reprise, 1995)
  • Kris Kristofferson — This Old Road (New West, 2006) — also played acoustic bass and piano; wrote liner notes
  • Norah Jones — Last Days At The Lodge (Blue Note, 2008)

Was also directed the Brian Wilson documentary I Just Wasn’t Made For These Times (1995). He has been President of Blue Note Records since 2012.
