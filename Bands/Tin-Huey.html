Tin Huey

American art-punk / new wave band formed in Akron, Ohio (USA). Active 1972–1983 and again 1999–2011. Discogs artist ID: 312146.

Overview

Tin Huey were one of the central acts of the late-1970s Akron punk/new-wave scene — the same concentrated milieu that produced Devo, The Bizarros, Rubber City Rebels, and The Waitresses. The band’s classic lineup was guitarist/vocalist Michael Aylward, drummer/percussionist Stuart Austin, multi-reed player Ralph Carney, guitarist/vocalist Chris Butler, bassist/vocalist Mark Price (4), and pianist/guitarist/keyboardist Harvey Gold.

Tin Huey debuted with the four-track 7” EP “Puppet Wipes” (1977, Clone Records, cat# CL 002) — the second release on the Akron-based indie founded by Nick Nicholis of The Bizarros. The label documented the emerging Akron scene through a series of singles and the From Akron LP (1977). A second Clone single, “Breakfast With The Hueys” (1978, CL004), followed. Warner Bros. Records then signed the band and released Contents Dislodged During Shipment (1979, BSK 3297), produced by Paul Wexler, which stands as one of the few major-label art-punk LPs of the era. A promotional single for “I’m A Believer” (a Neil Diamond cover) was also pressed.

After the band’s initial dissolution in 1983, a posthumous archival release appeared: Before Obscurity: The Bushflow Tapes (2009, Smog Veil Records, cat# SV83), an 18-track CD of early recordings including “Slide,” “Pink Berets,” and “Squirm You Worm.” The band later reunited for the 1999–2011 period.

Three members — Chris Butler, Ralph Carney, and Stuart Austin — went on to core roles in The Waitresses. Harvey Gold also released a solo single on Clone Records (“Experiments,” 1978, CL005) featuring Austin on drums. Ralph Carney became one of the most extensively credentialed session players from the Akron scene, appearing on Tom Waits albums (Rain Dogs, Frank’s Wild Years, Big Time, Night on Earth), B-52’s (Mesopotamia, Whammy!), Allen Ginsberg, and many others.

Clone Records Context

Clone Records (Discogs label ID: 43697) was founded in 1977 by Nick Nicholis of The Bizarros in Akron, Ohio. It operated until around 1981 and released approximately 23 titles. Key artists documented on the label include the Bizarros, Rubber City Rebels, Tin Huey, The Waitresses, Teacher’s Pet, the Human Switchboard, and Unit 5. Tin Huey appeared on three Clone releases: “Puppet Wipes” (CL 002), “Breakfast With The Hueys” (CL004), and the Harvey Gold solo single “Experiments” (CL005) — the latter featuring Stuart Austin on drums and Gold as engineer.

Member Projects and Connections

Chris Butler

See Chris Butler for full coverage. Butler founded and led The Waitresses (1978–1984), writing their entire catalog including “I Know What Boys Like” and “Christmas Wrapping.” Later solo output on Future Fossil Records (1995) and Smog Veil Records (2018, with Ralph Carney). Production credits for Scruffy the Cat, Freedy Johnston, The dB’s. Electronic alias Charas (Cowboy Records UK, 1992).

Ralph Carney

See Ralph Carney for full coverage. Carney’s post-Tin Huey career extended across jazz, avant-garde, and session work. Solo primary-credit releases: Happiness Finally Came To Them (1987, Shimmy Disc), Black Power (1994, Shimmy Disc), Ralph Sounds (1997, Birdman Records), “BoJack’s Theme” (2014, Nonesuch). Session and contributor credits include: the B-52’s (Mesopotamia 1982, Whammy! 1989), Tom Waits (Rain Dogs 1985, Frank’s Wild Years 1987, Big Time 1988, Night on Earth 1991), Allen Ginsberg (The Lion For Real 1989, Hydrogen Jukebox 1993), They Might Be Giants, Jim Carroll (Catholic Boy), Hunters & Collectors, and many more. 2018 joint release Songs For Unsung Holidays with Chris Butler (Smog Veil Records).

Harvey Gold

Discogs primary credit: the 7” solo single “Experiments” (1978, Clone Records CL005) — two tracks: “Keep A Close Watch” (written by John Cale) and “Armadillo” (Gold original). Performed with Tin Huey members Stuart Austin (drums), Mark Price (engineer), and John Mondl (engineer). Later group credit: Golems of the Red Planet (Discogs artist ID: 8701366). Gold was one of the principal songwriters for Tin Huey, credited as co-writer on the majority of the band’s recorded output.

Mark Price (4)

Discogs ID: 1810694. Bassist and vocalist for Tin Huey. Also a member of Wild Butter (Discogs artist ID: 1682354). Discogs shows no primary solo discography beyond Tin Huey and Wild Butter.

Michael Aylward

Stuart Austin
