The Waldos

Origin: New York City, USA Active: 1991–c. 2019 Also credited as: Walter Lure & The Waldos; Waldos

New York rock band led by Walter Lure, guitarist for The Heartbreakers (2). Formed after the Heartbreakers wound down, with two founding members — Joey Pinter (guitar) and Tony Coiro (bass) — drawn from Knots (2), a 1980 NYC punk single act. The Waldos served as Lure’s primary vehicle for the rest of his life, releasing two studio albums and several live records across three decades.

Discogs artist ID: 1218224

History

Formation (1991)

Walter Lure assembled the Waldos in 1991, shortly after The Heartbreakers (2) had effectively ceased to be a working band following Johnny Thunders’s death in April of that year. Lure recruited Joey Pinter and Tony Coiro — both alumni of Knots (2), a short-lived NYC punk band that had released one single on their own Ideal Records in 1980. The Waldos’ first release was the “Crazy Little Baby” 7” (Baylor Records, 1991), with Charlie Sox on drums. Lure co-produced with David Eng; John Ficarrotta engineered. The A-side was written by Lure; the B-side, “Cry Baby,” was a Coiro composition with backing vocals from Jynx Lynx and Odetta Mae Jackson.

Rent Party and Coiro’s death (1994–1995)

The band’s debut full-length, Rent Party, was released in 1994 on Sympathy For The Record Industry (SFTRI 269). The lineup for this record was Lure (guitar, vocals), Pinter (guitar), Coiro (bass, vocals), and Jeff West (4) (drums). The album draws heavily on Heartbreakers-era material — “Crazy Little Baby,” “Cry Baby,” and “Flight” among the 12 tracks — as well as Waldos originals. The record was pressed on CD in the US (manufactured in Canada by Americ Disc) and later reissued by Jungle Records in the UK in 2013 and again in 2021.

Tony Coiro died in 1995. His presence on subsequent releases (Thunderstorm In Detroit, 2001; Eve Of Destruction, 2004) is from archival recordings made before his death.

Continuation through the 2000s

The band continued with evolving lineups. A 2001 CDr on Pontiac Records attributed to Johnny Thunders (Such A Crime) featured Lure on guitar and vocals, but that release was an unofficial promo of Thunders’ own material with Lure as a backing player — not a Waldos record proper.

Walter Lure recorded Live In Berlin (Tornado Ride Records, 2007, released 2008) under his own name with an Italian backing band (Rina Regina, bass; Paolo Serlino, drums; Annette Gucci, guitar; Dee Jaywalker, guitar/vocals). This is a solo live document rather than a canonical Waldos release, though it drew from the same repertoire.

Late period: Live In Brooklyn and final album (2017–2018)

The Waldos had a productive late run. Live In Brooklyn was released as an LP on O-Rama (MVD9501LP) in 2017 as a Record Store Day limited edition. The lineup featured Lure (lead vocals, guitar), Takto Nakai (guitar), Joe Rizzo (drums), and EZ (6) (bass). Mas Yamagata co-produced; David Gereg recorded and mixed; Alan Douches mastered. The set leaned on Heartbreakers covers (“Chinese Rocks,” “Born To Lose,” “Pirate Love,” “London Boys”) alongside Waldos originals.

The final studio album, Wacka Lacka Loom Bop A Loom Bam Boo (Cleopatra, CLO0852, 2018), was credited to “Walter Lure & The Waldos” and produced by Andy Shernoff (of The Dictators). The lineup was Lure (guitar, vocals), Takanori Ichiuji (bass, vocals), Joe Rizzo (drums, vocals), and Takto Nakai (guitar, vocals). Joey Pinter played a guitar solo on “Take A Chance On Me” — his only documented appearance on a post-1994 Waldos release. The album featured guest harmonica from Randy Pratt and Brian Hurd, saxophone from Arno Hecht and Danny Ray (5), and backing vocals from Andy Shernoff himself and Tricia Scotti. J.Z. Barrell engineered; Joe Blaney mixed; Paul Gold mastered. Most originals were written by Lure, with co-writes with Nakai, Nolan, Rath, and Joe Rizzo’s collaborators Shige Matsumoto and Vincent Martone.

Walter Lure died on August 21, 2020.

Connections

  • The Heartbreakers (2) — Walter Lure’s prior band; the Waldos’ repertoire drew heavily on Heartbreakers songs
  • Knots (2) — source of Joey Pinter and Tony Coiro; the two bands share a direct NYC underground punk lineage
  • Johnny Thunders — Heartbreakers co-founder; several Thunders compositions appear in the Waldos live set
  • Jerry Nolan — Heartbreakers drummer; his compositions (“One Track Mind,” “Get Off The Phone,” “Count Down Love,” “Take A Chance”) recur in Waldos setlists
  • Andy Shernoff — produced Wacka Lacka; also fronted The Dictators, another first-wave NYC punk act
  • Billy Rath — Heartbreakers bassist; co-wrote “London Boys” and “Little Black Book” with Lure
  • Richard Hell — co-wrote “Chinese Rocks” (Thunders/Nolan/Ramone/Hell); Waldos covered it live

Open Questions

Possible the album was not entered into Discogs, or the label name may be misremembered — the studio albums are on Baylor, Sympathy For The Record Industry, and Cleopatra.
  • Tony Coiro’s cause of death — not documented in Discogs; year confirmed (1995) but circumstances unknown.
  • Waldos setlist evolution — the exact touring lineups between 1995 (Coiro’s death) and 2008 (Live In Berlin) are unclear from Discogs; only studio credits exist for that gap.
  • Charlie Sox — appears only on the 1991 single in Discogs; no other credits. Relationship to the band beyond this one session unknown.
  • Jeff West (4) — appears only on Rent Party; no other credits in Discogs.
  • Joey Pinter’s activity 1994–2018 — between the two Waldos studio albums, Pinter’s Discogs credits are sparse (a 1988 solo EP and two 2019–2020 songwriter credits). Whether he remained involved live is not documented in Discogs.
