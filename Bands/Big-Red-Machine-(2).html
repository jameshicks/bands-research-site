Overview

Big Red Machine is an American indie rock and folk collaboration between Aaron Dessner (The National) and Justin Vernon (Bon Iver), formed in 2018. The project released six records split evenly across the late 2010s and early 2020s, issued through Jagjaguwar and the duo’s own 37d03d imprint. Their catalogue drew in a broad network of guests—Robin Pecknold, Michael Stipe, Chris Messina, and others appear across multiple releases—making it as much a rotating creative collective as a conventional duo act. Production and engineering across the whole run was anchored by Brad Cook, Jonathan Low, and Greg Calbi, each appearing on fifteen credits.

Overall philosophy

Dessner has described Big Red Machine as “a laboratory for me to try ideas, work with people I wouldn’t have been able to before,” and the album’s wide guest roster flows from that ethos rather than any deliberate casting process. His core quote on recruitment: “Usually we’re together because we’re friends and then music happens.” He characterized the 13 singers on the record as creating something unified — “it feels like a band” rather than a compilation. Dessner has also attributed his instinct for collaboration to being a twin: “a born collaborator.” [ABC Double J, 2021: https://www.abc.net.au/listen/doublej/music-reads/features/big-red-machine-chat-aaron-dessner-taylor-swift-justin-vernon/13534210]

The album Dessner said he imagined as a kind of reimagining of The Band and Grateful Dead’s collaborative spirit. [BroadwayWorld, July 2021: https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwmusic/article/Big-Red-Machine-Release-Phoenix-Featuring-Fleet-Foxes-Anas-Mitchell-20210722]

Robin Pecknold (“Phoenix,” with Anaïs Mitchell)

Dessner shared a sketch of “Phoenix” with Pecknold, whom he described as an artist he had “been a fan of forever and who I’d been dreaming would join us on this record.” [KEXP Song of the Day, September 2021: https://www.kexp.org/podcasts/song-of-the-day/2021/9/24/big-red-machine-phoenix-feat-fleet-foxes-anais-mitchell/; BroadwayWorld, July 2021: https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwmusic/article/Big-Red-Machine-Release-Phoenix-Featuring-Fleet-Foxes-Anas-Mitchell-20210722]

“Phoenix” was one of the last songs written for the album. Vernon first heard Dessner’s sketch while driving and immediately developed a chorus melody. Dessner then sent it to Pecknold. Pecknold wrote the verses and pre-chorus as a dialogue with Vernon, drawing on “a conversation they once had backstage in Phoenix” — specifically the one time the two had previously met, by a loading bay in Phoenix, Arizona. Pecknold characterised his lyrical stance as: “I felt like a pilgrim putting questions to an elusive sage, not needing clear answers, but happy for the chance to ask.” Anaïs Mitchell subsequently contributed additional lyrics to the chorus. [KEXP Song of the Day, September 2021: https://www.kexp.org/podcasts/song-of-the-day/2021/9/24/big-red-machine-phoenix-feat-fleet-foxes-anais-mitchell/; Consequence, July 2021 (search snippet): https://consequence.net/2021/07/big-red-machine-phoenix-fleet-foxes-anais-mitchell-stream/]

Dessner said of the result: “I think it’s what I always imagined Big Red Machine would sound like.” [BroadwayWorld, July 2021: https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwmusic/article/Big-Red-Machine-Release-Phoenix-Featuring-Fleet-Foxes-Anas-Mitchell-20210722]

Taylor Swift (“Renegade,” “Birch”)

Swift and Dessner had already built an extensive working relationship through her albums folklore (2020) and evermore (2020). By the time Dessner returned to Big Red Machine, the two were close enough that her involvement was described as a given. Dessner called her “a super-cool, nice friend who happens to be mega-talented” and said she “was very important… in terms of the work that she put in and her writing, but also gave me a lot of advice and encouragement; sort of executive produced this record in a way.” [ABC Double J, 2021: https://www.abc.net.au/listen/doublej/music-reads/features/big-red-machine-chat-aaron-dessner-taylor-swift-justin-vernon/13534210]

Sharon Van Etten (“Hutch”)

Van Etten and Dessner had a pre-existing creative relationship: the two had crossed paths touring, shared songs, and collaborated on each other’s work. Dessner reached out to her directly while touring. Van Etten noted they had “ever since crossed paths and shared songs and collaborated on each other’s songs.” [NME, August 2021: https://www.nme.com/features/big-red-machine-aaron-dessner-justin-vernon-interview-2021-3038473]

General context

Big Red Machine had contributed to Sharon Van Etten’s anniversary album prior to recording How Long; Anaïs Mitchell’s involvement grew from existing ties within the same creative network. Dessner described the guests as sharing “an openness, a creative generosity and an emotional quality that connects it all together.” [Consequence, June 2021 (search snippet): https://consequence.net/2021/06/big-red-machine-how-long-do-you-think-its-gonna-last/]

The album was also a personal milestone for Dessner: the guest network provided emotional scaffolding as he sang lead vocals for the first time, and several tracks engage with themes of mental health and the loss of friends. [DIY Magazine, August 2021: https://diymag.com/2021/08/26/big-red-machine-how-long-do-you-think-its-gonna-last-august-2021-intervie]
