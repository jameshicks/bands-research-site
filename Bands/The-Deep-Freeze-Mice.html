The Deep Freeze Mice

Discogs ID: 294782
Origin: Leicester, UK
Active: 1979–1989
Labels: Mole Embalming Records (1979–1983), Cordelia Records (1984–1989)
Name variations: Deep Freeze Mice, Deep Freeze, The Mice

English new wave / art rock band from Leicester. Core trio of Alan Jenkins (guitar, vocals), Sherree Lawrence (organ, keyboards), and Mick Bunnage (bass), with two successive drummers: Graham Summers on the first four records and Peter Gregory from 1984 onward. Released ten studio LPs between 1979 and 1989, all on Jenkins’ own labels. Known for absurdist, literary titles; long experimental side pieces; prolific output; and a wry pop sensibility underneath the lo-fi noise. Listed on the Nurse With Wound list. Both Mole Embalming Records and Cordelia Records were Jenkins-run imprints. After dissolution Jenkins formed The Chrysanthemums, Ruth’s Refrigerator, and The Thurston Lava Tube.

Formation

Alan Jenkins, Sherree Lawrence, and Graham Summers were school friends in Leicester. Jenkins was playing guitar in various loose bands; Lawrence occasionally joined him after buying a Vox Continental organ with birthday money. Jenkins confirmed this in a 2022 interview: “making cassette recordings in my bedroom, sometimes with my school friends Sherree and Graham who went on to be in The Deep Freeze Mice.” psychedelicbabymag.com

In 1977, at the height of punk, Jenkins formed a duo with guitarist John Duffin, naming it after a song called “Bring Back To Life The Deep Freeze Mice” written by one of Jenkins’ friends — the name stuck. By 1979 Duffin had departed and Lawrence had taken his place. Needing a drummer, Jenkins and Lawrence recruited their old schoolmate Summers, who brought along his bandmate Mick Bunnage (bass) for the recording session. Summers and Bunnage had been playing together in a Leeds-based group called the Statics; the debut album’s liner notes thank “the Statics (the best Leeds group you haven’t heard of) for providing Mick and Graham.” lisathatcher.com.au

In a 2022 interview Jenkins noted that Bunnage had not yet formally joined the band at that point but “came to the recording session in Nottingham where we recorded and mixed side one in eight hours.” psychedelicbabymag.com The quartet recorded the debut LP My Geraniums Are Bulletproof in a single day in 1979. Bunnage later recalled Jenkins and Lawrence as “a right couple of weirdoes,” unfamiliar with recording processes but full of enthusiasm. manosfatisis.blogspot.com The initial print run of around 250 copies was self-financed and distributed by hand.
