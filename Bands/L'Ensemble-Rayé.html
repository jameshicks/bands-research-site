L’Ensemble Rayé

Swiss avant-garde ensemble from Neuchâtel, active 1982–2001+. Discogs artist ID: 500699. Name variation: L’Ensamble Rayé.

Background

L’Ensemble Rayé formed in 1982 in Neuchâtel, Switzerland. The ensemble grew out of the same milieu that produced Debile Menthol and Nimal, both rooted in the Swiss wing of the Rock In Opposition current. Where Debile Menthol took a post-punk angle and Nimal leaned into folk and avant-garde collision, L’Ensemble Rayé settled into something drier and more chamber-inflected — a rotating cast of instrumentalists playing composed, often witty short pieces that blended jazz, acoustic folk, and eclectic small-ensemble writing.

The ensemble’s core consisted of Jean-Maurice Rossel (bass, guitars, keyboards, percussion, and various odd instruments), Jean 20 Huguenin (guitar, various), and Cédric Vuille (guitar, clarinet, bass, and an exceptionally wide palette of secondary instruments). These three appear across the full discography. Other recurring members include Christian Addor (keyboards, accordion, vocals), Gilles Rieder (drums, percussion), and Pierre Kaufmann (clarinet, bass clarinet, saxophone). Later records added Yann Altermath (saxophone, horn) and Julien Baillod (guitar, bass, electronics). The ensemble name implied a collective, and the writing credits were spread across the membership rather than concentrated in one composer.

Jean-Maurice Rossel was a central organizational figure: he founded LabelUsineS, the Neuchâtel studio-label at the Usine (cultural factory space), and engineered or produced much of the ensemble’s work. His involvement with Nimal — which also included Tom Cora and Bratko Bibič — underlines the international reach of this Neuchâtel network.

Sound

L’Ensemble Rayé’s music is acoustic-weighted but not exclusively so. The palette across four albums spans: ukulele, hurdy-gurdy, bandurria, tuba, euphonium, trombone, e-bow, ocarina, spoons, sitar, prepared guitar, and various noise-making objects. This eclecticism is instrumental rather than confrontational — the music tends toward playful, intricately arranged short forms rather than noise or abstraction. The harmonic language is jazz-adjacent but the structures often resemble French chanson or European chamber miniatures. Humor surfaces regularly in track titles and in the choice of unusual timbres.

Each album has a somewhat different lineup, reflecting the ensemble’s open, workshop-like character. The 1989 debut is dominated by the Vuille/Huguenin/Rossel/Rieder/Addor core; the 1993 follow-up brings in Pierre Kaufmann more centrally and expands the cast with guest players; En Frac! (1996) consolidates around a tight quintet including new member Shirley Anne Hofmann on euphonium and trombone; and Vis-A-Vis Movers (2001) is leaner — four players, all writing, no drums, heavier on guitar textures and electronics.

Release Detail

Même En Hiver / Comme Un Pinson Dans L’Eau (1989, Ayaa CDT 0289)

Four suites / groupings of 16 tracks. Composers: Vuille (tracks 1–9, 12) and Huguenin (tracks 11, 13–16). Contains a cover: “Birdless Air” written by Tom Cora (of Nimal). Artwork by Tatjana Hauptmann. Personnel vary per track — the core four (Rossel, Vuille, Huguenin, Rieder, Addor) supplemented by Catherine Trottmann (piano), Maurus Trottmann (tuba), Victor De Bros (piano), Didier Pietton (saxophone).

Quelques Pièces Détachées (1993, Ayaa cdt 0993)

14 tracks. Pierre Kaufmann joins as central multi-reedist; Daniel Spahni on drums throughout; Maurus Trottmann on barrel organ; Jürg Frey contributes one written-by credit. Guest vocalists on “Les Dons De Castor.” Rossel engineers and mixes.

En Frac! (1996, LabelUsineS LUS 003)

12 tracks. Most tightly ensemble-written of the four albums — all five core members (Rossel, Huguenin, Vuille, Kaufmann, Hofmann) appear on nearly every track. Writing distributed across all five. Rossel plays accordion, guitar, bass, violin. Hofmann brings brass textures (euphonium, trombone, tuba). Recorded and mixed by Rossel. Released on LabelUsineS, Rossel’s own label at L’Usine in Neuchâtel. Genres tagged: Rock / Avantgarde, Prog Rock.

Vis-A-Vis Movers (2001, Les Disques Rayés 004)

13 tracks. The most stripped-back and guitar-centric. Huguenin on tenor guitar, sitar, noises; Vuille on guitar, ukulele, spoons; Kaufmann on winds and woodblock; Altermath on alto saxophone, horn, percussion; Baillod on guitar, bass, electronics. No drums. Composition credits split across Kaufmann (7), Baillod (4), Altermath (2), Huguenin (1). Mixed by Jeroen Visser.

Swiss Scene Context

L’Ensemble Rayé belongs to a tight cluster of Neuchâtel-based musicians who operated in the Rock In Opposition current during the 1980s–90s. The two anchor bands are Debile Menthol (formed earlier; RIO/post-punk; RecRec Music; Neuchâtel) and Nimal (folk/avant; RecRec Music; included international players Tom Cora and Bratko Bibič). The membership overlap is nearly total: Rossel, Huguenin, Vuille, Addor, and Rieder all appear in Debile Menthol; Rossel and Huguenin are also in Nimal.

The scene was institutionally supported by RecRec Music (Zurich), Switzerland’s main independent label for avant-garde and RIO-adjacent work, which issued both Debile Menthol and Nimal but not L’Ensemble Rayé (they moved to French label Ayaa instead). L’Usine in Neuchâtel, where Rossel’s LabelUsineS operated, served as a broader cultural infrastructure.

Gilles Rieder’s connections extend significantly outward: he is credited as a member of the US bands God Is My Co-Pilot and 1-2 Japanese, suggesting a transnational circulation of these Swiss players through the US downtown/experimental scene. Tom Cora (Nimal, Skeleton Crew) was the main vector for that American connection.
