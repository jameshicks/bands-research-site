Robert Glasper Trio

Discogs ID: 3020413 Active: 2003–present (intermittently) Genre: Acoustic Jazz, Post Bop, Contemporary Jazz Label: Blue Note (2003–2015); Fresh Sound New Talent (2003) Members: Robert Glasper (piano) · Vicente Archer (bass, primary) · Robert Hurst (bass, early sessions) · Damion Reid (drums, primary) · Chris Dave (drums, early/transitional sessions)

History

The Robert Glasper Trio is the acoustic piano trio vehicle for Robert Glasper, representing his straight-ahead jazz work as distinct from his electric and hip-hop-influenced projects. The Trio format has been the through-line of Glasper’s career — the acoustic foundation from which his broader stylistic exploration launches.

Formation and Debut (2003–2005)

The Trio’s earliest documented recording is Mood (2003, Fresh Sound New Talent), a debut on the Barcelona-based label with Robert Hurst on bass and Damion Reid on drums. Hurst (a veteran bassist associated with the Branford Marsalis Quartet) anchored this session alongside the then-19-to-20-year-old Glasper. Guest appearances from Bilal, Mike Moreno, John Ellis (5), and Marcus Strickland reflected Glasper’s emerging New York connections.

Canvas (2005, Blue Note) was Glasper’s label debut and introduced the lineup that would become the trio’s defining core: Vicente Archer on bass and Damion Reid on drums. Produced by Eli Wolf, the album was a pure acoustic statement — ten originals by Glasper plus a Herbie Hancock cover (“Riot”), with guest appearances by Mark Turner (2) (tenor saxophone on two tracks) and Bilal (vocals on “Chant” and “I Remember”). The rhythm section of Archer and Reid proved immediately cohesive.

An unofficial live document — Live Sunside Paris, 22 / 11 / 2005 — was issued as a limited release the same year on Not On Label, capturing the trio in performance.

The Core Trio Period (2007–2009)

In My Element (2007, Blue Note) consolidated the Archer/Reid lineup with Glasper adding co-producer credit alongside Eli Wolf. The album deepened the trio’s approach with Glasper’s originals alongside a medley combining Herbie Hancock’s “Maiden Voyage” with Radiohead’s “Everything in Its Right Place” — an early signal of his genre-agnostic repertoire thinking. Cover credits included compositions by Sam Rivers (“Beatrice”) and Herbie Hancock.

Double Booked (2009, Blue Note) was a pivotal structural document: a single album split cleanly between two band configurations. Tracks 1–6 (labeled “Trio”) feature the acoustic trio — Glasper, Vicente Archer on double bass, and Chris Dave on drums. This is the last full acoustic-trio side before the Experiment format took over. Tracks 7–12 (labeled “Experiment”) introduce Derrick Hodge on bass guitar, Casey Benjamin on saxophone and vocoder, and Chris Dave continuing on drums — the lineup that would formally become the Robert Glasper Experiment. A voice introduction to the Trio side is by Terence Blanchard; Mos Def raps on “4eva” (Experiment side).

Covered and the Acoustic Return (2015)

After several years dominated by the Experiment and Black Radio, Glasper returned to the acoustic trio format with Covered (The Robert Glasper Trio Recorded Live At Capitol Studios) (2015, Blue Note). The album restores the Archer/Reid rhythm section and is built almost entirely from covers — Radiohead’s “Reckoner,” Kendrick Lamar’s “I’m Dying of Thirst,” John Legend’s “So Beautiful,” Avicii’s “Levels,” and others — as well as an original, plus a duet with Harry Belafonte on “Got Over.” The recording was engineered live at Capitol Studios in Hollywood. The Discogs entry credits only primary artists (no additional credits logged), but the personnel is consistent with the Archer/Reid core.

Relationship to the Robert Glasper Experiment

The Trio and the Robert Glasper Experiment exist as parallel entities. The Experiment (Discogs ID: 2644196) was formed around the electric side of Double Booked and solidified with Black Radio (2012). Its core members are Glasper, Derrick Hodge (bass), Chris Dave (drums), Casey Benjamin (vocoder/sax), and later Mark Colenburg (drums). The Trio is the acoustic wing; the Experiment is the electric. Some personnel overlap: Chris Dave plays drums on both the Trio side of Double Booked and the full Black Radio sessions.
