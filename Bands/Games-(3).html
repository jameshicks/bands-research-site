Overview

Games (3) was the recording alias used by Joel Ford and Daniel Lopatin for their collaborative electronic work in 2010. Lopatin records primarily as Oneohtrix Point Never; Ford had previously led Tigercity and later ran the Software (2) sublabel of Mexican Summer alongside Lopatin. The Games name was used for a brief, concentrated burst of activity — two Hippos In Tanks releases and a series of free DJ mixes — before the duo rebranded as Ford & Lopatin for their 2011 album Channel Pressure.

The project sits at the intersection of hypnagogic pop, screwed-and-chopped aesthetics, and the early-2010s vaporwave/chillwave adjacency zone. The “Heaven Can Wait” mixtape series leaned into Italo-disco and Eurodance source material, while the two proper releases blended those influences with original songwriting. The dual credit “Games Is: Joel Ford / Daniel Lopatin” (used on both releases) frames the project less as a named band than as a shared performing persona.

Formation and Context

Ford and Lopatin met in sixth grade at a school in Wayland, Massachusetts, bonding over Goldie and jazz fusion. At 15 they formed a group called Polyphonic (drum machine + synthesizer). Ford later attended UMass Amherst; Lopatin went to Hampshire College. They stayed in contact and briefly formed another band before the Games project.

In summer 2009, following “an intense 72-hour internet writing frenzy” (Lopatin’s words), they developed the Games concept. They recorded demos at Ford’s Massachusetts home using vintage synthesizers they’d accumulated, then moved into a sparse apartment in February 2010 to focus entirely on the project. The name was chosen informally and was not formally defined.

The project ended as a named entity in February 2011 when Ford and Lopatin renamed themselves Ford & Lopatin “to preempt legal issues” with rapper The Game’s label Interscope Records. The Software (2) imprint launched simultaneously as the vehicle for their debut LP Channel Pressure. (Formation story resolved via Wikipedia / web, 2026-05-07.)

Heaven Can Wait Mixtape Series

Three self-released free-download DJ mixes, all 2010. Vol. I draws on Sandra (“Heaven Can Wait,” “Maria Magdalena”), Valerie Dore, Katy Gray, and The Voyagers, framing them in a screwed/slowed aesthetic. Vol. II (43:24) ranges across electro, Eurodance, and synth-pop. Vol. III (35:31) leans Europop and Italo-disco. The series collectively established the nostalgic, filtered-memory tone that would continue through That We Can Play and into Ford & Lopatin’s Channel Pressure.
