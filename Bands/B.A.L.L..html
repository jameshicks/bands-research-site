Overview

B.A.L.L. (pronounced “ball”) were a New York City alternative rock band formed in 1987 as a Shimmy Disc supergroup, uniting four figures from the NYC underground: multi-instrumentalist and Shimmy Disc founder Kramer (2) (bass, organ), guitarist and vocalist Don Fleming, drummer David Licht, and drummer Jay Spiegel. The band took its name as a baseball acronym (Bureaucratic, Administrative, Legislative, Linguistic — or simply nonsense, depending on the source) and released four studio albums in quick succession before dissolving in 1990.

All four albums were produced and engineered by Kramer at Noise New York. The records occupy a loose, high-volume space between the downtown experimental scene Kramer had helped build with Shockabilly and a more song-based noise-rock aesthetic, with Don Fleming handling guitar and vocals and the dual-drummer lineup (Licht and Spiegel) giving the band an unusually dense rhythmic floor. Covers were a recurring motif: the debut featured John Lennon’s “Always” and a Pretty Things track; Bird included T. Rex’s “Buick McKane” and a Bangladesh medley (Dylan/Harrison/Starkey).

According to Wikipedia (citing The Guinness Who’s Who of Indie and New Wave Music, 1992), B.A.L.L. toured extensively in the US and Europe alongside bands including Sonic Youth and Teenage Fanclub before the band “disintegrated in 1990, its members pursuing separate projects.”

The overlapping memberships were extensive: Kramer and Licht also played in Bongwater and Shockabilly; Dave Rick (who joined as guest on Trouble Doll) was the guitarist in both Bongwater and King Missile; Fleming and Spiegel had previously played together in The Velvet Monkeys. After B.A.L.L. broke up, Fleming and Spiegel briefly joined Dinosaur Jr (releasing “The Wagon” on Sub Pop, 1990) before forming Gumball (2).

A VHS release, T.V. B.A.L.L. (Shimmy Disc, no year), documents the band with video and live footage, and includes an uncredited cover of Neil Young’s “Out Of The Blue.” Also credited on T.V. B.A.L.L. are Macioce (photographer on multiple releases) and Sheena Dupuis (designer on Bird).

Connections

  • Kramer (2) — bassist, organist, sole producer and engineer on all releases; founder of Shimmy Disc; also played in Shockabilly and Bongwater with Licht
  • Don Fleming — guitarist/vocalist; previously of The Velvet Monkeys (with Spiegel); post-B.A.L.L. co-founded Gumball (2), briefly joined Dinosaur Jr, produced Sonic Youth, Hole (2), Teenage Fanclub, Screaming Trees; also served as executive director of Alan Lomax’s Association for Cultural Equity
  • David Licht — drummer; co-founder of The Klezmatics (1986); previously Shockabilly with Kramer; also played in Bongwater
  • Jay Spiegel — drums; alias “The Rummager”; longtime collaborator of Fleming from The Velvet Monkeys; later in Gumball (2)
  • Dave Rick — guest guitarist on Trouble Doll; core member of Bongwater and King Missile; also in Phantom Tollbooth
  • Bongwater — overlapping members (Kramer, Licht, Rick); both bands released on Shimmy Disc and used Noise New York studio
  • Shockabilly — predecessor formation for Kramer and Licht (with Eugene Chadbourne)
  • King Missile — shared Dave Rick; Kramer produced their Shimmy Disc releases
  • Gumball (2) — post-B.A.L.L. project by Fleming and Spiegel
  • Dinosaur Jr — Fleming and Spiegel briefly joined after B.A.L.L. disbanded (1990); released “The Wagon” 45 on Sub Pop
  • Shimmy Disc — home label for all US releases; Kramer’s label, founded 1987; 478 releases total; sublabels include Shimmy Disc Europe (Dutch/German releases) and Kokopop
