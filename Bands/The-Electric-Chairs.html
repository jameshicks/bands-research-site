The Electric Chairs

British-American punk band fronted by Wayne County. Discogs artist IDs: 532065 (The Electric Chairs), 1341064 (Jayne County & The Electric Chairs).

Overview

The Electric Chairs formed around 1976–77 in London, built around Wayne County — the American transgender performer (born Wayne Vernoy Rogers, Dallas, TX, 7 November 1947) who had been a fixture of the early New York punk underground, playing Max’s Kansas City and the Mercer Arts Center before relocating to the UK in the mid-1970s. The band’s name variations across their catalog reflect County’s own transition: releases appear as Wayne County & The Electric Chairs (1977–79), then Jayne County & The Electric Chairs from the early 1980s onward.

The core lineup on their primary Safari Records output (1978–79) was County on vocals, Greg Van Cook on lead guitar, Eliot Michaels on additional guitar, Val Haller on bass, and J.J. Johnson (3) on drums. Henry Padovani — who had just left The Police as their original guitarist — also played rhythm guitar on the Storm The Gates Of Heaven and So Many Ways sessions. The revolving cast of guitarists on the Blatantly Offenzive EP captures the transitional state of the lineup: Van Cook handles three tracks, while Michaels and Padovani share the fourth.

The band was known for confrontational, gender-bending live performances at a moment when such visibility was genuinely dangerous. They are considered an early example of proto-queer punk, predating many later discussions of LGBTQ+ punk history by years. Their recordings vary from hard-driving garage punk (“Fuck Off”, “Eddie & Sheena”) to more experimental territory — “So Many Ways” and “Berlin (Long Version)” were both produced by David Cunningham of The Flying Lizards, giving those tracks a noticeably cooler, more post-punk texture.

Primary producer on the two Safari LPs was Martin Birch, who was simultaneously working with major hard rock acts (Deep Purple, Rainbow, Iron Maiden) — an unlikely pairing that says something about the porous genre boundaries of the late-1970s UK music industry.

Wayne County released a live album as Jayne County in 1980 (Rock ‘n’ Roll Resurrection) and continued recording sporadically through subsequent decades; Val Haller remained the one truly constant member of the Electric Chairs line-up throughout.
