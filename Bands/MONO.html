MONO

Instrumental post-rock / orchestral rock group from Tokyo, Japan. Formed 1999. Active continuously through 2025. Discogs artist ID: 208568 (listed as “Mono (7)”).

Core sound: cinematic, heavily orchestrated guitar music combining extreme dynamic range — quiet passages building to thunderous climaxes — with elaborate string arrangements. From Walking Cloud (2004) onward the band recorded almost exclusively with Steve Albini at Electrical Audio, Chicago.

Primary labels: Temporary Residence Limited (US, 2003–present) and Pelagic Records (Europe, 2013–present). Early releases on Tzadik (John Zorn’s label) and Japanese label Human Highway.

Orchestral Collaborations

Wordless Music Orchestra (New York, 2009–2010)

MONO performed with the Wordless Music Orchestra at (Le) Poisson Rouge, NYC. The concert was released as Holy Ground: NYC Live With The Wordless Music Orchestra (2010, TRL). Conducted by Jeffrey Milarsky; organized by Ronen Givony (founder of the Wordless Music concert series). Musicians included Nadia Sirota (viola/contractor), Clarice Jensen (cello), and others from the NYC new-music scene.

Platinum Anniversary Orchestra (London, 2021)

For their 20th anniversary, MONO performed in London with the Platinum Anniversary Orchestra (Discogs ID: 8607546). The concert featured special guests A.A.Williams and Jo Quail. Released as Beyond the Past (Live in London With the Platinum Anniversary Orchestra) (2021, TRL).

Orchestra PITREZA (Japan)

Orchestra PITREZA (Discogs ID: 16940011) appears in collaborator data with 4 shared releases — a Japanese ensemble associated with MONO’s domestic orchestral performances.

Hymn to the Immortal Wind — Chicago String Orchestra (2009)

For Hymn to the Immortal Wind, MONO assembled approximately 28 Chicago-based string and wind players at Electrical Audio, conducted by Dave Max Crawford and Paul Von Mertens.

Connections

  • Steve Albini — principal engineer/producer from 2004 onward; recorded almost every album at Electrical Audio, Chicago. Oath (2024) was among his final sessions before his death.
  • John Zorn — executive produced debut Under the Pipal Tree on his Tzadik label; gave the band their first US platform.
  • World’s End Girlfriend — split LP Palmless Prayer / Mass Murder Refrain (2005); shared Japanese post-rock/experimental scene.
  • Pelican (2) — split 7” (2005, Hydra Head / TRL); shared post-metal/post-rock milieu.
  • Jeremy DeVine — TRL co-founder; handled all art direction and design across most MONO releases.
  • Rafael Anton Irisarri — ambient/drone artist with 13 shared credits; associated with TRL/Pelagic overlapping roster.
  • Susan Voelz — recurring violinist and string arranger, most prominently on Walking Cloud, You Are There, Hymn, and Oath.
  • Alison Chesley — cellist (also records solo as Helen Money); appears on Walking Cloud, You Are There, Hymn to the Immortal Wind.
  • Nadia Sirota — viola / contractor for Wordless Music Orchestra (2010 live album).
  • Clarice Jensen — cellist on 2010 live album.
  • A.A.Williams — guest vocalist/performer at 2021 London 20th anniversary concert.
  • Jo Quail — cellist, guest at 2021 London concert.
  • Tetsuya Fukagawa — vocalist on Rays of Darkness (2014).

Open Questions

  • The brief mentioned “American Contemporary Music Ensemble” as an orchestral collaborator — this entity does not appear by that name in Discogs credits. The Wordless Music Orchestra (NYC) and Platinum Anniversary Orchestra (London) are the two documented orchestral ensembles. Worth verifying via web whether ACME collaboration was released or only performed live.
  • Human Highway label (Japanese) — not currently in vault; worth a separate label stub if researching the Japanese post-rock scene.
  • Forty-4 label (Hey, You. EP, 2000) — early Japanese imprint; minimal Discogs data.
  • Discogs credits for Requiem for Hell (2016) and For My Parents (2012) and Pilgrimage of the Soul (2021) returned empty additional credits — personnel for those albums would require web or Japanese press sources.
  • Tamaki’s alias “Tamaki Yuasa” (married name) appears on Pelagic-era releases; both IDs (691266 and 4064054) resolve to the same person in collaborator data.
