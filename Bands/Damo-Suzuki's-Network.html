Damo Suzuki’s Network

Improvisation network/collective centred on Damo Suzuki. Active c.1997–2024. Discogs artist ID: 1112929. The earlier fixed-band iteration is Damo Suzuki Band (Discogs ID: 1122161), active c.1986–2000.

The Concept

The Network is not a band in any conventional sense. Damo Suzuki arrives in a city with no fixed musicians behind him. He contacts local players — collectively called sound carriers — who agree to perform without rehearsal. The show happens once, is improvised in its entirety, and is never repeated. A different city means a different group of sound carriers; a different night in the same city means a different set.

The model consciously extends Can’s “instant composition” doctrine — the idea that every performance generates its own logic in real time — into a globally distributed, perpetually rotating structure. Damo described the result as a kind of worldwide sonic network: each city’s performers become nodes, connected only by the presence of his voice and the shared commitment to pure improvisation.

He returned to music in 1983 after a decade-long withdrawal following his conversion to the Jehovah’s Witnesses (he left Can in 1973 at the height of their powers). The Network model consolidated from the mid-1990s onwards, eventually producing well over a hundred documented shows across Europe, North America, Japan, and beyond.

The Network — Releases

Damo’s Net Work (1997, Damo’s Net Work DNW 001–006)

The foundational release — six CDs drawn from two Tokyo shows (Tokyo On Air West, 30 April and 2 May) and one Osaka show (Muse Hall, 4 May), all from the same Japan tour. All tracks untitled; pure improvisation. The release establishes the format: no composed material, no track titles, venue and date as the only anchors.

Released on Damo’s self-operated Damo’s Net Work imprint; catalogue number spans DNW 001 to 006 (one per CD). Genres: Experimental, Krautrock.

Metaphysical Transfer (2001, Captain Trip Records CTCD-377/378)

The first widely distributed Network release, on Japan’s Captain Trip Records. Two CDs of live improvisation from a Los Angeles session.

The Black Midi Session (2018)

One of the most documented single Network shows: Damo Suzuki with Black Midi as sound carriers at the Windmill Brixton, London, 2018. Released as a limited cassette (cat no bm001) on self-released labels (Black Midi self-released / Damo Suzuki self-released). Two untitled improvisations, Side A (19:01) and Side B (19:06). Recorded by Max Goulding. This show brought together the Network and black midi at a very early point in the latter band’s public existence, predating their debut album.

The Omar Rodriguez-Lopez EP (2007)

Open Questions / Gaps

  • Total number of Network shows and cities is not quantifiable from Discogs — Damo claimed hundreds of events across decades; web/press sources would be needed
  • Cause of Damo’s death (9 February 2024) not in Discogs; web sources report recurrence of colon cancer (first diagnosed c.2004)
  • Many listed sound carriers from the Discogs profile text (Ann Shenton, Stephen McBean, Helena Espvall, Cul de Sac, Do Make Say Think, Broken Social Scene, Tay Zonday, etc.) are not tied to specific Discogs releases — they may have performed at undocumented/unreleased shows
  • The Mugstar connection listed in the prior stub (6 shared releases) is confirmed but via Damo Suzuki’s solo discography (ID: 110310) rather than the Network entity (ID: 1112929) — releases include Start From Zero (2014, Important Records) and Invisible Wind Factory (2020, The Weird Beard). Mugstar appear as sound carriers on those records, but they are credited to Damo Suzuki as primary artist, not Damo Suzuki’s Network. The wiki-link in the Connections section is accurate but the relationship belongs primarily in Damo Suzuki’s article.
  • Sixtoo (Ninja Tune, 2004) collaboration noted in Discogs profile but not in discography — may be a credit on Sixtoo’s album Chewing on Glass & Other Miracle Cures rather than a Network release
