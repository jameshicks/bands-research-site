Though they weren’t around for long, Advertising managed one brilliantly eccentric album, 1978’s Jingles. The punk-era Cambridge pop band formed in 1976 around Tot Taylor and Simon Boswell, both of whom strummed and sang. Taylor had appeared earlier in A Special Moment and the Blue Kittens, a theatrical band which released no material but did compose a musical. The punk explosion inspired Taylor to form a pop band, and with Boswell, he decided on the name Advertising. The early single “Lipstick” just missed the UK charts in 1977 (and deserved much better), but the group signed with EMI Records for their debut album, Jingles. Its lack of success, however, caused the band’s break-up. While Taylor began a solo career soon after, Boswell emerged first as a producer and later as the founder of Live Wire and Data.

Overview

Advertising were a Cambridge new wave/power pop group formed in 1976 around vocalist-guitarists Tot Taylor and Simon Boswell, both previously members of the theatrical Blue Kittens project. Their 1977 debut single “Lipstick” narrowly missed the UK charts, after which they signed with EMI and recorded their sole album Jingles (1978), drawing on power pop, mod, and punk influences. The album’s commercial failure ended the band: Taylor moved into a solo career while Boswell went on to produce records and found the Live Wire and Data labels.

Notes (web)

Kenny Laguna and Jingles (1979)

Kenny Laguna was not the sole or primary producer of Jingles. Production was split across the album: Laguna produced four tracks (A1, A4, B3, B6), while Andy Arthurs produced the remaining eight (A2, A3, A5–A7, B1, B2, B4, B5). Arthurs also remixed the four Laguna-produced tracks. (Discogs release 404239; confirmed via Trouser Press)

Laguna’s high credit count relative to other collaborators (9 shared releases on Discogs) likely reflects his appearance across multiple single/album pressings rather than a deeper creative partnership than Arthurs.

How the connection came about. No surviving interview or contemporary press piece explicitly describes how Advertising came to work with Laguna. The most plausible route is through Laguna’s established presence in the British market: he relocated to the UK in the mid-1970s and was an active producer of UK rock and new wave acts at exactly the period Advertising were recording for EMI — his official discography lists Bethnal (Dangerous Times, 1978) and Tyla Gang (Moonproof, 1978) as concurrent projects, alongside earlier work with the Steve Gibbons Band. (kennylaguna.com/discography; Wikipedia on Laguna notes the UK period: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Laguna, as of 2026-11-06)

Tot Taylor himself, in a since-deleted PRS for Music interview, referred to Laguna as “Joan Jett’s producer Kenny Laguna” — describing him by the reputation he later acquired — without elaborating on how they met. (Source: search-result snippet from prsformusic.com/m-magazine/features/interview-tot-taylor; page now returns 404, snippet only.)

Summary: Laguna was a co-producer on roughly one-third of Jingles, with Andy Arthurs holding primary production credit for the album overall. The connection almost certainly came through Laguna’s UK production circuit in the late 1970s rather than any trans-Atlantic outreach, but the specific introduction is undocumented.
