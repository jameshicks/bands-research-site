Aug 05, 20269 min read

Blind Light

Discogs ID: 105170
Active: 1992–1994
Origin: USA / Japan
Genre: Electronic, Experimental, Ambient, Downtempo

A one-off project conceived by Bill Laswell as a re-interpretation of The Golden Palominos material. Blind Light assembled the core Golden Palominos rhythm section — Anton Fier and Laswell — with guitarist Nicky Skopelitis, a second guitarist in Knox Chandler, percussionist Aiyb Dieng, vocalists Lori Carson and Lydia Kavanagh, and Japanese vocalist/composer Phew (Hiromi Moritani). Songwriting is credited jointly to Fier, Laswell, and Phew throughout.

Discogs lists the following as official members: Aiyb Dieng, Anton Fier, Bill Laswell, Hiromi Moritani (Phew), Knox Chandler, Lori Carson, and Nicky Skopelitis. Lydia Kavanagh, while credited as vocalist on both releases, does not appear in the formal members list.

Context

The Golden Palominos was Anton Fier’s flagship collective — a shifting lineup of downtown New York and global musicians. Lori Carson was a Golden Palominos vocalist (she lists the group on her Discogs page). Blind Light’s “re-interpretation” is sourced specifically from the sessions that produced The Golden Palominos’ Pure (1994): both projects were recorded at Greenpoint Studio, Brooklyn, and The Absence of Time draws on outtakes and alternate takes from those sessions, with Phew’s Japanese-language vocals replacing Lori Carson’s English-language ones. The two albums are effectively parallel constructions from the same raw material. (Source: Wikipedia — The Golden Palominos, as of 2026-05-29; Silent-Watcher Laswell discography; billlaswell.net.) The Fier/Laswell/Phew songwriting credit suggests Phew co-wrote the material rather than simply contributing vocals.

Knox Chandler brought a post-punk and art-rock thread into the project — he was simultaneously or recently playing with Siouxsie And The Banshees and The Psychedelic Furs.

On Dreamspeed, the notable inversion: Bootsy Collins is credited as guitarist, not bassist — an atypical role for Parliament/Funkadelic’s bass icon. Kazu Makino (of Blonde Redhead) contributes voice to three tracks on Disc 1 and co-wrote “Clouds Without Water” (track 1-4). Her appearance here (recorded 1992–1994) predates Blonde Redhead’s wider recognition. Matt Stein (Discogs ID: 130030) served as a key engineering and creative collaborator on the Dreamspeed disc — contributing samples, loops, recording, mixing, and co-writing credits — but has no separate vault page yet.

Phew’s songwriting role differs between the two releases: the original Alida release credits all songs to Fier, Laswell, and Phew jointly; the Tzadik compilation’s written-by credits attribute most tracks to Phew explicitly, with only the first two Dreamspeed tracks credited to Fier alone, suggesting either a contractual discrepancy between pressings or a re-attribution at the time of the 2003 compilation.

Web Research Notes

(Added 2026-05-29 from web sources; see source links below)

Source material: relationship to The Golden Palominos’ Pure

Multiple sources describe The Absence of Time as drawing directly from the same sessions as The Golden Palominos’ Pure (1994). Wikipedia’s Golden Palominos article states the album “used outtakes from Pure for much of its framework” (wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Golden_Palominos, as of 2026-05-29). The Bill Laswell Silent-Watcher discography page describes Blind Light as “stocks up on members of Pure-era Palominos and sounds very similar to that album” (silent-watcher.net).

Both Pure and The Absence of Time were recorded at Greenpoint Studio, Brooklyn. The Blind Light sessions are documented as September 1993 and December 1993, with mixing in January 1994 — overlapping with or immediately preceding the Pure sessions at the same studio (Pure was released October 1994). The Laswell Bandcamp / BASSMATTER page for the album confirms: “Greenpoint Studio, September ‘93, December ‘93; mixed January 1994” (billlaswell.net/album/the-absence-of-time).

The consensus across reviewers is that Blind Light is not a remix of finished Pure recordings but rather a parallel construction from the same raw session material — outtakes and alternate takes from the Greenpoint sessions — with Phew’s Japanese-language vocals substituted for Lori Carson’s English-language ones. One summary: “nearly identical to The Golden Palominos’ Pure with Japanese instead of English vocals… numerous samples from Pure… in the case of ‘Our Completion,’ a whole Golden Palominos song with Japanese vocals put over the top.” No source confirms that finished Pure master tapes were ingested wholesale; the weight of evidence points to shared session recordings used selectively, not a remix of the completed album.

Conflict note: The Wikipedia Pure album article makes no mention of Blind Light or shared sessions (wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_(The_Golden_Palominos_album)). The connection is asserted in the main Golden Palominos Wikipedia article and in multiple review sources but is not confirmed by liner note documentation found online.

Songwriting credits discrepancy

No interview or liner note source found that explains the per-track vs. blanket Phew credit difference between the Alida and Tzadik pressings. The Alida CD credits all tracks jointly to Fier, Laswell, and Phew; the Tzadik compilation attributes individual tracks differently. No explanation has been located in published interviews with Fier, Laswell, or Phew. This remains unresolved. Likely explanations (re-attribution during 2003 compilation preparation, or a contractual licensing adjustment for the Japanese pressing) are speculative and have no sourced support.

Tzadik catalog numbers: 2003 vs. 2016

The 2003 Tzadik release (VolumeID 154) carries catalog number TZ 7609-2. The 2016 reissue (VolumeID 807) carries catalog number TZ 7609b-2 — a distinct suffix indicating a new pressing, not merely a restock. Tzadik’s own catalog pages confirm the two entries (tzadik.com/volume.php?VolumeID=154 and tzadik.com/volume.php?VolumeID=807). The 2016 edition is described as “long out of print” having been restocked, and Tzadik’s own page notes “two unreleased bonus tracks” — confirmed by Discogs as tracks 1-10 (Dreamspeed disc) and 2-08 “Bait and Switch” (Blind Light disc). The tracklist on the Tzadik track-list page confirms “Bait and Switch” as the eighth and final track on Disc 2 (tzadik.com/track_list.php?VolumeID=807).

The 2016 pressing is also described as remastered (Discogs release r8198712 lists it as “Compilation, Reissue, Remastered”). The BASSMATTER Bandcamp upload (December 2022) credits remastering to James Dellatacoma rather than the original Howie Weinberg mastering on the 1994 Alida pressing.

Additional contextual detail

The Tzadik release page describes the work as “the definitive document of one of the Downtown scene’s most elusive musicians” and calls it “A Tzadik Classic!” (tzadik.com/volume.php?VolumeID=807).

The Fier interview in The Wire (2010) was located but does not discuss Blind Light or The Absence of Time — it focuses on the reunion-era Golden Palominos with Syd Straw.
