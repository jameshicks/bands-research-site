Paul Butterfield’s Better Days

Discogs ID: 1995045 Active: 1972–1974 Genre: Blues, Roots Rock, Acoustic Blues Location: Woodstock, NY Associated acts: The Paul Butterfield Blues Band, Bearsville Records, Geoff Muldaur, Maria Muldaur, Jim Kweskin - The Jug Band

History

Paul Butterfield’s Better Days formed in 1972 following the dissolution of The Paul Butterfield Blues Band, which had been one of the defining Chicago-style electric blues outfits of the 1960s. Butterfield regrouped in Woodstock, NY, then home to a loose community of musicians orbiting Bearsville Records — the label founded by manager Albert Grossman in 1970. The new band reflected a shift away from amplified Chicago blues toward a looser, acoustic-inflected roots sound incorporating R&B, country blues, and New Orleans influences.

The band’s core was built around Geoff Muldaur, who had come out of the Cambridge folk/jug band scene via Jim Kweskin - The Jug Band and who served as co-producer alongside Butterfield. The lineup was rounded out by guitarist Amos Garrett, pianist/vocalist Ronnie Barron, bassist Bill Rich, and drummer Chris Parker (2). Engineer on both albums was Nick Jameson, who had his own artist presence on Bearsville.

The debut, Better Days, was recorded in 1972–73 and released in 1973 on Bearsville (cat. BR 2119), credited solely to “Paul Butterfield.” It featured a dense cast of session guests and drew on material by Percy Mayfield, Blind Willie Johnson (via Nina Simone’s arrangement), Eric Von Schmidt, Nick Gravenites, Bobby Charles, and Big Joe Williams, alongside a Robert Johnson cover. David Sanborn contributed alto saxophone on two tracks; Howard Johnson (3) arranged and played baritone saxophone on the horn tracks. Maria Muldaur appeared on two tracks as vocalist and fiddler. Bobby Charles — himself a Bearsville artist and New Orleans songwriter — sang backing vocals and contributed two compositions.

The second album, It All Comes Back (Bearsville BR 2170, 1973), was credited to “Paul Butterfield’s Better Days” and continued in a similar vein. Co-producers again were Butterfield and Geoff Muldaur; Nick Jameson is also credited as co-producer on this record. The repertoire leaned further into New Orleans and R&B material: songs by Mose Allison, Bobby Charles (including “Small Town Talk,” co-written with Rick Danko of The Band), Mac Rebennack (Dr. John), and a track credited to Blind Boy Fuller. Maria Muldaur and Bobby Charles again appeared as guests. Additional session color came from Bobbye Hall on congas and Howard Johnson (3) on horns.

The band dissolved in 1974 after the two albums failed to generate significant commercial traction. Butterfield later recorded solo for Bearsville (Put It In Your Ear, 1975; North South, 1980) and worked with the Woodstock Mountains Revue and The RCO All-Stars before his death in 1987. Geoff Muldaur went on to record as a solo artist. A posthumous live album, Live At Winterland Ballroom, appeared in 1999 (Japan).

Bearsville Scene

Bearsville Records was founded in 1970 by manager Albert Grossman, who had previously managed Bob Dylan, Janis Joplin, and The Band. The label operated out of Woodstock, NY, distributed first by Ampex Records and then by Warner Bros. from 1972, until its closure in 1984.

By the time Better Days recorded there, Bearsville was home to a loose but distinctive roster. Todd Rundgren was the label’s most commercially significant act and its de facto in-house production presence (though he did not produce Better Days). Foghat provided the label’s heaviest rock presence. Utopia (5) (Rundgren’s art-rock outfit) and Sparks (early period) rounded out the early-70s catalog. Bobby Charles — who appears on both Better Days albums — was also a Bearsville recording artist. Jesse Winchester recorded his early albums for the label.

The label’s art direction was handled by Milton Glaser, the designer behind the famous Bob Dylan “Greatest Hits” poster, who designed both Better Days albums. Engineer Nick Jameson worked across much of the Bearsville catalog and later released solo material on the label. Bob Ludwig mastered both Better Days LPs, working under his well-known “RL” etching initials.
