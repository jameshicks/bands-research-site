Overview

Sugar Ray started as a hardcore band in the mid-1980s before shifting toward funk metal. Their debut proper, Lemonade and Brownies (1995), was produced by McG and received little attention. A pivot toward pop rock — catalyzed by the radio success of “Fly” (1997) — made them one of the most commercially successful American rock acts of the late 1990s. Four of their first five albums were produced or co-produced by David Kahne, who also co-wrote extensively with the band. Their run of late-1990s singles (“Fly,” “Every Morning,” “Someday,” “Falls Apart”) dominated Mainstream Rock and pop radio.

Produced, engineered, and mixed by David Kahne (ID 240971). Kahne also played keyboards and programmed. Tracking engineer: John Travis (ID 256209). Mastered by Stephen Marcussen (ID 254942).

Sugar Ray (2001, Atlantic/Lava, ID 620450)

Produced by Don Gilmore (ID 231758) on tracks 1–11 (except track 2 and 6). Kahne is absent from this record. Mixed by Chris Lord-Alge (tracks 1, 3–5, 8) and Don Gilmore (tracks 7, 10, 11). Mastered by Stephen Marcussen. Engineers: Don Gilmore, John Ewing Jr., Steve Gallagher. The band played their own instruments (Karges: bass; Sheppard: guitar; Frazier: drums; McGrath: vocals; DJ Homicide: turntables).

In The Pursuit Of Leisure (2003, Atlantic, ID 1133303)

Produced, engineered, mixed, and programmed by David Kahne. Additional mixing by Michael H. Brauer. Kahne co-wrote tracks 1, 3, 5–12 with the band. DJ Homicide credited as Craig “DJ Homicide” Bullock (turntables, backing vocals). The band played full instruments throughout.

Music For Cougars (2009, Pulse Recording, ID 1915641)

Produced by Josh Abraham and Luke Walker (3). Mixed and recorded by Ryan Williams and Luke Walker (3). Mastered by Eddy Schreyer. No Kahne involvement.

The David Kahne Production Thread

David Kahne produced four of Sugar Ray’s seven studio albums: Floored (1997), 14:59 (1999), In The Pursuit of Leisure (2003), and was the engineer on In The Pursuit of Leisure. He is absent from Sugar Ray (2001), which went to Don Gilmore. On Floored and 14:59, Kahne also played keyboards, programmed, and co-wrote material — a deeper creative involvement than typical producer-for-hire work. Sugar Ray is thus one of the longest-running Kahne artist relationships in the DB, comparable in duration to his Bangles work.

Note: The David Kahne vault page credits Kahne as producer on Sugar Ray (2001) — this should be corrected; Discogs credits that record to Don Gilmore.

”Fly” and Super Cat

The “Fly” single (Discogs ID 1091884) is credited solely to Sugar Ray (2) in Discogs credits for that pressing. Super Cat (2) (William Anthony Maragh, Discogs ID 158787) appears on the track as a guest artist but is not credited on this specific release entry. Super Cat’s verse is a key element of the track; his Discogs page (top labels: Wild Apache, Columbia, Power House) places him firmly in the dancehall reggae world.
