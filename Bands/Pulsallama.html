Aug 08, 20265 min read

Pulsallama

Discogs artist ID: 336483

All-female bass-and-percussion ensemble from New York City, active 1981–1983, associated with the No Wave / post-punk downtown Manhattan scene. Originally a 12-piece collective, later trimmed to 7 members. Released two 12” singles on Y Records and a posthumous compilation on Modern Harmonic in 2020.

Biography

Formation (1981)

Pulsallama coalesced in 1981 out of Manhattan’s Lower East Side nightlife. The band was initially a 12-piece all-girl group built around bass and percussion — no guitars, no conventional song structures. Their sound, in their own orbit of description, was “13 girls fighting over a cowbell.” They performed regularly at the clubs and loft spaces of the downtown scene — the same circuit as Bush Tetras, ESG, Liquid Liquid, and The Contortions.

Theatrical stage antics, props, costumes, verbal and physical altercations mid-performance, and awkward time signatures made them a cult attraction. They were not, strictly speaking, a recording band for most of their existence — the live show was the product.

Active Years (1982–1983)

In 1982 Pulsallama opened for The Clash at a 6,000-capacity show. The crowd reportedly showered them with coins and cups of beer. Whether this was hostile or celebratory seems to have been genuinely ambiguous — both, probably.

Their debut 12” single — “Ungawa Pt. II (Way Out Guiana)” / “The Devil Lives in My Husband’s Body” — was released in 1982 on Y Records (cat. 12Y 25), produced by Disc O’Dell (a.k.a. Dick O’Dell), the British label’s founder. It was distributed by Rough Trade and became a minor college radio and cult hit. The single brought them a small but devoted following on both sides of the Atlantic.

A second 12” followed in 1983: “Oui-Oui (A Canadian in Paris)” / “Pulsallama on the Rag” (Y Records, YT 103), again produced by Disc O’Dell. The A-side was a disco-inflected track (Discogs genres it as Funk/Soul → Disco), marking a slight pivot away from the raw percussion assault of the debut.

The band dissolved around 1983. No album was ever released during their lifetime.

Posthumous Compilation (2020)

In 2020, Modern Harmonic (MHCD-216) issued Pulsallama, a CD compilation gathering the band’s recorded output. Compiled and produced by Jay Millar and Brian Thompson. The package included liner notes by Erika Elizabeth and cover photography by George DuBose. Several original members — including Wendy Wild, April Palmieri, Jean Caffeine (2), Stace Elkin, Kimberly Davis (4), and Min Sanchez — are credited on the release.

Disc O’Dell (Dick O’Dell) was the founder of Y Records, a Bristol-linked UK post-punk/funk label whose roster included Pigbag, Maximum Joy, The Slits, and Rip Rig + Panic. His producing Pulsallama brought a UK downtown-funk sensibility to the NYC percussion ensemble.

Jay Burnett (also known as Burzootie) was a New York-based producer/engineer active in the early 1980s No Wave and dance scenes.

Mark Kamins — best known for producing early Madonna — appears in the credits on the “Oui-Oui” release, indicating a connection to the Danceteria/Downtown Music Scene production circuit.

Scene Connections

Pulsallama operated squarely inside the No Wave / post-No Wave downtown Manhattan circuit:

  • Bush Tetras — fellow all-female or female-fronted percussion-heavy group from the same Lower East Side scene. Pat Place of Bush Tetras circulated in the same spaces.
  • ESG — South Bronx sisters whose bass-and-rhythm minimalism ran parallel to Pulsallama’s percussion aesthetic; both on the Y Records / 99 Records radar.
  • The Contortions / James White & The Blacks — James Chance and the downtown No Wave nucleus that preceded and overlapped with Pulsallama.
  • Liquid Liquid — shared the No Wave / dance hybrid terrain and downtown venue circuit.
  • 8 Eyed Spy — Lydia Lunch’s group, overlapping personnel and venue worlds.
  • Y Records — the Bristol/London label that released both Pulsallama singles; also home to Pigbag, Maximum Joy, and The Slits reunion material.
  • 99 Records — the Greenwich Village label (Ed Bahlman) central to the ESG / Liquid Liquid downtown scene; Pulsallama ran in overlapping circles.
  • No Wave — Pulsallama is often categorized in the post-No Wave / “mutant disco” phase rather than the hardcore No Wave of 1978–80, but the genealogy is direct.
  • Downtown Music Scene — their performance circuit: Mudd Club, Danceteria, loft spaces.
  • Danceteria — the key club connection; Mark Kamins’s involvement ties in directly.
  • Ann Magnuson — vocalist/performance artist, Pulsallama member (early lineup), also a Mudd Club fixture and later fronted Bongwater; contributed liner notes to the Bush Tetras retrospective in 2021.
  • Mad Violets — Wendy Wild co-founded this neo-psychedelic garage band in 1982 with Dino Sorbello, overlapping with her final Pulsallama years. Keith Streng (Fleshtones) also played in the Mad Violets.
