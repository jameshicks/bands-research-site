Overview

Faust (7) is the post-2004 continuation of the krautrock group led by Jean-Hervé Peron, with drummer Werner “Zappi” Diermaier participating until 2021, alongside rotating English and French collaborators including Amaury Cambuzat, Geraldine Swayne, Gunther Wüsthoff, and James Hodson. This incarnation is distinct from the parallel Faust (6) entity led by Hans Joachim Irmler, representing the division of the original band into two separate ongoing projects. The catalogue spans three decades — eight releases in the 2000s, seven in the 2010s, and two in the 2020s — with Bureau B and Dirter Promotions as the principal label homes. The group has been notably collaborative, sharing credits across multiple releases with Keiji Haino, Nurse With Wound, Family Fodder, Kommissar Hjuler Und Frau, and producer Johann Scheerer.

Notes (web)

Why Zappi and Péron formed their own Faust faction in 2005

The 2005 divergence into two parallel Fausts — the Péron/Diermaier-led entity (later styled “faUSt”) and Hans-Joachim Irmler’s continuing version — stems from a combination of a personal falling-out in 2004 and longer-running creative incompatibilities.

The 2004 break with Irmler. Zappi Diermaier stated directly: “In 2004, I had trouble with Jochen Irmler. Jean-Hervé and me got together again.” He offers no further detail on the nature of the conflict, but the result was that Zappi stopped working with Irmler and resumed contact with Péron (who had been absent from Faust for several years). Source: Perfect Sound Forever interview with Zappi Diermaier, https://www.furious.com/perfect/faustzappi.html (date not stated on page; interview reflects post-2005 situation).

Creative differences between the two configurations. Zappi also noted a fundamental difference in how the two lineups worked: “with Jochen Irmler, we improvise better and with Jean-Hervé, we play more conceptual.” This suggests the Péron/Zappi collaboration answered a different musical instinct — structured, concept-driven work — that Irmler’s improvisation-centred approach did not satisfy. Same source as above.

Péron’s account: clashing personalities and diverging artistic attitudes. Jean-Hervé Péron, asked why Faust exists as two incarnations, stated: “We had to separate because our personalities clashed and our attitudes towards art diverged. So now, we have two Faust’s. Isn’t that better than just one?” He offered no further specifics beyond this summary. Source: Psychedelic Baby Mag interview (2011), https://www.psychedelicbabymag.com/2011/05/faust-interview-with-jean-herve-peron.html.

Péron elsewhere on the relationship between the two Fausts. In another interview Péron described the ongoing state as: “now it’s just the two of us doing one entity of faUSt, and another one doing another entity of Faust. We are on absolute neutral terms. We don’t talk to each other, we splendidly ignore each other.” Source: Little Village Magazine interview, https://littlevillagemag.com/interview-deep-cut-faust/ (date not stated on page).

Irmler’s perspective: the split as consistent with Faust’s anarchic logic. Irmler acknowledged that Diermaier and Péron “broke the rules, in a way” (the original understanding being that any of the six founders could use the Faust name, but there should never be two Fausts simultaneously). He said he needed time to adjust but ultimately concluded: “Why not?!” and framed the dual existence as “the same as it ever was … how it was in the beginning.” He did not characterise the events of 2004–2005 as driven by personal animosity. Source: The Quietus interview with Irmler (re: Faust Is Last), https://thequietus.com/interviews/faust-interview-with-hans-joachim-irmler-for-album-faust-is-last/.

Earlier precedent: Irmler’s 1990s characterisation of Péron’s departure. A 1998 freq.org interview has Irmler describing Péron’s earlier exit from the 1990s Faust reconvening: “We had real differences in the thinking about and making music. We diverged – in a way he was into things we as a group could not agree with.” This pre-dates the 2005 events but suggests that creative incompatibility between Péron and Irmler was a recurring rather than sudden condition. Source: freq.org interview (October 1998), https://freq.org.uk/interviews/a-splendid-chaos-an-interview-with-faust/.

Summary. The available record shows: (1) a specific but undescribed personal conflict between Zappi and Irmler in 2004 triggered the immediate break; (2) Péron and Zappi’s shared artistic preference for conceptual rather than free-improvisation work made a Péron/Zappi pairing the natural outcome; (3) a longer history of creative incompatibility between Péron and Irmler stretching back to the 1990s suggests the 2005 split reflected a structural mismatch as much as a single incident. No source specifies what the 2004 “trouble” between Zappi and Irmler actually was.
