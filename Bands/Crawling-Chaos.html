Crawling Chaos

Post-punk / industrial / experimental group from Tyneside (north-east England), active from around 1980 through the mid-1980s with archival activity extending into 2012. Associated primarily with Factory Records (FAC 17, 1980) and Factory Benelux (FAC BN.6, 1982), and ran their own imprint Foetus Products for the bulk of their catalog. Their sound sits at the abrasive intersection of no-wave, art-rock, and proto-industrial experimentation — wilfully chaotic, sardonic, and difficult to classify. Discogs artist ID: 11280.

History

Formation and early membership (c. 1979–1980)

Crawling Chaos formed on Tyneside in the late 1970s. The core creative nucleus coalesced around Martin Rees (3) (also credited as Strangely Perfect) and Jeff Crowe (also credited under the alias Doomage Khult). The Discogs profile also lists David Halton, Garry Clennell, and Eddie Fenn as members, while early participants Dave Cook and Steve Smith had departed before the first release. The band operated their own recording studio, called The Pits, which served as the base for most of their recorded output.

Factory Records and debut single (1980)

In June 1980 the band released their debut single “Sex Machine” b/w “Berlin” on Factory Records (catalog number FAC 17). The sleeve was designed by Jon Savage (credited as Sage (25)), marking an early link between the band and the Factory orbit. Both tracks on the single were written by Jeff Crowe and Martin Rees (uncredited on the pressing). The self-produced single draws on minimal synth textures, skewed rhythms, and a confrontational vocal approach. “Sex Machine” runs 5:40; “Berlin” runs 6:55.

Foetus Products and cassette era (1981)

Later in 1981 the band issued the cassette album Homunculus Equinox on their own Foetus Products imprint (no catalog number). Fifteen tracks across two sides, the cassette has no individual personnel credits on Discogs and bears no external producer. It is placed stylistically as avantgarde / experimental / new wave art rock. Both this release and the debut LP are noted as stemming from sessions at The Pits in 1981.

Factory Benelux LP (1982)

The band’s first vinyl LP, The Gas Chair, appeared in January 1982 on Factory Benelux (catalog FAC BN.6 / FAC BN6) — manufactured in Belgium. The title was intended as an anagram of “Crawling Chaos” with the fuller working title “The Gas Chair Clown.” Illustrations were contributed by Denyse Willem. Nine tracks across two sides, ranging from the 11-minute “Disierta Membra” to short provocations under two minutes. The band self-produced. The matrix etchings read “NOBODY DOES IT BETTER FOON” (side A) and “NOBODY DOES IT BETTER” (side B).

Blood Samples From The Pox Clinic and The Big C (1984)

In 1984 the band produced a Foetus Products compilation cassette, Blood Samples From The Pox Clinic, featuring various Tyneside acts including Infected (14), Dionysian Heights, Freak Electric, Vex Dhole, B.U.E.M., Granville Strobe, The Blonde Etheopian, and Arse (3). Crawling Chaos appear in a producer capacity rather than as performers.

The same year saw the release of their second full LP, The Big C, on Foetus Products (FOETUS 3). Credited to “The Crawling Chaos” on the sleeve, it is another self-produced set of eleven tracks — again recorded at their own studio. Stylistically it broadened into alternative rock and avantgarde territory.

Around this period the band also ran their own club night in Newcastle.

Later archival activity (2012 and beyond)

Spookhouse (2012), released as a CDr on Foetus Products (foetus101), collects what are described as “the last ever recordings (1987).” Fourteen tracks in a more song-structured format, the release carries genres spanning electronic, rock, stage & screen, and non-music, reflecting the band’s wide range. No individual personnel credits appear in the Discogs record for this release. Discogs lists activity continuing to 2024, suggesting some ongoing presence or reissue activity.

Foetus Products / Foetus (Thirlwell) name question

Research date: 2026-05-16 (web trawl)

The question of whether Crawling Chaos’s Foetus Products imprint has any connection to Foetus (JG Thirlwell)‘s various “Foetus” imprint names was investigated via web sources. Summary of findings:

Timeline overlap. Both “Foetus” entities emerged at almost the same moment: Crawling Chaos’s Homunculus Equinox on Foetus Products appeared in November 1981 (Cerysmatic Factory discography entry); Thirlwell’s first Foetus single (“O.K.F.M.” / “Spite Your Face”) was released 1 January 1981 on his Self Immolation label in London (foetus.org biography). Both names therefore entered circulation within the same calendar year. Foetus Products may have been set up slightly before — the Crawling Chaos official site notes it was founded “in the basement of Baldy Chester’s flat in Tynemouth” via a “mutual agreement” with local band “Danger in Paradise,” as part of the studio/label operation that also recorded material for Homunculus Equinox and The Gas Chair (crawlingchaos.co.uk/myths).

No documented connection. No interview, liner note, or contemporary press source found makes any link between the two “Foetus” entities. The Crawling Chaos official site, Strangely Perfect’s personal site (strangelyperfect.tv), LTM Recordings’ biography, and every Thirlwell interview consulted (TapeOp #141, foetus.org/biography, furious.com, 1995 Permission Magazine interview) are entirely silent on the other party. Thirlwell’s name does not appear anywhere in Crawling Chaos’s documentation; Crawling Chaos does not appear in any Thirlwell source.

Geographic separation. Thirlwell was based in London from 1978 to 1983 and moved in the Some Bizzare / post-punk London orbit (prag VEC, Nurse With Wound, Cabaret Voltaire contacts). Crawling Chaos were rooted in Tyneside. Both had a Factory Records connection — FAC 17 for Crawling Chaos in 1980 — but Thirlwell had no documented Factory relationship.

Thirlwell’s name choice. The available record indicates Thirlwell chose the name as a deliberately provocative gesture in the context of anti-abortion politics (1995 Permission Magazine interview). He also created a fictional corporate mythology around the name (multiple fake personnel aliases) partly inspired by The Residents. No source links this choice to awareness of any northern England label.

Crawling Chaos’s name choice. The origin of the “Foetus Products” name is unexplained in all available sources. The band’s own extensive Myths section on crawlingchaos.co.uk describes the label’s founding circumstances but gives no etymology.

Conclusion (provisional). The most likely explanation is coincidence — two acts in different cities independently reached for the same provocative word in 1980–81. The word “foetus” carried shock/transgression value in the early post-punk period and would have been a natural choice for confrontational DIY outfits with no connection to each other. No source substantiates influence in either direction. The question cannot be definitively closed without a direct statement from Crawling Chaos members (e.g. Strangely Perfect / Martin Rees), which no published interview provides.

Open sub-question. Whether Crawling Chaos or Thirlwell were ever aware of each other remains unknown.
