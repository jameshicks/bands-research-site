Reggie And The Full Effect

American indie/emo/electronic project led by James Dewees. Discogs artist ID: 316152. Based in Kansas City, Missouri. Active from 1999; ongoing.

Name variations: Reggie, Reggie & The Full Effect

Biography

Origins and Second Nature Era (1999–2000)

Reggie And The Full Effect began as the solo/side project of James Dewees (born March 13, 1976, Liberty, Missouri), keyboardist and multi-instrumentalist for The Get Up Kids. In 1999, while the Get Up Kids were rising in the midwest emo scene, Dewees released the debut album Greatest Hits ‘84–‘87 on Second Nature Recordings (SN015), recorded and engineered by Ed Rose at his Kansas City studio. The record is a genre-hopping mix of power pop, emo, and humor-driven interludes — short spoken skits bookmarking actual songs — setting the template for RATFE’s irreverent aesthetic. Ed Rose mastered by Steve Heritage.

A second record, Promotional Copy, followed in 2000 on Second Nature (SN021), produced by Ed Rose. It expanded the template with guest appearances: Sean-O-Tronic (an alias of Sean Ingram, also of Coalesce) featured on “Something I’m Not,” Fluxuation on “Gloves,” and Common Denominator on “Dwarf Invasion.” Both names — Fluxuation and Common Denominator — recur as Dewees’s own alter-egos throughout the RATFE catalog. Promotional Copy was later reissued on vinyl.

Vagrant Records and National Profile (2000–2005)

By 2000 Dewees had signed RATFE to Vagrant Records, home to The Get Up Kids and much of the midwest/second-wave emo scene. The first Vagrant release was a split 7” with Koufax (VR346 / Heroes & Villains Records HV005, 2000): RATFE contributed “Everyone Is Crazy,” tracked by Ed Rose (credited as “Ed ‘Axl’ Rose”); Koufax contributed “Minor Chords,” recorded by Dave Trumfio. Cover art/design by Travis Millard (credited as Travis M. Millard, also known under the alias Fudgefactorycomics).

In 2003, Vagrant released Inside The Dust Sleeve… (VR-0379-1), a compilation/outtakes LP — later released in Germany on Motor/Universal (2003, “Your Bleedin Heart”). Layout credited jointly to Hungry Bear (another Dewees alter-ego, alias of Sean Ingram) and Reggie (Dewees). Produced and recorded by Ed Rose.

Songs Not To Get Married To (VR405, 2005) was the most fully-realized Vagrant-era proper album, featuring a live band lineup for the first time. Personnel:

Last Stop: Crappy Town and Concept Work (2008)

Last Stop: Crappy Town (VR493, 2008) was a concept record structured around New York City subway line names. RATFE’s most sonically adventurous record to this point, incorporating post-hardcore and electronic textures alongside rock. Band lineup:

RolePerson
Vocals, KeyboardsJames Dewees

Sean Beavan (producer) is known for work with Nine Inch Nails and Marilyn Manson. The album marked a notable departure in sonic ambition from the earlier emo-humor records. It was the final RATFE release on Vagrant.

Pure Noise Era (2013–2018)

After a five-year gap, RATFE signed to Pure Noise Records and released No Country For Old Musicians (PNE 139, 2013). The album functioned partly as a reunion/showcase of friends and collaborators, billed with a large cast of guest “starring” performers:

41 (PNE210, 2018), named for Dewees’s age at recording, returned to a tighter lineup. Key personnel:

Guest vocals by Jeramiah Pauly on “Alone Again” and “The Horrible Year.” Recurring personas Fluxuation and Common Denominator appear again. The album brought Frank Iero and Ray Toro — both My Chemical Romance members and RATFE collaborators across multiple records — into production roles.

Recurring Alter-Egos and In-House Personas

Dewees credits recurring fictional artists throughout the RATFE catalog, all functioning as humor devices or in-universe guests:

  • Fluxuation — appears on Promotional Copy, No Country For Old Musicians, 41
  • Common Denominator — appears on Promotional Copy, Inside The Dust Sleeve…, Songs Not To Get Married To, No Country For Old Musicians, 41
  • Hungry Bear / Hungary Bear — layout credit and featured performer on Inside The Dust Sleeve…; an alias of Sean Ingram (also Coalesce vocalist)
  • Sean-O-Tronic — featured on Promotional Copy; also an alias of Sean Ingram
  • Floppy Disk-0 — featured on No Country For Old Musicians
  • Reggie (17) — production credit on No Country For Old Musicians; listed as alias of James Dewees (Discogs artist ID: 3568628)
