Overview

Turing Machine is a New York City instrumental rock band formed in 1998 by Justin Chearno, Scott DeSimon, and Gerhardt “Jerry” Fuchs, drawing from math rock, post-rock, krautrock, and experimental psychedelic territory. Their catalogue spans four releases split across the 2000s and 2010s, moving from early output on Jade Tree and Frenchkiss Records to Temporary Residence Limited for their later work. Recurring collaborators include James Murphy and Tim Goldsworthy — both central to the DFA Records orbit — alongside Dennis McNany, who appears across the bulk of their releases.

Notes (web)

Murphy / Goldsworthy / DFA connection

Turing Machine’s debut A New Machine for Living (recorded October 1999, released January 2000 on Jade Tree) was produced, mixed, and programmed by James Murphy & Tim Goldsworthy “for DFA at Plantain Recording House, NYC” — liner notes credit confirmed via the Jade Tree Bandcamp page (jadetree.bandcamp.com). Dennis McNany assisted; Bill Kellum recorded; Alan Douches mastered.

The credit line uses the “DFA” name even though DFA Records was not formally founded until 2001 (with Jonathan Galkin as label manager alongside Murphy and Goldsworthy — DFA Records, Wikipedia, as of 2024-12-01). The Turing Machine album is widely cited as the DFA production duo’s inaugural or very early joint production; one 2017 retrospective piece notes “The duo’s first production was, I believe, the Turing Machine album, A New Machine for Living, in 2000” (Rough Guide to DFA, ystrickler.com).

The working relationship appears to have grown from Murphy’s position in the New York indie rock engineering circuit — prior to DFA he had engineered and produced records for Six Finger Satellite, Les Savy Fav, and the pre-DFA incarnation of the Rapture (James Murphy, Wikipedia, as of 2024-12-01). Turing Machine (Chearno and DeSimon previously of DC noise-rock band Pitchblende, Fuchs formerly of Vineland) were part of the same NY art-rock milieu. No interview or press piece located explicitly names the intermediary or event that brought Murphy and Goldsworthy to the project.

Timeline note / potential conflict: Multiple sources state Murphy met Goldsworthy while engineering David Holmes’ Bow Down to the Exit Sign, released June–September 2000 (James Murphy, Wikipedia). However, the Turing Machine album was recorded October 1999 — before that release date. The Holmes album may have been recorded during 1999 while still in post-production for its 2000 release, which would reconcile the dates. An alternate version of the story in some sources names the earlier Holmes album Let’s Get Killed (1997, produced by Goldsworthy) as the point of contact (DFA Records, Wikipedia). This conflict is unresolved by available web sources.

The connection was therefore most likely through shared studio circles in New York’s late-1990s indie/post-rock scene — not through the DFA dance/electronic scene, which postdates the Turing Machine recording — with Murphy’s pre-existing production relationships bringing both parties together before the DFA label identity was formalised.
