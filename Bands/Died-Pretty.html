Died Pretty

Discogs ID: 291453. Australian indie/alternative rock band formed in Sydney, New South Wales, in early 1984. Active 1984–2002, with regular reunion shows from 2008 through their final performances in April 2021. In 1984 credited briefly as “The Died Pretty.”

Overview

Died Pretty were a key act in Sydney’s post-punk and garage-influenced underground of the mid-1980s, bridging the world of Rob Younger’s Radio Birdman-adjacent scene and the broader Australian indie circuit. Signed first to the independent Sydney label Citadel (7) (and its sublabel Blue Mosque), they later reached international audiences via Beggars Banquet in the US and, by the early 1990s, via Columbia in Australia and North America. Their core creative engine was the singer-songwriter partnership of vocalist Ronald S. Peno and guitarist Brett Myers.

The band formed around Peno (born 26 July 1955, Boggabri NSW) and Myers, with co-founder Frank Brunetti on keyboards. Died Pretty recorded nine full-length albums and a significant run of singles and EPs before parting ways in 2002. Peno died 11 August 2023 in Melbourne.

Key Producers and Collaborators

Rob Younger (Radio Birdman, The New Christs) produced all early Citadel releases: the 1984 singles, Free Dirt (1986), Lost (1988), and the Pre Deity compilation. His involvement was a direct link to the Radio Birdman/Stooges-influenced Sydney underground.

Hugh Jones (London-based; worked with Echo & The Bunnymen, Bauhaus, Modern English) produced Doughboy Hollow (1991) and Trace (1993), the two Columbia-era albums that represented the band’s most polished sound.

Alan Thorne engineered almost all the Citadel-era recordings and early sessions.

Don Walker (Cold Chisel) played piano on the Lost track “Free Dirt.”

Evil Graham Lee (The Triffids) played pedal steel on “Through Another Door” from Free Dirt.

Louis Tillett (also in No Dance with Brett Myers) played piano on “Life To Go (Landsakes)” from Free Dirt.

Amanda Brown (The Go-Betweens) played violin on Doughboy Hollow.
