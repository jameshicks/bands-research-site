One Dove

Scottish electronic group, active 1991–1993. Discogs artist ID: 6519.

Biography

One Dove formed in Glasgow in the late 1980s around the trio of Dot Allison (vocals, keyboards, programming), Ian Carmichael (keyboards, programming), and Jim McKinven (keyboards, programming, bass, guitar). McKinven had previous history in Altered Images and Berlin Blondes.

The group’s name variants on early pressings include “Dove” and “Black Dove” — the Discogs name variations confirm both were in use.

Their debut single “Fallen” appeared in 1991 on Soma Quality Recordings (Glasgow), the label run by Slam (Orde Meikle and Stuart McMillan), situating them firmly within the emerging Scottish house scene. The record was credited on the Discogs pressing as “Dove” rather than One Dove.

The Soma connection brought them into the orbit of Boy’s Own, the London collective and label. Boy’s Own Productions — a division under the London Records umbrella — signed One Dove, and in 1992 they released “Transient Truth” (BOIX 10), their first record under the full group name. The single was produced by Andrew Weatherall with the band, with additional production and remixes from Weatherall and the team of Jagz & Gary Burns. Styles on the 12” span dub and progressive house, signalling the hybrid approach that would define the album.

Throughout 1993 the group released three further 12” singles on Boy’s Own Productions before the album appeared:

  • “White Love (The Slam Remixes)” (BOIXR 14, early 1993) — remixed by Glasgow duo Slam, with The Sabres Of Paradise credited as additional producers.
  • “Breakdown (The Secret Knowledge Remixes)” (BOIXR 15) — remixed by Secret Knowledge (Bent Recknagel, Kris Needs, Wonder Schneider), again with Sabres Of Paradise production input.
  • “Why Don’t You Take Me (Secret Knowledge / Underworld Remixes)” (BOIXR 16) — remixes by Secret Knowledge and Underworld, with Stephen Hague handling vocal recording and mix on one version.

The album Morning Dove White was released in 1993 as a double LP (Boy’s Own Productions 828 352-1 / 828 444-1, under London Records distribution). It was produced by Andrew Weatherall and the band. Engineering and mixing was split: Hugo Nicolson handled “Fallen” (A1) and “Breakdown (Cellophane Boat Mix)” (B1); Jagz Kooner and Gary Burns handled the remaining tracks. The album featured session players including Jah Wobble (bass on “There Goes The Cure”), Andrew Innes (acoustic guitar on “Breakdown”), drummer/percussionist Ed Higgins, and guitarists Phil Mossman and Tom Baeppler. Weatherall co-wrote “Sirens” (C2) with the band.

A US promo CD (FFRR DPRO 100-2) compiled four tracks: “White Love (Guitar Paradise Edit)”, “Breakdown (Radio Mix)”, “Fallen”, and “Why Don’t You Take Me”, with a radio mix of “Breakdown” produced additionally by Stephen Hague.

Boy’s Own Productions was dropped by London Records in 1993; the label’s dissolution corresponded with the end of One Dove’s recording career. The group released no further material. Dot Allison went on to a solo career; Ian Carmichael joined The Orchids (2) and later formed Montjuïc and I.C.D.W.. Jim McKinven continued in Organs Of Love and other Glasgow projects.

In 2006, “Fallen” appeared on Soma Classics Volume 3, a Soma Quality Recordings compilation.

Labels Thread

One Dove’s release history maps a clear path through the Glasgow-to-London axis of early 1990s UK dance music:

  • Soma Quality Recordings (Glasgow, label ID 18): Glasgow-based label co-founded by Slam. One Dove’s “Fallen” (1991) was their debut, issued as “Dove.” Soma later compiled it on Soma Classics Volume 3 (2006).
  • Boy’s Own Productions (London, label ID 680176): UK imprint under London Records, co-founded by Terry Farley, Andrew Weatherall, and others; stylised variously as “Boys Own Productions” or “Boys Own.” Issued all One Dove’s subsequent 12”s (BOIX 10, BOIXR 14–16) and the album (828 352-1). The label was dropped by London in 1993. Sublabels include Junior Boy’s Own and Boy’s Own Recordings.
  • FFRR (London, label ID 297, parent: FFRR Records Ltd.): London Records imprint used for the US promo CD (DPRO 100-2) in 1993. FFRR was London’s primary dance/pop imprint.

Scene Context

One Dove sat at the intersection of the Glasgow house scene (via Soma and Slam) and the broader Balearic/post-acid-house network centred on Boy’s Own and Andrew Weatherall. Their records were stylistically diverse — the debut 12” “Fallen” and early singles leaned toward progressive house and dub; the album incorporated indie-pop textures, session guitar, and Dot Allison’s soul-influenced vocals. Genres on Discogs span Electronic, Pop, Dub, Progressive House, Indie Pop, House, Techno, Synth-pop, Trip Hop — reflecting the eclecticism of their moment.

Weatherall’s concurrent work on the Sabres Of Paradise project (and earlier the Primal Scream Screamadelica sessions with Hugo Nicolson) overlaps heavily with One Dove. Hugo Nicolson engineered both Screamadelica-era Primal Scream work and One Dove’s album. Andrew Innes of Primal Scream played acoustic guitar on the album. These personnel links place One Dove squarely within the Weatherall production nexus of 1991–1993.

The Slam / Soma connection further ties them into the Glasgow techno scene that would develop through the mid-1990s independently of the London Boys Own orbit.
