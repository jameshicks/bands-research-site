Bass Communion

Discogs ID: 529
Real name: Steven John Wilson (ID: 227943)
Active: 1995–present (intermittent)
Origin: UK
Genres: Ambient, Drone, Electronic, Experimental
Name variations: BC, BC:

Ambient/drone/electronic solo project of Steven Wilson, operating in parallel with his work in Porcupine Tree, No-Man, and later solo career. The Bass Communion name covers textural experiments built from processing instrumental performances, loops, and field recordings. Wilson has described the earlier records as drawing on electronic sources, while later albums moved more fully into drone-based processing of acoustic material. The project is intermittent — periods of silence followed by bursts of releases — and has always operated outside the commercial prog infrastructure, gravitating toward specialist experimental labels.

Aliases and Parallel Projects

Steven Wilson uses an unusually large number of pseudonyms: Digit (4), I.E.M., JC Camillioni, Linton Samuel Dawson, Mr Jelly, Sebastian Tweetle Blampton III, Sir Tarquin Underspoon, Timothy Tadpole Jones — each covering different one-off or minor release contexts. Bass Communion is his principal name for the ambient/experimental strand.

Discography by Era

Phase 1: Early Cassette / Electronic (1995–1999)

The earliest Bass Communion recordings circulated on cassette in the mid-1990s (pre-dating the first CD). The first commercial release consolidated recordings from this period.

Bass Communion (1998, 3rd Stone, cat# stone 036 cd)
Debut CD. Tracklist: “Shopping,” “Drugged” (with Theo Travis on soprano saxophone), “Sleep Etc,” “Orphan Coal,” “Drugged” (long version, featuring a Robert Fripp guitar sample). Written/recorded by Steven Wilson; design/photography by Carl Glover (4). Label: 3rd Stone — the Porcupine Tree-adjacent independent label. Styles: Abstract, Ambient, Minimal.

Bass Communion (II) (1999, Hidden Art, cat# hi-art 4)
Double CD with bonus EP disc. Tracklist includes “Advert,” “16 Second Swarm,” “Grammatic Oil,” “Drugged 3,” “Dwarf Artillery,” “Wide Open Killingfeld” (co-written with Theo Travis), “A Grapefruit In The World Of Park” (featuring Robert Fripp soundscape, Wilson on effects/filters), “Snakebird” (remix by Mark Poysden). Travis plays flute and saxophone across the album. Label: Hidden Art (the Hidden Art imprint connected to Porcupine Tree’s circle).

Bass Communion v Muslimgauze (1999, Soleilmoon Recordings, cat# SOL 89 CD)
Five tracks (titled simply “One” through “Five”), total runtime approx. 44 minutes. Primary credit: Bass Communion v Muslimgauze. Additional credits: Steven Wilson — Instruments, Editing; Bryn Jones (Muslimgauze) — Rhythms, Loops; Carl Glover (4) — Graphic Design, Photography. A posthumous collaboration: Bryn Jones died in January 1999 before the album was completed; Wilson assembled the final record from Jones’s contributed loops and rhythms. One of the most discussed records in the Muslimgauze catalog for its structural contrast — Wilson’s processed ambience laid over Jones’s percussive patterns. Styles: Ambient, Dub, Experimental, Techno, Tribal. See also the follow-up EP below.

Phase 2: Soleilmoon Period / Proliferation (2000–2006)

Bass Communion v Muslimgauze EP (2000, Soleilmoon Recordings, cat# SOL 106 CD)
Two further tracks (“Six” and “Seven”) continuing the same sessions, totalling approx. 18 minutes. Same credit structure as the album — Wilson credited for Instruments, Processing, Editing; Jones for Rhythms, Loops. Wilson contributes liner notes credited under his own name. This completes the collaborative material from the 1998–1999 sessions.

Bass Communion (III) (2001, Burning Shed, cat# none)
CDr. Archival/compilation framing — track dates in titles span 1995–1999 (e.g., “Amphead (1997),” “Slut 2.1 (1995),” “Sickness (1996),” “43553E99.01 (1999)”). Theo Travis credited on one track (“Reformat Spiders”). Design by Aleph (10) (a Carl Glover alias). Label: Burning Shed (the Porcupine Tree mail-order/digital operation).

Jonathan Coleclough • Bass Communion • Colin Potter (2003, ICR, cat# icr 39)
Double CD. Three-way collaboration: each artist contributed source material; each mixed one or more tracks. Track-level credits: “Passed” — mix by Colin Potter, sources by Bass Communion; “Yossaria” — mix by Bass Communion, sources by Potter and Jonathan Coleclough; “Raiser” — mix by Potter, sources by Bass Communion; “Pethidine” — mix by Coleclough, sources by Bass Communion and Coleclough (Theo Travis on saxophone, uncredited); “Epidural” (73:57) — mix by Coleclough, sources by Bass Communion (Theo Travis on saxophone, uncredited). Label: ICR.

Ghosts On Magnetic Tape (2004, Headphone Dust, cat# HDBCCD9)
Five-part suite, each titled “Ghosts On Magnetic Tape I–V.” Track V features Theo Travis on flutes. Additional sample material by Jonathan Coleclough. Created by Steven Wilson (producer credit). Styles: Abstract, Dark Ambient, Drone, Psychedelic. Label: Headphone Dust. Also issued in a reconstructed version:

Ghosts On Magnetic Tape (Reconstruction By Andrew Liles) (2004, Headphone Dust, cat# separate)
Andrew Liles reconstruction of the above. Same label.

Indicates Void (2005, Tonefloat, cat# tf27 equivalent)
Tonefloat (Netherlands). First of several releases on this label.

Continuum (2005, Soleilmoon Recordings, cat# SOL 136 CD)
Three tracks (“Construct I–III”), each roughly 20 minutes. Credited to Continuum (2) — ambient project of Steven Wilson and Dirk Serries (Dirk Serries, a.k.a. Vidna Obmana, Fear Falls Burning). Bass Communion and Vidna Obmana credited as Performers. Photography/design by Lasse Hoile. Styles: Ambient, Drone, Minimal.

Loss (2006, Soleilmoon Recordings, cat# SOLV 011)
Vinyl LP only. Two sides: “Loss Side 1” (19:34) and “Loss Side 2” (18:52). No additional credits beyond Bass Communion. Minimal format — no personnel listed. Styles: Ambient, Minimal. One of the most stripped-back Bass Communion releases.

Pacific Codex (2006, Equation Records (2), cat# listed)
Release on Equation Records (2).

The Continuum Recyclings, Volume One (2006, Tonefloat, cat# tf27)
Double LP. Primary credit: Continuum (2) / Bass Communion & Vidnaobmana. Four untitled sides (~19–20 minutes each). Bass Communion credited Composed By; Dirk Serries (as Vidna Obmana) credited Remix [Recyclings]. Design/photography: Carl Glover (4). Styles: Ambient.

Phase 3: Live Work and Heavier Drone (2007–2009)

Track titles are timestamps: “2.29,” “17.12,” “18.54.” Design: Carl Glover (4). Photography: Lasse Hoile. Styles: Drone, Experimental, Noise. Label: Important Records (2).

Further live/experimental material. Important Records.

Litany (2009, Tonefloat, cat# tf71)
12” EP. Two tracks: “Litany 1-403-1204” (6:23) and “Litany 2-923-0402” (15:25). Wilson uncredited as performer in Discogs. Design/photography: Carl Glover (4). Styles: Ambient.

Phase 4: Return and Late Work (2011–present)

Cenotaph (2011, Tonefloat, cat# tf116)
Four lengthy tracks (each ~17–21 minutes): “Citadel (q),” “Carrion (u),” “Cenotaph (r),” “Conflux (n).” Created by Steven Wilson. Design/photography: Carl Glover (4). Styles: Dark Ambient, Deep Techno, Drone. Final Tonefloat release before a long hiatus.

Bass Communion / Freiband (2015, Important Records (2))
Split/collaboration with Freiband (Frans de Waard, ID: 4788). Important Records.

Sisters Oregon (2017, Drone Records)
Drone Records (Germany). Features Schola Cantorum Of The Cardinal Vaughan Memorial School (choral contribution) and Aleph (10) (Carl Glover alias) on design.

And No Birds Sing (2021, self-released)
Digital release. Two tracks: “And No Birds Sing (Soundtrack)” (27:52) and “And No Birds Sing (Theme)” (13:24). Genres: Electronic, Stage & Screen — framed as soundtrack material. Voice contributions by Mia Wilson and Yali Wilson (family members). Design: Carl Glover (4). Self-released on the Bass Communion Bandcamp.

The Itself Of Itself (2024, Lumberton Trading Company, cat# LUMB036CD)
Seven tracks. Styles: Abstract, Ambient, Electroacoustic, Experimental, Noise. Wilson credited as Performer and Producer. Design: Carl Glover (4). Label: Lumberton Trading Company. Most recent full-length.

Thief of Snow (1999) (2024, self-released)
Archival release — material from 1999, self-released digitally in 2024.

Recurring Personnel

Carl Glover (4) (Discogs ID: 1835910) — design and photography across virtually the entire Bass Communion catalog (72 shared releases). Operates under aliases Aleph (10) and Carl Glover Archive. The consistent visual identity of Bass Communion is his work.

Theo Travis — saxophone and flute on early releases; credited on 16 Bass Communion releases. One of the few outside musicians to appear on core (non-collaborative) albums.

Lasse Hoile — photography and design on selected releases including Continuum and Live In Mexico City. A regular Porcupine Tree visual collaborator who crossed over to BC.

Continuum (2)

Continuum (2) (Discogs ID: 294502) is an ongoing ambient project with Dirk Serries (Belgium). Profile: “Ambient project of Steven Wilson (Porcupine Tree, Bass Communion, Blackfield) and Dirk Serries (Vidna Obmana, Fear Falls Burning).” Members per Discogs: Steven Wilson and Dirk Serries. Releases include:

  • Continuum (2005, Soleilmoon, SOL 136 CD)
  • The Continuum Recyclings, Volume One (2006, Tonefloat, tf27) — credited Continuum (2) / Bass Communion & Vidna Obmana; remixes by Dirk Serries
  • Continuum I + II (2014, Tonefloat) — reissue/compilation

Relationship to Other Wilson Projects

  • Porcupine Tree — Wilson’s main band, progressive rock. Several Bass Communion personnel (Theo Travis, Carl Glover, Lasse Hoile) overlap with the PT world.
  • No-Man — Wilson’s art-pop duo with Tim Bowness. Emotionally adjacent but stylistically separate.
  • I.E.M. — Wilson alias for more rhythmic/electronic material, distinct from BC’s drone focus.
  • Continuum (2) — Ongoing duo with Dirk Serries; the most sustained external collaboration in the BC orbit.

Notes on the Muslimgauze Collaboration

Bryn Jones sent Wilson tapes of loops and rhythms; Jones died on 14 January 1999. Wilson completed the album posthumously, released later that year. The collaboration is notable in the Muslimgauze archive as one of the few records where Jones’s material was assembled by someone outside his own circle. The follow-up EP (SOL 106 CD) released the remaining tracks from the same sessions.
