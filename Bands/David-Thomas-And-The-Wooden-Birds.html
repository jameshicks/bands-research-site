David Thomas And The Wooden Birds

American avant-rock ensemble led by David Thomas (2) (vocalist of Pere Ubu). Active mid-1980s, 1986–1987. Discogs artist ID: 666290.

Name variations: David Thomas & The Wooden Birds; The Wooden Birds; Wooden Birds.

Overview

David Thomas And The Wooden Birds was one of several rotating solo ensembles through which David Thomas (2) explored territory outside Pere Ubu during the 1980s. The group was formed from a core of Cleveland / New York musicians who were variously also in Pere Ubu and its adjacent circles: Tony Maimone (bass), Allen Ravenstine (EML synthesizer), Daved Hild (drums), and George Cartwright (saxophone). Their sound was a stripped-back, acoustically inflected avant-rock — compared to the Wooden Birds’ immediate predecessor outfit David Thomas And The Pedestrians, the arrangements are sparser, with accordion, cello, and EML synthesizer doing much of the atmospheric work.

The group recorded two studio LPs for Rough Trade in 1986–1987 and documented a 1986 Belgrade concert on the Yugoslav cassette label Nikad Robom. The LPs were produced by Paul Hamann (first) and Chris Cutler (second), both also appearing as performers. A 1997 box set Monster (Cooking Vinyl / Tim/Kerr) compiled the two LPs alongside material from David Thomas And The Pedestrians and David Thomas And Two Pale Boys, crediting David Thomas (2) as the sole primary artist with Wooden Birds material appearing on discs 3 and 4.

The Wooden Birds are distinct from The Wooden Birds, an unrelated Austin indie project.

Release Detail

April U Beogradu (Nikad Robom 003, 1986)

Split cassette, Yugoslavia. Cassette-only release from the Nikad Robom catalog. Captured a 1986 Belgrade performance.

  • Side A: Joëlle Léandre (double bass, vocals) — solo improvisations. Repertoire includes pieces by Jacob Druckman, Betsy Jolas, Steve Lacy, Giacinto Scelsi, John Cage, and Léandre originals.
  • Side B: David Thomas And The Wooden Birds — eight tracks including “What Happened To Me”, “Happy To See You”, “Red Tin Bus”, “Monster Thinks Of The Good Days”, “Pedestrian Walk / Codex”, “Street Waves / Non Alignment Pact”, “Sloop John B.”, “Coffee Dance / Modern Dance / About True Friends”.

Lineup on Side B: David Thomas (2) (vocals, harmonica), Tony Maimone (bass), Daved Hild (drums), George Cartwright (saxophone), Allen Ravenstine (EML synthesizer).

Several Side B track titles overlap with material on Monster Walks The Winter Lake, suggesting this Belgrade cassette documents the same touring repertoire.

Monster Walks The Winter Lake (Rough Trade ROUGH 90, 1986)

LP, 45 RPM. UK. Produced, recorded, mixed, and edited by Paul Hamann.

No saxophone; George Cartwright does not appear on this LP — his credited role is limited to the Belgrade cassette. Garo Yellin’s cello presence on Side A is a key textural element absent from the cassette recording.

Blame The Messenger (Rough Trade ROUGH 120, 1987)

LP. UK. Substantially different lineup from Monster Walks: Chris Cutler (drums) and Jim Jones (3) (acoustic guitar, electric guitar, vocals) join; Hild, Cartwright, and Yellin are absent. Produced by Chris Cutler. Engineered by Paul Hamann. Mastered by Michael Bishop.

Tracklist: “My Town” / “A Fact About Trains” / “King Knut” / “When Love Is Uneven” / “The Storm Breaks” // “The Long Rain” / “Having Time” / “Friends Of Stone” / “The Velikovsky 2-Step”.

Compositions credited to David Thomas individually (“My Town”, “Friends Of Stone”) and to Wooden Birds collectively (all other tracks). The nine-track LP runs approximately 38 minutes. Cutler’s involvement brought a more compositionally structured approach; the writing credits show a shift toward collective band authorship.

Monster box set (Cooking Vinyl / Tim/Kerr HR 110, 1997)

5-CD retrospective of David Thomas’s 1980s solo output. Disc 3 (“More Places Forever”) contains Monster Walks The Winter Lake in full; Disc 4 (“Blame The Messenger”) contains Blame The Messenger in full. Both discs credit the Wooden Birds as the performing ensemble within the box set’s overall attribution to David Thomas (2). The box also includes new overdubs on earlier material: Allen Ravenstine contributed overdubs to Disc 1 (Pedestrians material), and Jim Jones (3) to Disc 2 (with Richard Thompson). Paul Hamann handled digital transfers and EQ for the Wooden Birds discs.

Research Queue

  • What is the recording date(s) and venue for the April U Beogradu cassette — the title implies April 1986 in Belgrade, but no venue or exact date is listed in Discogs or on the cassette itself; a contemporary Yugoslav concert listing or festival program would confirm.
  • Monster Walks The Winter Lake is pressed at 45 RPM for a full LP — did Rough Trade issue any statement or liner note explaining this choice? The 1997 Monster box transfers it at standard speed; whether there is an audible pitch difference between the formats is unresolved.
  • Roz Ilett plays accordion on “Bicycle” (B1 of Monster Walks) but has no other Discogs credits — is this a stage name, and what was her affiliation to Thomas or the band?
  • George Cartwright’s participation appears to be limited to the Belgrade cassette — is there any documented reason he was not on the Rough Trade LPs (scheduling, lineup change at recording time)?
  • Was Blame The Messenger recorded at Suma Recording (Paul Hamann’s Cleveland studio), which is the location associated with most David Thomas recordings of this era?
