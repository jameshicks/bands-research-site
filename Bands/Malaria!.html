Malaria!

Oct 29, 20268 min read

Malaria!

Discogs artist ID: 47470
Origin: Berlin, Germany
Active: 1981–1984 (disbanded); 1991 partial reunion (Köster, Gut, Duursma); subsequent reissue activity into the 2020s
Genres: Post-punk, no wave, neue Deutsche Welle (NDW)

All-female Berlin post-punk/no wave group. One of the more internationally connected bands in the early-1980s Berlin underground, releasing records on Belgian label Les Disques Du Crépuscule and performing at New York’s Danceteria. Their sound mixed abrasive rhythms, synthesizer, and Bettina Köster’s confrontational vocals with an aesthetic rooted equally in the New York no wave scene and Berlin’s post-Wall experimental underground.

Overview

Malaria! was formed in Berlin in January 1981, initially as a duo of Bettina Köster and Gudrun Gut (real name Gudrun Bredemann), both formerly of Mania D., the short-lived Berlin all-female post-punk group that also included Beate Bartel. The band quickly expanded to a five-piece with the addition of Manon Duursma (guitar), Christine Hahn (drums, organ), and Susanne Kuhnke (synthesizer).

Their debut 12” EP — a self-titled five-track release on Marat Records (catalog MARAT 006, a sublabel of Zensor) — appeared in 1981. It was recorded at Christoph Franke Studio in Berlin and mixed by Klaus Krüger, the drummer and Berlin scene figure best known for his work with Tangerine Dream and Iggy Pop. Gudrun Gut and Bettina Köster are credited as “Music By.”

In 1982 the band released a cluster of records across multiple international labels: the Emotion LP on the Berlin imprint Moabit Musik (subsequently licensed to Belgium’s Les Disques Du Crépuscule and Italy’s Base Record, and pressed in Japan on Columbia); singles “How Do You Like My New Dog? / Pernod.” and “Weisses Wasser: White Water” on Les Disques Du Crépuscule; and the EP New York Passage on Das Büro (Germany), Jungle Records (UK), and Cachalot Records (US). This international distribution reflected the label connections fostered by the Belgian post-punk network around Les Disques Du Crépuscule.

The Emotion LP was mixed by Ken Thomas (who mixed most of the album), Mark Reeder, Martin Culverwell, and Gudrun Gut (tracks A3/B5). Bettina Köster and Gudrun Gut are credited for lyrics throughout.

A notable lineup detail on “Weisses Wasser”: Calli (3) (Discogs ID 1789746) — who appears in the Malaria! member list — is credited for drums and bass, suggesting a fluid lineup or guest appearance on that single. Beate Bartel (see below) is not credited on any Malaria! studio recordings; she appears only as live sound engineer on …Revisited.

The 1983 cassette …Revisited (ROIR, US) was recorded live at Danceteria in New York City, documenting a US tour stop. The five-piece lineup — Gut (drums, guitar, backing vocals), Hahn (drums, organ), Duursma (guitar), Kuhnke (synthesizer), Köster (vocals, harmonica) — is fully credited. Production mix by John Hanti (ROIR staff producer). Beate Bartel is credited as live sound engineer.

Beat The Distance appeared on Rebel Rec. in 1985, after the band’s 1984 disbanding (dating discrepancy: the label year is 1985 but the profile states they disbanded after the 1984 “Beat the Distance” release — this may indicate a post-disbanding release). The full five-piece is credited as performers.

In 1991, Köster, Gut, and Duursma briefly reconvened. Moabit Musik issued the compilation Compiled (later expanded as Compiled 1981-1984 in 2001 and Compiled 2.0 in 2019) and new single “Kaltes Klares Wasser,” which became an unexpected later-career hit, generating an extensive run of remixes and reissues through 2001 on Superstar Recordings/Universal and Monika Enterprise. They also released Elation (1992) and Cheerio (1993) on Moabit Musik, and Delirium: Remixed, Remade, Remodelled (1993) on MFS.

M_Sessions (2021, Moabit Musik / Monika Enterprise) represents their most recent activity.

Note on Beate Bartel: The original stub listed Bartel as a member, but Discogs credits do not support this. She is credited only as “Sound Engineer Live” on …Revisited (1983). Her primary connection to Malaria! is through Mania D., the precursor group she shared with Köster and Gut (as Gudrun Bredemann). Bartel’s main recording project is Liaisons Dangereuses, where she co-produced and co-wrote all material with Chrislo Haas and Krishna Goineau.

Note on Christine Hahn: The vault previously had her split across two pages with a disambiguation error. Discogs ID 375281 is the same person: an American drummer (born Ohio, 1951; died May 28, 2025) who moved to Berlin after touring with The Static, then joined Malaria!. See Christine Hahn (No Wave) for the full biography.

On the Einstürzende Neubauten connection: The original stub described Gudrun Gut as being “in the Einstürzende Neubauten circle.” Discogs does not show any direct shared releases between Gut and EN. A path of depth 2 exists via Anita Lane, but no direct collaboration is in the DB. The “circle” characterization likely reflects shared Berlin scene geography rather than recorded collaboration. The Mania D. → Malaria! lineage is the more precise story.
