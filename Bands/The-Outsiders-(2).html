The Outsiders (2)

UK punk/post-punk band. Discogs ID: 131374. Active c. 1976–1979, South London.

Overview

The Outsiders were a South London punk band formed in the mid-to-late 1970s around Adrian Borland (vocals, guitar) and Adrian Janes (drums). They are primarily significant as the immediate precursor to The Sound (2) and as the earliest recorded vehicle for Borland’s songwriting. The band released all their original material on their own self-run imprint, Raw Edge Records, making them an early example of UK DIY punk self-release.

Their membership overlapped with other South London pre-punk and post-punk formations — most notably The Crazies (3), a concurrent or earlier unit that shared personnel with the Outsiders before the same core group reconvened as The Sound. Graham Bailey (also known as Graham Green), who became Borland’s long-term musical partner through Second Layer and The Sound, appears in Outsiders credits from the demo period onward.

The band’s original LPs were largely overlooked on release. Reissue interest began in 1993 with Vital Years on Three Lines Records, and reached a peak with Cherry Red’s five-disc Count For Something box set in 2021, which collected the albums, demos, and a live recording at LSE.

Lineup

The core lineup across the band’s active period:

  • Adrian Borland — vocals, guitar, primary songwriter. Also credited with cover design on Calling On Youth.
  • Adrian Janes (alias Jan (17)) — drums, co-songwriter. Discogs ID: 603202.
  • Bob Lawrence — bass on Calling On Youth (1977) and One To Infinity (1977), and on the majority of the sessions compiled into Count For Something. Discogs ID: 432534.
  • Graham Bailey — bass on later demo sessions (credited on Count For Something tracks 4-14 to 4-23). Also known as Graham Green. The same Graham Bailey who played on all The Sound records and Second Layer. Discogs ID: 400406.
  • Graham Green — also credited on Close Up (1979) as a second bassist alongside Bob Lawrence, though Discogs identifies Graham Green as an alias of Graham Bailey. The exact lineup in the Close Up session may have involved a lineup transition.

Songwriting was primarily by Borland, with Janes co-credited on most tracks. A handful of tracks on later sessions carry three-way writing credits (Borland / Janes / Lawrence).

Compilations and reissues

Vital Years (1993) CD compilation. Three Lines Records LINE 04 (UK); also issued on Gift Of Life (Germany). Twelve-track selection drawing from both albums and the EP. First reissue of the band’s material.

The Collection (2000) CD. Mob Records (UK). Five pressings documented on Discogs. No detailed tracklisting on file.

Count For Something (Albums | Demos | Live | Unreleased 1976–1978) (2021) Five-disc box set. Cherry Red CRCDBOX110. The definitive archival release. Discs 1–2: Calling On Youth LP + One To Infinity EP, plus a full disc of demos, early songs, and Pathway Studio sessions (1976–1978). Discs 3–4: Close Up LP plus a full disc of Close Up demos and unreleased late-period songs. Disc 5: live recording at LSE, ten tracks including a cover of Iggy Pop / James Williamson’s “Raw Power.” Liner notes by Adrian Janes and John Clarkson (5). Engineers on the box set material: Bob Borland (most early sessions), Chas Herington (selected tracks), Gary Lucas (2) (Close Up sessions).

Also issued in Japan on Solid Records (6) (2021).

Calling On Youth / One To Infinity Demos And Early Songs (2024) LP. Lantern Rec. LANR040. Limited edition vinyl drawn from the Count For Something demo material. Eleven tracks including alternate versions, Pathway sessions, and early originals (“Anniversary,” “I’m Gonna Be Free,” “Perfect Girl (Demo)”). Europe-only pressing.

Context

The Outsiders occupied an interesting position in the South London punk ecosystem. Raw Edge Records (which Discogs describes as a “label created for” the band) placed them firmly in the self-release DIY tradition that characterised UK punk at the margins of the 1977–1979 period. Their sound progressed across the three Raw Edge releases: the debut LP sits squarely in punk, while Close Up (1979) shows the longer song structures and emotional directness that Borland would develop fully with The Sound.

The band’s demo archive (now documented in the Count For Something box) reveals a productive parallel track of studio experimentation — Pathway Studio sessions alongside home demos — running concurrently with the official releases. The live set at LSE, drawn from the Close Up period, confirms the band was performing “Raw Power” in their set, situating them within the broader Stooges-influenced UK punk tradition.

Adrian Janes did not follow Borland and Bailey into The Sound but remained a co-writer: he is credited on two tracks on The Sound’s debut Jeopardy (1980). Adrian Janes also contributed liner notes to the 2021 Count For Something box set.

The other South London formation sharing this personnel was The Crazies (3), whose 1978 recordings (released 2021 on Optic Nerve Recordings as A Simple Vision) document an earlier stage of essentially the same circle.
