Discogs ID: 20784
Origin: Glasgow, Scotland
Active: 1995–present

Scottish post-rock band. One of the definitive acts of the post-rock genre alongside Explosions In The Sky. The Bad Fire (2025, Rock Action Records / Temporary Residence Limited) produced and mixed by John Congleton.

Notes (web)

John Congleton — how the relationship began

The Bad Fire (2025) is the first Mogwai album produced by John Congleton; earlier albums attributed to him in some sources (Rave Tapes, Every Country’s Sun, As the Love Continues) were in fact produced by Dave Fridmann.

The connection came via Franz Ferdinand. Stuart Braithwaite first encountered Congleton in Los Angeles, where Congleton had worked on FFS (2015), the Franz Ferdinand / Sparks collaboration. Franz Ferdinand drummer Paul Thomson recommended Congleton strongly. Braithwaite also followed Congleton on Instagram; Congleton replied casually (“Hey, how’s it going?”), opening an informal channel. (Northern Transmissions, LeftLion, Feb 2026)

The collaboration was cemented by practical circumstance: when Barry Burns’ daughter required medical treatment, Burns could not leave Scotland, so the band needed a producer willing to travel north. Congleton agreed to work at Chem 19 Studios in Blantyre. Braithwaite also cited Congleton’s work with Angel Olsen as a draw: “I know he worked with Angel Olsen before and I’m a massive fan of hers, so it just seemed like a perfect fit to work with John.” (LeftLion; Arts Fuse)

Braithwaite described the personal fit: Congleton “started out in a punk rock band, he worked with Steve Albini” and felt like “one of us.” Working method aligned with the band’s prior experience: Congleton prefers recording bands playing live, similar to Fridmann’s approach. Congleton was described as fast, efficient, and with a compatible sense of humour — qualities Mogwai said matter to them. (Silent Radio, Mar 2026; Arts Fuse)

Barry Burns (Post Trash interview, Jan 2025): “As soon as we finished the session, we were just like, go and do what you want with us. And he did.”
