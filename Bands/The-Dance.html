The Dance

No wave / new wave band. New York City. Active c. 1980–1982; retrospective compilation 2022. Discogs ID: 47969.

Fronted by Eugenie Diserio — vocalist, organist, and fashion designer whose homemade looks were as much a part of the band’s identity as the music. Spun off from Model Citizens (3) after that group’s 1979 breakup; the other faction of Model Citizens became Polyrock. The Dance occupied the downtown NYC no wave / dance-punk scene, playing clubs including Danceteria and the Mudd Club. They also produced and backed a parallel project fronted by Chandra Oppenheim (then 12 years old), daughter of Diserio’s art professor Dennis Oppenheim.

Origins: Model Citizens (3)

Model Citizens (3) (Discogs ID 4680657) formed in NYC c. 1978–1979. The lineup included Eugenie Diserio, Steve Alexander, Billy Robertson, Gloria Richards, Robert Medici, and Tomek Lamprecht. Their sole release was the Shift The Blame EP (Spy Records, 1979), produced by John Cale — who reportedly took the job after losing out to Brian Eno on the chance to produce Television.

When Model Citizens split, the group fractured into two distinct projects:

  • The Dance — Diserio, Steve Alexander, Louis Watterson, Robey Newsom, and later Jim Martin; oriented toward danceable no-wave funk
  • Polyrock — Billy Robertson-led outfit; produced by Philip Glass, who performed on their first two albums

The Dance’s sound shared with Polyrock a repetitive keyboard-groove minimalism with hints of Glass, but offset it with Diserio’s sultry vocals, suggestive lyrics, and a danceable groove.

Parallel Project: Chandra

The Dance produced and backed Chandra Oppenheim’s debut EP Transportation (Go Go Records / ON (3), 1980), with Chandra then aged 12. Her father, conceptual artist Dennis Oppenheim, had been Eugenie Diserio’s art professor; Chandra grew up attending parties at her family’s downtown loft where Model Citizens and The Dance played.

Eugenie Diserio and Steve Alexander produced, arranged, and played all instruments on the EP; Fred Maher drummed on three tracks (A1, B1, B2); Chandra wrote the lyrics and sang. The 12-year-old fronting a no-wave dance band played well in the downtown NYC arts scene — they toured the northeast US on double bills of Chandra + The Dance, playing Danceteria and other key venues.

A second unreleased EP with an all-kid band (“The Chandra Dimension”) was shelved until 2008 when Cantor Records reissued both EPs together. Further reissues followed: Transportation EP’s (Telephone Explosion Records, Canada, 2018), Blue Stage Sessions (Third Man Records, US, 2019), Part Time Punks Session (We Are Time, US, 2022).
