Battle of Mice

Post-metal / sludge metal duo formed in New York, c. 2005–2009. Discogs artist ID: 624596. Core duo: Julie Christmas (vocals) and Josh Graham (guitar, keyboards, visual art). Extended live/studio lineup included Joel Hamilton (drums, producer, engineer), Tony Maimone (bass), and Joe Tomino (drums on later recordings).

Overview

Battle of Mice emerged from a 2005 tour pairing of Made Out Of Babies and Red Sparowes. Christmas (frontwoman of Made Out of Babies) and Graham (touring guitarist and visual artist for Red Sparowes and Neurosis) initially clashed on that tour but eventually formed a personal relationship and began writing together. The band’s music was built around Christmas’s volatile, wide-ranging vocals — from near-whispered fragility to confrontational screaming — and Graham’s dense, slow-moving guitar architecture.

Their first recorded output was two tracks on the three-way split EP Triad (Neurot Recordings, NR-044, 2006), shared with Red Sparowes and Made Out of Babies. The two Battle of Mice tracks (“Sleep and Dream” and “The Lamb and the Labrador”) were produced by Joel Hamilton and the band together, with Andrew Schneider handling some tracking. Hamilton also mixed the Red Sparowes live tracks on the same EP, a sign of the tight production network around these bands.

The debut (and only) full-length, A Day of Nights (Neurot Recordings, NR-047, 2006), was produced, engineered, mixed, and partially performed by Hamilton, who also played guitar and bass on several tracks. Mastering was by Doug Henderson. The album’s seven tracks document — per Graham’s own account — the breakdown of his and Christmas’s relationship, the duo reportedly refusing to be in the same room together by the end of the sessions. Josh Graham designed and laid out the record; Brendan Tobin shot the cover and band photography. All songs were written jointly by Graham and Christmas.

In 2008, Battle of Mice contributed two new tracks (“The Bishop” and “Yellow and Black”) to a split CD with Jesu on Robotic Empire (ROBO 070). These tracks introduced Joe Tomino on drums and featured Hamilton again on guitar, production, and engineering. Nick Zampiello handled mastering. Graham designed artwork for the split under his suspendedinlight.com imprint.

The band announced their split in September 2009. In 2016, Belgian label Consouling Sounds issued All Your Sympathy’s Gone | The Complete Recordings (SOULLXXX), a double-LP compilation collecting the full album and the Jesu split tracks, mastered again by Doug Henderson, with new layout and cemetery photography by Graham.

Open Questions

  • Solo release date for Julie Christmas: Discogs profile mentions The Bad Wife (2010), another source names Mayhem (2010, Translation Loss) — which is correct?
  • Joe Tomino’s role: listed as a full member on Discogs, confirmed on drums for the Jesu split tracks (2008). Tomino was also in Dub Trio simultaneously; he is not credited on A Day of Nights or Triad, suggesting he joined the live/session lineup for the Jesu split sessions only. Whether he toured with the band remains unclear.
  • Andrew Schneider’s exact credit scope: listed as “Recorded By” on Triad track 2 and A Day of Nights track 3 only — was he a secondary engineer throughout or only those sessions?
