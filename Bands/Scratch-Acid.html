Scratch Acid

Austin, Texas noise rock band. Active 1982–1987; reunions 2006, 2011–2012. Discogs ID: 219388.

Scratch Acid is one of the earliest and most influential noise rock bands to emerge from the American underground. Formed in Austin, Texas in 1982, the band built a reputation for confrontational, abrasive punk-informed noise over five years and three releases before disbanding in 1987. Frontman David Yow and bassist David Sims subsequently formed The Jesus Lizard, giving Scratch Acid an outsized legacy relative to their small recorded output.

Steve Anderson

Anderson was involved in Austin’s early 1980s punk scene (Toxic Shock, Cry Babies, Screws) and was present at the band’s formation. He was dismissed before Scratch Acid booked their first performance — the show was scheduled without him being informed. Yow then moved from bass to vocals, and Sims shifted from guitar to bass. Anderson remained on friendly terms with the other members. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26); https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2006-09-01/399553/]

History

Formation (1982–1983)

Scratch Acid formed in Austin, Texas in 1982. The earliest version included Steve Anderson on vocals, David Wm. Sims on guitar, Brett Bradford on guitar, David Yow on bass, and Rey Washam on drums. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]

According to Bradford, the band coalesced around a flyer he posted at a record store seeking musicians. Yow — already a friend attending shows — joined, and Yow’s roommate David Sims was also pulled in. [source: http://post-trash.com/news/2025/10/6/scratch-acid-feature-interview]

The band played their first show on January 20, 1983, at Studio 29 in Austin, performing only instrumentals. Their official debut with Yow as frontman was March 10, 1983, at the Skyline Club, on a bill with TSOL, the Big Boys, and the Butthole Surfers. Washam briefly left during 1983 to play with the Big Boys but rejoined by late 1983. [source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2006-09-01/399553/]

Recordings and Peak Years (1984–1987)

The band released their self-titled EP in 1984 on the Austin indie Rabid Cat Records (cat# RAB-004-SA). Eight songs; recorded at Earth & Sky Studio. The EP peaked at UK Indie Chart no. 26. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26); https://rateyourmusic.com/release/ep/scratch-acid/scratch-acid.p/]

Their debut full-length, Just Keep Eating, followed in 1986 on Rabid Cat (cat# RAB/SA/007). The band characterized the title as an inside joke related to their shared living situation. Band members reportedly never received compensation from Rabid Cat or distributor Fundamental Records. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]

The final release while the band was active was the Berserker EP (1987) on Touch And Go (T&G #18). It reached UK Indie no. 7. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]

Over their five-year active run, Scratch Acid played only 146 shows. [source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2006-09-01/399553/]

Dissolution (1987)

Scratch Acid disbanded in 1987. The breakup is attributed to general exhaustion and internal strains from touring and recording rather than any single incident. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]

Post-Dissolution

David Yow and David Sims → The Jesus Lizard

In 1987, immediately after Scratch Acid’s dissolution, Sims joined Steve Albini’s new band Rapeman along with Rey Washam. Rapeman disbanded in 1989. Sims then reunited with Yow — who had relocated to Chicago — and they formed The Jesus Lizard in 1989, linking up with guitarist Duane Denison and drummer Mac McNeilly. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]

Rey Washam

Washam joined Rapeman with Sims and Steve Albini in 1987, following Scratch Acid’s disbandment. After Rapeman dissolved in 1989, he went on to drum for Helios Creed, The Didjits, Lard, and Tad. From approximately 1995 to 1999 he was drummer for Ministry; the band received a Grammy nomination for Best Metal Performance at the 42nd Annual Grammy Awards (2000) for “Bad Blood” from Dark Side of the Spoon (1999). [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey_Washam (as of 2025-06-26); https://www.protonicreversal.com/ep027-rey-washam-scratch-acid-rapeman-ministry/]

Brett Bradford

After Scratch Acid, Bradford went to Colorado State University to study wildlife biology. While there he formed Great Caesar’s Ghost, a Colorado punk band that disbanded in 1993. He also played in Sangre de Toro, Areola 51, Insect Sex Act, and Gorch Fock. He eventually returned to Austin, where he formed Suckling (active 2010s onward), a noise-rock/post-punk/psych outfit. [source: http://post-trash.com/news/2025/10/6/scratch-acid-feature-interview; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]

Steve Anderson (original vocalist)

Anderson remained in the Austin music scene after his dismissal, playing in Cry Babies, Screws, and Toxic Shock. No significant post-1980s music activity found in available sources. He died in 2015 (noted in Austin Chronicle obituary context, though the Chronicle piece retrieved concerned a brewing pioneer named Steve Anderson — see conflict note below). [source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2006-09-01/399553/]

Conflict/uncertainty: An Austin Chronicle piece from December 2015 titled “Remembering Steve Anderson” concerns a craft brewing pioneer, not the Scratch Acid vocalist. No confirmed separate obituary for the Scratch Acid Steve Anderson was found. The Austin Chronicle 2006 piece names him as ex-Toxic Shock but gives no post-1983 activity. Do not conflate the two Steve Andersons.

Reunions

  • September 2006: All four post-Anderson members reunited for three shows — Emo’s 14th anniversary in Austin (September 2), Touch and Go Records’ 25th anniversary in Chicago (September 9), and Seattle’s Showbox Theater (September 16). The Seattle set covered 20 of the band’s 28 recorded songs. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]
  • Fall–Winter 2011: Brief reunion tour. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey_Washam (as of 2025-06-26)]
  • March 2012: First UK performance since 1987, at the All Tomorrow’s Parties festival curated by Jeff Mangum (Neutral Milk Hotel). Extended North America/Europe tour dates followed through December 2012. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]

Legacy

AllMusic’s Stephen Thomas Erlewine credited Scratch Acid with having “laid the groundwork for much of the distorted, grinding alternative/punk rockers of the ’90s.” [source: https://www.allmusic.com/artist/scratch-acid-mn0000307332]

The self-titled 1984 EP featured “Lay Screaming,” which Yow described as inspired by the Marquis de Sade. Contemporary critics drew comparisons to The Birthday Party, though journalist Andrew Earles suggested this reflected limited available reference points rather than direct influence. [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_Acid (as of 2025-06-26)]
