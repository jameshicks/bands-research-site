From Discogs

Amp is an English group based around the core members Richard Walker and Karine Charff. Walker was a member of a band in Bristol called The Secret Garden that incubated a number of groups including Flying Saucer Attack and Third Eye Foundation. In 1992 he left the band; singles from Amp began to come out on a number of small English labels and a debut album called Sirènes was released in 1996 on the Wurlitzer Jukebox label in the UK and a short-lived American label called Petrol. Walker made some guest appearances on Third Eye Foundation recordings, and Matt Elliot did a TEF remix of Amp music for the In Version album. A London-based, live Amp unit coalesced around Walker and Charff with Walker’s bandmate from The Secret Garden, Gareth Mitchell, joining on guitar. Amp contacted Kranky when their American label dissolved and began work on a new studio album in summer 1996.

Overview

Amp is an English ambient/shoegaze group built around the core partnership of Richard Walker and Karine Charff, with roots in the Bristol scene that also produced Flying Saucer Attack and Third Eye Foundation — Walker left that incubator band, The Secret Garden, in 1992. Early singles appeared on a string of small UK labels before the debut album Sirènes (1996), and a live London unit formed around Walker, Charff, and Secret Garden alumnus Gareth Mitchell; the group moved to Kranky after their US label folded. Output was heaviest in the 1990s across Enraptured, Wurlitzer Jukebox, Darla, and Kranky, then slowed sharply before the group shifted to self-releasing on their own Ampbase imprint from 2005, with releases continuing through 2025.

Notes (web)

Robert Hampson collaboration — origin

Discogs credits Robert Hampson (Loop / Main) across four shared releases with Amp. The only release for which web sources document his specific role is Stenorette (Kranky, KRANK 031, October 1998), where he is credited as producer and mixing engineer; additional credits are David Wrench (engineering), Olivier Gauthier (rhythm programming), and Simon Heyworth (mastering). (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amp_(band), as of 2026-10-13; opus.ing/reviews/stenorette-amp)

No web source — including interviews with Hampson in The Quietus and Drowned in Sound, the Wikipedia articles for Amp, Robert Hampson, Main, and Loop, and the Bandcamp discography piece — explains how or why Hampson came to work with Amp. Hampson’s own statements in interviews describe a preference for working in isolation and a deliberate avoidance of being attached to scenes (source: thequietus.com/interviews/robert-hampson-loop-main-holding-title/). No shared label is documented: Main’s 1990s output was on Beggars Banquet / Situation Two; Amp moved through Enraptured, Wurlitzer Jukebox, Darla, and Kranky. Both had Kranky connections by the late 1990s (Amp released Astralmoonbeamprojections in 1997 on Kranky; Hampson produced Stenorette for the same label in 1998), making a Kranky introduction plausible, but no source confirms this.

The three other shared releases beyond Stenorette are not individually named by web sources; they may include remix contributions or compilation tracks not separately written up.

Conclusion: The origin of the collaboration is not documented on the open web as of October 2026. The Kranky label context is the strongest circumstantial thread, but remains unconfirmed.
