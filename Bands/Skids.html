Scottish punk band.

Formed in Dunfermline in 1977 by Richard Jobson (vocals), Stuart Adamson (guitar), William Simpson (bass) and Tom Kellichan (drums).

Rusty Egan replaced Tom Kellichan in 1979. William Simpson departed after the recording of the second album, following differences about the musical direction Egan and their producer Bill Nelson wanted to take. As a result the line-up changed with Russell Webb (bass) and Mike Baillie (drums) joining Jobson and Adamson. Adamson left in 1981 to form Big Country. Following Adamson’s departure, the band disbanded. They reformed in 2017.

Overview

Skids were a Scottish punk and new wave band formed in Dunfermline in 1977 around vocalist Richard Jobson and guitarist Stuart Adamson, whose later work with Big Country would eclipse the group in public memory. Bill Nelson anchored their production in the early years (58 shared credits), alongside regular studio collaborators Mick Glossop and David Batchelor; Rusty Egan replaced founding drummer Tom Kellichan in 1979 and pushed the band toward a more electronic direction, contributing to bassist William Simpson’s departure before Adamson himself left in 1981 to form Big Country. The catalogue runs to 21 releases across the 1970s and 1980s — almost entirely on Virgin — with sporadic activity through the 2000s and a 2017 reformation that has since generated four further releases on Last Night From Glasgow with an entirely new backing lineup.

Notes (web)

Why Bruce Fairbairn was chosen for the Days in Europa remix (1980)

What is established:

The original Days in Europa (October 1979, produced by Bill Nelson) was withdrawn by Virgin within a year. Richard Jobson confirmed in a later interview that Virgin rejected the Bill Nelson mix on sonic grounds — it wanted a “punchier” sound — rather than because of the controversial sleeve imagery (he explicitly called the artwork-withdrawal story “absolute rubbish”). Virgin commissioned Bruce Fairbairn to remix most tracks, re-record “Working for the Yankee Dollar”, add “Masquerade”, and redesign the sleeve for the 1980 reissue. Contemporary sources describe Fairbairn at this point as “up-and-coming Canadian” — his brief was “to bring out the best in Bill Nelson’s original production.” (Wikipedia snippet / search result)

Richard Jobson interview confirming the sonic rationale, dispelling the artwork myth, and noting that fans were offered a choice between the two versions: ukmusicreviews.co.uk

Fairbairn’s credentials at the time:

In 1979–1980 Fairbairn was a Vancouver-based producer/trumpeter, then still a member of the band Prism, for whom he had produced four studio albums (1977–1980), all platinum or double-platinum in Canada. His Armageddon (1979) won him the Juno Award for Producer of the Year in 1980 — his first Juno. He also produced Ian Lloyd’s Goose Bumps (1979, Scotti Bros. Records). His management connection was with Bruce Allen (Vancouver), who encouraged him to leave Prism and produce full-time in 1980; his next project after the Skids remix would be Loverboy’s debut. He had no documented prior relationship with Virgin Records or with the Skids. (The Canadian Encyclopedia; earofnewt.com 1998 interview; Wikipedia: Bruce Fairbairn)
