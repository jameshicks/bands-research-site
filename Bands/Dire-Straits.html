Overview

Dire Straits were a British rock band formed in London in 1977 by brothers Mark and David Knopfler, John Illsley, and Pick Withers. Mark Knopfler’s distinctive fingerpicked guitar style anchored a sound that blended Classic Rock, Blues Rock, and Pop Rock, carrying them from their 1978 debut through a commercially dominant run in the 1980s — roughly 21 releases that decade alone. The band split in 1988, reformed in 1991 with a partly changed lineup, and disbanded for good in 1995. Their catalogue appeared primarily on Vertigo and Mercury in the UK and Warner Bros. in North America, with producer Neil Dorfsman their most consistent studio collaborator across the late career.
