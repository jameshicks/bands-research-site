A Silver Mt. Zion

Montreal post-rock/folk collective founded by Efrim Menuck circa 1999–2000. Discogs artist ID: 43637. All releases on Constellation Records except The West Will Rise Again (2012, self-released via Ruined City Birdsong Combos imprint).

Operated under a long succession of name variations across its run — all unified under Discogs ID 43637.

Biography

Origins (1999–2000)

A Silver Mt. Zion began as a small-group vehicle for Efrim Menuck, guitarist and vocalist in Godspeed You Black Emperor!, during a period when GY!BE went on hiatus. Where Godspeed required democratic negotiation among nine musicians, ASMZ gave Menuck a more direct channel for his own compositions. The founding trio was Menuck (guitar, piano, organ, electronics), Sophie Trudeau (violin, voice), and Thierry Amar (contrabass, electric bass) — all three also core GY!BE members. Their debut, He Has Left Us Alone, But Shafts Of Light Sometimes Grace The Corner Of Our Rooms… (CST009, 2000), was recorded without a producer credit; mastering and sequencing by Harris Newman.

The sound on the debut leaned toward sparse, mournful chamber music — close-mic’d strings, piano, quietly hovering guitar, and very little percussion. The title and lyrical sensibility drew on Jewish mourning music traditions and a broadly elegiac, apocalyptic folk idiom.

Expansion and the “Tra-la-la Band” era (2001–2005)

For the second album, Born Into Trouble As The Sparks Fly Upward. (CST018, 2001), the ensemble expanded significantly. Ian Ilavsky (guitar), Jessica Moss (violin), and Rebecca Foon (cello) joined, bringing the band to six. The release was credited to “The Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra & Tra-la-la Band” — the first of many name variants. Production was handled by Howard Bilerman, who would remain a recurring engineer throughout the project’s run.

The name mutations multiplied with the third album, This Is Our Punk-Rock, Thee Rusted Satellites Gather+Sing, (CST027, 2003), credited to “The Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra & Tra-La-La Band With Choir.” This record expanded the ensemble concept to its most communal: a large “Rusted Satellite Choir” was assembled, including members of the broader Constellation/GY!BE community (among them Aidan Girt, Bruce Cawdron, Eric Craven, and Scott Levine Gilmore). The record marked the ensemble’s fullest expression of choir-rock — the integration of a non-professional, community chorus into the orchestral post-rock structure.

The 2004 EP The “Pretty Little Lightning Paw” E.P. (CST030) was released under the unusual one-off name “Thee Silver Mountain Reveries.” Horses In The Sky (CST033, 2005) returned to the full Tra-la-la Band name and a seven-piece lineup: Menuck, Trudeau, Amar, Moss, Foon, Ilavsky, and Levine Gilmore. For Horses, mastering shifted from Harris Newman to Jean-François Chicoine; Howard Bilerman remained on engineering. The album’s credit language became characteristically baroque, with each player’s role described in elaborate poetic epithets (e.g. Sophie Trudeau: “Broken Red Tooth Echo Hiccups, Stubborn Downward Gluestick Plummets, Busted Collarbone + Mighty Treble Needles”).

Streamlining (2007–2010)

The band shed the “Tra-la-la Band” suffix for 13 Blues For Thirteen Moons (CST051, 2008), simply “Thee Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra & Tra-La-La Band” on the sleeve but commonly shortened. The lineup also changed: Rebecca Foon departed, and Eric Craven (drums) became a credited member for this record. Recording was shared between Bilerman and Radwan Moumneh at Hotel2Tango. Alongside the regular member credits, photographer Jem Cohen contributed photography.

In 2010, the band reduced further to a quintet — Efrim, Thierry, Jessica, Sophie, and new member David Payant on drums, organ, and piano — shedding guitar players Ian Ilavsky and Scott Levine Gilmore. The resulting album, Kollaps Tradixionales (CST063, 2010), was recorded by Bilerman and Moumneh. The name settled on “Thee Silver Mt. Zion Memorial Orchestra” without any ensemble suffix.

Late period and final releases (2012–2014)

The West Will Rise Again (2012) was a limited self-release under the band’s own Ruined City Birdsong Combos imprint (RCBC-001), with the quintet lineup (Menuck, Amar, Moss, Trudeau, Payant). The band returned to Constellation for Fuck Off Get Free We Pour Light On Everything (CST099, 2014), recorded by Greg Norman, who also mixed it alongside the band. The lineup was the same quintet.

The final release under the ASMZ name was Hang On To Each Other (CST105, 2014), a 7” credited to “Thee Silver Mt Zion Memorial Orchestra feat. Ariel Engle.” Ariel Engle (of Aidan Knight / La Force) received co-primary credit. The release has no detailed production credits beyond lacquer cut.

After 2014, Menuck pursued solo work (his debut High Gospel had come out on Constellation in 2011), and the band entered an indefinite hiatus. No ASMZ releases appear on Discogs after 2014, confirming dormancy. Discogs lists ASMZ years_active as 2000–2021, but the 2021 figure likely reflects a reissue or pressing variant rather than new material — no new ASMZ primary release is catalogued after the 2014 Hang On To Each Other 7”.

Menuck has been active as a solo artist through at least 2020: Pissing Stars (Constellation, 2018), are SING SINCK, SING (Constellation, 2019), and Starling Troubles Sparrow (Longform Editions, 2020). ASMZ is therefore dormant as of the Discogs record — not formally dissolved, but with Menuck’s creative output channeled entirely into solo work since 2014.
