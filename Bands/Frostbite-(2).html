Overview

Frostbite (2) is a one-off Icelandic collaborative electronic project formed in 1993 by Einar Örn Benediktsson (vocals, trumpet, guitar) and Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson (instruments, engineering). Both members were central figures in the Reykjavík underground going back to the early 1980s — Einar Örn as a founder of Kukl and then vocalist in The Sugarcubes, Hilmar as a film composer and musician active across both the Icelandic experimental scene and the UK occult/industrial underground.

The project released a single album, The Second Coming (1993, One Little Indian), on the same UK indie that had launched The Sugarcubes internationally. The music is classified on Discogs as Electronic with Leftfield, Dance-pop, and Experimental styles — a reflection of the duo’s willingness to blend post-punk sensibility with then-current electronic production. Guest vocals appear on three tracks by Katie Jane Garside of Daisy Chainsaw / Queenadreena, who was also on the One Little Indian roster at the time.

The project has an alias listed on Discogs — Kali (63) (artist ID 10788817), shared between Einar Örn and Hilmar — though no separate Kali (63) releases appear in the DB.

The moniker H3ÖH (Discogs ID 775426) is a separate one-time alias used specifically for a remix collaboration in 1993, when Einar Örn and Hilmar teamed up with Andrew McKenzie of The Hafler Trio to produce The Hafler Trio Bootleg (Ash International, 1993). H3ÖH = HOH (Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson’s initials) + H3 (a Hafler Trio shorthand).

Scene Connections

One Little Indian (label ID 311) is the binding thread. The label was founded in 1985 by Derek Birkett (formerly of Flux of Pink Indians) and signed The Sugarcubes in 1986 — giving Einar Örn an existing relationship with the label that made The Second Coming a natural home. Daisy Chainsaw (and hence Katie Jane Garside) were also on One Little Indian at this time, explaining the guest vocals.

Ken Thomas had already engineered The Sugarcubes’ debut Life’s Too Good (1988, One Little Indian) — one of the threads connecting the post-punk London underground to Reykjavík. His mixing credit on The Second Coming continues that relationship. Thomas was also a member of Psychic TV, the same orbit Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson moved in (Hilmar is credited as a member of Psychic TV and the associated Thee Temple Ov Psychick Youth on Discogs).

Kjartan Kjartansson had been active in the Reykjavík scene since 1982 — engineering the Kukl Söngull 7” (1983) and the Sugarcubes debut — making his “Audiofiled By” technician credit on The Second Coming another continuity from the earliest Reykjavík underground.

Me Company (Paul White’s London-based design studio) were the visual designers of record for the One Little Indian / Björk / Sugarcubes world in the early-to-mid 1990s, responsible for Björk’s early solo artwork among many other commissions.
