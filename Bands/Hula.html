Hula

Active: 1981–c.1991; archive releases 2016 Based: Sheffield, UK Labels: Red Rhino Records, Sub Rosa, Wax Trax! Also credited as: Kula (alias) Genre: Post-punk, industrial funk, electro-funk, experimental

The Hula Kula house

Hula formed in Sheffield in 1981. The founding three members — Mark Albrow, Alan Fisch, and Ron Wright — shared a house with:

  • Stephen Mallinder (Cabaret Voltaire)
  • Paul Widger (They Must Be Russians, Clock DVA, The Box)

The house was called Hula Kula — from which both the band name and its alias “Kula” derived. This domestic arrangement is the root of every significant early connection: the Cabaret Voltaire link, the Sheffield post-punk network, and the particular sonic mix (cut-ups, steady percussion, paranoid vocals, white funk) that defined the early records.

Note on Justin Bennett: Percussion on Black Wall Blue (1986), Voice (1987), “Cut Me Loose” (1987) — credited “Drums, Percussion, Effects [Treatments], Violin” on Voice. Discogs ID: 63962. The Cabaret Voltaire Thread

The Hula Kula house produced direct musical overlap with Cabaret Voltaire:

  • Stephen Mallinder credited on “Black Pop Workout” and “Cut From Inside” (3 shared releases)
  • Chris Watson credited on “Black Pop Workout”, “Cut From Inside”, and “Spiral” (3 shared releases) — Watson’s electronics/tape work is audible in Hula’s cut-up approach
  • Nort (real name Paul Anthony Nortcliffe) — the drummer who replaced Alan Fisch in Hula also drummed for Cabaret Voltaire on the following specific recordings:
    • 2X45 (Rough Trade, 1982) — credited on sides C and D (the live 1982 Sheffield recordings); Alan Fisch played sides A and B. Nort’s credit: “Drums, Percussion.”
    • Live In Sheffield 19 Jan 82 (Paradox, 1982) — same live recording, separate release
    • The Drain Train / Live In Sheffield 19 Jan 82 (Mute/The Grey Area, 1991) — archival reissue
    • The Original Sound Of Sheffield ‘78/’82 Best Of (Mute/Virgin, 2002) — compilation drawing from these sessions
    • 1981–83 Demos (Sheffield Tape Archive, 2016) — archival
    Nort is not listed as a formal Cabaret Voltaire member on Discogs; his Cabs involvement was as a live drummer for the 1982 sessions recorded in Sheffield. His Discogs groups are: Hula (2), Chain, Sascha (12), Surface Mutants, The Pressure Company.

The Mute Connection: Daniel Miller and Paul Kendall

Daniel Miller (Mute Records founder, The Normal) and Paul Kendall (Mute in-house engineer/producer — Depeche Mode, Nitzer Ebb) both appear with 5 shared releases on Poison and Voice. John Fryer (Blackwing Studios — This Mortal Coil, Cocteau Twins) has confirmed credits on Poison (1986) as “Engineer [Engineered By (Mixing)]” and on Voice (1987) as “Engineer [Blackwing]” — the credit explicitly names Blackwing Studios.

Why John Fryer on Hula records? Fryer’s Hula work fits squarely in his mid-1980s orbit: by 1985–87 he was the go-to engineer at Blackwing Studios, London, working across 4AD (It’ll End In Tears for This Mortal Coil, 1984–85; Clan of Xymox, 1985–86), Mute (Depeche Mode, A Broken Frame multiple pressings), and adjacent indie labels. The Mute connection to Hula via Daniel Miller and Paul Kendall is the most likely route — Fryer was already embedded in their network. He is not credited as producer on the Hula records, only as mixing engineer, suggesting he was brought in for post-production mixing sessions at Blackwing rather than full studio direction.

This is a significant cluster: the Mute production team working on Red Rhino releases. It coincides with Hula supporting Depeche Mode at Wembley Arena — a booking that came with this Mute adjacency. The Mute/Red Rhino relationship here reflects the interconnection between independent UK labels in the mid-1980s.

John Dent — again

John Dent (The Sound Clinic) appears with 6 shared releases on Voice, “Cut Me Loose”, and Threshold (1987). This is the same mastering engineer credited on:

  • 23 Skidoo’s Urban Gamelan (1984)
  • Normil Hawaiians LP
  • James White & The Blacks (ZE Records)
  • Hula (1987)

Dent is a recurring node across this entire research — connecting Sheffield post-punk, London industrial/funk, and NYC No Wave via the mastering room.

Mark Brydon → Chakk → Moloko

Mark Brydon played bass on the early Hula recordings (3 shared releases on “Cut From Inside”, the Cut From Inside / Murmur reissue, and The Best Of). He subsequently joined Chakk — another Sheffield post-industrial band on FON Records — and then co-founded Moloko with Roisin Murphy in 1994. “Sing It Back” (1999) reached UK #4 in its Boris Dlugosch remix. One of the more remarkable career trajectories connected to this scene.

Sub Rosa (Belgium) and System Of Flux & Energies (1985)

The Belgian release on Sub Rosa (run by Guy Marc Hinant in Brussels) places Hula in the European experimental/industrial distribution network.

Credits from Discogs (release 293953): The primary credit on the release reads “SPK / Hula (2) / General Strike” — confirming all three acts as co-primary artists, not just guests. This is a shared release, not a compilation appearance. Sub Rosa (7) is credited for “Concept, Production.” Daniel Léon handled editing; Waving Ondulata contributed layout and illustrations; Guy Marc Hinant wrote sleeve notes.

SPK involvement: SPK (Discogs: 22501) are credited as full primary artist alongside Hula and General Strike. The release does not attribute specific tracks to specific acts, nor are individual SPK members credited — Discogs records the group credit only. A second version of the release (15568205) adds “Les Archives Sonores S.R.” as a fourth primary credit.

General Strike (Discogs: 156118) — similarly credited as primary co-artist. General Strike were a British dub/experimental trio founded in 1979, composed of Steve Beresford (Discogs: 123904), David Toop (Discogs: 391), and David Cunningham (Discogs: 41910), with guests including Lol Coxhill, Dawn Roberts, and Maartje Ten Hoorn. Beresford and Toop are both significant figures in British experimental/improvised music; Cunningham is known for work with The Flying Lizards and as a producer in the post-punk experimental field. Their inclusion on this Sub Rosa release places them firmly in the same European experimental/dub network as Hula and SPK.

Sub Rosa was a key European conduit for industrial, musique concrète, and experimental music — connecting the Sheffield scene to a Brussels-centred distribution and curatorial network that overlaps with the Les Disques du Crépuscule orbit.

Wax Trax! and the End

After Red Rhino Records went bankrupt (c.1988), Hula moved to Wax Trax! Records (Chicago — Ministry, KMFDM, Front 242, My Life With The Thrill Kill Kult). Their one Wax Trax! release — “VC1”, a cover of Jimi Hendrix’s “Voodoo Chile” — didn’t achieve the desired commercial effect. The band continued with Jo Cammack joining officially; new material was recorded in 1991, described by insiders as “the best Hula ever made.” It remains unreleased in studio vaults.

Live and Video

John Peel sessions recorded and broadcast; VPRO sessions (Dutch radio). Live shows featured extensive video material by Peter Care — who went on to direct major music videos for R.E.M., Nine Inch Nails, and Bruce Springsteen.
