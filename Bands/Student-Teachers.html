Biography

Formation (1977–1978)

The Student Teachers formed out of the social orbit of the downtown New York punk and new wave scene in the fall of 1977. The founding nucleus coalesced at a John Cale concert at CBGB: Bill Arning (Discogs ID 2298397), then sixteen and serving as fan-club president for the Mumps, met David Scharff (stage name Jan David Cruel; Discogs ID 867165 / 745552) and Phillip Shelley (Discogs ID 3791388). All three had come to see Cale; they found themselves at adjacent tables and began a friendship. Shortly after, the same group reconvened at a Patti Smith poetry reading, where Arning introduced his friends Lori Reese (Discogs ID 1982477) and Michael Alago.

The five soon resolved to start a band. Arning and Scharff financed rehearsal space; Shelley took lead guitar, Reese bass, and Laura Davis (full name Laura K. Davis; Discogs ID 740729) drums — all learning on second-hand instruments purchased from their rehearsal studio. Early songs included: “The Quake” (rooted in the Quaker school Arning and Davis attended — Friends’ Seminary on East 15th Street), “Captain Kelly,” “Zero Cool,” “Realworldfun,” “Channel 13,” “We’re All Damaged,” and “Christmas Weather” (written after a car accident on the way to Max’s Kansas City). Arning played keyboards, Scharff handled vocals (credited as Jan David Cruel on early releases).

Their first public performance was at the Friends’ Seminary gymnasium, sharing a bill with teenage punk band The Blessed. The Blessed’s invitation led to an opening slot at Max’s Kansas City on March 26, 1978 — bypassing the usual audition process entirely. A few weeks later they secured a CBGB booking, added as an opener for Teenage Jesus and the Jerks (arranged by Lydia Lunch directly).

Peak Activity (1978–1980)

The Student Teachers worked the downtown circuit — CBGB, Max’s Kansas City — alongside contemporaries in the No Wave and nascent new wave scenes. They recorded a debut single for Ork Records (the Terry Ork–founded label that had also released Television’s first single) in 1979: “Channel 13” b/w “Christmas Weather” (Ork Records, 1979; Discogs release ID 1606712). The single was produced by Jimmy Destri (keyboardist of Blondie) and engineered by Ray Bardani (Discogs ID 132144). Jerry Silber (Discogs ID 7102826) served as executive producer.

Personnel on the single:

  • Phillip Shelley — guitar
  • Lori Reese — bass (“Basic Black Bass”)
  • Laura Davis — drums (“Pink Drums”)
  • Bill Arning — keyboards (“After Eight Keyboards”)
  • Jan David Cruel (David Scharff) — vocals

In 1980, the band contributed two tracks — “Looks” and “What I Can’t Feel” — to the Red Star Records compilation Marty Thau Presents 2x5 (RED 100; Discogs release ID 1144322). The album placed two tracks each from five New York acts: The Fleshtones, Revelons, Bloodless Pharaohs (Brian Setzer’s pre-Stray Cats outfit), Comateens, and the Student Teachers. The compilation was again produced by Jimmy Destri and curated by label founder Marty Thau. Cover design was by Peter Zaremba of the Fleshtones.

A second lineup phase introduced Joe Katz (guitar; Discogs ID 1659675, also a member of Mumps (3)) and Hayden Brasseur (drums; Discogs ID 1982474) replacing some original members for later recordings.

The band dissolved around 1980, having left a small but sharp recorded legacy: one single and two compilation tracks.

Retrospective (2013–2014)

In 2013, Nacional Records issued Invitation To… The Student Teachers: 1978–1980 — The Complete Syllabus (Discogs release ID 5138218), a comprehensive retrospective compiling original recordings. The release credits two distinct recording phases:

  • Tracks 1–4: produced by Jimmy Destri; Laura Davis on drums; Phillip Shelley on guitar
  • Tracks 6–9: produced by Antone DeSantis (Discogs ID 1074245) and Jay Burnett (Discogs ID 65673), and the band itself; Joe Katz on guitar; Hayden Brasseur on drums

David Scharff (vocals) and Lori Reese (bass) appear across both phases. Phillip Shelley contributed all songwriting; the album is credited “Written-By: The Student Teachers.”

An Italian vinyl edition was issued in 2014 by Backstreet One Records as a limited-edition LP.

Scene Connections

The Student Teachers emerged directly from the CBGB / Max’s Kansas City scene. Their first bill was shared with The Blessed; their first CBGB slot was opened for Teenage Jesus and the Jerks (Lydia Lunch). They recorded on Ork Records, the Terry Ork label that had also released Television’s debut single and was a hub for the downtown avant/punk scene. Their Red Star compilation appearance placed them alongside The Fleshtones, Comateens, and Bloodless Pharaohs — all New York acts working the same circuit.

Phillip Shelley went on to Pianosaurus. Joe Katz had overlapping membership with the Mumps (the band that indirectly triggered the formation of the Student Teachers via the 1977 CBGB show). Bill Arning later became a prominent figure in the art world as a museum curator and director.
