Average White Band

Discogs ID: 97073 Origin: Glasgow, Scotland Active: 1972–1982, 1989–present (various reformations) Genres: Funk, Soul, R&B, Disco Labels: Atlantic Records (1974–1980), RCA (1978–1982), Arista (1980–1982), Track Record Company (1988–1989), SPV (1996)

Biography

Formation (1972–1973)

Average White Band formed in Glasgow in 1972, an unusual proposition: a Scottish R&B group devoted to the American soul and funk tradition. Original members included Alan Gorrie (bass, vocals), Mike Rosen (trumpet, guitar), Owen “Onnie” McIntyre (guitar), Malcolm “Mollie” Duncan (tenor saxophone), Roger Ball (saxophone, keyboards), and Robbie McIntosh (drums). Rosen departed early and was replaced by Hamish Stuart (guitar, vocals), establishing the classic lineup.

The band signed to MCA Records in the UK, releasing their debut album Show Your Hand (1973), self-produced with Robin Turner. The record received limited commercial traction but established their sound: tight, horn-driven funk with multiple lead vocalists shared between Gorrie and Stuart. MCA releases also included singles and the compilation This World Has Music (1973).

Atlantic Breakthrough and Tragedy (1974)

Atlantic Records signed AWB and brought in Arif Mardin to produce. The result was the AWB album (1974, Atlantic, cat# SD 7308 in the US), recorded partly at Atlantic Recording Studios (New York) and Criteria Sound Studios (Miami). Engineers included Gene Paul, Lew Hahn, Jimmy Douglass, and Bobby Warner at Atlantic; Howard Albert, Ron Albert, Karl Richardson, and Steve Klein at Criteria. Additional musicians brought in for the sessions included Ralph MacDonald (congas, percussion), Michael Brecker (tenor saxophone), Glenn Ferris (trombone), Marvin Stamm, Mel Davis, and Randy Brecker (trumpets). Mardin remixed the album with Gene Paul.

The AWB album yielded “Pick Up The Pieces,” an instrumental funk track that reached #1 on the US Billboard Hot 100 in 1975. The record broke the band in America.

The triumph was shadowed by tragedy. On 23 September 1974, drummer Robbie McIntosh (born 6 May 1950, Dundee) died in Hollywood, California from a heroin overdose at a party — he was 24. He remains listed among the band’s members in Discogs (ID: 468943), and his writing credits persist on subsequent releases (notably “Pick Up The Pieces” on Cut The Cake). The band recruited Steve Ferrone (born Stephen Ferrone; Discogs ID: 241521) to replace him on drums.

Peak Atlantic Years (1975–1978)

With Ferrone in the drum chair, AWB delivered their strongest run of studio albums:

Cut The Cake (1975, Atlantic) — Mardin again produced. Ferrone took full drum credit (as “Stephen Ferrone”), with Ray Barretto adding congas on select tracks. The core writing partnership of Gorrie, Stuart, McIntyre, Ball, Duncan, and Ferrone (and posthumous McIntosh credits) is evident throughout.

Person To Person (1976, Atlantic) — double live album recorded in concert.

Soul Searching (1976, Atlantic) — third Mardin-produced studio LP. Expanded session players: Ronnie Cuber (baritone sax), Michael Brecker and Barry Rogers returning, Ken Bichel on synthesizer, a string section (Alan Shulman, Jesse Levy, Seymour Barab on cellos), and additional brass (Marvin Stamm, Randy Brecker). Song credits primarily Gorrie, Stuart, and Ball; Ned Doheny co-wrote “A Love Of Your Own.”

Benny And Us (1977, Atlantic) — a full-album collaboration with soul legend Ben E. King (Discogs ID: 155327; born Benjamin Earl Nelson). Jerry Greenberg co-produced with Mardin. Notable addition: Luther Vandross appears as backing vocalist on most tracks, alongside Robin Clark. The band’s own instrumental core (Gorrie, Stuart, McIntyre, Ball, Duncan, Ferrone) remained intact; additional brass from Brecker, Brecker, Stamm, Barry Rogers, and Lew Del Gatto.

Warmer Communications (1978, Atlantic) — final Mardin-produced Atlantic studio album (Mardin mixed it with Gene Paul; mastering credits to Dennis King and George Piros). Additional players included Cornell Dupree (guitar), Rubens Bassini and Ray Barretto (percussion), Tom Malone (trombone), and a returning Brecker Brothers brass section.

A Person To Person live set (1976) documented the peak touring outfit. The single “Queen Of My Soul” charted in the UK.

Label Transition and Dissolution (1978–1982)

After Warmer Communications, AWB split between two labels simultaneously: Feel No Fret (1979) was issued on both Atlantic (US) and RCA (UK), with the band and Gene Paul jointly producing. The subsequent studio output moved fully to RCA for the UK market.

Shine (1980, RCA) — marked a significant production shift: David Foster produced (and played keyboards), with Jerry Hey assisting on horn/string arrangements. This was a polished, more commercial direction. The core lineup (Gorrie, Stuart, McIntyre, Ball, Duncan, Ferrone) remained, with session players Brenda Russell (vocals), Lenny Castro, Paulinho Da Costa, and Paul Lani on percussion, and multiple LA woodwind players.

Cupid’s In Fashion (1982, RCA/Arista) — produced by Dan Hartman, who also played piano. Ferrone retained on drums. Malcolm Duncan’s saxophone and flute remained. Randy Brecker continued to contribute. This album marked the end of the band’s first active period; they dissolved around 1982.

Hiatus and Reunion (1982–1989)

The band went dormant following Cupid’s In Fashion. A 1988 comeback album, Aftershock (Track Record Company, US; Track Record Company), marked the reformation, though without Hamish Stuart in the producing core. Eliot Lewis (Discogs ID: 380508) was co-producer on several tracks, with John Robie handling production on the majority of the album. The reformed lineup centered on Gorrie, McIntyre, Ball, and Lewis (new multi-instrumentalist). The Spirit Of Love (1988, Track Record Company) followed; both albums featured contributions from Chaka Khan and Ronnie Laws.

By 1989 a further regrouping was underway, documented by the single “Sticky Situation” (JAWS Records, Netherlands, 1989).

Later Career (1992–present)

AWB continued to record and tour into the 1990s and 2000s:

  • Soul Tattoo (1996, SPV) — Gorrie, McIntyre, Ball, Lewis, with Pete Abbott (drums). Producer: Peter Moshay.
  • Face To Face Live (1999, EMI-Capitol Special Markets) — live document.
  • Living In Colour (2003, William Morris Agency) — studio album.
  • Access All Areas (2015, Demon Records) — live.
  • Gold (2019, Demon Music Group) — compilation.

The band has maintained activity through live performances. The Discogs member roster as of the most recent entries includes Alan Gorrie, Onnie McIntyre, Roger Ball, Eliot Lewis, Pete Abbott (4), and others depending on era; Alex Ligertwood (Discogs ID: 493177) appeared in the Aftershock-era lineup.

Scene Connections

  • Arif Mardin — core producer, Atlantic 1974–1978; produced all peak-era studio albums
  • Gene Paul — house engineer at Atlantic; present across all Mardin-era sessions
  • Ben E. King — joint LP Benny And Us (1977); King was an Atlantic label veteran
  • Luther Vandross — session backing vocalist on Benny And Us before his own solo career
  • Randy Brecker and Michael Brecker — recurring brass; played across AWB, Soul Searching, Warmer Communications, Cupid’s In Fashion
  • Steve Ferrone — joined after Robbie McIntosh’s 1974 death; later joined Tom Petty And The Heartbreakers via Brian Auger’s Oblivion Express
  • Hamish Stuart — also later joined Tom Petty’s touring band
  • David Foster — produced Shine (1980); Foster went on to become one of the dominant pop producers of the 1980s
  • Dan Hartman — produced Cupid’s In Fashion (1982)
  • Eliot Lewis — key member and producer of the post-1988 AWB
  • Bonnie Bramlett — guest on early MCA material
  • Atlantic Records — label home 1974–1980
  • Jerry Greenberg — Atlantic executive; co-produced Benny And Us
