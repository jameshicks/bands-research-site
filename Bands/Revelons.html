Overview

The Revelons were a New York group active from 1977 to 1983, rooted in the city’s downtown punk and new wave underground at CBGB, Max’s Kansas City, the Mudd Club, Danceteria, and Hurrah’s. Led by Gregory Lee Pickard on guitar and lead vocals, they recorded for Ork Records in 1979 — the label closely associated with Television and the era’s NYC post-punk scene. The band worked alongside members of Television (Fred Smith), the Patti Smith Group (Jay Dee Daugherty), Blondie, and Madonna’s early collaborators, reflecting the tight-knit camaraderie of that downtown community. Material appeared on Sepia Tone (2004), “Hip-Pop” Records (2007), and HoZac Records (2022), well after their original run ended.

Notes (web)

Sepia Tone (2004) and HoZac Records (2022) releases — archival or new?

Both post-dissolution releases are archival compilations, not new recordings.

Sepia Tone — Anthology (2004, STONE 16, released 20 July 2004): A CD collecting studio and live recordings from the band’s original 1977–1983 active period, many appearing on CD for the first time. The release was described at the time as rescuing material from a “great but largely forgotten” New York punk/new wave band. No specific stated prompt for the release is documented in available sources beyond the label’s archival/reissue focus. othermusic.com snippet, December 2004

HoZac Records — 1977–82 Archival LP (October 7, 2022, HZR-223): An archival vinyl LP limited to 500 black vinyl copies, comprising the band’s only original 7” single — “The Way (You Touch My Hand)” / “97 Tears” (Ork Records, 1979) — plus ten additional period recordings. The HoZac Bandcamp description explicitly frames this as the vinyl debut for nearly all the material: “a few Revelons collections have been available over the years on compact disc, this edition serves as the vinyl debut for nearly everything nestled into its grooves.” No specific prompting event (reunion, anniversary, member death) is documented in available sources — HoZac positioned the release as correcting a historical oversight in NYC punk documentation. hozacrecords.bandcamp.com; The Vinyl District review by Joseph Neff, December 2022

Notable musicians on the HoZac LP include Fred Smith (7) of Television and Jay Dee Daugherty of the Patti Smith Group, consistent with Discogs credits. The HoZac description also names Richard Lloyd (Television) as appearing in the band’s rotating live lineup, though this conflicts with Discogs credits which list Fred Smith rather than Richard Lloyd — possible both appeared at different points.

Conflict: The HoZac Bandcamp description names Richard Lloyd (Television) as a collaborator; Discogs credits Fred Smith (Television) with 3 shared releases. These may not be mutually exclusive (both could have participated at different times) but the discrepancy is noted.
