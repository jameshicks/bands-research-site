The Pastels

Discogs artist ID: 2805 Formed: Glasgow, Scotland, 1981 Active: 1981–present Genres: Indie Pop, Indie Rock, Jangle Pop, Alternative Rock Labels: Whaam! Records, Creation Records, Glass Records (2), Chapter 22, Paperhouse Records, Domino Records, Geographic

Scottish independent group formed in Glasgow in 1981 by Brian Taylor (Brian Pastel/Brian Superstar), Stephen McRobbie (Stephen Pastel), Martin Hayward, and Bernice Simpson. Originally rooted in a raw DIY aesthetic that put them in the orbit of Postcard Records-era Glasgow, the Pastels became a foundational figure in UK indie pop through a series of singles on Creation, Glass, and other small labels in the mid-1980s. Their debut LP Up For A Bit With The Pastels (Glass Records, 1987) influenced a generation of artists — cited by members of Sonic Youth, Yo La Tengo, and Nirvana — and is broadly credited with demonstrating that Glasgow bands could record independently without relocating south. The group underwent significant lineup changes across the late 1980s and early 1990s, eventually settling around a core of Stephen McRobbie (Stephen Pastel) and Katrina Mitchell, with a rotating cast that at various points included Norman Blake and Gerard Love of Teenage Fanclub, David Keegan, and Tom Crossley. They operate their own Geographic imprint through Domino, and are co-owners of Glasgow record shop Monorail Music.

History

Formation and Early Singles (1981–1986)

The group formed in 1981 during the Postcard Records era. Brian Taylor (a friend of Postcard’s Alan Horne) recruited Stephen McRobbie, Martin Hayward, and Bernice Simpson. Their first performance was at Bearsden Burgh Hall, booked by McRobbie after he attended a Crass gig there. The debut single “Songs For Children” appeared on Whaam! Records in 1982, followed by the cassette Entertaining Edward (Action Tapes, 1982). Subsequent singles came out on a succession of labels — Creation Records (“A Million Tears,” 1984; “Something Going On,” 1984; “I’m Alright With You,” 1985), Glass Records (“Truck Train Tractor,” 1986; “Crawl Babies,” 1987; “Comin’ Through,” 1987). McRobbie reportedly visited Rough Trade’s Geoff Travis to pitch the band as “the next big thing out of Scotland,” but the relationship with Rough Trade faded as the label focused on Scritti Politti and the Smiths. The Pastels appeared on the NME’s C86 compilation in 1986, though they consistently distanced themselves from the “twee” and “shambling” labels those releases attracted. McRobbie undertook a master’s degree in librarianship at Glasgow University during this period, keeping the band based in Glasgow rather than following the usual route south.

Up For A Bit With The Pastels (1987)

The debut album was recorded for Glass Records (2) (cat. GLALP 021) and produced by John A. Rivers. The core lineup on the record was Stephen Pastel (guitar, vocals), Aggi/Annabel Wright (vocals, keyboards), Brian Taylor (guitar), Martin Hayward (bass, guitar), and Bernice Simpson (drums), with “contributions by” John A. Rivers, Jowe Head, Lucy Cameron, and The Impressive Toy Orchestra. Rivers also produced. The record moved from garage pop-punk to ballads with synth-orchestral elements. It was named the 37th best Scottish album by The Scotsman in 2003 and credited by The Guardian with inspiring confidence in the Glasgow scene — attributed as a precursor to Belle and Sebastian’s Tigermilk, Mogwai’s Young Team, and Franz Ferdinand’s debut. (Source: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pastels)

Sittin’ Pretty and Lineup Changes (1989)

The second album Sittin’ Pretty (Chapter 22, CHAP LP 43, 1989) was produced by Richard Mazda and was harder but less eclectic than the debut. The lineup retained the core of Stephen Pastel, Aggi, Martin Hayward, Brian Taylor, and Bernice Simpson; guests included Eugene Kelly (vocals on two tracks), David Keegan (guitar on “Swerve”), Nicola Geddes (cello), and Sophie Pragnell (viola). Engineers included “Dubfire” Smith (Mark Addis), Beeg Al, and Neil Ross. After its release, Taylor, Hayward, and Simpson departed.

Domino Period: Mobile Safari and Illumination (1994–1997)

A reconstituted lineup emerged around Stephen McRobbie (now consistently credited as Stephen Pastel or Stephen McRobbie interchangeably), Aggi/Annabel Wright, and Katrina Mitchell (drums, vocals, keyboards, violin), supplemented by David Keegan and Gerard Love on guitar and Norman Blake on guitar/vocals. This lineup signed to the emerging Domino Records.

Mobile Safari (Domino, WIGCD 17, 1994) was produced by Paul Chisholm and The Pastels, recorded by Paul Chisholm, with Gregor Reid and Tony Doogan as assistant engineers. The personnel roster expanded to include Dean Wareham (guitar), a wind section (Sarah Ward on flute, Dawn Kelly on French horn, Maureen McRoberts on saxophone, Darren Ramsay on trumpet, Luke Williams (2) on trombone, Laura Mitchell on viola), and Judi Mitchell on cor anglais. Bill Wells played piano.

Illumination (Domino, WIGCD 34, 1997) was co-produced by The Pastels and Gregor Reid (tracks 1–3, 5, 7–11, 13) and by Ian Carmichael (tracks 4, 6, 12). Gregor Reid engineered throughout. Additional players included Isobel Campbell (cello), Bill Wells (piano), Dean Wareham and Norman Blake (guitar), Jonathan Kilgour (guitar), Sarah Ward (flute), Dawn Kelly (French horn), and Katrina Mitchell (drums, keyboards, violin, arranger). Steve Rooke mastered.

A remix album, Illuminati (Domino, 1998), featured contributions from My Bloody Valentine, Jim O’Rourke, and others.

Annabel Wright’s Departure and Geographic (2000–2009)

Annabel Wright/Aggi left in 2000 to focus on her work as an illustrator, though she continued to provide artwork. The Pastels’ next release was the soundtrack to David Mackenzie’s film The Last Great Wilderness (Geographic, GEOG18LP, 2003), which included a track with Jarvis Cocker of Pulp.

In 2006, the Pastels created music for a theatre production by Glasgow-based company 12 Stars.

In 2009, The Pastels collaborated with Tokyo-based duo Tenniscoats (Saya Ueno and Takashi Ueno) on Two Sunsets (Geographic, GEOG37CDP, 2009), with songwriting credits shared across both groups; Jim Reid (The Jesus and Mary Chain) contributed the written-by credit on one track (“About You”). The same year they released the double-sided single Vivid Youth / About You (Geographic/Domino, GEOG34, 2009).

Slow Summits and Later Activity (2013–present)

Slow Summits (Domino, WIGLP185, 2013) was produced by John McEntire and The Pastels, with string arrangements on “Kicking Leaves” by Craig Armstrong. Engineers included Bal Cooke, Gregor Reid, John McEntire, Paul Savage, and Tony Doogan; Steve Rooke mastered. The expanded musician roster included Norman Blake, Gerard Love, Bill Wells, Colin McIlroy, Stefan Schneider, Ronald Lippok (of To Rococo Rot), Saya Ueno and Takashi Ueno (Tenniscoats), Takuji Aoyagi, Tom Crossley, and Annabel Wright. The album was shortlisted for the 2013 Scottish Album of the Year Award and described by The Guardian as “their most complete set since Up for a Bit.” (Source: Wikipedia)

The geographic label has continued to release new Scottish artists including Spinning Coin (from 2016) and the project Lightships (Gerard Love, from 2012).

In 2024, Stephen Pastel released This Is Memorial Device (Geographic, GEOG56LP) in collaboration with Gavin Thomson.

The Nu Forest

A side project / collective listing Stephen McRobbie, Katrina Mitchell, Alison Mitchell, Tom Crossley, Gerard Love, Bal Cooke, and unusually also Jarvis Cocker, Steve Mackey (Pulp), Richard Hawley, and John McEntire as members (Discogs artist ID: 396178). The Nu Forest released I Picked A Flower on Geographic (GEOG21CD, 2003).
