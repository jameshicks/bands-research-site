Vonbrigði

Origin: Reykjavík, Iceland Discogs artist ID: 607402 Name variations: Vonbrigdi, Von Brigdi, Vonb. Active: 1981–1983 (original run); reformed 2004; continued into 2010s Genres: Punk, New Wave, Post-Punk Labels: Gramm (1982–1983); self-released (2004–2017); Iron Lung Records / Reykjavík Record Shop (2021)

Overview

Vonbrigði (“disappointment” in Icelandic) were a Reykjavík punk band originally active 1981–1983. They were contemporaries of the more internationally-known acts in the early 1980s Icelandic underground — Purrkur Pillnikk, Þeyr, Kukl — but operated at one remove from the Kukl/Björk circle centred around Einar Örn Benediktsson. Vonbrigði shared the same label (the pivotal Reykjavík indie Gramm, where their two original releases appeared) and the same recording engineer (Kjartan Kjartansson), but the personnel did not overlap with the Kukl or The Sugarcubes lineups.

The band reformed in 2004 and continued to release self-produced material through the 2010s. In 2021, a substantial archival double-LP, Hanagal (cat. LUNGS-189 / RRS-027), appeared on Seattle’s Iron Lung Records co-released with the Icelandic Reykjavík Record Shop, drawing renewed attention to the original 1982–83 recordings.

Formation and Scene Context

The band’s original run falls squarely in the brief, intense flowering of Icelandic post-punk: 1981–1983, when Reykjavík produced a cluster of idiosyncratic groups on a handful of small labels. Gramm, the label that released both Vonbrigði’s debut EP and their 1983 mini-album, was founded by Ásmundur Jónsson and Einar Örn Benediktsson (later of Purrkur Pillnikk, Kukl, and The Sugarcubes). Despite their shared label infrastructure, Vonbrigði’s lineup was entirely separate from that network — the four core members (Gunnar Ellertsson, Þórarinn Kristjánsson, Árni Kristjánsson, Jóhann Vilhjálmsson) do not appear on any Kukl or Sugarcubes sessions.

The Gramm label’s broader roster at this moment also included Tappi Tíkarrass (featuring the young Björk Guðmundsdóttir), Purrkur Pillnikk, and Þeyr — a concentrated document of what Reykjavík’s underground could produce in a two-year window. Gramm would later evolve into Smekkleysa (Bad Taste), the Sugarcubes’ own Icelandic imprint.

Sound

The 1982 EP is raw punk with melodic undertow — four tracks in under thirteen minutes. The 1983 mini-LP Kakófónia opens up the palette: longer tracks, piano alongside guitar, a stronger presence of atmospheric space, and lyrics contributed in part by Didda (Sigurlaug Didda Jónsdóttir), a lyricist and percussionist. The recorded sound on both releases bears the stamp of Kjartan Kjartansson, whose engineering style on Gramm-era recordings is dry and immediate. Both records were classified by Discogs as Punk and New Wave; Kakófónia additionally carries a Post-Punk designation. Árni Kristjánsson plays guitar throughout but adds piano on the mini-LP.

The 2004 comeback album Eðli Annarra (self-released, cat. VON001) expands to Alternative Rock and Experimental territory alongside Punk and Post-Punk, reflecting two decades of distance from the original sessions.

19 tracks across four sides. Lyrics credits split: Vonbrigði (tracks A1–B4, C1–C4) and Didda credited as “Sigurlaug ‘Didda’ Jónsdóttir” (C5–C6, D1–D4). Hallur Ingólfsson handled mixing and mastering. Ragnar Jónsson is credited as “Transferred By,” indicating he handled the tape-to-digital transfer of the 1982 source recordings. Þórir G. Jónsson (also known under aliases including Automatic Aggressor, Plagan, Óreiða) did artwork.

The release marked a return for Kjartan Kjartansson as “Recorded By” credit — ambiguously placed, as the original recordings date to 1982 but this is listed on the 2021 release, suggesting the archival recordings are attributed to him. This is consistent with his 1982 EP credit.

Hanagal shares its Discogs master (800676) with Studio Hana-Gal 1982 (2013 CDr), confirming Discogs treats them as variants of the same session documentation.

Iron Lung Records is a Seattle-based punk/hardcore label (founded by vocalist/drummer Jensen Ward and vocalist/guitarist Jon Kortbein of the band Iron Lung). Its catalog (790 releases) spans international punk and hardcore; the Vonbrigði retrospective (LUNGS-189) is among its archive-recovery releases. The co-release partner, Reykjavík Record Shop, is an Icelandic label/retail operation that has co-released several archival and contemporary Icelandic music titles.

Compilation appearances

The band appeared on the Icelandic music documentary compilation Screaming Masterpiece (2005, Ergis Film Production), credited as “Performer [Performances By].” This documentary surveyed Icelandic pop/rock from the 1980s onward, with other participants including Björk, Sigur Rós, The Sugarcubes, and mum.

Scene Connections

Vonbrigði occupied a specific niche in the early-1980s Reykjavík underground: they were on Gramm — the label co-founded by Einar Örn Benediktsson — without being part of his circle. The other Gramm acts of the same moment include:

  • Purrkur Pillnikk — Gramm’s first release; featured Einar Örn
  • Tappi Tíkarrass — featured Björk Guðmundsdóttir
  • Kukl — featured Björk, Einar Örn, Siggi Baldursson; shared engineer Kjartan Kjartansson
  • Þeyr — contemporaries, different label network (Eskvímó/Fálkinn/Mjöt)

The engineer Kjartan Kjartansson is the strongest link between Vonbrigði and the Kukl/Sugarcubes lineage: he recorded Vonbrigði in 1982, recorded/mixed Kukl’s Söngull in 1983, and engineered the Sugarcubes’ debut Life’s Too Good in 1988.

Didda (Sigurlaug Didda Jónsdóttir), who provided lyrics on Kakófónia (1983) and continues to have a lyric credit on Hanagal (2021), is a Reykjavík-based lyricist and percussionist connected to Slagverkur. Her sustained presence across four decades of Vonbrigði releases makes her an important extended collaborator.

Hallur Ingólfsson, who mixed and mastered Hanagal (2021) and co-mixed Eðli Annarra (2004), is associated with the Icelandic metal/rock band Ham as well as several other Icelandic projects (Bleeding Volcano, XIII (2), Brimbrot, Látún, Skepna).

Open Questions

  • The Discogs profile says the band was “re-started in 2004” but no founding or dissolution context is available from Discogs alone — what prompted the 2004 reformation?
  • “Hana-Gal” appears as a studio name in the 2013 CDr title (Studio Hana-Gal 1982); what studio was this and where was it located in Reykjavík?
  • Gunnar Ellertsson (ID 865905) appears in Discogs only under Vonbrigði; there is a separate entry “Gunnar E. Knudsen” (ID 16680967) credited on Eðli Annarra — is this the same person? The name similarity and band context strongly suggest yes, but Discogs has not merged them.
  • Ragnar Jónsson (ID 3548944) — no profile or other credits in Discogs; identity and professional context unknown beyond the “Transferred By” credit. (Separate from the Ragnar Jónsson credited on Kjartan Kjartansson’s 1986 Með Góðum Mat session, which uses a different Discogs ID.)
  • Sigurður Bjóla Garðarsson — recorded/mixed most of Kakófónia but no further career information available from Discogs.
  • The 2017 Sauðanes release has no credited personnel in Discogs; format and full details unknown.
  • Were there other live recordings or unreleased sessions from 1981–1983 beyond what appeared on Hanagal and Studio Hana-Gal 1982?
