Pelican (2)

Discogs ID: 335124 Active: 1999–present Origin: Chicago, Illinois Also known as: PLCN Genre: Post-metal, instrumental rock

Chicago instrumental post-metal band. Founded 1999; emerged from the same underground network as Isis and the Hydra Head Records ecosystem. Core lineup of four — guitars, bass, drums — with no vocalist, ever. Label trajectory: self-released debut → Hydra Head → Southern Lord → Run For Cover. Most-pressed release: The Fire In Our Throats Will Beckon The Thaw (2005) at 33 pressings, the highest count across the Hydra Head catalog.

History

Self-released debut and early HH years (1999–2004)

Pelican formed in Chicago in 1999 — Trevor de Brauw (guitar), Laurent Lebec (guitar), Bryan Herweg (bass), Larry Herweg (drums). Debut self-titled EP released 2001 on their own imprint (14 pressings); recorded by Sanford Parker, mastered by Douglas Ward. Notably featured a liner-notes quote from jazz guitarist Marc Ribot (see Web sources below) and cover art handled by Peter Nilges.

Hydra Head Records picked them up for Australasia (2003, HH666-75), their first full-length. 28 pressings. Sanford Parker again recorded and mixed; Nick Zampiello mastered; Aaron Turner (with his Feral Pig design entity) handled layout and design — a pattern that would continue.

The Fire In Our Throats — peak Hydra Head era (2005)

2005 was Pelican’s most concentrated output year: an EP, a split with Mono, a split 7” with Playing Enemy, and their defining album.

March Into The Sea EP (HH666-84, 2005, 13 pressings): a single 20-minute track recorded and mixed by Greg Norman, mastered by Zampiello. Featured flute from Chris de Brauw (Trevor’s sibling) and a Justin K Broadrick (JK Broadrick) remix of “Angel Tears.”

Mono / Pelican split LP (2005, Hydra Head / Temporary Residence Limited, 7 pressings): paired Pelican with Japanese post-rock band Mono (7).

The Fire In Our Throats Will Beckon The Thaw (HH666-91, 2005, 33 pressings): the label’s most-pressed release. Greg Norman recorded and mixed; John Golden mastered; Aaron Turner and Feral Pig did the artwork. Seven tracks of slow-building post-metal; no remixes, no guests — the four-piece alone.

City Of Echoes and transition (2007)

After The Ceiling Cracked EP (2007, Hydra Head, 3 pressings): first collaboration with Chris Common, who would become a key production partner for the next two records. Justin K Broadrick also credited.

City Of Echoes (2007, Daymare Recordings Japan, 17 pressings): engineered and mixed by Andrew Schneider (Translator Audio, NYC); Greg Norman as recording assistant; John Golden mastered. Lineup clearly credited: Bryan Herweg (bass), Larry Herweg (drums), Laurent Schroeder-Lebec (guitar), Trevor de Brauw (guitar). Came with a bonus DVD of a live LA set.

Southern Lord era and the guest-heavy pivot (2009–2013)

Pelican moved to Southern Lord — founded by Greg Anderson (Sunn O))) — for their third and fourth LPs.

What We All Come To Need (2009, Southern Lord LORD110, 16 pressings): produced, engineered, and mixed by Chris Common; mastered by Ed Brooks. The most guest-laden Pelican release to date — four tracks featuring outside collaborators:

  • Track 1 “Glimmer”: Ben Verellen (Harkonen / Helms Alee) guest bass
  • Track 2 “The Creeper”: Greg Anderson guest guitar
  • Track 7 “What We All Come To Need”: Aaron Turner guest guitar (billed as “Aaron Turnip”)
  • Track 8 “Final Breath”: Allen Epley (The Life and Times / Shiner) guest vocals

Artwork by Seldon Hunt.

Laurent Lebec announced his departure in 2012. Dallas Thomas (ex-Asschapel) joined on second guitar.

Ataraxia / Taraxis (2012, Southern Lord, 8 pressings): first release with the new lineup. Stephen O’Malley (Sunn O))), KTL) contributed collage design.

Arktika (Live From Russia) (2013, Southern Lord, 7 pressings): live document of the Thomas-era lineup.

Forever Becoming (2013, Southern Lord LORD182, 12 pressings): Chris Common produced, recorded, and mixed; Joe Lambert mastered; Stephen O’Malley collage design again. Guests: Bruce Lamont (Yakuza) baritone saxophone on “Terminal”; Chris Common played additional drums, mellotron, and piano on multiple tracks.

Recent era (2015–present)

The Cliff (2015, Southern Lord, 3 pressings): Geoff Rickly (Thursday) credited; Mika Jussila mastered.

Nighttime Stories (2019, Southern Lord, 12 pressings): produced by Matt Bayles — first Bayles collaboration with Pelican.

Gasoline / For Your Entertainment (2023, Numero Group, singles).

Flickering Resonance (2025, Run For Cover Records (2), 10 pressings) and Ascending (2026, Run For Cover Records (2), 6 pressings): current label, first non-Southern Lord/HH home in 20 years. Matthew Barnhart and Scott Evans credited on both.

Connections

Web sources — Marc Ribot / debut EP insert

Nature of connection

Marc Ribot did not perform on or produce the debut EP. His connection is as a quoted author: a passage from his essay “Earplugs” (originally published in Arcana: Musicians on Music, ed. John Zorn, Granary Books, 2000) was printed on the insert of the self-released 2001 CD. The essay later appeared in Ribot’s own collection Unstrung: Rants and Stories of a Noise Guitarist (Akashic Books, 2021). [source: https://lithub.com/marc-ribot-makes-the-case-for-loud-music/]

The essay argues that playing at high volume is simultaneously a technical necessity, an artistic choice, a social negotiation within bands, and a physical endurance ritual — resonating thematically with Pelican’s loud, riff-driven instrumental approach. Representative passages include:

“We seem to love broken voices in general: vocal cords eroded by whiskey and screaming, voices that distort, damage, but don’t actually die.”

“When acoustic pain occurs in the theater of rock, the pain of the audience is compensated by their pleasure at the spectacle of the sacrifice of the musicians.”

“On a really good night, nothing hurts — not howling volume, not airless rooms at sauna temperatures, not bleeding callouses. Nothing much. Not till later.”

“If the birth of symphony orchestras foreshadowed the arrival of parliamentary government, and the Beatles prefigured the hippie commune, one can only imagine what post-Bosnian nightmare or total failure of language is lurking noisily in our futures, blocked out/symbolized/invited by the earplugs I wore at the gig last night.” [source: https://lithub.com/marc-ribot-makes-the-case-for-loud-music/; confirmed in web search results 2026-07-19]

Note on the specific insert passage: Multiple sources confirm the insert quote is drawn from “Earplugs” / Arcana, but the exact excerpt Pelican selected is not reproduced in any publicly accessible source found during web research (searched 2026-07-15; re-searched 2026-07-19 with no new findings). The four passages above are from the published essay and are candidates, but cannot be confirmed as the precise insert text without access to the physical CD or a scan of the insert. No forum posts, reviews, or digitized liner notes reproducing the insert text were found across Reddit, AllMusic, Rate Your Music, Bandcamp, or any archived source. [source: https://www.discogs.com/release/4942025-Pelican-Pelican]

Ribot is a New York-based avant-garde and jazz guitarist known for work with Tom Waits, John Zorn, Elvis Costello, and others — no direct musical collaboration with Pelican is documented. The quote is an endorsement-by-inclusion: the band placing his writing on their debut signals a cross-genre aesthetic alignment (noise, volume, endurance) rather than a working relationship.
