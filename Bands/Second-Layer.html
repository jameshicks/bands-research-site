Second Layer

Discogs ID: 59911 | Also credited as: 2nd Layer | Origin: South London, UK | Active: 1979–1981 (reissues to 2025)

UK post-punk/industrial duo. Members: Adrian Borland (vocals, guitar) and Graham Bailey (keyboards, bass guitar, drum programming). Ran concurrently with and immediately preceded The Sound (2), sharing personnel and the self-run Tortch Records imprint. Second Layer was the more abrasive, experimental face of the same South London scene — angular, machine-driven, closer to early industrial than to new wave.

Formation and Context (1979)

Borland and Bailey had already been bandmates in The Outsiders (2) (South London punk, 1976–1979) before Second Layer. In 1979 they launched Tortch Records as a self-run vehicle and released material under both the Second Layer name and the nascent Sound simultaneously. The two projects existed in parallel: The Sound pursued a more accessible post-punk direction; Second Layer was where the drum-machine-driven, claustrophobic experimentation lived.

The Flesh As Property E.P. was the first release on Tortch (cat. TOR 001, also issued on Fresh Records (14) as FRESH 5 in a white-label reissue pressing). Three tracks: “Courts Or Wars,” “Metal Sheet,” and “Germany.” Styles straddle post-punk, new wave, and experimental. Plating credited to Maxwell Anandappa (Discogs ID: 2481548).

State of Emergency E.P. (1980)

The second release (Tortch TOR 006, 7” 45 RPM, 1980) featured three tracks: “State Of Emergency,” “I Need Noise,” and “The Cutting Motion.” Credited under the name 2nd Layer on the label. Co-produced by Second Layer and Nick Robbins, who also engineered. Lacquer cut by Steve Angel. Graphics by K. Grey. Running times: 4:37 / 2:19 / 1:48. The production is stark and tightly controlled — drum machine locked to synth bass lines, Borland’s vocals raw and declamatory.

The record sits alongside contemporaneous releases on Industrial Records and early 4AD as a high-water mark of UK post-punk’s machine-driven wing.

Dissolution and Parallel Career in The Sound

Second Layer ceased activity after World Of Rubber. Borland and Bailey continued together — and remain together — as the core of The Sound (2), which had released its debut Physical World E.P. (Tortch, 1979) while Second Layer was active. The Sound went on to sign to Korova / WEA and release seven albums between 1980 and 1987.

The Second Layer name lay dormant throughout the Sound years. Bailey’s post-Sound activity is not documented as a primary artist outside Second Layer and The Sound.

Open Questions

  • Second Layer appear to have been primarily a studio project — they dissolved when The Sound signed to Warners/Korova in 1980, before any significant live activity. No live credits in Discogs; no live documentation found in web sources either. (Checked 2026-05-07.)
  • The 2015 Dark Entries “previously unreleased” tracks (Sides C/D: “Intro,” “Split Screen,” “Death Process,” “The Telephone Call…,” “Deadly Norwegian Attack”) — their original recording dates are not specified in the Discogs data
  • Adrian Janes is credited with lyrics on “Germany” and “State Of Emergency” in the 2015 and 2023 reissues — his connection to the original recording sessions (whether he co-wrote or provided new liner text) is not clear from Discogs alone
  • The LD Records 1987 Belgian LP is noted as “Partially Unofficial” — the circumstances of that release are not documented in the DB
  • Howard Devoto / Magazine connection: no evidence in Discogs or web sources. Wikipedia, press coverage, and Discogs credits show no link. Likely scene-era conflation — both are late-70s UK post-punk acts — not an actual collaboration or relationship. (Resolved as unfounded, web check 2026-05-07.)
