The Fugs

New York countercultural rock/poetry band. Discogs artist ID: 262227. Active: 1964–1969 (original run); reunion 1984; revived 1985–2003; occasional activity 2023.

Overview

The Fugs were a satirical, politically confrontational rock band formed in New York City in 1964 by poets Ed Sanders and Tuli Kupferberg, with drummer Ken Weaver. Later that year they were joined by Peter Stampfel and Steve Weber of The Holy Modal Rounders. The band occupied a unique position bridging the Greenwich Village poetry/folk underground and the emerging rock and psychedelic scenes, fusing original songs, improvisation, and spoken-word performance with blunt political satire — frank treatments of sex, drugs, and Vietnam War opposition that caused censorship problems and built a devoted countercultural following.

The name was taken by Kupferberg from Norman Mailer’s novel The Naked and the Dead, where “fug” serves as a euphemism for “fuck.” The band later appeared in Mailer’s Armies of the Night, performing at the 1967 Pentagon march. Their early recordings on ESP-Disk’ (1965–1967) are core documents of the label’s non-jazz catalog; they then moved to Reprise Records for four albums (1967–1970). The original band played its final concert of the 1960s in 1969 at Hersheypark Arena, sharing a bill with the Grateful Dead. A reunion (without Weaver) took place at the Bottom Line, New York, in 1984; the band resumed as a going concern from 1985 onward with an evolving lineup centered on Sanders and Kupferberg.

Name variations on Discogs: The Village Fugs (early 1965 usage), Fugs, The Fugs Chorus, Virgin Fugs, ALL The FUGS.

Connections

  • Ed Sanders — co-founder, poet, producer; solo records on Reprise (Sanders’ Truckstop, 1969; Beer Cans On The Moon, 1972)
  • Tuli Kupferberg — co-founder, poet; solo LP No Deposit No Return (ESP-Disk’, 1967); Tuli & Friends (Shimmy Disc, 1989, prod. Kramer (2))
  • Ken Weaver — original drummer; absent from post-1969 work
  • Richard Alderson — primary engineer on ESP and early Reprise sessions; the defining technical figure behind the band’s first sound
  • Allen Ginsberg — contributed liner notes to The Fugs (ESP-1028, 1966)
  • Peter Stampfel — guitar/fiddle on debut and Virgin Fugs; also member of The Holy Modal Rounders
  • Steve Weber — guitar on debut and Virgin Fugs; also member of The Holy Modal Rounders
  • The Holy Modal Rounders — overlapping membership (Stampfel, Weber); shared Lee Crabtree
  • Kramer (2) — bass/keyboards on Refuse To Be Burnt-Out (1985); produced Tuli Kupferberg’s Tuli & Friends (1989)
  • Danny Kortchmar — guitar/violin on Tenderness Junction (1967); later session work with James Taylor, Carole King, and others
  • Charles Larkey — bass on three Reprise LPs; later played with Carole King
