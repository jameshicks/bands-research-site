The White Stripes

Discogs artist ID: 116345 Members: Jack White (guitar, vocals, piano) · Meg White (drums, percussion, vocals) Origin: Detroit, Michigan, USA Active: 1997–2011 Genres: Garage Rock, Blues Rock, Lo-Fi, Indie Rock

Lo-fi garage rock and blues-rock duo. Presented themselves as siblings despite having been married and divorced (1996–2000). Dressed exclusively in red, white, and black. Deliberately stripped to two instruments — guitar and drums — as a formal constraint. One of the central acts of the early-2000s garage rock revival.

Biography

Detroit origins (1997–1999)

Jack White (born John Anthony Gillis, legally Jack White by 2001) and Meg White formed the duo in Detroit in 1997. Jack had previously played in the Detroit garage band The Go. Their debut single “Let’s Shake Hands” (1998, Italy Records) was recorded and engineered by Jim Diamond (2) at Ghetto Recorders, Detroit. A second single “Lafayette Blues / Sugar Never Tasted So Good” followed on Italy Records the same year.

The debut album, The White Stripes (1999), was released on Sympathy For The Record Industry (Long Gone John’s LA indie, home to The Gun Club and countless garage/punk acts). Produced by Jack White with Jim Diamond engineering at Ghetto Recorders; no mastering credit on the original pressing. The band mixed cover versions (Robert Johnson’s “Stop Breaking Down Blues,” Josh White’s “St. James Infirmary Blues”) with originals.

Indie breakthrough (2000–2001)

De Stijl (2000, Sympathy For The Record Industry) cemented their Detroit scene credentials. Named after the Dutch art movement; jack produced and the album credits no engineer besides Jack himself on the Discogs primary pressing. The album paid direct homage to blues forebear Son House via covers.

White Blood Cells (2001) was the breakthrough record. Recorded by Stuart Sikes at Easley McCain Studio, Memphis, and mixed by Jack White and Stuart Sikes. Mastered by Fred Kevorkian at Kevorkian Mastering/Avatar Studios, NYC. Released in the US on Sympathy For The Record Industry and internationally via XL Recordings (UK/Europe) and V2. The album received widespread critical acclaim in the UK — John Peel compared the duo’s importance to Jimi Hendrix and the Sex Pistols. Kevorkian’s credit appears on every pressing worldwide, making this the most Discogs-documented entry in his mastering catalog (~55 release variants across US, UK, Europe, Australia, South Africa, Brazil, Thailand, China, Scandinavia).

Peak and major label era (2002–2007)

Elephant (2003) was recorded entirely at Toe Rag Studios, London — an 8-track studio with pre-1960s analogue equipment only. Engineered by Liam Watson (who also co-mixed to quarter-inch reel-to-reel). Mastered by Noel Summerville. Produced by Jack White. The deliberate analogue constraint gave the album its sonic density. Released on XL Recordings internationally and co-credited on Jack White’s own fledgling imprint Third Man Records — the first prominent appearance of the Third Man name on a White Stripes release. The album reached the top ten in the US and went platinum in the UK. Singles: “Seven Nation Army,” “Fell In Love With A Girl,” “The Hardest Button To Button,” “I Just Don’t Know What To Do With Myself.”

Get Behind Me Satan (2005) saw the duo shift toward piano, marimba, and slide guitar. Recorded by Matthew Kettle, mixed by John Hampton and Jack White, mastered by Howie Weinberg. Released on Third Man Records / V2 in the US, XL Recordings in the UK/Europe. Singles: “Blue Orchid,” “My Doorbell,” “The Denial Twist.”

Icky Thump (2007) was the last studio album. Recorded and mixed by Joe Chiccarelli and Jack White, mastered by Vlado Meller. Released on Third Man Records / XL Recordings. Singles: “Icky Thump,” “Conquest,” “Rag And Bone,” “You Don’t Know What Love Is (You Just Do As You’re Told).”

Hiatus and disbandment (2008–2011)

After Icky Thump the band’s output slowed. Under Great White Northern Lights (2010, Third Man Records) was a live album/film documenting their 2007 Canadian tour (every province and territory). Mixed by Jack White and Vance Powell, mastered by Andrew Mendelson. The documentary film was co-directed by Emmett Malloy.

On February 2, 2011, the band announced permanent disbandment via a statement on their website. The reason cited was that Meg White’s health would not allow the band to continue.

Jack White has continued with The Raconteurs (with Brendan Benson, Jack Lawrence, and Patrick Keeler), The Dead Weather (with Alison Mosshart, Dean Fertita, and Jack Lawrence), and a solo career. Meg White has remained almost entirely out of public life since 2011.

Members

Jack White

Discogs ID: 278763. Born John Anthony Gillis; legally changed name to Jack White after marrying Meg. Vocals, guitar, piano, keyboards. Producer on all White Stripes albums. Aliases on Discogs: “The Third Man (5),” “III (7),” “Jack White III,” “Blind Jack Lazarus,” “Jack Doc Gillis,” and others. Also a member of The Go, The Upholsterers, The Raconteurs, The Dead Weather, and 2 Star Tabernacle. Founded Third Man Records.

Meg White

Discogs ID: 367269. Drums and percussion; occasional vocals. Has maintained near-total privacy since the band’s end in 2011. Discogs does not list any post-White-Stripes credits.

The Kevorkian Question — Answered

Fred Kevorkian mastered White Blood Cells (2001) across every pressing worldwide — US, UK, Europe, Australia, Brazil, South Africa, Thailand, China, Scandinavia. The ~55-entry count is accurate.

He does not appear on any subsequent White Stripes studio album. The break is explained by a change in recording approach: Elephant (2003) was recorded entirely at Toe Rag Studios on pre-1960s analogue 8-track equipment and mastered by Noel Summerville at a UK facility in keeping with the analogue ethos. For Get Behind Me Satan, mastering moved to Howie Weinberg (LA/NYC). For Icky Thump, to Vlado Meller. Each album had a different engineer and a different mastering house — Jack White did not use a consistent mastering engineer across his career. Kevorkian was a one-album engagement for the White Stripes, even if the record went on to generate dozens of pressings worldwide that kept his name in circulation.

Connections

  • Third Man Records — Jack White’s label, founded by Jack White; first appeared co-crediting Elephant (2003); now the catalog home of all White Stripes posthumous reissues and archival releases
  • The Raconteurs — Jack White’s band with Brendan Benson, Jack Lawrence, Patrick Keeler
  • The Dead Weather — Jack White’s band with Alison Mosshart, Dean Fertita, Jack Lawrence
  • The Go — Detroit garage band Jack White played in before The White Stripes
  • Sympathy For The Record Industry — foundational indie label (Long Gone John, LA) that released their first three albums in the US
  • XL Recordings — UK/international home from 2001 through disbandment
  • Fred Kevorkian — mastered White Blood Cells; one-album relationship that generated ~55 pressing variants
  • Liam Watson — Toe Rag Studios; the analogue-only engineering approach on Elephant was fundamental to that album’s sound
  • Son House — blues forebear; De Stijl includes a cover of his work; Jack White cited Son House as a primary influence
