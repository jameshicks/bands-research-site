Rubella Ballet

Anarcho-punk / post-punk band formed in 1979 in London, UK. Active 1979 to present (as of 2024). Discogs artist ID: 278401

Overview

Rubella Ballet formed at a Crass gig at Conway Hall, London, when Crass invited members of the audience to use their equipment. The founding nucleus — Sid Attion (drums, from Flux Of Pink Indians), Zillah Minx (vocals), Pete Fender (guitar, vocals), and Gem Stone (bass, vocals, from Fatal Microbes) — emerged from the heart of the British anarcho-punk scene. Pete Fender and Gem Stone are the children of Vi Subversa of Poison Girls, which made the band a genuine family affair embedded in the Xntrix / anarcho network.

Despite the Crass-scene origin, Rubella Ballet developed a sound distinct from raw anarcho orthodoxy: bright day-glo aesthetics, synth textures, post-punk rhythms, and a goth-tinged colour that set them apart from more austere contemporaries like Crass or Conflict (2). Zillah Minx’s flamboyant visual identity — vivid hair, theatrical stage presence — became the band’s most recognizable element.

The band ran their own Ubiquitous Records label through most of the 1980s, self-releasing a string of LPs and 12” singles before going dormant in the early 1990s. They reformed and have continued recording and performing into the 2020s, with new studio albums on Overground Records and a 2024 reissue of “Money Talks” on Crass Records.

Xntrix Records was a sublabel within the Poison Girls / Vi Subversa orbit, distributed through Rough Trade. Ubiquitous Records was entirely self-run; its “DAYGLO” catalog number series ran from the 1985 Money Talks 12” through the 1987 Cocktail Mix LP.

Scene Connections

Rubella Ballet are woven into the anarcho-punk scene through family ties and direct collaboration:

  • Poison Girls / Vi Subversa: Pete Fender (guitar) and Gem Stone (bass) are Vi Subversa’s children. Richard Famous (guitarist of Poison Girls) produced the debut single Ballet Dance and the Ballet Bag cassette and Cocktail Mix LP.
  • Flux Of Pink Indians: Sid Attion was drummer of Flux before and alongside his time with Rubella Ballet. Discogs ID: 213077.
  • Fatal Microbes: Gem Stone’s other band; both groups shared the Xntrix / Poison Girls label infrastructure.
  • Crass: The band was literally formed at a Crass gig. The 2024 Money Talks reissue on Crass Records formalizes the scene kinship.
  • Crass Records: Spiritual home; the 2024 reissue is the first official Crass Records release.
  • Conway Hall, London: The original formation site — Crass’s gig invitation to the audience.
