The Homosexuals

Discogs ID: 282975 Also known as: George Harrasment (Discogs ID: 1077058), Ici La Bas Formed: 1978, Battersea, South London Genres: Post-punk, Art Rock, Experimental, Lo-Fi, New Wave

Fiercely independent post-punk group founded by Bruno Wizard (born Bruno McQuillan, Discogs ID: 4417261) and Anton Hayman (Discogs ID: 2283273) in Battersea, South London in 1978. Their commitment to complete autonomy — self-releasing or releasing on micro-labels, refusing commercial compromise, operating across aliases — made them a canonical figure in the UK DIY underground even as they remained largely unknown outside it. The group produced a small but unusually dense body of recordings between 1978 and 1984, fell dormant for two decades, then resurfaced with a studio album in 2016.

Origins: The Rejects (7) and the South London Punk Scene (1976–1978)

The Homosexuals grew out of The Rejects (7) (Discogs ID: 1872787), a punk group Bruno Wizard formed in New Cross, South London in 1976 with Ian Kane (6) (Discogs ID: 12053119) as founding songwriter. The Rejects played their first gig in January 1977 supporting The Damned and The Vibrators, and over the following months shared bills with Wire, Generation X, The Jam, Eater, 999, and Sham 69. In the summer of 1977, Wizard recruited Jim Welton (Discogs ID: 2283274) on bass and Anton Hayman on guitar; a drummer named David Dus (associated with Wayne County, later Jayne County) also passed through. No recordings were released under The Rejects name.

By 1978, with the lineup coalescing around Wizard, Hayman, and Welton, the group relocated to Battersea and renamed themselves The Homosexuals — a provocative, confrontational choice that matched their art-damaged, wilfully uncommercial stance. The name change and the move from New Cross to Battersea mark the break from punk proper into the post-punk art rock territory the band would inhabit.

First Recordings (1978–1979)

Hearts In Exile (1978, Lorelei No. 1, cat. PF 151) was their debut: a two-track 7” single, sides labelled A and AA, containing “Hearts In Exile” and “Soft South Africans.” The sole writing credit goes to Labjabs — a group pseudonym or collective alias rather than an individual. The single appeared on Lorelei No. 1 (Discogs ID: 76834), a one-release label created specifically for this pressing; there is no documented parent or relationship to any other Lorelei imprint. Styles tagged: Art Rock, New Wave, Punk.

The Homosexuals EP (1979, Black Noise (2), cat. No.2) followed as a 12” six-track EP. Tracks: “Astral Glamour,” “Collected Of You,” “The Birds Have Risen,” “Divorce Proceeding(s) From Reality,” “Mecho Madness,” “Vociferous Slam.” No additional personnel credits appear in the Discogs entry. Black Noise (2) (Discogs ID: 41902) was their primary label home for this period; it is listed as a sublabel of Electrisco and has its URL pointing to astralglamour.com, suggesting a direct link to the band’s own infrastructure. Tagged: Experimental, New Wave.

George Harrasment: Masai Sleep Walking (1980)

In 1980, the group released an LP under the alias George Harrasment (Discogs ID: 1077058): Masai Sleep Walking (Black Noise (2), cat. BN 6). The alias is listed as having Ici La Bas as a further alias — so the chain runs: The Homosexuals → George Harrasment → Ici La Bas.

Masai Sleep Walking is their most extensively credited release from the early period. Across its 26 tracks (A1–A16, B1–B10), personnel included:

The Homosexuals’ Record (1984)

After a gap of four years, The Homosexuals’ Record (1984, Recommended Records, cat. RR 18) appeared on Chris Cutler’s Recommended Records label — at that point the leading UK outlet for post-punk experimental and avant-garde music. The LP/compilation format (45 RPM, Compilation, LP) collects 16 tracks across two sides, drawing on recordings from 1978–1984:

Side A: “My Night Out,” “Technique Street,” “Vociferous Slam,” “Soft South Africans (1st Version),” “Neutron Lover,” “A Million Keys,” “Naming Of Parts,” “Kiss With Venom,” “Divorce (Proceedings) From Reality”

Side B: “Hearts In Exile,” “All About Cheap,” “Soft South Africans (2nd Version),” “False Sentiments,” “Mecho Madness,” “Astral Glamour,” “The Birds Have Risen”

No additional personnel credits appear in the Discogs entry. The Recommended Records release gave the band their widest distribution and anchored their reputation in the UK experimental / art-rock underground. Tagged: Art Rock, Experimental, New Wave, Post-Punk.

After The Homosexuals’ Record, the group went largely dormant as primary recording artists for two decades, though Jim Welton continued extensive activity under his many aliases (L. Voag, Xentos, Amos, etc.) through It’s War Boys.

Archival Compilations: Astral Glamour Series (2004)

Two major archival compilations appeared on Hyped To Death (Discogs ID: 80378) in 2004, overseen by Chuck Warner (Discogs ID: 1793197), who produced, remastered, and co-executive-produced both:

An Introduction To Astral Glamour (2004, Hyped To Death / Messthetics #204, Discogs IDs: 10885902 / 19423279) — a 27-track compilation/promo of archival material from 1978–1984. The Messthetics series documented UK DIY music of the late-punk/post-punk era; issue #204 was devoted entirely to The Homosexuals.

Astral Glamour (2004, Hyped To Death, Discogs ID: 2916532) — the more substantial archival set, a multi-disc compilation credited as remastered/reissued. Personnel credits document the full original-era lineup in detail:

A further cassette reissue, Astral Glamour (Suplex Cassettes, 2014), appeared as a limited-edition compilation.

Love Guns? (2008) and the Transatlantic Reformation

Before the 2016 album, the band had already re-formed in a transatlantic configuration. Love Guns? (2008, Serious Business Records, cat. SBR33, Discogs ID: 1754028) was a 10” EP (limited edition, numbered) with five tracks: “Fanfare,” “Slow Guns,” “3AM (Pink Pony),” “Don’t Touch My Hair,” and “Daddy Sent Johnny To Baghdad = Johnny Came Home In A Bag Dad.” The lineup was the same nucleus as the 2016 album — Bruno Wizard (vocals, guitar), Mike Dos Santos (bass, piano), Dave Siegal (guitar), Travis Harrison (drums, engineer, producer) — produced by Travis Harrison on Serious Business Records (US). Tagged: Art Rock, Indie Rock, Post-Punk, Punk. Several tracks from this EP (“Fanfare,” “Slow Guns,” “3AM (Pink Pony),” “Don’t Touch My Hair”) appear in revised form on Important If True, suggesting the 2008 sessions were a dry run for the later album.

Important If True (2016)

The Homosexuals returned as an active recording group with Important If True (2016, self-released, Discogs ID: 9529239) — a nine-track digital album. The lineup reflects a transatlantic reconfiguration: Bruno Wizard reunited with Mike Dos Santos and Anton Hayman (as co-writer) but brought in American collaborators for the actual sessions:

Jim Welton has no credit on this release, which documents the Wizard-led strand of the band rather than the full original partnership.
