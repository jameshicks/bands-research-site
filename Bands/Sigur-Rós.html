Sigur Rós

Sigur Rós

Sigur Rós

Discogs ID: 8299 Origin: Reykjavík, Iceland Active: January 1994–present Genres: Post-Rock, Ambient, Ethereal, Modern Classical Labels: Smekkleysa, FatCat Records, Krúnk, EMI, Geffen Records, Parlophone, XL Recordings, BMG

Icelandic post-rock and ambient band from Reykjavík. Known for Jónsi’s falsetto vocals, bowed guitar technique, use of the invented language “Vonlenska” (Hopelandic), and dense orchestral arrangements that fold strings, brass, and choir into slow-building, glacially paced song structures. The name “Sigur Rós” is Icelandic for “Victory Rose,” named after frontman Jónsi’s newborn sister Sigurrós Elín Birgisdóttir at the time of the band’s formation.

Biography

Formation and Von (1994–1997)

Jón Þór “Jónsi” Birgisson (guitar, vocals), Georg Hólm (bass), and Ágúst Ævar Gunnarsson (drums) formed the group in Reykjavík in January 1994. Debut album Von (Smekkleysa, 1997) was entirely self-produced — all recording, editing, mixing, mastering, design, and arrangement credited to the band collectively. The album is in Icelandic; the three original members are credited by name as “Sigur Rós Er.”

Ágætis Byrjun and the FatCat Era (1998–2003)

Kjartan Sveinsson joined on keyboards in 1998. The second album Ágætis Byrjun (Smekkleysa, 1999) was produced with Ken Thomas and became the breakthrough internationally. FatCat Records signed them for UK/international release. The album’s 10 tracks — including “Svefn-g-englar,” “Starálfur,” and “Viðrar Vel Til Loftárása” — established the band’s signature: falsetto over bowed guitar swells, string orchestration, and texts mixing Icelandic and invented Vonlenska (Hopelandic). Ágúst Ævar Gunnarsson departed after this record; Orri Páll Dýrason took over on drums.

The remix/recycle album Von Brigði appeared in 1998, and the Svefn-G-Englar EP (FatCat, 1999) was the first UK single.

The third album ( ) (FatCat Records, 2002) was famously untitled with eight untitled tracks. All credits were listed uncredited in the original pressings — Ken Thomas’s production role, Amiina’s string arranging and performance, and the individual members’ contributions were all post-facto documented. The lyrical language is entirely Vonlenska.

Takk… and EMI (2004–2007)

The band signed to EMI (Geffen in the US). Takk… (2005) was their most melodically accessible album, with “Hoppípolla” becoming the most widely licensed track from their catalog (used in BBC and many film/TV contexts). Ken Thomas remained co-producer and engineer. Ted Jensen at Sterling Sound mastered. Amiina performed strings throughout; a brass section drawn from Icelandic players (Eiríkur Orri Ólafsson, Samúel Jón Samúelsson, Helgi Hrafn Jónsson, Össur Geirsson) provided additional color.

The Heima concert film (2007) documented their 2006 free concerts in Iceland, directed by Dean DeBlois (later of How to Train Your Dragon). Field recordings from Chris Watson and Jana Winderen were incorporated.

Með Suð… and Flood (2008)

Með Suð Í Eyrum Við Spilum Endalaust (2008) marked a shift in approach — sunnier, more open, and produced with Flood (Mark Ellis) alongside the band. Recording sessions spanned London (Assault and Battery), New York (Sear Sound), Havana (Egrem Studio), and Iceland (Sundlaugin). Ryan McGinley shot the photography. Ted Jensen mastered again.

Kjartan’s Departure, Valtari, Kveikur (2012–2013)

Kjartan Sveinsson’s final album as a member was Valtari (Krúnk / Parlophone, 2012), produced by the band with Alex Somers as co-producer, mixer, and engineer. The Sixteen choir (directed by Eamonn Dougan) sang on two tracks. Valgeir Sigurðsson recorded additional strings.

After Kjartan’s departure, the remaining trio made Kveikur (XL Recordings, 2013) — a darker, more abrasive album. Self-produced; mixed by Alex Somers and Rich Costey separately; strings arranged by Daníel Bjarnason. Mastered by Ted Jensen.

Orri’s Departure, Hiatus, and Return (2018–present)

Following Orri Páll Dýrason’s exit in 2018, the band entered an extended hiatus. They released ambient/sleep-oriented material (Sigur Rós Presents Liminal Sleep, 2019; Variations On Darkness, 2019; 22° Lunar Halo, 2019), and collaborated with the Kronos Quartet on Odin’s Raven Magic (2020), a piece originally composed for the Reykjavík Arts Festival 2002 with poet Sigurður Pálsson and rímur-singer Steindór Andersen.

Kjartan Sveinsson rejoined in February 2022 with new drummer Ólafur Björn Ólafsson. The ninth studio album Átta (Krúnk, 2023) features the London Contemporary Orchestra (Robert Ames, conductor) and was the first to credit BMG alongside Krúnk internationally.

Studio

Sigur Rós recorded extensively at Sundlaugin (“The Swimming Pool Studio”) — a studio they converted from a swimming pool in Álafoss, Mosfellsbær, just outside Reykjavík. Birgir Jón Birgisson served as house engineer there. Multiple other Icelandic artists recorded at Sundlaugin, including The Album Leaf (In A Safe Place, 2004).
