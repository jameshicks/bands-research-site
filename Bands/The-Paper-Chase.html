Post-punk / art-rock band formed 1998 by John Congleton in Dallas, Texas. Described by AllMusic as “a jagged structure of avant-garde jazz, noise, indie, and punk.” Congleton was the sole constant member — songwriter, vocalist, guitarist, and producer throughout. The band dissolved around 2009–2012, eventually giving way to The Nighty Nite.

Biography

Formation. Congleton formed The Paper Chase in Dallas in 1998 while still developing his production career. The first show was in a parking lot in 1999: “Super sunny, but sounding terrible. We sucked.” The band’s debut was a CDr split released on Congleton’s own Clandestine Project Recordings imprint.

Early years (1999–2002). A 7” on Dallas-area Magister Ludi Records (…And The Machines Are Winning, 1999) followed — two tracks engineered by Congleton and Chris Godbey, mastered by John Golden. The first proper LP, Young Bodies Heal Quickly, You Know (2000, Beatville Records), established the band’s sound. Cntrl-Alt-Delete-U (2001, Divot) was an EP that was later reissued. By 2002 the band had signed to UK indie Southern Records for Hide The Kitchen Knives.

Southern Records period (2003–2004). The What Big Teeth You Have EP (2003, Southern Records) added Sean Kirkpatrick to the lineup and showed a more arranged side — two Congleton originals alongside covers of Jacques Brel’s “My Death” and Roger Waters’s “Go Fishing”, with cellist Kris Youmans and production/recording/mixing by Congleton. An enhanced CD with a video for “Don’t You Wish You Had Some More” directed by Youmans and Chris Hardy. The same year, God Bless Your Black Heart (2004, Trost Records, Austria) appeared as a double LP — the band credited as “The Bleeding Hearts” in some personnel notes; Congleton contributed vocals, guitar, organ, synth, sampler, string arrangements, production, engineering; Youmans (cello) and Elliot Figg (piano) on strings alongside Rebecca Phares (violin). Also in 2004: a split 7” on Dallas indie Idol Records pairing The Paper Chase with Will Johnson (The Riot Jack / I Did A Terrible Thing); A-side by The Paper Chase, B-side by Will Johnson; Congleton wrote the Paper Chase track, Johnson wrote both. Artwork by Angelique Congleton; Erv Karwelis credited as uncredited executive producer. And a collaboration release with Italian act Red Worms’ Farm (The Paper Chase Meet Red Worms’ Farm, Robot Radio Records, Italy).

Kill Rock Stars period (2005–2007). A split CD with Xiu Xiu covering Nick Cave (Cover Nick Cave, 2005). Then the Kill Rock Stars album Now You Are One Of Us (2006, KRS 445) — Congleton: vocals, guitar, keyboards, piano, sampler, theremin, sequencer, producer. Considered the band’s most fully realized record. A UK/Europe edition and an Austrian pressing followed. Cntrl-Alt-Delete-U was reissued on a different label in 2007.

Final period (2008–2012). Someday This Could All Be Yours Vol. 1 (2009, Magic Bullet Records / Southern Records) was the final studio album. Final lineup included drummer Jason Garner (Paper Chase’s last drummer, later a core member of The Nighty Nite) and Doug Edwards (5). The band dissolved around 2009–2012. A Vol. 2 — songs about natural disasters, analogous to Vol. 1’s themes — was written and partially recorded but Congleton chose not to finish it, describing it as “a very defeated and highly depressive record.” It has never been released.

Legacy. After the dissolution Congleton formed The Nighty Nite with Garner, debuting at The Double Wide in Dallas on June 11, 2011.
