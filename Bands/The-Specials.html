The Specials

Coventry ska/new wave band. Discogs artist ID: 27937. Active 1977–1984, reunited 2008–present. Founded Two-Tone Records, the defining label of the British ska revival.

Overview

The Specials formed in Coventry in 1977 as The Coventry Automatics (Discogs ID: 1174362), becoming the vanguard of the Two-Tone movement. Founded by keyboardist Jerry Dammers (real name Jeremy David Hounsell Dammers), they combined Jamaican ska and rocksteady with punk energy and urgent social commentary. The band signed to Two-Tone Records — which Dammers had created — distributed through Chrysalis, and their debut single “Gangsters” (1979) launched the label and the movement.

Between 1979 and 1981 they released seven singles and two studio albums. “Too Much Too Young” and “Ghost Town” both reached UK #1. “Ghost Town” (1981), with its desolate portrait of deindustrialised Britain, hit #1 amid the Brixton and Toxteth riots and became one of the most significant political singles of the era.

By the time “Ghost Town” peaked the band had already fractured. Terry Hall, Neville Staple, and Lynval Golding departed to form Fun Boy Three. Jerry Dammers and John Bradbury continued under the name The Special AKA, releasing In The Studio (1984) and the anti-apartheid anthem “Free Nelson Mandela” (1984). Horace Panter continued with Special AKA to a degree, then joined General Public.

The band reunited in 2008 (without Dammers) for a thirtieth anniversary tour. The 2019 album Encore marked the first studio album in 35 years, produced by Hall, Golding, Panter, and Nikolaj Torp Larsen; the reunion lineup did not include Dammers.
