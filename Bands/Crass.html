Crass

Active: 1977–1984 (reformed sporadically post-2010; Crass Records restarted 2019) Based: Dial House, North Weald, Essex, UK Label: Crass Records (Discogs label ID: 13168) Genre: Anarcho-punk, art punk, post-punk Discogs Artist ID: 83076

Overview

Crass were a British anarcho-punk collective formed in 1977 at Dial House, a communal farmhouse in North Weald, Essex, co-founded by drummer/lyricist Penny Rimbaud (Jeremy John Ratter). They released their debut LP The Feeding of the Five Thousand in 1979 on Crass Records, a label they founded that same year and ran until 1992. Over seven years the band recorded a string of studio albums and singles that were foundational to the anarcho-punk movement: confrontational politics, pacifism, feminism, and DIY ethics were their constant themes. They disbanded in 1984, deliberately — the year that had served as their countdown (Crass Records catalogue numbers were designed as a literal countdown to 1984). Penny Rimbaud continued as a solo artist and poet; Steve Ignorant performed and toured under his name and with various groups; Crass Records was revived by Rimbaud and others in 2019 with a new wave of archival and remix releases.

Crass Records Label Context

Crass Records (ID: 13168) was founded by Crass in 1979. Its catalogue numbers counted down to 1984 (e.g., 521984 = “5 years until 1984,” 421984 = “4 years until 1984”). The label released not only Crass’s own records but also those of aligned bands, becoming a hub of the anarcho-punk movement. Sublabels include Corpus Christi, Crassical Collection, Creative Recording And Sound Services, As It Was In The Beginning, and 2 By 2 And Back Again.

Political & Aesthetic Philosophy

Crass operated as an anarchist collective, rejecting conventional band structures and record-industry norms. Every release was self-produced (though John Loder at Southern Studios engineered most of the studio work from Penis Envy onward). Visual identity was central: Gee Vaucher’s photomontage artwork and the stark black-and-white packaging were inseparable from the music. The collective coined the phrase “there is no authority but yourself.” Their feminism was pronounced — Penis Envy (1981) was an intentional corrective to the male-dominated punk world, with all lead vocals performed by women.

Crass also engaged in direct action: their hoax tape Thatchergate (1983) — allegedly spliced from a real conversation between Ronald Reagan and Margaret Thatcher — was circulated internationally before being exposed as fabricated.
