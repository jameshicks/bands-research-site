Overview

Deaf School were a Liverpool art rock and new wave band formed in January 1974, active through an initial run that produced nine releases on Warner Bros. Records before they split in 1978, then briefly reuniting in 1988–1989 and returning more durably from 2009 onward. The core membership included Anne Martin, Clive Langer, Eric Shark, Gregg Braden, and Ian Ritchie, spanning styles from new wave and power pop to experimental and art rock. Their later-era catalogue spread across several smaller labels — Lemon Recordings, Hayabusa Landings, Strange Days Records, and their own Deaf School imprint. Prominent collaborators across their output include Warner Bros. figure Rob Dickins (35 shared credits), Robert John Lange (17 shared credits), and engineer-producers Steve Churchyard and Muff Winwood.

Notes (web) — 1988–1989 reunion vs. 2009 revival

1988 reunion: trigger and scope

Ken Testi — Deaf School’s original road manager and co-founder of Eric’s Club — was the direct organiser: “In 1988 Testi persuaded Deaf School to reform for a series of shows, from which Clive Langer produced a live album, 2nd Coming: Liverpool ‘88.” en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Testi (as of 2025-10-23)

The reunion had at least two live components: the 1988 shows captured on 2nd Coming: Liverpool ‘88 (Demon Records), and a further gig in July 1989 as part of the Big Beat festival, a benefit for the Hillsborough disaster fund. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf_School (as of 2025-10-23)

A contemporary Penny Kiley review describes the reunion as “one of those occasions that felt like, well, an occasion” but notes the band were “very much a cult band, and massive in their home city” — framing it as a local-scene event rather than a national comeback. pennykiley.substack.com/p/deaf-school-june-1988 (snippet only — full article is paywalled; checked 2025-10-23)

No source located gives an explicit reason why the reunion ended after the Hillsborough benefit gig and the single live album. Members’ solo careers (notably Clive Langer’s production work, which was at its commercial peak in the late 1980s) and the absence of any new studio material or label interest are the likely structural reasons, but no interview or press article found to date states this directly.

2009 revival: trigger and sustained activity

The 2009 return was catalysed by Cherry Red’s Lemon label remastering and reissuing all three original Warner Bros. studio albums in September 2009. The band accompanied the reissues with ten live shows that year — Dublin Castle and The Garage in London, four sell-out nights at Liverpool Everyman, the Hope Street Festival, and two Christmas Bash shows (100 Club London; Liverpool O2 Academy). This was the first time the full band had played ten dates in a single year since the 1970s. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf_School (as of 2025-10-23)

Ken Testi continued as the organising force: by 2008 he had established Eric’s Productions Limited and produced an “Eric’s Show” at Liverpool Academy featuring acts connected to the original club. Deaf School became a regular fixture in his Eric’s Productions annual programme, with collaborators including members of Madness and Dexys Midnight Runners. en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Testi (as of 2025-10-23)

The contrast with 1988 is structural: the 2009 revival was accompanied by fresh product (the remastered albums), a wider touring programme outside Liverpool, and an ongoing institutional home in Eric’s Productions, none of which the 1988 reunion had.
