The Paul Butterfield Blues Band

Integrated Chicago blues/rock band, 1964–1971. Discogs artist ID: 437880.

Led by harmonica player and vocalist Paul Butterfield, the band was among the first racially integrated rock groups to perform authentic Chicago electric blues for white rock audiences, and a central force in bringing the genre to mainstream attention. Their seven albums for Elektra Records (1965–1971) chart a trajectory from raw South Side electric blues through jazz-inflected psychedelia and horn-driven soul. Formed in Chicago around 1963–64, they dissolved in 1971 following the departure of most members; Butterfield went on to lead Better Days before his death in 1987. A posthumous archival album, Strawberry Jam (Winner, 1995), produced by Mark Naftalin, surfaced unreleased recordings from the mid-to-late 1960s.

Notes:

  • Paul A. Rothchild produced the debut, East-West, and the final studio album. He was Elektra’s in-house producer at the time.
  • John Court produced the two 1967–68 albums and also sang with “The Icebag Four” vocal group on In My Own Dream.
  • Jerry Ragovoy — an R&B songwriter/producer known for Janis Joplin and Howard Tate sessions — produced Keep On Moving.
  • Todd Rundgren produced Live (1970) and mixed Sometimes I Just Feel Like Smilin’ (1971) — his early-career production work.
  • Mark Abramson appears across the earliest sessions as assistant/co-producer and later supervised The Original Lost Elektra Sessions (1995).

Notable Outside Connections

Paul Butterfield on Deadline — Down By Law (1985)

Butterfield plays harmonica on “Makossa Rock” (track B1) of Deadline’s Down By Law LP (Celluloid, 1985). Deadline was co-led by Phillip Wilson (his former drummer) and Bill Laswell, with Jaco Pastorius on bass, Bernie Worrell on synthesizer, Manu Dibango on tenor sax, and Olu Dara on cornet. This was one of Butterfield’s last recorded appearances; he died May 4, 1987.

Mike Bloomfield post-Butterfield

After leaving the band in 1966, Bloomfield co-founded The Electric Flag (Chess/Columbia, 1967–68), then recorded Super Session (Columbia, 1968) with Al Kooper and Stephen Stills — one of the first jam-oriented rock albums. He maintained an extensive if fitful solo career through Takoma, Columbia, and other labels until his death on February 15, 1981. His groups on Discogs include The Electric Flag, KGB (7), and Mill Valley Bunch.

Elvin Bishop post-Butterfield

After leaving in 1968, Bishop recorded for Epic before signing to Capricorn Records, where he had a run of blues-rock albums (1974–1978) and a major pop hit with “Fooled Around and Fell in Love” (1975, with vocalist Mickey Thomas). He continued recording for Alligator Records from the late 1980s onward.

David Sanborn post-Butterfield

Sanborn became one of the most commercially successful saxophonists of the 1970s–80s, with a long run of albums on Warner Bros. Records (from Taking Off, 1975) and later Elektra and Reprise. He was also a prominent session player — appearing on recordings by David Bowie, Paul Simon, James Taylor, Gil Evans, and many others. His Warner Bros. debut as a leader was Taking Off (1975).

Phillip Wilson post-Butterfield

Wilson bridged Chicago blues-rock and the AACM avant-garde simultaneously during his Butterfield tenure. After the band he became central to both the Art Ensemble of Chicago circle and the downtown NYC scene around Bill Laswell. See Phillip Wilson for his full career documentation.

Buzzy Feiten

After the Butterfield band, Feiten became an in-demand session guitarist and recorded solo albums on Warner Bros. / Full Moon, including Full Moon (1982) and Phantom Of The Footlights (1982). His Discogs groups listing also includes membership in the Rascals (though he joined post-peak). He later became known for the Buzz Feiten Tuning System.

Al Kooper Guest Credit

Al Kooper appears on In My Own Dream (1968) playing organ on tracks A4 and B2 (piano on B2 is credited to Keith Johnson). Kooper had been involved with the Dylan-electric-set context (he played keyboards at Newport 1965 with Dylan) and recorded Super Session with Bloomfield later the same year.
