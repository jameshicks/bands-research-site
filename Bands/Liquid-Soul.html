Liquid Soul

Discogs ID: 211538
Active: 1995–2006+
Origin: Chicago, Illinois, USA
Genres: Jazz-Funk, Acid Jazz, Hip Hop
Labels: Soul What Records · Ark 21 Records · Shanachie · Narada Jazz

Chicago jazz and urban dance band co-founded and led by saxophonist Mars Williams. Blended jazz-funk, acid jazz, and hip-hop with DJ turntablism and occasional MC vocals. Received a Grammy nomination for Best Contemporary Jazz Record (2001). The band was more active than the original stub suggested — Discogs shows a 2006 album on Telarc, indicating the project extended well past 2002.

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1995)

Liquid Soul formed in Chicago in 1995, anchored by Mars Williams (born Marc Charles Williams, May 29, 1955, Elmhurst, IL — died November 20, 2023, Chicago, IL), a prolific saxophonist already known from new-wave work with The Waitresses and The Psychedelic Furs. The debut self-titled album appeared on the band’s own Soul What Records imprint (cat. SW 0000) the same year. Produced by Mars Williams, it featured DJ Ajax on scratches and guest MC appearances by The Dirty M.F. (Walter Sargent), Frost, S.P.O., and V. The record was Discogs-tagged as both Hip Hop and Jazz / Jazz-Funk — a mix characteristic of the mid-1990s acid jazz scene.

Make Some Noise and Mainstream Breakthrough (1998)

The second album Make Some Noise (1998) moved to Ark 21 Records (cat. 61868 10021 2 3). Production again credited to Mars Williams, with Jesse De La Pena on scratches. Notable guest: vocalist Kurt Elling on “Salt Peanuts / Chocolate Covered Nut”; Yvonne Gage and Norwegian pop singer Trine Rein on “I Want You To Want Me” (produced separately by Mike Rogers). The album covered an Ornette Coleman composition (“Ramblin’”) alongside originals. DJ Ajax provided scratches throughout multiple tracks.

Here’s The Deal and Grammy Nomination (2000)

Here’s The Deal (2000, Shanachie, cat. SH 5065) was a live-and-studio hybrid: studio tracks recorded by Rick Barnes, live tracks recorded at unspecified venues by Timothy Powell (live recording), with Mike Drazin and Mike Ways assisting. The album featured a consolidated lineup: Mars Williams (tenor, alto, sopranino sax, flute, toy), Ricky Showalter (bass), Dan Leali (drums), Tom Sanchez (guitar), Chris “Hambone” Cameron (keyboards), Ron Haynes (trumpet, flugelhorn), John Janowiak (trombone), Newt Cole (percussion), and DJ Ajax (turntables). Rick Barnes handled mixing; Jeff Hillman mastered. This album earned the Grammy nomination for Best Contemporary Jazz Record in 2001.

A companion Here’s The Deal EP (2000) was issued on Liquid Sound Lounge.

One-Two Punch (2006)

A later album, One-Two Punch (2006), appeared on Telarc (Discogs master 783576). By this point the personnel had substantially changed: Phil Ajjarapu on bass, Tony “Kick Drum” Taylor on drums, Tom Klein on guitar, Chris Cameron on keyboards, Ledie Garcia on percussion, Andy Baker on trombone, Doug Corcoran and Hugh Ragin on trumpet. Mars Williams played electronics and saxophones. DJ Logic (Jason Kibler), Eddie Mills, Scott Paskon, and Redlox all contributed turntables and/or vocals. Van Christie (aka CVC3) served as engineer, mixer, producer, and programmer. Paul Blakemore mastered.

Lisa Simone (vocals) is listed as a member in the Discogs artist record but does not appear in the release-level credits for any specific album in the DB — her role may have been primarily live or on tracks not individually credited.

Connections

  • Mars Williams — bandleader, also played with The Psychedelic Furs, The Waitresses, NRG Ensemble, Peter Brötzmann Chicago Tentet, Vandermark 5. Died November 2023 from ampullary cancer.
  • Lisa Simone — vocalist, daughter of Nina Simone and Andy Stroud. Also known as Lisa Stroud / Simone (11). Broadway actress and solo artist (labels: Sound Surveyor Music, Elektra France, Laborie Jazz).
  • Ornette Coleman — composed “Ramblin’” covered on Make Some Noise.
  • Kurt Elling — Chicago jazz vocalist, guested on Make Some Noise track 3.
  • DJ Logic — turntablist, appeared on One-Two Punch.
