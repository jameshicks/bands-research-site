Surfin Dave And The Absent Legends

Discogs ID: 1409008 Origin: Leeds, UK Active: c. 1982–1986 (formed when Haskett joined; disbanded 1986) Genres: Rock, Post-Punk, Surf Styles: Garage Rock, Surf, Punk, Rock & Roll Labels: Nine Mile, Crammed Discs

Biography

Surfin Dave And The Absent Legends were a Leeds-based band active from around 1982 to 1986, centred on vocalist and guitarist David Coleman — who performed and recorded under the stage name Surfin Dave (Discogs artist ID: 2050500). Coleman was a guitarist, vocalist, and fanzine writer based in Yorkshire; he also contributed to the mid-1980s Leeds fanzine Rouska.

Formation and name history

Coleman’s band predates the Absent Legends name. It was originally called “The Beany T’s” — a name Coleman had been using before the lineup coalesced. In 1982, American guitarist Chris Haskett moved to Leeds to complete an undergraduate degree in Philosophy, joined Coleman’s group, and the band was rechristened The Absent Legends. The formation of the classic lineup (Coleman, Haskett, Willis, Green) therefore dates to around 1982, not 1984–85 as Discogs data alone suggested. Wikipedia/Chris Haskett, as of 2025-06-02

Andy Kershaw, who was active as Entertainments Secretary at Leeds University in 1981 and would later become a Radio 1 DJ, first encountered the band when Coleman sent him a demo tape around that period, which led to the band playing at Leeds University. Kershaw subsequently championed the band, had them perform on the Old Grey Whistle Test (BBC2) in the mid-1980s, and wrote the liner notes for the LP. [Andy Kershaw Fandom Wiki — source blocked by Cloudflare; claim also sourced from ReverbNation bio and search aggregate]

The band released a 7” single, “Stateside Centre” / “Release Me” (Nine Mile, NM1, 1985), and a full-length album, In Search Of A Decent Haircut (Crammed Discs, CRAM041LP, 1985). The LP was co-produced by Coleman, Haskett, and engineer Tony Bonner. The band gigged extensively in the UK and toured Europe. [Wikipedia/Chris Haskett; ReverbNation bio]

The album was released in Belgium on Crammed Discs (Brussels, founded 1980 by Marc Hollander of Aksak Maboul) — a notable international placement. Crammed’s A&R philosophy was explicitly eclectic and non-geographic; the label signed acts “more or less by chance” based on fit with their post-punk/avant sensibility rather than targeting specific national scenes. The specific route by which the Absent Legends reached Crammed is not documented in any available source, but the Andy Kershaw connection and the band’s profile on the UK indie circuit (Nine Mile / Red Rhino co-op distribution) are the most plausible conduits. [Wikipedia/Crammed Discs, as of 2025-06-02; Bandcamp Daily label profile]

Dissolution: The Absent Legends disbanded in 1986, the year after the album’s release. Haskett returned to Washington D.C. during the summer of 1986. [Wikipedia/Chris Haskett, as of 2025-06-02]

The band placed squarely within the mid-1980s stratum of the Leeds independent scene — on the 2023 retrospective compilation Where Were You- (Independent Music From Leeds 1978-1989), they appear on CD 2 (the goth/alternative/industrial 1982–1985 disc) at track 2-15 with “Stateside Centre”, alongside contemporaries including The Cassandra Complex, Age Of Chance, The Sisters Of Mercy, and Red Lorry Yellow Lorry.

The band’s sound combined garage-rock energy, surf idioms, and the post-punk indie aesthetic of the Leeds scene. Album track titles — “Living White Hell”, “Pistols At Dawn”, “Health Farm”, “Snack Bar” — suggest a sardonic, slice-of-life lyrical sensibility.

David Coleman after the Absent Legends

After the band split, Coleman returned to Dorset (he is based in Poole, Dorset as of the ReverbNation profile). He performed sporadically, including support slots with The Clash and appearances on BBC’s Whistle Test, and alongside Billy Bragg and Phill Jupitus. He spent time with a project called Pronghorn before returning to Dorset in 1993. Coleman still performs occasionally, solo or with friends. [ReverbNation bio, https://www.reverbnation.com/surfindave — weak source, fan/self-submitted, treat with caution]

Coleman continued as Surfin’ Dave & The B’Nee Teas (Discogs: 890197) — a project whose name is a deliberate phonetic respelling of “The Beany T’s”, the original pre-Absent Legends band name. The B’Nee Teas thus represents a revival/continuation of that earlier identity. Last.fm shows one track attributed to them (“Exchange & Mart”, also an Absent Legends album track) with 44 listeners, and Discogs lists 1 album and 1 compilation appearance; no standalone releases have been located through web research.

Key member connections

Guitarist Chris Haskett (Discogs: 79631, born 1962, Washington D.C.) moved to Leeds in 1982 to finish a Philosophy degree and joined Coleman’s band, triggering the rename to “The Absent Legends”. He was with the band from 1982 to the 1986 split. He then returned to Washington D.C. and went on to major recognition as the guitarist of Rollins Band, where he served through their most prominent period. He also appeared in Henrietta Collins And The Wifebeating Childhaters and Iron Cross, and later Pigface and The Joy Thieves. [Wikipedia/Chris Haskett, as of 2025-06-02]

Bassist Jez Willis (real name Jeremy Philip John Willis; Discogs: 288907) became a significant figure in Leeds electronic music. After the Absent Legends he was a member of The Cassandra Complex, and crucially became a founding member and core songwriter of Utah Saints — the Leeds rave/electronic act whose early-90s records “What Can You Do For Me” (1991) and “Something Good” (1992) were major UK chart hits on FFRR. His songwriting credits on Utah Saints releases span from 1991 through to the 2000s. Willis is also listed as a professor at Leeds Conservatoire and Leeds Beckett University. He also appeared in MDMA (2) and W.M.T.I.D. — both of which appear on the Where Were You? compilation (tracks 3-9 and 3-8 respectively).

Drummer Mick Green (2) (Discogs: 819999) also appeared in Rollins Band and Henrietta Collins And The Wifebeating Childhaters alongside Haskett, suggesting the Haskett/Green pairing continued after the Absent Legends.

Scene Connections

Surfin Dave And The Absent Legends fit into the mid-1980s stratum of the Leeds independent scene, sitting between the early post-punk DIY wave (1978–81) and the late-80s indie/rave generation.
