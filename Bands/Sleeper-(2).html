Genre: Britpop, Indie Rock

English Britpop band led by singer-guitarist Louise Wener, originally active 1993–1998 and reformed from 2017. Originally known as Surrender Dorothy before changing their name to Sleeper. One of the key acts on Indolent Records alongside Dubstar, releasing three studio albums between 1995 and 1997.

Biography

Formation and Early EPs (1993–1994)

Sleeper formed in London in the early 1990s, initially under the name Surrender Dorothy before settling on Sleeper. The original lineup comprised Louise Wener (vocals, guitar), Jon Stewart (guitar), Diid Osman (bass), and Andy MacLure (drums). They signed to the nascent Indolent Records label, which operated in partnership with RCA/BMG.

Their debut release was the Alice EP (1993), produced by Anjali Dutt, followed immediately by the Swallow EP (1993), also produced by Dutt. In 1994 they issued the “Bucket And Spade” single and the “Delicious” single — the latter seeing Ian Broudie (of Lightning Seeds) produce the lead track, with the band and Brian O’Shaughnessy handling B-sides. A promo compilation, Gorgeous and Fully Equipped, gathered the early material that year.

Britpop Peak: Smart and The It Girl (1995–1996)

The band’s breakthrough came with the “Inbetweener” single (1994/1995) and debut album Smart (1995), co-produced by Paul Corkett and Sleeper, with additional mixing from Stephen Street on several tracks and Ian Broudie producing/mixing “Delicious.” Jon Stewart co-wrote a number of tracks. Smart was released on Indolent Records / RCA / BMG (cat no. 74321 25825-2) and established Wener as one of Britpop’s most distinctive voices. Two further singles from the album followed: “Vegas” and “What Do I Do Now?” (both 1995).

The second album The It Girl (1996) marked a shift in production approach. Stephen Street produced the majority of the album (tracks 2–13), with engineer John Smith (4) producing track 12. Street also contributed keyboards and programming as an additional musician. The album was released on Indolent Records / BMG (cat no. 74321 36477-2). It spawned four singles: “Nice Guy Eddie,” “Sale Of The Century,” “Statuesque,” and “Atomic” (the last on EMI France). At its peak Sleeper charted consistently in the UK Top 40.

Third Album and Dissolution (1997–1998)

Pleased To Meet You (1997) was again produced primarily by Stephen Street, with Cenzo Townshend co-producing “Motorway Man” and also handling much of the mixing. The album featured contributions from The Kick Horns (brass on “She’s A Good Girl”), John Metcalfe (string arrangements on “Because Of You”), and The Duke Quartet (strings). It was released solely on Indolent Records (cat no. SLEEPCD016) without the earlier BMG/RCA co-credit, suggesting a changed distribution arrangement. Singles from the album included “She’s A Good Girl,” “Romeo Me,” and “Pleased To Meet You.” The band dissolved in 1998.

Post-Sleeper: Louise Wener

Louise Wener (born 30 July 1966, London) went on to become a novelist and teaches novel writing courses. A 2007 Greatest Hits compilation appeared on Sony BMG. Cherry Red issued expanded reissues of Smart and The It Girl in 2011.

Reformation (2017–present)

Sleeper reformed in 2017 with a new bassist, Kieron Pepper, replacing Diid Osman. The reformation lineup — Wener, Stewart, MacLure, Pepper — released the single “Look At You Now” (2018) through Not On Label, followed by the full reunion album The Modern Age (2019) on their own Gorsky Records. Stephen Street produced and mixed the album; mastering by John Davis (4). A companion single “The Sun Also Rises” was also released on Gorsky.

A second reunion album, This Time Tomorrow (2020), followed on Gorsky Records, self-produced in a distributed fashion: tracks handled variously by Andy MacLure, James Jackman, Louise Wener, and Sleeper collectively, with mixing split between MacLure, Cenzo Townshend, Toby Pitman, and others. Note: Kieron Pepper does not appear on This Time Tomorrow but does play on the Live It Girl Anniversary album (2022).

The Live At The Ritz live album (2019) and At The BBC compilation (2020) were also released on Gorsky Records. The BBC compilation appeared on vinyl as a Record Store Day release.
