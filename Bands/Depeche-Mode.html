Depeche Mode

Synth-pop / electronic band from Basildon, Essex. Formed 1980. Discogs artist ID: 2725.

Overview

Depeche Mode formed in Basildon in 1980 around the songwriting partnership of Vince Clarke and the vocal presence of Dave Gahan, with Martin Gore and Andy Fletcher rounding out the lineup. After Clarke’s departure following the debut album (he went on to found Yazoo and later Erasure), Gore became the band’s sole songwriter — a role he retained for over four decades. Alan Wilder joined in 1982 as the fourth member, significantly expanding their live and studio capability, and stayed until his resignation in 1995. From that point the core trio of Gahan, Gore, and Fletcher continued, with Fletcher’s death in May 2022 reducing the group to a duo.

Their career traces an arc from catchy early synth-pop (Mute, 1981–1983) through increasingly dark and industrial textures (the Flood-produced Violator, 1990; Songs Of Faith And Devotion, 1993), a post-Wilder repositioning (Ultra, 1997), and a sustained run of well-received late-period albums culminating in Memento Mori (Columbia, 2023), recorded after Fletcher’s death. The band’s relationship with Mute lasted from 1981 to 2017 and defined their catalog; they moved to Columbia for the final two albums.

Their total Discogs footprint as primary artists is over 13,000 releases — including an enormous volume of single pressings, remixes, and compilations. This article focuses on original studio albums and key production lineage.

Production lineage notes

  • Daniel Miller produced or co-produced the first six albums (1981–1986). Miller is also the founder of Mute.
  • Gareth Jones entered as engineer on Construction Time Again (1983) and remained as producer/engineer through Some Great Reward (1984) and Black Celebration (1986), and returned for Ultra (1997, additional vocals) and Exciter (2001, pre-production/additional production).
  • Flood (Mark Ellis) produced Violator (1990) and Songs Of Faith And Devotion (1993) — the commercial and creative peak. He returned to mix Delta Machine (2013).
  • Tim Simenon (Bomb the Bass) produced Ultra (1997).
  • Mark Bell (LFO) produced Exciter (2001) in full.
  • Ben Hillier produced the three-album run 2005–2013.
  • James Ford (Simian Mobile Disco) produced Spirit (2017) and Memento Mori (2023), also playing drums and contributing additional instrumentation.
  • Marta Salogni engineered and mixed Memento Mori, and handled additional programming — a significant expansion of her role compared to standard engineer credits.

Volume note on singles / remixes

Depeche Mode’s Discogs entry carries an enormous appendix of material beyond studio albums: 100+ single masters, extensive remix 12”s and promos, and multiple official compilation and live album series. The Mute singles series is particularly dense, spanning 1981–2017. That material is not cataloged here.

VCMG

Martin Gore and Vince Clarke collaborated as VCMG (Discogs artist ID: 2532459), releasing the album Ssss (2012, Mute) — a full electronic/techno album, marking their first studio collaboration since Speak & Spell in 1981.
