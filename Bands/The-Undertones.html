The Undertones

Punk/new wave band. Formed Derry, Northern Ireland, 1975. Active 1978–1983; reunited 2003–present (without original vocalist). Discogs artist ID: 64687

Overview

The Undertones formed in Derry, Northern Ireland in 1975, coalescing around brothers John O’Neill (rhythm guitar, principal songwriter) and Damian O’Neill (lead guitar), Feargal Sharkey (vocals), Michael Bradley (bass), and Billy Doherty (drums). Their debut EP “Teenage Kicks” was released in September 1978 on Terry Hooley’s Good Vibrations Records (Belfast), catalogue GOT 4. John Peel heard and played it on his BBC Radio 1 show, leading directly to a deal with Sire Records, who re-released “Teenage Kicks” and signed the band for their first two LPs.

Across four studio albums (1979–1983), the Undertones established themselves as one of the finest melodic punk acts in Britain, combining high-energy guitar-pop with Sharkey’s distinctive high tenor and John O’Neill’s concise, emotionally direct songwriting. Their first three albums — The Undertones (1979), Hypnotised (1980), and Positive Touch (1981) — were produced by Roger Bechirian, a key figure in the late-1970s new wave production world (Elvis Costello, Nick Lowe, Squeeze). The fourth, The Sin of Pride (1983), was co-produced by the band alongside Mike Hedges (The Cure, Associates, Siouxsie and the Banshees), signalling a more polished direction. The band split in summer 1983 after a run of summer festival dates.

Feargal Sharkey launched a solo career, reaching UK #1 in 1985 with “A Good Heart”. John and Damian O’Neill formed That Petrol Emotion in 1985. The band reunited in 2003 — without Sharkey — with Paul McLoone on vocals, releasing two further studio albums: Get What You Need (2003, Sanctuary) and Dig Yourself Deep (2007, Cooking Vinyl). A third post-reunion studio album, Dig What You Need, appeared in 2021 on Dimple Discs. The band remained active into 2024.
