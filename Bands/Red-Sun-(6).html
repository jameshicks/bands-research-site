Red Sun (6)

Type: International quartet (Austrian-based) Active: c. 1988–1997 Discogs artist ID: 993082 Name variations: Red Sun, Red Sun Group, Redsun, 레드 썬

Austrian/international avant-jazz ensemble blending free jazz, world music, and Korean traditional percussion. The group’s consistent core was Wolfgang Puschnig (alto saxophone, flute), Linda Sharrock (voice, visual art), Jamaaladeen Tacuma (bass guitar), and Uli Scherer (piano, keyboards). Their defining collaboration was with the Korean percussion ensemble Samulnori (사물놀이), led by Kim Duk-Soo, which shaped all five of their released albums and produced the ECM Records release Then Comes The White Tiger (1994) — the label’s highest-profile document of their work.

Biography

Formation and the Austrian Jazz Scene (c. 1986–1988)

Red Sun grew out of the Vienna avant-jazz scene of the mid-1980s, orbiting the circle of musicians gathered around Wolfgang Puschnig and the Vienna Art Orchestra. Puschnig had been a mainstay of the Austrian experimental jazz world since the late 1970s, working with Hat Hut Records ensembles and the Vienna Art Orchestra. Linda Sharrock — the Philadelphia-born vocalist and former partner of Sonny Sharrock — had relocated to Vienna by the mid-1980s. Uli Scherer was a Swiss-Austrian pianist who shared stage time with Puschnig and Sharrock under multiple group names including AM 4.

The fourth member, Jamaaladeen Tacuma, came from a different trajectory: the Philadelphia funk-avant-jazz world, having been Ornette Coleman’s electric bassist in Prime Time and a Gramavision solo artist. His alignment with the European jazz circuit (and his appearance on records like The Golden Palominos debut on Celluloid Records) made him a natural fit.

The group Puschnig, Sharrock, Tacuma, and Scherer operated under several allied names — including AM 4 and Alpine Aspects — before settling on Red Sun as the name for the collaboration that explicitly included SamulNori.

First Encounter with SamulNori: Red Sun · SamulNori (1989)

The debut album Red Sun · SamulNori (1989, Amadeo, cat. 841 222-2) documented a collision between the quartet and the four-piece Korean court-music percussion ensemble Samulnori. The SamulNori lineup on this release comprised Kim Duk-Soo (janggu/changgo), Lee Kwang Soo (kkwaengwari, vocals), Kang Min Seok (ching), and Choi Jong Sil (buk). Puschnig produced and co-mixed the album alongside engineer Hartmut Pfannmüller.

The twelve-track album interleaved longer collaborative pieces (co-written by Sharrock and Puschnig with traditional Korean material) with short solo percussion interludes that gave each SamulNori member a showcase — “The K’Kwaenggwari” (Lee Kwang Soo), “The Buk” (Choi Jong Sil), “The Changgo” (Kim Duk-Soo), “The Ching” (Kang Min Seok). The genre tags on Discogs are Folk/World/Country and Jazz, with styles Free Jazz and Folk.

Linda Sharrock designed the cover; photographer Wolfgang Plenk handled sleeve imagery. Executive producer was Wulf Müller. The album appeared on Amadeo — the Austrian jazz imprint of what became Universal Music — and saw at least seven pressing variants.

Then Comes The White Tiger (1994, ECM Records)

The band’s best-known release and their only ECM Records document. Co-credited to Red Sun / SamulNori, it was recorded in Germany and released in 1994 (cat. ECM 1510; also issued on BMG Classics in the US the following year). Wolfgang Puschnig produced again. Engineering was handled by Jan Erik Kongshaug at Rainbow Studio, Oslo — the ECM house engineer who had previously worked with Puschnig and Sharrock on … And She Answered: (1989).

The album expanded the ensemble: guitarist Rick Iannacone joined as an additional voice, and the SamulNori lineup included Kim Woon Tae (buk, ching, bara drum) and Kim Sung Woon (geomungo, gayageum) alongside returning core members Kim Duk-Soo (janggu, duduk/piri, hojok, ching) and Lee Kwang Soo (kkwaengwari, vocals, ching). Linda Sharrock designed the cover in addition to singing.

Hanullim was credited for liner photography.

South Korean Releases (1995–1997)

Following the ECM album the ensemble shifted its center of gravity eastward, releasing material on South Korean labels aimed at the Korean market.

Nanjang – A New Horizon (1995, King Record, South Korea) saw Kim Duk-Soo take a producer credit alongside Puschnig as co-producer. The recording introduced additional Korean instrumentalists: Kim Jae Young (piri/taepyungso/daegeum), Kim Jung Kuk (ching, bara), Lee Tae Baek (ajaeng, gayageum), and vocalists Ahn Sook Sun (tracks 1 and 9) and Kang Kwon Soon (tracks 4 and 5), with a rap appearance from Koo Joon Yub on track 2. Mixing was split between engineer Jamie Lane (tracks 2–9) and Lee Hoon Seok (track 1). The ensemble is again listed jointly as Red Sun · SamulNori.

From The Earth, To The Sky (1997, ENE Media, South Korea) credited the primary artists as Kim Duk Soo, Samulnori & Red Sun Group — the first time Kim Duk-Soo received individual billing ahead of the ensemble name. The cover painting was by artist Lee UFan. Puschnig and Kim Duk-Soo shared compositional and production credits. Rick Iannacone returned on electric guitar. The recording included additional Korean players not present on earlier albums.

風花 (Punghwa) (1997, Samsung Music / Sound Space (3), South Korea) was credited as Lee Kwang Soo & Red Sun — Lee Kwang Soo, the SamulNori kkwaengwari player, now took individual billing. The lineup stripped down relative to earlier albums: Tacuma (bass), Puschnig (saxophone), Sharrock (vocals), plus Lee Kwang Soo (kkwaengwari), Park Byung Won (janggu), Kim Suk Chul (taepyungso), and saxophonist 이정식. Uli Scherer does not appear in the credits for this release.
