Josef K

Post-punk band, Edinburgh, Scotland. Active 1979–1982. Discogs artist ID: 155979.

Not to be confused with Josef K. Band (Swedish band, Discogs ID: 2083024).

Overview

Josef K were one of the defining Scottish post-punk acts of the early 1980s. Formed in Edinburgh in 1979 — originally as TV Art — by Paul Haig (vocals, guitar) and Ronnie Torrance (drums), later joined by Malcolm Ross (guitar, keyboards) and David Weddell (bass). An earlier bass player, Gary McCormack, left to join The Exploited; Weddell replaced him. The band name came from the protagonist of Franz Kafka’s The Trial, chosen hurriedly when they were asked at the last minute to open for Adam and the Ants at Clouds Ballroom, Edinburgh, on 20 July 1979.

Their first release was a single on Absolute Records (5) — the label run by Orange Juice drummer Steven Daly — in December 1979. They then signed to Postcard Records, the Glasgow independent run by Alan Horne and Daly, releasing a run of critically acclaimed singles through 1980–81 alongside labelmates Orange Juice (3) and Aztec Camera.

Musically Josef K fused post-punk guitar attack with funk and disco rhythms, placing them in the same orbit as Orange Juice, but with a markedly more austere and anxious character. Lyrical references drew heavily on Kafka, Camus, Dostoyevsky, Hesse, and Knut Hamsun; musical touchstones included Pere Ubu, Television, Talking Heads, The Voidoids, Subway Sect, and Joy Division. The band adopted an explicit “anti-rock stance” — no encores, no stage banter, no autographs.

Their sole album The Only Fun In Town (1981) was recorded in Belgium after a rejected first attempt. The shelved first recording — titled Sorry For Laughing — was later released in reissue. The band played their last show on 23 August 1981 and dissolved shortly after; Haig felt they had already peaked. Their final release was The Farewell Single (Les Disques du Crépuscule, 1982).

Per Wikipedia (Josef K (band)), they were described in 2006 as “one of the most influential bands in Britain.” Later admirers include Franz Ferdinand, The Futureheads, The Rapture, The Wedding Present, and King Krule. “Sorry for Laughing” was covered by Propaganda (1985) and Nouvelle Vague (2004).
