Sublime (2)

Discogs ID: 98600 Active: 1988–1996 Location: Long Beach, California, USA Genres: Ska-Punk, Reggae Rock, Alternative Rock Members: Brad Nowell · Eric Wilson · Bud Gaugh Primary Labels: Skunk Records (2) · Gasoline Alley · MCA Records

History

Formation and Early Years (1988–1991)

Sublime formed in Long Beach, California in 1988, built around vocalist/guitarist Brad Nowell (born Bradley James Nowell, February 22, 1968), bassist Eric Wilson, and drummer Bud Gaugh. The band’s sound drew from ska, reggae, dub, hardcore punk, folk, and hip-hop — a multi-genre stew that reflected the eclectic tastes of the Long Beach scene and Nowell’s own record collection.

In 1990, Nowell and Marshall Goodman (a.k.a. Marshall “Field Marshall” Arts) co-founded Skunk Records (2), a Long Beach independent label that became the vehicle for Sublime’s first recordings and releases. Skunk also provided the band artistic control they couldn’t have gotten at a major. The label’s early catalog included collaborations with Michael -Miguel- Happoldt (a.k.a. D.J. Spock), who became a key extended member of the Sublime circle and production collaborator.

Early singles on Skunk — “Date Rape” (1991) and “Jah Won’t Pay The Bills” (1991) — circulated in the Southern California underground and built a grassroots following on the touring circuit.

Skunk Records Era (1992–1994)

40oz. To Freedom (1992, Skunk Records) — Sublime’s debut album — was self-released on Skunk and became a regional word-of-mouth hit driven by constant touring. The album’s ensemble cast stretched well beyond the core trio: Michael -Miguel- Happoldt contributed sampler, guitar, piano, organ, and electronics; Marshall Goodman played drums and turntables; Todd Foreman played saxophone; Nick Martin (2) played trombone; Chris Hauser (2) played trumpet; Jack Maness sang and played guitar; Adam Bausch (a.k.a. Adam “Short”) sang and played congas. The album was produced by Sublime themselves, with the original pressing crediting “Elephant Levitation” as producer and listing band members under their stage aliases (Nowell as Texas, Wilson as Cricket). The album was remastered by Brian Gardner (“Big Bass Brian”) for later pressings.

Robbin’ The Hood (1994, Skunk Records) — the second album — was recorded in more lo-fi fashion, largely at Nowell’s house. Core trio credits list Eric Wilson (bass), Bud Gaugh (drums, credited as “Bud (Floyd I)”), and Brad Nowell (vocals, guitar). The lo-fi approach was a deliberate aesthetic choice, influenced by cassette-culture underground rap and DIY punk ethics.

The band also released EPs and singles on Skunk throughout this period: “Chiva Kenevil” (1993), “13 Fl Oz.” (1994), and “Badfish” (1995). A split single, “Work That We Do” (1994), appeared on Insta-Noise Records and Skunk.

Major Label Signing and the Self-Titled Album (1995–1996)

Sublime signed to Gasoline Alley (a sublabel of MCA Records) and began recording their major-label debut. The album was produced by two separate producers working on different track groups: Paul Leary (guitarist of the Butthole Surfers) took the majority of the album, and David Kahne handled four tracks. Engineers were Stuart Sullivan (Leary tracks) and Eddie Ashworth (Kahne tracks). The album was mastered by Brian Gardner.

Brad Nowell died of a heroin overdose in San Francisco on May 25, 1996, two months before the album’s release. He was 28 years old.

Posthumous Success (1996–present)

Sublime (1996) was released on July 30, 1996, becoming one of the most commercially successful posthumous albums in rock history. It eventually sold over five million copies in the US alone. Singles “What I Got,” “Santeria,” “Wrong Way,” and “Doin’ Time” all received significant radio play.

Skunk Records was purchased by Geffen Records parent Universal on September 16, 2009.

In 1997, surviving members Eric Wilson and Bud Gaugh formed Long Beach Dub Allstars (Discogs ID: 719179) with Marshall Goodman, Michael -Miguel- Happoldt, Opie Gibran Ortiz, Jack Maness, and others from the Long Beach scene. Long Beach Dub Allstars released several albums on Skunk and DreamWorks.

In 2009, Wilson and Gaugh attempted to reform Sublime with Rome Ramirez as vocalist/guitarist, but were blocked by a legal dispute and renamed the project Sublime With Rome.

In 2023, Jakob Nowell — Brad Nowell’s son — joined the live band, and the group announced a return to using the Sublime name.
