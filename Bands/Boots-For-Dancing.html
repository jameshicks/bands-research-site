Overview

Boots for Dancing were a five-piece post-punk funk band from Edinburgh, Scotland, active from 1979 to 1982 and briefly reformed in 2015. Led by Dave Carson, the band also featured Dickie Fusco, Douglas Barrie, Gavin Fraser, and Graeme High. Their original run yielded two releases through the Scottish independent labels Pop Aural and Elpeco/Repop-x; their 2015 return produced a further release on Athens Of The North, the Edinburgh-based archival reissue label. Bob Last, a central figure in Scottish post-punk as manager and producer, is their most significant outside collaborator with two shared releases.

Notes (web)

What prompted the 2015 reunion

The 2015 return was initiated by Athens Of The North label operator Euan Fryer, not by the band members themselves. Fryer spent roughly two years assembling archival recordings and images “from all over the city (and some from Spain)” — including BBC Peel Sessions material — to compile what became The Undisco Kidds. In a 2015 interview with The Vinyl Factory, Fryer described the project as “embodying everything he loves about music: energy, youth and the charming perfection of imperfect music being more than the sum of its parts,” and said he felt the recordings “would have made them stars.” The Bandcamp release credits “extra special thanks to Euan Fryer for his drive and enthusiasm in realising this project,” corroborating that he was the primary catalyst.

The archival release then sparked live activity. Neil Cooper’s sleeve-notes piece (Substack) records that “a reignited nouveau Boots would soon see Dave stepping out once more” with a reconstituted line-up including guitarists Michael Barclay and Gavin Fraser, bassist Colin Whitson, drummer Russell Burn, and backing vocalist Tonia Todd — a partially new roster around original frontman Dave Carson. The band subsequently played Edinburgh venues and festival appearances including Newport Sound.

No band-member statement giving a personal reason for agreeing to the reunion has surfaced in press coverage; the available record points to Fryer’s archival initiative as what brought the band back into public view, with live shows following from there.
