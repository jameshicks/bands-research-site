Overview

White Rose Transmission is a Dutch indie/post-punk project founded as a collaboration between Carlo van Putten — vocalist of The Convent and Dead Guitars — and Adrian Borland, frontman of The Sound. Their two albums in the mid-to-late 1990s drew in prominent guest contributors including Marty Wilson Piper (The Church), Mark Burgess (The Chameleons), and Amanda Palmer (Dresden Dolls). Borland’s sudden death in 1999 halted the project; van Putten resumed it in 2006 with Rob Keijzer, and Frank Weyzig (ex-Clan of Xymox, Born for Bliss) joined for the fourth album. The catalogue runs from 1995 through a 2024 release, primarily through Red Sun Records and Sireena Records.

Notes (web)

How Willson-Piper, Burgess, and Palmer came to contribute

Source: Carlo van Putten, interviewed by Didier Becu, Peek-A-Boo Magazine, 7 June 2011 — http://www.peek-a-boo-magazine.be/en/interviews/white-rose-transmission/ (accessed via agent-browser 2026-10-23)

Van Putten described the connections in his own words:

“Mark and I became close friends before he knew that I was involved in music. Marty and I became friends when I owned Red Sun Records and he wanted to bring a solo album out. Our telephone conversations were that long, because we had something in common, that I choose to fly him over, because that was cheaper. We became friends as well as Marty met Mark Burgess and Mark Burgess met Adrian Borland in my house.”

Mark Burgess: The friendship pre-dates van Putten’s music career being known to Burgess — they became close friends first, through personal rather than industry channels. No further detail is given in this interview about when or where they met.

Marty Willson-Piper: The connection formed through Red Sun Records, which van Putten owned. Willson-Piper approached the label wanting to release a solo album; their telephone conversations ran so long (shared sensibilities were evident immediately) that van Putten decided it was cheaper to fly him to the Netherlands than continue the calls. They became close friends. Willson-Piper’s visit to van Putten’s house is also the occasion on which he met Mark Burgess — and where Burgess in turn met Adrian Borland.

Mark Burgess on 700 Miles Of Desert (1999): Discogs credits Burgess for Bass and Voice on that album. The 2011 interview also notes: “Mark Burgess helped us out in the studio and we recorded the album in Bremen.” This studio was the same facility used for the first two WRT albums. Burgess’s contribution to Bewitched and Bewildered sessions is not mentioned in the interview; his bass/voice credit is the established Discogs fact for the 1999 album.

Marty Willson-Piper on Bewitched and Bewildered (2006): Performed on the bonus tracks “Martha’s Harbour” and “Circle.” This is consistent with the existing Discogs credit and is confirmed by the MWP discography page at https://martywillson-piper.com/discography/.

Amanda Palmer: The 2011 Peek-A-Boo interview does not mention Palmer at all. The English Wikipedia article on White Rose Transmission states verbatim: “Van Putten went on tour with Amanda Palmer of The Dresden Dolls in 2000 supporting The Chameleons UK” — but offers no background on how this came about. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rose_Transmission (as of 2026-10-24)

No web source found explains the mechanism — whether Palmer was booked as an unknown support act, approached by van Putten, recommended by Burgess, or already known to either party. Palmer’s own Wikipedia article, her blog (amandapalmer.net/blog), her 2014 autobiography The Art of Asking, and multiple interviews checked (Louder Than War, Chronogram, Devolution Magazine, bighassle.com) make no mention of this tour, van Putten, White Rose Transmission, or The Chameleons. The earliest career point any of these sources anchor is her meeting Brian Viglione at a Halloween party in October 2000, with no detail about activity earlier that year.

Conflict — year and band name of the tour: The German Wikipedia article on Carlo van Putten (https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_van_Putten, as of 2026-10-24) states that The Convent (not White Rose Transmission) supported The Chameleons on a tour, with the last performance in October 2002 in Bochum. This raises two possibilities: (a) the 2000 tour described in the English WRT Wikipedia article and the 2002 Convent/Chameleons tour are two separate events — van Putten was active with both projects simultaneously and may have supported The Chameleons under different band names at different times; or (b) the “2000” date in the English WRT article is an error, and the actual tour was 2002 with The Convent. The English WRT article does not cite a source for the “2000” date. Amanda Palmer cannot have toured with van Putten supporting The Chameleons in October 2002 under a Convent billing — her Dresden Dolls career was already underway by then.

How van Putten and Borland met — origin of the collaboration

The connection between Carlo Van Putten and Adrian Borland originated through The Convent covering The Sound’s song “Winning.” The 2011 Peek-A-Boo interview has van Putten describe it directly: “Adrian and I met after Adrian heard a cover version by The Convent of ‘Winning’. I had him on the phone saying that he loved the version, very Joy Divisionish. We met later in Groningen.” Borland was sufficiently taken with the band that he sang the song himself, both as a duet with van Putten and as sole vocalist. He effectively became a recurring special guest of The Convent. One confirmed live appearance together was at the Kulturzentrum Kleinbahnhof (KUZ) in Osterholz-Scharmbeck in 1994. The cover version with Borland appeared on The Convent’s 1998 live album For a Fistful of Deutschmark.

The two became close friends — Borland frequently visited van Putten, who lived in Vollersode in northern Germany. White Rose Transmission emerged directly from that friendship in 1995. The band name combined the story of Hans and Sophie Scholl’s German resistance organisation Die Weiße Rose with “Transmission,” a word Borland proposed (“We keep transmitting; hope you are receiving”).

There is no evidence from any source that van Putten and Borland knew each other before The Convent’s “Winning” cover inquiry. The connection was initiated by The Convent approaching Borland, not by a pre-existing mutual scene network.
