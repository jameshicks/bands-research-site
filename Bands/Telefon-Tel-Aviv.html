Telefon Tel Aviv

Electronic duo formed 1999 in New Orleans, Louisiana; signed to Chicago’s Hefty Records. Discogs artist ID: 19150

Members: Charles Cooper · Joshua Eustis Genres: Electronic / IDM / Ambient / Glitch / Downtempo Years active: 1999–2009 (duo); 2019–present (Eustis solo revival) Labels: Hefty Records · BPitch Control · Ghostly International Name variations: TT, Tefeon Tel Aviv, Telefon Aviv, Telephone Tel Aviv

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1999–2001)

Telefon Tel Aviv was formed in New Orleans in 1999 by Charles Wesley Cooper III (born April 12, 1977) and Joshua Leeds Eustis (born 1977, New Orleans). Both were multi-instrumentalists working in electronic production. They built their early sound around dense, melodic synthesis, glitch processing, and atmospheric textures — a style that sat at the intersection of IDM, ambient, and leftfield electronic music.

The duo relocated to Chicago, where they connected with John Hughes III’s Hefty Records, a label closely aligned with the post-IDM and art-electronic scenes of the early 2000s. They also scored the 2001 film New Port South during this period.

Hefty Period: Fahrenheit Fair Enough (2001)

Their debut album Fahrenheit Fair Enough was released in autumn 2001 on Hefty Records (HEFTY 35, HDCD). Mastered by Roger Seibel. The album established the duo’s signature: long-form tracks with slow-building melodic arcs, chopped rhythms, and careful attention to space and texture. Styles tagged on Discogs: Ambient, Experimental, IDM, Leftfield.

The following year, Hefty issued the Immediate Action #8 12” EP (HEFTY 41, 2002) — part of Hefty’s ongoing Immediate Action series of limited vinyl EPs. It included the new track “Sound In A Dark Room” (featuring vocalist Lindsay Anderson), a Slicker remix of “TTV,” and a Prefuse 73 remix of “Fahrenheit Fair Enough.”

Map Of What Is Effortless (2004)

The second album, Map Of What Is Effortless, appeared in January 2004 on Hefty Records (HEFTY 042; 2xLP). It marked a significant expansion: the duo brought in the Loyola University Chamber Orchestra, arranged and conducted by Eustis, recording strings across five tracks. Vocalists Damon Aaron and Lindsay Anderson contributed to multiple tracks. The record shifted toward a more organic, orchestral palette while retaining glitch and abstract electronic processing. Styles: Abstract, Ambient, Downtempo, Glitch.

A 12” single, My Week Beats Your Year (HEFTYWHITE004), followed on Hefty’s white-label series.

Cooper and Eustis also worked as producers and engineers for other artists during this period, producing Jem’s releases on Peacefrog Records (2005).

Move to BPitch Control and Immolate Yourself (2009)

The third album, Immolate Yourself, was released in January 2009 on Berlin’s BPitch Control (BPC 188 CD). The record was darker and more synth-pop influenced than its predecessors — styles: Electro, Leftfield, Synth-pop. Turk Dietrich contributed production on one track (“Mostly Translucent”). Ryan Rapsys played drums and Rolan Vega contributed electronics on the closing title track.

Charles Cooper died on January 22, 2009, shortly after the album’s release. His death effectively ended Telefon Tel Aviv as a duo. The album’s themes — self-destruction, transformation — acquired an unavoidable retrospective weight.

BPitch Control issued two remix records: You Are The Worst Thing In The World (Remixes) (2009) and Immolate Yourself Remixes (2010, featuring remixes by Tim Xavier / Collin Jordan among others).

Dissolution and the Cooper Years in Retrospect

Cooper’s death at 31 closed the original chapter. His Discogs credits are almost entirely tied to Telefon Tel Aviv; his only other listed group is Dropd. He is credited as Charles Wesley Cooper III in the Discogs database.

Eustis Solo Revival: Dreams Are Not Enough (2019)

Joshua Eustis revived the Telefon Tel Aviv name as a solo project. Dreams Are Not Enough was released in 2019 on Ghostly International (GI-343), ten years after Cooper’s death. The record returned to ambient/IDM territory — styles: Ambient, Experimental, IDM. It was credited as a sole Eustis production; no additional performers are listed in the Discogs credits. The album was pressed on CD and issued digitally (FLAC); a Japanese edition came out on the Plancha imprint.

Eustis operates the alias Sons of Magdalene for some solo work.
