System Of A Down

Armenian-American heavy metal band from Glendale, California. Discogs artist ID: 85889. Also abbreviated SOAD. Formerly recorded under the name Soil (17) (alias entry). Formed February 1995.

Overview

System Of A Down emerged from the Armenian-American community in the greater Los Angeles area, drawing heavily on political themes including the Armenian Genocide alongside nu-metal and alternative metal sonics. Their rise coincided with the late 1990s American Recordings rock roster built by Rick Rubin. Five studio albums across 1998–2005, three of which debuted at number one on the Billboard 200. The band went on hiatus in August 2006 and reformed in November 2010. In 2020 they returned with new material for the first time in 15 years, releasing the double A-side Protect The Land / Genocidal Humanoidz in response to the Azerbaijani offensive on Nagorno-Karabakh.

Lineup note: Andy Khachaturian was the original drummer before John Dolmayan replaced him circa 1997. Khachaturian went on to The Apex Theory, which shared a scene with SOAD and appeared on the same NU-Metal compilations.

Early History (1994–1997)

Formed as Soil (17) in February 1995, renaming to System Of A Down shortly after. The early lineup featured Andy Khachaturian on drums. Three pre-album demos circulated: Sugar / Suitepee / Dam / P.L.U.C.K. (1995, self-released on System Management), Honey / Temper / Soil (1996, Systema label, produced by Alex Newport) and Know / War / Peephole / Untitled (1996, Velvet Hammer Management). Manager David Benveniste of Velvet Hammer Music And Management Group was involved from the earliest period.

A 1997 demo titled System Of A Down circulated on Universal before the band was signed. They were picked up by Rick Rubin’s American Recordings imprint, distributed through Columbia.

Steal This Album! (2002)

Compiled from studio outtakes and B-sides; conceived as a response to bootlegging of unreleased material. Produced by Rick Rubin and Daron Malakian. Mixed by Andy Wallace. All band credits listed as uncredited (a deliberate statement). Recorded by David Schiffman and Thom Russo. Mastered by Vlado Meller (uncredited).

Mezmerize (2005)

First of two simultaneous concept albums released six months apart. #1 Billboard 200. Produced by Rick Rubin and Daron Malakian. Daron Malakian sings lead on more tracks than on previous records. Artwork by Vartan Malakian (Daron’s father). String arrangements by Marc Mann and Serj Tankian.

Hiatus and Reformation

The band announced an indefinite hiatus in August 2006 following the Hypnotize world tour. Members pursued solo projects throughout the hiatus period. They reunited publicly in November 2010 and embarked on an extensive touring cycle 2011–2013. No new studio album followed the reunion until 2020, when Protect The Land / Genocidal Humanoidz was released — motivated specifically by the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War and framed as advocacy for Armenian territorial integrity.

Grammy / Awards Notes

Nominated for four Grammy Awards. “B.Y.O.B.” won Best Hard Rock Performance at the 2006 Grammy Awards. “Aerials” and “Hypnotize” both reached #1 on Billboard’s Alternative Songs chart.
