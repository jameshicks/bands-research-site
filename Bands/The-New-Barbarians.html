The New Barbarians

Short-lived English rock supergroup assembled in 1979 by Ron Wood and Keith Richards, both of The Rolling Stones. Discogs artist ID: 1709529. Also known as “Keith Richards & The New Barbarians,” “Ron Wood & The New Barbarians,” and occasionally “The Barbarians” or “The Vegetarians.” The band name was reportedly coined by Neil Young. Managed by Jason Cooper (2).

Overview

The New Barbarians formed in early 1979, partly to fulfill legal obligations surrounding Ron Wood’s parole conditions after a drug arrest — touring under his own name was a condition of his sentence. The band was assembled to support Wood’s third solo album Gimme Some Neck (Columbia, 1979), produced by Roy Thomas Baker. Richards joined as a co-leader, bringing with him Stones touring associate Bobby Keys on saxophone. The rest of the lineup drew from unlikely sources: Stanley Clarke, the jazz-fusion bassist of Return To Forever, on bass; Joseph Modeliste (known as “Zigaboo”), drummer of The Meters, on drums; and Ian McLagan of Faces (3) and Small Faces on keyboards.

The band undertook a North American tour in spring 1979, playing roughly 40 dates. One of the most celebrated shows was a benefit concert for the Canadian National Institute for the Blind (C.N.I.B.) in Toronto, widely known as the “Blind Date” show. They also performed at the Knebworth Festival in the UK in 1979. The tour set lists blended Wood’s solo material (“Mystifies Me,” “Infekshun,” “I Can Feel The Fire,” “Lost & Lonely”) with rock and R&B covers (“Sweet Little Rock N Roller,” “Rock Me Baby,” “Love In Vain,” “Worried Life Blues,” “Jumping Jack Flash,” “Honky Tonk Women”) and some Richards-sung material (“Before They Make Me Run,” “Seven Days”).

The group dissolved after the tour ended with no studio album produced. Live recordings circulated on bootleg for years before legitimate releases began appearing in 2006 and 2016. The combination of a jazz-funk rhythm section (Clarke and Modeliste) with the Stones’ blues-rock axis made for an unusually eclectic live sound.

Connections

  • The Rolling Stones — Richards and Wood’s primary band; Keys a long-running touring member
  • Faces (3) — McLagan’s former band; Wood was also a Face before joining the Stones
  • Small Faces — McLagan’s original group with Steve Marriott, Ronnie Lane, Kenny Jones
  • The Meters — source of Modeliste; New Orleans funk legends
  • Return To Forever — Clarke’s main jazz-fusion vehicle with Chick Corea
  • Neil Young — reportedly named the band
  • The First Barbarians — related Richards/Wood project name used for other recordings
