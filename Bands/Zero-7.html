Overview

Zero 7 is an English electronic duo formed in 1997 in London by Henry Binns and Sam Hardaker. The two met while studying sound engineering and began their careers in the 1990s at Mickie Most’s RAK Studios, engineering sessions for artists including the Pet Shop Boys, Young Disciples, and Robert Plant. The name Zero 7 originated from a bar called Cero Siete on the Honduran island Utila; it was first used publicly on their 1997 remix of Radiohead’s “Climbing Up the Walls”. Binns also contributed additional programming to the Radiohead B-side “Meeting in the Aisle” and received a credit for additional sampling on Kid A. (Source: Wikipedia)

Their debut album Simple Things (2001) made them a leading name in UK downtempo and trip-hop, peaking at number 28 on the UK albums chart, going platinum, earning a Mercury Prize nomination, and winning the Best Newcomer Muzik Award. When It Falls (2004) peaked at number 3 in the UK, their highest chart position. The Garden (2006) received a Grammy nomination for Best Electronic/Dance Album. Yeah Ghost (2009) took a markedly more experimental and instrumental direction, incorporating jazz, folk, and brass. Following a long hiatus the duo released In The Half Light (2024), a collaboration with California artist Swim Surreal, effectively their fifth studio album.

A consistent feature of Zero 7’s work is a rotating cast of guest vocalists. Sia (Sia Furler) and Sophie Barker were the primary vocalists on Simple Things. Mozez (Osmond Wright) also appeared on the debut. Tina Dickow (credited as Tina Dico) joined on When It Falls. José González sang on The Garden and the 2019 single “Aurora”. Eska Mtungwazi and Martha Tilston contributed to Yeah Ghost. Jem Cooke appeared on the 2019 single “Swimmers”.

Aside from Zero 7, Binns and Hardaker also collaborated as Ingrid Eto (instrumental project, 2007) and released a 2009 instrumental EP as Kling. In 2016 Binns formed Equador (2) with Bo Bruce and Jodi Milliner.

Simple Things was produced entirely by Zero 7; released in the UK on Ultimate Dilemma, in the US on Palm Pictures / Quango Records. Phil Thornalley co-produced on at least four tracks. Dan Litman, Miguel Barradas, and other session players also credited. Strings arranged by Sally Herbert.

When It Falls (2004)

Produced by Zero 7. Credits show Blue Source for artwork/design; management by Carol Crabtree and Solar. Vocalists: Sia, Sophie Barker, Mozez, Tina Dico. Session players include Neil Cowley (keyboards), Jeremy Stacey (drums), Robin Mullarkey (bass), Crispin Taylor (percussion), Sally Herbert (strings). Mastered by Kevin Metcalfe.

The Garden (2006)

Vocals by Sia, José González, and Henry Binns. Mixed by Phill Brown (additional recording) and Mike “Spike” Drake. Mastered by John Dent. Session players: Eddie Stevens (electronics/keys), Tom Skinner (drums), Robin Mullarkey (bass), Darren Wiles, Richard Woodcraft, Ferg Peterkin. Strings by Sally Herbert. Mastered by Kevin Metcalfe.

Yeah Ghost (2009)

Produced by Zero 7. Vocalists: Eska Mtungwazi (tracks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10), Martha Tilston (track 5), Jackie Daniels (track 7), Rowdy Superstar (track 8). Henry Binns sang on track 4. Session players: Tom Skinner (drums), Robin Mullarkey (bass/guitar/steel drums), Eddie Stevens (electronics/keyboards/marimba), Finn Peters (flute/saxophone), Sid Gauld (flugelhorn/trumpet), Trevor Mires (trombone/euphonium). Mixed by Phill Brown and Mike “Spike” Drake. Mastered by Kevin Metcalfe.

Connections

  • Nigel Godrich — credited on When It Falls and The Garden; primarily a Radiohead-associated producer/engineer
  • Phill Brown — mixing engineer on The Garden, Yeah Ghost, and several singles; best known for his work on Talk Talk’s late albums

  • Tina Dickow — Danish singer-songwriter, vocals on When It Falls; own career on Disco Gecko and others
