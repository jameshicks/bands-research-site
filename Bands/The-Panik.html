The Panik

Discogs ID: 1249314

Manchester punk band, active 1977. One of the earliest nodes in the network that produced Joy Division: drummer Steve Brotherdale was simultaneously playing in Warsaw (3) (the precursor to Joy Division), and the band’s single was co-produced by Rob Gretton — who would go on to manage Joy Division and New Order. According to Discogs: “Ian Curtis almost joined [The Panik]. They had in their ranks Steve Brotherdale who had drummed with Warsaw.”

”It Won’t Sell!” (1977)

Three tracks: “Modern Politics” / “Urban Damnation” / “Murder”. Side labels marked “X” and “XX”. Produced by Rob Gretton and The Panik. Sleeve design by Steve McGarry. Back cover photograph by Kevin Cummins — listed in the credits as “Expensive back photo.” Lacquer cut by John Martin (13); plated by Eddy Gorecki.

After The Panik

Eric Random had the most substantial post-Panik trajectory. He released a solo LP, That’s What I Like About Me (1980), on New Hormones (ORG 6), produced alongside Stephen Mallinder of Cabaret Voltaire; sleeve by Richard Boon. He then fronted Eric Random And The Bedlamites (1981–1986), whose lineup included Graham Dowdall (alias “Dids,” also in Pere Ubu and Ludus), and was still releasing on Emergency Hearts as recently as 2022–23.

Ian Nance and Steve Brotherdale both went on to V2 (5) after The Panik. Nance also appeared in Obimen.

Rob Gretton went on to manage Joy Division and New Order, and co-founded Factory Records.

Notes (web)

Steve McGarry — identity confirmed (2026-08-05)

Steve McGarry the sleeve designer of “It Won’t Sell!” (1977) is the same person as Steve McGarry the British cartoonist (born 24 January 1953, Wythenshawe, Manchester). He was not a member of The Panik — his role was graphic design only.

In his own words (Manchester Digital Music Archive): “I knocked out the logo for this … that’s my customary scrawl, which we used on everything from Joy Division to Slaughter & The Dogs. I designed the sleeve for ‘It Won’t Sell’ by The Panik.” He sourced a photograph of “rentboys hustling on a New York street in the 1960s” from a magazine that Rob Gretton had found, and appropriated it for the sleeve. (Manchester Digital Music Archive — Steve McGarry artefacts)

McGarry was at the centre of the 1976–78 Manchester punk scene as a sleeve/logo designer, working for Joy Division, Slaughter & The Dogs, Jilted John, Ed Banger & The Nosebleeds, John Cooper Clarke, and The Panik. He also served as in-house designer for venues The Oaks and Rafters. He fronted his own band, Steve McGarry’s First Offence (not The Panik), whose members included future Primal Scream drummer Toby Toman and A Certain Ratio percussionist Donald Johnson. (downthetubes.net, 12 Apr 2016)

The Joy Division “An Ideal for Living” 12” sleeve (1978) — also designed by McGarry — was later acquired for the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art, New York. (MoMA collection; stevemcgarry.com)

McGarry and Rob Gretton were childhood schoolmates at St. Bede’s (photograph dated April 1965 in the MDMA archive), which explains Gretton’s connection to McGarry for both The Panik sleeve and the Joy Division commission. (MDMA artefact 6128)
