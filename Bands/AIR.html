AIR

Sep 27, 202613 min read

AIR

Discogs ID: 2770 Full name: Amour, Imagination, Rêve (though the band has given conflicting statements about whether it is an acronym) Members: Jean-Benoît Dunckel · Nicolas Godin Active: 1995–present (as of 2025 still releasing under AIR) Origin: Versailles, France Genres: French Touch, Space Pop, Electronic, Ambient, Downtempo Labels: Source, Astralwerks, Virgin, EMI, Parlophone Aliases (early): Rainbow Brothers · The Flower Pistols

AIR is a French electronic duo composed of Jean-Benoît Dunckel and Nicolas Godin, active since 1995. Their 1997 debut Moon Safari became one of the defining records of the French Touch era and introduced the world to their signature blend of vintage synthesizers, Fender Rhodes electric piano, Moog bass, vocoders, and lush orchestrated strings. Over five studio albums spanning 1997–2012, they moved from Versailles-studio experimentation to Hollywood soundtracks to silent-film accompaniment, maintaining a distinct identity rooted in dreamy retro-futurism and meticulous analog craft.

Biography

Formation and Early Work (1995–1997)

Dunckel and Godin met at school in Versailles and began making music together in the mid-1990s under early project names including Rainbow Brothers and The Flower Pistols (both listed as Discogs aliases). Their first released recordings appeared under the AIR name on the 1995 EP Modulor Mix (Source Lab), which established their palette: Fender Rhodes, Minimoog, Solina String Ensemble, and vocoder — the instruments that would define Moon Safari two years later. The 1996 single Casanova 70 followed, with Nicolas Godin handling the bulk of the instrumentation and Etienne de Crécy (Discogs ID: 409899) producing and mixing the lead track “Modulor Mix.”

The 1997 collection Premiers Symptômes (Source, cat. 724389428766) — released as a 12-inch EP in Europe but later packaged as a CD compilation — gathers these early tracks and serves as a document of the duo’s pre-Moon Safari aesthetic. Track-level credits on the EP reveal the depth of their instrument fluency: Godin plays bass, electric guitar, Fender Rhodes, Minimoog, sitar, talkbox, and Solina; Dunckel plays Fender Rhodes, Wurlitzer, Korg MS-20, Clavinet, and choir organs. Tuba player Patrick Woodcock and organist Eric Regert appear on multiple tracks. The EP was produced by AIR, with some tracks co-produced and mixed by Etienne de Crécy.

Moon Safari and French Touch Breakthrough (1997–1999)

Moon Safari (Source, cat. VISA 4212; 1997) was recorded by Dunckel, Godin, and engineer Peter Cobbin in Paris, mixed by Jérôme Blondel and Stéphane “Alf” Briat, and mastered by Nilesh Patel. Its most distinctive production element — the sweeping string and orchestral arrangements — was contributed by British arranger and conductor David Whitaker, credited as “Arranged By, Directed By.” Whitaker’s involvement placed the record in direct conversation with 1960s orchestral pop and easy-listening traditions.

The album’s ten tracks include the vocoder showcase “La Femme D’Argent,” the chugging groove of “Sexy Boy,” and two vocal features from American singer Beth Hirsch (“All I Need,” “You Make It Easy”). “Remember” incorporates a sample with additional writing credited to French electronic pioneer Jean-Jacques Perrey. The album reached No. 6 in the UK and sold over a million copies worldwide, making it one of the best-selling French albums of the decade.

Companion singles were released via Source and Virgin: Sexy Boy (1997), All I Need (1998), and Kelly Watch The Stars (1998). The latter’s B-side featured a contribution from Jean-Jacques Perrey, tying the single explicitly to the Ondioline-era synthesis tradition AIR was drawing from.

Also in this period, AIR contributed a remix to the UK-released single Purple (Jive, 1997), demonstrating early cross-genre reach.

The Virgin Suicides Soundtrack (1999–2000)

Sofia Coppola’s film The Virgin Suicides (1999) commissioned AIR as composers, resulting in the album The Virgin Suicides (Source, cat. VISA 4914; 1999). Unlike Moon Safari, the score relies less on orchestration and more on ambient texture, melancholy drone, and minimal instrumentation. Opening track “Playground Love” features vocals from Gordon Tracks (Discogs ID: 40401) — a pseudonym for Thomas Mars of Phoenix, who is credited here as Gordon Tracks. The soundtrack added “Stage & Screen” to AIR’s genre profile and cemented their international visibility.

Brian Reitzell (Discogs ID: 260529) contributed as a musician and co-credit on the film score side (also credited on 10,000 Hz Legend), and is listed on this album’s detailed expanded pressings. Phoenix’s connection to AIR runs through this period — both acts were from Versailles and shared orbits in the Paris scene. The duo also released the standalone single Playground Love (Source, 1999).

The documentary film Eating Sleeping Waiting And Playing (Source/Virgin, 1999) chronicled the duo’s tour of this period.

In 2000, AIR contributed music to the Charlotte Gainsbourg album 5:55 — though that album was released in 2006, production credit dates partly from this period — and the same year delivered the score for the Lost in Translation soundtrack (in expanded pressings form, Discogs ID: 1433546, crediting AIR as producer in the 2008 Japanese pressing).

10,000 Hz Legend and Hollywood Collaborations (2001–2002)

10,000 Hz Legend (Astralwerks/Virgin, cat. ASW 10332-2; 2001) marked AIR’s most ambitious and eccentric studio album. Co-produced by Tony Hoffer, engineered by Brian Kehew and Bruce Keen, and mixed by Hoffer, it featured an extensive cast:

  • Beck Hansen (Discogs ID: 104321): harmonica and vocals on “The Vagabond,” vocals on “Don’t Be Light”
  • Roger Joseph Manning Jr. (Discogs ID: 8591): keyboards and vocals on multiple tracks
  • Justin Meldal-Johnsen (Discogs ID: 293885): bass and drum programming on “How Does It Make You Feel?”, “Radio #1,” “Don’t Be Light”
  • Brian Reitzell (Discogs ID: 260529): drums throughout
  • Jason Falkner (Discogs ID: 325726): vocals on “Radio #1,” “Lucky And Unhappy,” “People In The City”
  • Ken Andrews (Discogs ID: 130492): vocals on “Radio #1,” “Lucky And Unhappy,” “People In The City”
  • Lisa Papineau (Discogs ID: 172055): vocals on “Lucky And Unhappy”
  • Buffalo Daughter (Japanese noise-pop group): vocals on “Sex Born Poison”
  • Corky Hale (Discogs ID: 253346): harp on “Radian”
  • Strings, choir, and flutes arranged by AIR and Roger Neill (Discogs ID: 415234), who also conducted

The album was more experimental and song-structured than Moon Safari, incorporating prog-rock length, vocoder textures, and an unprecedented LA-session-musician roster. Accompanied single Radio #1 (Source/Virgin, 2001).

The EP Everybody Hertz (Source/Virgin, 2002) followed as a B-sides/remixes document.

Talkie Walkie and Return to Form (2003–2004)

Talkie Walkie (Source/Astralwerks, 2003) pulled back from the experimental excess of its predecessor toward the melodic sensibility of Moon Safari. Mastered by Bob Ludwig (Discogs ID: 271098) and engineered with Darrell Thorp (Discogs ID: 292581), the album contained no external vocal guests — it is the most self-contained of the AIR studio albums. “Alone In Kyoto” appeared in Sofia Coppola’s subsequent film Lost in Translation (2003), deepening the Coppola connection. Strings were arranged by Michel Colombier (Discogs ID: 52312). Key singles: Cherry Blossom Girl (2003) and Surfing On A Rocket (2004).

City Reading and Literary Detour (2003)

City Reading (Tre Storie Western) (Source/Record Makers, 2003) is a collaborative album with Italian author and intellectual Alessandro Baricco (Discogs ID: 444002), who narrates three spoken-word stories. Mixed by Nigel Godrich (Discogs ID: 169094) with assistant Florian Lagatta. A departure from AIR’s usual commercial-release format, it was issued on Record Makers (cat. REC-09) and Source jointly.

Charlotte Gainsbourg Contribution and Pocket Symphony (2006)

AIR wrote and performed the music for Charlotte Gainsbourg’s album 5:55 (Because Music, 2006) — though technically credited to Gainsbourg as primary artist, AIR are credited as “Composed By, Performer.” Lyricists on that project included Neil Hannon (Discogs ID: 271133) of The Divine Comedy and Jarvis Cocker (Discogs ID: 76828) of Pulp. This project is Discogs release ID 770049.

Their own Pocket Symphony (Source, 2006) employed arranger David Campbell (Discogs ID: 341947) and conductor Joby Talbot (Discogs ID: 271182), with Neil Hannon and Jarvis Cocker again contributing to certain tracks. Mastered by Bob Ludwig, engineered by Darrell Thorp, and recorded by Simon Hayes (Discogs ID: 686256) and Andrew Rugg (Discogs ID: 587349).

The compilation/mix album LateNightTales (LateNightTales, 2005) shows the duo’s listening taste: a selection mixing Scott Walker, The Cure, Elliott Smith, Robert Wyatt, Lee Hazlewood, Nino Rota, Minnie Riperton, Japan, and Sébastien Tellier, plus one exclusive AIR track (“Musica”) and a continuous mix by the duo.

Mer Du Japon, Once Upon A Time, and Live (2007–2008)

Two retrospective releases appeared in 2007: Mer Du Japon (Virgin, 2007), a single/EP, and Once Upon A Time (Aircheology/Virgin, 2007), an early-recordings retrospective. The concert document Time Machine (Live 2008) (EMI, 2008) was also released this year.

Love 2 and Later Period (2009–2012)

Love 2 (self-released/EMI, 2009) is AIR’s fifth studio album, credited informally as a “mashup” in promo form. Contributors include Joey Waronker (Discogs ID: 293885) and Louis Arlette (Discogs ID: 1586314) as engineer. Singles: Sing Sang Sung (2009), So Light Is Her Footfall (2009).

Le Voyage Dans La Lune (Aircheology/Revolvair/Virgin, 2012) provided a new score for the restored 1902 Georges Méliès film. Victoria Legrand (Discogs ID: 592309) of Beach House contributed vocals and lyrics to “Seven Stars.” Other vocal features included Au Revoir Simone (Discogs ID: 356550) on “Who Am I Now?“. Drummer Vincent Taeger played on several tracks. Brian Reitzell contributed to the “Who Am I Now?” arrangement. The album was mixed by Stéphane “Alf” Briat and mastered by Chab (Discogs ID: 391606).

Twentyears, More Hertz, and 25th Anniversary (2016–2024)

Twentyears (Parlophone/Aircheology, 2016) is a double-vinyl retrospective compilation. Mastering was handled by Alex Gopher (Discogs ID: 1090) — the same Alex Gopher who appears on early AIR recordings from 1995.

More Hertz (Parlophone, 2021) collects additional tracks and remixes. In 2023, Italian producer Federico Romanzi (Discogs ID: 304866) issued remix singles of “Ce Matin La” and “Bathroom Girl.” In 2024, UK producer Vegyn (Discogs ID: 4209810) produced Blue Moon Safari, a reworking of the debut album. The Moon Safari Rarities (25th Anniversary Edition) (Parlophone/Warner Music France, 2024) collected unreleased material from the debut album sessions.
