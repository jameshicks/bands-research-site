Overview

The Revillos were a Scottish New Wave and punk band who emerged in 1979 as a direct offshoot of The Rezillos, mixing punk energy with horror rock, rockabilly-tinged pop rock, and rock & roll. The core of their recorded output landed in the 1980s across nine releases, distributed primarily through Snatzo Recordi, Dindisc, and Virgin, before the band broke up in 1985. Despite that, the catalogue kept circulating — reissues and new material appeared on Damaged Goods and Beat Generation as late as 2019–2020, suggesting at least one later reformation. Their most persistent working relationships were with backing vocal group The Revettes (32 shared releases) and collaborator Tony Pilley (30), with production credits going to Mike Howlett and Steve Nye.

Notes (web)

Reunions after the 1985 breakup

The band broke up in 1985 and was inactive for nearly a decade. Two separate reunion phases followed.

1994 — Japan tour (first reunion). The Revillos reformed in 1994 for a run of dates in Japan, featuring Eugene Reynolds, Fay Fife (Rocky Rhythm and others), with Kid Krupa on guitar and Mekon on bass, and the The Revettes (Frankie and Polly) as backing vocalists. The Tokyo show from that run was recorded and released in 1995 as Live and On Fire in Japan on Vinyl Japan; the Yeah Yeah EP (Vinyl Japan, 1994) of re-recorded and new material accompanied it. What specifically prompted this initial reunion is not stated in any source examined. The band’s own biography page at Snatzo gives no reason; it simply records that they “reformed for some gigs in Japan.” revillos.snatzo.com

One plausible contextual factor: in 1993, former Revillos guitarist Trevor Sewell (performing as Max Atom) had released an unauthorised CD5, Love Bug, under the name The Revettes on Trident Music International. The release used a copy of the 24-track master of the original Bitten By A Love Bug single, replacing Fay Fife’s vocals and re-recording the instruments. The band’s own site describes this release as unauthorised. A music blogger (Post Punk Monk) speculates — but does not confirm with a band statement — that this opportunistic release may have motivated the actual Revillos to reconvene the following year. This remains unconfirmed; no interview or band statement has been located that names the Sewell release as the trigger. postpunkmonk.com, 2019-07-19 · postpunkmonk.com, 2019-07-23

1996 — UK tour (second reunion). Following the Japan live album’s release and the interest it generated, the band reformed again for a short UK tour in 1996, timed to coincide with the rarities compilation From The Freezer (Damaged Goods / Snatzo Recordi). This reunion is explicitly attributed to audience interest sparked by the Japanese release on the band’s own Snatzo biography page. revillos.snatzo.com

After the 1996 dates the band continued to release recorded material (compilations, unreleased 1980s recordings, EPs) through 1999–2003 without significant touring.
