The Tesco Bombers

Oct 19, 20266 min read

The Tesco Bombers

Discogs ID: 531543 Origin: London, UK Active: c. 1982 Genres: Post-Punk, Latin (Tango) Label: Y Records (Y14)

London post-punk project with a floating lineup drawn from the early-1980s squat and DIY scene. Existed long enough to record one single, released on Dick O’Dell’s Y Records in 1982. Self-described by Discogs as “London based post-punk project with a floating line-up of musicians.”

Members and Personnel

Five musicians are listed as core members on Discogs:

  • Jim Welton (Discogs ID: 2283274) — London-based musician and sound collagist; operated out of the punk squat scene under a large network of aliases including L. Voag, Amos (3), and Xentos. On the session he appears as Amos (3) (his Discogs-listed alias, ID: 282974). Founder of the micro-label It’s War Boys; associated with The Homosexuals, The Work, and Die Trip Computer Die.

  • Keith Allen (Discogs ID: 56831) — Welsh comedian, actor, and musician, based in London. By 1982 already known as one of the breakthrough acts from the Comedy Store (from 1979) and appearing in Channel 4’s The Comic Strip Presents… series. Also a member of Fat Les (alongside Damien Hirst and Blur’s Alex James) and co-writer of New Order’s “World In Motion” (their first UK number one). Father of Lily Allen.

  • Richard Dudanski (Discogs ID: 519954) — drummer, nicknamed “Snakehips Dudanski” by Joe Strummer when Dudanski joined The 101’ers. His post-punk résumé by 1982 included Public Image Limited (Metal Box, 1979–1980), Basement 5, and The Raincoats. Co-founder with (a782160) of what his Discogs profile calls an unnamed joint project; author of the book Squat City Rocks. Lives in Granada, Spain.

  • Neal Brown (Discogs ID: 928923) — London-based musician, artist, designer, and writer. Also a member of The Vincent Units alongside Inigo Batterham.

  • Inigo Batterham (Discogs ID: 6113568) — London-based saxophonist. Also a member of The Vincent Units alongside Neal Brown.

The full performer roster on the Hernando’s Hideaway session (as credited on Discogs release 704676) adds three further names: Chris Gray (2) (ID: 260648), Lance Hunter (ID: 618087), and Sara (21) (ID: 1077045). Sara (21) and Chris Gray (2) are recurring figures in Jim Welton’s wider network — both appear on It’s War Boys releases, and Sara (21) partnered with Welton on the Amos & Sara cassette series.

Photography for the sleeve is credited to Mike Laye (no Discogs ID in the release record).

Discography

The Tesco Bombers have a single release documented on Discogs.

Hernando’s Hideaway (1982)

Label: Y Records · Cat No.: Y14 · Format: 7” vinyl · Country: UK · Discogs ID: 704676 Genres: Latin, Rock · Styles: Post-Punk, Tango

The A-side is a tango cover of “Hernando’s Hideaway,” a song originally written by Richard Adler and Jerry Ross for the 1954 Broadway musical The Pajama Game. The B-side opens with “Break The Ice At Parties” — an original composition — then closes with an instrumental reading of Antônio Carlos Jobim’s “The Girl From Ipanema.” The juxtaposition of Broadway tango, original post-punk material, and a bossa nova instrumental gives the single a deliberately eclectic, almost comedic register consistent with both the DIY scene’s irreverence and Keith Allen’s comic sensibility.

Production is credited collectively to The Tesco Bombers. No external producer.

Context: The Squat / DIY Scene

The Tesco Bombers sit at the intersection of two overlapping London communities in the early 1980s.

The first is Jim Welton’s squat-music network: a loose DIY cluster that also produced The Homosexuals, the It’s War Boys label, and a sprawling ecosystem of one-off projects and aliases. This world operated outside the conventional music industry — releases were pressed in small quantities, distributed through independent channels, and typically self-produced.

The second is a more ad hoc creative circle around Keith Allen, who by 1982 was moving between London’s comedy, performance art, and post-punk social scenes. Allen’s presence suggests The Tesco Bombers were less a band in the conventional sense than a one-time gathering of people from overlapping milieus.

Richard Dudanski’s involvement connects the project to the Clash / PiL lineage — he had played on Public Image Limited’s Metal Box (1979) and with Basement 5 before this session.

The choice of Y Records as the outlet is notable: Dick O’Dell’s label was at the time home to The Pop Group, Maximum Joy, Pigbag, Shriekback, and Diamanda Galás, among others. Y14 slots the Tesco Bombers single between Maximum Joy’s White And Green Place 12” (Y15) and Pigbag’s Getting Up (Y16) — right in the middle of the label’s most active period.

The two Tesco Bombers members who appear on other Y Records releases are Jim Welton (via his Amos (3) alias and Sara (21), both connected to the Amos & Sara It’s War Boys releases) and The Vincent Units — Neal Brown and Inigo Batterham’s other project — who had released Carnival Song (Y8, 1980) on Y Records two years earlier.
