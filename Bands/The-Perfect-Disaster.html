The Perfect Disaster

Discogs ID: 300952
Origin: Bedfordshire / Hertfordshire, UK
Active: 1980–1991 (as Orange Disaster 1980–1983; as The Perfect Disaster 1984–1991)
Labels: Neuter Records (as Orange Disaster), Kampa / Disc’Az (France), Glass Records, Fire Records
Genres: Alternative Rock, Indie Rock, Shoegaze
Name variations / aliases: Perfect Disaster, The Pd., Pd

UK alternative rock band formed in Hertfordshire, England. The sole constant member throughout was singer/guitarist/saxophonist Phil Parfitt (Discogs ID: 657025), with the band functioning as a rotating collective around him across four distinct lineup eras. The band evolved from earlier projects Orange Disaster and The Architects of Disaster before settling on the final name in 1984. They released four studio albums between 1985 and 1990 before splitting in 1991. Their most commercially successful period was the Fire Records era (1988–1990), when the album Up (1989) reached number fifteen on the UK Indie Chart. Notable alumni include Josephine Wiggs (bass, cello, multi-instrumentalist, producer) and Jon Mattock (drums), both of whom went on to join The Breeders and other prominent projects. (Wikipedia; Discogs)

History

Origins — Orange Disaster and The Architects of Disaster (1980–1984)

The band’s earliest incarnation, Orange Disaster, was formed around 1980 in Bedfordshire/Hertfordshire by Phil Parfitt, joined by Ken Renny (bass), Rolande Box, and Alison Pate (guitar). This lineup released a seven-inch EP, “Something’s Got to Give,” on Neuter Records (catalogue number OD 01, dated 1 September 1980). A second version of the same single was issued on the French Vogue label in 1981. (Wikipedia)

After the Orange Disaster period, Parfitt and Pate recruited Tony Pettitt (bass), Nod Wright (Alexander Wright, drums), and Paul Wright (guitar) under the name The Architects of Disaster, releasing the single “Cucumber Sandwich” / “Friendly Fire” on Neuter Records (1982). Nod Wright and Tony Pettitt subsequently left to form Fields of the Nephilim. (Wikipedia)

Parfitt then brought in Grant Davidson (bass), later replaced by John Saltwell, alongside Dan Cross (guitar) and Malcolm Catto (drums). This lineup returned in 1984 as The Perfect Disaster.

Kampa / Disc’Az era (1985)

The debut album Perfect Disaster was recorded with the lineup of Parfitt, Dan Cross, Alison Pate, John Saltwell, and Malcolm Catto, and issued in France on the Kampa label (co-released on Disc’Az, France, 1985). Steve Lindsey and Noel Thompson produced. Parfitt and Cross were primary songwriters throughout, with most tracks co-written across the band. The French release included a cover of Lou Reed’s “Over You” (on the reissue track listing) — Reed appears in the Discogs collaborator graph for this release (Discogs ID: 11879). (Discogs)

Glass Records era (1987)

In 1987 the band signed to Glass Records (London), who reissued the debut album (GLALP 027) with a revised track order, adding “Over You,” and followed it with two twelve-inch EPs: Hey Hey Hey (GLAEP 107) and Bluebell (GLAEP 109). Discogs carries Bluebell (release ID 1534461) as a sparse Glass Records credit with no additional personnel listed. (Wikipedia; Discogs; Wikipedia — Glass Records article)

Glass Records was a key 1980s UK indie label whose roster also included Spacemen 3, The Pastels, The Jazz Butcher, and Walkingseeds — artists who similarly bridged psychedelic and post-punk currents and several of whom also moved to Fire Records. (Wikipedia — Glass Records)

Line-up changes accompanied the Glass period: Alison Pate and John Saltwell departed; multi-instrumentalist Josephine Wiggs joined.

Fire Records era — Asylum Road (1988)

Moving to Fire Records, the band released Asylum Road (1988, FIRE LP 13 / FIRE CD 13) as their first major-label indie statement. Produced by Noel Thompson and The Perfect Disaster (as “Pd”), it featured the core lineup of Parfitt, Dan Cross (electric and rhythm guitar), Alison Pate (rhythm guitar, backing vocals), Josephine Wiggs (bass, cello, double bass), and Martin Langshaw (percussion). Jessica Corcoran engineered. (Discogs)

Two singles preceded / accompanied the album: T.V. (Girl On Fire) (1988) and Time To Kill (1988), both on Fire Records.

Fire Records era — Up (1989)

Up (1989, FIRE LP 18) became the band’s highest-charting release, reaching number fifteen on the UK Indie Chart. The band also toured with The Jesus and Mary Chain during this period. (Wikipedia) Personnel shifted again: Malcolm Catto had departed, replaced by Phil Outram and Martin Langshaw sharing drumming duties (Langshaw on tracks 1, 7, 11; Outram on tracks 2, 6, 8, 9). Josephine Wiggs played bass, cello, double bass, piano, and backing vocals; Dan Cross handled lead guitar, organ, and piano. Songwriting was shared between Parfitt (lyrics throughout) and Cross (music, with Wiggs credited on “Hey Now”). (Wikipedia; Discogs)

The Mood Elevators E.P. (1989, Fire Records) was produced by the band, with Phil Vinall handling remixes. Howard Turner and Crow (37) engineered alongside Mick Robbins. Wiggs contributed bass, backing vocals, and photography. (Discogs)

Fire Records era — Heaven Scent and dissolution (1990–1991)

By 1989 Josephine Wiggs had left to join The Breeders (with John Saltwell returning to the bass role per Wikipedia). The final album, Heaven Scent (1990, Fire Records, catalogue FIRE CD27), was produced by Phil Vinall, who also arranged and mixed it alongside Dan Cross and Parfitt. Personnel: Parfitt (vocals, guitars), Dan Cross (multiple guitars, organ, backing vocals, arranging, mixing), Josephine Wiggs (bass, cello, backing vocals — retained on record despite her 1989 departure), Jon Mattock (drums/percussion on tracks 1–10), Phil Outram (drums on tracks 11–13). (Discogs)

The Rise EP (1990) was co-produced by Phil Vinall and the band, engineered by Colin Richardson and Stephen Harris. Jon Mattock and Phil Outram again split drumming duties; Wiggs appeared on bass, cello, and backing vocals. (Discogs)

The band split in 1991.

Post-dissolution — Phil Parfitt

After The Perfect Disaster, Parfitt worked with Spiritualized’s Jason Pierce. He subsequently formed Oedipussy (Discogs ID: 200255), which released the album Divan (Hansome Records, 1994/1995), produced and arranged by Parfitt, with contributions from Terry Bickers (former House of Love / Levitation guitarist) and Phil Vinall producing. In 2014 Parfitt released a solo album, I’m Not the Man I Used To Be, on Milltone Head Recordings (released under “Philip Parfitt”). Further solo releases followed on A Turntable Friend (2020, Mental Home Recordings) and Tip Top Recordings (2024, Dark Light). (Wikipedia; Discogs)
