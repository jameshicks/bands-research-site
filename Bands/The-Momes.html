The Momes

British post-punk/avant-rock power trio, active 1988–1991. Discogs artist ID: 1416914. The band originated from the circle around The Work and released one LP, Spiralling (1989), before dissolving.

Overview

The Momes were formed at the end of 1988 / beginning of 1989 at the initiative of Mick Hobbs, who wanted a trio that would showcase his The Work bandmate Tim Hodgkinson’s technique on Hawaiian lap/flat guitar. The two recruited Andy Wake (2) after he had drummed with Officer! on a European tour.

The group originally operated under the name Mome Raths — drawn from Lewis Carroll’s poem “The Jabberwocky” — before shortening it to The Momes, which doubles as French slang for “street kids.”

History

Formation and Touring (1988–1989)

After coalescing at the end of 1988, the trio spent the first half of 1989 on an extensive Western European tour (February to July). In the summer of 1989, following the tour, they entered Cold Storage studio for two days and recorded what would become their sole album, Spiralling. The album was self-produced by the band, with engineering handled jointly by Hodgkinson and Charles Bullen (of This Heat).

Remission and Return (1989–1991)

After the recording session, the band went into remission. Andy Wake departed for Java to spend a year studying gamelan. During that absence, The Work reconvened with Hobbs and Hodgkinson. When Wake returned, both were occupied with The Work, but The Momes still managed to complete another couple of European tours before definitively splitting following shows in Germany in February and March 1991. A second studio LP was never made, though reportedly enough original material existed for one.
