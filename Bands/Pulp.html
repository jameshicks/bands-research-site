Pulp

Britpop / indie pop / art rock band. Sheffield, England. Discogs ID: 2811. Formed 1978 by Jarvis Cocker. Active 1978–2002, reunions 2011–2013 and 2022–present.

The band’s “kitchen sink drama” lyrics — class, sexuality, hedonism, and alienation — coupled with Cocker’s charismatic presence made Pulp one of the defining acts of the Britpop era. They are regarded alongside Blur, Oasis, and Suede as the “Britpop big four.” Different Class (1995) won the Mercury Prize, debuted at UK #1, and spawned “Common People” — frequently voted the defining Britpop anthem. (Wikipedia)

Overview

Cocker formed Pulp at The City School, Sheffield, in 1978. The early band (variously called Arabicus Pulp, then simply Pulp) went through numerous lineups across the late 1970s and early 1980s before finding a stable nucleus by the mid-1980s that included Russell Senior (guitar), Candida Doyle (keyboards), and later Nick Banks (drums). After a long, difficult stint on Fire Records (1985–1992) punctuated by poor promotion and delayed releases, Pulp signed to Island Records via Gift Records (a Warp-distributed imprint) and broke into the mainstream with His ‘n’ Hers (1994) and then Different Class (1995). Steve Mackey (bass) joined in 1988; Mark Webber became a full member in 1995.

The band split in 2002, reunited for festival and arena shows in 2011–2013, and reunited again in 2022. More (2025, Rough Trade) was their first studio album since We Love Life (2001) and debuted at UK #1 to universal critical acclaim. Steve Mackey, who had declined the 2022 reunion tour, died in March 2023. (Wikipedia)

Key Release Personnel

Freaks (1987, Fire Records FIRE LP5)

  • Pulp self-produced
  • Russell Senior: guitar, violin; Candida Doyle: organ, piano; Peter Mansell: bass; Magnus Doyle: drums; Jarvis Cocker: vocals, guitar
  • Mastered by Graeme Durham

Separations (1992, Fire Records)

  • Produced by Alan Smyth and Pulp; recorded at FON Studios, Sheffield
  • Steve Mackey: bass; Nick Banks: drums; Russell Senior: guitar/violin; Candida Doyle: keyboards; Jarvis Cocker: voice, guitar
  • Engineer: Alan Fisch

His ‘n’ Hers (1994, Island Records CID 8025)

  • Produced by Ed Buller
  • Jarvis Cocker: vocals, guitar, synth; Russell Senior: guitar, violin; Candida Doyle: Farfisa organ, Stylophone, Korg Trident, Wurlitzer; Steve Mackey: Fender Jazz Bass; Nick Banks: drums, timpani
  • Artwork: The Designers Republic (Matching Accessories)

Different Class (1995, Island Records)

  • Produced by Chris Thomas
  • Full classic six-piece: Cocker (vocals/guitar/keys), Senior (guitar/violin), Candida Doyle (keys), Mackey (bass), Banks (drums), Webber (guitar/keys, first full album)

This Is Hardcore (1998, Island Records CID 8066)

  • Produced by Chris Thomas
  • String arrangements by Anne Dudley on multiple tracks; orchestrations by Nicholas Dodd
  • Featuring Neneh Cherry on “Seductive Barry”
  • Art direction: Peter Saville (2) and John Currin
  • Antony Genn co-wrote “Glory Days” (track 11)
  • Peter Thomas Sound Orchestra sampled on title track

We Love Life (2001, Island Records CIDX 8110)

  • Produced by Scott Walker; co-produced and engineered by Peter Walsh
  • Richard Hawley: lap steel guitar, twelve-string guitar; The Swingle Singers: backing vocals; Metro Voices: choir
  • Brian Gascoigne: string/bass arrangements; Howie B.: programming on “Weeds II”
  • Art direction: Peter Saville (2)
