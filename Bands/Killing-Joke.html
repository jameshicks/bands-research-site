Killing Joke

Post-punk / industrial rock band from London. Formed 1978. Discogs artist ID: 13408.

Overview

Core members: Jaz Coleman (vocals, keyboards), Geordie Walker (guitar), Paul Raven (bass), Youth (bass — various periods). Dave Grohl drummed on their 2003 self-titled album.

Killing Joke were a major force in post-punk, industrial, and proto-metal, active across multiple decades with several breakups and reformations.

EG Records Period (1980–1988)

Killing Joke signed with EG in 1980 — the label’s progressive-rock management pedigree (King Crimson, Roxy Music) was apparently sufficient common ground. The signing involved friction from the start: Jaz Coleman reportedly rushed the debut album to press before a label-controlled version could appear, resulting in a lawsuit over the release. They stayed on EG through eight years of records:

Killing Joke (EGS 109, 1980) → What’s This For…! (1981) → Revelations (1982) → Fire Dances (1983) → Night Time (1985) → Brighter Than A Thousand Suns (1986) → Outside The Gate (1988)

Brighter Than A Thousand Suns (1986) was partially produced by Chris Kimsey (tracks A1–A4, B2–B4), with Julian Mendelsohn mixing those same tracks. It is the most polished and commercially oriented album of their EG period. Engineer: Thomas Stiehler.

After Outside The Gate they moved to Noise Entertainment for Extremities, Dirt And Various Repressed Emotions (1990).
