Heldon

Oct 13, 20267 min read

Heldon

Discogs ID: 83138 Origin: Paris, France Active: 1974–1983 (original run); 2001 (reunion); 2022 (further activity) Genres: Electronic Rock, Space Rock, Krautrock-influenced, Progressive Rock

French electronic rock band founded and led by Richard Pinhas. One of the most significant French experimental rock acts of the 1970s, Heldon fused Krautrock texture, heavy guitar, synthesizers, and tape electronics into a body of work that runs from anarchic noise-rock to kosmische drift. The band’s name was taken from a fictional totalitarian entity in Norman Spinrad’s 1969 sci-fi novel Bug Jack Barron. Philosopher Gilles Deleuze — Pinhas’s teacher — contributed spoken word to the debut album.

Biography by Era

Formation and Debut (1974)

Pinhas had studied philosophy at the Sorbonne under Gilles Deleuze and Jean-François Lyotard before turning to music full-time. He had earlier played in the blues-rock group Blues Convention and in Schizo (4). In 1974 he convened Heldon and released the debut Electronique Guerilla (Disjuncta), a visceral collision of fuzz guitar, VCS3 synthesizer, and feedback. The record featured Deleuze delivering spoken-word text on the side-long “Troisième Mouvement: Assault”. Pinhas wrote all compositions; Patrick Gauthier played piano and VCS3 on the key live-band track B1; Alain Renaud added rhythm guitar; Pierrot Roussel bass; Coco Roussel drums; Georges Grünblatt synthesizer. Label: Disjuncta (Pinhas’s own imprint).

The Early Trilogy (1975)

Heldon 2 – Allez-Teia (αλετεια) (Urus Records, 1975) was a largely duo affair — Pinhas and Grünblatt — deploying Mellotron, ARP and VCS3 in dense layered improvisations. The title references the Greek concept of truth/unconcealment (ἀλήθεια).

Heldon Third – “It’s Always Rock’n’Roll” (Disjuncta, 1975) was a sprawling double LP expanding the lineup to a live band configuration — Pinhas, Renaud, Aurore (6) — with production credited to Joe Chip (a Phillip K. Dick reference). This album began the band’s practice of embedding literary/philosophical SF references throughout.

Also in 1975: Soutien À La RAF (Disjuncta), a limited-edition political single expressing solidarity with the Red Army Faction — a provocateur move that established Heldon’s confrontational stance.

The Studio Peak (1976–1977)

Heldon IV – “Agneta Nilsson” (Urus Records, 1976): An almost entirely solo Pinhas album — guitar and electronics, produced under the pseudonym Glenn Runciter (from Philip K. Dick’s Ubik). Engineer: Daniel Schaez. Sparse and ominous.

Heldon V – Un Rêve Sans Conséquence Spéciale (Cobra, 1976): The album title quotes Deleuze. Lineup broadened; produced by Pinhas under alias Moogi-Le-Moog. Sound engineers: Phil Ross (2) and Franck Dathis. Jannick Top (of Magma) contributed bass. The most harmonically sophisticated Heldon record up to this point.

Heldon.6. Interface (Cobra, 1977): Co-produced by Pinhas and Heldon collectively. Engineers: Phil Ross (2) and Franck Dathis; remix at Davout Studios by Roger Roche. Design/photos: Patrick Jelin. The densest, most fully realized Heldon studio record — heavy synth sequencing locked with motorik-adjacent rhythms, anticipating industrial and post-punk.

Final Studio Album and Dissolution (1979)

Stand By (Egg/Barclay, 1979): The last studio album of the original run. Produced jointly by Pinhas and drummer François Auger. Engineered by Roger Roche; assistant engineers: Phil Ross (2) and Christian (8). Design/photography: Patrick Jelin. The major-label move to Egg (a Barclay sublabel) brought somewhat cleaner production without abandoning Heldon’s abrasive character. Didier Batard played bass. The album ends with Heldon’s most overtly rock-structured writing.

After Stand By the group wound down. A 1982 live appearance at The Venue in London (with a lineup including Bernard Paganotti, Jean-Philippe Goude, Klaus Blasquiz) appears to have been a late-period or reunion performance.

Compilation Era (1983)

Perspective – Compilation 1976–1982 (WEA, 1983): An overview LP issued on a major, confirming retrospective status. The 1982 London lineup features extensively.

Dormancy and Reissue Activity (1980s–1990s)

US label Cuneiform Records reissued/compiled It’s Always Rock ‘N’ Roll / Electronique Guerilla (1993), introducing Heldon to an American underground audience. Steven Feigenbaum (Cuneiform) was closely involved in this rehabilitation.

Revival: Only Chaos Is Real (2001)

After nearly two decades of dormancy, Heldon reconvened for Only Chaos Is Real (25pm, 2001), a genuinely different-sounding album that drew on cyberpunk themes. Lyricists: Maurice G. Dantec and Norman Spinrad (the author whose novel inspired the band’s name). Personnel: Pinhas (guitar, producer), Olivier Manchion (bass), Antoine Paganotti (drums), Benoît Widemann (synthesizer), Maurice G. Dantec (synthesizer, lyrics), David Korn (vocals), Norman Spinrad (vocals). Mixed: Laurent Peyron and Mazarin (2). A harder, more industrial-metal direction.

Later Activity (2015–2022)

  • Live In Paris 1975 (SouffleContinu Records, 2015) — archival release
  • Single Collection (Bam Balam, 2017) — compilation
  • 1976–1979 (SouffleContinu Records, 2017) — archival compilation
  • Antelast (Bam Balam, 2022): New studio album, the most recent. Personnel included Florian Tatard and Arthur Narcy alongside Pinhas.

French Electronic/Rock Scene Context

Heldon operated alongside a small cluster of French experimental rock acts in the mid-1970s: Magma (6) (whose alumni Jannick Top, Klaus Blasquiz, and Bernard Paganotti all crossed into Heldon’s orbit), Lard Free (Pinhas’s other principal band), and Weidorje (Gauthier’s post-Magma project). The Disjuncta label was Pinhas’s own imprint — a self-release infrastructure that predated the UK DIY wave by several years.

The Cobra label that issued Heldon V and VI was a French imprint unrelated to the US jazz label of the same name.

Heldon’s synthesis of philosophy (Deleuze, Spinrad’s SF) and electronics anticipates both the French electro underground and the academic electroacoustic tradition, while the guitar-forward noise of the early records fed directly into what critics later called “zeuhl” and “rock in opposition” aesthetics.

The band’s US rehabilitation via Cuneiform Records in 1993 placed them in the RIO/avant-prog canon alongside Henry Cow, Univers Zéro, and Samla Mammas Manna.
