Magnetic Pull

Active: c.1982–1983 Based: New York City Genre: Post-punk / Experimental (presumed) Recorded: Nothing — entirely unrecorded

History

After Martha Tilson departed A Certain Ratio (early 1982), she and Pam Meyer returned to New York City and formed this all-female band. Georgia Hubley — who would go on to co-found Yo La Tengo in 1984 — played drums.

The band made no recordings and left no documented discography. It exists only in sleeve notes and scene accounts.

Notes (web research — 2026-07-04, supplemented 2026-07-04, 2026-08-08)

No web sources independently document Magnetic Pull’s existence, lineup, or any venue appearances. Searches for “Magnetic Pull” + Martha Tilson + Georgia Hubley + New York returned no relevant results. Martha Tilson has no locatable public interview or statement mentioning Magnetic Pull. Georgia Hubley’s interviews about her pre-YLT drumming describe “an all-female band” she played with at art school (which later became The Dangerous Curves after she left) — but she does not name it as Magnetic Pull or name Tilson as a bandmate in any located statement.

Source chain clarification (2026-07-04): The description of Magnetic Pull on Discogs r15406580 (Twilight Zoners “Zero Zero One” sleeve) appears to draw from the liner notes of the Messthetics #107: DIY ‘78-81 London III CD compilation (Hyped to Death, 2009; compiler: Chuck Warner), which includes a 24-page booklet with histories, photos, and documentation of the Dining Out / Twilight Zoners scene. That booklet is the likely primary source for the Magnetic Pull / Georgia Hubley detail. The booklet is not reproduced online in extractable form; its content is known only via Discogs submission and search-engine paraphrases. The chain is therefore: Messthetics #107 booklet (physical, 2009) → Discogs r15406580 notes → this vault entry.

Georgia Hubley’s primary-source account (re: pre-YLT band): In every located interview, Hubley describes playing drums in an unnamed all-girl band she joined through art school — a group that “never had a name” or a show while she was in it, and that became The Dangerous Curves after she left (Tom Tom Magazine, 2012; DRUM! Magazine). The DRUM! Magazine piece names The Dangerous Curves directly: “After a short stint in an all-female band, The Dangerous Curves, Hubley… hooked up with music journalist/guitarist Kaplan.” No mention of Magnetic Pull, Martha Tilson, or a prior London-connected band appears in any located interview. If Hubley was in Magnetic Pull, she has not said so publicly. The possibility that the unnamed art-school band and Magnetic Pull are the same (or overlapping) projects cannot be confirmed or excluded from available sources.

Messthetics #107 booklet — web search result (2026-08-08): A targeted search specifically for the Messthetics #107 booklet text online — including Archive.org, blog posts (dezji.wordpress.com, reynoldsretro.blogspot.com), reviews (Collapse Board, Record Collector Magazine, Forced Exposure, Midheaven), Chuck Warner interviews (PopMatters, Reynolds Retro), and combination searches for “Magnetic Pull” + “Georgia Hubley” / “Messthetics” / “Tilson” / “Meyer” — confirmed that the booklet text has not been reproduced online in any extractable form. All search-engine AI summaries of “Magnetic Pull” simply paraphrase the Discogs r15406580 sleeve notes, which themselves derive from the booklet. No independent web source quotes or transcribes any passage of the #107 booklet. The Chickfactor interview with Georgia Hubley (chickfactor.com) covers her animation work and family background; no pre-YLT band history. The Chuck Warner / Messthetics Reynolds Retro interview (reynoldsretro.blogspot.com, 2018) discusses his curatorial approach but names no specific artists from #107. The booklet remains physical-only.
