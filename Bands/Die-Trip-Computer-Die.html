Independent experimental / sound collage project from London. Initially a duo of Lepke Buchvalter and Jim Welton (operating under the alias Xentos Fray Bentos / “Xentos (Flycraken) Jones”). Ted Barrow joined in 1994, completing the trio. The group’s sole Discogs release is the self-released 1995 cassette Sibling Family Research, though their Bandcamp presence suggests continued activity.

Biography

Die Trip Computer Die emerged from the overlap of two threads in London’s underground experimental scene. Jim Welton (b. London) had been a foundational figure in the city’s post-punk DIY world since the late 1970s — a co-founder of The Homosexuals, operator of the It’s War Boys micro-label, and an extraordinarily prolific alias-user whose output under names like L. Voag, Amos (3), Narky Brillans, Gus Coma, and dozens of others ran through cassettes, vinyl, radio art, and sound collage from 1979 onward. Lepke Buchvalter (aliases: Gus Coma, Yakko Banovic) was another consistent presence in that same network: a performer on the It’s War Boys catalog (Nancy Sesay And The Melodaires, 1982; The Just Measurers Flagellation, 1983), a member of Milk From Cheltenham, and a collaborator with Welton in Modern Shit.

When Welton and Buchvalter formed Die Trip Computer Die — exact date unconfirmed on Discogs, likely early 1990s given the 1995 release — it represented a continuation of the sound-collage and lo-fi experimental aesthetic that had characterised their earlier work rather than a departure from it. The name itself has the mock-aggressive, deliberately awkward quality typical of the It’s War Boys circle. Ted Barrow (name variations: Bongo Bongo Barrow, Ted Mantee) joined in 1994, per Discogs. Barrow’s prior credits outside Die Trip Computer Die are sparse: a “Very Special Guest” credit on a 1983 Woof Records release (The Lowest Note On The Organ), drums on a 1989 Distortion Records (4) release (Not Quite ‘It’), and percussion on the 2019 Unhappy Fly album (Emotional Response (2)). The last of these is interesting — Unhappy Fly (ID: 6503980) is also a Jim Welton group, placing Barrow back in Welton’s orbit over 25 years after Sibling Family Research.

The 1995 cassette Sibling Family Research is the only documented Discogs release, but the group’s active Bandcamp page indicates the project has continued or resumed beyond that. Jim Welton’s Discogs profile describes him as having “resurfaced in the late 90’s” and “currently works with experimental radio art, circuit bending and sample based collage” — suggesting Die Trip Computer Die may be part of his ongoing practice. Welton was the subject of a 2003 film documentary by Luke Fowler and Kosten Koper titled Xentos (Fray Bentos) Jones: Found Documents Concerning The Life And Work Of Xentos Jones (linked on UbuWeb from his Discogs page), situating him firmly within the history of UK experimental music.

Scene Context

Die Trip Computer Die sits at the intersection of two trajectories in London underground music. The first is the It’s War Boys / Homosexuals lineage: lo-fi cassette culture, post-punk squat-scene provenance, deliberate amateurism-as-aesthetic, and an almost compulsive proliferation of aliases. The second is the broader UK DIY experimental tape circuit of the 1980s–90s, represented by labels like Woof Records (run by Bill Gilonis) and later Alga Marghen (which reissued Milk From Cheltenham material in Italy, 2005 and 2008).

Lepke Buchvalter’s Discogs footprint points to this same cluster: appearance on the Woof Records LP Slow Crimes (1982) alongside Die Trip collaborators, and the Murphy Working Stiffs roster, which also includes Bill Gilonis, Chris Gray (2), and Robert Storey.

The 2022 Emotional Response (2) reissue of Amos & Sara’s Go Home Soldier (original It’s War Boys £9, 1983) — with Lepke Buchvalter credited for “Electronics [AMS DMX Sample Lock Ins]” and “Performer [Horn Tapes]” — suggests the network remained active or at least in archival contact into the 2020s. Emotional Response (2) is a US label that has released material by Unhappy Fly (another Welton group, with Ted Barrow on percussion), placing all three Die Trip members in its orbit across different releases.
