History

Thick Pigeon were a New York art-pop duo formed by Stanton Miranda and Carter Burwell. Their first documented recording is “Silhouettes,” which appeared on the Les Disques du Crépuscule Christmas compilation Chantons Noël — Ghosts of Christmas Past (TWI-058, December 1981). The 2016 digital reissue metadata credits the track as: “Silhouettes · Thick Pigeon · Stanton Miranda · Martha Tilson” — confirming Martha Tilson’s participation.

The Factory/Crépuscule connection was structural rather than coincidental: Stanton Miranda’s partner Michael Shamberg ran Factory Records’ New York office. Les Disques du Crépuscule was the Belgian sister label to Factory, founded by Michel Duval and Annik Honoré (Joy Division’s correspondent). Martha Tilson, still a member of A Certain Ratio in December 1981, was squarely embedded in this Factory/Crépuscule world.

Their album Too Crazy Cowboys (Factory FACT-85, 1984) was produced by Stephen Morris and Gillian Gilbert of New Order — another indicator of how deeply embedded in the Factory orbit they were.

Carter Burwell went on to become one of Hollywood’s most prolific film composers, scoring virtually every Coen Brothers film (Blood Simple, Barton Fink, Fargo, No Country for Old Men, True Grit, The Ballad of Buster Scruggs, etc.).

The Stanton Miranda / Miranda Dali Thread

Stanton Miranda also recorded solo material under various credits. Before Thick Pigeon she was part of CKM — an informal art-project trio assembled by conceptual artist Dan Graham to restage his piece Performer/Audience/Mirror (1977). The initials stood for:

  • C = Christine Hahn (drums) — founding member of The Static (1978–79, with Glenn Branca and Barbara Ess); subsequently joined Malaria! in Berlin
  • K = Kim Gordon (guitar) — CKM was her first-ever musical experience; she had just acquired a guitar and had never played an instrument
  • M = Miranda (Stanton Miranda)

CKM performed Dan Graham’s piece once or a handful of times and wrote a few original songs (“Cosmopolitan Girl,” “Soft Posh Separates” — lyrics drawn from magazine ad copy, deliberately ironic). Their only documented recording is “Working Youth” on Just Another Asshole #5 (1981, Barbara Ess/Glenn Branca), credited as “Kim Gordon and Miranda” rather than CKM. Through Stanton Miranda, Gordon met Thurston Moore; Sonic Youth formed in mid-1981 shortly after.

Arthur Russell played cello on at least one Miranda Dali track (the 1991 solo album).

Notes on Martha Tilson

Martha Tilson’s involvement is confirmed to a single appearance: “Silhouettes” (1981). The phrase “one or two appearances” that sometimes circulates likely reflects the fact that this track appears twice in discographies — on the original 1981 Crépuscule vinyl compilation and as bonus track 16 on the 2004 LTM reissue Miranda Dali + Singles. All release credits for the two 1982 Crépuscule singles and the 1984 LP have been checked: she does not appear. The original sleeve credits for the 1982 singles are sparse and leave a small theoretical window, but no source supports an uncredited appearance.
