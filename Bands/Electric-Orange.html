Overview

Electric Orange are a German psychedelic rock band rooted in the Krautrock and space-rock tradition, active since 1993 with a lineup anchored by Dirk Bittner, Dirk Jan Müller, Eric Karow, Frank Burkhardt, and Georg Monheim. Their catalogue has expanded across four decades — sparse in the 1990s, building steadily through the 2000s, and peaking in the 2010s — across labels including Delerium, Sulatron-Records, and Studio Fleisch, with whom they placed eleven releases between 2010 and 2023. The most striking feature of their credit history is an unusually deep collaboration with Eroc (73 shared credits) and Double D (58), pointing to long-running production or studio partnerships that run well beyond occasional features.

Notes (web)

Eroc’s role with Electric Orange

Eroc (Jürgen Dräger, drummer of Grobschnitt) functions as the band’s mastering engineer, not as a producer or co-performer. Multiple independent reviews confirm this across a span of releases: Abgelaufen! (2001), Platte (2003), Netto (2011), XX (2012/20th anniversary), and Misophonia are each described as “mastered by Eroc” with no producing or performance credits mentioned.

The mastering was done at Eroc’s Mastering Ranch (also referred to as “The Ranch”), his professional studio facility in Breckerfeld, Germany. This is consistent with Eroc’s wider activity as a remaster/mastering engineer for Grobschnitt reissues and other krautrock-adjacent releases on labels such as Repertoire and SPV.

The German Wikipedia article on Electric Orange states explicitly: “Alle Alben ab 2001 wurden von Eroc, Mitglied der Band Grobschnitt, gemastert” (“All albums from 2001 onwards were mastered by Eroc, a member of the band Grobschnitt”). [https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_Orange, as of 2026-11-17]

Start of the partnership: The first documented instance is Abgelaufen! (2001), where liner-note-level credits confirm mastering at Eroc’s Mastering Ranch. No source found explains the personal or professional connection that led to the first engagement; it most plausibly reflects a shared German krautrock/progressive-rock scene rather than any documented introduction event.
