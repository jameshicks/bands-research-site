The Count Bishops

Pub rock / proto-punk band. London, UK, active 1974–1979. Discogs artist ID: 262595. Also credited as “Count Bishops” and “The Bishops.”

Overview

The Count Bishops were one of the key acts in the mid-1970s London pub rock scene, with a sound rooted firmly in Chicago R&B, rock & roll, and garage rock. Formed around 1974, they built their reputation as a ferocious live act on the London circuit before signing to Chiswick Records, the independent label co-founded by Roger Armstrong and Ted Carroll out of the Rock On market stalls. Their debut EP Speedball (1975) was among the very first releases on Chiswick (catalog number SW 1), making them foundational to the label’s identity alongside The 101’ers.

The band’s repertoire drew heavily on American R&B and blues covers — Chuck Berry, Slim Harpo, Howlin’ Wolf — alongside original material by members Zenon De Fleur and Dave Tice. They were contemporaries and circuit-mates of other pub rock acts while also anticipating the energy and stripped-down directness that would characterize UK punk a year later. The self-titled 1977 LP is often cited as their strongest studio statement; the live album Live! (1978) captured the raw power of their stage show.

Lineup changes marked the band’s history: vocalist Mike Spenser appeared on the debut EP but had left by the self-titled LP, where Dave Tice took over lead vocals. Bass player Steve Lewins was replaced by Pat McMullan (who also played with Dr. Feelgood) by the time of the live album. After a second studio LP, Cross Cuts (1979), the band dissolved.

Label Relationship: Chiswick Records

The Count Bishops were among the founding roster of Chiswick Records, co-founded by Roger Armstrong and Ted Carroll. Their debut EP Speedball carried catalog number SW 1, placing it at the very start of the label’s catalog. Armstrong produced the debut EP; subsequent releases were self-produced by the band or, in the case of Live!, by Neil Richmond. The label continued to issue Count Bishops / The Bishops material through 1979 and later released the 1995 retrospective The Best Of The Count Bishops.

The band appeared on both Chiswick Chartbusters samplers, the label’s primary promotional vehicle for showcasing its roster to a wider audience.

Pub Rock Context

The Count Bishops emerged from the pub rock circuit that flourished in London from roughly 1972 to 1977, alongside acts like Dr. Feelgood, Brinsley Schwarz, and The 101’ers. Pub rock was a reaction against the excess of early 1970s progressive and glam rock — a return to R&B basics, small venues, and direct performance. The Bishops fit the mode but leaned harder toward American garage rock and Chicago electric blues than most of their contemporaries. Their connection to Dr. Feelgood runs through bassist Pat McMullan, who played in both bands.

The Bishops’ proto-punk energy made them a transitional figure: their Speedball EP appeared in 1975, a full year before the punk explosion of 1976–77, and their raw, cover-heavy set predated the DIY aesthetic that pub rock passed on to punk. Mike Spenser, the original vocalist/harpist, left to form The Cannibals, another Chiswick act with similar R&B leanings.
