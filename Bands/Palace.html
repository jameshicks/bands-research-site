Palace

Discogs artist ID: 135307 Real name: Joseph William Oldham (Will Oldham) Active: 1991–1999 (under Palace-era aliases); continued as Bonnie “Prince” Billy from 1999 Primary labels: Drag City (US); Domino Records (UK/European); Palace Records (own imprint, sublabel of Drag City)

Palace is the umbrella Discogs entry for Will Oldham’s recordings from 1991 through the late 1990s, spanning four recording aliases: Palace Brothers (1991–1994), Palace Songs (1994–1995), Palace Music (1994–1996), and Palace (1995–1997). A fifth alias, Palace Soundtrack, appears on one release. All are consolidated under Discogs ID 135307. The alias Bonnie “Prince” Billy (Discogs ID: 66089) is a separate but linked entity covering 1999 onward.

See Will Oldham for the full biography, career arc, and post-Palace discography.

Aliases and Naming History

Oldham cycled through the Palace names with no precise public explanation; the shifts roughly track shifts in band membership and approach rather than clean breaks.

AliasApprox. periodNotes
Palace Brothers1991–1994Debut single and first two LPs; name implies a band but was largely Oldham-led
Palace Songs1994–1995UK EP releases (Hope, 1994) with a more varied supporting cast
Palace Music1994–1996Used for Viva Last Blues (1995) and some singles
Palace1995–1997Arise Therefore (1996), Songs Put Together For (The Broken Giant) (1996)
Palace Soundtrack1996Soundtrack release only

After Lost Blues And Other Songs (1997, a compilation credited to Palace Music), Oldham made one release as Will Oldham (Joya, Drag City, 1997) before adopting the Bonnie “Prince” Billy alias. The first Bonnie “Prince” Billy release — the 7” “I Am Drinking Again” (1998) — is considered the formal transition point, though I See A Darkness (1999) was the alias’s breakthrough.

Key Release Personnel

There Is No-One What Will Take Care Of You (1993)

First LP; released as Palace Brothers on Big Cat (UK, ABB 50) and Drag City (US). 12 tracks including the closing cover “I Had A Good Mother And Father” (written by Washington Phillips). Minimal Discogs credits on this pressing — recorded by Grant Barger (per the Will Oldham page). Louisville session players: Britt Walford and Brian McMahan (Slint), Todd Brashear, and Paul Greenlaw.

Tracklist: “Idle Hands Are The Devil’s Playthings” / “Long Before” / “I Tried To Stay Healthy For You” / “The Cellar Song” / “(I Was Drunk At The) Pulpit” / “There Is No-One What Will Take Care Of You” / “O Lord Are You In Need?” / “Merida” / “King Me” / “I Had A Good Mother And Father” / “Riding” / “O Paul”

Palace Brothers (1994)

Second LP, self-titled; very sparse credits in Discogs. Will Oldham: acoustic guitar, vocals, written-by (all uncredited on release). Grant Barger credited on bass in other sources. Cover photography by Sean Childress.

Hope EP (1994, as Palace Songs)

UK EP produced by Rian Murphy, recorded by Charlie Francis, Konrad Strauss, and Reza Ebrahimi. Full band: “Pushkin Will” (Will Oldham alias) — bass, guitar, vocals; Liam Hayes — piano, organ, electric guitar; Sean O’Hagan — piano, vocals (harmonies); Rob Allum — drums; Rian Murphy — drums, vocals; Briana Corrigan — vocals (harmonies).

Transition to Bonnie “Prince” Billy

The Palace name was quietly retired between 1997 and 1999. Lost Blues And Other Songs (1997) functioned as a retrospective closure of the Palace era. Joya (Drag City, 1997) appeared under “Will Oldham” without a Palace alias. The first “Bonnie ‘Prince’ Billy” credited release appeared in 1998; I See A Darkness (Palace Records, 1999) — mixed with David Pajo and Paul Oldham — became the alias’s defining statement.

The name shift is generally understood as an aesthetic pivot toward a more explicitly country-influenced and confessional mode, though the musical territory overlapped significantly with later Palace releases. See Bonnie “Prince” Billy for the post-1999 discography.
