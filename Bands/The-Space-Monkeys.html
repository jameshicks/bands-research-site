From Discogs

Indie group active from 1994 through the late ’90s. The four-piece were from Manchester, UK, and had a sound inspired by acid house and danceable indie rock from the late 1980s and early 1990s. As a result, The Space Monkeys were often compared to groups considered “baggy” or “Madchester” (Happy Mondays, The Stone Roses, Soup Dragons).

The band played their first gig in the basement of the legendary Manchester music club/venue the Hacienda in November of 1995 and one week later signed a six-album deal with Tony Wilson’s Factory Too label. In their own words, they signed to Wilson’s label “for exactly the same reason that Joy Division and Happy Mondays had done years before. Nobody else wanted us.” In late 1997, the group released their debut album “The Daddy Of Them All”, featuring the minor drug-related hit “Sugar Cane”.

A second full length release (reputedly titled “Escape from the 20th Century”) was recorded, including some tracks produced by notoriously creative hip hop producer Prince Paul (De La Soul, Stetsasonic). Since the Factory Too record label collapsed, the release of this album was delayed until 2013.

Members: Richard McNevin-Duff, Dom Morrison, Tony Pipes, Chas Morrison, Neil Walsh

Overview

The Space Monkeys were a Manchester four-piece — Richard McNevin-Duff, Dom Morrison, Tony Pipes, and Chas Morrison — who blended acid house with danceable indie rock in a style that drew regular comparisons to the Madchester scene (Happy Mondays, The Stone Roses, Soup Dragons). They played their first gig in the basement of the Haçienda in November 1995 and signed to Tony Wilson’s Factory Too the following week on a six-album deal. Their 1997 debut The Daddy of Them All included the minor hit “Sugar Cane”; a follow-up album recorded with hip-hop producer Prince Paul was shelved when Factory Too collapsed and finally surfaced in 2013 on Hercules Records UK. The bulk of their catalogue sits in the 1990s across Factory Records Limited, Interscope, and Chingon Records, with producer/engineer Johnny Jay as their most consistent collaborator (22 shared credits).

Notes (web)

“Escape from the 20th Century” — 2013 release circumstances

The second album was recorded at Public Enemy’s Green Street Studio in New York with Prince Paul producing. Factory Too had not paid the studio, the hotel, the engineer, or Prince Paul. According to a Louder Than War interview with Richard McNevin-Duff (snippet: louderthanwar.com), Prince Paul was sympathetic to the band’s position — he liked what they were doing — and handed them the master tapes, reportedly saying words to the effect of “Just don’t give it to those fuckers in the suits.” The band therefore held the tapes throughout the years the album sat unreleased.

The 2013 release appears to have been a band-initiated decision, not driven by a fan campaign or a formal rights-recovery action. It was issued as a digital-only release on 11 December 2013 via Hercules Records UK, a small label. No evidence of a reunion tour or external label push has surfaced. The band retained the tapes and chose when to put the album out; Factory Too’s collapse had left the original label deal void. There is no indication Prince Paul was involved in the 2013 release decision beyond having given the band the tapes years earlier.
