Spectrum (4)

Drone/psych project led by Sonic Boom (Pete Kember), formed in the UK in 1991 following the dissolution of Spacemen 3. Discogs artist ID: 125296

Overview and Formation

Spectrum was launched by Pete Kember (a.k.a. Sonic Boom, Discogs ID: 97498 / 561819) immediately after Spacemen 3 fractured in 1991. While Jason Pierce went on to found Spiritualized, Kember assembled a new band around guitarist/producer Richard Formby and bassist Mike Stout. The debut single “(I Love You) To The Moon & Back” appeared on Silvertone Records in 1991.

The early lineup — Kember, Formby, Stout, and percussionist Geoff Donkin — carried on the melodic, song-oriented thread of late-period Spacemen 3, filtered through 1960s garage influence. The debut album Soul Kiss (Glide Divine) (1992, Silvertone, ORE ZLP 518) added longer drone-based pieces alongside conventional songs. Formby departed immediately after the album’s release; Stout left shortly after.

A reconstituted lineup for Highs, Lows and Heavenly Blows (1994, Silvertone) brought in former Spacemen 3 bassist Pete Bassman (Pete Bain), guitarist/vocalist Scott Riley, and longtime engineering collaborator Alf Hardy. Pat Fish (The Jazz Butcher) contributed saxophone across multiple early releases.

From 1996 onward, the Spectrum name effectively became a solo vehicle for Kember. Songs For Owsley (1996, Reprise) and Forever Alien (1997, Space Age Recordings) featured Kember and Hardy working with vintage analogue synthesizers — particularly EMS instruments — in a vein parallel to Kember’s E.A.R. (Experimental Audio Research) project. Pete Bassman continued to contribute.

A long hiatus followed Forever Alien. In 1999 Kember issued A Lake Of Teardrops as a full Spectrum/Silver Apples collaboration (with Simeon (3) on oscillators/electronics and Will Carruthers on bass). The 2000s saw live work and archive releases but little new studio material until Indian Giver (2008, Birdman Records) — a collaboration with Captain Memphis (Jim Dickinson).

War Sucks (2009, Mind Expansion) marked the return of a proper band lineup: Kember on guitar/keyboards, Nolan Watkinson on bass guitar, Roger Brogan on percussion. Engineered by David Max; Kember produced and mixed. Alex Hancock (2) receives an uncredited lead guitar credit on Discogs.

Subsequent releases appeared sporadically on The Great Pop Supplement (2010–2013) and Space Age Recordings. A 2022 single appeared on Polytechnic Youth (cat. PY162). Reissues of Highs, Lows and Heavenly Blows came via Medical Records LLC and Music On Vinyl in 2018.

Connections

  • Sonic Boom (Pete Kember, Discogs IDs: 97498 / 561819) — sole continuous creative force; all albums trace to his production/composition
  • Spacemen 3 — direct precursor; Pete Kember’s pre-Spectrum band with Jason Pierce
  • Spiritualized — parallel post-Spacemen 3 project (Jason Pierce’s side)
  • Will Carruthers — bass; member of Spacemen 3 and Spiritualized before appearing on Spectrum’s A Lake Of Teardrops
  • Pete Bassman — bass; ex-Spacemen 3 (as Pete Bain), core to the Highs, Lows lineup
  • Richard Formby — early producer; later had major UK production career (Wild Beasts, Hookworms)
  • Alf Hardy — engineer/synthesizer; also member of Omnia Opera, Tubilah Dog, Mau Maus, Earth Lab
  • Pat Fish — Jazz Butcher frontman; recurring saxophone contributor 1992–1993
  • Silver Apples — full collaboration A Lake Of Teardrops (1999) — one of Simeon Coxe’s significant later-career appearances
  • David Max — engineered War Sucks; connected via Nick Kramer and the Tadpoles/Cheval Sombre psych-drone network
  • Silvertone Records — debut label 1991–1995; also home to Buddy Guy, The Stone Roses
  • Space Age Recordings — long-term home from 1996 onwards; sublabel of 3rd Stone
  • E.A.R. (Experimental Audio Research) — Kember’s parallel drone project (same era as Songs For Owsley / Forever Alien); referenced in the Discogs profile
  • 2008–2011 touring: Spectrum provided support on the Spacemen 3 2008 reformation tour; performed at All Tomorrow’s Parties (ATP) festival (label ID: 251341) May 2011
