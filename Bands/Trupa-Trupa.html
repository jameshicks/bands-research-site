Overview

Trupa Trupa are a rock band from Gdańsk, Poland, active since 2010, whose catalogue spans psychedelic rock, noise, art rock, and indie rock. The band — built around Grzegorz Kwiatkowski, Rafał Wojczal, Tomek Pawluczuk, and Wojtek Juchniewicz — attracted substantial international label interest across the 2010s, releasing through Ici D’Ailleurs, Blue Tapes And X-Ray Records, Glitterbeat, Lovitt Records, and Sub Pop. Producer Michał Kupicz is their most consistent collaborator, appearing across eleven shared works.

Sub Pop Signing — Catalyst

The Sub Pop deal (announced 26 February 2019) resulted from a relationship between the band and Sub Pop co-founder Jonathan Poneman that stretched back several years, with multiple documented steps:

  1. OFF Festival, Katowice (c. 2013): Poneman attended a Trupa Trupa set at the OFF Festival in Katowice, told them he’d like to work with them, and began following their progress. The Sub Pop announcement page notes he “has long taken a personal interest in the culture and history of Poland.” He is also quoted as saying he made the decision to sign them “three years” before the announcement (i.e. c. 2016) but “it took me a long time to get it done.” [Source: The Wire interview with Kwiatkowski, https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/interviews/trupa-trupa-interview-grzegorz-kwiatkowski; Sub Pop announcement, https://www.subpop.com/news/2019/02/26/trupa_trupa_signs_to_sub_pop_to_release_new_music_in_2019_shares_official_video_for_dream_about]

  2. Pitchfork / 2013: The Sub Pop signing announcement notes that Poneman had mentioned the band in a Pitchfork interview as early as 2013, at one point mistaking their second album ++ for a set of demos while saying he was deciding whether he liked it — confirming he had been tracking them since at least that year. [Source: Sub Pop announcement; Rock and Roll Globe, https://rockandrollglobe.com/experimental/sub-pop-signs-celebrated-polish-indie-rockers-trupa-trupa/]

  3. SXSW 2018 (proximate trigger): According to Kwiatkowski in The Wire interview, a chaotic SXSW 2018 performance — plagued by a broken amplifier and bass equipment failures — proved pivotal. The shambolic set impressed journalists present, including David Fricke (Rolling Stone) and Robin Hilton (NPR). The band was subsequently named among the best acts at SXSW 2018 by Rolling Stone and the Chicago Tribune listed the show in their top ten concerts of that year. This surge of US press attention appears to have been what finally pushed the long-standing Poneman interest over the line. [Source: The Wire interview]

  4. Café Oto, London: The Wire interview also mentions the band playing with Poneman at Café Oto in London, where they became friends — though the Rock and Roll Globe piece indicates this was separate from the formal signing process rather than its catalyst.

No single broker or intermediary is named in any source; the deal was driven by Poneman personally. No CMJ appearance is documented as a factor. The Rock and Roll Globe piece also notes that author Ron Hart had a prior Facebook friendship with Kwiatkowski and had recommended Sub Pop to them, though this is a secondary account and the primary sources centre on Poneman’s own initiative.
