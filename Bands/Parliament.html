Parliament

Sep 27, 20269 min read

Parliament

Discogs ID: 8887 Origin: Newark, New Jersey / Detroit, Michigan, USA Founded: 1955 (as The Parliaments); renamed Parliament 1969 Active: 1969–1981 (original run); various reunions; ongoing as of 2024 Labels: Invictus Records (1970–1974), Casablanca Records (1974–1980), One Nation Records (2), C Kunspyruhzy Records Inducted: Rock and Roll Hall of Fame 1997 (as Parliament-Funkadelic)

Sister act of Funkadelic within the P-Funk collective, both led by George Clinton. Parliament was the funk/soul vehicle; Funkadelic ran the psychedelic rock side. The two bands shared most of the same personnel throughout the 1970s, with Clinton operating them simultaneously on different labels.

Origins: The Parliaments (1955–1968)

George Clinton formed a doo-wop vocal group in Plainfield, New Jersey in 1955, which eventually became The Parliaments. The core vocal lineup that would persist into the Parliament era: Clinton, Fuzzy Haskins (Clarence Eugene Haskins), Ray Davis (Raymond Davis), Calvin Simon, and Grady Thomas. They recorded for Revilot Records and scored a minor hit with “(I Wanna) Testify” (1967) before losing the name in a contract dispute. Clinton renamed the backing band Funkadelic and the vocal group Parliament.

Invictus Era (1970–1974)

Signed to Holland-Dozier-Holland’s Invictus Records label in Detroit. Their debut LP, Osmium (1970, Invictus 1C 062-91763 in Germany), was an eclectic outlier — blending country, folk, gospel, and proto-funk, nothing like the Casablanca records that followed. The lineup was still anchored by the original five vocalists plus the emerging instrumental crew that also played in Funkadelic.

Key releases:

  • Osmium (1970, Invictus) — debut LP; folk/country/funk hybrid
  • Osmium (reissued 1974 as First Thangs, HDH/Invictus) — repackaged on HDH
  • I Call My Baby Pussycat / The Silent Boatman — singles (1970)
  • Breakdown / Come In Out Of The Rain — singles (1971–1972)

Casablanca Era — The Core Albums (1974–1980)

The defining run. Eight studio albums in six years, all produced by George Clinton, all on Casablanca Records. Mastering throughout by Allen Zentz (at his Los Angeles facility) until Trombipulation (1980), which was mastered by Brian Gardner. Engineers across the run included Jim Vitti, Jim Callon, and later Pete Bishop, Mike Iacopelli, Greg Reilly, and others. Artwork by Gribbitt! design collective.

Up For The Down Stroke (1974, Casablanca NBLP 7002)

Parliament’s first Casablanca LP. The first pure funk Parliament record, establishing the template.

Mothership Connection (1975, Casablanca — cat. CBLA 71013 in France)

The mythology-defining record. Introduced the Mothership stage concept, Dr. Funkenstein, Sir Nose D’Voidoffunk, and the Star Child characters that would run through the rest of the Casablanca catalog. Contains “Give Up The Funk (Tear The Roof Off The Sucker)” and “P Funk (Wants To Get Funked Up).” One of the most-pressed Parliament LPs (101 pressings across countries in Discogs).

The Clones Of Dr. Funkenstein (1976, Casablanca NBLP 7034)

Continued the P-Funk mythology. “Do That Stuff” was a major single. Introduced Taka Kahn and Debbie Edwards as the “Extra-Singing Clones” vocal group. Personnel largely identical to Mothership Connection; Michael Hampton consolidated as second lead guitarist alongside Garry Shider.

Funkentelechy Vs. The Placebo Syndrome (1977, Casablanca NBLP 7084)

Generally considered the creative peak. Contains two of the most-sampled P-Funk tracks: “Flash Light” (the first funk recording built predominantly around a synthesizer bass, Bernie Worrell on Moog) and “Bop Gun (Endangered Species).” “Sir Nose D’Voidoffunk” (10-minute B-side) deepened the mythology.

Live (P.Funk Earth Tour) (1977, Casablanca) — double live LP

The P-Funk Earth Tour was the highest-grossing funk tour of the mid-1970s, featuring the elaborate Mothership landing stage prop. The double LP documents the spectacle.

Motor Booty Affair (1978, Casablanca NBLP 7125)

Concept album set underwater (the city of Atlantis). Key arrival: Junie Morrison (Walter Morrison, ID 67807) — composer, multi-instrumentalist, musical director — credited on bass, drums, guitar, keyboards, vocals. Morrison became a key production voice for the remainder of the Casablanca run. Also notable: Catfish Collins (Phelps Collins, Bootsy’s brother) playing guitar across several tracks, and Rodney Curtis joining on bass.

“Aqua Boogie (A Psychoalphadiscobetabioaquadoloop)” won the Grammy for Best R&B Vocal Performance (Duo or Group) in 1979.

GloryHallaStoopid (Pin The Tale On The Funky) (1979, Casablanca NBLP 7195)

Late-period peak, transitioning into the new members era. Dennis Chambers appears here on drums. Junie Morrison co-produces two tracks (B2, B3). Philippe Wynne (formerly of The Spinners) features on vocals. Blackbyrd McKnight (Dewayne McKnight) arrives on guitar. The ensemble had grown enormous — more than 30 credited vocalists.

Trombipulation (1980, Casablanca NBLP 7249)

Final Casablanca studio LP. Marked a more commercial direction; the era of the grand spectacle was winding down. Junie Morrison co-produces three tracks (A2 “Trombipulation,” A3 “Long Way Around,” B2 “Let’s Play House”). Bootsy Collins producing on A1 “Crush It.” String and horn arrangements by Tony Camillo. Mastered by Brian Gardner (Mastered at Allen Zentz facility per runout — still the same plant but different mastering engineer).

Post-Casablanca

Parliament’s commercial run ended with Casablanca’s bankruptcy (1980). The collective migrated to various configurations through the 1980s under the P-Funk All Stars banner and as solo artists (George Clinton to Capitol, Bootsy to Warner Bros. solo). Later Parliament releases:

  • Dope Dogs (1993, One Nation Records 2) — returned to Parliament name
  • Medicaid Fraud Dogg (2018, C Kunspyruhzy Records) — recent-era release

Parliament continued touring regularly well into the 2010s and 2020s, with George Clinton as the constant.

P-Funk Universe

Parliament and Funkadelic were the two main satellites of the P-Funk collective. Parallel satellite projects signed to Casablanca and allied labels:

  • Bootsy’s Rubber Band — Bootsy Collins’s solo vehicle; Casablanca
  • Brides Of Funkenstein — Dawn Silva and Lynn Mabry (ID 76880); Casablanca
  • Parlet (ID 151464) — Mallia Franklin, Jeanette Washington, Debbie Wright; Casablanca
  • Horny Horns (ID 76878) — Fred Wesley and Maceo Parker; Atlantic
  • P-Funk All Stars — umbrella touring/recording unit post-1980

The stage production, particularly the Mothership landing sequence during the P-Funk Earth Tour (1976–77), was by far the most elaborate live show in funk history at that time.

Open Questions

  • Osmium (1970) personnel: the German pressing carries no additional credits — who played on the Invictus sessions?
  • Exact departure dates of the original five vocalists (Haskins, Davis, Simon, Thomas) are not confirmed in Discogs
  • Philippe Wynne’s joining date — credited on GloryHallaStoopid (1979) but may have been touring with the collective earlier
  • Glen Goins’s departure: Discogs shows him active through 1977–78, then absent; circumstances not documented in Discogs
  • Did Parliament and Funkadelic ever officially disband, or simply go dormant?
