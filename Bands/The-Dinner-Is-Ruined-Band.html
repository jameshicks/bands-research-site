The Dinner Is Ruined Band

Toronto experimental/indie rock band, active from at least 1991. Discogs artist ID: 1761017.

Also known as: Dinner Is Ruined, The Dinner Is Ruined

Core members: Dale Morningstar, Dave Clark (5), Al Kelso Labels: Not On Label (self-released, 1991), Raw Energy / A&M Records (1993), Gas Racket Records (1994, 1996)

Biography

Formation and Early Cassette (1991)

The Dinner Is Ruined Band emerged from the Toronto underground in 1991 as a vehicle for Dale Morningstar’s songwriting. The band self-released Burn Yer Dashiki as a cassette album — credited as “Dinner Is Ruined” on the sleeve — with no label other than the band’s own imprint. The lineup on that cassette was a loose collective: Morningstar (guitar, vocals, violin, piano, radio noises) and Al Kelso (bass, guitar) shared writing credits with Don Kerr (drums, cello, horns, backing vocals) on all but one track, with the sole exception being a cover of Lennon-McCartney’s “Everybody’s Got Something To Hide (Except Me And My Monkey).” Cover art was by Prince Miro Von Pressburg; photography by Susan Woollam. Morningstar and Kerr co-produced and co-engineered. The genres listed on Discogs (Rock / Experimental / Lo-Fi) place the cassette in the same terrain as early-1990s North American lo-fi.

Don Kerr is independently noted as a musician and composer in Toronto; he is also a member of Rheostatics (alongside Dave Clark (5)), Bidiniband, and other projects.

Major-Label Moment: Love Songs From The Lubritorium (1993)

The band’s only major-label release came via Raw Energy (a Toronto punk/ska imprint founded 1989 by Graeme Boyce and Chris Black) in a co-release arrangement with A&M Records, the parent. The album was released as “Dinner Is Ruined” on the A&M/Raw Energy joint catalog number 74242-1011-2. Morningstar served as sole producer and recording engineer, with Don Kerr assisting on both roles. Layout was shared between Morningstar and Graham Kennedy (5); photography by Graham Kennedy (5); lettering by Scott Townson; cover art again by Prince Miro Von Pressburg.

At 12 tracks, Love Songs From The Lubritorium is the band’s most fully realized studio statement in the DB. Genres: Rock / Alternative Rock / Indie Rock. The lack of musician-level track credits in Discogs for this release means the full personnel playing bass, drums, etc. is not currently documented beyond the production credits.

The Raw Energy roster around the same time included King Apparatus, King Cobb Steelie, and other Toronto acts — locating the band squarely in early-1990s Toronto indie/punk/ska adjacent territory even as the A&M co-release suggested mainstream interest.

Worm Pickers Brawl and the Gas Racket Era (1994)

The following year the band returned to independence via Gas Racket Records (catno D-2), this time as a rotating collective with a much more fractured, track-by-track lineup. Dave Clark (5) — then active as drummer in Rheostatics — appears on three tracks (“Vampire Boy,” “Laura Secords’ Birds,” “Mudslide,” “The Sears Jungle”), playing drums throughout. The percussionist Dr. Pee appears on multiple tracks (tracks 2, 3, 9, 10, 12, 15), also singing on “The Sears Jungle” and contributing Moog synthesizer on “Moop.” Al Kelso reappears on guitar, vocals, bass, and megaphone. Jim Field (of Rhea’s Obsession) plays bass and saxophone across several tracks. Rebecca Jenkins — Canadian singer and actress — guests on vocals for “Broken Water.” Don Bakeyman drums on one track. Prince Miro (a distinct credit from the earlier “Prince Miro Von Pressburg”) sings on “Dew Worms.”

Morningstar wrote all 16 tracks bar one (Dave Clark wrote track 6, “Home Time”; Dr. Pee wrote track 10, “Moop”), produced and engineered the entire record, and is credited with “Remaining Voces And Abuses.” Genres span Blues, Electronic, Folk/Country, Jazz, Pop, and Rock; styles include Avantgarde, Experimental, Folk Rock, Lo-Fi, Noise, and Psychedelic Rock — reflecting the album’s genre-sprawling, sketch-like character.

Ice Cream Drugs Rubber Goods (1996)

A second Gas Racket release followed in 1996 (catno DM 1111), credited as “The Dinner Is Ruined” on the sleeve. The 15-track CD has no additional credits logged in Discogs — no session players, no producers, no engineers beyond the primary artist credit. Styles: Indie Rock. This is the last Discogs entry with the band as primary artist.

Open Questions

  • Morningstar’s nominal listing as a member alongside Dave Clark (5) and Al Kelso in the Discogs entry doesn’t fully reflect the rotating lineup evident in the releases — Kelso and Clark appear only on specific albums and as session players respectively. The “core” identity of the band may be Morningstar alone as a solo vehicle.
  • No Discogs compilations include the band. Appearances on sampler CDs or Canadian indie compilations (e.g. the Raw Energy Dead On The Road comp from 1993, which they are absent from) are unconfirmed.
  • Ice Cream Drugs Rubber Goods (1996) has no personnel credits at all in Discogs — whether Morningstar recorded this solo or with a band is unknown.
  • The claim in the original stub that Derelict Dialect / Vernacular Violence is a Dinner Is Ruined Band release is incorrect per Discogs — it is a Slim Twig (Max Turnbull) LP on Paper Bag Records. Morningstar appears as session drummer and engineer on the A-side only.
  • Gas Racket Records has no profile in Discogs. Whether it was formally registered or simply a self-release imprint is unclear.
  • The band’s activity after 1996 is unconfirmed from Discogs. The lack of any compilations featuring the band and the absence of later releases suggests they were inactive from 1996 onward, but this is not confirmed.
