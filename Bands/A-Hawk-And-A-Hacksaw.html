A Hawk And A Hacksaw

Discogs ID: 241907

Folk duo from Albuquerque, New Mexico, led by Jeremy Barnes and Heather Trost, drawing on Balkan, Romani, and Eastern European acoustic traditions. Active from c. 2002; originally a solo project by Barnes before becoming a full duo with Trost.

Biography

Origins and Solo Debut (2002–2004)

A Hawk And A Hacksaw began as a solo project by Jeremy Barnes, who had spent the late 1990s as drummer and organist for Neutral Milk Hotel, playing on In The Aeroplane Over The Sea (1998) and recording with Bablicon and The Gerbils during the Elephant Six orbit. After NMH dissolved in 1998 Barnes moved to Europe — France, then England, then eventually Budapest.

The debut self-titled album was recorded and released in 2002 on Cloud Recordings (cat: cld 002), a US pressing. It is densely populated by the broader Elephant Six / Athens scene: Jeff Mangum and Scott Spillane (both of NMH) appear on individual tracks, as do Derek Almstead, John Fernandes, and Eric Harris (Elf Power). The tracklist includes a cover of a Jacques Thollot composition (“Quand Le Son Devient Aigu, Jeter La Girafe A La Mer”) alongside original compositions that blend field-recording-style textures with Eastern European modes. Barnes recorded and arranged all material; the press cited influences from Eastern European folk music, Pierre Schaeffer, and Spike Jones.

Leaf Era, First Phase (2004–2007)

Barnes signed to Birmingham-based Leaf and the project grew into a collaborative ensemble. Heather Trost — violinist from Albuquerque — became the consistent co-performer and, over successive records, co-writer and co-arranger.

Darkness At Noon (2005, BAY 43CD) was the first Leaf LP. The lineup was the Albuquerque circle: Trost on violin and vocals, Joseph Garcia on oud, Mark Weaver on tuba, Dan Clucas on trumpet/cornet, Horacio Samaniego on flute, and Sheila Barnes on harp. Barnes played a wide battery: accordion, Hammond B-3, piano, percussion, cumbus, Jew’s harp, mandolin. Genres listed are Folk; the record is more consciously Eastern European than the debut.

The Way The Wind Blows (2006, BAY 51V) was released as a vinyl LP only. It marks the first appearance of Zach Condon (of Beirut) as a session player — on trumpet for tracks A1 and A4. Romanian musicians enter here: Constantin “Sulo” Calin (euphonium), Daniel Ivancea (alto saxophone), Costica “Cimai” Trifan (trumpet), and Constantin “Pinca” Cantea (tuba) form a brass section on the Romani-influenced tracks. Additional vocals come from Griffin Rodriguez (of Bablicon) and a group of Albuquerque friends. The album was released in a limited LP edition; a companion single, God Bless The Ottoman Empire (HAWK 1, 2007), was issued as a promo CDr.

A Hawk And A Hacksaw And The Hun Hangár Ensemble EP (2007, DOCK 47CDVD) is a CD/DVD package recorded with the Budapest-based The Hun Hangár Ensemble — Balázs Unger (cimbalom), Ferenc Kovács (trumpet/violin), Zsolt Kürtösi (double bass), Ágoston Béla (Hungarian bagpipes, clarinet, alto saxophone). Zach Condon returns on mandolin, trumpet, and ukulele. The eight CD tracks are genre-tagged Folk, Klezmer, Népzene — the closest the project comes to direct traditional music. The DVD contains a 22-minute documentary introduction. Barnes and Trost arranged and recorded; Rodriguez mastered.

Délivrance and Transition (2008–2009)

Foni Tu Argile (2009, DOCK 53) is a limited 10”/78 RPM vinyl single — three tracks including an adapted Romani piece and two Délivrance-era tracks. It predates the full LP.

Délivrance (2009, BAY 64CD) is the most elaborate Leaf album. The Hun Hangár Ensemble members return (Unger, Kovács, Béla), joined by Kálmán Balogh (cimbalom on two tracks), Christoph Hladowski (bouzouki, baglama), Ariel Muniz (cello), Sari Kovács (flute), Péter Pataj (upright bass), and Péter Bede (saxophone). Trost plays accordion, cello, violin, viola, Stroh violin, and vocals; she co-arranges and co-writes several tracks. Rodriguez mixed. The album ranges across Hungarian, Romanian, Turkish, and Romani musical references, including a track (“Vasalisa Carries A Flaming Skull Through The Forest”) entirely written by Trost.

A promotional sampler, Selections From The Album “Délivrance” (2009, Europe), was issued as a separate entry.

Self-Releasing Era: LM Dupli-Cation (2011–2018)

After Délivrance, Barnes and Trost left Leaf and began releasing through their own imprint LM Dupli-Cation (Albuquerque). The records become more stripped-back, more purely a duo affair.

Cervantine (2011, LM 3) retains a broader ensemble: Christoph Hladowski on bouzouki, Ariel Muniz on cello, Joseph Friedman (2) on clarinet/alto sax, Elise Southwick on dobro, Issa Malluf on dumbek/riq, and a brass section of Samuel Johnson (2) and Mark Weaver. Trost and Barnes credit themselves on violin/viola and accordion respectively; Rodriguez co-recorded.

You Have Already Gone To The Other World (2013, LM 5) is a double LP exploring Hungarian, Romanian, and Ukrainian field-sourced melodic material. John Dieterich (Deerhoof) plays guitar on three tracks and co-recorded. The credit line says simply “all instruments played by Heather Trost and Jeremy Barnes” — a fully internalised duo record.

The Coral Casino (2016, LM Dupli-Cation) is listed under Barnes’s credited role as “All Sounds By” — a solo detour for Barnes separate from the duo’s main discography.

Forest Bathing (2018, LM19) is the most recent duo LP. It introduces Cüneyt Sepetçi (clarinet) and returns Balázs Unger (cimbalom). Trost and Barnes each take writing credit on separate tracks; Barnes plays an expanded battery (accordion, santur/dulcimer, organ, mellotron, autoharp, davul, tapan, riq). Trost plays violin, viola, autoharp, mellotron.

Production and Wider Collaborations

Jeremy Barnes has been an active session drummer and producer outside the duo. Notable credits include:

  • Beirut — played accordion and percussion on Gulag Orkestar (2006) and the The Way The Wind Blows album sessions
  • Swans / Michael Gira — recorded by Barnes on Leaving Meaning. (Young God Records, 2019)
  • Thor & Friends — producer and multi-instrumentalist (2016, LM Dupli-Cation)
  • Dhana Jeera — joint project with Heather Trost (2024, Cloud Recordings), which Barnes engineered and mixed
  • Various Elephant Six-adjacent artists throughout 1998–2005

Heather Trost has worked with Zach Condon on Beirut albums (per Discogs profile: “she also assisted Zach Condon on his Beirut albums”). She also records as Opion Somnium and is co-credited in Dhana Jeera (2024).

Open Questions

  • The stub references “the Hungarian State Folk Ensemble” as a separate collaborator from the Hun Hangár Ensemble — Discogs data shows only Hun Hangár; the Hungarian State Folk Ensemble credit may be web-sourced or conflated.
  • The Coral Casino (2016) is credited to Barnes alone under the LM Dupli-Cation imprint; unclear if it is considered an AHAAH release or a Barnes solo release — Discogs lists it under artist ID 241907 with role “All Sounds By” as_primary = true.
  • The first album was released in 2002 on Cloud Recordings; the stub listed 2004 / “Bay 35cd” for a presumed Leaf reissue — the Leaf cat number BAY 35CD may correspond to a later Leaf pressing of the debut. The master (74870) has 6 pressings total across both labels.
  • No Discogs compilations featuring AHAAH were found — the compilation appearance list is empty.
  • Web sources may have more detail on the Albuquerque scene context and Barnes’s time living in Budapest/Hungary.
