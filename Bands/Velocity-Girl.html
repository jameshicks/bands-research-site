Velocity Girl

American indie rock / dream pop band formed in 1989 in College Park, Maryland; associated with the Washington D.C. scene. Active 1989–1996, with reunion shows in 2002 and again in 2023–2024. Released three studio albums on Sub Pop Records and a series of 7-inch singles on Slumberland Records (which Velocity Girl members helped found) and Sub Pop Records. Also styled “The Velocity Girl” (Discogs name variation). Discogs artist ID: 378135.

The band began as the duo of Kelly Riles (later Kelly Young) and guitarist/vocalist Archie Moore, who had been a member of Black Tambourine, and who recruited Brian Nelson (also ex-Black Tambourine), Jim Spellman (of The High-Back Chairs), and vocalist Bridget Cross. The band initially went by the name The Gotterdammacrats (per Wikipedia). Cross departed after the debut single to join Unrest (2); Sarah Shannon took over lead vocals and the classic lineup was established. Wikipedia notes the band took its name from a Primal Scream B-side from the C86 compilation.

The band was noted for a steady output of 7-inch vinyl singles, shoegaze influences on their early recordings, and a shift toward melodic indie pop and clean-guitar two-part harmonies post-1993. They described their influences as the Rough Trade and Postcard labels and early Creation Records acts, as well as The Wedding Present (Wikipedia). After disbanding in 1996, Shannon, Riles, and Spellman briefly reunited as Starry Eyes, releasing one EP in 1998. Moore formed Heartworms before the split, then The Saturday People with Terry Banks.

Connections

  • Slumberland Records — label co-founded in 1989 by members of Velocity Girl and other D.C.-area bands (also including Big Jesus Trash Can (2)); the label remained Velocity Girl’s home for early singles
  • Sub Pop Records — primary label for all three studio albums and main singles
  • Black Tambourine — Archie Moore and Brian Nelson both ex-members; Black Tambourine was also on Slumberland Records
  • Chisel — split 7” on Shute Records (1997); Chisel featured Ted Leo (guitar/vocals)
  • Tsunami (6) — split 7” (Sub Pop, 1992); Virginia-based indie rock band on Simple Machines
  • Unrest (2) — Bridget Cross departed Velocity Girl to join Unrest; shared D.C. scene and Slumberland orbit
  • The High-Back Chairs — Jim Spellman’s prior band (Dischord Records)
  • Heartworms — Archie Moore post-Velocity Girl project
  • The Saturday People — Archie Moore with Terry Banks of Tree Fort Angst; on Slumberland
  • Starry Eyes — post-split project of Shannon, Riles, and Spellman; one EP (1998)
  • Robert Weston — engineer for Copacetic and early singles; also known for Chicago Mastering Service
  • John Porter — produced ¡Simpatico!; British producer known for The Smiths
  • Don Zientara — Inner Ear Studios engineer; recorded early singles
  • Eli Janney — Girls Against Boys producer/engineer; recorded My Forgotten Favorite
  • Barrett Jones — recorded debut single; also produced early Foo Fighters and Nirvana demos
  • Bridget Cross — vocalist on debut single only; later Air Miami
