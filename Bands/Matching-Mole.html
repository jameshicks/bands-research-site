Matching Mole

Discogs ID: 222802
Active: 1971–1973
Origin: UK (Canterbury scene)
Genres: Jazz-Rock, Prog Rock, Psychedelic Rock
Labels: CBS (original albums); Cuneiform Records (archival reissues); Hux Records (BBC recordings)

Canterbury scene group formed by Robert Wyatt in October 1971 after leaving Soft Machine. The name is a pun on Machine Molle, the French translation of Soft Machine. The band recorded two studio albums and toured regularly until June 1973, when Wyatt’s fall from a window at a party left him paralyzed below the waist. The group never formally dissolved but ceased operating from that point.

Formation

Wyatt departed Soft Machine after a period of creative friction in 1971, having already released his first solo album The End of an Ear (CBS, 1970). He assembled Matching Mole in October 1971:

  • Robert Wyatt — drums, vocals, mellotron, piano (and primary compositional voice)
  • David Sinclair — organ, piano (recruited from Caravan)
  • Phil Miller — guitar (formerly of Delivery)
  • Bill MacCormick — bass (formerly of Quiet Sun)

The debut album was recorded with this lineup. Dave MacRae joined as a second keyboardist (electric piano) for the debut recording sessions, appearing on the first album as a guest; he became a full member for Little Red Record.

“O Caroline” became the group’s best-known song — a gentle, affecting pop piece that stands slightly apart from the album’s more experimental material. “Signed Curtain” is notable for its self-referential text (Wyatt sings the song’s structural notes aloud rather than conventional lyrics). The album was self-produced, an unusual arrangement for a debut CBS release.

Second Album — Matching Mole’s Little Red Record (1972)

Discogs release ID: 435761 (UK first pressing)
Master ID: 15069
Label: CBS, cat. S 65260
Format: LP

The second album brought a new lineup with Dave MacRae now fully installed on keyboards, replacing David Sinclair. Notably produced by Robert Fripp of King Crimson. The title is a pun on Mao Zedong’s Little Red Book, reflecting the overtly political mood of several tracks, particularly the Wyatt/MacCormick co-write “Gloria Gloom.” Brian Eno appears on “Gloria Gloom,” playing VCS3 synthesizer — one of several early Eno guest credits in the Canterbury orbit.

The album was recorded with a vocal chorus, Der Mütter Korus (David Gale, Ruby Crystal, Little Honest Injun), adding texture to specific tracks.

David Sinclair left after the debut sessions, returning briefly to his Canterbury orbit — he went on to Hatfield And The North and Camel. Dave MacRae, a New Zealand jazz musician then based in London, stepped in as a full member for Little Red Record and brought a more overtly jazz-inflected keyboard approach.

Live and BBC Recordings

On The Radio (2007, Hux Records — HUX 083)

Discogs release ID: 3245619
Master ID: 1293688
Format: CD, Stereo + Mono

Compiled from BBC sessions, this release assembles recordings from two distinct BBC appearances: a longer session (tracks 1–5, in-studio live) and the five tracks (6–10) from the original BBC Radio 1 Live In Concert CD. Jeff Griffin produced the Radio 1 broadcasts (tracks 6–10); tracks 4 and 5 were recorded off-air by David Davies.

The detailed instrument credits on this release confirm the specific gear: Wyatt played custom Ludwig drums with Gretsch snare, Zildjian cymbals and hi-hats, using an Echoplex; MacCormick used a Gibson EB3 bass with fuzz pedal; Miller used a Gibson SG Special; MacRae played a modified Fender Rhodes with wah-wah pedal; and David Sinclair appeared on Hammond A100 Organ for tracks “Part Of The Dance” and “No ‘Alf Measures” — indicating his participation in at least some live/BBC work.

Cover design by Alfreda Benge (credited as Alfie Benge).

Dissolution

In June 1973, Wyatt fell from a fourth-floor window at a party held at Pip Pyle’s flat (Pyle was the drummer of Gong, later Hatfield And The North). The fall left him paralyzed from the waist down. He subsequently recorded Rock Bottom (1974, Virgin) — widely regarded as his masterpiece — while in Stoke Mandeville Hospital and recovery; it was produced by Nick Mason.

The surviving members of Matching Mole dispersed into other Canterbury-affiliated projects:

  • Phil Miller — Hatfield And The North (1972–1975), then National Health
  • Bill MacCormick — 801 (with Phil Manzanera, 1976), then Random Hold
  • Dave MacRae — Nucleus (3), Pacific Eardrum, session work
  • David Sinclair — Hatfield And The North, Camel

Member Profiles

Robert Wyatt

See full page: Robert Wyatt (Discogs ID: 5049)

Drummer, vocalist, composer. Real name Robert Wyatt-Ellidge. Born 28 January 1945, Bristol. Co-founder of Wilde Flowers and Soft Machine. After the accident, continued as vocalist and composer for decades — Rock Bottom (1974), Ruth Is Stranger Than Richard (1975), Rough Trade singles era (1980–1987), Old Rottenhat (1985), Dondestan (1991), Shleep (1997), Cuckooland (2003), Comicopera (2007), ‘…For The Ghosts Within’ (2010). Married Alfreda Benge in 1974, who has been his lyricist and cover artist throughout his solo career.

David Sinclair

See full page: David Sinclair (Discogs ID: 300121)

Keyboardist. Also known as Dave Sinclair. Cousin of Richard Sinclair. Founding member of Caravan. Appeared in Wilde Flowers from approximately 1966. His Hammond organ work is the sonic signature of early Caravan. Left Matching Mole after the debut album; joined Hatfield And The North and Camel. Also appeared with Hatfield And The North and Richard Sinclair’s Caravan Of Dreams.

Phil Miller

See full page: Phil Miller (Discogs ID: 300123)

Guitarist. Previously in Delivery. After Matching Mole: Hatfield And The North (1972–1975), National Health, In Cahoots, Short Wave. One of the principal composers on both Matching Mole albums and the archival recordings.

Bill MacCormick

See full page: Bill MacCormick (Discogs ID: 201110)

Bassist. Real name William MacCormick. Previously in Quiet Sun (with Phil Manzanera and Charles Hayward). After Matching Mole: 801 (Phil Manzanera’s project, 1976), Random Hold. Composed “Flora Fidgit” and co-wrote “Gloria Gloom” with Wyatt on Little Red Record.

Dave MacRae

See full page: Dave MacRae (Discogs ID: 322415)

Keyboardist, electric pianist. Real name David Scott MacRae. New Zealand-born, London-based. Before Matching Mole: member of Nucleus (3) (Ian Carr’s jazz-fusion group). After Matching Mole: Pacific Eardrum, Paz, The Mike Gibbs Band, Back Door, session work including WMWM with Wyatt. Contributed the majority of compositions to Little Red Record and the archival releases. Married Joy Yates (the duo performed as Dave MacRae & Joy Yates).
