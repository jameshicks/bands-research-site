Translator (3)

Rock band from San Francisco (and originally Los Angeles), California. Formed 1979. Discogs artist ID: 313686. (Disambiguated as “(3)” on Discogs.)

Members: Steve Barton (3) (guitar, vocals), Robert Darlington (guitar, vocals, harmonica), Larry Dekker (bass), Dave Scheff (drums).

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1979–1981)

Translator formed in 1979, initially with roots in Los Angeles before settling in San Francisco. The band coalesced around two songwriting voices: Steve Barton and Robert Darlington, both guitarist-vocalists. Their sound sat squarely in the post-punk / new wave moment — melodic, guitar-forward, with layered harmonies — but with a directness and hook-consciousness that distinguished them from more abrasive SF contemporaries. By the early 1980s they had become a fixture of the Bay Area live circuit and attracted the attention of 415 Records, the SF-based independent label distributed through Columbia that was home to Romeo Void, Rank & File, and other regional post-punk acts.

415 Records Period (1982–1983)

Translator signed to 415 Records and recorded two studio albums with producer/engineer David Kahne, who was the de facto house producer for the label. Mastering on both albums was handled by Paul Stubblebine.

Heartbeats And Triggers (1982, 415 Records / Columbia, cat. ARC 38162) was the debut LP. Barton wrote or co-wrote the majority of the material (all of side A except “Everything You See,” plus most of side B), while Darlington contributed “Everything You See” and co-wrote several cuts. The album was recorded live-to-mobile-unit via Phil Edwards Recording Services, with Phil De Lancie as remote assistant — an unusual choice that gave the album a tight, live-room feel. Cover design was by Robin A. Spear. The lead single, “Everywhere That I’m Not,” was also released on 7” (415 Records / Columbia) and later issued on a separate US promotional 12” through Columbia.

No Time Like Now (1983, 415 Records / Columbia, cat. BFC 38927) continued the Kahne collaboration. The album showed expanded sonic ambition: several tracks featured guest saxophonists — Benjamin Bossi on tenor sax on “I Heard You Follow,” Doug Wieselman on tenor sax on “Break Down Barriers” (which also featured Beverley Wilshire on square-dance calls), and Steve Berlin (then a member of Los Lobos and the Blasters) on soprano sax on “The End Of Their Love.” Darlington also played harmonica throughout. Additional engineering was by Ken Kessie, with Maureen Droney as assistant engineer. The album cover was a collaboration between Darlington and photographer Trudy Fisher, who also shot the cover of the debut. Management at this time was through First Base Management and Alan Mintz.

Singles from the 415 era include the “Everywhere That I’m Not” / “When I Am With You” 7” (1982), “Break Down Barriers” (1983), and “Un-Alone” (1983). The BBC recorded the band for their College Concert series in 1982, hosted by Pete Larkin — one of the earliest documented UK broadcast appearances for an SF new wave act of this generation.

Columbia and the Self-Titled Album (1985)

By 1985, Translator had moved from 415 (still a Columbia distributed imprint) to Columbia proper. Their self-titled third album Translator (1985, Columbia, cat. BFC 39984) marked a change in producer: Ed Stasium replaced Kahne, and the record was engineered by Fred Stadium (a Stasium alias). The album reflected a mid-decade sound shift — more polished alternative rock, slightly less raw than the 415 material. Singles from this period include “Come With Me” / “Dizzy Miss Lizzy” (1985) and “Evening Of The Harvest” (1986).

A retrospective compilation, Everywhere That I’m Not – A Retrospective, was issued in 1986 on 415 / Columbia while the band was still nominally active.

Hiatus and Compilation (1986–2004)

The band went on hiatus after the self-titled album failed to break commercially. In 1996, Columbia’s Legacy imprint issued Everywhere That We Were: The Best Of Translator (415 Records / Columbia / Legacy), a comprehensive compilation produced by Don DeVito and covering tracks from all three studio albums. The compilation credits Kahne as producer on the 415-era tracks and Stasium on the Columbia-era tracks. Mastering was by Vic Anesini; photography again by Trudy Fisher.

During the hiatus, Dave Scheff played with Winter Hours and later Bounty (4); Larry Dekker went on to Map Of Wyoming; Steve Barton (3) participated in the project Waking Bartonomous.

Reunion and Later Recordings (2005–present)

Translator reunited and self-released Different Time (2005, Not On Label) — credited on Discogs with no additional personnel beyond the band. Sometimes People Forget (2015) was released on Omnivore Recordings, the LA-based reissue and new-releases label. Carriage Of Days (2017, self-released) features the classic four-piece lineup: Barton (vocals, guitar, written-by), Darlington (vocals, guitar), Dekker (bass), Scheff (drums). A live album, Translator Live! (2022, self-released), followed.
