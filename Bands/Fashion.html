Fashion

British new wave / post-punk band from Birmingham, England. Discogs artist ID: 47966. Active 1978–1984, with a brief revival in 2009 and reissue activity 2019–2022.

Overview

Fashion formed in Birmingham around 1978 as a post-punk outfit operating on their own self-distributed label, Fàshiön Music. The early lineup — Lûke (Alan Victor James) on guitar/vocals, Mulligan (Johnathan Salvator Mulligan) on bass/keyboards, and Dik Davis (Richard Davies) on drums/vocals — produced the debut LP Pröduct Perfect (1979) with producer Miki Cottrell and distributed through Faulty Products. The band’s sound in this phase mixed angular post-punk riffs with DIY funk rhythm structures and a notably irreverent typographic identity (heavy use of diacritics in all names and titles).

By 1981 the lineup had shifted. Lûke departed after Pröduct Perfect and moved to the US; Dave Harris (credited as De Harris) became the primary vocalist and guitarist. Marlon Recchi joined on bass. The band signed to Arista and began working with German producer-keyboardist Zeus B. Held, who had built a reputation in the European electronic and new wave scene. The Arista period yielded Fabrique (1982), the band’s major-label LP and their most commercially-oriented effort, blending synth-pop textures with a persistent funk undertow. Three singles charted in the UK lower reaches: “Move On,” “Eye Talk” (1983), and “Dreaming” (1984).

The second album, Twilight Of Idols (1984), appeared on De Stijl (2) / Epic, again with Zeus B. Held producing. By this point Alan Darby had replaced Dave Harris as guitarist and primary songwriter. The record leaned fully into electro-influenced synth-pop. The band dissolved around 1984.

Compilations drew on the catalog throughout the 1990s–2010s, primarily via Cherry Red (The Height Of Fashiøn, 2001, 2014) and Gonzo Multimedia (Fabrique De Luxe box set, 2021). Easy Action released Fàshiön Music, a vinyl compilation of early material, in 2022. Lûke published a memoir, Stairway To Nowhere (Brummie Git Press, 2010), about the band’s early years.

Alias / Name Variation Notes

The band used heavily stylized spellings throughout their career:

  • Fàshiön Music — name used for the self-run label and early releases (1978–1979)
  • Fashiøn / Fàshiön / Fáshiön / Fashiøn Music — variant spellings across releases
  • Alan Victor & The Victims — listed as an alias on Discogs; pre-Fashion or parallel project

The self-run label (Fàshiön Music, id 75853) is distinct from the band name/alias.

Research Queue

  • On Pröduct Perfect (1979), Al James (16) is credited as “Lûke” playing guitar and lead vocals, while Lûke (id 665303) is a separate Discogs entry for Alan Victor James. Are these truly duplicate entries for the same person, or did two different members use the “Lûke” credit at different points?
  • Dave Harris later formed Zee (3) with Richard Wright (Pink Floyd) — the album Identity was released in 1984. Did Fashion’s dissolution contribute directly to the timing of that project, or was Zee running in parallel with Fashion’s final period?
  • Alan Darby’s Discogs profile states he replaced Dave Harris “1983/1984.” Was there an overlap period where both appeared on sessions, or was the transition clean between Fabrique and Twilight Of Idols?
  • Troy Tate and Tony Deary are listed as Fashion members in Discogs but appear on no specific releases in the DB. Were these live-only members, or do they appear on releases under different credits?
  • The “Blitz scene orbit” association with Steve Strange: did Fashion play the Blitz club or appear on Steve Strange-curated releases? No direct Discogs link is surfaced in the current data.
  • Fabrique (1982) was recorded in part in Paris (Christian Gence engineered there) and in part in London (Femi Jiya). Was the Paris recording at a specific studio? One source should be able to confirm this.
  • “Alan Victor & The Victims” appears as a Fashion alias on Discogs — is this an early name for the band before “Fashion,” or a side project? No releases are documented under this name.
