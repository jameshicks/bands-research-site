Ten City

Chicago house vocal group. Discogs artist ID: 5310. Active 1987–1996; reformed 2021.

Overview

Ten City were one of the few Chicago house acts to cross over into mainstream R&B visibility during the late 1980s and early 1990s. The trio of Byron Stingily (lead vocals), Herb Lawson (guitar), and Byron Burke (keyboards/programming) originally performed as Ragtyme before signing to Atlantic Records in 1987. Their name also appears in the Discogs alias list as Burstinglaw — a portmanteau of the three surnames used for their publishing/production entity (Burstinglaw Music Productions). Under producer Marshall Jefferson, they recorded a debut single “Devotion” (1987) and then their debut LP Foundation (1989), fusing house rhythms with gospel-inflected soul, live horns, strings, and Stingily’s powerful tenor.

Atlantic originally wanted to sign Stingily as a solo artist; he insisted on bringing in Lawson and Burke. The trio released two Atlantic LPs (Foundation, 1989; State of Mind, 1990), then moved to the EastWest Records America imprint (No House Big Enough, 1992) before signing to Columbia. Their Columbia album That Was Then, This Is Now (1994) drew in a wider set of collaborators — Masters At Work, Larry Heard, Kerri Chandler — but failed to break through commercially. The group dissolved around 1994–1996; a final pair of singles appeared on Spiritual Life Music in 1996. Byron Stingily went on to a prolific solo career on Nervous Records.

Ten City reformed in 2021 with Stingily and Jefferson only — Lawson and Burke were not involved. The reunion album Judgement (Ultra Records, 2021) was recorded remotely during lockdown (Jefferson in Manchester, Stingily in Chicago). A sixth album Love Is Love (Helix Records (7), 2023) followed.

History

Origins: Ragtyme and the Trax Records connection (mid-1980s)

Stingily met Marshall Jefferson at Trax Records after Jefferson heard his vocals on a Dezz 7 track (“Funny Love”). The two recorded approximately five songs together (unreleased), then produced “Devotion” — Ten City’s debut single. Stingily had been working with Lawson and Burke under the name Ragtyme. Atlantic’s offer of a solo deal was declined; instead the full trio signed as Ten City.

Atlantic era: Foundation and State of Mind (1987–1990)

“Devotion” (Atlantic, 1987) — debut single. Produced and arranged by Marshall Jefferson, co-written by Stingily and Jefferson. Appeared in multiple US and UK pressings.

“Right Back to You” / “One Kiss Will Make It Better” (Atlantic, 1988) — follow-up single.

“That’s the Way Love Is” (Atlantic, 1989) — lead single from Foundation. Written by Burke, Stingily, and Lawson; produced by Jefferson and Ten City. Features live acoustic drums by Earl Young, with mixing by Timmy Regisford. Charted in R&B and dance markets.

“Where Do We Go?” (Atlantic, 1989) — co-produced by Stingily and Guy Vaughn; mixed by Steve “Silk” Hurley.

“Suspicious” (Atlantic, 1989) — written by Stingily and Jefferson; mixed by Marshall Jefferson.

Foundation (Atlantic, 1989, cat# 81939-1) — debut LP. Produced by Marshall Jefferson (most tracks) and Ten City (co-production on several). Burke handled drum programming, acoustic piano, synthesizer, and synthesizer bass, plus horn arrangements (shared with Jefferson). Lawson played lead guitar, rhythm guitar, and bass. Full string and horn sections: brass arranged by Burke and Jefferson; strings/vocals by Stingily and Jefferson. Additional percussion from Dave Josias. Backing vocals include all three members. Executive producers: DeWayne A. Powell and Merlin Bobb. Cover illustration by Don Brautigam. Mastered by Dennis King. Engineers included Iain Burgess, Tom Hanson, Julian Herzfeld, Steve Spapperi, Chris Cuben-Tatum, and Grant Austin.

Track-level collaborators:

  • “That’s the Way Love Is”: additional backing vocals by Eddie Stockley and Kenny Bobien; mixed by Timmy Regisford
  • “Where Do We Go?”: additional backing vocals by Shawn Christopher, Kenny Bobien, Eddie Stockley; produced and written by Stingily and Guy Vaughn; mixed by Steve “Silk” Hurley
  • “Suspicious”: written by Stingily and Jefferson; mixed by Jefferson
  • “Right Back to You”: mixed by Steve “Silk” Hurley
  • “For You”: mixed by Burke (solo credit)
  • “Devotion”: produced solely by Stingily; mixed by Jefferson

State of Mind (Atlantic, 1990, cat# 7 82146-2) — second LP. Produced by Jefferson and Ten City. Stingily handles lead vocals throughout; Burke and Lawson take additional lead vocals. A large backing choir appears across the record, including Shawn Christopher, Yvonne Gage, Kenny Bobien, Renee Mohannon, Vicky Ryan, Lonnie Polk, Mark Ingram, Vanessa Goss, David Josias, Reggie Hall, and Grant. Executive producers: Merlin Bobb and Sylvia Rhone. Track “Heartache” has a co-production credit for Rick Brown. Genres: Electronic / Garage House. Full track listing: “State of Mind,” “Livin’ Easy,” “Whatever Makes You Happy,” “I Should Learn to Love You,” “Destiny,” “Nothing’s Changed” (Instrumental), “Superficial People,” “Put Love Where You Want It,” “It Ain’t No Big Thing,” “Nothing’s Changed,” “Heartache.”

Singles from State of Mind: “Whatever Makes You Happy,” “Superficial People,” “I Should Learn to Love You,” “Nothing’s Changed.”

EastWest era: No House Big Enough (1992)

Atlantic dropped Ten City after State of Mind. The trio moved to the EastWest Records America imprint (Atlantic subsidiary) for their third LP.

No House Big Enough (EastWest Records America, 1992) — third LP. Singles included “My Peace Of Heaven,” “Only Time Will Tell,” and “No House Big Enough.” Production details in the main Discogs record are sparse (the primary LP credits do not list additional personnel). “My Peace Of Heaven” credits note Production Coordinator Jerome Sydenham and engineer Gary Wright; “Only Time Will Tell” was released across multiple US and European pressings. The Discogs genres list this as Electronic / Garage House / House.

Columbia era: That Was Then, This Is Now (1993–1994)

Atlantic released Ten City from their contract. A new deal with Columbia followed.

“Fantasy” (Columbia, 1993) — lead Columbia single and the group’s biggest post-Atlantic success. Written and produced by Ten City; the 12” version added production by Masters At Work (Louie Vega and Kenny “Dope” Gonzalez) and remixes credited to them. Burke on keyboards, Lawson on guitar, Stingily on vocals. Engineering on the MAW remixes by David Darlington and Phil Pagano; editing by Mike Delgado.

That Was Then, This Is Now (Columbia, 1994, cat# C 57183) — fourth LP. Tracklist: “Fantasy (Demo Version),” “The Way You Make Me Feel,” “Goin’ Up In Smoke,” “When I’m Gone, I’m Gone,” “Love In A Day,” “What My Love Can Do,” “Interlude: Then,” “Under You,” “All This Love,” “Joy & Pain,” “All I Want,” “Say Something,” “Fantasy (Funky Ginger Mix),” “Interlude: Now.” The UK sampler pressing reveals track-specific production credits:

The album marked a widening of Ten City’s sonic palette toward contemporary R&B and soul-house, bridging house scene credibility with mainstream structures. It did not translate to commercial success, and the group soon dissolved.

“Goin’ Up In Smoke” (Columbia, 1994) — additional single.

Winding down: Spiritual Life Music and Ibadan (1995–2001)

After the Columbia period, Ten City released material through smaller labels:

  • “All Loved Out” (Spiritual Life Music, 1996) — exec-produced by Jerome Sydenham and Joe Claussell; mixed by Joe Claussell; percussion by José “Cochise” Claussell.
  • “Nothing’s Changed” (Spiritual Life Music, 1996) — same executive producers; mixed and additionally produced by Joe Claussell; acoustic guitar by Carlos Roberto Ferreira, rhythm guitar by Johny Sender. Written by Burke, Stingily, and Lawson.
  • “Nothing’s Changed (Joe Claussell Remixes)” (Ibadan, 1996) — remix 12”.
  • “Whatever Makes You Happy (The Kerri Chandler Remixes)” (Ibadan, 1996) — Kerri Chandler remixes on the Ibadan label.

Ibadan (run by Jerome Sydenham) subsequently became the home for Ten City reissues and catalog releases:

  • Dub Life Volume 1 (Ibadan, 1998) — dub/instrumental versions from the catalog
  • Suspicious (Ibadan, 1998) — reissue of the 1989 single
  • My Peace of Heaven (Ibadan, 1997/1998) — reissues
  • All Loved Out (Ibadan, 1997) — reissue
  • Classics 1–4 (Ibadan, 2001) — four-volume retrospective series
  • The Best Of Ten City (Ibadan, 2001 / 2010)

There was also a one-off appearance on Nervous Records: Ten City are credited as producers (uncredited on some pressings) on “Time For Love” (Nervous Records, 1995) — a Blaze recording on which Ten City contributed production and writing. The track appeared on multiple labels internationally (Hi Life Recordings UK, More Vinyl France).

Non-album ten city credits (compilation appearances and guest features)

  • “Hypnotised” (Parlophone, 1989) — Ten City credited as Backing Vocals / Featuring on this UK single by an uncredited artist. Multiple UK pressings across 1989 confirm the credit.
  • “Day To Day (Blaze Remixes)” (EMI USA, 1989) — Ten City credited as Producer on this Blaze release; Burke/Stingily as writers.
  • “U + Me = Love” (Atlantic, 1989) — Ten City credited as Remix.
  • “This Is Your Life” (RCA, Spain, 1989) — Ten City credited as Co-producer and Remix.
  • “Choices” (Atlantic, 1992) — Ten City credited as Producer.
  • “Privately” (BPM King Street Sounds, 1995) — Ten City credited as Producer.

Reunion era (2021–present)

Ten City reformed as a duo of Stingily and Jefferson. Burke and Lawson were not involved in either album.

Judgement (Ultra Records, 2021) — reunion LP. Recorded remotely during COVID lockdown. Spawned several singles including “Be Free” (with Emmaculate and Shannon Chambers) and “Feel It Too.” “Devotion” received a new remix from Illyus & Barrientos (Ultra, 2021) and a Louie Vega remix of “That’s the Way Love Is” appeared (Ultra US, 2021).

Love Is Love (Helix Records (7), 2023) — sixth album. Singles included “A Girl Named Phil” (Helix, 2022), “Love Is Love” (Helix, 2022), and “I Love Me” (Helix, 2023). The “Love Is Love” single received a DRAMA remix. Various other remixes of “That’s the Way Love Is” continued to appear: Soul Avengerz (Ultra US, 2023), Divorce From New York & PIEK (Buenobueno Edits, Spain, 2023).

The Next Generation (Nervous Records, 2025) — a further album on Nervous Records.

Open Questions

  • That Was Then, This Is Now (1994) main US pressing has no additional credits in Discogs; the UK sampler (cat# XPR 2037) reveals the co-producers above, but this is not a complete picture.

  • “Devotion” (1987) — the Discogs profile notes approximately five pre-Devotion recordings made with Jefferson that remain unreleased.
  • Byron Burke’s alias “B Rude, Inc.” on DJ International — no Discogs releases confirmed under that exact entity.
  • “Rejoice” (Big Beat, 1992) — Ten City credited as “Presenter” on this compilation; unclear what the credit entails.
