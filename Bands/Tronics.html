Tronics

Discogs ID: 916505 (listed as “Tronics (2)”) Also known as: The Tronics Active: 1978–c.1983 (original run); reissue activity 2001, 2013 Country: UK (London — Earls Court, SW5) Genres: Punk, New Wave, Lo-Fi, Art Rock Styles tagged on Discogs: Punk, New Wave, Lo-Fi, Art Rock, Glam, Psychedelic, Rockabilly, No Wave, Indie Pop

Overview

Tronics were a UK punk/post-punk band operating out of Earls Court, London from 1978 into the early 1980s. They were a self-contained operation run primarily by the songwriter/vocalist known as Ziro Baby, who wrote all words and music, produced the recordings, and ran the band’s own Tronics Label out of a personal address (4 Philbeach Gardens, Earls Court, London SW5 — registered to one Angela Marvin). The band’s approach was do-it-yourself in the most literal sense: no management, no established label deal, and a sound that drew on punk’s raw energy while pulling in glam, lo-fi, and art-rock directions that anticipated later post-punk eclecticism.

Their records are notable for deliberately provocative titles (“Shark Fucks”, “Love Backed By Force”) and track names that carry the period’s confrontational attitude. The self-titled 1980 cassette release reportedly included “a few assorted sound effects, one track being 15 mins of war — guns, planes, bombs” (as noted in Zigzag Cassette Book 1981), suggesting an experimental edge beyond the basic punk format.

The band was reissued twice in the 2000s–2010s: a 2001 CD on Wrench Records and a 2013 US vinyl compilation on M’lady’s Records (remastered by Warren Defever of His Name Is Alive), indicating the band found retrospective cult audiences outside the UK.

Release Detail

”Favorite Girls / Suzie” (1978, T. 001)

Debut 7” on self-run Tronics Label. Personnel confirmed by Discogs:

  • Ziro Baby — Written-By
  • Glenn Evans (5) — Drums
  • Rick Gregerson — Guitar
  • Denis Blackham (BilBo) — Mastered By

Matrix etchings: “T + 001 + A OR BILBO COTTON LUNG” / “T + 001 + B OR BILBO TRONICS"

"Goodbye / Time Off” (1979, T002)

Second 7”. Ziro Baby sole credited writer on both tracks (credited per-track). Matrix includes “EG” stamp, suggesting cutting at a commercial facility.

Tronics cassette (1980, T0003)

Seven-track cassette on Tronics Label. Tracklist: “Time Off”, “Favorite Girls”, “Look At You”, “Goodbye”, “She Goes Crazy”, “Rock Around”, “Suzy”. Includes re-recordings of the first two singles’ A and B sides. The Zigzag Cassette Book 1981 entry describes it as “produced by Ziro Baby, contains songs and a few assorted sound effects, one track being 15 mins of war - guns, planes, bombs."

"Shark Fucks / Time Off” (1981, Alien18)

7” on Alien Records (7). “Time Off” appears here as a B-side as well as the 1980 cassette, suggesting it was a persistent repertoire piece. Styles: Art Rock, Punk.

Love Backed By Force (1981, BEALIEN 3)

Full LP on Alien Records (7). The most elaborate release of the original run: 15 tracks across two sides (headed “Side Odin” and “Side DVA”). Personnel:

  • Ziro Baby — Performer, Written-By
  • Gaby De Vivienne — Performer
  • Diamond Eyes (2) — Producer
  • Harold (26) — Ziro Baby’s hair

Track titles include “Charlie Manson”, “T.V. On In Bed”, “Ice Flod Festival”, “Ultra Pol”, “Min Dama”, “Love Tan (In A Two Way Circus)”, “Le Monde” — a mix of English, Danish/Scandinavian, and French titles, and references both banal and provocative.

”Wild Cat Rock / Tonight” (1983, RED 31)

Final original-run single, on Red Rhino Records (York indie powerhouse; part of The Cartel distribution network). Credited as The Tronics. Pressed in mono. Matrix etchings include “I BOOGIED WITH BOOGIE” and “ZAR JAZ”, the latter possibly a Ziro Baby reference (his alias is “The Zarjaz”). Mastered by Porky (5) (George Peckham). Styles: Art Rock, Punk, Rockabilly.

Band History (confirmed by multiple sources)

Tronics began in 1977 as The Gits, a thrash/punk outfit. Early lineup: Ziro Baby (vocals; calling himself “Ronnie Git”), Rick Gregorson (guitar), Glenn Evans (drums), Phil Anderson (bass). They debuted at Westfield College, played London club and pub gigs, then Anderson was replaced by Rick Kenyer. Source: Last.fm Tronics biography; NTS artist page; Wikipedia (as of 2025-12-30).

Demos and the first single were recorded at Gooseberry Studios, Soho. Source: NTS artist page; Last.fm.

Debut single (“Favorite Girls / Suzie”) was produced by John Edwards (connected to Shel Talmy, The Who’s producer), and Rough Trade’s Geoff Travis “encouraged and partly funded the pressing.” Source: Obatik.com review; search result summary attributing to The Quietus interview. Note: Discogs credits only Denis Blackham (mastering) and Ziro Baby (written-by) — the John Edwards production credit and Geoff Travis funding do not appear in Discogs. Conflict/gap: This may mean Edwards is an uncredited session producer or the claim comes only from the Quietus interview; treat as unverified until a primary source can be consulted.

After Glenn Evans departed (to join the Reds), the original lineup fell apart. Zarjaz regrouped with new musicians, drawing increasingly on his side project The Cathedrals, which pushed the sound toward more complex arrangements and “bizarre artistry.” Source: NTS; Last.fm.

By the time of Love Backed By Force (1981), Tronics had contracted to a duo: Zarjaz and Gaby De Vivienne. Source: NTS; Unbreakable Records blog.

Dissolution: Tronics broke up in 1984 due to “hard drugs & personal differences.” Source: Unbreakable Records blog — single-source; treat as soft.

Sound and Genre

Web sources converge on a clearer picture than Discogs tags alone:

  • Self-coined term: Zarjaz describes his style as “baroquabilly” — a fusion of synthesized baroque music (Wendy Carlos-influenced) with rock and roll. Source: Jigsaw Underground interview, 2012; Katana2023 blog.
  • Clockwork Orange/Droog influence: Described as “the first Droog influenced band,” going beyond surface Clockwork Orange imagery. Source: Katana2023.
  • Sonic character on Love Backed By Force: “Simple pop songs” with “rock guitars alongside nursery rhyme melodies with repetitive lyrics delivered deadpan”; “discordant melodies, candied guitars and minimal rhythms made up of bongos, tambourines and handclaps”; “warped keys sound pulled from a ‘70 sci-fi flick” on the opener; “primal mix of competing riffs, a bongo backbeat.” Sources: Agit Reader; Gilded Gutter blog.
  • Retrospective comparison points: Left Banke and Television Personalities (predecessors); Beat Happening and Comet Gain (successors). Influenced “a legion of UK indie bands who came after, from the Smiths on down.” Source: Agit Reader; Gilded Gutter.
  • Comparative refs (What’s the Hubub Bub): Described as “in the style of Cleaners From Venus or Swell Maps.” Source: Jigsaw Records product page.
  • Influences cited by Zarjaz: Marc Bolan/T. Rex, New York Dolls, Johnny Burnette, Buddy Holly, the Ronettes, Jimi Hendrix, Velvet Underground. Favourite bands of the era: Ramones, Teenage Jesus, Red Balune. Source: Wikipedia (as of 2025-12-30); Jigsaw Underground interview.
  • Fact magazine on Love Backed By Force: “a bizarre, brilliant DIY record.” AllMusic: 4 out of 5 stars. Source: Wikipedia Love Backed by Force stub (as of 2025-12-30).

Scene Context

  • Venues: Zarjaz auditioned at the Speakeasy Club (48 Margaret Street, London) in 1978; played “several club and pub gigs” in the punk circuit. Jigsaw Underground interview.
  • London scene: Part of the Kings Road punk scene in the late 1970s, including documented conflicts between Teds/Rockers and Punks on Saturday gatherings. Jigsaw Underground interview.
  • Distribution: Primarily through Rough Trade’s distribution network. “Independent really meant independent” — Zarjaz’s characterization. Jigsaw Underground interview.
  • NME, 1982: Reported that “gangs of Hells Angels followed Tronics and were seen lining the back of Tronics audiences.” Source: Wikipedia (as of 2025-12-30).
  • Melody Maker called Zarjaz “the essence of Rock n Roll.” Source: Katana2023; Dischord/M’lady’s product page.
  • Alan McGee / Creation Records (1983): McGee approached Zarjaz in 1983 wanting him to “redo Tronics.” This did not result in a Tronics release; instead, Zarjaz later released a 45 on Creation as Les Zarjaz (“One Charming Nite” b/w “My Baby Owns A Fallout Zone”). Source: Jigsaw Underground interview; search result summary.
  • Tony James (Sigue Sigue Sputnik): Zarjaz worked with Tony James around 1982, recording demos together. He later claimed James plagiarised his “futuristic Clockwork Orange concept” for what became Sigue Sigue Sputnik. Source: Jigsaw Underground interview — single source, Zarjaz’s own account.
  • Sic Alps / Drag City: Tronics devotees Sic Alps (Mike Donovan) released Tronics covers on Drag City; a split single with Zarjaz’s Freakapuss also appeared on Drag City. Source: Psychedelic Baby Mag interview; NTS.

Gaby De Vivienne

An actress with no prior involvement in music, Gaby de Vivienne was recruited by Zarjaz after he approached her on Bond Street. She had a “try out rehearsal and she was in.” Described as approximately 17 at the time of recording. After Tronics, she “went back to playing Shakespeare on a big stage.” Source: Jigsaw Underground interview; age detail from Unbreakable Records blog.

What’s the Hubub Bub cassette (1980 / 1981)

Web sources mention a second cassette, What’s The Hubub Bub, as a 1980 or 1981 release on Tronics Label — separate from the Tronics cassette (T0003). It was reissued on vinyl by M’lady’s Records in 2013 (alongside Say! What Is This?). The Discogs article as researched by trawler listed the What’s The Hubub Bub CD (WRENCH 17, 2001) but not a distinct original cassette release — this may be a gap in the Discogs catalog, or the Wrench CD may have been the first proper release. Needs Discogs re-check by discogs-trawler. Source: Jigsaw Records product listing; NTS.

M’lady’s Records / What’s Your Rupture? reissues (2012–2013)

The reissue process involved label What’s Your Rupture? (US indie, run by Kevin Pedersen) as well as M’lady’s Records. Zarjaz says he was persuaded to permit reissues “about a year” before the 2012 Jigsaw interview, after years of withholding. Say! What Is This? (MLADYS 10) carries a U-Card quote by Mike Donovan (of Sic Alps) and song info/dates written by Ziro Baby himself. Liner notes full text not retrieved — M’lady’s/Dischord product page contains no quote text. Source: Jigsaw Underground interview; Dischord product page.

Conflict Notes

  • Alien Records founder: One web source (search result summary from AllMusic/Wikipedia) states “Baby started Alien Records to distribute Tronics,” implying self-ownership. Discogs lists Alien Records (7) as releasing other artists (Rexy, Colour Vision (2), Bruce Kato) alongside Tronics, suggesting it was an independent label that also housed other acts, not purely a Tronics vanity label. Resolution unclear — possible that Zarjaz started it and released others through it, or that it was an unrelated label that signed Tronics.
  • Release date for Love Backed By Force: Discogs says 1981; Wikipedia stub and some web sources say 1980. Discogs is likely authoritative here given matrix/catalog evidence.
  • “Suzie’s Vibrator” vs “Favorite Girls”: Some web sources name the A-side as “Suzie’s Vibrator”; Discogs credits “Favorite Girls” as the A-side and “Suzie” as the B-side. 45cat confirms T001 as “Favorite Girls / Suzie.” The variation may reflect a working title or early listing error.
