The Sorts

Washington, D.C. post-rock band, formed autumn 1994. Discogs artist ID: 1605068. Active 1994–2002; released six albums across five labels.

Overview

The Sorts emerged from the remnants of the DC post-hardcore orbit — drummer Christopher Farrall had played in Hoover (3) and singer/guitarist Joshua Larue had previously drummed in Austin’s Jack O’Fire and DC’s Rain Like The Sound Of Trains. The original trio of Larue (guitar), Stuart Fletcher (2) (bass), and Farrall (drums) debuted in late 1995 with Common Time on Atlas (3). Their second record, This Is… Gateway Sounds (1996), was produced by Tim Kerr and released on the German label Spring Records (4) — the same label that had put out Rain Like The Sound Of Trains’ Incoming (1995), a direct scene connection.

Over subsequent albums the group expanded its palette considerably. Fred Erskine (of Hoover (3) and June Of 44) contributed trumpet on More There (1998, Slowdime). That same year, The Hawaiian Bronco (Aesthetics) brought in guest saxophonist Carlo Cennamo and Erskine again, and showcased Larue’s multi-instrumental range (guitar, bass, trombone, electric piano across different tracks). Contemporary Music (1999, Slowdime) saw the group expand to a five-piece with the addition of Vin Novara (keyboards, electric piano, percussion, synthesizer) — formerly drummer in The Crownhate Ruin. The final record, Six Plus (2002, Luckyhorse Industries), included former Hoover / Crownhate Ruin guitarist Joseph McRedmond and was released on CD, marking a departure from the all-vinyl run of earlier records.

A parallel project, Sea Tiger, operated concurrently: Stuart Fletcher and Larue recording instrumentally in their home studio, with roles reversed — Fletcher on guitar, Larue on drums. Sea Tiger released two records on Troubleman Unlimited (1996, 1998) and a single on Southern Records (1997), and played live as a five-piece adding Farrall, McRedmond, and David Batista.

Common Time (1995, Atlas (3)) — Release ID: 2648906

Cover photography by Stuart Fletcher (2); insert photos by Kathe Hashimoto. Genres logged on Discogs: Funk / Soul, Jazz, Rock; style: Avant-garde Jazz, Indie Rock.

Tracks: “The Marauder” / “Powerful Mad” / “The Decil Came” / “Hesitation Pitch” // “I Said You” / “Brown Bird” / “My Astronomy”

This Is… Gateway Sounds (1996, Spring Records (4)) — Release ID: 2650353

Ten tracks. Produced by Tim Kerr. Engineered by Paul Stautinger. Layout by Kristin Thomson. Photography by Pat Graham. Personnel: Larue, Farrall, Stuart Fletcher (2). Released in Germany on Spring Records (4) — the label had previously issued Rain Like The Sound Of Trains’ Incoming (1995, Spring 04), connecting the two Larue-associated DC acts.

Tracks: “Great Caesar” / “Turnabout Is Fairplay” / “On Paper” / “Outsides” / “Champion Sounds Believe It” // “Chestnut” / “Great Caesar’s Ghost” / “Scientific American” / “A Lot Of Prayers” / “Taco’s Moonwalk”

The Hawaiian Bronco (1998, Aesthetics) — Release ID: 1975299

Six-track 12” mini-album. Recorded in part by Juan Carrera (A1, A2, B1, B3), Paul Stautinger (B2), and Tim Kerr (B2); A3 self-recorded. Mixed by Juan Carrera and The Sorts. Photography: Cynthia Connolly.

  • Farrall: drums/percussion (all), keyboards (A3)
  • Carlo Cennamo: saxophone (A1, B1)
  • Fred Erskine: trumpet (A1, B1)

Tracks: “Aesthetics-Style” / “Still Got Lost” / “Magnet Dub” // “New Drawn Bounds” / “Unfair Play” / “Drummer; Drummer (Disc-Go-mix)“

Contemporary Music (1999, Slowdime) — Release ID: 2559937

Seven tracks. First record featuring Vin Novara (electric piano, percussion, synthesizer). Recorded and mixed by Juan Carrera. Mastered by John Golden. Layout: Jake Kump. Back cover art: Tim Kerr. Insert drawings: Dan Bodner. Ken Schweitzer on congas.

Tracks B2 and B3 are covers: Ornette Coleman’s “Una Muy Bonita” (B2) and Kool & the Gang’s “Let The Music Take Your Mind” (B3, written-by credit to G. Redd and Kool And The Gang).

Tracks: “Ninth Wonder” / “Clint” / “AstroNut” / “StarCastle” // “Pete’s Cafe” / “Una Muy Bonita” / “Let The Music Take Your Mind”

Six Plus (2002, Luckyhorse Industries) — Release ID: 2893507

Eight tracks. CD-only. Expanded lineup including Joseph McRedmond. No individual credits listed on Discogs. Style: Indie Rock, Math Rock.

Tracks: “Reporting the Report” / “Erase the Spice” / “Swamp Cooler” / “Clusters” / “Bob James” / “Rapatrol” / “South Carolina” / “Robot Seagulls”
