Shriekback

English post-punk / art-funk group. Formed 1981 in Kentish Town, London. Discogs artist ID: 25725.

Active: 1981–c. 1992 (first run), 2002–present (ongoing).

Overview

Shriekback was founded in 1981 by Dave Allen (Discogs: 842383), bassist and co-founder of Gang of Four, alongside Barry Andrews (Discogs: 64092), keyboardist and former XTC member, and Carl Marsh (Discogs: 102207), guitarist/vocalist. The band built a dense, polyrhythmic sound combining post-punk austerity, funk bass, synthesizers, and art-song lyricism — a style distinct from the new wave mainstream and rooted in the same art-school punk axis as the founders’ previous bands.

Their creative center across the entire catalog was Andrews, who became the only original member to participate in every phase of activity. Allen left before Go Bang! (1988) but returned briefly for Sacred City (1992) and again from 2002 onward. Marsh departed after Oil and Gold (1985) but came back as a guest from 2010 and full-time again from 2015. Drummer Martyn Barker (Discogs: 270605) joined in 1984 and became a core member through to the present.

Biography by Era

Formation and Y Records Period (1981–1984)

Shriekback emerged from the post-Gang of Four dissolving. Allen had left Gang of Four in 1981 shortly after Solid Gold; Andrews had already departed XTC in 1979 after their debut LP. The two met and formed Shriekback with Marsh — the name an archaic English word for the shrieks of bats (though the band have offered multiple explanations over the years).

Their first recordings appeared on Y Records (Discogs: 5338), the Bristol-rooted post-punk label run by Disc O’Dell that also carried The Pop Group, The Slits, and Maximum Joy. The early EPs established the Shriekback template: taut, low-slung funk bass figures from Allen, hypnotic synthesizer patterns from Andrews, Marsh’s guitar adding texture and slide, with dense, often surreal lyrical content.

Key early releases:

  • My Spine Is the Bassline (1982, Y Records) — debut single; established the bass-heavy art-funk identity
  • Tench (1982, Y Records) — debut mini-LP; 10 tracks
  • Care (1983, Y Records) — first full LP; produced with Ian Caple (production assistance); tracklist includes “Lined Up,” “Cleartrails,” “Hapax Legomena,” “Sway”
  • Lined Up (1983, Y Records) — single; Andrews / Marsh / Allen all share producer credit

The early catalog was also distributed in the US via Y America. A US compilation, The Infinite (1984, Kaz Records), gathered highlights from this period and introduced the band to American alternative radio.

Arista and Island Records Period (1984–1988)

In 1984 the band moved to Arista for Jam Science, produced by Shriekback and Paul “Groucho” Smykle, with Ian Caple engineering. Drummer Martyn Barker formally joined the lineup at this point, having toured with the band and played sessions before. Barry Andrews and Carl Marsh co-write the bulk of the material, with Barker credited on two tracks.

The Nemesis single (1985, Arista) became their most widely-heard release, charting in the UK and receiving sustained college-radio play in the US. The album Oil and Gold (1985, Arista/Island) featured Marsh’s final full-LP contribution; the lineup for that record was Andrews (keyboards, synthesizer, vocals), Allen (bass), Marsh (guitar, vocals), Barker (drums), and Lu Edmonds (guitar) — with Gavin MacKillop mixing and multiple engineers including Hans Zimmer credited. Shortly after touring Oil and Gold, Marsh departed to pursue solo work.

Big Night Music (1986, Island Records) was recorded as a trio of Andrews, Allen, and Barker — the core “Shriekback are” lineup — augmented by the “Shriekback Big Live Band” which included Michael Cozzi (guitar), Wendy Partridge and Sarah Partridge (vocals), and Steve Halliwell (guitar/bass). The album was produced by Andrews and Gavin MacKillop, engineered by MacKillop. Styles shifted toward a more tribal/dance direction: Discogs catalogs the record under genres Electronic, and styles Disco and Tribal.

Go Bang! (1988, Island Records) marked the end of the first major-label run. Allen had left the active band before recording began, leaving Andrews and Barker as the core. The record was produced by Richard James Burgess and featured session bassist Doug Wimbish on several tracks, plus Cozzi on guitar and the Partridge sisters on backing vocals. The Shriekback catalog was rounded out by the Island-issued retrospective The Dancing Years (1990).

Hiatus and Sacred City (1988–1992)

After Go Bang!, Shriekback was effectively inactive as a band. Andrews pursued solo work and side projects (the Restaurant For Dogs collective, Monstrance, and others). Allen founded World Domination Recordings in Seattle. Marsh had been working solo under the alias Kylie Brush.

In 1992 Andrews reconvened a lineup with Allen (returning), Barker, and new recruits including Karl Hyde (Discogs: 39117; later of Underworld) and Jon Klein (Discogs: 421572) on guitar, Mark Raudva on didgeridoo/percussion, the Partridge sisters on backing vocals, and Lu Edmonds contributing on “Signs.” Produced by Andrews and Ian Caple, Sacred City was released on World Domination Recordings (distributed by Capitol) and has been cited as the album where Andrews’s songwriting reached its most expansive. Allen left again after the record and its touring cycle.

Post-1992 Dormancy and Reunion (1993–2010)

A long quiet period followed. The 1990s output was minimal: Naked Apes & Pond Life (1999, Mauve Records Ltd), a stopgap release. The band began self-releasing archival material in the early 2000s.

Andrews re-mobilized with Having A Moment (2003) — a fan-financed six-track mini-album, the first new studio recording in over a decade. The lineup for that record included Allen and Marsh (both returning, credited on loops and guitar/vocals), Barker on drums, Lu Edmonds, and Finn Andrews (Discogs: 635387; Barry’s son) on keyboards and backing vocals.

Cormorant (2005, Malicious Damage) and Glory Bumps (2007, Malicious Damage) followed as Andrews continued releasing material with fluctuating collaborators, with Andy Partridge (Discogs: 229492; XTC) credited on both. Life in the Loading Bay (2010, Malicious Damage) featured Marsh returning as a guest.

Current Era (2015–present)

Marsh rejoined full-time from 2015. The band has operated entirely self-released from this period. Key releases:

  • Without Real String or Fish (2015) — full studio LP; Marsh fully integrated
  • Why Anything? Why This? (2018) — credited to Andrews, Marsh, Barker with additional players including Christoph Skirl on winds
  • Clink Jam (2020) — sessions recorded during COVID
  • 1000 Books (2021) — with Joe Del Tufo and Raphaël Dahan in the production/saxophone credits
  • Bowlahoola (2022)
  • Monument (2025); Heaven to Deinze (2025) — Andrews and Marsh’s final studio recordings with the ailing Allen, released in his last months. Allen died 5 April 2025.
