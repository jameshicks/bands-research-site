Watter (2)

Ambient/post-rock group, Louisville, Kentucky. Discogs ID: 3789910. Active c. 2014–2017. Released on Temporary Residence Limited (cat nos. TRR233 and TRR296).

Originally described as a trio — Britt Walford (drums), Zak Riles (guitars/oud/synthesizer/drum machine/baglama/bass), and Tyler Trotter (keyboards/effects/drum machine/lap steel/melodica) — with additional members Dominic Cipolla and Van Campbell in the Discogs member listing. Both LPs drew on a wider cast of Louisville-connected guests.

Riles and Trotter both come from Grails; the Discogs bio frames Watter as an extension of the Grails aesthetic (multi-instrumentalist, textural, world-music-inflected), anchored by Walford as one of the defining drummers of American underground music. The music sits in the quieter, contemplative end of post-rock — ambient, slow-burning, far from the dynamic-surge post-rock of Mogwai or Explosions in the Sky.

Notes on membership: The Discogs profile lists all five as members. On This World (2014), only the core trio (Walford, Riles, Trotter) is credited as “Musician [Watter]”; Cipolla and Campbell are not credited. On History of the Future (2017), Cipolla appears as drummer on some tracks alongside Walford, while Campbell remains uncredited. Cipolla also has a separate Discogs alias entry (Dahm Majuri Cipolla, ID 7059425) under which he is credited on the second LP — this is the same individual as Dominic Cipolla (ID 997289).

Van Campbell is a Louisville multi-instrumentalist who appears in Black Diamond Heavies (ID 1216524), Ghostfinger (ID 1162964), King Mud (ID 4876665), and Bonnie Stillwatter (see below). His specific contribution to Watter is not captured in the Discogs credits.

Background and Context

Riles and Trotter in Grails

Zak Riles (real name Wm. Zachary Riles; also records as Peace Harbor) and Tyler Trotter are both members of Grails (Portland/LA, ID: 251435), the psychedelic/post-rock collective on TRL. Riles plays guitar, oud, baglama, and a range of stringed instruments in Grails; Trotter is a keyboardist also credited on the Grails LP The Devil Is People (TRL, 2015) on electric piano, piano, and synthesizers. Both also appear in the project Bonnie Stillwatter alongside Van Campbell and Will Oldham.

Riles has a substantial parallel career as a recording engineer and mixer. His non-Watter credits include: mixing and engineering Joan Shelley’s Live At The Bomhard (2020), mixing Sloths’ Three Feral Pieces (No Quarter, 2021), engineering and mixing תהלים = Psalms (No Quarter, 2021), and additional engineering on Sleep’s Dopesmoker reissue (Third Man Records, 2022). He operates Earthwave Studio.

Walford’s Return

The Discogs bio explicitly frames Watter as Walford’s first active band in “nearly twenty years” at the time of formation — situating it relative to Slint’s original dissolution (1994) and the sparse credits in the intervening period. His involvement was a significant draw: Slint’s Spiderland (1991) remains one of the most influential records in American post-rock/post-hardcore, and Walford had been largely absent from recording activity since The For Carnation era and his Breeders sessions.

Guests

Tony Levin — bass on “Small Business” (This World)

Tony Levin (real name Anthony Frederick Levin, b. 1946 Boston; Discogs ID: 256225). Bassist, Chapman Stick player. Long-running member of King Crimson (1981–84, 1994–96, 2014–2021) and de facto bassist in Peter Gabriel’s live and studio band from 1977 onward. His presence on a Louisville post-rock record is an outlier in his catalog — the connection is presumably personal via TRL networks. His is the only appearance on Watter material for bass on “Small Business” (track 3, 13:33), the longest track on the debut.

Rachel Grimes — piano on tracks 2 and 6 (This World); piano on “Final Sunrise” (History of the Future)

Rachel Grimes (Discogs ID: 345116). Louisville composer and pianist. Co-founder of Rachel’s (with Jason B. Noble and Christian Frederickson), the chamber-post-rock ensemble that ran through the 1990s and 2000s on Quarterstick Records. Her involvement across both Watter LPs is consistent with her Louisville-based network. She co-wrote “Final Sunrise” (with Nathan Salsburg, Trotter, and Riles).

Cheyenne Mize — violin on “Bloody Monday” (This World)

Cheyenne Mize (Discogs ID: 695429; also credited as Cheyenne Marie Mize). Louisville violinist and vocalist. Member of Bonafide United Musicians (ID: 5613241), Century of Aeroplanes (ID: 678974), Maiden Radio (ID: 3315648), and The Picket Line (ID: 1661125). Her solo work on Bandcamp extends into singer-songwriter territory. The violin credit on “Bloody Monday” (track 4) is her principal Watter contribution.

Bundy K. Brown — bass on “Liquid Of Life” (History of the Future); co-wrote the track

Bundy K. Brown (real name Kenneth Brown; Discogs ID: 10945). Chicago multi-instrumentalist and producer. Founding bassist in Tortoise (left 1995); member of Bastro and Gastr Del Sol (with David Grubbs and John McEntire). Also active in Pullman, Seam, and Loftus. Operates as Directions and has aliases Gray Market Goods, Slowpoke (4), The Cheech Diva. His co-writing credit on “Liquid Of Life” is notable — he is not merely a session player here.

Nathan Salsburg — electric guitar on “Final Sunrise” (History of the Future); co-wrote the track

Nathan Salsburg (Discogs ID: 477572; also As Right Angles alias). Louisville guitarist and folklorist. Member of Halifax Pier (chamber-indie; Put Your Gloves On And Wave, 2001, TRL). Known also as archivist/musicologist (Alan Lomax Archive). His co-writing and performance credit on “Final Sunrise” alongside Rachel Grimes creates a track with two prominent Louisville collaborators.

Todd Cook — bass on “Seawater” (This World); bass and co-writing on History of the Future

Todd Cook (Discogs ID: 583618). Louisville-connected bassist. Appears on The For Carnation’s self-titled LP (2000, Touch And Go) alongside Brian McMahan, and co-writes and plays bass on multiple tracks of History of the Future (“Macho Milano,” “History Of The Future”). His involvement ties Watter back to the post-Slint Louisville network specifically via The For Carnation.

Dane Waters — voice on “Small Business” (This World)

Dane Waters (Discogs ID: 1766563). No other notable Discogs credits found; the voice appearance on track 3 is his only Watter credit.

Jacob Duncan — tenor saxophone on “History Of The Future” (History of the Future); co-wrote the title track

Jacob Duncan (Discogs ID: 2301420). Co-writes and plays tenor sax on the title track alongside Walford, Cook, Trotter, and Riles.

Shawn Trail — marimba on “The Cloud Sanctuary” (History of the Future); co-wrote the track

Shawn Trail (3) (Discogs ID: 6134756). Marimba player; co-writes and performs on track 7.

Related Projects

Bonnie Stillwatter

Riles, Trotter, and Van Campbell form three-fifths of Bonnie Stillwatter (Discogs ID: 4363325), alongside Will Oldham. This is the most concrete documentation of Campbell’s role in the Watter extended network — as a Bonnie Stillwatter member alongside two Watter principals.

Grails

Riles is a core member of Grails (ID: 251435). Grails members include Emil Amos (drums; Holy Sons), Alex John Hall (guitar), William Slater (bass), Timothy Horner (drums), and others. The Grails catalog on TRL — Burning Off Impurities (TRR 118, 2007), Doomsdayer’s Holiday (2008), Deep Politics (2011), The Devil Is People (2015), Chalice Hymnal (2017) — runs parallel to Watter’s active period. Trotter played piano/keyboards on The Devil Is People (2015).

OM (8)

Tyler Trotter is also listed as a member of OM (8) (Discogs ID: 535319). No release credits confirmed in DB.

The Children (11)

Trotter and Cipolla are both members of The Children (11) (Discogs ID: 4217690). No releases confirmed in DB; likely a Louisville collective.

Dead Child

Dominic Cipolla is a member of Dead Child (Discogs ID: 1236313), a Louisville metal band. This connects the Watter lineage to Louisville’s heavier underground.

Production and Engineering

Jeremy deVine (Discogs ID: 285784) — TRL founder; co-mixed both LPs; handled layout on This World. A consistent presence across TRL production as founder/designer (303 design credits across the label, 33 mixing/engineering credits). His mixing role on both Watter records is typical of his hands-on involvement in the label’s artists.

Zak Riles — primary mixer on both LPs, in addition to his performance credits.

Carl Saff (Discogs ID: 436674) — mastered This World. Second-most-frequent mastering engineer across the TRL catalog (71 releases).

Robert Weston (Discogs ID: 252921) — mastered History of the Future at Electrical Audio, Chicago. Third-most-frequent TRL mastering engineer (62 releases).
