Jaga Jazzist

Norwegian large-ensemble instrumental band. Discogs artist ID: 17501. Formed in Tønsberg (near Oslo), 1994, by Lars Horntveth at age 14. Active through mid-2020s. Released on Smalltown Supersound, Ninja Tune, and Brainfeeder; early self-released and WEA/Atlantic distribution.

Name variations: Jaga, J. Jazzist, Jagga Jazzist.

Overview

Jaga Jazzist began as a teenage jazz collective in Tønsberg and gradually expanded into one of Norway’s most internationally recognized experimental ensembles. The band blends jazz, Krautrock, prog, IDM, and electronic music into an orchestral format. At peak lineup the group numbered nine or more members, all multi-instrumentalists. Founding songwriter and leader Lars Horntveth has been the compositional core throughout; his siblings Martin Horntveth (drums, production) and Line Horntveth (tuba, flute) are also long-standing members.

Producer Jørgen Træen co-produced and mixed the early WEA albums (A Livingroom Hush, The Stix), shaping the band’s sound in that period. The Ninja Tune era (2005–2015) brought international distribution and a move toward longer, more through-composed pieces, culminating in the live orchestral collaboration with Britten Sinfonia in 2013. The band signed to Flying Lotus’s Brainfeeder for the 2020 album Pyramid.

Key Release Notes

A Livingroom Hush (2001, WEA)

The international breakthrough. Produced and mixed by Jørgen Træen (with the band co-mixing). Genres: Electronic/Jazz; Drum n Bass, Experimental, Future Jazz. Personnel highlights: Jørgen Munkeby (flute, saxophones, bass clarinet), Mathias Eick (trumpet, upright bass), Andreas Mjøs (vibraphone, marimba), Ivar Christian Johansen (organ, Fender Rhodes, Roland Jupiter), Lars Horntveth (baritone sax, guitar, mellotron). Mastered by Audun Strype and Ingar Hunskaar.

The Stix (2002, WEA)

Second WEA album; produced and recorded by Jørgen Træen. New addition: Morten Qvenild (piano, synthesizer, cassette tape-recorder). Styles: Fusion, Future Jazz, IDM. Mostly Lars Horntveth compositions, with contributions from Jørgen Munkeby and Morten Qvenild.

What We Must (2005, Ninja Tune / Smalltown Supersound)

Ninja Tune debut. Produced by Kåre Chr. Vestrheim and Marcus Schmickler (Cologne-based electronic composer). Mixed and mastered by Vestrheim and Mike Hartung. Kim Hiorthøy handled design, photography, and illustration. Lineup expanded; Andreas Hessen Schei added as synthesizer/piano player. Styles: Fusion, Future Jazz, Post Rock.

One-Armed Bandit (2009, Ninja Tune)

Widely regarded as a peak album; Discogs promo copy (ID 4971909) has no detailed credits, but the title track became a set staple, lasting over 15 minutes in the live version recorded with Britten Sinfonia.

Starfire (2015, Ninja Tune)

Return after One-Armed Bandit. All songs written by Lars Horntveth. Mixed by Jørgen Træen. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Notable session contributions: Susanne Sundfør (handclaps, A1), Ola Kvernberg (violin, B3), Leon Dewan (Swarmatron synthesizer, B1). Recorded across multiple studios (Pooka Studio, EngfeltForsgren Studio, Albatross Recorders, Wallpaper).

Jaga Jazzist Live With Britten Sinfonia (2013, Ninja Tune)

Arranged and orchestrated by Lars Horntveth and Erik Johannessen. Conducted by Christian Eggen. Double LP on Ninja Tune (ZEN196). Repertoire drawn from One-Armed Bandit and What We Must era.

Pyramid (2020, Brainfeeder)

Four extended tracks (avg. ~10 min). Signed to Flying Lotus’s Brainfeeder. Singles remixed by Lindstrøm & Prins Thomas (released as separate 12-inches in 2020). Genres: Electronic, Folk/World, Jazz, Rock; Post Rock, Psychedelic Rock.
