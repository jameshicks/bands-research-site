The Ohms

Punk/new wave band from Syracuse, NY. Discogs artist ID: 2227289.

Overview

The Ohms were a three-piece punk and power-pop act operating out of Syracuse, NY in the late 1970s. Their only documented release is “Teenage Alcoholic” / “Chain Letter” (1979), a 7” single on the Syracuse independent label Northside (2) (catalog number NS 703). The record was classified under Rock / Power Pop / Punk on Discogs.

The band was a vehicle for its three members: vocalist and guitarist Zenny Caucasian (also co-producer, engineer, and sole songwriter), drummer and co-producer Ducky Carlisle, and bassist Rick Suburban. Carlisle would go on to a long career as a Grammy award-winning mixing engineer and producer, primarily out of Boston; the Ohms single appears to be the earliest Discogs credit of his career.

Scene Context

The Ohms were part of the same late-1970s Syracuse independent scene that produced The Flashcubes, the city’s better-documented power-pop act. Both bands released records on Northside (2) (the Flashcubes’ first single “Christi Girl” appeared on NS 701 in 1978, just ahead of the Ohms’ NS 703). The Northside (2) label described itself as a “1970s–1980s punk, power pop, and new wave label from Syracuse, New York.”

Barb Penoyar, who provided photography for the Ohms’ single, also appeared on the 1.4.5. release Pink Invasion (Archive Records (2), 1981) — a separate Syracuse/upstate NY punk-adjacent release on which Carlisle also played drums and co-produced. This suggests a degree of overlap in the Syracuse underground photography and music community of the era.

Ducky Carlisle’s production company, Ramjet Enterprises (Discogs label ID: 1733720, a Syracuse-based music production outfit he operated), likely facilitated independent recording work during this period. He subsequently relocated to Boston, where he built Room 9 From Outer Space studio and a decades-long career in production and engineering.
