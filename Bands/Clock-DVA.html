Clock DVA

Industrial / post-punk / EBM / electronic. Sheffield, England. Active 1978–1981, 1982–1984, 1988–1994, 2008–present. Discogs artist ID: 21351.

Overview

Clock DVA were formed in 1978 by Adi Newton (born Gary Coates) and Steven “Judd” Turner in Sheffield. The name comes from Anthony Burgess’s A Clockwork Orange — “dva” is Russian for “two” — a connection also made by Heaven 17, another Sheffield act. Newton had previously worked with Ian Craig Marsh and Martyn Ware in The Future, the precursor to The Human League, and with members of Cabaret Voltaire in a collective called The Studs. (Source: Wikipedia)

The original lineup combined tape-loop experimentalism and musique concrète techniques with rock instrumentation. Their cassette debut on Throbbing Gristle’s Industrial Records in 1980 placed them firmly in the UK industrial underground; their 1981 Fetish Records LP Thirst — produced by Ken Thomas and sleeve-designed by Neville Brody — topped the NME indie charts and was later cited by critic Paul Morley as one of the great albums of the decade. Following the 1981 split and the death of co-founder Turner (September 1981, accidental drug overdose), Newton rebuilt a new lineup and signed to Polydor for the funk-inflected Advantage (1983). A second hiatus followed, during which Newton pursued his parallel project The Anti Group (T.A.G.C.). He reactivated Clock DVA in 1988 for the digital-sampling-driven Buried Dreams (1989, Interfisch Records), widely regarded as the band’s masterpiece, and the EBM-oriented Man-Amplified (1992) and Sign (1993) on Italian label Contempo. After a 15-year pause Newton reactivated the band again in 2008, with Maurizio “TeZ” Martinucci as a constant collaborator from 2011 onward. New studio material began appearing again with Noesis (2023) and Second Sight (2025).

Key Release Personnel Details

White Souls In Black Suits (1980)

Lineup: Adi Newton (vocals, violin, EMS Synthi E, tapes), Steven James Turner (bass, vocals, treatments), David J. Hammond (guitar), Roger Quail (drums), Charlie Collins (saxophone, flute, percussion). Simon Mark Elliot-Kemp (synthesizer) had already departed. Engineered by Jon Mills (2); mixed by Richard H. Kirk and Stephen Mallinder of Cabaret Voltaire.

Thirst (1981)

Produced and engineered by Ken Thomas. Newton (voice, clarinet, tape, producer), Turner (bass), Widger (guitar), Collins (saxophone, flute), Quail (drums). Sleeve designed by Neville Brody. Liner notes by Genesis P-Orridge. One of Fetish Records’ landmark releases.

Advantage (1983)

Produced by Hugh Jones (additional keyboards). Newton (vocals, trumpet, additional instrumentation), Dean Dennis (fretless and fretted bass), John Valentine Carruthers (guitar, treatments), Nick Sanderson (drums, percussion), Paul Browse (saxophone). Design concept by Newton; design by Peter Barrett (3).

Buried Dreams (1989)

Newton, Dennis, Browse (all credited as “Performer [Clock DVA]”). Extensively programmed — first use of digital sampling synthesizers and computer sequencing developed through TAGC work. Sleeve artwork references Giorgio de Chirico and Marcel Duchamp.

Man-Amplified (1992)

Newton (cyphers, semantics), Dean Dennis (digital/analogue machines), Robert Baker (coding, decoding, sonoric syntactics). All three credited as written-by. Front cover: El Lissitzky artwork. Mastered by Marcello Spiridioni.

Sign (1993)

Newton and Robert Baker (composed by / developed by). Features sampled voices of Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin.
