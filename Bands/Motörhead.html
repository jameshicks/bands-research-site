From Discogs

Motörhead were an English rock band formed in June 1975 by bassist, singer, and songwriter Ian Fraser Kilmister (1945–2015), professionally known by his stage name Lemmy, who had remained the sole constant member. The band are often considered a precursor to, or one of the earliest members of, the New Wave of British Heavy Metal, which re-energised heavy metal in the late 1970s and early 1980s. Despite this, Lemmy had always dubbed their music as simply “rock and roll”.

To its end, Motörhead had released twenty-three studio albums, ten live recordings, twelve compilation albums and five EPs. Usually a power trio, they had particular success in the early 1980s with several successful singles in the UK Top 40 chart. The albums Overkill, Bomber, Ace of Spades, and particularly No Sleep ‘til Hammersmith, cemented Motörhead’s reputation as a top-tier rock band. As of 2012, Motörhead have sold more than 15 million albums worldwide.

Motörhead are typically classified as Rock N Roll by Lemmy and band members, and their fusion of punk rock into the genre helped to pioneer speed metal and thrash metal. Their lyrics typically covered such topics as war, good versus evil, abuse of power, promiscuous sex, substance abuse, and, most famously, gambling. The name “Motörhead” is a reference to users of the drug amphetamine. Overview

Motörhead were an English rock band formed in June 1975 by bassist-vocalist Ian “Lemmy” Kilmister, who remained the sole constant member until his death in December 2015. Though widely cited as a forerunner of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal and an acknowledged catalyst for speed and thrash metal, Lemmy consistently framed the band as playing nothing more than rock and roll — a fusion of hard rock, heavy metal, and punk that defied easy categorisation. Their most productive decade was the 1980s (thirteen releases), with Overkill, Bomber, Ace of Spades, and the live No Sleep ‘til Hammersmith cementing their reputation; by 2012 total sales had exceeded 15 million albums worldwide. Key studio collaborators included Vic Maile and Jimmy Miller, and the band’s extensive catalogue moved through labels including Bronze in the early years before settling into long relationships with BMG, Sanctuary, and their own Motörhead Music imprint.
