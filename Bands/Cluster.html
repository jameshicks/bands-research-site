Cluster

German electronic duo of Hans-Joachim Roedelius (Discogs ID: 105272) and Dieter Moebius (Discogs ID: 105273). Formed 1971 in Berlin, active with interruptions through 2015. Discogs artist ID: 40226.

Key figures of Krautrock and kosmische Musik. Rooted in the earlier trio Kluster (3) with Conrad Schnitzler. Influential on ambient, electronic, and post-rock through collaborations with Brian Eno and formation of Harmonia with Michael Rother.

Biography

Kluster Era (1969–1971)

Cluster’s prehistory is the experimental trio Kluster (3), co-founded in 1969 in Berlin by Roedelius and Moebius alongside Conrad Schnitzler — a friend of Roedelius who had helped establish the “Zodiac Free Arts Lab” underground venue. Roedelius had co-founded a music commune called Human Being (3) in 1968 before moving fully into electronics.

As Kluster (billed initially as “Ensemble Kluster”), the trio released two LPs on the tiny Schwann AMS Studio imprint — Klopfzeichen (1970) and Zwei-Osterei (1971) — in editions of 500 or fewer copies, engineered by Conny Plank (credited as Konrad Plank). A third LP, Eruption, followed in 1971. All three are now highly sought collectors’ items. After Eruption, Schnitzler departed to pursue a solo career, retaining rights to the Kluster name. Moebius and Roedelius continued as a duo, spelling the name “Cluster.”

Early Cluster: Self-Titled and Cluster II (1971–1972)

The first Cluster LP (1971, Philips) was made in tight partnership with Conny Plank, who is credited with engineering, electronics (uncredited on the sleeve), co-production, and co-composition alongside the two members. The instrumentation reflects the duo’s workbench approach: organ, cello, lap steel guitar, audio generators, and amplifiers. Plank’s Conny’s Studio — his farm studio near Wolperath — became the principal recording location for this and subsequent records.

Cluster II (1972, Brain / Metronome) continued the improvised noise-electronics direction, again co-produced and recorded by Plank. The release additionally credited two technicians — Knut Obermayr and Peter Leunig — for “elektronische Apparate,” indicating that custom-built hardware was a production tool throughout this period.

The duo also released Ausgeflippt (1972, Metronome), a live/documentary record featuring contributions from Neu! and others from the Berlin/Düsseldorf circuit.

Kosmische Peak: Zuckerzeit and Sowiesoso (1974–1976)

Zuckerzeit (1974, Brain) marked a striking turn: the duo pursued a sequenced, motorik electronic pop sound — spiky, rhythmically locked, not unlike early Kraftwerk — co-produced with Michael Rother of Neu!, who had by then become a close collaborator. Rother was the only external co-producer Cluster used during this period. Roedelius and Moebius split songwriting credits track by track throughout the album.

The association with Rother had broader consequences: it led to the formation of Harmonia, a trio of Rother + Roedelius + Moebius that recorded Musik Von Harmonia (1974, Brain) and Deluxe (1975, Brain). Harmonia’s sessions in 1976 with Brian Eno — later released as Tracks and Traces under the name Harmonia 76 — directly influenced Cluster’s subsequent sound.

Sowiesoso (1976, Sky Records) is the most meditative of the classic-era records: self-produced by Cluster, no Plank credit, drifting into ambient territory. The cover and photography were handled internally. The shift to Sky Records — the newly formed Krautrock-oriented Munich imprint — began a long relationship.

Brian Eno Collaborations (1977–1978)

Following the Harmonia 76 sessions, Eno worked directly with Cluster on two records that placed them at the centre of the emerging ambient / kosmische synthesis:

Cluster & Eno (1977, Sky Records) — a full duo collaboration. Co-produced and engineered by Conny Plank, with J. Krämer as assistant engineer. Eno and both Cluster members share “Music By” credits. The record’s interplay between Eno’s tape-processing and the duo’s hardware improvisation is its defining quality.

After The Heat (1978, Sky Records) — credited to Eno / Moebius / Roedelius (no Cluster name on sleeve). Co-produced by all four principals: Eno, Plank, Moebius, Roedelius. Holger Czukay of Can appears on bass on track B5. Eno wrote all the lyrics for side B. The record deepened the ambient texture of Sowiesoso while incorporating Eno’s side-B song structures.

Plank’s engineering runs continuously from Klopfzeichen (1970) through Cluster & Eno (1977) and After The Heat (1978) — by the count in Discogs credits, he appears on 79 releases touching the Cluster orbit.

Post-Eno and First Dissolution (1979–1981)

Grosses Wasser (1979, Sky Records) was produced by Peter Baumann, then recently departed from Tangerine Dream, with engineer Will Roper. It remains somewhat apart from the rest of the catalog in its production texture.

Curiosum (1981, Sky Records) — self-produced by Moebius and Roedelius — was the last studio LP before the duo dissolved. In the early 1980s Roedelius moved to Austria, effectively putting Cluster on hiatus. Both members pursued substantial solo and duo work:

  • Moebius and Plank released two collaborative albums (1981, 1982) and a third in 1986 before Plank’s death. Moebius also recorded with Gerd Beerbohm (Strange Music, 1982; Double Cut, 1983) and made the solo Tonspuren (1983).
  • Roedelius began his own solo career in 1978 (first album released that year, collaboration begun with Plank in 1975) and through the 1990s worked extensively with Tim Story in the duo Hans-Joachim Roedelius & Tim Story and the trio Aquarello.

The 1985 Sky Records release Old Land is catalogued on Discogs as a Cluster album/compilation drawing on this period.

Reunion and Later Period (1994–2015)

Cluster reunited in the early 1990s, releasing One Hour (1994, Música Secreta / Prudence). Live recordings from the 1996 tour were issued as First Encounter Tour 1996 (Purple Pyramid) and Japan 1996 Live (Soul Static Sound). Tim Story appears on the 1996 tour records as a collaborator/additional performer.

Later studio work includes Berlin 07 (2008, Important Records) and Qua (2009, Nepenthe Music), recorded with Roedelius and Moebius alongside Story.

After Moebius’s death on July 20, 2015, Roedelius continued performing and recording under the name Qluster (a further spelling variation).

Bureau B, the Hamburg label that has reissued much of the Krautrock canon, handled extensive reissues: the Apropos Cluster anthology (2014), the 1971–1981 box set (2016), and the Kollektion 06 LP compilation (2016, compiled by John McEntire).
