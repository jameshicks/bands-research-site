Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band

New York retro big-band/disco ensemble. Formed in the Bronx in 1974. Discogs artist ID: 88534.

Overview

Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band was co-founded by Stony Browder Jr. (guitar, piano, compositions, arrangements, musical direction) and his half-brother August Darnell (Thomas Browder; bass, lyrics). The group’s defining conceit was the anachronistic fusion of 1930s–1940s big-band swing — à la Cab Calloway — with 1970s disco rhythms and lush orchestral arrangements. The concept was Bronx-bred but drew stylistically on swing archetypes, theatrical kitsch, and the retro-vaudevillian.

Their self-titled debut (RCA Victor, 1976) was produced by Sandy Linzer with orchestrations by Charlie Calello and Stony Browder. The single “Cherchez La Femme” became a disco-era hit; the album reached the pop Top 40, achieved gold certification, and received a Grammy nomination. Manager Tommy Mottola (Champion Entertainment) secured the RCA deal after the label initially rejected them. Mottola departed circa 1979, citing disarray around the group.

The second album, Meets King Penett (RCA Victor, 1978), was self-produced by Stony Browder, with Darnell as associate producer. The third, James Monroe H.S. Presents Dr. Buzzard’s Original Savannah Band Goes To Washington (Elektra, 1979), was produced by David Wolfert and Gary Klein. After that release, Darnell and Andy Hernandez departed to form Kid Creole And The Coconuts. A fourth album, Calling All Beatniks! (Passport Records, 1984), was released under the abbreviated name “Dr. Buzzard’s Savannah Band” with Stony Browder and Cory Daye but without Darnell or Hernandez.

The group’s earliest Discogs trace is a 1971 promo 7” on P.I.P. Records (“Oh, Black Day / The First Good Thing”), credited under the name “Savannah,” with Darnell billed as “Tom Darnell.”
