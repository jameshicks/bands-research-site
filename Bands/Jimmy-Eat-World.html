Jimmy Eat World

Jimmy Eat World

Oct 06, 20267 min read

Jimmy Eat World

Discogs ID: 82079
Formed: 1993, Mesa, Arizona, USA
Genre: Emo, alternative rock, post-hardcore
Labels: Wooden Blue Records → Capitol Records → DreamWorks Records → Interscope Records / Tiny Evil → Exotic Location Recordings / RCA

Emo/alternative rock band from Mesa, Arizona. One of the most commercially successful bands to emerge from the mid-1990s emo underground; broke into mainstream radio with “The Middle” from Bleed American (2001). Self-funded Clarity (1999) after being dropped by Capitol, which became an influential record in the post-hardcore/emo canon. Still active as of 2022 on Exotic Location Recordings.

Discography

Early Period — Wooden Blue / Capitol (1994–1999)

Jimmy Eat World (1994, Wooden Blue Records)
Self-produced debut. Mitchel Porter on bass; Steve Naughton co-produced. Released before the classic lineup solidified.

Static Prevails (1996, Capitol Records)
Discogs release ID: 1098472
First major-label album. Core four-piece lineup (Adkins, Linton, Burch, Lind). No producer credited beyond the band in Discogs; straightforward guitar-driven emo. Capitol A&R sign.

Clarity (1999, Capitol Records)
Discogs release ID: 1345629
Produced and engineered by Mark Trombino; string arrangements by Trombino and Adkins, performed by Suzie Katayama. Mastered by Brian Gardner. Engineered with assistance from Nick Raskulinecz. Self-funded after Capitol dropped them — the band paid for the recording themselves and Capitol released it contractually. Became a touchstone of late-1990s emo. All songs written by Jimmy Eat World.

Breakthrough — DreamWorks (2001–2003)

Bleed American (2001, DreamWorks Records)
Discogs master IDs: 89141 (Europe), 89148 (US)
Discogs release ID (European): 383817
Produced and engineered by Mark Trombino; mixed by Trombino. Mastered by Bob Ludwig. Cover photograph by William Eggleston. Design by Jeff Kleinsmith and Jesse LeDoux. Contains “The Middle,” which became a major radio hit.

Title note: Following the September 11 attacks, the album was pulled from shelves and re-released in multiple territories under the title Jimmy Eat World — Discogs lists separate pressings under that title (US, Europe, UK, Germany, Mexico, Turkey) dated 2001, alongside the original “Bleed American” pressings. Whether the retitling was universal or territory-specific is not fully documented in the DB (see Open Questions).

Singles from this era: “The Middle,” “Sweetness,” “A Praise Chorus,” “Salt Sweat Sugar”

Futures — Interscope / Tiny Evil (2004–2005)

Futures (2004, Interscope Records / Tiny Evil)
Discogs release ID: 412204
Produced by Gil Norton. Engineered by David Schiffman; mixed by Rich Costey, assisted by Claudius Mittendorfer. Mastered by Ted Jensen at Sterling Sound. Additional engineering by Jake Davies. Tiny Evil is credited as co-label alongside Interscope — on Discogs it is listed as an Interscope sublabel (ID: 102445), not an independent imprint.

Stay On My Side Tonight EP (2005, Interscope / Tiny Evil)

Chase This Light — Interscope / Tiny Evil (2007–2008)

Chase This Light (2007, Interscope Records / Tiny Evil)
Discogs release ID: 1102757
Produced by Chris Testa and Jimmy Eat World. Executive producer: Butch Vig. Mixed by Chris Lord-Alge, assisted by Keith Armstrong. Mastered by Ted Jensen. Additional engineering by John Fields and Ross Hogarth.

Invented — DGC / Interscope (2010)

Invented (2010, DGC Records / Interscope Records)
Discogs release ID: 2468131
Produced by Mark Trombino and Jimmy Eat World. Recorded by Jim Adkins; additional recording by Trombino. Mixed by Mark Trombino (tracks 1–13) and Jim Adkins (tracks 13–16). Mastered by Ted Jensen (most tracks) and Roger Seibel (tracks 13, 14, 16). No Butch Vig credit on this album — the brief’s claim that he executive-produced Invented is not supported by Discogs; his role was limited to Chase This Light.

Cover songs on Invented: Track 13 written by Jeff Tweedy (Wilco); track 15 written by Mac McCaughan, Laura Ballance, Jim Wilbur, and Jon Wurster (Superchunk); track 16 written by Jimmy Eat World.

Damage — Exotic Location Recordings / RCA (2013)

Damage (2013, Exotic Location Recordings / RCA / Sony Music)
Discogs release ID (US): 4646965
Produced by Alain Johannes and Jimmy Eat World. Mixed by James Brown (2). Mastered by Ted Jensen. Core four-piece lineup. Design by Morning Breath Inc.

Integrity Blues — Exotic Location Recordings / RCA (2016)

Integrity Blues (2016, Exotic Location Recordings / RCA)
Discogs release ID: 9216508
Produced by Justin Meldal-Johnsen and Jimmy Eat World. Engineered by Carlos De La Garza, Justin Meldal-Johnsen, Jim Adkins, Zach Lind, and Mike Schuppan. Mixed by Ken Andrews. Mastered by Dave Cooley. Strings/brass/woodwinds arranged by Roger Joseph Manning Jr. Additional keyboards: Justin Meldal-Johnsen and Robin Vining. Additional vocals: Courtney Marie Andrews and Robin Vining.

Surviving — Exotic Location Recordings / RCA (2019)

Surviving (2019, Exotic Location Recordings / RCA)
Discogs release ID: 14271985
Produced by Justin Meldal-Johnsen and Jimmy Eat World. Engineered by David Schiffman, Jim Adkins, Justin Meldal-Johnsen, Mike Schuppan. Mixed by Ken Andrews. Mastered by Dave Cooley.

Place Your Debts (2022, Exotic Location Recordings)

Discogs release ID: 24872906. Credits sparse in DB.

Open Questions

  • Was Bleed American retitled Jimmy Eat World on all US pressings after September 2001, or only on certain print runs? Discogs shows multiple US and European pressings under both titles in 2001 — unclear if this was a full recall-and-retitle or a partial territory decision. Answerable from DreamWorks press releases or physical sleeve comparison.
  • Is Mark Trombino credited as producer on the original Capitol pressing of Clarity, or only on the DreamWorks reissue? Discogs release 1345629 (labeled Capitol, Europe) credits Trombino as producer — but the circumstances of the Capitol/DreamWorks split on Clarity are not documented in the DB.
  • What is the exact nature of Tiny Evil Records? Discogs lists it as an Interscope sublabel (parent_id = Interscope). Was it set up by the band as a vanity imprint within a distribution deal, or is it purely an Interscope internal designation?
  • Who produced Static Prevails (1996, Capitol)? The Discogs credits list only the four band members with their instruments — no producer credit appears. The DB may be incomplete here.
  • What are the full producer/engineer credits for Damage (2013) beyond what appears on the US CD pressing? The UK/Europe vinyl pressing (4638899) has no additional credits listed.
  • What are the production credits for Place Your Debts (2022)? The DB entry has no additional credits.
  • Did the band self-fund Clarity entirely, or did Capitol advance part of the recording cost before the drop? The self-funding narrative is widely cited but not verifiable from Discogs data.
