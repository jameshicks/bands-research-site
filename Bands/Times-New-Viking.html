Overview

Times New Viking were a lo-fi indie rock trio from Columbus, Ohio, formed in 2003, with Jared Phillips on guitar, Adam Elliott on drums and vocals, and Beth Murphy on keyboards and vocals. Their output skews heavily toward the 2000s — eight releases in that decade — moving from small noise-leaning imprints like Siltbreeze, 300%, and Columbus Discount Records to Matador between 2007 and 2009, then briefly to the UK’s Wichita Recordings around 2011–2012. Their collaborator network is rooted in the Columbus underground, with recurring appearances from Mike Rep and Matt Horseshit alongside mastering regulars Roger Seibel and JJ Golden, and an unexpected cross-Pacific link to New Zealand’s Axemen.

Notes (web) — Matador to Merge/Wichita transition

The move away from Matador after Born Again Revisited (2009) appears to have been an amicable parting driven by a creative impasse rather than a formal dispute. Drummer Adam Elliott told DIY Magazine (2011): “We just felt we had hit the wall with what we were doing … I don’t want to say anything bad about Matador, they’re still our friends … We’re still friends with everyone at Matador.” He described the new arrangement as simply following a personal connection: “We were in contact with a friend from Wichita Recordings, so we said we’re just going to make this record for Wichita and see who wants to put it out in the US and Merge wanted to put it out.” — DIY Magazine, 2011

A Columbus Monthly retrospective (2016) adds some commercial context: Born Again Revisited “didn’t sell as well as the band or label had hoped,” and quotes a Matador representative as noting that “another question might be whether or not a label as large as Matador was able to properly focus on Times New Viking amidst everything else that was happening in the company.” The band recorded Dancer Equired! before Merge was involved; Wichita funded the sessions at Musicol Recording in Columbus, and Merge then picked it up for US release. — Columbus Monthly, 2016 (paywalled; detail from search snippet)

Adam Elliott confirmed in a PopMatters interview (2011) that the new arrangement was different “in some positive ways,” citing the album streaming on NPR as a concrete benefit of the Merge relationship. — PopMatters

Interview Magazine (May 2011) confirmed all labels — Siltbreeze, Matador, Merge — gave the band “100% creative control and never told us to change a thing,” suggesting label interference was not a factor. — Interview Magazine, 2011

Summary: No single dramatic rupture; the split was mutual and cordial. The proximate causes were a sense of creative stagnation (“hit the wall”), modest commercial returns on Born Again Revisited, and a pre-existing personal connection to Wichita. Matador’s own representative acknowledged the label may not have been able to give them adequate focus at that point.
