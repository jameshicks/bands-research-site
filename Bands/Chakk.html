Overview

Chakk formed in Sheffield in 1981, centred on Alan Cross’s four-track tape machine, home-built effects boxes, and a small mixing desk. Their core philosophy was that the studio itself was the primary instrument. This outlook shaped not only their sound but their legacy: the MCA advance they received in 1985 was large enough to fund the construction of Sheffield’s first large commercial recording studio, FON Studios — an institution that trained a generation of engineers and remained active until 1996.

The band split in 1987 after their MCA album underperformed commercially. Their significance runs wider than the discography: they brought Richard H. Kirk and Stephen Mallinder into the production process early on, provided Mark Brydon his route to Sheffield’s studio world before Moloko, and gave The Designers Republic some of their earliest sleeve credits.

Recorded at Western Works — Cabaret Voltaire’s Sheffield studio — over two six-hour marathon sessions. Richard H. Kirk was heavily involved in the recording; secondary sources (Wikipedia, contemporary interviews) credit him as producer, though the Discogs sleeve credit gives production to Chakk and engineering/mixing to Alan Cross. Stephen Mallinder was also present. The original tapes were subsequently edited and further developed by Alan Cross at his flat before final release. Alan Cross engineered and mixed; Porky (5) cut the lacquer. Reached No. 3 in the UK Indie Chart, autumn 1984. Styles: Electro / Industrial.

The drummer on this single is Mark Tattersall, who also drummed on the Clocks And Babies cassette. Around the same time — Micro-Phonies was recorded at Western Works in February–March 1984 — Cabaret Voltaire recruited Tattersall as their live and session drummer. The personnel exchange was mutual: Chakk used CV’s studio and were released on CV’s label; CV used Chakk’s drummer on their biggest album of the period.

FON Records and FON Studios

Chakk launched Fon Records as their own imprint with “You / They Say” in March 1985. The MCA advance — reportedly £100,000 — funded the construction of FON Studios, Sheffield’s first large commercial recording studio, completed summer 1985. The studio was designed jointly by Alan Cross, Mark Brydon, and Marcus O’Hagan, all of whom had architecture backgrounds. Alan Cross had prior work experience designing recording studios and interior acoustics with Andy Munroe Associates; building in Sheffield was also simply cheaper than recording in London.

FON Studios became a training ground for Sheffield engineering talent: Robert Gordon (Dr Bob 4), Alan Fisch, Steve Cobby (Fila Brazillia), and Phil Jones (5) all spent time there. The studio also attracted high-profile outside bookings including David Bowie and Yazz. Alan Cross continued to produce and remix under aliases (DJ Chakk, Alana Gold, etc.) outside his Chakk role. FON Studios operated until 1996.

The most consequential person to train at FON was Robert Gordon. He worked alongside Alan Cross, then partnered with Mark Brydon as Fon Force, producing and engineering for Hula, The Mighty Device, Age Of Chance, and Pop Will Eat Itself. In 1989 Gordon co-founded Warp Records with Steve Beckett and Rob Mitchell — the first Warp release (Forgemasters’ “Track With No Name”) featured Gordon as a performer alongside Winston Hazel and Sean Maher. The FON Studios → Warp pipeline is direct: the building Chakk’s advance funded was the incubator for one of the most significant labels in British electronic music.

The Cabaret Voltaire Connection

Three interlocking threads connect Chakk to Cabaret Voltaire:

1. Western Works / Doublevision. Doublevision was co-founded by Cabaret Voltaire and Paul Smith in 1982, initially for CV’s own video releases, then expanded to records from 1983. It served as the release hub for the Sheffield/UK industrial network — Lydia Lunch, The Hafler Trio, Eric Random, Clock DVA, The Box, Einstürzende Neubauten all released there. Chakk’s “Out Of The Flesh” (DVR6, 1984) sits in a run of releases where Kirk and Mallinder were serving as in-house producers: DVR7 (Eric Random, produced by Kirk and Mallinder) and DVR10 (The Box, produced by Kirk) bracket it in the catalog. The label was based in Nottingham, not Sheffield, and operated semi-independently.

2. The Recording Sessions. “Out Of The Flesh” was recorded at Western Works — CV’s Sheffield studio — across two six-hour marathon sessions. Kirk was heavily involved in the production; secondary sources credit him as producer, though the formal sleeve credit gives production to Chakk and engineering/mixing to Alan Cross, who developed the tapes further at his flat after the sessions.

3. Mark Tattersall — the shared drummer. Chakk’s original drummer, Mark Tattersall, became Cabaret Voltaire’s drummer for Micro-Phonies (Virgin, November 1984), recorded at Western Works in February–March 1984 — overlapping with the “Out Of The Flesh” sessions. It was a genuine personnel exchange: Chakk’s drummer played on CV’s biggest album; CV gave Chakk their studio and label.

A fourth connection emerged later: Mark Brydon played bass on CV’s Code (Parlophone, 1987), produced by Adrian Sherwood.

The Designers Republic

The Designers Republic — Sheffield graphic design studio founded 14 July 1986 by Ian Anderson (8) — designed the “Bloodsport” sleeve (1986) and the “Timebomb” sleeve (1986). These are among TDR’s earliest commercial credits, predating their later dominance in electronic/techno sleeve design through Warp Records.

Post-Split

Alan Cross moved back to London, continued as a songwriter and producer for Warner Chappell Music. Dance chart credits include Judy Cheeks’ “So In Love (The Real Deal)” (Positiva, 1993) and Clubland’s “Set Me Free” (Great Jones, 1992). Under his DJ Chakk alias, remixed “Timebomb (Bombed-Out Remixes)” (Fon, 1987). As The Carry On Gang (with DJ Fergie/Robert Ferguson), released “2 Da Top (Reach!)” on Xtrax London (1999). The Rhythm Doctors (with Paul Heron) and Pranksterz are listed as groups on his Discogs page but have no traced releases.

Mark Brydon partnered with Robert Gordon as Fon Force at FON Studios, producing Age Of Chance, Pop Will Eat Itself, Krush, and others through the late 1980s. Played bass on Cabaret Voltaire’s Code (1987). Co-founded Moloko with Roisin Murphy in the early 1990s; “Sing It Back” (Boris Dlugosch remix) reached UK #4 in 1999.

Diarmuid Boyle joined Longpigs, a Sheffield alt-rock group on Mother Records, as drummer — appearing on The Sun Is Often Out (1996) and related singles. Richard Hawley was also in the Longpigs lineup. Diarmuid Boyle died 14 April 2017.

Jake Harries appeared on a Galaxy single (Parlophone, 1988), then co-founded Heights Of Abraham in 1992 with Chakk bandmate Sim Lister and Steve Cobby (Fila Brazillia). Heights Of Abraham released on Pork Recordings: Humidity (1993), Electric Hush (1995), Scenic Vibe EP (1995).

Sim Lister co-founded Heights Of Abraham with Harries and Cobby. Later co-founded Steel Tiger Records (with Steve Cobby and Dave McSherry, 1999) and Twentythree Records (with Steve Cobby, 2006). Also appears as one half of J.S.T.A.R.S. (with Steve Cobby).

Robert Gordon (FON Studios alumni, not a band member) co-founded Warp Records in 1989 with Steve Beckett and Rob Mitchell.
