Graham Parker And The Rumour

Discogs ID: 263521 Formed: London, 1975 Genres: Pub Rock, New Wave, Rock Labels: Vertigo, Arista, Stiff Records Years active: 1975–1980, 2011–2015

British rock group formed in London in 1975. Graham Parker fronting The Rumour, assembled from former members of Brinsley Schwarz by Parker’s management. A major live draw bridging the pub-rock and new wave scenes; recorded five albums together until splitting in 1980. Reunited 2011–2015, recording two further albums. Final concert at the London Forum, 17 October 2015.

History

Formation (1975)

Graham Parker was an unknown singer-songwriter working as a garage attendant in Hampshire when he came to the attention of Dave Robinson (3) (ID: 554666), co-founder of Stiff Records, who took on management duties. Robinson assembled a full band for Parker by drawing on members of the recently dissolved pub-rock outfit Brinsley Schwarz: guitarist Brinsley Schwarz himself, keyboard player Bob Andrews, and bassist Andrew Bodnar. Martin Belmont (formerly of Ducks Deluxe) came in on second guitar, and Steve Goulding on drums. This five-piece became The Rumour.

The combined act quickly built a reputation as an electrifying live draw on the London pub-rock circuit, playing small venues and building a devoted following before releasing a single note on record.

Vertigo Period (1976–1978)

The group signed to Vertigo in the UK. Their debut, Howlin’ Wind (1976), was produced by Nick Lowe with Dave Robinson credited as Executive Producer — a lean, urgent record that placed Parker’s voice over a tight R&B-rooted rhythm section. It was credited to Graham Parker alone (without the Rumour name on the sleeve).

Heat Treatment (1976) arrived the same year and expanded the sound with a full brass section. Produced by Robert John Lange (credited as “Mutt” Lange — ID: 239886), who also engineered alongside Dave Charles at Rockfield and Peter Kelsey at Trident, it featured John Earle and Albie Donnelly on saxes, Danny Ellis on trombone, and Dick Hanson on trumpet. This became the definitive template for the group’s Vertigo-era sound: high-energy rock threaded with horn arrangements.

Stick To Me (1977) returned to Nick Lowe as producer, with David Bedford arranging strings, John Altman on baritone sax, and the brass section again in attendance (John Earle on tenor sax, Dick Hanson on trumpet, Chris Gower on trombone). Executive producers were Dave Robinson and Jake Riviera (ID: 755945), the two founders of Stiff Records.

The Parkerilla (1978) was a live double album, recorded on tour. Bob Andrews served as musical director and arranged the brass parts. The Rumour Brass (ID: 627671) — the associated horn section — is credited collectively. Robert John Lange received a mixing credit alongside Ted Sharp. The Parkerilla documents the band at peak live power and includes a studio side (Side D) with a new recording of “Hey Lord, Don’t Ask Me Questions.”

Also in 1977: The Pink Parker, a four-track pink-vinyl EP (Vertigo) capturing the group’s commercial profile at its Vertigo-era height.

Arista Period (1979–1980)

The group moved to Arista for what became their best-regarded record. Squeezing Out Sparks (1979) was produced by Jack Nitzsche (ID: 256156), representing a deliberate stripping-down of the sound: no brass, no strings, no embellishment. The lineup was the core six-piece, with Mark Howlett engineering and co-mastering. Jeff Sanders also received a mastering credit. The album is widely cited as Parker’s peak achievement and a landmark of the new wave era. UK release was on Vertigo; US on Arista.

The Up Escalator (1980) was produced by Jimmy Iovine (ID: 170807) with Shelly Yakus engineering, recorded in New York. It brought in guest players: Nicky Hopkins on acoustic piano, Danny Federici on organ, Jimmy Maelen on percussion, Peter Wood on synthesizer. Greg Calbi handled mastering. The album was released on Stiff Records in the UK and Arista in the US. It was the final album of the original run.

The group split at the end of 1980. The Rumour subsequently released three albums as an independent act without Parker (Max, 1977 [pre-split]; Frogs Sprouts Clogs And Krauts, 1978; Purity Of Essence, 1980), all on Stiff Records or Mercury.

Reunion (2011–2015)

The group reunited in 2011 and recorded Three Chords Good (2012), released on EMI in the US and Proper Records (2) in the UK. The core six-piece lineup was intact; no outside producers are credited in the Discogs entry, suggesting a self-produced record. The Judd Apatow film This Is 40 (2012) featured Parker and The Rumour in a cameo and helped drive the reunion’s profile; Apatow is also credited on the 2014 documentary Don’t Ask Me Questions: The Unsung Life Of Graham Parker And The Rumour.

Mystery Glue (2015) was produced by Dave Cook (ID: 344088), who also engineered and mixed, with Graham Parker as co-producer. Ed Woods mastered. Released on Cadet Concept / Universal Music Catalogue (UK) and Hip-O Records (US). The label assignment — Cadet Concept, a Universal sublabel historically associated with jazz and later reactivated — is notable.

The band undertook a short final UK tour in October 2015, finishing with a concert at the London Forum on 17 October 2015. At that show, the surviving members of the original horn section reunited for the first time in 33 years.

Aftermath

After the split Parker continued under multiple configurations. He later assembled Graham Parker & The Goldtops (ID: 8984356), a later-career UK band featuring Martin Belmont returning on guitar. The Rumour’s members scattered into various projects; Brinsley Schwarz (2) and Bob Andrews maintained lower profiles while Belmont remained active in UK roots and country scenes.

Open Questions

  • Bruce Springsteen is credited in the Discogs DB for backing vocals on The Up Escalator (appearing on multiple pressings of master 451409). The popular account that he played organ on the album appears to be an embellishment or conflation — Danny Federici (E Street Band) is separately credited for organ. Whether Springsteen’s backing vocals credit reflects sleeve notes or a later reissue correction needs physical confirmation.
  • No producer is listed in the Discogs entry for Three Chords Good — the album may be self-produced or credits may be missing from the DB.
  • The initial Howlin’ Wind album is credited to “Graham Parker” only (without The Rumour) on the Discogs primary credit, though The Rumour are the backing band. Whether the original sleeve named them needs physical confirmation.
  • The Judd Apatow connection (the 2012 film This Is The End placed Parker and The Rumour in a cameo that helped drive the reunion’s profile) is mentioned in Discogs credit data but the details are web-only.
  • Catalog numbers for the original Vertigo LP pressings are not fully documented in the DB entries used here and would benefit from physical record confirmation.
