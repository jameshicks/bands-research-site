Nancy Sinatra & Lee Hazlewood

Discogs ID: 50182 Members: Nancy Sinatra (ID 135246), Lee Hazlewood (ID 225403) Active as duo: 1965–1972, with reunion recording 2004 Primary label: Reprise Records (1966–1971); RCA Victor (1971–1972) Reissue curator: Light In The Attic (2012–2023)

Overview

Nancy Sinatra & Lee Hazlewood were an American vocal duo whose recordings across 1965–1972 sit at the intersection of country, pop, and psychedelic folk. Hazlewood — singer, songwriter, and producer — had already shaped Nancy Sinatra’s solo career, writing and producing her breakthrough “These Boots Are Made for Walkin’” (1966) before formalizing their pairing as a credited duo. The partnership produced some of the most distinctive records of the late-1960s American pop landscape: recordings that oscillated between tongue-in-cheek theatrics and genuinely strange psychedelic narrative, all wrapped in lush, orchestrated country-pop arrangements.

Their creative peak runs from 1966 to 1971. The duo’s work was substantially reissued and re-evaluated from the 2010s onward, primarily through Light In The Attic, with Hunter Lea as the central curatorial figure.

Formation and Context

The partnership formed in 1965. Lee Hazlewood had already established himself as a maverick producer and songwriter — he had worked extensively with Duane Eddy in the late 1950s and early 1960s, pioneering a distinctive echo-heavy sound in Phoenix, and had subsequently founded LHI Records as a vehicle for his own output. He met Nancy Sinatra through Jimmy Bowen and became her primary collaborator at Reprise Records, writing and producing her entire run of 1966–1967 hits.

Nancy Sinatra (born Nancy Sandra Sinatra, June 8, 1940) is the daughter of Frank Sinatra, who had co-founded Reprise. She had initially struggled to break through in the early 1960s before Hazlewood’s guidance produced her signature hit. The duo’s credited recordings began appearing on Reprise in 1966 with “Summer Wine,” formalizing an arrangement that had already been operating informally.

Billy Strange was the essential third element: he served as arranger and conductor on nearly all of their Reprise-era recordings, providing the lush orchestrations that defined the duo’s sound across both their joint and their solo Reprise work. Nancy’s Discogs profile notes that “in 1966 and 1967, Sinatra charted with 13 titles, all of which featured Billy Strange as arranger and conductor.”

Key Recordings

Singles Era (1966–1968)

“Summer Wine” (1966, Reprise) — The first credited duo single. A waltz-time piece with Hazlewood playing the rake and Sinatra the ingenue. Written by Hazlewood. Arranged by Billy Strange. Released in Canada and several European markets before the US LP appearance.

“Jackson” (1967, Reprise) — Co-written by Billy Edd Wheeler and Gaby Rodgers. Arranged by Billy Strange, produced by Hazlewood. An uptempo country-pop duet that prefigured the Johnny Cash / June Carter Cash version recorded the same year; the Sinatra/Hazlewood version was released earlier. One of the duo’s strongest chart performances internationally.

“Some Velvet Morning” (1967, Reprise, master 275959) — Hazlewood’s own composition. Their most critically celebrated recording: a split-narrative psychedelic country-pop piece in which Sinatra sings as Phaedra and Hazlewood as a wandering narrator. Produced by Hazlewood. The song’s structure — two voices inhabiting separate lyrical universes that occasionally intersect — is genuinely unusual for the era. It appeared in the opening sequence of Quentin Tarantino’s Kill Bill Vol. 1 (2003), a placement that significantly expanded its contemporary audience. Discogs lists 20 pressings from the master alone.

“Lady Bird” (1967, Reprise, master 100431) — Written by Hazlewood, arranged by Billy Strange. One of their more straightforwardly pop outings, with strong European chart performance; 26 pressings tracked from the German single alone (catalog RA 0629 and variants), with 20 additional pressings across other territories.

“You Only Live Twice / Jackson” (1967, Reprise) — A coupling of Nancy’s James Bond theme with the duo’s Jackson; released as a double-A side in the US and several territories.

“Elusive Dreams” (1968, Reprise) — Written by Billy Sherrill and Curly Putman. A lush country-pop ballad that appeared as a single and on the Nancy & Lee LP.

Nancy & Lee LP (1968, Reprise RS 6273)

The duo’s first full album together. 122+ pressings tracked on Discogs (master 38885) — one of the most widely pressed albums in the vault. Released on Reprise Records (catalog RS 6273 / 6273), also issued on Command and Playtape in the US.

The album consolidates their singles output with new recordings, draws heavily on Hazlewood compositions for the B-side, and opens with a Reprise-era pop standard (“You’ve Lost That Lovin’ Feelin’”). Billy Strange’s arrangements are present throughout — the album’s orchestral warmth is entirely his work.

Did You Ever? LP (1971, RCA Victor LSP 4645)

The second studio album, released on RCA Victor — a shift from Reprise. Co-produced by both Nancy Sinatra and Lee Hazlewood (the single “Did You Ever / Back On The Road” credits both as producers). Arrangements by Clark Gassman (tracks A1–A4, B2, B3) and Larry Muhoberac (tracks A5, B1, B4). Largely Hazlewood compositions, with “Down From Dover” written by Dolly Parton and the title track by Bobby Braddock.

The title track “Did You Ever” became a Top 20 hit in the UK. This album was issued in the US as Nancy & Lee Again (the two titles appear to refer to the same master, LSP 4645, issued under variant titles across territories; Discogs treats them under master 145200). The LITA 2023 reissue used the title Nancy & Lee Again.

Nancy & Lee 3 (2004, Warner Strategic Marketing Australia)

A reunion album recorded approximately three decades after the duo’s initial run. Produced by both Nancy Sinatra and Lee Hazlewood; engineered by Chuck Haines; mastered by Chris Milfred. Tracklist includes Hazlewood originals (“Gypsies & Indians,” “Strangers, Lovers, Friends,” “Loving You Loving Me,” “Is Making A Little Love Out Of The Question?,” “Pack Saddle Saloon”), country covers (“Barricades & Brickwalls” by Kasey Chambers, “Down From Dover” by Dolly Parton, “Goin’ Down Rockin’” by Tony Joe White/Leann White), and pop standards (“Save The Last Dance For Me”). Duane Eddy — a longtime Hazlewood collaborator from the earliest phase of his career — appears as featured artist on “She Won’t.” Billy Strange contributed booklet photography (with Jeannie Strange and Nancy Sinatra).

Formats: CD with HDCD encoding. Genres: Pop, Rock; Styles: Ballad, Country Rock, Folk Rock.

  • “Down From Dover / Paris Summer” (1972, RCA Victor Germany) — European single.

Sound and Style

The duo’s recordings are built around Hazlewood’s compositional sensibility — his lyrics deal heavily in archetypes (the wanderer, the faithless man, the patient woman), delivered with a self-aware theatrical quality that could shade into irony. His baritone is low and dry against Sinatra’s brighter, more conventionally pop voice; the contrast is the point.

The sonic character of the Reprise-era work is almost entirely Billy Strange’s creation: lush strings, horns, and rhythm section deployed in a country-inflected orchestral pop style that owes something to Nashville but is distinctly Los Angeles in execution. The Did You Ever? era substituted Clark Gassman and Larry Muhoberac as arrangers, producing a slightly more stripped-down, country-rock-adjacent sound.

“Some Velvet Morning” stands apart from both tendencies: its arrangement is spare, its narrative structure genuinely experimental — two vocal characters who do not quite share a song.

Compilations and Later Legacy

“Fairy Tales & Fantasies: The Best Of Nancy & Lee” (1989, Rhino Records, master 1449756) — The canonical US retrospective. Arranged by Billy Strange (tracks 1–5, 7–12) and Clark Gassman (tracks 6, 14, 15); liner notes by Todd Everett; produced for CD by Bill Inglot; original recordings produced by Lee Hazlewood.

Light In The Attic Reissues (2012–2023): The label undertook a systematic reissue program of the duo’s catalog and the broader Hazlewood universe, with Hunter Lea as curator:

  • A House Safe For Tigers (Lee Hazlewood solo, 2012, LITA 087)
  • The LHI Years: Singles, Nudes & Backsides (1968–71) (Lee Hazlewood, 2012, LITA 084)
  • Trouble Is A Lonesome Town (Lee Hazlewood, 2013, LITA 096)
  • There’s A Dream I’ve Been Saving: Lee Hazlewood Industries Sampler (2013, LITA 109-S)
  • Lee Hazlewoodism — Its Cause And Cure (Lee Hazlewood, 2015, LITA 132)
  • The Very Special World Of Lee Hazlewood (2015, LITA 131)
  • Something Special (Lee Hazlewood, 2015, LITA 133)
  • Cowboy In Sweden (Lee Hazlewood, 2016, LITA 153)
  • 13 (Lee Hazlewood, 2017, LITA 142)
  • Forty (Lee Hazlewood, 2017, LITA 161)
  • Requiem For An Almost Lady (Lee Hazlewood, 2017, LITA 162)
  • The Cowboy & The Lady (Ann Margret / Lee Hazlewood, 2017, LITA 160)
  • 400 Miles From L.A. 1955–56 (Lee Hazlewood, 2019, LITA 176)
  • Nancy & Lee (duo reissue, 2022, LITA 198-2) — remastered by John Baldwin (5)
  • Nancy & Lee Again (duo reissue, 2023, LITA199) — remastered by John Baldwin (5)

LITA also reissued extensive Nancy Sinatra solo catalog: Boots (2021, LITA 197), Start Walkin’ 1965–1976 (2021, LITA 195), Keep Walkin’: Singles, Demos & Rarities (2023, LITA 208), and multiple individual album reissues in 2024.

The LITA reissue campaign, coordinated by Hunter Lea, substantially restored the duo’s catalog to print and introduced their work to new audiences. The Kill Bill placement (2003) preceded and likely contributed to the renewed critical interest.
