Isis

Discogs ID: 225447 (listed as Isis (6)) Active: 1997–2010 Origin: Boston, Massachusetts (founded); Los Angeles, California (relocated 2003) Genre: Post-metal, sludge metal, experimental Name variations: ISIS And Associates; The Isis Riverbed Chorus

Instrumental-leaning post-metal band anchored by Hydra Head Records founder Aaron Turner. Founded in Boston in 1997, relocated to Los Angeles in 2003. Disbanded May 2010 on their own terms — the band’s statement cited having “done everything we wanted to do, said everything we wanted to say.” Most-pressed release: Panopticon (2004) at 59 pressings — the highest in this research corpus. Reunited once in October 2018, performing as “Celestial” for a benefit concert in tribute to Caleb Scofield.

History

Boston formation and early releases (1997–2000)

Aaron Turner founded Isis in Boston in 1997 while simultaneously running Hydra Head Records. The earliest lineup included Chris Mereschuk (electronics/vocals) and went through several personnel changes before the final five-piece solidified around 1999. The Mosquito Control EP (1998, Escape Artist Records, 25 pressings) is the earliest widely-circulated release; The Red Sea (1999, Second Nature Recordings) was the debut full-length, at 46 pressings the band’s third-most-pressed title.

Celestial (2000, Hydra Head Europe, HH666-59, 34 pressings) brought them fully into the HH orbit and established the aesthetic: massive slow-building dynamics, Aaron Turner’s design work throughout, and unusual artwork sources — Celestial used video stills from Chris Marker’s 1962 short film La Jetée. Matt Bayles mixed; Dave Merullo mastered.

SGNL>05 and the Neurot period (2001)

SGNL>05 (2001, Neurot Recordings, 17 pressings) — released on the imprint run by Neurosis members Steve Von Till and Scott Kelly — placed Isis in direct dialogue with the Neurosis-adjacent drone/doom world. Matt Bayles recorded, mixed, and produced alongside the band.

Ipecac era: peak output (2002–2006)

Isis signed to Mike Patton’s Ipecac Recordings for Oceanic (2002, IPC-32, 49 pressings): their first major critical moment. Matt Bayles engineered and mixed; Ed Brooks mastered; Aaron Turner did the artwork; additional musicians Ayal Naor and Maria Christopher appeared on three tracks.

The band relocated to Los Angeles in 2003. Panopticon (2004, IPC-57, 59 pressings) — their most-pressed and most conceptually elaborate release: a meditation on surveillance, power, and control. Matt Bayles engineered/mixed; Bill Dooley mastered; Aaron Turner did photography and design. Conceptual liner notes quoted from:

  • Jeremy Bentham (The Panopticon Letters, 1787)
  • Michel Foucault (Discipline & Punish: The Birth Of The Prison, 1973)
  • Howard Rheingold (The Virtual Community, 1993)
  • Alex Steffen (2002 essay on singularity)

Track “Altered Course” featured Justin Chancellor (Tool bassist) on additional bass.

In The Absence Of Truth (2006, IPC-81, 32 pressings): Matt Bayles engineered/mixed; Ed Brooks mastered; Aaron Turner design. Guest appearances: Caleb Scofield (Cave In) vocals on “1000 Shards”; Charley Turner additional vocals on “Not In Rivers, But In Drops” and “Holy Tears.”

Also 2006: In The Fishtank 14 (Konkurrent, Netherlands) — collaboration with Scottish post-rock band Aereogramme.

Clearing The Eye (2006, Ipecac) — DVD/compilation release.

Final record and dissolution (2009–2010)

Wavering Radiant (2009, Conspiracy Records (2), CORE078, 25 pressings): a departure in production — Joe Barresi (“Evil Joe Barresi”) produced, recorded, and mixed, the first time the band worked outside the Turner/Bayles combination. Brian Gardner mastered; Faith Coloccia handled photography and background coloring. Adam Jones (Tool guitarist) contributed additional guitar on “Hall Of The Dead” and keyboards on “Wavering Radiant.”

Melvins / ISIS split (2010, Hydra Head, 5 pressings): final release. The band announced dissolution on May 18, 2010; last show June 23, 2010.

Temporal (2012, Ipecac) — posthumous rarities/B-sides compilation released by Ipecac.

Legacy and reissues

Daymare Recordings (Japan) was the most prolific reissue partner, releasing Japanese editions of every LP and several live documents. Robotic Empire released the four Oceanic Remixes volumes (2004). The remixes attracted a notable roster including Justin K Broadrick, Tim Hecker, and Oktopus.

Connections

  • Hydra Head Records — Aaron Turner’s own label; Celestial (HH Europe) and the Melvins split both on HH; Turner’s design work defines the HH visual identity as much as his band defines its sound
  • Ipecac Recordings — Mike Patton’s label; home for the central Isis catalog (2002–2012); the HH → Ipecac move was a step up in distribution without a major-label compromise
  • Neurot Recordings — Neurosis imprint; SGNL>05 places Isis directly in the Neurosis lineage
  • Neurosis — direct influence cited in Discogs profile; Neurot connection; shared post-metal vocabulary
  • Cave In — fellow HH band; Caleb Scofield guest on In The Absence Of Truth; both bands bridge hardcore/metal/post-rock; 2018 benefit reunion was for Scofield after his death
  • Sumac — Aaron Turner post-Isis project with Brian Cook (Russian Circles) and Nick Yacyshyn
  • Old Man Gloom — Aaron Turner supergroup (with Caleb Scofield, Nate Newton, Santos Montano)
  • Mamiffer — Bryant Clifford Meyer + Faith Coloccia; shares personnel and aesthetic space with Isis
  • Tool: Two members (Justin Chancellor, Adam Jones) guested on separate albums — a notable crossover between mainstream metal and the underground post-metal world
  • Chris Marker: La Jetée stills on Celestial — Isis as cinephiles
