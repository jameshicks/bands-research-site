Lustmord

Discogs ID: 8265 (project)
Primary person: Brian Williams (ID 226979; credited as Brian Lustmord, ID 583687)
Name variations: Lustmørd, Lust Mord, Lustomord
Genre: Dark ambient, industrial, isolationism
Active: 1981–present

Dark ambient project founded by Brian Williams during his time assisting SPK in the UK, 1981–1982. Originally operated as a duo with John Murphy (2); Geoff Rushton (John Balance of Coil) is also listed as a member on Discogs. Became a predominantly solo project from the late 1980s onward and a foundational name in dark ambient and isolationist music.

Biography

Origins with SPK (1981–1982)

Brian Williams came into contact with the Australian/UK industrial group SPK during their UK period in the early 1980s, initially in an assistant and technical capacity. The “Lustmord” name first appears on SPK’s Leichenschrei LP (1982, Thermidor) — Williams is credited as “Lust Mord” for “Additional Vocals” alongside text contributions. The Lustmord project proper launched the same year as a duo between Williams and John Murphy (2), who would also go on to work with SPK and later the post-industrial scene.

The earliest Lustmord releases appeared on the UK Sterile Records imprint: the self-titled Lustmørd (1981) and Lustmørdekay (Live Evil) (1982). A third 1982 release, Whisky 23.5.82 / Live Evil, appeared on the French imprint Sterile Records (2). These documented raw, confrontational early material — reportedly made with power electronics, crude tape manipulation, and industrial noise techniques consistent with the SPK orbit.

Geoff Rushton — who would later become John Balance of Coil — appears credited on the archival release A Document Of Early Acoustic & Tactical Experimentation (1991, Dark Vinyl Records), where he is listed as performing “Treated Device” and contributing voice alongside Williams, a figure named Nigel Dunster, and others. This documents material from the project’s first period. Chris Carter (Chris Carter (2) of Throbbing Gristle / Chris & Cosey) is credited on that same release for transferring and processing the material from two-track for CD.

Side Effects Era (1985–1992)

Around 1985, Brian Williams inherited the Side Effects label from SPK — the label SPK had founded for their own releases — and used it to release both his own work and music by other artists. Lustmord’s Paradise Disowned (1986, Side Effects) was the first album to surface under Williams’s stewardship of the label. Credits show Williams performing noise, electronics, pipes, machinery, voice, and gong, alongside John Murphy (2) in a similar capacity. An uncredited alias Isolrubin BK — one of Williams’s alter-egos — is also listed for “Construction, Demolition, Combustion” sounds.

The Monstrous Soul (1992, Side Effects) — notable for a co-production credit alongside Adi Newton (Adi Newton, the conceptualist behind Clock DVA and The Anti Group) — marked a continued evolution toward denser, more textural ambient work.

The Place Where The Black Stars Hang (1994, Side Effects) is often cited as a landmark Lustmord release. Williams conceived, produced, and wrote the liner notes; the album represents the mature Lustmord sound of deep, cavernous drones and manipulated environmental recordings. Mastered by Denis Blackham.

Side Effects ran until April 1999, distributing through Rough Trade (until TG’s bankruptcy forced a pause) and later World Serpent and Soleilmoon / Dark Vinyl. After 1999 the catalog was absorbed by Soleilmoon.

Soleilmoon Years (1990–2012)

Soleilmoon Recordings (Portland, Oregon) became the primary international distributor for Lustmord, with 16 releases across 1990–2012. The relationship began with the landmark Heresy (SOL-9, 1990) and extended through several Isolrubin BK and Arecibo releases as well as the Lustmord main line.

Heresy (1990) is the signature early document and the release that introduced Lustmord to the American post-industrial audience. Credited as Lustmørd. Post-production processing by Chris Carter (2); programming credited to Andrew Lagowski (listed as “Andrew Lagowski” but his project is known as Lagowski, Discogs ID 33339). Williams is credited under his “Brian Lustmord” alias for “Programming: Aural Architecture.” Cover painting by John Martin (25).

Collaboration and Film Scoring (1994–2009)

A significant strand of Lustmord’s output from the mid-1990s onward involved collaborative projects and film scoring:

  • Robert Rich & B. Lustmord — Stalker (1995, Fathom): A full collaborative album with ambient artist Robert Rich. Williams co-composed and performed; Rich handled the final mix and mastering.
  • Lustmord vs Metal Beast (1997, Side Effects): A collaboration or remixed work with an outfit called Metal Beast.
  • Pigs Of The Roman Empire (2004, Ipecac Recordings): High-profile collaboration with Melvins. Williams produced, programmed, and handled sound design; King Buzzo (vocals, guitar, electronics), Dale Crover (drums), and Kevin Rutmanis (bass, guitar, electronics) performed. Adam Jones of Tool contributed guitar on the record. Recorded by Toshi Kasai; mastered by John Golden.
  • Puscifer connections: Williams provided uncredited programming on Puscifer’s “V” Is For Vagina (2007); contributed to the “D” Is For Dubby remix EP (2008) and Dozo EP (2008). He co-wrote material with Maynard James Keenan.
  • Tool connections: Williams is credited on Tool’s Vicarious (2007) as Sound Designer, and on Fear Inoculum (2019) for “Water/Waves” performer credit and sounds/effects.
  • [Other] (2008, Hydra Head Records): Ambient/experimental solo LP with Adam Jones (Tool) contributing guitar on three tracks; artwork by Jones and Tracey Roberts.
  • [Beyond] and [The Dark Places Of The Earth] (both 2009, Vaultworks): Twin companion albums released simultaneously.
  • Zoetrope (2001/2010, Nextera/Burning World Records): Williams performed, produced, and scored this release; the Burning World reissue credits him as sound designer.
  • Underworld OST (2003, Edel Records): Williams is credited as Producer on this soundtrack release.
  • Juggernaut (2007, Hydra Head Records / Vaultworks): Solo release combining dark ambient with industrial electronics.
  • The Cynic (2010, Blast First Petite): Production and additional programming for this release.
  • First Reformed (Extended Motion Picture Soundtrack) (2019): Williams composed and produced the score for Paul Schrader’s film First Reformed — his most prominent film scoring credit.
  • Scorn: Original Soundtrack (2022): Score for the Ebb Software video game.
  • The Empty Man (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack) (2020): Composed and produced.

Vaultworks and Later Albums (2006–present)

Williams launched the Vaultworks imprint for self-released and small-batch material from 2006 onward. Key Vaultworks releases include Rising (06.06.06) (2006), [Other] (2008), [Beyond] and [The Dark Places Of The Earth] (2009), Songs Of Gods And Demons (2011), and a series of live document releases (Kraków, Stockholm, Hobart, Maschinenfest, Berlin, Lublin) drawn from concert recordings between 2010 and 2018.

The Word As Power (2013, Blackest Ever Black): Released on the influential UK label founded by Kiran Sande; mastered by Noel Summerville. A four-sided LP with illustration by Simon Fowler (3).

Dark Matter (2016, Touch): Released on Jon Wozencroft’s Touch imprint — a significant pairing given Touch’s reputation for spatial/ambient music. Williams produced, recorded, and mastered the album himself; Wozencroft contributed photography.

Alter (2021, Pelagic Records): A collaboration with Swedish vocalist Karin Park. Subsequent solo albums The Other and The Others [Lustmord Deconstructed] followed on Pelagic (2022), along with Much Unseen Is Also Here (2024).

Songs Of Gods And Demons (2011, Vaultworks): A collaboration credited to Lustmord and Som (the project of vocalist Sum), which would be the basis for The Word As Power.

Connections

  • SPK — Williams assisted SPK during their UK period 1981–1982; credited as “Lust Mord” on Leichenschrei for additional vocals and text. Subsequently inherited the Side Effects label from SPK c.1985.
  • John Murphy (2) — Co-founder of Lustmord as a duo; performed on early releases through at least Paradise Disowned (1986). Murphy has a separate solo career in the industrial/experimental scene.
  • Geoff Rushton — Listed as a Lustmord member on Discogs; credited on A Document Of Early Acoustic & Tactical Experimentation (archival, 1991). Went on to co-found Coil with Peter Christopherson.
  • Chris Carter (2) — Throbbing Gristle / Chris & Cosey; post-production on Heresy and CD transfer on A Document…
  • Andrew Lagowski — UK electronic musician (project: Lagowski); programming on Heresy.
  • Adi Newton — Clock DVA / The Anti Group; co-produced The Monstrous Soul (1992).
  • Coil — Connected via Geoff Rushton’s membership in both projects; also via Soleilmoon Recordings where both were early artists.
  • Melvins — Collaborative LP Pigs Of The Roman Empire (2004, Ipecac).
  • Puscifer — Maynard James Keenan’s project; Williams contributed programming and co-writing 2007–2008.
  • Tool — Williams credited on Vicarious (sound design) and Fear Inoculum (water/waves sounds).
  • Touch — Jon Wozencroft’s UK ambient/experimental imprint; Dark Matter (2016).
  • Blackest Ever Black — Kiran Sande’s UK label; The Word As Power (2013).
  • Pelagic Records — German label; most recent home for Lustmord studio albums (2021–2024).
