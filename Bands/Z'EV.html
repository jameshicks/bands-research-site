Z’EV

Real name: Stefan Joel Weisser
Born: February 8, 1951, Los Angeles, California, USA
Died: December 16, 2017 (aged 66)
Discogs artist ID: 79684
Aliases / working names: Element/L, Magneet Bond, Rax Werx, Saul Z’EV, Sha’ul, Yoel, uns, Stefan Weisser
Name variations on releases: Z, Z’VE, ZE’V, ZEV, Z`EV, Ze’V, Ze’v, Zev

Biography

Origins and the Los Angeles / San Francisco Years (1951–1978)

Stefan Joel Weisser began playing drums at the age of four. Growing up in Los Angeles, he developed an approach to percussion rooted in extended technique, found-object instrumentation, and the sonic properties of metal — industrial detritus, salvaged drums, gongs, bells, and structural steel he would suspend and strike in performance. By the late 1960s he was working under the name Yoel and releasing very early recordings (the 1968-1990: One Foot In The Grave retrospective documents activity from 1968 onward).

During his San Francisco period he was active in the avant-garde and noise underground. His earliest documented release is from 1976 (the “As Is As” piece, later re-released on Touch). He appeared in the 1983 Target Video compilation Six Examples, placing him in the Bay Area video-art-meets-music circuit.

New York Arrival (1979–1984)

In 1979 Weisser moved from San Francisco to New York City and adopted the name Z’EV permanently. The New York downtown scene was at its most concentrated — No Wave had just detonated and what followed drew from many points: contemporary classical, industrial, tribal drumming, and noise performance. Z’EV’s metal percussion practice — improvised and formidably loud in live settings — immediately attracted the attention of key figures.

The profile line in his Discogs bio names three people who took notice: rock symphonist Glenn Branca (ID 55972), a dancer, and guitarist Rhys Chatham (ID 584). All three were central to the minimalist-noise intersection forming around lower Manhattan.

His first LP, Production And Decay Of Spacial Relations (Backstreet Backlash Records, BBR 006, Netherlands, 1981), was recorded with Martin Van Der Leer (recording/mixing) and Peter Graute. That same year, Salts Of Heavy Metals appeared on Infidelity (2) — a cassette release that would become one of the most-archived items in his discography (108 pressings/format variations tracked on Discogs, indicating wide distribution and bootleg culture around it).

50 Gates (Staaltape, 1984, Netherlands) extended his European footprint, connecting him to the Dutch experimental label network around Staalplaat.

Industrial Years and European Connections (1985–1991)

The mid-1980s produced Z’EV’s most firmly “industrial” aligned work. My Favorite Things (Subterranean Records, SUB 33, 1985, US) collects live recordings from 1979–1983 across WKCR Columbia Radio New York, the Red Door in Baltimore, Melkweg Amsterdam, and venues in Den Bosch, Stockholm, and Venlo — a portrait of an artist on constant transatlantic circuit. Photography by Barbara Ess; mastered by George Horn at Fantasy Studios, Berkeley.

Schönste Muziek (Dossier, ST 7510, 1986, Germany) — five tracks of improvised metallic percussion, recorded by Dick Lucas (who recurs throughout the mid-period) and produced by Manfred Schiek of Berlin’s Dossier label. Alongside Z’EV’s name on the label, the sleeve credits include “neutral listener,” “provocative listener,” and “time keeper” — conceptualist humor embedded in the documentation.

He appeared on the Temple Records compilation Direction Ov Travel (1991, UK) alongside Genesis P-Orridge (ID 22533) of Psychic TV and Throbbing Gristle, with whom he shared orbit in the industrial / TOPY network. This release credits him for “Drums, Temple Bells [Tibetan], Performer [Processing]; Written-By.”

The major retrospective of this era is the Touch double-CD box 1968-1990: One Foot In The Grave (Touch, TO:13, 1991, UK). This 49-track compilation documented 22 years of activity, with Z’EV himself as the primary editor (liner notes, digital transfer mixing, final edit mastering). Andrew McKenzie (The Hafler Trio; ID 61290) contributed to the first assembly and remixed one track. Jon Wozencroft (ID 430994) handled artwork editing and final mastering. Naut Humon (ID 73477) contributed mixing. The genres span classical, electronic, non-music; styles include abstract, acoustic, experimental, industrial, and spoken word — a measure of how hard Z’EV’s practice resists single-genre categorization.

He appears on Glenn Branca’s Symphony No. 2 (The Peak Of The Sacred) (Atavistic, ALP05CS, 1992) — a landmark of minimalist noise orchestra. The line-up for this live recording includes Wharton Tiers (mixing), Lee Ranaldo, Thurston Moore, Kim Gordon (photography), Barbara Ess, David Linton, and others from the New York downtown core.

Staalplaat and Dutch Network (1990–1998)

Opus 3 (Staalplaat / Helmholtz Theater, ST-CD 010 / HT1, 1990, Netherlands) is a 20-track catalogue of his percussion instruments — each track named for the object being played (Kliks, Containers, Base Drums, Bon Po Bells, Gongs, Titans, etc.). Producer Van Lagestein (ID 480643), engineer Dick Lucas (ID 548472). This album functioned as both documentation and manifesto: an inventory of Z’EV’s sonic materials.

Opus 3.1 (Soleilmoon Recordings, SOL 8 CD, 1998, US) is the reissued and re-sequenced version of the same Staalplaat recording, divided into five “Acts.” Producer Van Lagestein, engineer Dick Lucas, artwork by Steve Speer. The re-release on Soleilmoon brought this early material to the wider post-industrial underground.

The mid-1990s saw him branch into electronics and harder noise. Chemical Dependance (Fukem, 1997, Netherlands) brought in DJ Dano, connecting him to the gabber/industrial crossover; No Sweat also credited.

Ghost Stories (SOL 64 CD, 1998) is a single 68-minute piece; the only credit beyond Z’EV is producer/engineer Van Lagestein. The record demonstrates his approach to drone-ambient sustained percussion, contrasting sharply with the kinetic live work on My Favorite Things.

Soleilmoon was well-suited to Z’EV: the label’s aesthetic — dark electronic, industrial, ambient, experimental — aligned with his practice, and its sister relationship with Staalplaat ensured European distribution continuity.

Touch Period (2004)

Headphone Musics 1 To 6 B/W As Is As [1976] (Touch, TO:63, 2004, UK) gathered six “headphone” pieces plus the archival 1976 “As Is As” recording. Design by Jon Wozencroft; mastered by Denis Blackham. This is a musique concrète / electroacoustic presentation — one of his most formally abstract solo releases. The 1976 track is the oldest documented Z’EV composition in his discography.

Die Stadt Era (2004–2006)

Three consecutive untitled releases on Die Stadt (Germany, 2004–2006) placed him in the context of that label’s catalog of drone, industrial, and dark ambient. Fear Falls Burning (Dirk Serries, ID 294537) and Aidan Baker (ID 69673) appear across the same releases as collaborators.

Late Period: Collaborations and the 2009–2015 Catalog

Sub Rosa brought two major releases: As / If / When (Sub Rosa, SRV229, 2010, Belgium) — an LP of two long-form pieces, “As” and “If,” recorded by Carl Stone (ID 402, side A) and Paul Young (side B). Z’EV’s own liner notes accompany the release. Then Rubhitbangklanghear Rubhitbangklangear (Sub Rosa, SRV340, 2013, Belgium) — a duet with Charlemagne Palestine (ID 62927), recorded live; Palestine played carillon, Z’EV percussion. One of his most prominent late-career collaborative documents.

Cold Spring released Sum Things (CSR101CD, 2009, UK) — six tracks with track titles presented as mathematical formulae. Artwork by Abby Helasdottir; mastered by Jeff Mortimer.

Fragment Factory released There Must Be A Way Across This River / The Abject (2011, Germany) — shared release with John Duncan (ID 60364).

Blossoming Noise released Symphony #2 - Elementalities (2006, US) — a further exploration of the symphony format. Graham Moore (ID 319782) contributes.

Klanggalerie (Austria) released two compilations gathering earlier material: The Subterranean Years (2009) and The Dossier Years (2011) — both co-credited with Lisa Robotka (ID 756296), contextualizing his 1985 Subterranean Records and 1986 Dossier label periods.

Psych.KG released Wurde Etwas Vergessen (2019, Germany) — the last entry in his primary discography on Discogs, released posthumously.

He also collaborated with Hati (Polish percussion ensemble, ID 301347) on multiple releases through the 2010s.

Sound and Approach

Z’EV’s practice centered on the physical and tonal properties of metal objects. He built custom rigs of salvaged industrial materials — oil drums, pipes, sheets, springs — and performed with them in ways that emphasized sustained resonance over rhythmic pattern. The term “industrial percussion” fits, but the work also draws from:

  • African and Asian drumming traditions — the Bon Po Bells, wayang gongs, and Tibetan temple bells in his inventory reflect serious ethnomusicological engagement
  • Minimalism — the sustained drone of pieces like Ghost Stories (one 68-minute track) aligns with La Monte Young and Tony Conrad as much as with Einstürzende Neubauten
  • Performance noise — live sets were volume-intensive, physically demanding for both performer and audience
  • Mysticism and Kabbalah — the name Z’EV is derived from Hebrew (wolf); aliases Sha’ul and Yoel are Hebrew names; his writing engaged kabbalic and magical traditions. He used the term “rhythmajik” (his website domain) to describe the relationship between rhythm, trance, and ritual

The track titles on Opus 3 (Kliks, Containers, Base Drums, Bon Po Bells, Gongs, Titans) function as a taxonomy of his instrument collection. His releases through the 1990s and 2000s shifted toward sustained electronics and processing while retaining percussion as a foundation.
