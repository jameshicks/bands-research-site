His Name Is Alive

Experimental rock project. Livonia, Michigan. Discogs ID: 61796. Active 1988–present.

Core project of Warren Defever (also credited as Warn Defever; aliases: Control Panel, WMD, WAR). Began as home-recording cassette project in the mid-1980s; signed to 4AD in 1990 and remained their primary experimental US act through 2001. Career encompasses ethereal dream-pop, dub, ’60s pop, garage rock, gospel, R&B, and experimental smooth soul — often within the span of a single album. Defever is also a mastering engineer and in-house engineer at Third Man Records’ Detroit studio (where he recorded Chandra Oppenheim’s Blue Stage Sessions, 2019).

Era 1 — Home Tapes and Pre-History (mid-1980s–1989)

Defever began recording under the His Name Is Alive name in Livonia in the early-to-mid 1980s (the Disciples archival releases collect material dating to 1979). The cassette I Had Sex With God (1988, self-released) circulated on the underground tape network. These recordings — lo-fi, heavily layered, collage-like — came to the attention of Ivo Watts-Russell at 4AD, who signed the project.

The debut LP. Mixed by 4AD founder Ivo Watts-Russell and house producer John Fryer — the same team behind Cocteau Twins, Dead Can Dance, and early This Mortal Coil. Karin Oliver (who shares surname but is not a documented relation to Vaughan Oliver) is the primary vocalist. Styles: Art Rock, Ethereal, Experimental. 4AD catalog number CAD 0008 makes it one of the earliest 1990 4AD releases.

A sprawling double LP, 23 tracks. The expanded lineup introduces Melissa Elliott and Denise James as key vocalist/songwriters. Recorded across many sessions; Watts-Russell and Fryer again mixed. The LP established the band’s reputation for dense, layered dream-pop with multiple competing voices.

Era 4 — Collaboration Peak and R&B Turn (1994–1998)

Sound Of Mexico (time STEREO, 1994)

A collaboration with ambient composer Harold Budd. Released on Defever’s own time STEREO imprint.

Origin of the collaboration (web sources): HNIA played the Musicas Visuales Festival in Mexico alongside Budd, Paul Horn, and Jorge Reyes. Defever recorded Budd’s set at the festival. Back in Livonia he added his own guitar to some of Budd’s songs, then edited the tapes — looping favourite sections, cutting others, and adding further layers of reverb and echo. The result became Sound Of Mexico. Defever recalled: “I remember the surreal moment when from across the room Harold Budd walked in and greeted me as ‘Mr. Defever.‘” The initiative appears to have been Defever’s: he made the recording and performed all subsequent editing unilaterally, with no documented indication that Budd commissioned or co-directed the project. Dusted Magazine / “Listed: His Name Is Alive”, Warren Defever, 19 Feb 2021

Note — possible festival discrepancy: Bill Nelson’s website states that he and Harold Budd were invited to perform at a music and arts festival in Mexico City that collapsed after two days when the promoter ran into financial difficulties (billnelson.com/post/for-harold). It is unclear whether Nelson is describing the same Musicas Visuales Festival that HNIA attended, or a separate event. If it is the same festival, the festival may have continued partially (HNIA’s and Budd’s sets occurring before or during the collapse). This is unresolved.

The most collaborative album. Tracks 1–14 are co-credited to Denise James, the ESP Beetles, Erika Hoffmann, Ian Masters (Pale Saints), and Mark Kozelek (Red House Painters) as a group-written collective. Track 15 “Last One” is written by the ESP Family collective including Lovetta Pippen. The album moves toward gospel, smooth R&B, and dub textures — the beginning of Defever’s fixation with soul music.

Origin of the Ian Masters collaboration (web sources): The connection was brokered by 4AD founder Ivo Watts-Russell. After the Pale Saints ended and Masters released his solo project Spoonfed Hybrid (1993), Watts-Russell proposed: “Would you like to go to America and record with Warren Defever?” Masters accepted. He travelled to Michigan for approximately two weeks of sessions — working about six hours daily, partly from existing demos, mostly jamming until ideas emerged — and described the trip as “one of the most enjoyable paid holidays I’ve ever had.” The primary documented result of these sessions was Mars is a Ten, released under the name ESP Summer (Time Stereo, 1994/95). The co-writing credits Masters carries on Stars On E.S.P. (1996) most likely arise from the same Watts-Russell-brokered period of collaboration: both projects draw on the 1994–95 Michigan sessions and the ongoing Defever–Masters relationship. No source explicitly states whether the Stars on E.S.P. material was written in the same visit or in subsequent exchanges; this remains unconfirmed. Ian Masters in interview with Alasdair Dickson, Dicksonianinstitute Substack, 6 Dec 2023

Note — “E.S.P. Dolphins”: Scaruffi’s HNIA biography (scaruffi.com/vol5/hisname.html) states that Defever “had created the impromptu E.S.P. Dolphins project the previous year” with Masters (and possibly Kozelek), apparently c. 1995 before Stars On E.S.P. (1996). No other source confirms this project name and Scaruffi cites no primary source; treat as weakly sourced until corroborated.

Deep blues and jazz. Covers Duke Ellington’s “Solitude” and Hank Williams’ “Your Cheating Heart.” Fred Thomas (later of Saturday Looks Good To Me) plays bass; Ida Pearle on violin. The most acoustic, stripped-back HNIA release to date.

Era 6 — Self-Released and Silver Mountain (2004–2012)

After Someday My Blues Will Cover The Earth, HNIA moved away from 4AD. Defever released a string of records on time STEREO and Silver Mountain Media Group — his own imprints. Output included Detrola (2006), The Eclipse (2010), and Silver Family (2012). Sound shifted further toward psychedelic rock.

Era 7 — Archive Excavation and Return (2019–present)

The UK label Disciples released three volumes of archival home recordings:

  • All The Mirrors In The House (Home Recordings 1979–1986) (2019)
  • Return To Never (Home Recordings 1979–1986 Vol. 2) (2020)
  • Hope Is A Candle (Home Recordings 1985–1990 Vol. 3) (2021)

In 2024 HNIA returned to 4AD with How Ghosts Affect Relationships 1990–1993 — a deluxe limited edition compilation from the early 4AD period.
