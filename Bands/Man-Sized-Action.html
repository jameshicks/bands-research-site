Man Sized Action

Post-punk/hardcore band from Minneapolis, Minnesota. Active in the early 1980s. Discogs artist ID: 1661804.

Overview

Man Sized Action formed in Minneapolis in the early 1980s as part of the city’s punk and hardcore underground. They released two full-length LPs on Reflex Records (5) — the St. Paul-based label co-founded by Terry Katzman and Hüsker Dü to promote the Minneapolis scene after Twin/Tone Records rejected Hüsker Dü’s first single. Both records were produced with the involvement of Bob Mould.

The band is notable in the broader indie rock history for featuring Brian Paulson (guitar), who went on to become one of the most significant recording engineers and producers in American independent music, with credits including Slint’s Spiderland, Uncle Tupelo’s Anodyne, Son Volt’s Trace, Wilco’s A.M., and Beck’s Odelay. Man Sized Action’s second LP, Five Story Garage (1984), is Paulson’s first known production credit.

The band sat at the convergence of the Minneapolis hardcore/punk scene (Hüsker Dü, Articles of Faith) and the early Chicago–Minneapolis touring circuit that later produced a distinct indie rock lineage through figures like Steve Albini and David Grubbs.

Label Context: Reflex Records (5)

Reflex Records (5) (Discogs label ID: 173010) was a punk/hardcore label based out of St. Paul, MN (731 Pontiac Place, Mendota Heights), co-founded by Terry Katzman and Hüsker Dü after Twin/Tone Records rejected Hüsker Dü’s first single in 1979. All releases carried alphabetical catalog numbers (e.g. “Reflex F”, “Reflex K”). The label was briefly distributed through a parent relationship with Dig It International.

Member Careers Beyond Man Sized Action

Brian Paulson (guitar, producer)

Paulson became one of the defining recording engineers and producers of American indie rock from the late 1980s onward. See Brian Paulson for full career. His earliest production credit is Five Story Garage (co-produced with Terry Katzman). Subsequent landmark credits include: Slint Spiderland (1991, engineer), Uncle Tupelo Anodyne (1993, producer/engineer), Wilco A.M. (1995), Son Volt Trace (1995) and Straightaways (1997), Archers of Loaf All The Nations Airports (1996), Beck Odelay (1996, partial), Superchunk, Future Islands, and many others. Aliases: James Bunchberry Lane, Sir James Bunchberry.

Tony Pucci (drums)

After Man Sized Action, Pucci joined Breaking Circus, a Minneapolis post-punk/noise band on Homestead Records. Breaking Circus released three LPs — The Very Long Fuse (1985), The Ice Machine (1986), Smokers’ Paradise (1987) — and a fanzine cassette (Home Of The Brave, 1989). Members of Breaking Circus included Todd Trainer (later of Shellac), Steve Björklund, Pete Conway, and Paul Hiraga. Pucci also later contributed design work to a 2014 release by The Arctic Flow.

Kelly Linehan (bass)

Linehan (Minneapolis; bass) was a member of Dragnet (3), a Minneapolis band who released a self-titled cassette (Never The Groom, 1985, self-released) and one LP, So Blind (1986, Tuesday Records). Linehan is credited on So Blind as bassist and co-writer. Other Dragnet (3) members: Greg Schaefer, Jim Crego.

Tippy (guitar)

Also credited on both Man Sized Action albums under the single-name “Tippy.” Later a member of The Silverteens, a Minneapolis band whose other members included Eric Pierson, Jerry Johnson, Mark Engebretson, Steve Olson, and Terry Isachsen. No studio discography for The Silverteens is present in Discogs.

Pat Woods (vocals)

Pat Woods (full Discogs name: Pat Woods (3)) is credited as vocalist on both Man Sized Action records. No other projects found in Discogs beyond Man Sized Action.
