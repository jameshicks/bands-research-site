Monopoly Child Star Searchers

Oct 26, 20265 min read

Monopoly Child Star Searchers

Primary alias of Spencer Clark, an American experimental musician. Discogs artist ID: 1001175. Not an alias of VEKTROID (Ramona Andra Xavier) — this is a common misattribution. The Discogs alias list for Monopoly Child Star Searchers matches Spencer Clark’s alias cluster exactly; VEKTROID’s alias list does not include this name.

Spencer Clark also operates as James Ferraro’s one-time collaborator The Skaters (with Ferraro), and runs the Pacific City label family (Pacific City Sound Visions, Pacific City Discs).

Overview

Monopoly Child Star Searchers is Spencer Clark’s most-documented project name, used primarily for his solo work in the hypnagogic pop, New Age, and tribal-ambient register. The name sits within a constellation of Clark aliases — Monopoly Child, Star Searchers, Monopoly Child’s Nightlife Band, Typhonian Highlife, Vodka Soap, Charles Berlitz, CB Cenobyte, Black Joker, HR Giger’s Studiolo, Fourth World Magazine, Melon Brains — all documenting a consistently ornate, tropical-fantasia sensibility rooted in fourth-world cassette culture, found-sound collage, and elaborate pseudonymic mythology.

The project emerged from the Pacific City scene Clark built in Los Angeles / Pacific Northwest in the late 2000s, informed by the same cassette-underground networks as James Ferraro, Lieven Martens (Dolphins Into The Future), and the Belgian tape circuit. Releases appeared on Clark’s own Pacific City labels, on Olde English Spelling Bee (one vinyl LP), and via collaborations with Lieven Martens’ Cetacean Nation label. The project was still active as of 2024.

Sonically, Monopoly Child Star Searchers recordings lean into extended, slow-burn New Age drift, gamelan and tribal percussion textures, forest/bird/water field recordings, and a kind of elaborately named exotica that treats the title card as part of the artwork. Track titles are characteristically maximalist: “A Coconut’s Shadow Thrown Across The Stem Of A Rose,” “Aviary’s Black Satin Stargate,” “Chicada Flashmasques.”
