Venom P. Stinger

Australian noise rock / post-punk band from Melbourne, Victoria. Discogs artist ID: 589548. Active approximately 1985–1996, with a compilation retrospective released in 2013 on Drag City. Pre-history for Mick Turner and Jim White (2), both of whom went on to form Dirty Three in 1992.

Biography

Formation and Early Period (1985–1987)

Venom P. Stinger formed in Melbourne in 1985. The founding core consisted of Mick Turner (guitar), Jim White (2) (drums), Alan Secher-Jensen (bass), and Dugald McKenzie (vocals). Their debut release, the EP Meet My Friend Venom (1986), came out on No Masters Voice, a Melbourne independent. Recording engineer was Kaj Dahlstrom; cover art by Anna White.

The band’s sound from the start was rooted in abrasive noise rock, drawing on American underground (Big Black, Scratch Acid) and Australian post-punk traditions alongside the broader Melbourne underground scene that included The Moodists, Crime & The City Solution, and Blackeyed Susans.

Aberrant Records Period (1988–1990)

Two full-lengths came out on Aberrant Records, an Australian independent that was a key document of the Melbourne underground scene:

  • Walking About (1988) — the classic lineup (Turner / White / Secher-Jensen / McKenzie), no recorded producer credit; cover art again by Anna White.
  • What’s Yours Is Mine (1990) — same lineup with Simon Grounds producing. Grounds was also associated with The Shower Scene From Psycho and Rococo Pops.

Mid-period and Lineup Transition (1991)

1991 saw two releases. Waiting Room (1991) came out on Au Go Go, a significant Melbourne independent. Production handled by Locky (2) and Des Amos. The Live album (1991) appeared on Anopheles, a US label, and was recorded without additional production credits on Discogs.

Around this period vocalist Dugald McKenzie departed and was replaced by Nick Palmer, who is credited as vocalist on the Thickskin 7” (1994) and as primary lyricist on Tearbucketer (1996). The transition in vocalists is one of the more significant lineup changes in the band’s history.

Later Period and Dissolution (1994–1996)

Thickskin (1994, Death Valley Records, cat. DV-004) was a limited, numbered 7” with two tracks: “Thickskin” (lyrics: Nick Palmer) and “Tearbucketer” (lyrics: Alan Secher-Jensen). Side A recorded by Cameron Paine; Side B produced by Mick Turner and Terry McDermott.

Tearbucketer (1996), released on the US label Siltbreeze, was the band’s final studio release. Credited lineup: Turner, White, Secher-Jensen, Palmer. Produced and engineered by Cameron Paine and Mick Turner. Mastered by Lindsay Gravina. The record featured lyrics split between Secher-Jensen (tracks 3, 4, 8) and Palmer (tracks 1, 2, 5–7, 9).

By this point Jim White and Mick Turner had already formed Dirty Three (1992), which became their primary vehicle. Venom P. Stinger wound down after Tearbucketer.

Retrospective (2013)

1986–1991 (2013) was released on Drag City (US) and R.I.P Society (Australia). The compilation collects material from the debut EP through to 1991 and was also issued as a promo. It credits the core lineup (Turner, White, Secher-Jensen, McKenzie) and names the original engineers and producers track-by-track.

Scene Context

Venom P. Stinger were embedded in the Melbourne underground of the mid-to-late 1980s, alongside bands including The Moodists, Crime & The City Solution, Blackeyed Susans, Kim Salmon And The Surrealists, and Fungus Brains. Au Go Go and Aberrant Records were the key local conduits for this scene. The US connection to Siltbreeze and Anopheles reflects the band’s reach into American underground networks.

Warren Ellis — no Discogs credit connecting Ellis to Venom P. Stinger was found. Ellis does not appear in the band’s credits on any release. His connection to the Melbourne underground runs through Dirty Three (which he co-founded) rather than through Venom P. Stinger.
