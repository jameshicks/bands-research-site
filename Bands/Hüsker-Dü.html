Hüsker Dü

Oct 12, 20264 min read

Hüsker Dü

Discogs ID: 129885
Origin: Saint Paul, Minnesota
Active: 1979–1988
Genres: Hardcore punk, post-punk, alternative rock

Minneapolis/Saint Paul trio: Bob Mould (guitar, vocals), Grant Hart (drums, vocals), Greg Norton (bass). One of the most influential American hardcore bands; transitioned from hardcore to melodic punk/alternative across their SST discography.

Numero Group reissued their pre-SST recordings as Savage Young Dü (NUM200, 2017) — a 4LP + 7” box covering demos and early recordings, produced for reissue by Ken Shipley, Rob Sevier, Dennis Pelowski, and Adam Luksetich; original recordings produced by Spot; mastered Jeff Lipton; design Henry H. Owings.

An earlier Numero release, Amusement (NUM704, 2013), preceded the main box.

Notes (web) — Numero Group / Savage Young Dü rights acquisition

The pre-SST reissue project ran roughly 2010–2017 and involved navigating two separate rights situations.

SST attempt abandoned. Numero initially wanted to reissue the SST-era catalog but could not clear it. An ongoing royalties dispute between the band and SST Records had long blocked catalogue activity for a band of Hüsker Dü’s stature. Numero pivoted to the pre-SST period (1979–1983) as a result. New Noise Magazine interview with Ken Shipley and Greg Norton, Oct 2017; The Second Disc, Sep 2017

Rights holders for the pre-SST material. The early recordings were released on two labels:

  • Reflex Records — the band’s own imprint, co-founded by Bob Mould and Terry Katzman (live soundman 1980–1983, also owner of Minneapolis shop Garage D’or). Reflex released Everything Falls Apart (1982) and related singles. Katzman remained active with Reflex into the 1990s (he wrote the 1993 CD reissue liner notes). The pre-SST rights thus effectively resided with the band/Katzman rather than a commercial label. Wikipedia, Everything Falls Apart, as of 2025-09-30
  • Tone Records — the Minneapolis label recorded a 1981 live set that became the alternate Land Speed Record tape included in the box. Wikipedia, Land Speed Record, as of 2025-09-30

Grant Hart as gatekeeper. Ken Shipley was advised that Hart would be the hardest of the three members to win over and targeted him first; Shipley described it as requiring “a herculean amount of effort.” All three members (Hart, Mould, Greg Norton) were kept informed throughout via email. New Noise Magazine, Oct 2017

Test release (2013). Before committing to the full box, Numero established the working relationship by reissuing the 1981 single “Statues”/“Amusement” (NUM704) for Record Store Day 2013. slicingupeyeballs.com, Jun 2017

Grant Hart’s reservations (post-deal). In a 2017 interview shortly before his death, Hart expressed ambivalence about how the archive access was arranged: “somebody involved opened up the whole archive to this Numero group, and they did it for all the wrong reasons. It was like, ‘We can get these transferred for nothing if we just let them use it.’ It’s not the deal that we signed. There was never a marketing strategy whatsoever as far as this box set is concerned.” Hart did not name the other party. The Current, Nov 2017

Note: Hart died of liver cancer on September 13, 2017, two months before Savage Young Dü was released in November 2017.
