Schola Cantorum Reykjavík

Discogs ID: 996770 Founded: 1996, Reykjavík, Iceland Type: Chamber choir Conductor / Artistic Director: Hörður Áskelsson Home: Hallgrímskirkja (Hallgrím’s Church), Reykjavík Website: https://www.scholacantorum.is/

Icelandic chamber choir founded in 1996 by conductor Hörður Áskelsson, who is the cantor at Hallgrímskirkja (Hallgrím’s Church) in Reykjavík. The choir’s repertoire spans Renaissance polyphony to contemporary commissioned works. Discogs name variations: Schola Cantorum, Schola Cantorum Og Kammersveit, Schola Cantorum Reykjavicensis, Schola Contorum.

Overview

Schola Cantorum Reykjavík operates as both an independent ensemble with its own recordings on Icelandic labels (Hallgrímskirkja label, Smekkleysa, Skálholtsútgáfan, Geimsteinn) and as a hired chorus on international label recordings via BIS Records and Resonus Classics. Their recorded output since 1999 ranges from Renaissance masters (Tallis, Byrd, Gesualdo, Schein) through the Icelandic hymn repertoire to large-scale contemporary orchestral works by Jón Leifs and contemporary Icelandic composers. By 2023 the choir’s profile had expanded internationally, contributing chorus to Bear McCreary’s God of War: Ragnarök (Sony Classical/PlayStation) and to new-music releases on XXIM Records and Dacapo.

Biography

Founding and Early Releases (1996–2002)

The choir was established in 1996 by Hörður Áskelsson in his capacity as cantor at Hallgrímskirkja, the landmark Lutheran church that dominates Reykjavík’s skyline. The debut album Principium (Hallgrímskirkja label, 1999) was a primary-artist release credited jointly to Schola Cantorum Reykjavík, Hörður Áskelsson, and four Renaissance composers: Thomas Tallis, Johann Hermann Schein, Carlo Gesualdo, and William Byrd. The album documented the choir’s early membership in detail — a SATB roster including sopranos Helga Dögg Sigurðardóttir, Hera Björk (later an Eurovision contestant), Hildigunnur Halldórsdóttir, Hugrún Hólmgeirsdóttir, Ingibjörg Eyþórsdóttir, Jónína Guðrún Kristinsdóttir, and Kirstín Erna Blöndal; altos Anna Hinriksdóttir, Guðrún Edda Gunnarsdóttir, Guðrún Finnbjarnardóttir, and Valgerður Ólafsdóttir; tenors Björgvin Tómasson, Gísli Magnason, Guðlaugur Viktorsson, Heimir S. Jónatansson, and Örn Arnarson; and basses Benedikt Ingólfsson, Hjálmar Pétursson, Robert Darling, Sigurjón Halldórsson, Sigurður Snorrason, and Valdimar Másson. Recording was by Sveinn Kjartansson.

In 2000, the choir was engaged as chorus on the BIS Records recording of Jón Leifs’s Hafís and Other Works for Voices and Orchestra, alongside the Iceland Symphony Orchestra conducted by Anne Manson. This was the choir’s first appearance on an international-label release and introduced the Áskelsson/Schola pairing to the wider Leifs discography.

Sálmar Í Gleði (Skálholtsútgáfan, 2002), an Icelandic hymn recording, documented a slightly evolved roster with organ accompaniment from Kári Þormar (most tracks), Áskelsson himself (one track), and Aðalheiður Þorsteinsdóttir (one track), plus Martial Nardeau on flute and Ásgeir H. Steingrímsson and Eiríkur Örn Pálsson on trumpets. Music director Inga Rós Ingólfsdóttir assisted; Sveinn Kjartansson recorded.

Mid-Period: Smekkleysa and BIS (2004–2009)

The choir appeared in the Screaming Masterpiece documentary (Ergis Film Production, 2005), the internationally distributed film portrait of Icelandic music — performances listed under the project. Also in 2004, Barn Er Oss Fætt / Unto Us (A Christmas Oratorio) (Smekkleysa) featured Schola alongside the Iceland Symphony Orchestra, with Garðar Þór Cortes (bass-baritone) and soprano Elín Ósk Óskarsdóttir. The same year saw Tvísöngur (Smekkleysa, 2004), a release that also connected Schola to Árni Heimir Ingólfsson and to ensemble Feðranna Frægð.

The major recording of this period was Edda, Part 1: The Creation of the World (BIS, 2007), Jón Leifs’s monumental choral-orchestral work, with Schola as chorus master by Áskelsson, bass-baritone Bjarni Thor Kristinsson, tenor Gunnar Guðbjörnsson, the Iceland Symphony Orchestra, and conductor Hermann Bäumer. Production was by Ingo Petry; engineering/mixing by Jens Braun.

2011–2016: Icelandic Contemporary and Resonus Classics

Two 2011 releases further wove the choir into Reykjavík’s contemporary-music fabric: Hallgrímspassía (Not On Label) was a joint primary-artist release with composer Sigurður Sævarsson, The Caput Ensemble, and vocalist Jóhann Smári Sævarsson, conducted by Áskelsson. Flétta (Listvinafélag Hallgrímskirkju, 2011) was a joint release with composer Haukur Tómasson, Mótettukór Hallgrímskirkju, and the Reykjavík Chamber Orchestra.

Foldarskart (self-released, 2012) added Sveinn Kjartansson and Sverrir Guðjónsson as contributors.

Choral Works (Resonus Classics, 2013) — credited jointly to Icelandic composer Hafliði Hallgrímsson, Schola, and Áskelsson — was their first UK-label release. The recording brought in harpist Elísabet Waage, organist Björn Steinar Sólbergsson, percussionist Steef van Oosterhout, and trumpeter Ásgeir H. Steingrímsson, with Sveinn Kjartansson as producer and engineer.

Meditatio: Music for Mixed Choir (BIS, 2016) was another primary-artist BIS release — Schola and Áskelsson as co-headliners — produced and engineered by Jens Braun, with liner notes by Halldór Gunnlaugur Hauksson.

2019–2025: Expanded International Reach

Edda, Part II (BIS, 2019) completed the Jón Leifs Edda cycle, again with Hermann Bäumer conducting and the Iceland Symphony Orchestra, adding tenor Kristinn Sigmundsson and mezzo Hanna Dóra Sturludóttir.

Kom Vinur (Sono Luminus, 2021) brought the choir into a new collaborative context: María Huld Markan Sigfúsdóttir’s solo choral single, setting poems by Vilborg Dagbjartsdóttir, with Áskelsson conducting. Credited on-sleeve as “Schola Cantorum.”

Three major 2023 releases showed the choir’s widening scope:

  • The Gospel of Mary (Dacapo, 2023) — music by Hugi Guðmundsson, with soprano Berit Norbakken Solset and the Århus Sinfonietta; the most thoroughly documented later-period member roster in Discogs (see Personnel table below).
  • The Light (XXIM Records, 2023) — choir credit alongside contemporary ambient/orchestral production.
  • God of War: Ragnarök (Sony Classical/PlayStation, 2023) — chorus alongside Hymnodia Choir, with Bear McCreary composing and conducting; Áskelsson credited specifically as “Conductor [Schola Cantorum and Hymnodia Choir].”

Meditatio II (Music for Mixed Choir) (BIS, 2023) continued the Meditatio series with Jens Braun.

Oceanic Mirror (XXIM Records, 2025) is the most recent Discogs-documented credit.
