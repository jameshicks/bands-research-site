General Strike

Experimental/dub trio. UK. Discogs artist ID: 156118. Founded 1979. Active 1979–1985 (recordings); reissue activity continued through 2023.

Name variations on Discogs: General/Strike, General, Strike, General-Strike, General/Strike, Strike/General.

Overview

General Strike formed in 1979 from the extended collective around The Flying Lizards. All three core members — David Cunningham, David Toop, and Steve Beresford — were also Flying Lizards members, and their writing credits appear on the Flying Lizards’ debut LP (1980, Virgin) and the TV single (1979). The group functioned as a more deliberately experimental outlet, operating in the space between dub, electro-acoustic improvisation, and post-punk.

Their debut, My Body (1979, Canal (4)), is a two-sided 7” produced by Cunningham. Their principal work, Danger In Paradise (1984, Touch), was released on Touch Records as a cassette — one of the label’s early releases — and is a 14-track record of dub-drenched experimental improvisation, drawing on Sun Ra compositions (“Interplanetary Music,” “We Travel The Spaceways”) alongside originals. Lol Coxhill guests on soprano and tenor saxophone across three tracks; Dawn Roberts contributes vocals on one track; Maartje Ten Hoorn plays violin on two tracks. The record was co-produced by all three members, with Cunningham providing tape treatments and effects, Toop playing prepared guitar, flute, and percussion, and Beresford covering an unusually wide range: bass, piano, glockenspiel, Farfisa organ, Prophet 5 synthesizer, trumpet, flugelhorn, euphonium, toy piano, melodica, and percussion.

System Of Flux & Energies (1985, Sub Rosa) is a three-way split LP alongside SPK and Hula, released on the Belgian experimental label Sub Rosa (cat. SUB33002-3). General Strike contribute one track: “Reach For N.” (5:45), with Toop and Beresford as instrumentalists, mixed by Toop, Beresford, and Dave Hunt.

Danger In Paradise was reissued on CD by Piano in 1996 (PIANO 503), and on digital platforms and vinyl by Staubgold in 2012 (staubgold digital 18) and again in 2023. The reissues were compiled by Cunningham and Jon Wozencroft.

The group also holds co-producer credits (uncredited on some pressings) on Rockin’ Class (2013, France) — a release on their own General Strike imprint in partnership with The Midnight Rovers Productions.

Scene Connections

  • UK experimental / post-punk: All three members were embedded in the same network as This Heat, Family Fodder, and the wider DIY experimental scene c.1979–1985.
  • The Flying Lizards: All three are Flying Lizards members. Writing credits from General Strike appear on the Flying Lizards debut. The overlap is near-total in personnel.
  • Touch: Danger In Paradise was an early Touch release (cat. TO: 2), placing General Strike in the catalogue alongside later Touch artists such as Jon Wozencroft’s long-running curation project.
  • Free improvisation: Beresford and Coxhill both moved in the same London free improv circles — Company (2), Alterations, etc. The Sun Ra covers on Danger In Paradise signal the group’s free jazz awareness.
  • Belgian experimental (Sub Rosa): Inclusion on the 1985 Sub Rosa compilation placed them alongside SPK (Graeme Revell era) and Hula, connecting them to the European industrial/experimental axis.
  • Dub: The dub orientation runs throughout Danger In Paradise — track titles like “Interplanetary Dub,” “Babycart To Hell,” and “Bamboo House Of Dolls” invoke B-movie exotica and dub production aesthetics.
