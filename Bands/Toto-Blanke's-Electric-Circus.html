Toto Blanke’s Electric Circus

German-international jazz-fusion band led by guitarist Toto Blanke (Hans Otto Blanke, 1939–2013). Discogs ID: 752250. The band was an explicit sequel project to Electric Circus (3), Blanke’s earlier fusion outfit, and ran from roughly 1979 to 1983, releasing three LPs on German labels.

The band’s most distinctive feature was its rotating international cast — Stu Goldberg (ex-Mahavishnu Orchestra) provided the American fusion keyboard pedigree, Trilok Gurtu (Indian percussionist, later associated with Oregon and John McLaughlin) brought a world-music percussion dimension, and a core German rhythm section anchored the group. By the third album (Bella Donna, 1983), both Goldberg and Gurtu had departed and the lineup had contracted significantly.

Biography

Background: Toto Blanke Before Electric Circus

Toto Blanke made his professional debut in 1969 with his own trio after classical guitar studies. Through the early-to-mid 1970s he worked primarily as a sideman on the German and Dutch jazz-fusion circuit:

  • Association P.C. (with Jasper Van’t Hof and Pierre Courbois) — Earwax (1970), Confrontation (1972)
  • Electric Circus (3) (with Jasper Van’t Hof and Edward Vesala) — Sun Rotation (1972, MPS Records)
  • Various MPS Records projects: Erna Morena (1973), Rock Around The Cock (1973), Cinemascope (1974), Mama Kuku (1974)

He launched his solo career in 1975 with Spider’s Dance (Vertigo), followed by Electric Circus (1976, Vertigo) — the album that gave the sequel band its name logic. Both were released under his own name on Vertigo.

Toto Blanke’s Electric Circus (1979–1983)

The band formed for the 1979 debut Friends on Bacillus Records (a sublabel of Bellaphon Records GmbH). The record was credited as “Toto Blanke’s Electric Circus featuring Stu Goldberg,” signalling Goldberg’s co-equal billing. Blanke and Norbert Dömling co-produced; Blanke wrote seven of the eight tracks, with Goldberg contributing the closing title track “Friends (Dedicated To T. Blanke).” Blanke also supplied the cover artwork.

The 1980 follow-up Family (Aliso Music, catalog 1010) retained the core lineup but added guest saxophonist Charlie Mariano on one track (“Zana,” B3), and shifted production to Elmar Hartmann. The label change — from Bacillus to Aliso Music (a sublabel of Aliso Records) — marked a move away from the Bellaphon distribution chain to a smaller independent. Engineers Andreas Spreer and Reinhard Metzger handled the sessions; mixing was by Manfred Battran.

By Bella Donna (1983, Aliso Records, catalog 1014), the lineup had changed substantially: Goldberg and Gurtu were gone, replaced by Hungarian drummer Szende Gábor and saxophonist Tony Lakatos. Bassist Norbert Dömling remained. Blanke’s daughter (or relation) Billi Blanke played accordion on the closing track “Märchen Aus Fernen Ländern.” Co-production was shared with Heiner Beringmeier. The album dropped jazz-rock and prog-rock style tags from Discogs, suggesting a somewhat different sonic direction.

Bacillus Records was a significant West German jazz and progressive imprint, part of the larger Bellaphon Records GmbH group. The shift to Aliso Records for the second and third albums likely reflects a smaller, more autonomous distribution arrangement; Blanke continued to record for Aliso into the mid-1980s (Kirchenmusik!, 1984).

Toto Blanke Solo Context

Blanke’s work with the Electric Circus sits mid-career, between his Vertigo solo albums and his later 1980s output:

Earlier sideman credits include the MPS Records orbit (Association P.C., Electric Circus (3), Rolf Kühn Group) and a guest appearance on Edward Vesala’s Rodina (Love Records, 1977). He also appeared on Live At The Quartier Latin (Vinyl Records, 1979).
