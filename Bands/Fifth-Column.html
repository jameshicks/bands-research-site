Overview

Fifth Column are a Canadian all-women experimental post-punk band from Toronto, widely regarded as one of the founding acts of the queercore movement. Active from 1980 to 1995, they released four full-length records (plus a soundtrack cassette), two 7” singles, and contributed tracks to several notable compilations. Their song “The Fairview Mall Story” — about police entrapment of gay men at a Toronto shopping mall — is cited as instrumental in paving the way for the emergence of the queercore scene. Their live shows frequently incorporated films projected over the band and a go-go dancer (Bruce LaBruce, who later became a noted filmmaker).

The band was independent-minded and self-releasing throughout most of their run, issuing the majority of their catalog on their own Hide imprint. Their final label relationship was with K Records (Olympia, WA), which released both their 1992 single and their last full-length, 36c (1994).

Biography by Era

Formation and Early Period (1980–1986)

The band began as a trio called Second Unit, consisting of GB Jones on drums, Kathleen Pirrie Adams on bass, and Janet Martin on guitar. Caroline Azar saw the group perform in the fall of 1980, auditioned successfully as singer, and the four-piece played their first gig as Fifth Column in late 1980.

The first recorded output was the Boy / Girl EP (1983, Voicespondence, VSP 009) — a 7” 33⅓ RPM release produced by Clive Robertson and the band. GB Jones contributed photography and cover design. The three-track EP establishes the post-punk template: tightly wound guitar, minimal production, confrontational subject matter.

Kathleen Pirrie Adams departed around 1982; Anita Smith (3) joined on bass and remained through 1986. Janet Martin played guitar until 1985, replaced by Charlotte Briedé (1985–1990).

Their debut full-length, To Sir With Hate (1986, Hide, cat. .006 1/2 / WRC1-4512), was a nine-track LP co-produced with Michael Phillip Wojewoda. The title is a direct pun on the Lulu song from the British film To Sir with Love. Key track: “The Fairview Mall Story,” based on real events in which a Toronto newspaper published the names of men arrested after police entrapment at Fairview Mall — a landmark song in queer punk history. A video for the track was directed by Steven Rumbelow, intercut with 1950s cruising imagery and go-go dancer Bruce LaBruce. A second video, “Where Are They Now?”, was directed by Marc DeGuerre.

GB Jones moved from drums to guitar shortly after To Sir With Hate, and Beverly Breckenridge joined on bass in 1986. From this point the band rotated through a series of second guitarists and (mostly male) drummers.

Middle Period (1989–1992)

Work Soundtrack (1989, Hide, HIDE SIX) was a cassette-only release. It served as the score to a film project; several tracks were remixed by GB Jones (credited as “Gloria B.”) and Peter Hudson, who co-engineered portions of the project. Producer: Sandor Ajenstadt.

The band contributed percussion to Improvise With Eric Nagler (1989, Rounder Records, ROUNDER CD 8018), a children’s music album — an unusual detour.

All-Time Queen Of The World (1990, Hide, HIDE 009) is their second full-length LP. Recorded track-by-track with two engineer-producers: Walter Sobczak and Peter Hudson, who split engineering duties across the nine tracks. The band self-produced. A cassette edition (HIDE 009-C) was also issued. Bruce LaBruce directed the video for “Like This.”

Donna / All Women Are Bitches (1992, K Records, IPU 33) is their first major-label 7” single, released on Calvin Johnson’s K Records (Olympia). The A-side “Donna” features Beverly Breckenridge (bass/backing vocals), Chas Salmon/Charlie Salmon (guitar), Michelle Breslin (guitar), GB Jones (guitar/backing vocals), Caroline Azar (vocals/keyboards), with Don Pyle on drums. The B-side “All Women Are Bitches” features Joel Wasson on drums and was co-produced with Walter Sobczak. Lyrics credited jointly to Caroline Azar and GB Jones. Despite limited airplay, it was reviewed by Everett True in Melody Maker and voted “Single Of The Week.” The song was later covered as “Bitchsy” by Lesbians On Ecstasy (2004) and remixed/sampled by Kids on TV for a 2005 compilation.

Late Period (1993–1995)

Chas Salmon/Charlotte Briedé’s era ends; Donna Dresch (of Team Dresch) joined briefly as second guitarist in 1993. Michelle Breslin remained on guitar through the end of the band (1992–1995). Don Pyle (of Shadowy Men On A Shadowy Planet) drummed 1992–1993; Torry Colichio took over 1993–1994; Luc Menard played the final shows in 1995.

Split Single (1993, Outpunk Records, OUT9) — a 7” 45 RPM split with God Is My Co-Pilot. Fifth Column contribute the A-side track “Don’t”; God Is My Co-Pilot fill the B-side with three tracks (“Probable Cause,” “Life Under Occupation,” “Held Down”). Fifth Column side produced and engineered by Anita Smith (3). Photography on the Fifth Column side by Jena Von Brücker; Michelle Breslin contributes backing vocals. The split was issued on Matt Wobensmith’s San Francisco queercore label, linking the Toronto and NYC queercore scenes.

36c (1994, K Records / Denon, KLP 26CD / KLP 26CAS / KLP 26) is their final full-length and the most fully documented release in the Discogs database. A 10-track CD (also released on cassette and vinyl). Full lineup:

  • Caroline Azar — vocals (all tracks), organ (tracks 3, 5, 6, 8–10), guitar (credited as “10”), lyrics on most tracks
  • GB Jones — guitar (tracks 1–4, 6–10), drums (tracks 4, 8, 10), backing vocals (tracks 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10), lyrics
  • Beverly Breckenridge — bass (all tracks), backing vocals (tracks 3, 4, 7, 10)
  • Michelle Breslin — guitar (tracks 3–9), backing vocals (tracks 3, 4)
  • Donna Dresch — guitar (tracks 2, 9), lyrics on “Donna” (track 6)
  • Chas Salmon — guitar (track 1)
  • Don Pyle — drums (track 6)
  • Joel Wasson — drums (track 1)
  • Torry Colichio — drums (tracks 2, 3, 5, 7, 9)
  • Human Ashtrays — background vocals (track 1)

Producers: Fifth Column (most tracks), Walter Sobczak (tracks 1, 3–5, 7, 8, 10), Anita Smith (3) (track 6), Caroline Azar (track 5). Mixed by Grant Ethier and Fifth Column (tracks 2, 9). Recorded by Dale Morningstar (tracks 2, 9). Mastered by George Graves. Cover concept: Beverly Breckenridge, Caroline Azar, GB Jones. Layout and design: Antoine Moonen. Jena Von Brücker contributed drawings, cover model photography, and band photographs.

I Love You But… / These Boots Were Made For Walking (1995, Dark Beloved Cloud, DBC019) — a split 7” with Trailer Queen; Fifth Column contribute “I Love You, But…” The band played their final show in Ottawa in early 1995.

Post-Dissolution

A 2002 Kill Rock Stars double-LP/CD compilation, Fields and Streams (KRS), includes Fifth Column’s track “Imbecile” (disc 2, track 3), written by Caroline Azar, Beverly Breckenridge, and GB Jones — the band’s last recorded appearance.

Members have pursued various projects: Caroline Azar recorded with Kickstand, Shadowy Men On A Shadowy Planet, Greek Buck, and Hidden Cameras. Beverly Breckenridge was also a member of Phono-Comb and It’s Patrick!. GB Jones had been a member of the synth-punk band Bunny & The Lakers prior to Fifth Column, and has since performed with Mariae Nascenti (Italian dark ambient) and Opera Arcana.

The band appears in the film Fifth Column at the Funnel (dir. John Porter, Toronto experimental filmmaker). All recent-era members, plus some former ones, appear in GB Jones’ film The Yo-Yo Gang, for which the band recorded the theme song.

Open Questions

  • Discogs lists “active 1980–1995” but the profile mentions the band’s “last release was in 2002” (Fields and Streams) — the period 1995–2002 is not documented in the primary discography; it is unclear if there was any activity between the final show and the compilation appearance.
  • Exact birth/real names for several members (GB Jones, Caroline Azar, Charlotte Briedé) are not listed in Discogs.
  • The Work Soundtrack cassette (1989) — unclear what film it accompanied. Discogs credits it to the “Stage & Screen” genre but does not identify the film.
  • Anne McLean (bass, unknown years) — no Discogs page found; exact tenure unclear.
  • Bruce LaBruce’s go-go dancer role and subsequent film career: well-documented web-side but not in Discogs.
  • The relationship between Fifth Column’s activities and the Toronto zine scene (GB Jones published J.D.s zine with Bruce LaBruce, considered foundational to queercore) is not capturable via Discogs alone — web escalation recommended to document this.
  • The Discogs compilations tool returned no results for artist 1213991 — this likely reflects a DB indexing gap rather than absence of compilation appearances (the Fields and Streams track is confirmed via track-level SQL).
