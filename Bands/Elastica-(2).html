Elastica (2)

British indie rock / post-punk band formed in London in October 1992. Discogs artist ID: 232940. The “(2)” disambiguation is Discogs convention. Active 1992–2001. Disbanded amicably in October 2001 after failed sessions for a third album.

Overview

Formed in mid-1992 when Suede refugees Justine Frischmann and Justin Welch recruited bassist Annie Holland and guitarist Donna Matthews. They played early shows under the name “Onk” before settling on Elastica. The band were stylistically rooted in post-punk and new wave — Wire and The Stranglers were explicit influences, to a degree that landed them in plagiarism disputes settled out of court. (Wire’s “Three Girl Rhumba” riff is audible in “Connection”; “Waking Up” closely resembles The Stranglers’ “No More Heroes”.)

Their debut single “Stutter” (November 1993, Deceptive Records) was championed by BBC Radio 1 DJ and Deceptive label boss Steve Lamacq. “Line Up” and “Connection” followed in 1994, both UK Top 20. The debut album Elastica (March 1995) entered the UK Albums Chart at No. 1 and became the fastest-selling debut in UK history at the time — a record it held until the Arctic Monkeys’ debut in 2006. It sold over a million copies worldwide and went gold in the UK, Canada, and the US.

The band toured North America four times in 1995, including the Lollapalooza tour. Annie Holland quit from exhaustion in mid-1995 (replaced temporarily by session bassist Abby Travis, then permanently by Sheila Chipperfield in spring 1996). Keyboardist Dave Bush (ex-The Fall) joined the same year. By late 1998, Donna Matthews had left; she was replaced by guitarist Paul Jones (13) (of Linoleum) and keyboardist Mew (3) (Sharon Mew). Annie Holland returned.

Damon Albarn — Frischmann’s partner 1991–1998 — played keyboards on both albums, credited under anagrams (“Dan Abnormal” on Elastica, “Norman Balda” on The Menace). Development of The Menace was slowed by line-up changes and Frischmann’s heroin addiction. The album appeared in April 2000 on Deceptive/Atlantic and was significantly less successful. When Deceptive collapsed financially in February 2001 and Atlantic dropped the band, Elastica announced their split in October 2001. Their farewell single “The Bitch Don’t Work” appeared on Wichita in November 2001. (Source: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastica)

Key Credits — The Menace (2000)

Co-produced by Elastica and Marc Waterman (most tracks), with Alan Moulder co-producing “Image Change” and Bruce Lampcov co-producing “My Sex”. Engineered by Philip Bagenal and Tom Rixton. Mixed by Phil Vinall, Miti Adhikari, Alan Moulder, Marc Waterman.

Notable: “Human” co-written with all four members of Wire (Bruce Gilbert, Colin Newman, Graham Lewis, Robert Gotobed). “How He Wrote Elastica Man” and “KB” co-written with Mark E. Smith and Julia Nagle (The Fall). “Da Da Da” is a cover of Trio (Gert Krawinkel / Stephan Remmler). Cover photography by M.I.A. (credited as M. Arulpragasm — Maya Arulpragasam, later developed by Frischmann post-Elastica).

Connections

  • Suede — Frischmann and Welch were founding members before starting Elastica
  • Blur — Frischmann dated Damon Albarn 1991–1998; Albarn played keyboards on both Elastica albums under pseudonyms
  • Wire — primary musical influence; plagiarism dispute settled out of court; Wire members co-wrote “Human” on The Menace
  • The Stranglers — shared London rehearsal studios 1994; “Waking Up” resembles “No More Heroes”; publishers sued Elastica, dispute settled
  • The Fall — Dave Bush joined Elastica after his time in The Fall; Mark E. Smith co-wrote and appeared on 6 Track EP and The Menace
  • Lush — Justin Welch drummed for Lush’s 2015–16 reunion shows
  • Piroshka — post-Elastica band featuring Justin Welch and Mew Welch (Sharon Mew); two albums on Bella Union
  • Deceptive — home label; also released Collapsed Lung, Scarfo, Snuff (3), Earl Brutus, Jonathan Fire-Eater, Placebo (debut), Idlewild (debut)
  • Marc Waterman — primary producer/engineer across Elastica’s career
  • Alan Moulder — key mixing engineer; appeared on both albums
  • Phil Vinall — frequent mixing engineer; worked on both albums
  • John Leckie — mixed several tracks on the debut album
  • Justine Frischmann — founder, vocalist; post-Elastica developed M.I.A. and became a visual artist
  • Justin Welch — co-founder, drummer; remains musically active via Piroshka
  • Donna Matthews — lead guitarist 1992–1998; later formed Klang (2) and became a pastor
  • Annie Holland — bassist; original and final lineup member
  • Dave Bush — keyboards; also played keyboards on The Fall’s Shift-Work, Code: Selfish, and subsequent albums
  • Damon Albarn — guest keyboardist on both albums under anagrammatic pseudonyms
  • Mark E. Smith — collaborated on 6 Track EP and The Menace
  • Brett Anderson — co-wrote “See That Animal” (dated Frischmann before Albarn)

Post-Breakup

  • Justine Frischmann: moved to Boulder, Colorado (2005); studied visual arts at Naropa University; became a painter; co-produced early M.I.A. demos including “Galang” (2003); married Ian Faloona (2008); lives in the San Francisco Bay Area.
  • Justin Welch: drummed for Lush 2015–16 reunion; now in Piroshka (with Sharon Mew, aka Mew Welch) releasing two albums on Bella Union.
  • Donna Matthews: formed Klang (2) around 2004; became a pastor in Totnes.
  • Annie Holland: lives in Brighton.
  • Dave Bush: continued design work alongside music; credits include graphic design for jazz releases on Whirlwind Recordings.
  • Paul Jones (13): became A&R man at Rough Trade; signed Warpaint; previously ran the Slogan label (released The Fall’s Fall Heads Roll).
  • 2017: Three-quarters of the original lineup (Matthews, Holland, Welch) visited Abbey Road to remaster the debut album; Frischmann also contributed. Elastica reissued April 2017 by Rough Trade.
