Rich Kids

Origin: London, England Active: 1977–1979 (reunion shows 2010, 2016, 2019) Label: EMI Genre: New wave, power pop, punk rock

British new wave band formed in London in early 1977 by Glen Matlock following his departure from the Sex Pistols. The classic four-piece — Matlock on bass, Midge Ure on guitar/vocals, Steve New on lead guitar, and Rusty Egan on drums — became a direct personnel bridge between post-Sex Pistols punk and the emerging New Romantic movement: Ure and Egan began forming Visage while still active in Rich Kids.

History

Formation (1977)

Glen Matlock was fired from the Sex Pistols in February 1977 and immediately began assembling a new band. An early lineup included keyboardist Bill Smyth, drummer Rusty Egan, lead guitarist Steve New, and briefly Mick Jones of The Clash. By mid-1977, Scottish musician Midge Ure — previously of Slik — was recruited as lead vocalist and rhythm guitarist, completing the classic lineup. Smyth and Jones departed.

Active years and recording (1977–1978)

The band signed to EMI and released their debut single “Rich Kids” / “Empty Words” in January 1978 (EMI 2738), reaching UK #24. Two further singles followed, and the debut album Ghosts of Princes in Towers — produced by Mick Ronson — was released in August 1978 (EMI 3263), reaching UK #51. The band recorded sessions for BBC Radio 1’s John Peel Show and performed on Top of the Pops.

Creative tensions emerged when Midge Ure purchased a synthesizer; only Ure and Rusty Egan wished to incorporate synthesizers into the band’s sound. This division — combined with their mutual enthusiasm for Ultravox’s Systems of Romance (1978) — led Ure and Egan to begin working on a parallel synth project that would become Visage, while Rich Kids were still nominally active.

Mick Ronson as producer

The connection between Mick Ronson and the Rich Kids came through shared management: Ronson himself was a client of the same management outfit that handled the Rich Kids. As Matlock put it in 2024: “He was managed by these two guys who also managed the Rich Kids.” The Rich Kids’ manager was Pete Walmesley (management company: Double Rich Management) — roadstories.co.uk; the identity of the second manager and whether the firm was also formally Ronson’s management company remains unconfirmed in available sources.

Glen Matlock has described the introduction in interviews promoting his 2024 memoir Triggers: A Life in Music: while hanging around the managers’ office, he answered the phone and heard a northern accent asking for “Peter.” Matlock recognised the voice and asked if it was “Michael Ronson”; Ronson confirmed. Matlock’s immediate response was to invite him to a rehearsal the next day — “We’re looking for a record producer and we’re rehearsing tomorrow. Do you want to come down?” Ronson attended, they had “a bit of a jam,” went to the pub “and got hammered drunk, all of us, and had a laugh,” and subsequently entered the studio together. Rock Cellar Magazine interview with Glen Matlock, published 14 May 2024

Rusty Egan later corroborated the shared-manager route, and recalled the band’s regard for Ronson before the collaboration: “The Rich Kids, The Skids… we were all influenced by Ronson.” maggironson.com/about-mick/ The album was recorded at John Kongo’s home studio. punk77.co.uk Steve New spoke of the significance of the collaboration to him personally: “Working with Mick was really important for me, I respected him so much, as a musician Mick was incredible… he produced ‘Walk On The Wild Side’, and did the whole Transformer LP, it’s credited as Ronson and Bowie but it’s all Ronson’s arrangements.” 3AM Magazine interview with Steve New

Ronson also played keyboards on several tracks (tracks 1, 3, 6, and 10) and performed live with the band — appearing on guitar at their Lyceum show in spring 1978 (Ian McLagan on keyboards that same night). Matlock described Ronson’s production approach as accepting the band “pretty much as they were, but kind of getting the most out of them.” Recording took place partly at Abbey Road Studio 2, where Ronson experimented with running drums through a dustbin placed in the middle of the double-wide studio — an experiment Ronson himself judged a failure (“Well, it’s shit, innit?”). Ronson was credited by Matlock as collaborative and ego-free: when Matlock objected to a guitar part Ronson had added, Ronson simply agreed and removed it. For the song “Lovers and Fools” Ronson contributed an arrangement using clapping in place of drums. Rock Cellar Magazine, 2024

Matlock also recalled a prior encounter with Ronson that pre-dated Rich Kids: when Matlock was working in Malcolm McLaren’s shop Let It Rock on the King’s Road, Ronson (accompanied by Ian Hunter and Suzi) came in to buy shoes, and helped Matlock restack the shoeboxes afterwards. Matlock described him as “a lovely bloke from the off.” maggironson.com/about-mick/

Dissolution (late 1978–1979)

Rich Kids disbanded in December 1978 due to the creative impasse over synthesizers and direction. Midge Ure joined Ultravox in April 1979.

Reunion shows

Brief reunion shows took place in 2010, 2016, and 2019. Steve New’s final live performance was 7 January 2010 at Islington Academy with a reformed Rich Kids. He died of cancer on 24 May 2010, aged 50.

Connection to Visage

The Rich Kids are a direct personnel bridge from post-Sex Pistols punk into the New Romantic movement. While still members of Rich Kids, Midge Ure and Rusty Egan began hosting David Bowie and Roxy Music-themed club nights at Billy’s nightclub in Soho, alongside Steve Strange. These nights became the seedbed for Visage.

Ure and Egan began working with Strange during Rich Kids’ final months (late 1978), motivated by their shared fondness for synthesizer-led music that the rest of Rich Kids rejected. Strange had recently left the new wave band The Photons. Together they recruited Billy Currie (from Ultravox) and three members of Magazine: John McGeoch (guitar/saxophone), Dave Formula (keyboards), and Barry Adamson (bass — who departed after the debut single).

Visage’s debut single “Tar” was released on Radar Records in September 1979 — after Rich Kids had dissolved (December 1978) and after Ure had already joined Ultravox (April 1979). Ure thus participated in Visage’s first phase before withdrawing as Ultravox demands grew.

Overlap timeline:

  • Late 1978: Ure and Egan begin Visage sessions while Rich Kids still active
  • December 1978: Rich Kids dissolve
  • April 1979: Midge Ure joins Ultravox
  • September 1979: Visage debut single released

Members’ Onward Paths

Midge Ure

Joined Ultravox in April 1979, becoming their lead vocalist and driving their most commercially successful period (Vienna, 1980). Also a founding member of Visage. Co-organised Band Aid with Bob Geldof (1984).

Rusty Egan

Co-founded Visage (1978) and became the resident DJ at the Blitz club in Covent Garden (1979–1981) alongside Steve Strange — one of the key venues and figures of the New Romantic movement. Co-opened Camden Palace nightclub with Strange in 1982. Also drummed on a remixed version of Phil Lynott’s “Yellow Pearl” (used as the BBC Top of the Pops theme, 1981–1986). Published autobiography 2025.

Glen Matlock

After Rich Kids, briefly played with Steve New in a one-off show as the Vicious White Kids (with Sid Vicious, Electric Ballroom, 15 August 1978 — technically before the band fully dissolved). Then toured with Iggy Pop in 1979 and played bass on Iggy Pop’s Soldier LP (1980). Formed The Spectres with Danny Kustow; later the Hot Club (1982) with James Stevenson and Steve Allen. Rejoined the original Sex Pistols for reunion tours from 1996 onward. Extensive subsequent session and band work.

Steve New

After Rich Kids, session work for Public Image Ltd, Wasted Youth, Chrissie Hynde, Johnny Thunders, Patti Palladin. Played on Iggy Pop’s Soldier LP (1980). Rehearsed with Generation X for Kiss Me Deadly but was rejected due to drug unreliability. Also rejected an early offer from Duran Duran. Released solo album Here Comes Everybody (2001). In the early 2000s adopted the name Stella Nova and formed the experimental pop band Beastellabeast with Beatrice Brown (three albums, 2004–2010). His final live performance was 7 January 2010 with a reformed Rich Kids. Died of cancer 24 May 2010, aged 50.

Sources: Wikipedia Steve New, as of 2026-06-25; Wikipedia Glen Matlock; Wikipedia Rusty Egan]
