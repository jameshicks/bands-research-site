Overview

The Swinging Laurels are a Leicester new-pop/new-wave act, formed in 1980 by saxophonist Gaz Birtles (ex-The Wendy Tunes) and saxophonist John Barrow (ex-collaborator of Black Gorilla). They released their debut single as a duo in 1981, then expanded to a quartet with the addition of synth/trumpet player Dean Sargent and keyboardist Mark O’Hara.

Their sound is built on interlocking saxophones with synth and rhythm-machine backings — rooted in Two-Tone-adjacent territory but leaning toward the smoother, more pop-oriented new wave of the early 1980s. They released six singles between 1981 and 1989 and were in-demand session horn players for other Leicester and UK acts in the same period. The band has continued sporadically into the 2020s, releasing a new album in 2024.

The WEA deal (1982–83) was their commercial peak, with “Rodeo” and “Lonely Boy” getting national distribution. “Lonely Boy” was released in multiple countries (UK, Japan, West Germany).

Scene Context

The Swinging Laurels sit at the intersection of Leicester post-punk and the broader Two-Tone / new-pop axis. Their horn-driven sound made them compatible with:

  • The Two-Tone world: session brass for The Apollinaires (produced by Jerry Dammers); contemporaries with The Specials, The Selecter, Madness
  • The Fun Boy Three orbit: horn session on Chrysalis-released single alongside Terry Hall, Neville Staple, Lynval Golding
  • Leicester scene: Kev Reverb connects to The S+T Label and the Where The Hell Is… Leicester compilation circuit; their “..A Taste Of” EP appeared on Dining Out Records (label ID 66567), a Dining Out Records sub-label also home to Leicester contemporaries

Gaz Birtles later joined The South (3) (the continuation of The Beautiful South) and Yellowbelly.

John Peel Connection

The 2017 archive release The John Peel Sessions 1982 confirms at least one BBC Radio 1 Peel session in 1982, placing them within the Peel-approved post-punk network of that year.
