Overview

Scars — originally billed as The Scars — were a post-punk band from Edinburgh, Scotland, active in the late 1970s and early 1980s as part of the city’s fertile punk and new wave scene. Their recorded output is concentrated in 1979–1981: a debut on Fast Product in 1979 followed by the bulk of their catalogue on PRE, with Charisma distributing work in their final year. The five-piece lineup of Paul Research, Calumn Mackay, John Mackie, Robert King, and Stephen McLaughlin drew on punk, new wave, and post-punk forms, and their releases have attracted archival reissue interest as recently as 2020–2021 via Cherry Red. Robert Blamire and Laurence Diana are the most prominent recurring names in their production credits.

Dissolution and post-Scars activities

The band dissolved around 1981–82. The proximate cause was vocalist Robert King (6) quitting; the remaining members — with Paul Research taking over lead vocal duties alongside bassist John Mackie (2) — attempted to continue but ultimately decided to disband rather than play at a reduced level. Paul Research described it as the band running “out of road, in terms of support; it’s difficult to get gigs at the right level.” The Louder Than War piece adds that the band “dissolv[ed] in acrimony and bad advice” following the major-label release of Author! Author!, suggesting label/management friction as a contributing factor. [source: https://louderthanwar.com/news-heavy-drapes-meet-scars-exclusive/] [source: https://vivelerock.net/blogs/interviews/scars-exclusive-bonus-interview]

Member activities after the split

  • Robert King (6) — relocated away from Scotland (latterly based in France, though he “returns to Edinburgh regularly”). Fronted two post-Scars projects: Opium Kitchen and Groucho Handjob. Also earned a doctorate in ancient languages. [source: https://thenewvinylvillain.com/2021/10/30/saturdays-scottish-song-282-scars/]
  • Paul Research — remained in Edinburgh; after the split spent time “regrouping and doing other things.” Later joined Edinburgh band Heavy Drapes as bassist (noted in a 2017 Louder Than War article). Also released solo albums including Skate The Royal Mile and The Silent Treatment (available via Bandcamp at paulresearch.bandcamp.com). [source: https://louderthanwar.com/news-heavy-drapes-meet-scars-exclusive/] [source: https://thenewvinylvillain.com/2021/10/30/saturdays-scottish-song-282-scars/]
  • John Mackie (2) — worked in retail design as an artist and formed his own band, Bitter Moon. [source: https://thenewvinylvillain.com/2021/10/30/saturdays-scottish-song-282-scars/]
  • Calumn Mackay — relocated to France for hi-tech work; played drums for a band called Garvin. [source: https://thenewvinylvillain.com/2021/10/30/saturdays-scottish-song-282-scars/]
  • Stephen McLaughlin — moved to London and became a film music producer. [source: https://thenewvinylvillain.com/2021/10/30/saturdays-scottish-song-282-scars/]

The band’s profile was revived when electronic duo Lemon Jelly sampled ‘Horrorshow’ for their 2005 UK #21 hit ‘The Shouty Track’. A one-off reunion gig took place in Edinburgh on 29 December 2010 — their first live appearance in roughly 25 years — with support from reformed TV21 and former Orange Juice/Josef K guitarist Malcolm Ross. A brief reformation was attempted subsequently but reportedly “old tensions quickly resurfaced.” [source: https://www.jockrock.org/news/old-scars-healed/] [source: https://louderthanwar.com/news-heavy-drapes-meet-scars-exclusive/]
