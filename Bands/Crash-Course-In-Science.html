Crash Course In Science

Philadelphia minimal-synth / post-punk band formed 1979. Discogs artist ID: 83061. Also known as CCIS.

Overview

Crash Course In Science formed in Philadelphia in 1979, when three art school students — Dale Feliciello (vocals), Mallory Yago (keyboards, electronics), and Michael Zodorozny (keyboards, electronics) — began experimenting with crude electronics, toy instruments, and kitchen appliances alongside distorted guitar and synthesized beats. Their self-description emphasizes the unconventional: they cite a deliberate avoidance of conventional instrumentation and an affinity with Throbbing Gristle, though they view themselves as working “in a parallel universe rather than being influenced by them.”

Their debut 7” single “Cakes In The Home” (GO GO Records, 1979, cat. GO GO 004) was followed in 1981 by the four-track 12” EP Signals From Pier Thirteen (Press Records), named after an abandoned coal-loading pier on the Delaware River near where the band rehearsed. The imagery of silent machinery, shapes, and industrial decay that the pier inspired became central to the record’s aesthetic. “Cardboard Lamb” and “Flying Turns” — both from the EP — became club favorites in early-1980s electronic circles and are widely credited with anticipating techno and industrial music developments that followed.

The band went dormant after their early-1980s activity and was largely unknown outside niche circles until a surge of reissue and remix interest in the mid-2000s brought them back. From 2005 onward, their music appeared on French and Dutch electronic labels (Planete Rouge, From Jupiter, Pressure Traxx), and Vinyl-on-demand issued an exhaustive retrospective box set in 2009. The band signed to Schematic for the 2011 digital album Near Marineland, then moved to Dark Entries for the 2016 Jump Over Barrels 12” EP and multiple reissues of Signals From Pier Thirteen. A new LP, Situational Awareness, appeared on Dutch label Electronic Emergencies in 2017.

Connections

  • Throbbing Gristle — named by CCIS as a spiritual parallel (not an influence), the closest aesthetic reference the band acknowledged in their Discogs profile.
  • Gen Ken Montgomery — East Village sound artist and electronics explorer; Michael Zodorozny’s collaborator in KMZ. Gen Ken also in God Is My Co-Pilot.
  • The Dead Milkmen — Philadelphia punk band produced by John Wicks (4), the same engineer/producer who worked on CCIS’s key early material.
  • Dark Entries — primary home for CCIS reissues and new material from 2013 onward; a key node in the contemporary archival underground electronic scene.
  • Vinyl-on-demand — issued the 2009 retrospective box set covering the band’s complete studio, demo, and live archive.
  • Schematic — Miami electronic label that signed CCIS for Near Marineland (2011).
  • Vitalic, David Carretta, Terence Fixmer, Danton Eeprom — European electronic producers/DJs who remixed CCIS material in the mid-to-late 2000s, indicating the band’s currency in European minimal/techno circles of that era.
