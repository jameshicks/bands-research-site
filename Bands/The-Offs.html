The Offs

Punk / ska band from San Francisco, CA; later relocated to New York. Active late 1970s–1984. Discogs ID: 255074.

Overview

The Offs were formed in San Francisco around 1978 by guitarist Billy Hawk and singer Don Vinil. Drummer Bob Steeler — who had previously played with Hot Tuna — joined early on, and the band rotated through a number of horn players and bassists over the following years. They were active in the earliest days of the San Francisco punk rock scene, sharing bills with contemporaries like the Dead Kennedys (the Kennedys played their first show with the Offs in 1978, per Wikipedia) and regularly performing at the Mabuhay Gardens in San Francisco.

Their debut single was a 7” cover of The Slickers’ “Johnny Too Bad” (Crack In The World, 1978), the B-side being the Billy Hawk original “624803.” Their follow-up, “Everyone’s A Bigot / 0°” (415 Records, 1980, cat. 008/009), was the first-ever release on 415 Records and was produced by Peter Kaukonen alongside the band. That track later appeared on the Alternative Tentacles punk/hardcore compilation Let Them Eat Jellybeans! (per Wikipedia).

Around 1980 Eric Peterson joined as bassist, and the band relocated to New York, where they became regulars at the Mudd Club, Danceteria, and Max’s Kansas City. In New York they moved in the same circles as artists Jean-Michel Basquiat and Keith Haring, musician/actor Richard Edson (who played trumpet with the band), and writer Glenn O’Brien (chronicler of the scene for Andy Warhol’s Interview magazine, per Wikipedia).

Their sole full-length album, First Record (CD Presents, Ltd., 1984), was produced by Roland Young and the band together. The cover art was by Jean-Michel Basquiat. Richard Edson played trumpet; Roland Young also contributes saxophone and sings lead on “Body Hesitation” (B1). The album was not commercially released until 1984, though the band had been building this material through the early 1980s.

Connections

  • 415 Records — “Everyone’s A Bigot / 0°” (1980) was the first-ever 415 release; the label went on to define the SF new wave scene through 1987
  • Richard Edson — trumpet on First Record (1984); also original drummer of Sonic Youth, member of Konk, actor in Stranger Than Paradise and Ferris Bueller’s Day Off
  • Jean-Michel Basquiat — cover art on First Record (1984); friend of the band from the NYC downtown scene
  • Roland Young — producer and saxophonist on First Record; also releases solo material on Flow Chart Records (from 1980) and EM Records (Japan); member of Infinite Sound
  • Hot Tuna — Bob Steeler (drums) was previously a Hot Tuna member
  • Dead Kennedys — played first-ever show with The Offs in 1978 (Wikipedia); contemporaries in the early San Francisco punk scene
  • Earl Zero — split 12” Zero Vs. The Offs (Epiphany Records, 1981)
  • Peter Kaukonen — co-produced “Everyone’s A Bigot” (1980); Discogs ID: 520017
