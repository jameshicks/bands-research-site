Ira Kaplan — Pre-Band Career and Scene Connections

Before co-founding Yo La Tengo in 1984, Ira Kaplan (born January 7, 1957) spent the late 1970s and early 1980s as a rock critic and journalist embedded in the New York downtown scene.

Publications. Kaplan wrote for the SoHo Weekly News starting around age 20 (c. 1977), covering emerging acts and live performances. He later contributed to New York Rocker, Village Voice, and Creem. His most sustained commitment was to New York Rocker (founded 1976 by Alan Betrock, edited from c. 1978 by Andy Schwartz), where he served as an editor — specifically expanding and editing the record reviews section. He described his time there as the closest he came to being proud of his rock-writing, crediting the paper’s quality to editor Andy Schwartz and colleagues Michael Hill and Glenn Morrow. The paper ran 54 issues and folded in November 1982. (furious.com/perfect/newyorkrocker.html, as of 2026-08-20; stereogum.com/2211423, as of 2026-08-20)

Music For Dozens. After New York Rocker closed, Kaplan and managing editor Michael Hill booked a series of concerts at Gerde’s Folk City, New York, under the name Music For Dozens. The series featured the first New York appearances by the Replacements and the Violent Femmes, among others. Hill subsequently moved into A&R at Warner Bros. (furious.com/perfect/newyorkrocker.html, as of 2026-08-20)

Soundman and roadie work. Alongside his journalism, Kaplan worked as a soundman, roadie, and occasional backup musician for Mofungo and other New York-area bands. He also worked as soundman at Maxwell’s, the Hoboken venue that became central to the indie scene. (encyclopedia.com, as of 2026-08-20)

Hoboken migration. The New York Rocker circle was pivotal to the Hoboken scene’s formation. Kaplan moved to Hoboken following the lead of Glenn Morrow (who had been managing editor at the magazine and co-founder of The Individuals), who was in turn drawn there by Steve Fallon, whose family had bought Maxwell’s. The dB’s and the Feelies were already Hoboken fixtures; Kaplan and Georgia Hubley joined the community and began making music together in Maxwell’s basement, forming Yo La Tengo in 1984. (psychedelicbabymag.com/2026/08/glenn-morrow.html, as of 2026-08-20; encyclopedia.com, as of 2026-08-20)

NRBQ blindfold test. One editorial highlight Kaplan cited: he pitched and conducted a reverse blindfold test with NRBQ for New York Rocker — the band selected songs for him to discuss rather than the traditional format — which he described as a “profound experience” that exposed him to song-poem recordings and other obscure music that shaped his tastes. (stereogum.com/2211423, as of 2026-08-20)

Connection to Dining Out Records

Via Georgia Hubley: Before co-founding Yo La Tengo in 1984, Hubley played drums in Magnetic Pull — an unrecorded all-female New York band co-led by Martha Tilson and Pam Meyer.

This makes Yo La Tengo one of the most distant nodes in the network — reachable from a 1979 London DIY label via an unrecorded New York band formed by an ACR/Dining Out alumna.

Matador Records Catalog

Signed to Matador Records from 1993; 339 total releases (their deepest label relationship). Key LPs: Painful (OLE 069, 1993), Electr-O-Pura (OLE 132, 1995), I Can Hear The Heart Beating As One (OLE 222, 1997), And Then Nothing Turned Itself Inside Out (2000), Summer Sun (2003), I Am Not Afraid Of You And I Will Beat Your Ass (2006), Popular Songs (2009), Fade (2013), There’s A Riot Going On (2018), This Stupid World (2023). Produced across the catalog by Roger Moutenot.
