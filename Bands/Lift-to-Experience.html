Lift to Experience

Discogs ID: 56005
Origin: Denton, Texas, USA
Active: c. 1997–2001
Genre: Post-rock, Space-rock, Shoegaze, Psychedelic Rock
Labels: Bella Union (2000–2001), Mute (2017 reissue)

Texan trio formed in the Denton/Dallas area. Best known for their sole double album The Texas-Jerusalem Crossroads (2001), a sprawling, apocalyptic work of spiritually charged guitar rock that has attained cult-classic status. The band dissolved shortly after its release; frontman Josh Pearson went on to a solo career.

Simon Raymonde and Robin Guthrie — both founding members of Cocteau Twins — mixed the album. Raymonde had co-founded Bella Union in 1997. Karen Raymonde (sleeve design) is likely related to Simon Raymonde (family/partner).

Scott Danbom’s fiddle contribution connects the album to the Denton scene: Danbom is the fiddle/keyboard player in Centro-Matic and South San Gabriel, both led by Will Johnson.

Dave Willingham (engineer, mastering) is a Texas recording figure, also a member of Spoonfed Tribe and Whitey (6).

2017 Reissue: The Texas-Jerusalem Crossroads (Mute, STUMM398)

Mute reissued the album as a 2×LP with a new mix. Key changes from the original:

  • New mix by Matt Pence (Centro-Matic drummer; Echo Lab, Denton TX)
  • Mastered by Dave McNair (replacing Dave Willingham)
  • Scott Danbom again credited for Fiddle — suggests new or re-recorded fiddle parts for the reissue sessions
  • Josh T. Pearson credited for Vocals, Guitar, Words By, Art Direction, Producer
  • Dave Willingham retained for Engineer and Recorded By credits
  • Additional engineering by Dan Williams (Daniel Williams (11))
  • The 12-track LP version omits the two untimed intros (“Texas”, “Jerusalem”) and the hidden/silence tracks, condensing to four sides

The reissue was accompanied by the Xfm X-Posure Session 17/07/2001 12” — an acoustic session from July 2001 released for the first time, featuring Pearson performing alone (no Browning or Young credited).

Notes (web)

Signing to Bella Union

Two sources — one from Simon Raymonde, one from Josh T. Pearson — document the signing.

Raymonde’s account: In an interview published 24 August 2017 in which he described nine pivotal moments in Bella Union’s history, Raymonde identified signing Lift to Experience as one of them. He described discovering them during “his annual pilgrimage to the SXSW festival in Austin, Texas” in 2000 and signing them on the strength of that performance. He also noted that before the band broke up, they tipped him off about another Texan band — Midlake — leading to Bella Union’s next significant signing. [source: https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/interviews/nine-pivotal-moments-of-20-years-of-bella-union, published 24 Aug 2017]

Josh T. Pearson’s account: In a March 2017 interview archived at Drowned in Sound, Pearson described the signing circumstances as financially constrained. According to Pearson, Bella Union agreed to release the album but could not “pay for the studio time already for the recording of the record” nor fund the mixing. The band was also facing eviction and had access to their studio for only “another week and a half.” In that narrow window, the album was recorded and mixed — with Raymonde himself handling the mix, something Pearson later reflected was not ideal given Raymonde “hadn’t done [mixing] every day for 20 years.” This account is consistent with Raymonde’s own mixing credit on the album. [source: https://www.drownedinsound.org/archive-this-is-for-the-love-of-the-art-dis-meets-lift-to-experience-4150829/, published 2 Mar 2017]

Summary: Raymonde saw the band live at SXSW 2000 and signed them; the label’s financial situation at the time meant it could not fund recording or professional mixing, so Raymonde mixed the album himself under time pressure. The band tipped Raymonde toward Midlake before dissolving.
