The Six Parts Seven

Instrumental post-rock / ambient band formed in Kent, Ohio, 1995. Discogs artist ID: 474055. Also listed under name variation “Six Parts Seven.”

Overview

The Six Parts Seven were founded by brothers Allen Karpinski (guitar) and Jay Karpinski (drums) in Kent, OH. Their sound drew from the ambient minimalism of Brian Eno and the American primitive / folk-guitar tradition of John Fahey, resulting in slow-building, wordless compositions that positioned them within the early-2000s post-rock surge without sounding like any of its noisier exponents. The band released their most important work on Suicide Squeeze between 2002 and 2006 before going quiet, then reactivated for a 2024 split album with Goodmorning Valentine.

Biography

Origins and Early Recordings (1995–1999)

Allen and Jay Karpinski founded the band in Kent, Ohio in 1995. The earliest documented release is an untitled self-released cassette on Donut Friends (cat# 38), featuring Allen and Jay alongside Damion Lazzara and Mary Mazzer, and re-recorded by Jamie Stillman. A second Donut Friends release, …In Lines And Patterns (1999), followed.

In 1998 the band recorded Three Reworkings of “From California to Houston, On Light Speed” — a three-version exploration of a single piece, released as a self-pressed, single-sided cassette. Tim Gerak mixed it. This document of their early process circulated on a self-released / not-on-label basis.

Troubleman Unlimited and Expansion (2000–2001)

Silence Magnifies Sound (2000) appeared on Troubleman Unlimited, expanding the lineup to include Tim Gerak (guitars, samples), and Brad Visker (bass). Discogs credits for this pressing are sparse — no additional credits listed — but it marks their first proper album release.

Suicide Squeeze Era — Peak Work (2002–2004)

By 2002 the band had grown into a sextet. Things Shaped In Passing (March 2002, Suicide Squeeze) introduced James Matthew Haas on lap steel / high-lonesome guitar and Steven Clements on grand piano, alongside the core Karpinski brothers, Gerak, and Visker. Engineer and occasional performer Chris Keffer joined the inner circle and would remain closely involved through the mid-period. Allen Karpinski handled arrangements.

The same year saw the band contribute production assistance to Crosby, Stills & Nash… — actually, to Philadelphia Songs (Burnt Toast Vinyl, 2002, credited role: Assistant Producer / Arranged By), a different project.

In late 2003 two further records appeared in quick succession:

  • Lost Notes From Forgotten Songs (Suicide Squeeze, 2003) — a denser record that kept the sextet intact, with Steve Clements (here credited on piano) and Chris Keffer taking on full engineer/producer roles alongside the band.
  • The Attitudes Of Collapse (Burnt Toast Vinyl, 2003) — released on the same year via a different imprint, featuring the same core lineup with Brad Visker still on bass.

Also in 2003: a split 7” with The Black Keys (Suicide Squeeze), one of the more unexpected pairings of the era — the Black Keys were at that point a scruffy Ohio blues-rock duo, sharing geography if not aesthetics with Six Parts Seven.

Everywhere And Right Here (Suicide Squeeze, 2004) marked a personnel pivot. Brad Visker and Steven Clements stepped back; Mike Tolan came in on bass, Rhodes, and electric guitar; Brian Straw contributed acoustic guitar; Ben Vaughan added lap steel and electric guitar; Eric Koltnow played vibraphone and percussion. Engineer/producer Chris Keffer continued. Roger Seibel mastered. Jesse LeDoux did the artwork (he also did the 2003 split artwork). Steve Clements returned on grand piano and keyboards. Allen Karpinski added a Juno synthesizer to his palette. This lineup produced a somewhat more textured, layered sound.

Casually Smashed To Pieces and Hiatus (2006–2023)

The final album of the active period, Casually Smashed To Pieces (Suicide Squeeze, 2006) — released under the name variation “Six Parts Seven” rather than “The Six Parts Seven” — was produced by Matt Bayles (known for his work with Isis, Mastodon, Pelican). The lineup was the largest yet: Tim Gerak and James Matthew Haas on electric/acoustic guitars, Allen Karpinski on guitar / bass mid-range / Juno / samples, Jay Karpinski on drums, Mike Tolan on bass / banjo / electric guitar, Steve Clements on Rhodes / toy piano (including whistling), Brian Straw on lap steel, Jacob Trombetta on lap steel and additional recording, Jen Court on clarinet, and Keith Freund on pocket trumpet. Ed Brooks mastered. Brett Holzworth did artwork and design.

After this release the band went largely quiet for the better part of two decades.

Return — Kissing Distance (2024)

In 2024 the band resurfaced with Kissing Distance (Suicide Squeeze), a split / collaborative album billed jointly with Goodmorning Valentine. The lineup on this release: Allen Karpinski (electric guitar, bass, slide guitar), Jay Karpinski (drums), Tim Gerak (electric guitar), Mike Tolan (bass, electric guitar), Matt Haas (electric guitar), Elizabeth Williams (piano, cello, bass guitar), and Joseph Allen Beltram (vocals, acoustic guitar, synth) — all of whom represent Goodmorning Valentine’s side as well as the band’s core. This marks the first Six Parts Seven release in approximately 18 years.

Crucially, the Kissing Distance recordings date from 2006, not 2024. The album is an archival release, not new studio work. See the Reunion section below for the full context.

Reunion (2024) — Kissing Distance Context

What Kissing Distance Actually Is

The 2024 release is not a reunion album of new material — it is an archival release of recordings made in late 2006 / early 2007, between the completion of Casually Smashed to Pieces and its January 2007 street date. The band had returned from touring Washington State for the Casually Smashed sessions. During the resulting downtime, most members were keeping active through other projects: Mike Tolan with his solo project Talons’, and Allen Karpinski with Beaten Awake (a Kent, OH band). The core Six Parts Seven members were also rehearsing and playing local shows.

The 2006 Sessions

The sessions took place over two weekend days at a house called the Saint Ledger House in the Akron/Kent area. All songs were cut without prior rehearsal. Joey Beltram (Goodmorning Valentine) had written “Meditation in D” — a song Allen Karpinski described as “the decided spark that set the fire blazing” — and it became the impetus for the collaboration. The attendees included Six Parts Seven members alongside Goodmorning Valentine players. As the liner notes describe: “There were handles of whiskey, there was weed, stacks of Marlboro Reds for the ones still dragging butts.” The bands also went dancing in Akron on the Saturday night between sessions.

Minimal recording technique was central: several tracks used a single SM58 for both vocal and guitar — “making it impossible to over-think anything.” The band celebrated the sessions with one live performance at The Beachland Tavern in Cleveland.

Why It Took 18 Years

Mixing took nearly two years after the initial sessions, with Joey Beltram completing the work slowly alongside his Goodmorning Valentine touring and writing. After the initial mixing period ended (c. 2008–2009), the tapes appear to have sat unreleased until Suicide Squeeze brought it out in 2024.

”Instrumental #2” — The Last Six Parts Seven Recording

The album’s most significant track from the band’s perspective: “Instrumental #2” is identified in the liner notes as “the last full/core band recording by Six Parts Seven” — and, notably, the first piece in their catalog written and arranged by Tim Gerak rather than Allen Karpinski. The liner notes are explicit about the loss this represents: “This song would have been developed on our follow-up to Casually Smashed to Pieces. Alas, an album never came to be. The ache in this is real.” The track was recorded in three takes without rehearsal, using Allen Karpinski’s bass tuned high and open, played with a slide, and a pre-EarthQuaker Devices fuzz pedal belonging to Suicide Squeeze founder Jamie Stillman.

Hiatus / Dissolution (c. 2007–2023)

No formal announcement of dissolution or hiatus has been located. The band appears to have simply wound down after 2006–2007 as members moved into other projects. Wikipedia notes a 2008 disbanding but cites no source. The liner notes’ phrasing — “an album never came to be” — suggests the follow-up to Casually Smashed was planned but collapsed; no statement from the band on why has surfaced in available sources.

Known member activity during the hiatus:

  • Mike Tolan — prolific output under Talons’, releasing experimental post-folk music. His 2006 Rustic Bullshit (recorded at his home on South Portage Path, Akron) was a notable early release. Tolan toured extensively and released multiple albums. Jacob Trombetta (lap steel, also on Casually Smashed) is a regular Talons’ collaborator; Sommer Tolan (credited on Kissing Distance) appears on Talons’ recordings as well.
  • Allen Karpinski — involved with Beaten Awake, a Kent/Akron band. (Beaten Awake is documented on Fat Possum Records; personnel details beyond Allen’s involvement require further research.)
  • Allen and Jay Karpinski — also involved in Noah (2) and Old Hearts Club (dates of activity unknown).
  • Tim Gerak — no independent activity documented beyond Six Parts Seven.

The 2024 Release

Suicide Squeeze released Kissing Distance on February 16, 2024 (cat# SSQ218). Limited first pressing: 500 copies on 180g black vinyl, case-wrapped jacket, printed insert and black poly-lined innerbag. Mixed by Matt Bayles (same producer as Casually Smashed); mastered by Ed Brooks (same engineer). The album is a full collaborative work credited jointly to both bands, not a split in the traditional sense — all tracks are performed by a combined lineup from both bands. Additional players on the release include Jake Trombetta (lap steel) and Sommer Tolan (vocals).

Track listing: Instrumental 1 / Meditation in D / Drunk From The Bottle / Red Lights / Instrumental 2 / Winter’s Early Moon (Part 1) / Standing In Her Raincoat / 12 Hours / Lonely Daughter / Winter’s Early Moon (Part 2).

  • No formal band statement on why the Casually Smashed follow-up collapsed or why the band dissolved c. 2007–2008. The liner notes acknowledge it (“Alas, an album never came to be”) but give no explanation.
  • Allen Karpinski’s involvement with Beaten Awake is confirmed by the Kissing Distance liner notes (“Al w/ Beaten Awake”) but the extent of his role (member, collaborator?) and dates of involvement need further research.
  • “Damion Lazzara” and “Mary Mazzer” (early cassette) — no further Discogs profile information; origins unknown
  • Steven Clements (ID 670529) and Steve Clements (ID 590717) are confirmed the same person — Discogs data duplication. “Steven” was used on the 2002 and 2003 Burnt Toast Vinyl credits; “Steve” on all Suicide Squeeze credits from 2003 onward.
