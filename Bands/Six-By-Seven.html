Six By Seven

English indie/post-rock band formed in Nottingham. Discogs artist ID: 71002.

Biography

Origins (1992–1996)

Six By Seven trace their roots to late 1992, when the founding nucleus began playing together under the name Friends Of…, with their first gig at the Old Angel in Nottingham. The band officially formed under the Six By Seven name in 1996, when the classic line-up coalesced: Chris Olley (vocals, guitar), James Flower (keyboards, Hammond organ, tenor saxophone), Chris Davis (4) (drums), Paul Douglas (5) (bass), Martin Cooper (6) (guitar), and Sam Hempton (guitar).

Breakthrough: Mantra Recordings Era (1997–2001)

In 1997 the band released their debut 12” single “European Me” on MFS (2), produced by the band and Trevor Curwen, who also recorded it. The single attracted critical attention and set the template for their sprawling, guitar-dense sound — often described as post-rock with a strong shoegaze and noise-rock edge.

Their debut album The Things We Make (1998, Mantra Recordings / MFS (2)) was recorded across multiple sessions by different engineers — Paul Corkett handled tracks X3, X4, Y3; Ric Peet tracked X1; and Trevor Curwen covered the remainder. The band and Corkett received a co-production credit. The album was released in the US on Beggars Banquet. Martin Cooper left the band after this record, and Sam Hempton departed in 2000.

The second album The Closer You Get (2000, Mantra Recordings / Beggars Banquet) brought in John Leckie as outside producer on four tracks (2, 7, 8, 11), with Ric Peet co-producing and engineering the remaining eight alongside the band. Sam Toolan assisted with engineering and programming. Photographer Steve Gullick shot the band portraits for both this album and The Things We Make.

Eat Junk Become Junk (2000, Mantra Recordings) was also co-produced by Ric Peet. The same year the band released the single “New Year” on MFS (2) / Mantra.

A double album, These Compulsory Meatless Days (2001, Mantra Recordings), followed. Mastering was handled by Chris Blair. Also in 2001, Mantra released the singles “I Owe You Love” and “So Close”.

Paul Douglas departed after All My New Best Friends (2002, Mantra Recordings), the last studio album on that label.

Saturday Night Sunday Morning Records Era (2003–2008)

After leaving Mantra, the band moved to the self-run imprint Saturday Night Sunday Morning Records (Discogs label ID: 53262). The album 04 (2004) was followed by singles and the release of archival material: Demo’s 1997–1999 (2004) and Left Luggage At The Peveril Hotel (2004), a live/compilation set.

Artists Cannibals Poets Thieves (2005) stripped the line-up to a trio of Olley, Flower, and Davis, who self-produced and engineered. It was the last album with Davis before he departed in 2005. The Peel Sessions album (2008) collected their BBC Radio 1 recordings.

If Symptoms Persist, Kill Your Doctor (2007) brought Davis back for a final stint alongside Pete Stevenson on bass and Sam Hempton returning on guitar. Tony Doggen Foster and Ady Fletcher both briefly held guitar and bass roles respectively in 2006, alongside Ian Bissett on drums.

Chris Olley left in November 2008, effectively ending the classic band. Live recordings from this period — Live At Glastonbury (2008), Live And Alternative (2008), Strangled On The Vine (2008) — were issued on Saturday Night Sunday Morning Records and as self-releases.

Post-split: Olley’s Solo/Bootleg Phase (2009–2012)

Following the split, Olley began releasing material under the Six By Seven name via the band’s own site. The other members did not sanction these releases, classifying them as bootlegs. Releases from this period include Just Because I’m Paranoid (2009), Live At The Peveril Hotel (2009), and 17.10.08 - Live At Junktion 7 Nottingham (2010), as well as The Way I Feel Today (2011) and The Things We Make Unreleased Session Songs And Friends Of Demos (2011), both on Olley’s own self-released imprint.

In 2011 Olley formed (The Death Of) Six. By Seven, which played two semi-acoustic gigs (no drummer) and released one album.

Reformed Line-Up (2013–present)

Six By Seven officially reformed in 2013 with a new line-up: Olley, Flower, Martin Cooper returning on guitar, Pete Stevenson on bass, and Steve Hewitt on drums. Love And Peace And Sympathy (2013, Borrowed Tunes Motion Pictures) was recorded by Dan Austin.

From 2013 onwards the band became extremely prolific on Saturday Night Sunday Morning Records and various self-released imprints, issuing a long series of studio recordings, EPs, demo collections, live albums, and digital-only releases through the 2010s, 2020s, and into the mid-2020s. A 2016 Greatest Hits compilation appeared on Mantra Recordings. The later catalogue includes EX / Ex - Anglo German Post Brexit Kosmik Soundtraks (2017), the Kosmik Punks series (2021–2022), and multiple ongoing Studio Recordings volumes (2023–2024).
