The Rezillos

Edinburgh punk band, formed March 1976, split December 1978. Discogs artist ID: 5328753. Often cited as Scotland’s first punk band, distinguished from their contemporaries by a campy, cartoonish B-movie and sci-fi aesthetic that set them apart from the grimmer strains of UK punk. Reunited sporadically from the early 2000s onward.

Formation and Early Lineup

The Rezillos were founded in Edinburgh in March 1976 by Alan Forbes (who adopted the stage name Eugene Reynolds), initially playing drums and vocals, alongside Dave Smythe (later Dr. D. K. Smythe) on bass, Jo Callis (later Luke Warm) on lead guitar and vocals, and Mark Harris (later Hi-Fi Harris) on rhythm guitar and vocals. The group rehearsed for months before settling on their image, aliases, and expanded lineup.

By August 1976 they had grown to an eight-piece, adding Fay Fife (real name Sheila Wilma Hynd) and Gail Warning (Gail Jamieson) on backing vocals, Angel Paterson (Alistair Paterson) on drums (with Reynolds moving to front-of-house vocals), and William Mysterious (Alastair Donaldson) on saxophone. Their first live performance was on 5 November 1976 at Teviot Row Student Union, Edinburgh.

The Edinburgh College of Art connection is well-established in the band’s origin story — Fife and Reynolds were both associated with the college, and the art-school sensibility informed their visual approach throughout.

Sound and Aesthetic

The Rezillos played fast, energetic pop-punk with roots in early rock and roll and girl-group pop as much as in the contemporary punk explosion. Jo Callis was the primary songwriter for original material. Their B-movie sci-fi imagery — flying saucers, Thunderbirds, campy horror — gave them a distinct visual identity that contrasted with both the working-class realism and nihilism of many UK punk contemporaries. Covers in their live set included Fleetwood Mac’s “Somebody’s Gonna Get Their Head Kicked In Tonight” (a Jeremy Spencer composition), the Dave Clark Five’s “Glad All Over,” and Rossini’s William Tell Overture (rendered as “20,000 Rezillos Under the Sea”).

Sensible Records and Early Singles (1977)

Their debut single, “I Can’t Stand My Baby” / “I Wanna Be Your Man” (Sensible Records (2), cat. Fab 1, 1977), was self-released on a label they controlled. The A-side was written by Jo Callis (credited as L. Warm); the B-side was a cover of Lennon/McCartney. Personnel on the single: Fay Fife and Eugene Reynolds (vocals), Jo Callis / Luke Warm and Hi-Fi Harris (guitars), Dr. D. K. Smythe (bass), Angel Paterson (drums). Produced jointly by The Rezillos and Tony Pilley.

A second single, “(My Baby Does) Good Sculptures” / “Flying Saucer Attack!” (Sire, cat. 6078 612, 1977), followed after the band attracted major-label interest. Both sides written by Jo Callis (as Luke Warm).

Sire Records and Can’t Stand The Rezillos (1978)

Signed to Sire (label ID: 27031, a Warner Music Group subsidiary), the band recorded their sole studio LP, Can’t Stand The Rezillos (Sire K 56530 / SRK 6057, 1978). Personnel: Fay Fife and Eugene Reynolds (vocals), Jo Callis (electric guitar), William Mysterious (bass guitar), Angel Paterson (drums). Produced by The Rezillos, Tony Bongiovi, and Bob Clearmountain (who also served as engineer). The tracklist was dominated by Jo Callis originals — “Flying Saucer Attack,” “No,” “Top Of The Pops,” “2000 A.D.” (co-written with William Mysterious), “I Can’t Stand My Baby,” “(My Baby Does) Good Sculptures,” “Cold Wars,” “Bad Guy Reaction,” “Getting Me Down” — alongside covers including “Somebody’s Gonna Get Their Head Kicked In Tonight” (Jeremy Spencer), “Glad All Over” (Dave Clark / Mike Smith), and “I Like It” (Scott/Carter/Thomas).

Three singles were drawn from the Sire period:

  • “Top Of The Pops” (Sire SIR 4001, 1978) — A-side produced and engineered by Ian Taylor; B-side “20,000 Rezillos Under the Sea” produced by Tony Bongiovi, Bob Clearmountain, and Lance Quinn. Personnel: Fay Fife and Eugene Reynolds (vocals), Jo Callis (guitar), William Mysterious and Simon Templar (bass), Angel Paterson (drums).
  • “Destination Venus” / “Mystery Action” (Sire SIR 4008, 1978) — produced by Martin Rushent, engineered by Richard Manwaring. Both sides written by Jo Callis.
  • “Cold Wars” (Sire SIR 4014, 1979) — produced by The Rezillos and Tim Pearce, engineered by Tim Summerhayes. Written by Jo Callis.

Split and Mission Accomplished Live LP (1978–1979)

The Rezillos played their final concert at Glasgow Apollo on 31 December 1978. A live album, Mission Accomplished… But The Beat Goes On (Sire SRK 6069, 1979), documented the farewell show. Personnel from the live set: Fay Fife (female vocals), Eugene Reynolds (male vocals), Jo Callis (guitar, vocals), William Mysterious (saxophone), Simon Templar (bass), Angel Paterson (drums), Gail Warning (backing vocals), with the Glasgow Apollo Audience credited for backing vocals. Produced by Tim Pearce; sound engineering by Tim Summerhayes. Art direction by the band; artwork by Iain McIntosh. Callis is credited as writer on nearly every original track on the live set.

Reunion and Later Output

The band reformed in the early 2000s and has released several records since, operating as a going concern with a current lineup of Reynolds, Fife, and Angel Paterson alongside later members. Post-reunion releases include:

  • Out Of This World (Rezillos Records, 2011)
  • Live (Rezillos Records / Threads Of Sound, 2012)
  • Zero (Metropolis, 2015)
  • Flying Saucer Attack (The Complete Recordings 1977–1979) (Cherry Red, 2018) — compilation of original-era recordings

The Revillos

After the 1978 split, Eugene Reynolds and Fay Fife immediately formed The Revillos, carrying the B-movie aesthetic into a new project. Hi-Fi Harris and Dr. D. K. Smythe also moved to The Revillos. Jo Callis did not follow — he went on to join The Human League, co-writing their breakthrough hits in the early 1980s.
