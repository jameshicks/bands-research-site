Overview

ISAN is a UK ambient electronics duo formed in 1996 by Antony Ryan and Robin Saville. Working with analogue synthesisers and sequencers, they developed a sound characterised by warmth, melodic intricacy, and a wry playfulness within the ambient / IDM tradition. They were among the first five artists signed to Morr Music on its founding in 1999, with Salamander as their debut on the label (catalog number MM003). Over the following two decades they remained among Morr’s most consistent contributors, with the largest proportion of their catalog on that label.

Formation and Early Releases (1996–1999)

The duo’s earliest documented release is Damil 85 (Wurlitzer Jukebox, 1997). The following year they placed material on Bad Jazz (Betty’s Lament, Titled, Not Tithed / Island Magic), Tugboat Records (Beautronics), and Earworm, and appeared on a split for Atomic Recordings. They also contributed to a split for Audio Dregs (2002).

Their sound in this period established the template: analogue electronics composed, performed, and produced entirely by Ryan and Saville, typically without guest musicians. The Elefant Records (Spain) release Dampen (1999) and Liquefaction Empire release Digitalis (1999) extended their reach to continental and US markets.

Morr Music Founding Era (1999–2004)

Salamander (MM003, 1999) placed ISAN as the third entry in the Morr catalog, following B. Fleischmann’s Poploops For Breakfast (MM001) and Lali Puna’s Tridecoder (MM002). The record is notable for a guest contribution from Charles Atlas — a shifting collective that included members of Piano Magic — on track B5, making it one of the few ISAN releases with an outside collaborator.

Three albums followed in close succession on Morr:

  • Lucky Cat (MM021, 2001) — Ryan and Saville as composers/performers/producers; cover by Jan Kruse (2)
  • Salle D’ISAN (MM022, 2001) — live document; same credits; Rashad Becker cut lacquers
  • Clockwork Menagerie (MM032, 2002) — cover by Jan Kruse (2)

The duo also appeared on the Morr compilation Putting The Morr Back In Morrissey (MM012, 2000) and the major label showcase Blue Skied An’ Clear (MM030, 2002).

Static Caravan released two non-Morr records in this era: Parochi / A Gentle Man (1999) and Exquisite Honeyed Tart (2001), on which Ryan is also credited with uncredited artwork.

Meet Next Life (MM046, 2004) continued the run; the lacquer was again cut by Rashad Becker, and cover design was by Jan Kruse (2).

Mid-Period (2005–2010)

Plans Drawn In Pencil (MM065, 2006) was released with cover design by Human Empire. In the same year ISAN released Trois Gymnopedies on A Number Of Small Things (the Morr sublabel used for 7” singles) — reinterpretations of Erik Satie’s Gymnopédies, credited as “Reinterpreted By ISAN.”

Glow In The Dark Safari Set (MM096, 2010) returned to the main Morr catalog. Artwork on both this release and Glass Bird Movement (2016) was by Julia Guther, the Morr house designer. An album, Eastside (Kesh Recordings, 2011), ventured outside Morr.

A collaboration credit from outside their own discography: ISAN provided a remix for the Maps & Diagrams release Delius Remixed (False Industries, 2013), alongside remixes from Pimmon, Ran Slavin, and Yair Etziony.
