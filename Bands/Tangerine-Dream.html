Tangerine Dream

Oct 22, 202611 min read

Tangerine Dream

German electronic group formed in West Berlin in 1967 by Edgar Froese. Premier exponent of the Berlin School of electronic music; one of the most influential synthesizer-based acts in rock history. Discogs artist ID: 10343.

Biography

Formation and Early Experimental Period (1967–1973, Ohr label)

Tangerine Dream coalesced from the fertile experimental scene around the Zodiak Free Arts Lab in West Berlin, where avant-garde improvisers crossed paths with American psychedelia and Fluxus-adjacent noise. Edgar Froese (b. 1944, Tilsit) founded the group, and the earliest lineup was fluid and collective.

Their debut album, Electronic Meditation (Ohr, 1970), featured a trio of Froese, Conrad Schnitzler (cello, violin, guitar, Addiator noise-making), and Klaus Schulze (drums, percussion — credited on the original pressing as “Claus Schultze”). Two uncredited guests appear: flautist Thomas Keyserling and organist Jimmy Jackson (2). Froese handled guitar, organ, piano, and glass-shard noises; production credit went to Hans Ulrich Weigel and Klaus Freudigmann. The result was chaotic free-rock with industrial textures, closer to musique concrète than anything resembling the synthesizer work to come. Both Schnitzler and Schulze departed immediately after the record — Schulze going on to co-found Ash Ra Tempel and become the other pillar of the Berlin School; Schnitzler eventually forming Cluster.

Alpha Centauri (Ohr, 1971) introduced Christopher Franke (percussion, flute, zither, synthesizer) replacing Schulze, plus Steve Schroyder (organ, electronics) and Roland Paulick (synthesizer). Recording was handled by Dieter Dierks. The sound opened up into long droning passages with acoustic instrument coloration. Monique Froese — Edgar’s wife — co-designed the sleeve and would remain a recurring creative and visual collaborator for decades.

Zeit (Ohr, 1972) was the pivotal pivot. Now a trio of Froese, Franke, and newly added Peter Baumann (VCS 3 synthesizer, organ, vibraphone), the album dispensed with conventional rhythm and structure entirely, presenting four side-length movements of slow synthesizer drone. A cello quartet — Christian Vallbracht, Hans Joachim Brüne, Joachim von Grumbkow, Johannes Lücke — added bowed texture. Rolf-Ulrich Kaiser supervised. Dieter Dierks engineered. Zeit is generally marked as the first full statement of what would become the Berlin School aesthetic.

Atem (Polydor, 1973) was the last of the Ohr-era records and was voted album of the year by John Peel at BBC Radio 1, creating the British attention that led directly to the Virgin signing. Steve Schroyder returned briefly, though the core trio of Froese/Franke/Baumann was now the settled lineup.

Virgin Records Classic Period (1974–1983)

The core Froese–Franke–Baumann trio signed to Virgin Records and recorded Phaedra (1974) at Virgin’s Manor Studio. Phil Becque engineered. Instrumentation: Froese on mellotron, guitar, bass, VCS 3, organ; Baumann on organ, electric piano, VCS 3, flute; Franke on Moog, VCS 3, keyboards. The title track’s sequencer pulse — one of the earliest prominent uses of a Moog sequencer in a rock context — became an archetype for the Berlin School and influenced the entire lineage of electronic and ambient music. The sleeve painting was by Froese himself. Phaedra went to #15 in the UK charts, an unprecedented achievement for purely instrumental electronic music.

Rubycon (Virgin, 1975): same trio, with Mick Glossop engineering. Baumann on Synthi A, electric piano, organ; Franke on double Moog and Synthi A; Froese on guitar, mellotron, VCS 3. Two continuous 17-minute sides. Widely considered the peak of the classic period.

Ricochet (Virgin, 1975) was a live album recorded in France and the UK; Stratosfear (Virgin, 1976) was the final Froese/Franke/Baumann studio set, with Ottmar Bergler recording and the band self-producing and mixing.

Michael Hoenig temporarily replaced Baumann for an Australian tour in 1975 but did not appear on studio records. Baumann departed fully in 1978 to pursue a solo career (later founding the Private Music label).

Encore (Virgin, 1977) documented the trio live. The Sorcerer soundtrack (MCA, 1977) — scored for William Friedkin’s film — was recorded during Baumann’s last period with the band; the same Froese/Franke/Baumann lineup deployed Oberheim Polyphonic, Arp Omni String, PPG, Moog, Fender Rhodes, mellotrons, and an array of Project Electronik sequencers.

Cyclone (Virgin, 1978) marked the most radical post-Baumann experiment: Steve Jolliffe joined on vocals, woodwinds (bass flute, cor anglais, bass clarinet, lyricon), and keyboards, while Klaus Krüger played drums and electronic drums. This was the only TD record to feature conventional rock drumming and sung lyrics. Critical reception was mixed.

Force Majeure (Virgin, 1979): Froese and Franke as a duo, with Klaus Krüger on drums and Eduard Meyer on cello and engineering. Self-produced. The last Virgin record before the addition of Johannes Schmölling.

Tangram (Virgin, 1980) introduced Schmölling (keyboards) as a full member, forming the trio that would record through the mid-1980s. Exit (Virgin, 1981), White Eagle (Virgin, 1982), Logos Live (Virgin, 1982), Hyperborea (Virgin, 1983).

Film Scoring Era (1977–1987)

Alongside their studio catalog, Tangerine Dream became the foremost European practitioners of synthesizer-based film scoring:

  • Sorcerer (MCA, 1977) — William Friedkin’s remake of Wages of Fear; Froese/Franke/Baumann
  • Thief (Virgin, 1981) — Michael Mann’s debut feature; Froese/Franke/Schmölling. Schmölling credited on keyboards and electronics.
  • Wavelength (Varèse Sarabande, 1983)
  • Firestarter (MCA, 1984) — Stephen King adaptation; credited to the group collectively as composer/performer/producer
  • Flashpoint (EMI America, 1984)
  • Legend (MCA, 1986) — Ridley Scott film; US release used the TD score, though Goldie Hawn–era UK release used a different score
  • Near Dark (Silva Screen, 1987) — Kathryn Bigelow’s vampire film
  • Shy People (Varèse Sarabande, 1987)
  • Three O’Clock High (Varèse Sarabande, 1987)

The Keep (1983), scored for Michael Mann, received a limited official release only on a 1993 bootleg label, making it a cult rarity. Miracle Mile (Private Music, 1989) and Destination Berlin (Hansa, 1989) rounded out the decade.

Mid-Period: Label Moves and Lineup Changes (1984–1994)

Paul Haslinger replaced Schmölling in 1985 (Schmölling had left over creative differences). The Haslinger/Froese/Franke lineup appeared on Le Parc (Relativity, 1985), Underwater Sunlight (Relativity, 1986), and Tyger (Jive Electro, 1987, featuring vocals by Jocelyn Bernadette Smith reading William Blake texts).

Christopher Franke departed in 1987, also citing creative differences. He relocated to Los Angeles and built a career composing for television (Babylon 5). Haslinger remained with Froese for Optical Race (Private Music, 1988) — the first Private Music TD release, with Franke absent. Haslinger left in 1990 and also relocated to Hollywood, composing for film/TV.

Jerome Froese (Edgar’s son) replaced Haslinger and became a full member from 1990. This father-son duo recorded for TDI Music (Edgar Froese’s own label, founded mid-1990s) through Rockoon (Virgin/Miramar, 1992), Turn Of The Tides (Virgin/Miramar, 1994), Tyranny Of Beauty (Miramar, 1995), Goblins Club (When! Recordings, 1995), and then the TDI catalog from 1996 onward. The TDI label was later renamed Eastgate.

A brief period on Jive Electro (1984–1988) produced Poland (The Warsaw Concert) (1984) and Tyger before the move to Private Music.

Post-Froese Era (2015–present)

Edgar Froese died in Vienna on 20 January 2015 from a pulmonary embolism. He had left substantial unreleased material and had reportedly prepared for the band’s continuation.

The band continues under the musical direction of Thorsten Quaeschning, who had been a TD member since the 2000s. The post-2015 lineup for Quantum Gate (Eastgate/Kscope, 2017) — the first post-Froese studio record — included Quaeschning and Ulrich Schnauss, with compositions credited to both Quaeschning and the late Edgar Froese (from pre-existing materials). Quaeschning directed musically and handled mixing alongside Schnauss; mastering by Birgir Jón Birgisson (Bibbi).

Raum (Eastgate/Kscope, 2022) featured Quaeschning (synthesizer, sequencer, piano, music director, mix), Paul Frick (synthesizer, sequencer, piano, mix), and Hoshiko Yamane (violin, electric viola). Edgar Froese’s synthesizer/sequencer parts from archive material appear on several tracks. Cover artwork by Bianca Froese-Acquaye (Edgar’s second wife/manager).

The group continues to tour and record as of 2025, with new live sessions appearing on Eastgate.

Scene Context

Tangerine Dream are central to three overlapping currents in the history of electronic music:

Krautrock (1970–1973): The debut album and Ohr-era work belong to the same Düsseldorf/Berlin experimental milieu as Cluster, Ash Ra Tempel, and Neu!. Free improvisation, prepared instruments, tape manipulation. Froese had personal connections to Salvador Dalí and the Fluxus circle through the Zodiak Free Arts Lab.

Berlin School (1972–1983): TD effectively defined this subgenre alongside Klaus Schulze (solo) and Cluster. Characteristics: long-form sequencer-driven pieces, layered analog synthesizers, minimal percussion, gradual evolution rather than development. Zeit is the founding document; Phaedra the canonical peak. Brian Eno cited TD as an influence on his ambient records.

Electronic / New Age crossover (1983–1995): The shift toward digital synthesis, uplifting melody, and film scoring placed TD at the junction of the emerging New Age market and mainstream soundtrack work. Their label moves (Relativity, Private Music, Miramar) track this repositioning. Peter Baumann’s Private Music label itself became a major New Age imprint.

Post-Berlin synthesis (2015–present): The Quaeschning-led continuation reconnects with the classic sequencer aesthetic while incorporating contemporary production. The Kscope partnership gives the catalog a profile in progressive/ambient circles.
