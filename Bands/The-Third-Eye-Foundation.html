Overview

The Third Eye Foundation is an electronic/illbient project from Bristol, UK, launched in 1994 by Matt Elliott as a solo vehicle. Between 1996 and 1997 it briefly expanded into a duo when Debbie Parsons (also known as Foehn) contributed vocals, then contracted back to Elliott alone. The catalogue is densest in the 1990s — ten releases in that decade against four in the 2000s and two in the 2010s — spanning drum ‘n’ bass, noise, and abstract electronics, with Domino as the principal label across more than two decades and Merge Records and Ici D’Ailleurs handling the US and French markets respectively. Flying Saucer Attack is the project’s closest recorded collaborator, sharing six releases, underscoring a deep connection to the Bristol experimental underground.

Notes (web)

Why Matt Elliott tapered Third Eye Foundation activity from the mid-2000s onward

Two interviews shed direct light on Elliott’s motivations. The clearest statement comes from an interview with soundofviolence.net (date not visible in fetch, but the page is at https://www.soundofviolence.net/articles/interview/394/matt_elliott.html), in which Elliott described reaching “a certain limit and fatigue in this experimental electronic style to which I had devoted several albums.” He said he had been working as a sound engineer mastering samples and sequencing, but that he was not “really a musician in the noble sense of the term.” The core dissatisfaction was with the mechanical repetitiveness of programming loops, which prompted him to “perfect [himself] on a musical instrument and leave technology aside for a while.”

A December 2008 Chain DLK interview (https://www.chaindlk.com/interviews/Matt-Elliott/) adds two further factors: “a growing dissatisfaction and boredom with the way I was working,” and the move from Bristol to the French countryside in the early 2000s, which “changed my working method as well and gave me more time to study guitar.” Elliott also cited the limits of purely programming-based composition: “In learning the guitar, I learnt many things I could have never learned only using programming.” As of that 2008 interview he described still working on a new TEF album “mainly for fun,” indicating the project was not abandoned but deprioritised.

Taken together, the picture is: relocation from Bristol to rural France, disenchantment with electronic loop-programming as a compositional method, and the desire to become a practising instrumentalist all converged in the early-to-mid 2000s. TEF continued sporadically (The Dark, 2010; Wake the Dead, 2018) but became a secondary concern once Elliott found acoustic/folk music more personally expressive and technically demanding.
