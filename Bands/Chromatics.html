Chromatics

Chromatics

Jul 17, 20264 min read

Chromatics

Discogs ID: 415506
Origin: Portland, Oregon
Formed: 2001
Disbanded: 2021
Members: Ruth Radelet (845038), Adam Miller (4) (921231), Nat Walker (2) (1249848), Devin Welch (608232), Hannah Blilie (701774), and others
Labels: Gold Standard Laboratories (2002–2004) → Italians Do It Better

American electronic band. Two distinct eras separated by a sound transformation:

GSL era (2002–2004): Lo-fi punk/post-punk. 10 releases on Gold Standard Laboratories including Chrome Rats vs. Basement Ruts (GSL59, 2003) and Plaster Hounds (GSL79, 2004). This phase predates Ruth Radelet and the electronic sound entirely.

Italians Do It Better era (2006–2021): The 2006 EP In The City introduced the synth-pop/dream-pop direction. Moved to Italians Do It Better (label owned by Johnny Jewel). Key albums: Night Drive (2007), Kill For Love (2012), Closer to Grey (2019). The long-promised sixth album Dear Tommy was announced complete in late 2014 but was never officially released before the band’s 2021 disbandment. Shadow is a single/EP (2015), not the unreleased album — see below. Ruth Radelet, Adam Miller, and Nat Walker decided to disband in 2021.

The Dear Tommy Situation (sixth album, announced 2015, never released)

Note: The vault’s earlier shorthand “Shadow (2015, completed but withheld until 2019)” was a conflation. Shadow is a single/EP released September 2015 (also performed on Twin Peaks: The Return, May 2017). The unreleased album is Dear Tommy.

Timeline:

  • Late 2014: Johnny Jewel announces Dear Tommy is complete; Valentine’s Day 2015 release hinted.
  • Feb 2015: Lead single “Just Like You” released; Valentine’s Day release date missed with no explanation.
  • Sept 2015: “Shadow” released as a standalone single.
  • Christmas Day 2015: Jewel nearly drowns in Hawaii. His manager Alexis Rivera later disclosed: “Christmas day 2015 Johnny almost died in Hawaii. I don’t want to go into details.” (The FADER, 2017-05-04)
  • Jan 2016: On returning to Los Angeles, Jewel allegedly destroys all manufactured copies — “15K CDs & 10K vinyl in the Italians warehouse in Glendale, all gone” (Rivera, via Twitter, as reported by The FADER). Jewel’s stated motive: he wanted to improve the album before release.
  • 2016–2019: Jewel releases 20+ side and solo projects. Dear Tommy singles continue to appear sporadically (eleven tracks originally intended for the album were released as singles between 2014 and 2020).
  • Oct 2019: Closer to Grey released — band confirmed it contained no Dear Tommy material and that Dear Tommy would still be released someday. (Rolling Stone, 2019)
  • April 2020: Revised 18-track Dear Tommy tracklist surfaces when “Teacher” is released.
  • Aug 2021: Band disbands. Dear Tommy still unreleased.
  • Dec 2021: Pitchfork reports that Jewel’s destruction story is disputed by “multiple parties close to the band, including by the CD and vinyl pressing plants” — casting doubt on whether the 25,000-copy destruction actually occurred. (Wikipedia summary of Pitchfork report)

Why it was withheld — summary: The official account is Jewel’s near-death swimming experience on Christmas 2015 prompted him to scrap the finished album for aesthetic/spiritual reasons and start over; he then kept revising. However, the 2021 Pitchfork reporting casts doubt on the destruction claim itself, and the album’s non-appearance may reflect a combination of Jewel’s chronic perfectionism, label control, and possible embellishment of the narrative.

Precedent: Jewel also destroyed the first version of Kill For Love (2012) before re-recording it — the Dear Tommy destruction follows the same pattern. (Willamette Week, 2019-06-05)

Status: Unreleased as of the band’s 2021 disbandment. Rolling Stone compared it to Brian Wilson’s Smile — “the [Smile] of the 2010s.”
