Swamp Children

Post-punk / jazz-funk group. Formed in Manchester, UK, c.1980. Active c.1980–1983 before mutating into Kalima. Released on Factory Records (FAC 49) and Factory Benelux (FBN 16, FBN 21). Discogs artist ID: 11894.

Overview

Swamp Children were a Manchester group assembled around a core of Ann and Tony Quigley, who had also circulated through A Certain Ratio’s extended orbit. Their sound moved rapidly from brittle post-punk funk toward polyrhythmic jazz-funk with Latin and African percussion textures, anticipating the direction the band would take when they reconstituted as Kalima in the mid-1980s. All principal releases came through Factory and its Belgian satellite Factory Benelux, placing them squarely inside the Factory Records scene alongside Joy Division, New Order, Section 25, and A Certain Ratio.

The group’s producer on the debut single was Simon Topping (A Certain Ratio vocalist/trumpeter) and Stephen Mallinder (Cabaret Voltaire), reflecting the dense cross-pollination of Factory-adjacent Manchester. Their only proper LP, So Hot (1982), appeared on Factory Benelux rather than the UK parent label — the Benelux imprint often handled releases that were experimental or had a narrower commercial window.

History

Formation, c.1980

Formed in Manchester around 1980. Core members were Ann Quigley (vocals), Tony Quigley (saxophone, bass), John Kirkham (guitar), Ceri Evans (keyboards, bass), Cliff Saffer (saxophone), and Martin Moscrop (drums). Moscrop and Tony Quigley were simultaneously active in A Certain Ratio; the group shared rehearsal space and Factory infrastructure.

Little Voices 12” (1981)

Debut release on Factory (FAC 49). The A-side “Little Voices” (7:10) was produced by Simon Topping of A Certain Ratio; the B-side tracks credited Stephen Mallinder (Cabaret Voltaire) as engineer “Tim” (Tim Dewey). The cover art involved Ann Quigley and a collaborator credited as Graham. Mastered at Strawberry Studios (Manchester), as indicated by the runout etchings. Genres on Discogs: Free Funk, New Wave.

Taste What’s Rhythm 12” (1982)

Released on Factory Benelux (FBN 16). Engineered by Phil Bush. B-side track “Softly Saying Goodbye” features an uncredited appearance by A Certain Ratio. Styles: Jazz-Funk, Jazzdance. The runout etching reads “CONVERSATION ON A TRAIN” — an in-joke or oblique title.

So Hot LP (1982)

Full-length album on Factory Benelux (FBN 21). Self-produced, self-arranged, self-written. Flute on several tracks by Elvio Ghigliordini. Recorded by Stuart (Stewart) Pickering. Nine tracks including “Samba Zippy,” “El Figaro,” “Tender Game,” “No Sunshine,” and “Spark The Flame.” Styles: Jazzdance, Latin. The album represents the fullest statement of their jazz-funk direction and was pressed five times across different territories.

Transition to Kalima, c.1982–1983

After So Hot, the group reconstituted under a new name, Kalima, with an expanded and shifting roster. The Discogs profile notes explicitly that Swamp Children “later mutated into the acclaimed latin / jazz / funk outfit Kalima.” Core members Ann Quigley, Tony Quigley, John Kirkham, Ceri Evans, Cliff Saffer, and Martin Moscrop all appear in Kalima’s membership. Additional Kalima members included Andy Connell (Swing Out Sister), Jez Kerr (A Certain Ratio), and others.

Flyaway Compilation (1989)

Factory (FACD 219) issued a compilation in 1989 titled Flyaway credited primarily to Kalima but including two early Swamp Children tracks (“Samba Zippy” and “Tender Games”) as the first two entries. The compilation documents the continuity of the Swamp Children–Kalima lineage. Also issued as a promo in Japan.

Factory Benelux (parent ID 857 = Factory) was the Belgian subsidiary that handled a number of Factory acts for continental release, and also served as the primary outlet for some recordings that didn’t appear on the UK parent — as in Swamp Children’s case with their LP.

Open Questions

  • No confirmed founding date — “c.1980” per Discogs profile; exact month/year unknown
  • No confirmed dissolution date — Discogs shows releases only through 1982 (as Swamp Children); when exactly the Kalima name adopted is unresolved
  • Ann Quigley’s background before Swamp Children: not in Discogs beyond this group and Kalima
  • John Kirkham: no solo work or other groups found in Discogs beyond Swamp Children / Kalima
  • Cliff Saffer: same — only Swamp Children and Kalima entries
  • Whether “Little Voices” / FAC 49 was co-produced by both Topping and Mallinder or if credits are per-side — Discogs credits Topping as producer on A-side track only, Mallinder as engineer on B-sides
  • Live activity: no live releases documented in Discogs
  • Martin Hannett: no production credit on any Swamp Children release (despite being the Factory house producer for many acts)
