8 Eyed Spy

Discogs ID: 172027
Active: c. 1979–1981
Based: New York City
Genre: No Wave, Post-Punk
Labels: Fetish Records (UK), ROIR (US), Atavistic (reissue)
Also known as: 8-Eyed Spy; Eight Eyed Spy

Overview

8 Eyed Spy were a New York City no wave / post-punk band, active from approximately 1979 until the death of bassist George Scott III in August 1980. The group formed around Lydia Lunch following her work with Teenage Jesus And The Jerks and Beirut Slump. The lineup was a convergence of key downtown NYC underground figures: Jim Sclavunos (drums) had also come from Teenage Jesus and Beirut Slump; George Scott (2) (bass, credited as George Scott III) was a veteran of The Contortions, James White & The Blacks, and was in the process of co-forming The Raybeats; Pat Irwin (bass, guitar, saxophone, piano) was a multi-instrumentalist who had played on Lydia Lunch’s Queen Of Siam (ZE Records, 1980); and Michael Paumgardhen (guitar) had been in Richard Hell & The Voidoids.

The band was cut short by George Scott III’s death on 5 August 1980 (heroin overdose, aged 26). Their recordings — a studio session at Blank Tape Studios produced by Bob Blank, and separately captured live material — were assembled posthumously into a single LP released on Fetish Records in 1981.

Formation and Context

The exact formation date is not confirmed in Discogs data. Lydia Lunch dissolved Teenage Jesus And The Jerks and Beirut Slump by c. 1979. Given Scott’s concurrent involvement with The Contortions and the early Raybeats sessions, the band was likely active from late 1979 into 1980. Sclavunos’s Discogs credits show a pattern consistent with a 1979–80 active period.

The personnel reflects the tight social network of the NYC downtown underground, centred on venues including the Mudd Club. Scott had played in James Chance’s bands; Irwin was already embedded in the ZE Records circle (through Queen Of Siam); Paumgardhen would later be in Richard Hell & The Voidoids.

George Scott III’s Death

George Leonard Scott III died on 5 August 1980 from a heroin overdose, aged 26. He had been born 16 October 1953 in Burlington, Iowa. His death effectively ended the active phase of 8 Eyed Spy. The posthumously assembled LP credits him as “George Scott III” and assigns him bass on the live sides (B1–B8); Pat Irwin covered bass on the studio sides (A1–A5), suggesting those may have been recorded after Scott’s death or that the lineup split duties differently in the studio.

Scott’s death had a wide impact on the no-wave scene — he was simultaneously a central figure in The Contortions, James White & The Blacks, 8 Eyed Spy, and The Raybeats. The Raybeats (with Jody Harris, Don Christensen, and Pat Irwin) continued after his death.

Recording Sessions

The self-titled Fetish LP combines two distinct recording contexts:

Studio Side (A1–A5): Recorded at Blank Tape Studios (Bob Blank’s studio on the Lower East Side), produced and engineered by Bob Blank. Mixed by Chris Stamey and Scott Litt (with Sclavunos and Irwin credited as “Remixed With”). Pat Irwin plays bass, guitar, piano, and saxophone on these tracks; Jim Sclavunos plays drums and percussion. Lydia Lunch sings.

Live Side (B1–B8): Recorded live; separate soundmen credited (Steve Cohen for most tracks; Neil Catchura for “I Want Candy”). Edited and reprocessed by Chris Stamey; Paul Stevens (2) as engineer; George Peckham (a.k.a. Porky) for mastering. George Scott III appears on bass for the live side. Pat Irwin plays guitar and saxophone.

The track “Swamp / Run Through The Jungle” (A5) is an extended cover of the Creedence Clearwater Revival song written by John Fogerty. Other covers include Bo Diddley’s “Diddy Wah Diddy” (written by Ellas McDaniel and Willie Dixon), Lee Hazlewood’s “Lightning’s Girl,” “I Want Candy” (written by Bert Berns, Bob Feldman, Jerry Goldstein, Richard Gottehrer), and “White Rabbit” (Grace Slick). Original compositions are credited to the full five-member band.

The “Diddy Wah Diddy” / “Dead You Me B Side” single (Fetish FE19, 1982) was a posthumous 7” release; the B-side appears on the LP as “Get You Me B Side” (retitled for the single).

Sleeve design for both the LP and the single was by Neville Brody, Fetish Records’ design director.

Deal Timeline and Fetish Connection

A key finding from Pat Irwin’s April 2023 interview with Leah Roseman: “8 Eyed Spy had a record deal, but we were just about ready to go into the studio and record, and George died.” This confirms the Fetish deal (or a precursor deal) was in place before George Scott III’s death on 5 August 1980, and that the October 1980 Blank Tapes session likely fulfilled a pre-existing commitment rather than representing a fresh posthumous arrangement.

Irwin adds: “Lydia just didn’t want to do a band without George, and I really respect that. At the time, I didn’t understand. I was like, ‘Well, let’s just do it.‘” — Consistent with the fromthearchives.org claim that the remaining members convinced Lunch to participate in the October sessions, and with Lunch having no involvement in the remixing/compiling process.

Bob Singerman (LP management credit) was the band’s acting manager and booking agent in this period; he issued press releases on the band’s behalf to New York Rocker in August 1980, around the time of Scott’s death and breakup. He is an uninterviewed primary source on who held the deal.

Whether the label on the pre-death deal was Fetish specifically — or another party, with the Fetish deal negotiated afterward — is not documented in any accessible interview. [Sources: Leah Roseman podcast — Pat Irwin transcript (April 2023); PleaseKillMe — Pat Irwin interview (Valerie Simadis, 2019); fromthearchives.org — 8 Eyed Spy Concert Chronology]

Open Questions

  • Exact formation date for 8 Eyed Spy — was the band active before or after Queen Of Siam was recorded (early 1980)?
  • Were any studio-side tracks recorded after George Scott III’s death (which would explain Pat Irwin covering bass duties on Side A)?
  • Any live performances documented beyond what appears on the ROIR cassette?
  • Michael Paumgardhen’s instrument on the studio side is listed on the LP credits as “Guitar” without track scope — was he present for both the studio and live recordings?
  • The ROIR cassette Live (A101, 1981) and the live side of the Fetish LP appear to draw from the same or overlapping recordings — relationship between the two not fully clarified by Discogs credits alone.
  • Bob Singerman credited as Management on the LP — Robert Singerman, a prominent NYC music manager/entrepreneur who worked with R.E.M., Gipsy Kings, James Brown, Suzanne Vega, Violent Femmes, 10,000 Maniacs, Bad Brains, pre-Beastie Boys, King Sunny Ade, Fela Kuti, Allen Toussaint, and Professor Longhair. His 8 Eyed Spy credit places him in the No Wave orbit early in his career; he went on to far broader representation. (Resolved via web, 2026-05-07.) He was also the band’s active manager/agent in the months around Scott’s death; as an uninterviewed source he remains relevant to the deal question.
  • Byron Coley’s liner notes on the ROIR cassette: date of writing relative to Scott’s death?
  • Was the pre-death deal with Fetish directly, or with another label? See Rod Pearce Research Queue for full context on remaining untested sources.
