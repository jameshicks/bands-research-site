Blurt

UK experimental jazz-punk trio, active 1979/1980–present. Centered on Ted Milton’s alto saxophone, vocals, and poetry. Described variously as Dada-avantgarde-jazz, paranoid jazz-mutant funk, and post-punk. Discogs artist ID: 44615. Also credited as: Blurt!

Overview

Blurt formed in Stroud, Gloucestershire, around 1979–1980, built around Ted Milton (poet, puppeteer, saxophonist). Milton’s brother Jake Milton played drums in the founding lineup alongside guitarist Pete Creese (credited as both Pete Creese and Peter Creese on different releases). The band emerged from the same post-punk ferment as the early Factory Records scene — their debut single appeared on Test Pressings in 1980 and four tracks appeared on the Factory Records double LP A Factory Quartet (FACT 24, 1980), produced by Martin Hannett with Jake Milton and Pete Creese alongside Milton.

Their early records on Armageddon Records (In Berlin, The Fish Needs A Bike, both 1981) established the core sound: abrasive, minimalist sax-led post-punk with lyrics rooted in Milton’s poetry. The self-titled LP (Blurt, 1982, Red Flame) followed, cementing them in the UK experimental underground. They toured widely across Europe through the 1980s, releasing records on Belgian, French, German, and UK labels.

The transitional moment came with Friday The 12th (1985, Another Side, Belgium), a live LP that already features Steve Eagles on guitar — not Pete Creese — while Jake Milton is still on drums. This recording shows the lineup in flux. By Poppycock (1986), both Jake and Creese had been replaced. The mid-to-late 1980s brought a consolidated second lineup: drummer Paul Wigens and guitarist Steve Eagles replaced the founding rhythm section. This trio recorded several LPs including Poppycock (1986, Toeblock) and Smoke Time (1987, Toeblock/Line), with Nosedive (2) and Beam (6) as co-producers. By Pagan Strings (1992, Toeblock), Chris Vine was playing guitar and co-producing.

The band continued through the 1990s and 2000s in lower-profile mode, with releases on Bahia Music (Hungary) and the Salamander label before a revival around 2008’s Cut It! (The Orchestra Pit Recording Co.), produced by Sam Britton (aka Isambard Khroustaliov). LTM reissued the early catalog extensively from 2009 onward. From the 2010s the active lineup settled on Ted Milton, Steve Eagles, and drummer David Aylward.

Recent releases include Beneath Discordant Skies (2015, Metadrone/Salamander), Live At Oto (Bomb) (2016, Salamander), Uzi (2021, self-released), a 2024 reissue of the debut single on All City Records (3), and the new studio album The Mecanno Giraffe (2025, All City Records (3)).

Ted Milton also has a substantial solo career (recordings as Ted Milton, Ted Milton And The BoBonus Beats, Ted Milton And The Blurt Big Band, and as part of Elegiac (2) and The Odes (2)), and appeared as guest saxophonist on releases by Playgroup, Grand Drive, and others.
