Øresund Space Collective

Loose Danish/Swedish/American space-rock collective formed in Denmark in 2004. Discogs artist ID: 1990285. Name-variants: ØSC, OSC, The OSC, Öresund Space Collective. Based across the Øresund region (Copenhagen/Malmö). Primary label: Space Rock Productions (ID: 244104). Also released on Transubstans Records, Sulatron-records, Kommun 2, Clostridium Records, and The Laser’s Edge.

Overview

Øresund Space Collective (ØSC) is a large, rotating collective — approaching 60 credited musicians as of 2020 — built around long-form, fully improvised space-rock. There are no fixed studio compositions; every recording is a live improvisation, captured in one take and released as-is. The collective plays concerts and festivals across Europe and Scandinavia (Roadburn, Freak Valley, Roskilde, Copenhagen Jazz Festival, Burg Herzberg, Psycho Las Vegas, Reverence Festival, Slotskoggen Goes Progressive, and others) and makes a large archive of live recordings freely available through the Internet Archive.

The central organising figure is Scott Heller (performing as Dr. Space), an American based in the Øresund region who plays synthesizers, handles mixing, and co-founded Space Rock Productions in 2009 with Nicholas Hill (2). Heller’s alias Dr. Space (2) (Discogs ID: 2126969) appears on virtually every ØSC release. The collective draws from Danish bands Mantric Muse, The Universals, and Gas Giant, and Swedish bands Bland Bladen, Carpet Knights, First Band From Outer Space, Drahk von Trip, Siena Root, and Sgt. Sunshine. Later-era lineups have included members of Black Moon Circle (Norway), Fri Galaxe, Agusa, and Camper Van Beethoven.

The name refers to the Øresund strait and the binational urban region it divides — the group’s membership physically straddles Denmark and Sweden.

Formation and Early Releases (2004–2009)

The collective coalesced in 2004. Its first commercially released album, the self-titled Øresund Space Collective (2006, Transubstans Records, TRANS020), was followed quickly by It’s All About Delay (2006, Transubstans, TRANS026) — a sprawling double-CD with tracks running to over 25 minutes, with Dr. Space (2) on synthesizer. The Black Tomato (2007, Transubstans) continued the run on that Swedish label.

In 2009, Dead Man In Space (Kommun 2 / Pariah Child) broadened the label spread. That same year, Good Planets Are Hard To Find (Transubstans) appeared. The personnel across this period included core players Mogens Pedersen (organ/synthesizer), Magnus Hannibal (guitar/synth), Jocke Jönsson (guitar), Lars Lundholm (engineer/recording), Tobbe Wulff (mixing), Stefan Krey (guitar), Thomas Carstensen (bass), and Søren Holm Hvilsby.

Space Rock Productions Era (2011–present)

From 2011 the bulk of ØSC’s studio and live output shifted to Space Rock Productions, the label Heller co-founded specifically to release the collective’s music alongside other Scandinavian space-psychedelic acts. The SRP catalog number sequence tracks the label’s growth closely with ØSC albums.

Key Releases in Detail

It’s All About Delay (2006, Transubstans TRANS026, 2xCD) — The first major statement: twelve tracks totalling well over two hours, entirely improvised. Side two opens with “Jupiter Flyby (In Memory Of Doug Walker)” at 25 minutes and continues through 17- and 20-minute pieces. Personnel: Dr. Space (2) on synthesizer.

Dead Man In Space (2009, Kommun 2 K2-7 / Pariah Child LP002, LP) — Three-track vinyl. Notable for the breadth of personnel credited per side: Pär Hallgren and Thomas Carstensen on bass (different sides), Jocke Jönsson and Magnus Hannibal and Stefan Krey on guitar, Mogens Pedersen on organ/synthesizer, Anders Honoré on saxophone, Anders Gärtner (as Kaufmann) on drums. Recorded and engineered by Lars Lundholm.

Damo Suzuki Møder Oresund Space Collective (2014, Space Rock Productions SRP020, 3xLP, limited/numbered) — Live triple-LP collaboration with Damo Suzuki, the former Can vocalist turned peripatetic sound carrier. Six untitled-in-English tracks over six sides (Danish titles: “Damo’s Første ØSC Flyvtur,” “Energisk Reaktion 1 & 2,” “Jeg Kan Ikke Vokse Up,” “Dit Glimtende Øje,” “Datid, Nutid, Og Fremtid”). Dr. Space (2) credited: synthesizer and mixing. The release fits squarely within the Damo Suzuki’s Network model of one-time full-improvisation collaborations.

Ode To A Black Hole (2016, Space Rock Productions SRP033, LP, limited/numbered) — Two side-long tracks (25:59 and 26:46). Lineup: Jonathan Segel (violin, guitar), Hasse Horrigmoe (bass guitar, drums), Alexander Skepp (drums/percussion), Mathias Danielsson (pedal steel, electric guitar), KG West (synthesizer), Dr. Space (2) (synthesizer, mixing), Jonas Berge (synthesizer, organ).

Experiments In The Subconscious (2020, Space Rock Productions SRP063, CD) — Notable for incorporating African percussion (Hasse Horrigmoe on doundouns and talking drum) alongside the usual synthesizer/guitar palette. Five tracks totalling over 70 minutes. Lineup: Jiri Jon Hjorth (bass), Tim Wallander (drums), Hasse Horrigmoe (doundouns, talking drum), Jonathan Segel (guitar/theremin/violin), KG West (guitar, synth), Magnus Hannibal (guitar, synth), Scott Heller (synth). Recorded by Lars Lundholm; mixed by Jonathan Segel.
