Ultravox

Discogs ID: 70735
Active: 1973–1988, 2008–2012
Origin: London, England
Genres: New Wave, Synth-Pop, Art Rock, Post-Punk

British new wave/synth-pop band. Formed 1973 as Tiger Lily (2); renamed Ultravox! (with exclamation mark) in 1976; became Ultravox from 1980 after John Foxx’s departure. Seven UK Top Ten albums, 17 Top 40 singles. Best known for “Vienna” (1981), which spent four weeks at UK #2 — famously held off the top by Joe Dolce’s “Shaddap You Face.”

History

Tiger Lily and Early Ultravox! (1973–1978)

The band formed in 1973 as Tiger Lily (2) (Discogs ID 708017), a London art-rock outfit built around vocalist Dennis Leigh (later John Foxx), multi-instrumentalist Billy Currie, bassist Chris Cross (born Christopher Allen), and drummer Warren Cann. The name changed to Ultravox! in 1976; guitarist Stevie Shears joined to complete the lineup.

The self-titled debut Ultravox! (1977, Island Records, 28 193 XOT) was co-produced by Brian Eno and Steve Lillywhite, engineered by Terry Barham. John Foxx sang; Billy Currie played violin and keyboards; Stevie Shears on guitar. Sleeve concept credited to Dennis Leigh (Foxx’s legal name). The record was art-rock adjacent and commercially marginal.

Ha!-Ha!-Ha! (1977, Island) followed the same year with the same lineup.

For Systems of Romance (1978, Antilles AN-7069), Shears was replaced by Robin Simon (guitar/vocals). The album was produced and engineered by Conny Plank with Dave Hutchins as engineer. Cann deployed early drum machines alongside live drums. This was John Foxx’s final record with the band; he departed afterward for a solo career.

Vienna Era (1980–1983)

With Foxx gone, the band recruited Midge Ure (born James Ure), formerly of Rich Kids and briefly Thin Lizzy. The exclamation mark was dropped. The core quartet of Ure, Currie, Cross, and Cann would carry the band through its commercial peak.

Vienna (1980, Chrysalis 202 701) was produced by Conny Plank. Ure took lead vocals, guitar, and synthesizer; Currie contributed piano, violin, viola, and synth. Photography by Brian Griffin (3); design by Glenn Travis. The title track reached UK #2 in early 1981 and became the band’s signature.

Rage In Eden (1981, Chrysalis CDL 1338) continued with Conny Plank producing and engineering. Lacquer cutting by John Hudson at Town House Studios (runout inscribed “CHR 1338 A//1▽ETH JH TOWN HOUSE”).

Quartet (1982, Chrysalis CDL 1394) marked a commercial high point and a new production relationship: George Martin produced, with Geoff Emerick engineering. Sleeve design by Peter Saville (Peter Saville Associates); illustration drawing by Ken Kennedy (4), colouring by Bill Philpot. Singles: “Reap The Wild Wind,” “Hymn,” “Visions In Blue,” “We Came To Dance.”

Lament and U-Vox (1984–1988)

Lament (1984, Chrysalis 206 175) was self-produced by the band, with John Hudson engineering. Design again by Peter Saville Associates. Notable personnel: Mae McKenna sang Gaelic vocals on “Man Of Two Worlds”; a string quartet — Amanda Woods, Jacqueline Woods, Margaret Roseberry, and Robert Woollard — appeared on “Heart Of The Country”; backing vocals on “A Friend I Call Desire” by Debi Doss and Shirlie Roden. Lead single “Dancing With Tears In My Eyes” reached UK #3.

By U-Vox (1986, Chrysalis 830 589-1), Warren Cann had been ousted. Mark Brzezicki, drummer of Big Country, replaced him. Conny Plank returned as producer/engineer; additional engineers: Stuart Barry (Conny’s studio and Hot Food), Rik Walton (Music Fest), Karl Lever (mix). George Martin arranged and conducted the orchestral “All In One Day.” The Chieftains appeared on “All Fall Down.” Carol Kenyon provided backing vocals; Beggar & Co. provided horns on “Same Old Story” and “The Prize.”

The band effectively dissolved in 1988 following Midge Ure’s departure.

Reunion (2008–2012)

Midge Ure, Billy Currie, Chris Cross, and Warren Cann reunited in 2008 for touring and new material. Steven Wilson contributed remixes during this period.
