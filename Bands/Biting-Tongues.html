Biting Tongues

Manchester post-punk / industrial collective. Discogs artist ID: 12089. Initially active c.1979–1989; reunion activity from 2009 onwards. Recorded for Situation Two, New Hormones, Paragon (2), Factory Records, and Cut Deep.

Biography

Formation and Early Period (1979–1982)

Biting Tongues formed in 1979 at the Factory Club (the Russell Club, Hulme), where Howard Walmsley, Ken Hollings, Eddie Sherwood and a few others performed a live soundtrack to a 16mm silent film Walmsley had made — also called “Biting Tongues.” The one-off performance was at Tony Wilson’s club, placing the band’s origins squarely within the Factory milieu before they had any formal record deal. A few weeks later, Colin Seddon and Graham Massey joined, having recently disbanded their previous group, the Post Natals. The stabilised five-piece was Walmsley (sax), Seddon (bass), Sherwood (drums), Massey (guitar, violin, woodwinds), and Hollings (text, vocals).

The group was from the outset a collective and an audiovisual laboratory, guided by the principle “Never do what’s easy: never make it look difficult.” Performances in the 1979–1980 period mixed spoken word, percussion, random tapes, films and freeform soloing, mostly in clubs in Manchester and London. Ken Hollings was at the same time working in London with John Cage’s ideas about text processing — scrambling, cutting up and rearranging source material drawn from overheard conversations, news reports, old movies, and the writings of criminals, occultists and political theorists.

Their debut release was the LP Don’t Heal (1981, Situation Two, cat: SITU 1; release 236220) — the first release on the Situation Two label, which was a Beggars Banquet subsidiary. Produced by Stuart James, a Piccadilly Radio producer, it was recorded in two separate sessions: side one (“Dark Room Skin Transfers”) at Drone Studios, Chorlton; side two (“The White Valise”) recorded a year later at Pennine Studios in Oldham. The first session was played straight through without a break between tracks, with random tapes left running throughout and their accidental intrusions incorporated into the mix. Massey is credited as “Graham V. Massey” (guitar), Seddon on bass, Sherwood on drums, Walmsley on saxophone, and Hollings under the alias Capalula. Sales were modest but the album established the group’s post-punk/industrial identity, drawing comparisons to Cabaret Voltaire and Pere Ubu.

In 1981, Biting Tongues also released Live It — the third release in the New Hormones cassette series (cat: CAT 3; release 231260), issued in a batch of 500 in September 1981 for the new Walkman generation. Richard Boon’s New Hormones label had originally wanted to release the group’s first recording but had to pass it to Situation Two; Live It drew from various northwest studio sessions and was felt to be more representative of the band’s raw, energetic live sound. New Hormones had initially recorded the sessions for what became Don’t Heal but financial and logistical reasons meant Situation Two picked it up instead.

In 1982, Biting Tongues appeared on a split 12” with Belgian group Tank Of Danzig (release 243687, Antler Records, Belgium; cat: ANT 006). The Biting Tongues side contributes two tracks — “Evening State” and “Lock Up State” — with Massey on guitar, trumpet and percussion; Seddon on bass and percussion; Sherwood on drums; Walmsley on piano, saxophone and percussion; and Hollings on voice, piano and percussion.

Libreville and the Paragon Period (1983)

Libreville (1983, Paragon (2), cat: VIRTUE 1; release 231909) is the group’s most fully-realised early statement. The LP was recorded at Revolution Studios, Cheadle Hulme, with Belgian producer Roland Beelen at the controls for some tracks, and at Bootleg Studios for others. The album had originally been intended as a New Hormones release (it was logged as New Hormones catalogue number ORG 26), but when New Hormones ran out of money it was eventually released on the short-lived Paragon label instead. The LP was recorded in separate sessions — tracks A2, A4 and B3 produced by the band themselves with engineer Roger Salmon (2); tracks A1, B1, B2 and B4 produced by Roland Beelen with engineer Stewart Pickering. The full lineup of five — Massey, Seddon, Sherwood, Walmsley, and Hollings (credited as “Kenneth Hollings”) — is named explicitly in the sleeve as “Musician [Biting Tongues].” Track “Smash The Strategic Hamlets” received additional engineering credit from Tim Harris (6). Sleeve design by Garrard Martin.

The album’s styles are catalogued as Avantgarde, New Wave, and Post-Punk. Track titles like “First Use All The G’s” (10:05), “Smash The Strategic Hamlets” and “Doctor Restore He Sight” suggest a surrealist-literary sensibility that Hollings — a writer and cultural critic — brought to the group’s aesthetic.

Factory Records Period and Feverhouse (1984–1987)

By 1984, the group had developed a life of its own but the collective structure was becoming harder to maintain. Work began that year on Feverhouse, a film written by Ken Hollings and directed by Howard Walmsley. The film is a black-and-white feature set in “an all-purpose institution that might be part hospital, part museum, part asylum and part morgue,” in which three characters — a thief (played by Graham Massey), a nurse (Joanne Hill), and a blind man (Patrick Nyland) — share indeterminate relationships of desire and power. It was partially financed by the North West Arts Association. The completed film was released on VHS and Betamax in 1985 through Ikon FCL, a Factory Records video offshoot; Factory catalogued it as FAC 105 (Ikon release number IKON 5). The film was co-edited by Walmsley and Dave Horsfield.

The group moved to Factory Records for the accompanying soundtrack LP: Feverhouse — The Soundtrack (1985, Factory, cat: FACT 105; release 12508). The LP features a new lineup — with Seddon and Sherwood having departed and been replaced by Mark Derby (bass), Phil Kirby (drums), and Tom Barnish (trombone) — but the soundtrack is credited to the band collectively for “all instruments, tapes, voices, produced by,” with engineer Dave Pringle. Written-by credits go to Massey, Seddon, and Walmsley.

Colin Seddon had left to pursue his growing obsession with African and Brazilian percussion. He went on to co-found the Inner Sense Percussion Ensemble with Eddie Sherwood in 1985, a long-running Manchester institution based initially in a Levenshulme bedsit and dedicated to samba batucada and world percussion. Eddie Sherwood had by this point also become the first drummer in Simply Red before that work led him toward Inner Sense full time.

The 12” single Trouble Hand (1985, Factory, cat: fac 134; release 12522) documents the new lineup in full: Mark Derby on bass, Phil Kirby on drums, Massey on guitar, Walmsley on saxophone, and Tom Barnish on trombone. Produced jointly by Biting Tongues and Dave Pringle, with design by Johnson Panas.

By 1986, Paddy Steer had taken over bass duties from Mark Derby. Compressor (1987, Factory, cat: FAC 188; release 93222) documents this lineup and Massey’s advancing move toward electronics: his credit shifts to “Vocals, Synthesizer [ST20, DX7, VSS100].” Paddy Steer (as “Patrick Steer”) plays bass guitar and double bass. The 12” also had various vocal contributions pushed through the mix, including Basil Clarke of Yargo. Production is split between Chris Nagle (tracks A and B2) and Dave Pringle (track B1), with design again by Johnson Panas. Shortly after, the rhythm section of Kirby and Steer left to concentrate on Yargo full time — a significant blow.

Late Period and Cut Deep (1988–1989)

By 1988, Biting Tongues had contracted to a duo: Graham Massey and Howard Walmsley. Massey had completed a recording course at Manchester’s Spirit Studios (where he had also been working as a live sound engineer at the Boardwalk Club), and was steering the group decisively toward MIDI and electronics. In November 1988, he simultaneously began work on two albums: Recharge for Biting Tongues, and Quadrastate for the newly-formed 808 State — a situation that would eventually tip the balance toward 808 State permanently.

Love Out (1989, Cut Deep, cat: CUT 12004; release 19830) is a four-track 12” that makes the band’s new electronic direction explicit. Massey takes producer, engineer, and multi-instrumentalist credits (“Programmed By, Keyboards, Guitar, Vocals”); Walmsley plays “Sax, Additional Keyboards”; Tom Barnish adds trombone. The track “Love Out (Edit Mix)” features guest vocals from Denise Johnson — credited simply as “Denise” — who would appear the following year on 808 State material. Cut Deep was a new label set up to release the final Biting Tongues work, but it was short-lived.

Recharge — the full album recorded in late 1988 — was intended as Biting Tongues’ proper final statement. It featured adventurous dance and electronica, with Denise Johnson contributing guest vocals on at least two tracks, but the material found itself out of step with early club culture. The album ran out of juice at the test pressing stage when Cut Deep ceased trading in 1989. White label promo copies circulated for years among fans of early 808 State — in part because Recharge and 808 State’s Quadrastate were recorded in the same sessions, and the story goes that if Howard Walmsley had not left his soprano saxophone in the studio overnight, “Pacific State” might have sounded different. The white label Recharge (1989, Cut Deep, cat: CUT 6; release 241518) is the 8-track double 12” promo that did circulate; a proper LTM CD release followed in 2003 (see below). In 1988, Ikon also released a 50-minute video compilation Wall of Surf (Ikon 26), drawing on the group’s audiovisual archive.

By the end of 1989, Biting Tongues went into hiatus. Massey moved on to 808 State, which had been forming since 1988 and became the focus of his efforts from 1989 onwards. Tom Barnish, Phil Kirby, and Paddy Steer had all passed through the Yargo orbit.

LTM Retrospectives and Recharge Proper Release (2003)

Three releases appeared on LTM Recordings in 2003 to coincide with the ICA reunion gig (see below): After The Click — Retrospective 1980–89 (LTM (4), cat: LTMCD 2371; release 257030), Compressed (The Factory Recordings 1984–87) (LTM (4), cat: LTMCD 2373; release 256964), and the long-delayed Recharge (LTM, cat: LTMCD 2376) — the lost 1988 album finally given a proper commercial release. The After The Click retrospective is significant for its track-level drummer credits: Eddie Sherwood is confirmed on the early material through Libreville (1981–83); Phil Kirby takes over from Trouble Hand onward (1985–87); one track (“Everywhere But Here (Live)”) credits Colin Seddon on drums. Graham Massey compiled the retrospective and provided the cover image. Compressed collects the complete Factory-period output including all of the Feverhouse soundtrack.

First Reunion and the ICA Gig (2003)

The original Biting Tongues lineup — Massey, Hollings, Walmsley, Seddon, and Sherwood — reunited for a one-off performance at the Institute of Contemporary Arts (ICA) in London on 29 May 2003, sharing the bill with former rehearsal room contemporaries Crispy Ambulance. This was the first time all five had performed together in nearly twenty years. The three LTM releases (see above) were timed to coincide with this event.

Second Reunion: Brussels Factory Night (2009)

In December 2009, Biting Tongues performed at the “A Factory Night” event at Plan K in Brussels — a tribute event to the Factory Records world. This was a separate Biting Tongues reunion gig, not to be confused with the Sisters Of Transistors project Massey was also developing at the same time.

Sisters Of Transistors (2009)

Massey released At The Ferranti Institute in 2009 under the name Sisters Of Transistors (This Is Music, cat: THISIM018; release 2272542). This is a Massey side project — not a Biting Tongues release — though it draws on related aesthetic territory. The album is a concept piece framed around north-west English electric organ culture, involving an all-female collective (Mandy Wigby, Henrietta Smith-Rolla, Naomi Hart, Ragna Skinner, Seaming To as guest) with Massey appearing throughout under the alias Prof Vernon World. Paddy Steer plays timpani on one track. The distinction is clear: At The Ferranti Institute does not include the original Biting Tongues collective and is Massey’s solo conceptual project. Ken Hollings contributed liner notes to a later archival release.

Clear Your Screens and the True Reunion Album (2020)

Clear Your Screens (2020, The Wormhole, cat: WHO#19; release 13786316) is a twelve-track CD of live recordings representing the group’s 21st-century reunion activity. Venues documented include the ICA, London (2003); Islington Mill, Salford (2007); Shunt Vaults, London (2008); Islington Mill Moonlanding Party (2009); and Plan K, Brussels (2009). The recordings were remixed using modern digital techniques years after the original performances, with Ken Hollings compiling the selection. The core lineup present — Massey (guitar, trumpet, woodwind, electric violin, tapes), Seddon (bass), Sherwood (drums), Walmsley (tenor sax, organ, Moog) — is effectively the 1981 original five-piece with Hollings contributing text and voice. Mastered by Stephan Mathieu. Released on The Wormhole label, distributed via The Tapeworm. The track titles append venue names as parenthetical sub-titles — “First Use All The Gs (Moon Landing)”, “Evening State (ICA)” — anchoring the recordings to specific performances. This is the definitive reunion document.

Still On Hawaiian Time (2016)

A separate archival cassette release, Still On Hawaiian Time (The Tapeworm, cat: TTW#91), documents two early Biting Tongues performances: the Library Theatre, Manchester (16 June 1983) and Riverside Studios, London (5 June 1982). This release captures the original five-piece lineup in live performance at the height of the Libreville era.

Critical Reception and Scene Context

Biting Tongues were formed in the same environment as the wider Factory Records milieu — Joy Division, A Certain Ratio, the Russell Club scene — though they were never formally Factory acts in their early period. Their approach was consistently described as audiovisual and conceptual rather than narrowly musical, placing them closer to Cabaret Voltaire (Sheffield) and Pere Ubu (Cleveland) than to the guitar-pop of their contemporaries.

Don’t Heal (1981) received modest sales on Situation Two. Trouser Press reviewer Mark Fleischmann described it as “monotonic spoken-word vocals over a no-wavish backing of sax, guitar, and rhythm section,” calling vocalist Capalula (Ken Hollings) “a pretender to the throne once occupied by Joy Division’s late Ian Curtis” — though he acknowledged that Hollings “lacks the dark depths of black feeling that made Curtis unique.” Libreville drew more positive critical notice, described by listeners as “excellent experimental post punk with a smell of avant jazz mixed in.” The group’s relationship with the Factory network gave them exposure through venues like the Factory Club and the Hacienda without turning them into a mainstream Factory signing until the mid-1980s.

The 2003 reunions and LTM reissue campaign brought renewed attention, framing the group’s archive as an important and underappreciated document of the post-punk/industrial moment. The Recharge white-label had already circulated for years as a collectors’ item before its proper LTM release.

Connections

  • Graham Massey — core member; before Biting Tongues played in the Post Natals with Colin Seddon; after Biting Tongues formed 808 State in 1988
  • 808 State — Massey’s post-Biting Tongues project; Recharge and Quadrastate recorded simultaneously in 1988; Denise Johnson connects the two
  • Simply Red — Eddie Sherwood became Simply Red’s first drummer after leaving Biting Tongues
  • A Certain Ratio — Manchester Factory contemporaries; overlapping scene
  • Joy Division / New Order — Factory Records shared context
  • Cabaret Voltaire — industrial / post-punk parallel (Sheffield); similar avantgarde approach
  • Yargo — Paddy Steer, Phil Kirby, and Tom Barnish all passed through this Manchester jazz-funk group; Yargo was co-founded by members who left Biting Tongues
  • Homelife — later ensemble on Ninja Tune including Massey and Steer
  • Inner Sense Percussion Ensemble — Colin Seddon and Eddie Sherwood co-founded this Manchester world percussion collective after leaving Biting Tongues (1985)
  • Factory Records — principal label for the mid-period; Factory connection predates signing, as the group formed at the Factory Club in 1979
  • LTM Recordings — issued three retrospective/archival releases in 2003; James Nice documented the Factory era
  • Situation Two — first release on the label (SITU 1)
  • New Hormones — Richard Boon’s label; Live It was third in their cassette series; Libreville was originally catalogued as ORG 26 before NH ran out of money
