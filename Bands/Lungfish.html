Lungfish

Discogs ID: 266523 Origin: Baltimore, Maryland (based in Washington DC scene) Active: 1987–2005 (on indefinite hiatus since 2006) Label: Dischord Records (primary, 1990–2012 including archival) Genre: Post-hardcore, minimalist rock, art rock

American band formed in 1987 in Baltimore by vocalist Daniel Higgs and bassist John Chriest, who had been collaborating on sound collage / poetry projects. Guitarist Asa Osborne joined after a stint with Higgs in Reptile House (2); Mitchell W. Feldstein completed the lineup on drums. One of the most singular voices in the Dischord Records catalog: eleven studio albums over fifteen years, all recorded by Don Zientara at Inner Ear Studios (with a single exception), built from hypnotic, mantra-like song structures and Higgs’s densely spiritual/mystical lyrical register.

History

Formation and Debut (1987–1991)

The group grew out of Higgs and Chriest’s collaborative experimentation — Chriest building loops and sound collages for Higgs’s poetry. Osborne, who had played with Higgs in the short-lived Reptile House (2) (also a Dischord act), was recruited on guitar, and Mitchell Feldstein joined on drums. Early on Higgs temporarily relocated to San Francisco, leaving the band’s future uncertain. Their debut LP, Necklace of Heads (1990), was co-released by Dischord Records and Simple Machines as a document of their songs to that point, engineered by Tim Miskimon and produced by Lawrence Bertoldi. Positive response to it prompted the band to commit to touring and to a sustained relationship with Dischord.

The Dischord Run and Classic Period (1992–1994)

Beginning with Talking Songs For Walking (Dischord 66, 1992), Lungfish worked with Don Zientara as their recording engineer — a relationship that would hold for most of their career. Ian MacKaye produced the Zientara-engineered tracks on Talking Songs, with Lawrence Bertoldi producing the Tim Miskimon sessions. Jeff Nelson handled graphics on Talking Songs alongside Osborne.

Rainbows From Atoms (Dischord 78, 1993) and Pass And Stow (Dischord 92, 1994) followed quickly, both engineered by Zientara and produced by MacKaye. On Pass And Stow, Higgs is credited under the alias “A. Astronomo Erdman” and Osborne as “Asa Sargent” — the first of Higgs’s many alias appearances on Lungfish records. Chriest left after Pass And Stow and was replaced by Sean Meadows of June Of 44.

In 1993 the band also appeared on a split 7” (February, Simple Machines) with The Tinklers (2).

Middle Period: Meadows Era (1995–1997)

The Meadows lineup produced two LPs on Dischord: Sound In Time (1996, Dischord 95) and Indivisible (1997, Dischord 107). Both were engineered and mixed by Zientara (with Lungfish also credited as mixer on Sound In Time). Jason Farrell — the dominant Dischord graphic designer — handled layout on both. The band’s composition became more minimal and cyclical across these records.

The 1995 EP 10 East was a slight departure: recorded by Mark Strazza and mixed by the band, with no Zientara involvement.

Nathan Bell Era (1998–2003)

Beginning with Artificial Horizon (Dischord 115, 1998), Nathan Bell took over as bassist. Recorded and engineered by Zientara; the band self-mixed, with Zientara credited on Artificial Horizon as “Recorded By, Engineer.” Higgs appears on that record under the alias “Omon Ortsa,” credited for “Voice, Guitar [Supplemental].” MacKaye is not a producer — the band began to take full production control.

The LP also saw Cynthia Connolly and Pat Graham handling band photography, and Jason Farrell continuing on graphics.

The Unanimous Hour (Dischord 129, 1999) marked a new step: Ian MacKaye returned as co-mixer (alongside the band), and John Loder (Southern Studios, UK) mastered the vinyl.

Necrophones (Dischord 136, 2000) was tracked mostly by Zientara with one track recorded by Craig Bowen; Higgs is credited as “Daniel Nomo Higgs.”

Love Is Love (Dischord 144, 2003) saw Sean Meadows return to the bass chair, with MacKaye co-mixing and Chad Clark (of Beauty Pill) mastering. Zientara recorded.

Final Album and Hiatus (2005–2006)

Feral Hymns (Dischord 147, 2005) was the last studio LP and the only major Lungfish album recorded away from Inner Ear — Tim Green (2) engineered and recorded it, with John Golden and Tim Green (2) mastering. Meadows returned again for this record. The band produced themselves. Lungfish has been on indefinite hiatus since 2006.

Archival Release

A.C.R. 1999 (Dischord, 2012) — an archival release of a 1999 concert recording, with Craig Bowen credited for mixing and recording. The Nathan Bell lineup (Osborne, Higgs, Feldstein, Bell) is documented.

Sound and Aesthetic

Lungfish developed a highly distinctive approach within the Dischord post-hardcore idiom: rather than the dynamics-based catharsis of Fugazi or the post-punk sharpness of Jawbox, Lungfish built songs from reduced, repeating structures — guitar figures that accumulate slowly, bass and drums locking into groove-like ostinatos, with Higgs’s baritone voice delivering lyrics that read as spiritual incantation rather than narrative. Critics and fans have compared the effect to krautrock’s motorik impulse or to certain strains of free gospel, though Lungfish were essentially genre-free in practice.

Higgs’s use of aliases (A. Astronomo Erdman, Omon Ortsa, Minerva (9), Cone Of Light) reflects an interest in esoteric identity and naming that carried over into his solo career and visual art. He is also listed as “Daniel V. Strasser” in some contexts (Discogs alias ID 886574 — an alias credited on some early releases).

Osborne went on to lead the drone project Zomes and played in The Pupils (with Higgs). Both Osborne and Higgs were members of the noise/experimental band Reptile House (2) (Dischord 15¾) before forming Lungfish.
