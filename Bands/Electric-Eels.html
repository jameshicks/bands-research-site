Electric Eels

Proto-punk / avant-rock band. Cleveland, Ohio, USA, c. 1972–1975. Discogs artist ID: 252896.

Name variations: The Electric Eels, Die Electric Eels, The Eels.

Formation and Background

The Electric Eels were formed in Cleveland, Ohio in 1972 by guitarist and bandleader John Morton, alongside vocalist Dave E. McManus (who performed as “Dave E”), guitarist Brian McMahon, and drummer Nick Stephanoff (better known as Nick Knox, later of The Cramps). The group occupied the same tight orbit of Cleveland art students and bohemians that produced Mirrors, Rocket From The Tombs, and eventually Pere Ubu.

The Eels were part of what became one of the most compressed and ferocious proto-punk scenes in North America, predating the New York and London punk explosions by several years. Their world overlapped directly with Mirrors — Jamie Klimek and Paul Marotta played in both bands — and with Rocket From The Tombs, the short-lived group that fractured into Pere Ubu and Dead Boys.

Sound and Style

The Electric Eels’ sound was confrontational and often bizarre: two or more guitars locked in abrasive noise, minimal or no conventional bass for large stretches, a vocalist more interested in provocation than melody, and an approach to composition that blurred the line between rock and free jazz or noise improvisation. The Discogs genre tags across their releases run from Punk and Rock & Roll to Free Jazz, Experimental, Rhythmic Noise, and Avantgarde — an accurate spread. Their recorded material shows two somewhat distinct sonic poles: the core Morton/McMahon/Knox lineup (tight, fast, trashy rock & roll) and the expanded lineup with Klimek, Marotta, and Jones (more free and noisy).

Their song titles alone suggested their sensibility: “You Crummy Fags,” “Sewercide,” “You’re Full Of Shit,” “Spinach Blasters,” “No Nonsense.” John Morton’s artwork (he did covers for several releases) was similarly combative. Dave E. McManus was credited with vocals and clarinet across all configurations of the band.

Activity and Performances

The Electric Eels played very few live shows during their active period — perhaps a handful, and reportedly extremely confrontational ones, occasionally involving actual violence. Cleveland at the time had no established venue infrastructure for underground rock, and the Eels were extreme even by comparison with their peers.

Discogs records no studio release from their active years: the band dissolved c. 1975 without releasing anything. Their first appearance on record was the posthumous Rough Trade 7” in 1978 (see below). Nearly all their recorded material consisted of tapes made by Paul Marotta, who served as the group’s de facto archivist.

Key Release Detail

Agitated / Cyclotron (Rough Trade, 1978) — RT 008

The band’s debut release, appearing three years after they dissolved. Released on London’s Rough Trade as by “Die Electric Eels.” Personnel: John Morton (lead guitar), Brian McMahon (rhythm guitar), Dave E. McManus (vocals), Nick Knox (drums), Paul Marotta (recorded and mixed). Lacquer cut by Porky (George Peckham) at Porky’s Prime Cuts. The German titling (Haupt Gitarre, Rhythmus Gitarre, Trommeln, Vokal) was part of the sleeve concept.

“Agitated” was written by Dave E. McManus and Morton/McMahon/Marotta; “Cyclotron” by John Morton.

The Eyeball Of Hell (Scat Records, 2001) — scat62-2

The definitive compiled recording. 24 tracks drawing on both major configurations of the band. Tracks 1–18 feature the core lineup (Morton, McMahon, Knox on drums); tracks 19–24 shift to the expanded lineup (Klimek, Marotta, Jones on bass, Anton Fier on drums, Dave E. on vocals/clarinet). Paul Marotta produced and mixed nearly all tracks; Jim Jones mixed tracks 5–7. John Morton contributed cover art; liner notes by Morton and Marotta.

Songwriting credits across the record are split: Morton (Cyclotron, Refrigerator, You’re Full Of Shit, and many others), McMahon (Jaguar Ride, Cold Meat, Accident, Silver Daggers, others), Dave E. McManus (words on Agitated, and several other tracks).

Spin Age Blasters (Scat Records, 2023) — SCAT-89

The most comprehensive vinyl survey, a double LP. Sequenced by Robert Griffin (Scat Records). Shows the full range of both lineups; some tracks credit Jim Jones as recording engineer alongside Marotta.

Two distinct lineup configurations are documented across releases:

Core lineup (majority of material, most of 1972–1975):

  • John Morton — guitar, vocals
  • Brian McMahon — guitar, vocals, electric piano
  • Nick Knox (Nick Stephanoff) — drums
  • Dave E. McManus — vocals, clarinet

Connections

  • Mirrors — Jamie Klimek, Paul Marotta, and Jim Jones (3) all played in both bands; the 1997 Scat split CD documents both groups
  • The Styrenes — John Morton and Paul Marotta both led this successor project; Dave E. McManus appeared on one Styrenes track; the Scat split also includes Styrenes material
  • Pere Ubu — Jim Jones (3) and Anton Fier both became members; Peter Laughner (Pere Ubu co-founder) was part of the same Cleveland scene
  • Rocket From The Tombs — Peter Laughner and David Thomas connected Cleveland group; shared Craig Bell (Mirrors) overlap
  • The Cramps — Nick Knox (Nick Stephanoff) became the Cramps’ longtime drummer after leaving Cleveland
  • Swans — Anton Fier was a member before forming The Golden Palominos
  • Jon Savage — wrote liner notes for the 2014 Superior Viaduct LP Die Electric Eels
