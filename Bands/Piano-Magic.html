Piano Magic

Discogs ID: 65228. UK post-rock / chamber pop / experimental electronic collective. Active 1996–2016. Conceived and led throughout by Glen Johnson. Originally inspired by the studio-collective model of This Mortal Coil — a loose constellation of contributors orbiting a single creative core. Over 20 years and nine studio albums, the sound evolved from lo-fi electronic minimalism through post-rock, electropop, and into lush melancholy chamber pop. Top labels: Second Language, Rocket Girl, 4AD.

Last ever concert: 3 December 2016, The Lexington, Angel, London — exactly 20 years to the day from their first concert. The ending was deliberate and planned by Johnson: he timed it to the 20th anniversary, had a sense that the band had reached its artistic ceiling (see Dissolution section below), and was working through personal loss — his father died in 2015, and “The Last Engineer,” the final song of the final set, was dedicated to him.

Dissolution (2016)

Piano Magic’s ending was Johnson’s own decision, taken on his own terms. Three things converged:

The 20-year milestone. Johnson deliberately timed Closure and the final concert to land exactly on the 20th anniversary of the first concert. The symmetry was engineered, not coincidental — same city, 20 years apart. He framed Closure not as defeat but as “a very fitting lid… a mature set, lovingly played, straight to the heart.”

A sense of artistic completion. Johnson had articulated a ceiling: “If we can get Brendan Perry to sing on one of our records, there’s nowhere else to go.” Brendan Perry (Dead Can Dance) appeared on Ovations in 2009. Having landed that, Johnson felt the project had reached what it was capable of reaching.

Personal loss. Johnson’s father died in 2015. The final album’s themes are explicitly about accumulated loss — Johnson described Closure as being about “lost loves, lost friends, lost moments, lost dreams, lost opportunities, lost fathers.” The closing song “The Last Engineer” was written about and dedicated to his father; he named it from the stage at the final show.

This was nothing like the Disco Inferno split. No acrimony, no fractured relationships — Johnson simply decided twenty years was a complete arc and closed the loop. After Piano Magic he continued working with Cédric Pin in the project The Burning Skull.

Sources: Piano Magic official biography (piano-magic.co.uk); Pennyblackmusic interview with Glen Johnson, 2016; Since I Left You blog, 2017.

Formation

Conceived summer 1996 by Glen Johnson, Dominic Chennell, and Dick Rance. Johnson and Chennell were both Nottingham exiles sharing a first-floor flat at Brecknock Road, at the intersection of Kentish Town, Holloway, and Camden, North London. They recorded separately on Johnson’s Tascam 4-track portastudio, working with vintage synthesizers and limited equipment. Band name derived from “a kitsch, junkshop piano medley 10-inch” Johnson found lying around.

Demo tape impressed i/Che Records; deal struck at the Bunjies folk cellar bar in Soho. The debut single Wrong French (1996) received John Peel support and was named joint Single of the Week in Melody Maker. Early live lineup: Johnson on bass, Dick Rance on guitar, Paul Tornbohm on drums, Dominic Chennell on synth.

The project was deliberately structured as a loose collective — different voices and instrumentalists contributed on different tracks, with Johnson as the constant. By 1998 American musicians Sandy Rousmaniere, Ezra Feinberg, and Jen Adam joined, steering toward more melodic song-based material. The Fun Of The Century EP (1998) marked this shift. By 1999 only Johnson remained from the original trio, Chennell’s departure freeing him to develop a more stable international touring lineup anchored by a French-Spanish contingent — Jerome Tcherneyan, Miguel Marin, Alasdair Steer.

First ever concert: The Wag Club, Soho. Final concert: The Lexington, Angel, Islington, 3 December 2016 — exactly 20 years later to the day. The closing song was “The Last Engineer,” which Johnson dedicated to his recently deceased father.

Label “i” / i/Che Records and early period (1996–1998)

Three releases on i/Che Records (catalog prefix IRE):

  • Wrong French (12”, 1996, ire1071) — debut single
  • Popular Mechanics (1997, ire——) — produced by Johnson, Paul Tornbohm prominent
  • Wintersport / Cross-Country (1997, ire——)

Also: For Engineers EP (1997, Wurlitzer Jukebox). A Trick Of The Sea (Bliss Out Vol. 13) (1998, Darla Records, US) — first US exposure.

Aesthetic in this period: lo-fi, abstract, minimal electronics, sampler-heavy. Multiple guest vocalists — the “This Mortal Coil approach” of using different singers per track.

Rocket Girl period (1999–2000)

Signed to Rocket Girl. Two albums:

Low Birth Weight (1999, Rocket Girl, rgirl 6)

First fully realized album. Producer: Piano Magic. A fragmented collective record — different contributors per track, all engineered by members. The album is explicitly modelled on Disco Inferno’s sampling approach: Johnson has described Disco Inferno as his “biggest influence” when he started Piano Magic, and the album’s sonic methodology — unconventional objects, found sounds, “narrative sampling” — directly mirrors what he took from them. The cover of “Waking Up” (Disco Inferno, Science EP, 1991) was a deliberate tribute: Johnson had sent his original demo tape to i/Che Records specifically because Disco Inferno were on the label, and this album is the direct descendant of that influence. Low Birth Weight is the most Disco Inferno-shaped record Piano Magic made.

Artists’ Rifles (2000, Rocket Girl, rgirl 19)

Produced and engineered by John A. Rivers at Woodbine Street Studios. The album was warmly received in Spain, covered extensively there and opening up the European touring circuit. Core lineup crystallizes: Johnson + John Cheves + Miguel Marin + Paul Tornbohm. Adrienne Quartly plays cello on four tracks.

4AD period (2001–2002)

Son De Mar (2001, 4AD, MAD 2105 CD)

Film score commissioned for the Bigas Luna film Son De Mar. Six untitled tracks. Personnel: Glen Johnson, Jerome Tcherneyan, Miguel Marin, Angèle David-Guillou (voice), Charles Wyatt (guitar), Matt Simpson (keyboards/programming), James Topham (viola). Recorded at home and at Woodbine Street Studios (John A. Rivers). Art direction by v23 (Vaughan Oliver, 4AD’s longtime design house).

Writers Without Homes (2002, 4AD, CAD 2209 CD)

The most critically noted Piano Magic record. Vaughan Oliver / v23 art direction. Core band: Johnson + Tcherneyan + Marin + Steer. Simon Raymonde (Cocteau Twins) plays piano on three tracks. Notable guest vocalists:

The international touring band (2003–2007)

By 2003 Piano Magic were a consistent five-piece with a French-Spanish core: Johnson, Tcherneyan, Marin, Steer, Franck Alba, with Angèle David-Guillou and Cedric Pin also prominent. The Troubled Sleep Of Piano Magic (2003, Green UFOs, Spain) was a transitional compilation/rarities release — one critic noted it as the first Piano Magic record where “musicianship trumps intention.”

Disaffected (2005, Darla Records, US)

US release. John Grant contributes (guest credit).

Part Monster (2007, Important Records (2), IMPREC151)

Produced by Guy Fixsen (Stereolab associate). Core five: Johnson, Tcherneyan, Alba, Steer, Pin. Guy Fixsen plays double bass. Terry Edwards plays trumpet. Angèle David-Guillou and Simon Rivers also feature as guest performers. Mastered by Mandy Parnell.

  • Sleep At The Bottom / Green Acres — split with Low and Transient Waves
  • Music For Rolex / The Soldering Social (1998, Lissy’s Records) — split/collab with Matmos

Ovations (2009, Make Mine Music, MMM060)

Produced by Gareth Parton. Core band now: Johnson, Tcherneyan, Alba, Steer, David-Guillou. Special guests:

  • Brendan Perry (Dead Can Dance) — vocals on tracks 1 and 8
  • Peter Ulrich (Dead Can Dance) — hammered dulcimer, darabuka/claves
  • Ruth Elder — violin, viola, strings
  • Kwesi Edman — strings, cello

Life Has Not Finished With Me Yet (2012, Second Language, SL017)

Produced by Johnson and Jerome Tcherneyan. Densely orchestrated. Choir credited: Johnson, David-Guillou, Alba, Oliver Cherer, Simone Clark, Alex Apodaca. Extended string/wind section: Katie English (Isnaj Dui) on flute, cello; Jenny Brand on clarinet; Jennifer Barron on viol; Simone Clark on violin; Áine O’Dwyer on harp. Mastered by Rudi Arapahoe.

Closure (2016, Second Language, SL036LP)

Final studio album. Vinyl LP. Core: Johnson, Tcherneyan, Alba, Steer. Oliver Cherer on trumpet. Audrey Riley on cello. Paul Tornbohm on keyboards. Josh Hight on backing vocals. Peter Milton Walsh (The Apartments) — vocals and words on “Attention To Life” (B3). Jim Moginie (Midnight Oil) recorded sessions. Announced as their final album. Mastered by Noel Summerville.
