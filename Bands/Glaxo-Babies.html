Glaxo Babies

Post-punk band. Bristol, UK. Discogs ID: 95809. Formed late 1977.

Glaxo Babies were one of the central acts in the late-1970s Bristol post-punk scene, sharing that milieu with The Pop Group and feeding directly into Maximum Joy. They recorded prolifically between 1979 and 1980 — two LPs, two 12”/EP releases, two 7” singles, and a key track on the city’s defining compilation — then dissolved. Three of their core members (Tony Wrafter, Charlie Llewellin, Dan Catsis) went on to co-found Maximum Joy. A second phase ran 1985–1990 with unreleased studio material eventually collected on the 2007 Cherry Red retrospective; a brief 2015 reunion (the Un-Peeled event) featured Steve Street on bass and Tony Wrafter’s son Oscar on vocals.

History

Formation (1977–1979)

The band formed in Bristol in late 1977. The founding nucleus included vocalist Rob Chapman, guitarist/bassist Dan Catsis, and saxophonist/trumpeter Tony Wrafter, with various drum configurations in the earliest period. Tim Aylett contributed as producer and musician on several early sessions. Their first documented studio output was the “Christine Keeler” 7” single in 1979, which featured an unusual lineup — Charlie Llewellin on drums (credited as “Charles Llewelyn”), Slow Death (3) on electric bass, Melancholy Baby on guitar and guitar synth, and Tony Wrafter on tenor sax. The production credited the band and Tim Aylett jointly, with David Lord engineering and Kevin Metcalfe cutting lacquers at The Sound House.

The band appeared on the Heartbeat Records compilation “Avon Calling (The Bristol Compilation)” (1979, HB1), contributing “It’s Irrational”. Alongside them were Europeans, Private Dicks, Moskow, Essential Bop, Vice Squad, and others — a snapshot of the Bristol scene as Heartbeat Records understood it. John Sparrow produced the Glaxo Babies track; the rest of the compilation was engineered by David Lord and Glenn Tommey.

Phase One: Studio Recordings (1979–1980)

The 12” EP “This Is Your Life” (1979, Heartbeat PULSE THREE / 12 PULSE 3) marked a more settled lineup: Tom Nichols on bass and vocals, Geoff Alsopp on drums, Dan Catsis on guitar and vocals, and Rob Chapman on vocals. Co-produced with Simon Edwards (4) — co-founder of Heartbeat Records. Four tracks: “This Is Your Life”, “Stay Awake”, “Because Of You”, “Who Killed Bruce Lee?“.

The “Limited Entertainment” 7” EP (1980, Y Records Y6) was their only release outside Heartbeat, landing on Disc O’Dell’s Y Records — the same label as The Pop Group and later Maximum Joy. Four tracks: “Limited Entertainment”, “Dahij”, “There’ll Be No Room For You In The Shelter”, “Permission To Be Wrong”. The credited musicians were Alan Jones, Charlie Llewellin, Dan Catsis, Tim Aylett, and Tom Nichols; John Etchells co-produced. Mastered by Porky (George Peckham).

Their debut LP, “Nine Months To The Disco” (1980, Cherry Red/Heartbeat HB2), was the fullest statement of the band’s first phase — 12 tracks of post-punk with jazz and funk undertow. Engineers: David Lord. Written-by credits go to Llewellin, Catsis, Aylett, and Wrafter; artwork by Danica Gacesa. Released on both Cherry Red and Heartbeat imprints simultaneously.

The “Shake!” 7” (1980, Heartbeat PULSE 8) appeared alongside the LP: A-side “Shake (The Foundations)” (band written-by), B-side “She Went To Pieces (Live 28-3-79)” (Rob Chapman written-by). Kevin Metcalfe cut the lacquers.

The live LP “Put Me On The Guest List” (1980, Heartbeat HBM 3) is the most fully credited document of the Phase One lineup. Tom Nichols on bass and vocals, Dan Catsis on guitar and vocals, Tony Wrafter on saxophone, Rob Chapman on vocals and all written-by. Two drum configurations: Geoff Alsopp on tracks A1–B4, Charlie Llewellin on B5. Engineers split accordingly: Steve Street on A1–B4, David Lord on B5. Ten tracks including “Avoiding The Issue”, “Police State”, “This Is Your Life” and “Who Killed Bruce Lee (Version)“.

The band dissolved following these recordings in 1980.

Post-Breakup Trajectories (1980–1985)

Three core members departed to form Maximum Joy in 1979–1980, taking the rhythmic and jazz-inflected energy of the Babies into a more overtly funk and dub-influenced direction:

Dan Catsis’s move to The Pop Group was temporally overlapping — he was already contributing to their second album while Glaxo Babies were still active.

Phase Two (1985–1990)

The band reformed in 1985 with a core of Rob Chapman (vocals), Dan Catsis (guitar, bass), Charlie Llewellin (drums, drum programming, engineering), and Tony Wrafter (saxophone). Mark Anderson (3) added keyboards on selected tracks. This phase produced no standalone releases during its lifetime; recordings were compiled posthumously on Cherry Red’s 2007 retrospective.

Retrospectives and Archiving

“Dreams Interrupted (The Bewilderbeat Years 1978–1980)” (2006, Cherry Red CDMRED291): 20-track CD collecting Phase One material — the “This Is Your Life” EP, the two LPs, the singles, and unreleased tracks “It’s Irrational”, “Time Out: Lost Causes”, “Tell Me”, and an untitled piece. Liner notes by Gaspari Walter and Rob Chapman. Artwork by Andy Jenks, Dan Catsis, and Helen White.

“The Porlock Factor (Psych Dreams And Other Schemes 1985–1990)” (2007, Cherry Red CDMRED322): 18-track CD of the Phase Two recordings. The style shifted — Discogs categorises it as Indie Rock rather than Post-Punk. Lyrics all by Rob Chapman; music by Catsis, Llewellin, and Chapman throughout.

Both releases were reissued again in the early 2020s under the Lantern Rec. / Right Honourable Records imprints (2021).

“In The Beginning” (1980, Trio Records AW-25004, Japan): Compilation LP of Phase One material released in Japan. The sole Trio Records appearance.

2015 Reunion

A brief live reunion for the Un-Peeled 2015 event. Lineup included Steve Street on bass (also known from his engineering work on “Put Me On The Guest List” and as member of Europeans) and Tony Wrafter’s son Oscar on vocals.

Connections

  • Maximum Joy — co-founded by Wrafter, Llewellin, and Catsis immediately after the band’s first breakup; three of the seven Discogs-listed members crossed over
  • The Pop Group — Dan Catsis joined as bassist; both bands orbited the same Bristol post-punk scene, both released on Y Records
  • Heartbeat Records — the band’s primary home and the label that defined the Bristol scene (also released Europeans, Vice Squad, Art Objects, The Transmitters)
  • Y Records / Disc O’Dell — “Limited Entertainment” (1980) was their sole Y Records release, placing them alongside The Pop Group and The Slits
  • David Lord — key engineer throughout Phase One; concurrently working with Bearz and later producing Peter Gabriel’s Security (1982)
  • Steve Street — Phase One engineer; also in Europeans (2) and Affairs Of The Heart (Heartbeat); played bass at 2015 reunion
  • The Transmitters — Rob Chapman joined after the band’s first breakup
  • Nellee Hooper — not a direct collaborator, but Hooper’s trajectory through Maximum Joy (where he played percussion) links back through Llewellin and Wrafter to Glaxo Babies
