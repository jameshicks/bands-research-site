Tuxedomoon

Discogs ID: 6268 Origin: San Francisco, CA, USA Active: 1977–present (intermittently; years active on Discogs: 1978–2025) Genres: Electronic, Rock Styles: Post-Punk, New Wave, Experimental, Avantgarde, Synth-pop, Future Jazz Aliases / name variations: Tuxedemoon, Tuxedo Men, Tuxedo Moon, WInston Tong w/ Tuxedomoon Early alter-ego projects: Creatures Of The Night, Joeboy

Biography

Tuxedomoon is an avant-garde, electronics-driven collective formed in San Francisco in 1977 by two electronic music students at San Francisco City College: Blaine L. Reininger (keyboards, violin) and Steven Brown (keyboards, saxophone, and various instruments). Their early local theater connections supplied equipment and occasional vocalists in Gregory Cruikshank and Victoria Lowe, alongside the more sustained contributions of performer and singer Winston Tong.

The San Francisco punk and new wave scene was opening as they formed — they landed an opening slot for Devo in 1978, around the same time they cut their first single, “Pinheads on the Move,” on the self-funded Tidal Wave Records. The debut 12” EP No Tears (1978, self-released) established their synthesizer-and-violin-over-punk-drums template. Victoria Lowe departed before No Tears; off-and-on members Michael Belfer (guitar) and Paul Zahl (drums) appeared on it. After Tong and Belfer departed temporarily, Peter Principle (b. Peter Dachert) joined as a full-time member.

Tuxedomoon signed to the Residents’ Ralph Records in 1979, gaining European exposure. Half-Mute (1980, Ralph Records / Celluloid) was their first LP and remains a landmark of cold wave and post-punk electronics: the core trio of Reininger, Brown, and Principle self-produced it, playing all instruments. They toured Europe after the record; Tong returned, as did filmmaker and visual artist Bruce Geduldig who handled lights and film/mise-en-scène for live shows.

Desire (1981, Expanded Music / PRE) was recorded in Italy and produced with engineer Gareth Jones. It expanded their palette towards jazz and experimental territory, with string arrangements by Reininger and cello from Ali Robinson and violin from Vicky Aspinall. After the Desire tour the band relocated to Rotterdam, Netherlands, in an artists’ commune, then were forced to leave and moved to Brussels where they found early refuge at the Plan K venue and scene.

In Brussels, Tuxedomoon was commissioned by choreographer Maurice Béjart to score a ballet; the results were released as Divine (1982, Philips) — a major divergence into modern classical / chamber territory. The record was produced by the Tuxedomoon alter-ego project Joeboy. Reininger left for a solo career in 1983 and was replaced by Frankie Lievaart and horn player Luc Van Lieshout. Short film-score and transitional releases appeared on Les Disques Du Crépuscule around this period.

Holy Wars (1985, Cramboy / Normal) became the band’s biggest commercial success in Europe. Tong departed permanently after this record, leaving Brown and Principle the sole original San Francisco-era members. Multi-instrumentalist Ivan Georgiev was hired to augment the group for Ship Of Fools (1986, Cramboy). After a period of live activity and compilations on Cramboy through the late 1980s, the group recorded The Ghost Sonata (1991, LTM) and went quiet through much of the 1990s.

The 2000s brought renewed activity: the LTM (UK) reissue label repackaged early material (Soundtracks / Urban Leisure, 2002), remixes appeared on International Deejay Gigolo Records around 2000–01, and Cabin In The Sky (2004, CramBoy / Crammed Discs) signalled a full return. Later releases include Vapour Trails (2007), Pink Narcissus (2014), Blue Velvet Revisited (2015), and The Vinyl Box (2015, Crammed Discs). The band’s primary recurring label home from the 1980s onward is Cramboy (a sublabel of Crammed Discs), with key early material on Ralph Records and single releases on Celluloid, Expanded Music, and Les Disques Du Crépuscule.

Reininger, Brown, Principle, and Lievaart have continued to record as Tuxedomoon into the 2020s; Reininger, Brown, Principle, and Tong have all maintained parallel solo careers.

Greg Langston (ID 41208) is not Greg Langston the Tuxedomoon member. Discogs ID 41208 maps to a drummer active in San Francisco punk/hardcore (credits include drums on Backtracks, Subterranean Records 1980; drums on Not So Quiet On The Western Front, Alternative Tentacles 1982; drums on Sea Hags demo 1986; drums on various US hardcore releases 1989–1993). This appears to be a separate person with the same name who crossed the same SF scene, not the Tuxedomoon Greg Langston. Tuxedomoon’s Greg Langston’s actual Discogs credits could not be isolated — his contribution may be uncredited in the DB or he may have a different ID. Cannot resolve from DB alone. Gregory Cruikshank (ID 1590053) has exactly two credits in the DB, both as “Voice”: 77o7 Tm (The 30th Anniversary Box) (Cramboy, 2007, Europe) and Unearthed (Lost Cords + Found Films) (Cramboy, 2011, Belgium). Both are Tuxedomoon archival/compilation releases. No other credits outside Tuxedomoon exist in the DB. His role is solely “Voice” — consistent with the early-vocalist/theater-performer characterization. He does not appear as a primary artist on any release. Relationship to theater collective cannot be confirmed from DB alone; requires web research. Yes. His full credits span well beyond Tuxedomoon: drums on On Our Way Back Home (Brainchild Records, 1977, pre-Tuxedomoon); No Regrets (Mutiny Shadow International, 1981); Tell Me No Lies (Lolita, France, 1984); Don’t Slander Me (Pink Dust, 1986); One Night Stand (AIM, Australia, 1986); El Paso - Bay Bridge (Normal, Germany, 1988); Step Up (Aim, Australia, 1991 — listed as Flamin’ Groovies band member); Live At The Festival Of The Sun Barcelona Spain (AIM, Australia, 1994); San Francisco: Mostly Alive (Rave Up, 2009); Always Comes Back — The Authorized Recordings (Liberation Hall, 2020); Wounds And Blessings (Les Disques Du Crépuscule, 2021, as guest). He also has producer/mixer credits on Ruffians (Victory Records, 1985) and a co-producer/drums credit on La Grâce Du Tombeur (LTM, 1990). The Flamin’ Groovies affiliation (confirmed by the 1991 AIM releases) is the main non-Tuxedomoon throughline. Confirmed. Gareth Jones (ID 2719) is definitively the same person. His DB credits span from 1980 (engineer on John Foxx’s Metamatic and associated Metal Beat/Virgin singles) through Tuxedomoon’s Desire (1981, engineer/co-producer) and then deep into the Depeche Mode catalog: engineer/Tonmeister on Construction Time Again (1983), Some Great Reward (1984, produced by), Black Celebration (1986, produced by), and all subsequent 1980s–1990s DM albums and singles. He also produced Einstürzende Neubauten (½ Mensch, Some Bizzare, 1985; Yü-Gung, 1985), Wire (The Ideal Copy, Mute, 1987; A Bell Is A Cup Until It Is Struck, 1988), Diamanda Galás, Erasure, Nick Cave (The Good Son, mixed), and many others. His Tuxedomoon credit (Desire, 1981) predates his DM work and is one of his earliest continental European credits. He has released solo electronic music under his own name as recently as 2025 (self-released).
