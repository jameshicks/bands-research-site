Poison Girls

British anarcho-punk band formed in Brighton, 1976/1977. Discogs artist ID: 261846. Active through the mid-1990s; Discogs credits extend to 2023.

Biography

Origins (Brighton, 1975–1978)

Poison Girls coalesced from a collective of Brighton musicians around Vi Subversa (born Frances Sokolov Sansom, 20 June 1935). Vi’s first public performance came on her 40th birthday in 1975 as part of a collective presenting The Body Show at Sussex University — an early indication of the band’s roots in performance art and radical politics as much as conventional rock. The fluid group of players solidified into Poison Girls by 1977.

Vi Subversa was already well into middle age — a mother of two — when the band took shape. Her daughter Gem Stone (born Gemma Sansom, 1967) and son Pete Fender (born Daniel Sansom, 1964) both subsequently became musicians, joining Fatal Microbes and then Rubella Ballet, orbiting the same anarcho-punk network as their mother. Vi’s lyrics were explicitly from a radical feminist perspective, which set Poison Girls somewhat apart from the mostly male anarcho-punk milieu of the period.

The band’s name variations include “The Poison Girls,” “PG’s,” and “Poisongirls” (frequently used on sleeve credits on their own releases).

First Recordings and Small Wonder (1979)

Poison Girls entered the scene in 1979 via Small Wonder Records, releasing two records: the split 7” Closed Shop / Violence Grows (Weeny 3) shared with Fatal Microbes, and the debut album Hex (WEENY 4). These records were also issued on the Xntrix imprint.

The Closed Shop / Violence Grows split featured the Poison Girls side (A1, A2) with Vi Subversa (as “Vi Squad”) on vocals/guitar, Richard Famous on guitar/vocals, Lance D’Boyle on drums, and Bernhardt Rebours on bass. The Fatal Microbes side (B1, B2) featured Vi’s own children — Pete Fender on guitar/vocals and Gem Stone on drums — alongside Scott Barker on bass/vocals and Honey Bane on lead vocals. The record was produced by Adrian Grey-Turner and Poison Girls (A side) and Richard Famous (B side).

Hex (1979) featured the core early lineup: Vi Subversa (vocals/guitar), Richard Famous (guitar/vocals), Bernhardt Rebours (bass), Lance D’Boyle (drums). Produced by Penny Rimbaud of Crass and engineered by John Loder — the beginning of Poison Girls’ close relationship with the Crass collective.

Crass Records Era (1980)

In 1980 Poison Girls released three records on Crass Records:

Bloody Revolutions / Persons Unknown (cat no 421984/1, 1980) — a split single with Crass, one of the defining artifacts of British anarcho-punk. Crass contribute “Bloody Revolutions,” Poison Girls contribute “Persons Unknown” (a song in solidarity with the Persons Unknown anarchist trial defendants). Artwork by Gee Vaucher and both bands; engineered and recorded by John Loder.

Chappaquiddick Bridge (cat no 421984/2, 1980) — the band’s debut full album on Crass Records. Lineup: Vi Subversa and Richard Famous on vocals/guitar, Bernhardt Rebours on bass/synthesizer/piano, Lance D’Boyle on drums/percussion, Nil (6) on electric violin. Produced by Penny Rimbaud; engineered by John Loder; design by Gee Vaucher, Penny Rimbaud, and the band; cover photo by Gee Vaucher. Additional vocals by Gem Stone on track B5.

Hex (cat no 421984/9) — Crass Records reissue of the 1979 album.

Xntrix Label and Later LPs (1981–1985)

From 1981 onwards the band released primarily on their own Xntrix label (parent: Xntrix Records, distributed through Rough Trade).

Total Exposure (Live) (XN2003, 1981) — a live album with the same core lineup (Vi Subversa, Richard Famous, Bernhardt Rebours, Lance D’Boyle) plus Nil (6) handling tapes and lights. Co-produced by Poison Girls and Paul Tandy.

Where’s The Pleasure (XN2006, 1982) — credited on sleeve as “Poisongirls.” The lineup shifts: new members Pete Fender (bass guitar) and Chris Grace (fretless bass/backing vocals) appear alongside Richard Famous, Lance D’Boyle, and Vi Subversa. Adrian Grey-Turner plays piano/guitar/backing vocals; the record expands to include saxophone (Dave Bitelli, John Scott), trombone (John Bennett), trumpet and piano (Barney Unwin), and a large ensemble of additional voices. Produced by Simaen Skolfield and Stuart James, with the band co-producing.

7 Year Scratch (77284) (RM 101, 1984) — a 4-LP retrospective compilation documenting 1977–1984. Studio sides A and B cover the early years; live sides C and D document later periods. Features contributions from the full range of bassists who passed through the band: Bella Donna, Scott Barker, Bernhardt Rebours, Pete Fender, Mark Dunn, Sian Daniels on synthesizer/vocals.

Are You Happy Now (Illuminated Records, 1983) — released on Illuminated Records rather than Xntrix. Lineup: Vi Subversa, Richard Famous, Mark Dunn (bass/backing vocals), Lance D’Boyle (drums/backing vocals), Cynth Ethics (keyboards/backing vocals). Produced and arranged by Bernie Clarke; remixed by Bernie Clarke and John Brand.

I’m Not A Real Woman (XN2010, 1984) — 12” single, four tracks. Vi Subversa as “Subversa” on songwriter credit. Produced by Bernie Clarke. Artwork by Domino (46).

Songs Of Praise (XN 2008, 1985) — the final Xntrix studio album. The settled lineup: Vi Subversa (vocals/guitar), Richard Famous (guitar/vocals), Lance D’Boyle (drums/percussion/vocals), Martin Heath (bass/harmonica/steel guitar), Cynth Ethics (keyboards/vocals). Lyrics by Vi Subversa. Produced by Bernie Clarke. Illustration and design by Clifford Harper.

Vi Subversa Solo and Vindaloo (1986)

In 1986 Vi Subversa appeared as lead vocalist on a solo release, Keep Lying, I Love It, on Vindaloo Records.

Lenya Hobnoobs Theatre Company (1987–1992)

Several Poison Girls members — Vi Subversa, Richard Famous, Max Vol, and others — participated in the Lenya Hobnoobs Theatre Company, a radical gay theatre co-operative founded in London in 1987 by David Bunnett. The company staged AIDS the Musical, Desert Storm, and Mother Russia, with recordings distributed on cassette on the Xntrix label. Other participants included Gem Stone, Dan Sansom, and Michael Coates.

Cooking Vinyl Retrospectives (1995–1997)

In 1995 Cooking Vinyl issued two major retrospective collections:

Real Woman (1995) — compilation. Includes some tracks produced by Penny Rimbaud (tracks 2, 3, 12).

Statement (1995) — a comprehensive 4-disc boxed set collecting recordings across the full span of the band’s career, with period-accurate production credits (Penny Rimbaud on the early Crass-era material; Simaen Skolfield on Where’s The Pleasure; Bernie Clarke on the later Xntrix period; Richard Famous on the Songs Of Praise-era tracks). Remastered by Donal Whelan.

Their Finest Moments (1997) — further compilation, released on Nectar Masters. Reissued in 1998 on Reactive Records.

Late Work and Legacy (2003–2015)

Poison Girls were included in the 2003 Angry Songs And Bitter Words compilation on Ruptured Ambitions.

Persons Unknown (All The Madmen Records, MAD35, 2015) — a late-career 12” single, released shortly before Vi Subversa’s death. Side A: “Persons Unknown” (the 1980 original), featuring Vi Subversa, Richard Famous, Bernhardt Rebours, Lance D’Boyle, produced by Penny Rimbaud, engineered by John Loder. Side B: “Statement (Orchestral Version),” featuring an orchestra (National Youth Orchestra of Great Britain) conducted by Jason Osborn, with Richard Famous on guitar/bass, Vi Subversa on vocals/guitar, Lance D’Boyle on drums, Gem Stone on backing vocals. Score arranged by Barney Unwin.

Vi Subversa died on 19 February 2016.
