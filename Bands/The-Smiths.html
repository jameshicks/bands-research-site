The Smiths

The Smiths

Oct 02, 20267 min read

The Smiths

Discogs ID: 83080
Origin: Manchester, England
Genres: Rock — Indie Rock, Jangle Pop, Alternative Rock
Active: 1982–1987
Labels: Rough Trade (UK), Sire (US)

English rock band formed in Manchester in 1982. Widely considered one of the most significant bands to emerge from the British independent music scene. The writing partnership of vocalist Morrissey and guitarist Johnny Marr produced four studio albums in five years before Marr’s departure ended the band in 1987. They have declined all reunion overtures since.

History

Formation (1982)

Morrissey (born Steven Patrick Morrissey, May 22, 1959, Manchester) and Johnny Marr (born John Martin Maher, October 31, 1963, Ardwick, Manchester) met in 1982. Morrissey had been writing for music papers and had briefly fronted a punk band with Billy Duffy (later of The Cult); Marr was already a committed guitarist with deep roots in Motown, soul, and rock. They agreed immediately to form a songwriting partnership. Drummer Mike Joyce and bassist Andy Rourke — both from Manchester — completed the initial lineup. Dale Hibbert played bass on the very first demos before Rourke took the slot. James Maker, a dancer and friend of Morrissey’s, performed briefly with the band at early gigs. Drummer Simon Wolstencroft also played a handful of early shows before Joyce joined.

Debut and Breakthrough (1983–1984)

“Hand In Glove” (May 1983, Rough Trade RT 131) was the debut single, released on the same day that Rough Trade signed the band. “This Charming Man” (November 1983) was the commercial breakthrough — Marr’s intricate guitar figure over Rourke’s melodic bass; it became their first UK top-40 hit. The debut album The Smiths (February 1984) had a complicated genesis: a first version was recorded with producer Troy Tate in 1983 but was scrapped; John Porter produced the released version. Paul Carrack played piano and organ on three tracks. The cover starred Joe Dallesandro in a still from a Warhol film.

Hatful of Hollow (November 1984) collected Peel and Jensen BBC sessions alongside non-LP singles and B-sides, presenting the band’s early period with a rawer energy than the studio album.

Meat Is Murder and Rising Stature (1985)

Meat Is Murder (February 1985) was self-produced, with Stephen Street engineering — his first Smiths credit. The band commanded their own production for the first time; the eight-minute “Barbarism Begins At Home” was a funk-influenced departure. Craig Gannon briefly joined as a second guitarist during the 1985 UK tour.

The Queen Is Dead (1986)

The Queen Is Dead (June 1986) is generally considered the band’s high-water mark. Co-produced by Morrissey and Marr, engineered by Stephen Street. Marr arranged strings for several tracks under the alias The Hated Salford Ensemble; Morrissey sang a falsetto backing vocal on “Bigmouth Strikes Again” credited pseudonymously as “Ann Coates.” The cover starred Alain Delon in a still from the 1964 film L’Insoumis. “There Is A Light That Never Goes Out” and “Cemetry Gates” are among the most beloved tracks in the catalog.

Craig Gannon rejoined in 1986 — initially as bassist when Andy Rourke was briefly fired (over heroin use), then as rhythm guitarist when Rourke returned. Kirsty MacColl sang backing vocals on “Ask” (September 1986 single). The World Won’t Listen and Louder Than Bombs (both 1987) were UK and US compilations respectively, gathering non-album singles and B-sides.

Strangeways, Here We Come and Dissolution (1987)

Strangeways, Here We Come was recorded in spring 1987 and co-produced by Morrissey, Marr, and Stephen Street — Street’s first full production credit on a Smiths LP. Orchestrazia Ardwick (another Marr alias) provided orchestration; Morrissey played piano on “Death Of A Disco Dancer.” The album was completed but not yet released when Johnny Marr left on July 30, 1987, citing exhaustion and a desire to broaden his music. The Smiths dissolved in the wake of his departure. Ivor Perry briefly joined as guitarist in the final weeks, and Gannon had been let go earlier that year.

Strangeways was released posthumously in September 1987. It remains the band’s final studio statement.

Aftermath

Morrissey launched a solo career in 1988, initially with Stephen Street producing (Viva Hate, HMV). Johnny Marr co-founded Electronic with Bernard Sumner (New Order), then worked with The The, Modest Mouse, The Pretenders, The Cribs, and eventually released solo records. Stephen Street went on to produce Blur’s run of albums from Leisure (1991) through The Great Escape (1995) and Blur (1997), as well as The Cranberries’ debut. In 1996, Mike Joyce won a court case against Morrissey and Marr for unpaid royalties. Andy Rourke died from pancreatic cancer on May 19, 2023, in New York City.
