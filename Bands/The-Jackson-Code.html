The Jackson Code

Australian alternative/acoustic rock group, active 1989–1995. Discogs artist ID: 1251829. Name variation: Jackson Code.

Based around songwriter and vocalist/guitarist Mark Snarski, with a rotating cast that also served the Sydney–Melbourne underground. Their four albums span from acoustic country-inflected folk-rock (Del Musical Del Mismo Nombre, 1989) through a more polished alternative rock sound (The Things You Need, 1995, WEA/RA Records). Self-produced throughout.

Biography

Formation and Debut (1989)

The Jackson Code emerged from the same Sydney alternative milieu that produced Chad’s Tree and Wet Taxis. The core creative engine was Mark Snarski, who wrote all original material across the band’s output. Several members — including Snarski, Jason Kain, Kathy Wemyss, and Kenny Davis Jnr. — were also members of Chad’s Tree, making the two groups effectively overlapping ensembles.

The debut album Del Musical Del Mismo Nombre (1989, Waterfront Records, DAMP 115) was released on vinyl — the band’s only LP format release. The title is Spanish, roughly meaning “From the Musical of the Same Name,” signalling a theatrical or conceptual tilt. The band produced and arranged it themselves, with Kathy Wemyss handling brass arrangements. The album’s twelve tracks are heavily acoustic and eclectic, drawing on country, folk, and cabaret textures. “Have Yourself a Merry Little Christmas” (B6) — written by Hugh Martin and Ralph Blane — is the sole non-Snarski composition, performed with guest vocals from Mandy Pearson and piano by Wemyss. Guest appearances include Richard Armstrong (3) on harmonica (A3, B2) and Bevan Reed on tenor saxophone and Glad Reed on trombone (B1 “Strange Lullaby”). Barry Turnbull plays electric bass on B3 and B4.

Strange Cargo (1992)

The second album Strange Cargo (1992, Citadel (7), citcd528) was released on CD — a format shift reflecting the era. Discogs carries no individual personnel credits for this release beyond the primary credit to The Jackson Code itself. The twelve tracks include “Paris Bay,” “Sophie’s Torch Song,” “Angel Of Uncertain Times,” and “Lucifer’s Polka.” Styles tagged as Acoustic, Alternative Rock, and Indie Rock — a broadening of the debut’s palette.

Draggin’ The River (1993)

Draggin’ The River (1993, Ra Records, 4509938842) is the most fully documented of the later albums in Discogs terms. The band again produced and arranged itself, with Kathy Wemyss credited for scoring strings and brass arrangements. Tom Colley mixed; Guy Dickerson recorded; Don Bartley mastered. The cover was designed by Simon Alderson with assistance from Leonie Watson. Photography by Ian Macpherson (9) and Bleddyn Butcher (band photograph). Twelve tracks; one co-written with Paul Keating (2) — “The Shiver Waiting To Crawl Up My Spine” (track 2). Ra Records was a subsidiary of rooArt Pty. Ltd.

The Things You Need (1995) — Major Label

The final album The Things You Need (1995, RA Records / WEA, 0630111812) represents the group’s only major-label release. WEA co-issued it alongside RA Records, suggesting a distribution or licensing arrangement. Twelve tracks, running to 50+ minutes (including the 8:26 closer “Helpless”). Discogs carries no individual personnel credits for this release. The stylistic tags remain Acoustic / Alternative Rock / Indie Rock.
