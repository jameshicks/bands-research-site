Sum 41

Discogs ID: 115650 Active: 1996–2025 (disbanded March 2025) Origin: Ajax, Ontario, Canada Genres: Pop-Punk, Hard Rock, Punk Rock, Alternative Metal Labels: Island Records, Hopeless Records, Rise Records (3)

Canadian pop-punk band formed in Ajax, Ontario in 1996. Best known for the breakthrough album All Killer No Filler (2001), produced by Jerry Finn, which established them as one of the leading acts of early-2000s pop-punk. Over a seven-album career spanning nearly three decades, the band moved progressively heavier — from radio-ready punk-pop toward melodic hardcore and alternative metal — before disbanding in March 2025.

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1996–1999)

Sum 41 formed in Ajax, Ontario in 1996. The founding lineup centred on vocalist/guitarist Deryck Whibley (Discogs ID: 517029, aliases: Bizzy D., Biz (4), Gunner (3)) and Steve Jocz (drums, Discogs ID: 517034, aliases: Stevo 32, Pain (27)). Guitarist Dave Baksh (Discogs ID: 517031, alias: Brown Sound) and bassist Jason “Cone” McCaslin (Discogs ID: 704484, alias: Cone McCaslin) completed the core lineup. The band self-released a demo in 1997 on Funtrip Records and a further 1998 Demo on their own imprint, building a following on the Toronto punk circuit.

Half Hour Of Power and Major-Label Debut (2000)

Signed to Island Records’ Big Rig imprint, Sum 41 released the Half Hour Of Power EP in 2000. Produced by Whibley and Greig Nori (Nettwerk Management), the record showcased the band’s fusion of punk aggression and hip-hop swagger — a track on it features a guest appearance from rapper MC Shan. Mixing duties were split across Blair Calibaba, Jerry Finn, L. Stu Young, and Matt Hyde; mastering was handled by Howie Weinberg and Roger Lian.

All Killer No Filler and Breakthrough (2001)

All Killer No Filler (Island/Mercury, 2001) remains the band’s signature album. Produced by Jerry Finn, it yielded the global hit “Fat Lip” and the pop-crossover single “In Too Deep.” The album was mixed by Tom Lord-Alge and mastered by Brian Gardner. It reached platinum certification in multiple countries and is widely regarded as a touchstone of early-2000s pop-punk alongside records by Blink-182 and Green Day. Deryck Whibley used the stage/writing alias “Bizzy D.” on songwriting credits throughout this period; Dave Baksh was credited as “Brown Sound.”

Does This Look Infected? (2002)

The follow-up, Does This Look Infected? (Mercury, 2002), retained producer Greig Nori and brought in Andy Wallace for most of the mixing, with Tom Lord-Alge handling “The Hell Song.” The album pushed further into hard rock territory. Its singles — “Still Waiting,” “The Hell Song,” and “Over My Head (Better Off Dead)” — maintained the band’s commercial profile in the UK and North America. UK editions included bonus tracks “Reign In Pain (Heavy Metal Jamboree)” and “WWVII Parts 1 & 2,” credited to the alias act Pain For Pleasure (Steve Jocz on vocals as “Pain”; Deryck Whibley on drums as “Gunner”), reflecting the band’s metal parody side project.

Chuck and the Heavier Turn (2004)

Chuck (Island Records, 2004) marked a decisive shift toward harder rock. Named in tribute to a United Nations soldier the band met during a visit to the Democratic Republic of Congo in 2003, the album was produced by Greig Nori and engineered by Matt Hyde. Mixing was again split between Andy Wallace and Tom Lord-Alge; mastering by Brian Gardner. The album’s styles span emo, hardcore, and punk, and the ballad “Pieces” received significant radio play. The Discogs entry lists the record under “Emo / Hardcore / Punk” — a notable departure from the pop-punk categorisation of the first two albums.

Dave Baksh Departure and Underclass Hero (2005–2007)

Dave Baksh left Sum 41 in 2006 to focus on his side project Brown Brigade. The band continued as a four-piece, releasing Underclass Hero (Island Records, 2007). The album was produced by the band and features a streamlined pop-punk sound. Credited musicians on the US release are slim — just Whibley (guitar, vocals), McCaslin (bass), and Jocz (drums). The album included string arrangements by David Campbell.

8 Years of Blood, Sake and Tears and Hiatus (2008–2010)

In 2008 Island Records issued the compilation 8 Years of Blood, Sake and Tears: The Best of Sum 41 2000–2008, primarily for the Japanese market. The band went on extended hiatus during 2008–2010, during which Deryck Whibley struggled with serious health problems related to alcohol dependency.

Screaming Bloody Murder and Lineup Changes (2011)

The band reconvened with an altered lineup for Screaming Bloody Murder (Island Records, 2011). Tom Thacker (Discogs ID: 1516631) joined on guitar, and the album is notably the last Sum 41 album to include Steve Jocz (drums). Produced and largely mixed by Deryck Whibley, the album was engineered by Ryan Hewitt and mastered by Ted Jensen. Tom Lord-Alge and Chris Lord-Alge both handled mixing on select tracks. The sound is the band’s most alternative rock-inflected effort.

13 Voices and Return (2016)

After Steve Jocz’s departure, Frank Zummo (Discogs ID: 2463792) joined on drums. Dave Baksh also returned. 13 Voices (Hopeless Records, 2016) was the first release on a new label and a deliberate return to heavier punk-rock, with production by Matt Squire. Ted Jensen again mastered. The album represented a commercial and creative reset as the band shifted away from Island after years of inactivity.

Order In Decline (2019)

Order In Decline (Hopeless Records, 2019) continued in the heavier direction, produced and mixed by Deryck Whibley and co-produced by Mike Green (3). Drum recording by Doug McKean; mastered by Ted Jensen. The lineup was Whibley, McCaslin, Baksh, Thacker, and Zummo — the most stable of the band’s later iterations. The closing track “Catching Fire” features gang vocals from a group including Cody Carson of Set It Off.

Heaven :x: Hell and Disbandment (2024–2025)

The band’s final album, Heaven :x: Hell (Rise Records, 2024), is a double CD split into “Heaven” (pop-punk side) and “Hell” (metal/hardcore side). Produced by Whibley and Mike Green (3), with mastering by Ted Jensen and Kris Crummett. On 6 January 2025 the band announced they would disband; they played their final concerts in March 2025, ending a career that spanned nearly three decades.
