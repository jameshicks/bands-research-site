Dislocation Dance

Discogs ID: 198408
Active: 1978–1986 (original run); 2000–present (reformed)
Origin: Manchester, UK
Genres: New Wave, Indie Pop, Cool Jazz, Post-Punk
Labels: New Hormones; Rough Trade; Ariola; Vinyl Japan; LTM Recordings; Emotional Rescue; Marina Records

Manchester new wave / cool jazz / mutant disco collective. Formed November 1978 when bassist Paul Emmerson met guitarist-singer Ian Runacres via a musicians’ wanted ad at the Virgin Records store on Newton Street. Signed to New Hormones (the Buzzcocks’ label) in 1980; moved to Rough Trade for the Midnight Shift LP (1983, UK Indie Chart No. 14). Dropped by Rough Trade c.1986–87 and dissolved. Influences spanned funk, soul, Krautrock, jazz (Coltrane, Bacharach), disco, and 1960s French pop — the NME called them “the real mutant disco.” Performed at Factory and recorded John Peel sessions.

The Midnight Shift sessions brought in a substantial roster of guest musicians — saxophonists, string players — giving the band an unusually orchestrated texture. A Dennis Bovell remix of “Show Me” and a cover of a Donna Summer/Giorgio Moroder song mark the breadth of reference. BBC sessions produced by Dale Griffin (Mott the Hoople) and Chris Lycett document the live band across multiple years.

Compiled and reissued extensively by LTM in the 2000s. Reformed for a Japan tour in 2000; subsequent studio albums Cromer (2005, Vinyl Japan), The Ruins of Manchester (2012, LTM), Are We There Yet? (2017, LTM), and Road To Happiness (2025, Marina Records — sixth studio album). The reformed band brought in additional members: Phil Lukes (bass/vocals), Jon Board (trumpet, from 2007), Andrew Weaver (keyboards, from 2007), Chris Gravestock (drums, from 2009), Sam Heywood (vocals, from 2017).

Connections

  • New Hormones (ORG label): The Buzzcocks’ own imprint housed DD alongside other post-punk/new wave acts; the ORG catalog number sequence places them in notable company.
  • Andy Diagram → James: Diagram joined the Manchester band James in 1989 during their commercial peak; rejoined in 2007 for the reunion. His trumpet style defines much of their 1990s sound.
  • Richard Harrison → Stereolab: Harrison drummed with Stereolab at some point (listed as member, Discogs ID 388); also collaborated with Graham Massey of 808 State and is a core member of Spaceheads.
  • Dennis Bovell: His remixing of “Show Me” places DD in the orbit of British post-punk’s dub/reggae crossover thread (Bovell also produced The Slits, Linton Kwesi Johnson, etc.).
  • Dale Griffin as BBC producer: Griffin (Buffin of Mott the Hoople) was a prolific BBC session producer for post-punk and new wave acts from 1978 onwards.
