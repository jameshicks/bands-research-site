Swervedriver

Discogs ID: 159997 Origin: Oxford, England Active: 1989–1998, 2008–present Genre: Shoegaze, alternative rock, noise rock, space rock

Oxford guitar band formed in 1989, anchored by the songwriting partnership of Adam Franklin and Jim Hartridge. Through their time on Creation Records (1990–1995) they released four albums and a string of singles that placed them alongside Ride and Slowdive in the British shoegaze surge — though Swervedriver’s sound leaned harder into American noise rock and Sonic Youth-adjacent distortion than the purely ethereal end of the genre. After a late-nineties spell on DGC and a period of inactivity, the band reunited in 2008 and has remained sporadically but steadily active, issuing two new studio albums on Dangerbird Records and archival material on Outer Battery Records.

Biography

Origins and Creation Records (1989–1993)

Swervedriver formed in Oxford in 1989, with Adam Franklin on vocals and guitar and Jim Hartridge on guitar as the core partnership. The original lineup included Adi Vines on bass and Graham Bonnar on drums. Their early material — including the self-produced “Rave Down” single (CRE 093, 1990), co-produced with Anjali Dutt — appeared on Creation Records, where they were quickly absorbed into the label’s shoegaze-adjacent roster.

The debut full-length, Raise (CRELP 093, 1990), was engineered by Martin Nichols and established their template: stacked guitar distortion, Franklin’s semi-mumbled melodic phrasing, and kinetic rhythm-section drive that recalled Dinosaur Jr. more than contemporaries like Slowdive. It was also released by A&M Records in the US as part of Creation’s distribution arrangement there.

Between Raise and their second album, the band issued several standalone Creation singles — “Son Of Mustang Ford” (1990), “Sandblasted” (1991, produced by the band and engineered by Philip Ames; mixed by Anjali Dutt), and “Never Lose That Feeling” (1992) — all of which were self-produced. By Mezcal Head, Adi Vines had departed and the lineup had shifted, with Jez Hindmarsh (credited as “Jez (2)”) taking over on drums.

Mezcal Head and peak Creation period (1993–1995)

Mezcal Head (CRELP 143, 1993) — co-produced by the band and Alan Moulder, engineered by Jez Hindmarsh, Nick Addison, and assistant Ronan Tal, mastered by Howie Weinberg — was the commercial and artistic high point of their Creation tenure. The album runs from compact noise-rock (“For Seeking Heat,” “Duel”) to sprawling motorik workouts (“Last Train To Satansville,” “Duress,” the latter co-written with Marc Waterman). “Duel” was issued as a UK single (CRE 143, 1993) and “Last Train To Satansville” received a separate US single on A&M.

Alan Moulder became their primary production collaborator from this point: on Mezcal Head he served as co-producer and engineer; on the “Duel” single and on all subsequent Creation-era work he was integral. The band self-produced their recordings with Moulder shaping the sonic results.

Steve George (2) — real name Steven George Totterdell — had joined on bass by this period and appears on all later Creation material.

Ejector Seat Reservation (CRECD157, 1995) — again co-produced by the band and Alan Moulder — expanded the sonic palette, adding string arrangements by cellist Audrey Riley on several tracks (“Single Finger Salute,” “Bring Me The Head Of The Fortune Teller,” “Last Day On Earth”), horn players, and a cover of a Bacharach/David song (“How Does It Feel To Look Like Candy?”). Jez Hindmarsh is credited on both drums and engineering. The “Last Day On Earth” single (CRE 193) was also released in 1995, co-produced with Moulder.

DGC era and break-up (1996–1998)

After their Creation contract ended, Swervedriver signed to DGC Records in the United States — part of a brief early-nineties major-label appetite for British indie bands. 99th Dream (DGC, 1997) was again co-produced by the band and Alan Moulder and represented a more streamlined, American-radio-adjacent approach. The drummer for this album was Danny Ingram (not the Jez or Graham Bonnar of earlier lineups). The album received limited promotion; DGC was in financial difficulty at the time (in the process of being absorbed by Interscope), and the album effectively disappeared. A brief Australian-only release (“Space Travel, Rock ‘n’ Roll,” Cortex, 1998) followed before the band dissolved.

A Sonic Wave Discs release (“Wrong Treats,” 1999) documented a post-breakup live or archival track under the band’s name.

Hiatus (1998–2008)

During the decade-long break, Adam Franklin pursued a solo and collaborative career: the project Adam Franklin & Bolts Of Melody released Bolts Of Melody (Hi-Speed Soul, 2007) and Spent Bullets (Hi-Speed Soul / Second Motion Records, 2009), among others. He also contributed to Sophia (3) and formed Magnetic Morning and Toshack Highway. Jim Hartridge and Adi Vines both appeared in Shake Appeal (4). Graham Bonnar went on to drum for the Brian Jonestown Massacre, Creeper Lagoon, and other acts.

A Castle Music retrospective, Juggernaut Rides ‘89–‘98 (2005), compiled the band’s Creation and DGC work and helped maintain their reputation during the hiatus.

Reunion (2008–present)

Swervedriver reunited in 2008 and returned to touring, initially without releasing new material. The reunion lineup solidified around Adam Franklin, Jim Hartridge, Steve George (2) on bass, and Mikey Jones on drums.

A series of recordings preceded the first reunion album proper: Planes Over The Skyline (Alti Philosophi, 2011) and Deep Wound (Tym Records (2), 2013) appeared, followed by the “Setting Sun” single (Cobraside, 2014).

I Wasn’t Born To Lose You (Cobraside / Dangerbird Records, CSDCD 1244, 2015) — the first proper studio album in eighteen years — featured the core Franklin/Hartridge/George/Jones lineup, with Adam Franklin credited on “Vocals, Guitar [Left Channel]” and Jim Hartridge on “Guitar [Right Channel].” The album was mixed and mastered through John Catlin and Tim Turan’s audio chain, which also handled subsequent releases.

Future Ruins was issued in 2019 across multiple territories: Rock Action Records in the UK/Europe (ROCKACT121LP/CD), Dangerbird Records in the US (DGB180-1), Vinyl Junkie Recordings in Japan (VJR-3214), and Poison City Records in Australia. A promo CDr had appeared on Rock Action in 2018 (ROCKACT121CDP). Discogs production credits are sparse on this record (only band self-credit for written-by), but the John Catlin/Tim Turan engineering chain is consistent with the I Wasn’t Born To Lose You sessions.

Doremi Faso Latido (Outer Battery Records, OBR 032, 2024) is a limited-edition LP of outtakes and demos from the 99th Dream sessions, presented with Czech-language alternative titles. Mixed by John Catlin, mastered by Tim Turan. Guest vocals from Beth Thompson (track B2) and Christine Marie (track B6). Design by Sohrab Habibion.

The World’s Fair EP followed in 2025 on Outer Battery Records (USA & Europe pressing).
