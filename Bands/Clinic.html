Clinic

Discogs artist ID: 42650 Origin: Liverpool, UK Active: 1997–present Genre: Indie rock, post-punk, psychedelic rock Label: Domino Records (1999–present); Aladdins Cave Of Golf (1997–1998)

Liverpool quartet formed in 1997 from the ashes of Pure Morning (2), known for performing live in surgical scrubs and masks to preserve anonymity. Self-produced throughout most of their career. One of the longest-running acts on Domino, with over 163 release entries in the label’s catalog.

Members

All four members maintained strict public anonymity for most of the band’s early career, with full names only surfacing gradually via Discogs credits and related group entries.

Biography

Origins: Pure Morning (1995–1997)

Before Clinic, all four members played in Pure Morning (2) (Discogs ID 291961) — a Liverpool group that appears to have been the direct predecessor. The transition to Clinic brought the surgical-uniform aesthetic and anonymity policy that defined the band’s public persona.

Early Releases: Aladdins Cave Of Golf (1997–1998)

Clinic’s debut single was “I.P.C. Subeditors Dictate Our Youth” (1997), released on the small Liverpool label Aladdins Cave Of Golf. The single made John Peel’s end-of-year Festive Fifty — an early sign of credibility in the UK indie underground. Recording was handled by Rob Ferrier (also credited on the 1998 singles), who became an early recurring engineer for the band.

Two further singles followed on the same label:

  • Cement Mixer (1998) — recorded by Rob Ferrier
  • Monkey On Your Back (1998) — recorded by Rob Ferrier

Domino Signing (1999)

In 1999, Clinic signed to Domino Records, appearing in early label catalogs alongside American lo-fi acts being introduced to UK audiences. Two releases established them as a going concern:

  • Clinic EP (WIGCD 64 / RUG64CD, 1999) — compilation of early material
  • The Second Line (1999) — first new Domino single; recorded by Gareth Jones

The Clinic EP made John Peel’s Festive Fifty again, cementing the band’s connection to that scene.

Internal Wrangler (2000)

Debut album Internal Wrangler (WIGLP 78 / WIGCD 78, 2000) was co-produced by the band and Gareth Jones, with a team of engineers including Andy Baker, Carl Brown, Lem Latimer, Luke Gifford, and assistant engineers Roger Johnson and Tim Bruzon. The album’s track “Second Line” was later used in a Levi’s jeans TV advertisement. Management was credited to Out Management.

The album was also released in the US as DOMUS01CD / DNO01CD.

Walking With Thee (2001–2002)

Second album Walking With Thee (WIGLP 100 / WIGCD 100, 2001; DOMUS 05 for US CD) was released with minimal external credits — the Discogs primary pressing shows no additional credited personnel aside from the band itself. The album contains “Walking With Thee” (featured on The David Letterman Show in the US) and “Come Into Our Room” (later licensed to CSI and The O.C.). A single version of “Come Into Our Room” was also released (2002, Domino).

Winchester Cathedral (2004)

Third album Winchester Cathedral (WIGLP 144 / WIGCD 144, 2004; DNO35CD/LP for US) brought in outside production for the first time: co-produced with Ken Thomas (veteran British producer known for Sigur Rós, 23 Skidoo, Cocteau Twins). Three people credited as “Assisted By” in production: Andrea Wright (2) (Discogs ID 351549), Erasmus Benedict (ID 407590), and Matt Edge (ID 407589). Photography by Jason Evans (2) (inner) and “Elizabeth” (front cover).

Visitations (2006)

Fourth album Visitations (WIGLP 181 / WIGCD 181, 2006) was self-produced and recorded. Gareth Jones returned as mixer, his most prominent credit since the debut. Also that year, the Harvest EP (2006) featured engineer Jeff Knowler on mix, mastering by Sean Magee (credited as “Sean McGee”), and Mark Bishop as assistant technician.

The track “If You Could Read Your Mind” appeared in the 2007 film Hallam Foe.

Funf (2007) and Do It! (2008)

Funf (WIGCD 192 / WIGLP 192, 2007) was a B-sides/rarities compilation rather than a proper studio album. Recording credits are split across multiple sessions: Gareth Jones recorded track 1, Rob Ferrier recorded tracks 2/4/5/8/10/12, Lance Thomas (“Lance ‘Tommy’ Thomas,” ID 214911) recorded track 7, and the band recorded tracks 3/6/9/11 themselves.

Do It! (WIGCD 165 / WIGLP 165, 2008) was the fifth studio album. Most tracks were mixed by Jacquire King (credited as “Jaquire King”), with the band handling recording themselves.

Free Reign / Free Reign II (2012–2013)

Free Reign (WIGLP 295 / WIGCD 295, 2012) brought a significant shift in the band’s production approach. Robert Whiteley (ID 471545) served as additional producer and co-mixer. Daniel Lopatin (Oneohtrix Point Never, ID 1104928) mixed tracks 6 and 8 alongside Al Carlson (ID 889289). Mastering by Simon Heyworth (ID 201005).

Free Reign II (WIGLP 303, 2013) was an accompanying remix/rework album. Lopatin mixed throughout, with Al Carlson credited “with.” Robert Whiteley again co-produced. Mastering again by Simon Heyworth.

Wheeltappers And Shunters (2019)

Sixth studio album Wheeltappers And Shunters (WIGCD 424 / WIGLP 424, 2019) — first album in six years. Mixed by Dilip Harris (ID 151161). Mastered by Chris Potter (ID 39210). Design by Hartley and Matthew Cooper (2) (the Domino in-house designer). Photography by Michael Langford (3). Writing credited explicitly to Blackburn and Hartley.

Fantasy Island (2021)

Seventh studio album Fantasy Island (WIGLP 486 / WIGCD 486, 2021). Self-produced; artwork by the band. Mixed by Claudius Mittendorfer (ID 396145). Mastered by Matt Colton. Design by Matthew Cooper (2) and Paul J Street. The album includes a cover of a song credited to Ann Peebles, Don Bryant, and Bernard Miller (track B3).

Aesthetic / Context Notes

  • Performing in white surgical scrubs and masks is a defining visual identity going back to the earliest shows. Discogs profile specifically notes “faces hidden by surgical masks.”
  • The anonymity policy meant real names were kept out of press materials for years; DB member entries reveal first names gradually — Blackburn’s full name (Adrian Everett Blackburn) is the most complete; Campbell and Turney have no realname in Discogs.
  • The band self-produce virtually throughout, either alone or co-produced with a single external figure (Jones for the debut, Thomas for Winchester Cathedral). No single external producer dominates their catalog.
  • The Lopatin connection on Free Reign (2012) is notable — it represents the most experimental moment in their production history, inviting an American synth/drone artist to shape the mixing of their seventh LP.
