Oct 30, 20269 min read

Cap’n Jazz

Discogs ID: 690592 Active: 1989–1995; reunion 2010, 2017, 2024 Origin: Buffalo Grove / Chicago, Illinois Genres: Emo, Midwest Emo, Indie Rock, Post-Hardcore Labels: Further Beyond Records · Underdog Records · Man With Gun · Shakefork Records · Jade Tree

Foundational Chicago emo band; one of the most influential acts in the Midwest emo lineage. Their chaotic, emotionally unguarded sound — two intertwining guitars, yelped vocals, constantly shifting tempos — established a template that reverberated through an entire generation of post-1995 emo bands. All five members went on to significant post-breakup activity, making Cap’n Jazz as much a talent factory as a standalone act.

Biography

Formation and early years (1989–1993)

Cap’n Jazz formed in Buffalo Grove, Illinois (a Chicago suburb) in 1989 — initially under the name Toe Jam — by high school friends Tim Kinsella (vocals), Victor Villarreal (guitar), and Sam Zurick (bass), joined by Tim’s younger brother Mike Kinsella (drums). The band renamed themselves Cap’n Jazz before their first releases. The lineup was four-piece through their first recordings. [Source: Wikipedia, as of 2026-04-09 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap%27n_Jazz]

Their debut release was the Boys 16 To 18 Years…Age Of Action 7” EP (1993), on local label Further Beyond Records (FB004) — four tracks including “AOK,” “Geheim,” “Sergio Valente,” and “Easy Driver.” Engineer credits went to Neal Jensen. The same year brought the Sometimes If You Stand Further Away From Something… 7” EP on Underdog Records (UDS 9402) — three tracks (“We Are Scientists!”, “Sea Tea,” “Troubled By Insects”), produced by Kevin J. Frank, who also co-engineered with Neal Jensen. Sam Zurick played bass on the second EP under the alias Sudden Porthole.

Burritos LP and breakup (1994–1995)

Milwaukee native Davey von Bohlen joined as second guitarist in 1994, completing the classic five-piece lineup. The expanded band recorded their sole studio LP: Burritos, Inspiration Point, Fork Balloon Sports, Cards In The Spokes, Automatic Biographies, Kites, Kung Fu, Trophies, Banana Peels We’ve Slipped On, And Egg Shells We’ve Tippy Toed Over (1995) on Man With Gun (MWG CD02). The LP was recorded and mixed by Casey Rice, who would remain a key figure in the band’s legacy. Notable guests included Bob Nanna (lyrics on “Yes, I Am Talking To You”) and Anja Westerweck (backing vocals on “Little League”).

Also in 1995, Cap’n Jazz appeared on the Nothing Dies With Blue Skies split 7” on Shakefork Records (#10), sharing a side with Friction (8) (featuring Bob Nanna). Cap’n Jazz contributed “Rocky Rococo (Butterfly Version)” with Kevin J. Frank recording.

The band broke up in the summer of 1995 while on tour. The breakup scattered its members into multiple influential projects.

Post-breakup legacy and Analphabetapolothology (1996–1998)

Interest in the band grew after the breakup, driven partly by the post-Cap’n Jazz activities of its members. In 1998, Jade Tree Records (JT1036) released Analphabetapolothology, a double-CD compilation gathering nearly all recorded Cap’n Jazz material — 34 tracks across two discs — compiled by Casey Rice and mastered by Alan Douches. The compilation drew on the two early EPs, the LP, previously unreleased recordings, and a bonus disc of live/demo material. It became one of the defining documents of the Midwest emo genre, reprinted continuously through 2024.

Owls reunion (2001)

In 2001, the original four-piece (Mike, Tim, Victor, and Sam — not Davey von Bohlen, who by then was in The Promise Ring) reconvened under the name Owls and released a self-titled LP on Jade Tree, recorded by Steve Albini.

2010, 2017, 2024 reunions

Cap’n Jazz reunited as a full band (all five members, including Davey von Bohlen) for shows around Chicago in January 2010 and at Forecastle Festival in July 2010. Further reunion shows followed in 2017 and 2024, with von Bohlen absent from both later rounds; his second guitar slot was filled by Tim and Mike’s cousin Nate Kinsella (guitar, backing vocals). Nate Kinsella is credited as a touring member, not a core member. [Source: Wikipedia, as of 2026-04-09 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap%27n_Jazz]

Notes: Indian Summer • Cap’n Jazz • Heroin cassette

An unofficial bootleg cassette (Discogs release ID 17024811) compiling three live/radio recordings from separate events: Indian Summer live at Gilman (July 1, 1994); Cap’n Jazz live at the Daily Grind, Kansas City MO (July 15, 1995); Heroin on WFMU (July 1992). Format: cassette only; Not on Label. J-card was a handwritten old postcard — tracklist is incomplete and not all tracks are named. The tape circulated via hardcore/emo tape trading networks, likely assembled in the mid-to-late 1990s; no commercial pressing or other format known. No label origin confirmed. Scene Context

Cap’n Jazz are frequently positioned at the origin of the “Midwest emo” or “emo revival” lineage of the early-to-mid 1990s. Their influence on bands like Sunny Day Real Estate, The Promise Ring, American Football, Mineral, and subsequent generations of emo has been extensively noted. The Analphabetapolothology compilation effectively served as the genre’s founding document for many listeners who encountered it years after the band’s dissolution.

Their geographic and personnel overlap with Braid, Bob Nanna’s band (who contributed a lyric to the Burritos LP and co-appeared on a split), places them within a broader Chicago/suburban Illinois emo cluster of the early 1990s.
