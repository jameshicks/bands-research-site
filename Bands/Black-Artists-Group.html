Black Artists Group

Multi-disciplinary arts collective. St. Louis, Missouri. Active 1968–1972. Discogs artist ID: 2147434. Also known as BAG.

Overview

The Black Artists Group (BAG) was incorporated as a not-for-profit organization in St. Louis, Missouri in 1968 and remained active until approximately 1972. Modelled explicitly on Chicago’s Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM), BAG was unique among its peers in extending beyond music to encompass theater, dance, visual art, and poetry — all under a single institutional umbrella committed to Black avant-garde expression.

The musical core centred on free jazz and experimental improvisation: saxophonists Julius Hemphill, Oliver Lake, J.D. Parran, and Hamiet Bluiett; trumpeters Baikida Carroll and Floyd LeFlore; trombonist Joseph Bowie; drummers Charles Bobo Shaw and Bensid Thigpen. Beyond the musicians, BAG’s membership included stage directors Malinke Robert Elliott, Vincent Terrell, and Muthal Naidoo; poet Shirley LeFlore and Ajule (Bruce) Rutlin; dancers Georgia Collins and Luisah Teish; and painters Emilio Cruz and Oliver Lee Jackson (the latter closely associated though not formally a member). Drummer Ronnie Burrage was among the youngest participants, performing with BAG members at around age 11–12.

By 1972–1973 the institutional base in St. Louis had dissolved, and key members relocated — primarily to Paris and then New York — carrying the BAG ethos into the broader international avant-garde circuit. The Paris chapter produced the one self-released BAG recording, In Paris, Aries 1973. Members subsequently anchored major post-BAG formations: Julius Hemphill and Oliver Lake co-founded the World Saxophone Quartet with Hamiet Bluiett and David Murray; Joseph Bowie founded Defunkt; Oliver Lake sustained a long solo and ensemble career; Hamiet Bluiett became a leading baritone voice in avant-garde jazz.

The family connection to Chicago was also personal: Joseph Bowie’s brother Lester Bowie was a founding member of The Art Ensemble Of Chicago and a central figure in the AACM.

Open Questions

  • BAG founding date ambiguity: Discogs profile states 1968; some sources date active music performances to 1969–1970. The 1968 date refers to the not-for-profit incorporation.
  • Dissolution date: Discogs profile gives 1968–1972. The Paris recordings are from late 1972 and early 1973, suggesting the collective was still functioning into at least early 1973.
  • Other recordings: The Paris sessions captured on the 2024 INA reissues suggest more archival material may exist. Extent of INA archive holdings unknown without web research.
  • Julius Hemphill’s Discogs member listing: Hemphill is named in the Discogs profile text as a BAG member but does not appear in the Discogs member list for artist 2147434. He appears in the Human Arts Ensemble member list and has extensive solo Discogs credits (ID: 593607). His role in BAG recordings is not documented in the Discogs release credits for the Paris album. His Dogon A.D. (1972, Mbari) — recorded in St. Louis with Baikida Carroll, Abdul Wadud, and Phillip Wilson — is the primary document of his BAG-era work, though it is credited to him as a solo leader rather than to BAG.
  • Visual/theater work documentation: BAG’s theater, dance, and poetry activities are entirely undocumented in Discogs — only the musical recordings survive in the DB.
  • Benjamin Looker’s BAG history book: Looker (Discogs ID: 6610333) is credited on the 2024 reissues for liner notes and is identified in Discogs as a BAG historian. He wrote Point from Which Creation Begins: The Black Artists’ Group of St. Louis (2004). Discogs confirms shared credits; web research would confirm the book and further historiography.
