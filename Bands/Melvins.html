Oct 14, 202613 min read

Melvins

Discogs ID: 87652
Formed: 1983, Montesano, Washington
Core members: King Buzzo (Roger “Buzz” Osborne, guitar/vocals) · Dale Crover (drums/vocals)
Genres: Sludge metal, heavy rock, noise rock, experimental
Key labels: Boner Records · Amphetamine Reptile Records · Atlantic Records · Ipecac Recordings · Joyful Noise Recordings · Third Man Records

Heavy rock / sludge metal band, one of the most prolific and influential American underground acts of the past four decades. Formed in Montesano, WA (near Aberdeen) in 1983, the Melvins relocated to San Francisco in 1988 and eventually settled in Los Angeles’ San Fernando Valley. Their sound — crushing slow tempos, dense distortion, and whiplash time signatures — was a primary template for grunge, doom metal, and sludge. Despite never achieving mainstream commercial success, they have released more than 20 studio albums and hundreds of singles, splits, and live records across an extraordinary range of labels.

The band’s name came from a grocery clerk at the Thriftway in Montesano where Buzz Osborne worked — Melvin was the most hated fellow employee, which made it, in the band’s estimation, an appropriately ridiculous name.

Name variations used on releases: The Melvins, (The) Melvins, Los Melvins, Melvins 1983, Melvins Lite.

History

Origins and Early Lineup (1983–1988)

The Melvins formed in Montesano in 1983. The earliest lineup featured Mike Dillard on drums and Matt Lukin on bass, with Buzz Osborne on guitar and vocals. Dale Crover replaced Dillard on drums by 1984 and has been a fixture ever since. Lukin departed around 1987 to join Mudhoney.

A key figure in the early Aberdeen/Montesano scene was a young Kurt Cobain, who briefly served as a roadie and driver for the band during this period. Dale Crover appeared as drummer on some of Nirvana’s earliest recordings. The Melvins gave Cobain early exposure to avant-garde and punk influences; the connection resurfaces in Cobain’s later production work on Houdini (1993).

Their first proper release was the Easy As It Was 7” on C/Z Records (1986). The debut full-length, Gluey Porch Treatments (Alchemy Records, 1987), was produced by Mark Deutrom — who would later serve as bassist. Victor Hayden (Captain Beefheart’s cousin, known as the Mascara Snake) served as executive producer on the debut.

Boner Records Era (1989–1993)

After relocating to San Francisco, the Melvins released several essential records on Boner Records, many co-released in the UK on Tupelo Recording Company: Ozma (1989), Bullhead (1991), Eggnog EP (1991), Lysol (1992), and three simultaneous solo EPs for Buzz (King Buzzo), Dale (Dale Crover), and bassist Joe Preston (Joe Preston) in 1992.

Lori Black (daughter of actress Shirley Temple; performing as Lorax) played bass from roughly 1987 through 1991/1992. Joe Preston covered bass from 1991–1992. Lysol (1992) was recorded as one continuous piece; it is widely regarded as one of the first sludge metal records.

The band also maintained an active singles presence on Amphetamine Reptile Records from 1992 onward, releasing the Night Goat 7” (1992) and many subsequent singles and EPs on that label throughout the decade and beyond.

Atlantic Records Era (1993–1997)

Signed to Atlantic, the Melvins released three studio albums: Houdini (1993), Stoner Witch (1994), and Stag (1996). These represent the most commercially-facing period of the band’s career, though the label relationship ended after Stag.

Houdini (1993, Atlantic / Amphetamine Reptile) — Lineup: King Buzzo (guitar/vocals), Dale Crover (drums/vocals), Lori Black / Lorax (bass). Produced by Garth “GGGarth” Richardson (main producer on most tracks), Kurt Cobain (produced tracks A1, B1, B2, B3, B6 — five tracks; also mixed A1 and B1 with the Melvins), and the Melvins themselves on additional tracks. Engineers: Billy Anderson (several tracks), Jonathan Burnside (several tracks). Mastered by Stephen Marcussen (CD), Ron Boustead (vinyl). Art direction and illustration by Frank Kozik.

Stoner Witch (1994, Atlantic) — Lineup: King Buzzo (guitar/bass/vocals), Dale Crover (drums/guitar/backing vocals), Mark Deutrom (bass/guitar/backing vocals). Produced by Gggarth and the Melvins. Engineered, recorded, and mixed by Joe Barresi. Paul Dicarli played Moog synthesizer. Art direction by Mackie Osborne (Buzz’s wife — her first Melvins credit, continuing through the present).

Stag (1996, Atlantic / Mammoth Records) — Lineup: King Buzzo (guitar/vocals), Dale Crover (drums/percussion), Mark Deutrom (bass). The production team of Gggarth, Joe Barresi, and the Melvins handled production, engineering, and mixing collectively across tracks. Mastered by both Stephen Marcussen and Ron Boustead. A sprawling double album, the most ambitious of the Atlantic period. Mark Deutrom’s final album as a member of the regular lineup.

Also during the Atlantic phase, Honky (1997, Amphetamine Reptile) closed out Mark Deutrom’s tenure: produced by Joe Barresi and the Melvins; lineup as Stag plus David Scott Stone (bowed cymbal, oscillators) and Mac Mann (piano, bells, synthesizer) as guests; Kat Bjelland (Babes in Toyland) contributed additional vocals.

Amphetamine Reptile / Kevin Rutmanis Years (1997–2005)

After the Atlantic deal ended, Kevin Rutmanis (bass, 1998–2005) anchored the next phase. The “Trilogy” records — The Maggot, The Bootlicker (both 1999), and The Crybaby (2000) — were released in quick succession on Ipecac. The Maggot was co-produced by Tim Green. Colossus Of Destiny (2001, Ipecac) was a deliberately abrasive single-track noise/feedback album. Hostile Ambient Takeover (2002) followed.

In 2003, the band released a series of singles on Ipecac (Antivermin, Black Stooges, Brain Center, Dr. Geek, Foaming, Fool, Judas Chang). In 2004–2005, they collaborated with Jello Biafra on two Alternative Tentacles albums: Never Breathe What You Can’t See (2004) and Sieg Howdy! (2005). Pigs Of The Roman Empire (2004, Ipecac) was a collaboration with Lustmord. Mangled Demos From 1983 (2005, Ipecac) compiled early archival material.

Throughout this era, Amphetamine Reptile remained the home for a steady stream of singles, splits, and EPs.

Ipecac Era / Big Business Lineup (2006–2012)

The band expanded to a quartet (briefly a quintet on stage) by adding Jared Warren (bass/vocals) and Coady Willis (drums/vocals), both formerly of Big Business (2), running alongside Dale Crover. This “double drummer” configuration recorded (A) Senile Animal (2006, Ipecac), with Toshi Kasai as recording and mixing engineer and the Melvins producing. John Golden handled mastering (his first of many Melvins credits). The expanded lineup also recorded Nude With Boots (2008), The Bride Screamed Murder (2010), and Chicken Switch (2009, a remix/collaboration album on Ipecac).

Sub-projects emerged from this period:

  • Melvins Lite — trio with Trevor Dunn on upright bass; recorded Freak Puke (2012, Ipecac)
  • Melvins 1983 — revisiting the original Montesano lineup, featuring Mike Dillard on drums with Dale Crover moving to bass; released 1983 (2012, Amphetamine Reptile) and later Tres Cabrones (2013, Ipecac)

Joyful Noise Recordings Phase (2013–2022)

The Melvins developed a significant relationship with Joyful Noise Recordings (Indianapolis, IN), releasing approximately 10 records on the label between 2013 and 2022, including Buzz Osborne solo releases as King Buzzo. Toshi Kasai continued as primary engineer/producer throughout this phase.

Key Joyful Noise releases: Billy Fish Alive (2013), Rebel Girl (2015), Broken Pipe EP (2021).

Jeffrey Pinkus (bass/vocals, ex-Butthole Surfers) joined as bassist from approximately 2013–2018, appearing on Hold It In (2014, Ipecac) and Basses Loaded (2016, Ipecac), among others. Basses Loaded was a guest-bassist record featuring a different bassist on each track.

Three Men And A Baby (2016, Sub Pop) was a one-off 12” release on the Seattle label.

Third Man Records — Stag Re-Recording (2016)

Live At Third Man Records (2013, Third Man Records) documents a Melvins live session at Jack White’s Nashville studio/venue. Separately, in 2016 Third Man Records released a new recording under the title Stag (Discogs ID 8679420), distinct from the original 1996 Atlantic album. This 2016 version featured the reunited Atlantic-era lineup of King Buzzo (guitar/vocals), Dale Crover (drums), and Mark Deutrom (bass), with Mac Mann (organ, grand piano) and Walter Kibby (valve trombone) as additional players. Producers: Alex Newport, Gggarth Richardson, Joe Barresi, Chris Kozlowski, and the Melvins.

Steven McDonald Era (2017–present)

Steven McDonald (of Redd Kross) joined as bassist and has remained the current member. Key releases in this period:

  • A Walk With Love & Death (2017, Ipecac) — Steven McDonald on bass
  • Pinkus Abortion Technician (2018, Amphetamine Reptile) — Jeffrey Pinkus returned for a final appearance
  • Working With God (2021, Ipecac) — a return to the original three-piece of Buzz, Dale (bass/drums/vocals), and Mike Dillard (drums); recorded by Toshi Kasai, mastered by John Golden
  • Five Legged Dog (2021, Ipecac) — acoustic covers album
  • Tarantula Heart (2024, Ipecac) — lineup: Steven McDonald (bass/vocals), Dale Crover (drums/vocals), Roy Mayorga (drums/synthesizer), Gary Chester (3) (guitar), Buzz Osborne (guitar/vocals); design by Mackie Osborne
  • Savage Imperial Death March (2025, Amphetamine Reptile) and Thunderball (2025, Ipecac) continue the run
