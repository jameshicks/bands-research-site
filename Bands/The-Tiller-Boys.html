The Tiller Boys

Discogs ID: 352474. Manchester group, active 1978–1979. Supported Joy Division on several occasions. Recorded a single for Factory Records which the label did not release; eventually issued on New Hormones as ORG 3 (Big Noise From The Jungle, 1980) — after the band had already disbanded.

Factory Records — Unreleased Single

The Tiller Boys recorded their single at Arrow Studio, Manchester, in January 1979. The session produced three tracks: Big Noise From The Jungle, Slaves and Pyramids, and What Me Worry? — described as “pounding percussion and Neu!-like guitar clangour.” The recording was slated to be issued as FAC 3 on Factory Records. (Note: FAC 3 as a catalogue number was also used for a Peter Saville-designed poster for the Factory Club night of 20 October 1978 featuring Cabaret Voltaire, Joy Division, and The Tiller Boys — the numbering overlap is a known quirk of Factory’s early catalogue.)

The single was not released. The LTM Recordings biography of Eric Random gives the reason only as “for various reasons these tracks…were fated not to appear on Tony Wilson’s radical Manchester indie” — no specific cause is named. (ltmrecordings.com, lesdisquesducrepuscule.com) No other published source documents the specific internal reason for the refusal.

The specific reason — whether a quality judgement by Wilson, a contractual issue arising from Pete Shelley’s concurrent Buzzcocks commitments, a scheduling decision, or something else — is not documented in any currently web-accessible source. It would require the physical Use Hearing Protection box-set booklet, the full text of James Nice’s Shadowplayers, or a direct interview with Eric Random or surviving Factory personnel.

The white-label 12” pressing of the intended FAC 3 was included as a bonus item in the 2019 Use Hearing Protection: Factory Records 1978–79 box set (Rhino, limited to 4,000 copies). (electricityclub.co.uk, superdeluxeedition.com, bigtakeover.com)

The tracks were eventually released commercially by New Hormones as ORG 3 in February 1980, reviving that label after a dormant period.
