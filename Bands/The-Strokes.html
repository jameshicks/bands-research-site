The Strokes

American rock band from New York City. Formed 1998. Discogs artist ID: 55980.

Biography

Formation and Early Years (1998–2001)

The Strokes coalesced in New York City in 1998 around vocalist Julian Casablancas (b. 1978), who had grown up partly in Europe and attended the Dwight School in Manhattan alongside future bandmates. The lineup settled into Casablancas on vocals, Nick Valensi and Albert Hammond Jr. on guitars, Nikolai Fraiture on bass, and Fabrizio Moretti on drums.

The band self-released a four-track demo cassette in 2000 (listed on Discogs as a self-released EP, 79 pressings/editions documented). Their early club performances in Lower Manhattan attracted attention from UK press before major US labels came knocking. They signed to Rough Trade in the UK and RCA in the US.

Their first proper single, The Modern Age, was released on Rough Trade in 2001. UK hype built quickly, and by the time Hard To Explain / New York City Cops followed on RCA Europe, the band was positioned as the spearhead of a garage rock revival.

Is This It and the Garage Rock Revival (2001)

Debut album Is This It was recorded with producer Gordon Raphael at Transporterraum in New York. Released in 2001, it arrived as a fully formed statement: taut, lo-fi guitar rock built on Casablancas’ detached baritone. All songs written by Casablancas. Mastered by Greg Calbi. Manager Ryan Gentles (Wiz Kid Management) is credited on the sleeve.

Critical reception was rapturous, particularly in the UK. The album was subsequently ranked No. 8 on Rolling Stone’s list of best debut albums, No. 2 on their best albums of the 2000s, and No. 199 on their all-time 500. NME placed it No. 4 on their top 500 albums of all time. The US pressing omitted “New York City Cops” (replaced by “When It Started”) following the September 11 attacks.

Accompanying singles: Hard To Explain / New York City Cops (RCA Europe, 2001), Last Nite (RCA Europe, 2001), Someday (BMG, 2002).

Room On Fire (2003)

Second album, again produced by Gordon Raphael and mastered by Greg Calbi and Steve Fallone. Engineering handled by Toshikazu Yoshioka (head) and Will Kelly (2nd engineer). Art direction by Brett Kilroe. The album continued the compressed guitar-pop formula of the debut while tightening arrangements. Singles included 12:51 (Rough Trade UK), Reptilia (BMG/RCA), and The End Has No End (Rough Trade UK). A collaboration with Regina Spektor, Modern Girls & Old Fashion Men, appeared as a single in 2004.

First Impressions Of Earth (2005–2006)

Third album, with the bulk produced by David Kahne and three tracks produced by Gordon Raphael. Mixed by Andy Wallace. Mastering split between Howie Weinberg (tracks 1–8, 11–14) and others. This was a more expansive, harder-rocking record — 14 tracks and a heavier production aesthetic. Released on RCA/Sony BMG and Rough Trade UK.

Individual members began receiving specific writing credits here: Valensi received “Music By” (as N.Valensi) on track 7, “Ask Me Anything”; Moretti on track 13, “Evening Sun”; Fraiture on track 9, “Killing Lies”. All other tracks credited solely to J.Casablancas. This suggests the band was starting to acknowledge individual compositional contributions for tracks with a clearly distinct authorship.

Singles: Juicebox (Rough Trade UK, 2005), Heart In A Cage (Rough Trade UK, 2005), You Only Live Once (Sony BMG Australia, 2005).

Hiatus and Solo Work (2006–2010)

Following First Impressions, the band entered an extended informal hiatus. Members pursued solo projects:

  • Julian Casablancas released a solo album Phrazes for the Young in 2009 and subsequently formed Julian Casablancas+The Voidz (later known as The Voidz).
  • Albert Hammond Jr. launched a solo career with multiple albums on his own.
  • Fabrizio Moretti formed Little Joy (2008) with Rodrigo Amarante and Binki Shapiro — self-titled debut on Rough Trade, produced by Noah Georgeson.

Angles (2011)

Return after the hiatus. Produced primarily by Gus Oberg (tracks 1–9) and Joe Chiccarelli (tracks 2, 3, 8, 10), with the band co-producing throughout. Noah Georgeson mixed most tracks; Oberg mixed track 2. Engineering by Gus Oberg and Justin Gerrish. Mastered by George Marino. The band was more openly collaborative in songwriting, and the production is noticeably more polished than the Raphael-era albums. Released on RCA/Sony Music. Single: Under Cover Of Darkness (RCA, 2011), Taken For A Fool (RCA, 2011).

Comedown Machine (2013) and Future Present Past EP (2016)

Comedown Machine (2013) was produced and mixed by Gus Oberg, engineered by Oberg and Phil Joly, mastered by Dave Kutch. Art direction again by Brett Kilroe and Fabrizio Moretti jointly. Released on RCA. Single: All The Time (RCA, 2013).

Future Present Past (2016) was a four-track EP — their first release on Julian Casablancas’ Cult Records (3) label. Produced by Gus Oberg.

The New Abnormal (2020)

Fifth proper studio album, produced by Rick Rubin — a significant shift in collaborator. Engineered primarily by Jason Lader, who also mixed most tracks. Additional engineering by Pete Min. Mastered by Stephen Marcussen and Stewart Whitmore. Cover artwork is a painting by Jean-Michel Basquiat (titled Bird on Money, 1981). Art direction and design by Tina Ibañez. Released on Cult Records (3) / RCA.

One track, “Ode to the Mets” (B6), has a Music By credit to Paul Vassallo alongside the band. Track “At the Door” (A5) has Music By credits to Richard Butler and Tim Butler (of the Psychedelic Furs) — suggesting significant melodic borrowing or co-write.

A compilation The Singles, Vol 1 followed in 2023 on Legacy.
