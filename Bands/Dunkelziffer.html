Dunkelziffer

Cologne-based post-punk/funk group, active 1982–1990. Discogs artist ID: 372658.

Formed by musicians from the Can constellation — including former Can vocalist Damo Suzuki and drummer Jaki Liebezeit — alongside Cologne session players. One of the key post-Krautrock outfits of the 1980s Cologne scene, fusing Afro-funk rhythms, new wave texture, and jazz-inflected improvisation. Released seven albums, all but the debut on the independent label FünfUndVierzig.

Biography

Formation and Origins (1981–1982)

Dunkelziffer coalesced in Cologne in 1981 around a network of musicians who had orbited Can and its associated projects. The name translates roughly as “dark figure” (the German statistical term for unrecorded criminal incidences). The band formed explicitly as a vehicle to bring together Damo Suzuki — who had left Can in 1973 and spent most of the intervening decade as a Jehovah’s Witness — and Jaki Liebezeit, Can’s defining drummer.

The core personnel that crystallised for the debut included guitarist Dominik Von Senger (also later of Phantom Band, The Ya-Ya’s, and the Damo Suzuki Band), keyboardist Helmut Zerlett (a prolific Cologne session musician also involved in Trance Groove, The Unknown Cases, and later German television), drummer Stefan Krachten, bassist Matthias Keul, and percussionists Olek Gelba (also of Phantom Band and Drums Off Chaos) and Reiner Linke. Vocalist Josefa Martens and woodwind/saxophone player Wolfgang Schubert (4) rounded out the early ensemble. The band operated collectively and credited all composition and production to Dunkelziffer as a unit.

The debut, Stil Der Neuen Zeit (1982), was a 12” maxi-single on the Düsseldorf-based punk/new wave label Pure Freude (cat# PF 14 CK 7), placing the band squarely in the post-punk infrastructure of the Rhine–Ruhr corridor. Damo Suzuki is not credited on the debut by name — production and performance credits go to Dunkelziffer collectively, with engineer/mixer Robert Hartmann (“Bobby Hartmann”) credited separately.

The FünfUndVierzig Years: Early Period (1983–1986)

From the second release onward, Dunkelziffer moved to FünfUndVierzig, the Cologne/Schleswig-Holstein independent run by Piet Manns. The label would carry all subsequent Dunkelziffer output through the end of the band’s run.

Colours And Soul (1983, FünfUndVierzig 00) was the first full-length LP, a double-sided 14-track record. It expanded the lineup considerably: Traffic/Blind Faith percussionist Rebop Kwaku Baah (credited as “Reebop Kwaku Baah”) contributed percussion and vocals on multiple tracks; Josefa Martens returned for vocal duties; Coco Claus and Achim Fink (trombone) added colour to several sides. Jaki Liebezeit contributed bongos on one track (B2). Notably, Damo Suzuki is absent from the personnel credits on this LP — this appears to be the one album in the core run where he was not involved.

In The Night (1984) marked Damo Suzuki’s return as credited vocalist, along with the introduction of bassist Rike Gratt, who replaced Matthias Keul on bass duties (Keul shifting to piano and synthesizer alongside bass). Engineer Frank Norden joined the production team. The In The Night LP appeared on FünfUndVierzig in joint release with EFA distribution.

Around the same time, the band released the 7” single I See Your Smile / Q (FünfUndVierzig 03, 1984), with the core lineup of Suzuki, Krachten, Von Senger, Zerlett, Keul, Gelba, Linke, and Schubert, produced jointly by the band and Frank Norden.

You Make Me Happy (1985, FünfUndVierzig 10) retained the same core, with Norden co-producing and recording. By this point the lineup of Suzuki (vocals), Von Senger (guitar), Krachten (drums), Keul (keyboards), Gratt (bass), Gelba and Linke (percussion), and Schubert (saxophone) had stabilised into the band’s most consistent formation.

III (1986, FünfUndVierzig 23) was the third studio LP in strict sequence, produced collectively by Dunkelziffer with Norden engineering and Hilmar Kerp mastering. Cover design again by Klaus Winterfeld, who had handled artwork since the debut.

Later Period and Dissolution (1989–1990)

After a gap of three years, Songs For Everyone appeared in 1989 (FünfUndVierzig 43). The lineup shifted: Rike Gratt and Stefan Krachten are absent; Jorge Guarin Quintaro (credited as “Jorge Guarin”) took the piano and synthesizer role alongside Von Senger (guitar/keyboards) and Schubert (oboe, saxophone, trumpet). Vocalists changed too — Irene Lorenz is credited on this album rather than Suzuki. Olek Gelba and Reiner Linke continued on percussion and timbales.

Illuminate (1990, FünfUndVierzig, cat# “none”) was a 7” single and the band’s final release, a two-track vinyl — “Illuminate” / “See It” — with no additional credits beyond the Dunkelziffer primary. This appears to have been a farewell statement rather than a full production.

Live Album and Compilations (1997, 2013)

A live album (Live, 1997) was released on the Japanese label Captain Trip Records — the version in the Discogs catalog has Damo Suzuki on vocals, Rike Gratt on bass, Stefan Krachten on drums, Dominik Von Senger on guitar, Matthias Keul on keyboards, and Olek Gelba and Reiner Linke on percussion, with Christian “Kalau” Keul handling recording and mixing. This documents a lineup that reprised the core mid-period band.

In 2013, the UK label Emotional Rescue issued the Retrospection series — three individual volumes (Part 1, Part 2, Part 3) and a combined Parts 1-3 package — drawing from the band’s back catalog.

Labels

FünfUndVierzig (Discogs ID: 16689)

German independent label founded 1984, run by Piet Manns. Based originally at Hauptstraße 49, Sandesneben, Schleswig-Holstein; later at Schmiedetwiete 6, Labenz. Label code LC 8759. Distributed by EFA until 2004, then by Indigo. Published krautrock, industrial, post-punk, and reggae; sublabel 45Trance. Dunkelziffer were the label’s founding act (catalog number 00). Notable FünfUndVierzig roster beyond Dunkelziffer includes: Anna Domino, The Fall (Bend Sinister, 1986), John Cale, Paul Haig, Bourbonese Qualk, Foyer Des Arts, Sleep Chamber, Gary Clail’s Tackhead Sound System, Guru Guru, Kraan, Conrad Schnitzler, Max Goldt, S.Y.P.H., Einstürzende Neubauten (The Jewels, 2008), Brainticket.

Pure Freude (Discogs ID: 11937)

Düsseldorf-based independent punk/new wave label, active late 1970s–early 1980s. Defunct. Home to Mittagspause, S.Y.P.H., The Raincoats (German pressing of Odyshape), Red Krayola, Die Tödliche Doris, Camp Sophisto. Dunkelziffer’s debut Stil Der Neuen Zeit (PF 14 CK 7) was the label’s fourteenth release.
