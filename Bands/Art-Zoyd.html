Art Zoyd

Art Zoyd

Oct 26, 20267 min read

Art Zoyd

Discogs ID: 229368 Origin: Valenciennes, France Active: 1971–2024 (per Discogs) Genre: French avant-garde / Rock in Opposition / electro-acoustic / contemporary composed music Name variations: Art Zöyd, Art Zoyd 3, Art Zoyd Studio

French avant-garde ensemble founded in 1968 (initially as a free jazz/rock unit) and active through 2024. One of the key ensembles of the Rock In Opposition movement, alongside Univers Zero and Henry Cow. Their music mixes free jazz, progressive rock, orchestral composition, electronic manipulation, and sound-for-image work (theatre, dance, silent film accompaniment). The group’s core identity shifted across roughly three eras: an early acoustic/RIO phase (1971–1982), a chamber-electro period under the leadership of Gérard Hourbette and Thierry Zaboitzeff (1983–1997), and a later phase driven by Hourbette alone with expanded ensembles (2001–2024).

History

Origins and the RIO Years (1971–1982)

Art Zoyd’s first document on Discogs is Sangria (1971, Opaline Records (2)), credited under the spelling “Art Zöyd” — an early student-band recording with minimal personnel data (arranged and produced by Alain Loewenguth). The group reformed and found its voice through the mid-1970s.

Symphonie Pour Le Jour Où Brûleront Les Cités was recorded in 1976 (AZ Production Michel Besset) and reissued in a new version on Atem in 1981. It is their breakthrough statement: a single extended symphonic work built from fractured chamber rock, dissonance, and dramatic dynamic swings, placing them squarely in the Rock in Opposition current. The ensemble at this point included violinist/viola player Gérard Hourbette, cellist/bassist/vocalist Thierry Zaboitzeff, saxophonist/percussionist Michel Thomas, trumpeter Jean-Pierre Soarez, oboist/bassoonist Michel Berckmans, violinist Franck Cardon, and percussionist Daniel Denis (also of Univers Zero).

Musique Pour L’Odyssée (1979, Atem / Recommended Records) was co-released with Chris Cutler’s Recommended Records — the key UK conduit for RIO material. Primary composers: Gérard Hourbette (most tracks) and Thierry Zaboitzeff (B1). Mixed by Pierre Desmedt alongside Art Zoyd.

Génération Sans Futur (1980, Atem) expanded the lineup to include guitarist Alain Eckert, pianist Patricia Dallio, and saxophonist Gilles Renard. Compositions shared among Hourbette, Zaboitzeff, Eckert, and Renard. Produced alongside Gérard Nguyen; recorded/mixed by Etienne Conod.

Phase IV (1982, Recommended Records) was UK-released and marks the end of the ensemble’s direct Recommended/RIO-circuit period.

The Cryonic Inc. Era (1983–1989)

Cryonic Inc. (Discogs ID: 58944) became Art Zoyd’s own imprint for their pivotal middle period. Three studio albums and one compilation/archive set appeared here:

  • Les Espaces Inquiets (1983) — core trio of Hourbette, Zaboitzeff, Dallio with Didier Pietton (alto/tenor sax, percussion), Jean-Pierre Soarez (trumpet/flugelhorn), and Thierry Zaboitzeff adding guitar, tape, and synthesizer. Produced with Divine Records (9). Recorded/mixed by Robert Vogel and Art Zoyd.
  • Le Mariage Du Ciel Et De L’Enfer (1985) — named after William Blake. Cover design by Unsafe Graphics with drawing by Keith Haring. Recorded by Robert Vogel, mixed by Gilles Martin and Art Zoyd. The trio of Hourbette, Zaboitzeff, and Dallio is the performing core; Jean-Pierre Soarez remains on trumpet.
  • Berlin (1987) — André Mergenthaler joins on cello/alto saxophone/bass vocals alongside the Hourbette/Zaboitzeff/Dallio trio. Compositions split: Hourbette’s “A” side (Side A) and Zaboitzeff’s “B” side. Cover design again by Unsafe Graphics; William Shakespeare excerpts from Macbeth cited as lyrics.
  • Les Espaces Inquiets / Phase IV / Archives II (1989, Mantra) — a retrospective compilation.

Atonal Records and the Silent Film Period (1990–1997)

Art Zoyd relocated to the German independent Atonal Records (a sublabel of Konnex Records) for three releases, each a score for a silent film:

  • Marathonnerre I & II (1992) — live recordings
  • Faust (1996) — score for F.W. Murnau’s Faust (1926). Core quartet: Hourbette, Zaboitzeff, Dallio, Denis. All music composed by Hourbette and Zaboitzeff.
  • Häxan (1997) — score for Benjamin Christensen’s Häxan (1922). Same quartet, self-produced.

A collaboration album Art Zoyd - J. A. Deane - J. Greinke (1990, Ear-Rational Records) appeared during this stretch, co-credited with American trombonist/electronics player J.A. Deane and Seattle composer Jeff Greinke.

The 1990 album Nosferatu (Mantra) was a score for F.W. Murnau’s 1922 film — no additional credits in the DB.

In-Possible Records and the Ensemble Phase (2001–2002)

The final studio period documented in Discogs moved to Art Zoyd’s own In-Possible Records (ID: 76111):

  • u·B·I·Q·U·e (2001) — a massive orchestral/ensemble project. The Orchestre d’u.B.I.Q.U.e was assembled for this work, comprising dozens of players across saxophones, brass, guitars, drums, bass, and electronics. Core members Hourbette, Dallio, Daniel Denis, and Mireille Bauer appear. Directed by Michel Berckmans. Music composed by Gérard Hourbette.
  • Metropolis (2002) — score for Fritz Lang’s 1927 film. A tighter group: Hourbette, Dallio, Kasper T Toeplitz (electronics/bass guitar), Laurence Chave and Didier Casamitjana (percussion), Yukari Hamada-Bertocchi (keyboards/sampler), and Serge Bertocchi (saxophones/tubax). Illustrations by Philippe Druillet. Liner notes include comments from choreographer Karole Armitage.

Open Questions

  • Precise founding date: Discogs profile says 1968 formation but first documented release is 1971 (Sangria). The period 1968–1971 is undocumented in the DB.
  • Didier Pietton’s role after 1983 (he is listed as a Discogs member but only credited on Les Espaces Inquiets) — web sources would clarify if this was a longer tenure.
  • Full details on the Mantra label — no Discogs label file pulled; unclear if it is Art Zoyd’s own imprint or an independent.
  • The 1999 release The Hard Quest on Cuneiform Records (ID: 810417) lists Art Zoyd as co-producer but not primary artist — co-production context unclear from Discogs alone.
  • Activity between 2002 and 2024 is not documented on Discogs (no releases after Metropolis); the “active to 2024” figure presumably reflects live activity or non-Discogs releases.
