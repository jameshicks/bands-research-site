Teenage Jesus And The Jerks

Discogs Artist ID: 88519
Active: 1977–1980 (original run); reunion/compilations from 1995
Origin: New York City
Genre: No Wave
Key label aliases: Migraine, ZE Records, Antilles

New York No Wave group fronted by Lydia Lunch (Lydia Anne Koch); one of the four acts on No New York (Antilles, 1978, prod. Brian Eno). Regarded as the most sonically abrasive of the four No New York bands — brutally short song structures, Lunch’s unorthodox slide guitar, minimal percussion. The band was effectively Lydia Lunch’s vehicle: lineup rotated around her throughout the active period.

Biography

Formation and early activity (1977–1978)

Teenage Jesus And The Jerks formed in late 1977 in New York City around Lydia Lunch on vocals and guitar. The earliest lineup included Gordon Stevenson on bass and Bradly Field on percussion. Stevenson — who had met Arto Lindsay while a student at Eckerd College in St. Petersburg, Florida — was bassist from November 1977 through June 1978, the period covering the No New York sessions.

In spring 1978, Brian Eno attended the “Bands at Artists Space” festival in SoHo (organized by Michael Zwack and Robert Longo) and selected Teenage Jesus And The Jerks as one of four acts to record for a compilation documenting the No Wave scene. The four tracks recorded — “Burning Rubber,” “Closet,” “Red Alert,” and “I Woke Up Dreaming” — all written by Lunch — appeared on Side A of No New York (Antilles, AN-7067), released late 1978. The recording was done live with minimal production at Big Apple Studio, NYC.

Also in 1978, the band released the double A-side single “Orphans” on the Migraine label, produced by Robert Quine of Richard Hell & The Voidoids. The lineup for “Orphans” was Lunch (guitar, vocals), Stevenson (bass), and Field (drums).

Lineup changes and later releases (1979–1980)

After Stevenson’s departure in mid-1978, Jim Sclavunos took over on bass. Sclavunos appears on the 1979 Migraine singles “Baby Doll” and the EP Teenage Jesus And The Jerks, both produced by Robert Quine.

Japanese bassist Reck (2) (Kawashima Akiyoshi, later of Friction (5)) also served a stint on bass and appears on the ZE Records release Pre Teenage Jesus And The Jerks (1979), alongside Field on drums and Lunch on guitar/vocals. That release notably features James Chance on saxophone and mixing — the two downtown scenes briefly intersecting. Sleeve photography on ZE and Migraine releases was handled by Julia Gorton.

Other members at various times included Jay Korber and Paul Costuros. Thurston Moore (later of Sonic Youth) is credited as a member on Discogs, though his exact role and tenure in the band is not documented in the database.

The band dissolved around 1980 as Lunch moved on to Beirut Slump (with Sclavunos) and 8 Eyed Spy.

Compilations and reissues (1995–present)

Everything (1995, Atavistic) collected the studio output. Live At Max’s Kansas City 1977 (2006, Widowspeak Productions) and Live 1977–1979 (2015, Other People) document live performances. Shut Up And Bleed (2008, Cherry Red) compiled both Teenage Jesus And The Jerks material and Beirut Slump tracks together, produced and remastered by Tom Garretson.

Notes (web)

Robert Quine / Lydia Lunch connection

The relationship between Robert Quine and Lydia Lunch that underpins all three Migraine productions was personal and predates Teenage Jesus And The Jerks. Lunch attended one of the earliest Richard Hell & the Voidoids shows — ca. 1976–77, when she was approximately 17 — and went up to Quine afterwards to tell him “You are God.” She later described herself as one of his very first fans, and said “because I was his first fan and a baby-faced killer, he kind of took a shine to me.” (ARTnews interview with Lydia Lunch, 2015)

The scene geography reinforced the connection. By spring 1977 Lunch was living in a loft on Warren Street in lower Manhattan — a communal space known informally as “The Home For Teenage Dirt” — alongside Bradly Field (who would become TJ&TJ’s drummer) and Miriam Linna. This placed Lunch and Field inside the same tight downtown circle as the CBGB bands, which Quine and the Voidoids were at the centre of. (TheHoundBlog, October 2008)

Quine himself confirmed producing both Teenage Jesus and DNA in a Perfect Sound Forever interview (furious.com), noting it matter-of-factly without elaboration. The relationship extended beyond TJ&TJ: Quine played guitar on multiple tracks of Lunch’s 1980 solo album Queen of Siam (“Lady Scarface,” “A Cruise to the Moon,” “Knives in the Drain,” “Blood of Tin”). (Wikipedia, Queen of Siam, as of 2025-07-28)

Production split on the 1979 Migraine LP: The self-titled LP was co-produced — Quine produced tracks A1–A3, B1, and B2; Woody Payne produced A4 and A5. (Wikipedia, Teenage Jesus and the Jerks album, as of 2025-07-28). The earlier two Migraine releases (“Orphans” 1978 and “Baby Doll” 1979) are credited to Quine alone on Discogs.
