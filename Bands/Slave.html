Slave

Funk/R&B group formed in Dayton, Ohio, 1975. Discogs artist ID: 49609. Active through the mid-1990s across two label homes: Cotillion/Atlantic (1977–1984) and Ichiban Records (1985–1995). Their commercial peak coincided with Steve Arrington’s tenure as lead vocalist (1978–1982). Jimmy Douglass was their primary producer from 1979 onward, with credits across multiple releases including Stone Jam (1980), Show Time (1981), Bad Enuff (1983), and New Plateau (1984).

Overview

Founded by New Jersey-born trumpeter Steve Washington (whose uncle was Ralph “Pee Wee” Middlebrook of the Ohio Players) and trombonist Floyd Miller. The band emerged from two former Ohio groups, the Young Mystics and Black Satin Soul. They signed to Cotillion Records and scored their first hit with “Slide” (1977, #1 R&B). Their self-titled debut went Gold.

Steve Arrington left Slave in 1982 to form Steve Arrington’s Hall Of Fame. Per Discogs credits, he did not return to the group — the Ichiban-era recordings feature no Arrington credit. The 1994 Rhino compilation Stellar Fungk: The Best of Slave Featuring Steve Arrington was a retrospective anthology, not a reunion.

Era 2: The Arrington Years (1978–1982)

Key albums: The Concept (1978), Just A Touch Of Love (1979), Stone Jam (1980), Show Time (1981)

New additions in 1978: Steve Arrington (drums, percussion, lead vocals), Starleana Young (vocals), Curt Jones (vocals), Ray Turner (keyboards). Arrington progressively took over as lead vocalist, displacing Dozier in that role.

Charles Carter (saxophone, keyboards, flute) and Sam Carter (keyboards, percussion) added by the Show Time era (1981).

1980 departure: Steve Washington left (along with Curt Jones and Starleana Young) to form Aurra. This happened around the time of Stone Jam (1980); Washington is still credited as co-producer and executive producer on that record.

1982 departure: Steve Arrington left after Show Time (1981) — he is absent from Visions Of The Lite (1982) credits. Charles Carter and Sam Carter also departed to join Arrington’s new group, Steve Arrington’s Hall Of Fame. Ronny Cochran stepped in as drummer.

Era 3: Post-Arrington / Cotillion Wind-Down (1982–1984)

Key albums: Visions Of The Lite (1982), Bad Enuff (1983), New Plateau (1984)

Core trio of Mark Adams, Danny Webster, and Floyd Miller carried the group forward. Producer: Jimmy Douglass (Bad Enuff, New Plateau).
