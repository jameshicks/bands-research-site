Shockabilly

Shockabilly was an American avant-rock trio formed in New York City in 1982, comprising Eugene Chadbourne (guitar, vocals), Kramer (2) (bass, organ, tapes, producer), and David Licht (drums, percussion). The group emerged directly from the quintet The Chadbournes, which also included John Zorn and Tom Cora; when Zorn and Cora departed, the remaining three carried on under the new name. Despite the rockabilly suggestion of the name, the band’s music was emphatically avant-garde — a restless, frequently confrontational mash of blues, country, folk-rock, noise rock, psychedelia, surf, and lo-fi, with covers of familiar songs rendered nearly unrecognizable through radical deconstruction. All studio albums were produced by Kramer.

The group released three LPs on Rough Trade (UK), one on the American independent Fundamental, and one on the German label What’s So Funny About.. before disbanding around 1985. A live album appeared on Shimmy Disc in 1989 (Kramer’s own label) and compilation releases followed through 1990. According to Wikipedia, the band’s experimental approach is cited as a possible influence on later bands including Sonic Youth and Primus. Kramer and Licht went on to co-found Bongwater (with Ann Magnuson) and B.A.L.L., while Chadbourne pursued an extraordinarily prolific solo and collaborative career.

Style Notes

Although the name implies rockabilly, only The Dawn Of Shockabilly showed any meaningful rockabilly influence (Wikipedia). The band was fundamentally an avant-rock unit deploying covers of mainstream rock, country, blues, and pop songs — Jagger/Richards, Roger Miller, Roger McGuinn, Paul Simon, John Lennon, Marc Bolan — in aggressive avant-garde arrangements that made originals nearly unrecognizable. Kramer’s use of cheap organ and tapes, combined with Chadbourne’s guitar noise and Licht’s unrelenting percussion, created a sound that anticipates elements of noise rock and lo-fi. Wikipedia notes that Primus vocalist Les Claypool has been compared stylistically to Chadbourne’s vocal approach in this band.
