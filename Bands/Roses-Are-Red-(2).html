Roses Are Red (2)

UK punk band. Discogs artist ID: 1535119

Overview

Roses Are Red were a UK punk group whose only known recording is the two-sided 7” single “Can’t Understand” / “Your Love Is Like A Ballistic Missile,” self-produced and originally issued in 1979 on the micro-label Posthumous Petal (cat# PET-1). The band operated under pseudonyms — Miss Directed (bass, vocals), C. Slug (drums, vocals), Miss Guided (guitar, vocals) — alongside two members credited under real or full names: Chris Thompson (15) (vocals) and Nicky Hallam (vocals, guitar). The real identities of Miss Directed and C. Slug are confirmed in Discogs via their member entries: Ian Carnochan (better known as Knox of The Vibrators) and Jonathan William Edwards respectively. Miss Guided was Greg Van Cook, also a member of The Vibrators and The Electric Chairs.

The single was reissued in 2009 on Sing Sing Records (3) (cat# SING 001), a US imprint, with track durations documented for the first time (A-side: 3:05, B-side: 3:04). “Can’t Understand” was later included on Punk 45 Vol. 2 - There Is No Such Thing As Society (Soul Jazz Records, 2013/2014), where it appears as track 23.

Connections and Context

Three of the five members were concurrently active in The Vibrators, the London punk group that formed in 1976. Ian Carnochan (Knox) was the Vibrators’ lead guitarist and a primary songwriter — his discography through the band runs from Pure Mania (1977) to the 2020s. Jon Edwards (C. Slug) drummed on Pure Mania (1977) and V2 (1978) before leaving. Greg Van Cook (Miss Guided) played guitar with both the Vibrators and Wayne County’s backing group The Electric Chairs / Wayne County And The Back Street Boys in the late 1970s.

The pseudonyms are consistent with the broader UK punk era practice of stage names, though in this case all three Vibrators members adopted new aliases specifically for this project. The band’s Vibrators connection places the 1979 recording squarely within the same circle as one of the UK’s earliest signed punk acts.

The Soul Jazz Punk 45 Vol. 2 compilation framed “Can’t Understand” alongside a range of UK post-punk and DIY punk from 1977–81, positioning Roses Are Red in the same curatorial bracket as Josef K, Swell Maps, The Mekons, The Cravats, The Prefects, and others on that collection. The band’s geographic origin within the UK is not specified in any Discogs record.
