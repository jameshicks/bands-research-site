God Is My Co-Pilot

Discogs ID: 273481
Origin: New York City, USA
Active: 1991–2019 (per Discogs; possibly ongoing)
Also known as: GodCo, G-d Is My Co-Pilot, God Is My Copilot
Label (own): The Making Of Americans

Overview

God Is My Co-Pilot (commonly shortened to GodCo) is a New York-based experimental/avant-punk ensemble built around the permanent core of guitarist/multi-instrumentalist Craig Flanagin (Discogs ID: 273482) and vocalist/multi-instrumentalist Sharon Topper (Discogs ID: 484560). Around that axis they assembled a revolving cast of up to 30-odd collaborators at any given time, including saxophonists, cellists, percussionists, and guest vocalists drawn from the downtown New York and international avant-garde scenes.

Their music draws on punk, no wave, hardcore, free jazz, Klezmer and Yiddish folk, Finnish song, Irish trad, and queered pop — often in the same release. Track lengths are deliberately brief (many under two minutes), the aesthetic is deliberately lo-fi and confrontational, and the lyrical territory centers on gender, sexuality, queerness, and bodily politics. Sharon Topper/Flanagin formed the group in 1991 and self-released their debut 7” on their own imprint The Making Of Americans, which they ran throughout their most active period.

Biography by Era

Formation and First Releases (1991–1992)

The band formed in New York City around 1991. The debut 7” Songs of Praise (The Making Of Americans, 1991) was a four-song statement with Craig Flanagin on guitar/vocals, Sharon Topper on vocals, James Garrison on bass, Michael Evans on drums, and Siobhan Duffy on drums/vocals — produced by Flanagin and Don Fury. All tracks run under two minutes.

In 1992 they released four albums in rapid succession, an unusual burst that established their methodology: densely packed song-per-song montages recorded with shifting lineups. Speed Yr Trip (Les Disques Du Soleil Et De L’Acier, DSA 54237; also on The Making Of Americans MA-07) and I Am Not This Body (Les Disques Du Soleil Et De L’Acier, DSA 54236; also on The Making Of Americans MA-04) appeared on the French label. Gender Is As Gender Does came out on Funky Mushroom Records (FM-008), and How I Got Over on Ajax Records (2) (AJAX 022), the latter featuring Jad Fair and John Zorn alongside Flanagin. The French releases were their European breakthrough; Les Disques Du Soleil et de l’Acier was run by Pascal Blua and was an important venue for the downtown NY scene in France.

Personnel on I Am Not This Body (34 tracks, CD): Garrison/bass, Michael Evans/drums, Rick Brown/drums & vocals, Siobhan Duffy/drums & vocals, Craig Flanagin/guitar, drums & vocals, Gen Ken Montgomery/violin, Sharon Topper/vocals.

Prolific Middle Period (1993–1995)

1993 saw another cluster of releases across several labels, anchored by two LPs of note:

  • Straight Not (Outpunk Records, OUT 8V) — their most explicitly queercore-identified release, issued on Matt Wobensmith’s San Francisco queercore label. A 26-track LP with a cast that includes Elliott Sharp (bass), Fredrick Lonberg-Holm (cello), Anthony Coleman (sampler/keyboards), Andy Haas (didgeridoo/sax), Louis Belogenis (sax), Christine Bard, David Simons, Siobhan Duffy, and Sharon Topper (drums). It was also released on cassette (OUT 8CA) and CD (OUT 8CD), and a Japanese pressing appeared on MELDAC (MECI-25017).
  • My Sinister Secret Agenda (Blackout! Records, BL-17) — five tracks, 7” 33⅓ RPM, featuring the core band plus Andy Haas on didgeridoo on “Sound Of Wings.” Also appeared on Blackout! in multiple variant pressings.
  • Pissing & Hooting (The Making Of Americans, MA-06) — 7” EP with Jad Fair, Elliott Sharp, Alex Klein, Michael Evans, Siobhan Duffy, Craig Flanagin, Sharon Topper; cover art by Julie Doucet. Contains a cover of Hank Williams’ “Lost Highway” and a song with text by Elliott Sharp.
  • Tight Like Fist: Live Recording (Knitting Factory Works, KFW 148 / Factory Outlet) — 1993 live document; a Japanese edition appeared on Factory Outlet (TKCB-70401).
  • What Doctors Don’t Tell You (Shrimper, SHR 46) — cassette release.
  • Getting Out Of Boring Time Biting Into Boring Pie (Quinnah Records, Q-2) — 1993.

Also notable from 1993: Split Single (Outpunk, OUT9) with Fifth Column.

In 1994 GodCo deepened their Japanese connections with two important releases:

  • Mir Shlufn Nisht = 眠らない (Avant, AVAN 032) — a full CD drawing heavily on Yiddish, Hebrew, Finnish, Irish, and other folk sources, arranged and rearranged by the band. Executive produced by John Zorn and Kazunori Sugiyama (Avant label head). Credits include Alex Klein, Fredrick Lonberg-Holm, Andy Haas, Siobhan Duffy, Michael Evans, Laura Cromwell, Don Fury (engineer), Elliott Sharp (lap steel guitar), Anthony Coleman (sampling keyboard), Samara Lubelski (fiddle), Louis Belogenis (sax), Mia Keinanen, Tanja Lahtinen (vocals on some songs). The title translates to “We Don’t Sleep.”
  • Melt-Banana / God Is My Co-Pilot (HG Fact, HG-024) — split release with Tokyo noise-punk duo Melt-Banana.
  • How To Be (The Making Of Americans, MA 11) — 23-track CD, one of their most sprawling releases: credits include Jad Fair, Elliott Sharp, Fredrick Lonberg-Holm, Anthony Coleman, Andy Haas, Louis Belogenis, Siobhan Duffy, Christine Bard, Chris Pellettieri, David Simons, Tanja Lahtinen, Mia Keinanen, and Fly (9) among others.
  • Sex Is For Making Babies (Les Disques Du Soleil Et De L’Acier, CDSA 54033 / DSA 54033) — French CD.
  • Sharon Quite Fancies Jo EP (Soul Static Sound, Soul 03) — UK release.
  • This Is No Time To Be Frail! (Rough Trade, 45REV 29) — UK 7” single.
  • More Pretty Girls Than One (Hedonist Productions, CAKE 003F) — UK release.
  • ¿Ootko Sä Poika Vai Tyttö? (Trash Can Records (3), CAN-16) — Finnish 7”.

1995 brought a major release in Puss O2 (Dark Beloved Cloud / The Making Of Americans, DBC202 / MA-20), a 29-track CD co-released with Dark Beloved Cloud. Notable for guest appearances by Chan Marshall (Cat Power) on “Pocketful of Sugar,” “Pretty Spoiled,” “Teenage Boyfriend,” and “Numbness & Tingling”; Yoshimi (Boredoms) on “Dance,” “Slow Dismemberment,” “Pretty Spoiled,” “Puss In Boots,” and “Teenage Boyfriend”; and Danny Lilker (Brutal Truth/Nuclear Assault) on “Numbness & Tingling,” “Puss In Boots,” and “Can’t Be What I Am.” A Japanese edition appeared on MELDAC (MECI-25042).

Also in 1995: Neko No Akubi: Nihon No Fi (The Making Of Americans, MA-14) and Grow Your Own Country Wide Primary School Band E.P. (Guided Missile, UK, Guide 003).

MELDAC released Japanese editions of several back-catalog titles throughout 1995–1996, plus two compilations: History Of Music: Volume One, 1989-1991 (MELDAC, MECI-25046, 1995) and History Of Music: Volume Two 1991-1993 (MELDAC, MECI-25063, 1996).

UK, Peel Sessions, and Best-Of (1996)

The 1996 The Peel Sessions (Strange Fruit, SFRSCD004) documents BBC radio sessions recorded with three different line-up configurations, each with a different BBC producer (Mike Robinson, Miti Adhikari, Adam Askew). Line-up rotations included Margaret Fiedler (guitar/vocals), Ann Rupel (bass/organ/vocals), Fly (9) (bass/jew’s harp/vocals), Dan Brown (drums), Christine Bard (drums), Michael Evans (drums), and Siobhan Duffy (drums), alongside Flanagin and Topper. Marion Coutts (trumpet/vocals) contributed to several sessions. The CD is credited with design by Pretty Pictures and photography by Marion Coutts and Sharon Topper.

Also in 1996: The Best Of God Is My Co-Pilot (Atavistic, ALP82CD) — a 22-track compilation on the Chicago experimental label. It features tracks spanning their catalog including a cover of The Fall’s “Totally Wired,” a cover of Chumbawamba’s “Behave,” and a Turkish traditional song (“Kendine Gel”). Also includes the 7-minute “Queer Disco Anthem (Remix)” and “Behave (Remix).” Don Fury recorded several tracks (credited on the sleeve).

Late 1990s (1997–1999)

  • Children Can Be So Cruel (Miguel (2) / Hemiola Records, 1997) — Switzerland.
  • Je Suis Trop Content - A Goddess Micropilot Tour CD (Dark Beloved Cloud, DBC210, 1997) — tour CD.
  • An Appeal To Reason (Runt, #03, 1997) — Italy.
  • Get Busy (Atavistic, alp87cd, 1998) — their second Atavistic full-length, 14 tracks. Regular lineup features Fly (9) and Daria Klotz on bass, Fredrik Haake on drums, with Craig Flanagin (guitar) and Sharon Topper (vocals/clarinet/keyboards). Guest credits include Edward Ratliff (trombone/trumpet/euphonium) and Gilles Rieder (sampler). More electronic and textured than earlier work, still multilingual.
  • Split EP (Music ‘A La Coque, #01, 1998) — Italy, split with Bz Bz Ueu.
  • Erase Yer Head # 9 (Pandemonium Records (2), PAN 036, 1999) — France, a four-way split 7” with Melt-Banana, Camp Blackfoot, and Tears Of A Doll. GodCo contributes “On Death.”

Hiatus and Return (2018–2019)

After a long gap in the primary discography, two releases on Mintimacy (their own label) appeared late in the band’s documented activity:

  • shakemore 2018 k7 (Mintimacy, 2018) — cassette featuring Kevin Shea (drums), Normandy Sherwood, Jason Blackkat, Hajnal Pivnick, Kate Gentile among the credits.
  • Muttering Under the Bunny Wedge (Mintimacy, 2019) — the last release in the Discogs discography.

Side Projects

Both core members are also members of The Hattifatteners (Discogs ID: 534195), a related ensemble also featuring Tanja Lahtinen, Alexa Firat, James Kavoussi, and Tanya Small. The Hattifatteners have releases on Dark Beloved Cloud.

Craig Flanagin also appeared as guitarist on John Zorn’s Cobra: Live At The Knitting Factory (Knitting Factory Works, 1995) and on various one-off sessions.
