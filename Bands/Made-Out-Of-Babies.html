Made Out Of Babies

Discogs ID: 399334 Active: ~2003–2012 Origin: New York City (Brooklyn)

New York noise rock / post-hardcore band fronted by Julie Christmas. Abrasive, heavy sound; released four records on Neurot Recordings and The End Records.

Dissolution (2008–2012)

After The Ruiner (June 2008), the band went on an extended hiatus during which Julie Christmas released her solo album The Bad Wife (2010). On March 13, 2012, Christmas announced via Facebook that the band was “no longer together.” [source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_Out_of_Babies — as of 2026-06-01]

The most substantive public explanation for the split came from guitarist Brendan Tobin in a 2012 press release for his subsequent band Bad Powers: “We no longer felt the same about songwriting (structure, intent, complexity, tone).” The statement frames the dissolution as a breakdown in creative alignment between Christmas and the other members (Tobin, Cooper (6), and Matthew Egan) regarding song structure, intent, complexity, and tone — rather than any specific incident or interpersonal conflict. [source: https://www.prescriptionmusicpruk.com/press-releases/2012/9/25/bad-powers-album-out-12-november-on-the-end-records.html]

Tobin, Cooper, and Egan subsequently formed Bad Powers with new vocalist Megan Tweed (ex-Family Curse) and released a self-titled debut on The End Records in September 2012. Tobin noted of Tweed’s arrival: “Once Megan was in place it all suddenly felt right.” [source: https://www.prescriptionmusicpruk.com/press-releases/2012/9/25/bad-powers-album-out-12-november-on-the-end-records.html; also reported at https://www.spin.com/2012/08/hear-bad-powers-new-band-featuring-34ths-made-out-babies/]

No public statement from Christmas herself elaborates on her reasons for leaving beyond the Facebook dissolution announcement. In a later interview (Soundsphere), she discussed her solo work and artistic evolution but did not address the breakup directly. [source: https://www.soundspheremag.com/features/interviews/julie-christmas-discusses-new-album-ridiculous-and-full-of-blood/]

Notes (web)

The four-year gap between The Ruiner (2008) and the 2012 announcement was noted by contemporary observers as a signal that something was amiss — one fan blog noted as early as April 2012 that the gap itself (“only Christmas’s solo work in between”) suggested internal problems predating the formal announcement. [source: https://stillfinditsohard.wordpress.com/2012/04/03/i-still-can-hardly-believe-it-but-julie-christmas-said-it/ — fan blog, weak source]

The bellaonline.com interview (pre-Coward, c. 2006) reveals that Christmas and Tobin had previously been a couple and broke up before reuniting to form the band — context that may be relevant to the eventual creative divergence, though no source connects the two directly. [source: http://www.bellaonline.com/articles/art21644.asp]
