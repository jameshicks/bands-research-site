Queenadreena

Discogs ID: 169845
Also credited as: Queen Adreena
Origin: UK
Active: 1998–2008 (hiatus), reformations thereafter
Genre: Post-punk, noise-pop, art rock, indie rock
Core members: Katie Jane Garside (vocals), Crispin Gray (guitar)

Overview

Queenadreena formed in 1998 when Katie Jane Garside and Crispin Gray — the core duo of Daisy Chainsaw — reconvened under a new name after Daisy Chainsaw dissolved. The project carried forward the raw, confrontational aesthetic of their previous band into darker territory: noise-pop abrasiveness, theatrical confessional lyrics, and Garside’s wildly expressive, often harrowing vocal style. The duo remained the constant creative axis across four studio albums and various line-ups of session and touring musicians.

Their debut album Taxidermy (2000) and its lead single “Jolene” — a cover of Dolly Parton’s song — established their sound and drew comparisons to acts like PJ Harvey and early-period Hole while maintaining a uniquely unsettling, post-industrial character. A collaboration with Daisy Chainsaw’s earlier producer Ken Thomas grounded the first phase of their work. They moved to Rough Trade for their second album Drink Me (2002), then to One Little Indian — Daisy Chainsaw’s original home — for The Butcher And The Butterfly (2005). The final studio album Djin (2008) appeared on Japan’s Imperial Records. A self-released compilation Ride A Cock Horse (2007) collected rarities, demos and re-recordings.

The band is also noted for composing a live soundtrack to David Cronenberg’s film adaptation of Crash.

Background: Daisy Chainsaw

Daisy Chainsaw — the duo’s preceding band — was active from approximately 1989 and signed to One Little Indian and Deva Records (a One Little Indian sublabel). Their debut album Eleventeen (1992) was produced with Ken Thomas as engineer and co-producer, with the band self-producing several tracks and Garside producing others. A follow-up, For They Know Not What They Do, emerged in 1994 on FFRR / London Records. The band dissolved shortly after, with Garside and Gray reconvening as Queenadreena by 1998.

Discography

Pre-album singles (Blanco Y Negro, 1999–2000)

Queenadreena signed to Blanco Y Negro (a Warner Music UK imprint) and released a run of singles before the debut album. The debut single “Cold Fish / Yesterday’s Hymn” (1999) appeared as a limited CD single and 5” picture disc. A promo sampler I’ll Lock Her Up Inside Safe And Warm (1999) preceded the album. The singles “X-ing Off The Days / A Heavenly Surrender” and “I Adore You / Weeds” followed in 1999–2000.

Taxidermy (2000, Blanco Y Negro)

Discogs release ID: 903816
Cat no: 8573806622 (Blanco Y Negro / Warner)
Format: CD (Album; Enhanced) — includes unlisted video
Genres/styles: Rock — Experimental, Indie Rock, Industrial

Queenadreena’s debut album. Credited on sleeve as “Queen Adreena.” Co-produced by Ken Thomas and the band. The album’s sound sits between noise-pop and post-punk, with Gray’s guitar-glockenspiel-harmonica palette providing texture against Garside’s intense vocal performances. “Jolene” (a Dolly Parton cover) and “Pretty Polly” (traditional) were concurrent single releases; “Pretty Polly” is an arrangement credited to Gray and Garside. On “Sleepwalking,” Ken Thomas plays drums. On “Hide From Time,” Andy Waterworth plays double bass.

”Jolene / Pretty Polly” single (2000, Blanco Y Negro)

Discogs release ID: 1830620
Cat no: NEG128CDX

A cover of Dolly Parton’s “Jolene” with post-production and mixing by Alan Moulder. “Pretty Polly” is the album version (traditional, arranged by Gray and Garside). The single’s video was directed by Martina Hoogland Ivanow. Co-produced by Ken Thomas and the band. This is the release the broader Ken Thomas / Queenadreena association is most often noted around.

Drink Me (2002, Rough Trade)

Discogs release ID: 234632
Cat no: RTRADECD 043
Format: CD (Album)
Genres/styles: Rock — Alternative Rock, Art Rock, Indie Rock, Noise

Second album; label move to Rough Trade. Production was split across two teams: tracks 1–6 and 9 produced by Morgan Nicholls and Peter Reardon (mixed by Reardon), while tracks 7, 8, 10 and 11 were produced by Ken Thomas and the band (mixed by Thomas and the band). The additional instrument credits on tracks 10 and 11 are by Jason Harris. The band had expanded to a four-piece for the record, with Orson Wajih on bass and Pete Howard on drums (both also received songwriting credits on several tracks).

The Butcher And The Butterfly (2005, One Little Indian)

Discogs release ID: 428617
Cat no: TPLP476 CDP
Format: CD (Album; Promo)
Genres/styles: Rock — Indie Rock

Third studio album; return to One Little Indian, the label that had signed Daisy Chainsaw. Produced entirely by Paul Corkett, departing from the Ken Thomas involvement on earlier releases. Corkett’s background with acts like The Cure and Placebo fits the album’s somewhat more polished post-punk palette. At 16 tracks it is the band’s longest album.
