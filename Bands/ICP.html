ICP (Instant Composers Pool)

Discogs ID: 65739 Real name: Instant Composers Pool Name variations: I.C.P., Instant Composer Pool, Instant Composers Pool, The Instant Composers Pool Country: Netherlands Active: 1967–present Website: http://www.icporchestra.com/

Overview

The Instant Composers Pool (ICP) is a Dutch free improvisation collective and organizational entity founded in Amsterdam in 1967 by drummer Han Bennink, pianist Misha Mengelberg, and reedist Willem Breuker. Inspired by the Fluxus art movement, the ICP was established as a vehicle for avant-garde Dutch jazz performance and recording. It is simultaneously an organization, a record label (the Instant Composers Pool label, Discogs ID: 99922), and — in its most publicly visible current form — a trio and the larger ICP Orchestra.

Discogs separates “ICP” (artist ID 65739, the organizational entity) from “ICP Orchestra” (artist ID 1669446, the larger ensemble). The two are closely related but technically distinct on the database: ICP as an entity tends to appear in credits on early releases and on releases where the full trio (Bennink, Baars, Wierbos) performs without the extended Orchestra. The ICP Orchestra involves a rotating cast of up to fifteen or more musicians.

The trio’s current core membership — as documented on the 2002 In The Fishtank 9 release — is Han Bennink (drums), Ab Baars (reeds), and Wolter Wierbos (trombone). Misha Mengelberg was a founding member and the principal composer of the ICP Orchestra’s repertoire; Willem Breuker co-founded the organization but departed to form his own Willem Breuker Kollektief.

The ICP self-released all its output on the Instant Composers Pool label, which began with ICP 001 in 1967 and continued to at least ICP 069 (2025). The label functions as a direct-to-audience operation with no major-label affiliation.

Founding and Early Period (1967–1976)

The ICP was formed in 1967. The first label release, New Acoustic Swing Duo (ICP 001, 1967), was a duet of Bennink and Breuker. The second release, Instant Composers Pool (ICP 002, 1968), was a trio with Bennink, Mengelberg, and Danish-American saxophonist John Tchicai.

Early releases engaged a broad network of European free jazz figures. ICP 006, Groupcomposing (1978), brought together Mengelberg, Peter Brötzmann, Evan Parker, Paul Rutherford (2), Derek Bailey, Peter Bennink, and Han Bennink in a collective improvisation credited to “The Instant Composers Pool” as producer. ICP 004, Han Bennink & Derek Bailey (1971), documented the important Bennink / Bailey duo partnership (which later generated further releases on Incus Records).

Breuker left to form his own organization (Willem Breuker Kollektief) during the early 1970s. The ICP Tentet — a rotating large ensemble — recorded Tetterettet (ICP 020, 1977), with a lineup including Brötzmann, Tchicai, Tristan Honsinger, Alan Silva, Michel Waisvisz (electronics/Crackle Box), and others, with compositions by Mengelberg, Bert Koppelaar, and Gilius Van Bergeijk.

ICP Orchestra (1979–present)

The ensemble that became the ICP Orchestra crystallized in the late 1970s. Its first release under that name appears as Live Soncino (ICP 022, 1979, AdLib/ICP), a live Italian recording. The Orchestra’s most widely-pressed release is Bospaadje Konijnehol I (ICP label, 1992), which documents a lineup including Bennink (drums), Mengelberg (piano), Ab Baars (reeds), Michael Moore (4) (reeds), Wolter Wierbos (trombone), George Lewis (trombone, on select tracks), Ernst Glerum (double bass), Evert Hekkema (trumpet), Ernst Reijseger (cello), Maartje Ten Hoorn (violin), and Maurice Horsthuis (viola). This release has 27 pressings in Discogs — the most of any ICP Orchestra release.

The ICP Orchestra as a Discogs entity (ID 1669446) carries its own separate discography and membership list. Members over time documented in Discogs include: Ab Baars, Ernst Reijseger, Tristan Honsinger, Michael Moore (4), Guus Janssen, Han Bennink, Misha Mengelberg, Wolter Wierbos, Thomas Heberer, Ernst Glerum, Garrett List, George Lewis, Peter Brötzmann, Steve Lacy, Sean Bergin, Tobias Delius, Toshinori Kondo, Keshavan Maslak, and many others.

Instant Composers Pool Label (ICP Label)

The Instant Composers Pool label (Discogs ID: 99922; 122 releases) is the organizational and commercial backbone of ICP. Founded alongside the collective in 1967, it operates without a parent company and has released all primary ICP, ICP Orchestra, and related-artist output.

In The Fishtank 9 (2002)

ICP’s highest-profile international crossover credit is In The Fishtank 9 (Konkurrent, 2002, cat no. 9), a three-way collaboration with Sonic Youth and The Ex recorded under the Fishtank format: two days in a Dutch studio, all improvised. The album was engineered, mixed, and mastered by Zlaya Hadzich at Studio Loud, Amsterdam. Artwork and design by Isabelle Vigier, with additional artwork by Jos Kley.

The ICP lineup on this recording was the current trio — Bennink (drums), Baars (reeds), Wierbos (trombone) — rather than the full Orchestra. The Sonic Youth lineup was Kim Gordon (absent from this recording), Lee Ranaldo (guitar), Thurston Moore (guitar), Steve Shelley (drums), Jim O’Rourke (electronics), and William Winant (percussion). The Ex contributed Luc Ex (bass guitar) and Terrie Ex (guitar). All nine participants are credited as composers of all music.

The eight tracks are titled by Roman numerals (II–X, skipping I and VII), running approximately 30 minutes total. Reissued on LP (2013). The release is catalogued in the Jazz and Rock genres with styles of Experimental, Free Improvisation, and Noise Rock.
