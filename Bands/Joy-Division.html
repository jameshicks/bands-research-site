Joy Division

Post-punk / new wave. Salford / Manchester. Active 1976–1980. Discogs artist ID: 3898.

Formed 1976 in Salford as Stiff Kittens (2) (Discogs ID: 695777), renamed Warsaw (3) (Discogs ID: 310128) for most of 1977, then renamed Joy Division at the start of 1978 before their debut release. Dissolved on the suicide of vocalist Ian Curtis on 18 May 1980. The three surviving members regrouped as New Order.

Formation and Early History (1976–1977)

The band formed in Salford in 1976. Their first gig flyer listed them as Stiff Kittens (a name on a bill with Buzzcocks and Penetration (2)), but they announced themselves onstage as Warsaw — the name under which they operated through 1977. Terry Mason served as early manager and roadie; Steve Brotherdale drummed briefly before Stephen Morris joined.

Rob Gretton encountered them at Manchester’s Rafters club and became their manager — a relationship that lasted until Curtis’s death and continued with the surviving members into the New Order era.

The name change to Joy Division occurred at the start of 1978. (The name derives from the prostitution wing in a Nazi concentration camp, from Ka-tzetnik’s 1955 novel House of Dolls.)

Factory Records Context

Factory Records was founded by Tony Wilson and Alan Erasmus in Manchester in 1978. Joy Division were the label’s foundational act — Unknown Pleasures (1979) and Closer (1980) defined Factory’s sonic identity and global reputation. The label never signed conventional contracts; artists retained their master rights.

Martin Hannett served as in-house producer and was responsible for the characteristic Factory sound: extreme drum separation, heavy digital delay (AMS units), vast reverb, and the blending of electronic and acoustic elements. He produced every Joy Division studio track released during the band’s lifetime. See Martin Hannett for the full production history including his acrimonious split from Factory in 1982.

Peter Saville (2) designed all Factory-era sleeves. The Unknown Pleasures pulsar diagram and the stark typographic grid of Closer became canonical examples of post-punk graphic design.

BBC / Radio Sessions

Joy Division recorded two John Peel sessions and other BBC appearances. The 1978 session (recorded Jan 1979) and a 1979 session are collected on The Complete BBC Recordings (Strange Fruit / BBC, 2000; Discogs 128630). Bob Sargeant produced some Peel session recordings. John Peel was an early champion; Peel sessions were recorded at Maida Vale Studios, London.

Post-Joy Division: New Order

Following Curtis’s death, the surviving three members had agreed — by a prior pact — that if any member left, the group would change its name. With Gillian Gilbert joining on keyboards and guitar, they became New Order in late 1980. Martin Hannett produced New Order’s debut LP Movement (FACT 50, 1981). New Order subsequently moved away from Hannett and self-produced their subsequent catalog, developing the electronic dance aesthetic that made them Factory’s commercial backbone through the 1980s.
