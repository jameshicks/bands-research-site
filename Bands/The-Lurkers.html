Overview

The Lurkers are an English punk rock band formed in 1976 in Fulham, West London, initially managed through the Beggars Banquet retail operation on North End Road. Their catalogue runs from 1977 into the 2020s, with the bulk concentrated in the late 1970s (ten releases that decade), and spans punk, new wave, and garage rock. Beggars Banquet remained their primary label relationship across four decades. Since 2010 the band has effectively split into two concurrent lineups: one led by original member Arturo and another — The Lurkers GLM, comprising Esso, Stride, and Moore — both recording and performing under the Lurkers name.

Notes (web) — 2010 split: Arturo vs Lurkers GLM

The split has roots going back further than 2010, but 2010 is when Esso, Stride and Moore began operating publicly as a separate entity.

Arturo’s account (two sources):

After the 1988 German reunion, Pete Stride expected the band to stop once he withdrew. Arturo had driven the van, sold merchandise, secured gigs and record deals, and co-written material for the comeback album. When Stride declined to continue touring — including pulling out of already-contracted Spain dates — Arturo proceeded without him. Arturo told Brighton and Hove News (2019): “Pete Stride left twenty-five years ago and expected me to stop the group. I drove the van, sold the T-shirts, got the gigs, got the record deals, and wrote half of the comeback album. Because he didn’t want to do it anymore he expected me to give up my livelihood!” The Punky Gibbon interview (undated, accessed 2026) adds that after the Spain commitment Arturo honoured without Stride, “from then on he wouldn’t play live again and hasn’t done with anyone.” Arturo characterises Stride’s position not as hostility but as simply not liking touring: “he just didn’t like gigging much anyway.” https://www.brightonandhovenews.org/2019/07/29/exclusive-interview-with-arturo-bassick-from-the-lurkers-and-999/ · https://punkygibbon.co.uk/bands/l/lurkers_interview.html

Pete Stride’s account (search snippet from Uber Rock interview, full text inaccessible):

Stride objected strongly to Arturo continuing under The Lurkers name. Per the Uber Rock interview snippet: “I really don’t like it at all. It’s actually quite painful for me. It feels like someone has taken a big part of my life, especially as he was only in the original band for about six months before he decided he didn’t like it and left.” When Stride, Moore and Esso later resumed recording as Lurkers GLM / The Lurkers GLM, Stride justified their own use of the name on the basis that they were original members who created and named the band. Source: search-result snippet from https://uber-rock.co.uk/pete-stride-nigel-moore-the-lurkers-glm-uber-rock-interview-exclusive/ (full page inaccessible, JS-heavy; snippet only).

Summary of dispute: The core disagreement is over who holds legitimate claim to The Lurkers name. Stride’s position: he wrote the songs, named the band, and was a founding original member; Arturo’s tenure in the original lineup was brief (reportedly ~six months). Arturo’s position: he kept the band active through decades of touring, management, and recording after Stride walked away, and had a livelihood stake in the name. No third-party arbitration or legal resolution has been reported in available sources. The GLM trio (Stride, Moore, Esso) initially distanced themselves from the dispute by recording as “God’s Lonely Men” / “The Lurkers: God’s Lonely Men” from 2010, releasing Chemical Landslide (2012) under that banner, before reverting to The Lurkers name. Source for GLM timeline: search-result snippet from https://www.codamusic.me/artists/the-lurkers/ and https://formalcontentsonly.wordpress.com/tag/the-lurkers-the-lurkers-glm-manic-esso/.
