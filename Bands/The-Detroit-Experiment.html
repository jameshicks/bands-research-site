History & Context

The Detroit Experiment emerged from Carl Craig’s ambition to frame Detroit’s jazz heritage inside an electronic and hip-hop production aesthetic. Craig — by 2002 already a decade into his career as one of Detroit techno’s defining architects (Planet E Communications, aliases 69, Paperclip People, Innerzone Orchestra) — convened a multi-generational ensemble that read like a cross-section of the city’s musical lineage.

The anchor figures on the jazz side were three Detroit veterans with deep national credentials: Marcus Belgrave (trumpet), who had recorded with Charles Mingus, Ray Charles, and Clifford Brown before becoming a pillar of the Detroit scene; Bennie Maupin (tenor sax, bass clarinet), known from Herbie Hancock’s Mwandishi and Headhunters records and Miles Davis’s Bitches Brew; and Geri Allen (piano), whose post-bop work placed her in the front rank of jazz pianists of her generation. Regina Carter (violin) brought a distinctive voice from her work with the String Trio of New York and beyond.

On the hip-hop and electronic side, Amp Fiddler (Fender Rhodes, vocals, keyboards, synthesizers) — a pivotal figure in both Detroit soul and the wider neo-soul world, and a key early influence on J Dilla — served as the main electronic/soul bridge. Karriem Riggins (drums, tambourine) brought hip-hop-inflected rhythmic sensibility alongside his jazz credentials (Ray Brown Trio, later Jahari Massamba Unit with Madlib). Athletic Mic League, a Detroit hip-hop collective, contributed backing vocals.

Francisco Mora Catlett (percussion) connected threads further — he was also a member of Innerzone Orchestra, Craig’s earlier jazz-techno project with Belgrave.

The result was a large-ensemble record that avoided easy genre description. Discogs tags it Electronic / Deep House / Future Jazz, but the vinyl 12” single placed it alongside Conscious, Contemporary Jazz, Funk, and Space-Age tags — the breadth itself is the point.

Web Sources

Research conducted 2026-07-25. Question: did J Dilla have any direct connection to The Detroit Experiment sessions?

Finding: No direct connection confirmed. Searches across press coverage, album reviews, artist interviews, and Wikipedia returned no mention of J Dilla (Jay Dee) in relation to The Detroit Experiment album or sessions. He is absent from all credited personnel in every source consulted.

The context for the question is real: Amp Fiddler mentored J Dilla (introducing him to the MPC in the early 1990s), and Karriem Riggins had an active working relationship with Dilla from 1996 onward (Riggins played drums on Dilla’s Welcome 2 Detroit and The Shining). Both Fiddler and Riggins appear on The Detroit Experiment LP. However, this mutual orbit does not extend to Dilla appearing on the project.

The clearest corroborating source is a Grammy.com interview with Carl Craig in which he describes The Detroit Experiment’s participants — naming Bennie Maupin, Karriem Riggins, Marcus Belgrave, Francisco Mora, and Amp Fiddler — with no mention of J Dilla. Craig discusses J Dilla separately in the same interview, treating him as a distinct figure in the Detroit music ecosystem rather than a participant in this project. Grammy.com — Record Store Recs: Carl Craig

Assessment: The absence is consistent across every source. Given that the LP credits are detailed and corroborated by multiple independent reviews and Craig’s own description of the project, the most defensible conclusion is that J Dilla was not a participant. His connection to this project is indirect — via the Amp Fiddler mentorship and the Riggins friendship — but there is no evidence of session involvement, production credit, or even an informal guest appearance.
