The Psychedelic Furs

The Psychedelic Furs

Oct 21, 20266 min read

The Psychedelic Furs

Discogs ID: 125410
Origin: London, UK
Active: 1977–1991, reformed 2000–present
Labels: CBS, Columbia, EastWest, Cooking Vinyl

Post-punk / new wave band formed in London by brothers Richard Butler (vocals) and Tim Butler (bass). Founding lineup included John Ashton (guitar), Roger Morris (guitar), Duncan Kilburn (saxophone, keyboards), and Vince Ely (drums). Produced across their career by Steve Lillywhite, Todd Rundgren, Keith Forsey, Chris Kimsey, David M. Allen, and Stephen Street.

Biography

Formation and Early Releases (1977–1980)

The Psychedelic Furs formed in London in 1977 around the Butler brothers. Their first release was the single “We Love You” (1979, Epic), produced by Howard Thompson and Ian Taylor alongside the band. Lineup: Tim Butler (bass), Richard Butler (vocals), John Ashton (guitar), Roger Morris (guitar), Duncan Kilburn (saxophone), Vince Ely (drums). The band subsequently signed to CBS.

Their self-titled debut album (1980, CBS/Columbia) was split between two producers: Steve Lillywhite handled the majority of tracks (A1–A4, B2, B3 on the European pressing), while Howard Thompson, Ian Taylor, and the band themselves produced three tracks (A5, B1, B4). The US pressing had a different tracklist and running order, with Martin Hannett producing tracks A3 and B1 — making this one of Hannett’s most commercially prominent credits outside the Factory Records/Manchester orbit. Phil Thornalley engineered both versions; Wolf Gosling/Will Gosling assisted. Executive producer: Howard Thompson.

Talk Talk Talk (1981)

The second album, again produced by Lillywhite, contains “Pretty In Pink” (later to become far more prominent) and was the last record with the full original lineup. Roger Morris and Duncan Kilburn departed shortly after.

Forever Now (1982)

Produced by Todd Rundgren at his Utopia Sound Studio. Backing vocals from Flo & Eddie (Howard Kaylan and Mark Volman). Todd Rundgren himself played keyboards and saxophone solo on “No Easy Street.” “Love My Way” reached the US Top 50.

Mirror Moves (1984)

Produced by Keith Forsey. Mars Williams of The Waitresses joined on saxophone. Drummer Paul Garisto played on several tracks alongside Forsey (who doubled as drummer). Contains “The Ghost In You” and “Heaven,” two of their most enduring songs. Vince Ely had departed by this point.

Midnight To Midnight (1987)

Produced by Chris Kimsey. Paul Garisto on drums; Mars Williams on horns and saxophone; Jon Carin on keyboards; Ed Buller on additional keyboards; Marty Williamson on 2nd guitar. Mixed by Steve Thompson & Michael Barbiero (most tracks); Kimsey mixed B3 and B5 directly. Mastered by George Marino. Art direction and design by Nick Egan. Contains “Heartbreak Beat,” their biggest US hit (US Top 30). Re-recorded “Pretty In Pink” appears as a bonus track.

Book Of Days (1989)

Produced by David M. Allen. Vince Ely returned. Additional musicians included Knox Chandler, Joe McGinty, Jem Finer (of The Pogues), Emily Burridge, Gena Dry, John Hymas, Anthony Thistlethwaite.

World Outside (1991) and Hiatus

Produced by Stephen Street (who also engineered). The credited performers on World Outside are: Richard Butler, Tim Butler, John Ashton, Knox Chandler, Joe McGinty, and Don Yallech. Artwork/design by Vaughan Oliver; photography by Simon Larbalestier; mastered by George Marino. After the album the Furs went on hiatus; the Butler brothers formed Love Spit Love, which released two albums (1994, 1997). Richard Butler also released a solo album in 2005 (Koch Records).

Return and Made Of Rain (2000–present)

Reformed in 2000 for touring; live albums Beautiful Chaos and Live From House Of Blues appeared in 2001. Made Of Rain (2020, Cooking Vinyl) was their first studio album in 29 years. Produced by Richard Fortus (also guitarist on the record) with additional production from David Maurice; mixed by Tim Palmer. Lineup: Richard Butler (vocals), Tim Butler (bass), Paul Garisto (drums/percussion), Richard Good (guitar), Amanda Kramer (keyboards), Mars Williams (saxophone).

Open Questions

  • Roger Morris departure date (between Talk Talk Talk 1981 and Forever Now 1982) not confirmed from Discogs
  • Duncan Kilburn departure date similarly unconfirmed
  • Vince Ely status on Mirror Moves: Discogs does not list him in the credits — Keith Forsey played drums on most tracks, with Thommy Price on A2 and A3. Ely returned for Book Of Days (1989).
  • Shock (1987, Columbia) — confirmed as a 12” single (Shep Pettibone remix of “Shock”), NOT a separate album. Three tracks: “Shock (Shep Pettibone Mix),” “Shock (Instrumental),” “Shock (Single Mix).” Mixed by Shep Pettibone and Steve Thompson & Michael Barbiero; produced by Chris Kimsey.
  • Roger Morris and Duncan Kilburn departure dates (both gone by Forever Now 1982) not confirmed precisely from Discogs
  • Richard Butler solo album: Discogs lists 2005 (Koch Records) not 1996 as commonly cited — check whether there was also a 1996 release
