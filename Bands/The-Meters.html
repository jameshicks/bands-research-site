The Meters

The Meters

Aug 06, 20266 min read

The Meters

Discogs ID: 86616
Origin: New Orleans, Louisiana, USA
Active: 1965–1977; reunions 1989–1994; continued as The Funky Meters (Art Neville + George Porter Jr. faction, from 1994)
Labels: Josie Records (1968–1971), Reprise Records (1972–1976), Warner Bros. Records (1977)
Alias: The Rhine Oaks

The definitive New Orleans funk band and one of the most sampled rhythm sections in American music. Built from the nucleus of Art Neville’s earlier groups, the classic lineup — Art Neville (organ/keyboards/vocals), Leo Nocentelli (guitar), George Porter Jr. (bass), and Joseph Modeliste (drums, a.k.a. “Zigaboo”) — recorded three instrumental LPs for Josie Records starting in 1968, defining a stripped-down, deeply syncopated groove that became the template for funk and hip-hop producers for decades. Cyril Neville (congas/vocals) joined as a fifth member in 1975. Primary producer throughout Josie and Reprise: Allen Toussaint (with Marshall E. Sehorn as co-producer/executive). The Meters also served as the de facto house rhythm band for Toussaint’s Sea-Saint Studios, backing Dr. John, LaBelle, and numerous other New Orleans recordings.

Origins (1965–1968)

Art Neville had led The Neville Sounds before assembling the Meters lineup around 1965–1968 with Porter, Nocentelli, and Modeliste. The group signed to Josie Records (a Jubilee/Josie subsidiary based in New York) and recorded their debut under Toussaint and Sehorn’s Sansu Enterprises production umbrella.

Josie Records Era (1968–1971)

Three studio LPs on Josie — largely instrumental, built on interlocking rhythmic interplay. All original compositions written by the four members collectively, with occasional outside songs.

Struttin’ (1970, Josie JOS 4012)

Third and final Josie LP. Same core lineup and production team (Toussaint + Sehorn). The Josie run established the Meters as innovators of stripped-down funk — no horns, no orchestration, just the four interlocking instruments.

Reprise Records Era (1972–1976)

After Josie folded, the Meters signed to Reprise Records. The Reprise albums added vocals and wider arrangements while retaining the core rhythmic identity. Allen Toussaint remained the primary producer (often co-credited with the band by Rejuvenation). This is the era most cited as the artistic peak.

Cyril Neville (Discogs ID 98586) appears for the first time. Wardell Quezergue contributes horn arrangements — one of the New Orleans orchestrators most associated with the Toussaint circle. Includes “Mardi Gras Mambo” and a cover of Russ Ballard’s “Liar.” Engineers: Ken Laxton (ID 396986) and Roberta Grace (ID 396987).

Trick Bag (1976, Reprise)

Final Reprise LP. Toussaint still producing. Band increasingly pushing against label expectations; Warner Bros. signing followed shortly after.

Includes a cover of Peter Tosh’s “Stop That Train” and Allen Toussaint’s “I’m Gone.” Mastered by George Horn and Paul Stubblebine. The band dissolved after this LP.
