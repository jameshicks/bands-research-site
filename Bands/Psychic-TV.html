Psychic TV

Discogs ID: 17926
Active: 1981–present (as PTV3 from 2003)
Origin: Hackney, London → Brighton → New York
Genres: Industrial, post-punk, psychedelic, acid house, electronic

Psychic TV formed in 1981 immediately from the dissolution of Throbbing Gristle, founded by Genesis P-Orridge (vocals, bass, concept) and Peter Christopherson (“Sleazy”, visuals/electronics). Alex Fergusson joined as guitarist from the start. The group was conceived as both a band and a multimedia art/spiritual project, entwined from the beginning with the Temple ov Psychick Youth (TOPY), a loose networked organisation that produced its own cassette releases and publications in parallel with the records.

Genesis P-Orridge died 14 March 2020 after a battle with leukaemia.

Formation and Some Bizzare Era (1981–1984)

Throbbing Gristle announced their split on 23 July 1981 at San Francisco’s Kezar Pavilion. Genesis P-Orridge and Peter Christopherson immediately began work on what became Psychic TV; Alex Fergusson (previously in the Alternative TV orbit) came in as guitarist. Paula P-Orridge (Genesis’s partner) was a consistent presence throughout these years.

Their first two albums appeared on Some Bizzare Records, produced by Ken Thomas:

Coil Split and Iceland (1984)

In January 1984, Peter Christopherson and Geoff Rushton (John Balance) left Psychic TV to form Coil. This was not a hostile split — the network of connections remained dense throughout the 1980s — but it marked a clean division: Coil went deeper into occult electronics and drone; Psychic TV pivoted toward a more rock-based “hyperdelic” sound.

The same year, Psychic TV recorded in Iceland for the Gramm label, resulting in Those Who Do Not — an unusual document of the TOPY/Icelandic underground crossover:

Those Who Do Not (1984)

CatalogLabelFormat
GRAMM 23Gramm (Iceland)LP

Produced by Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson. Performers include Guðlaugur Kristinn Óttarsson (as Godkrist), Grey Wolf, Tryggvi Þór Herbertsson, and John Balance (credited as Lorec). The Gramm label was Kukl’s Icelandic imprint; this session connects Psychic TV directly to the Reykjavík underground that would later produce The Sugarcubes.

Guðlaugur Kristinn Óttarsson appeared on two further Psychic TV releases in 1987:

  • Crowleymass (Maldoror UK) — “Wickedest Guitar In The World”
  • Live In Reykjavík (Temple Records)

Temple Records Era (1984–1990)

Psychic TV established their own imprint, Temple Records, which became the primary vehicle for a flood of live albums, “Jack” series acid house singles, and archival releases. The catalog was enormous — dozens of titles through the 1980s, many simultaneously available only through TOPY’s network.

Fred Giannelli emerged as a central collaborator through this period, co-producing and programming across many releases. Richard Schiessl was also heavily involved (29 shared credits with Genesis).

Rose McDowall (of Strawberry Switchblade) contributed to 12 releases in this era.

Acid House Phase and Towards Thee Infinite Beat (1988–1990)

Beginning in 1988, Psychic TV became unexpected participants in the UK acid house explosion. Under aliases including Jack The Tab, they released a series of 12”s leaning into the TR-808/303 sound. This was not ironic — Genesis P-Orridge had been following Chicago house and saw the trance/ritual dimension in the music as continuous with TOPY’s project.

US Relocation and Later History (1993–present)

Genesis P-Orridge relocated to the United States in 1993. Temple Records ceased operation around 1994. Through the mid-to-late 1990s, Psychic TV activity was sporadic. Genesis formed Thee Majesty with Bryin Dall as a parallel project; a “final concert” was held at the Royal Festival Hall, London in May 1999.

PTV3 Revival (2003–2020)

In 2003, Genesis relaunched the project as PTV3, a full electric band touring and recording extensively in the US with a “hyperdelic” psychedelic rock sound. The reactivated Temple Records issued a run of new live albums from this tour. PTV3 was also, quietly, a TOPY revival.

Key PTV3 studio releases: Hell Is Invisible…Heaven Is Her/e (2007), Mr. Alien Brain Vs. The Skinwalkers (2008), Alien Brain Vs. Maggot Brain (2010), Thank You (2011), Snakes (2014), Alienist (2016), Allegory And Self (2017), The Evening Sun Turns Crimson (2019). Later studio albums issued on Dais Records and Angry Love Productions.

Genesis P-Orridge and Lady Jaye Breyer P-Orridge undertook the “Pandrogeny Project” — extensive body modification to mirror each other’s appearance, framed as a long-term art/life piece. Lady Jaye died 9 October 2007; Genesis continued the project as tribute.

Genesis P-Orridge was diagnosed with chronic myelogenous leukaemia in 2017 and died 14 March 2020.
