Joe Strummer & The Mescaleros

Band formed by Joe Strummer in 1999 as his primary vehicle in the last years of his life. Discogs artist ID: 272595.

Overview

After years of sporadic solo work post-Clash, Strummer assembled The Mescaleros and released three albums on Hellcat Records (US) / Mercury: Rock Art and the X-Ray Style (1999), Global a Go-Go (2001), and Streetcore (2003, posthumous). The band drew on world music, dub, punk, and rock. Strummer died December 22, 2002; Streetcore was completed from sessions he had finished before his death. A posthumous compilation Assembly (2021, Dark Horse Records) was also released. Members included Antony Genn, Martin Slattery, Scott Shields, Pablo Cook, Luke Bullen, Simon Stafford, Tymon Dogg, and Richard Flack.

Hellcat Records Signing

Strummer completed the Rock Art and the X-Ray Style sessions in North London in early 1999, then shopped the finished album to UK labels. Reaction was lukewarm; the LouderSound retrospective (loudersound.com, paywalled) reports that even avowed Clash fans such as Creation Records boss Alan McGee declined. McGee later disputed this account in a 2017 Facebook post, writing that he was never directly approached, but added that had Strummer asked, “just like Kevin Rowland, we would have signed him.” The McGee dispute is reported in the same LouderSound feature; the original claim’s primary source has not been traced to a named interview.

The deal with Hellcat Records came together in Los Angeles, apparently through a personal introduction to Tim Armstrong (Rancid frontman, Hellcat founder). Strummer gave a direct account in a July 31, 2001 interview at the Chateau Marmont in Hollywood, promoting Global a Go-Go:

“Tim said to me, ‘What are you doing?’ And I said, ‘I’m looking for a label.’ And Tim said, ‘Sign on mine.’ And I said, ‘You’ve got it!’ It was all done in one second.” — Joe Strummer, Rebel Noise interview, 31 July 2001 (rebelnoise.com)

Strummer also said: “Signing with Hellcat was a bloody stroke of luck” and “I was relieved that I didn’t have to go through the torture of signing with a major label again.” He had been in Los Angeles working on music for a charity record when the introduction to Armstrong took place; a friend brokered the meeting, though the friend is not named in the Rebel Noise interview. Armstrong, who had publicly modeled much of Rancid’s sound on the Clash, was described by Strummer as “one of his biggest fans.” The initial deal was reportedly a one-album arrangement; no cited primary source confirms the deal value that sometimes circulates ($250,000 figure appears in secondary coverage but the original source has not been identified).

The signing gave Hellcat all three Mescaleros studio albums: Rock Art and the X-Ray Style (1999), Global a Go-Go (2001), and Streetcore (2003, posthumous). After Strummer’s death in December 2002, Hellcat/Epitaph released The Hellcat Years compilation (2008).
