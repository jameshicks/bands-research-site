Beauty Pill

Washington, D.C. indie/post-rock band led by Chad Clark. Discogs artist ID: 1298210. Formed c. 2000 from the dissolution of Smart Went Crazy; active through the 2020s with an ever-shifting cast of collaborators around Clark’s core songwriting and production.

Biography

Origins and Formation (2000–2001)

Beauty Pill grew directly out of Smart Went Crazy, the DC post-hardcore group Clark had fronted through the late 1990s. When Smart Went Crazy dissolved (1998), Clark turned to a more expansive, studio-centric conception of a band — one that could accommodate orchestral guests, spoken-word elements, and layered production rather than a fixed live lineup.

The inaugural release, the EP The Cigarette Girl From The Future (2001), was a joint issue between DeSoto Records and Dischord Records (cat. DIS128.5CD / BE39). The credited “band” for this release was a fluid trio: Clark (voice, guitar, sounds/treatments, words), Abram Goodrich (drums, bass, guitar — also a Smart Went Crazy alum), and Joanne Gholl (voice, bass, guitar). Beyond that core, the EP pulled in a constellation of DC-scene guests on individual tracks: J. Robbins (guitar on “Here Lies Rachel Wallace”), Erik Denno (guitar), Jason Caddell (slide guitar), Jerry Busher (trumpet), Johanna Claasen (double bass), Amy Domingues (cello), Josh Belknap (drums), Lary Hoffman (vibraphone), and Nikhil Ranade (handclaps). Clark recorded and mixed; Charlie Pilsner mastered.

The EP is tagged Indie Rock on Dischord but its textures — cello, vibraphone, double bass, trumpet, atmospheric production — signal immediately that Beauty Pill was not going to be a straightforward post-hardcore band.

You Are Right To Be Afraid (2003)

The 2003 EP You Are Right To Be Afraid (Dischord, DIS138CD) introduced the lineup that would define the early-2000s band: Clark (guitar, vocals), Basla Andolsun (bass), Drew Doucette (guitar), Ryan Nelson (drums, guitar, voice), and Rachel Burke (vocals, Wurlitzer). This five-piece recorded a tight set of five songs, with Nelson also handling the cover art. Clark wrote the bulk of the material; Doucette and Nelson share co-writes on the title track.

The Unsustainable Lifestyle (2004)

The debut full-length The Unsustainable Lifestyle (Dischord, DIS139CD, 2004) is the definitive document of the early Beauty Pill. The core lineup of Clark, Andolsun, Doucette, Nelson, and Burke is intact, with Devin Ocampo added on drums and guitar. Ocampo was a fellow Smart Went Crazy alumnus (he’d drummed for SWC 1993–1996) and also played in Faraquet and Medications from the same DC scene. The record is sprawling — twelve tracks, guest percussion from Abram Goodrich and others, with the mix divided among Clark, Doucette, Nick Anderson, and T.J. Lipple. Liner art was contributed by Bread And Puppet Theater. Mastered by Emily Lazar.

Rachel Burke’s Wurlitzer, Doucette’s and Nelson’s multi-instrumental contributions, and Clark’s dense sound treatments create a record that sits at the intersection of post-hardcore, chamber pop, and experimental electronics. Despite the Dischord imprint it was clearly operating in a different register from the label’s hardcore roots.

Hiatus: The Health Crisis and Gap Years (2004–2014)

The eleven-year gap between The Unsustainable Lifestyle and Beauty Pill’s next album was driven primarily by a near-fatal cardiac illness that overtook Clark in 2007, compounded by an earlier loss of confidence following the reception of The Unsustainable Lifestyle.

Critical reception and creative doubt (2004–2007). According to the Paste Magazine interview (2023), Clark retreated from public music-making in the wake of The Unsustainable Lifestyle partly due to critical disappointment around that album; he focused on production work, largely at Inner Ear Studios in Arlington, Virginia, and seriously considered abandoning recording. He later recalled: “I really wanted to quit making music. I tried to quit…music would not let me go.” [source: Paste Magazine]

Cardiac crisis (2007–2008). In 2007, Clark developed what he initially thought was flu. Within days, he was critically ill. He had contracted viral cardiomyopathy — a virus that had invaded and inflamed his heart muscle, causing the heart wall to tear with each beat. Doctors gave him a 22 percent chance of surviving one year. He underwent emergency open-heart surgery in 2008. Clark described the surgery in the Post-Trash interview (2021): “They break your fucking ribcage. That’s the only way to get to the heart, is they have to basically pop you open like a crab.” Recovery left him unable to lift a guitar for an extended period. [source: KUNC/NPR, April 2015; Post-Trash, November 2021]

Mechanical heart and redirected creativity. The condition required ongoing cardiac support; by the time Describes Things As They Are was being made (2011), Clark was living with a mechanical heart device. His inability to play guitar during recovery redirected his practice toward laptop-based composition, drawing influence from hip-hop and electronic music — a shift that would shape the more expansive sonic palette of the 2015 record. Basla Andolsun noted during 2015 press that Clark “has a battery that he has attached to him that’s keeping his heart pumping.” [source: KUNC/NPR, April 2015; Talkhouse, 2015]

The Artisphere residency and making the album (2011). Recovery and false starts notwithstanding, Beauty Pill eventually recorded Describes Things As They Are through an unconventional public residency at Artisphere museum in Arlington, Virginia, in 2011 — part of a three-part project Clark called “Beauty Pill’s Immersive Ideal.” The band recorded one song per day as a live art installation, with observers watching through a window. Most band members had not heard the songs before arriving each day; arrangements were developed and recorded the same day with no prior rehearsal. Clark explained: “Most of the band had not heard the songs before we started working on them. So we did one song each day.” The approach was partly inspired by the idea of the Beatles’ Abbey Road sessions as a spectacle. The album was eventually released April 28, 2015 — four years after the recording residency. [source: Allston Pudding, 2015; KUNC/NPR, April 2015]

Subsequent health history. Clark’s cardiac problems continued after the 2015 release. His mechanical heart device was eventually recalled by the manufacturer; he received a human donor heart transplant (the Talkhouse 2015 piece notes he “wasn’t used to having a heartbeat” after the transplant). By 2021, the transplanted heart was showing rejection signals, leaving him “running at half power” per the Talkhouse interview. By 2023 he was described as being “on his third heart” — cardiomyopathy, mechanical device, then human transplant — in the Riot Riot substack interview. Clark has said: “I have now survived many situations that I’m going to say should have killed me.” [source: Talkhouse, 2015; Riot Riot substack, 2023; Paste Magazine, 2023]

Describes Things As They Are (2015)

After the Artisphere residency recording and several years of post-production, Beauty Pill returned on Butterscotch Records (BSR011, 2015) with the double LP Describes Things As They Are — a significant formal expansion. The core band by this point was Clark, Andolsun, Ocampo, and Doucette, with Jean Cook joining as a key member (violin, Wurlitzer, piano, synthesizers, vocals). Cook is also a member of Ida (3) and other ensembles.

The record is formally ambitious: individual tracks feature Colin Stetson (bass clarinet), Zeena Parkins (treated electric harp), Kenta Nagai (shamisen), Arto Lindsay, Melvin Gibbs, and Andres Levin (writing credits on “The Prize”), plus an elaborate horn section arranged by Chris Kallmyer on “Afrikaner Barista.” The D-side closer “Ann The Word” was co-produced by Clark, Guy Picciotto, and Joel Hamilton, a rare instance of a Fugazi figure appearing in the production credits of a Beauty Pill release. Mastered by Emily Lazar (assisted by Chris Allgood) at The Lodge; lacquer cut by Carl Rowatti.

The album was received as one of the year’s best — cited by NPR, Robert Christgau, and Time magazine (which later ranked it among the ten best albums of the 2010s). Clark reflected: “It feels alive and it feels electric and it feels like it has color.” [source: KUNC/NPR, April 2015] The Talkhouse piece quotes Clark on the album’s lasting significance: “Everything we do now, probably everything I do for the rest of my life, is going to be post-Describes music.” [source: Talkhouse, 2015]

Genres expanded on Discogs to Electronic / Jazz / Rock; styles include Avantgarde and Experimental alongside Indie Rock.

Please Advise and Instant Night (2020–2021)

Moving to Northern Spy (New York), Beauty Pill released Please Advise (NS128, 2020) — a 12″ EP with production handed primarily to Brown Eno (also credited as Pocket / Pocket 808 / Sameer Sengupta). The lineup for this release is Clark, Andolsun, Ocampo, Doucette, and Erin Nelson (3) (voice), with a horn ensemble of Aaron Harman (bassoon), Matt Harman (euphonium), Sarah Hughes (saxophone), and Victoria Banos (trumpet). The record includes a cover of The Pretenders’ “Tattooed Love Boys” with T.J. Lipple drumming. J. Robbins appears in the recording credits. Design by White Bicycle.

Instant Night (NS137, 2021) is a digital EP (four tracks including a Brown Eno remix), retaining the horn ensemble and featuring Erin Nelson (3) alongside Clark. Mastered by Heba Kadry.

Blue Period (2023)

Blue Period (Ernest Jenning Record Co., EJRC193, 2023) is a double LP compilation drawing material from across the catalog alongside new and alternate recordings — a kind of retrospective digest. It was assembled, recorded, and remastered by Clark, with Ryan Nelson handling artwork and layout. The guest photographer list is a snapshot of long-term DC connections: Cynthia Connolly (of Dischord Records design history), Jeff Nelson (Dischord co-founder), PJ Sykes, Ben Tankersley, and others.
