The Pagans

Discogs ID: 426529 (listed as “Pagans”; name variation: “The Pagans”) Origin: Cleveland, Ohio, USA Active: 1977–c.1990, various reunions through 2018+ Genres: Punk, Garage Rock

Raw, antagonistic Cleveland punk band, one of the defining acts of the Cleveland underground’s second wave. Emerged slightly later than the proto-punk vanguard (Rocket From The Tombs, Electric Eels, Mirrors) but continued the city’s tradition of confrontational noise-forward rock. Anchored throughout by vocalist Mike Hudson (4) and guitarist Mike Metoff. Primary label was Drome for their classic 1977–1979 run; later activity spread across Terminal Records, Treehouse Records (2), and Crypt Records.

Biography by Era

Formation and Early Singles (1977–1979)

The Pagans formed in Cleveland in 1977, with Mike Hudson (4) as lead vocalist and co-founder alongside his brother Brian Hudson (drums). The earliest documented release is the single “Six And Change” (1977, Neck Records, cat# PP1143) — a 7”/45 RPM with no credited personnel beyond the band. Neck Records was a Mentor, Ohio label run by the Wild Giraffes (“Neck Reckords, Very LTD.”).

Their main creative period is defined by three singles on Drome, the Cleveland punk label run (at least in its early identity) by Johnny Dromette (real name John Thompson; also recorded as “Idrome”):

  • “Street Where Nobody Lives / What’s This Shit Called Love?” (1978, Drome DR1) — produced and designed by Johnny Dromette. Personnel: Mike Hudson (4) (vocals, credited as Michael Hudson), Mike Metoff (guitar, credited as Tommy Metoff), Tim Allee (bass), Brian Hudson (drums, credited as Brian Morgan (3)). Engineer: Paul Hamann (uncredited).
  • “Dead End America” (1979, Drome DR7) — produced by Johnny Dromette. Same core lineup: Hudson, Metoff, Allee, Brian Hudson, plus Tommy Gunn (3) on guitar. Design by Dromette.
  • “Not Now No Way” (1979, Drome DR5) — produced by David Thomas (2) of Pere Ubu. Same lineup as Dead End America. Photography by Dr. Ellen Zoslov.

The David Thomas production credit on “Not Now No Way” is confirmed in Discogs. Thomas also co-produced (with Johnny Dromette and Mark Trehus) the later “Buried Alive” LP (1986). This is a direct cross-pollination between The Pagans and the Pere Ubu axis of the Cleveland underground.

The Drome label roster also included x_x and The Lepers, situating the Pagans in a tightly clustered local scene.

Terminal Records Period (1980–1983)

Mike Hudson (4) and Mike Metoff co-ran Terminal Records (Discogs ID: 83177), a Cleveland label they created themselves. The 1980 compilation Cleveland Confidential (Terminal Records) was compiled by Hudson, gathering local Cleveland acts. The 1983 Pagans self-titled LP (Terminal Records) featured a reconfigured lineup:

  • Mike Hudson (4) — vocals, guitar (credited as Michael Hudson)
  • Tommy Gunn (3) — vocals, lead guitar
  • Chas Smith (2) — keyboards
  • Robert Conn — bass (also vocals on track B6)
  • Bob Richey (2) — drums (credited as Bobby Morgan)

Mastered by Todd Deasey. Executive producer: Thomas Richey.

Buried Alive and Treehouse Years (1986–1990)

The 1986 LP Buried Alive (Treehouse Records (2)) is the most fully-produced Pagans album, with multiple engineers and producers. Lineup returns to the classic core:

  • Mike Hudson (4) — lead vocals (credited as Michael Hudson)
  • Mike Metoff — guitar (credited as Mike “Tommy Gunn” Metoff)
  • Tim Allee — bass, backing vocals
  • Brian Hudson — drums

Produced by David Thomas (2), Johnny Dromette, and Mark Trehus. Engineered by Paul Hamann, Paul Marotta, and Tom Herbers. Liner notes by Byron Coley, John Thompson (17), Mark Trehus, and Tesco Vee.

The Treehouse Records (2) run through 1988–1990 produced several more releases: Dead End America ‘87 (1987), Don’t Leave Me Alone (1987, Bona Fide Records), The Godlike Power Of The Pagans Live (1987), (Us &) All Our Friends Are So Messed Up (1988), Her Name Was Jane (1990).

Hudson’s solo project released A Monster And The Devil (1989, Mustard (2) / The Tinnitus Label) during this period.

Compilations and Later Reissues (1986–2001)

Buried Alive (1986, Treehouse Records (2)) was released in part as a compilation of earlier material. Street Where Nobody Lives (1986, Mustard (2); reissued 1989 on Resonance (2)) compiled early Drome material. Everybody Hates You (1994, Crypt Records, Germany) was the major posthumous career overview — a 30-track collection with songwriting credited to Hudson, Metoff, Allee, and Bob Richey as the core four.

Shit Street (2001, Crypt Records) and The Pink Album Plus! (2001, Crypt Records) followed. Both are Crypt-era reissues/compilations consolidating the classic catalog. The Blue Album (2008, Smog Veil Records) featured the reunion lineup of Hudson, Metoff, Allee, and Bob Richey (credited as Bobby Richey).

Later Activity and Hudson’s Death

Reunion-era activity continued into the 2010s: Not Now, No Way / Neutron Bomb (2013, Soul Jazz Records UK), Hollywood High (2014, Ruin Discos), What’s This Shit? 1977/1979 (2014, Thermionic Records / Lion Productions), Hopped Up b/w Nowhere Girl (2016, Get Hip Recordings), splits with The Guillotines (2016, Just 4 Fun) and other acts, and Hollywood Or Die (2018, Jett Plastic Recordings).

Mike Hudson (4) died October 29, 2017, in Los Angeles, California, aged 61. He had been living in LA at the end of his life, having previously lived and worked in NYC, Pennsylvania, and Niagara Falls. He also founded the Niagara Falls Reporter newspaper. He was a singer, sometime guitarist, published author, and journalist — Creem magazine connections are not documented in Discogs but noted in existing research as a biographical gap.

Cleveland Scene Connections

The Pagans sit squarely in the Cleveland punk/underground continuum:

  • Pere Ubu — David Thomas (2) produced two Pagans releases; the two bands represent parallel streams of Cleveland’s noise-rock underground.
  • Rocket From The Tombs — proto-punk precursor; Thomas connects both bands.
  • Electric Eels — Paul Marotta worked with both (Electric Eels member; co-produced Buried Alive).
  • The Styrenes — Mike Hudson (4) is listed as a member; Tim Allee also has Styrenes connections; Paul Marotta and Jamie Klimek connect the two bands.
  • The Cramps — Mike Metoff is listed as a member of The Cramps (Discogs confirmed), making him one of the more remarkable connectors in the Cleveland punk ecosystem.
  • Cheetah Chrome And The Ghetto Dogs — Mike Metoff member.
  • Radio Alarm Clocks / The Clocks (2) — Mike Metoff member (with Dave DeLuca).
  • The Murder Junkies — Mike Hudson (4) joined as third singer in 1994.
  • Womanhaters — Mike Hudson (4) and Brian Hudson both members.
  • Mirrors — same Cleveland scene; Jamie Klimek connection via Styrenes.
