Cop Shoot Cop

NYC noise-rock / industrial band, founded 1987, disbanded 1996. Discogs artist ID: 96051.

Name variations: C.S.C., CSC, Cop.

Overview

Cop Shoot Cop were a New York band built around one of the more unusual instrumental configurations in American underground rock: twin bass guitars (one high-end, one low-end), no lead guitar, electronics/sampling, and drums reinforced with found metal percussion. Founded in 1987, they disbanded in 1996 after three albums on Interscope Records and a significant run of UK releases on Big Cat.

The core lineup stabilized around Tod Ashley (high-end bass, vocals, credited throughout as “Tod A.”), Jack Natz (low-end bass, real name William C. Nantz), Jim Coleman (electronics, sampler, credited as “J F Coleman” or “Filer”), and Phil Puleo (drums, metal percussion). David Ouimet (sampler) and Michael Kaminski appear on the earliest releases; guitarist Steve McMillen joined for the final single period. Their primary recording space was Martin Bisi’s BC Studio in Brooklyn, where the bulk of the catalog was tracked and mixed.

Discogs classification covers noise rock, alternative rock, industrial, and punk, which reflects the band’s movement from confrontational no-wave aggression on early releases to a more song-based but still abrasive approach by Ask Questions Later (1993) and Release (1994). Their first proper label release was Headkick Facsimile (Supernatural Organization, 1989, Japan), following a self-released cassette from 1988. The Enemy Records connection mentioned in earlier vault notes does not appear in Discogs — no CSC releases are cataloged on Enemy Records (label ID 12005).

Key Release Personnel Details

Headkick Facsimile (1989)

Earliest label release; Japan-only mini-LP on Supernatural Organization (same label that issued Sonic Youth, Pussy Galore, Current 93, Rapeman in Japan). Engineered and mixed by Wharton Tiers. Band produced. Credits show Ashley (bass/vocals) and Puleo (drums/metal percussion) as the lineup; Ouimet on sampler. Coleman not individually credited on this release.

Consumer Revolt (1990)

First full US LP on Circuit Records (2), a small NYC label also home to Unsane, Surgery (2), and Monster Magnet’s early work. Co-produced, recorded, and mixed with Martin Bisi at BC Studio. Full lineup: Ashley (hi-end bass/vocals), Natz (lo-end bass), Puleo (drums/metal percussion), Ouimet (sampler), Coleman (sampler). Track-level credits show Wharton Tiers on two tracks (“Smash Retro!,” “Fire In The Hole”).

White Noise (1991)

UK/US release on Big Cat. Notable for split mixing credits: Martin Bisi (most tracks), J.G. Thirlwell (credited “Jim Thirlwell,” tracks 5, 6, 8, 10, 12), and Dave Sardy (track 9). Cripple Jim (Jim Coleman alias) on sampler. Features vocal appearances: Killjoy (3) on “Empires Collapse,” Hugh Foley on “Corporate Protopop.”

Ask Questions Later (1993)

Major-label debut on Interscope. Most detailed personnel record in the discography. Produced by Cop Shoot Cop and Martin Bisi; mixed by Bisi and Todd A. Roli Mosimann credited for remix of “Surprise, Surprise.” Features April Chung (violin), Joe Ben Plummer (saxophone), Jim Colarusso (trumpet), David Ouimet (trombone on “$10 Bill” and “Got No Soul”). Mastered by Howie Weinberg.

Release (1994)

Second Interscope LP. Discogs carries no additional personnel credits beyond the primary band listing — no producer, engineer, or mixer named. 13 tracks.

Any Day Now (1995)

Final single (UK, Big Cat). Produced by Cop Shoot Cop and Dave Sardy. Recorded by Sardy and Greg Gordon. Steve McMillen on guitar (his only Discogs credit with the band). Coleman on sampler/piano/organ.

Member Post-CSC Activity

  • Tod Ashley — formed Firewater (2) (debut Get Off The Cross on Jetset Records, 1996); multiple Firewater albums through 2012; also Lubricated Goat, Shithaus, Dig Dat Hole
  • Jim Coleman — solo album as Phylr (Stuff, Nail, 2002); collaborated with Dead Voices On Air, Baby Zizanie, Here; later joined Human Impact (2) with Puleo; scored indie films
  • Phil Puleo — joined Swans (1996/7 era through final lineup); Angels of Light; joined Human Impact (2) with Coleman; solo releases (self-released, 2019–2025)
  • Jack Natz — Black Snakes, The Undead (2), Virus (36), Red Expendables, Lubricated Goat
  • David Ouimet — Motherhead Bug, Firewater (2), Of Cabbages And Kings, Sulfur, Monsters In Your Ear

Connections

  • Martin Bisi — primary engineer/producer throughout, working at BC Studio, Brooklyn
  • Wharton Tiers — early engineer (Headkick Facsimile, Consumer Revolt); Tiers is also associated with the Glenn Branca Ensemble and Theoretical Girls
  • J.G. Thirlwell — mixed five tracks on White Noise (1991); separately credited Tod Ashley as bassist on Gash (Foetus, 1994)
  • Dave Sardy — producer/engineer on final single-period releases; Sardy later became a major rock producer
  • Phil Puleo — drummer throughout; continued into Swans and then Human Impact
  • Jim Coleman — electronics/sampler throughout; founding musical architect; also cited as the “musical founder” in his Discogs profile
  • Tod Ashley — primary lyricist and vocalist; high-end bassist
  • Jack Natz — low-end bass anchoring the no-guitar sonic approach
  • Big Cat — UK label home for most of the catalog’s European exposure
  • Human Impact (2) — post-CSC band reuniting Coleman and Puleo with Unsane / Swans members
  • Firewater (2) — Tod Ashley’s subsequent band, also featuring David Ouimet
  • Swans — Phil Puleo joined Swans after CSC disbanded
  • Helmet (2) — split single on Micro Records (1992)
  • Circuit Records (2) — also home to early Unsane (Improvised Munitions, 1989), Surgery (2), Monster Magnet
  • Supernatural Organization — Japanese label placing CSC alongside Sonic Youth, Pussy Galore, Rapeman, Current 93, Death In June
