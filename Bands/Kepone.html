Kepone

Noise rock trio from Richmond, Virginia. Active 1991–1997. Reunited for a tour in 2013. Discogs ID: 764924.

Core lineup: Michael Bishop (2) (bass, vocals), Tim Harriss (guitar, vocals), Seth Harris (drums). A second drummer, Ed Trask, replaced Seth Harris for the second album Skin (1995). All studio work produced by Scott Wolfe.

Overview

Kepone formed in Richmond, Virginia around 1991, drawing on the city’s fertile underground scene (which also produced Avail, Honor Role, and GWAR). The band’s sound combined grinding, heavy guitar noise with rhythmically precise post-hardcore and dual vocals from Harriss and Bishop. The name references a neurotoxic pesticide that contaminated the James River in a major 1975 industrial accident at a Hopewell, Virginia plant — a loaded local reference.

They recorded a self-released cassette demo in 1993, then signed to Quarterstick Records (a Touch And Go imprint based in Chicago). Three full-lengths followed between 1994 and 1997. Kepone dissolved after the self-titled third album; their Richmond peers Mule had a comparable run on the same label at roughly the same moment.

Two members, Bishop and Harriss, had concurrent involvement with GWAR (the Richmond shock-metal collective) under stage names — Bishop as “Beefcake The Mighty (2)” and later “Blothar The Berserker,” Harriss as “Flattus Maximus (2).” Scott Wolfe, their producer, was also a member of Burma Jam alongside Harriss.

The band reunited for a 2013 tour, documented via Bandcamp (kepone.bandcamp.com).

Scene Context

Kepone occupied the Richmond noise-rock moment alongside Mule (P.W. Long’s project, also on Quarterstick) and existed in the broader shadow of GWAR’s long Richmond tenure. The overlap between Kepone and GWAR is direct: both Bishop and Harriss performed in GWAR under stage personas throughout and after the Kepone years.

Seth Harris had come from Honor Role, one of Richmond’s key early-1980s hardcore bands (eight releases from 1983 to 1997 across Eskimo Records, Homestead, and Merge Records).

Ed Trask, who drummed on Skin, was simultaneously in Avail, Richmond’s widely respected melodic hardcore band, as well as Holy Rollers.
