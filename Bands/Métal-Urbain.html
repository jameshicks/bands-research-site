Métal Urbain

French punk band from Paris. Active 1976–1980, with later reunion activity from 2006 onward. Discogs artist ID: 683196. Name variations: Metal U, Metal Urbain.

One of the most significant groups to emerge from the French first-wave punk scene. Among the first acts to combine a drum machine with electric guitars and synthesizers in a punk context. Also notable as the first act ever to release a record on Rough Trade — their 1977 single “Paris Maquis” / “Clé De Contact” was Rough Trade RT 001. One of the few French bands to record sessions for John Peel.

Biography

Formation and Early Lineup (1976–1977)

Métal Urbain formed in Paris in June 1976. The initial lineup was:

  • Clode Panik (b. Claude Peronne) — vocals
  • Eric Débris (b. Eric Daugu) — synthesizer, drum machine
  • Zip Zinc (b. Jean-Pierre Zing) — synthesizer
  • Rikky Darling (b. Eric Feidt) — guitar

This lineup recorded 1976 demo material that would surface decades later on the Chef D’Oeuvre (2003) retrospective, including versions of “Snuff Movie,” “Lady Coca Cola,” and a French-language cover of the Sex Pistols’ “Anarchy in the U.K.” retitled “Anarchie En France.” The demos also include a Stooges cover (“No Fun”) and a song literally titled “Métal Urbain.”

In 1977 they cut the single “Panik” / “Lady Coca Cola” for the French label Cobra (cat no. COB 47004). The record was credited as “Metal U” on the sleeve. Written-by credits go to Eric Débris and Rikky Darling (“Panik”) and the same pair for “Lady Coca Cola.” The release is classified on Discogs under styles Industrial, Punk, and Synthpunk — an early marker of the drum-machine-driven sound.

Rough Trade Debut and Second Lineup (1977–1978)

In May 1977 Rikky Darling and Zip Zinc departed and were replaced by:

  • Hermann Schwartz (b. Jean-Louis Boulanger) — guitar
  • Pat Lüger (b. Patrick Boulanger) — guitar

(Hermann Schwartz and Pat Lüger share the same surname and were brothers.)

This reconstituted lineup traveled to London and recorded “Paris Maquis” / “Clé De Contact” with Tony Platt and Ross Crighton producing and engineering. It was pressed and released in the UK by Rough Trade as RT 001 — the label’s very first release, in 1977. The runout etchings on the vinyl read “PARIS MAQUIS THIS ONE BITES ! JONZ TB” and “CLE DE CONTACT THIS ONE AS WELL TB.” The record placed Métal Urbain in the orbit of the emerging UK post-punk infrastructure at a moment when Rough Trade was still a shop-cum-distribution-operation.

Third Single and Clode Panik’s Departure (1978–1979)

The lineup of Clode Panik / Eric Débris / Hermann Schwartz / Pat Lüger recorded one further single: “Hystérie Connective” / “Pas Poubelle,” released in 1979 on the UK label Radar Records (5) (cat no. ADA 20). Doug Bennett engineered. This was the final release to feature Clode Panik on lead vocals.

Clode Panik Exits; Eric Débris Takes Lead (December 1978–July 1979)

After Clode Panik left, Eric Débris moved to lead vocals and synthesizer:

  • Eric Débris — voice and synthesizer
  • Hermann Schwartz — guitar
  • Pat Lüger — guitar

Charlie H Joins; Final Pre-Hiatus Lineup (July 1979–February 1980)

The last pre-hiatus lineup added:

  • Charles Hurbier (alias Charlie H.) — synthesizer

This lineup (Débris / Hurbier / Schwartz / Lüger) recorded material that would appear on the posthumous compilation Les Hommes Morts Sont Dangereux (1981).

Metal Boys Parallel Project

Concurrently with the later Métal Urbain period, Eric Débris, Charles Hurbier, and Hermann Schwartz also operated as Metal Boys — effectively the same core unit under a different name. Metal Boys recorded two releases: “Sweet Marylin” (Rough Trade, 1979) and the album Tokio Airport (Celluloid, 1980). The personnel overlap is total; Metal Boys should be understood as a sister project rather than a successor.

1982 Reunion Session

A brief reunion in 1982 produced remixed versions of earlier tracks (“Crève Salope,” “Snuff Movie”) and new recordings (“Tango Sudiste,” “Panik Instrumental”). Lineup credited: Eric Débris, Charlie H (Charles Hurbier), and Rikky Darling. These recordings appeared on L’Âge D’Or (1985) and Chef D’Oeuvre (2003).

Hiatus and Compilations (1980–2005)

Following the initial dissolution, the band’s catalog circulated through a series of retrospective pressings and compilations:

  • Les Hommes Morts Sont Dangereux (1981, Byzz/Celluloid) — the main archival LP; 16 tracks drawn from singles and unreleased material, plus a bonus 7” with “Hystérie Connective (Mix 2)” and “Atlantis.” Featured additional participants: Miss O.D., Rikky Darling (as guest), Clode Panik, and Zip Zinc as performers.
  • L’Âge D’Or (1985, Fan Club, FC 011) — double LP compilation; 24 tracks including Metal Boys material (“Colt 45”) and a cover of the Sex Pistols’ “Anarchy in the U.K.” as “Anarchie En France” (French adaptation by Eric Débris). Distributed through Musidisc and New Rose. Guest participants: Gass (2), Miss O.D., Pierre Jean.
  • Anarchy In Paris! (2003, Acute Records ACT003) — US CD retrospective; 24 tracks covering both Métal Urbain and Metal Boys material. Remastered by Eric Débris and Sean Cotter. Produced for reissue by David Fakrikian. Liner notes by Jacques Amsellem.
  • Chef D’Oeuvre (2003, Seventeen Records ST 001) — French double-CD retrospective; 37 tracks including all singles, album cuts, 1982 remixes, and 1976–1979 demos. Supervised by David Fakrikian; remastered by Sean Cotter.

Reunion Album: J’Irai Chier Dans Ton Vomi (2006)

A full reunion album, produced by Jello Biafra (of the Dead Kennedys / Alternative Tentacles). Lineup: Eric Débris (vocals, machines, artwork), Hermann Schwartz (guitar, backing vocals), a guitarist credited as “Vovot,” and Jérôme Solo (machines, backing vocals). Mastered by George Horn; mixed by Marshall Lawless and Matt Kelley. Released on Jello Biafra’s Alternative Tentacles subsidiary Exclaim (cat. 8345115332). Biafra also co-wrote one track (“Ici C’Est La Guerre,” with Débris, Solo, and Vovot). The reunion reconnected the band with the Anglophone punk underground via the California connection.

Scene Context

Métal Urbain emerged from the Paris punk scene alongside Asphalt Jungle (3), Gazoline, and others documented in Punk 45 - Les Punks The French Connection. Their early 1976 demos show them playing Stooges covers and French-language adaptations of UK punk songs before establishing their own material. The use of drum machines and synthesizers alongside punk guitars — at a time when this was genuinely unusual — anticipates the electronic direction much UK post-punk would take. Their connection to Rough Trade’s inception gives them a peculiar dual position: part of the French scene but also one of the founding nodes of the UK independent network.

The 2006 reunion album produced by Jello Biafra, released on his own label, suggests the transatlantic legacy was well understood — the band was being claimed by both the European punk-industrial genealogy and the Californian hardcore/anarcho-punk tradition.
