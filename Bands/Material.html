Material

From: New York City Active: 1979–1983 (Ze/Celluloid era); production unit through 1990s; sporadically thereafter Labels: ZE Records, ZÙ Records, Celluloid Records, Rough Trade, Elektra Discogs ID: 8796

Overview

Material was the project through which bassist-producer Bill Laswell built the most ambitious intersection of New York’s downtown avant-garde scene and funk/dance music at the turn of the 1980s. Named as a group but functioning in practice as a production collective, the core unit for the Ze Records/Celluloid period was Laswell, keyboardist-electronics operator Michael Beinhorn, and drummer Fred Maher — three musicians barely in their twenties pulling in a rotating cast of avant-jazz and No Wave figures that constituted a cross-section of downtown NYC.

The ZE Records connection placed them in the orbit of the label’s founding aesthetic: mutant disco and No Wave as a shared cultural project. Material were the most compositionally ambitious act on Ze’s roster — less punk-funk than avant-punk-jazz-funk, using the record as an assembly site for musicians from distinct downtown circles. Each release from 1979 through 1982 expanded the guest list and the genre range.

Martin Bisi’s BC Studio in Brooklyn — co-established with Laswell in 1980, originally as OAO Studios — was the production home for the key albums. Giorgio Gomelsky, the Georgian-born impresario who had managed the early Rolling Stones and Yardbirds and relocated to New York in 1978, produced the debut.

History

1978–1979: Ze Records and formation

Material emerged from the New York downtown scene in 1979, signing to ZE Records — the label co-founded by Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban in 1978. Ze had grown out of Spy Records Ltd. and was backed by Island Records for distribution; its identity was built on bridging No Wave antagonism with dancefloor usability. James Chance/The Contortions, Cristina, Kid Creole & the Coconuts, Lizzy Mercier Descloux, Don Armando’s Second Avenue Rhumba Band — Material occupied the most compositionally challenging position on the roster.

Their debut release, “Temporary Music 1” (ZÙ Records, 1979), established the template: a small core of Laswell on bass, Beinhorn on keyboards, Maher on drums, Cliff Cultreri on guitar — but with a significant outside figure at the production level. Giorgio Gomelsky produced; Eddy Offord and Rob Davis (6) engineered. Tom Coyne cut the lacquer.

1980: Discourse and the Celluloid connection

Discourse (Celluloid, 1980) was a stripped production — Bobby Nathan engineered; the sleeve credited music to Material, words to Laswell alone (printed as “B. Laswell”). Celluloid Records, the New York-based French independent run by Jean Karakos, became the primary vehicle for Material’s album-length work through 1982.

The Temporary Music Compilation (Ze Records, 1980) collected the Temporary Music material, functioning as the proper commercial introduction for Ze’s audience.

1981: Memory Serves — the avant-jazz summit

Memory Serves (Celluloid, 1981) is the defining document of Material’s first phase. The core duo of Laswell and Beinhorn — with Fred Maher on drums, guitar, and percussion — invited in a cross-section of avant-jazz New York: Henry Threadgill on alto saxophone, George Lewis on trombone, Sonny Sharrock on guitar, Olu Dara on trumpet, Fred Frith on guitar, violin, and vibraphone (Frith also co-mixed), Charles K. Noyes on drums and bells, and Billy Bang on violin.

Martin Bisi co-engineered and co-produced. The album was made at BC Studio (then OAO Studios), which Bisi and Laswell had jointly established in Brooklyn the year before — a room that would go on to record Sonic Youth, Swans, Herbie Hancock’s “Rockit,” John Zorn, and many others.

Bustin’ Out / Over & Over (Ze Records/Island, 1981) was the commercial single — the Island-distributed release aimed at the dancefloor.

Temporary Music 2 (1981) continued the series with further downtown collaborations.

1982: One Down — the maximum guest album

One Down (Celluloid, 1982) pushed the ensemble approach to its logical extreme. The Laswell/Beinhorn core remained, with Jean Karakos as executive producer. The guest list was extraordinary:

  • Nile Rodgers — guitar (Chic; at peak commercial power following “Good Times” and disco’s mainstream breakthrough)
  • Fred Frith — guitar (returning from Memory Serves)
  • Archie Shepp — tenor saxophone (free jazz elder statesman)
  • Oliver Lake — tenor and alto saxophone (World Saxophone Quartet)
  • Nona Hendryx — vocals and backing vocals (ex-LaBelle)
  • Whitney Houston — vocals (pre-debut; one of her earliest recorded appearances)
  • Bernard Fowler — vocals
  • Yogi Horton / J.T. Lewis / Tony Thompson (2) — drums
  • Daniel Ponce — bongos
  • Nicky Skopelitis — guitar
  • Ronald “Head” Drayton — guitar
  • B.J. Nelson — vocals; Nicky Marrero — percussion; Raymond Jones — electric piano

Paula Scher handled art direction. Martin Bisi and Robert Musso engineered.

The presence of Whitney Houston — pre-debut, singing on an avant-garde funk record on a French independent — is the most striking single credit in the Material discography.

Fred Maher left after Memory Serves, joining Scritti Politti in 1984. Beinhorn exited the downtown scene around the same period, moving into major-label rock production (Grammy-winning later work with Red Hot Chili Peppers, Soundgarden, Hole).

Axiom-Era Material (1993–1994)

After the Celluloid phase, Material continued as Laswell’s production alias into the 1990s, with two major releases on Axiom Records (his Island Records imprint):

Mantra (1993): Bill Laswell (bass/producer/percussion), Zakir Hussain (tabla), Vikku Vinayakram (ghatam), Shankar (nadhaswaram arrangements). A focused Indian-fusion set, leaning into the Carnatic collaborators Laswell had been working with.

Hallucination Engine (1994): A maximum-cast global session — Laswell alongside Zakir Hussain, Vikku Vinayakram, Shankar, Nicky Skopelitis, Jonas Hellborg, Bootsy Collins, Trilok Gurtu, Wayne Shorter, Sly Dunbar, Bernie Worrell, William S. Burroughs (voice), Simon Shaheen, Liu Sola, Aiyb Dieng, and others. Produced and arranged by Laswell; recorded and mixed by Robert Musso and Oz Fritz.

ZE Records Context

ZE Records was co-founded in 1978 by Michael Zilkha and Michel Esteban, growing out of their earlier Spy Records Ltd. Zilkha had come from publishing in London (MA, Lincoln College Oxford, 1975); Esteban had run a Paris rock merchandise shop (“Harry Cover”), published the punk magazine Rock News, and organised the first Sex Pistols concerts in France. Ze was backed by Island Records for US/international distribution.

The label’s aesthetic was the crossroads of No Wave and dance: James Chance and The Contortions (“Buy”), Cristina (“Off White”), Lizzy Mercier Descloux (“Press Color”), Kid Creole And The Coconuts, Was (Not Was). Material were the most compositionally adventurous act on the roster — more interested in avant-jazz improvisation than punk nihilism, using the Ze connection as a gateway to the dancefloor while maintaining downtown credibility.

ZE shut down in 1985. Michel Esteban revived the label from Brazil in 2002.

Giorgio Gomelsky

Giorgio Gomelsky (born 28 February 1934, Tbilisi; died 13 January 2016, New York) is the figure who connects Material to British rock history. Georgian-born, raised across Soviet Russia, Syria, Egypt, and Switzerland, he moved to England in the late 1950s to document the British jazz scene. He became manager of the early Rolling Stones (before Andrew Loog Oldham), then managed the Yardbirds. In 1978 he relocated to New York and ran Blue Door studio near Chelsea, working to connect European progressive rock acts to the American downtown avant-garde. His production of Material’s debut was part of that bridging work.
