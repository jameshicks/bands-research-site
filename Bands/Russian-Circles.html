History

Formation and Enter (2004–2007)

Formed in Chicago in late 2004 by Mike Sullivan and Colin DeKuiper, both formerly of dakota dakota, joined shortly after by Dave Turncrantz (ex-Riddle Of Steel). Debuted with a self-titled EP in 2004 on their own imprint. First full-length Enter released in 2006 on Friction Records 2 / Hewhocorrupts Inc. — engineered by Greg Norman, with Rob Lowe (Lichens) contributing piano and mellotron. Enter became their most-pressed pre-SH release (21 pressings). Colin DeKuiper departed in 2007.

Station and the Brian Cook era (2007–2009)

Brian Cook (2) joined on bass in 2007, completing the trio that has remained stable since. Matt Bayles produced, engineered, and mixed Station (2008, Suicide Squeeze) — the first release with the settled lineup. Morgan Henderson contributed additional double bass; mastered by Ed Brooks. Sargent House (then primarily a management company under Cathy Pellow) began managing the band around this period. Second Suicide Squeeze album Geneva (2009) marked the beginning of the Brandon Curtis production partnership — Brandon Curtis (of Secret Machines) produced and added piano; Greg Norman engineered; strings from Alison Chesley (cello) and Susan Voelz (violin); mastered by Joe Lambert.

How Curtis came to produce Geneva: Russian Circles shared tour dates with Secret Machines and were struck by Secret Machines’ ability to create a dense wall of sound as a trio. They investigated Secret Machines’ self-titled LP (2008) and discovered Curtis had produced it himself. Cook: “We did some dates with his band Secret Machines and we were just floored by their ability to make such a dense wall of sound with only three people. And their self-titled record really captured their live sound. So we hit up Brandon to find out who they recorded with and it wound up that he did it.” (Brian Cook, You Indie interview, October 2011 — russiancircles.tumblr.com.) Geneva was recorded in May 2009 at Electrical Audio, Chicago. Between Geneva and Empros, Curtis joined Interpol’s touring lineup (June 2010, keyboards and vocals), and worked with Paul Banks on the 2012 EP Julian Plenti Lives… — he was an active collaborator in the broader indie/post-punk world throughout the trilogy period, not merely waiting between RC sessions. (Wikipedia: Brandon Curtis, as of 2026-07-14.)

SH studio albums and the Curtis trilogy (2011–2013)

Empros (2011) was the first studio album released directly on Sargent House, cementing the label relationship. Composition began January 2011; recording took place in April 2011 at Phantom Manor (a converted warehouse office space in Chicago) rather than Electrical Audio — a deliberate move toward a rawer sound. (Wikipedia: Empros, as of 2026-07-14.) Brandon Curtis produced and mixed; Mike Lust co-engineered (Wikipedia; not in Discogs credits). Phil Karnats (Secret Machines guitarist, Discogs ID 3162498) added accordion and cello, extending the Secret Machines personnel connection beyond Curtis himself. Joe Lambert mastered. Cook on the rawness tradeoff: Curtis helped the band distinguish “deliberate lack of polish” from “lazy sloppiness” — a key part of his role as objective outside ear. Cook acknowledged overall: “In some ways I don’t think it sounds as good as Geneva. But we kinda knew that going into the process.” (You Indie interview, October 2011 — russiancircles.tumblr.com.) Memorial (2013, SH111) continued the Curtis trilogy and became the most-pressed Russian Circles album overall (19 pressings). Greg Norman returned as engineer; Curtis mixed. Cook: “I can’t really stress the importance of Brandon Curtis enough” — praising his “really special blend of skill sets” combining music theory, studio technique, and eclectic taste (from Teenage Jesus & The Jerks to classical). For Memorial specifically, Curtis helped balance “the ugliness of Empros’ darker moments and the lushness of Geneva.” (Bearded Gentlemen Music interview, October 2013 — beardedgentlemenmusic.com.) Chelsea Wolfe contributed guest vocals on the title track (not credited in Discogs). David Knudson (Botch / Minus The Bear) handled art design.

Kurt Ballou era (2016–present)

Guidance (2016, SH159) began a new production partnership with Kurt Ballou (guitarist of Converge), who recorded, mixed, and produced. Carl Saff mastered; Ben Chisholm (Chelsea Wolfe’s primary collaborator) did design. Ballou continued on Blood Year (2019), mastered by Magnus Lindberg (2); and Gnosis (2021, 11 pressings), where Zach Weeks engineered, Ballou engineered and mixed, and Magnus Lindberg (2) mastered. Alexis Marshall (Daughters) is credited as “Text By” on Blood Year (Discogs: “Text By,” release 13947453) — not a vocal performance credit.

Connections

  • Converge — Kurt Ballou is Converge’s guitarist; produced RC’s Guidance, Blood Year, Gnosis
  • Secret Machines — Brandon Curtis fronted Secret Machines; produced RC’s Geneva, Empros, Memorial
  • Chelsea Wolfe — fellow SH artist; guest vocals on the title track of Memorial (2013); the band had been fans since her Apokalypsis record and toured with her in summer 2012, during which a collaboration was discussed; they sent her a rough demo of “Memorial” and she returned her vocal track two hours later — Brian Cook called it “the most natural collaboration ever” (Ghost Cult Magazine interview); Rolling Stone premiered the track in September 2013 (chelseawolfe.com); Wolfe also joined RC on their first European tour in support of the album (Oct–Nov 2013); credit does not appear on Discogs; confirmed by Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-29); Ben Chisholm (Chelsea’s collaborator) designed Guidance
  • Sargent House — management relationship predates label relationship; SH managed RC from ~2008, studio albums from 2011
