Christian Death

Christian Death

Discogs ID: 94647
Origin: Los Angeles, USA
Active: 1979–present

Los Angeles gothic rock / deathrock band. Founded by Rozz Williams (1963–1998). Released The Decomposition Of Violets (An Evening With Christian Death Live In Hollywood) on ROIR (A138, 1985). Debut album Only Theatre Of Pain (Frontier Records, 1982) is the key record.

L’Invitation Au Suicide issued the first European pressings of their core catalog: Only Theater Of Pain (SUICIDE DIFFÉRÉ N° 1, 1983), Deathwish (SD 4, 1984), Catastrophe Ballet (SD 5, 1984), and “Ashes” (SD 7, 1985) — nine LAS releases in total, making Christian Death the label’s most-issued artist. The LAS Christian Death catalog runs under its own sub-series prefix (SUICIDE DIFFÉRÉ).

Label / Licensing History — LAS and Frontier Records

The relationship between L’Invitation Au Suicide and Frontier Records for the Christian Death catalog involved a formal license sale for the debut, followed by a separate original recording deal for subsequent European releases.

Frontier → LAS license (the debut LP): According to Valor Kand’s account (vamp.org, “A History According to Valor”), Frontier Records proactively contacted Yann Farcy of LAS and sold him the French license for Only Theatre of Pain. Farcy agreed to take the license on the condition that the band come to France for promotional purposes. Multiple secondary sources (Encyclopedia.com, musicianguide.com) confirm the same: “Farcy licensed the album and EP in France.” The Deathwish EP (pre-LP demos) was also subsequently issued on LAS in 1984 under this framework. This was a commercial license transaction initiated by Frontier, not an informal bootleg or handshake arrangement.

LAS as original recording label (Catastrophe Ballet onward): Once the band relocated to Paris, LAS became the originating label for new recordings — Catastrophe Ballet (1984), Ashes (1985), and associated material — not a sub-licensee. These recordings were owned by LAS directly, not derived from Frontier masters.

Practical problems on the ground: The Valor Kand account (vamp.org) documents that Farcy’s fulfillment of the deal was highly problematic — promised airfare, per diem, and recording funds failed to materialize or arrived only partially. Farcy leveraged the fact that he “had already bought the license” to insist the band perform under the Christian Death name (rather than Pompeii 99) as it allowed him to simultaneously promote both LPs. The arrangement was thus formally legitimate on paper (a real license purchase from Frontier) but operationally chaotic, with Farcy acting in bad faith toward the band.

No evidence of sublicensing terms or exclusivity clauses has surfaced in any source consulted. Frontier continued to hold US rights throughout; later European LAS catalog editions show distribution shifting to Nostradamus, Contempo (Italy), and Normal (Germany), which may reflect LAS sublicensing or distribution deals downstream, but no documentation of those terms has been located.
