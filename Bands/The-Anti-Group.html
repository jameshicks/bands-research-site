The Anti Group

Industrial / electronic / psychoacoustic research project. Sheffield / UK. Active from 1978 (conceptually) with first performances 1985. Also known as TAGC — The Anti Group Communications. Discogs artist ID: 44599.

Overview

The Anti Group (T.A.G.) was conceived by Adi Newton and Steven James Turner as early as 1978, structured as a multi-dimensional research and development collective operating across sound, film, video, and performance. The project’s stated “modus operandi” was documentation of each research strand. Under its expanded designation T.A.G.C. (The Anti Group Communications), it pushed progressively into psychoacoustics, psychophysics, and Meontological research — exploring non-existence and consciousness through frequency and ambisonic audio technologies.

Newton is the constant creative force throughout; TAGC functions simultaneously as an alias, an extension, and a philosophical counterpart to his primary group Clock DVA.

The name variation T·A·G·C (with interpuncts) appears on the Digitaria LP sleeve; T.A.G. on early releases including The Delivery and Big Sex; T.A.G.C. becomes the standard from Meontological Research Recording Record 2 onward.

Origins and Early Activity (1978–1984)

The Anti Group concept was formed by Adi Newton and Steven James Turner — Turner was also a founding member of Clock DVA — though no recordings were released until 1985. The initial non-theoretical works were:

  • “The Delivery” — a 16mm triptych film projection and soundtrack
  • “The Discussion” — anti-theatre performance for five tape recorders and multi-video projection

Both were first performed at De Doelen, Rotterdam, on 22 September 1985. “The Delivery” also appeared at the 2nd Atonal Festival at Ballhaus Tiergarten, Berlin, 18 February 1985, where its soundtrack was recorded on a mobile 24-track system and subsequently released on Atonal Records.

Key Releases

Ha - Zulu (1985, Sweatbox)

Debut UK 12”, Discogs release ID 185540. Credited to The Anti Group. Produced with the core early lineup: Adi Newton (trumpet, tape, organ, voice), David Heppenstall (keyboards, computer), Darrel D’Silva (saxophone), Barry R. Harden (bass), Mark Holmes (4) (guitar), Robert Baker (kinetic engineering). Remixed and recorded by Richard H. Kirk and Stephen Mallinder of Cabaret Voltaire — a significant Sheffield scene intersection.

The Delivery (1985, Atonal Records)

Live-in-Berlin document, Discogs release ID 185538, master 32495. Credited as T A G. Produced by Dimitri Hegemann and Manfred Schiek. Oskar M contributed drums, percussion, and violin. Engineered by Reinhard Zwierlein and Robert Baker. Newton credited as organ, tape, trumpet, voice. The core ensemble matched the Ha - Zulu lineup.

Digitaria (1986, Sweatbox)

Ambisonic LP, Discogs release ID 184344, master 32508. The group’s most ambitious early statement — described in the artist profile as “a Technological and Ethnological work based on the ideas of the Sabean cults of ancient Khem and the Dogon tribe of Mali.” Credited as T·A·G·C. Performances of this material took place at the 3rd Atonal Festival (Alte TU-Mensa, Berlin, 13 December 1986), SO 36 (Berlin, 14 February 1987), and the Museum of Contemporary Art, Prato, Italy (23 September 1988).

12”, Discogs release ID 577754, master 32509. Credited as T.A.G.C. Programmed by TAGC and Techno Geist Associates. Artwork by Artwork Device.

Audiophile (1994, Anterior Research Recordings / Side Effects)

US compilation / retrospective, Discogs release ID 214475, master 32512. Edited and remastered by Adi Newton and Maurizio Fasolo. Draws together key earlier material.

Iso-Erotic Calibrations (1994, Anterior Research Recordings / Musica Maxima Magnetica)

Italian CD, Discogs release ID 100595, master 32516. One of the most widely pressed TAGC releases (11 pressings). Features collaborations with Martin Bowes (Attrition) and Abby Helasdottir (aka Bestia Astrum / Gydja / Xindur X) — 6 shared releases with TAGC across this period.

Later Work and Revivals

TAGC activity became intermittent through the 1990s and 2000s, with Newton focusing on Clock DVA. Documented later releases:

  • TAG / TAGC performances at Electro-Acoustic Music Academy Stockholm (23 August 1990) and ARS Electronica symposium on Virtual Reality, Linz, Austria (8 September 1990)
  • Phantom Airwaves Presents: (2016, Somnimage, with Matar (4) and Mykel Boyd)
  • Chaos Variation III (2018, NUKFM) — TAGC credited as producer/mixer; collaboration with Obsolete Capitalism Sound System, Jacques Beloeil, Gabriele Fantuzzi, and others from the Italian noise/experimental circuit
  • Organ Needles (2020, Anterior Research Media Comm)
  • Meontological Research Recording III Transmission From The Trans Yuggothian Broadcast Station (2021, Rizosfera) — series resumed, featuring Maurizio Martinucci (aka TEZ) and Shara Vasilenko
  • AMMA (2022, Armcomm Europe / Rizosfera)

Newton’s own label, Anterior Research Media Comm (later Armcomm Europe), handles TAGC and Clock DVA releases from the 2010s onward.

Sheffield Scene Context

TAGC sits at the industrial/electronic extreme of the Sheffield underground, operating in parallel with Clock DVA (Newton’s primary vehicle) and in loose orbit around Cabaret Voltaire — as demonstrated by Kirk and Mallinder’s remix contributions to Ha - Zulu (1985). Newton is also documented on the Dreams To Fill The Vacuum compilation (Cherry Red, CRCDBOX83, 2019) with “Zulu” (Disc 4, track 7), cross-referencing the Ha - Zulu 12” material.

The connection between Sheffield industrial and the Berlin Atonal scene is significant: The Delivery (1985) was produced by Dimitri Hegemann, who later founded the Tresor club; the Berlin recordings place TAGC at the heart of early European industrial/noise activity.
