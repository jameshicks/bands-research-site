Palais Schaumburg

Avant-garde / neue deutsche Welle band from Hamburg, Germany. Discogs artist ID: 26992. Active 1979–1984. Name variations: Palais Schaumberg, Palais Schaumburg Telefon, Palais Shaunburg.

Overview

Palais Schaumburg were formed in Hamburg in 1979 by Holger Hiller and Thomas Fehlmann, who drew inspiration from the US avant-garde group DNA (3) (James Chance / DNA territory — the specific Discogs ID referenced in their profile is a6708, the No Wave band). The founding lineup also included F.M. Einheit (Frank Martin Strauß, real name) on drums and a bassist credited only as Chris Lunch; Fehlmann had also been involved with the punk act ZK (a58780 per Discogs profile). The band named themselves after the German chancellor’s official residence in Bonn.

Their early singles — “Rote Lichter” (1980/1981, Zickzack ZZ 23) and “Telephon / Kinder Der Tod” (Zickzack ZZ 33) — appeared on the Hamburg independent label Zickzack, positioning them squarely within the Hamburg NDW underground. Cover art for “Rote Lichter” was by painter Albert Oehlen.

F.M. Einheit left in mid-1981 to join the industrial/avant-garde group Einstürzende Neubauten (Discogs a38794). He was replaced on drums by Ralf Hertwig; Timo Blunck took over on bass. After F.M. Einheit’s departure, Holger Hiller also departed for a solo career, leaving Fehlmann as the senior co-founder.

The self-titled debut LP Palais Schaumburg (1981, Palais Schaumburg / Phonogram 6435 139; also issued on Virgin in France and Kamera Records in the UK) was produced and mixed by David Cunningham (The Flying Lizards), an unusual British/German experimental connection. Cunningham also played guitar on at least one track. The album’s lineup was Hiller (vocals/guitar/piano), Fehlmann (synthesizer/trumpet/saxophone), Blunck (bass), Hertwig (drums), and Wigbert Zelfel on saxophone.

Lupa (1982, Palais Schaumburg / Phonogram 6435 182) marked a significant stylistic shift, introducing horns, vibraphone, trombone, and a slight jazz-fusion influence. Producer was Andy Hernandez (“Sugar Coated” Andy Hernandez, of Kid Creole and the Coconuts); mixing was split between Julian McBrowne (most tracks), Mel Jefferson (one track), and Mike Theodore (one track). Lyrics on the entire album were credited to new vocalist Walter Thielsch; Stefan Bauer joined on vibraphone, trombone, and piano. Anton Corbijn photographed both Lupa and Hockey.

Hockey (1983, Phonogram / Mercury) brought another lineup change: Moritz von Oswald joined on drums and percussion, replacing Hertwig as primary drummer (Hertwig remained credited as a performer). The album was co-produced by Mel Jefferson and the band, with no outside producer of note. Thomas Fehlmann, Hertwig, Blunck, Thielsch, and Bauer all performed; Christian Kellersmann contributed saxophone.

The third and final LP Parlez-Vous Schaumburg? (1984, Mercury 822 739-1) was co-produced by Inga Humpe (of Neonbabies / DÖF) and Gareth Jones, who also recorded and mixed it. Humpe arranged vocals and sang backing vocals; John Barker (Discogs a310533) arranged orchestra and conducted. The lineup had contracted to Fehlmann, Hertwig, and von Oswald. It incorporated Latin and big-band touches but failed commercially. The band dissolved shortly after its release, which Discogs dates to 1984 (the profile says dissolved 1984; the brief says 1985 — sources differ slightly).

The catalog was first reissued on CD by Tapete Records in 2002 (Palais Schaumburg LP) and 2004 (Lupa), and then reissued on vinyl by Bureau B from 2008 onward: the “Wir Bauen Eine Neue Stadt / Madonna” single (BB17, 2008) and the debut LP remaster (BB100, 2012). Bureau B remains the primary physical reissue home.

  • F.M. Einheit — founding drummer (Frank Martin Strauß); left 1981 to join Einstürzende Neubauten; extensive solo and collaborative career

  • David Cunningham — produced debut LP; also produced This Heat and lead of The Flying Lizards
