The Heartbreakers (2)

Origin: New York City, USA
Active: 1975–1984 (intermittently)

New York punk band formed in 1975 by Johnny Thunders and Jerry Nolan (ex-New York Dolls) and Richard Hell (after he left Television). Hell departed in May 1976; Billy Rath replaced him on bass. Walter Lure had joined on second guitar in July 1975 while Hell was still in the band. Key album: L.A.M.F. (Track Records, 1977).

History

Formation and Richard Hell era (May 1975 – May 1976)

The band formed in May 1975. Thunders and Nolan had quit the New York Dolls earlier that year; Hell had just left Television over a creative-control dispute with Tom Verlaine. The three met and agreed to form a band together. Their first performance was 30 May 1975 at the Coventry, Queens. Hell played bass and shared vocal duties with Thunders.

Walter Lure joined as second guitarist shortly after, debuting at CBGB on 4 July 1975. He was not a replacement for Hell — he joined while Hell was still in the band, adding a second guitar to what had been a three-piece.

The core conflict with Hell was over stage and vocal dominance. At a rehearsal in early 1976 Hell issued an ultimatum: he would sing most of the songs in the set, with Thunders reduced to one or two per set. Thunders, Nolan, and Lure united against this demand. Hell was effectively forced out; his final show with the band was 14 May 1976 at the Rathskeller, Boston. Billy Rath then joined as bassist.

Hell took his songs with him — notably “Blank Generation,” which he had originally written for Television and brought into the Heartbreakers. He went on to form Richard Hell & The Voidoids.

Recordings from the Hell era: No studio material was released during his tenure. Four demo tracks recorded at SBS Studios, NYC on 23 January 1976 (with Hell on bass) were later released on the L.A.M.F. Definitive Edition box set (Jungle Records, 2012). Live recordings from CBGB (7 July 1975) and Mother’s (16 November 1975) appeared on the posthumous live LP What Goes Around… (Bomp!, 1991).
