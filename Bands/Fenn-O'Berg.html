Fenn O’Berg

Laptop improvisation trio. Discogs artist ID: 1513105. Active 2001–2012; inactive since the death of Peter Rehberg in July 2021.

Members: Fennesz (Christian Fennesz), Jim O’Rourke, Peter Rehberg (also recorded as Pita).

Overview

Fenn O’Berg were one of the defining live-laptop improvisation trios of the early 2000s. All three members were already central figures on the Vienna-based Mego label — Fennesz as its most celebrated solo artist, Peter Rehberg as a co-operator of the label and its successor Editions Mego, and Jim O’Rourke as a Chicago-via-Tokyo musician whose range spanned free improvisation, avant-rock production, and electronic composition. The trio’s practice was real-time computer improvisation: each performer working on a laptop, with no fixed score, no overdubbing, and no studio correction after the fact.

Their name is a portmanteau of the members’ surnames: Fennesz, O’Rourke, and Rehberg.

The group’s debut was released in 2002 under the individual members’ names (Fennesz / O’Rourke / Rehberg) rather than the Fenn O’Berg artist credit. When they returned in 2009, Magic & Return reissued the debut alongside new material, and subsequent releases used the consolidated group name.

Peter Rehberg died on 23 July 2021. His death brought the trio’s activity to a permanent end — no posthumous releases or continuations as Fenn O’Berg have been announced, and the group’s identity was inseparable from all three members performing together.

Peter Rehberg’s Death and the Trio’s End

Peter Rehberg died on 23 July 2021 in Vienna, at the age of 53. As founder and operator of Editions Mego, his death also marked the end of the label’s active direction. For Fenn O’Berg, Rehberg’s death is definitive: the group had no permanent continuations planned, no session-replacement mechanism, and no posthumous activity has been attributed to the Fenn O’Berg name. In Hell (2012) stands as the trio’s final release.

Fennesz and Jim O’Rourke have continued as separate solo artists. The Fenn O’Berg project is now a closed body of work — six releases (including the two Japan LPs as discrete releases) across a decade, all on Mego or Editions Mego, documenting an approach to laptop improvisation rooted in the Vienna experimental scene Peter Rehberg helped build.
