Blackeyed Susans

Discogs ID: 422723 Origin: Perth, Western Australia (formed); Melbourne, Victoria (based from 1992) Years active: 1989–2018 (Discogs credit span; last release 2017) Genres: Alternative Rock, Country Rock, Folk Rock, Indie Rock

The Blackeyed Susans are an Australian rock band formed in Perth in 1989, who relocated to Melbourne in 1992 and became central figures in the city’s underground music scene of the 1990s. Their sound combined country, folk, and rock influences with a dark, emotionally searching quality. The band cycled through an unusually wide membership over nearly three decades — functioning at times as a loose collective — but was anchored throughout by vocalist/guitarist Rob Snarski and bassist/multi-instrumentalist Phil Kakulas.

Two notable members — Warren Ellis and Jim White (2) — went on to co-found Dirty Three (1992). Both appear on the band’s second album All Souls Alive (1993), with Ellis also credited on the 2009 retrospective Reveal Yourself.

Biography

Formation and Perth Years (1989–1991)

The Blackeyed Susans formed in Perth, Western Australia in 1989. The debut release, the Some Births Are Worse Than Murders 12” EP (1990, Waterfront Records, DAMP 127), established the core sound: alternative rock with country touches, self-produced alongside engineer James Hewgill. The founding Perth lineup was built around Rob Snarski and Phil Kakulas, with David McComb (also of The Triffids) contributing vocals, guitar, and songwriting, and Ross Bolleter on organ, accordion, and piano. Drums were handled by Alsy MacDonald. Artwork on the debut was by Martyn P. Casey.

The Welcome Stranger compilation/album (1992, Waterfront, DAMP 171) gathered three distinct recording sessions into a single CD — an unusual structure that documents the band in transition. The three sessions each had slightly different lineups: the first four tracks reprised the Perth lineup (Snarski, McComb, Kakulas, MacDonald, Bolleter); tracks 5–8 and 15 introduced Evil Graham Lee (pedal steel), Timothy Rollinson (electric guitar), James Cruickshank (organ), Mark Dawson (2) (percussion), and Kathy Wemyss (trumpet, vocals); tracks 9–14 were a McComb-led session with Kenny Davis Jnr. on keyboards. David McComb co-produced the third session.

Melbourne Years: The Classic Lineup (1992–1994)

After moving to Melbourne in 1992, the band recorded All Souls Alive (1993, Torn & Frayed, TORN CD5) — their most celebrated album and the one that documents the nexus with Dirty Three. The lineup for most of the album was: Snarski (vocals/guitar), Kakulas (bass/double bass), Evil Graham Lee (pedal steel/guitars), Warren Ellis (violin, organ, accordion, piano, harpsichord), Jim White (2) (drums), and Mark C. Halstead (mandolin, backing vocals). Warren Ellis and Jim White appear on eight of the ten tracks. Kakulas produced; Andy Parsons engineered. The record draws on country, folk, and post-punk elements, with strong songwriting from the McComb/Kakulas partnership.

The Hard Liquor…Soft Music album (1994, self-released, BS693) was a trio recording — Snarski, Kakulas, and Evil Graham Lee — recorded by Andy Parsons (3) with guest piano from Chris Copping on one track. It represents a stripped-back, rawer phase.

Hi-Gloss Era (1995–1998)

Mouth To Mouth (1995, Hi-Gloss Records/MDS, HIG006) was produced by Kakulas, recorded by Victor Van Vugt, and mixed by Tony Cohen — a significant production upgrade. The lineup shifted: Evil Graham Lee continued on pedal steel, Kiernan Box joined on piano/organ, and Mark Dawson (2) took over on drums. Dan Luscombe first appears here.

Some Night, Somewhere (1996, Hi-Gloss/MDS, HIG010) and Spin The Bottle (1997, Hi-Gloss/MDS, HIG015) continued the collaboration with Van Vugt (recording/mixing) and Kakulas producing. Spin The Bottle saw the fullest sound yet: brass arrangements (Matthew Habben, Adam Hutterer, Ken Gardner (2)), string players (Helen Mountfort, Deirdre Dowling, Jen Anderson, Sue Simpson), multiple backing vocalists. Dan Luscombe is now a full member (guitar, organ, keyboards, vocals). Evil Graham Lee retained on pedal steel for some tracks.

The La Mascara EP (1998, Hi-Gloss, HIG025) was a five-track Spanish-inflected release with a stylised Latinised credit as “Las Blackeyed Susans / Las Susanas de Ojos Morados” — a characteristic bit of Blackeyed Susans wit. Van Vugt produced again.

Hiatus and Covers Album (2001)

Dedicated To The Ones We Love (2001, Not On Label, BS 693) was a covers album — 16 tracks of American and international pop, country, and folk standards (Alex Chilton, Bruce Springsteen, Bacharach/David, etc.) — with no personnel credits in the Discogs entry. It was a self-released project in the quiet period between their regular albums.

Retrospective and Late Career (2009–2017)

Reveal Yourself (2009, Liberation Music, LMCD0057) is a major three-CD + DVD retrospective box set spanning the full career. It documents all the lineup variations, with separate producers credited per session. Warren Ellis appears on the All Souls Alive era tracks (CD1-8 to CD1-13: violin, accordion, piano). Jim White (2) plays drums on the same session. The retrospective confirmed the band’s standing in Australian alternative music and introduced them to a new audience.

Close Your Eyes And See (2017, Teardrop (2), BS699) is the most recent studio album. The core lineup by this point: Snarski (vocals), Kakulas (bass/guitar/keyboards), Mark Dawson (2) (drums), Evil Graham Lee (pedal steel/guitars), Kiernan Box (keyboards/harmonica), Dan Luscombe (guitars/keyboards/synth, producer), and J.P. Shilo (guitar/violin), who joins as a credited member for the first time. Matthew Habben, Adam Hutterer, and Ken Gardner (2) continue the brass section from the Hi-Gloss era. Dan Luscombe produced and mixed.

Connections

  • The Triffids — David McComb (vocalist/songwriter), Phil Kakulas (bass), and Evil Graham Lee (pedal steel) all came from The Triffids; the Blackeyed Susans were partly a post-Triffids project
  • Dirty Three — Warren Ellis (violin) and Jim White (2) (drums) were members of Blackeyed Susans before forming Dirty Three in 1992 alongside Mick Turner
  • Nick Cave & The Bad Seeds — Warren Ellis became a permanent Bad Seed and close collaborator with Nick Cave
  • Crime & The City Solution — Jim White (2) played with Crime & The City Solution; their Melbourne underground scene overlapped
  • Venom P. Stinger — Jim White (2) also associated with this Melbourne band
  • The Drones (2) — Dan Luscombe is a member of The Drones, connecting the Blackeyed Susans to the 2000s Melbourne underground rock scene
  • Essendon Airport — Evil Graham Lee also associated with this Melbourne band

Name Variations (Discogs)

The band traded under several names: “The Blackeyed Susans”, “The Blackeyed Susans Trio” (for Hard Liquor…Soft Music), “Las Blackeyed Susans”, “Las Susanas de Ojos Morados” (La Mascara Spanish-themed EP), and “Black Eyed Susan”.

Open Questions

  • Exact dates of Warren Ellis’s membership — Discogs credits him on All Souls Alive (1993) and Reveal Yourself (2009 retrospective), but the brief says “before Dirty Three formed in 1992.” Whether Ellis was a full member or a session player, and whether he left before or after the Melbourne relocation, is unclear from Discogs alone.
  • David McComb died in 1999; the extent of his involvement past the Welcome Stranger sessions (1991–1992) is not fully documented in Discogs credits.
