3 Teens Kill 4

East Village, New York City noise/post-punk/no wave collective, active 1982–1985, with a 2017 archival reissue. Discogs artist ID: 397742. Also known as 3TK4.

Overview

3 Teens Kill 4 were a multi-media performance collective rooted in the East Village’s early-1980s underground. The group blurred the line between music and visual art — most notably because their central figure, David Wojnarowicz, was simultaneously one of the most significant visual artists of the decade. The band occupied the same milieu as the No Wave and post-punk scenes but resisted easy genre assignment: their records combine minimal percussion, tape noise, found-sound collage, spoken word, and confrontational energy. Their name was reportedly drawn from a tabloid headline, encapsulating their interest in the raw violence of urban life.

The group released a self-produced cassette/acetate single in 1982, a 12” mini-album on the short-lived NYC imprint Point Blank Records (2) in 1983, and a cassette-only two-tracker in 1985. The sole LP, No Motive, was reissued in 2017 by San Francisco’s Dark Entries (catalog DE-170), exposing the band to a new generation of listeners interested in the NYC underground.

Biography

Formation and East Village Context (1982–1983)

The group came together in the East Village at the moment when that neighborhood was the fulcrum of New York’s underground — the same blocks that housed the Mudd Club, ABC No Rio, and Club 57 and their attendant art-noise ecosystems. The founding lineup comprised David Wojnarowicz, Jesse Hultberg, Brian Butterick, Doug Bressler, and Julie Hair, with Bill Gerstel also listed as a member on Discogs.

Their first release was a hand-made 7” acetate, Tell Me Something Good / Visitation (1982, Not On Label), pressed in what was almost certainly a tiny run. The A-side is a cover of Stevie Wonder’s “Tell Me Something Good” (written by Wonder, though Discogs credits it under Stevie Wonder’s name — see Open Questions). The B-side, “Visitation,” was co-written by Wojnarowicz and Hultberg. The record was entirely self-made: Wojnarowicz hand-decorated the labels with marker, ink stamps, and holographic foil, making each copy a unique art object.

No Motive LP (1983)

The full-length No Motive was released in 1983 as a 12” mini-album on Point Blank Records (2) (catalog number 3TK4), produced by the band itself (credited as “3TK4”). Engineer was Peter Darmi; executive producers were Alan Mace and Robert Bradley. Ivan Ivan mixed the opening track “Hold Up.” A lyric credit on “Hut / Bean Song” goes to Rachel Wolfe. The Stevie Wonder cover reappears as track A2.

The same year the LP appeared in a French pressing on L’Invitation Au Suicide (1984), a French post-punk/industrial label that had documented acts from across the European and American underground.

Liner notes for the 2017 reissue were contributed by surviving members Brian Butterick, Doug Bressler, Jesse Hultberg, Julie Hair, and an excerpt from a 1988 David Wojnarowicz interview with Matthew Rose in Arts Magazine (issue 62, May 1988). The nightlife figure Haoui Montaug also contributed liner notes, placing the band firmly within the broader East Village/downtown scene.

Cassette and Dissolution (1985)

The final release, Falling Out/Place To Hide (1985, Not On Label), was a cassette-only two-tracker. Doug Bressler is credited as sole producer. After 1985 the group ceased activity.

2017 Reissue

Dark Entries reissued No Motive as a full LP (DE-170) in 2017, remastered by George Horn. The reissue expanded the original mini-album with additional tracks (tracklist runs A1–B5, ten tracks total, versus the original six). Cover photo by Seiji Kakizaki; artwork includes a painting by Wojnarowicz. The reissue prompted renewed critical attention to the band in the context of Wojnarowicz’s growing posthumous reputation.

Connections

  • David Wojnarowicz — core member, visual artist, writer; 3TK4 was his primary band; his Discogs profile lists only three releases as a primary artist (ITSOFOMO, Optic Nerve, Cross Country)
  • Dark Entries — reissued No Motive in 2017 (DE-170); San Francisco label specializing in post-punk and electronic reissues
  • L’Invitation Au Suicide — French label that pressed the European edition of No Motive (1984)
  • Point Blank Records (2) — original NYC label for No Motive (1983), appears to have released only this one record
  • Haoui Montaug — NYC downtown scene figure, door person at Danceteria, contributed liner notes to 2017 reissue
  • Ivan Ivan — mixed “Hold Up” on No Motive
  • No Wave scene broadly: the band shared the East Village underground with acts like DNA, Teenage Jesus and the Jerks, and the wider post-punk/experimental community

Open Questions

  • Bill Gerstel is listed as a member on Discogs but does not appear in any release credits — his specific role in the group is unclear
  • The A-side of the 1982 single covers Stevie Wonder’s “Tell Me Something Good” — the Discogs credit attributes it to Stevie Wonder (artist ID 18956), but the original song “Tell Me Something Good” was written by Wonder for Rufus (1974). Whether the 3TK4 version is a straight cover or a reworking is not resolvable from Discogs data alone
  • Rachel Wolfe (lyrics) and Ivan Ivan (mixing) appear on No Motive but have very sparse Discogs profiles — their broader affiliations are unknown from this DB
  • The French pressing on L’Invitation Au Suicide (1984) — unclear whether this was a licensed reissue or a co-production
  • The 2017 Dark Entries LP adds tracks not on the original 1983 mini-album (ten tracks vs six) — source of the additional material (unreleased recordings, cassette tracks) is not documented in Discogs
  • Haoui Montaug’s specific connection to the band beyond the liner note credit is not documented in this DB
  • No compilation appearances are documented in Discogs for the band, though given the scene context this seems unlikely to be the full picture
