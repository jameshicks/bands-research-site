Last Few Days

UK post-industrial/experimental collective, formed 1979 by Daniel Landin with Si Joyce and Keir Fraser; active 1979–1986 (original run); returned as a pop/house act 1990. Discogs artist ID: 76769. Also credited as LFD, LFD!, Last Few Days!

Overview

Last Few Days were a deliberately obscure experimental collective from the early 1980s UK post-industrial scene. Formed in 1979 by Daniel Landin, Si Joyce, and Keir Fraser, Wikipedia’s article on Landin describes them as a “highly conceptual” and “experimental militant classicist group” whose primary aim was live performance in unconventional venues — chapels, cinemas, burger bars, silos, tunnels. (Wikipedia — Daniel Landin, as of 2026-08-07)

They consciously avoided releasing material, kept live appearances rare, and maintained a low profile — briefly elevated by their association with Laibach. Their concerts ran through the night (sometimes to 7am), mixing live music with film screenings. Sonically harsh and confrontational: heavy use of megaphones, guitars played with broken bottles, and unrelenting percussion — described in the Discogs profile as “apocalyptic music.”

The collective was centred on three core members: Daniel Landin (alias D. Styme / Stan Bingo), a former Throbbing Gristle roadie/soundman who had been working with Super 8 and VHS video from 1978 and had collaborated with Throbbing Gristle documenting live performances and art events before forming LFD; Si Joyce (alias Si Gross / S Gross); and Keir Wahid (aliases K. Warhead / K. Fraser). Fritz Catlin (also of 23 Skidoo) began performing with them regularly from 1981, and Sam Mills (also of 23 Skidoo) contributed occasionally. Catlin also performed with Laibach during the joint 1983 European tour. (LTM Recordings; Kill Your Pet Puppy)

Their aim was a “total 3D experience” — music, film, and confrontational performance combined. In Eastern Europe during the 1983 tour with Laibach, audiences ran to approximately 3,000 per night, against the ~300 typical at UK shows — a stark contrast that the surviving members noted when discussing the reinvention of the 1990 pop phase. (Last.fm wiki; Selfish Few blog, 2019)

The group recorded extensively from their tours and live concerts — over 100 hours of material from the 1983–84 European tour period alone. In June 1986, K Warhead and S Gross sat with Dave Henderson, a pile of cassettes, an equalisation unit, and a Revox to compile this into Pure Spirit And Saliva Live (Dead Man’s Curve DMC 002). (Last.fm wiki)

In 1990, the group reconstituted as a stripped-down pop/house duo (Keir and Si), signed to Fontana, and released two 12” singles. The reinvention was deliberate: “We started listening to Radio One, and just got more into regular music after years of extremism.” (Melody Maker, 27 January 1990)

Discography

1982 — Pow-Wow (Fetish Records FM2010)

12” mini-album, primary artist Stephen Mallinder — Last Few Days credited as “Performer [Chance Element].” Also features Alan Fish (drum assistance) and sleeve by Neville Brody. Last Few Days’ earliest documented appearance on record, the same year as the Western Works sessions.

1982 — Final Academy Brixton 29.9.82 / 30.9.82 (Artificial Eye, unofficial cassette)

Split cassette with 23 Skidoo. Side A: 23 Skidoo at the Final Academy (29 Sep 1982); Side B: Last Few Days at the Final Academy (30 Sep 1982). Released unofficially. See Final Academy.

1983 — Laibach / Last Few Days (Galerija ŠKUC Izdaja; Yugoslavia)

Cassette. Side A: Laibach (7 tracks). Side B: Last Few Days (5 tracks — “Polar Vision,” “I Lie,” “F1. Redemption,” “Blind Knives,” “Solemn Warnings”). Recorded at Western Works, Sheffield, May 1982 (B1, B4); released in conjunction with the April 1983 Zagreb Music Biennial performances. Limited to approximately 400 copies. Side B engineering on “Polar Vision” and “Blind Knives” by Stephen Mallinder (credited S. Mallinder); “F1. Redemption” mixed by Johnny Turnbull. Catalogue number: none.

The Wikipedia article on Daniel Landin specifically describes the Western Works recordings as a “Polavision” soundtrack produced by Cabaret Voltaire — the only named recording project from this session. (Wikipedia — Daniel Landin, as of 2026-08-07)

Cassette. Live recordings from Amsterdam (NL Centrum, 1 Dec 1983) and Eindhoven (De Effenaar, 2 Dec 1983). All tracks untitled. Dutch label Staaltape.

1985 — Ritual: Magnetic North (Touch T5.45)

7” split single with S/Z. LFD contribute “Too Much Is Not Enough” (Side A); S/Z contribute “Terrified Of Soap” (Side B). Touch catalogue number T5.45.

1986 — Too Much Is Not Enough (Touch T9:45)

12” single, 45 RPM. Three tracks: “Too Much Is Not Enough” / “Solemn Warnings” / “If The Bonds Are Not To Burst.” Mastered at MPO.

1986 — Pure Spirit And Saliva Live (Dead Man’s Curve DMC 002)

LP. Compiled by Dave Henderson, K Warhead, and S Gross from approximately 100 hours of live recordings spanning the 1983–84 European tours. Compiled in a single session in June 1986 using cassettes, an equalisation unit, and a Revox. Performers credited: F Häaman (Fritz Catlin), K Warhead (Keir Fraser), S Gross (Si Joyce), D Styme (Daniel Landin). Photography by Andrew Catlin and Steve Pyke. This is the closest the group came to a definitive document of their live practice — raw, confrontational, eight tracks running over 40 minutes. (Last.fm wiki)

1990 Pop Phase

With the core duo of Keir (Fraser) and Si (Joyce), the band signed to Fontana and released house/pop singles as LFD! The shift was explicit and total — from harsh industrial to Radio One-ready dance pop.

Kicks (Fontana; multiple pressings 1990)

12” single with several mix variants across multiple pressings (“The Feelin’ Good Mix,” “Sub Mix,” “The Dungeons Mix”). Cat no. LFD 122.

Kicks (The Club Mixes) (Fontana LFD 122)

12” with three mixes: “The Feelin’ Good Mix” (6:13), “Sub Mix” (3:36), “The Dungeons Mix” (6:10).

Your Love Is Super Funky (Fontana LFD 212)

12” single, 1990. Three versions: 7” edit, Basement Mix (mixed by Andy Montgomery), and “The Frankie Foncett Mix” (remixed by Frankie Foncett). Barcode: 042287561911.

Last Few Days promo cassette (Product Inc., 1990)

Single-sided promo sampler. Tracks: “Kicks (7” edit),” “Hot Tonite (rough mix),” “Yes I Would,” “Soul Destroyer,” “Wild.”

Post-band careers

Daniel Landin — cinematographer / director

Landin (born 1962) had been working with Super 8 and VHS video from 1978 onward, including collaborations with Throbbing Gristle before Last Few Days formed. After the band dissolved he pursued a parallel path as a short-film director through the late 1980s: The Child and the Saw won the Golden Dancer at the Huesca International Film Festival (1987) and his films screened at Berlin (1986, 1989, 1990) and Edinburgh. (Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-27) He also directed Laibach: A Film from Slovenia (also known as Bravo), a 1993 documentary about Laibach, released on DVD by Mute in 2004. (Laibach biography)

From 1991 he worked as a cinematographer (DP), first on music videos for artists including Björk, David Bowie, Madonna, The Rolling Stones, Oasis, Blur, Pulp, Massive Attack, and Franz Ferdinand. He transitioned into features and longer-form work. Notable credits as DP: (IMDB nm0484779; Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-27; IndieWire interview)

  • Sexy Beast (2000, Jonathan Glazer dir.) — one-day pick-up shoot; this began a long Glazer collaboration
  • Under the Skin (2013, Jonathan Glazer dir.) — Scarlett Johansson; Dublin IFF Cinematography Prize 2014; widely praised for its hidden-camera approach and otherworldly visual language
  • The Yellow Birds (2017) — Sundance U.S. Dramatic Special Jury Award for Cinematography
  • Ray & Liz (2019, Richard Billingham dir.) — Buenos Aires Film Festival Best Cinematography

Also shot Danny Boyle’s 2012 London Olympic Opening Ceremony and collaborated with designer Alexander McQueen on fashion shows. IMDB lists 65 cinematography credits, 8 directing credits, and 3 writing credits. (IMDB nm0484779)
