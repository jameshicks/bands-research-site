Smack (9)

Manchester post-punk/new wave band, active around 1980–1981. Released two 7” singles on their own Asprin imprint. Featured on Manchester North Of England (A Story Of Independent Music Greater Manchester 1977 - 1993) with “Edward Fox”. Discogs artist ID: 1079234.

Overview

Smack (9) were a four-piece from Manchester, operating in the immediate post-punk / new wave moment around 1980–1981. They founded their own label, Asprin (catalog prefix ASP), specifically to release their own records — a common move in the Manchester DIY scene of the period. The debut single “Edward Fox” was produced by Rowland Jones and engineered at a Manchester studio session, with the original Asprin pressing later picked up and redistributed on Pinnacle Records (PIN 38). Their second and final single “Doctor Doctor” appeared in 1981 with no credited production personnel beyond the band themselves. The Discogs entry lists no profile text and no later activity, suggesting the band dissolved sometime in 1981.

“Edward Fox” was later compiled on Disc 2 (“Big Noise In The Jungle”) of Cherry Red’s 2017 box set Manchester North Of England (A Story Of Independent Music Greater Manchester 1977 - 1993), alongside contemporaries including A Certain Ratio, Crispy Ambulance, The Chameleons, Manchester Mekon, and Bet Lynch’s Legs.

The single was pressed on the band’s self-founded Asprin label. Matrix etchings reference Lyntone pressing plant (LYN-8171 / LYN-8172) and the “GRAEME – SOUND CLINIC” cutting notation (Graeme Durham, lacquer cutting).
