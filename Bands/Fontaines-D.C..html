Fontaines D.C.

Discogs ID: 6322880
Also credited as: Fontaines DC, Fontaines

Post-punk band from Dublin, Ireland. Collaborated with Massive Attack and Young Fathers on the Ceasefire 12” EP (2024, Battle Box). Their track “In Ár gCroithe Go Deo” appears on the EP in a remix by 3D (Massive Attack) and a remix by Orbital. Guitarist Conor Curley contributed a remix of the Massive Attack / Young Fathers track “Give.”

”In Ár gCroíthe Go Deo” — phrase background and Ceasefire EP context

Answer (researched 2026-08-16):

Phrase meaning and origin. “In ár gCroíthe go deo” is an Irish-language phrase meaning “In our hearts forever.” It is a traditional Irish expression of remembrance, used as a gravestone epitaph.

The Margaret Keane case (the direct inspiration for the song). The phrase’s specific public prominence — and Fontaines D.C.’s use of it — traces directly to a legal case in Coventry, England. Margaret Keane, a County Meath-born woman active in Coventry’s Irish community and the GAA, died in 2018. Her family sought to engrave “in ár gCroíthe go deo” on her gravestone at St Giles Church in Exhall, Coventry. The Chancellor of the Diocese of Coventry initially refused on the grounds that the Irish-only phrase could be misinterpreted as a political slogan and would not be understood by many people. The Church of England Consistory Court upheld this refusal in 2020. The family appealed; on 24 February 2021, the Court of Arches overturned the decision, ruling the refusal constituted discrimination on grounds of Irish race under the Human Rights Act and Article 14 of the European Convention on Human Rights. The judgment also directed all diocesan chancellors to review churchyard regulations to prevent discriminatory treatment of non-English inscriptions. [sources: Coventry Telegraph · Irish Legal News · RTÉ · Irish Times]

Fontaines D.C.’s use of the phrase (album track, Skinty Fia, 2022). Frontman Grian Chatten, who had recently relocated to London, was moved by the Keane case. The band recorded vocals for “In ár gCroíthe go deo” the day the appeal ruling was announced. The track opens their 2022 album Skinty Fia, which explores Irish identity in diaspora. In May 2022 band members met the Keane family at Margaret Keane’s graveside; the family described the meeting as “deeply moving.” [sources: NME · Nova.ie · Wikipedia, Skinty Fia article, as of 2026-08-16: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinty_Fia]

Bobby Sands / 1981 hunger strikers connection. The original brief noted the phrase appears on Bobby Sands’s headstone. Web research could not confirm this: the Wikipedia article on Bobby Sands does not mention the phrase, and no search result found a source attesting “in ár gCroíthe go deo” specifically on Sands’s headstone at Milltown Cemetery. His memorial is noted to include a plaque with a quote from his prison diary, but the exact text is not documented in sources found. The phrase is an established Irish epitaph formula and may have appeared on republican graves, but the direct link to Sands specifically remains unconfirmed. This is a conflict with the brief’s premise — treat as unverified until a source directly documents Sands’s headstone text.

The Ceasefire EP (2024) — why this track. No press statement or interview found explains specifically why Fontaines D.C. chose “In ár gCroíthe go deo” (rather than another track) for the Ceasefire EP. The song was already recorded and released on Skinty Fia (2022). Its use on the 2024 EP appears to be a contribution of existing material. The EP was a limited-edition 12” vinyl released on 1 March 2024 on Battle Box, bringing together Fontaines D.C., Massive Attack, and Young Fathers to support Médecins Sans Frontières (Doctors Without Borders) emergency operations in Gaza and the West Bank. 100% of profits were donated, with the total donation expected to exceed £150,000. The sleeve was designed by 3D (Robert Del Naja of Massive Attack); the reverse features text written on a Gaza hospital whiteboard by MSF medic Dr Mahmoud Abu Nujaila — “WHOEVER STAYS UNTIL THE END WILL TELL THE STORY, WE DID WHAT WE COULD. REMEMBER US” — which went viral on social media. The artists’ joint statement called for “an immediate ceasefire in Gaza and the West Bank” and expressed solidarity with “the Palestinian people living under a brutal military occupation.” The EP appeared on the Ceasefire EP in remixed form: a remix by 3D (Massive Attack) and a remix by Orbital. Conor Curley also contributed a remix of the Massive Attack / Young Fathers track “Give.” [sources: Brooklyn Vegan announcement · Electronic Groove · Crack Magazine · Bandcamp]

Thematic resonance between song and EP. The song’s subject — an Irish family denied the right to grieve in their own language by an English institutional authority — maps onto the EP’s themes of solidarity with a people under occupation and the vulnerability of civilian life. Whether the band explicitly articulated this connection in public statements has not been found in available press.
