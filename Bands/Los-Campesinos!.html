Los Campesinos! (a Spanish name that roughly translates to “The Peasants”), formed in early 2006 at Cardiff University in Wales, are a seven-piece indie band with a cult following characterized by an array of instrumental sounds and lyricism. Their lineup, which has changed several times across the band’s lifetime, currently features Gareth Paisey (lead vocals, lyrics, glockenspiel), brother of Kim Paisey (keyboards, vocals), Neil Turner (guitar), Tom Bromley (guitar), Rob Taylor (guitar, various instruments), Matt Fidler (bass) and Jason Adelinia (drums) as of 2024. Previous line-ups included Aleksandra “Aleks” Berditchevskaia (keyboards, vocals, melodica), Ollie Briggs (drums), Harriet Coleman (violin, string arrangements, keyboards, vocals), and Ellen Waddell (bass, vocals). Each member depicts their last name as “Campesinos!”

Overview

Los Campesinos! formed in early 2006 at Cardiff University in Cardiff, Wales and quickly built a cult following as a multi-piece indie band with dense, layered instrumentation and lyric-heavy songs. Their lineup has rotated considerably over the years — earlier configurations included Aleksandra Berditchevskaia, Harriet Coleman (violin/strings/keyboards), Ellen Waddell (bass), and Ollie Briggs (drums) — with a current (2024) lineup still anchored by Gareth Paisey on lead vocals and glockenspiel. Their catalogue is concentrated in the 2000s and 2010s, predominantly released on Wichita and Arts & Crafts; their own Heart Swells imprint accounts for eight releases stretching to 2025. Producer John Goodmanson is the dominant studio collaborator across the discography, with Guy Davie and David Newfeld also recurring across multiple releases.

Notes (web)

Heart Swells label — transition from Wichita Recordings

Heart Swells is Los Campesinos!‘s own self-run imprint. The label appears in credits as early as 2011 (Hello Sadness North American release via Heart Swells / Turnstile) and was used for select self-released items throughout the 2010s, but the full move away from Wichita as the primary label happened with All Hell (2024).

All Hell is described by the band and press as their first album that is “self-funded, self-produced, and self-released” entirely on Heart Swells. Wikipedia — All Hell DIY Magazine, June 2024

On announcing All Hell, the band acknowledged the relationship publicly via Twitter (15 May 2024): “All Hell is our first album without Wichita. We’d not be the band we are without them.” This statement is warm but contains no explanation of why they parted — it reads as a natural end to a long partnership rather than a falling-out. Wikipedia — All Hell, citing band tweet

The clearest motivation on record comes from guitarist/producer Tom Bromley in a Line of Best Fit interview (exact date not given, published around 2024): “there’s always a certain amount of commercial pressure and that is completely non-existent now. I think that, in a way more than you realise, frees you up.” The Line of Best Fit

The DIY Magazine interview further characterises the move as part of a deliberate “direct to fans” strategy: the band aim to do “everything as direct to fans as they possibly can,” and Gareth was quoted as saying they are “fed up of being dictated to by billion dollar platforms and a messed-up algorithm.” DIY Magazine, June 2024

The Dork interview (August 2024) adds corroborating context: “We used to have relatively big management, huge record labels, big PR campaigns and marketing teams around us” — the band have shed all of that, now operating without management or PR. Dork, August 2024

Summary of what is and is not sourced: No interview explicitly states that the Wichita contract expired by a specific date or that the band chose not to renew it. The picture across sources is: (a) the band had already been running Heart Swells as a subsidiary outlet since ~2011; (b) Sick Scenes (2017) was the last full Wichita-primary album; (c) after a seven-year gap, All Hell (2024) was fully self-funded and self-released, with Tom Bromley citing freedom from commercial pressure as the key benefit; and (d) the band’s parting statement to Wichita was explicitly grateful. The transition appears to reflect a deliberate choice for independence rather than a break, but no primary source confirms whether it was driven by contract expiry, a mutual decision to part, or a unilateral band choice.
