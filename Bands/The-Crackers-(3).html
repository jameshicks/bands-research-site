The Crackers (3)

Minneapolis power-pop / indie rock trio, active c. 1981. Discogs artist ID: 3168524.

Overview

The Crackers were a Minneapolis-based trio who released a single four-track 12” maxi-single, Sir Crackers!, on Twin-Tone Records in 1981 (cat TTR 8122). The band comprised Steve Almaas (bass, vocals, principal songwriter), Jay Peck (drums, vocals), and Karen Haglof (guitar, vocals — credited on the sleeve as “Karen Indiana,” a stage name she used at the time).

The Crackers exist at an intersection of Minneapolis punk/power-pop history and the downtown New York scene that all three members would later inhabit. Discogs lists the group’s profile as “New York City,” though the Twin/Tone affiliation and Almaas’s roots place the band in the Minneapolis orbit. According to the Wikipedia article on the Suicide Commandos, when that group broke up in 1980, Steve Almaas “went to New York and formed a band called The Crackers” — placing the band’s formation in New York but with Minneapolis-scene DNA.

Sir Crackers! was issued in the same run as early Replacements and Suburbs releases on Twin/Tone (catalog numbers TTR 8120–TTR 8127), making it part of the first wave of the Minneapolis indie scene. Genres: Rock / Indie Rock / Power Pop (Discogs).

Member Biographies (Summary)

Steve Almaas

Steve Almaas (Discogs ID: 633930) was the primary songwriter and bassist. He had come from the Suicide Commandos, the Minneapolis punk trio formed in 1975 that Wikipedia credits with “jump-starting a punk rock music scene in the Twin Cities” and which released Make A Record (1978, Blank Records) and the live album The Commandos Commit Suicide Dance Concert (1979, Twin/Tone). After the Commandos broke up c. 1980, Almaas formed The Crackers in New York, then went solo with a Beat Rodeo EP (1982, Coyote Records). He then formed a four-man group called Beat Rodeo, releasing Staying Out Late W/ The Beat Rodeo (1984, Zensor) and two albums on I.R.S. Records in 1986. Later credits include bass on Terra Incognita (1996, Work), production on Songs For Slim (2013, New West Records), and the solo album Everywhere You’ve Been (2021, Lonesome Whippoorwill) on which Haglof served as executive producer.

Karen Haglof (Karen Indiana)

Karen Haglof (Discogs ID: 452626) used the stage name “Karen Indiana” on Sir Crackers!. The Discogs group page for The Crackers (3) explicitly lists her real name as the member. Her profile notes she moved from Minneapolis to New York City. She went on to appear in Rhys Chatham’s guitar ensemble on Factor X (1983, Moers Music, Germany), then joined Band Of Susans for the Hard Light and Love Agenda sessions (both 1989, Blast First), departing alongside Page Hamilton before Band Of Susans’ “classic lineup.” She returned to Chatham’s ensemble on Die Donnergötter (2006, Radium (3)), and executive-produced Almaas’s 2021 solo album — a reunion of the original Crackers partnership across four decades.

Jay Peck

Jay Peck (Discogs ID: 1934638) is also listed as a Minneapolis-based musician. Beyond The Crackers, his Discogs footprint includes membership in Figures (3) (Discogs ID: 1934679), a Minneapolis group whose other members were Jeff Waryan, Steve Brantseg, and Steve Fjelstad. Figures (3) released one record on Twin/Tone: In A Chalk Circle (1985, TTR 8547). Peck also has a 2017 engineer credit on the Suicide Commandos’ Time Bomb (Twin/Tone). No other primary releases under his name.
