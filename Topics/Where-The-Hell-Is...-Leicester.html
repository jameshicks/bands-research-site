Where The Hell Is… Leicester

Discogs ID: 1635367 Format: LP, Album, Compilation Label: The S+T Label — Cat: STAB 1 Year: 1981 Country: UK Genres: Electronic, Rock Styles: Minimal, New Wave, Post-Punk, Punk

Overview

A 12-track compilation LP documenting the Leicester post-punk and new wave underground circa 1980–81. Released on the locally-run The S+T Label, the record came with an A4 double-sided printed insert and a 16-page b/w booklet containing information on all 12 contributing artists. Some copies were packaged with a badge and a free 7” single — Observers’ This Age / Suicide (ST 2, 1980), recorded 28 June 1980 — and bore a sticker reading “This package includes FREE SINGLE (ST 2) 16-page booklet and button badge”.

John Peel played several tracks from the LP on his radio show. The Deep Freeze Mice’s appearance here is notable as the band later appeared on the Nurse With Wound list.

The album is the third of four releases on The S+T Label (STAB 1 was the LP; ST-3, a Future Toys 7”, followed later in 1981).

The S+T Label

Discogs ID: 64882 Founded c.1979 by four Leicester-area individuals who ran it as a sideline alongside full-time jobs: Chris Freer, Paul Betts (artwork), Kev Reverb (Kevin Bayliss, from Countesthorpe — musician and recording engineer), and Mick Dawkeye (Mike Dawkins, from Countesthorpe). Freer and Betts had previously co-run the punk fanzine Terminally Blitzed (September 1977–April 1978), then transitioned to mod culture by 1979 and launched a new publication called “S+T”. The label ran affiliated publications including 0533 (1980–1981) and Biggles up The Fosse (1981–1982). Production costs were covered by the founders, with bands financing their own recording sessions; profits were split 50/50.

Note on Discogs: The label profile credits only three founders (IDs a596027, a518669, a8107754); Paul Betts is absent from Discogs but was the artwork co-founder per the label’s own account.

Contributing Artists — Notes

The Danceable Solution (Discogs ID 1353553)

“Mysterious act whose track just turned up and sounded good.” Per the label, suspected to be northern students who submitted their work opportunistically. No further releases known.

Tribal Games (Discogs ID 1353554)

Described as a “trash kind of band.” Early contributors to the project. No further releases known.

The Deep Freeze Mice (Discogs ID 294782)

The most extensively documented artist on the compilation. Leicester new wave band active 1979–1989; living on Belgrave Road (Leicester’s curry district), though not originally from the city. Listed on the Nurse With Wound list. Both Mole Embalming Records and Cordelia Records were Alan Jenkins’ self-owned labels. Core members: Alan Jenkins (guitar, vocals), Sherree Lawrence (organ, keyboards), Mick Bunnage (bass); drummer Graham Summers (also of The Statics) departed after The Gates Of Lunch (1982) and was replaced by Peter Gregory. After the band dissolved Jenkins went on to The Chrysanthemums, Ruth’s Refrigerator, and The Thurston Lava Tube (psychedelic surf instrumental, active since 2001). Recorded under the name “Deep Freeze Mice” on the comp sleeve.

Ronnie Slicker & The Banditz (Discogs ID 1353555)

“Reverb’s old band” — i.e., Kev Reverb’s previous group. Marketed as Leicester’s “Ugliest Band.” Contributed “Disco Music” (A5). No further releases known beyond this comp appearance.

Mental Notes (Discogs ID 1353550)

“White Reggae” outfit; approached the label with a completed recording. No further releases known.

The Silent (Discogs ID 1353551)

Possibly run by the operator of the Street Music studio (the Leicester record shop / studio space where New Age recorded). Described as “someone’s personal artistic expression rather than a promotional priority.” No further releases known.

Modern Living (Discogs ID 1727078)

“Basically a couple of hippies living in Leicester.” John Peel notably favored this track (“Drink Is A Drug”, B2). No further releases known.

Last Resort (Discogs ID 1727077)

Quality musicians from Melton Mowbray (town east of Leicester). No further releases known.

The Amber Squad (Discogs ID 1353552)

Mod Power Pop band from Oakham, Rutland (county adjacent to Leicester). Per the label, “four pub musicians performing self-penned pop material” — the mod association was a misconception. Members: Dubber Rawlings (bass), Tuzzy Tyers (drums), Robert Miller (14) (guitar), Paul Fairey (guitar, vocals), Richard Beechey (lead vocals). Their ST 1 single “(I Can’t) Put My Finger On You” was engineered by Derek Tompkins; photography by Mick Dawkeye.

New Age (Discogs ID 742848)

New wave band from Groby, a village on the western edge of Leicester. “Can’t Get In” was their earliest known recording, made at Street Music. They accepted offers from competing labels mid-project (before the comp was released) and went on to release on Dining Out Records; their 1982 LP was produced by Dave Anderson (3) at Foel Studio, Wales. See full article: New Age.

Observers (Discogs ID 830690)

“A talented five-piece whose music defies description.” Disbanded shortly after their ST 2 single (1980). Contributed “Crisis” to the comp; their single was the free 7” with some copies.

Future Toys (Discogs ID 536995)

New Romantic act with a “Spandau Duran-ish” aesthetic, from the Leicester area. Contributed “R C U” (B6) to the comp. Later released their own ST-3 single Perfect Strangers (1981) on The S+T Label — this release is absent from Discogs.
