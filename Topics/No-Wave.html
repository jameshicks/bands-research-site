No Wave

Art/music movement. New York City. c.1976–1983.

Deliberate negation of punk’s “New Wave” commercialisation; also punning on “no wave” (as in “no future”). Short-lived, intentionally anti-commercial, and anti-rock.

Key Venues / Spaces

  • CBGB — shared with punk, but No Wave had its own spaces
  • Artists Space (SoHo) — key early venue; the “Bands at Artists Space” five-night festival (May 2–6, 1978) that produced the Eno-produced compilation. Organized by Michael Zwack and Robert Longo. Ten bands performed across five nights; Brian Eno, in New York to produce the Talking Heads album More Songs About Buildings and Food, attended on the advice of Diego Cortez and proposed what became No New York. Anya Phillips is also credited in some accounts with introducing Eno to the No Wave scene through her management of the Contortions and her involvement in the Mudd Club’s founding. Nightly breakdown (partial — first three nights not documented in available sources): nights 1–3 featured The Gynecologists (Rhys Chatham), Communists, Theoretical Girls (Glenn Branca), Terminal, Tone Death (Rhys Chatham, performing Guitar Trio), and Daily Life (Glenn Branca; members: Barbara Ess, Paul McMahon, Christine Hahn); Friday night: DNA and The Contortions; Saturday night (final): Mars and Teenage Jesus and the Jerks. Sources: Artists Space Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-26); No New York Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-26); No Wave Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-26)
  • Mudd Club (77 White St) — key social hub; 1978–1983
  • Danceteria, Pyramid Club — later

Key Bands

  • The Contortions — James Chance / James White; avant-funk + free jazz
  • DNA (4) — Arto Lindsay, Ikue Mori, Robin Lee Crutchfield
  • Teenage Jesus And The Jerks — Lydia Lunch
  • Mars — China Burg, Sumner Crane, Mark Cunningham, Nancy Arlen; the most dissonant of the four
  • Theoretical Girls — Glenn Branca; performed at Artists Space but excluded from No New York
  • Daily Life — another Branca project; also excluded
  • Terminal (No Wave) — all-female; excluded from No New York
  • Tone Death — Rhys Chatham project; excluded
  • Red Transistor — Rudolph Grey, Von LMO, Jim Sclavunos; excluded; only release came out in 1990
  • Communists — performed at Artists Space festival (May 1978); no recordings or members documented in available sources
  • James White & The Blacks — James Chance’s funk/disco project
  • 8 Eyed Spy — Lydia Lunch post-Teenage Jesus
  • Lydia Lunch — solo and various groups
  • Sonic Youth — second-generation, emerged from the scene
  • Glenn Branca — guitar ensembles; theorist of the scene
  • Swans — emerged from the same milieu

Key Organizers / Curators

  • Diego Cortez — advised Eno to attend Artists Space; co-founded Mudd Club with Anya Phillips; curated “New York, No Wave” at PS1 (1981). Wikipedia’s No New York and Artists Space articles both credit Cortez as the one who advised Eno. Cortez also served as “NYC production advisor” on No New York per his Wikipedia page. Diego Cortez Wikipedia (as of 2026-06-26)
  • Anya Phillips — co-organized events; James Chance’s manager; co-founded Mudd Club. Some sources (Reynolds Retro blog; Far Out Magazine) credit Phillips with introducing Eno to the No Wave scene via the Mudd Club / Steve Maas connection, rather than directing him to Artists Space specifically. Conflict: the two accounts are compatible (Anya introduced Eno to the scene broadly; Cortez specifically advised him to attend Artists Space), but no source spells out this division clearly. See also her entry at Anya Phillips.
  • Michael Zwack — co-organized “Bands at Artists Space” festival
  • Robert Longo — co-organized “Bands at Artists Space” festival

Key Documents

  • No New York (Antilles AN-7067, 1978) — Brian Eno-produced compilation; four bands: The Contortions, DNA, Teenage Jesus And The Jerks, Mars. The canonical document. Emerged from “Bands at Artists Space” (May 1978), organized in part by Anya Phillips.
  • Mars EP (Infidelity, 1980) — recorded December 1978, same sessions as No New York recordings
  • DNA A Taste Of DNA (Italian Records, 1981)
  • The Contortions Buy (ZE/Island, 1979) — debut LP

Key Labels

  • ZE Records — adjacent; more commercial/art-school crossover
  • 99 Records — second wave; dance-punk, funk-punk
  • Neutral Records — noise rock extension
  • Lydia Lunch’s Widowspeak Productions

Key Facts

  • The movement was intentionally self-limiting; most bands lasted 1–3 years.
  • Brian Eno’s production of No New York gave it visibility beyond downtown Manhattan.
  • The No Wave aesthetic fed directly into noise rock, post-punk, and eventually alternative rock.
  • Glenn Branca’s guitar ensembles are the most theoretically elaborated extension of the scene.

Key Personnel (cross-band)

  • James Chance — Contortions, James White & The Blacks; born April 20, 1953 Milwaukee; died June 18, 2024 New York
  • Lydia Lunch — Teenage Jesus And The Jerks, 8 Eyed Spy; key downtown figure
  • Arto Lindsay — DNA; Brazilian-American; also Lounge Lizards, Ambitious Lovers
  • Ikue Mori — DNA; Tokyo-born; no prior drum experience when she joined
  • Pat Place — Contortions → Bush Tetras
  • Adele Bertei — Contortions organ
  • George Scott (2) — Contortions / James White / Raybeats; died 1980, heroin OD
  • Gordon Stevenson — Teenage Jesus; died 1982, AIDS
  • Bradly Field — Teenage Jesus; died mid-1990s
  • Sumner Crane — Mars; died 2003, cancer
  • Anya Phillips — b. February 1955, Taipei County, Taiwan; d. June 19, 1981, brain cancer. Fashion designer, co-founder of the Mudd Club, manager of James Chance and briefly Lydia Lunch. Grew up on US military bases; her brother is singer Fei Xiang; attended Taipei American School where she befriended Sylvia Reed (later Lou Reed’s wife). Arrived NYC 1974; enrolled at Parsons on full scholarship but left almost immediately. Designed clothes by tying and knotting latex and fabric strips (she did not sew); Debbie Harry wore her pieces on Blondie’s Plastic Letters (1978) and the “Rush Rush” single (1983); she also shot and designed the cover of James Chance’s Buy LP. Co-founded the Mudd Club at 77 White St with Steve Maas and Diego Cortez (1978), envisioned it as “the Molotov Cocktail Lounge,” then departed after a dispute with Maas. Worked as a photojournalist for New York Rocker and Punk magazine (bylined “Anya Chowmein”). Made a rare vocal appearance on the Contortions’ 1980 Rotterdam concert (released 1990). Diagnosed with cancer 1979; a May 1981 benefit at Bond’s featured Debbie Harry, Nile Rodgers, and Bernard Edwards. Died June 19, 1981, age 26. James Chance dedicated Sax Maniac (1982) to her. Wikipedia, as of 2026-06-25; Far Out Magazine

Deaths in the Scene

The no wave circle suffered unusual mortality: George Scott III (1980, OD), Gordon Stevenson (1982, AIDS), Anya Phillips (1981, brain cancer, age 26), Bradly Field (mid-1990s), Sumner Crane (2003, cancer), James Chance (2024).
