NO Magazine

Underground fanzine. New York City. c. 1977–1979. Four issues (Vol. 1, Nos. 1–4).
Full title: NO: Instant Artifact of the New Order
Associated with: Information, No Group, Jim Sclavunos, Chris Nelson (5), Philip Dray, Seth Goldstein.

Overview

NO Magazine was a short-run no wave fanzine published at NYU School of Arts in New York City. It ran for four issues (Vol. 1, Nos. 1–4) across roughly 1977–1979, with print runs of approximately 100 copies per issue. Despite its tiny circulation, the publication is credited as one of the defining documents of the early No Wave scene and, by some accounts, the origin point of the phrase “no wave” itself.

The magazine’s subtitle — Instant Artifact of the New Order — gave it both its initials (N.O.) and its self-conscious framing: ephemera as cultural monument. According to one account, the publication initially circulated under the name “New Order” before becoming known by its initials. [source: brownoutspassed.bandcamp.com — Tim Holmes essay on Tape #1]

Essay by Tim Holmes (writing on Tape #1 liner notes) described it as “at least as influential a herald to a movement as house organ ‘Punk’ had been to a prior youth coup.” [source: brownoutspassed.bandcamp.com]

”No Wave” — Coinage Claim

The phrase “no wave” as a label for the NYC avant-punk scene has multiple competing origin claims. The claim most directly connected to NO Magazine centres on Chris Nelson (5):

“Nelson may even be the one responsible for the term ‘no wave,’ as one of his graphic designs featured a surfer with a printed plea to ‘make no new waves.‘” — Bandcamp Daily — Tape #1 is No Wave History

The Bandcamp Daily piece phrases the surfer text as “make no new waves.” A second account (sourced through search result summaries from the same article) renders the caption as “make (new) (no) waves” — a parenthetical formulation suggesting the graphic layered the words typographically rather than using a single linear phrase.

The AbeBooks listing for Vol. 1 No. 2 states that issue is “credited with giving the no wave movement its name,” which would place the coinage in 1977. No primary scan of the graphic has been located in web-accessible archives.

Other coinage claims:

  • Lydia Lunch in an interview with Roy Trakin in New York Rocker (Wikipedia; no date given)
  • Chris Nelson, also in New York Rocker (Wikipedia — same source, different attribution)
  • Thurston Moore spotted the phrase spray-painted on CBGB’s theater at 66 Second Avenue before seeing it in print (Wikipedia — No wave, as of 2026-06-26)

No source establishes a definitive single origin. The NO Magazine graphic is the most material candidate for print origination.

Information served as NO Magazine’s house band. The band’s founding lineup of Dray, Nelson, and Sclavunos were all editorial staff of the magazine.

Content

Beyond the flexidisc, available descriptions of individual issues are sparse:

  • Issue #2 — 28 pages, saddle-stitched. Credited by booksellers as the issue that named “no wave.” Likely contains the surfer graphic.
  • Issue #4 — distributed as a 9×12 manila envelope; the flexi-disc is accompanied by smaller fanzines and ephemera; content varied copy to copy.
  • General scope — the magazine covered NYC punk rock and the no wave scene broadly. Contributors and coverage beyond the band-affiliated staff are not documented in available web sources.

Chris Nelson and Robert Sietsema of Mofungo subsequently joined the staff of New York Rocker, extending their editorial work beyond NO Magazine’s run. [source: Bandcamp Daily]

“New Order” Subtitle — Connection to the Band New Order

The subtitle Instant Artifact of the New Order predates the Manchester band New Order (band) (formed 1980 after the death of Ian Curtis of Joy Division). The Manchester band’s name derived from a Guardian article about Kampuchean rebels, not from the New York fanzine. No documented connection or influence between the two “New Order” references has been found. [source: Radio X — Why Joy Division changed their name]

Legacy

NO Magazine is recognised as a foundational document of the No Wave scene, credited with: (a) possibly coining the term “no wave” in print; (b) acting as a hub that connected the founders of Information, No Group, and NNB; and (c) demonstrating the DIY zine-as-artifact form that became characteristic of the scene. Its influence spread well beyond its ~100-copy print run.

Archives / Digitization

No digitized run of NO Magazine (NYU/No Wave) has been located in publicly accessible web archives as of 2026-06-26. The complete four-issue run was sold through Boo-Hooray Gallery (sold listing: boo-hooray.com). Individual issues appear very occasionally in the rare books market.

Note: The LA punk zine also titled “No Mag” or “NO” (founded 1978, 14 issues through 1985, associated with Michael Gira / Bruce Kalberg) is a distinct publication. It is digitized at archive.org. Do not conflate the two.
