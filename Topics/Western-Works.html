Western Works

Western Works was the private recording studio owned and operated by Cabaret Voltaire in Sheffield, England, functioning as their primary creative base from 1977/1978 until they abandoned the building in 1987. The Portobello Street building was subsequently demolished in 1993. The studio was also made available to affiliated and local artists, making it one of the key recording sites in the Sheffield industrial/post-punk scene.

  • Location: Top floor, Portobello Street at Regent Street, Sheffield (former cutlery/scissors manufacturers’ building, originally built c.1859 for Christopher Johnson and Co.); no street number confirmed
  • Active (Portobello St): 1977/78–1987 (studio abandoned 1987 due to break-ins and financial difficulties; building demolished 1993 for Sheffield University — now Regent Court, Dept. of Computer Science)
  • Relocated studio: 1987 onwards — near Sheffield’s football ground and railway station; large control room with natural light
  • Operators: Cabaret Voltaire — Richard H. Kirk, Stephen Mallinder, Chris Watson
  • Discogs label ID: 267832

Overview

Western Works began as Cabaret Voltaire’s home base when they needed space to work outside commercial studio constraints. Wikipedia and several sources date the studio’s establishment to 1977, with Watson financing the initial rental; Discogs release credits confirm recording activity there from 1978. The building on Portobello Street (at the corner of Regent Street) had previously housed the cutlery manufacturers Christopher Johnson and Co. (c.1859) and later J. Donnelly and Co.; it had become derelict by the time CV took over the top floor. The studio was integral to their philosophy: they treated it as an instrument and laboratory as much as a conventional recording space, using it to develop their dense, layered tape-loop and electronics work. Richard H. Kirk described it as “like our own version of Warhol’s Factory.” [source: DJ Mag interview, https://djmag.com/node/4152]

From the outset, the trio produced and recorded themselves — Discogs liner notes confirm “Produced and recorded at ‘Western Works’ 1978” for their debut Extended Play EP, and every major CV release through Drinking Gasoline (recorded Dec ‘84/Jan ‘85) carries a “Western Works” recording credit. The studio started on a four-track Revox machine and was later upgraded to 16-track on 2” tape ahead of the The Crackdown sessions. The studio’s role shifted as Cabaret Voltaire moved toward more electronic and rhythm-machine-based production: early releases feature live drum contributions from Haydn Boyes-Weston and others, while later records like The Crackdown (1983) and Micro-Phonies (1984) brought in external engineer Flood, with final mixing often moving to a commercial facility (Trident Studios for The Crackdown; Sarm West for Micro-Phonies).

Chris Watson left Cabaret Voltaire in 1981; after his departure, Western Works continued under Kirk and Mallinder. Watson did use it for at least one project after leaving — the The Men With The Deadly Dreams cassette (1981) credits him as having “master tape processed and produced by Chris Watson at the Western Works, Sheffield.”

Beyond Cabaret Voltaire’s own output, Western Works was available to Sheffield-adjacent and affiliated acts, particularly through CV’s Doublevision label. Richard H. Kirk’s extensive solo/side-project discography — Disposable Half-Truths, Time High Fiction, Leather Hands (with Peter Hope), Black Jesus Voice, Hipnotic, Ugly Spirit, Hoodoo Talk — was entirely or substantially recorded there through the mid-1980s.

By 1987, the Portobello Street building had deteriorated badly and suffered a series of break-ins; financial difficulties also played a role. Kirk and Mallinder abandoned the original Western Works and moved to a new studio near Sheffield’s football ground and railway station, described as having a large control room with windows and natural light plus a separate live room. [source: Sound on Sound interview with Kirk, https://www.soundonsound.com/people/richard-kirk-cabaret-voltaire-electronica] The Portobello Street building stood empty until it was demolished in 1993 and replaced by Regent Court, Sheffield University’s Department of Computer Science building. [source: Sheffield History forum / Sheffield Music Map, https://www.sheffieldhistory.co.uk/forums/topic/3896-western-works-cabaret-voltaire/]

Note on “Mary Street”: The vault’s previous wording said the studio relocated to “Mary Street.” No source consulted during web research specifically names “Mary Street” — the Sound on Sound 1994 interview describes the post-1987 studio as “near the football ground and the railway station” without giving a street name. This detail requires verification.

Equipment

Portobello Street era (1977/78–1987):

  • Started with a four-track Revox tape machine (provided by Rough Trade Records as part of their 1978 deal, in lieu of an advance)
  • Upgraded to 16-track on 2” tape ahead of the The Crackdown sessions (c.1982–83)

Doublevision Connection

Doublevision was Cabaret Voltaire’s own label, founded in 1982 initially for video releases and expanded to records from 1983. Western Works and Doublevision were tightly linked: Doublevision releases were largely recorded there, and Kirk used both Doublevision and Western Works as parallel instruments for releasing his own solo work and producing/releasing affiliated Sheffield acts. The Box’s Muscle In (1984) is a clear case: the promo sheet reads “Produced by Richard H. Kirk (of Cabaret Voltaire) and recorded at Sheffield’s Western Works Studio.” Doublevision was not physically in Western Works, but functioned as its release vehicle and the administrative arm of the CV creative operation during this period.

Key Engineers / Producers

Cabaret Voltaire self-produced and self-engineered throughout their Western Works period — Discogs credits consistently show “Recorded By, Producer: Cabaret Voltaire” with no separate engineer for most Rough Trade-era releases. The main exceptions:

  • Flood — brought in as engineer/co-producer on The Crackdown (1983) and Micro-Phonies (1984); Paul Gomersall assisted on Micro-Phonies. This marks the transition to a more polished, commercial sound.
  • Stephen Mallinder — produced and engineered his own solo release Temperature Drop (1981) at Western Works, and produced/engineered external sessions for 23 Skidoo (“Last Words,” “Gospel Comes To New Guinea,” both July 1981) and They Must Be Russians (1980).
  • Richard H. Kirk — produced most of his own solo sessions at Western Works, as well as The Box’s Muscle In and They Must Be Russians. Credited as “Mixed By” on Clock DVA’s White Souls In Black Suits (1980), which was engineered by Jon Mills.
  • Chris Watson — produced Hula’s Black Pop Workout (1981/82) and processed/produced the Men With The Deadly Dreams cassette (1981) after his departure from CV.
  • Phil Bush — engineer and co-producer on side C/D of 2x45 (recorded Pluto Studios, Manchester — not Western Works, but contemporaneous).
