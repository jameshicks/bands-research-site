Danceteria

Nightclub. New York City. Discogs venue ID: 286331 (no releases — venue-only entry).

Operated 1979–1993 across three Manhattan locations. Central venue of the downtown NYC new wave, post-punk, hip-hop, and no wave scene through its peak years (1980–1986). Unlike most downtown clubs of its era, Danceteria was a multi-floor operation mixing live performance, DJ culture, and art — it was as important to the emergence of hip-hop and club music as it was to post-punk and industrial. It did not operate its own record label; the French label Danceteria (France) (Discogs ID 18434) is a completely separate entity.

History

Location 1: 252 W 37th Street (1979–1980)

The original Danceteria opened in 1979 in a former garment-district building at 252 West 37th Street. Founders Rudolf Pieper and Jim Fouratt ran it as a late-night dance/performance club. This first location was shut down by the city within about a year.

Location 2: 30 W 21st Street (1982–1986)

The definitive era. Reopened at 30 West 21st Street in 1982, the club expanded to four floors: live performance, a roof deck bar, video lounge (a very early dedicated VJ space), and a dance floor. This location became the crossroads of multiple parallel scenes — UK post-punk and goth acts on US tours booked Danceteria as a marquee stop; hip-hop crews performed alongside industrial and EBM acts; ZE Records and 99 Records roster acts regularly appeared. DJs who worked here included Mark Kamins (who famously played Madonna’s early demos, leading to her Warner deal) and John “Jellybean” Benitez.

Chandra Oppenheim and The Dance performed here c. 1980–81 during the first location’s run, while Chandra was a pre-teenager. This tenure is documented in The Dance’s discography and Chandra’s own page.

Location 3: 30 E 30th Street (1990–1993)

A third iteration opened in 1990 under the Danceteria name at a new address. Smaller in cultural footprint than the 21st Street peak; closed 1993.

Discogs — What the Database Shows

Discogs documents Danceteria (ID 286331) solely as a venue/company credit — it appears on releases in the release_company table (e.g., “Recorded At” or venue-stamped live releases). There are no label releases under this ID.

Note: “Danceteria” (ID 18434) on Discogs is a French indie label based in Lille/Paris that licensed and distributed international indie records in France from the mid-1980s to mid-1990s. It shares only the name; it is not connected to the NYC club.

Key Acts and Scene Connections

Post-Punk / Goth / Industrial (UK acts on US tours)

Danceteria was the premier NYC stop for UK post-punk and goth acts: Sisters of Mercy, Sex Gang Children, Psychic TV, Front 242 (Belgian EBM), Shriekback, and Malaria! (Berlin) all recorded live there.

No Wave / Downtown NYC

Acts from the ZE Records and 99 Records scenes regularly played the 21st Street location. 8 Eyed Spy (Lydia Lunch), James White & The Blacks / James Chance, ESG, Bush Tetras, Liquid Liquid — all documented in the Discogs ZE/99 orbits and in contemporary accounts.

Hip-Hop

Danceteria’s multi-floor format made it one of the first downtown Manhattan venues to programme hip-hop alongside rock. The Smokin’ Raps compilation (1986) documents Cold Crush Brothers, Spoonie Gee, and others performing at or associated with the club. DJ Mark Kamins was a resident who bridged hip-hop and new wave programming.
