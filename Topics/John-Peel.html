John Peel

John Peel

Aug 20, 20264 min read

John Peel

Role: BBC Radio 1 DJ, tastemaker, broadcaster
Show: Peel Sessions / late-night Radio 1
Sessions recorded: 1967–2004 at Maida Vale Studios, London
Sessions produced: c.4,000 sessions by c.2,000+ artists over 37 years

Connection to Dining Out Records

John Peel was an important early supporter of several Dining Out Records bands, giving them national radio exposure at a time when the DIY post-punk underground had few other routes to broadcast. Two bands — Normil Hawaiians and The Adicts — recorded confirmed official Peel Sessions. Several others received Peel radio plays without formal sessions.

Confirmed Peel Sessions — Dining Out Network

Normil Hawaiians

Session recorded: 20 May 1980, Maida Vale Studios
Broadcast dates: 19 June 1980; 7 July 1980; 2 October 1980 (rebroadcast three times over six months)

Tracks:

  1. The Beat Goes On
  2. Memories
  3. Levels of Water
  4. I Wanna

Personnel: Guy Smith, Jim Lusted, Nick (guitars/vocals), Brian Kealy (drums), Sue Leeves (backing vocals). Session was the direct result of a self-produced demo. The single “The Beat Goes On” followed, reaching #44 on the UK Independent Chart in November 1980. All four session tracks were later released on the Dark World (79–81) compilation (Upset The Rhythm, 2022), with Peel Session versions explicitly credited.

Sources: peel.fandom.com/wiki/Normil_Hawaiians (as of 2026-06-25); normilhawaiians.bandcamp.com; dandelionradio.com

The Adicts

Session recorded: 20 November 1979, Maida Vale Studios
Produced by: Mike Sparrow for John Peel / BBC Radio 1
Broadcast: November/December 1979 (broadcast referenced on the 17 December 1979 show per Peel Wiki)

Tracks:

  1. Get Addicted
  2. Distortion
  3. Numbers
  4. Sensitive

The four tracks were later released on the This Is Your Life compilation (Razor Records, 1985) via arrangement with BBC Records. They were also issued as a limited 4-track 7” EP (“The Peel Sessions 20/11/1979”) on the Russian Pirate Love label. This session pre-dates their signing to Dining Out Records (whose debut release, the Lunch With The Adicts EP (TUX 1), was also in 1979) — the Peel Session and the Dining Out EP appear to be roughly contemporaneous.

Sources: peel.fandom.com/wiki/Adicts (as of 2026-06-25); detourrecords.co.uk; punkygibbon.co.uk

Peel Plays (Radio Plays, Not Sessions)

The following bands received record plays on Peel’s programme but have no confirmed official studio sessions recorded at Maida Vale:

Disco Zombies

Peel played “Drums Over London” (South Circular, 1979) — already noted in the Disco Zombies vault entry as a “Peel favourite”. The band also appeared at a John Peel Roadshow gig on 16 June 1979 at The Paddock, Harpole, Northants (on a bill that included Bauhaus). No evidence of a formal Maida Vale session found.

Source: peel.fandom.com/wiki/Gigography_1979 (as of 2026-06-25)

Behaviour Red

Their Dining Out Records single was favourably played by Peel (noted in band’s vault entry; source: earstofeed.com). No evidence of a formal Peel Session found.

Zounds

Peel played “Demystification” (Rough Trade RT 069) on his 18 June 1981 programme. This was a record play, not a studio session. The Peel Wiki for Zounds confirms Peel played tracks from the group in his early-1980s programmes but documents no session recording. Note: vault file is Zounds (2) — confirm disambiguation.

Source: peel.fandom.com/wiki/18_June_1981 (as of 2026-06-25)

The Mob

Peel played “Witch Hunt” and, per the Kill Your Pet Puppy archive, “Crying Again” (22 April 1980 show). The Mob are not confirmed to have recorded a formal Peel Session. Note: vault file is The Mob (3).

Source: killyourpetpuppy.co.uk (as of 2026-06-25)

Confirmed Non-Sessions

Flux of Pink Indians

Flux of Pink Indians did not record a Peel Session. Bassist Derek Birkett stated in an interview with No Class fanzine: “We’ve never been asked to do one. There’s no point is there really? … I wouldn’t like to do a Peel session cos they’re too dictatorial, you have to use their producers, you have to use their engineers, you have to have it done in two days.” When the band later recorded as Flux (1986’s Uncarved Block), Peel did play tracks from that release. Flux of Pink Indians are in the Dining Out orbit but their Crass Records releases came after the Dining Out period.

Source: noclass.co.uk/fluxinterview.html via peel.fandom.com/wiki/Flux_Of_Pink_Indians (as of 2026-06-25)
