Isaac Brock — Florida Connections

Research note. Sources: web (Tape Op, Miami New Times, Jitney Books, The Ringer, Broward Palm Beach New Times, Sub Pop, Spin, Indy Week). Compiled 2026-04-26.

Summary

Isaac Brock has two distinct Florida threads: (1) a personal one—he lived in Gainesville circa 1999–2000, which is almost certainly where he met John Orth; and (2) a creative/mythological one, where Florida imagery (Andrew Cunanan, Miami vice aesthetics) surfaces in songs unconnected to firsthand experience.

1. How Brock Met John Orth / Holopaw

The short answer: Brock likely met John Orth in Gainesville while living there, and Brian Deck connected them professionally as the producer of both Ugly Casanova and Holopaw’s debut. No source spells this out in a single sentence, but the pieces fit together clearly.

Brock’s time in Gainesville

After finishing The Moon & Antarctica in Chicago (recorded July–November 1999 at Brian Deck’s Clava Studios), Brock moved to Gainesville with his girlfriend, who was from there. He has said in multiple interviews: “my girlfriend was from Gainesville and I was like maybe I should take her to a place where she knows people and that’ll be good for her.” He stayed roughly one to two years. The move was also precipitated by his need to leave Seattle — a sexual assault allegation had circulated there (later withdrawn), and he described fleeing for “the relative solitude of Gainesville, Florida.”

  • Source (Brock quotes): Jitney Books full interview; Miami New Times “Perfect Disguise” (2004)

Holopaw as a named band was not yet formed when Brock arrived — the band coalesced around 2001–2002 — but John Orth was already active in the Gainesville scene. Orth had been a fine arts student at Florida State (graduated 1992) and was working as a visual artist and musician in the area. The Miami New Times “Tourist Trappings” piece (2003) identifies Orth as the band’s central figure from before Holopaw’s formal lineup.

The Ugly Casanova sessions

Ugly Casanova’s Sharpen Your Teeth was recorded at Brock’s house in Cottage Grove, Oregon in 2001. Brock had already moved back out of Florida by this point. The Tape Op interview with Brian Deck (issue 36) is the most specific account of how the project was assembled:

“I was working with him on the Modest Mouse record. We really enjoy working together — it’s a really good partnership. If I remember right, he was coming through town on a Modest Mouse tour and we saw each other that night and he was just like, ‘I’m doing this record with Sub Pop and I’d like to do it with you.‘”

Deck flew out to Brock’s house repeatedly (“I’d fly out there and work at his house for a week and a half and then come home and he’d spruce up the tracks and I’d go back out there”). The Tape Op interview also confirms that Deck then produced the Holopaw debut: he mentions “another one for a band from Florida called Hollow Paw [Holopaw] that’s coming out on Sub Pop,” noting that their singer “is the other singer in Ugly Casanova.” This is the closest any primary source comes to naming Deck as the bridge between both records.

The working group that assembled for Sharpen Your Teeth was: Isaac Brock, John Orth (Holopaw), Tim Rutili (Califone/Red Red Meat), Pall Jenkins (The Black Heart Procession), Tyler Reilly (In the Drink), and Brian Deck. According to the Indy Week “Edgar Graham?” piece, they drove through the night to Brock’s home, cooked food, and worked through song ideas together. Orth co-wrote four tracks (1, 4, 7, 11); sub-sources single out “Barnacles” and “Hotcha Girls” as featuring Orth co-writes prominently.

The Sub Pop introduction

Sub Pop’s own description of the Holopaw signing makes the causal chain explicit: Holopaw “came to our attention by way of Isaac Brock (he and Holopaw’s John Orth co-wrote some of our favorite songs on the Ugly Casanova record).” Brock played Sub Pop Holopaw’s demo and they were signed. Holopaw (self-titled debut) was released January 21, 2003 — about eight months after Sharpen Your Teeth (May 2002). Brian Deck produced the debut.

Most plausible reconstruction

The most likely sequence: Brock met Orth in Gainesville around 1999–2000 while living there. When assembling the Ugly Casanova project in 2001, he invited Orth to Oregon. Brian Deck, who was producing both sessions, is the professional link that formalized the Sub Pop relationship for Holopaw. No interview has Brock or Orth explicitly saying “we met in Gainesville,” but there is no other plausible window — by the time Ugly Casanova recording began, Brock was back in the Pacific Northwest, and the Gainesville residency is the only documented period when Brock was embedded in Orth’s scene.

Gap: No interview with Brock or Orth directly confirming they met in Gainesville, or describing the first meeting. The Tape Op Deck interview is the most useful primary source and doesn’t specify this either.

2. The Song “Florida” (We Were Dead Before The Ship Even Sank, 2007)

Track 4, runtime 2:56. James Mercer (The Shins) sings backing vocals on this track and two others: “Missed the Boat” and “We’ve Got Everything.” The album was produced by Dennis Herring.

Is it about actual Florida experience?

Yes, partially. Brock has confirmed in a 2021 Jitney Books interview that his Gainesville residency “feeds into that song ‘Florida’.” However, no 2007-era interview has surfaced with specific lyrics breakdown. The song’s themes — unfulfilled dreams, failed escape attempts, Florida as a metaphor for broken plans — are likely layered rather than purely autobiographical.

The Andrew Cunanan connection (mentioned in some search summaries) belongs to a different Modest Mouse track: “Pistol (A. Cunanan, Miami, FL. 1996)” from Strangers to Ourselves (2015), not “Florida.” Brock explained in Spotify commentary that the 1996 date is intentionally wrong — Cunanan wasn’t in Miami then — but he needed Miami as the setting for the narrative atmosphere. He described it as inspired by Bad Lieutenant and Miami Vice aesthetics, not personal experience.

Johnny Marr has noted in 2022 retrospective interviews (Spin) that “Florida” was one of the tracks where he and Brock began to play like each other — it’s described as among the more collaborative compositions on the album.

James Mercer’s involvement

Mercer’s backing vocals were part of a broader collaboration that appears to have been arranged during the We Were Dead sessions. No specific account of why Mercer was brought in for “Florida” specifically has been found. His presence on three tracks suggests an ongoing working relationship rather than a one-off cameo.

3. Other Florida Connections

  • Miami visit, circa 2002: Brock visited Miami (approximately two years before the 2004 Miami New Times interview) to see Sam Beam (Iron & Wine). He performed at an art gallery with Beam, bought a Speedo, and wandered around the Versace mansion area on Ocean Drive. Lightweight anecdote, no musical significance.

  • Miami Fillmore show, Hurricane Irma (2017): Modest Mouse played the Fillmore Miami Beach during Hurricane Irma; only 800 audience members remained before the band evacuated north. Cited in The Ringer interview.

  • “Pistol (A. Cunanan, Miami, Fl. 1996)” (2015): Separate track from Strangers to Ourselves. Florida-set but based on historical crime mythology, not personal experience.
